# Tenochtitlan Builder (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-7-civilizations-of-the-americas, grade 7. Codes: D2.His.1.6-8, D2.Geo.4.6-8
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Build the Mexica capital on an island in Lake Texcoco: lay chinampas to feed a growing city, causeways to the shore, an aqueduct from Chapultepec for fresh water and a long dike to hold back salty floodwater, then test each build against floods, hunger and thirst; a closing gallery shows how the Inca and Maya solved the same problems in mountains and rain forest.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a small island in a wide lake, a population counter and three needs (food, fresh water, flood safety).
  - Core loop: place pieces, then run a test year (a flood, a dry season, a larger city); weak spots fail and the student rebuilds. Guides explain the real works (the dike built under Nezahualcoyotl, the Chapultepec aqueduct, the market at Tlatelolco).
  - The aha or the end state: the city works because it changes the lake on purpose; the epilogue shows the lake drained after 1521 and Mexico City sinking today, then the Inca terraces and Maya reservoirs gallery.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a build-off against a target population.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.1.6-8: the gallery and epilogue place Tenochtitlan in the wider Americas and in what came after.
  - D2.Geo.4.6-8: every build choice changes the lake, and the city's daily life depends on it.
- Not like: river-kingdom (river valleys of the Near East); nations-of-north-america (grade 5 map of Native nations); settle-the-valley (grade 4 settlers).
- Art: generated scenes of the lake city; public domain codex pages and the 1524 Nuremberg map (to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Build Tenochtitlan on Lake Texcoco with chinampas, causeways, an aqueduct and a flood dike, test it against floods, hunger and thirst, then see how the Inca and Maya solved the same problems elsewhere.
- Flags: sensitive (Indigenous history and religion, the conquest and its aftermath, told plainly and with care; fresh review); needs a data source (population estimates, which vary)
