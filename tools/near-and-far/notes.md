## What it teaches

Students arrange a picture using size, placement, overlap and fading to make depth cues agree.
They can explain why an object looks near or far and identify deliberate perspective mistakes in a historical print.

## Key skills

- Make farther objects smaller and closer to the horizon.
- Put the closer object in front when pictures overlap.
- Use pale, soft detail to suggest distance.
- Compare a picture with a side view and revise conflicting clues.
- Explain a perspective mistake using evidence from an artwork.

## Support at home

- Look down a sidewalk together. Ask which distant things look small and where their bottoms seem to meet the sky.
- Hold two leaves at different distances. Move them until one covers the other, and talk about which is nearer.
- Look through a window at a hazy day. Compare the color and detail of near and distant trees.

## A 5-minute start

1. In Valley, tap cow. Drag it downward while keeping its size. Read the two distances in the Side View.
2. Pull its round corner until its back meets the dashed horizon. Size and place now agree.
3. Open Puzzles and try Two Trees. Make one tree small and near the horizon, the other larger and lower.
4. Invite a partner to explain the two depth cues before moving on.

## The three modes

- **Valley:** a free composition using eleven cutouts. Select an object, drag it or resize it, then use Front,
  Back and Fade to arrange the scene. Boats stay on the lake. The Side View makes conflicting size and place cues visible.
- **Puzzles:** eight unlocked postcards introduce near and far, a fence line, overlap, three distances, a barnyard,
  atmospheric fading, boats on water, and elevated balloons. Goals tick live. Before and after compares a solved
  composition with its own flat starting row. Use Previous, Next postcard or the list to choose any puzzle.
- **Mixed-Up Picture:** find eight intentional depth mistakes in Luke Sullivan's engraving after William Hogarth.
  Tap a spot or use Show me one. Whole picture or Escape returns to the full print. There is no timer.

## Talk about it

- What changed in the Side View when you resized without moving?
- Why do the tops of solid fence posts meet the horizon?
- Can a picture have the right sizes but the wrong overlap?
- Why do distant balloons move downward toward the horizon instead of upward?
- Which of Hogarth's mistakes depends on size? Which depends on covering something?

## Good to know

The eye is 1.5 m above the ground in this imaginary scene. Object heights on the readout are scene settings,
not claims about every real tree, barn or animal. Each balloon basket is 40 m above the ground.
The postcard always has the same 16:10 shape, so resizing the window does not change its camera.

Solid allows up to a 1.25 ratio between size and placement distances. Exact agreement puts cow backs and post
tops on the horizon; a Solid label alone does not require pixel-perfect alignment. The hills are a scenic band,
checked by its top band and fading rather than the ordinary object's size formula.

Keyboard: Tab selects controls, then postcard objects. Arrow keys move an object one percent of the postcard;
Shift plus arrows move five percent. Plus or equals grows it five percent; minus shrinks it five percent.
F brings it to the front, B sends it back, M cycles fade. Delete removes objects in Valley.

Valley and solved puzzle numbers are saved only on this device. Clear valley and Clear progress remove their
respective saved data. Each puzzle's current layout and the print hunt are remembered only while the page is open.
Sound controls narration and tones. Read speaks the current hint even with Sound off. Motion off leaves all controls usable.
Clicking an object also focuses it for arrow-key adjustments. The Side View scales its vertical axis to the objects
present, with the eye always at 1.5 m on that scale; horizontal distance uses the labeled logarithmic scale.
Fade advice appears in Valley and Misty Hills. In the other postcards, concentrate on the listed goals.
The requested gravel-ranger voice is listed for future recording; this build uses the kit's browser narration fallback.

## Standards and sources

NCAS VA:Cr2 is practiced by composing and revising postcards. VA:Re7 is practiced by examining and explaining
the deliberately contradictory cues in the Hogarth print.

- [National Core Arts Standards](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/), Visual Arts, Creating 2 and Responding 7.
- [The Met, object 365241](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/365241), Luke Sullivan after William Hogarth,
  *Satire on False Perspective: Frontispiece to Kirby's Perspective*, February 1754. Met Open Access, CC0.
- [Satire on False Perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satire_on_False_Perspective), the eight listed mistakes,
  checked visually against the supplied Met print in this build.
- Joseph D'Amelio, *Perspective Drawing Handbook*, chapter 2, eye level and the ground plane.
- Gene Mittler and Rosalind Ragans, *ArtTalk*, chapter on space, and the Getty's Elements of Art: Space resource.
