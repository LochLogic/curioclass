## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Travel**. Fourteen works of art are pinned on the world map where they were made.
2. Tap a picture to visit it. The first visit stamps the class passport.
3. Ask the **See, Think, Wonder** questions (tap the question to move to the next one). Then press **Find 1**: the picture zooms in on a detail to look for.
4. Ask how it makes everyone feel and tap the faces to tally the class. Finish with the **Try it** movement.

## The modes

- **Travel**: the map. **Surprise trip** picks a place the class hasn't visited yet.
- **Look**: one artwork with who made it, when, what it is made of and why, plus the look-closer spots, questions, feelings and a movement. Arrow keys move between artworks.
- **Compare**: two artworks side by side. What is the same? What is different? **A clue** gives one idea.
- **Passport**: the stamps collected. Stamps are kept on this device; **Start a new passport** clears them for a new class.

## Talking about art from many cultures

- Some of these objects had a purpose beyond being looked at: dancing, honoring an ancestor, wearing, holding food. Talk about what each was for, and that people in those places still make art today.
- The amo (house post) represents an ancestor and is treasured by Māori people; the ci wara is worn in dances that honor farming. Treat them as respected objects. The movement prompts for these two are about the shape and the dance, not pretend play with the figure.
- The cultural text has not yet been reviewed by members of the cultures shown. Teachers with connections to these communities are warmly invited to suggest changes.

## Good to know

- The museum labels use the real titles; the big headings use child-friendly names.
- Dates are rounded to "about how many years ago" for young children.
