# Question Lab (idea card)

- Module: math-6-statistical-variability, grade 6. Codes: 6.SP.A
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good for statistical questions, with a survey run added so center, spread and shape are seen, not only named.
- One idea: Sort questions into statistical or not by asking "would the answers vary?", then send your own question to a simulated grade 6 class and watch the answers pile up into a distribution with a center, a spread and a shape.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a lab bench of question cards and two bins. "How tall am I?" "How tall are the students in my grade?" "How many pets does each student in my class have?" Lab lead Priya: "Would the answers vary?"
  - Sort rounds: the student sorts cards and gives the reason; near misses teach (one person's answer vs many people's answers; a fact vs a question that expects variety).
  - Rewrite rounds: turn "How long did I sleep last night?" into a statistical question.
  - Survey rounds: the student picks or writes a statistical question with a number answer; a simulated class of 30 answers (labeled as simulated), and a dot plot builds dot by dot. The student describes it: where is the middle, how spread out, is it lumped, lopsided or flat?
  - Center and spread: one number for the middle (median or mean) and one for how much it varies; two questions with the same center but very different spreads sit side by side.
  - The aha or the end state: a statistical question expects variety, and variety has a shape, a center and a spread you can describe.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): run the survey with the real class; answers stay on the device, said on screen.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 6.SP.A: telling statistical questions from others by expected variability (6.SP.1); describing a distribution by center, spread and overall shape (6.SP.2); a measure of center as one summarizing number and a measure of variation as how values vary, contrasted on two plots (6.SP.3).
- Not like: box-plot-builder (grade 6 computing and displaying summaries), poll-workshop (grade 11 sampling and bias), lunch-survey-cleanup (grade 6-8 cleaning data).
- Art: people (illustrated) for Priya and a varied simulated class; generated lab-bench scene; dot plots drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Sort questions by asking "would the answers vary?", rewrite non-statistical ones, then send your own question to a simulated grade 6 class and describe the center, spread and shape of the answers.
