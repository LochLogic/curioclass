# Supreme Court Simulator (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-civil-rights-and-liberties, grade 12. Codes: D2.Civ.12.9-12, D2.Civ.10.9-12, D3.4.9-12
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Hear five landmark cases brought by ordinary people (Brown, Gideon, Tinker, New Jersey v. T.L.O., Mahanoy v. B.L.), argue both sides, decide as a justice, then compare your ruling and reasoning with the real Court's majority and dissent.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the bench and five case folders, each opening with the person who brought the case (a Topeka family, a man in a Florida prison who wrote his own petition, students in black armbands, a teen whose purse was searched, a cheerleader's snap).
  - Core loop: read the facts and the law, then build an argument for each side (claim, precedent, counterclaim); illustrated justices question the weak points, and the student refines the claim. Then rule and write a short opinion.
  - Reveal: the real decision, a passage from the majority and the dissent, and what changed afterward.
  - The aha or the end state: a "whose interest" panel shows how each party's interests and the justices' views of rights shaped the outcome, and the student marks where their own view tilted their ruling.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two teams argue, the class rules.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Civ.12.9-12: each case starts with how a person used or challenged the law to address a public issue, and shows what followed.
  - D2.Civ.10.9-12: the whose-interest panel analyzes how personal interests and perspectives shape the application of rights.
  - D3.4.9-12: arguing both sides and answering the justices' questions refines claims and counterclaims, with strengths and limits named.
- Not like: checks-and-balances (branch powers); jury-room (a trial jury deliberating facts); empire-debate (a Senate debate).
- Art: people (illustrated) for justices and the people who brought each case, and a generated courtroom, because the human side of each case leads.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Hear five landmark cases brought by ordinary people, from Brown to a cheerleader's snap, argue both sides, rule as a justice, then compare your reasoning with the real majority and dissent.
- Flags: none (court opinions are public domain; any oral-argument audio needs a license check)
