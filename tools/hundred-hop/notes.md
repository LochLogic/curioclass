## A 5-minute start

1. The frog waits on the **Start** rock. Press **Hop!** and count "one!" together. Keep pressing, or press **Keep hopping** and count along out loud.
2. Switch to **10s**. The frog goes back to the start rock, and now it jumps a whole row at a time: 10, 20, 30... all the way to 100. The tens all sit on lily pads with a flower.
3. Tap any lily pad, like 46, to start counting from there.

## Where will it land?

The numbers hide. The frog sits on a pad the class can see, and the sign shows how many hops it will take.

1. Ask: what number is the frog on?
2. Press **Go!** and count along together as it hops. The dots on the sign fill in, one for each hop.
3. Ask: where did it land? Let the class call it out.
4. Press **Reveal**. The number appears, then every pad it passed lights up so the class can check their counting.

**New puzzle** picks a random start and number of hops. Turn on **Show the tens** for a helping hand. You can also tap a pad to choose the start, and use **−** and **+** to set the hops.

## The big idea

Counting to 100 is about patterns as much as memory. After every 9 comes a new ten: 19 to 20, 29 to 30. Counting by tens (10, 20, 30...) is a shortcut that follows the same pattern as 1, 2, 3.

## Talk about it

- What number comes after 29? After 39? What do you notice?
- When we count by tens, which pads does the frog land on? What do they all have?
- The frog is on 52 and hops 3 times. Where will it land? How do you know?

## Good to know

- The path snakes back and forth across the pond, ten pads to a row, so the frog always hops to the very next pad. 11 sits right under 10.
- Numbers are read aloud with a recorded natural voice. Turn off **Say the numbers** to let the class do all the counting.
- Teacher-led on a projector or smartboard.
