## What it teaches
Students can account for carbon, hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen in biological reactions, compare energy captured as ATP with heat and energy retained in fermentation products, and explain seasonal carbon dioxide changes.

## Key skills
- Trace the same atoms between air, water and living things.
- Read a balanced reaction as a transfer of matter.
- Compare glucose use for equal amounts of cell work.
- Use a seasonal graph as evidence for a biology explanation.
- Distinguish a simplified model from the many steps in a real cell.

## Support at home
- Look at a tree together and ask where the material for its growth comes from. Explain that carbon arrives in carbon dioxide from air; soil minerals still matter.
- When bread dough rises, talk about yeast turning sugar into ethanol and carbon dioxide. The trapped gas helps the dough expand.
- Notice seasonal plant growth and discuss how plants both photosynthesize and respire.

## Five-minute start
Open Leaf by day and press Photosynthesize twice. Watch Air lose carbon and Sugar store gain it. Post Respire once and compare the energy columns. Then set Oxygen switch to Work 32, press Tick, turn oxygen off and press Tick again. The same ATP costs sixteen times as much glucose in the model.

## Modes
Ledger supplies balanced reactions. Each scene has separate opening stocks; its entries join the same reaction ledger. Clear ledger empties postings and restores those stocks. Night restores a minimum of two glucose when entered, representing the day's production. Deer and dough start with twelve glucose; Soil starts with twelve glucose equivalents in dead leaves. These are explicit model stocks, not measured biological capacities.

Ledger totals sum the input and output flows of all postings, not matter remaining in accounts. A reused atom can therefore be counted in several successive rows. Energy columns are kJ per mole of reaction units per posting; the totals add those postings. The account cards show the remaining stocks separately.

Each C bar shows that account's share of all carbon in its scene. Respiration can therefore grow Air's bar as carbon leaves sugar or dead leaves; it does not saturate at an arbitrary initial Air stock.

The wood card asks for a prediction, without scoring. Its mass breakdown describes glucose, not complete wood composition. One mole of glucose is 180 g using rounded atomic masses. Oxygen gas from photosynthesis comes from water; the atom animation includes the gross water exchange that the compact net equation cancels.

Oxygen switch uses a continuous external fuel supply. Work ranges from 8 to 64 ATP per tick. Counters accumulate actual ticks in each oxygen condition, not hypothetical paired runs. Keep work and tick counts equal for a fair comparison. Amounts are mole equivalents; energy is in kJ. Muscle and yeast can share a column, with their separate waste products listed. Run takes ten ticks at 0.4 seconds each; settings apply on the next tick. Re-pressing Run restarts the ten-tick sequence, and Clear cancels it.

Planet's books plots 2023 NOAA monthly means only. Swing subtracts each station's own baseline, using the listed 2023 annual mean and half the 2024 minus 2022 annual difference. It is a seasonal comparison, not a trend or climate-policy exercise. Chart axes stay fixed while stations toggle. Show table exposes all 36 source rows and computed swings, with horizontal scrolling inside the table.

## Talk questions
- Why can a leaf respire at night while photosynthesis stops?
- What is still stored in fermentation products after ATP has been made?
- Why does a large drop in atmospheric carbon dioxide appear in northern summer?
- How can one carbon atom pass through all four Earth-system spheres?

## Good to know
Real cells use dozens of steps. ATP yield varies, about 30 to 32 per glucose here; the model uses 32 and standard ATP free energy of 30.5 kJ per mole. Aerobic glucose bookkeeping is 2,870 = 976 ATP + 1,894 heat. Muscle fermentation is 2,870 = 2,674 retained + 61 ATP + 135 heat; yeast is 2,870 = 2,635 retained + 61 ATP + 174 heat. These are simplified standard free-energy allocations, not measured cellular heating rates.

Sucrose construction has qualitative energy only. Alanine construction is labeled an atom summary, not one real reaction; plant nitrogen from soil nitrate and deer dietary nitrogen are represented as NH3 solely for counting. The nitrogen supply account stays visible in both scenes, so its atoms have a visible source. Their energy cells are not quantified, and totals explicitly disclose this. Fermentation products are kept in the Body account; the ledger identifies them as lactic acid or ethanol. Soil respiration sends water products to the Soil account.

The glucose hexagon is a fuel token, not glucose's molecular structure. The passport's C marker follows the current stop; connected stamps record a possible route, without a score. Oxygen counters label mole equivalents at every size. At most sixteen glucose icons are drawn, with any remainder written below them. Waste particles remain decorative, capped at sixty-four; numerical products are authoritative. The sixteen-to-one fuel comparison applies to equal ATP output, not unequal cumulative runs.

The carbon passport is a possible journey, not a claim that every atom follows it. The final route uses volcanic release. A shell is built by life; after burial its carbon can become part of the geosphere. Carbonate equations show charges as well as balanced atoms.

## Accessibility and privacy
All controls work with keyboard and show focus. Motion off stops moving atoms and idle light. A ledger posting keeps its 1.5 second timing; pressing a second entry completes the first immediately. Run timing stays four seconds. Sound controls narration; Read speaks the current instruction. No student data is saved or transmitted. Only the kit's shared motion preference stays on the device. No recordings were made during this Build; narration uses browser speech with the calm-professor script prepared for later recording.
