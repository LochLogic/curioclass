# Cold War Crisis Room (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-10-the-cold-war, grade 10. Codes: D2.His.1.9-12, D2.His.5.9-12
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Advise the White House through October 1962, then replay the same thirteen days from Moscow and Havana, choosing among the real options each side had and seeing how each side's history and fears shaped what it could see.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: reconnaissance photos of missile sites on a table and a calendar at October 16, 1962: "The President wants your advice."
  - Core loop: each day brings new intelligence and a decision among real options (air strike, invasion, blockade, quiet talks); illustrated advisers argue, the student chooses, and the record then shows what was really chosen and why.
  - Second and third runs: the same days seen from the Soviet leadership (US missiles in Turkey, a nuclear gap) and from Cuba (the Bay of Pigs, fear of invasion, sovereignty), plus the submarine B-59 moment at sea.
  - The aha or the end state: a three-column view of one event from three capitals, and a short write-up: what about 1962 made this crisis unique (the arsenals, the recent Bay of Pigs, the secret Turkey deal), and how each side's past shaped its choices.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class splits into three delegations.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.1.9-12: day-by-day context shows how the moment and the wider Cold War shaped the options.
  - D2.His.5.9-12: three replays from three capitals show how history shaped each perspective then, and how each country remembers it now.
- Not like: space-race (grade 11, US and Soviet milestones); alliance-web (a cause chain, not decisions); supreme-court-simulator (courtroom roleplay).
- Art: public domain US government reconnaissance photos (checked) plus people (illustrated) advisers, because real evidence sits on the table.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Advise leaders through the Cuban Missile Crisis day by day using their real options, then replay the thirteen days from Moscow and Havana to see how each side's history shaped its choices.
- Flags: none
