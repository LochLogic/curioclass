# Wave Tank (idea card)

- Module: science-8-waves, grade 8. Codes: MS-PS4-1, MS-PS4-2, MS-PS4-3
- Format / family: sandbox / none. Fit: good for MS-PS4-1 and MS-PS4-2
- One idea: Make waves in a water tank, on a rope and with a light beam, change amplitude and frequency to see how they shape the wave and its energy, then set materials in the way to see waves bounce back, pass through or fade out.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a glass-sided tank with a paddle, amplitude and frequency sliders, a ruler, a timer and a cork floating halfway.
  - Core loop: wiggle the paddle and measure wavelength, frequency and height; the cork bobs but does not travel, so waves move energy, not water. A wave-energy meter shows energy growing with amplitude, and the student plots the data. Switch to the rope, then to a light beam. Drop blocks into the path: a wall, a sponge and a gap for water waves; a mirror, glass, water and black cloth for light; tile and foam for sound.
  - The aha or the end state: a summary table the student fills in from their tests: which materials reflect, transmit or absorb which waves, and how amplitude relates to energy.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): free sandbox with no goals at all.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-PS4-1: the measurements and plot give a simple math model of a wave, with energy rising with amplitude.
  - MS-PS4-2: the material blocks model how light and mechanical waves are reflected, absorbed or transmitted.
  - MOVE MS-PS4-3: new tool: "Copy of a Copy" (grade 8), send the same message as a smooth analog wave and as digital pulses through a chain of noisy relays, and see which arrives intact.
- Not like: secret-code-signals (grade 4 codes); spectrum-explorer (grade 11 EM spectrum); field-lines (grade 11 fields).
- Art: drawn in code (waves must be exact), with a generated lab backdrop.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Make waves in a tank, on a rope and with a light beam; change amplitude and frequency and measure what they do, then set mirrors, glass, foam and tile in the way to see waves reflect, pass or fade.
- Flags: none
