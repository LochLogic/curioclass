# Walk in Their Shoes

## One idea
Replay the same small moment in each character's shoes and find the one thing each of them knew that nobody else did, then choose what happens next and see how it lands on everyone.

## Not like
- not-yet-gym: it trains one creature through challenges with goals and unlocks; this is a short storybook with no goals, no score and no growth meter, where the mechanic is switching viewpoint on one fixed moment.
- feelings-faces (K-2): it reads one face's eyebrows, eyes and mouth; here the faces only show what others could see, and the point is the hidden reason behind them.
- feelings-weather-report and calm-corner (K-2): those name and calm your own feelings; this never asks about the student's own feelings, only about fictional characters'.

## Who and how
- Grades 3-5, Social and Emotional Learning, module Perspective Taking. Format: Story (branching).
- Driver: either. Teacher on a projector for a morning meeting (class votes on the choice), or pairs on a laptop.
- Minutes: 10-20 (one story is about 7 minutes; three stories in the box).
- Standards: CASEL.Social Awareness (understanding the perspectives of others).
- No student text input. Nothing personal is asked. Nothing is saved except the kit's Motion setting.

## Screens and modes
No mode buttons. Two screens.

**1. Story shelf** (start screen). Three book covers (each story's painted setting with its three characters), side by side (stacked at 375 wide): "The Muddy Sleeve", "The Saved Seat", "The Last Goal". Each cover shows its three animal characters and a one-line teaser. Click a cover to open it. Header: title "Walk in Their Shoes", Motion, Sound, Read.

**2. Story view.**
- Stage (left or top): the scene for the current beat: the painted setting plus the three characters with their expression for this beat. Under the scene, beat dots (1 to 5) and buttons **Back** and **Next**.
- In **Narrator** view the narration line sits in a caption strip under the scene. In a character's view that character gets a soft spotlight ring, the other two are shown at 85 percent opacity, and the character's thought bubble appears above their head with their thought for this beat. The caption strip still shows the narration in small type.
- **Shoe rack** (a row under the stage, always visible): four buttons. **Narrator** (an open book icon) and one button per character showing a painting of that character's shoes, with the name under them ("Pip's shoes"). The selected one is raised and outlined. A small tracker beside the rack: "Shoes tried: 1 of 3" (counts characters whose view the student opened in this story; Narrator does not count).
- Panel (right or bottom):
  - Hint paragraph (`hint`), changes with context (lines in Content, "UI lines").
  - **Only I know** card: in a character's view, that character's secret (Content). Hidden in Narrator view. Shown from beat 1 on, so the secret is the first thing a student finds.
  - **Guess first** box: only on beat 3 (the moment) in a character's view, before that thought is revealed. Twelve feeling chips; pick up to two; then **See what they think** (label uses the name: "See what Juniper thinks") or **Just show me**.
  - **What next?** box: on beat 4 in any view. The decision question and three choice buttons (Content). Picking one goes to beat 5.
  - **Talk it over** button: opens a card with that story's two discussion questions plus the general question. **Close** hides it.
  - **Start over** (clears guesses, tracker and choice for this story, back to beat 1 in Narrator view) and **Stories** (back to the shelf).
- Beat 5 (after a choice): the ending narration, and each character's ending thought in their view. Extra buttons in the panel: **Try another choice** (back to beat 4, choice cleared) and **Stories**.

## Content
Feeling chips (one set for all stories): happy, proud, worried, embarrassed, frustrated, left out, confused, sorry, hopeful, nervous, relieved, annoyed.

Expressions: calm, happy, sad, upset, worried, surprised, listed below per beat as (expression). Each character is painted only in the expressions its story uses (see Art).

### Story 1: The Muddy Sleeve
Teaser: "A borrowed jacket comes back muddy."
Setting: school playground, autumn. Characters and shoes: Juniper the fox (red rain boots), Pip the rabbit (green sneakers), Otto the bear (big blue high-tops).
Secrets (Only I know):
- Juniper: "My grandma gave me this jacket for my birthday. I promised her I'd take good care of it."
- Pip: "I slipped in the mud because I was helping a little kid who fell off the slide."
- Otto: "I saw the whole thing. Pip ran over to help that kid before anyone else did."
Beats (narration; then Juniper / Pip / Otto thought; expressions J, P, O):
1. N: "It was a cold, windy recess. Pip forgot a jacket, so Juniper lent hers." Juniper: "I hope Pip is careful with it." Pip: "Juniper is so nice. I'm finally warm!" Otto: "Brr. I wish I had a jacket like that." (happy, happy, calm)
2. N: "Near the slide, a little kid tumbled into a puddle. Pip ran over." Juniper: "Where is Pip running? I can't see from here." Pip: "Oh no, that kid is crying. I'll help them up." Otto: "Pip got there fast. Nobody else even moved." (calm, worried, surprised)
3. N: "When the bell rang, Pip gave the jacket back. One sleeve was covered in mud." Juniper: "My grandma's jacket! It's ruined, and I'll have to tell her." (own words: worried, frustrated) Pip: "Uh oh. I didn't even notice the sleeve. Juniper looks so upset." (own words: embarrassed, sorry) Otto: "Juniper doesn't know why it's muddy. Should I say something?" (own words: nervous, confused) (upset, worried, worried)
4. N: "Everyone stood still for a moment. Juniper had to decide what to say." Juniper: "I want to yell. But I also want to know what happened." Pip: "Please don't be mad. I can explain." Otto: "This feels awkward." (upset, worried, worried)
Decision: "What should Juniper do?"
- A "Yell: You ruined it!" Ending N: "Juniper shouted and stomped inside. Pip didn't get a chance to explain." Juniper: "I'm still upset, and now I feel bad for yelling too." Pip: "I wanted to say sorry, but she didn't let me." Otto: "I didn't speak up. Maybe I should have."
- B "Ask: What happened to the sleeve?" Ending N: "Pip explained about the little kid, and Otto nodded. They found wipes in the classroom." Juniper: "Oh. Pip was helping someone. The mud mostly came off, too." Pip: "I'm glad she asked. I'll be more careful next time." Otto: "I'm glad I could back Pip up."
- C "Say nothing and walk away" Ending N: "Juniper took the jacket and walked off without a word. Pip wondered all afternoon." Juniper: "I'm still upset, and I don't even know what happened." Pip: "Is she mad at me? I wish I knew." Otto: "Nobody talked about it. It felt weird all day."
Talk it over: "What did Otto know that Juniper didn't?" "If you were Pip, what would you want Juniper to know?"

### Story 2: The Saved Seat
Teaser: "A new kid looks for a place to sit."
Setting: school lunchroom. Characters and shoes: Tamsin the heron, new to school (yellow striped slip-ons), Rory the badger (orange sneakers), Wren the owl (purple boots).
Secrets:
- Tamsin: "At my old school I always sat with my cousin. Today I don't know anyone."
- Rory: "I promised Wren I'd save her a seat. She's coming late from band practice."
- Wren: "I asked Rory to save my seat because I get nervous walking in late."
Beats (Tamsin / Rory / Wren; expressions T, R, W):
1. N: "It was Tamsin's first day. She carried her tray into the noisy lunchroom." Tamsin: "So many tables. Where do I go?" Rory: "Same table as always. I'll keep this spot for Wren." Wren: "Band practice is running long today." (Wren is shown at the band room door, small, in the corner of the scene) (worried, happy, calm)
2. N: "Tamsin saw an empty seat next to Rory and walked over." Tamsin: "That seat is empty. Maybe they'll let me sit there." Rory: "Who's that? I don't think I know her." Wren: "I hope Rory remembers my seat." (worried, surprised, worried)
3. N: "Rory said, That seat's taken. Tamsin walked away fast." Tamsin: "Nobody wants me here. I should have stayed home." (own words: left out, embarrassed) Rory: "Wait, was she new? I didn't mean it like that." (own words: confused, sorry) Wren: "Here I come. I'm glad my seat is ready." (own words: relieved, hopeful) (sad, worried, calm)
4. N: "Wren sat down. Across the room, Tamsin ate alone. Rory had to decide what to do." Tamsin: "I'll just look at my food until lunch is over." Rory: "I kept my promise. So why do I feel bad?" Wren: "Who is that sitting by herself?" (sad, worried, surprised)
Decision: "What should Rory do?"
- A "Go invite Tamsin over" Ending N: "Rory walked over and said, Sorry, that seat was saved. Want to sit with us? There's room on the end." Tamsin: "They weren't saying no to me. It was just a saved seat." Rory: "Keeping a promise and being kind can both happen." Wren: "Now I know someone new, too."
- B "Stay put and keep eating" Ending N: "Rory stayed put. Tamsin ate alone and left early." Tamsin: "Maybe tomorrow will be better. Maybe not." Rory: "I keep thinking about her face." Wren: "Rory seems quiet. Is something wrong?"
- C "Tell Wren and decide together" Ending N: "Rory whispered to Wren. Wren said, Let's both go say hi." Tamsin: "Two kids came over to say hi. My shoulders relaxed." Rory: "Wren made it easier to go." Wren: "I know how it feels to walk in alone."
Talk it over: "Why did Rory say the seat was taken? What did Tamsin think it meant?" "Was Rory wrong to keep the promise? Was there a way to do both?"

### Story 3: The Last Goal
Teaser: "One goal decides the game."
Setting: grassy soccer field at the end of the day. Characters and shoes: Bea the turtle, goalie (cleats with green laces), Max the goat (white cleats), Dot the hedgehog (pink cleats).
Secrets:
- Bea: "My glove came loose right before the last kick. I tried to fix it, but there was no time."
- Max: "My big brother came to watch. I really wanted to win in front of him."
- Dot: "I lost the ball right before that kick. That's how the other team got it."
Beats (Bea / Max / Dot; expressions B, M, D):
1. N: "The score was tied, one to one, with one minute left." Bea: "Stay ready. Watch the ball." Max: "My brother is watching. We have to win!" Dot: "Here comes the ball. I've got it." (calm, happy, happy)
2. N: "Dot lost the ball. The other team kicked hard at the goal." Bea: "My glove is loose! Not now!" Max: "No, no, no! Stop it, Bea!" Dot: "Oh no, that was my ball." (worried, surprised, worried)
3. N: "The ball slipped past Bea. The whistle blew. Max shouted, We lost because of you!" Bea: "I tried so hard. Everyone thinks it's my fault." (own words: embarrassed, frustrated) Max: "We were so close. My brother saw us lose." (own words: frustrated, annoyed) Dot: "It started with me, not Bea. Should I say so?" (own words: sorry, nervous) (sad, upset, worried)
4. N: "The team walked off the field. Max had to decide what to say next." Bea: "I just want to go home." Max: "I'm still mad. But was it really Bea?" Dot: "If I speak up, will Max be mad at me instead?" (sad, upset, worried)
Decision: "What should Max do?"
- A "Keep blaming Bea" Ending N: "Max kept grumbling about Bea all the way to the bus." Bea: "Maybe I shouldn't play goalie anymore." Max: "Blaming her didn't make me feel any better." Dot: "I never said it was my ball. I feel bad."
- B "Say: Sorry I yelled. We all tried." Ending N: "Max stopped and said sorry. Dot said, I lost the ball first. Bea showed them her loose glove." Bea: "So it wasn't only me. I'll check my glove next time." Max: "Losing still stinks, but we're still a team." Dot: "I'm glad I said it. It felt like a weight came off."
- C "Walk off and stay quiet" Ending N: "Max walked off alone. Nobody talked on the way to the bus." Bea: "Max is still mad at me. I'm sure of it." Max: "I didn't really mean what I said. I just didn't say so." Dot: "Everybody went quiet. I don't like it."
Talk it over: "Max's brother was watching. How might that change how Max acted?" "What did Dot know that Max didn't?"

General talk question (all stories, last line of the card): "Think of a time a story looked different once you knew more. You don't have to share it."

### UI lines
- shelf: "Pick a story. You'll get to step into each character's shoes."
- story start (beat 1, Narrator view): "Press Next to read, or click someone's shoes to step into them."
- in shoes, beats 1, 2, 4: "You're in [Name]'s shoes. Read the thought bubble and the Only I know card."
- beat 3 in shoes, before reveal: "How might [Name] feel right now? Pick up to two words, or just look."
- reveal with overlap: "You and [Name] both picked [word]." Reveal with no overlap: "[Name] used different words: [w1] and [w2]." (no right or wrong wording anywhere)
- beat 3 after reveal: "Now try someone else's shoes at this same moment."
- beat 4, tracker under 2: "Before you choose, try at least one other pair of shoes." (a nudge only; choices are never locked)
- beat 4, tracker 2 or more: "What should [Name] do? Pick one and see what happens."
- beat 5: "Step into each pair of shoes to see how this ending felt to them."
- all 3 shoes tried (shown once per story, in the tracker): "You've walked in everyone's shoes."

## Interactions in detail
- Shelf: covers are buttons (keyboard focusable, Enter opens). Opening a story starts beat 1 in Narrator view with a clean state.
- Back is disabled on beat 1. Next is disabled on beat 4 (the choice moves you on) and on beat 5. Left and Right arrows do Back and Next. Keys 1 to 4 pick Narrator and the three shoes in rack order.
- Switching shoes keeps the beat. Switching view stops any speech and queues that view's line for the beat (thought in a character view, narration in Narrator view).
- Beat 3 in a character view: the thought bubble is hidden behind a "?" bubble until the student presses See what [Name] thinks or Just show me. Chips toggle; a third pick replaces the oldest. Pressing See with no chips works like Just show me. After reveal, the chips become plain text showing the student's picks beside the character's own words; matching words are bold with a small brand-colored dot (no check marks, no green or red). Once revealed, that character stays revealed for this story, even after Back.
- The tracker counts a character when their view is opened at any beat. It never blocks anything.
- Beat 4 choice: in teacher use the class votes out loud; nothing counts votes. Choosing goes to beat 5 in the current view. Beat 5 shows the ending scene: same setting, expressions from the ending per choice: Story 1 A: J upset, P sad, O worried; B: J calm, P happy, O happy; C: J upset, P worried, O sad. Story 2 A: all happy; B: T sad, R worried, W surprised; C: all happy. Story 3 A: B sad, M upset, D worried; B: B calm, M calm, D happy; C: B sad, M sad, D worried.
- Try another choice: back to beat 4, keeps reveals and tracker. Start over clears everything for that story. Stories goes to the shelf; reopening a story starts clean.
- Talk it over card overlays the panel, Close or Escape returns focus to the button.
- Motion off: no bubble pop or spotlight pulse, scene changes cut instead of fade. Pacing unchanged.
- Nothing is timed and nothing is scored. No end screen, no stars, no confetti.
