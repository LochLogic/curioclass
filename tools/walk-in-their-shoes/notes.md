## A 5-minute start

1. Open **The Muddy Sleeve**. Press **Next** twice and read each line with the class: Pip borrows Juniper's jacket and runs to help a little kid who fell in a puddle.
2. On page 3 (the sleeve comes back muddy), click **Otto's shoes**. Before the thought bubble opens, ask the class how Otto might feel. Pick up to two words, then press **See what Otto thinks**. Read the **Only I know** card: Otto saw the whole thing.
3. Click **Juniper's shoes**, then **Pip's shoes**, at the same moment. Ask: what does each of them know that the others don't?
4. Press **Next** to page 4. The class votes out loud on what Juniper should do, then click that choice and step into each pair of shoes to see how the ending felt to everyone.

## The two screens

- **Story shelf:** three short storybooks, each with three animal characters and about 7 minutes long. The Muddy Sleeve (a borrowed jacket), The Saved Seat (a new kid at lunch) and The Last Goal (a lost soccer game).
- **Story view:** five pages. Pages 1 to 3 build to the moment, page 4 is the choice, page 5 is the ending. The **shoe rack** under the scene switches between the **Narrator** and each character's view without changing the page. In a character's view you see their thought bubble and their **Only I know** card, the one thing they know that nobody else does.
- **Guess first** (page 3 only, in a character's view): twelve feeling words. Students can pick up to two (a third pick replaces the oldest) or press **Just show me**. When the thought opens, the tool shows their words beside the character's own words and marks any that match with a dot. Nothing is graded: different words are just different words.
- **What next?** (page 4): three choices. Any choice can be picked at any time. The "Shoes tried" counter only nudges students to look through at least one other pair of shoes first.
- **Try another choice** goes back to page 4 and keeps the thoughts already opened. **Start over** clears the story. **Stories** goes back to the shelf.

## Talk about it

Each story's **Talk it over** card has two questions for that story plus one for everyone:

- The Muddy Sleeve: What did Otto know that Juniper didn't? If you were Pip, what would you want Juniper to know?
- The Saved Seat: Why did Rory say the seat was taken? What did Tamsin think it meant? Was Rory wrong to keep the promise? Was there a way to do both?
- The Last Goal: Max's brother was watching. How might that change how Max acted? What did Dot know that Max didn't?
- All stories: Think of a time a story looked different once you knew more. Students don't have to share.

## Good to know

- Social Awareness in the CASEL framework includes understanding the perspectives of others and empathizing with them. The tool practices that with fictional characters only. It never asks students about their own feelings, families or classmates.
- If a story brings up something real for a student, or a student seems upset, your school counselor is the right next step.
- Morning meeting on a projector: the class votes out loud on page 4 and you click the winning choice. Nothing counts votes. Pairs on a laptop work just as well.
- Keys: Left and Right arrows go Back and Next; keys 1 to 4 pick Narrator and the three pairs of shoes; Escape closes Talk it over.
- One storyteller voice reads every line, as in a read-aloud. Each page's narration or thought plays when you open it. **Read** speaks the hint, the Only I know card and the thought bubble (or the narration). **Sound** turns the automatic reading off.
- Nothing is typed, scored, timed or saved, apart from the shared Motion setting. Motion off removes the bubble pop, the spotlight pulse and the page fade; the pacing stays the same.
- The characters are painted illustrations (OpenAI gpt-image) and the settings, shoes and props are painted with FLUX.2 [klein], all edited for Curio Class. No people anywhere: every character is an animal.
