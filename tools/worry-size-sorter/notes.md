## What it teaches
Students size a worry by the help it needs, practice four coping tools, notice their own before-and-after rating,
and set a week goal. Big worries always go to a trusted adult.

## Key skills
- Choose help for a written situation without judging another person's feelings.
- Notice five body clues that can come with worry.
- Practice breathing, helpful self-talk, muscle relaxation and a worry box.
- Make a plan, check practice days and reflect on what helped or got in the way.

## Support at home
- Practice a short comfortable breathing square together when things are calm. Never strain to hold a breath.
- Make a worry box with scrap paper. Pick about ten minutes with a grown-up to open it and talk, away from bedtime.
- Help your child choose a time to practice. Ask what helped, rather than whether they got a lower meter number.

## A five-minute start
Open Sort. Invite the student to move one card into a basket, then read the help beside it.
Explain that Small and Medium can differ for different people. Choosing an adult is always allowed.
Open Tools and practice one tool together, using the before and after meter as a personal rating.

## Modes
- Sort: thirteen fictional written situations. The first two are one Small and one Medium. Big cards never remain outside Big.
- Body clues: five rings on the illustrated child open facts and tools to try. Expand the help card in compact views.
- Tools: rate before, practice, then rate after. A tool need not lower a rating to be worth practicing. Stop is available during timed practice.
- My week: choose a tool, time, helper and reason. A saved helper appears first. Check today or forgotten past days.
  Future days stay locked. Day eight or Finish my week opens reflection. No score, timer challenge or streak reward.

## Talk questions
What changes the help a worry needs? Why is telling never wrong? Which kind of reply feels useful: Facts, Plan or Brave?
Keep discussion about the written cards. Never ask a student to disclose their own worry to the class.

## Sensitive content and disclosures
NHES.S1 and S7 here include abuse, being hurt, online exploitation, family illness and persistent worry.
The five Big cards may prompt a real disclosure. If a student tells you about abuse, being hurt or not being safe,
stay calm, thank them, do not promise to keep it secret, and follow your school's reporting procedure.
US reporting duties depend on state law and staff role. Follow applicable reporting law as well as school policy;
an internal report to a supervisor may not satisfy a duty to report directly to the designated agency.
You never need to ask students to share their worries aloud. Help is one trusted adult, with another told if the first does not help.
The on-screen emergency instruction is US-specific: danger right now, call 911.

## Good to know
No typing, accounts, data collection or network calls. Only the goal, start date, checks and helpers save under
`wss-week`; the kit also saves Motion. Storage failure leaves the chart usable for this visit and changes the privacy line.
The meter is a self-report, not a diagnosis, quiz, measurement of health or proof that a student did a physical exercise.
A lower rating reports feeling calmer; it does not prove the tool caused it. An early Stop is marked as an unfinished practice.
An early week reflection says "so far" and never assumes missed practice. Reflection chips are optional.
The chart records the student's report of practice, not verified behavior. Browser time controls the calendar.
Motion off removes movement but keeps breathing and relaxation timing. The same child demonstrates each pose.
Narration uses the kit's browser voice fallback in this Build-only version. The friendly-dad voice manifest is ready for later recording.
