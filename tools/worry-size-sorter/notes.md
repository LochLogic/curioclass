## What it teaches
Students explore how a worry's need for help can change, notice body clues, and practice four coping tools.
Big situations bring up trusted-adult help. This is an open-ended Curiosity with no assigned goal or finish.

## Key skills
- Choose help for a fictional situation without judging another person's feelings.
- Notice five body clues that can come with worry.
- Try comfortable breathing, helpful self-talk, muscle relaxation and a worry box.
- Describe a before-and-after rating without treating a lower number as a target or proof of an effect.

## Support at home
- Try the comfortable breathing square together when things are calm. Stop rather than strain.
- Make a worry box with scrap paper. Pick about ten minutes with a grown-up to open it and talk, away from bedtime.
  YoungMinds advises against using the box just before bed; NHS Every Mind Matters supplies the ten-minute option.
- If a Big card brings up something real, listen calmly, thank your child, and contact the school counselor or doctor.
  For concerns about abuse, Childhelp National Child Abuse Hotline: 1-800-422-4453. It offers support;
  it does not replace a report required by law or an emergency response.

## A five-minute start
Move a written worry into a basket and explore the help beside it. Small and Medium are flexible;
choosing an adult is always allowed. Try Body clues or any coping tool in any order. Nothing needs to be finished.

## Modes
- Sort: thirteen fictional situations on a continuing desk. Big cards settle into Big. After the last card,
  Next worry returns to the first with no end panel. Each card has at most one placement in the baskets.
- Body clues: five rings around the child point to the head, chest, breathing area, tummy and hands.
  Try buttons are derived from the same clue data, including the tummy, and open the named tool.
- Tools: a personal rating before and after, with breathing, helpful replies, muscle relaxation and a worry box.
  Stop is immediate. Box breathing defaults to three rounds, then an optional fourth. Count 4 takes 48 seconds;
  count 3 takes 36 seconds. Motion off changes movement, not timing. No lower rating is required.
- Manual: the lower-left drawer explains the controls, readouts, limits, sources and permissions.

## Talk questions
What changes the help a worry needs? Which reply feels useful: Facts, Plan or Brave?
Keep discussion about the written situations. Never ask students to share their own worries aloud.

## Sensitive content and disclosures
NHES.S1 and S7 here include abuse, being hurt, online exploitation, family illness and persistent worry.
The five Big cards may prompt a disclosure. Stay calm and thank the child. Listen; do not question for details
or investigate. Write down the child's own words and tell them who you will tell next. Do not promise secrecy.
Follow applicable reporting law as well as school procedure. US duties vary by state and staff role;
an internal report to a supervisor may not satisfy a duty to report directly to the designated agency.

For an online photo request or a shared image of a child, report to NCMEC's CyberTipline
(report.cybertip.org, 1-800-843-5678) as well as through your school; Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org)
can help limit sharing of eligible nude, partially nude or sexually explicit images taken before age 18 on
participating platforms. It cannot guarantee removal everywhere. No such images should be downloaded for this purpose.
These routes are adult-facing only. The settled on-screen instruction remains: Danger right now? Call 911 (US-specific).

## Good to know
The meter is a self-report, not a diagnosis, health measurement or verified physical practice. It has no pass threshold.
A lower number describes a report, not a proven effect of the tool. An early Stop is labelled as a check-in.
The tool never collects a typed disclosure, uses accounts or makes network requests.
Helper-tool choices save under `wss-helpers`; Motion uses the shared kit setting. The privacy line appears at the save
control, including a blocked-storage message. Old `wss-week` records are ignored and never read or written by this version.
Read speaks the current registered hint and relevant content. Only the first quiet hint may speak automatically;
subsequent voice is on demand, following the Curiosity brief. The manifest remains ready for recordings.
S6 weekly goal/check-off/reflection activities were removed under the user's Curiosity instruction. This version makes
no claim to teach the S6 goal-setting indicators. The shared curriculum mapping is outside this edit boundary.
