# Decades Explorer (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-11-modern-america, grade 11. Codes: D2.His.2.9-12, D2.His.7.9-12
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Walk through the 1980s, 1990s, 2000s and 2010s room by room, comparing how each decade saw itself at the time with how it is remembered and argued about now, while change and continuity charts track daily life across all four.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a hallway of four rooms, each styled after its decade; a shelf of everyday objects (a rotary phone, a pager, a flip phone, a smartphone) hints at the theme.
  - Core loop: in each room open drawers by theme (politics, technology, economy, culture, events); each item pairs a "then" source (a headline, a poll, an ad, a news photo) with a "now" view (a retrospective, a later poll, how the event is taught). Change charts across the hallway track real measures (internet use, median income, cost of college, who votes).
  - Memory check: for each decade, the student marks where today's view differs from the then view and why (hindsight, nostalgia, new evidence, new values).
  - The aha or the end state: the student picks one thread through all four rooms and writes what changed, what stayed the same, and how our present shapes the way we tell it.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): students add a room for the 2020s.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.9-12: the change charts and the chosen thread analyze change and continuity across four decades.
  - D2.His.7.9-12: the then and now pairs and memory check explain how present perspectives shape interpretations of the past.
- Not like: space-race (Cold War milestones); missing-voices-archive (an archive's gaps); behind-the-podcast (one secondary work).
- Art: generated scenes (four decade rooms and objects), with no copyrighted music or photos; period sources quoted briefly or public domain, because the rooms set the mood without licensing risk.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Walk four decade rooms from the 1980s to the 2010s, pairing how each saw itself at the time with how it is remembered now, while charts track what changed and what stayed the same.
- Flags: sensitive (recent politics; balance checked by a fresh review agent); needs a data source (Census, Pew and BLS series, checked)
