## What it teaches
Students explain why higher interest rates help a saver while raising borrowing costs. They distinguish a signed fixed rate from a variable rate and calculate whether a made-up business loan leaves enough earnings.

## Key skills
- Compare interest earned and interest paid at different rates.
- Recognize what signing a fixed-rate loan locks in.
- Compare minimum payments with a larger monthly card payment.
- Multiply a loan by its annual rate and subtract the interest from earnings.
- Predict outcomes using each neighbor's situation.

## Support at home
- Make up a $100 loan at two different annual rates. Ask which costs more and work out the difference together.
- Talk about why someone saving and someone borrowing might feel differently about the same rate change.
- Use fictional amounts to compare a fixed agreement with a changing rate. No family financial information is needed.

## A 5-minute start
Open Street. Turn the bank-door dial and watch all four neighbors. Pin a low rate, then raise it. Sign Keisha's loan and turn again. Compare Luis's two payment plans.

## Modes
Street is an open model. All add-ons, neighbors and amounts are invented. Abe earns monthly compound interest on $2,000 for 60 months with no deposits. Keisha's $18,000 car loan has 60 monthly payments. Her rate locks only when signed.

Luis owes $1,500 from a car repair, makes no new charges and pays each month. The selected rate is held constant over repayment. Interest, payments and balances round monthly using the specified ties-to-even calculation, which can differ by a few cents from other rounding conventions. Minimum is the larger of $25 or 1% of the remaining balance plus that month's interest, capped at the amount owed. The $100 plan pays up to $100 each month. Real cards use different terms and often daily interest.

Priya's cafe earns the stated annual amount before interest. The model charges annual interest, with principal repaid later from the shop. Her $2,000 requirement represents extra work and risk, not a rule for real businesses.

Predict has six rounds. Read the start and destination rates. Choose for all four neighbors before turning. Priya's earnings and loan change, so a rate direction alone cannot answer her question. A tally records calls, not a grade. Replay shuffles order. Nothing is saved except the kit's Motion setting.

On small screens, More holds the rate ladder, context and revealed reasons. After the final reveal, students choose See your recap when they have finished reading. Wrong calls show the chosen answer and the outcome together.

## Talk about it
Who benefits when rates rise? What changed after Keisha signed? Why can Priya reject a loan even after rates fall? Why is having earnings above interest not enough for her?

## Good to know
Grades 6-8, personal finance. 20-35 minutes total. Adjust Street, Predict and discussion time to the class. Students or teacher.

The model uses base minus 3 points for savings, plus 1 for the car, plus 14 for the card and plus 3 for the cafe. These are made-up add-ons. They do not describe today's rates. No inflation, credit scores or economy-wide effects are modeled.
