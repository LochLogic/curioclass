# Interest Rate Dial (idea card)

- Module: cte-6-8-interest-rate-dial, grade 6-8. Codes: D2.Eco.10.6-8, NSPFE.Saving, NSPFE.Managing Credit
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good, because one input (the rate) drives four visible outputs.
- One idea: Turn one interest rate dial from 1 to 10 percent and watch four neighbors react on one street: a saver's account grows faster or slower, a car buyer's monthly payment jumps or falls before she signs, a credit card balance that tracks the rate swells, and a cafe owner decides whether borrowing to open a second shop still pays.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a street with a bank in the middle, four houses and a big dial on the bank's door. "Turn the dial and watch the street."
  - Core loop: each neighbor's card updates live: the saver's $2,000 after 5 years; the car buyer's $18,000, 5-year loan payment; the card holder's $1,500 balance paying only the minimum; the cafe owner's expected profit from a $50,000 expansion loan vs its interest.
  - Lock-in moment: the car buyer signs at the current rate; turning the dial afterwards no longer changes her payment, while the card balance keeps moving (a variable rate).
  - Wrap-up questions: at which rate does the cafe owner say no? who on the street wants rates high, and who wants them low?
  - The aha or the end state: the same rate is good news for savers and bad news for borrowers, and businesses invest less when borrowing costs more.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.10.6-8: see how a rate change shifts borrowing and the cafe owner's decision to invest.
  - NSPFE.Saving: watch the saver's balance grow at different rates and compare.
  - NSPFE.Managing Credit: compare a fixed-rate loan with a variable-rate card balance and see the cost of paying only the minimum.
- Not like: compound-interest-head-start (grade 9-12 time and compounding); the-credit-card-diaries (grade 9-12 credit reports); market-time-machine (investing).
- Art: generated street scene with people (illustrated) neighbors; cards and dial drawn in code.
