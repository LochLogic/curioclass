Who turns the dial?

On a real street there is no single dial. Each bank sets a base rate called the prime rate, and most set it about 3 percentage points above the top of the Federal Reserve's target range.

When the Fed raises or lowers its rate, prime usually follows, and so do rates tied to it, like most credit card rates (prime plus an add-on). A fixed-rate loan keeps the rate it was signed at.

Here the base and all four add-ons are made up. Luis's balance is from a car repair. This monthly model holds each selected rate steady, with no fees or new charges. Real cards often calculate interest daily. Minimum payments use the larger of $25 or 1% of the balance plus monthly interest. Card terms differ; dollar floors are often $25 to $40.
