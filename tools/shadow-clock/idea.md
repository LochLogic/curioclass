# Shadow Clock (idea card)

- Module: science-5-sun-stars-and-shadows, grade 5. Codes: 5-ESS1-1, 5-ESS1-2
- Format / family: lab / sky. Fit: good
- One idea: Plant a stick in a schoolyard, chalk its shadow tip every hour on four days of the year, graph length and direction, and log which star groups are up at night, so the daily and seasonal patterns come out of the student's own data and Earth's spin and orbit explain them.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a schoolyard seen from above at sunrise, a stick, a chalk tool, a calendar with the solstices and equinoxes flagged, and a latitude picker (a list of bands, no location access).
  - Core loop: scrub the clock and tap to chalk the shadow tip each hour; the tool logs length and compass direction in a table, and the student builds a line graph of length against time and a direction fan. Repeat on the other dates. After dark, the same yard shows the evening sky on each date; the student notes which star groups are up (Orion on winter evenings, Scorpius on summer evenings in the north).
  - The aha or the end state: a split view, the yard on the left and Earth spinning and orbiting on the right; drag time and both move together, so the short June noon shadow and the star group that vanishes for half a year are explained by one model.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare yards at three latitudes, including one south of the equator.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 5-ESS1-2: the student graphs shadow length and direction over a day and across seasons, day and night, and the seasonal appearance of star groups.
  - MOVE 5-ESS1-1: new tool: "Star Lanterns" (grade 5), carry identical lanterns farther and farther across a dark field and measure how dim they look, then find that the sun looks brightest only because it is the closest star.
- Not like: sky-diary (grade 1, predicts sun and moon positions, no measuring); moon-phase-model (phases and eclipses); drop-zone (gravity).
- Art: generated scenes for the schoolyard by day and by night; shadows, chalk marks, graphs and the Earth model drawn in code for accuracy.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Chalk a stick's shadow every hour on four days of the year, graph its length and direction, log which star groups are up at night, then explain each pattern with Earth's spin and orbit.
- Flags: none
