# Where's My Phone From? (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-10-globalization-today, grade 10. Codes: D2.Geo.7.9-12, D2.Geo.11.9-12, D2.Eco.15.9-12
- Format / family: data / data-desk. Fit: good
- One idea: Take a phone apart into its materials and parts, follow each to where it is mined, made and assembled using real trade and mineral data, and investigate who gains and who bears the costs along the chain.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an exploded phone (battery, screen, chips, case) with a question: "How many countries are in your pocket?"
  - Core loop: pick a part; the data desk shows the real top producers and trade flows (cobalt and lithium mining, chip fabrication, final assembly) as a flow map and table; filter by year to see the chain shift.
  - Investigations: three guided questions, each with data and a short human story: Why do factory workers migrate to assembly cities? Why do cobalt and tin regions see conflict as well as income? Who earns what share of the phone's price?
  - The aha or the end state: the student builds a one-chart answer to one question, with a caption naming effects on growth, workers, rights and the environment in two countries.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare a phone from 2010 with one today.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.7.9-12: the assembly-city investigation ties the spread of manufacturing technology to migration into factory regions.
  - D2.Geo.11.9-12: the mining investigation uses data on scarce minerals to show conflict and cooperation within and between countries.
  - D2.Eco.15.9-12: the value-share and labor views explain globalization's effects on growth, labor markets, rights, environment and income across nations.
- Not like: trade-island (comparative advantage model, grade 12); layers-and-scales (thematic maps); region-compare (grade 6 region stats).
- Art: drawn in code (flow maps, charts) plus generated scenes of mines, fabs and assembly floors, because the places should feel real.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Break a phone into its parts and follow each to where it is mined, made and assembled using real trade and mineral data; investigate migration, conflict over minerals and who earns what along the chain.
- Flags: needs a data source (USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries, UN Comtrade or similar, checked, bundled offline); sensitive (child labor in artisanal mining)
