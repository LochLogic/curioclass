# Puppet Show Feelings (idea card)

- Module: arts-k-2-puppets-and-feelings, grade k-2. Codes: TH:Re7, TH:Re8, TH:Cn10
- Format / family: showdown / none. Fit: change to story, because a solo child pausing a scene, reading feelings and choosing an ending is a branching story; the class vote becomes an extra.
- One idea: Pause a sock-puppet show to say how each puppet feels and how you can tell, then peek backstage and see the puppeteer's hand make the feeling (a droopy head, a slow voice, a fast bounce), so the child learns that a puppet's feeling is something a performer builds.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a little puppet theater with two puppets (a sock dragon who lost a kite, a paper-bag friend) and a big pause button.
  - Core loop: the scene plays; at each pause the child taps a puppet, picks a feeling face and a clue (face, voice, body, words). The backstage button turns the theater around: the same moment from behind, with the puppeteer's hand and voice choices labeled.
  - The aha or the end state: the same line ("I'm fine") played two ways by the puppeteer, and the child hears it change meaning. Then pick one of two endings, say why you liked it, and tap a "Me too" card: a time you felt like the dragon.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes on the ending on the projector.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - TH:Re7: recalling and naming the puppeteer's choices that showed each feeling.
  - TH:Re8: comparing the child's own feelings and preferences with the puppets' (which ending, and why).
  - TH:Cn10: the "Me too" card links a puppet's experience to the child's own.
- Not like: act-it-out (the class performs a folktale); feelings-faces (SEL emotion naming, not theater choices); character-voice-booth (grade 3-5 voice recording).
- Art: generated scenes for the theater; puppets as illustrated rigged characters, with a real puppeteer's hand backstage.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pause a sock-puppet show to say how each puppet feels and how you can tell, peek backstage at how the puppeteer made that feeling, then pick the ending you like and find a time you felt the same.
- Flags: overlap risk (feelings-faces; keep this one about how performers show feelings)
