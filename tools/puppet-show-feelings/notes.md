## What it teaches
Children recognize how a puppet's posture, voice speed and movement suggest a feeling. They explain a preferred ending and connect a character's feelings with their own experiences.

## Key skills
- Watch closely for head and movement clues.
- Listen for changes in voice speed.
- Change one thing and describe the difference.
- Explain why an ending appeals to you.
- Talk about a time you felt a similar feeling.

## Support at home
- Make a puppet with a spare sock. Keep its face the same and try showing two feelings by changing its movement.
- Say a friendly sentence slowly, then quickly. Ask what clues your child hears.
- Tell your child about a small moment when you felt proud or worried. Invite a story, without requiring one.

## A five-minute start
Open Show. Tap Again to hear the first line, let children name a feeling, then tap Show me. Watch the next scene and ask which clue changed. Open Backstage and try Down, Slow and Still, then change only Head to Straight and Up. Tap Audience says after each guess.

## Modes
**Show:** Six scenes tell Pom's original kite story. Pause for a guess before Show me. Back, Next and Again interrupt playback immediately. At the ending choice, select Dot helps, A new kite or Wait for the wind. All endings are valid preferences.

**Backstage:** Head, Voice and Bounce each have three settings. Read the same line with different settings. Audience says reveals the simplified model's interpretation. Try it offers six rotating targets. H, V and B cycle the rows; A reveals the audience card.

**Like me:** Tap a feeling and share a memory aloud. Ending vote lets the teacher select the class's preferred ending by raised hands, without on-screen counting. Votes can change.

## Talk questions
- What did Pom's head do? What did the voice do?
- Only one thing changed. What difference did you notice?
- Which ending do you prefer? Why?
- When did you feel like Pom? You may pass if you prefer.

## Good to know
Teacher led for Kindergarten through Grade 2. Kindergarten can name one clue; Grade 1 can compare two versions; Grade 2 can direct a combination and explain its effect. There is no score, saved work, recording or microphone access. The shared Motion preference is stored by the Curio Class kit.

The 27-setting table is a simplified classroom model, not a rule about real people. Different viewers can read the same clues differently. A child's interpretation with a reason is welcome. No feeling is wrong or bad. The puppet's button eyes stay fixed; the mouth moves only to babble.

Motion off removes bounce and tilt easing while keeping babble timing. Sound off silences narration and effects. Read can still speak the current text. Browser narration is used in this build; the specified smooth-narrator recordings have not been made.

Standards: NCAS TH:Re7 through identifying body and voice cues; TH:Re8 through interpreting choices and choosing a preferred ending; TH:Cn10 through the Like me memory prompts.
