# Money and Meters (idea card)

- Module: math-4-decimals, grade 4. Codes: 4.NF.C
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: One amount lives in four linked views (dimes and pennies, a meter stick in tenths and hundredths, a 10 by 10 grid, and a number line), so changing any one shows that 0.5, 0.50, 5/10 and 50/100 are the same place.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: four panels at a ribbon counter at a school craft fair. A ribbon of 0.3 m lies on the meter stick; the other panels already show 3 dimes, 3 grid columns and a dot at 0.3.
  - Core loop: the student changes any panel (add a penny, drag the ribbon end, shade a square) and the rest follow, with the fraction and decimal written under each.
  - Challenge rounds: make 3/10 + 4/100 with coins only (30/100 + 4/100 = 34/100 = 0.34); place 0.7 and 0.07 on the number line; "Ana says 0.45 is bigger than 0.5 because 45 is bigger than 5. Show her." The meter stick settles it.
  - Same-whole round: 0.3 of a meter of ribbon vs 0.3 of a dollar; the tool refuses to compare them, and the clerk explains why the wholes must match.
  - The aha or the end state: tenths and hundredths are the same pieces in coins, centimeters and squares, and more digits does not mean bigger.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a projector view with one panel hidden for class predictions.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 4.NF.C: tenths rewritten as hundredths and added (3/10 + 4/100) with coins and the grid (4.NF.5); decimal notation for tenths and hundredths, placed on a number line (4.NF.6); comparing two decimals to hundredths with <, = or >, justified with a model and the same-whole rule (4.NF.7).
- Not like: powers-of-ten-zoom (grade 5 place value to thousandths), stadium-count (grade 4 whole-number place value), corner-store (grade 2 counting coins and change).
- Art: generated scene for the craft-fair counter; stick, grid, line and plain-style coins drawn in code for exact amounts.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: One amount in four linked views (dimes and pennies, a meter stick, a 10 by 10 grid, a number line): change one and the rest follow, then compare decimals like 0.45 and 0.5 with proof.
- Flags: none
