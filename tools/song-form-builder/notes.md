## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Build**. Tap the orange **A** block on the shelf, then tap space 1 in the song strip. Do the same with **B** in space 2 and **A** in space 3.
2. Press **Play song**. Watch the playhead walk the blocks. At the end the two A blocks lift, the Form card reads "Ternary form: away and back home." and the voice says "A B A. You went away and came back home."
3. Tap **Edit** on the shelf's A block and change one note. Both A blocks in the strip ripple: same letter, same music. Press **Play song** again and listen for the change in both places.

## The two modes

- **Build:** up to four sections (A, B, then **+ New section** adds C and D), each 8 beats on a 6-row grid (do re mi sol la high do), each with its own instrument. Put blocks in the 8-space strip and press **Play song**. The Form card names the shape: strophic, "A and its twin", binary, ternary, AABA, rondo, through-composed, or "no special name". Tap a strip block for **Hear**, **Remove** or **Make it A′**: a twin whose first 6 beats follow A and whose last 2 beats are its own. Starter songs (Sunny Day, Rondo for Recess, Blank) load from the panel. Work is saved on this device; **Clear song** also clears the save.
- **Listen:** pick a song (Hot Cross Buns, Mary Had a Little Lamb, Twinkle Twinkle, Ode to Joy, Row Row Row Your Boat, Frere Jacques) and press **Listen**. The first phrase becomes A. For each next phrase, tap **Same as A**, **Almost A** (a twin), **Same as A′** (once a twin has been labeled) or **New**; the big button becomes **Hear it again**. A right answer gets a short "Yes!" and why. A wrong answer plays the two phrases one after the other at once so students can compare, then asks again; after two misses the tool shows the answer and why. At the end the Form card names the song's form and how it feels, and the big button becomes **Play whole song**. **Start over** (under the answers) asks Yes or No first.

## Talk about it

- Why does changing one A change every A? What would a song sound like if the letters didn't promise the same music?
- Which feels more finished, AB or ABA? Why does coming back home feel like an ending?
- In a rondo, what does A do for the listener? What do the other parts do?
- When is a part "almost the same" instead of "new"? What did you listen for?
- Hot Cross Buns is AABA. Can you build your own AABA song in Build?

## Good to know

- On a projector, Listen works as a whole-class game: the class calls out "same", "almost" or "new" before a student taps.
- Speed (Slow, Medium, Quick) changes even while a song is playing.
- **Clear song** (after a Yes) empties the strip, puts the sections back to the first ones and removes the save, so the next student on a shared laptop starts fresh.
- In real music A′ can be any small change to A (a new rhythm, a changed middle). This tool shows the most common kind, a new ending.
- Ode to Joy is the classroom version of Beethoven's tune: in C, without the tied note before the last phrase.
- Escape clears a picked block. In the note grid, the arrow keys move and Delete makes a rest.
- Sound off still runs the playhead, so the form can be followed silently.
- Everything is drawn in code, so it stays sharp on any screen. Nothing is sent anywhere.
