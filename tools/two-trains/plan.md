minutes: 45
focus: 8.EE.C
goal: I can find where two lines meet on a graph and with algebra, and tell when a system has one solution, no solution or infinitely many.
materials: A laptop per student or pair. The tool on the projector. A notebook page with a table: preset, banner, where the lines cross.
grouping: Pairs on laptops, with whole-class debriefs.
---
## Before class

- Run the 5-minute start in the teacher notes yourself, so you have seen **Head on**, **Same speed** and **Same schedule**.
- Keep the Dispatch answer key from the teacher notes handy.
- Put the table headings on the board: preset, banner, where the lines cross.

## Hook (5 min)

1. Say: "Two trains leave at noon from opposite ends of a 300-mile line. One goes 60 miles per hour, the other 40. Where do they pass?" Write a few guesses on the board.
2. Ask: "Is there any way two trains on one straight track could pass each other twice?" Take a hands-up vote and leave it up.

## Explore (15 min)

1. Pairs open **Yard**. The default, **Head on**, is the hook's setup. Before anyone presses Play: "Where will each train be at 1 pm?" (A at mile 60, B at mile 260.)
2. Press **Play**. Point at the route ladder beside the graph: "Why does each train sit at the height of its line?" (The height is how far it is from Ashford.) The trains pass at Pine Hollow, 3:00 pm, mile 180. Check the hook guesses.
3. Pairs work through the **Presets** in order and fill in the table for each: **Catch up** (meet 3:00 pm at Millbrook, mile 120), **Same speed**, **Same schedule**, **Too slow to catch**.
4. On **Same speed**, turn on **Show gap** and drag the clock. "What happens to the gap?" (It stays 45 miles at every time.)

## Make sense (10 min)

1. On the projector, load **Head on** and tick **Show the algebra**. "Why do we set the two equations equal?" (We want the time when both trains are at the same mile.) Walk through 60t = −40t + 300, so 100t = 300 and t = 3.
2. Load **Same speed**: the algebra ends "45 = 0. That is never true: no solution." "What do the two lines look like?" (Parallel. Same slope, different starting point.)
3. Load **Same schedule**: "0 = 0", infinitely many solutions. "Why is every point a solution?" (It is the same line, so the trains are side by side from 2:00 pm to 5:00 pm.)
4. Load **Too slow to catch**. "The algebra gives t = −4.5. Did the trains meet?" (No. That is 4.5 hours before noon at mile −180, before either train left and off the track. Right for the equations, wrong for the story.)
5. Back to the hook vote. Write the three cases on the board: lines cross once (one solution), parallel (no solution), same line (infinitely many).

## Apply (10 min)

1. Pairs switch to **Dispatch** and do ticket 1, **Meet at Millbrook**: set B's speed, then press **Check**. If they are stuck, **Show a hint**.
2. Everyone does ticket 6, **Call it first**, on paper before the screen. A leaves Ashford at 1:00 pm at 75; B leaves Brookport at noon at 50. Pairs type their answers in **Your meeting time** and **Your mile**, then Play. "What equation did you solve?" (75(t − 1) = 300 − 50t, so t = 3: 3:00 pm at mile 150.)
3. Then ticket 7, **Meet twice**. Pairs try, then decide whether to press **It can't be done**. "Why can't two straight lines cross twice?" (Two different lines cross at most once. The same line meets everywhere, not twice.) Settle the hook vote.
4. Fast pairs: ticket 8, **Junction at four** (both speeds; A 15, B 60).

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: "Train A: d = 50t. Train B: d = −30t + 240. When and where do they meet? Then change one number in B's equation so the trains never meet, and say why." (50t = −30t + 240, so 80t = 240, t = 3: 3:00 pm at mile 150. Never meet: any B with rate 50 and a different starting number, such as d = 50t + 240, because the lines are parallel.)

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** stay on **Head on** and **Same speed**. Drag the round dot and the square handle and say what each changes.
- **Support:** use **Jump to meeting** to land on the crossing, then read the time and mile off the graph.
- **Stretch:** in **Yard**, build a setup where the lines cross but the trains don't meet because one has already arrived. Explain it with the solid and dashed parts of the lines.
- **Stretch:** do ticket 2, **The catch**, with algebra first: when must B leave so it catches A at Granite Falls? (3:00 pm.)

## Short on time

- Do **Head on** with **Play** and **Show the algebra** on the projector (6 min).
- Pairs run **Same speed** and **Same schedule** and name the case for each (7 min).
- Finish with the exit card (5 min).
