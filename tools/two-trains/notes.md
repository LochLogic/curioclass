## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Yard**. The default, **Head on**, has Train A leaving Ashford eastbound at noon at 60 miles per hour and Train B leaving Brookport westbound at noon at 40. The banner already says "They meet at 3:00 pm, 180 miles from Ashford (Pine Hollow)." Ask the class to predict where the trains will be at 1 pm, then press **Play** (or Space).
2. Watch the strip beside the graph (the route ladder): it is the railroad turned on its side, so each train sits at the height of its line. At 3 pm the trains pass at Pine Hollow and the two lines cross at (3, 180).
3. Turn on **Show the algebra**: 60t = minus 40t + 300, so 100t = 300 and t = 3.
4. Pick preset **3. Same speed** and turn on **Show gap**. Drag the clock: the gap is 45 miles at every time. The algebra ends "45 = 0. That is never true: no solution."
5. Pick preset **4. Same schedule**: one line on top of the other, "0 = 0", infinitely many solutions.

## The two modes

- **Yard:** free play. Each train has a direction, a start mile (0 to 300, step 5), a departure time (12:00 pm to 4:00 pm, step 15 minutes) and a speed (10 to 100 miles per hour, step 5). Change them with the sliders and boxes, by dragging the round departure dot (start time and mile), or the square speed handle (it sits one hour after departure, so its height above the dot is the speed). Both forms of each equation update live: story form d = m(t minus s) + p and slope-intercept form d = mt + b. Five presets load common setups and restart the clock.
- **Dispatch:** eight ticket cards. Each locks part of a schedule (greyed, with a lock) and asks the students to set the rest. **Check** says what happened in plain words without giving the answer. A solved ticket gets an "On time" stamp and keeps a check mark on this device only.

## The four banners

- **One meeting:** the lines cross while both trains are running.
- **Never meet:** same speed and direction, different starting line. Parallel lines, no solution.
- **Always together:** the same equation. One line, infinitely many solutions.
- **Lines cross, trains don't meet:** the equations do have a solution, but at a time when the trains are not both on the track (before one leaves, after one arrives, or after 7 pm). Preset 5, **Too slow to catch**, crosses at 4.5 hours before noon at mile minus 180. This is a good place to talk about a solution that is right for the algebra and wrong for the story.

## Talk about it

- Where on the graph do you look to find when and where the trains meet? Why there?
- What does the steepness of a line tell you about its train? What does a line going down mean?
- Two trains with the same speed in the same direction: why can the gap never change?
- Card 7 asks for two meetings. Why can two different straight lines never cross twice?
- Preset 5 has a solution, t = minus 4.5. Is it a real meeting? What does the story say?

- Card 6: Play stays off until both boxes are filled. Until the trains meet, the meeting, the ring, the algebra and Show gap stay hidden, each line is drawn only as far as the clock (it grows as the trains run), and the clock moves only with Play. The equations stay visible: that is what students predict from. A prediction within 1 minute and 1 mile counts. The time box takes "3:00 pm", "3 pm", "3:00" or hours after noon such as "3".
- Card 7: if a student gets the two trains on the same line, the card says that is infinitely many meetings, not two.

## Good to know

- Times are hours after noon (t = 0 is 12:00 pm, t = 7 is 7:00 pm). A train stops at its end of the line and waits at its station before it leaves; only the solid part of its line is the train actually running. The faint dashed part is the same equation outside that time.
- Two trains sitting at the same end of the line after both arrived do not count as a meeting.
- An eastbound train cannot start at Brookport (and a westbound one at Ashford): switching direction there moves the start to the other end and the hint says so.
- Train A always rides the upper track in the picture and Train B the lower one, so they pass without overlapping.
- Every meeting time and mile in the presets and cards was recomputed with exact fractions. Times that are not whole minutes say "about".
- Keyboard: Space plays or pauses and R restarts the clock (not while typing in a box). Arrow keys move the dots, handles, sliders and clock tab.
- With Motion off, Play jumps straight to 7:00 pm and the meeting label shows at once.
- The Prairie Line, its towns and the trains are invented.
- The speed fact on screen ("on Class 4 track, freight trains may run up to 60 miles per hour and passenger trains up to 80") comes from the US federal track safety rules: 49 CFR 213.9, Classes of track: operating speed limits. https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-II/part-213/subpart-A/section-213.9 (also https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/213.9). Railroads often set lower limits of their own.
