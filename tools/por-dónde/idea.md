# Ciudad Perdida (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-6-12-travel-and-directions, grade 6-12. Codes: ACTFL.Intermediate Low
- Format / family: map / spanish. Fit: good: directions only mean something on a map.
- One idea: On your first day in a Spanish-speaking city you ask locals the way and follow their spoken directions (sigue derecho, dobla a la izquierda, cruza la plaza) by bus, metro and on foot; then a lost tourist asks you, and they walk exactly what you say, so a wrong "derecha" sends them the wrong way.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a map of a made-up city drawn on a real Latin American street-grid style, a phone with a short list of places to reach (la farmacia, la estación de metro, el museo). "Pregunta a alguien."
  - Core loop: part 1 (listening): the student picks a polite question (Disculpe, ¿dónde está...?), a local answers aloud, and the student traces the route; transport choices (el autobús, el metro, a pie) change the route. Part 2 (speaking): a tourist asks the way; the student builds directions from tiles and the tourist walks them literally on the map, step by step.
  - The aha or the end state: the student guides three tourists to their stops with no wrong turns, and the city map fills with every place name learned.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): pairs, one giving directions and one tracing on a printed map.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Intermediate Low: asking and answering questions about places, transport and directions in simple sentences, using commands and prepositions of place.
- Not like: mundo-hispano (culture map of real countries); mi-casa (rooms in a house); verb-conjugation-race (forms without place).
- Art: drawn in code for the map (clarity); illustrated locals and tourists (people, illustrated).
- Proposed title: ¿Por Dónde? (Ciudad Perdida is the name of a real archaeological site in Colombia, and the tool is not about it)
- Proposed concept line: Find your way around a Spanish-speaking city by asking locals and following their spoken directions by bus, metro and on foot, then give directions to lost tourists who walk exactly what you say.
- Flags: needs a data source (recorded Spanish voices); none else.
