# Wolf Pack Survival (idea card)

- Module: science-3-living-in-groups, grade 3. Codes: 3-LS2-1
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Run seasons for wolves, sardines and honeybees, choosing whether each lives alone or in a group, and use the survival counts to argue how groups help members find food, stay safe and raise young, and when a group costs something.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a winter valley with elk; a lone wolf and a pack of six on a choice card. "Hunt alone or with the pack? Run a winter and see."
  - Core loop: pick alone or group size, run a sped-up season, read a simple tally (meals found, pups raised, wolves alive in spring). Then sardines with a dolphin and seabirds: alone, or in a school that swirls into a bait ball. Then bees: a lone bee, or a hive that divides the work (foragers, guards, nurses) and keeps the hive warm in winter.
  - Cost cards keep it honest: a big pack shares each kill among more mouths; a school is easier to spot from far away.
  - The aha or the end state: an argument builder: the student picks a claim, drags in two pieces of evidence from their own tallies, and adds one reason; the tool checks that the evidence supports the claim.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3-LS2-1: running the three cases and building an argument from the tallies that groups help members survive.
- Not like: food-web-wobble (grade 5 whole food webs); beaver-builders (animals changing a place).
- Art: generated scenes (wolves in snow, a sardine bait ball, a hive inside), because real animal behavior is the evidence. Hunts are shown plainly and without gore.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run seasons for wolves, sardines and honeybees living alone or in groups, read the survival tallies, and build an argument with evidence about how groups help members survive and what a group costs.
- Flags: none (survival numbers are modeled; the spec should base them on real field findings, such as Yellowstone wolf studies)
