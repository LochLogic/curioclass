# Research packet: festival-parade
Concept: Floats from real festivals roll past; guess each celebration from its food, music, clothes and season, hear a kid who celebrates it, and follow the map to see how it traveled with the people who carried it.
Grade: 1   Standards: D2.Geo.6.K-2, D2.Geo.7.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Geo.6.K-2: "Identify some cultural and environmental characteristics of specific places." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Identify traditional clothes, seasonal timing, foods, and music that define cultural celebrations around the world.
- D2.Geo.7.K-2: "Explain why and how people, goods, and ideas move from place to place." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain how cultural celebrations and customs traveled to new cities and countries as families migrated.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing Día de los Muertos with Halloween: Students frequently assume Day of the Dead is spooky or scary like Halloween, missing that it is an affectionate family honoring of ancestors (Source: California History-Social Science Framework Grade 1).
- Assuming all celebrations take place in winter: Young children often expect all major holidays in December, unaware of spring, harvest, and lunisolar festivals (Source: C3 Framework Geography indicators D2.Geo.6.K-2).
- Viewing traditional festive dress as "costumes": Children often describe garments like saris, guayaberas, or hanbok as party costumes rather than meaningful cultural clothing (Source: Smithsonian Folklife Educational Guidelines).
- Thinking traditions only exist in far-away lands: Failing to recognize that diaspora communities celebrate these vibrant festivals right here in American towns and cities (Source: California History-Social Science Content Standards 1.4).

## Sensitivity
- Authentic voice and representation: Ensure festival customs (such as Día de los Muertos, Diwali, and Lunar New Year) are represented through the authentic traditions of the cultural communities who practice them.
- Respecting religious roots without sectarian proselytizing: Public school standards approach cultural celebrations from a historical and geographic lens, focusing on community unity, harvest seasons, music, and food.
