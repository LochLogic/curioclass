# Festival Parade (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-1-cultures-around-the-world, grade 1. Codes: D2.Geo.6.K-2, D2.Geo.7.K-2
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Floats from real festivals roll past; guess the celebration from clues in the food, music, clothes and season, then follow a dotted line on the map to see how the festival traveled with the people who moved and now celebrate it far from where it began.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a parade route and a drumbeat. "A float is coming. Tap it to look closer."
  - Core loop: each float (for example Lunar New Year, Diwali, Día de los Muertos, Songkran, Carnival) shows three clues; the student picks which celebration it is, then hears a short story told by a kid who celebrates it, including how the place's weather or season shapes it (Songkran's water in the hottest month).
  - Travel line: the map draws where the festival began and where families carried it (Lunar New Year parades in San Francisco, Diwali in London and Toronto), with why people moved.
  - The aha or the end state: a passport page shows each festival's home place, its environmental and cultural clues, and the places it reached; one gentle song or move to try at the end where appropriate.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): class guesses together on the projector.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.6.K-2: each float's clues identify cultural (food, clothes, music) and environmental (season, weather) characteristics of its place.
  - D2.Geo.7.K-2: the travel line shows why and how people carried the festival, its foods and ideas to new places.
- Not like: day-in-the-life (grade 3 daily life and where people live); symbol-spotter (national civic symbols); pen-pal-parcels (products and trade).
- Art: people (illustrated) and generated scenes, because festivals must be shown with accurate, respectful detail.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Floats from real festivals roll past; guess each celebration from its food, music, clothes and season, hear a kid who celebrates it, and follow the map to see how it traveled with the people who carried it.
- Flags: sensitive (religious and cultural festivals; fresh review agent per rule 5; the song or move must be respectful and rights-clear)
