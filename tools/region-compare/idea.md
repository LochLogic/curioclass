# Region Compare (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-6-world-regions-today, grade 6. Codes: D2.Geo.3.6-8, D2.Geo.8.6-8, D2.Geo.10.6-8, D2.Geo.5.6-8
- Format / family: data / data-desk. Fit: good
- One idea: Pick two world regions and a question (Why is South Asia crowded while North Africa is mostly empty?), lay real data (population density, rainfall, income, main languages, cropland) on maps and charts you choose, and find which layers make the regions alike, which make them different, and how rivers and rain pull people in.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a world map with a question card and two region pickers.
  - Core loop: add a data layer; choose how to show it (map shading, bar chart, dot plot); a notebook panel asks one question per layer. Night-lights and river layers can be stacked on population.
  - The aha or the end state: a two-column "alike / different" board filled from the layers, and a sentence on how land and water stretch or limit where people live (the Nile valley and the Ganges plain as clear cases).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): question cards for other region pairs.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.3.6-8: the student picks map and chart types to show each layer's pattern.
  - D2.Geo.8.6-8: rain, rivers and cropland layers over population show how environment extends or limits settlement.
  - D2.Geo.10.6-8: comparing cultural and environmental layers across the two regions.
  - D2.Geo.5.6-8: the alike-different board names the mix of traits that makes the regions similar and different.
- Not like: layers-and-scales (grade 9, class breaks and scale); five-themes-field-trip (one city up close); where-s-my-phone-from (trade data). Shares the data-desk engine; differs in content and framing.
- Art: drawn in code (the maps and charts are the instrument) with public domain NASA night-lights imagery.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Ask why two world regions differ, then lay real population, rainfall, income and language data on maps and charts you choose; find what makes the regions alike and different, and how rivers and rain pull people in.
- Flags: needs a data source (World Bank and UN population data; an open language data source, checked)
