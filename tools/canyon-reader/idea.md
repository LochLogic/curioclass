# Canyon Reader (idea card)

- Module: science-4-rock-layers-and-earth-history, grade 4. Codes: 4-ESS1-1
- Format / family: mystery / fossils. Fit: good
- One idea: Hike down a cliff wall of the Grand Canyon layer by layer, collect clues from each rock (shells and sea lily stems, slanted sand dune lines with reptile tracks, fern prints, ripple marks) and solve the case of how this place changed from sea to desert to sea again before a river cut through it all.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the canyon rim at sunrise and a case file: "The rocks here tell a story. Read it from the bottom up."
  - Core loop: step down the trail to each layer; use a hand lens, a field guide of today's places (a beach, a sand dune desert, a swamp, a shallow sea) and the fossils inside to decide what the place was like when that layer formed. Each answer goes on a stacked case board with a reason.
  - Pattern clues: the rule that lower layers are older, a tilted layer cut flat under level ones (a gap in time), the river cutting down through all of them; each one is a clue the student applies.
  - The aha or the end state: the student closes the case with a story of the landscape from oldest to youngest, citing a clue for each change, and compares it to the geologist's account.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second wall from another real place to solve alone.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 4-ESS1-1: using patterns in rock layers and fossils as evidence for how the landscape changed over time.
- Not like: fossil-clues (grade 3 fossils and one environment change, same engine); erosion-table (how the river carves, not what the layers record); earth-s-calendar (deep time scale at grade 6).
- Art: generated scenes of the real canyon layers and close-ups of fossils, plus public domain National Park Service photos where checked, because the real wall is the evidence.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Hike down the Grand Canyon layer by layer, read each rock's fossils and patterns as clues, and solve how the place changed from sea to desert to sea before the river cut through it.
- Flags: sensitive (the Grand Canyon is the homeland of several Native nations, including the Havasupai, Hualapai, Hopi, Navajo, Zuni and Southern Paiute; the case file should name this with care and needs a fresh review agent per rule 5); needs a data source (layer facts from the National Park Service, to verify)
