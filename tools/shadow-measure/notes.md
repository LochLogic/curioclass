## What it teaches
Students find an inaccessible height from measured lengths, explain why two right triangles are similar, and compare their estimate with the real height. Three light arrangements give different corresponding sides.

## Key skills
- Tape from the correct base to the correct endpoint.
- Match two pairs of equal angles.
- Use proportional sides to calculate a height.
- Interpret measuring error without rounding too early.
- Explore a dilation with its center at a shadow tip.

## Support at home
- On a sunny day, compare the shadows of two objects from a window. Which is taller, and what lengths would you need to know?
- Make two triangles with the same angles on scrap paper. Compare the matching sides.
- Ask your student to explain why a shadow's color cannot prove two angles equal.

## Five-minute start
Open Sun. Choose Flagpole, leave Sun height at 40 degrees, then tap Measure. Drag A to the stick shadow tip and B to the flagpole shadow tip. Refine each endpoint with arrow keys. Record both readings, choose two angle reasons, and Reveal. A careful trial reads 1.19 m and 10.01 m and estimates 8.41 m, compared with 8.40 m, off by 0.1 percent.

## Modes
Sun uses a 1.00 m stick and two shadows under the same sunlight. Hoop measures the rim, with its base directly below the rim, rather than the backboard or support post. The two shadow lanes represent ground strips at different depths, with the same scale.

Mirror uses a 1.50 m eyepiece. Move the mirror 4 to 12 m from the wall. Move the tripod until the reflected wall top reaches the crosshair. Tape mirror to wall and mirror to the vertical ground projection of the eyepiece. At 9.00 m and 2.00 m the estimated wall height is 6.75 m. The sight has a 0.10 m wall-top tolerance. The reflection law also gives equal acute angles with the horizontal ground.

Streetlight uses a 1.00 m stick. Tape from the stick toward the lamp for distance A, and away from it for shadow B. Lamp height is stick height times (A + B) divided by B. Show dilation maps the lamp triangle to the stick triangle with the shadow tip as center. The exact scale is 1 / 4.8, displayed as 0.21. The second stick is a ghost for comparison.
The numeric scale appears after Reveal so it cannot supply the unknown height before measuring. The triangles and corresponding-point mapping remain available throughout. Focus the scene and use arrow keys for keyboard mapping; Home returns the point to the lamp top.

## Talk questions
- Which measurement matters most when the reference shadow is very short?
- Why are two right angles alone not enough to prove similarity?
- Why does the streetlight formula include distance plus shadow?
- What stays the same about the dilation when the stick moves?

## Good to know
Tapes never snap or show a correctness meter. Estimates use the recorded readings rounded to 0.01 m. Percent error uses the unrounded estimate and rounds only for display. A reading that is off is preserved honestly.

The six reason cards are checked as a pair, without a partial-correctness clue. A refused pair requires a fresh two-tape measurement before another attempt. The notebook retains unfinished rows. There are no points, badges, best scores or saved answers.

A correct ratio can come from invented lengths. The numerical error remains honest, but the close-result narration also checks whether both tape ends plausibly reach their marked endpoints. A matching ratio with mismatched endpoints asks for a fresh measurement.
The notebook marks such completed readings with "remeasure" instead of presenting their percentage as a passed measurement. A positive result headline appears only after both the height error and endpoint checks pass.
After Reveal, pink dotted gaps compare the student's tape ends with the physical endpoints. Arrowheads point to those endpoints, so a poor estimate leads back to a visible measuring choice.

New trial keeps the session notebook. Clear notebook empties it. Closing or reloading the page clears it. No student information leaves the device. Motion off stops ambient movement and the reveal animation while preserving all results. Read speaks the current hint; fixed narration falls back to browser speech because no recordings are made during Build.
