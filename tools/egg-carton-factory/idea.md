# Prime Garden (idea card)

- Module: math-4-factors-and-multiples, grade 4. Codes: 4.OA.B
- Format / family: puzzle / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: An egg packer must box every order in a rectangular carton with no gaps, so finding every carton shape for 36 eggs finds every factor pair, and a prime order like 13 only fits a single long row.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a farm packing shed, a conveyor of eggs and Auntie Rosa. "36 eggs. Find every carton shape that fits them exactly."
  - Core loop: the child drags carton corners to set rows and columns; the carton accepts only shapes with no empty cups and no extra eggs. Each shape found joins a shelf (1 by 36, 2 by 18, 3 by 12, 4 by 9, 6 by 6). Turning a carton (4 by 9 and 9 by 4) counts as the same pair.
  - Orders get stranger: 13 eggs only fit a 1 by 13 carton, "a prime order." 1 egg gets its own joke (neither prime nor composite).
  - Multiple checks: "Can 72 eggs go in cartons of 6?" The child tests and sees 72 is a multiple of 6 because 6 is a factor of 72.
  - The aha or the end state: a 1 to 100 board colors primes and composites as orders are filled; the child sees why the factor search can stop at 6 x 6.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "hardest order" challenge for the class.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 4.OA.B: finding all factor pairs for numbers 1 to 100, seeing a number as a multiple of each of its factors, testing whether a number is a multiple of a one-digit number, and telling prime from composite (4.OA.4).
- Not like: equal-groups-zoo (grade 3 arrays for meaning), garden-grid (grade 2 rows and columns of plants), factor-forge (grade 6 prime factorization, GCF and LCM).
- Art: generated scenes (a warm packing shed, real-looking eggs and cartons); cartons drawn in code so cups line up.
- Proposed title: Egg Carton Factory (the garden look repeats garden-grid's rows and columns of plants one grade-band down; egg cartons are real arrays kids know)
- Proposed concept line: Pack egg orders into rectangular cartons with no empty cups; find every carton for 36 eggs (every factor pair), test whether 72 fits cartons of 6, and see that a prime order only fits one long row.
- Flags: overlap risk (garden-grid and equal-groups-zoo also show rows-by-columns arrays; the title change separates the look)
