## What it teaches

Students find every factor pair for orders from 1 to 100, explain why turned rectangles repeat,
and distinguish prime, composite and the number 1. They test whether an order is a multiple of a carton size.

## Key skills

- Search for rectangular arrangements systematically.
- Recognize two turned cartons as the same factor pair.
- Use a fitting rectangle as evidence that a number is composite.
- Connect full cartons, skip counting, factors and multiples.
- Calculate the exact number of full cartons.

## Support at home

- Arrange a handful of small objects in rectangles with no gaps. Keep the total fixed and find another shape.
- Choose a number under 30. Ask whether groups of 2, 3 or 5 would use every object.
- Compare 1, 2 and 9 objects. Talk about why one is neither prime nor composite and why nine is composite.

## Five-minute start

Open Pack Station with 36 eggs in five rows. Drag the bottom edge upward by one row or tap Rows minus.
Ask what the four empty cups meant and why four rows fits. Find the other shapes and compare nine rows with four rows.
The carton fills column by column, from top to bottom. Only the final column can be short.

## Modes

- Pack Station is exploration. Changing rows automatically tests a carton. Every distinct shape earns a shelf stamp.
  A finished order seals, lists its factors and colors the Order Board. Factor chips open a filling-line example.
- Fill Line fills this shed's cartons, not a claim about standard commercial carton layouts. Choose a size and run.
  Full cartons demonstrate a multiple. Empty cups in the final carton demonstrate a nonmultiple.
- Order Board opens any order. Gold with a long-row mark means prime, blue with dots means composite,
  and gray with one egg means 1. A small square marks an order that has a square carton.
- Rush Orders has four shifts of three cards. Find every shape; sort with proof cartons; select sizes and exact counts;
  then combine larger searches with prime candidates. Each card allows two Ships. A second miss is recorded as Gus helped.
  Switching away from an unfinished card changes its variant, so exploration cannot simply supply the answer.

## Talk questions

Why does turning a carton not make a new factor pair? What changes at a square carton?
Why can an odd order be composite? Why does one full carton size prove a factor, but not prove primality?
How can skip counting tell you the number of full cartons?

## Good to know

Grade 4, CCSS 4.OA.B.4. Suggested time: 15 to 35 minutes. There is no timer or speed score.
Orders and progress are saved on this device only. Start over on the board asks before clearing them.
The fictional farmer Gus and his farm orders are made up for practice. There are no personal prompts.
Motion off removes animation. Sound off silences tones and narration; Read can still read the current instruction.
Rows buttons and the carton handle's up/down arrows provide alternatives to dragging. All modes support keyboard controls.
In small previews and on phones, the whole Order Board stays visible with smaller number targets.
Use the keyboard or open full screen for larger targets. This is a known conflict with the 44-pixel target guideline.
