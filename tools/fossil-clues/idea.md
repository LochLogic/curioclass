# Fossil Clues (idea card)

- Module: science-3-fossils-and-changing-environments, grade 3. Codes: 3-LS4-1, 3-LS4-3, 3-LS4-4
- Format / family: lab / fossils. Fit: good
- One idea: Split rock slabs from a real dry sagebrush basin in southwest Wyoming, find fossil fish, palm leaves, turtles and a crocodile relative, match each to the place it must have lived, and conclude that a warm lake was here about 50 million years ago; then judge which animals survive today's sagebrush and weigh a real solution for one of them.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a quarry in the high desert with a hammer, a brush and a field notebook. "Split the slab. What lived here?"
  - Core loop: split a slab, brush a fossil, compare it to living relatives on a reference shelf, and log what kind of place it needed (warm water, frost-free land). The notebook builds a table of fossils and places.
  - Then and now: a slider moves the scene from the ancient lake to today's sagebrush basin. The student sorts animals (lake fish, crocodiles, pronghorn, sage grouse, a palm tree, sagebrush) into survive well, survive less well, or cannot survive here, with a reason from each one's needs.
  - Change today: pronghorn migrate through the area and a highway crosses their path. The student compares solutions (warning signs, a fence, wildlife overpasses) using collision data, and makes a claim about which works best and why.
  - The aha or the end state: a field report: what lived here then, what lives here now, and the student's claim about the crossing.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3-LS4-1: fossils compared to living relatives are the data for the past environment.
  - 3-LS4-3: the survive-well sorting with reasons from each organism's needs.
  - 3-LS4-4: the highway step, making a claim about the merit of a solution to a problem caused by a changed environment.
- Not like: canyon-reader (grade 4 reading layers for landscape change, same engine); puppy-traits (variation within one species).
- Art: generated scenes for the ancient lake and today's basin, with fossil close-ups; real fossil photos from public domain sources if found and checked.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Split rock slabs in a dry Wyoming basin to find fossil fish and palm leaves, work out that a warm lake was there long ago, sort which animals survive there today, and judge solutions for pronghorn crossing a highway.
- Flags: needs a data source (fossil facts from the Green River Formation and Fossil Butte National Monument; pronghorn crossing results from the Trappers Point overpasses on US 191; all to verify at spec time)
