## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Story**. At stop 1, pick a card for **Place**, **Extra** and **Photo**, then press **Send**. Watch the invite get cut into 8 numbered pieces, and read the list of what the message gave away.
2. Press **Next** at each stop and make one choice: which Wi-Fi, what to do with the pop-up, what the routers will do about a closed road (a prediction), who sees your profile, and whether Juno may share it. On the way, Juno's tablet looks up the way to the party on a map website. The safer card is not always on the same side, so read both.
3. At stop 7, read the **Where copies live** board together. Then press **Try other choices** and pick the opposite cards.

## The two modes

- **Story:** seven stops from your tablet to Juno's tablet: your tablet, your Wi-Fi router, your internet provider, the routers, the message app's server, then Juno's side. Each stop has one choice with two cards. The pieces travel on after each choice, and the story ends with the **Where copies live** board: every place that now holds a copy, with red chips for any private details that went along, who the trip touched, and one good thing it did for them.
- **Trip map:** the whole map with no story. Tap any stop to read what it does. **Send a test** sends "Hello Juno!" in 8 pieces; odd pieces go one way and even pieces another, so they land out of order and the numbers sort them. Turn on **Close a road** and send again: the pieces find another way around Router A. Tap a moving piece (or Tab to it and press Enter) to see its envelope: the outside says where it goes, the words inside are locked.

## What to listen for

- "A message is cut into pieces." "The numbers put the pieces back in order." "Routers pass pieces along and find another road."
- "A copy can't be taken back." "Ask a grown-up before sharing private details."
- Accept "parts" or "chunks" for pieces. The tool says "pieces"; the grown-up word is *packets*, and older students may like to hear it.

## Talk about it

- Why does each piece have a number on it?
- Router A closed. How did the message still get there?
- Look at the board. Which copies can you get back? Which details would you leave out next time?
- Is a pop-up that says "You won!" a friend or a stranger? How can you check?
- Who did this app touch? Name one good thing it did and one risk.

## Projector tip

On a projector, let the class vote by hands on each choice. Run it twice: once with the safe cards and once with the risky ones, then compare the two boards.

## Good to know

- Story plays at the slow speed so pieces are easy to follow. In Trip map, **Speed** starts at Slow and switches to Quick.
- There are four endings. One of them comes when no private details went along but Juno shared the invite: copies still went to 12 phones of kids you don't know.
- The phone number 555-0142 and the address are made up (555-01xx numbers are reserved for stories).
- The map, icons and envelopes are drawn in code, so they stay sharp on any projector. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is saved.
