# Moral of the Story (idea card)

- Module: ela-2-fables-and-folktales, grade 2. Codes: RL.2.2
- Format / family: cards / folktales. Fit: good.
- One idea: A card game of fables and folktales from many cultures: the child first lays a story's event cards in order to recount it, then plays the lesson card that fits and the event card that teaches it, so they learn that a lesson comes from what happens in the story.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a storyteller's table and a deck. "Each story hides a lesson. Tell it, then find it."
  - Core loop: hear the story, lay four to five event cards in order (recount), then choose from three lesson cards and play the event card that proves it. Close lessons (be kind versus be patient) need the right proof card to win. Stories: Aesop (The Lion and the Mouse, The Ant and the Grasshopper), Anansi tales (West African and Caribbean), Panchatantra fables (India), a Jataka tale, and a Chinese fable (The Frog in the Well).
  - The aha or the end state: two stories from different places share one lesson card, and the child sees how many peoples tell that lesson.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): invent a fable for a lesson card with an animal and a mistake (a writing extra).
- Standards, one line per code: RL.2.2: recounting each story in order and naming its central message, lesson or moral, with the event that teaches it, across fables and folktales from diverse cultures.
- Not like: Story Map Treasure (grade 1 lesson, one story). Alike and Different Books (two versions of one tale). Myth Museum (grade 4 myths).
- Art: generated scenes (one illustrated style per tradition, drawn with care for its origin), because a deck of beautiful cards invites play.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A card game of fables and folktales from many cultures: lay each story's events in order, then play the lesson card and the event that proves it. Two stories from different lands can share one lesson.
- Flags: sensitive (folktales from many cultures: fresh review agent for accuracy and respect, rule 5; avoid sacred stories)
