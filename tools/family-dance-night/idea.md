# Family Dance Night (idea card)

- Module: arts-k-2-dances-of-many-places, grade k-2. Codes: DA:Re8, DA:Re9, DA:Cn11
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: At family dance night in the school gym, each family teaches one real dance from home and tells who taught it to them; the child watches it, copies one move, describes it with dance words, and finds the clue in the dance that comes from its people and place (a stamp like a harvest, a step like a bird, a circle that holds everyone).
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a gym with paper lanterns and five or six family tables; tap a family to meet them.
  - Core loop: a family member (illustrated) says where the dance comes from and who taught it to them. The animated dancers perform a short real phrase. The child copies one move standing up, then describes it with word chips (fast, slow, big, small, high, low, turn, stomp, circle, partner). A "where it comes from" panel shows one picture of the place and the clue the dance carries.
  - The aha or the end state: a dance card filled with each move the child tried, their favorite part with a reason, and a matching game: which dance, which place, which clue.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class dances each move together on the projector.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - DA:Re8: describing each move with simple dance words and saying what it shows.
  - DA:Re9: choosing a favorite part and saying why.
  - DA:Cn11: relating each dance to the people and place it comes from, told by the family who carries it.
- Not like: dance-pattern-puzzle (patterns in move cards; Seven Jumps and tinikling are used there, so not here); dance-styles-explorer (grade 9-12 critique); art-passport (visual art on a map).
- Art: people (illustrated) animated from real dance reference, with care for real clothing and steps.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: sensitive (cultural dances: a fresh review agent checks each dance, its steps, clothing and the family's words for respect and accuracy, rule 5); needs a data source (each dance's origin and steps, checked)
