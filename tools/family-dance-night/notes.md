## What it teaches

Children copy and describe movements in seven dances, explain a favorite part, and connect dances to people, places and occasions. This is an introduction to noticing movement, not a lesson in performing a complete traditional dance.

## Key skills

- Watch a movement closely and copy its shape and timing.
- Describe what you see with dance words such as high, low, smooth and sharp.
- Choose a favorite part and explain why you like it.
- Connect a dance with its community, place and occasion.

## Support at home

- Share a dance your family enjoys. Tell your child who taught it to you and when you dance it.
- Take turns making one gentle movement and copying it like a mirror. Try describing it as big, small, high or low.
- Ask which part of a dance your child likes and why. There is no single right favorite.

## Five-minute start

Open one table and tap Play. Watch both moves, then tap Copy and one move. Leave comfortable space around each dancer, or copy with seated arm movements. Tap Words and choose a word the class notices. Open Where from, then vote for a Favorite part and explain why. Return to the gym to light that family's string.

## The gym and dance views

The teacher drives the screen. Tables can be visited in any order and revisited. Two or three tables in a day give the class time to copy and talk; the full activity can take 15 to 25 minutes.

Watch plays an original 16-count musical sketch with two eight-count moves. Slow uses 60 percent of the original tempo. Copy plays one eight-count move at that slower tempo, mirrored to the class. Count me in adds four counts before the selected move. The large count shows when to begin. Space plays or stops, Escape returns to the gym, and Tab plus Enter reaches every control. Arrow keys move among the four tabs.

Words responds to the last move played. After a complete Watch loop, both moves count. Yes and look-again responses are invitations to observe and discuss, not a score. Where from shows the geographic home and the two spoken sentences. Favorite part records one class choice per dance for this page visit and asks children to explain it to a friend.

Each first return lights a string. All seven brighten the gym. Revisited tables have a check mark as well as a lit color. Nothing about the class's visits or votes is saved. The shared Motion button remembers only its setting on this device.

## Talk questions

- Which part of the move looked high or low? Show us the part you noticed.
- How do the feet and arms move differently in these two dances?
- Which part do you like best? What makes you say that?
- Who dances this dance, and when? Is there a dance your family shares?

## Good to know

The standards are NCAS Dance DA:Re8, DA:Re9 and DA:Cn11 for K-2. Children describe actual displayed moves, make and explain a favorite choice, and open each community card themselves. The tool is also usable alone, with spoken next actions and Read.

These are simplified movement studies. Costume colors and objects are examples, not a claim that everyone in a community dresses or dances alike. Invite a child's connection without asking them to represent a whole people. The Korean fan dance often makes group formations; this tool isolates two movements rather than reproducing a group performance. The lion is one costume over two temporary kit figures. Final painted dancers are deferred by the owner.

The music is original synthesis suggesting each ensemble's rhythm and timbre. It is not a recording or an authentic traditional tune. Sound off silences music and speech; Read still speaks by request. Motion off freezes each count's pose without shortening the clock. Recorded clear-teacher narration is not included in this build, so fixed lines use browser speech.

## Sources and real performances

Use the source institutions below to select a real performance for your class. These outbound links open only when clicked; the downloaded tool makes no network requests.

- [National Core Arts Standards](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org): Dance, K-2, responding and connecting.
- [Smithsonian Folklife Festival](https://festival.si.edu) and [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov): southern Chinese lion dance, its two dancers and Lunar New Year setting.
- [Britannica: jarabe](https://www.britannica.com/art/jarabe) and Ballet Folklorico de Mexico de Amalia Hernandez repertoire: Jalisco and rhythmic footwork.
- [Britannica: step dancing](https://www.britannica.com/art/step-dancing): Irish step dance.
- [Britannica: bhangra](https://www.britannica.com/art/bhangra): Punjab, harvest connections and later celebrations.
- [Korea.net](https://www.korea.net): buchaechum, peony fans, flowers and waves.
- [New Orleans Jazz National Historical Park](https://www.nps.gov/jazz): second line, brass bands, umbrellas and handkerchiefs.
- [C. K. Ladzekpo teaching pages](https://www.columbia.edu/itc/music/): kpanlogo and Ga dance. Britannica Ga entries are also named in the specification.
- [GeoNames](https://www.geonames.org): the seven city or region map coordinates supplied by the specification.
- [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com): public-domain 1:110m land outline, locally bundled with no country borders; Equal Earth projection.
