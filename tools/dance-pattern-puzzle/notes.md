## What it teaches
Students find and extend dance patterns in moves, sides, levels and facings. They use letters to describe a phrase and perform it at different tempos.

## Key skills
- Find which movement detail repeats or changes.
- Continue a pattern and explain the rule.
- Read movement symbols, including a prime for the other side.
- Build and revise a dance phrase.
- Keep a count while a tempo changes.

## Support at home
- Make a clap, clap, tap pattern together. Let your child add the next three actions.
- Try one safe step at a high, middle and low level. Ask which detail changed.
- Take turns creating a short pattern for the other person to finish. Stay in a clear space.

## A five-minute start
Open Moves, puzzle 1. Tap a question card, then Jump. Fill the second question card and tap Dance it.
Watch the two counts per card. Ask what the letters tell you that the pictures also show.
Open Sides next: the same move can change sides without changing its name.

## Modes
Puzzles has six chapters and 18 open puzzles. A wrong answer stops at the first broken attribute in this order: move, side, level, facing.
Pattern lens highlights the changing marks without filling a card. Solved dances replay with their letters.
Adding-on puzzles have thicker borders at the start of each round. Seven Jumps shows round 3 then round 4.
My dance holds eight cards. Copy repeats a card; Mirror changes its side. A prime means the same card on the other side.
Letters compare move, side, level and facing; Clap and Jump ignore side. Named patterns treat each prime as its own letter.
The optional challenge card checks the whole filled sequence. Adding-on means A, AB, ABC, exactly six cards.

## Talk questions
- Which attribute changes? Which ones stay the same?
- Could two dances have the same letters but look different?
- Why do the facing puzzles need arrows as well as letters?
- What is added in each round of an adding-on dance?

## Good to know
Students can work in pairs or a teacher can project the tool. Moving physically is optional; looking and composing teach the pattern too.
Use safe, comfortable versions of movements. For Tinikling, use tape lines or ropes on the floor instead of poles.
The dancers are painted paper puppets driven by a jointed movement model. The same pose appears on stage and on its card.
They simplify movement to make patterns readable; they do not teach full dance technique.
Each card takes two counts except Tinikling, which takes one. The soundtrack is original synthesized music, not traditional melodies.
Motion off holds end poses while the count continues. Sound off silences music and automatic narration. Read explicitly reads the hint and culture card.
Save is local only; nothing leaves the device. Clear asks before deleting the dance and all puzzle progress. Storage may be unavailable in private browsing.
Keyboard: Tab and Enter; arrows within the strip; 1-8 moves; Q/W side; A/S/D level; Z/X/C/V facing; Space dances.

## Standards and scope
The activities address NCAS Dance DA:Cr2.1.3a through DA:Cr2.1.5a (phrase structure), DA:Pr5.1.3b (tempo),
DA:Re7.1.3a (movement patterns), and DA:Cn11.1.3a through DA:Cn11.1.5a (cultural dances).
My dance builds a beginning, middle and end within an eight-card phrase. The folk cards offer brief cultural context, not complete instruction in each dance.
The grapevine is a general movement exercise. Seven Jumps is explicitly a short classroom adaptation using knee lifts and kneeling;
Burchenal's historical version begins with right and left stamps before kneeling, then elbow and head actions.
The spec also mentions K-2 codes; no K-2 activity was added to this grades 3-5 tool. Catalog changes are outside this build's allowed folders.
