# Scramble for Africa (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-9-imperialism, grade 9. Codes: D2.His.4.9-12, D2.His.14.9-12, D2.Geo.5.9-12
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Slide a map of Africa from 1870 to 1914 and open each region's layers (African states and peoples, European claims, resistance, the economy imposed) to see why Europe carved up the continent, how Africans saw it and fought it, and what the borders still shape.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: Africa in 1870 drawn with its own states and peoples (Ethiopia, Asante, the Sokoto Caliphate, the Zulu kingdom, Buganda), not blank, beside a 1914 map almost entirely claimed.
  - Core loop: drag the year slider; tap a region to open layers: who lived and ruled there, which European claim arrived and why (rubber, gold, cotton, rivalry, railways, the Berlin Conference), and voices from the time (African rulers' letters, a missionary, a colonial officer, a trader).
  - Resistance layer: Adwa (Ethiopia wins, 1896), Yaa Asantewaa's war, Maji Maji, Samori Toure, and the Herero and Nama genocide, told plainly.
  - The aha or the end state: a "lasting effects" layer shows borders cutting through peoples, mining and cash-crop economies, railways that run only to the coast; the student writes why two people at the time saw the same event so differently.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): perspective-pair cards for discussion.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.4.9-12: voice cards from several sides, each naming the factors (faith, profit, sovereignty, race science) that shaped that view.
  - D2.His.14.9-12: a cause and effect panel per region links economic, political and technological causes to short and long effects.
  - D2.Geo.5.9-12: the lasting effects layer traces how colonial decisions on borders and economies changed languages, land use and environments.
- Not like: independence-timeline (the way out of empire, grade 10); empire-debate (US debate over the Philippines).
- Art: public domain archival maps and photographs (Library of Congress and similar, each item checked) plus generated map art, because real maps of the time carry the evidence.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Slide Africa's map from 1870 to 1914 and open each region's layers: African states, European motives, voices from all sides, resistance from Adwa to Maji Maji, and the borders and economies that last today.
- Flags: sensitive (genocide, colonial violence, racist period sources; fresh review agent per rule 5); needs a data source (historical boundary data and public domain sources, checked)
