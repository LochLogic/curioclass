## What it teaches
Students put six school emergency responses into a safe sequence and read why each step belongs there.
They also practice a steady hands-only CPR rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute on a keyboard or screen.

## Key skills
- Check the situation before acting and get emergency help on the way.
- Choose the next response and read why it comes before another.
- Recognize dangerous actions and learn from their consequences.
- Sustain a target rhythm and use a rate trace to assess consistency.

## Support at home
- Ask your teen to point out where they would find an AED at school or another familiar public place.
- Talk through who could call 911 and who could get equipment in a school emergency.
- Ask your school about a hands-on first aid and CPR class. This screen does not teach compression depth or certify anyone.

## A 5-minute start
Open Scenes, choose Collapse in the gym, and read the briefing before touching a card. Reading time is free.
Let a student place the first step, then ask the class to justify the next one. A wrong move returns with a reason.
Turn Timer Off when students need more discussion time. It disables saving for that entire attempt.

## The modes
Scenes contains collapse, choking, heavy bleeding, a burn, heat stroke and a seizure. Same-rank steps may go in either order.
Each mistake adds five seconds to a timed run. The clock is a practice choice, not a recommended emergency decision deadline.
The ghost replays the best local run's step times. Clear best times clears both scene records and the CPR trace after one confirmation.

CPR rhythm accepts a chest-pad tap, Space or Enter. A held key or click counts once. Press Start for a two-minute round,
or practice freely first. The metronome is 110 per minute, optional, and silenced by Sound Off.
The needle normally uses the median of five intervals, smoothing one stray tap. If two intervals in the last ten are outside
440 to 700 milliseconds and the five-interval mean is outside the target, it shows that mean instead, exposing repeated bursts or waits.
A gap over two seconds clears the needle and resets the window.
The score is the share of elapsed round time at a continuous target rhythm, not the share of taps.
Credit requires both median and mean rates in 100 to 120, an interval from 440 to 700 milliseconds, and is capped at 600 milliseconds per interval.
Those tool scoring guards allow ordinary timing variation while rejecting bursts that hide long waits.
Average rate is total compressions divided by elapsed round time; pauses therefore reduce it.
Longest pause includes time before the first compression and after the final compression in a round.
Partial rounds and rounds with a pause over ten seconds cannot set a best. The faint chart is the best full round on this device.
Old tap-based CPR ghosts are discarded when loading, since their scores are not comparable to elapsed-time scores.
The completed summary and trace stay available until Start, including after reflex taps and mode returns.
Read speaks the retained result. Gaps longer than two seconds break the trace line. Metronome starts on; a strong round suggests trying it off.

Field guide has five short reference cards with Practice it links. Read speaks the open card or current scene feedback.
Keys 1, 2 and 3 change modes. Tab and Enter operate every card and control. Before a choice, keyboard focus lets Read speak the card.
After a choice, Read explains that choice; pointer taps never move focus to an unrelated card.

## Talk questions
- Why does getting help on the way belong early in the sequence?
- Why can sending someone to call 911 happen while you put on gloves?
- What happens to your rate trace when you pause? Why does rate alone not establish good CPR?
- Which local places have an AED, and who could show you where it is?

## Good to know
Practice and awareness, not certification. Take a hands-on CPR and first aid class (American Heart Association or
American Red Cross) to learn it for real. In an emergency, call 911.
Use the keyboard or the screen for rhythm practice. Do not practice compressions, thrusts or back blows on classmates.
Hands-only CPR here is for a teen or adult who suddenly collapses. A class teaches CPR with breaths for young children.
This module addresses physical emergencies. It includes no overdose scene and no graphic injury pictures.
Some students may have witnessed an emergency or a death; offer Field guide or rhythm practice instead of a scene.
NHES.S7 is practiced through each student's choice of safe actions, correction of unsafe actions and rhythm practice.
Best runs are stored locally with no names, accounts or network requests. Storage failure does not stop play.
