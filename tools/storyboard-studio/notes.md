## What it teaches
Students plan and revise a short scene using camera position, lens, height, shot order and timing. They can explain how a cut preserves screen direction, when a similar shot becomes a jump cut, and how a neutral view permits a change of side.

## Key skills
- Frame a subject by moving a camera and choosing a lens.
- Connect a floor plan to what appears on screen.
- Diagnose and revise continuity between consecutive shots.
- Cover the script's beats with an intentional sequence.
- Refine pacing by watching an animatic and changing durations and transitions.

## Support at home
- Put two household objects on a table. Walk to the opposite side and notice how their left-to-right order changes.
- Tell a short event in three imaginary shots. Ask what a wide shot reveals that a detail cannot.
- Imagine the same moment held for one second and then four seconds. Talk about how the wait changes its feeling.

## A five-minute start
1. In Plan, drag the camera on the floor plan. Watch the shot name and the side label change.
2. Choose Aim at Ash, select 50 mm, and move until the frame feels intentional.
3. Tap Add panel. It keeps the camera and advances the beat; Duplicate keeps the beat as well.
4. Open Board, then Fix this board. This practice copy contains cuts to investigate.
5. Tap a marker and Go to panel. Repair one cut, then open Animatic and press Play.

## The modes
**Plan:** The camera aims at the selected target; Overhead looks straight down. Arrow keys move it 0.25 meter, or 1 meter with Shift. Low and Eye cameras stay 0.3 meter clear of the table and characters. High and Overhead cameras can pass above them. If lowering the camera or choosing another beat would place it inside an object, it moves to a nearby clear spot and tells you to reframe. The camera can leave the set by up to four meters, and the floor plan pans to keep it visible. Ghosts show earlier camera positions. Each scene keeps its own board of one to twelve panels.

**Board:** Select panels and use Move earlier or Move later, or drag to reorder. Each card shows shot scale, lens, height, side, beat, duration and transition. Markers explain the cut into that panel. Missing beats and total running time appear below the grid. Fix this board uses a separate practice copy; My board returns to saved student work. Print board makes grayscale pages of six panels with notes.

**Animatic:** Play starts at the beginning. From panel starts at the selected panel. Stop holds the current image; Step and Back stop and move one panel. Timeline blocks jump at once. Show problems adds a one-second split-view pause at a line crossing; tap anywhere to dismiss it. Captions show the script beat. Dissolves and fades use up to the first half second within the panel's duration. Motion off replaces those transitions with a held image and keeps the timing.

## Talk questions
- What does the audience learn from your first shot?
- Which cut made a character seem to change direction? What changed on the floor plan?
- Can an overhead or on-the-line shot bridge two sides?
- Which beat deserves the longest hold? Show its effect in Animatic.
- A convention can be broken intentionally. What would make a line crossing useful in your scene?

## Good to know
The two original scripts are The Last Slice and Late Bus. Blocking is fixed by the chosen beat. The drawing uses faceless colored mannequins and marker grays, like a working storyboard, rather than a finished film. Nico's violet backpack distinguishes the runner. An overhead camera looks straight down from its actual floor-plan position; move it near the subject to include that subject.

Shot names use this tool's cutoffs, based on frame height at the aim point: extreme wide above 6 meters; wide from 2 to 6; medium from 1 to under 2; medium close-up from 0.6 to under 1; close-up from 0.3 to under 0.6; extreme close-up below 0.3. At a shared boundary, the larger shot-size category takes the boundary, except 6 meters remains wide. The full-frame image is 36 by 20.25 mm. Frame height is 20.25 times straight-line aim distance divided by focal length. These ranges are a transparent model, not universal industry measurements.

The continuity checks are conventions, not rules of artistic merit. A neutral camera is within 0.3 meter of the line or overhead. A jump cut requires the same aim, less than 30 degrees of change in the three-dimensional viewing direction, and shot sizes at most one step apart. Changing the beat does not by itself prevent a jump-cut marker.

Saved work stays only in this browser on this device, under `storyboard-studio-boards`. Private browsing or disabled storage can prevent saving; the screen reports this. Clear board requires confirmation and affects the current board only. There is no account, upload, recording, analytics or network content.

Read uses the current hint. Sound controls short synthesized camera feedback and narration. The selected voice bank is smooth-narrator; fixed lines currently use the browser's available English voice because no recorded audio is included in this build.

The cut explanation and Clear board confirmation have their own Read buttons. These read the active dialog and its next controls. Playback ends with a Finished panel count and running time. On the sharing beat, Ash holds two separated halves of the slice; choose a wider shot or aim at Ash to show the action.

## Standards in the activity
MA:Cr1.1.HSI: generate a plan by choosing shots to cover beats. MA:Cr2.1.HSI: develop that plan into an ordered board. MA:Cr3.1.HSI: repair continuity and retime panels after watching. MA:Re7.1.HSI: inspect cuts and compare how framing and timing guide audience understanding.
