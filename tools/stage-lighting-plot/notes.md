## What it teaches

Students research period light sources, build a modern instrument plot, and refine cues through rehearsal. They can explain how beam shape, color, intensity and timing support a dramatic turn.

## Key skills

- Use research to motivate a design choice.
- Hang, aim and color different theatrical instruments.
- Record levels and fade times in a cue stack.
- Compare an intended focus with the light reaching each actor.
- Revise a design after watching the scene and its graph.

## Support at home

- Notice which existing room lights make objects look warm or cool. Ask where each light seems to come from.
- Use an existing lamp to compare light from above and from the side, without touching a hot bulb.
- Read a short scene aloud together. Discuss where you would change the light and why.

## A 5-minute start

1. In Research, open the lamp and window cards. Choose a motivation.
2. In Hang, drag an ERS 36° to the first electric and drag its ring onto Nora's mark.
3. Choose a gel. Toggle Preview to inspect the room.
4. In Cues, select beat 1, set the faders and Record cue. Select beat 5, change levels and record again.
5. In Run, start the 40-second scene. Compare your light with the marked story turns, then revise.

## The modes

Research offers five illustrated cards and optional motivation arrows. Hang provides four instrument types, five positions and editable channel, gel and beam settings. Cues has the eight script beats, live faders and a cue stack. Run rehearses the fixed blocking with your lighting, completed-look graph and plain observations.

Nothing is locked or scored. Work is saved only on this device, when browser storage is available. Pairs can exchange seats at the same laptop.

## Talk about it

- Where should the light seem to come from, and how does your plot suggest that source?
- When does Nora become visually separate from Helmer?
- Which changes belong on a story turn? Which would you deliberately delay?
- Does a brighter face always mean stronger dramatic focus?
- How did a rehearsal change one of your original research choices?

## Good to know

The script is a cut of Act III of Henrik Ibsen's A Doll's House, translated by R. Farquharson Sharp, Project Gutenberg eBook 2542. It addresses marriage, identity and separation. Cuts are marked [...]; the scene is captioned and never narrated.

NCAS TH:Cr1 is practiced through research and motivated plotting; TH:Cr3 through repeated rehearsal and revision; TH:Pr5 through instrument selection, focus, gels, levels and cues.

Keyboard: select a shelf light, choose a Pipe and press Hang on pipe. Tab to the plan and use arrow keys to aim, or Shift plus arrows to move the selected instrument along a pipe. The channel selector chooses among hung instruments. All faders and cue controls use normal keyboard input. Escape closes a dialog.

A cue holds until the next. Run starts from black; with no cue on beat 1, the scene starts dark. Step, Back and beat buttons show completed looks. Stop freezes the current fade and actor positions. During a run, the cue board follows live levels. Changing a fader overrides that channel until the next cue while other channels keep fading. Multiple cues on one beat fire in stack order, with the last one defining the beat's target.

The light model is explicitly simplified, with arbitrary outputs and approximate gel colors and transmissions. Brightness is 1 minus exp of negative energy; ambient is 0.03. Hard, medium and soft edges differ. PAR has an oval beam and rotatable long axis. The focus ring projects onto the floor; floor and back-wall light are mapped over the painting. Furniture has no independent geometry or shadows. The graph's warmth and face difference have different units from its brightness line; their ranges are stated in the graph's accessible description and How the light works.

Instrument gels belong to the whole design, not individual cues. Cue snapshots store levels. The practical channels are 41 (lamp), 42 (stove) and 43 (window), with a separately selectable window gel.

Research facts, gel names, stage geometry and blocking come from spec.md and its listed sources. The recorded gravel-ranger voice remains to be produced; browser speech is the current fallback.
