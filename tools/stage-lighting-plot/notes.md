## What it teaches

Students research period light sources, build a modern instrument plot, and refine cues through rehearsal. They can explain how beam shape, color, intensity and timing support a dramatic turn.

## Key skills

- Use research to motivate a design choice.
- Hang, aim and color different theatrical instruments.
- Record levels and fade times in a cue stack.
- Compare an intended focus with the light reaching each actor.
- Revise a design after watching the scene and its graph.

## Support at home

- Notice which existing room lights make objects look warm or cool. Ask where each light seems to come from.
- Use an existing lamp to compare light from above and from the side, without touching a hot bulb.
- Read a short scene aloud together. Discuss where you would change the light and why.

## A 5-minute start

1. In Research, open the lamp and window cards. Choose a motivation.
2. In Hang, drag an ERS 36° to the first electric and drag its ring onto Nora's mark.
3. Choose a gel. Toggle Preview to inspect the room.
4. In Cues, select beat 1, set the faders and Record cue. Select beat 5, change levels and record again.
5. In Run, start the 40-second scene. Compare your light with the marked story turns, then revise.

## The modes

Research offers five illustrated cards and optional motivation arrows. Hang provides four instrument types, five positions and editable channel, gel and beam settings. Cues has the eight script beats, live faders and a cue stack. Run rehearses the fixed blocking with your lighting, completed-look graph and plain observations.

Nothing is locked or scored. Work is saved only on this device, when browser storage is available. Pairs can exchange seats at the same laptop.

## Talk about it

- Where should the light seem to come from, and how does your plot suggest that source?
- When does Nora become visually separate from Helmer?
- Which changes belong on a story turn? Which would you deliberately delay?
- Does a brighter face always mean stronger dramatic focus?
- How did a rehearsal change one of your original research choices?

## Good to know

The script is a cut of Act III of Henrik Ibsen's A Doll's House, translated by R. Farquharson Sharp, Project Gutenberg eBook 2542. It addresses marriage, identity and separation. The eight excerpts omit intervening dialogue; an internal cut is marked [...]. The scene is captioned and never narrated. Use Step for unhurried reading.

NCAS TH:Cr1 is practiced through research and motivated plotting; TH:Cr3 through repeated rehearsal and revision; TH:Pr5 through instrument selection, focus, gels, levels and cues.

Keyboard: select a shelf light, choose a Pipe and press Hang on pipe. In compact layouts, focus the plan; keys 1 to 5 choose a pipe and Enter hangs the light. Tab to the plan and use arrow keys to aim, or Shift plus arrows to move the selected instrument along a pipe. The channel selector chooses among hung instruments. All faders and cue controls use normal keyboard input. Escape closes a dialog.

A cue holds until the next. Run starts from black; with no cue on beat 1, the scene starts dark. Step, Back and beat buttons show completed looks. Stop freezes the current fade and actor positions. During a run, the cue board follows live levels. Changing a fader overrides that channel until the next cue while other channels keep fading. Multiple cues on one beat fire in stack order, with the last one defining the beat's target.

The light model is explicitly simplified, with arbitrary outputs and approximate gel colors and transmissions. Brightness is 1 minus exp of negative energy; ambient is 0.03. Hard, medium and soft edges differ. PAR has an oval beam and rotatable long axis. The focus ring projects onto the floor; floor and back-wall light are mapped over the painting. Furniture has no independent geometry or shadows. The graph's warmth and face difference have different units from its brightness line; their ranges are stated in the graph's accessible description and How the light works.

Instrument gels belong to the whole design, not individual cues. Cue snapshots store levels. The practical channels are 41 (lamp), 42 (stove) and 43 (window), with a separately selectable window gel.

Research facts, gel names, stage geometry and blocking come from spec.md and its listed sources. The recorded gravel-ranger voice remains to be produced; browser speech is the current fallback.


## Reading the observations

The graph includes changes caused by fixed actor movement. Cue-change observations compare the previous and current light at the same actor marks, so an unchanged cue cannot claim a design change. A change means at least 0.08 in brightness, warmth or face difference; turns are compared within one beat. This describes timing, never artistic quality. Removing every turn gives an invitation to add one, not positive alignment feedback.

Source-arrow matching uses instrument position within 2 meters of the arrow and a level of at least 30. It does not evaluate focus direction or dramatic motivation; use Preview and your own judgment. Gel choices apply throughout the design, while cues store levels. Preview opens when first entering Cues unless the student has already chosen its setting.

## Named research sources

- Script, lamp, stove, winter, party, open hall and final door: [Ibsen, Sharp translation, Project Gutenberg 2542](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/2542/pg2542-images.html).
- Premiere: [University of Oslo, Virtual Ibsen Centre](https://www.hf.uio.no/is/english/services/virtual-ibsen-centre/on-ibsen-s-work/du/).
- Savoy electricity in 1881: [The Times, 29 December 1881, archived contemporary report](https://gsarchive.net/carte/savoy/electric.html).
- Period kerosene lamps: [Smithsonian, 1878 Spencer lamp](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_305002).
- Approximate candle and daylight temperatures: [Color temperature, source table named by the spec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_temperature). Cross-checks: [Illuminating Engineering Society, D65 daylight at about 6500 K](https://ies.org/definitions/standard-illuminant-d65/) and [Chautauqua Lighting Design Guidelines, glossary: candlelight 2000 K and daylight/flash 5500 K](https://www.chautauqua.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Colorado-Chautauqua-Lighting-Design-Guidelines.pdf). Flame type and daylight conditions vary.
- Oslo day length: [NOAA calculation details](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html), checked against [Oslo, December 2026](https://www.timeanddate.com/sun/norway/oslo?month=12).
- Gel names: [Rosco Roscolux specification](https://us.rosco.com/sites/default/files/content/resource/2016-09/rosco_roscolux.pdf), [LEE 201](https://leefilters.com/colour/201-full-c-t-blue/) and [LEE 204](https://leefilters.com/colour/204-full-c-t-orange/). Colors, transmission and warmth scores here are model assumptions.
- Instrument families: [ETC Source Four lens documentation](https://www.etcconnect.com/Products/Entertainment-Fixtures/Source-Four-LED-Series-2/Documentation.aspx) and [ETC Fresnel spot/flood control](https://www.etcconnect.com/webdocs/Fixtures/fos4_Fresnel/Content/fos4/Overview_Fresnel.htm). Beam angles and output in this tool are simplified choices.

## Content and sensitivity

The Norwegian play concerns unequal marriage roles, identity, parenting and separation. Its historical dialogue is not advice about present-day families. Invite analysis of design choices without asking students to disclose family circumstances. The two faceless period silhouettes show fictional Nora and Helmer, with seated and standing poses determined by the supplied blocking.
