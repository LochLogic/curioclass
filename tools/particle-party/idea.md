# Particle Party (idea card)

- Module: science-7-states-of-matter, grade 7. Codes: MS-PS1-4
- Format / family: simulator / particles. Fit: good
- One idea: Add or remove heat from a pure substance and watch its particles speed up, slow down and change arrangement, while a live temperature graph shows flat plateaus where the energy goes into changing state instead of raising temperature.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a sealed jar of ice at minus 20 C on a stand with a burner knob and a freezer knob, a particle zoom window and an empty graph.
  - Core loop: before each run the student sketches the graph they expect, then runs it. Heat in: particles jiggle faster and temperature climbs; at 0 C the line goes flat while particles break out of their places; at 100 C it goes flat again as they fly apart. Run it in reverse with the freezer. Other pure substances (iron, which melts near 1,538 C, and nitrogen, which boils near minus 196 C) show the same shape at different temperatures.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's sketch sits next to the real graph; temperature tracks average particle motion, and on the plateaus the energy goes into rearranging particles.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "mystery substance" round where the plateaus identify it.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-PS1-4: the model predicts and describes changes in particle motion, temperature and state when thermal energy is added or removed.
- Not like: particle-zoom (grade 5 dissolving and air); gas-law-lab (grade 10 pressure and volume); hot-meets-cold (grade 11 heat flow between objects).
- Art: particles and graph drawn in code so the motion stays accurate; a generated lab backdrop.
