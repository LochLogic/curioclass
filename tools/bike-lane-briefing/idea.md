# Bike Lane Briefing (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-conflicting-information, grade 8. Codes: RI.8.9, RI.8.8
- Format / family: data / fact-check. Fit: good: the desk has the data both texts cite, so the student checks claims against numbers.
- One idea: Two articles disagree about whether a town's new bike lanes made streets safer, and the student, at a newspaper fact-check desk with the town's crash data on file, sorts each disagreement into a dispute about facts or about interpretation, traces each claim to its evidence, and catches the argument that brings in irrelevant evidence, so they learn where texts really disagree and which side the evidence supports.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a desk with two articles (a city blog and a neighborhood newsletter) and a data drawer: crash counts by month for three years, traffic counts, a map.
  - Core loop: the student pulls each claim onto a claim board and links it to the evidence the article gives. They stamp each disagreement Fact (the numbers themselves differ: one counts all crashes, one only bike crashes) or Interpretation (same numbers, different meaning: is a drop from 14 to 11 real or chance?). The data drawer lets them filter and chart to check claims; a cherry-picked month glows when charted in full. A third kind of stamp catches irrelevant evidence ("Bikes are better for the planet").
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes a fact-check note: where the texts disagree on fact, where on interpretation, which claims the data supports, and one thing nobody can know yet.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second case (a school's new lunch schedule and tardies).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RI.8.9: identifying where the two texts disagree on fact and where on interpretation.
  - RI.8.8: tracing and evaluating each argument's reasoning and evidence, and recognizing irrelevant evidence.
- Not like: fake-or-fact-lab (6-8, lateral reading to test made-up web pages); two-authors-one-event (grade 7, same facts, different spin); source-mixer (grade 11, several media for one answer).
- Art: drawn in code for the data charts and map; illustrated newsroom (people, illustrated).
- Proposed title: Bike Lane Briefing (applied in the 2026-10-27 audit)
- Proposed concept line (applied in the 2026-10-27 audit, docs/agents/audit/clashes-k8.md K12; it replaces the One idea above where they differ): Two articles disagree about whether new bike lanes made a town safer; as the mayor's aide with the crash data open, sort each disagreement into fact or interpretation, trace claims to evidence, and flag the irrelevant ones for the council.
- Flags: none
