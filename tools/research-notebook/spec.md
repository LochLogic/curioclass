# Research Notebook

## One idea
Every sentence you write keeps a thread back to the exact source words it came from: pull a sentence and the notebook lights its note cards and the lines in the sources, so you can see at a glance which sentences put two sources together, which lean on one, which copy too closely, which have no source at all, and which quietly pick one side of a disagreement the shelf contains.

## Not like
- research-quest (6-8 library, wave 1): a branching story through the research process, judged at the end by a council. Here there is no story and no ending: one real question, a shelf of real sources, and the thread from each written sentence back to its source is the whole tool.
- fake-or-fact-lab (6-8 library, wave 1): decides whether a page can be trusted by leaving it. Here every source is genuine; the question is what each one actually says and how your sentences use it.
- Portrait in Four Media (ela 10, this group): compares how four media tell one life. Here all sources are text and the work is writing your own synthesis from them.
- Source Mixer (ela 11, this group): weighs sources of different media for one claim on a mixing desk. Here the product is a cited paragraph and the check is sentence by sentence.
- Source Portfolio (library 9-12, this group): chooses which sources belong in a balanced set. Here the shelf is fixed and small; choosing is not the skill, using is.
- Question Sharpener (ela 12, this group): the question is the product. Here the question is given, with only a small Narrow or broaden view.
- Fact Fusion (ela 5) and Note Card Shuffle (ela 3), research family, not specced: track which source said what, and sort notes. They will share this engine; here the grade 9 additions are quote versus paraphrase checking, conflicts between sources and MLA or APA citations.

## Who and how
- Grade 9 ELA, module Research Paper (Writing): "Sustained research, synthesis and citation".
- Topic line: "taking notes, synthesizing and citing sources".
- Standards: W.9-10.7, W.9-10.8.

## Screens and modes
Kit frame. Header: "Research Notebook", small line "Why did the passenger pigeon disappear?"; Motion, Sound, Read. No mode buttons: the notebook has five tabs down the left edge like notebook dividers, each with an icon and a word: **Question**, **Shelf**, **Cards**, **Draft**, **Works Cited**. It opens on **Shelf**. A toggle **Threads** (on by default) in the header shows or hides the thread lines.

**Question tab.** The working question at the top in large type. Below it, **Narrow or broaden**: three buttons, **Broader**, **Working**, **Narrower**, each showing its version of the question (Content). Choosing one re-marks the Shelf: each source card shows "Speaks to it: n sentences" and sentences that speak to the chosen version get a small margin dot. A one-line readout says what the choice does (Content). Choosing **Working** again restores everything. Nothing else changes; cards and draft are kept.

**Shelf tab.** Six source cards in two rows, each with its letter (A to F) in its thread color, title, author, year, kind ("Eyewitness naturalist, 1831"), and a context line. Tap a card: the **Reader** opens in the stage: the source's sentences, each its own line with its locator (p. 320) in the margin. Tap a sentence to select it; the Reader's bar then offers **Quote it**, **Paraphrase it** and **Note an idea**. Tap the first and last word of a range inside a sentence to quote only part of it (cutting from the start or end needs no ellipsis; one range per card, so the tool never cuts words from the middle). Summary sources (E and F) have no **Quote it** button (Interactions). **Back to shelf** returns.

**Cards tab.** A board with four columns, the sub-questions (Content), plus **Unsorted** at the left. Every note card shows its kind badge (QUOTE, PARAPHRASE or IDEA), its text, and its source letter and locator. Drag a card to a column, or focus it and press 1 to 5. A column with no cards says "Nothing yet. Which source could answer this?"; a column with 6 or more says "Crowded: are some cards saying the same thing?". **Delete card** on each card (asks Yes / No if the card is used in the Draft).

**Draft tab.** Up to 8 sentence boxes in a column (start with 3; **Add sentence**). Each box has its text field and, under it, its attached cards as chips, plus **Attach card** (opens a picker of all cards, grouped by column) and the toggle **My own thinking** (marks a sentence that needs no source). Each sentence gets a status tag in the margin (Content: sentence statuses). With **Threads** on, a thread runs from each attached chip to a narrow rail at the right of the stage that lists the six sources; tap a sentence and its threads brighten, the rail shows the exact source lines it rests on, and the others dim. **Insert citations** writes in-text citations at the end of each sentence in the chosen style. Below: the paragraph read as one block ("Read as a paragraph"), with citations.

**Works Cited tab.** A style switch **MLA 9** / **APA 7**. The list shows only sources used by at least one Draft sentence, in alphabetical order, formatted in that style (Content), with a hanging indent. A side note shows "How the styles differ" (Content). **Copy list** copies the plain text.

Panel (right, all tabs): `#hint`, a **Draft check** box (counts: sentences 3, synthesis 1, one source 1, uncited 1, too close 0, conflicts 1), and **Clear notebook** (asks Yes / No).

## Interactions in detail
- Quote it: makes a QUOTE card with the exact words in quotation marks and the locator. Quote text can't be edited (only re-made). Partial quotes keep word boundaries.
- Paraphrase it: makes a PARAPHRASE card with an empty text box under the source sentence (shown greyed above it). The closeness check runs as the student types (400 ms after the last key): lowercase both, strip punctuation, split on spaces; if 5 or more words in a row match the source sentence, the card shows "Too close: n words in a row match the source. Quote them or say it your own way." and the matching words are underlined in the source line. If 60 percent or more of the source's words of 4 or more letters reappear, it shows "Mostly the source's words in a new order." Both are advice, not a block; the card keeps its status.
- Note an idea: an IDEA card linked to the selected sentence ("prompted by B7") but counted as your thinking, not the source's.
- Summary sources: E and F sentences can only be paraphrased or noted; the Reader says "This is our summary, not the author's words, so it can't be quoted. Paraphrase it and cite the source."
- Draft citations: Insert citations adds one citation per source per sentence, in order of first attachment, combining the same source's locators ("(Pokagon 54-55)"; MLA joins two sources with a semicolon: "(Audubon 325; Greenberg)"). Re-inserting replaces the old citations, never doubles them. Typing inside a citation is allowed; the next Insert rewrites it.
- Sides differ: the margin note names the other side ("Source E dates the last wild bird to 1902. Say so, or explain why you chose D.") and a button **Show me** opens both sentences side by side in the Reader. Citing both clears it; so does a contrast word (Content).
- Threads: drawn in SVG from chip to rail; with Motion off they appear without the draw-in animation. A source with no attached cards shows a hollow rail marker. More than 12 threads: lines thin to 1 px and only the selected sentence's stay at full strength.
- Save: the notebook (cards, columns, draft sentences, toggles, style, question version) saves to localStorage `rn-notebook` (try/catch) after each change. Clear notebook (after Yes) empties it and returns to the Shelf. Nothing leaves the page; the header says "Saved on this device" after each save.
- Extremes: 8 sentences maximum (Add sentence disabled with the reason in its tooltip); 60 cards maximum ("The notebook is full. Delete a card to add one."); an empty Draft shows "Write your first sentence. Then attach the cards it uses."; Works Cited with no used sources shows "No sources used yet."
- Keyboard: tabs, then the stage, then the panel. In the Reader, arrow keys move between sentences, Q, P and N make cards. Visible focus everywhere. Every thread has a text equivalent in the sentence's chip list.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 and 1280x720 the tabs, stage and panel sit side by side and the Reader's action bar is pinned at the top of the stage. At 680x425 the tab rail shows icons only and the panel collapses to the hint plus a **Check** button that opens the Draft check. At 760 and 375 wide the tabs become a top strip and the rail sits under the Draft.
