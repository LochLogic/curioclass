# Folk Song Arranger (idea card)

- Module: arts-6-8-arranging-a-song, grade 6-8. Codes: MU:Cr2, MU:Cr3, MU:Pr4
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Take a public-domain folk tune and arrange it for a five-piece band for a named occasion (a farewell, a street festival, a school assembly), add an intro and a new section, then run the arranger's checks (can every player reach their notes, can you still hear the melody, does the new section belong) and revise until it fits.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the tune's card (where it comes from, who sang it and for what) with the melody playing, and the occasion card.
  - Core loop: choose instruments, give the melody to one, write a bass line and a rhythm from pattern blocks, add an intro and a contrasting section. The checks are real craft: notes out of an instrument's range turn red; the mix meter shows when the accompaniment buries the melody; the form view shows how the new section relates to the tune.
  - The aha or the end state: the student plays the arrangement against the occasion and writes a rationale for each revision ("I moved the melody to the trumpet because the guitars covered it"). A final version exports as a lead sheet and audio.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): swap arrangements with a partner and leave one note.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MU:Cr2: selecting and developing the arrangement's ideas and documenting them in notation.
  - MU:Cr3: evaluating with the checks and describing the reason for each revision, then presenting the final version.
  - MU:Pr4: choosing tempo, dynamics and style to interpret the tune for its occasion, and analyzing the tune's structure first.
- Not like: song-form-builder (grade 3-5 forms); beat-lab (grade 9-12 production); chord-explorer (progressions).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the band; the tune's card uses a public domain image of its time and place where one exists (to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Arrange a public-domain folk tune for a small band and a named occasion: pick instruments, add an intro and a new section, run the arranger's checks for range and balance, revise and explain each change.
- Flags: sensitive (each tune's community and history: a fresh review agent checks the tune cards, rule 5); needs a data source (tune origins and public domain status, checked)
