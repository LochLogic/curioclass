## What it teaches
Students measure their own perception and memory, then explain how context, attention, interference and reconstruction shape experience.
They distinguish a personal observation from a population finding and a simplified model from the whole visual system.

## Key skills
- Make a judgment before seeing the reference.
- Control rehearsal and record an experiment honestly.
- Compare personal observations with research while noticing differences in the tasks.
- Explain how context and attention change what is perceived.
- Separate confidence in a memory from evidence of its accuracy.

## Support at home
- Ask your student to describe how the two checkerboard squares can share one gray. Compare a light object in shade and sunlight.
- Together, recall the details of an ordinary room before looking again. Discuss differences without treating them as a test of ability.
- When a memory differs, ask what evidence could help, rather than who sounds most confident.

## Five-minute start
Open Line Lengths. Judge the shafts without measuring them. Finish all eight adjustments, then compare the angles and controls.
Open Why and ask whether this small personal sample follows the research pattern. No result ranks the student.

## Stations
- Line Lengths: six fin conditions and two no-fin controls. No length readout or per-trial correction. Controls estimate baseline matching error.
- Shadow Squares: match A then B on two gray sets. Bridge and Hide the scene reveal identical pixel values.
- Inside the Eye: visit seven stages, predict Edge or Middle, and compare three patterns with neighbor inhibition. The prediction concerns differences between neighboring outputs, rather than the absolute drop from input to output.
- Spot the Change: six scenes with a brief gray gap, followed by one without a gap. A correct kind chosen after a wrong kind is retained as a correction, excluded from uncued-find averages. Older finds with misses and no kind history are also excluded from those averages. Manual Flip is available and is the Motion off default. Repeated flips retain at least 560 ms of each image and a 160 ms blank, so rapid taps cannot speed up the flashes.
- Count Backward: ten letter/digit trials. Count by threes while retaining the target. Trials below 70% counting accuracy are marked hollow.
- Word Lists: visual free recall then recognition, including Picture it and Just know judgments. Restarted lists are marked seen twice.
- Notebook retains first and latest complete runs on this device. Reset all asks for confirmation. Repeated change scenes are practice and not saved.
- Eyewitness connects reconstructive memory with historical research. It requests no personal testimony and has no score.

## Talk questions
What did the controls rule out? Which explanations need the retina, and which also need an inference about lighting?
Did the first or digit-switch trial differ from the middle trials? Why is the counting task part of the experiment?
How should a jury distinguish confidence from evidence? A word-list result cannot establish whether a particular witness is accurate.

## Good to know
These are classroom demonstrations, not standardized assessments, diagnoses or replications with research-grade equipment.
Timing uses the browser display clock. A hidden page interrupts a timed memory run. Screen brightness, input speed and scene familiarity affect results.
The retinal calculation is a deliberately simple neighbor model. Historical percentages describe particular study samples.
Peterson and Peterson fitted a stimulus-fluctuation model. The falling endpoints alone do not establish decay as the cause.
All results remain on this device. Storage may be unavailable in private browsing or restricted school settings.
The specified calm-professor voice is listed for later recording. This build uses the kit's browser speech fallback; no audio was recorded.

## Standards and sensitivity
APA.Biological: follow sensory transduction and the visual pathway; predict and test edge enhancement; compare perception with physical gray values.
APA.Cognition: attention in change detection, proactive interference in short-term recall, and reconstruction in word recall and recognition.
Cross-cultural illusion research is presented as a disagreement, never as a ranking of societies. Eyewitness material concerns wrongful convictions.
Allow discussion without requiring a student to disclose personal or family experience. No body, intelligence or personality labels are assigned.
