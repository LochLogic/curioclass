## What it teaches
Students describe how a tune's speed, loudness, pitch and feeling can help it fit a moment in daily life.
They explain a listening choice and hear the same melody change from a march toward bedtime music.

## Key skills
- Listen for fast and slow, loud and soft.
- Connect music to bedtime, parades, birthdays and rainy days.
- Give a reason for a choice using a musical clue.
- Say what you like about a favorite tune.
- Change speed and loudness while keeping the notes the same.

## Support at home
- Hum a familiar tune softly and slowly at bedtime. Try the same tune a little faster the next day and talk about how it feels.
- Listen to rain or a celebration together. Ask what music might belong there and why.
- Take turns choosing a favorite song. Ask what each person likes about its sound.

## A five-minute start
Project Sort. Tap Tune 1, let it finish, and invite the class to vote for a scene. Tap the scene and ask for a clue.
Try both words in a pair and listen again if needed. A tune can fit more than its composer's intended moment.
Switch to Turn it, tap Play, and lower Speed and Loudness. Hear the same notes in a different setting.

## The modes
Sort has eight original melodies. Tap once to play, again to stop. Tap a scene after selecting a tune, or drag its card.
The tune's shape and number appear in the scene. Its listening button stays in the row so replay is always easy.
The clue tray has five pairs. A check mark identifies an earned clue, and its icon travels to the tune card when Motion is on.
Earned clues stay as badges; the card's accessible name includes their words. Scenes closes the tray.
Composer's note reveals the intended moment gently. Our favorite keeps one class favorite, with a prompt to explain why.
Place a tune in Somewhere else if the class has another moment in mind. Nothing is marked wrong for its scene choice.

Turn it loops Big Drum March. Change Speed or Loudness with plus and minus. The meter weights the two dials equally.
Fast and soft can be In between, just as slow and loud can. March again returns to 120 bpm and loudness 9.

## Talk about it
- Which sound helped you choose this moment?
- Could someone put this tune somewhere else? What might their reason be?
- What do you like about our favorite?
- When the notes stayed the same, what changed the music's feeling?

## Good to know
Teacher led, intended for 15 to 20 minutes with listening, votes and spoken reasons. No microphone is used.
Keyboard: 1 to 8 play tunes in Sort. Tab and Enter select scenes and clues. In Turn it, arrow keys change the dial
whose controls hold focus; otherwise they change Speed. Space on Play or the page starts or stops the march.
Read pauses the tune and speaks the current hint. Sound off keeps the visual beat timing. Motion off uses three scene states.
The app saves no tunes, placements, favorite or badges. The shared kit remembers only the Motion setting on this device.
All music is original and synthesized offline. The narrator uses the browser voice until friendly-dad recordings are supplied.
K-2 MU:Re8 is practiced by choosing expressive clues, MU:Re9 by choosing and explaining a favorite, and MU:Cn11
by connecting listening to everyday moments. Spoken discussion is central to this teacher-led activity.
