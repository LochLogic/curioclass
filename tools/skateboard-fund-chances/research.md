# Research packet: skateboard-fund-chances

Concept: Save toward a skateboard with chance cards each month: keep money in a jar or buy shares in the class juice company that may grow or shrink; then lock, insure or risk the skateboard as rain, a cracked wheel and a theft come up.

Grade: 4   Standards: NSPFE.Investing, NSPFE.Managing Risk

## Standards in plain words

- NSPFE Investing introduces fourth graders to setting money aside for future goals, understanding that investing in enterprise shares carries opportunities for growth alongside the risk of loss.
- NSPFE Managing Risk teaches fourth graders that risk is an unavoidable part of life, and individuals can reduce, avoid, or cope with unexpected losses through preventive habits, emergency savings, and insurance.

## Real data

- Council for Economic Education / Jump$tart National Standards for Personal Financial Education (Grade 4): Managing Risk benchmarks 4-1 (understanding risk), 4-2 (risk reduction), and 4-3 (emergency savings).
- FDIC Money Smart for Young People (Grades 3-5): Concepts of business investment risk, loss prevention, and insurance fundamentals.

## Common mistakes

- Treating investing like gambling: Fourth graders often view investment gains as pure luck rather than tied to business performance and customer sales.
- Believing insurance is free money: Students frequently overlook that insurance requires upfront premiums whether an accident happens or not.
- Assuming risk can be eliminated entirely: Students often think buying a lock or buying insurance makes risk zero, missing that accidents and market declines remain possible.

## Sensitivity

- Household financial disparities: Many students come from families without discretionary investment assets or comprehensive insurance; frame decisions around kid-scale items (a skateboard, class juice stand) rather than adult wealth comparisons.
- Misfortune and theft: Depict theft and weather damage neutrally as probabilistic events rather than moral failings of victims.

## Similar tools elsewhere

- CFPB Youth Financial Education: "Managing Unexpected Expenses" and "Save for What You Want" activity cards.
- BizWorld: Classroom entrepreneurship and investment simulations for upper elementary grades.

## Gaps

- Fourth-grade math should keep share valuation and damage expenses transparent (e.g., each share costs $1, monthly earnings either add $0.20 or subtract $0.10, lock costs $2) so Curio Class students focus on risk-reward trade-offs rather than complex fractional calculus.
