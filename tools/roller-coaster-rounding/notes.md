## What it teaches
Students round whole numbers to the nearest ten or hundred by comparing distances to stations. They use estimates to check exact sums and differences, trade place-value tickets, and connect a train array to multiplying tens.

## Key skills
- Compare distances and use the agreed rule at a halfway point.
- Add and subtract within 1000 using hundreds, tens and ones.
- Bundle ten small pieces or break one larger piece into ten.
- Use an estimate to catch a large error, then check columns for small errors.
- Count equal groups of tens and compare seats with riders.

## Support at home
- Pick a number of steps or objects. Ask which nearby ten is closer and how you know.
- Use small drawn marks as ones and circles of ten as tens. Trade ten marks for one circle while adding.
- Imagine several groups with ten places each. Ask how many places there are in all.

## A five-minute start
Open Track and tap Drop. Ask why the held car rolls to 50. Use Tricky drop to compare 45 with 44, then switch to Hundreds. In Line, work Card 1 together, including both manual bundles. Try Train 1 and distinguish its number of cars from its number of seats.

## Modes
Track is free exploration. Guesses are never scored. The two neighboring stations are shown at a useful close scale. The smaller ten-hill overview highlights that interval within the full window. Tap either track to move the held car; Back 100 and Ahead 100 move to another Tens window.

Line has nine unrestricted cards, four clerk checks and a More riders generator. Round each count, enter the estimate, then work the ones, tens and hundreds. Subtraction requires a legal Take away before a remainder entry. Break roll and Break strip trade one bigger piece for ten smaller ones. A strip always contains exactly ten separately drawn single-ticket images.

Trains begins as an array you can change. Each painted car represents ten fictional seats. Cards hide the completed equation until the student enters both the tens and the seats. Cards 6 and 7 also ask whether the seats carry 278 riders.

## Talk questions
- On a hilltop, why does the car need a park rule?
- Why does bundling change the pieces but keep the value?
- Can an answer inside the sensible stretch still be wrong?
- Why is 24 tens different from 24 seats?

## Good to know
Hilltop Park, the Hilltopper ride and all capacities are made up. This is a rounding and place-value model, not a physics or ride-safety simulation. Station brakes stop the car in each valley. Halfway values round toward the bigger number by an agreed convention.

The sensible stretch extends one rounding station on either side of the estimate, clipped to the tool's zero-to-1000 range. An exact answer can lie on its edge. Being inside is a reasonableness check, not proof.

The park board counts completed fixed cards once each and drops on this page only. Corrective retries can reveal answers, so completion is an activity record, not evidence of independent mastery. It stores no student work, scores or timers. Start over resets the current activity; reloading resets the board. Motion removes movement while retaining the length of the roll and lap. Sound and Read use the shared kit; fixed narration is currently browser speech because this build does not record audio.
