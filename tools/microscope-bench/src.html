Microscope Bench / Manual

At the bench

Turn either focus knob by dragging up or down, by scrolling, or with arrow keys. The coarse knob moves 50 µm per step. Shift with arrows on coarse focus moves 1 µm. Fine focus spans ±50 µm in 1 µm steps. Home and End reach each limit.

The stage moves 0.05 mm per step. Shift moves 1 mm. Dragging the view moves the slide: push right and the image moves left. When the eyepiece has focus, arrows move the stage.

Choose an objective with 1, 2, 3 or 4. The labelled total magnification is ten times the nominal objective power. E inserts the eyepiece reticle; R turns it a quarter turn. X washes the slide; K opens the sketchbook.

Making a mount

Open Specimen tray or Prepared slides, choose a glass slide, then Place on stage. A tray item can also be dragged to the eyepiece. With the keyboard, Enter selects a tray item and returns to the eyepiece; Enter there puts it on the stage.

Prepare mount holds the water dropper, dry glass, iodine and cover slips. Water lets the pond organisms move. Dry glass stops them. Lowering a cover slip changes the focus model; dropping it flat traps modelled air bubbles. Lift and lower gently removes those bubbles. Wash slide or X leaves empty glass.

Iodine is available for onion skin. It exchanges a real unstained photographic field for real Lugol-stained fields. These are different cells from different preparations, not a before-and-after record of one cell. Prepared slides keep their original stain and medium. The prepared cheek field is already stained with methylene blue; its biological-size scale is approximate, so use the blood fields for measurement. No unstained version of that same cheek field is supplied, so it has no stain-switch bottle.

The small glass locator shows the slide outline and which area the eyepiece covers. Finds are not marked. Empty space beyond the photographs is glass, not additional sampled tissue. Scan at low power before changing the objective.

Reading the instrument

MAG is the labelled objective power × 10. FIELD uses the modelled true objective powers: 3.9, 9.8, 41 and 98 times. Their small deviations from the labels stand for manufacturing variation; they are declared model settings, not measurements of a physical microscope. These are optical field diameters, independent of screen size. STAGE is slide displacement; FOCUS is displacement from the reference focal plane.

DEPTH is an estimate using 0.55 µm × refractive index / numerical aperture squared. It is the wave-optical term, not a full model of a real microscope or the eye. At 100×, oil uses index 1.515 and aperture 1.25. Without oil, aperture is modelled as 0.65; the image is softer and dimmer. Lower lamp settings darken the field.

Closing the iris reduces light, raises contrast and softens small details. This bounded rendering effect is an optical approximation, not a measurement of the source microscope’s aperture. The depth readout is the nominal wave-optical estimate, before the iris approximation.

Calibrating and measuring

The eyepiece has a 10 mm reticle with 100 divisions, fixed in the eyepiece plane. Its nominal specimen division is 100 µm divided by objective power. Calibration is kept separately for each objective.

Focus the micrometer. Rotate the reticle if needed and move the stage so both scales begin together. Count the micrometer intervals and the reticle intervals over the same distance. Calibrate opens beside the live view: keep moving the slide if needed, then enter the two counts. The factor is 10 µm × micrometer intervals / reticle intervals.

The numbers you enter determine the scale. There is no automatic approval or adjustment of your observation. The micrometer geometry always keeps its exact dimensions. Changing the objective recalls its own calibration.

Sketchbook

Pencil draws freehand. Eraser removes a stroke where it touches. Write a label, choose Label pin and tap the paper. For keyboard drawing, focus the paper: arrows move the pencil, Space puts it down or lifts it, Enter adds a label in Label pin.

Notes, labels, date, mount and magnification save after each change. New page adds a blank sheet. Your sketches stay on this device. Print prints the current page alone. If storage is unavailable, a visible message says so.

Photographs, depth and motion

All biological and mineral material is photographed. The body, glass, bubbles, lamp, exact stage micrometer and reticle are code. No cell, organism, vein, grain or tissue structure has been generated or drawn. Crop boundaries remain finite; enlarging cannot recover information absent from the source. Some fine structures remain unresolved even with the strongest objective.

The pond is assembled from six distinct photographic cutouts, each placed once. This assemblage does not represent natural abundance or one natural pond field. Moving cutouts follow wandering computed paths; it is not recorded swimming. Motion off freezes that path exactly. Dry mounting also stops movement. The still cutouts do not animate their hairs: this source cannot show its cilia beating.

Most defocus is computed blur around each mount’s reference plane, scaled to the field diameter. Its logarithmic response compresses the full mechanical travel so focus keeps changing visibly. Defocus uses a reduced-resolution layer with linear reconstruction, avoiding an expensive Gaussian filter on every pan. This is a rendering approximation, not an optical point-spread measurement. Dry mounting and lifting a cover slip shift the modelled focal plane; the offsets are declared instrument settings. Hair spans a modelled depth gradient of 15 micrometres per 100 micrometres along its length: focus blurs different lengths of the single photograph, not newly measured surfaces. The fixed pond colony is different: nine registered photographs, taken 25 µm apart, are blended as focus travels from +100 to −100 µm. Outside that range, extra blur is computed. Its original darkfield and pseudocolour are retained. This is not fluorescence, living motion or a reconstruction of hidden anatomy.

The sand source uses reflected light. Other source contrast methods and stains remain as photographed; this instrument cannot turn them into new measurements. Water, cover glass, air and immersion oil change a simplified optical model, not the chemistry inside those photographs. The 100× objective is dimmer and softer without oil. Turret changes shift focus by only a few micrometres in the model.

Each material is one particular specimen someone else prepared. Source scale bars were measured in the exact downloaded files, usually with about one pixel endpoint uncertainty. Hair uses a standard counting-grid pitch, an inference stated in its source entry. Objective power alone was never used to invent a digital pixel size.
