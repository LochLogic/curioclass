# Art Through Time

## One idea
Every work on the timeline is tied by threads to what it answered: a new invention, a trade route, an artist it learned from. Pull a thread and you see why art changed, not just when.

## Not like
- art-style-match (3-5, cards, same family and group): names a style from what you can see. Here what you see matters less than what was going on around it; no painting appears in both tools.
- art-passport (K-2, explorer, same family): a world map of objects to look at and feel about. Here the axis is time and the links are causes.
- music-history-radio (9-12, timeline): tuning a dial through musical eras and listening. Here nothing plays; you trace threads between works and events.
- gallery-critique (9-12, roleplay, same family and group): judges one work in depth. Here twenty-one works, each met briefly, connected.
- design-fixer (6-8, same group): no history in it; all made-up posters.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, visual arts, module "Art History Timeline (Visual Arts)".
- Topic line: art history and what changed it
- Driver: students or teacher (laptops; or a projector, with the class predicting which work an event changed). 15 to 30 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS VA:Re7 (perceive and analyze artistic work), VA:Cn11 (relate artistic ideas and works with societal, cultural and historical context).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound`. Modes (`.seg`): `Timeline` (explore, the default), `What Changed?` (optional challenge).

**Timeline.** Focus: the band and the thread being drawn on it; an opened work takes the panel (or an overlay under 1000 px wide), never covering the thread it starts. The stage is a long walnut-colored band with a time axis through the middle, from 1500 BCE to 1925 CE, drawn at real pixels. Works sit above the line as small framed cards with year labels; event cards (inventions, trade, ideas) sit below the line as parchment tags, each with its small painted picture (Art) and year. The scale is compressed before 1000 CE (a break mark shows where) so the crowded 1800s get room; tick labels read "1500 BCE", "500 BCE", "1 CE", "500", "1000", "1400", "1600", "1800", "1900".
- Scroll or drag the band sideways; `Zoom in` and `Zoom out` (and the mouse wheel with Ctrl) change the scale; a mini-map strip above the band shows where you are and jumps on tap.
- Tap a work: it opens in the art-museum frame (Family engine) in the panel or an overlay, with its wall label, a short "Around it" line (Content), and its threads as buttons ("Learned from Caravaggio", "Invention: photography, 1839"). Tapping a thread button draws that thread on the band (an arc between the two cards, with its type icon and a word) and scrolls to the other end.
- Tap an event card: its line and the works it threads to.
- Thread types, each with its own line style and icon (never color alone): `Learned from` (solid arc, an open book), `Invention` (dashed arc, a gear), `Trade and travel` (dotted arc, a ship), `Compare` (thin double arc, two eyes; "same idea, far apart", never claims influence).
- Panel controls: `All threads` toggle (shows every thread faintly), thread type filters (four toggles), `Era` jump buttons (`Ancient`, `1000 to 1500`, `1500 to 1800`, `1800s`, `1900s`), `Spotlight` (zooms to a random work not yet opened). Opened works get a small check (session only).

**What Changed?** Focus: the dealt event card (shown large at the top of the panel with its painted picture) and the band it goes onto. An event card is dealt in the panel (Content events with a Learned from, Invention or Trade thread; Compare threads are never asked). The student drags it onto the work it changed (or taps the work, then `Place`). Right: the thread draws, the event card settles on the band, one sentence explains it. Wrong and the event is later than the work: "That work was made before this happened. Look later on the timeline." Wrong otherwise: "Not this one. Look for what is new in the picture." After two misses, `Show me` highlights three candidate works. `Next event` deals the next. A strip shows how many events are placed (out of 11). No timer, no score beyond the count.

## Interactions in detail
- Positioning: a work sits at its year on the axis; cards that would overlap stack in up to three rows. Events sit below at their year, also in up to three rows. The compressed scale: 1500 BCE to 1000 CE takes the first 30% of the band length; 1000 to 1925 takes 70%. At zoom 1 the band is 6,000 px long (about 4.5 px a year after 1000), the shortest length at which every card, in an 80 px slot, fits in three rows (the 1800s are the crowded part). Zoom runs from showing the whole band in the view to 2 times; when the band is too short for cards to fit in three rows, cards shrink to year dots with a count badge for each cluster, and tapping a cluster zooms in on it.
- Threads draw as SVG arcs (above the line for work to work, crossing the line for event to work) in 400 ms; instant with Motion off. At most 8 drawn at once in Timeline (older ones fade out) unless `All threads` is on.
- What Changed? order: the 11 events except lostwax, shuffled; an event with two right works (photo, cezanne, abstraction) accepts either and draws both after the first is placed. japan's right work here is mucha only: mishima was printed in 1833, before trade opened, so dropping japan on it gives the "made before" line (its Trade thread still shows in Timeline, where it explains how the print reached Paris).
- Nothing waits on a motion: a tap or drag while a thread draws (400 ms), the band scrolls to a thread's far end or a card settles acts at once (the motion jumps to its end) and the old line stops.
- Placed events are remembered for the session only. Opened-work checks are session only. Nothing is stored.
- Keyboard: the band is a list of buttons in time order (after the panel controls); Left and Right move between cards; Enter opens; in What Changed?, Enter on a work places the dealt event.
- Sizes: the band height is 60% of the stage at 1366x768 with cards at least 72 px tall; in the 680x425 preview only the mini-map and one era are shown with cards 56 px tall and labels at least 11 px. At 375 wide the band scrolls under a full-width work view.

## Look
- Accent (`--brand`) #8a2a2b oxblood. Paper #f4ecdc (parchment), ink #2a2118, muted #6a5c4c; band walnut #3b2a20 with parchment text; thread colors by type are extra (Learned from #e0a64b, Invention #8fc3e8, Trade #9fd39a, Compare #e8b4d8 on walnut), always with their line style and icon.
- Contrast: white on brand 8.6:1; brand on paper 7.3:1; ink on paper 13.5:1; parchment on walnut 11.6:1; muted on paper 5.5:1. Thread colors on walnut: the builder computes and keeps each at 3:1 or more (non-text).
- Different from art-style-match (magenta on teal felt), art-passport (cream and blue), design-fixer (indigo and mat green).

## Family engine
art-museum (full description in art-style-match's spec). This tool uses `frame`, `label`, `zoomTo` (works have one or two optional detail spots for Read-aloud details, not required) and the image rules, and feeds 21 work records with a `year`. The band, threads and event cards are this tool's own.

## Done when

2. Every thread in Content draws between the right ends with its type's line style, icon and word.
3. What Changed?: each of the 11 events can be placed; an event dropped on an earlier work gives the "made before" line (check rhino with photo, and mishima with japan).
4. Compare threads never appear in What Changed?.
5. The Benin note shows with the plaque.
6. The band scrolls with drag, keys and the mini-map; Zoom works at every size; cards stay 44 px or larger to tap.
7. How lines once in five opens in a row; right and miss lines every time, dropped when stale.
8. The bundled file is under 2 MB.
9. Focus: at 1366x768 and 680x425 the band is the largest thing in Timeline, and in What Changed? the dealt card's painted picture and the band are both in view without scrolling.
10. Tap while it plays: a tap during a thread draw, a scroll to a thread's far end or a card settle acts at once, and no stale line plays after.
