## What it teaches
Students use gravity to build and change orbiting systems. They can explain why changing a body's speed, mass or distance changes its path, and compare a pocket model with the scale and motion of the real Solar System.

## Key skills
- Try a throw, watch its path and change one thing at a time.
- Use a rewind to compare two possible futures.
- Follow a planet to investigate moons and apparent backward motion.
- Compare sizes, distances and orbit times in a model.
- Explain which bodies pull on each other.

## Support at home
- Ask your child to describe a universe they made, including a change that surprised them.
- Walk a small circle and then a large circle together. Talk about how the distance changes the time to go around.
- Look for the Moon together. Ask what keeps it near Earth while Earth moves around the Sun.

## A 5-minute start
Open Pocket and let students throw without a tutorial. Press in space, drag in the direction of travel and release.
A longer drag gives a faster throw. After a crash, drag the time bar backward and try a different throw.
The first useful conversation is: "What pulled what, and what did you change?"

## Modes
**Pocket:** deliberately exaggerated masses let moons fit on screen. An open-ended instrument, with a Manual field guide and a book of Setups. There is no score or finish line.
Try Rescue the moon: rewind to the beginning, select the moon and tap Slower five times while following the giant.
Try Three-body dance: select a dancer, open Body, tap Faster once and watch with the Time dial at x4.

**Real Solar System:** starts on 1 January 2000, using JPL J2000 elements in one plane and NASA masses and diameters.
Open World for Fit, which cycles Inner, Giants and All. Planets normally appear larger than life. True size uses physical radii with locating rings;
its Earth diameter label changes with the actual screen zoom. At 100 world pixels per AU, Earth is 0.0085 world pixels across.
Follow Earth with Trails Forever to see Mars's backward motion and Venus's flower. At x1 an Earth year takes about 1.99 seconds.

## Three ways to use a period
1. Free play for 15 minutes, then each pair shows one thing they made and names what pulled what.
2. Open any Setup and change one variable. Rewind and compare why some arrangements survive.
3. Follow Earth with Forever trails. Describe a pattern, then browse Things people have found in the Manual.

## Talk questions
- What changed when you made the same throw faster?
- How does following a planet change the trails you see?
- Why does a black hole of the same mass give the same distant pull as a star?
- Which parts of Pocket would be misleading without its mass-ratio note?
- What does turning gravity off show about the body's motion?

## Good to know
The simulation uses Newton's inverse-square gravity. Every massive body pulls every other; dust feels the pull but contributes none.
A softened force (one world pixel), leapfrog integration at 1/480 second and adaptive substeps keep encounters tractable.
Collisions merge mass and momentum. This models orbital motion, not tides, stellar evolution, relativity or a real galaxy's mass distribution.
The black hole is Newtonian and only absorbs bodies that come very close.

The Gravity dial scales the gravitational constant in this model. Real space does not have that dial.
Pocket uses Star 1000, Giant 100 and Planet 10 mass units. Real Jupiter is about 1/1048 of the Sun's mass.
The measured classroom budget is 20 massive bodies and 160 dust motes, using the spec's permitted dust fallback.
If physics cannot keep pace, the Time readout reports the measured running speed.
Motion off stops decorative pulsing, sparks and twinkling; orbital motion is the content and continues.

The finite planet mass matters in Lightweight. Halving the Starter star alone leaves its planet slightly bound;
one Faster nudge on the planet before halving the star makes the escape achievable. The finding line reflects this computation.

Save and My universes use only local storage on this device, including the replay history. No information leaves the device.
The tool still plays if storage is unavailable. Up to twelve named saves are retained. Delete and replacing the oldest save ask first.
All 27 gouache illustrations are embedded in the offline file. Narration uses the browser voice until recordings are supplied.

## Keyboard
Space plays or pauses. Ctrl+Z undoes. Number keys 1 to 8 choose tray items.
Enter on a tray item places it 200 world pixels to the right of the followed or heaviest body at that spot's circular speed.
Tab within the space view selects successive bodies. F follows the selected body; Delete removes it.
G toggles Ghost; plus and minus zoom; 0 fits. Left and right on the time bar step one second.
Wheel zooms at the pointer; right drag or a second touch pans. Space activates a focused button; in the space view it plays or pauses.

## Instrument console
The sky fills the screen. The lower-left Manual folds out from the console beside the maker's plate.
Setups opens from either the Manual or the console, including a fresh Real Solar System. Pocket and Real
also have their own console switch and independently retained worlds while the page stays open.
Open World for Gravity, Ghost, trails, Fit, zoom, True size and Clear. Tap a body and open Body to change it.
The Time dial turns by pointer or arrow keys to x0.25, x1, x4 or x16. The tape rewinds the last 150 world seconds.

Readouts use pocket mass units, world pixels and world seconds in Pocket; kg, km/s and AU in Real.
Speed and distance are relative to the named parent, or to the simulation origin if there is no parent.
A parent is the nearest bound body at least three times heavier; completed laps reset when that parent changes.
The Manual's 24 unnumbered field notes have no targets, progress or completion checks. Old local stickers
are ignored when a saved library is read, and are omitted on the next save. Named universes remain usable.
