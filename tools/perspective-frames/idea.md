# Perspective Frames (idea card)

- Module: eld-6-8-comparing-perspectives, grade 6-8. Codes: WIDA.ELD-5, WIDA.Argue
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good: sorting statements by speaker comes first, then the comparison is written.
- One idea: A town is deciding something real (whether to build a highway through a neighborhood in the 1950s, whether to turn a parking lot into a park today), and you sort quotes by who said them (a planner, a shop owner, a family who lives there, a commuter), then use comparison frames (Whereas..., Both agree that..., Unlike...) to explain why they see it differently.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a town meeting poster and a pile of quote cards, each with a short clue about the speaker. "Who said this? Why would they think so?"
  - Core loop: the student sorts quotes into speaker columns, using clues in the words (my customers, my kids' school, my commute). Then for pairs of speakers the student picks what each wants and why, and builds comparison sentences from frames. A sentence that compares without a reason ("The planner and the family disagree.") is nudged to add the why.
  - The aha or the end state: a perspective map showing what each speaker gains and loses, and the student writes a short paragraph comparing two viewpoints, then says which they find most convincing and why.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): Help Lots / Some / Little; the class adds a speaker the cards left out.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - WIDA.ELD-5: the language of social studies: stakeholders, decisions and consequences in a community.
  - WIDA.Argue: comparing viewpoints with reasons and taking a supported position.
- Not like: is-the-historian-right (grade 9-12, judging a historian's claim); summary-scaffold (one source); i-choose-because (K-2, one choice).
- Art: illustrated speakers (people, illustrated); public domain photos for the historical case (Library of Congress, ids to check).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Sort quotes from a town decision (a 1950s highway through a neighborhood, a parking lot turned park) by who said them, then explain why the speakers see it differently with comparison frames and take a position.
- Flags: sensitive (the highway case touches displacement of Black and immigrant neighborhoods: fresh review, rule 5, plain facts with care); home-language glossary may matter (open question, not decided).
