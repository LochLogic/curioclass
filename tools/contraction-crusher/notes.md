## A 5-minute start

1. Read the sentence strip together: "I do not like getting wet."
2. Ask the class which letters will fall out when **do** and **not** squish together. Tap those letters (they turn red).
3. Press **Crush!** The press squishes the second word, the letters that drop out tumble away, and the robot throws the apostrophe into the gap. The sentence changes to "I don't like getting wet."

## The big idea

A contraction is two words squished into one. Some letters fall out, and the apostrophe takes their place. It marks the exact spot where the letters used to be: in **don't**, it sits where the **o** was.

## Talk about it

- Where did the apostrophe land? What letter used to be there?
- Read the sentence both ways. Does it mean the same thing?
- Which families drop just one letter (n't, 's, 're, 'm)? Which drop two ('ll, 've)?

## Good to know

- You don't have to guess first. Press **Crush!** with nothing tapped and it just shows the answer.
- **Uncrush** runs it backwards: the apostrophe pops out and the letters come home.
- **Word families** limit the words to one pattern (only n't, only 'll...). **All** mixes them, starting with "do not".
- Every contraction you make goes on the **Made today** shelf. Tap one to hear it again.
- "Will not" becomes "won't", which breaks the pattern, so it's left out. It makes a good surprise to share once the pattern is solid. "Can not" usually becomes "cannot" first, but the press shows the same result: can't.
- Sound starts on. There's no score.

## Voice

- A warm teacher voice welcomes the class after the first tap and, after each crush, says whether the marked letters were right, what the two words became and the new sentence. **Sound** turns this off; **Read** still works.
- **Read** says the sentence and asks which letters fall out, or, once crushed, says what the words became and the new sentence.
