## A 5-minute start

1. In **Block**, The Note is open on beat 1: Ava sits at the table, reading the note. Open **Stage words** below the
   panel for stage right, upstage, cheat out and the five body positions. Tap **Next beat**: Ben has come in by the Door.
   Tap **Next beat** again: Ava turned to talk to Ben, who stands behind her, and the plan marks her "upstaged".
2. Tap **House**. This is seat B3: Ava shows her back and the eye goes to Ben, and the list says why (each actor's
   focus reasons). Tap a corner seat, such as A1, in the seat buttons.
3. Back in **Block**, drag Ben down level with Ava, or select Ava and tap **Turn left** until she cheats out. The
   note turns to a check. Tap **Run** at the top to watch the scene. The notes still to fix come first; tap one to jump
   to its beat, and the hint carries that note.

## The three modes

- **Block:** the ground plan from above, with the house below it and a live view from the chosen seat in the corner.
  Drag actors from the wings, drag set pieces from the Set tab, set each area's light (off, half, full) and a Spot.
  Toggles: **Sightlines** (lines from the seat to each face, red where blocked), **Focus** (a bar and score per actor,
  an eye on where the eye goes), **Areas**, **Seat** (cycles the 16 seats). Each beat starts as a copy of the one
  before; a beat you changed keeps its own layout (a dot marks it in the beat list). The beat's notes stay pinned
  above the Actor, Set and Lights tabs while you work.
- **House:** the view from one seat in perspective. Hidden actors show as a dashed ghost; each actor carries a body
  position tag with initials ("Av full back"), and the list under the seat buttons gives each actor's focus reasons.
- **Run:** plays the whole scene from your seat with captions and cue tags, then checks every director's note from all
  sixteen seats. Tap a note to jump to its beat.

Scenes: The Note (7 beats), Bus Stop (6), The Trophy (6), and Free stage (six actors you can rename, up to 12 beats
you write; **Save on this device** keeps it in this browser only).

## Talk questions

- Why does a director say "stage right" for the actor's right, not the audience's?
- Ava is speaking but the eye goes to Ben. What three things could you change? Which one keeps the scene truest?
- A seat on the far side sees something the center seat doesn't. Whose seat do you block for?
- Light, level, body position, movement: which pulled focus most in your run?

## Good to know

- Sizes (a 10 m opening, 7 m deep, seats 3 m apart), the hidden and partly hidden thresholds and the focus scores are
  this tool's own rough guide, said on screen. Real houses and directors differ.
- Body positions are measured to the seat you pick, so the same actor can be profile from one seat and one-quarter
  from another. Upstaging is judged from seat B3.
- "Has the focus" and "not upstaged" need the actor open to the house (profile or better), so a speaker with her back
  to the house never passes them.
- "Enters by" counts the wing or Door the actor waited in during the beat before (the nearest entrance only when that
  is not known); "exits" counts the wing they go to. Dropping an actor into the house is refused: they go back.
- Free stage saves only when you press Save, and only in this browser. Clear asks first, then removes it.
