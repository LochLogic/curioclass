# Research packet: then-and-now-overlay
Concept: Slide back through one real Main Street in the 1890s, 1940s and today using public-domain insurance maps, photos and a directory; compare what each source tells you, and add your own town as an extra.
Grade: 3   Standards: D2.His.1.3-5, D2.His.2.3-5, D2.His.9.3-5, D2.His.10.3-5

## Standards in plain words
- D2.His.1.3-5: "Create and use a chronological sequence of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Place maps, photographs, and directory listings in chronological order across the 1890s, 1940s, and today to trace community changes over time.
- D2.His.2.3-5: "Compare life in specific historical time periods to life today." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Contrast daily life, shops, and transportation on a late-19th-century and mid-20th-century Main Street with modern town centers.
- D2.His.9.3-5: "Summarize how different kinds of historical sources are used to explain events in the past." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain what unique information is provided by a fire insurance map (building materials), a photograph (visual appearance), and a city directory (occupations).
- D2.His.10.3-5: "Compare information provided by different historical sources about the past." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Cross-reference a building footprint on a Sanborn map with a photograph of its storefront and its merchant listing in a town directory.

## Real data
- Library of Congress Sanborn Maps Collection: over 700,000 digitized sheets showing fire insurance maps of American towns and cities from 1867 to 1970. URL: https://www.loc.gov/collections/sanborn-maps/. Licence: Public Domain (pre-1929 sheets and government holdings).
- Library of Congress Prints and Photographs Division (FSA/OWI and Detroit Publishing Collections): high-resolution streetscapes from the 1890s and 1940s. URL: https://www.loc.gov/pictures/. Licence: Public Domain.
- Library of Congress U.S. City Directories: commercial trade directories indexing urban businesses and residents. URL: https://www.loc.gov/rr/genealogy/bib_guid/citydir.html. Licence: Public Domain.

## Common mistakes
- Expecting a map to list personal names: Third graders often expect insurance maps to show the names of residents living in homes, not realizing fire insurance maps documented building materials, fire walls, and hazard risks rather than names (Source: Library of Congress Sanborn Maps Guide).
- Believing a single photo captures everything: Children frequently treat one historical photograph as the complete record of a town, missing that a camera only records what was in the frame on one specific day (Source: National Archives Photograph Analysis Framework).
- Assuming historical streets changed instantly: Students often think entire streets were demolished and rebuilt all at once, rather than understanding buildings are replaced or modernized gradually over decades (Source: C3 Framework History Indicators D2.His.2.3-5).
- Confusing past street technologies: Young learners often assume 1890s streets had modern asphalt, electric signals, and cars, not realizing streets were paved with cobblestones or dirt and populated by horses and wagons (Source: Smithsonian National Museum of American History).

## Sensitivity
- Historical exclusion in commercial records: Acknowledge that historical business directories and maps in the 1890s and 1940s frequently omitted businesses owned by marginalized groups or segregated commercial districts.
- Local community pride: Encourage students to investigate their own neighborhood's historical evolution with curiosity and respect for preserved historic structures.

## Similar tools elsewhere
- Library of Congress: "Sanborn Maps Navigator". URL: https://www.loc.gov/collections/sanborn-maps/. Core mechanic: Interactive pan, zoom, and sheet navigator for historical town insurance maps.
- New York Public Library (NYPL): "OldNYC" and "Map Warper". URL: https://maps.nypl.org/. Core mechanic: Historical insurance maps georeferenced directly over modern maps with historical street photographs linked to addresses.
- Smithsonian Learning Lab: "Main Street Through Time". URL: https://learninglab.si.edu/. Core mechanic: Photo comparison tool allowing students to slide between 1900, 1940, and modern street views.

## Gaps
- Exact annual volume of telephone wires strung along a specific town Main Street cross-arm in 1895: Local utility records often survive in municipal archives rather than national digital databases.
