# Choreography for a Cause (idea card)

- Module: arts-9-12-choreographing-an-issue, grade 9-12. Codes: DA:Cr1, DA:Cr2, DA:Cr3, DA:Cn10
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Research an issue in a short fact file, then turn what you find into movement by abstraction (a graph's shape becomes a floor path, a quote's rhythm becomes accents, a gesture from a photo becomes a motif), choose a dance structure that carries your point, and revise from viewers' notes until they can say what the dance is about.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: issue files (for example, a town losing its only grocery store, a river after a flood, older people living alone), each with real data, a quote and a photo, or the student's own issue.
  - Core loop: pull material from the file into motifs with the abstraction tools; the on-screen dancers perform each motif. Choose a structure (ABA, theme and variation, narrative, accumulation) and arrange the motifs on it. Viewers (illustrated) watch and leave notes keyed to time ("I lost the thread here", "this repeat is where it hit me").
  - The aha or the end state: the student revises and the viewers watch again; the final run plays with a statement of intent, and a last card asks how the issue touches the student's own life. The student can learn and perform a section.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): real classmates leave the viewer notes.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - DA:Cr1: generating movement from research using abstraction.
  - DA:Cr2: choosing and explaining a dance structure that supports the intent.
  - DA:Cr3: revising from viewer feedback to make the intent clear.
  - DA:Cn10: research into the issue and its link to the student's own experience.
- Not like: choreography-board (grade 6-8: revising one phrase with devices, compare versions); poem-to-movement (grade 3-5 stimulus to phrase); dance-styles-explorer.
- Art: people (illustrated) for dancers and viewers, their own figures; real photos in issue files from public domain sources (to check).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Research an issue in a fact file, turn its data, quotes and photos into movement motifs, arrange them in a dance structure that carries your point, and revise from viewers' notes until they can say what it is about.
- Flags: needs a data source (issue files' data and photos); sensitive (issue choice; keep issues classroom-safe and factual); overlap risk (choreography-board)
