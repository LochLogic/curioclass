## What it teaches
Students translate research stimuli into original movement motifs, compose them in a dance form and revise the composition from audience readings. They explain how their personal movement choices relate to a source.

## Key skills
- Find movement ideas in a number, an invented voice or a picture.
- Connect pose, dynamics, level, pathway and group size to meaning.
- Build contrast and return within a dance form.
- Interpret feedback and revise a composition with a reason.
- Compare model readings with a classmate's own reading.

## Support at home
- Choose an everyday concern and make two safe still shapes that express it. Ask a family member what they notice.
- Look at a curved path or a night sky together. Describe a movement inspired by its shape or quality.
- Talk about how repetition makes a gesture memorable, and when a change makes its meaning clearer.

## A 5-minute start
1. Open the Light pollution folder. Compare the data about hidden stars with the two painted pictures.
2. Choose the issue, select Hidden and tap Make a motif. Watch its eight counts.
3. Make Glow dome and Starfield too. Name each motif and write What it shows in your own words.
4. In Structure, select an A slot and place Hidden. Place Glow dome in B. Play the dance.
5. Show viewers. Notice which movement they remember, then revise the placement and save a version.

## The modes
- Fact file: three issue folders, each with two data cards, one invented voice and two paintings. Every card gives its source.
- Motifs: up to six motifs across all issues. Four pose choices come from each source card; movement and meaning remain editable. Stand up and try the motif with enough clear space, adapting movements to your body.
- Structure: ABA, Rondo ABACA, Theme and variations, or Narrative. Select a slot and tap a motif, or drag it into the strip. A repeated letter uses the same motif. Each slot has independent repeat, dancer count, unison or canon and device choices.
- Viewers: four explicitly simulated viewers read one motif each. Their cards fill as the relevant part ends. A classmate can add a real reading. Return to Structure and Save version to keep that card with a version.

## Talk about it
- Which feature of the source did you preserve in the movement? What comes from your own experience?
- Did the viewers remember the movement you meant them to notice? How can the form change that?
- Where does a model viewer's reading fail to describe what you actually saw?
- What specific revision made the research clearer without turning the dance into literal illustration?

## Good to know
The model is deliberately simple. The friend reads the motif with greatest total presence (8 × repeats × dancers, summed across slots). The teacher reads the first motif, the stranger the last, and the kid the greatest level span plus travel, with presence breaking a tie. Source words determine issue recognition; changing names, written meanings or devices does not alter those words. This is a composition experiment, not a real audience prediction or a measure of artistic value.

The teacher's contrast rule compares dynamic, the start/end level pair, pathway and slot dancer count. Fewer than two differences fires the contrast note. The stranger's ending note fires unless the final motif appeared earlier or three dancers finish in unison. Empty slots are skipped.

The food figures describe US retail and household loss in 2010, not a current global estimate. One in three is an approximate movement analogy for 31 percent. The river's 15 million acre-feet allocation is historical compact arithmetic, not an estimate of present river flow. The paintings are illustrative stimuli, not documentary photographs. All three voice cards are invented and labeled.

Lantern is an original synthesized A minor, F major, C major, G major groove at 100 beats a minute. Ninety-six dance counts take 57.6 seconds; the four-count introduction adds 2.4 seconds. Browser narration is the current fallback; no voice recordings were made. Motion off retains count timing and changes poses once a count. Sound off silences music and cues; Read can still read the current hint.

Work stays in localStorage on this device, key curio-choreo-cause. Up to eight version snapshots include motifs, structure, model results and real viewer notes. Clear all requires confirmation. The kit figures are temporary; the owner requires painted dancers in the finished tool.

## Standards in the activity
DA:Cr1 is practiced by generating motifs from three kinds of stimuli. DA:Cr2 is practiced through form, group relationships and devices. DA:Cr3 is practiced by revising after model and real viewer feedback and saving successive versions. DA:Cn10 is practiced through the source connection and each student's What it shows statement. No sensitive personal disclosure is required.
