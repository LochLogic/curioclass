# Ship's Log (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-5-exploration-and-colonization, grade 5. Codes: D2.His.14.3-5, D2.Geo.7.3-5
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good, if it follows one real voyage instead of an invented one.
- One idea: Keep the log of one real 1600s voyage (for example the Mayflower in 1620), making the choices its planners faced about supplies, season and route, then follow the probable causes and effects through the first year for the passengers and for the Wampanoag people whose land they reached.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an open logbook and a harbor in England. "Why are these families leaving? Read the first page."
  - Core loop: log entries pose real decisions (how much food and beer to load, leaving late in the year after a second ship failed, which way to sail with the winds and currents). After each, the log shows what really happened and why (storms, a cracked beam, landing far north of their planned destination).
  - Two sides of the shore: the student reads that an epidemic had already killed many Wampanoag people, including nearly all of the Patuxet town where the colonists settled, and meets the Wampanoag view of the newcomers through their own leaders' choices.
  - The aha or the end state: a cause-and-effect map links religion, money, winds, season and earlier epidemics to the first year's outcomes, including that about half the passengers died that winter and how the Wampanoag alliance decision shaped what came next.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second voyage (Jamestown, 1607) for comparison.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.14.3-5: each decision is followed by its probable causes and effects, gathered on the cause-and-effect map.
  - D2.Geo.7.3-5: winds, currents, season and the people's beliefs shape where the ship went and who and what moved with it.
- Not like: colonial-trade-routes (colonial economies and trade); revolution-road (later causes of the Revolution); nations-of-north-america (Native nations map).
- Art: people (illustrated) and public domain (period maps and ship plans, items chosen and checked at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Keep the log of a real 1600s voyage such as the Mayflower, make the choices its planners faced, then trace the causes and effects of the first year for the passengers and the Wampanoag people whose land they reached.
- Flags: sensitive (Native history and epidemic deaths; fresh review agent per rule 5); needs a data source (voyage facts and Wampanoag perspective, sourced)
