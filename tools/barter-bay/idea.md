# Barter Bay (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-4-barter-bay, grade 4. Codes: D2.Eco.5.3-5, D2.Eco.11.3-5
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Fishers, bakers and potters on Barter Bay trade by swapping until shells become money and trading speeds up; then add too many shells, too few, or close the cannery, and watch inflation, deflation and unemployment play out in the market.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a harbor market, the student as a potter with three pots. "You want bread. Find someone who will take a pot."
  - Core loop, barter: the baker doesn't want pots, so the student hunts a chain of swaps; a timer shows how long one loaf takes.
  - Money arrives: the bay agrees that a rare shell is money; the same errand takes seconds. A trades-per-day counter jumps.
  - Shocks: the student drops a sack of new shells into the bay (prices climb: inflation), lets shells wash away (prices fall and sellers hold back: deflation), or closes the cannery (workers without jobs, fewer buyers: unemployment). A price board and a jobs board track each one.
  - The aha or the end state: the student explains in a short card why money made trade easier and what happened to people (a saver, a borrower, a cannery worker) in each shock.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): class trades with printed goods cards.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.5.3-5: the barter chain vs shell money shows how money eases exchange.
  - D2.Eco.11.3-5: shell shocks and the cannery closing show inflation, deflation and unemployment with prices and people.
- Not like: border-trade-post (grade 6 barter to tariffs between countries); paper-plane-assembly-line (production).
- Art: generated scenes and people (illustrated), because the market folk show who wins and loses.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: overlap risk (border-trade-post opens with barter to money; keep this one about money's value and the three shocks)
