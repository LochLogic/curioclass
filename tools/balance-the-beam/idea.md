# Balance the Beam (idea card)

- Module: math-1-the-equal-sign, grade 1. Codes: 1.OA.D
- Format / family: puzzle / balance. Fit: good.
- One idea: Number blocks hang on both ends of a balance beam, and the equal sign is the level beam itself: the beam only levels when both sides are worth the same, even when the answer comes first or both sides hold sums.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a gym balance beam with hooks on each end and a mouse acrobat. "Make the beam level so Pip can cross."
  - Core loop: true-or-false levels: an equation hangs on the beam (7 = 8 - 1, 5 + 2 = 2 + 5, 4 + 1 = 5 + 2) and the child taps Level or Tips; the beam then moves to show it. Missing-block levels: one block is blank (8 + ? = 11, 5 = ? - 3, 6 + 6 = ? + 4) and the child picks the block from a tray.
  - Each block shows its dots, so a child can check by counting.
  - The aha or the end state: 4 + 1 = 5 + 2 seems to "read" right but the beam tips; 6 = 6 is true even with no plus sign. The equal sign means "same as," not "the answer is next."
  - Class mode or extras (optional): build-your-own equations that a friend tests.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 1.OA.D: judging equations true or false by the meaning of the equal sign, in every form (1.OA.7), and finding the unknown in any position of an addition or subtraction equation (1.OA.8).
- Not like: snack-scale (kindergarten, compares counts with crackers), balance-puzzle (grade 6, solving with mystery bags), equation-golf (grade 9 strokes).
- Art: generated scenes (a gym with a cheerful mouse acrobat); beam and blocks drawn in code so the tilt is exact.
