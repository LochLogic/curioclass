# Mini Documentary Crew

## One idea
You can only cut what you shot: a three-week shooting schedule, built around a shop owner's hours, a crew's after-school time and the weather, decides which clips reach the edit, and a test audience's attention line under your five-minute cut shows where it sags and where viewers got lost, so planning and editing turn out to be one story.

## Not like
- storyboard-studio (9-12, studio): plans shots and cuts for a fiction scene before filming. Here it's nonfiction: you can't script what people say, only schedule when you can be there, then find the story in what you got.
- director-s-chair (9-12): interprets a written scene. Here there is no script.
- morning-show-crew (6-8, this batch, tycoon) and viral-video-breakdown (6-8): run a live show or analyze a video. Here you produce one short documentary from schedule to premiere.
- recital-practice-plan and museum-courtyard-performance (this band, tycoon): spend practice minutes or money on one performance. Here the scarce things are people's time and light, and the result is an edit.
- sketchbook-process-journal (this band): tracks how an idea changed. Here the material itself decides what the story can be.

## Who and how
- Grade 9-12, media arts, module "Documentary Production (Media Arts)".
- Topic line: producing a short documentary
- Driver: students on laptops, alone or in pairs. 35 to 50 minutes; saved on this device.
- Standards: NCAS MA:Pr4 (integrate content and form into a media work), MA:Pr5 (production skills and leadership), MA:Pr6 (present and distribute), MA:Cn10 (relate the work to community and personal experience).
- No camera, microphone or upload: every clip is made of given painted frames and text.

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound`. Own light palette (a production whiteboard, Look). Modes (`.seg`): `Schedule` (default), `Edit`, `Screen`, `Premiere`. Edit opens after `Wrap shooting`; Screen once the cut is 4:30 or longer; Premiere after one screening.

**Schedule.** Focus: the calendar, filling the stage: three weeks as rows, Monday to Saturday as columns, each day with its slots (weekdays: `After school 3:30 to 6`; Saturday: `Morning 9 to 12`, `Afternoon 1 to 5`; week 2 Friday is a teacher work day and also has `Morning 9 to 12`). Weekday mornings show "In school". Each day shows the forecast icon and word (Content). Panel: the brief (one paragraph, Content), the crew cards (who, role, when free), and the shoot cards (Content). Drag a shoot card onto a slot (or select the card and tap a slot). The slot checks the rules at once and shows either a check with the crew assigned ("Jae camera, Priya sound") or the reasons in words ("Jae isn't free Wednesdays. You can run camera yourself: tap Fill in."). `Fill in` puts you (the producer) in a missing role; you can fill one role per shoot. One shoot per slot. `Remove` takes a card back. `Wrap shooting` (confirm if cards are unscheduled: "3 shoots not scheduled. Their clips won't exist.") turns scheduled shoots into clips.

**Edit.** Focus: the viewer and the timeline: a 16:9 viewer above, the timeline across the full width below, both larger than the bin. Left: the clip bin, grouped by shoot (thumbnail, id, length, first words of the transcript; noisy clips marked "noisy audio"). Drag clips onto the timeline (or select and `Add to end`); drag to reorder; `Trim` (a clip's in and out handles, 1 s steps, never under 3 s); `Cover` (on an interview clip: lay a B-roll clip over its last seconds; the interview's sound continues); `Lower third` (on an interview clip: shows the speaker's name and role, typed in, 40 characters); `Caption` (on an archive photo: a date and place line, 40 characters); `Closer` (mark one clip as the ending line). Runtime readout "4:52 of 4:30 to 5:30". `Play from here` plays the timeline at real speed from the selected clip in the viewer (painted frame with a slow push or pan, the transcript as subtitles, lower thirds and captions drawn in code); `Stop`. Any edit while playing applies at once (the playhead stays).

**Screen.** Focus: the attention line under a miniature of the timeline, filling the stage. `Screen it` plays the cut at 8x (a 5-minute cut takes about 38 s; it is the screening) in the viewer with the attention line drawing in step under the timeline. Viewer questions pop up as tags at the moment they happen ("Who is she?", "When was this?", "I couldn't hear her."). After: the line, its lowest point ("Lowest: 54 at 1:42, after 31 s of one interview with nothing to look at"), the average, the questions list, and "Good ending" if the closer landed. `Stop`, `Back to Edit` (the low point is highlighted on the timeline). Screenings are kept (up to 5) to compare lines.

**Premiere.** Focus: the premiere plan card, centered. `Where`: Library screening, Shop window (a screen in Ana's window on Main Street), Online (school channel). `Captions` (on or off; Online requires on and says why). Permissions checklist (auto-checked from Schedule: release forms done before every shoot with people; archive license requested): items not met show in words and block `Lock the plan`. `Invite` (280 characters), `Who should see this, and why?` (300 characters). A poster preview: the painted storefront with your title and date written in code on its blank sign band. `Lock the plan` shows the final sheet (title, runtime, best screening line, venue, captions, permissions, invite). `Print sheet`, `Start over` (confirm).

## Content
**Brief.** "Ruiz Shoe Repair has been on Main Street since 1962. Ana Ruiz, 71, runs it alone and plans to close next spring. Your crew has three weeks to shoot and one week to cut a five-minute documentary for the town." (Ana, Walt, Dr. Park, the shop and the town are made up for this tool.)

**Crew.** Jae, camera: weekdays except Wednesday after school; Saturday morning. Priya, sound: Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday (both slots). Sam, editor: edits after the wrap (not needed on shoots). You, producer: any slot, and can fill one missing role per shoot.

**Forecast** (rain days; others clear): week 1 Wednesday and Thursday; week 2 Tuesday; week 3 Monday and Saturday.

**Shoot cards** (rules; crew needed; clips it yields):
1. Release forms: deliver and collect forms. Any slot. Producer only. Must come before every shoot with people (2, 3, 4).
2. Interview Ana: Tuesday or Thursday after school (she closes early: clean sound) or Saturday morning or week 2 Friday morning (shop open: "noisy audio" on every clip). Camera and sound. Clips A1 to A6.
3. Interview Walt, a customer: Saturday only. Camera and sound. Clips W1 to W3.
4. Interview Dr. Lena Park, city historian: Wednesday or Thursday after school, at the library. Camera and sound. Clips H1 to H3.
5. Resoling a boot (B-roll): Monday or Wednesday after school (her boot days). Camera. Clips B1a, B1b, B1c.
6. Storefront in evening light (B-roll): any weekday after school on a clear day. Camera. Clip B2.
7. Main Street storefronts (B-roll): any slot on a clear day. Camera. Clips B3, B4 (tools wall, shot the same visit).
8. Archive scan at the historical society: Friday after school or week 2 Friday morning. Producer only. Photos P1 to P4.
9. Shop sounds (door bell, machines): Tuesday or Thursday after school, Saturday, or week 2 Friday morning. Sound. Clip B5 (sound over the storefront picture).

**Clips** (id, length in seconds, transcript or description; interviews show the speaker's frame):
- A1 28 "My father opened this shop in 1962. I swept the floor here when I was seven."
- A2 40 "People think a shoe is done when the sole wears through. That's when I start. New sole, new heel, stitch it by hand, and it's good for ten more years."
- A3 35 "There were three shoe repair shops on this street when I was a girl. Now there's just me."
- A4 45 "Shoes are cheap now. A lot of them are glued, not stitched. Glue is hard to fix the same way."
- A5 30 "Every pair that comes in has a story. Wedding shoes. A soldier's boots. A kid's first cleats."
- A6 25 "When I close, I don't know who'll fix this town's shoes. Maybe nobody. Maybe you." (suggested closer)
- W1 25 "I've brought my boots here for forty years. Same boots."
- W2 30 "She fixed my wife's dancing shoes the day before our fiftieth anniversary."
- W3 20 "You don't throw away something that can be fixed."
- H1 30 "In 1960, Main Street had three shoe repair shops, two tailors and a hat shop."
- H2 35 "When the mall opened out by the highway in 1974, fewer people walked downtown every year."
- H3 25 "Repair shops are how a town keeps things instead of replacing them."
- B1a 12 a worn boot on an iron last; B1b 10 a new sole laid on; B1c 15 an awl through leather, close; B2 10 the storefront in evening light; B3 12 Main Street storefronts, several empty; B4 8 the wall of tools; B5 10 shop sounds over the storefront.
- P1 6 the storefront on opening day, 1962 (black and white); P2 6 Main Street, 1960 (black and white); P3 6 a 1974 newspaper page with a blank ad block; P4 6 the shop's shelves of tagged shoes, 1980s (faded color).

**Attention model** (a `How the screening works` note: "a model of a test audience, not real viewers"): starts at 80. Each second of an interview not covered by B-roll after the first 20 s in a row: -0.5. Each second of B-roll, a covered interview or a photo: +0.5 (up to 90). A change of kind (interview to B-roll or photo, and back): +5 (up to 100). A speaker's first clip without a lower third: -5 and "Who is she?" (or he). A photo with no caption before any of Dr. Park's clips: -5 and "When was this?". Each second of noisy audio not covered: -1, and "I couldn't hear her." once. A closer as the last clip: +10 at the end and "Good ending". Never below 0.

## Interactions in detail
- Schedule rules are checked on drop and listed in words; an illegal drop snaps back with the reasons. A legal slot shows the crew assigned. Rain on a slot blocks outdoor shoots (6, 7) only.
- Order rule: a shoot with people placed before the release forms (by date and slot) is flagged "Forms first" and blocked.
- Feasibility: every card can be placed legally in the three weeks (Sources has one full schedule). Some choices cost quality: Ana on a Saturday is legal but noisy.
- Unscheduled shoots at wrap: their clips never appear in the bin; the bin says "Not shot: Walt's interview."
- Edit limits: the timeline holds any length; the runtime readout warns outside 4:30 to 5:30 and `Screen it` is disabled under 4:30 with its reason in words. A cover can't be longer than the interview clip minus 3 s.
- Extremes: a cut of only Ana's interview clips back to back plays as the model says (it sags but never errors); an empty timeline shows "Drag clips here".
- Saving: localStorage `curio-mini-doc` (schedule, wrap state, timeline, lower thirds, captions, screenings, premiere plan). `Start over` clears all.
- Sizes: at 1366 x 768 the calendar's 18 day cells and the panel fit without scrolling; at 680 x 425 the calendar shows one week at a time with week tabs; at 375 wide Edit stacks viewer, timeline and bin, with the timeline scrolling sideways and `Play from here` pinned.
