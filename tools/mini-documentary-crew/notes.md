## What it teaches

Students plan a nonfiction production around availability, weather and consent, then edit the material they actually secured. They can explain how shot selection, identification, sound and an ending affect a simulated screening, and plan a presentation for a community audience.

## Key skills

- Schedule people and equipment around real constraints.
- Select, trim and reorder material to build a nonfiction story.
- Identify interview speakers and date archival pictures.
- Interpret a screening line, revise a cut and compare versions.
- Choose a venue and explain who should see the work.

## Support at home

- Ask a family member about an everyday object they repair or keep. Discuss which details would help someone else understand its story.
- Look at an interview together and notice how pictures change while the speaker continues talking.
- Describe a local story to two different audiences. Discuss what each audience would need explained.

## A 5-minute start

1. Select Release forms, then tap week 1 Monday after school. The calendar stamps the crew assignment.
2. Try Ana on week 1 Tuesday after school. Compare it with Saturday morning: the open shop makes the Saturday recording noisy.
3. Try an outdoor shoot in the rain, then a library interview on the same rainy day. Only the outdoor shoot is weather dependent.
4. Place the other shoots, or wrap with missing material and accept the consequences.
5. In Edit, select a bin clip and Add to end. Select a timeline clip to trim it, identify it or cover it.

## The four modes

**Schedule:** nine shoot cards and a three-week calendar. Select then tap is an alternative to dragging. The producer can fill one missing role, never two. Release forms must stay before interviews, even if moved later. After wrap, the schedule is an immutable production record.

**Edit:** a source bin, large viewer and filmstrip. Drag a bin clip onto the timeline or use Add to end. Drag timeline clips to reorder, use the arrow buttons, or focus a clip and press Alt plus Left or Right. Trim uses one-second in and out handles. A cover holds a painted B-roll frame over the interview's last seconds while the interview transcript continues. Lower thirds require the correct speaker name and role; archive captions require the given date and a place. Mark one closer and move it to the end.

**Screen:** opens at 4:30. Screen it plays the whole cut at 8 times speed, in real elapsed time. Stop discards an incomplete test. Completed screenings are kept, up to five, as dashed comparison lines. The latest low point is outlined in Edit. The line is a fictional attention model, not evidence about real people or an overall story-quality grade. Repeating B-roll can keep this simplistic model high without telling a story. Discuss that limitation.

**Premiere:** available after a completed screening. Choose a venue, date, title, captions, invite and an audience reason. Online requires captions. Forms and archive license requests are derived from the shooting schedule. The current cut must have been screened and fit the 4:30 to 5:30 brief before its plan is locked. The final sheet can be printed. Nothing is uploaded, published or sent.

## Talk about it

- What did your shooting schedule make possible or impossible in the edit?
- Does a high attention line mean this is a truthful or meaningful film? What is missing from that model?
- Where should this story be shown, and what would that audience need to know?
- What does Ana's story connect to in your own community or experience?

## Good to know

- All people, dates, shop history, crew availability and audience behavior in this production are invented. The interview statements are character testimony, not general claims about footwear or town history.
- MA:Pr4 is practiced by combining interviews, pictures and text; MA:Pr5 by scheduling, crew assignments and editing; MA:Pr6 by screening and the premiere plan; MA:Cn10 by the student's audience and community explanation. This tool supports these broad anchor standards, rather than claiming mastery of every grade-specific substandard.
- The model starts at 80. More than 20 continuous seconds of uncovered interviews costs 0.5 per second. Visual kinds are interview, B-roll and photo; a cover counts as B-roll. Visual recovery caps at 90, kind changes add 5 up to 100, missing first-speaker identification or an undated archive before the historian costs 5, uncovered noisy audio costs 1 per second, and a final marked closer adds 10. Values stop at zero.
- The spec's sample screening numbers disagree with these rules. The build follows the formulas. Independent computations and exact differences are in the build report.
- Archive descriptions and dates are source-bin metadata. In the finished viewer only a student's Caption supplies that date and place; an uncaptioned photo has no automatic date subtitle.
- Identification checks require one correct named speaker and role, or one exact archive date and a place. Substrings and lists of every possible identity or date do not qualify. Saved screening curves are refreshed under these checks; the checks still do not judge the meaning of free prose.
- Repeated source seconds are checked across standalone clips and interview covers, including saved screenings from before this review. Repeated footage can still be part of a deliberate edit, but it cannot set the model's saved best line or trigger its clean result.
- Cover changes the picture while interview sound continues. The fictional model waives noise penalties during covers; that is a model limitation, not an audio repair technique.
- Consent and archive requests are rules of this fictional assignment. Real documentary permissions depend on context; the tool does not teach a universal legal requirement to license every archive. CMSI discusses fair use as well as permission.
- The specified mentor voice is friendly-dad. No recordings were made in this build. Curio Class uses the kit's available browser voice as fallback; interview text is never spoken by the mentor.
- Work is stored only on this device under `curio-mini-doc`. Storage failure is shown in the footer. Start over clears this documentary after confirmation. Motion and Sound are kit controls.
- At compact widths the calendar shows one week with week tabs. At phone width, the viewer, sideways timeline and sideways bin stack. Secondary premiere fields and long question lists may scroll.
- Three generated portraits depict fictional adults and are held for owner review. Interview footage currently uses the painted empty setup specified as the fallback. Review age, occupational representation, hands and cultural assumptions before release. No claim about a real Ruiz family is intended.
