# Research packet: community-helpers-village
Concept: Place helpers around a village (baker, mail carrier, firefighter, librarian, a snow-shoveling neighbor) and remove one to see what breaks; learn which the town pays for, how each earns a living, and how everyone shares a snow day.
Grade: 1   Standards: D2.Civ.2.K-2, D2.Civ.6.K-2, D2.Eco.12.K-2, D2.Eco.6.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Civ.2.K-2: "Explain how all people, not just official leaders, play important roles in a community." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Describe how different workers and helpful neighbors contribute to daily town life.
- D2.Civ.6.K-2: "Describe how communities work to accomplish common tasks, establish responsibilities, and fulfill roles of authority." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain how village members cooperate during challenges like heavy snowstorms.
- D2.Eco.6.K-2: "Explain how people earn income." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain that private business owners sell goods to customers, while municipal workers receive wages for public services.
- D2.Eco.12.K-2: "Describe examples of the goods and services that governments provide." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Distinguish tax-funded government services (fire protection, public libraries) from private businesses and voluntary mutual aid.

## Common mistakes
- Believing the government pays all workers in town: Students often think bakers, grocery clerks, and plumbers are paid municipal salaries by the mayor rather than earning money from customers (Source: C3 Framework Economics indicators D2.Eco.6.K-2).
- Mistaking voluntary neighborliness for paid employment: First graders regularly assume a neighbor shoveling snow or raking leaves receives a paycheck from the city (Source: C3 Framework Civics indicators D2.Civ.2.K-2).
- Thinking public services are free without costs: Children assume libraries and fire engines cost nothing, unaware of collective tax funding by community residents (Source: U.S. Census Bureau Statistics in Schools).
- Assuming single leaders do everything: Believing the mayor or police chief physically resolves every problem, overlooking interdependence and mutual aid among citizens (Source: California History-Social Science Content Standards K.3).

## Sensitivity
- Respect for all occupations: Portray service workers, business owners, municipal civil servants, and informal volunteers with equal dignity and civic importance.
- Family economic realities: Avoid assuming all children's families have single-family homes with driveways; frame snow day cooperation around shared public paths, steps, and neighborhood safety.
