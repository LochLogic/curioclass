# Claim Court (idea card)

- Module: ela-5-reasons-and-evidence, grade 5. Codes: RI.5.8
- Format / family: roleplay / evidence. Fit: good.
- One idea: An author's article is on trial, and the student, as the clerk who prepares the case, connects each reason and piece of evidence to the exact point it supports, then the judge tests every connection, so they learn to explain how an author holds up each point and to spot evidence that supports nothing.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a small courtroom. The judge: "This article claims our town should plant more trees downtown. It makes three points. Clerk, show me what holds up each one."
  - Core loop: the article sits on the bench; its three points hang as banners. The student drags each reason and piece of evidence from the article to the banner it supports, and marks it strong, weak or off topic. The judge cross-examines: "How does a fact about birds support a point about summer heat?" and the student picks the explanation or moves the piece.
  - The aha or the end state: the verdict board shows each point with its support stacked under it; a point with only weak support wobbles, and the student writes one sentence per point explaining how the author backs it up.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a jury vote on the weakest point.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RI.5.8: matching each reason and piece of evidence to the point it supports, and explaining how the author uses them.
- Not like: radio-hour-notes (listening to a speaker); quote-picker (grade 4, the student's own writing); argument-x-ray (grade 6, color-coding an op-ed).
- Art: people (illustrated) judge and clerk, generated scenes for the articles' topics.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: An author's article is on trial: as the court clerk, connect each reason and piece of evidence to the point it supports, then answer the judge's questions until every point's support is explained.
- Flags: none
