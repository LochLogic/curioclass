## What it teaches

Students can read a pay stub, explain the difference between gross and take-home pay, and reproduce its deductions. They can test how hours, retirement contributions, benefits and location change one check.

## Key skills

- Trace a deduction back to the numbers used to calculate it.
- Calculate overtime for each workweek separately.
- Distinguish money deducted from pay from benefits paid by an employer.
- Compare a prediction with a result, then explain the gap.
- Read current and year-to-date amounts.

## Support at home

- If you are comfortable, show your student a pay stub with identifying information covered. Find gross pay, deductions and net pay together.
- Ask your student to explain why a posted hourly rate is different from money deposited in an account.
- Talk about one benefit a job can provide besides wages. No personal income amounts are needed.

## A 5-minute start

1. Open Jordan's federal tax line. Why is it zero while Social Security is not?
2. Choose Sam and open overtime. Inspect each week before adding the pay.
3. In Change it, lower Sam's Week 1 to 40, then put it back at 44. Trace the signed changes.
4. Open Guess first, move Take-home guess and press Check. Compare the difference with the actual tax and benefit lines.

## Modes

**Stub:** Tap a current amount or its year-to-date amount. Plain words expands payroll abbreviations. Show me on the stub outlines the inputs used by a card. Escape closes a card and returns focus.

**Change it:** Edit hours, pay, schedule, benefits, workplace or check number. Changed current amounts show signed deltas for three seconds. Tap an amount to return to Stub with its math card open and your edits preserved. Back to the real stub restores the selected worker.

**Guess first:** Only gross is uncovered. Check reveals the deductions and take-home pay in about two seconds. Next stub and Try again interrupt a reveal immediately. The visit tally records only the first check of an unchanged original stub whose pay was not already available in Stub or Change it. Jordan starts as practice because the opening screen shows that check. Switch to an unseen worker for a first prediction. Later guesses are practice. Nothing is saved between visits.

## Talk about it

- Why does a 401(k) contribution lower federal income tax but leave Social Security wages unchanged?
- Why is the extra take-home from overtime smaller than the extra gross?
- With Avery at $200,000, compare Social Security on checks 22, 23 and 24.
- Which employer-paid amounts do not come out of the employee's net pay?

## Good to know

These are fictional employers, employees, addresses and pay calendars. The single-filer annualized estimate uses no credits, extra withholding or annual retirement contribution limit. It illustrates a calculation, not a payroll service or an individual tax return. Health is modeled as a qualified pre-tax employer plan. Very low pay can be insufficient for benefits and local tax; the form says so. Zero hours produces all-zero lines.

Schedules use 52, 26, 24 or 12 checks. Hours accept half-hour increments. When an alternating pair of workweeks is converted to another schedule, both weeks' earnings are preserved. Editing the new average-hours slider replaces that pattern with one repeated week. Period amounts are rounded half up; net subtracts the rounded lines. YTD adds prior checks when caps or monthly local charges make checks unequal.

The original state and city rates are deliberately simplified learning models from First Apartment Budget, not current state withholding instructions. Denver's monthly charge is applied to the first dated check in each month. The fictional calendar starts weekly and biweekly pay on January 2; semimonthly checks land on the 15th and last day, and monthly checks on the last day. The model does not apply Denver's monthly earnings threshold. Do not use it to file taxes.

The take-home bar now names the tax and employee-benefit totals. An underfunded setup says there is no deposit in this model, including after returning to Stub. Employer-paid changes carry their own signed delta tags, separate from employee deductions.

For zero gross pay, percentages of gross are undefined. The guess prompt names Check or Next stub, and the result explains the missing denominator. Try again becomes available after a guess changes or is checked; using it returns keyboard focus to the guess slider.
