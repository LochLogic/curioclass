# Train Your Dog (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-learning-and-development, grade 12. Codes: APA.Development and Learning
- Format / family: creature / none. Fit: good for learning; the lifespan development half of this code does not fit a dog-training tool (see below)
- One idea: Train a virtual shelter dog with classical conditioning (a leash and a walk), operant conditioning (sit, come, stop jumping) and four reinforcement schedules, and watch its behavior change in learning curves, including extinction and spontaneous recovery.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a shelter dog named on adoption day, a clicker, a treat jar and a goals board.
  - Core loop: pair stimuli (the leash before every walk) and watch the dog start to respond to the leash alone; reward or remove things after behaviors and choose a schedule (fixed or variable, ratio or interval); a live chart plots response rate and shows why variable schedules resist extinction. Shaping lets the student build a trick step by step.
  - Human link: each lesson ends with a human example (a phone notification, a slot machine, a fear after a dog bite, a sibling copying a skateboard trick for observational learning).
  - The aha or the end state: a "why it worked" report the student writes using the right terms, and a note that reward-based training works better than punishment for dogs and people.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): predict the curve before each run.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - APA.Development and Learning (learning half): classical, operant and observational learning, schedules, extinction and shaping, practiced on the dog and tied to human examples.
  - MOVE APA.Development and Learning (lifespan half): new tool: follow one person from infancy to old age through real developmental research (attachment, Piaget's stages, adolescence, aging) as a separate psychology tool.
- Not like: brain-explorer (biology); conformity-experiments (social pressure); illusion-lab (memory).
- Art: generated scenes and an illustrated dog with expressive animation, because the dog's behavior must read clearly.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Train a virtual shelter dog with classical and operant conditioning and four reinforcement schedules, watch its learning curves change, and connect each lesson to how people learn.
- Flags: none
