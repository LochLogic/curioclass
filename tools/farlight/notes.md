Farlight is a calm space journey with no enemies, no damage and no way to lose. A small ship has drifted about 620 light-years from Earth, and the player charts a way home one star jump at a time: scanning planets, landing on living worlds, walking or driving a rover, and building cozy research camps. Discovery Stars from scanning and exploring unlock faster ships. Reaching the Solar System opens one of four endings, and play can go on after that.

**Things to notice together.** Reading the galaxy map: which stars are in jump range, and which way the golden home beacon lies. Planning ahead: a jump needs solar charge, which tops up near a star. The Solar System at the end is the real one, with true facts (Mercury's 88-day year, Saturn's rings, Olympus Mons on Mars); the other worlds, plants and animals are made up, which makes a good talk: what would a real world need to have living things on it?

**How to play.** On a computer, drag the mouse to steer; W, A, S, D fly; Shift boosts; C cruises. M opens the galaxy map, N the system map, F scans, J jumps, Q opens the journal. Near a planet, L descends and G touches down; E steps out, K drives the rover, B builds a camp. H hides the menus for a clean picture. On a phone or tablet, a thumbstick and buttons appear: drag the screen to look around, use the thumbstick to fly, and tap the menu buttons for the map, scanning, landing and jumping.

**Good to know.** It plays on computers, Chromebooks, phones and tablets. It needs a browser with WebGPU, which recent browsers on most computers, phones and tablets have; older devices show a short message instead. Progress saves on the device only, and the journal can start a new journey. There is no setting yet that tones down motion, so a child who is sensitive to motion on screen may prefer another game.
