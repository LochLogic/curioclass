## What it teaches

Students measure, build, add and subtract angles in degrees. They read jump heights in eighths of a foot,
build line plots and use those plots to add and subtract lengths with the same fractional units.

## Key skills

- Relate a fraction of a full turn to 360 degrees.
- Count one-degree turns and combine two or three angle parts.
- Center a protractor and choose the scale that starts at the ground ray.
- Build a ramp to a given angle using a protractor.
- Plot lengths in eighths and calculate from repeated measurements.

## Support at home

- Turn in place by a quarter turn, half turn and full turn. Talk about 90, 180 and 360 degrees.
- Look for two edges that meet. Point to the corner and the two directions an angle measures between.
- Fold a strip of scrap paper in half three times. Count its eight equal pieces and compare 3/8 and 5/8.

## A five-minute start

1. Open Spin Deck. Drag the board clockwise, then tap Go. Arrow keys change one degree; Shift changes ten.
2. Make a 90 degree turn. Tap Add a spin and add 45 degrees. Follow both colored, labeled arcs.
3. Switch to Jobs and solve a fraction-of-a-turn job with the keypad.
4. Visit Ramp Shop, move the protractor to the ramp's lower corner and read from the ground's zero.
5. Let one jump freeze in Jump Log. Count eighths upward on the pole, then place its dot on the horizontal plot.

## Modes

Spin Deck starts in Explore. Degrees count a clockwise turn from the top. Backward dragging reduces the
current part; it never creates a negative angle. Up to three parts and 900 degrees fit on the dial.
Save as Dev's trick changes the spin shown in Park. Jobs have no dial ticks or angle readout until solved.

Ramp Shop has movable ramps and a movable, rotatable two-scale protractor. Drag the lip, or focus its handle
and use arrow keys. Focus Move to shift the protractor two pixels with arrows; Q and E rotate one degree.
During a drag it snaps within 24 pixels of the corner and ten degrees of the horizontal. Show angle is
Explore only. Sketch jobs start at least ten degrees away from their target.
The starting angle varies, so counting a fixed number of key presses cannot replace measuring.
Measurement jobs choose a ramp different from the current Explore ramp, so Show angle or Park's ramp label
cannot supply the first measurement answer.

Jump Log has three fictional sessions. Dot order is shuffled. A wrong tick is refused; after a second miss,
Rae places an outlined dot. Session switches preserve dots. Once all jumps are plotted, three questions ask
for lengths in feet. Type fractions with / and mixed numbers with a space. Equal fractions count.
The plot is also keyboard usable: focus the scene, use left and right, then Enter to place the selected tick.
Narrow screens also have full-size arrow and Place dot buttons for that same selection, so small tick spacing
does not require precise tapping. The selected height is shown above the scene and announced to screen readers.
Help is saved before a worked answer is revealed, even if the page closes during narration.
For Big's comparison question, Show Low kicker too draws the fixed Low data as faded rings.
Low's rings are dashed. Big's dots have solid edges, including the hollow dots that Rae placed.

Park adds six props from Spin jobs, five measured or sketched ramps and three scoreboard entries.
Ride restarts on every tap. Stop and changing modes end a ride. The first completion of a piece keeps
its original help credit, so replaying a shown answer never upgrades it to on your own.
Replaying a completed ramp job replaces that ramp's angle while keeping the original help credit.
Try another variant to replace a bank that makes Dev bail.

## Talk questions

- How does an angle show the amount of turning, even when the board's nose points the same way after another lap?
- Which zero should you use when the ramp opens the other way?
- Why is adding four jumps of 4/8 foot different from counting four dots?
- Which jump session has the largest spread? Show it using the plot.

## Good to know

Dev, Rae, Mina and Walt and every jump measurement are made up for practice. They are not measured skateboarding data.
The hop bands and bail threshold are story outcomes, not a skateboarding safety rule or a physics model.
At fixed launch speed, ideal projectile peak height grows with launch angle from zero to ninety degrees,
ignoring air resistance and changes in launch height. Students do not need this formula here.

Jobs have two tries. An assisted piece still builds the park, with help recorded. Measure jobs change ramp
after a miss. Leaving Jobs changes the open variant. Reset clears only the current mode's Explore state
or current jump session. Start over in Park clears all saved work after confirmation.
Progress is saved only on this device, with storage errors handled. Clearing browser data clears progress.

Fixed narration lines use the big-sister voice inventory. This Build-only version uses browser speech;
recording was explicitly out of scope. Sound and Motion can be changed independently. Read speaks the current card and hint.
