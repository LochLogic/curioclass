## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Sort**. Read the first wonder together: "How many legs does a spider have?"
2. Ask: how could we find out? Should we look it up, ask an expert or try it?
3. Drag the note to the bin the class picks, or tap the bin. The note explains why.

## The three modes

- **Sort:** ten wonders from a set of eighteen. Some fit two ways ("How do you take care of a sick dog?" fits Ask an expert and Look it up), and the tool says so. A bin that doesn't fit gets a nudge, not a "wrong."
- **Our wonders:** type your class's own wonders and pin them, then sort them together. Question starters (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How) help children begin. Wonders stay on this computer for next time; **Clear all** takes them down.
- **Better questions:** seven pairs. Which question helps us learn more? Usually the "what," "how" or "why" question beats the yes-or-no one.

## Teaching notes

- Look it up = books, encyclopedias, trusted websites, the library. Ask an expert = someone who does the job or studies it. Try it = an experiment or a careful look.
- After sorting, pick one class wonder and actually find out. That's the point of the wall.
- Turn the yes-or-no questions into wonder questions together: "Do frogs eat?" becomes "What do frogs eat?"

## Talk about it

- Who is an expert you know? What could they teach us?
- What's a wonder we could try right now in our classroom?
- Where in our library would we look for a book about sharks?

## Good to know

- Keys 1 to 3 switch modes.
- Class wonders are saved in this browser only, never sent anywhere. Avoid names or private details.
