# Baron or Builder? (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-11-industrialization-and-the-gilded-age, grade 11. Codes: D2.His.2.9-12, D2.Eco.4.9-12, D2.Geo.7.9-12
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good, with the solo default changed: the student places first, then hears from the people who lived it
- One idea: Place five industrialists (Carnegie, Rockefeller, Vanderbilt, Madam C. J. Walker, Pullman) on a line from robber baron to captain of industry using evidence cards, then face the people their businesses touched (a steelworker at Homestead, an immigrant in a company town, a small refiner, a customer) and defend or move each placement.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a long line across a Gilded Age street, "Robber baron" at one end and "Captain of industry" at the other, and five portrait cards.
  - Core loop: draw evidence cards per industrialist (wages, prices, market share, philanthropy, strikes, rebates, new products) and place them; then witnesses step forward in turn and challenge the placement with their own testimony; the student moves the card or answers with evidence.
  - Market panel: for each industry, a simple chart of how many sellers there were and how prices moved, so "monopoly" is measured, not assumed.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's line next to historians' placements and newspapers of the time, plus a city map showing the immigrant and rural migration these industries pulled in, and a change and continuity note (then vs now).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class compares lines and argues the hardest case.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.9-12: the then-and-now note and witnesses across 1870 to 1900 show change and continuity in the era.
  - D2.Eco.4.9-12: the market panel measures how much competition existed in oil, steel, rail and cosmetics.
  - D2.Geo.7.9-12: the city map shows industry's technologies drawing immigrants and rural migrants into mill and company towns.
- Not like: factory-floor (running a British mill, grade 9); the-buyout (a modern market model); muckraker-studio (reform writing).
- Art: people (illustrated) portraits and witnesses, plus public domain photographs and cartoons (Library of Congress, checked), because the faces make the judgment personal.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Place five industrialists on a line from robber baron to captain of industry with evidence cards, then hear from workers, immigrants and rivals their businesses touched, and defend or move each placement.
- Flags: none
