## What it teaches
Students build a five-number summary and a box plot, calculate mean, interquartile range and mean absolute deviation,
and explain why the shape of a distribution affects which summary fits it.

## Key skills
- Count observations and describe an attribute, its measurement method and units.
- Read the same values in a dot plot, histogram and box plot.
- Find medians and quartiles by position in an ordered list.
- Compute mean, IQR and average distance from the mean.
- Choose a center and spread that tell a fair story about the data.

## Support at home
- Write seven everyday numbers, such as minutes spent on a routine. Sort them and find the middle together.
- Change the largest number. Ask what happened to the middle and to the total shared equally.
- When a news story gives an average, ask what else you would want to know about the spread.

## Five-minute start
Open Explore on San Diego. Drag the hottest day farther right and watch the two markers. Put back, then move a cool
day across the middle. Ask why the second move changes the median too. Switch to Histogram and try different bins.

## Modes
Explore uses four real NOAA airport daily-high sets and Luka Dončić's 2023-24 regular-season points. Mean and Median
flags have no numeric readout; the tiles report changes from the original data. Whiskers reach the minimum and maximum.
Our class accepts up to 60 numbers and a unit. Saved numbers stay on this device. Clear removes them.

Build has separate session progress for San Diego, Chicago and Luka. First decode the source card, then sort the values.
For even counts, pick both middle cards. For odd counts, leave the median out of the two halves. Pick each half's middle,
then both ends. Compute IQR, mean and MAD. Finally choose the pair and shape reason and file the report.
Typed answers and card fields allow two attempts; other choices allow one. Sam's help is explicitly marked and never
counts as independently found. Mean and MAD accept answers within 0.05 of the true value.
Start over clears a build but keeps attempts and Sam's help marks for this page session, so repeated resets cannot
give extra guesses or turn supplied answers into independent credit. Reloading starts a new session. There is no saved best score.

Headline compares Miami and Denver in July 2024 on one fixed axis. Their means agree, but their spreads differ.
Students calculate both IQRs before taking one editorial pick.

## Talk questions
- Why can the mean move while the median stays still? When would the median have to move?
- Why must we pick a card by its position when several cards show the same number?
- Which matters for the headline: equal averages, or different spreads? What would a reader miss?
- Why does one far-off game move the mean only a little when there are many games?
