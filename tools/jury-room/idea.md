# Jury Room (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-jury-room, grade 12. Codes: D2.Civ.7.9-12, D2.Civ.9.9-12, AASL.Collaborate
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Take seat 7 on a twelve-person civil jury deciding who pays for a damaged delivery, deliberate with eleven illustrated jurors who each bring evidence, bias and a habit (the one who talks over others, the quiet one with the key detail), reach a verdict, then run the same question through a town hall and a consensus circle.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the jury room, the evidence binder (photos, the delivery log, two witness statements) and the judge's instructions on the burden of proof.
  - Core loop: each round, choose how to speak or act (ask the quiet juror, cite an exhibit, call out an assumption, propose a vote, summarize where the room stands); jurors respond to what the student does, and a deliberation meter tracks whether every voice and every exhibit was heard.
  - Bias checks: jurors' snap judgments (about the driver's accent, the company's size) appear, and the student can name and test them against the evidence.
  - The aha or the end state: the verdict and a replay of the deliberation's turning points; then the same dispute goes to a town hall (majority vote) and a consensus circle, and the student compares what each process is good for.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): twelve students take the seats with role cards.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Civ.7.9-12: listening, citing evidence and checking bias are the civic virtues the student applies in each round.
  - D2.Civ.9.9-12: the jury, town hall and consensus circle are three deliberative processes in different settings.
  - AASL.Collaborate: building on jurors' ideas, drawing out quiet voices and reaching a shared decision; the class mode makes the collaboration fully real.
- Not like: supreme-court-simulator (judges ruling on law); who-decides (local action); conformity-experiments (group pressure as a study).
- Art: people (illustrated) twelve distinct jurors and generated scenes (the jury room, a town hall), because each juror must feel like a real person.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Take a seat on a twelve-person civil jury, deliberate with jurors who bring evidence and bias, reach a verdict, then run the same dispute through a town hall and a consensus circle and compare.
- Flags: none (AASL.Collaborate is simulated in solo play; class mode carries the rest)
