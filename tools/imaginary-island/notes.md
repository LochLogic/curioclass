## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Explore**. Drag the lantern to the Orchard and read the panel together: "Traveler z = 3 + 2i, distance 3.61, heading 33.7 degrees."
2. Press **× i** once and read the worked line out loud: (3 + 2i) × i = 3i + 2i² = 3i − 2 = −2 + 3i. Ask where on the map the lantern went, and why the i² turned into −2.
3. Turn on **Distance rings** and press **× i** three more times. The lantern visits the Fox Den, the Salt Flats and the Waterfall, then lands back on the Orchard. Ask what stayed the same and what changed.

## The three modes

- **Explore:** the traveler z (the round lantern) and a step w (the diamond, drawn as an arrow from the harbor). Seven operations: × i, × (−1), × (−i), Reflect (the conjugate), Square it, + w and × w. Each one animates the trip (an arc, a spiral or a straight walk), leaves it as a labeled trail, and writes the arithmetic out with the i² step showing. The Powers of i dial counts × i presses in a row: i¹ = i, i² = −1, i³ = −i, i⁴ = 1, then round again.
- **Clue Trail (optional practice):** twelve clues such as "Start at the Orchard. Multiply by i. Where do you end up?" Students tap the map to drop a guess pin, then press **Walk it** to watch the trip and see how far off they were. A guess within 0.25 counts as landed. The tally counts each clue once, at its first walk. **Show the arithmetic** reveals the worked lines without walking.
- **Springs:** x² + bx + c = 0 with sliders for b and c (the leading coefficient stays 1). The two solutions are drawn as springs on the map, and a small graph of y = x² + bx + c sits in the corner. With b = 0, raise c from −4: the springs slide together along the Real Road, meet at 0 when c = 0, then split north and south as the parabola lifts off the x-axis.

## Talk about it

- Why does multiplying by i never change your distance from the harbor? What does change?
- Adding w moves every point the same way. Multiplying by w does not. What does × w do to a point far from the harbor compared to one close by?
- The Orchard, Fox Den, Salt Flats and Waterfall are all 3.61 from the harbor. Which other four landmarks share a distance? (Cave, Hermit Hut, Shipwreck and Tide Pools, at 4.12.)
- In Springs, why do the two complex solutions always come as a mirror pair across the Real Road?
- Clue 11 multiplies the Orchard by its own conjugate and lands on 13. Try it from other landmarks. Why is the answer always real?

## Good to know

- With the map focused: I is × i, R is Reflect, S is Square it, A is + w, M is × w, U is Undo. Arrow keys move the lantern, w or the guess pin by one snap step (Shift moves 1). In Clue Trail, Enter on the map drops a guess pin at the start. Tab reaches the operations before the map, and the landmarks are a single tab stop (arrow keys step from one landmark to the next).
- **Snap** cycles whole, half (the default) and off. Zoom with the buttons, the mouse wheel or a pinch; drag empty map to pan; **Fit** goes back to the default view.
- Squaring a point far from the harbor sends it far out to sea: the view zooms out up to 50 units, then pins the lantern to the edge with its value, and refuses anything past 10,000 ("Too far out to chart").
- In a small embed (a landscape box under 900 px wide or under 600 px tall) the map and panel stay side by side, and the layer and zoom buttons move to the end of the panel.
- With Motion off, every move jumps, but its arc, spiral or line is still drawn as a dashed trail.
- Headings are measured counterclockwise from east, from 0 up to 360 degrees. The "Turned by" line after × w uses −180 to 180 degrees.
- Sources: OpenStax College Algebra 2e, sections 2.4 and 2.5, and Precalculus 2e, section 8.5 (CC BY 4.0); Common Core State Standards N-CN. The landmarks, clues and presets are arithmetic, not outside facts.
