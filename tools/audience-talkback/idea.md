# Audience Talkback (idea card)

- Module: arts-3-5-responding-to-plays, grade 3-5. Codes: TH:Re8, TH:Re9
- Format / family: showdown / none. Fit: change to lab, because the core is a controlled test (swap one element between two versions) that a solo student runs; the class vote becomes an extra.
- One idea: Two versions of the same short scene feel very different; swap one element at a time between them (costumes, lights, voices, music) until you find which choice made the difference, then judge both with criteria you chose and back your verdict with your own experience.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a stage with Version A and Version B, the same one-minute scene (a kid tries to return a borrowed bike she broke).
  - Core loop: watch both. Pick three criteria from a bank (Can I hear every word? Do the lights match the feeling? Do I believe the character?). Then mix: A's lights with B's voices, B's costume with A's music, and replay. Mark which swap changed your feeling.
  - The aha or the end state: the talkback: the student writes a verdict with one criterion and one personal reason ("I believed B because my voice goes quiet like that when I'm scared"). Then three illustrated kids give theirs, each shaped by their own experience, and the student sees why people can judge the same scene differently.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes A or B and shares reasons.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - TH:Re8: explaining a response through the student's own experiences, and seeing how others' experiences shape theirs.
  - TH:Re9: applying chosen criteria and evaluating how technical elements (costumes, lights, sound) changed the scene.
- Not like: scene-staging-comparison (grade 6-8: two productions shaped by time and culture); director-s-chair (grade 9-12 directing); puppet-show-feelings (K-2).
- Art: generated scenes for the stage; people (illustrated) for actors and talkback kids; voice actors record both versions.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Two versions of one short scene feel different; swap costumes, lights, voices and music between them until you find what made the difference, then judge both with your own criteria and back it with your experience.
- Flags: overlap risk (scene-staging-comparison)
