# Audience Talkback

## One idea
The same one-minute scene, staged two ways, feels like two different stories; flip costumes, lights, voices and music between Version A and Version B one switch at a time, live while it plays, and your lab notebook records which single swap changed how it felt, so "I liked B better" becomes "the lights did it, and here's why for me".

## Not like
- scene-staging-comparison (6-8, A3's band): two productions of one scene shaped by their time and culture. Here the two versions are the same production with four switchable choices, and the student tests them one at a time.
- character-voice-booth (3-5, lab): the student records their own voice takes and compares voiceprints. Here voices are one of four switches, and the student is the audience, not the actor.
- director-s-chair (9-12) and stage-blocker (6-8): the student directs and blocks. Here nothing is placed; the student watches, tests and judges.
- puppet-show-feelings (K-2, A1's band): names feelings in a puppet show. Here the student evaluates a staging with chosen criteria.
- neighborhood-issue-play (this band): makes a play. Here the play is finished; the work is responding to it.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, theater, module "Responding to Plays (Theater)".
- Topic line: judging a scene two ways
- Driver: students (headphones, solo or a pair) or teacher (projector; `Class vote` extra). 15 to 25 minutes.
- Standards: TH:Re8 (explain a response through your own experience, and see how others' experiences shape theirs), TH:Re9 (apply chosen criteria; evaluate how costumes, lights and sound changed the scene).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons: `Watch` (default), `Swap lab`, `Talkback`.

**Watch.** Focus: the painted stage (16:9, a porch set inside a proscenium), the biggest thing. Under it two big buttons `Play Version A` and `Play Version B` (each shows its little four-chip recipe: costumes, lights, voices, music, all A or all B). Each version runs about 20 to 35 seconds (the builder writes the measured time on each button). A line bar under the stage shows the 10 lines as ticks with a playhead; tap a tick to jump to that line. After both have been watched once, `How did each one feel?` offers five feeling chips per version (Sad, Funny, Tense, Warm, Awkward; pick up to two each) and `Go to the lab`.

**Swap lab.** Focus: the stage, with the mixer directly under it: four switches, `Costumes`, `Lights`, `Voices`, `Music`, each an A/B toggle with its letter, color and a one-word description ("A: rain gear", "B: summer clothes"). `Play` (becomes `Stop`) runs the scene with whatever the switches say. Every switch acts at once while it plays (Interactions).
- Lab notebook (panel): `Base` (A or B, which version you compare against). After each play the notebook adds a row: which switches differ from the base, and the student marks "How much did it change the feeling?" `A lot`, `A little`, `Not at all`. A row with two or more switches different is marked "Two things changed. Which one did it?" and its rating is kept but greyed out as not a fair test.
- `Show my results`: a small bar chart (code) of the student's fair-test ratings, one bar per element, tallest first, with words on every bar. No right answer is shown; the chart is the student's own finding.
- `Reset switches` (all to the base).

**Talkback.** Focus: the criteria and verdict card, beside a small stage thumbnail that can replay either version.
1. `Pick three criteria` from the bank (Content). Each picked criterion gets a row: Version A and Version B, each `Yes`, `Mostly` or `Not really`.
2. `Your verdict`: two starters: "Version ___ works better for this scene because ___ (a criterion)." and "I think this because in my life ___." (typing, 140 characters each).
3. `Hear the talkback`: three audience members' cards (seat tags with initials, no faces; Content), each with the version they preferred and their reason from their own life. A line under them: "Same scene, three people, three reasons. Each one is right about their own experience."
- `Class vote` (extra): two counters, A and B, with + buttons and a reset, for a teacher at the projector.

## Content
**The scene** "The Borrowed Bike" (original; Kit, a young otter, returns a bike borrowed from Mrs. Holloway, an old bear; 10 lines):
1. KIT: (knocks) Mrs. Holloway? It's Kit.
2. HOLLOWAY: Kit! Did you bring my bike back?
3. KIT: I did. Sort of.
4. HOLLOWAY: Sort of?
5. KIT: The chain came off on the big hill. And then the wheel... bent.
6. HOLLOWAY: Oh. I rode that bike when I was your age.
7. KIT: I'm sorry. I'll fix it. I'll save my allowance. I'll...
8. HOLLOWAY: (a pause) Kit. Did you get hurt?
9. KIT: Just my knee.
10. HOLLOWAY: Then come in. We'll look at that wheel together.
Poses by line: Kit head-down on 1 to 7, looking-up on 8 to 10; Holloway at-door on 2 to 7, kneeling on 8 to 10 (she bends to look at the wheel).

**The two versions** (each element's A and B):
| element | Version A (rainy evening) | Version B (sunny morning) |
|---|---|---|
| Costumes | Kit in a dark green raincoat, hood up; Holloway in a long gray cardigan | Kit in a red T-shirt and a cap; Holloway in a straw sun hat and a garden apron |
| Lights | low, cool blue from one side, a warm porch lamp only by the door | bright, warm, even light over the whole porch |
| Voices | Kit quiet, slow, shaky; Holloway soft and slow, long pause at line 8 | Kit fast and loud with a nervous laugh; Holloway brisk and surprised, short pause |
| Music | a slow, soft piano tune in a minor key, under the whole scene | a bouncy pizzicato-style plucked tune in a major key |
Feeling chips: Sad, Funny, Tense, Warm, Awkward.

**Criteria bank:** "Can I hear every word?" / "Do the lights match the feeling?" / "Do I believe the characters?" / "Does the music help, or get in the way?" / "Do the costumes tell me who they are and where?" / "Does the ending land?"

**Talkback cards:**
- A.R. preferred A: "My grandma has things from when she was little. If I broke one, I'd talk quiet like Kit in A."
- L.M. preferred B: "When I'm in trouble I talk too fast and laugh, like Kit in B. That felt real to me."
- S.T. preferred A for the ending, B for the start: "B was funnier at first, but when she asked if Kit got hurt, I wanted the sad music."

## Interactions in detail
- Voices are recorded clips, one per line per delivery (20 clips). Lines play in order; the gap after each line is 0.4 s (A) or 0.2 s (B), plus a 1.2 s pause before line 8 in A and 0.5 s in B. A version's length is the sum of its clips and gaps (about 33 s for A and 21 s for B before recording; the builder measures the real clips and puts the totals on the Watch buttons).
- Live switches while playing: Costumes and Lights change in the same frame (cutout swap; light overlay crossfades over 200 ms, Motion off: instant). Music crossfades in 150 ms to the other tune at the same position in its loop. Voices: the line that is playing restarts at once in the other delivery from its start (never waits for the next line); the scene's timing then follows that delivery's gaps. A switch never stops the scene.
- Mixing voices A with music B, and so on, is allowed in every combination (16 in all); none is broken.
- Notebook rows are added only after a play that reached line 10 or that the student stopped after line 5 (a quick tap-through adds nothing). Ratings can be changed later.
- Talkback opens with the feelings and notebook carried over; verdicts can be written without the lab (the lab is encouraged, not required).
- Saved on this device (localStorage `audience-talkback`): feelings, notebook, criteria ratings and verdicts. `Clear my notes` clears them.
- Keyboard: Space plays or stops; C, L, V and M flip Costumes, Lights, Voices and Music; Tab order: mixer, notebook, then line ticks.
- Sizes: the stage fits 16:9 at real pixels with the mixer right under it at every size (the mixer is the main control); at 680x425 the notebook collapses to its last row with `Show my results`; at 375 wide the mixer is a 2 by 2 grid under the stage and the panel follows.

## Voice
Bank voice `knowing-guide` (Sadaltager), style "Say in a knowledgeable, friendly way, like a museum curator": the talkback host who asks the audience what they saw. Not used by any 3-5 arts neighbor or another tool in this band. `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.mode`.
- welcomeWatch (how; entering Watch; stale when a Play button is tapped): "Here are two versions of the same scene. Tap Play Version A, then Play Version B."
- feelHow (how; after both versions have played; stale when a feeling chip is tapped): "How did each one feel? Tap up to two feelings for each version."
- welcomeLab (how; entering Swap lab; stale when Play is tapped): "Flip one switch, then tap Play. Change one thing at a time, so you know what did it."
- rateHow (how; after the first lab play; stale when a rating is tapped): "Did that swap change the feeling? Tap A lot, A little or Not at all."

## Done when
1. Focus: at 1366x768, 680x425 and 375 wide the stage and the four switches are visible together without scrolling.
2. While the scene plays, each switch changes what is seen or heard within 200 ms (voices restart the current line in the other delivery) and the scene never stops.
3. All 16 combinations play to the end without a gap or overlap.
4. A play with two switches changed is flagged "Two things changed" and left out of the results chart.
5. The results chart shows only the student's own fair-test ratings, with words on every bar.
6. No narration plays during the scene.
7. Talkback saves three criteria ratings and both verdict sentences across a reload.
8. How lines are spoken once in a run of five plays.
