## A 5-minute start

1. Open the **Lab** and press **S1**. Read the page together: the logo, the "Independent. Nonpartisan." banner, the
   "40%" headline. Tap the lines the page says about itself; each one lands in the **Notebook** as an **Inside** card.
2. Take a class vote: **Trust it**, **Partly true, stretched** or **Don't trust it**. Press **Lock my call**.
3. Now the padlocks come off. Press **+** for a new tab and tap the suggestion chip with the site's name. Follow what
   comes up, press **Who runs this site?**, and vote again. Lock the second call and read **What a fact-checker
   would find** together: did the inside read or the outside read match? Which findings decided it?

## The two modes

- **Lab:** six made-up specimens (a site, a social post, two news stories, two blogs), in any order. Each one is
  read twice. **Pass 1, inside read:** only the page itself and links on the same site; new tabs, search, image
  search, the site lookup and cited links are locked. **Pass 2, outside read:** leave the page. Search the made-up
  web (Wayfind), the made-up encyclopedia (Openpedia), Search this image, Who runs this site?, and follow a cited
  link to see what the source really says (Trace). The outside call needs at least one outside move first. Each pass
  records the call, the seconds spent and the tabs opened. Then **What a fact-checker would find** shows the
  verdict, why, and the two or three findings that decide it, each marked found or not in the student's notebook.
  **Next specimen** moves on; **Retest** runs a specimen again, and the lab table keeps the first try (tagged
  "retested"), so a retest can't hide what the inside read did.
- **Report:** the lab table, one row per tested specimen, with both calls marked "Matches" or "Doesn't match" against
  the fact-checker, then the two counts ("Inside read matched the fact-checker on _ of _", "Outside read matched on _
  of _"), one line on the student's own numbers, the Stanford finding and the three questions.

## The three questions

Every outside finding is tagged with one of the questions professional fact-checkers ask (Digital Inquiry Group,
Civic Online Reasoning): **Who's behind it?**, **What's the evidence?**, **What do other sources say?**
Things a page says about itself are tagged **Inside**: useful to notice, never enough on their own.

## Talk about it

- Which specimen fooled your inside read? What on the page made it look trustworthy?
- Which specimen looked messy but turned out fine? Why is "looks professional" a weak test?
- S1's address ends in `.org` and calls itself "nonpartisan". Does `.org` tell you who runs a site? (No: anyone can
  register a `.org`, and S1's lookup shows an app company behind it.)
- What was the fastest outside move that changed your mind: a search, a lookup, an image search, or a cited link?
- A page says "studies show". What do you do next?
- Did your outside read take more time? Was it worth it?

## Standards taught

- **AASL Curate IV.B.3** (questioning and assessing the validity and accuracy of information): every specimen is
  called twice, and the outside read tests what the page claims.
- **AASL Engage VI.A.3** (evaluating information for accuracy, validity, social and cultural context): who runs the
  site, who wrote it and why, in each Who's behind it? finding.
- **C3 D3.2.6-8** (evaluating the credibility of a source by its relevance and intended use): the site lookup, the
  encyclopedia entries and the author searches.
- **CCSS W.6.8, W.7.8, W.8.8** (assess the credibility and accuracy of each source): the notebook cards and the two
  calls per source, compared in the lab table.

## Good to know

- Keys **1**, **2** and **3** pick a verdict; **Enter** on the picked verdict or on Lock my call locks the call.
- Every site, person, study, post, school and photo is made up for Curio Class, and the Lab says so under the
  specimen jars: "Every page in this lab is made up for practice." Every name was checked against the real web so
  none belongs to a real organization. Every web address ends in `.example` or is an `example.org` name, so nothing
  links to a real site, and links never leave the page.
- The seconds count only while the page is on screen.
- The lab table is saved on this device only. It never leaves this device. **Clear lab** (then Yes) empties it.
- A call "matches" only when it is exactly the fact-checker's verdict. "Partly true, stretched" is its own answer, not half
  credit.
- The study in the Report: Wineburg, S. and McGrew, S. (2019), "Lateral Reading and the Nature of Expertise",
  Teachers College Record 121(11). The wood frog facts come from the National Park Service (Denali).
