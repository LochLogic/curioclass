# How Many Fit? (idea card)

- Module: math-6-dividing-fractions, grade 6. Codes: 6.NS.A
- Format / family: sandbox / fraction-wall. Fit: good.
- One idea: Drag fraction bars into a container to see how many 3/4s fit in 2 1/2, and when the last bar only partly fits, read the leftover as a fraction of the bar, not of the whole; that is why 2/3 divided by 3/4 is 8/9.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a container bar of length 2/3 and a stack of 3/4 bars from the same fraction wall. The prompt: "How many 3/4s fit in 2/3?" No answer box yet; just drag.
  - Measuring rounds: the student fills the container; whole bars count, and a part bar is shaded with its own fraction (8/9 of a 3/4 bar). Contexts on cards: how many 3/4-cup servings in 2/3 cup of yogurt; how many 1/3-mile laps in 2 1/2 miles.
  - Sharing rounds: 3 friends share 1/2 lb of chocolate; the bar is split into 3 equal parts; each gets 1/6 lb. The student sees the same division as "how much in one group".
  - Missing-side rounds: a garden strip has area 1/2 square mile and length 3/4 mile; how wide is it? The rectangle model solves it.
  - Every answer is checked as multiplication (3/4 x 8/9 = 2/3). After enough cases, the student spots the shortcut (multiply by the flipped divisor) and tests it on new ones.
  - The aha or the end state: dividing fractions is measuring or sharing, and the leftover is a fraction of the divisor.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): students write their own story for an expression like 2/3 / 3/4.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 6.NS.A (6.NS.1): interpreting and computing quotients of fractions with bar and area models and equations, in measuring, sharing and missing-side word problems, and writing a story for a given quotient.
- Not like: fraction-wall-duel (same wall engine, grade 4 comparing), shrink-ray (grade 5 multiplying as scaling), smoothie-lab (grade 6 ratios).
- Art: drawn in code for the bars (exact lengths), with generated context illustrations on the story cards.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Drag fraction bars into a container to see how many 3/4s fit in 2/3, read the part bar as 8/9 of a divisor, and solve sharing and missing-side stories until the flip-and-multiply shortcut explains itself.
- Flags: none
