# Portrait in Four Media

## One idea
One life, four tellings: Anna Atkins, who made the first book illustrated with photographs, is told as an encyclopedia entry, an interview, a documentary storyboard and a museum photo archive; you mark what each one leans on, line up every claim across all four, and build one profile in which each sentence shows how many tellings back it.

## Not like
- page-to-screen (ela 10, wave 4 literature group): how a story changes from book to film. Here the subject is a real person and the question is accuracy and emphasis, not interpretation.
- Research Notebook (ela 9, this group): written sources, note cards and a cited paragraph. Here the sources are four different media and the work is comparing them claim by claim.
- Source Mixer (ela 11, this group): weighs sources of different media for an argument about a cause. Here there is no argument: one profile, built from what the accounts agree on.
- behind-the-documentary (social 9-12, not specced): checks a documentary's claims against primary sources. Here the documentary is one of four equal accounts, and the encyclopedia and the archive are checked just as hard.
- fake-or-fact-lab (6-8 library, wave 1): decides whether a page can be trusted. Here all four accounts are honest attempts; the lesson is what each medium chooses to show.
- Fact Fusion (ela 5), research family, not specced: combines facts from three short texts. Here grade 10 adds emphasis by medium, a claims-by-accounts grid and hedged wording for contested firsts.

## Who and how
- Grade 10 ELA, module One Subject in Many Media: "Comparing accounts across media".
- Topic line: "comparing accounts of one life across media".
- Standards: RI.9-10.7, SL.9-10.2.
- Driver: students on their own laptops or in pairs. 25 to 40 minutes.
- tool.json: driver "students", minutes "25-40", modes [] (pure explore), grades_label "Grade 10". Kit starter with `kit:theme-912` (dark) and an accent override.

## Screens and modes
Kit frame. Header: "Portrait in Four Media", small line "Anna Atkins, 1799-1871"; Motion, Sound, Read. Three steps as a segmented control: **1 Emphasis**, **2 Cross-check**, **3 Profile** (all open from the start; a small check mark appears on each step once used).

**1 Emphasis.** Four tabs across the stage, one per account, each with its medium icon and color: **Encyclopedia**, **Interview**, **Storyboard**, **Archive**. The chosen account fills the stage in its own form:
- Encyclopedia: a reference column, nine sentences, numbered in the margin.
- Interview: a transcript, interviewer's questions in muted type, six answers.
- Storyboard: eight frames in a 4 by 2 grid, each a drawn sketch with its narration line and a camera note under it.
- Archive: a grid of seven catalog cards, each a museum image with its catalog caption (title, maker, date, medium, credit).
Six emphasis tags sit in a bar above: **Facts and dates**, **Process**, **People**, **Firsts and legacy**, **Mood and drama**, **Objects**. Choose a tag, then tap sentences, answers, frames or cards to mark them (tap again to unmark; each item takes one tag). Under the tabs, four **emphasis strips**, one per account: a horizontal bar split by tag share, with the leading tag named ("Storyboard leans on: Mood and drama and Firsts"). **Compare with ours** overlays the reference tagging (Content) as a thin second strip; it never scores.

**2 Cross-check.** The claims grid: 12 claim rows (Content) by 4 account columns. Each empty cell reads "?". Tap a cell: the account opens in a side drawer; tap the item that addresses the claim, or **Not mentioned**. The cell then shows what that item says: **States** (agrees with the record), **Differs** (says something else, quoted in the cell tooltip), **Hedged** (states it with a qualifier), or **Silent**. An item that is not about the claim answers "That part is not about this claim. Try another, or choose Not mentioned." Each filled cell keeps its evidence (the item's text) in the **Evidence locker** list under the grid. **Fill the rest** completes the grid from the tool's key, marked as "filled by the tool" so the student can see what they found themselves.

**3 Profile.** On the left, one chip per claim with its support count ("3 of 4") and flags: **Differs** (some account disagrees), **Contested** (experts disagree), **One source** (only one account says it). On the right, a profile in three parts, **Life**, **Work**, **Legacy**: drag chips in (or focus and press L, W or G). Each placed chip becomes a sentence (Content) followed by the letters of the accounts that support it in small capitals (E I S A). A chip flagged Contested shows a wording switch, **As fact** / **Hedged** ("She is often called..."); left on As fact, its sentence is underlined with "Experts disagree on this" in the margin. A chip that only the storyboard supports asks "Only the storyboard says this, and it is staged. Keep it?" (Yes / No). **Read my profile** shows it as a plain paragraph, with **Copy**.

Panel (right): `#hint`, a **Grid** count (filled 0 of 48, differs found 0), **Images: The Met** credit line, and **Clear my work** (asks Yes / No).

## Content
All four accounts are written for Curio Class and say so in small type under each tab. The archive's captions are taken from The Met's catalog records; its images are public domain (Art). The facts behind them are checked against Sources.

Encyclopedia (E), a reference column:
- E1 "Anna Atkins (1799-1871) was an English botanist and photographer."
- E2 "She was born on 16 March 1799 in Tonbridge, Kent; her mother died the next year."
- E3 "Her father, John George Children, was a scientist at the British Museum, and she made the drawings for his 1823 English translation of Lamarck's Genera of Shells."
- E4 "In 1825 she married John Pelly Atkins, and in 1839 she was elected to the Botanical Society of London."
- E5 "Using the cyanotype, a process invented by the family's friend John Herschel in 1842, she made images of seaweeds by laying them on treated paper in sunlight."
- E6 "In October 1843 she issued the first part of Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions, which she published herself in parts until 1853."
- E7 "It is generally considered the first book illustrated with photographs."
- E8 "With her friend Anne Dixon she later made Cyanotypes of British and Foreign Ferns (1853)."
- E9 "She died on 9 June 1871 at Halstead Place, Kent."

Interview (I), "a conversation with Mara Quint, a photograph conservator" (a made-up person; said under the tab):
- I1 Q: "What does a cyanotype look like?" A: "Deep blue where light reached the paper, pale where the seaweed blocked it. No camera, no lens. The plant makes its own picture."
- I2 Q: "How did she make them?" A: "Paper brushed with two iron salts, ferric ammonium citrate and potassium ferricyanide, then dried in the dark. She laid a specimen on it, set it in the sun, then washed the sheet in plain water. Herschel had worked the process out in 1842."
- I3 Q: "Do all copies of her book match?" A: "No. Every plate was made by hand, so copies differ in shade and even in which pages they hold. Some plates are dated years after the first part came out."
- I4 Q: "Was it really the first photobook?" A: "Most historians say so. Talbot's The Pencil of Nature, which began in 1844, was the first sold to the public with photographs. Hers came first, made at home."
- I5 Q: "And the first woman photographer?" A: "That's debated. Some give the title to Constance Talbot. The record is too thin to settle it."
- I6 Q: "What do you do to protect them?" A: "Keep them out of strong light and handle them as little as we can."

Storyboard (S), "Blue Tide: a documentary in eight frames" (frame sketch, narration, camera note):
- S1 Storm on a rocky beach; a woman in a long dress gathers seaweed. "On the wild coast of England, a young woman braves the storm." Camera: wide, rain, music swells.
- S2 A study full of jars and books; a girl and her father. "Anna grew up surrounded by science, raised by her father, a chemist." Camera: slow push in.
- S3 Hands holding a blue sheet. "In 1841, she made her first cyanotype." Camera: close-up.
- S4 A sheet in sunlight, the blue appearing. "Sunlight, iron and water: a picture appears." Camera: time-lapse.
- S5 A bound book on a table. "In 1843 she published the first photobook in history." Camera: title card.
- S6 Anna alone at a window. "She was the first woman photographer." Camera: hold on her face.
- S7 Pages turning by lamplight. "Ten years of patient work, page after page, until the last part was done." Camera: montage, soft music.
- S8 A museum wall of blue prints, visitors. "Today her blue seaweeds hang in great museums around the world." Camera: pull back, music ends.

Archive (A), seven catalog cards from The Met (caption = title, maker, date, medium, object number):
- A1 Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions. Anna Atkins, British, 1799-1871. 1843-53. Cyanotypes. (286656)
- A2 Cystoseira granulata. Anna Atkins. ca. 1853. Cyanotype. (291505)
- A3 Dictyota dichotoma. Anna Atkins. ca. 1853. Cyanotype. (291515)
- A4 Rhodomenia Polycarpa. Anna Atkins. ca. 1853. Cyanotype. (286896)
- A5 Errata. Anna Atkins. ca. 1853. Cyanotype. (291500) Card note: "A page of corrections, printed the same way as the plates."
- A6 Spiraea aruncus (Tyrol). Anna Atkins. 1851-54. Cyanotype. (285421)
- A7 Sir John Herschel. Julia Margaret Cameron. April 1867. Albumen silver print from glass negative. (283097)

I1 Process, I2 Process, I3 Objects, I4 Firsts, I5 Firsts, I6 Objects. S1 Mood, S2 People, S3 Facts, S4 Process, S5 Firsts, S6 Firsts, S7 Mood, S8 Mood. A1 to A7 Objects. ## Interactions in detail
- Tagging: the active tag shows as a pressed button; tagged items get a colored left edge plus the tag's short word (FACT, PROC, PPL, FIRST, MOOD, OBJ), never color alone. Strips update at once; with Motion on they slide (200 ms).
- Archive cards open large on tap (the image at full width with its caption); Escape closes.
- Cross-check: one drawer at a time; tapping a filled cell shows its evidence and **Change**. Fill the rest asks first ("Fill the n empty cells from the key?", with the real count) and marks those cells with a dotted border.
- Profile: a chip can be used once; removing a sentence returns its chip. The paragraph is built in Life, Work, Legacy order. Copy puts plain text on the clipboard, with the account letters in brackets.
- Store: tags, grid cells and profile save to localStorage `p4m-work` (try/catch). Clear my work (after Yes) empties it.
- Keyboard: step control, account tabs, tag bar, then items in reading order (arrow keys inside the storyboard grid and archive grid). Cells in the claims grid are a table with arrow keys; Enter opens the drawer.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 and 1280x720 the storyboard grid shows 4 by 2 and the claims grid fits without scrolling. At 680x425 the storyboard becomes 2 by 4 with its own scroll and the claims grid shows one account column at a time with a column picker. At 760 and 375 wide the account tabs become a dropdown, items stack, and the profile chips sit above the profile.
