# Research packet: teddy-bear-makers

Concept: Put the steps of making a teddy bear in order, give each step its tool or machine and the worker with the right skill, and add up what one bear costs; then ask the bear maker questions and answer a visitor's.
Grade: 1
Standards: D2.Eco.7.K-2, D2.Eco.13.K-2, D4.2.K-2, D4.5.K-2

## Standards in plain words

- D2.Eco.7.K-2 (Describe examples of costs of production): First graders learn that making a toy costs money for fabric, stuffing, tools, and paying the workers for their time.
- D2.Eco.13.K-2 (Describe examples of capital goods and human capital): First graders discover that capital goods are tools like scissors and sewing machines, while human capital is the worker's knowledge, training, and skill.
- D4.2.K-2 (Construct explanations using correct sequence and relevant information): First graders put the steps of making a product into the right chronological order and explain each step clearly.
- D4.5.K-2 (Ask and answer questions about explanations): First graders ask thoughtful questions about how goods are made and explain to visitors why products cost what they do.

## Real data

Teddy bear production workflow:

| Step | Action | Raw material / component | Capital good (tool/machine) | Human capital (worker & skill) | Unit cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pattern Cutting | Trace and cut plush fur pieces from patterns | Plush fabric, pattern paper | Fabric cutting shears or die cutter | Pattern cutter (precision measuring, layout) | \$3.00 |
| 2. Machine Sewing | Stitch body panels inside-out, leave stuffing hole | Polyester thread | Electric sewing machine | Machine seamstress (curved stitching, seam allowance) | \$2.50 |
| 3. Safety Eyes & Nose | Insert safety eyes and nose into head and lock tight | Plastic safety eyes, nose, lock washers | Eye-setting press or snap pliers | Assembler (safety testing, symmetry placement) | \$1.00 |
| 4. Stuffing | Turn right-side out and fill body with soft stuffing | Synthetic polyester fiberfill | Turning stick, stuffing nozzle machine | Stuffer (firmness balancing, hugging feel) | \$1.50 |
| 5. Finishing & Tag | Hand stitch back seam, embroider mouth, tie ribbon | Embroidery floss, satin ribbon, tag | Hand sewing needle, thread snippers | Hand finisher (hidden ladder stitch, embroidery) | \$2.00 |

Total production cost calculation:
- Materials cost: \$3.00 (fabric) + \$1.00 (eyes/nose) + \$1.50 (stuffing) = \$5.50
- Labor and capital cost: \$2.50 (sewing) + \$2.00 (finishing) = \$4.50
- Total cost to produce one bear: \$10.00
- Shop selling price (retail): \$12.00 (covers workshop rent and shop owner earnings)

Inquiry questions:
- Question to bear maker: "Why are the bear pieces sewn inside out first?" (Answer: So the seams and thread edges are hidden neatly inside the bear when turned around.)
- Question to bear maker: "How do safety eyes stay on without falling off?" (Answer: A ridged post passes through the plush fabric and locks into a washer that cannot pull back through.)
- Question from visitor: "Why does a teddy bear cost ten dollars when a bag of stuffing is only one dollar?" (Answer: Making a bear takes fabric, thread, safety parts, sewing machines, and many skilled minutes of human work.)

## Common mistakes

1. Believing toys are made instantly by machines without any human hands or specialized skills.
2. Assuming capital goods only mean money, rather than physical tools, machines, and equipment used in production.
3. Forgetting that a worker's wages and time are real costs of production that must be paid alongside physical materials.
4. Thinking stuffed animals are stuffed while still flat and inside-out, rather than turned right-side out before filling.
5. Confusing the retail sale price with the raw material cost, ignoring overhead and labor.

## Sensitivity

- Keep monetary values simple whole or half numbers suitable for first grade addition without decimals confusion.
- Emphasize fair compensation for workers' time and skill in positive, child-friendly terms.
- Avoid commercial brand endorsement; focus on universal artisan workshop or factory craftsmanship.

- Cost numbers should use simple token units or single dollar bills for easy mental addition.
