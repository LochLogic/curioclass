# Missing Voices Archive (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-11-missing-voices-archive, grade 11. Codes: D2.His.8.9-12, D2.His.12.9-12, D1.4.9-12
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Open one real city's archive on the 1918 flu, which is full of the mayor's notices and the main newspaper, map whose voices are missing (nurses, immigrant families, Black neighborhoods), see how the gaps bend the story, then follow your own questions to sources that fill some of them.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an archive drawer with 20 official items and a city map; a counter shows whose words they contain, mostly officials and editors.
  - Core loop: read items and tag each speaker by group and neighborhood; the map lights up where voices exist and stays dark where they don't. A "story so far" panel writes the history those sources alone would tell.
  - Question step: the student writes supporting questions about the gaps ("What did nurses see?" "What did the Black press report?"); each good question unlocks a second drawer of real sources that exist but are rarely used (a visiting nurse's report, a Black newspaper, a foreign-language paper, a family letter, a death register).
  - The aha or the end state: the story panel rewrites itself with the new sources; the student notes what changed and what gaps remain unfillable.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): groups take different neighborhoods.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.8.9-12: the voice map and the story panel show how the archive's gaps limit the history it can tell.
  - D2.His.12.9-12: the student's questions about the first sources drive the search for further sources.
  - D1.4.9-12: supporting questions build the inquiry, and the new sources raise new questions.
- Not like: remembrance-archive (Holocaust sources and usefulness); behind-the-podcast (tracing a secondary work); decades-explorer (then and now memory).
- Art: public domain 1918 newspapers and photographs (Library of Congress Chronicling America, checked) and generated archive scenes, because real pages make the gaps visible.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Open a real city's 1918 flu archive, full of officials' notices but missing nurses, immigrant families and Black neighborhoods; map whose voices are absent, ask questions that lead to sources filling some gaps, and watch the story change.
- Flags: needs a data source (one real city with surviving sources from those groups, Philadelphia a candidate, all checked)
