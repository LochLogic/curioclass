# Cereal Ad Questions

## One idea
Take an ad apart and watch the wanting drain out: the class watches a silly made-up cereal ad, votes on whether they want the cereal, then pulls out the music, the pictures and the words one at a time and votes after each replay, so a bar chart of the class's own votes shows which part of the ad was doing the selling, until only a plain bowl is left.

## Not like
- line-safari, beat-buddies (k-2, showdown): name lines and keep a beat. Here the class votes on what it wants and the chart is made of their votes.
- act-it-out (k-2, showdown): the class acts a story. Here the class takes media apart and asks what it wants them to think.
- levels-and-pathways (k-2, showdown): copies a dancer. Here nobody moves; the class watches and votes.
- field-trip-slideshow and sound-and-picture-book (k-2, this batch): make media. Here the class takes someone else's media apart.
- toy-ad-reality-check (3-5, mystery, batch ARTS): checks an ad's claims against the real toy. Here, for younger children, the question is which part of the ad (music, pictures, words) makes you want it.

## Who and how
- Grade K-2, media arts, module "Media Messages".
- Driver: teacher on a projector or smartboard; the class votes with hands (the teacher taps the counts), names parts and messages out loud, and hunts for ads in pictures of a day. 15 to 25 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS MA:Re7 (perceive the parts of media: sound, pictures, words), MA:Re8 (interpret: what the ad wants you to think), MA:Re9 (evaluate: does it still work without its parts), MA:Cn11 (media in daily life: where ads show up).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons (`.seg`, icon plus word): `The ad` (TV icon, default), `What it wants` (thought bubble), `Ads in my day` (sun). Every control is 60 px or more with an icon and a word.

**The ad.** Focus: the TV screen (a big rounded screen frame drawn in code) in the middle, about 65% of the stage, where the ad plays. Right of it, the parts panel: three big part switches, `Music` (note), `Pictures` (sparkle), `Words` (speech bubble), each on or off with an icon, the word and "on" or "off" (never color alone). Under the TV: `Play ad` (gold), `Stop`, and the vote bar: `Want it!` (hand up) and `Not sure` (hand sideways), each with a big plus and a small minus and its count. Under that, the vote chart (code): one pair of bars per version played (Full ad, No music, No pictures, No words, Plain bowl), labeled with icons and counts.
- The suggested order is shown as three faint arrows on the switches (Music, then Pictures, then Words), but any order works.
- Turning all three off and playing gives the Plain bowl version.

**What it wants.** Focus: the ad's best frame (the mascot with the bowl) on the TV, with five message cards around it: `It's the most fun` (party hat), `You get a toy` (toy car), `Everybody has it` (crowd of bowls icon), `It makes you strong` (flexed arm drawn as a simple shape), `It tastes great` (spoon). Tap a card: it lights, and three part buttons appear on it: `Music`, `Pictures`, `Words`: "Which part made you think that?" The teacher taps the part the class names; the TV replays the 3 s clip of that part (Content). A `True?` button on each card shows a plain answer card (Content) the class discusses.

**Ads in my day.** Focus: one painted day scene filling the stage, with scene buttons `Breakfast` (bowl), `Street` (bus), `Store` (cart). Each scene has hidden hotspots (6 per scene, 4 ads and 2 not ads). Tap anything: a hotspot glows and its card says "An ad!" with what it wants, or "Not an ad" with what it is for. A scene counter shows ads found (for example "3 of 4"). No timer.

## Content
**The ad: "Zoom-O's"** (original; the cereal, mascot and slogan are made up). 15 s, five shots, 3 s each:
1. A cereal box zooms in over a rainbow burst with sparkles. Words: "NEW! Zoom-O's!"
2. Milk splashes over a bowl in slow motion. Words: "So crunchy! So fun!"
3. Comet the kangaroo bounces on a trampoline holding a bowl. Words: "Comet loves Zoom-O's!"
4. A toy race car pops out of the box. Words: "Free toy inside!"
5. The box and bowl on a sunny table, sparkles. Words: "Everybody loves Zoom-O's! Part of a happy breakfast!"
**Parts:**
- Music: an original bouncy jingle made in code (C major, 132 bpm, pluck melody C5 E5 G5 E5 | F5 A5 G5(2) | E5 G5 C6 G5 | A5 G5 C6(2), bass on every beat, claps on 2 and 4, a "boing" on shot 3, a "zip" on shot 4), plus whooshes on cuts. Off: silence (the cuts are silent).
- Pictures: the painted shots, sparkles (code), mascot bounces, zoom and slow motion. Off: every shot shows the same plain bowl painting, still, with no sparkles or moves.
- Words: the on-screen words (big bouncy code text) and the announcer saying them. Off: no text and no announcer.
- Plain bowl (all off): the plain bowl, still and silent, for 15 s (the TV shows a small clock in code so it does not look broken).
**Part clips** (What it wants): Music: the jingle's first 3 s over the plain bowl. Pictures: shot 3 with no words or music. Words: the announcer's five lines over the plain bowl.
**True? cards:**
- It's the most fun: "Cereal is food. The fun in the ad came from the music, the bouncing and the sparkles."
- You get a toy: "Some boxes have a small toy. The toy is not the cereal."
- Everybody has it: "The ad says everybody loves it. Did the ad ask everybody? Ads say this to make you want to join in."
- It makes you strong: "The ad never said that! Did you think it anyway? Pictures can make us think things nobody said."
- It tastes great: "Only a taste can tell you that. An ad can't."
**Ads in my day** (hotspot: ad or not; card):
- Breakfast: the cereal box with a mascot (ad: "The box itself is an ad. The mascot wants you to pick it."); a tablet game with a banner (ad: "Ads pop up in games."); a yogurt cup with a cartoon (ad: "Cartoons on food are there to catch kids' eyes."); a fridge magnet from a pizza shop (ad: "It wants you to order pizza."); a school lunch menu on the fridge (not an ad: "A menu tells you what's for lunch. It isn't selling anything."); a family photo (not an ad).
- Street: a bus with a side poster (ad: "Ads ride on buses."); a billboard (ad: "Billboards are big ads for people driving by."); a T-shirt in a shop window with a logo (ad: "A logo on a shirt is an ad you can wear."); a sneaker store sign (ad: "It wants you to come in and buy."); a stop sign (not an ad: "A stop sign keeps us safe."); a library "Story time" poster (not an ad: "It tells us about a free story time.").
- Store: kids' cereal boxes on a low shelf (ad: "Cereals for kids often sit low, right at kid eye level."); a mascot balloon at the end of the aisle (ad); candy at the checkout (ad: "Candy waits by the checkout, where kids wait too."); a "2 for 1" sign (ad: "It wants you to buy two."); an aisle number sign (not an ad); a "Wet floor" sign (not an ad: "It keeps us from slipping.").

## Interactions in detail
- One ad version plays at a time. The switches stay live during play: flipping one changes the ad at once (music stops or starts from the current point within 50 ms, pictures swap to or from the plain bowl at once, words and announcer stop or appear at the next line) and the version being played is relabeled; the vote then counts for the version that was on when the ad ended.
- Votes: `Want it!` and `Not sure` count from 0 to 40 (plus is disabled at 40 and minus at 0; the hint never names a disabled one). The counts belong to the version just played and are saved to the chart when `Play ad` is pressed again or a switch changes after the ad ended. The chart shows the latest count per version (a replayed version overwrites its bars).
- After the Plain bowl version has a vote, the narrator asks the big question (event): "When did you want it most? What part was doing the selling?"
- What it wants: one card lit at a time; a part clip plays 3 s and stops; True? can be opened any time.
- Ads in my day: hotspots are generous (at least 60 px); a tap on nothing does nothing. The scene counter counts ads only. All 4 found: the line for that scene (event).
- `Start over` (The ad bar, with `Yes, start over` and `Keep it`) clears the votes and chart and turns all switches on. Nothing is saved.
- Tap while it plays: Stop, a switch, a mode switch or a vote button acts at once.
- Motion off: no zooms, splash slow motion, bounces or sparkle twinkle (each shot is a still); the shot timing stays 3 s each.
- Keyboard: M, P, W flip the switches; Space plays or stops; Up and Down change the Want it count.

## Look

The ad itself is loud on purpose (rainbows, hot pink #ff4fa3, lemon #ffe14d, sky #3fb3ff) and stays inside the TV frame; everything outside the TV is calm, so the ad's loudness is something to look at.
- The chart: Want it bars in brand rust with a hand-up icon, Not sure bars in grey #8a8a8a with a sideways hand, counts as numbers on top.

## Voice
Bank voice `news-reader` (Rasalgethi), style "Say clearly and steadily, like a trusted radio news reader". The calm host who helps the class take the ad apart. The five ad lines (Content, shots 1 to 5) are recorded with the same voice and the style "Say it like an excited TV ad announcer", so the ad sounds like an ad and the host sounds like the host. Not used by any K-2 arts neighbor (line-safari bright-teacher, art-passport knowing-guide, beat-buddies lively-host, orchestra-zoo playful-friend, sound-elevator hero-coach, act-it-out gentle-storyteller, levels-and-pathways warm-teacher) or by another K-2 spec in this batch. `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.mode`. Host lines never play during the ad (they wait for it to end, or are dropped when stale); the ad lines are part of the ad (Words on) and are not queue lines.

## Done when
1. Focus: at 1366x768 and 680x425 the TV is the largest thing on The ad stage, with the three switches and the vote bar visible beside or under it without scrolling.
2. All 8 switch combinations play the right version (Full ad, any one off, any two off, Plain bowl) for 15 s.
3. Tap while it plays: flipping a switch mid-ad changes the ad at once (music within 50 ms, pictures at once, words from the next line); Stop stops everything.
4. Votes count 0 to 40, attach to the version just played, and draw the chart with labeled bars and counts.
5. The big question plays after the Plain bowl vote every time.
6. Every message card's part buttons play their 3 s clip; every True? card shows and speaks.
7. Each day scene has 4 ads and 2 not-ads, each hotspot 60 px or more, and the counter counts ads only.
8. Host lines never play during the ad; how-to lines are spoken once in five actions.
9. Every control is 60 px or more with an icon and a word.
10. Motion off: still shots with the same timing. No network requests; nothing is saved.
