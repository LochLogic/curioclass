# Cereal Ad Questions (idea card)

- Module: arts-k-2-media-messages, grade k-2. Codes: MA:Re7, MA:Re8, MA:Re9, MA:Cn11
- Format / family: showdown / none. Fit: good (a solo child plays every round; the projector vote is the extra)
- One idea: Watch a silly made-up cereal ad, then pull it apart like a toy: lift out the music, the bright pictures and the words one at a time, and the ad gets less and less exciting until only a plain bowl of cereal is left, so the child sees that the ad's parts did the work.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a TV showing "Crunchy Comet Flakes" with three pull tabs: Music, Pictures, Words.
  - Core loop: play the ad; pull one tab and replay (no music, plain pictures, or no words). Answer with picture cards: Which part made you want it? What does the ad want you to think (you will be fast, you will be happy, you will have friends)? Then fix it: pick one part to change to make the ad more honest.
  - The aha or the end state: the plain bowl on a plain table, next to the ad. The child chooses which parts they liked and which parts were tricks. A last round finds where ads show up in a day (the side of a bus, inside a game, before a video, on a cereal box).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes on each question on the projector.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MA:Re7: naming the parts of the ad (music, pictures, words) and what each one does.
  - MA:Re8: saying what the ad wants the viewer to think.
  - MA:Re9: picking the parts that work and the change that would make it more honest.
  - MA:Cn11: finding where ads show up in everyday life.
- Not like: toy-ad-reality-check (grade 3-5 camera tricks against the real toy); viral-video-breakdown (grade 6-8).
- Art: generated scenes for the ad worlds; made-up cereals and mascots, no real brands; music original to Curio Class.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Watch a silly made-up cereal ad, pull out its music, pictures and words one at a time until only a plain bowl is left, say what the ad wanted you to think, and find where ads show up in your day.
- Flags: overlap risk (toy-ad-reality-check; keep this one about the parts of an ad). The old line's "use screens safely" belongs to a digital citizenship tool, not this code, so it is dropped.
