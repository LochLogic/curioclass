# Stadium Count (idea card)

- Module: math-4-place-value-to-a-million, grade 4. Codes: 4.NBT.A
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Zoom out from one seat to a row, a section, a full stadium and a whole city, where each step holds ten times as many people, so thousands and hundred-thousands become crowds you can see and compare.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: one fan in one seat, a zoom slider and a place-value strip. "Zoom out. Each step is ten times as many people."
  - Core loop: each zoom multiplies by ten (1 seat, 10 seats, 100, 1,000, 10,000, 100,000) and the place-value strip shifts the digit one place left. The child reads crowd counts in numerals, words and expanded form (45,312 = 40,000 + 5,000 + 300 + 10 + 2).
  - Compare: two real stadiums' capacities side by side; the child picks >, = or <, digit by digit.
  - Headlines: a news ticker rounds the crowd ("about 45,000 fans", "about 50,000"); the child rounds to the place the headline needs and sees how the story changes.
  - The aha or the end state: the 4 in 4,000 is worth ten times the 4 in 400, and the zoom shows it.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): your town's population and how many stadiums it would fill.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 4.NBT.A: a digit is worth ten times the same digit one place to the right (4.NBT.1), reading and writing numbers to a million in numerals, words and expanded form and comparing them (4.NBT.2), and rounding to any place (4.NBT.3).
- Not like: size-of-everything (grade 8 scientific notation), place-value-vault (grade 2 to 1000), powers-of-ten-zoom (grade 5 decimals and powers of ten; similar zoom look, so vary it).
- Art: generated scenes (a stadium and a city at several zoom levels, crowds drawn with care), because the crowd is the lesson.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Zoom from one seat to a row, a section, a stadium and a city, ten times more people each step; read crowds in numerals, words and expanded form, compare real stadiums and round for the headline.
- Flags: needs a data source (stadium capacities and city populations, from stadium owners' published figures and the US Census Bureau, with the year shown); overlap risk (powers-of-ten-zoom, grade 5, shares the zoom mechanic)
