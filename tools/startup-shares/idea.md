# Startup Shares (idea card)

- Module: cte-6-8-startup-shares, grade 6-8. Codes: NSPFE.Investing, D2.Eco.7.6-8
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good, because the split of money is the input and many possible futures are the output.
- One idea: Meet five made-up young companies and the people who started them (a seaweed-packaging maker, a farm-drone start-up, a tutoring app, a bike-repair chain, a battery recycler), spread a pretend $1,000 across them or buy a basket of all five, then fast-forward ten years many times over and see how often each choice ends up ahead, even or behind.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: five founder booths at a pitch fair, each with the problem they solve, what they risked (savings, a steady job) and an honest "what could go wrong" card.
  - Core loop: split $1,000 with sliders; press Fast-forward. One future plays out year by year (the tutoring app flops in year 3, the battery recycler takes off in year 6). Then run 100 futures at once and see a spread chart of outcomes for your split vs the basket.
  - Realism: most young companies fail or stay small, and a few grow a lot, so a single pick usually loses and the basket rarely loses everything.
  - Innovation: each company's success depends on whether its new idea meets a real need, and its future card shows the jobs and products it added to the town.
  - The aha or the end state: spreading money out does not make the best result bigger, but it makes the worst result much less likely; and new ideas grow the economy when they meet real needs.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - NSPFE.Investing: compare risk and return of one pick vs a spread-out basket across many futures.
  - D2.Eco.7.6-8: read how each entrepreneur's new idea and risk-taking could create products, jobs and growth.
- Not like: market-time-machine (grade 9-12 historical index data); compound-interest-head-start (grade 9-12 savings); startup-booth (grade 9-12 running a business).
- Art: people (illustrated) founders at a pitch fair; charts drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: none (made-up companies only; educational, no picks or advice; outcome odds should match published start-up survival rates, sourced)
