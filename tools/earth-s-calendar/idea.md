# Earth's Calendar (idea card)

- Module: science-6-geologic-time, grade 6. Codes: MS-ESS1-4
- Format / family: timeline / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Stack rock layers from several real places into one column by matching their fossils, mark where life changes most sharply, and watch those marks become the eras and periods of the geologic time scale, then squeeze all 4.6 billion years into one calendar year.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three short rock columns from different real places, unlabeled, a fossil tray, and an empty calendar ribbon below.
  - Core loop: tap layers to see their fossils; the same index fossil in two columns means the same age, so the student slides the columns into one tall stack. Then place boundary markers where the fossils change most (a sudden end of many kinds at once). The tool snaps each marker to the named boundary it matches and adds the dated age.
  - The aha or the end state: drag the finished column onto the calendar: animals with shells appear in mid November, the dinosaurs vanish around December 26, and our species arrives in roughly the last half hour of December 31. The student writes why one boundary sits where it does, using the layers as evidence.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "your lifetime on the calendar" button (a fraction of a second).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-ESS1-4: the student builds the time scale from strata and fossils and explains, from that evidence, how the scale organizes Earth's history.
- Not like: canyon-reader (grade 4, one canyon's local story); fossil-clues (grade 3, past environments); star-life-story (a star's life line, grade 9).
- Art: generated scenes for each era's landscape strip; rock columns, markers and the calendar drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Match fossils to stack rock layers from three places, mark where life changes most, and watch the marks become the eras of the time scale; then squeeze 4.6 billion years into one calendar year.
- Flags: needs a data source (boundary ages from the International Commission on Stratigraphy chart and real index fossils with their sites, checked; calendar dates computed from them)
