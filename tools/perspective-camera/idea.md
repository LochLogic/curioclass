# Perspective Camera (idea card)

- Module: ela-6-point-of-view, grade 6. Codes: RL.6.6
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: One scene sits on a stage, and the student moves a camera between narrators (inside one character's head, over one shoulder, high above everyone, or a narrator who has a stake in the story), and the text rewrites itself while a "can know" panel lights what that narrator can and cannot know, so they learn how an author builds a narrator's point of view through what is told, hidden and judged.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a scene at a lost-and-found counter with three people and a missing backpack; a camera on a dial: first person, third person limited, third person all-knowing, second person.
  - Core loop: turn the dial and the passage rewrites; the "can know" panel shows thoughts, secrets and events the narrator can reach. Then a "spot the narrator" step: tap the words that show the narrator's attitude (poor old Mr. Diaz, that greedy kid) and see how word choice tilts the scene. Later levels give a passage and ask the student to identify the narrator and explain how the author built that view.
  - The aha or the end state: a twist: the first-person narrator is not telling everything, and the student finds the lines that gave it away, then rewrites one paragraph from a narrator of their choice.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second scene from a public-domain story.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.6.6: explaining how the author develops each narrator's point of view through what they know, what they tell and the words they choose.
- Not like: swap-the-teller (grades 3-5, three tellers of one mix-up); tone-dial (grade 10, tone words); who-said-that (grade 1).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the scene, with a drawn-in-code camera and knowledge panel.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Move a camera between narrators of one scene (first person, limited, all-knowing, second person) and watch the text rewrite while a panel shows what each can know; then tap the words that reveal a narrator's attitude.
- Flags: none
