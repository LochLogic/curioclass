arc: explore-first
minutes: 30
focus: 2.OA.C
goal: I can tell if a number is odd or even by pairing, and write an array as repeated addition.
materials: The tool on the big screen with sound on. Counters or cubes for each pair. Whiteboards.
grouping: Whole class on the carpet, then partners.
---
## Before class

- Open **Partners** with 7 musicians.
- There's no score. The point is noticing patterns and saying them out loud.
- Keyboard: + and − change the band, M marches. **Sound** starts on; click any musician to hear them play.

## Hook (3 min)

1. "Everyone find a partner!" If your class count is odd, notice who is left. "What happened?"
2. "Let's see which numbers always leave someone alone."

## Explore (12 min)

1. With 7 musicians, ask: "Does everyone have someone to march with?" The class spots the one alone.
2. Add one musician at a time. Before each click, the class calls out "partner" or "alone."
3. Collect the "alone" numbers on the board: odd. Collect the "partner" numbers: even.
4. Press **March!** with an even band and then an odd one.

## Make sense (5 min)

1. "Which numbers always leave someone alone? What do they end in?" (1, 3, 5, 7, 9.)
2. "An even number is two equal groups: 8 is 4 + 4." Show this with a band of 8.

## Apply (7 min)

1. Switch to **Rows**. Set 3 rows of 4. Read the sentence together: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.
2. "Can 12 musicians march in a different shape?" The tool keeps a list of every way the class finds.
3. Partners build arrays with counters and write the repeated addition on whiteboards.
4. Try 5 or 7. "Why won't it make a rectangle?"

## Exit check (3 min)

On whiteboards: "Is 9 odd or even? Draw it as partners to show how you know." Then: "Write 2 rows of 5 as an addition sentence." (5 + 5 = 10.)

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** pair counters on a desk before naming odd or even.
- **Support:** stay with numbers up to 10.
- **Stretch:** "Is 15 odd or even? How do you know without counting them all?"
- **Stretch:** find every array for 16 and 18.

## Short on time

- **Partners** from 7 to 12, calling out "partner" or "alone" (6 min).
- One array in **Rows** (4 min).
