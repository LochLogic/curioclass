# Sentence Remixer (idea card)

- Module: ela-6-pronouns-and-sentence-variety, grade 6. Codes: L.6.1, L.6.3
- Format / family: puzzle / none. Fit: good. Combining sentences is where pronoun problems are born, so one mechanic carries both codes.
- One idea: The student remixes a paragraph of short choppy sentences into varied ones, and every time a combination creates a pronoun problem (a "she" that could mean two people, a "me and him" in the subject, a shift from "you" to "they") the sentence glitches on screen until it is fixed, so they learn to vary sentences without losing who is who.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a mixing deck with eight short sentences about two friends fixing a bike before a race, and a rhythm strip showing every sentence the same length.
  - Core loop: the student merges sentences with joiners (and, but, because, when, who) or starts one with a phrase; the rhythm strip shows new lengths. Merging two sentences about Ana and Lena can make "she" ambiguous: the sentence glitches, the two possible readings flash as pictures, and the student fixes it with a name or a rebuild. Other glitches: wrong case ("Him and me checked the chain"), a pronoun shift in number or person, a missing intensive pronoun ("Lena fixed it herself"), and a remix that drops into chat slang in the middle of a formal paragraph.
  - The aha or the end state: the finished paragraph reads aloud with its new rhythm; a before-and-after strip shows the variety, and a glitch log names each fix. Later levels give a paragraph by a character who speaks a regional or informal English: the student decides where that voice belongs (dialogue) and where the narration needs standard forms.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "remix the same paragraph three ways" challenge, compared by rhythm strip.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - L.6.1: pronoun case, intensive pronouns, shifts in number and person, vague pronouns, and recognizing nonstandard forms in writing and speech, all as glitches to fix.
  - L.6.3: varying sentence patterns for meaning and interest, and keeping style and tone consistent across the paragraph.
- Not like: parallel-parking (grade 9, parallel structure); sentence-diagram-builder (grade 7, clause types as a tree); comma-chameleon (grade 5, commas that change a picture).
- Art: generated scenes for each paragraph's story (bike race, bakery, storm drill), drawn in code for the mixing deck and glitch effect.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Remix choppy sentences into varied ones; whenever a merge makes a pronoun unclear, the wrong case or a sudden shift, the sentence glitches until you fix who is who.
- Flags: sensitive (light: nonstandard English is presented as a voice that belongs in dialogue, never as wrong speech)
