# Remembrance Archive (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-10-world-war-ii-and-the-holocaust, grade 10. Codes: D2.His.1.9-12, D2.His.4.9-12, D2.His.11.9-12
- Format / family: explorer / primary-sources. Fit: good
- One idea: Follow a few real people and places through the Holocaust and the war in documents, photographs, maps and testimony, and for every item ask who made it, when, for whom and why, so the student learns both what happened and how we know.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a quiet archive room with a few folders, each a person or a place (a family from one town, a ghetto, a rescuers' village, a camp liberated in 1945); a short note says what the archive holds and that it has no game mechanics.
  - Core loop: open a folder and read items in time order; each item has a source card (maker, date, place, audience, purpose), and the student rates how useful it is for the folder's question (for example: "What could ordinary people know, and when?"). Perpetrator documents and propaganda appear only inside that framing.
  - Context strip: a map and timeline place each item in the wider war, showing what was particular to that town and year.
  - The aha or the end state: the student answers the folder's question using the three items they judged most useful, and writes a "why it matters" reflection that stays on the device (stated on screen).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): teacher notes with discussion guidance and support.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.1.9-12: the context strip ties each life to local circumstances and the wider war.
  - D2.His.4.9-12: items from victims, rescuers, bystanders and perpetrators, each with the factors that shaped that view.
  - D2.His.11.9-12: the source card and the usefulness rating for a specific question, on every item.
- Not like: voices-of-freedom (family sibling, US slavery sources, grade 8); missing-voices-archive (gaps in an archive, 1918 flu).
- Art: public domain or licensed archival photographs and documents (US National Archives, Library of Congress; permission checked item by item), no generated images of victims, because invented images would undermine the evidence.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Follow real people and places through the Holocaust and the war in documents, photographs and testimony; judge each source's maker, date and purpose to learn what happened and how we know. No game mechanics.
- Flags: sensitive (genocide; fresh review agent per rule 5; teacher notes per rule 8); needs a data source (testimony and photo permissions, checked per item)
