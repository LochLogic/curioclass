# Skate Park Energy (idea card)

- Module: science-8-kinetic-and-potential-energy, grade 8. Codes: MS-PS3-1, MS-PS3-2 (plus MS-PS3-5, moved here from cooler-challenge)
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Draw a ramp, drop a skater of a chosen mass from a chosen height, and watch kinetic and potential energy trade places in a live bar chart, with friction on by default so the energy that leaves shows up as warmth in the wheels and ramp.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a skater at the top of a simple half-pipe, a pen to redraw the ramp, mass and height sliders, and bars for kinetic, potential and thermal energy.
  - Core loop: drop the skater and watch the bars trade; a speed meter and height marker run alongside. Friction is on by default (rule 9): each pass ends a little lower and the thermal bar grows; the student can switch friction off to see the ideal case. A data drawer logs each run, and the student graphs kinetic energy against mass and against speed (doubling speed gives four times the energy).
  - Brake test: drag a brake pad onto the ramp and stop the skater; the kinetic bar drains into the thermal bar and the wheels glow warm on a heat view.
  - The aha or the end state: the total stays the same; height stores energy, speed carries it, and when the skater slows the energy has gone somewhere, into heating the wheels and ramp.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-PS3-1: the student graphs kinetic energy against mass and against speed from their own runs.
  - MS-PS3-2: changing the drop height (the skater's distance from Earth) shows different amounts of stored potential energy.
  - MS-PS3-5: the brake test and friction runs support an argument that when the skater's kinetic energy changes, energy is transferred out as heat.
- Not like: zipline-energy (grade 11, friction and brakes in numbers); bowling-lab (grade 4 speed and energy); curling-stones (grade 11 forces on ice).
- Art: people (illustrated) for a varied set of skaters; ramp, bars and graphs drawn in code.
- Proposed title: Half-Pipe Energy (the current title is very close to the name of a well-known free physics simulation)
- Proposed concept line: Draw a ramp and drop a skater: kinetic and potential energy trade places in a live bar chart, friction turns some into heat, and your runs graph how mass and speed set kinetic energy.
- Flags: overlap risk (zipline-energy; keep this one to bar charts and proportional graphs)
