# Voice Switcher (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-verbals-and-voice, grade 8. Codes: L.8.1, L.8.3
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good, as a switchboard: the same sentence runs through switches for voice, mood and a verb's job, and the student sees what each switch does to meaning.
- One idea: At a newsroom switchboard the student flips the same sentences between active and passive voice (and sees who gets the blame vanish from a headline), between moods (a fact, a command, a question, a "would", a "were"), and a verb between jobs (as a verb, a gerund, a participle, an infinitive), so they learn how verb forms change who is responsible, how certain a statement is, and what a word is doing in the sentence.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a newsroom with a headline board and three switch panels: Voice, Mood, Job.
  - Core loop: Voice panel: "The company dumped oil in the creek" becomes "Oil was dumped in the creek by the company" and then "Oil was dumped in the creek"; a blame meter shows the actor fading. The student decides which form fits each story (a science report where the action matters, a news story where the actor must be named). Mood panel: the student switches sentences into conditional ("If the bridge closed, buses would reroute") and subjunctive ("If I were mayor") to express uncertainty or a contrary-to-fact wish, and fixes paragraphs where the mood shifts by accident. Job panel: one verb ("repair") moves between jobs: "Repairing bikes is fun", "the repaired bike", "to repair a bike", and the student names the job in each.
  - The aha or the end state: an editor's challenge: rewrite one news brief twice, once to hold a company responsible and once to soften it, using only voice and mood, and explain the effect. A final paragraph has inappropriate shifts in voice and mood to fix.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a headline vote: which version is fair to readers?
- Standards, one line per code:
  - L.8.1: explaining verbals in general and in sentences, forming active and passive voice, forming the five moods, and correcting inappropriate shifts in voice and mood.
  - L.8.3: choosing active or passive and conditional or subjunctive for effect, such as naming the actor or expressing uncertainty.
- Not like: tone-dial (grade 10, word choice and tone); sentence-diagram-builder (grade 7, clauses and phrases); sentence-remixer (grade 6, pronouns and variety).
- Art: drawn in code for the switchboard; illustrated newsroom (people, illustrated) and generated scenes for the news briefs.
- Proposed title: Verb Switchboard (the tool now switches voice, mood and verbals, not voice alone)
- Proposed concept line: At a newsroom switchboard, flip sentences between active and passive voice (and watch who gets the blame vanish), between moods (fact, command, "would", "were"), and a verb between jobs as a gerund, participle or infinitive.
- Flags: too big (three panels; the spec must keep each one full, not trimmed)
