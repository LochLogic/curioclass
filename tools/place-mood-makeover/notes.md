## What it teaches

Students can read the visual clues that give a place a mood, then change light, time, color, weather and details
to create an intention of their own. They revise conflicting clues or explain why an unexpected clue belongs.

## Key skills

- Notice clues in a picture and explain how they affect a reading.
- Try different moods for the same ordinary place.
- Revise a picture so its clues support an intention.
- Give a reason for a surprising artistic choice.
- Connect a picture's mood to a place in everyday life.

## Support at home

- Look at the same outdoor place in morning and evening. Ask which light makes it feel different and why.
- Notice a small detail near home, such as a porch light or an empty bench. Share two ways someone might read it.
- Describe a familiar place that feels calm or cheerful. Name the colors, weather or objects that help it feel that way.

## A 5-minute start

Open Three artists. Each learner chooses a mood, then taps two visible clues in the painting. A different reading
still reveals the artist's intention and all four clues. Compare the three versions, then choose Make your own.
In Makeover, choose a goal and change at least three things. Watch each clue's arrow appear.

## Modes

**Three artists:** Three original bus-stop paintings use American Scene realism, warm storybook gouache, and
ink with gray wash. They illustrate styles; they are not reproductions of historical artworks. The neutral
Artist 1, Artist 2 and Artist 3 labels do not reveal the intended mood before the learner reads the clues.

**Makeover:** Bus stop, Front porch and Playground share the time, light, color and weather controls. Each has
ten different painted details. The compass outlines the goal in gold. Thick branches show a strong pull (2 in
the content table), thin branches show some pull (1). A dashed clue has no pull toward the goal; ↯ marks its tug.
A clue can pull several ways. These are the tool's teaching conventions, not measured facts or artistic laws.

Tap a My clues chip to tag the mood you meant. If the Reader disagrees, choose Change it, or choose
Keep it, here's why and type a reason of up to 100 characters. A kept reason directs that clue toward its tag;
an asterisk on the arrow and the chip's “your reason” label identify that interpretation.
At least three clues must support the goal before the ring turns gold and Write about it appears.
Finish both sentence starters to explain the choices and connect the picture to your own life.

Partner guess hides the intention, compass, clues and controls until another learner selects a mood.
Before and after offers a draggable divider with keyboard arrow control. Tap its track to toggle the two views.
Start over clears the current place's makeover, tags, reasons and sentences while retaining the other places.

## Talk questions

- Which clue changed your reading most? What could another viewer see differently?
- Which clue pulls away from your goal? Would you revise it, or defend it with a reason?
- Can you give the same place a second mood without replacing every clue?
- Which place in your life has this mood, and what makes it feel that way?

## Good to know

The picture and sentences save only on this device, with no network requests or accounts. Storage may be unavailable
in a private browser or restricted iframe; the studio then explains that work lasts while the page stays open.
Switching places carries the current time, light, colors and weather while restoring that place's own details,
tags, reasons and sentences. First visits have no details. Motion off stills weather, skies and the lamp flicker.
Sound controls optional spoken guidance; no music or recorded audio is included. Browser speech is the fallback
until narration is recorded with the specified soft-breath voice.

Every control is keyboard reachable. Tab to a painting region and press Enter to find it after choosing a mood.
The divider is a native range control. My clues follow the main controls in keyboard order.
Short landscape panels use a horizontally scrolling ten-detail strip as specified. Phone controls follow the
picture and compass; the picture stays in view while the controls scroll underneath. Lower details, clue chips
and reflection work still need scrolling. Compact clue cards keep their title and Close control in view.
A successful picture gains a gold frame and a title using the chosen mood. Partner guess hides that title's
mood as well as the compass, so the partner reads only the picture. Revising clears the old partner result.
Saved writing stays available when the clues agree again; revisions keep its text on this device.

## Standards in the activity

VA:Cr1: try different moods and choose an intention in Makeover. VA:Cr3: revise clues and explain a kept reading.
VA:Re8: interpret the three artists' paintings through two found clues each. VA:Cn10: write about a place in your
own life with the same mood. All four are actions a solo learner can complete.
