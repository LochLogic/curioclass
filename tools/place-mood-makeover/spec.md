# Place Mood Makeover

## One idea
The Mood Reader reads only your clues: change an ordinary place's time of day, light, colors, weather and small details, and a mood compass draws every clue as an arrow toward the mood most people read in it, so you see exactly which clue is pulling against the rest, and revise (or defend it) until every arrow points the way you meant.

## Not like
- near-and-far (3-5, puzzle): moves objects to fix depth cues. Here nothing moves for depth; each change is read for feeling.
- paint-mixer (3-5, lab): mixes pigments to hit a color. Here a palette is a whole-picture choice (bright, soft, dark, gray) read for mood, not mixed.
- art-style-match (3-5, cards): plays "tell" cards to name a style. Here the clues are the student's own changes, read for mood, and the student can keep a clue the Reader disagrees with by giving a reason.
- frame-it (6-8, lab): moves a camera for composition. Here the camera is fixed; the place itself changes.
- cartoon-soundtrack-mixer (this band): mood made by sound under fixed pictures. Here mood is made by the picture itself, with no sound.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, visual arts, module "Mood and Place in Art (Visual Arts)".
- Topic line: giving a place a mood
- Driver: students (solo or a pair) or teacher (projector, class calls out the mood). 15 to 25 minutes.
- Standards: VA:Cr1 (try several moods for one place, then choose), VA:Cr3 (revise until the clues agree; explain a kept clue), VA:Re8 (read three artists' versions by their clues), VA:Cn10 (connect the place to the student's own life in the closing sentence).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons: `Three artists` (default), `Makeover`.

**Three artists.** Focus: one painting at a time, filling the stage (16:9), three thumbnails under it (`Lonely?`, `Cozy?`, `Spooky?` are NOT shown; the thumbnails are labeled `Artist 1`, `Artist 2`, `Artist 3`). Panel: "What mood is this?" with five mood buttons (Lonely, Cozy, Spooky, Cheerful, Calm; each with its icon). Tap a mood: the panel says "Now tap the clues that told you." Tapping the painting finds the clue hotspots (Content); each found clue gets a ring and a chip ("one lit shelter, nobody waiting"). After two clues the artist's own mood and all clues are revealed, whether the guess matched or not ("Artist 1 meant lonely. You said calm. The cool light reads both ways."). `Next artist`. After all three: `Make your own` (switches to Makeover with the bus stop).

**Makeover (the sandbox).** Focus: the place, the biggest thing (16:9, layered, see Art), with the Mood Reader compass beside it on wide screens or under it on narrow ones (about a third of the stage).
- Top of the panel: `Place` (Bus stop, Front porch, Playground) and `I want it to feel` (the five moods; the goal).
- Controls (segmented rows, each starts on "Plain"): `Time` (Plain, Dawn, Noon, Dusk, Night), `Light` (Plain, Warm, Cool), `Colors` (Plain, Bright, Soft, Dark, Gray), `Weather` (None, Rain, Snow, Fog, Wind). `Details`: ten toggle chips for the current place (Content), each adds or removes a painted cutout.
- Every change that is not Plain or None is a clue. The place updates at once; the compass gains or moves an arrow.
- `My clues` list: every active clue as a chip with its arrow color and icon. Tap a chip to tag it with the mood you mean (the five mood buttons appear in a small popover). Untagged clues still show on the compass.
- The Mood Reader (compass): five directions (one per mood, with name and icon), the goal direction outlined in gold. Each clue is an arrow from the center toward its strongest mood; a clue that reads two ways shows a split arrow. Arrows that point toward the goal are solid; arrows with no pull toward the goal are dashed with a small "tug" mark. Under the compass one sentence: "6 clues: 5 point to Cozy, 1 pulls toward Lonely (lost glove)."
- When a tagged clue disagrees with the Reader (the student tagged string lights "lonely"), the chip shows "Most people read this as cheerful or cozy" and two buttons: `Change it` (removes the clue) and `Keep it, here's why` (a one-line box, 100 characters). A kept clue with a reason counts as pointing to the student's tag and shows "your reason" on its arrow.
- Done check (live, never a button): at least 3 clues, and every clue points toward the goal (by the Reader or by a kept reason). Then the compass ring turns gold with "Every clue points to Cozy." and `Write about it` appears.
- `Write about it`: two sentence starters in a card under the place: "I made the ___ feel ___ because ___." and "A place in my life that feels like this is ___." Saved on this device with the makeover.
- `Partner guess`: hides the compass, chips and goal; shows the five mood buttons to a partner; after their tap, everything comes back with "Your partner read Lonely. You meant Calm." `Before and after`: wipes between the Plain place and the makeover (drag the divider or tap to toggle).
- `Start over` (this place back to Plain).

## Interactions in detail
- Layers, back to front: sky (one of four painted skies for Dawn, Noon, Dusk, Night; Plain uses Noon at 40% saturation), the place plate (cutout), details (cutouts), weather (code: rain lines, snow dots, a fog gradient, blowing leaf cutouts), then light and color treatment in code: Warm is a soft-light overlay #ffb066 at 35%, Cool #6fa8ff at 35%; Dusk adds an orange-to-violet gradient at 30%; Night multiplies the plate by #27305c at 65% and makes every "glow" detail (shop window, lantern, lamp glow, string lights, flickering lamp) bright with a radial glow; Colors filters the place group (Bright: saturate 1.4; Soft: saturate .6, brightness 1.1, contrast .85; Dark: brightness .65, contrast 1.1; Gray: saturate .15).
- The compass and sentence update on every change at once. The flickering lamp flickers (1.3 s cycle; with Motion off it stays dim and steady); rain and snow fall (Motion off: a still frame of streaks and flakes).
- Switching Place keeps the time, light, colors and weather and clears the details (each place has its own); its own makeover is remembered per place for the session.
- Changing the goal mood redraws solid and dashed arrows at once; kept reasons stay with their clue.
- Edge cases: zero clues: the compass shows "Change something to add a clue." One clue: "One clue isn't a mood yet. Add two more." A tie between two moods for the most arrows is said as a tie.
- Saved on this device (localStorage `place-mood-makeover`): each place's makeover, tags, reasons and sentences. `Start over` clears the current place.
- Keyboard: every control is a button or radio group; chips are reachable after the controls.
- Sizes: the place is fitted 16:9 at real pixels; at 1366x768 the compass sits to the right of the place inside the stage; at 680x425 the panel is compact (goal, the four rows as small segmented controls, details in a scroll strip) and the compass shrinks under the place; at 375 wide the compass follows the place and the panel stacks below with the goal first.

## Voice
Bank voice `soft-breath` (Enceladus), style "Say softly and calmly, like a mindfulness teacher": someone who notices how a place feels. Not used by any 3-5 arts neighbor or another tool in this band. `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.mode`.
- welcomeArtists (how; entering Three artists; stale when a mood is tapped): "Look at this painting. What mood is it? Tap a mood."
- cluesHow (how; after the first mood tap; stale when a clue is found): "Now tap the things in the painting that told you."
- reveal (event; after two clues on each painting; stale on Next artist): the reveal line, for example "Artist 1 meant lonely. You said calm. Cool light can feel both."
- welcomeMakeover (how; entering Makeover with no clues; stale when any control changes): "Pick a mood at the top. Then change the time, the light, the colors or the weather."
- compassHow (how; after the first clue; stale after the third clue): "Each change is a clue. The compass shows which way it points."
- tagHow (how; after the third clue; stale when a chip is tapped): "Tap a clue in My clues to tag the mood you meant."
- tug (event; when a new clue pulls away from the goal; stale on the next change): "The lost glove pulls toward lonely." (built from the clue and its top mood)
- agree (event; when the done check first turns true for this place and goal; stale on the next change): "Every clue points to cozy. Tap Write about it."
- partner (event; after a partner guess): "Your partner read lonely. You meant calm."

## Done when
1. Focus: at 1366x768, 680x425 and 375 wide the place is the largest thing on screen and the compass is visible without scrolling beside or under it.
2. Every one of the 15 place and mood pairs can reach "Every clue points to ..." (a test drives one path for each).
3. A clue the student tags against the Reader shows "Most people read this as ...", and `Keep it, here's why` with a reason makes its arrow count.
4. Night makes every glow detail glow; Gray drains color; Fog covers the far plate; changes show within one frame of the tap.
5. Three artists: guessing wrong still reveals the artist's mood and all clues after two finds.
6. While rain falls or the lamp flickers, every control acts at once; Motion off stills both.
7. How lines are spoken once in a run of five changes; tug and agree lines are spoken every time.
8. `Partner guess` hides the compass, chips and goal until the partner taps a mood.
9. Makeovers, tags, reasons and sentences survive a reload; `Start over` clears only the current place.
