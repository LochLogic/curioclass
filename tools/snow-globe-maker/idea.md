# Snow Globe Maker (idea card)

- Module: math-8-volume-of-cylinders-cones-and-spheres, grade 8. Codes: 8.G.C
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Run a craft-fair booth that makes snow globes (spheres), cone-shaped candle molds and cylinder bases; pour tests show a cone holds a third of its cylinder and a sphere two thirds, and volume then sets how much water, wax and glitter each piece needs and what it costs.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a workbench at a winter craft fair, a cylinder, a cone and a sphere of the same radius and height, and a jug of water. The booth partner, Rafa: "How much water does each hold? Pour and see."
  - Pour tests: the student fills the cone and pours it into the cylinder (three cones fill it), then the sphere (one sphere fills two thirds of a cylinder as tall as it is wide). The formulas appear from the pours: V = pi r squared h, one third of that for a cone, four thirds pi r cubed for a sphere.
  - Booth days: orders arrive (12 small globes, 6 large candle molds); the student computes volumes, buys supplies by the liter, and sets prices. Doubling a globe's radius takes eight times the water, which surprises customers and costs.
  - Missing-dimension orders: a customer wants a globe that holds exactly 1 liter; what radius? A candle needs 500 mL of wax in a cone 12 cm tall; how wide?
  - The aha or the end state: three shapes, one family of formulas, and small changes in radius make big changes in volume.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare booth profits across the class.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 8.G.C (8.G.9): volume formulas for cones, cylinders and spheres, found by pouring and used to solve real supply, cost and missing-dimension problems.
- Not like: box-packer (grade 5 prisms by cubes), big-wheel-small-wheel (grade 7 circles), packaging-challenge (grade 10 least material).
- Art: generated scenes (the craft fair booth, finished globes and candles); people (illustrated) for Rafa and customers; solids and pours drawn in code so levels match the math.
