## What it teaches

Students lead a recorder ensemble by listening against clear criteria and giving a specific note to one player in one bar.
They hear the next take respond to that note, then practice concert manners and join a short performance.

## Key skills

- Listen for togetherness, clear tone, a steady beat and musical breathing.
- Locate a problem by player and bar using listening, Solo and Loop bar.
- Give feedback that names who, where and what to change.
- Compare takes and explain which rehearsal note made a difference.
- Wait, tune, stay attentive and finish together in a concert.

## Support at home

- Clap a familiar song together. Give one specific suggestion, such as starting on the same count, and try again.
- Take turns humming a short phrase. Describe one change you heard using kind, precise words.
- Before a family performance, practice waiting for quiet and holding still after the final sound, then bow together.

## A five-minute start

1. Tap Play take. Listen to all four players before opening Score view.
2. Mark a checklist line Needs work. This is your judgment, not an automatic grade.
3. Tap a player to hear a solo. Tap a bar to loop it; tap it again to hear the whole piece.
4. Choose Who, Where and one note card. Tap Give note, then Play take.
5. Compare what you heard with what your note asked for. A vague note or a note aimed at the wrong place changes nothing.

## Modes

### Rehearsal

Hot Cross Buns has four bars at 88 beats a minute. Its first rehearsal has Otis starting late, Bea squeaking in bar 2,
and everyone rushing the eighth notes of bar 3. Au Clair de la Lune has eight bars at 96 beats a minute: Finn plays sharp
in bar 2, Juniper breathes inside the phrase at bar 3, and Bea starts late. Three useful notes and a final clean take
can finish either scripted rehearsal in four takes.

New rehearsal draws three different, solvable problems for the chosen piece. Start over resets only that piece.
The concert invitation appears after the clean take has finished, so students hear what their final note changed.
The checklist is optional. Needs work marks are independent. Tap a marked line again to return it to OK; the latest selected criterion filters the cards. Without a marked criterion, all fixing cards and a vague card appear.
The other tone card can be right for a different tone problem, so listen to the sound before choosing.

Score view is a listening aid rather than a conventional score: each player has a lane on a beat grid. A late note begins
to the right, sharp notes rise with arrows, a squeak has a spark, and a breath leaves a gap with a breath mark.
Open it after listening first. No timer, score or prize rewards guessing.

### Concert

Work through the four numbered Stage manners moments. Every moment must be met before Play with them opens.
The tuning moment sounds a long A from each player, then the group. Retries are free.

Play with them uses the clean Au Clair de la Lune, with a four-click count-in, a scrolling five-line staff, letter names,
and the next fingering. Join with a real recorder, sing the names or clap the rhythm. The choice sets the instruction only.
Slower changes the tempo to 80 beats a minute; a normal-speed performance lasts 20 seconds after the count-in,
and the slower performance lasts 24 seconds. The tool never records or listens to a student.

Then and now lets students choose either piece and hear its first and latest completed take, separately or back to back.
The note history records all notes, including unsuccessful ones. A piece with no rehearsal history initially has the same
first and final settings. It is not labeled as improved until students actually change and hear it.

## Talk questions

- What did you hear that made you choose that player and bar?
- Did your note name one action the player could try?
- Why might a useful suggestion make no change when it names the wrong player?
- What changed between your first and final take? Which note caused it?
- How can a quiet start and a shared ending help the audience listen?

## Good to know

Use headphones to hear the gentle left-to-right placement of the players. Sound off keeps the same timing and pictures.
Read stops the music before reading the current instruction. Motion off removes lifts and continuous motion while keeping
musical timing. Space plays or stops except in a selector or on Read; number keys 1 to 4 select a solo.
Tab reaches the panel before the players and bar buttons. Enter activates focused buttons.

Recorder fingerings: B covers the thumb hole and hole 1; A covers thumb, 1 and 2; G covers thumb, 1, 2 and 3.
These are left-hand fingerings on both baroque and German soprano recorders. The soprano sounds one octave above its written part.
Gentle breath and fully covered holes help tone. A synthesized model makes the five differences deliberately audible;
real instruments and players can have more than one reason for a similar sound.

Notes and first/last take problem settings stay in localStorage on this device. No audio, student name or microphone data is stored.
The only other storage comes from the shared Motion preference. Browser narration is used where recorded clips are unavailable.

## Standards in the activity

- MU:Pr5: evaluate a take, give specific feedback, rehearse and compare the changed result.
- MU:Re9: apply the four checklist criteria to audible takes and compare first and final performances.
- MU:Pr6: choose concert manners and actively join the clean performance by playing, singing or clapping.
