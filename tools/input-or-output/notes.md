## What it teaches

Students identify how everyday gadgets receive information through inputs and show or do things through outputs.
They predict and explain what changes when one part is covered, including a touchscreen that is both.

## Key skills

- Name common hardware parts and explain their jobs.
- Follow information going into and out of a gadget.
- Predict what happens when a part cannot do its job.
- Compare a prediction with the visible result.

## Support at home

- Point to a familiar gadget together. Ask what helps it find things out and what helps it show or do things.
- Pretend a TV remote button is covered. Ask what the TV would miss, then imagine its screen is covered instead.
- Talk about a touchscreen: it feels a tap and shows a picture, so it does both jobs.

## A 5-minute start

1. Open Try It on the tablet. Have a helper tap the camera and follow the blue dots into the Think chip.
2. Tap the speaker. Follow orange dots out. Press Video call to see the whole flow.
3. Enter Cover It. Tap Sticky note, then Speaker. Ask the class to predict with thumbs or out loud.
4. Tap a prediction and Video call. Watch Otter moving without a hello from the tablet speaker.
5. Peel off and compare with the microphone covered.

## Modes

Try It is free exploration. Every part can be replayed. A check marks a gadget when every part has been tried.
Cover It lets the class cover one part at a time, predict, and run the job. Predictions are optional and have no score.
Switching gadgets peels off the note. The gold button always restarts immediately.

## Talk about it

- Which way did the information go? How do the arrows help us tell?
- When we cover the TV screen, does the game stop or do we stop seeing it?
- Why does a song still play with the volume button covered?
- Why will the microwave refuse to start when it cannot tell the door is shut?
- What two jobs does the touchscreen do?

## Good to know

This is a pictured simulation. It never uses a real microphone or camera and stores no student work.
Only the kit Motion preference is remembered on this device. Sound off silences speech and tones; Read speaks the current hint.
Motion off keeps the same five-second jobs, using still dotted trails and three fox positions.
All controls work with Tab and Enter. The teacher drives for Grade 2, with helpers tapping parts.

The pictured sticky note is a pretend way to block a signal, not a real experiment to try on appliances.
Never put notes inside or on a working microwave. The heater is internal and cannot be covered in this model.
The microwave never heats if the door switch cannot confirm the door is shut. Its 30-second countdown is shown six times faster.
Its heater represents the magnetron, which makes the microwaves that warm the food.
The pictured door and keypad take a short setup beat, followed by the full five-second countdown: 5.8 seconds in total.

The tool teaches E2-SYS-HW-11 and the 2017 crosswalk 1A-CS-02 and 1A-CS-03.
E2-SYS-SE-12 is not taught here; secret-keeper and internet-journey carry online consequences.
Narration uses browser speech until the big-sister / Leda recordings are made separately.
