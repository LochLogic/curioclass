# Research packet: my-life-timeline

Concept: Help Juno put her life in order from a shoebox of keepsakes (a baby photo, a tiny shoe, a birthday card, Grandpa's story), naming each kind of source, then build your own life timeline from stickers and drawings.
Grade: 1
Standards: D2.His.1.K-2, D2.His.9.K-2

## Standards in plain words

- D2.His.1.K-2 (Create a chronological sequence of multiple events): First graders place milestones from a child's life into order from babyhood to the present day.
- D2.His.9.K-2 (Identify different kinds of historical sources): First graders identify whether an item is a photograph, a physical object (artifact), a written document, or an oral story (sound recording/interview).

## Real data

| Keepsake | Source category | Clues on the source | Chronological stage | Sequence order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baby hospital photo | Photograph (visual source) | Tiny infant in yellow swaddle blanket with hospital bassinet card | Infancy (Birth / Age 0) | 1 |

| Grandpa's spoken audio story | Oral history (sound recording) | Grandpa's voice describing planting giant sunflowers together at the community garden | Preschool years (Age 3) | 4 |
| Kindergarten handprint drawing | Document / Artwork | Green fingerpaint handprint labeled "Juno - First Day of Kindergarten" | Kindergarten start (Age 5) | 5 |
| First grade self-portrait | Drawing / Current source | Colored marker drawing of Juno holding a reading book in grade 1 | Present day (Age 6) | 6 |

Timeline time markers for Grade 1:
1. Past (Before): Baby days, toddler days, preschool days.
2. Present (Now): First grade classroom, today's activities.
3. Future (Later): Growing taller, second grade, hopes for the future.

Student sticker timeline builder prompts:
- My first tooth or first steps (Artifact/Story).
- A favorite pet or stuffed animal (Photo/Artifact).
- Learning to ride a bike or scooter (Photo/Story).
- Starting kindergarten (Document/Photo).
- Today in first grade (Drawing/Now).

## Common mistakes

1. Assuming older events go on the right side of a timeline rather than reading left to right chronologically.
2. Thinking oral stories are just made-up tales rather than valuable spoken historical evidence passed down by witnesses.
3. Believing only printed books count as historical sources, overlooking clothing, toys, photographs, and cards.
4. Confusing the age of the person with the age of the keepsake (e.g., Grandpa is old, but his story is about when Juno was little).
5. Expecting exact calendar dates for every personal memory; relative sequence (before vs after) is sufficient for early childhood.

## Sensitivity

- Family configurations vary widely (single parents, foster families, adoptive parents, kinship care, multi-generational homes); ensure keepsakes and family references welcome all backgrounds.
- Some children may lack infant photos or early keepsakes due to displacement or family circumstances; provide optional generic milestones (favorite toy, first lost tooth, favorite park) so every student participates comfortably.
- Avoid invasive questions about personal or medical birth circumstances.
