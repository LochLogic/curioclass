# Dancers on Tour (idea card)

- Module: arts-6-8-performing-in-different-spaces, grade 6-8. Codes: DA:Pr4, DA:Pr5, DA:Pr6
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: A six-dancer piece tours a school gym, a city park and a small theater; in each venue the same dance breaks in a new way (formations hit the basketball hoop, the audience in the park sits on three sides, the theater stage is half as deep), so you adapt spacing, timing and focus, apply the choreographer's notes, and keep the dancers healthy on every surface.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the tour map and the piece playing in the studio where it was made.
  - Core loop: at each venue, scout it (size, floor, sightlines, sound echo), then adapt: shrink or reshape formations, shift the front to face the audience, slow a section the gym's echo blurs. Choose the warm-up and the surface plan (no big jumps on concrete; a sprung floor allows them). Run the show; the choreographer's notes come back; apply them.
  - The aha or the end state: the three performances play side by side, each fitted to its space. The student also does the warm-up themselves, guided, and learns which muscles and joints each part protects.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): students learn one formation change in a real gym or classroom.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - DA:Pr4: adapting spacing, timing and focus to each venue's space and audience.
  - DA:Pr5: warm-ups, surfaces and body-safe practice, done by the student too.
  - DA:Pr6: performance practice for each venue and applying the choreographer's notes.
- Not like: museum-courtyard-performance (grade 9-12: producing one concert as the leader); choreography-board (grade 6-8 revision devices); stage-blocker (theater sightlines).
- Art: generated scenes for each venue; people (illustrated) for the six dancers, their own figures.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A six-dancer piece tours a gym, a park and a small theater; scout each space, adapt spacing, timing and focus, apply the choreographer's notes, and keep the dancers safe with the right warm-up for each surface.
- Flags: needs a data source (dance injury and surface facts, checked); overlap risk (museum-courtyard-performance; this one is the performer's view across venues)
