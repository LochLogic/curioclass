## What it teaches
Students can adapt a fixed dance to different performing spaces by balancing fit, spacing, audience visibility and safe preparation. They can explain why the same piece needs different decisions in a gym, park and theater.

## Key skills
- Read a venue and identify conditions that affect a performance.
- Adjust formations without changing the dancers' set positions individually.
- Compare the needs of dancers and the view from different audience heights.
- Plan and order warm-up activities for a floor, temperature and movement demands.
- Use choreographer feedback to revise a performance.

## Support at home
- Look at a familiar room together. Discuss where an audience could sit and what could block its view.
- Use six small household objects to represent dancers. Try fitting a wide formation onto a smaller tabletop while keeping space between them.
- Ask your child why a cool outdoor morning and an indoor floor might need different preparation. No physical rehearsal is required.

## A 5-minute start
Tap the gym's floor ring, then Go to Adapt. Inspect Window at spread 1.0 and 1.3. Try the theater's original diagonal at small and large spreads, then use the split diagonal swap. Ask which constraint each change solves.

## Modes
Scout has five rings per venue: surface, audience, obstacle, conditions and exits. Adapt uses the same four eight-count phrases at every stop. Spread is per phrase; Front rotates the formation, Tempo applies to the whole piece, and swaps affect phrases two through four. Warm up adds and orders blocks on a 15-minute planning bar. Arrows are an alternative to dragging.

## Talk questions
- Which part of the piece needed the biggest change at each stop? What evidence supports that?
- Why can more spread improve spacing but make Fit worse?
- Why can a low audience lose floor work? Does raising the level solve every blocked sightline?
- Which warm-up changes affect the risk meter? Which preparation checks still need attention?

## Good to know
Six Lanterns is made up for Curio Class. The lights are simplified rules of thumb for this piece, not a general injury prediction or a venue certification. Fit uses dancer centers with a 0.75 m edge margin, spacing uses a 1.5 m center-to-center minimum, and Seen uses plan sightlines plus eye-height thresholds. The theater's high seats can see over standing dancers; its low seats still need checking.

The safety calculation follows the explicit points in the spec. A complete gym warm-up removes missing-part points, but the two points for hard-floor jumps remain. The gym note allows keeping jumps after Feet and calves plus Rehearse key moves, yet its Safety light stays medium. Swapping jumps for relevés can lower it further.

The warm-up checklist separately checks starting with Pulse raiser, minimum time, Dynamic stretches, Feet and calves when jumping, and avoiding long holds before the show. The specified risk formula does not penalize every failed checklist item. Read both. Over 15 minutes is shown honestly rather than silently dropping a block.

## Standards in action
DA:Pr4: change space and tempo while preserving the fixed piece. DA:Pr5: adapt movement demands and assemble preparation for surface and temperature. DA:Pr6: scout three audience arrangements and revise the performance using their notes.
