## What it teaches
Students identify intervals, tonal keys, cadence types and modulation pivots by reading a score and comparing musical evidence. They distinguish a collection of fitting notes from a phrase that ends on its tonic.

## Key skills
- Count letter names and half steps to identify interval number and quality.
- Use accidentals and a phrase ending to infer a major or minor key.
- Read Roman numerals and the soprano to distinguish cadence types.
- Compare two keys to locate the final shared chord before a modulation.

## Support at home
- Hum a familiar tune together and notice which ending sounds settled. Talk about what makes that note feel like home.
- Sing two nearby notes, then two farther apart. Describe their distance before trying to name it.
- Listen to a song ending and describe whether it sounds finished or as if it needs another phrase.

## A five-minute start
Open Lens and press Play passage. Move the blue lens from C major through G major to D major. Watch the C sharp fit, then ask whether the last note feels like home. Turn on Two lenses and compare D major with a minor lens that fits the notes. Enter Workout and choose Set 1, rep 1 to make the decision yourself.

## Modes
Lens contains all 17 original passages with no grading. Arrow buttons, Left and Right on a focused lens, or dragging the key name move one step around the circle of fifths. Major / Minor changes the scale family. The faint blue key signature is a reference belonging to the lens; the score itself has no key signature and writes accidentals on notes.

Workout has 8 interval reps, 6 key reps, 6 cadence reps and 5 pivot reps. A filled dot means solved without hints; a hollow dot means solved with hints. Progress stays on the device. Clear progress asks for confirmation. Compact displays open the four rows using Reps.

Play passage plays a soft synthesized piano. A chord or note tap plays it alone; in the pivot set it chooses the chord after both keys are locked. Moving a lens does not stop playback. Answers, rep changes and chord taps stop the passage immediately.

After a correct check, green frames mark the deciding interval notes, home note or chord, final cadence pair, or pivot. Rings pick out the upper notes for an interval, key ending or cadence. The keyboard shows that checked evidence. Changing a key or answer removes the checked frames; saved dots remain a record of earlier work.

## Talk questions
- Why can every note fit more than one key? What additional evidence does the ending provide?
- What does the soprano contribute to a perfect authentic cadence?
- Why is an earlier shared chord not necessarily the modulation pivot?
- How does changing the harmonic reading change your interpretation of a phrase ending?

## Good to know
This is a reading and analysis tool; it does not compose music. Spelling matters: F sharp and G flat are different written notes here. A minor lens permits natural minor plus raised sixth and seventh melody notes, but its chord vocabulary is exactly the one in the spec. A red ring and an x mark an outside note, so color is never the only signal. A question mark means the chord has no numeral in this lens's vocabulary.

All chords are in root position in the bass. Upper voices use the nearest permitted close voicing below the soprano. Cadence distinctions are those in Open Music Theory. Perfect authentic: V to I with root position and tonic soprano; imperfect authentic lacks at least one condition; half ends on V; plagal is IV to I; deceptive is V to vi or VI in minor. Pivot means the last shared chord immediately before the first new-key-only chord in these examples. A later chord may fit both and still be too late.

NCAS MU:Re7 is practiced through structural analysis of notes and harmony. MU:Re8 is practiced by relating the audible phrase ending to harmonic function and the soprano's role. Teachers can extend this with the interpretation questions above.
