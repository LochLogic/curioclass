arc: explore-first
minutes: 45
focus: 8.EE.A
goal: I can read and write very large and very small numbers in scientific notation, and estimate how many times bigger one thing is than another.
materials: The tool on the projector and on student devices. Notebooks.
grouping: Whole class for the hook, then pairs on devices.
---
## Before class

- Open **Zoom** at about the size of a person: 10⁰ meters.
- Sizes are typical published values, rounded. The "you" figure is a typical 13-year-old at 1.6 m.

## Hook (5 min)

1. Press **▶ Tour** and fly from a proton to the observable universe.
2. "How many powers of ten did we cross?" (About 42.) "How would you write the size of an atom without a hundred zeros?"

## Explore (15 min)

1. Pairs press **÷10 Smaller** a few times and read each card: the size in scientific notation, written out, and the units.
2. Pairs zoom to five things they choose and record each size both ways.
3. Pairs open **Compare** and pick two things. The card divides by subtracting exponents. "How many of the smaller thing would line up to match the bigger one?"
4. Pairs try **Place it** and drag the pin for ten things. Aim for three stars.

## Make sense (10 min)

1. On the board: "A negative exponent means a small number, not a negative number. Where do you see that on the Zoom slider?"
2. Work a comparison together: 8 × 10² ÷ 5 × 10⁻³. Round each coefficient to one digit, then subtract the exponents. (About 2 × 10⁵.)
3. "The jump from a proton to an atom is empty on purpose. Why?" (Atoms are almost all empty space.)

## Apply (10 min)

1. Pairs open **Write it**. They slide the decimal point to turn standard form into scientific notation and back. The exponent counts the hops.
2. Rule check: "Hop left, the exponent goes up. Hop right, it goes down. Does the value change?"
3. Pairs who finish pick the comparison that surprised them most and explain it.

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: "Write 0.00042 m and 93,000,000 mi in scientific notation. The universe is about 10²⁷ m and a proton about 10⁻¹⁵ m. How many times bigger is the universe?" (10⁴².) Cards that call 10⁻¹⁵ a negative size show who needs the slider again.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** stay between 10⁻³ and 10⁶ at first.
- **Support:** in **Write it**, say each hop out loud while sliding.
- **Stretch:** "Why do scientists use scientific notation instead of writing all the zeros?"
- **Stretch:** find two things in **Compare** that are almost exactly 1,000 times apart.

## Short on time

- **▶ Tour** in **Zoom** (4 min).
- **Compare** two things (6 min), then **Write it** (5 min).
