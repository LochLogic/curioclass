# Hallway Art Museum

## One idea
Every artwork needs a home that fits it: the class picks pieces for a theme, gives each one the home its material needs (a frame, a stand, a glass case, a wall rod or the window) and slides it to a height, then Kid eyes drops the view to a kindergartner's eye level and shows that art hung the grown-up way sails over small heads.

## Not like
- virtual-gallery (9-12, builder): a visitor walk tests order, sight lines and label length for a portfolio. Here there is no walk and no order: the test is material (what home a piece needs) and height (who can see it).
- art-passport (k-2, explorer): visits real artworks in faraway museums. Here the art is kids' work and the class runs the museum.
- weekend-drawing-share (k-2, studio, this batch): makes one drawing. Here the work is choosing, preparing and showing finished pieces.
- field-trip-slideshow (k-2, builder, this batch): presents media to families with crew jobs. Here the show is objects in a hallway and the roles are museum jobs.
- stage-blocker (6-8, builder): places actors for sight lines from seats. Here the sight line is one child's eye height, and the pieces are art, not people.

## Who and how
- Grade K-2, visual arts, module "Showing Our Art".
- Driver: teacher on a projector or smartboard; the class chooses, explains and acts out the museum jobs. 20 to 30 minutes, or two short sessions (Choose and Set up, then Opening day).
- Standards: NCAS VA:Pr4 (select work for presentation), VA:Pr5 (prepare and present: how art is displayed and protected), VA:Pr6 (convey meaning: what a museum does and the people who work there), VA:Re9 (say why a piece fits).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons (`.seg`, icon plus word): `Choose` (cart icon, default), `Set up` (hammer icon), `Opening day` (ribbon icon). All targets 60 px or more, icon plus word.

**Choose.** Focus: the art cart, 12 pieces in a 4 by 3 grid of big tiles on a wooden cart, under a theme banner. Theme buttons across the top: `Animals` (paw), `Weather` (sun and cloud), `Things That Grow` (sprout). Under the cart: five empty spot cards ("1" to "5") that fill with chosen pieces. Tap a tile: it grows into a look-closer card (the piece big, its name, what it is made of) with `Pick it` (gold) and `Back to cart`. After `Pick it`, three "Why?" chips appear: `It shows the theme`, `I like the colors`, `It tells a story` (one can be lit per piece; tapping a filled spot card reopens its card to change it). `Set up` is enabled when 5 pieces are picked.

**Set up.** Focus: the hallway wall, side view, filling the stage: five bays across a painted school hallway, bay 3 with a tall window. A height ruler on the left wall edge, drawn in code: 0 to 240 cm, with two lines: "Grown-up eyes" (158 cm, a tall eye icon) and "Kid eyes" (105 cm, a small eye icon).
- The five chosen pieces wait in a tray under the wall. Drag a piece onto a bay (or tap it, then tap the bay).
- A bay with a piece shows the home tray beside it: `Frame`, `Stand`, `Glass case`, `Wall rod`, `Window` (the last only in bay 3). Tap one to give the piece that home.
- Height: framed, rod and window pieces drag up and down on the wall (5 cm steps, centers 40 to 200 cm); a big up and down arrow pair (60 px) beside the selected piece does the same. Stand and Glass case pieces take `Low`, `Middle`, `Tall` buttons instead.
- New pieces arrive centered at 145 cm, where grown-up museums hang art.
- Bar: `Kid eyes` (toggle, small eye), `Check the hallway` (gold, clipboard), `Back to cart`.
- Kid eyes on: everything outside the kid view band (70 to 140 cm) fades to 35% and blurs 3 px, a soft kid-height eye marker sits on the ruler, and pieces whose center is outside the band show a little "too high" or "too low" tag with an arrow.
- Check the hallway: one row per bay with three badges: Theme (paw, sun or sprout, or a question mark), Home (a check, or a note), Height (a check, or an arrow). Notes are in Content. When all 15 badges are checks, a ribbon unrolls over the wall: "Ready for opening day!"

**Opening day.** Focus: the finished hallway (as set up), with a role card on the right. Role buttons: `Curator` (clipboard), `Guard` (velvet rope), `Guide` (pointer), `Visitor` (ticket). Tap a role: its painted prop appears on the card with its two prompts, one at a time (`Next prompt`). `Guide` and `Visitor` prompts make a bay glow (cycling through the five with `Next piece`). There is no checking here: the class plays the jobs.

**Home rules** (shown as notes when a home does not fit):
- Paper art (flat) in a Stand or Glass case: "Flat paper art hangs flat. Try a Frame."
- A 3D piece in a Frame or on a Wall rod: "This piece is not flat. Try a Stand or a Glass case."
- Small clay or rocks on a Stand: "It is small and could fall or break. A Glass case keeps it safe."
- A big piece (Robot) in a Glass case: "It is too big for the case. Try a Stand."
- A woven or hanging piece in a Frame: "It hangs and moves. Try a Wall rod."
- Tissue Paper Tree off the window: "Light can't shine through it here. Try the window." (Frame is "also fine": the Home badge is a check with a small sun note.)
- Anything but the tree in the window: "It blocks the window light. Try another bay."

**Heights.** Wall pieces: the center is where it is dragged. Stand tops: Low 30, Middle 60, Tall 90 cm; Glass case stand tops: Low 50, Middle 75, Tall 100 cm; the piece's center is the top plus half its size. Kid view band: centers 70 to 140 cm. Height notes: above 140 "Too high for kid eyes. Slide it down."; below 70 "Too low. Slide it up." Example: the Robot on Low is centered at 60 cm (too low), on Middle at 90 and Tall at 120 (both fine); a Glass case on Low is 57.5 (too low).

**Theme badge.** A piece that has the theme gets a check with the theme icon. A piece without it gets a question mark and "Does this fit our theme? Tell why, or choose another piece." until the class taps a reason chip for it (`I like the colors` or `It tells a story`, on its look-closer card or its spot card); then it gets a check with a speech bubble icon ("We told why"). Curators may include a piece for a reason; they just have to say it.

**Role prompts** (two each):
- Curator: "Curators choose the art. Tell the visitors our theme." / "Point to one piece. Tell why we chose it."
- Guard: "Guards keep the art safe. Show the rules: walk, look with your eyes, hands to yourself." / "Someone is running in the museum! Show what a guard says."
- Guide: "Guides tell visitors about the art. Tell one thing you see in the glowing piece." / "Tell what it is made of, and how you think the artist made it."
- Visitor: "Visitors look closely. Look at the glowing piece for five quiet seconds." / "Ask the guide one question. Then tell which piece you like best, and why."

## Interactions in detail
- Choose: 5 picks at most; a sixth `Pick it` says "The hallway has five spots. Tap a spot card, then Put back." A filled spot card opens the piece's card with `Put back` (returns it to the cart) and its reason chips. Changing the theme keeps picks and updates their theme badges.
- Set up: dropping a piece on a filled bay swaps the two. The `Window` home is shown only in bay 3; a piece in bay 3 can still use other homes. Moving a piece out of bay 3 with the Window home resets its home to none.
- Kid eyes changes the view only, never the pieces; it stays on while dragging, so the class can slide a piece into the clear band and watch it sharpen.
- Check the hallway can be pressed any time; badges for a bay with no home show "Pick a home".
- The ribbon is an event (spoken every time all 15 badges turn to checks after a change). A change after that hides the ribbon until the next check.
- Opening day uses the hallway as it is, even if not every check passed; empty bays show a "Coming soon" card.
- Reset: `Back to cart` keeps the hallway. A `Start over` button on the Choose cart (with `Yes, start over` and `Keep it` buttons) clears picks and the hallway. Nothing is saved between visits.
- Tap while anything moves (the ribbon unrolling, a piece sliding into a bay, the Kid eyes fade) acts at once: the animation jumps to its end.
- Motion off: pieces jump into bays, Kid eyes switches without a fade, the ribbon appears whole. Keyboard: arrows move the selected piece 5 cm, Tab order is tray, bays, home tray, bar.

## Sources
- National Core Arts Standards, Visual Arts, grades K to 2 (VA:Pr4, VA:Pr5, VA:Pr6, VA:Re9): https://www.nationalartsstandards.org
- Museums hang art with its center about 57 to 60 inches (145 to 152 cm) from the floor, near average adult eye height: Smithsonian Institution, Guide to Exhibition Design / common gallery practice, summarized in "The 57 Inch Rule", Artwork Archive (https://www.artworkarchive.com/blog/the-57-inch-rule). The tool uses 145 cm as the grown-up default and 158 cm for grown-up eyes (US adult mean height about 169 cm, CDC NHANES 2015 to 2018, eyes about 11 cm below the top of the head).
- Kid eyes 105 cm: CDC growth charts, 50th percentile stature at age 6 is about 115 cm (boys 115.7, girls 115.0; https://www.cdc.gov/growthcharts/clinical_charts.htm), less about 10 cm to the eyes. This is a rounded classroom estimate, labeled "kid eyes", not a measurement.
- Fragile and small objects in cases, flat works framed, textiles hung from rods: National Park Service Museum Handbook, Part I, chapter 7 (exhibits) (https://www.nps.gov/museum/publications/MHI/mushbkI.html).
- Checks run (Python, 2026-10-29): kid view band 105 plus or minus 35 cm at a 120 cm viewing distance is plus or minus 16.3 degrees, band 70 to 140 cm. 57 inches is 144.78 cm, so the 145 cm default fails the band (as intended). Stand centers: Robot Low 60 (too low), Middle 90, Tall 120 (fine). Case centers for a 15 cm piece: Low 57.5 (too low), Middle 82.5, Tall 107.5 (fine). All five themes and homes in the piece table were walked: each theme has exactly 4 matching pieces (Animals: 1, 2, 8, 9; Weather: 1, 3, 7, 12; Things That Grow: 4, 5, 9, 10), so five bays always hold at least one piece the class must argue for (the "Does this fit our theme? Tell why" note), which is on purpose. Contrast ratios as listed.
