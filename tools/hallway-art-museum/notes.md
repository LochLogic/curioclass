## What it teaches

Children choose art for a theme, explain their choices, and match an artwork's material to a way of displaying it.
They adjust the display for a child's view and act out the jobs that help a museum share and protect art.

## Key skills

- Look closely at materials and details in an artwork.
- Choose a group of pieces and explain why they belong together.
- Find a display that supports and protects each piece.
- Change the height so children can see the art.
- Take turns as a curator, guard, guide, and visitor.

## Support at home

- Choose three drawings or small objects together. Ask your child what connects them and why each belongs.
- Look at a drawing from your child's eye height. Find a place where both of you can see it comfortably.
- Take turns introducing a favorite object and being a visitor who asks a question. Nothing to buy or print.

## A five-minute start

Pick Animals and open Fox in the Snow. Tap Pick it and a reason. Choose four more works, including one that
does not show animals. Explain its colors or story. Tap Set up, tap a tray piece, and tap a bay.
Give a paper work a Frame, then turn on Kid eyes. The new center is 145 cm, so it is too high.
Tap Down until it becomes clear. The class has made its first museum decision.

## Modes

**Choose:** twelve original artworks sit on the cart. Five can be chosen. A matching subject earns its Theme
check. A different subject can belong when the class chooses I like the colors or It tells a story and explains
the reason aloud. It shows the theme alone does not make an unrelated work match. Theme changes keep all picks.
Tap a filled spot to change a reason or Put back. There are only four literal matches for each theme on purpose.

**Set up:** tap a tray piece, then a bay, or drag it. To select a piece already displayed, tap its tray button.
Dropping a selected displayed piece on another filled bay swaps them. The center starts at 145 cm.
Frame, Wall rod, and Window pieces move in 5 cm steps, from 40 to 200 cm. Up and Down arrows work too.
Stand and Glass case heights use Low, Middle, Tall. Turn on Kid eyes to see which centers fall outside 70 to 140 cm.
The view changes; the artwork positions do not. Check the hallway shows Theme, Home, and Height for each bay.
All fifteen checks earn the opening ribbon. A change hides it until the next successful check.

**Opening day:** use the museum as it is. It does not require every check. Empty bays say Coming soon.
Choose a job and act out its prompt. Next prompt alternates the two ideas. Guide and Visitor use Next piece
to highlight one bay at a time; the gold label names that bay's artwork. An empty bay gives directions to
find or choose art. There is no score, timer, or judgment of the roleplay.

## Talk together

- Which detail connects this piece to our theme? Why could a different piece belong too?
- What might fall, tear, or break in that home? What would help?
- Who can see our museum now? What changed when we lowered the art?
- What does each museum job help visitors do?

## Good to know

This is teacher led, with a solo path that still works through the on-screen controls and Read.
Kindergarten can name, choose, and lower a piece with a partner; grade 1 can explain a material and its home;
grade 2 can compare alternatives and justify including an unexpected piece. No student work is stored between visits.
Sound starts on. Read speaks the current card, check notes, role prompt, or next-action hint. Narration currently
uses browser speech; this build intentionally contains no recorded audio. Motion removes movement without changing choices.
Artwork cards, hallway checks, and Start over each have an accessible Read button inside the open card.

The eye heights are rounded classroom estimates, not measurements of individual children. Kid eyes is 105 cm;
the 70 to 140 cm center band is an illustrative viewing band, not a museum accessibility standard. It checks centers,
not every edge of a tall piece. The adult-eye line is 158 cm and the new-art center is 145 cm; these are different ideas.
Stand tops are 30, 60, 90 cm. Glass case stand tops are 50, 75, 100 cm. Centers add half the artwork's height.
The Robot's Stand centers are 60, 90, 120 cm. A 15 cm clay piece's case centers are 57.5, 82.5, 107.5 cm.

Clay Turtle and Little Clay Pot can use a Stand, with a safety note. Their best home is a Glass case.
Ladybug Rocks require the Glass case. The tree's Frame is accepted with a light note; Window is its best home.
Only bay 3 has the window. Moving a Window piece out of it removes the home.
For a low Stand or Glass case, the check names Tall and Middle rather than asking children to drag it.
Read the check notes as invitations to discuss protection, not as universal rules for every real museum object.
