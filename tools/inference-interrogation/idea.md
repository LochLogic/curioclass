# Inference Interrogation (idea card)

- Module: ela-5-quoting-and-inferring, grade 5. Codes: RL.5.1
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: The student solves a short mystery story by answering the detective's questions, and every answer only counts when they quote the exact line that proves it, marking whether the line says it outright or lets them infer it, so they learn the difference between what a text says and what it shows.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a mystery story in four short chapters ("The Case of the Missing Trophy" at a school science fair) and a detective's notebook with the first question: "Who was the last person in the gym?"
  - Core loop: after each chapter, answer two or three questions by dragging an exact quote into the notebook (paraphrases are bounced: "Quote it exactly"), then tag the answer stated or inferred and, for inferences, write the "so" (She had chalk on her sleeve, so she had been near the board). Wrong quotes still show what they do prove.
  - The aha or the end state: the accusation: name the culprit and back it with three quotes, at least two of them inferences; a red herring that only feels right gets exposed when its quote proves something else.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second case with harder inferences.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.5.1: quoting accurately to explain what the text says outright and what it lets the reader infer, at every question and in the accusation.
- Not like: eyewitness-vs-reporter (grade 4, comparing two accounts); main-idea-pinboard (pinning proof for a main idea); question-cube (k-2 question words).
- Art: people (illustrated) suspects and generated scenes of the school, because the case needs faces to suspect.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Solve a short mystery story by answering the detective's questions with exact quotes, tagging each as stated or inferred; the final accusation needs three quotes, and a red herring falls apart when its quote proves something else.
- Flags: none
