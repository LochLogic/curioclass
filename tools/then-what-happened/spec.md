# Then What Happened?

## One idea
A listener who only knows what you tell them: you narrate a wordless comic strip one panel at a time, and beside the real strip the listener redraws the story from your sentences alone, so a skipped panel shows up as a blank square with "Then what happened?", a missing or wrong time word puts their panels in the wrong order, and a verb in the wrong tense makes them ask whether it is happening right now.

## Not like
- bedtime-retell (eld K-2, not built): a puppet hears a story retold beginning, middle and end with picture prompts. Here the student narrates a story they see for the first time, panel by panel, and the listener's redrawn strip checks order, gaps and tense, not just three parts.
- time-machine-talk (eld 3-5, not built): describes past scenes, and the machine runs on correct verb forms. Here past tense is one of four story features (orientation, sequence, a complication, an ending) and the payoff is a story someone else can follow.
- cause-and-effect-rewind (eld 3-5, not built): links steps with because and so. Here the links are time words (then, suddenly, before that, in the end) and what they do to order.
- summary-scaffold (eld 6-8, not built): shortens an informational text. Here the student tells a story at full length.
- pacing-strip (ela 9-12): tags a finished scene by how much time passes per sentence. Here the student is still building the sentences, with frames, and the listener checks only whether the story can be followed.
- personal-essay-studio (ela 11): zooms a personal essay into close-ups. Here My story is short, frame-supported, and checks form only.
- idiom-island (eld 6-8, built): a map of idioms with literal and real pictures. Here no idioms; the art is a story strip.

## Who and how
- ELD grades 6 to 12 (WIDA grade clusters 6-8 and 9-12). Module: Telling Stories and Experiences (Language for Language Arts).
- Driver: a student alone on a laptop; pairs can share one screen and take turns telling panels. 15 to 30 minutes.
- Standards: WIDA.ELD-2 (Language for Language Arts) through the strips' story language; WIDA.Narrate taught in full: orient the listener (who, when, where in the first sentence), develop the events in order with time words and past-tense verbs, show the complication (Suddenly, But then), and close with a resolution (In the end). Each feature is a check the listener makes, so each is practiced, not just named.
- Help setting (top of the panel): **Lots of help**, **Some help**, **Little help**. Default Some help. Teacher notes map them to WIDA levels 1-2, 3-4 and 5-6 and say that grade does not change the content: a grade 6 and a grade 12 newcomer both start where their English is. Supports fade as help goes down (frames, then word banks, then picture cues).
- Topic line: "telling a story in order".
- tool.json: driver "students", minutes "15-30", modes ["Tell a strip", "My story", "Word wall"], grades_label "Grades 6-12". Kit starter only.
- Content notes line: none needed (everyday school stories).

## Screens and modes
Header: title "Then What Happened?"; mode buttons **Tell a strip** (opens first), **My story**, **Word wall**; Motion, Sound, Read. Panel: Help setting, the hint, the tip.

### Tell a strip (opens first)
- Strip picker: three cards, **The Chain**, **The Cake**, **The Robot**, each with its first panel as a thumbnail.
- Stage, top row: the real strip, six panels. Bottom row: **The listener's version**, six empty pencil-sketch frames, and a listener drawn in code as a notebook with two eyes and a pencil (no person), whose face is one of four states: listening, nodding, confused (a "?"), surprised (on a complication told with a surprise word).
- Tapping a panel in the top row opens the sentence builder under the strips, for that panel:
  - Lots of help: a full frame with four slots, `[time word] [who] [verb] [the rest].` Each slot has chips: the time words for the panel's job, the who chips, three verb chips (right past form, base form, a made-up regular form like "rided"), and two or three "rest" chips. Each chip has a small picture icon. **Hear it** reads the sentence as built.
  - Some help: the time word and who slots keep chips; the verb slot is a text box with the base form shown ("ride → ___"); the rest is typed, with a word bank behind **Words**.
  - Little help: one text box per panel. **Words** opens the time-word list and the panel's word bank.
- **Tell it** adds the sentence to the story list in the panel (numbered in the order told) and the listener draws: the panel's sketch appears in the listener row in the place the sentence puts it (see Interactions). Each sentence in the list has **Edit** and **Remove**; the list can be reordered by drag or with ↑ ↓ buttons.
- **Read my story** reads the whole story aloud in order, lighting each sentence.
- The listener's notebook keeps four check marks, each a word plus an icon, never color alone: **Who and when**, **In order**, **Surprise**, **Ending**. Each is filled when the story meets it (see Interactions).
- When all six panels are told, in order, in the past, with all four checks: the listener nods, the two rows match, and **Next strip** appears. No points or stars.

### My story
- A note at the top: "Pick a story you want to tell. You don't have to share anything private. Your story stays on this device."
- Six prompt cards (see Content). Tapping one opens a strip of empty moment boxes (four to start, up to eight with **Add a moment**).
- Each moment box takes one sentence, with the same three help levels as Tell a strip, minus the panel chips: Lots of help has a frame per box with time-word chips and the verb box shows a base-to-past helper ("I typed: go. Past: went"); Some and Little help as above.
- **Plan in any language**: a side note box. "Write your ideas in any language first. The listener only reads English." Saved on this device only, never checked.
- **Tell it** runs the listener's form checks (see Interactions). The listener cannot know what really happened, and says so in the tip: "I can't check what happened. I can check that I can follow it."
- **Save** keeps the story in this browser; **Clear** (asks first) deletes it. **Copy story** copies it as plain text.

### Word wall
- Five columns of time words by job: **Start** (when it began), **Next**, **Surprise**, **At the same time / before**, **End**. Tapping a word reads it and shows one example sentence from the strips.
- An irregular verb list, base → past (see Content), tappable to hear both forms, with a filter box. A note: "Most verbs add -ed. These don't."
- Three spelling rules for regular past tense, each with two examples from the strips: add -ed (laughed, finished); double the last letter after a short vowel (stopped, tripped, wrapped); change y to i (carried, tried).

## Content
Strips are wordless, six panels each, drawn as one sheet per strip (see Art). Each panel: picture, its job (start, next, surprise, end), the target sentence, verb chips, rest chips. The characters' names appear on the strip card ("Dev", "Lina", "Yuna and Marcus").

The Chain (Dev):
1. start: "One morning, Dev rode his bike to school." Verb chips: rode / ride / rided. Rest: "his bike to school", "to the store".
2. next: "Halfway there, the chain came off." Verb: came / come / comed. Rest: "off", "the chain".
3. next: "He tried to fix it, and his hands got black with grease." Verbs: tried / try / tryed; got / get / getted.
4. surprise: "Just then, a classmate stopped and held the bike." Verbs: stopped / stop / stoped; held / hold / holded.
5. next: "Together, they put the chain back on." Verb: put / putting / putted.
6. end: "In the end, they arrived late, but they laughed all the way in." Verbs: arrived / arrive / arriving; laughed / laugh / laughing.

The Cake (Lina):
1. start: "On Friday night, Lina baked a cake for the bake sale." Verb: baked / bake / baking.
2. next: "The next morning, she put it in a box." Verb: put / puts / putted.
3. next: "Then she carried it to school on the bus." Verb: carried / carry / carryed.
4. surprise: "Suddenly, she tripped on the steps." Verb: tripped / trip / triped.
5. next: "The cake fell upside down in the box." Verb: fell / fall / falled.
6. end: "In the end, her friends cut it into squares and sold every piece." Verbs: cut / cutting / cutted; sold / sell / selled.

The Robot (Yuna and Marcus):
1. start: "Last Saturday, Yuna and Marcus brought their robot to the contest." Verb: brought / bring / bringed.
2. surprise: "In the practice run, the robot stopped in the middle of the course." Verb: stopped / stops / stoped. (The surprise comes early in this strip, on purpose.)
3. next: "Marcus found a loose wire." Verb: found / find / finded.
4. next: "Yuna wrapped it with tape." Verb: wrapped / wrap / wraped.
5. next: "In the real run, the robot finished the course." Verb: finished / finish / finishing.
6. end: "In the end, they won second place." Verb: won / win / winned.

Time words by job (chips and Word wall): Start: One morning, One day, Last Saturday, Last week, Yesterday, On Friday night. Next: Then, Next, After that, A few minutes later, The next morning, Soon, Halfway there. Surprise: Suddenly, But then, Just then, Unfortunately. At the same time / before: While, Meanwhile, At the same time, Before that, Earlier. End: In the end, Finally, At last.

Prompt cards (My story): "A time something broke at the worst moment." "A time you learned to do something new." "A game, match or contest you remember." "A time you cooked or made something." "A funny mistake." "A day the weather changed your plans."

Irregular verbs (base → past), used by the checker and Word wall: be → was/were, become → became, begin → began, break → broke, bring → brought, build → built, buy → bought, catch → caught, choose → chose, come → came, cut → cut, do → did, draw → drew, drink → drank, drive → drove, eat → ate, fall → fell, feel → felt, find → found, fly → flew, forget → forgot, get → got, give → gave, go → went, grow → grew, have → had, hear → heard, hide → hid, hit → hit, hold → held, hurt → hurt, keep → kept, know → knew, leave → left, lend → lent, let → let, lose → lost, make → made, mean → meant, meet → met, pay → paid, put → put, read → read, ride → rode, ring → rang, run → ran, say → said, see → saw, sell → sold, send → sent, shut → shut, sing → sang, sit → sat, sleep → slept, speak → spoke, spend → spent, stand → stood, swim → swam, take → took, teach → taught, tell → told, think → thought, throw → threw, understand → understood, wake → woke, wear → wore, win → won, write → wrote.

## Interactions in detail
- Panel match (Lots and Some help): the sentence belongs to the panel it was built from. Little help and My story: typed text; in Tell a strip it is matched to the panel whose verb (any form) and one rest keyword it contains; no match asks "Which panel is this? Tap it." and the student taps.
- Tense check: the sentence's main verb must be a past form: a word from the irregular list's past column, or a word ending in -ed that is not in a small not-a-verb list (red, bed, need, speed, seed, shed, feed, bleed, proceed). A base form or -ing form makes the listener confused: "Is this happening now? Tell it in the past." with the base → past pair shown ("ride → rode"). A made-up form (rided, falled, putted) gets "Close! This verb is irregular: fall → fell." Spelling slips on regular verbs (stoped, carryed) get the rule card from Word wall.
- Placement: the listener places each told panel after the panel told before it, unless its time word is a before word (Before that, Earlier), which places it one step earlier, or a same-time word (While, Meanwhile, At the same time), which stacks it with the previous panel. Sentences with no time word are placed next. If the listener's order differs from the real order, the two panels out of place get a "?" and the listener asks "Wait. Did ___ happen before ___?" naming both events in a few words. The student fixes the order by moving the sentence in the list or changing its time word; the listener redraws.
- Gaps: whenever told panels skip one (1, 2, 4 told), the listener row shows an empty frame at 3 with "Then what happened?" in a speech bubble. A gap at the end (no panel 6) shows only after **Tell it** on the last told sentence.
- Checks: Who and when: sentence 1 has a Start time word and a who (a name or a pronoun with a name in the strip card). In order: the listener's order matches the real one with no gaps. Surprise: the strip's surprise panel is told with a Surprise word (Suddenly, But then, Just then, Unfortunately); without one the listener says "Was that a surprise?" once, as a nudge. Ending: the last panel is told with an End word.
- Fading: after three panels in a row told with no listener question at the current level, the tip offers "Try with less help?" with **Yes** and **Not now**. The tool never lowers help by itself; the student can raise it at any time.
- My story checks (form only): at least four moments; sentence 1 opens with a time word; every sentence has a past-tense verb (same rule); at least one Surprise word somewhere after sentence 1; the last sentence has an End word; "and then" used more than twice gets a gentle note with three other Next words. Each missing feature gets one listener question; the listener never judges the story's content.
- Reset: switching strips clears the story list for that strip after a confirm if it has sentences. Mode change calls `V.hush()`.
- Storage `then-what-happened:v1` (try/catch): the help setting, each strip's sentences, the My story text and the plan note. **Clear** deletes all of it.
- Sizes: wide layouts keep both strip rows and the builder visible at 1366x768; at 1000x625 and below the two rows shrink to three panels per row with a **Panels 1-3 / 4-6** toggle; at 760 the panel goes under the stage with the builder first; at 375 one strip row at a time with a **Real strip / Listener** switch, builder pinned under it. Panel images are at least 120 px wide at 680x425. No horizontal scroll.

## Voice
Bank voice `casual-peer` (Zubenelgenubi), style "Say casually and naturally, like a friendly teenager listening to a friend's story." The nearest ELD tools use the old Puck and Kore voices; no other eld-hs tool uses it. `Curio.voice`, `scope: () => state.mode`. Sentences the student builds are read with the same voice only when recorded lines exist; otherwise the browser voice reads them (Hear it, Read my story).
- w-strip (how): "Pick a strip. Tap the first panel, then build a sentence about it."
- built (how, first Tell it): "Nice. Now tap the next panel and tell me what happened."
- gap (event, each time a gap appears; stale when the gap is filled): "Wait. Then what happened?"
- order (event, each order mix-up; stale when fixed): "Hmm. Did that happen first? Move the sentence, or change the time word."
- tense (event, each present or -ing verb; stale when edited): "Is this happening now? Tell it in the past."
- irregular (event, each made-up form): "Close! This verb is irregular. Check the past form."
- surprise-nudge (how): "Was that a surprise? Try Suddenly or But then."
- ending-nudge (how): "Is that the end? Try In the end or Finally."
- done (event, strip complete): "I can see the whole story. Want to try the next strip?"
- less-help (how): "You told three panels with no questions. Try with less help?"
- w-mine (how): "Pick a prompt. Tell me your story, one moment in each box. Then press Tell it."
- mine-ok (event): "I could follow that from start to end."
- w-wall (how): "Tap a word to hear it. Use these when you tell a story."

## Done when
- The Chain told in order with the target sentences fills all four checks, the listener nods and the rows match.
- Telling panels 1, 2 and 4 shows an empty frame at 3 with "Then what happened?"; telling 3 fills it.
- "Before that, the chain came off." told after panel 3 places panel 2 before 3 and clears the "?"; told as "Then" after panel 3 puts the "?" on panels 2 and 3.
- "Dev ride his bike" gets the tense question with "ride → rode"; "rided" gets the irregular note; "carryed" gets the y to i rule.
- The Robot's surprise panel 2 told without a Surprise word gives one nudge, then nothing more for that panel.
- My story with three sentences and no End word asks for more moments and an ending; four past-tense sentences with a Start word, a Surprise word and In the end pass.
- The fade offer appears after three clean panels and never changes the help level unless Yes is pressed.
- Save, reload, Clear (with confirm) work; the plan note is never checked.
- Each how line is spoken once in five actions in a row; event lines repeat when their event repeats.
- No horizontal scroll at any listed size; at 375 the Real strip / Listener switch and the builder work.
