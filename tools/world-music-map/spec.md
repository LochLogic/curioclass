# World Music Map

## One idea
The piano is not the world's measuring stick: every stop on the map draws its music on two tools you can see, a pitch ruler where that tradition's notes fall against the piano keys (a maqam note between two keys, gamelan's five near-even steps) and a time circle where its rhythm cycle comes around (a 12-pulse bell, a 16-beat tala with an empty wave on 9), so "world music" turns into many different rule sets you can lay side by side.

## Not like
- orchestra-zoo (K-2, built, same family): the four Western orchestra families and real recordings, guessing by sound. Here ten traditions on a map, sorted by the museum system that fits every instrument (strings, air, struck body, skin), and the point is tuning and cycles, not naming the instrument.
- art-passport (K-2, art-museum family): a world map of artworks to look at and feel about. Same land outline, different look and purpose: here every pin opens a ruler and a circle you play.
- music-history-radio (this group): one tune re-dressed through Western eras on a radio dial. Here no tune travels; each place keeps its own music and its own rules.
- band-practice (this group): you perform in time with a band. Here you look at and hear cycles; nothing is scored by timing.
- theory-gym, song-form-builder (this group): Western key analysis and song form.
- art-through-time (6-8): visual art threads through time; no sound.
- chord-explorer (6-8): Western triads on a keyboard; here the keyboard is only the reference under the ruler.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, music, module "Music Around the World (Music)".
- Topic line: instruments, scales and rhythm cycles around the world
- Driver: students or teacher (laptops with headphones; or a projector, with the class clapping cycles). 15 to 35 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS MU:Cn11 (relate music to varied contexts and daily life); also MU:Re7 (how music is organized).
- Header: title, topic line, modes **Map** (default), **Compare**, **Where in the world?** (optional), then kit `motion`, `sound`, `read`.
- On every stop card, one line in small type: "Sounds are made in the browser: sketches to show the scale and the cycle, not recordings." and a link **Hear the real thing** (Smithsonian Folkways search for that stop, opens in a new tab).

## Screens and modes

### Map (explore, the default)
- Stage: world map (Equal Earth) with 10 round pins. Panel: `hint`, layer buttons **Instruments**, **Scales**, **Cycles** (one on at a time, default Instruments), the stop card when a stop is open, **Close**.
- Layers change only the pin badges: Instruments shows the instrument class icon (strings, air, struck body, skin; Content); Scales shows notes per octave; Cycles shows pulses per cycle. A small legend explains the current layer.
- Tap a pin: the map pans so the pin sits left, and the stop card opens on the right (below the map under 1000 px wide). Stop card, top to bottom: place and instrument names, photo or drawing, three fact lines, the **pitch ruler**, the **time circle**, buttons **Play scale**, **Play cycle** (loops, becomes **Stop**), **Play phrase**, and the stop's **Try this** toggle (Content).
- **Pitch ruler**: one octave left to right, 0 to 1200 cents, with a drawn piano octave under it (12 keys) as the reference. Each of the stop's notes is a tall mark with its name; a mark that sits off a piano key shows how far, for example "E half-flat: 50 below E". Tap a mark to hear that note (over the drone where the stop has one).
- **Time circle**: the cycle's pulses as dots around a circle, numbered, starting at the top and going clockwise. Each part is one ring (outer ring the main pattern, inner rings the others), its hits filled with that part's shape (circle, square, triangle). Special counts get a label (sam X on 1, khali 0 on 9 for teental; G on 16 for the gong). A hand sweeps around while the cycle plays, and each hit flashes.

### Compare
- Two pickers (or tap two pins). The two rulers stack, notes aligned in cents, with dotted lines down to the piano keys; the two circles sit side by side.
- **Play both scales** (one after the other), **Play both cycles** (together, at the same pulse speed, 200 pulses a minute). A readout: "A 12-pulse cycle and a 16-pulse cycle line up again every 48 pulses." (the least common multiple, worked from the two lengths).

### Where in the world? (optional)
- A mystery stop: its ruler and circle are shown with names hidden, and **Play phrase** plays it. Tap the pin you think it is. Right: the stop card opens. Wrong: "Not there. Look at the ruler: does it use piano keys?" or, after a second miss, "Look at the circle: how many pulses?". The third miss opens the answer. A strip counts "found 3 of 10". No timer, no score beyond the count. **Next mystery** deals a stop not yet found.

## Content

### Instrument classes (Instruments layer icons; the Hornbostel-Sachs system used by museums)
- Strings (chordophone): a vibrating string. Air (aerophone): vibrating air. Struck body (idiophone): the instrument's own body vibrates. Skin (membranophone): a stretched skin.

### The ten stops (lon, lat; instruments and class; image; facts; ruler in cents; cycle; phrase; Try this)
Phrases are short sketches written for Curio Class in each stop's scale and cycle, not traditional pieces; the card says so. Durations in pulses (1 if not given).

1. **Gamelan, Central Java, Indonesia** (110.4, -7.8). Saron (bronze keys over a trough) and gongs: struck body. Image: Met 501374 (Saron Panerus Slendro). Facts: "A gamelan is a set of bronze keys, gongs and drums made and tuned together." "Slendro tuning has five notes in each octave, spaced almost evenly." "No two gamelans are tuned exactly alike." Ruler (an even model, labeled so): 1 at 0, 2 at 240, 3 at 480, 5 at 720, 6 at 960 (Javanese number names). Cycle: lancaran, 16 beats at 80 a minute: kethuk on 1 3 5 7 9 11 13 15, kempul on 6 10 14, kenong on 4 8 12 16, gong on 16. Phrase (saron, one per beat): 2 3 2 1 2 3 5 6 5 3 2 1 3 2 1 2. Try this: **Gongs only**, and the line "The biggest sound, the gong, comes at the end of the cycle, on 16."
2. **Maqam music, Damascus, Syria** (36.3, 33.5). Ud: strings. Image: Met 500996 (Ud, Syrian, late 1800s). Facts: "The ud is a short-necked lute with no frets." "Maqam music is shared across the Arab world." "Maqam Rast has two notes that fall between the piano's keys." Ruler: maqam Rast on C: C 0, D 200, E half-flat 350, F 500, G 700, A 900, B half-flat 1050. Cycle: maqsum, 8 pulses at 200 a minute: dum (low) on 1 and 5, tek (high) on 2, 4 and 7. Phrase (ud): C D E half-flat F G(2) F E half-flat D E half-flat F E half-flat D(2) C(2). Try this: **Piano notes**: E half-flat becomes E and B half-flat becomes B, with "Now it is a plain major scale. Hear the color change?"
3. **Hindustani music, Varanasi, India** (83.0, 25.3). Sitar: strings; tabla: skin; tanpura drone: strings. Images: Met 500750 (Sitar), Met 500712 (Tabla). Facts: "The sitar has movable frets and extra strings that ring by themselves." "A raga is more than a scale: it has its own phrases and mood. Yaman is played in the evening." "Tabla players bend the left drum's pitch with the heel of the hand." Ruler: raga Yaman with Sa on C: Sa 0, Re 200, Ga 400, Ma sharp (tivra Ma) 600, Pa 700, Dha 900, Ni 1100. Cycle: teental, 16 beats at 80 a minute, tabla theka Dha Dhin Dhin Dha, Dha Dhin Dhin Dha, Dha Tin Tin Ta, Ta Dhin Dhin Dha; claps on 1 (sam, X), 5 (2) and 13 (3), a wave on 9 (khali, 0). Phrase (sitar, beats): Ni (below Sa) Re Ga(2) Re Sa(3) Ni (below) Re Ga Ma sharp Pa(4). Try this: **Drone** on or off (tanpura Pa, Sa, Sa, low Sa, repeating), on by default.
4. **Koto, Kyoto, Japan** (135.8, 35.0). Koto: strings. Image: Met 500576 (Koto, 1800s). Facts: "The koto has 13 strings, each over its own movable bridge." "Players move the bridges to retune between pieces." "Picks on the thumb and two fingers pluck the strings." Ruler: the hirajoshi notes on D: D 0, E flat 100, G 500, A 700, B flat 800 (every note on a piano key, but the gaps are 1, 4, 2, 1 and 4 half steps). The drawn koto's 13 strings, low to high: G3 A3 B flat 3 D4 E flat 4 G4 A4 B flat 4 D5 E flat 5 G5 A5 B flat 5. Cycle: 8 beats at 72 a minute (a plain beat; the phrase leaves rests on purpose). Phrase by string number: 7 7 8(2) 7 6 5(2), then 9 8 7(2) 5 4 5(2). Try this: **Move the bridges**: the bridges slide and strings 1 to 13 retune to G3 A3 B3 D4 E4 G4 A4 B4 D5 E5 G5 A5 B5 (a major pentatonic, labeled "a tuning for comparison"); Play phrase plays the same string numbers in the new tuning.
5. **Ewe drumming, Volta Region, Ghana** (0.5, 6.6). Gankogui (iron double bell) and axatse (gourd rattle): struck body; sogo and kidi drums: skin. Image: drawn in code (bell, rattle, two drums). Facts: "The bell plays one 12-pulse pattern, and every drummer keeps their place by it." "The pattern has 7 strokes spread over 4 main beats." "This music is about pattern, not scale." Ruler: two marks only, low bell and high bell (a fifth apart in this sketch). Cycle: 12 pulses at 300 a minute: bell on 1 3 5 6 8 10 12 (in this sketch the low bell on 1, the high bell on the rest); rattle on 1 4 7 10; drum sketch (written for this sketch) on 4 5 10 11. Phrase: the whole cycle twice. Try this: **Hear the four beats**: a soft foot tap on 1 4 7 10, so you hear the bell's 7 strokes against 4 beats.
6. **Mbira, Shona people, Zimbabwe** (31.05, -17.8). Mbira dzavadzimu: struck body (plucked metal keys). Image: drawn in code (wooden board, about 22 metal keys, in a gourd). Facts: "The keys are plucked with both thumbs and the right forefinger." "Shells or bottle caps on the board add a buzz on purpose." "Two players interlock: kushaura leads and kutsinhira fills the gaps." Ruler: G A B C D E F sharp on piano keys, with "Tunings differ from player to player. This sketch uses piano notes so you can follow." Cycle: 48 pulses in 4 quarters of 12 at 360 a minute; kushaura on odd pulses, kutsinhira on even pulses. Phrase: kushaura quarters (6 notes each, on pulses 1 3 5 7 9 11 of each quarter): G B D B G B / A C E C A C / G B D B G B / F sharp A D A F sharp A; kutsinhira on the even pulses: D G B G D G / E A C A E A / D G B G D G / D F sharp A F sharp D F sharp. Try this: **Kushaura**, **Kutsinhira**, **Both**.
7. **Siku panpipes, Lake Titicaca, Peru and Bolivia** (-69.5, -15.8). Siku: air. Image: Met 501326 (Panpipes, Bolivian). Facts: "A siku is split into two rows of pipes, ira and arka." "Two players alternate notes to make one tune." "Sikuri groups play with many pairs at once." Ruler: A 0, C 300, D 500, E 700, G 1000, A (octave) 1200, each mark tagged ira or arka: ira A, D, G; arka C, E, high A, so next-door notes sit on different rows. Cycle: 8 beats at 100 a minute, each hit marked ira or arka by its note. Phrase: A C D E G E D C E D C A(3) then a rest (2). Try this: **Ira**, **Arka**, **Both** (with one row only, the tune has holes).
8. **Son, Santiago de Cuba, Cuba** (-75.8, 20.0). Claves: struck body; bongos: skin; tres (a guitar with three pairs of strings): strings. Image: drawn in code (claves, bongos, tres). Facts: "Claves are two hardwood sticks struck together." "The clave pattern is 3 strokes then 2, and the band fits around it." "Son uses the piano's twelve notes; its rhythm is what makes it son." Ruler: C major on piano keys. Cycle: 16 pulses (two bars of eighths) at 200 a minute: clave 3-2 on 1 4 7 11 13; bass (sketch) on 4 7 12 15; tres (sketch) on 2 4 6 8 10 12 14 16. Phrase (tres, pulses): E G E C D(2) rest(2) G A G E F(2) rest(2). Try this: **3-2** or **2-3** (2-3 clave on 3 5 9 12 15).
9. **Great Highland bagpipe, Scotland** (-4.2, 57.5). Air. Image: Met 501474 (Great Highland Bagpipe). Facts: "The piper blows into a bag that keeps the sound going without a break." "Three drones hold one low note under the tune." "Because the sound never stops, pipers split repeated notes with quick grace notes." Ruler (a model tuned to the drone with simple ratios, labeled so; pipers tune by ear): low G -200, A 0, B 204, C sharp 386, D 498, E 702, F sharp 884, G 1000, high A 1200. Drone: A one and two octaves below the chanter's low A. Cycle: 8 pulses (a march in two bars) at 220 a minute. Phrase: A A B C sharp E(2) C sharp A B B C sharp B A(2) A A. Try this: **Grace notes** on or off (off: repeated notes join into one long sound; on: a 30 ms high G before each repeated note).
10. **Erhu, Beijing, China** (116.4, 39.9). Erhu: strings (bowed). Image: Met 500616 (Erhu, 1800s). Facts: "The erhu has two strings, and the bow hair runs between them." "There is no fingerboard: fingers press the strings in the air, so notes can slide." "The five-note scale gong, shang, jue, zhi, yu matches do, re, mi, sol, la." Ruler: D 0, E 200, F sharp 400, A 700, B 900. Cycle: 8 beats at 72 a minute. Phrase: A B A F sharp E F sharp D(2) E F sharp A B A(4). Try this: **Slides** on or off (on: a 120 ms glide into each note from the one before).

## Interactions in detail
- Map: drag to pan, wheel or **+** and **-** to zoom (1x to 3x). Pins at least 44 px; at 3x labels show place names. Pins are buttons in a list (after the panel controls) in the order above.
- Opening a stop stops any sound. Play cycle loops until Stop, a mode change or another stop. Play phrase plays once over two passes of the cycle's quietest ring (so the phrase sits inside its cycle); for Ghana it plays the ensemble twice.
- Ruler marks: a note within 5 cents of a piano key shows no offset; otherwise the offset in cents to the nearest key, shown as a whole number. Notes are tuned exactly from the cents values (Family engine).
- Try this toggles reset when the stop closes. Drone and Slides are remembered for the session.
- Compare: the readout is worked in code from the two cycle lengths. Same stop twice is not allowed (the second picker skips it).
- Where in the world? cycles through all 10 stops in a shuffled order before repeating.
- Keyboard: Tab reaches layer buttons, then pins; Enter opens; on the ruler Left and Right move between marks and Enter plays one; Space starts or stops Play cycle when the card has focus.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 map left (60%), card right with ruler and circle both visible without scrolling the page (the card scrolls inside itself if needed, buttons first). In the 680x425 preview the map with pins and the Instruments legend; scene text at least 11 px. At 375 wide the card opens full width over the map with a **Back to map** button.

## Family engine
instruments (no family doc yet; this spec holds the full description; orchestra-zoo, K-2, is the built reference). Lift orchestra-zoo's sound engine from its src.html into `kit/instruments.js`: `audio()` with a compressor on the master, `stopAll()` (fade and replace the master), the shared noise buffer, the Karplus-Strong string render (generalized from harp to any pitch and decay), `note(inst, pitch, t, dur)`, `playTune(inst, tune, beat, onNote)`, and the synth hits (snare, kick, triangle, timpani). One change: pitch may be given in cents above a base frequency (`{cents}`), so non-piano tunings are exact (playback rate or oscillator frequency = base x 2^(cents/1200)). New synth voices this tool adds to the engine, each a short recipe: `ks` plucked string with brightness and decay (koto, ud, sitar with a soft waveshaper buzz, tres); `bowed` (sawtooth through a lowpass, 5.5 Hz vibrato, slow attack: erhu); `breath` (sine plus bandpass noise: siku); `reed` (bright square-like wave: bagpipe chanter) and `drone` (sustained sawtooth, lowpass: bagpipe drones, tanpura with a slow pluck cycle); `bronze` (inharmonic partials 1, 2.76, 5.4 with long decay: saron) and `gong` (low partials, slow decay, slight pitch drop); `bell` (two metallic partials: gankogui); `block` (claves); `tabla` (dayan: ringing partials; bayan: low sine with a pitch bend); `hand` (drums: filtered noise plus a sine thump); `lamella` (pluck partials plus a buzz noise: mbira). orchestra-zoo keeps its copy until reworked. Cycles play on the lookahead clock described in band-practice's spec (rhythm engine), at the stop's pulse rate.
