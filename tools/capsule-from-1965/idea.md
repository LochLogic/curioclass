# Capsule From 1965 (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-1-old-objects-as-sources, grade 1. Codes: D2.His.4.K-2, D2.His.10.K-2, D2.His.11.K-2, D2.His.12.K-2
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Open a time capsule a first-grade class buried under the school in 1965 and pull out a photo, a letter, a lunch ticket and a toy catalog page; find who made each, when and where from clues on the object itself, ask your own questions, and compare what those kids wanted with what you want.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a dented metal box being lifted out of the ground at the school's new playground. "Open the lid and pick something up."
  - Core loop: each object has a magnifier: find the maker (signature, printed store name), the date (stamp, postmark) and the place (school name, town). A question jar lets the student pick or build questions ("Why was lunch so cheap?"), and some answers are revealed by other objects.
  - The aha or the end state: the letter from a 1965 kid says what they hoped the future would be; the student compares those wishes and favorite toys with their own on a then/now page and sees how sources let us learn about people we can't meet.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class writes a reply letter for a new capsule.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.4.K-2: compare the 1965 kids' wishes and views with the student's own.
  - D2.His.10.K-2: each object answers something about school life in 1965, showing how sources are used.
  - D2.His.11.K-2: find maker, date and place on each object.
  - D2.His.12.K-2: generate questions about each object and the school's past.
- Not like: then-and-now-flip (objects across eras, sorting); the-last-carousel-ride (comparing accounts of one event).
- Art: generated scenes for period-style replica objects (made for this tool, clearly fictional), because real 1965 catalogs and photos are not public domain.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Open a time capsule a class buried under the school in 1965 (a photo, a letter, a lunch ticket, a toy catalog page); find who made each, when and where, ask your own questions, and compare those kids' wishes with yours.
- Flags: none
