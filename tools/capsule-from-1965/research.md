# Research packet: capsule-from-1965

Concept: Open a time capsule a class buried under the school in 1965 (a photo, a letter, a lunch ticket, a toy catalog page); find who made each, when and where, ask your own questions, and compare those kids' wishes with yours.
Grade: 1
Standards: D2.His.4.K-2, D2.His.10.K-2, D2.His.11.K-2, D2.His.12.K-2

## Standards in plain words

- D2.His.4.K-2 (Compare perspectives of people in the past to those of people in the present): First graders look at what children in 1965 wished for and compare those ideas to their own wishes today.
- D2.His.10.K-2 (Explain how historical sources can be used to study the past): First graders learn that old objects, photographs, and letters give clues about how people lived long ago.
- D2.His.11.K-2 (Identify the maker, date, and place of origin for a historical source from information within the source itself): First graders inspect clues written or printed on artifacts, such as names, dates, postmarks, and locations.
- D2.His.12.K-2 (Generate questions about a particular historical source as it relates to a particular historical event or development): First graders ask questions about an artifact to find out who used it and what everyday life was like.

## Real data

Artifacts in the 1965 time capsule:

|---|---|---|---|

| Cafeteria lunch ticket | Ephemera artifact | Cardstock paper, printed school district name, stamped punch marks, price (25 cents) | Under National School Lunch Program; punched by cafeteria worker for a hot tray lunch |
| Toy catalog clipping | Printed advertisement | Glossy newsprint paper, color illustrations, store logo, printed year 1965 | Features classic mid-1960s toys such as Kenner Easy-Bake Oven and mechanical drawing boards |

Investigation prompt questions for Grade 1:
1. Maker: Who created this object? Was it a student, a teacher, a cafeteria worker, or a toy company?
2. Date: What year was it made? Where do you see numbers or dates written on the item?
3. Place: Where was this item used? In a classroom, a cafeteria, or a home playroom?
4. Purpose: Why did the person save or create this item?
5. Wondering: What question would you ask the child who put this inside the capsule?

## Common mistakes

1. Assuming everything in 1965 was primitive or completely different from today; children still ate lunch, learned to read, wrote letters, and played with toys.
2. Believing a single item tells the entire history of an era without asking who made it and why.
3. Confusing the maker of an item with the person who placed it in the capsule (e.g., Kenner made the toy catalog, but a student saved the page).
4. Forgetting that old photos show real people who had feelings, friendships, and dreams just like kids today.
5. Thinking historical questions must have immediate right or wrong answers, rather than prompting further investigation.

## Sensitivity

- Historical school photos from 1965 reflect the era before full desegregation in many parts of the country; ensure depictions are respectful, diverse, and representative.
- Lunch tickets can touch on family socioeconomic differences; present school lunch as a universal community service shared by all classmates.
- Avoid treating past children as quaint or silly; emphasize empathy and direct connection between their hopes and modern first graders.
