# Slow-Mo Writer (idea card)

- Module: ela-5-narrative-craft, grade 5. Codes: W.5.3
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: The student takes a story that rushes past its big moment ("I fell off my bike") and slows that one moment down with senses, thoughts and a line of dialogue while speeding up the parts that don't matter, and a film strip plays each sentence as a frame, so they learn that pacing is a choice about where to spend words.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a short story told at one speed and a film strip that zips past the big moment in one frame. "Your story blinked at the best part. Slow it down."
  - Core loop: tap the big moment to open slow-mo: add sentences from five lenses (see, hear, feel in your body, think, say), from detail cards or in the student's own words; each sentence adds a frame and the strip slows. Then fast-forward the boring parts (the drive to the park) with time words (later that day, after lunch). Finish with an ending that shows what the moment meant.
  - The aha or the end state: the pace graph shows words spent per moment, before and after, and the strip plays the new story with the slow part in slow motion; the student explains in one sentence why they slowed down where they did.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): trade stories and slow down someone else's moment.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.5.3: orienting the reader, sequence with time words, dialogue, description and pacing in the slow-mo moment, sensory details, and an ending that follows from the events.
- Not like: show-don-t-tell (grade 4, showing a feeling); personal-essay-studio (grade 11 zoom lens); dialogue-director (grade 3, comic to prose).
- Art: illustrated frames for a set of starter stories (bike fall, first dive, lost in a store), picked from tags on each sentence, because the strip is the reward.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A short story rushes past its big moment; slow that moment down with sights, sounds, thoughts and dialogue, speed up the dull parts with time words, and a film strip and pace graph show where your words went.
- Flags: none (stories the student types stay on the device: the privacy line is required)
