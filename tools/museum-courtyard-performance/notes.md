## What it teaches

Students adapt a given dance to an unusual venue and explain how architecture, sound, lighting, rehearsal and safe landings shape a performance. They compare what two audiences receive from their final production.

## Key skills

- Read sightlines from a floor plan and compare them with a seat view.
- Adapt movement level and pathways to a particular place.
- Balance money and crew time across six production weeks.
- Plan rehearsals that build familiarity while managing jump load.
- Explain production choices using the final program sheet.

## Support at home

- Look at an ordinary room together from a seated and a standing position. Talk about what furniture hides.
- Listen for background sounds from two places at home. Discuss how a performance could work with the sound.
- Imagine a dance in a courtyard or park. Identify a feature to use and a landing surface to avoid.

## Five-minute start

Drag Arrival and watch the two Seen readouts. Select Pool and compare floor work with standing. Open Seat view and choose a seat. In Week, book a site rehearsal and choose two sections, then End week. Use Undo week to compare another plan.

## Modes

Site is the 20 by 16 meter courtyard plan. The four 6 by 4 meter zones are the four 90-second sections of Water Over Stone, an original six-minute Curio Class dance. Drag zones or focus the plan and use arrow keys in 0.25 meter steps. After installing a floor, select it in the floor menu to move it with the same inputs. Select a section to return to zone movement.

Week offers twelve production actions. Bookings reserve budget and hours immediately; End week applies them. Select two rehearsal sections, with the first selected receiving a lobby rehearsal if rain intervenes. Full, half and marked jumps are rehearsal choices; opening night permits full or half. Earlier tabs display reports. Undo restores the latest completed week, including its bookings and pre-week plan, until the following week ends. The warm-up plan applies to later weeks, as stated in the spec.

Opening night unlocks after six weeks. The model renders each section in ten seconds for a 40-second show. Stop and Skip act immediately. Tap the seats or use the seat menus to change viewpoints. The program sheet compares Seen, Heard and Lit for each audience, reports site use and dancer health, and can be printed. Start over asks before clearing the saved production.

## Talk questions

- When did the columns become part of the dance rather than something to hide?
- What changed when Pool stood up, and what was lost?
- How would you explain a safe, low-load rehearsal to a funder who wanted full jumps every week?
- Which audience received the more complete performance, and which production action would change that most?

## Good to know

The museum, piece, prices, weather event and numerical model are made up. This is a simple model, not a law or a predictor of actual injury. Monday-only site rehearsal and the dusk rule are fictional museum conditions. The model multiplies geometric sightlines by level, light and spacing, so Seen is not just geometric visibility. Read the on-screen How the model works note for the tables and assumptions.

Vinyl marley over stone provides grip, not sprung-floor cushioning. The model deliberately gives these surfaces different load factors. The over-120 line is a teaching assumption. Follow appropriate professional guidance for real rehearsal and flooring decisions.

The given spec does not define basin radius. This build uses 0.75 meters and labels it in the model note. The balcony eye height is 6.2 meters, using the given 5 meter floor plus a 1.2 meter eye height. Seat views use painted architectural billboards in a perspective projection; the numerical sightlines use the exact stated 2D ray test. Dancers are provisional kit figures, ready for painted pose assets.

Saving uses localStorage on this device only; nothing is transmitted. Narration uses the browser speech fallback because no audio was recorded during this build. Sound controls narration, music and fountain noise; Read can speak the current hint independently. Motion off preserves show duration and changes poses on count boundaries.

## Standards and sources

DA:Pr4 is practiced by moving zones and adapting Pool, Columns and Arrival to the site. DA:Pr5 is practiced through floor choices, jump options and rehearsal load. DA:Pr6 is practiced through sound, light, audience sightlines, crew and opening-night production.
