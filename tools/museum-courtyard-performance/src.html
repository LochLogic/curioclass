How the model works

A simple model, not a law. The museum, dance, prices and numerical rules are made up for this tool.

Seen

A 9 by 9 grid clipped to each 6 by 4 meter ellipse samples the zone. Rays from 60 ground seats test columns and the running spray. Ground sightlines are averaged, then multiplied by level, light and spacing.

For floor work, row 1 sees 100%; rows 2 and 3 see 50%, or 90% with risers. Kneeling and sitting use 75%. Standing uses 100%. The balcony sees only zone samples at y 13 meters or less. Below two adaptation dots, spacing is 85%.

S3 and S4 receive 40% light after dusk, or 100% with LED wash. Column uplights add ten visibility points for S3 in an arcade, capped at 100%. A floor pattern reads from above when S1, S2 or S3 has balcony Seen of at least 70%.

Dancer load

Jumps × three runs × floor factor: stone 3, vinyl marley 2, sprung 1. The warm-up plan reduces later weeks' loads to 80%. Marking counts as 30% of jumps. Over 120 is a sore week. Two consecutive sore weeks injure one dancer; Rise then has five dancers. Studio rehearsal on a sprung floor is not counted.

The floor factor uses the hardest surface under the six planned Rise landing positions. A center on a panel does not protect landings beyond its edge.

Vinyl over stone gives grip, not cushioning. These simplified load factors are teaching assumptions, not medical advice or real injury probabilities. Here the fountain basin radius is 0.75 meters; spray radius is 1.5 meters. Rain in week 6 cancels site rehearsal; a booked lobby backup gives one section a dot.

The site

The floor is 20 by 16 meters. Columns stand at x 3 and 17, and y 4, 7, 10 and 13. Ground seats have a 1.2 meter eye height. Balcony floor height is 5 meters. This fictional museum permits Monday rehearsals only and a show starting 30 minutes before sunset. Keep zones and floors clear of the basin and audience.

Sources: NCAS Dance DA:Pr4, DA:Pr5 and DA:Pr6; Quin, Rafferty and Tomlinson, Safe Dance Practice (2015); Ausdance National, Safe dance floors; Kloetzel and Pavlik, Site Dance (2009).
