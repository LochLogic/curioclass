## What it teaches
Students analyze eight dance styles through movement, rhythm and cultural context. They compare evidence from short modeled phrases and write a critique that cites specific counts, vocabulary and evaluation criteria.

## Key skills
- Describe body, action, space, time and energy with precise words.
- Connect a movement observation to a count or count range.
- Compare two phrases on the same clock while noticing the effect of changed speed.
- Interpret movement in the context of a style's roots and history.
- Evaluate a phrase against a stated criterion and support the judgment with evidence.

## Support at home
- Watch a familiar movement together. Describe its level, direction and energy without deciding whether it is good or bad.
- Clap a short rhythm. Take turns adding an accent and describing how it changes the feeling.
- Ask your student to explain one observation and the moment that supports it. Talk about why a whole tradition cannot be judged from one short example.

## A 5-minute start
Open Ballet, turn on Trace and press Play. Pause at an interesting count, then use the count buttons to inspect the shape. Compare Ballet with Breaking using Overlay. Enter Critique, press Note and describe one visible change with a vocabulary term. Insert the note in What I saw, then add an interpretation and an evaluation criterion.

## Modes
**Styles:** eight open style files with gear still lifes, roots, people and milestones, five-part signatures and synthesized study tracks. Choosing a style immediately starts its phrase. There is no score or identification quiz.

**Compare:** two phrases share A's clock and A's music. B's movement is retimed, so this is a comparison of shapes on counts, not a claim that both traditions normally use this tempo. Flamenco's 12-count cycles and Ballet's 16-count phrases retain their own readouts. Overlay combines traces. Hands use solid lines and feet use dotted lines.

**Critique:** press Note or N to stamp a count. Hold the Note button during playback to stamp a range. Save a term and up to 140 characters, tap a saved note to revisit it, then Insert note under any draft box. A selected note is inserted; otherwise the latest note for this style is used. All notes shows notes across styles. Criteria chips add the chosen criterion to the evaluation box.

The checks are reminders, never grades: each box cites a count, three vocabulary terms appear, and the evaluation names a criterion. They cannot judge the quality of an interpretation. Copy text includes all notes and their styles. Print prepares the draft for the browser's print dialog.

## Talk questions
- What did Trace make visible that Live did not?
- How does the rhythm line support or challenge your first impression?
- Which part of your interpretation depends on cultural context rather than shape alone?
- What does putting B at A's speed help you compare, and what does it leave out?
- Would your evaluation change if you chose a different criterion?

## Good to know
These are short educational models created by Curio Class, not recordings or authoritative demonstrations of an entire tradition. Painted rehearsal figures follow modeled joints; front-view paintings are articulated and foreshortened, so the models simplify turns, facial expression and expert technique. Pataka and alapadma use distinct painted hands with enlarged details. A separately painted baby freeze shows its supports. Do not use these phrases to teach physical technique or ask students to copy demanding floor moves.

The still lifes depict footwear and equipment alone. Pointe shoes do not imply that all ballet is danced on pointe. Modern's bare feet are represented by an empty floor and towel.

Read pauses a running demo and reads the current next-action hint. Narration uses the browser fallback; the manifest selects upbeat-guide for future recorded narration. No audio was recorded in this build. Sound off silences the track, foot sounds and automatic speech. The kit's explicit Read action can still speak when Sound is off. Motion off retains the same timing and half-count trace samples while displaying discrete poses.

Notes and draft are saved on this device only, with storage errors shown in the critique panel. Clear critique asks before deleting both. The kit separately remembers its shared Motion preference. Nothing is sent over a network.

Measured values describe this model, not the dance style as a whole. Hip height is sampled every half count: High at least 0.52 body heights, Middle at least 0.28, Low below 0.28. Reach is maximum 3D hand span at those samples. Travel is sampled floor distance divided by a nominal 40-unit body height. Stillness counts intervals with no measured joint-angle change greater than 5 degrees. These analysis conventions are stated in the style file.

## Standards and context
High-school activities address NCAS Dance DA:Re7.1.HSI through observation and comparison, DA:Re8.1.HSI through interpretation with vocabulary and context, DA:Re9.1.HSI through criteria-based evaluation, and DA:Cn11.1.HSI through each file's roots and historical context. The inherited K-2 codes DA:Re7.1.1b and DA:Re7.1.2b are a spec/catalog mismatch; this high-school build does not add a K-2 activity.

The style files credit African American social dance in jazz, African American and Irish/Scottish roots in tap, Bronx hip hop culture in breaking, Gitano communities and Andalusian influences in flamenco, and hereditary women dancers and the later institutional reshaping of Bharatanatyam. Context is part of interpretation, not decoration. A fresh cultural and representation review passed during this cold review; its findings are recorded in refine/sensitivity.md.
