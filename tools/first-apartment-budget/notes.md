## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Plan**. Pick a challenge (or Free play), then a city, a starting job, a home, furniture, a way to get to work and benefits. The paycheck and monthly plan on the right update with every choice.
2. Read the gauges: housing against the 30% guideline, and needs, wants and saving against the 50/30/20 rule. The verdict box says whether the plan holds together.
3. Type a **class code** (any word, like PERIOD3) so the whole class gets the same surprises, then press **Move in**.
4. In **Live the year**, press **Live July**. The month plays out day by day: paydays every other Friday, bills on their due dates, and surprises that ask for a decision and how to pay (checking, savings or the credit card). You can change groceries, eating out, fun money, auto-save and the card settings between months.
5. After twelve months, **Report** grades the year and shows where every dollar went.

## What is real

| Piece | Source |
|---|---|
| Rent | HUD Fair Market Rents, FY2026, for each metro area. HUD's figure includes utilities, so the tool splits out an estimated utility bill. |
| Starting pay | BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2025: the 25th-percentile wage for that job in that metro (our stand-in for starting pay). |
| Federal income tax | 2026 brackets and the $16,100 standard deduction (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Social Security and Medicare at 7.65%. |
| 401(k) and health premiums | Taken out before income tax; health premiums also before Social Security and Medicare. |
| Groceries | USDA Food Plans (2025), one adult, three cost levels. |
| Employer health plan | KFF 2025: workers pay about $1,440 a year for single coverage. |
| Credit card | About 24% APR with a $1,500 limit, a typical first card. The 2026 average for cards carrying a balance was about 22% (Federal Reserve G.19). |
| Student loans | 6.39% (federal undergraduate rate for 2025-26), 10-year plan, payments start after a 6-month grace period. |

**Simplified on purpose:** state and local income taxes are approximations (Indiana plus Allen County as one flat rate, Ohio's flat rate above $26,050 plus Columbus's 2.5% city tax, Arizona 2.5%, Colorado 4.4% plus Denver's head tax, Massachusetts 5%, no income tax in Texas or Washington). Transit passes, car insurance for a young driver, phone plans and everyday costs are rounded typical figures. Withholding matches the tax owed, so there is no refund. It is a simulation for learning, not financial advice.

## The challenges

- **Free play**: any city and job. Goals: one month of expenses saved, no card debt, wellbeing 50 or higher.
- **Paycheck to paycheck**: line cook in Denver with $600. No overdrafts, no missed card payments, $500 saved.
- **Big city, small budget**: medical assistant in Boston. A studio does not work on this pay; students discover roommates.
- **The car question**: warehouse job in Houston. Compare a bus commute, an old car and a financed car.
- **New grad with loans**: staff accountant in Columbus with $29,000 in loans. Capture the full 401(k) match and save three months of expenses.
- **Seattle sprint**: junior web developer. Save $15,000 while keeping wellbeing at 60.

## How the year works

- **Wellbeing** (0-100) rises with comfort at home, fun money, eating out now and then, a gym and a short commute. It falls with long commutes, overtime, a second job, thin savings and card debt. A strong six-month review (with wellbeing of 55 or more) earns a bigger raise.
- **Overdrafts**: a bill that takes checking below zero costs a $35 fee (at most one a day). Debit purchases are declined and go on the credit card if there is room. **Overdraft protection** pulls from savings for free.
- **Credit score** starts at 660 (a thin file). On-time months and low card use raise it; a missed payment drops it 60 points; a medical bill sent to collections drops it 70.
- **Money crisis**: if checking ends a month more than $300 in the red, the student must change something: move to a shared house, take a second job or borrow from family.
- **Surprises** come one or two a month from a deck of about 25 (car trouble, the flu, a friend's wedding, a store-card offer, buy now pay later, a water leak, a roommate moving out...), plus fixed ones: holidays in December, the six-month review, student loans starting, tax time and the lease renewal.

## The report card (100 points)

| Area | Points | Full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Safety net | 25 | three months of needs in checking and savings |
| Debt and fees | 20 | no card balance, overdrafts, late payments or crises |
| Future you | 15 | the full 401(k) match and a credit score of 700+ |
| Growth | 15 | net worth up by 30% of take-home pay |
| Life | 25 | average wellbeing of 70 |

A frugal year with no fun scores poorly on Life; a fun year with a maxed card scores poorly on debt. An A takes balance.

## Talk about it

- What share of your gross pay went to housing? What did a roommate change?
- Which surprise hurt most? Would an emergency fund, renters insurance or health insurance have changed it?
- Compare two classmates with the same class code and different plans. Which single decision made the biggest difference?
- Why does the tool count the security deposit and your 401(k) in net worth, but subtract the card and loans?
- Is a big tax refund good news? (The tax-time card has an answer.)

## Good to know

- **Space** plays the next month. **Fast** and **Instant** speed up the days; surprises still stop the clock.
- Plans and best scores are kept on this device only.
- Same class code and same plan means the same surprises; different plans can draw different events (only car owners get flat tires).
