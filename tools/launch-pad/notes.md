## What it teaches

Students solve quadratic equations by factoring, square roots, completing the square and the quadratic formula.
They distinguish algebraic solutions from times that make sense for a flight, including repeated and complex roots.

## Key skills

- Turn a landing, crossing or catching question into an equation.
- Choose and check a way to solve a quadratic.
- Use both signs of a square root and interpret the discriminant.
- Write exact radical or complex answers, then interpret time in context.
- Compare an algebraic prediction with the same model played as a flight.

## Support at home

- Toss an imaginary ball and talk about passing the same height on the way up and on the way down.
- Ask what a negative time would mean if a clock starts when a ball launches.
- Sketch a hill-shaped curve together. Find a height it meets twice, once and never.

## A 5-minute start

Press Launch at the default settings. Compare the rising and falling ball with its height against time graph.
At a line height of 48 ft, explain why two times appear. Change the line to 80 ft and compare with the 64 ft peak.
Enter Missions and solve mission 1: Write it, choose 0 ft, Tidy, Factor with p = 0 and q = 4, and choose 4 seconds.
Launch to test it.

## The modes

Launch is an open checker with speed 0 to 120 ft/s in steps of 4, starting height 0 to 100 ft and target height
0 to 330 ft. A slider change stops the old flight. Launch during a flight starts a fresh one immediately.

Missions contains nine ordered setups. The curve is hidden until a prediction checks. A method checks the student's
work before the contextual answer. Formula includes a discriminant call; completing the square includes its identity
and both roots. Mission 7 also asks students to derive the general formula by filling its three denominators. A flight pauses at each accepted event time, showing its height. Progress saves on this device only.
Clear missions clears these local records. Every mission can be revisited.

## Talk questions

- Why is 0 a root in mission 1, but not the requested landing time?
- Why does mission 2 have a negative root even though the flight happens after launch?
- How does a repeated root appear in the drone scene?
- What does 2 ± i say about reaching the 80 ft drone?
- Which of the two balcony times describes a catch on the way down?
- How does adding the same square to both sides lead toward the quadratic formula?

## Good to know

This is a simple model with no air resistance and g = 32 ft/s². Standard gravity is about 32.174 ft/s².
The height equation measures the foam ball, with equipment drawn symbolically beneath its starting point.
The model is educational, not a real launcher or safety calculator.

Exact expressions accept fractions, sqrt(41), √41 and ±. Separate roots with a comma. Decimals within 0.01 s of
the exact answer count, and a correct answer shows its exact form. A leading minus applies after powers: -2^2 is -4. The flight uses the exact time, avoiding the
large height error that a rounded time can create near a fast landing.

Negative roots are valid algebra but precede launch. Mission 1 excludes its launch root; mission 9 excludes its
upward crossing. Mission 5 has two complex roots and no real flight time. Motion off draws the path at once and
shows still snapshots at event times while keeping the real clock and its pauses.

Narration uses the shared Curio Class voice queue and the browser's speech fallback. No recordings were made.
The intended bank voice is big-sister. Read speaks the current hint. Keyboard: Tab to controls, arrows for sliders,
Enter to submit a box. All files work offline. There are no network requests or accounts.
