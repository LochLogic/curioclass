## What it teaches

Students turn a question into useful search keywords. They learn to narrow a broad search, remove words that rule out useful pages, and try another word for the same idea.

## Key skills

- Pick words that name a topic and what you want to learn about it.
- Watch how one added or removed word changes a set of results.
- Try a different word when a useful idea catches nothing.
- Check whether a small catch actually answers the question.

## Support at home

- Say a question about something nearby. Together, choose two or three words that would help find an answer.
- Think of another word for an everyday idea, such as big and size. Talk about when each word helps.
- Name a word with two meanings, such as bat. Add one word that makes the meaning clear.

## A 5-minute start

1. Open Level 1. Tie on **what**, then **do**. Notice that all 30 fish still nibble.
2. Clear the line. Tie on **sea**, then **turtles**. Predict what adding **eat** will do.
3. Cast. Compare the two caught pages with the question. A small catch is useful only if it contains an answer.
4. Open Level 3. Select a word, then use **Take it off**. Let students explain why removing a word can improve a search.

## The modes

- **Levels:** six puzzles, all available from the start. Each question asks for three or fewer pages, including one that answers it. A gold fin and words identify answer pages only after casting. Solved stickers save on this device.
- **Free fishing:** the same pond without a question or goal. Five drawers hold all the pond's words. Students can type one word at a time, including words no page contains. Free fishing saves no work.

## Talk about it

A sticker records a successful query in this fixed pond, not proof of independent mastery. Trying words and watching results is intentional. Ask a student to explain why the caught page fits; later levels explicitly allow useful one-word queries.

- Why did **what do** catch the same fish as an empty line?
- Why could **bats food** work while **bats find food** catches nothing?
- How can a catch of two pages still fail to answer the question?
- What changed when you swapped **big** for **size**?

## Good to know

This is a deliberately simple catalog model: a fish bites only if it has every word on the line. The displayed count is exact for these 30 made-up pages. Every page has the same 22 little words. A word and its plural match when either is the other plus **s** or **es**, including turtle/turtles and volcano/volcanoes/volcanos. This rule does not handle every English plural.

Matching uses the supplied keyword list for each made-up page, not its displayed title or snippet. For example, the bat-hunting snippet uses **find**, but that page's keyword list has **hunt** instead. The tool follows those supplied lists so students can see why trying another word matters. Treat this as a model limitation when comparing it with web search.

Real web search engines rank results and use many other signals. They do not require every typed word in this way. The pond makes the effect of each word visible; its three-fish goal is a teaching choice, not a rule for real searches. The snippets describe topics without making factual claims. No live pages open.

Tap a line word to select it, then remove or swap it. A swap is available only for the words listed in the puzzle. A duplicate word cannot be tied on twice. Cast and word controls remain live during movement. Changing the line cancels a pending cast so old results cannot overwrite a newer choice.

Tap a fish or focus it and press Enter to read its title, site and snippet. **Read** speaks the current hint and the open page tag, or the current question. Narrow views reveal title tags on focus or hover; fish that left the bobber do the same to avoid a stack of overlapping labels. The bucket shows at most 12 pages plus the remaining count. At short preview heights it collapses to the catch count.

Tab order goes through tackle controls before fish. All controls work with the keyboard. Motion off removes movement; the spec explicitly makes casting immediate in that setting. Sound off silences tones and automatic narration; Read remains available. Fixed lines use the friendly-dad voice plan with the kit's browser speech fallback. No recordings were made.

Only earned level numbers are saved under **kf-levels**, on this device, inside guarded storage calls. **Clear stickers**, then **Yes**, removes them. The tool collects nothing and makes no network requests.

## Standards and sources

AASL National School Library Standards, Framework for Learners: Curate IV.A.2, identifying possible sources, is practiced by collecting matching fictional pages. Inquire I.A.1 asks learners to formulate questions; Levels supplies questions and practices turning them into keywords, so it supports that standard without independently teaching its full requirement. In Free fishing, invite students to ask their own question before choosing words. Full question formulation and source evaluation need additional learning beyond this tool.
