# Weekend Drawing Share (idea card)

- Module: arts-k-2-art-about-my-life, grade k-2. Codes: VA:Cr3, VA:Cn10
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Draw something that really happened to you, then Pip, a curious listener, asks one question at a time (Who was there? What was the weather? Why that color?), and you answer by adding to the drawing, not by typing, so the picture itself grows until it tells the whole story.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a blank page on an easel, Pip sitting beside it, and picture prompts read aloud (a trip, a new pet, a lost tooth, a rainy day inside).
  - Core loop: draw with real-feeling brushes (crayon, marker, paint, stamps). Pip taps a question card (read aloud). The student adds the answer as a detail, then taps the spot to pin it. While drawing, art-word chips (big, small, bright, dark, line, shape, color) let the student say "I made the sun big because it was hot", read back by the narrator.
  - The aha or the end state: a before-and-after flip of the first sketch and the finished picture, with Pip retelling the story only from the pinned details ("You were at the lake, with Grandpa, and it was windy"). The child hears that the picture told it.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a partner can take Pip's role and tap the cards; a print view with the pinned answers as a caption.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - VA:Cr3: art-word chips while creating name the choices and why (K explain the process, grade 1 use art words, grade 2 reflect on choices with Pip or a partner).
  - VA:Cn10: the whole tool is art about a real event from the child's own home, school or community life.
- Not like: line-safari (names lines and shapes, not a personal story); art-passport (looks at other people's art); sound-and-picture-book (a planned three-page story with sounds).
- Art: people (illustrated) for Pip and the prompt cards; the child's own drawing is the centerpiece, so the brushes must feel real.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Draw something that really happened to you; Pip asks one question at a time (Who was there? Why that color?), you answer by adding a detail to the picture and say why with art-word chips, until the drawing tells your story.
- Flags: privacy (drawings saved on the device only; the screen says nothing leaves the device)
