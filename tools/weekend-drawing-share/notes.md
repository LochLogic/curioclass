## What it teaches

Children make a drawing about an experience from their own lives, refine it with details, and explain their choices using art words. They can replay the steps to tell how their picture grew.

## Key skills

- Remember a place, people, and something that happened.
- Use lines and colors to show sound and feeling.
- Choose size and placement to help someone notice an important part.
- Add details and explain a choice.
- Tell a personal story in the order its picture grew.

## Support at home

- Invite your child to draw one small moment from today on any paper you already have.
- Ask, "What did you add next?" and let your child point to that part.
- Ask why they chose a color or made something big. Accept their own explanation.

## A 5-minute start

Open Draw, pick a color, and make a mark for a place from a real day. Ask the artist to explain it. Tap an art word together, then Next question. Children at their seats can draw the same experience on paper while one artist or the teacher draws on the board.

## The modes

Draw keeps seven parts: place, companions, action, sky, sound, feeling, and the most important part. Questions can return to any part. Each part can carry two art words. A third choice replaces the oldest choice for that part. Undo removes the most recently added mark across the drawing, without clearing art words.

Tell it shows one marked part at a time. Earlier parts are faded. Let the artist talk, then choose Next step. There is no time limit for speaking. Play step repeats the current part. Draw more returns to that part for revisions. Start over retells the same drawing.

Art-word badges appear as the marks finish, so the child can begin explaining while Pip narrates. Next step is always available to move on. Motion off keeps the same badge and stroke timing.

Pip's day at the pond is a built-in sample. It is read only. Draw more makes an editable copy. Stickers provide starting shapes; children can draw over them. The three people stamps are simple stick figures made in code. Choose one, drag it, then use the Size handle. Tap an older sticker to select it before dragging. A stroke over an unselected sticker draws normally. Keyboard arrows move a selected stamp or sticker; arrows on Size resize it, and Delete removes it.

## Talk questions

- Which part really happened to you?
- Why did you choose that color or line?
- What did you change or add to help us look closer?
- Which part do you want us to notice first?

Art-word lines offer gentle possibilities. A dark color can express many feelings, and a bright color does not have to mean happy. Ask the artist what they meant.

## Good to know

This is teacher led for grades K-2, with time for children to speak and draw on paper. Any real day is welcome; it need not be a weekend or a special outing. Children may leave a question unmarked.

Save keeps up to six drawings on this device. The built-in sample does not count. At six saves, replacing the oldest needs an explicit choice. Nothing is uploaded. Clearing browser storage removes saved drawings. Opening a different drawing or starting a new one asks before discarding unsaved changes.

On compact screens, Tools opens the crayon, width, and color tray. Art words and the next action stay beside or below the paper. The saved shelf is below the core workspace in short previews; scroll to reach it. A classroom laptop or projected display provides the fullest drawing space.

Sound starts on and uses browser speech because this Build does not record narration. Read repeats the current question, or the current replay question and art words. Motion off removes Pip's bob and reveals whole strokes at the same replay pace. Neither setting limits the child's speaking time.

## Standards and sources

VA:Cr3 is practiced by adding details, returning to questions, revising marks, and explaining art choices. VA:Cn10 is practiced by drawing a personal experience and telling its story.
