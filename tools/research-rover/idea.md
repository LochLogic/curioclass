# Research Rover (idea card)

- Module: ela-4-short-research-projects, grade 4. Codes: W.4.7, W.4.8, RI.4.9
- Format / family: explorer / research. Fit: good.
- One idea: The student drives a little rover through a mini library of kid-safe articles, maps, and an interview, to investigate one topic from several sides, logging notes and each source in the rover's cargo, so they learn that research looks at a topic from different angles and keeps track of where every fact came from.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a library floor plan with aisles (science, history, maps, interviews) and a mission: "Monarch butterflies: Where do they go, and why are there fewer?"
  - Core loop: steer the rover to a shelf, open the source, and beam notes into the cargo bay in a few words; each note is stamped with its source. The mission board shows the topic's sides (journey, life cycle, threats, helpers) and lights as notes arrive. At one stop two articles disagree on a number, and the student must use both to write one sentence that is true to each.
  - The aha or the end state: the rover unloads into an outline with categories and a sources list built from the stamps; a side with no notes sends the rover back out.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "my own observation" note slot for what the student has seen.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.4.7: a short project that investigates different sides of one topic.
  - W.4.8: recalling what they know, gathering from print and digital sources, notes in categories and a list of sources.
  - RI.4.9: combining two articles on the same topic into one accurate sentence and outline.
- Not like: note-card-shuffle (grade 3, notes and categories only); fact-fusion (grade 5 report with source tracking); research-notebook (high school).
- Art: drawn in code for the rover and floor plan, generated scenes for the articles (monarchs, milkweed, maps).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Drive a rover through a mini library of kid-safe articles, maps and an interview to investigate one topic from several sides; every note carries its source, two articles must be combined, and the cargo unloads into an outline and sources list.
- Flags: needs a data source (monarch facts and counts, checked)
