# Root Detective Cards (idea card)

- Module: ela-4-greek-and-latin-roots, grade 4. Codes: L.4.4
- Format / family: cards / word-lab. Fit: good.
- One idea: The student collects Greek and Latin root and affix cards and plays them to crack unknown words in case sentences (telegraph, portable, autograph), checking each guess against context and the dictionary, so they learn that a few roots unlock hundreds of words.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a detective's desk, a starter hand (tele, graph, port, auto, -able, re-, trans-) and Case 1: "The museum's new exhibit was transportable." "Which cards does this word use?"
  - Core loop: play cards to split the word and build its meaning (trans = across, port = carry, -able = can be), then check: does the meaning fit the sentence? Then open the dictionary card to confirm, with pronunciation. Solved cases win new root cards. A build round lets the student combine cards into real words and earn a bonus for each one the case files use later; a fake word (portgraph) gets a "no such word" stamp.
  - The aha or the end state: the case wall shows one root (port) linking a dozen words the student can now read (export, report, portable, transportation), and a final case with a word they have never seen.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two players trade cards to solve a shared case.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - L.4.4: context clues in each case sentence, Greek and Latin affixes and roots on the cards, and the dictionary card for pronunciation and exact meaning.
- Not like: word-lab (grade 2 beakers of word parts); glossary-makers (subject words in a passage); word-family-tree (grade 12 word histories).
- Art: illustrated cards with a small picture for each root (a phone pole for tele, a carried box for port), because the picture helps the meaning stick.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Collect Greek and Latin root and affix cards and play them to crack unknown words in case sentences; check each meaning against the sentence and the dictionary, and solved cases win new roots.
- Flags: none
