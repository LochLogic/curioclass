# Story Blanks (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-parts-of-speech, grade 3. Codes: L.3.1
- Format / family: arcade / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Word tiles stream past and the student grabs one for each blank in a silly story, where every blank names its job (a word that names, a word that acts, a word that tells how), so when the story is read aloud a wrong pick makes the sentence break and the student hears what each kind of word does.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a story page with blanks labeled by job and a moving belt of word tiles. "Grab a word for the first blank. It needs a word that does something."
  - Core loop: short timed rounds, each on one skill: naming and doing words, plurals (mouse to mice), past, present and future (yesterday she ___), irregular verbs (ran, not runned), agreement (the dogs ___), more and most (taller, tallest), and a glue round (and, but, so, because, when) that joins two lines into one.
  - The aha or the end state: the finished story is read aloud with the student's words; a mismatch makes the narrator stumble and the sentence crack on screen, then shows the fix. Score is for fit and speed, and the silliest correct story is kept.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two players race for the same blanks.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - L.3.1: each round targets one part: word functions, regular and irregular plurals and verbs, simple tenses, agreement, comparatives and superlatives, and joining words for compound and complex sentences.
- Not like: word-zoo (grade 1, sorting word kinds into habitats); contraction-crusher (grade 2 arcade); grammar-garage (grade 4 repair puzzles).
- Art: drawn in code for the belt and story page, with illustrated spot pictures that change to match the silly words.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Grab word tiles from a moving belt to fill a silly story whose blanks name each word's job; rounds cover plurals, tenses, agreement and joining words, and a wrong part of speech makes the read-aloud break.
- Flags: overlap risk (the fill-in-a-silly-story mechanic echoes a well-known commercial word game; never use its name, and keep the job-labeled blanks and the breaking read-aloud as the core)
