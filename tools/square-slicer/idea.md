# Square Slicer (idea card)

- Module: math-8-the-pythagorean-theorem, grade 8. Codes: 8.G.B
- Format / family: puzzle / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Cut the squares on a right triangle's two short sides into pieces and slide them to fill the square on the long side exactly, then use what that proves to square up a deck, find the longest pole that fits in a box, and measure shortcuts on a park map grid.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a right triangle with a square on each side, a cutting tool, and a builder named Mr. Ahmadi. "If these two squares fill that one, I'll trust the rule."
  - Proof levels: the student cuts and slides pieces (several classic dissections, each a level) until the two small squares fill the big one. Then a non-right triangle, where the pieces never fit.
  - Converse levels: Mr. Ahmadi is framing a deck; the student checks corners with the 3-4-5 rule (and 6-8-10, 5-12-13) and finds the corner that is not square.
  - Missing-side levels: a ladder against a wall, a TV's screen size from width and height, a ramp; then 3D: the longest pole that fits corner to corner in a moving box.
  - Park-map levels: on a coordinate map, the student finds the straight-line shortcut between two points by drawing the right triangle on the grid.
  - The aha or the end state: the two small squares really do fill the big one, only for right triangles, and that one fact measures distances everywhere.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a dissection gallery where students share their own cuts.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 8.G.B: explaining a proof of the theorem by dissection and its converse by testing corners (8.G.6); finding unknown sides in real 2D and 3D problems (8.G.7); distances between points on the coordinate plane (8.G.8).
- Not like: ramp-and-roof (grade 10 trigonometry), dance-floor-moves (grade 8 transformations), fire-lookout-triangles (grade 12 laws of sines and cosines).
- Art: drawn in code for the pieces (cuts must fit exactly); generated scenes for the deck and park; people (illustrated) for Mr. Ahmadi.
