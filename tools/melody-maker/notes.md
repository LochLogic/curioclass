## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Compose**. A short tune (it's Frère Jacques) is already on the grid. Press **Play** and watch each syllable pop up and the notes light up on the staff below.
2. Tap cells to change notes: one note per beat, and an empty column is a rest. Every row is in the scale, so every note sounds good.
3. Read the **Melody shape** card: how many steps, skips and leaps, and whether the tune starts and ends on do.
4. Change the **Key**. The tune moves higher or lower, but the shape and the syllables stay the same.

## The four modes

- **Compose**: a 16-beat grid with a row for each syllable, written on a treble staff underneath. Four instruments (marimba, flute, piano, music box), four keys (C, D, F, G), and three scales: major, pentatonic (no fa or ti) and minor, where la is home. **Surprise me** writes a tune that asks a question at the halfway point and answers it at the end.
- **Scale ladder**: the major scale as a ladder with rungs spaced by half steps, so mi-fa and ti-do are visibly close. A piano shows the whole-whole-half, whole-whole-whole-half pattern. Starting on G, D or F shows why those keys need a sharp or a flat.
- **Echo it**: the robin sings 3 to 5 notes, then students play them back on the solfege bells. Six levels, from sol-mi (the first interval most music programs teach) to the whole scale. Three perfect echoes earn the level's star.
- **Finish the tune**: phrases from public domain folk songs with the last note missing. Students try each ending and choose. Answer phrases end on do; question phrases end away from home (sol or re).

## Teaching notes

- The colors match the common classroom bell set (do red, re orange, mi yellow, fa green, sol blue, la indigo, ti violet), and every note always shows its syllable too.
- This is movable do: do is the home note of whatever key you choose. In minor, la is home.
- The staff writes the melody in the chosen key with its key signature, so students can connect solfege to notation (see Note Ninja for practice reading notes).

## Talk about it

- Which sounds more finished: ending on do or on ti? Why do you think so?
- Look at the ladder. Where are the half steps? What would happen if every step were the same size?
- Can you write a tune that asks a question in bar 2 and answers it in bar 4?
- Change your tune to minor. How does the mood change?

## Good to know

- Keys 1 to 8 play the bells in Echo it and the rungs on the ladder. Space plays your tune in Compose. Arrow keys move around the grid, and Enter places a note.
