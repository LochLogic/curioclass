# Ocean Conveyor (idea card)

- Module: science-6-climate-and-ocean-circulation, grade 6. Codes: MS-ESS2-6
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Heat a model Earth unevenly and set it spinning, and watch winds, surface currents and the deep salty conveyor form, then see why two coasts at about the same latitude get very different climates.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a globe with a heating slider (equator against poles), a spin switch (on by default, rule 9) and a dye dropper.
  - Core loop: turn spin off and air rises at the equator and flows straight toward the poles; turn it on and the flows bend into trade winds and westerlies. Drop dye in the oceans to see the wind-driven gyres. Cool and salt North Atlantic water until it sinks and starts the deep conveyor. Each change shows on the globe and on a side-view slice.
  - The aha or the end state: a climate panel with paired coastal cities at about the same latitude, one warmed by a current and one chilled (for example, the southwest coast of Britain and the coast of Labrador); the student explains the gap with the model.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): what-if switches: a slower spin, or Atlantic water that stops sinking.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-ESS2-6: the model shows how unequal heating and Earth's rotation cause atmospheric and ocean circulation patterns that set regional climates.
- Not like: raindrop-s-journey (one molecule's path); feedback-loop-builder (grade 9 climate loops); biome-atlas (grade 12 biomes from climate).
- Art: globe, flows and slices drawn in code; generated postcard scenes for each paired city.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Heat the equator, set Earth spinning and watch winds, ocean gyres and the deep salty conveyor form; then compare two coasts at the same latitude whose currents give them very different climates.
- Flags: needs a data source (climate normals for the paired cities, checked)
