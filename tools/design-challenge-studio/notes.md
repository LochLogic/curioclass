## What it teaches
Students break a complex engineering problem into smaller causes, distinguish criteria from constraints, and use a model to compare designs. They can explain how a weighted decision depends on evidence, assumptions and priorities.

## Key skills
- Group causes into manageable sub-problems.
- Set measurable goals and limits.
- Test combinations and interpret a simulation.
- Compare evidence using weights without hiding failed constraints.
- Explain a choice and acknowledge its trade-offs.

## Support at home
- Choose an everyday decision together. Name two things that would make one option better and one requirement every option must meet.
- Compare two ways of keeping a room comfortable. Discuss what each costs in money, time and energy, without buying anything.
- Ask your student to explain a choice that would change if a different priority mattered most.

## A 5-minute start
Start Cool classroom. Place two cause cards in the tree and check them. Open Prototype, save the baseline, then try an option and save again. Open Trade-offs and change a weight. The four stations are always open, so students can explore a model before sorting the problem.

## Modes
Break it down: tap a card and then a branch, or drag it. Keyboard users press Enter to pick a card, arrows to select a branch and Enter to drop it. Check tree gives feedback per card. Student-authored cards are not graded.

Criteria: every item can be a weighted criterion or a hard constraint. The suggested sort is feedback, not a lock. Zero weight means an item does not contribute to the total. Constraint edits apply to every saved design.

Prototype: test eight classroom interventions or up to three ordered water steps. Save up to four designs; saving a fifth asks which to replace. The results list all current criteria and constraints.

Trade-offs: only passing designs set each column's 0 to 10 scale. Equal values score 10. The total is the sum of weight times score, without rounding intermediate scores. All-zero weights tie. Flip finder searches integer weights 0 to 5 for the smallest single change, including a tie.

Your own challenge: name the problem and branches, add cards and criteria, then enter design values from 0 to 10. Direction is selectable. This is student evidence, not a simulation. The challenge saves on this device under design-challenge-studio. Clear my challenge removes it. The Why box is held only in the current session. Nothing is sent anywhere.

## Talk questions
- Which criterion do you treat as a requirement, and why?
- Does the winning design change because of better evidence or different priorities?
- What does the classroom model leave out that would matter in a real school?
- Why can a cheaper water sequence fail the germ-protection constraint?
- Why does a constraint-breaking design not set the best and worst scores?

## Good to know
This teaches HS-ETS1-1 through Criteria and challenge facts; HS-ETS1-2 through the tree; HS-ETS1-3 through weights and Flip finder; HS-ETS1-4 through the two computer models.

The room model uses sixty-second steps for three days from 30 degrees Celsius and reports day three. It assumes a 72 square meter roof and a 20 megajoule per kelvin room heat capacity. It omits humidity, different wall orientations, movement and individual heat-safety thresholds. Costs, geometry and the heat-wave curve are invented, rounded classroom assumptions. A comfort threshold is not a medical safety threshold. Trees represent mature shade in about 15 years. Electricity is counted across 24 hours, despite the school-day label.

Water is a simulation, not a recommendation for a specific household or water source. For real water follow the local health department or product label. These steps do not remove nitrate or lead. Boiling is one minute, or three above about 2,000 meters. The 15-minute batch estimate includes heating; it is not the boiling duration. Chlorine dioxide may require up to four hours for Cryptosporidium; the result shows a possible longer wait. Trade-offs scores the listed minimum batch time, not the possible full label wait. Cost and taste are ordinal classroom categories. Combining treatment does not create an actual guarantee of water safety.

## Cold review clarification
The combination bonus follows the supplied spec. On clear-source simulations, chemical treatment before microfiltration receives the same combined rating; in cloudy water, pre-cleaning must precede the chemical. Real guidance recommends filtering then disinfecting. This order-dependent specification issue is left for a spec decision. See [CDC water treatment guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/drinking-water/prevention/water-treatment-hiking-camping-traveling.html).
