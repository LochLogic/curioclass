# Consejero (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-9-12-subjunctive-and-opinions, grade 9-12. Codes: ACTFL.Intermediate High
- Format / family: roleplay / spanish. Fit: good: friends texting you everyday problems is a real reason to recommend, wish and doubt.
- One idea: Friends text you their everyday problems in Spanish (a fight over a group project, a lost phone, too many practices), and you answer with advice, wishes and doubts (Te recomiendo que hables con ella, Ojalá que lo encuentres, No creo que sea tu culpa), finding that the subjunctive appears when one person wants, doubts or recommends something about another.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a phone with a group chat. A friend: "¡Necesito un consejo! Mi compañero no hace nada en el proyecto."
  - Core loop: the student reads each message, picks what they want to do (recommend, wish, doubt, react with feeling) and builds the reply: a trigger (Te sugiero que, Es importante que, Dudo que, Me alegra que), the new subject and the verb form. Pairs show the rule: "Quiero hablar" (same person, infinitive) versus "Quiero que hables" (another person, subjunctive); "Creo que es" versus "No creo que sea". Friends reply in character, so a confusing answer gets "¿Qué?".
  - The aha or the end state: the student's chat history becomes a sorted chart of the triggers they used (wishes, emotions, doubts, recommendations), and they write one longer advice message on their own.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): an advice-column page where the class answers the same letter; vosotros toggle.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Intermediate High: connected sentences expressing wishes, doubts, emotions and recommendations with the present subjunctive.
- Not like: debate-en-español (positions on public issues); el-restaurante (likes and requests); detective-del-pasado (past narration).
- Art: drawn in code for the phone chat; illustrated friends' avatars (people, illustrated).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Friends text you everyday problems in Spanish; reply with advice, wishes and doubts, choosing the trigger and the verb, and find that the subjunctive appears when one person wants, doubts or recommends something about another.
- Flags: needs a data source (recorded Spanish voices optional); keep problems everyday (no mental health crises, rule 8 teacher note if a topic comes close).
