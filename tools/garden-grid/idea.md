# Garden Grid (idea card)

- Module: math-2-shapes-and-equal-shares, grade 2. Codes: 2.G.A
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Plan a community garden: build beds with the shapes the gardeners ask for, split rectangle beds into rows and columns of square plots and count them, and share beds fairly among two, three or four gardeners.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an empty lot, a garden club of neighbors with requests. "Mr. Diaz wants a bed with five sides. Drag corners to build it."
  - Core loop, three kinds of jobs:
    - Shape orders: build beds from attribute clues (a triangle, a quadrilateral, a pentagon, a hexagon; "four sides and four square corners"); a cube compost bin shows its six equal faces.
    - Plot grids: drag row and column lines across a rectangle bed to make same-size square plots, then count them (3 rows of 5 is 15 plots).
    - Fair shares: split identical beds into halves, thirds and fourths; one bed split into two triangles, another into two rectangles, and the club agrees both are halves.
  - The aha or the end state: equal shares of the same bed can look different and still be fair; the garden fills with plants.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a seasonal view where the planted garden grows.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 2.G.A: building and naming shapes from attributes (2.G.1), partitioning a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares and counting them (2.G.2), and partitioning circles and rectangles into halves, thirds and fourths, with equal shares that need not look alike (2.G.3).
- Not like: pizza-party (grade 1 halves and fourths), tile-the-floor (grade 3 area and multiplication), equal-groups-zoo (grade 3 arrays as multiplication).
- Art: generated scenes (a lively community garden), people (illustrated) for the garden club.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Plan a community garden: build beds the gardeners describe (a pentagon, four square corners), split rectangle beds into rows and columns of square plots and count them, and share beds in halves, thirds and fourths.
- Flags: overlap risk (prime-garden also plants rows and columns; see that card's title proposal)
