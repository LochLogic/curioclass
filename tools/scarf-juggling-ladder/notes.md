## What it teaches

Students practice a six-step progression from one scarf to a three-scarf cascade. They set an observable goal,
record their own practice, and read session bests and dips without comparing themselves with other students.

## Key skills

- Toss across the body and catch with a palm-down claw.
- Time a new toss near the previous scarf's highest point.
- Record a best run and check three movement cues.
- Find session bests and compare changes on a chart.
- Choose a reset after a drop and decide what to practice next.

## Support at home

- Ask your child to describe one cue they practiced and demonstrate its hand shape without a prop.
- Invite them to sketch a few practice results on scrap paper and point to a dip and what came afterward.
- During an everyday task that takes practice, try one slow breath or shaking out hands before trying again.

## A five-minute start

Use three light juggling scarves per student. Keep the practice space clear so arms can extend in every direction
without touching anyone or anything. Set devices on a stable surface outside the movement space.
Start with rung 1. Tap Show me, use Step to pause on a cue, then practice with real scarves for one minute.
Choose a reset before the try. Record the best run, with zero allowed, and optional cue checks afterward.
Choose Not needed if there was no drop or the reset was not used. Saving keeps the rung fixed until the entry is complete.
These are personal observations, not verified performance scores.

## Modes

Ladder: all six rungs are open. Suggested goals are 10, 10, 5, 5, 3 and 10, in the displayed rung units.
Rung 4 counts a good round only when all three scarves cross in order, even though they fall.
A ready mark means two recorded tries met the suggestion. It does not certify a motor skill or unlock anything.
Partner counts keeps the longest uninterrupted run; Drop ends a run, and Undo restores the previous action.
The timer is practice time, not a race. Done early can log after 10 seconds. Stop logs nothing.

My log: set a goal and prediction before opening personal results. Ren's sample is explicitly fictional and never
saves into the student's log. Read a first and latest session best, select a matching sentence, then find a dip.
An incorrect dot is explained and the correct best is ringed. There are no points for completing these steps.
The final card identifies session bests shown with help and a sentence corrected after a wrong choice.
Ren's prediction and recovery are labeled as Ren's; they are not observations about the student.
Strength and next reveals saved cue tallies only after two choices, with at least three self-checked tries.
Reset counts Yes as helped, reports A little separately, and reports ties honestly.

## Talk questions

- Which hand was free to catch after the second toss?
- What happened in the session after a dip?
- Does one lower result mean practice is not helping? What else could you check?
- Does the cue tally match the strength and next cue you picked?

## Good to know

One noisy chart is limited evidence. A change of at least two is called up or down; a difference within one is
about the same. Those are this tool's reading rules, not a scientific threshold for learning.
Practice spread across days can support motor learning (Shea, Lai, Black and Park, 2000; Lee and Genovese, 1988).
Those studies do not guarantee a particular gain for every child or skill. The tool does not make that promise.
The adult juggling study used a three-ball cascade, not this child scarf activity, and does not predict an
individual child's brain changes (Draganski et al., Nature 427, 2004; University of Washington summary).

Alberta Education's A(K-3)13 and Bethel's progression differ about catch height. Both teach palm-down catching;
this tool deliberately does not prescribe a catch height. The illustrated catch is a readable overhand example.
Adapt the reach to the student's comfortable range and let them practice seated if needed. Never require a
student to copy an illustrated body shape or range exactly.

SHAPE America 2024: 1.5.10 and 1.5.12 are practiced with real scarves; 4.5.6 through the goal;
4.5.7 through self-checks and cue reflection; 4.5.9 through reset selection and evaluation.
