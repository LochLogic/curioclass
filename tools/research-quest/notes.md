## What it teaches

Students plan and carry out a short research project, organize evidence for an audience, and connect an unanswered question to an earlier research choice. They practice keeping source details and distinguishing a quotation, a paraphrase, and a changed meaning.

## Key skills

- Choose a question that can guide a useful project.
- Plan visits to several kinds of sources.
- Keep a source with a note and preserve the source's meaning.
- Organize notes into an outline and choose supported plan steps.
- Present findings and use audience questions to revise a research process.

## Support at home

- Choose a small household question together. Ask what kinds of places could help answer it.
- Read a short passage aloud. Put it aside and explain its meaning in your own words, then compare.
- Ask your child to explain a recommendation and where one supporting fact came from.

## A 5-minute start

1. Read the garden brief and choose a research question.
2. Choose four places to visit. Discuss what each could contribute.
3. Make one note. Compare the source passage with the exact note that went into the pack.
4. Continue independently or in pairs. Leave the council's judgment until the presentation.

## One quest, five stops

Ask, Plan, Gather, Organize and Present form one branching story. There is no score and no answer marking during research. Students choose four of six places, make one note per place, place every note under a heading or in Not needed, and pick one to three plan steps.

Every note can be quoted, copied without quotation marks, or written using one of three paraphrases. Paraphrase order changes each run. A note can be saved with or without its source; Redo note replaces it before Organize.

At the council, five questions always appear. A meaning-changing note in the outline adds a source-comparison question. Choosing Butterfly Blend adds an unanswered ingredients question. A too-close paraphrase in the outline adds an aside. Unanswered questions decide approval, approval with named homework, or a request to research more.

## Talk about it

- What could each place tell us that another place could not?
- Which wording preserved a source's meaning? What changed in the other versions?
- Did your outline make an available note useful to the audience?
- Follow one Trail back line. What earlier choice would you revise, and why?
- In a second run, change one choice and predict which council answer will change.

On Trail back, hover over a question or reach it with Tab to isolate its line and destination. Open the question to inspect the exact saved choice before deciding where to rewind.

## Good to know

The school, county, people, documents and seed advertisement are made up. The biological claims follow the sources in the spec. Bramble County's regional recommendation belongs to the fictional setting; this is not a planting guide for a student's actual region. The video is not a hoax; it is less useful for this question.

The council follows the spec's deliberately limited rules. It is not a general truth checker. A changed-meaning note triggers a separate question even if its heading satisfies another question. Quotation marks are acceptable when a source is kept. Close paraphrasing receives a teaching aside rather than a separate failed question.

The run is saved only on this device using rq-run in localStorage. No personal information is entered or sent. Rewind asks for confirmation and preserves choices at the reopened stop while clearing later stops. Start over asks for confirmation. Keyboard users can tap a note and then a heading instead of dragging. Motion removes animation without delaying actions. Read speaks the open chapter or pack card. Narration uses the browser voice until the supplied smooth-narrator lines are recorded separately.

## Standards in the activity

AASL Inquire and C3 D1.5.6-8 are practiced in the question and visit plan. C3 D3.1.6-8 and CCSS W.6.7, W.7.7, W.8.7 are practiced through four kinds of collected evidence and an outline addressing the question. AASL Engage VI.B.1 is practiced through source retention, quoting and paraphrasing. AASL Collaborate is represented by presenting a plan to a group and responding to its questions; partners can discuss the automatic answers and revision decisions.
