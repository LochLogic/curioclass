## What it teaches

An open research desk exposes the relationship between source wording, citation, note placement and the claims an outline can support.
Question, Sources, Notes, Outline and Evidence are files that can be opened in any order. There is no score, grade or terminal result.

## Key skills

- Preserve meaning when keeping a note.
- Keep a source with quotations and paraphrases.
- Connect a question, relevant sources and an outline.
- Follow an evidence gap back to the choice that caused it.

## Support at home

Compare a saved note with its source. Move it to another heading and inspect how its evidence route changes.
Try quoting and paraphrasing the same passage, then compare the meaning.

## Operation

Notes is the first working file. Four useful source visits are selected as a starting setup; change them in Sources.
Every file opens immediately. Selecting a note opens its full wording over the note tray; tap a heading to place it.
Dragging also works. The evidence map is always available, including when no notes exist.
Revisit a file preserves work. Rewind in an evidence card asks before clearing later choices.
Clear desk is available through the research pack; confirmation is required.

## Evidence rules

Unvisited source: Sources. Missing note, changed meaning, missing citation or unquoted copy: Notes.
Wrong heading, note excluded or missing plan step: Outline. Unsuitable question: Question.
The source-credit question checks every outlined note, and its detail lists every citation issue.
Changed meanings do not support garden, milkweed, plant suitability or flower timing questions.
An outline of credited quotations is allowed; it invites an uncounted own-words question.
Close paraphrases invite comparison. These limited rules are not a general truth checker.
