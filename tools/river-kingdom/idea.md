# River Kingdom (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-6-mesopotamia-and-egypt, grade 6. Codes: D2.His.1.6-8, D2.Geo.4.6-8, D2.Civ.5.6-8
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Run a farming kingdom twice, first on the Nile, whose flood comes on a steady yearly schedule, then on the Tigris and Euphrates, whose floods come hard and at the wrong time; the same tools (canals, granaries, scribes, laws) lead to different results, and the student sees how each river shaped its people's work, land and government.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two river valleys side by side, one sealed until the first is played.
  - Core loop: each year, assign workers to dig canals, store grain, build and keep records; settle disputes from a case pile (a neighbor's broken dike flooded my field) and carve the rule on a stone. Irrigation without drainage slowly salts the Mesopotamian fields.
  - The aha or the end state: Egypt's predictable flood favors one ruler over a long valley; Mesopotamia's risky rivers favor walled city-states, strict water laws and constant repair. The student's carved laws are laid beside real ones from Hammurabi's code (for example, the law on a neglected dike).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "what if the Nile failed?" year.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.1.6-8: the side-by-side ending connects each kingdom's choices to its river and to the wider Near East.
  - D2.Geo.4.6-8: farming and irrigation choices change the land (salty soil, canals) and daily life.
  - D2.Civ.5.6-8: the carved laws and who makes them show the origin and structure of each government, compared with Hammurabi's code.
- Not like: hunter-to-farmer (the start of farming); museum-label-writer (how writing began); tenochtitlan-builder (engineering a lake city).
- Art: generated scenes for both valleys; public domain objects (Met open access Egyptian and Mesopotamian items, ids to check at spec), because real objects anchor the story.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run a farming kingdom on the steady Nile, then on the flood-prone Tigris and Euphrates: dig canals, store grain and carve laws in stone, and see how each river shaped its land, its work and its government.
- Flags: sensitive (simplified comparison; fresh review for accuracy)
