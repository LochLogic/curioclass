# Nerve Signal Relay (idea card)

- Module: science-7-senses-and-the-brain, grade 7. Codes: MS-LS1-8
- Format / family: race / none. Fit: good, made solo first (the concept line assumed a class)
- One idea: Race your own reaction time: tap when a light flashes or a tone plays, watch the signal's path light up from receptor to brain to finger, and find that a practiced sequence gets faster as it is stored as memory while a new choice stays slow.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an illustrated figure with the eye, ear and hand marked, and a big tap pad.
  - Core loop: short rounds against a ghost of the student's own best: a light round, a tone round, a choice round (tap only for green) and a sequence round repeated ten times. After each tap the path lights up (receptor, nerve, spinal cord or brain, muscle) with the measured time. Short info cards explain rods and cones, hair cells in the ear and touch receptors.
  - The aha or the end state: a graph of the student's own times: the choice round is slower than the simple round, and the practiced sequence speeds up over tries. The student writes one sentence each for "quick reaction" and "stored memory", using their data and the cards.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the classic ruler-drop or hand-squeeze chain as an off-screen activity with a results sheet.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-LS1-8: the info cards and the student's own timing data are synthesized into an explanation that receptors send messages to the brain for immediate behavior or storage as memories.
- Not like: motion-graph-match (built race about graphs); how-animals-see (built, other animals' eyes); body-thermostat (grade 9 feedback).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the figure, with the nerve path drawn in code over it.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Race your own reaction time: tap when a light flashes or a tone sounds, watch the signal travel from eye or ear to brain to finger, and see practice make a sequence faster while a new choice stays slow.
- Flags: accessibility (the tone round needs a visual alternative, and the flash round must stay within safe flash rates for photosensitive users)
