# Summary Squeeze (idea card)

- Module: ela-6-central-idea-and-summary, grade 6. Codes: RI.6.2, RL.6.2
- Format / family: race / none. Fit: change to puzzle, because summarizing is about what survives a shrinking word budget, not speed; a clock pushes students to drop words at random. The squeeze itself (50, 25, 10 words) is the level ladder.
- One idea: The student squeezes a passage through a press that allows 50, then 25, then 10 words, and a detail gauge shows which key details still survive each squeeze and which opinion words sneaked in, so they learn that a summary keeps the central idea and its main support, and that a story's last squeeze is its theme, not its plot.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a short article (how beavers change a whole valley) sits on the press bed; the press reads "Budget: 50 words".
  - Core loop: the student writes a summary in the press box; a word counter fills like a pressure gauge. Press "Squeeze" and the detail gauge lights the passage's key details the summary carries (matched by word families the student confirms, "you wrote 'dams', does that cover 'build barriers across streams'? yes / no") and flags opinion and judgment words ("amazing", "I think", "should") as leaks. Each level cuts the budget; the student decides which details are support and which are only interesting.
  - The aha or the end state: at 10 words, informational passages end on the central idea; for a short public-domain fable or story, a 10-word plot summary ("The fox tricks the crow and gets the cheese") fails the gauge, and the student must name what the details add up to (the theme). Side by side, the three squeezes show what each budget kept.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "beat your own squeeze" replay, the same passage scored for details kept per word.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RI.6.2: finding the central idea of an informational passage, deciding which details convey it, and writing an objective summary at three sizes.
  - RL.6.2: the story levels, where the last squeeze must name the theme and the gauge shows which details convey it, with personal opinion flagged out.
- Not like: main-idea-pinboard (grades 3-5, pinning sentences that prove a main idea); summary-scaffold (6-8 organizer with citation frames); theme-evidence-ladder (ranking evidence for a theme, not compressing).
- Art: generated scenes for each passage topic (beaver valley, a lighthouse keeper, a fable scene), drawn in code for the press and gauge.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Squeeze a passage into 50, then 25, then 10 words; a gauge shows which key details survive and which opinion words leak in, and a story's last squeeze must name its theme, not its plot.
- Flags: overlap risk (light, summary-scaffold covers objective summary in 6-8; this one owns the compression idea)
