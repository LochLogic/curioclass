# Research packet: revolution-road
Concept: Walk from 1763 to the Treaty of Paris on four parallel lanes (Patriot, Loyalist, Native nations, enslaved and free Black Americans), link causes to effects, and explain why people living at the same time chose different sides.
Grade: 5   Standards: D2.His.1.3-5, D2.His.4.3-5, D2.His.14.3-5, D2.His.5.3-5

## Standards in plain words

- D2.His.1.3-5: "Create and use a chronological sequence of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to use a timeline to compare what happened to Patriots, Loyalists, Native nations, and Black Americans across the same historical period.
- D2.His.4.3-5: "Explain why individuals and groups during the same historical period differed in their perspectives." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to explain the differing reasons people in the 1770s had for supporting independence, staying loyal to the King, or pursuing tribal sovereignty and personal freedom.
- D2.His.5.3-5: "Explain connections among historical contexts and people's perspectives at the time." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to connect each group's wartime choices to their legal status, land ownership, and economic interests.
- D2.His.14.3-5: "Explain probable causes and effects of events and developments." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to trace how British policies and wartime treaties caused specific outcomes for each community.

## Common mistakes

- Viewing the Revolution as a unanimous Patriot effort: Fifth graders often assume all colonists wanted independence, missing that roughly 20 percent remained loyal to the King and many stayed neutral (Museum of the American Revolution).
- Assuming all Black Americans supported the Patriots: Students frequently assume enslaved people sided with the founders, unaware that far more fled to British lines under promises of emancipation (NMAAHC).
- Seeing Native nations as minor participants: Students often view Native nations as background figures rather than sovereign nations executing diplomatic strategy to protect their homelands (Smithsonian NMAI NK360).
- Imagining the 1783 Treaty of Paris solved all conflicts: Students assume the treaty brought peace everywhere, missing that Britain handed over Native lands without tribal consent, sparking immediate Northwest frontier warfare (Museum of the American Revolution).

## Sensitivity

- Multi-perspective historical balance: Portray Patriots, Loyalists, Native nations, and Black Americans with equal historical seriousness. Present choices as rational decisions based on survival, land rights, legal protection, and freedom.
- Confronting enslavement contradictions: Accurately explain the paradox of Patriot leaders fighting for liberty while maintaining chattel slavery.
- Sovereign Indigenous agency: Highlight that Native nations fought to defend their own land and independence, not merely as pawns of European empires. Consult Smithsonian NMAI NK360.

## Gaps

- Parallel lane visualization: Presenting four lanes over twenty years simultaneously on a single screen can overwhelm fifth graders; Curio Class spec writers should divide the timeline into 4 pivotal milestone checkpoints (1763 Proclamation, 1775 Dunmore, 1776 Declaration, 1783 Treaty) allowing students to toggle and compare the 4 lanes cleanly at each step.
