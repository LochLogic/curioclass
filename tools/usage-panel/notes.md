## What it teaches
Students can explain that usage conventions depend on setting and can change over time. They weigh five scripted perspectives, compare dated evidence, and write a contextual usage note.

## Key skills
- Distinguishing a writer's use from a style guide's ruling.
- Comparing evidence from different dates and different test sentences.
- Weighing clarity, convention, rhythm and audience expectations.
- Making and explaining different choices for different settings.
- Revising a judgment after considering another perspective.

## Support at home
- Ask a family member about a language rule they learned at school. Discuss whether they use it in every setting.
- Compare a casual message and a news headline. Talk about why the wording might differ.
- Choose one word that people argue about. Discuss what evidence could help, without calling someone's home language wrong.

## A five-minute start
Open They in Hearing. Tap the Copy Editor, then the Linguist. Make a private margin note, vote on a sentence, then compare that vote with the dated AHD result. Open Record and compare Chicago in 2017 and 2024. Open Ruling and choose differently for a text and a news story.

## Modes
Hearing offers three or four sentences per case, five perspectives, private notes and dated evidence. Keys 1 to 5 select panelists. Previous and Next wrap through the sentences. Your sentence vote reveals the real panel result only for that sentence.

Record separates round in-use markers from square authority rulings. Leaders connect readable labels to their real year positions. The slider uses one-year arrow steps, Page Up and Page Down for the next and previous marker (including separate rulings in the same year), and Home and End for its limits. Play has eight seconds of moving time plus 1.5 seconds per evidence marker. Motion off keeps the same timing with year jumps. Any marker, slider interaction, mode change or case change stops playback.

Ruling has four independent radio groups and five reasons. A persuasive Hearing mark preselects its reason. The note rebuilds when settings or reasons change; edit it afterward. Rebuild note and Clear ruling ask for confirmation. Copy note copies only the note. The ruled docket label means at least one setting has a choice; it is not a grade or a claim that the student used evidence.

## Talk questions
- Why might the same sentence work in a text but distract readers in an essay?
- Which panelist persuaded you, and what evidence might change your mind?
- Does a writer using a form prove that every editor accepts it?
- What can a panel percentage establish, and what can it not establish?
- How does a dated preference become a supposedly timeless rule?

## Good to know
This is a judgment exercise with no right answers, score or timer. Panelists are scripted roles, not real people or quotations from the authorities. Their statements are arguments to evaluate, not advice that every student or reader shares.

Known-person they is handled separately. The Jordan sentence asks no acceptability vote and displays the pronoun-use note. The contextual rulings concern generic singular they. Treat people's pronouns respectfully; do not invite classmates to debate another student's identity.

The historical axis ends at 2026. It displays only the specified dated record, not a complete history or a claim that no earlier authority existed. A dashed unknown stance means no ruling in this dataset. An AHD percentage concerns the exact named sentence and year, not all uses of that word.

The stance board carries a recorded result forward until another dated result appears. Each percentage labels its survey year; moving to 2026 does not turn a 2015 survey into a new survey. In the evidence drawer, Read starts with the first fully visible card. Scroll to bring another card into view, then use Read again.

Approximate dates retain their labels: about 1300, early 1590s, early 1200s, 1300s and 1960s. Their numeric slider placements are 1300, 1592, 1200, 1350 and 1960. The Merriam-Webster Literally card is a current entry at 2026, not a claimed addition date.

Settings, votes, private notes and edited usage notes are stored only in this browser on this device when storage is available. Nothing leaves the device. Storage failures preserve this session's work. Clearing a ruling preserves sentence votes and Hearing marks.

Small previews retain the central sentence or settings grid. Longer reference material and the usage note can scroll in their own view. Read speaks the visible sentence, selected statement or selected evidence together with the current action hint. No audio is recorded in this Build run; browser speech provides fallback narration. The planned recorded voice is firm-captain / Alnilam.
