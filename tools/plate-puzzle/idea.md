# Plate Puzzle (idea card)

- Module: science-6-plate-tectonics, grade 6. Codes: MS-ESS2-1, MS-ESS2-2, MS-ESS2-3
- Format / family: puzzle / none. Fit: good for MS-ESS2-3 only
- One idea: Fit the continents back together, one kind of evidence per level (coastlines, fossils, rock belts, seafloor age stripes), then run the plates backward and forward 200 million years to test the fit.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a world map with the continents as loose pieces and a clue drawer.
  - Core loop: level 1 uses coastlines only (Africa and South America); level 2 adds fossils found on both sides of an ocean (Mesosaurus, Glossopteris); level 3 adds rock belts and old mountain chains that line up across the Atlantic; level 4 adds seafloor age stripes that mirror each other around the mid-ocean ridge; level 5 sets the speed (a few centimeters a year, about as fast as fingernails grow) and plays the motion through time.
  - The aha or the end state: four independent kinds of data agree. The human side: Alfred Wegener argued for drifting continents in 1912 and was widely dismissed; Marie Tharp's seafloor maps in the 1950s helped turn the argument.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): run the plates 50 million years into the future.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-ESS2-3: each level adds one data type named in the expectation (fossils and rocks, continental shapes, seafloor structures) as evidence of past plate motion.
  - MOVE MS-ESS2-1: new tool: "Rock Recycler" (grade 6), follow one mineral grain through melting, cooling, weathering, burial and uplift, with the sun and Earth's inner heat as the two energy sources.
  - MOVE MS-ESS2-2: the same new tool "Rock Recycler", where each step runs on its real clock (a landslide in seconds, a delta over centuries, uplift over millions of years), so change at different time and space scales is taught in full.
- Not like: canyon-reader (grade 4 rock layers); earth-s-calendar (time scale from strata); erosion-table (grade 4 surface processes).
- Art: map pieces drawn in code from public domain map data; generated illustrations for the fossil and rock clues.
