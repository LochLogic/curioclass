# Flashback Editor (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-narrative-techniques, grade 8. Codes: W.8.3
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good: the student rebuilds a story's timeline and writes the joins that make it work.
- One idea: The student takes a short story told in order, cuts it into scenes on a film editor's timeline, and rebuilds it with a flashback, but every jump in time needs a bridge they write (a transition, a sensory trigger, a line of reflection), and a test reader gets lost at any unbridged jump, so they learn that a flashback is a pacing tool and the time shifts must be signaled.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an editing bay with a story by Curio Class in seven scenes (a girl's last swim meet; the summer she nearly quit after a fall at practice) on a timeline in the order things happened.
  - Core loop: the student drags scenes into a new order (open at the starting block, flash back to the fall, return for the race). Each cut between times shows a gap marker; the student writes the bridge: a transition phrase ("Two summers ago..."), a sensory trigger (the smell of chlorine pulls her back), or a line of reflection on the way out. A pacing strip shows each scene's length; they can slow a key moment with description and dialogue or summarize a dull stretch. A test reader reads the cut and drops a "lost" flag where they can't tell when they are.
  - The aha or the end state: the original and the edit play side by side with the pacing strip, and the student writes an ending that reflects on the whole experience, then a note on what the flashback let the story do (hold back the fear until the start gun).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): students import their own narrative scenes and edit them.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.8.3: organizing the sequence with a flashback, transitions that signal time shifts, pacing with description and dialogue, reflection, and a conclusion that reflects on the experience.
- Not like: blizzard-scenes (6-8, writing a scene from new points of view and orders); tension-meter (grade 9, graphing suspense in a finished story); slow-mo-writer (grade 5, slowing down one moment).
- Art: generated scenes for each story scene (pool, starting block, the fall), drawn in code for the timeline and pacing strip.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Cut a story into scenes on an editor's timeline and rebuild it with a flashback; write the bridge at every time jump (transition, trigger or reflection) and a test reader flags anywhere the shift leaves them lost.
- Flags: overlap risk (blizzard-scenes also carries W.8.3; keep this one on flashback structure and time-shift transitions)
