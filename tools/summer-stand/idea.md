# Lemonade Stand (idea card)

- Module: math-6-dependent-and-independent-variables, grade 6. Codes: 6.EE.C, NSPFE.Earning Income
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Run a summer drink stand where each choice ties one quantity to another (cups sold to money earned, hours open to cups sold), shown at once as a table, a graph and an equation, then compare the stand's earnings with a babysitting job paid by the hour.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a sidewalk stand on a hot morning, a price board, a jar of coins, and a neighbor, Mrs. Chen. "Pick a price per cup, then open up."
  - Core loop: each day the student sets the price and the hours; customers come, and a ledger records cups and money. The tool asks which quantity depends on which, then shows earnings = 1.50 x cups as a table, a graph and an equation side by side. Changing the price changes the steepness of the line.
  - Variable rounds: the student names the independent and dependent variable in new pairs (hours open and cups sold, cups sold and lemons used, temperature and customers) and writes an equation when the pattern is steady.
  - Earning rounds: supplies cost money, so income is not profit; the student finds the day's profit. Then an offer: babysit at $12 an hour. Two lines on one graph show which pays more for 3 hours, and what the stand gives that a wage does not (being your own boss, the risk of a rainy day).
  - The aha or the end state: when one quantity depends on another, a table, a graph and an equation are three views of the same rule.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): class compares price choices and the lines they make.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 6.EE.C (6.EE.9): two quantities that change together, named as independent and dependent, written as an equation like e = 1.5c, and analyzed with tables and graphs.
  - NSPFE.Earning Income: income from selling goods vs wages by the hour, costs and profit, and the trade-offs of running your own small business.
- Not like: tax-and-tip (grade 7 percents and proportionality), corner-store (grade 2 coins and change), two-trains (grade 8 systems).
- Art: generated scenes (a summer street, the stand, weather); people (illustrated) for Mrs. Chen and varied customers; ledger, table and graph drawn in code.
- Proposed title: Summer Stand (the old title is also the name of a well-known classic computer game)
- Proposed concept line: Run a summer drink stand where cups sold and money earned move together in a table, graph and equation; name which quantity depends on which, find profit after supplies, and compare it with babysitting at $12 an hour.
- Flags: none beyond the title
