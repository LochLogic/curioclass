## What it teaches

Students compare seller concentration, buyer options and the consequences of a buyout. They use their own market runs and a remedy ledger to explain a government role and defend a policy tradeoff.

## Key skills

- Test how price, quality and research change sales and profit.
- Distinguish brands, owners, shoppers and shares of sales.
- Compare buyer gains with a total that includes owners and government costs.
- Support a policy choice with numbers and an explanation of its mechanism.

## Support at home

- Name a familiar product together. Discuss whether its different brands might have the same owner.
- Talk about a time you could switch sellers after a price change. What made switching easy or difficult?
- Compare two possible rules for a fictional market. Who benefits, who pays, and what could happen later?

## Five-minute start

Open Rivals. Keep Solid and Steady, raise Price, and press Next quarter. Try the same change in Buyout. Watch both the buyers and those keeping an old charger. Each painted figure represents about 5,000 people; allocation uses largest remainders, while the numerical readouts retain the full model calculation.

## Modes

Rivals and Buyout keep separate eight-quarter records. Rivals post prices before your next decision. Research launches appear the following quarter. Play to the end can be paused, and controls remain live. Run again asks before clearing only that run.

Compare shows played quarters only. Finish fills the remaining quarters using that run's current choices. Make three competition ratings, run the Switch test, and mark three buyer measures after at least four quarters per run. Ratings teach through feedback and carry no score.

The 7,500-point concentration increase compares four equal owners (HHI 2,500) with one owner (10,000). Your own final Rivals HHI can differ. In the Switch test, the short tracks show buyers who stay, switch to rivals, or go without; stripes mark going without.

Chart patterns also identify series: the run's price is solid and its share dotted; Compare uses solid Rivals, dotted Buyout and a dashed profit-seeking reference. The same labels and numbers remain readable in grayscale.

Compare price and quality use sales weights. The quality name rounds that average to the nearest tier; newest model means the highest wattage sold. The Switch test fixes Solid, 20 W chargers and excludes research spending so only the price change is compared.

Regulator replays model firms, not the student's runs. Open several remedies and inspect both views. The default Buyers' view ranks Block it first; Everyone's view ranks Do nothing first. A different price cap can alter that order. Memo has Reasons, Evidence and Write leaves. Its check verifies structure, reason fit and evidence provenance, not whether the student's prose is persuasive. Save stores the explanation and selected evidence on the same device. Nothing is sent.

An explanation needs twelve words and eight different words. Isolated alphabet letters do not count as words, apart from “a” and “I”. Passing this structure check is a starting point for discussion, not a grade for economic reasoning.

## Talk questions

- Four equal sellers have HHI 2,500. Why can a shopper still have a meaningful choice?
- Can a generous owner give buyers a good deal? What forces it to keep doing so?
- Which costs are left outside the two-year model?
- How does the result change when buyers' losses and owners' gains count differently?
- Why would monitoring a real price cap need to watch quality too?

## Good to know

This is a fictional logit choice model, not an estimate of a real charger market. Each quarter has 100,000 shoppers, each buying one charger or none. Served totals count visits across quarters, not distinct people. Quality, power and price determine shares; research is deterministic. Buyer gain is the model's aggregate value net of price, not money refunded to buyers. Everyone's total equals buyer gain plus all owners' profit minus government cost. It does not measure fairness, employment, environmental costs or outcomes beyond two years.

Model parameters: spread $5; base value $10; each model step adds $4; quality values $0, $4, $7, $9.50, $11.50; costs $6, $8, $10, $13, $16. Fixed cost is $60,000 per brand per quarter. Research is $0, $30,000 or $60,000 per owner, adding 0, 35 or 55 points. Each 100 cumulative points launches the next model in the next quarter. Top model is 65 W; research then costs nothing. Buyout savings are $0.50 per unit. Breakup savings are $0.25 after Q4. Government costs are illustrative, not real-case costs.

## Sources and limits

- [DOJ HHI](https://www.justice.gov/atr/herfindahl-hirschman-index) and [2023 Merger Guidelines, section 2.1](https://www.justice.gov/atr/merger-guidelines/applying-merger-guidelines/guideline-1): squared owner shares, 1,800 and 100 thresholds. These are dated US guidance, not a universal rule that a merger is automatically illegal.
- [FTC Staples case](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/9710008-staples-inc-office-depot-inc) and FTC v. Staples, 970 F. Supp. 1066 (1997): blocked merger and pricing evidence.
- [Standard Oil opinion](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/221/1): 1911 ruling under the Sherman Act of 1890; the number of successor companies is described conservatively as more than 30.
- [Federal Judicial Center, AT&T](https://www.fjc.gov/history/spotlight-judicial-history/breakup-ma-bell): 1974 suit, 1982 agreement and 1984 separation into seven regional operating companies, retaining long distance.
- [Littlechild's 2024 account](https://www.jbs.cam.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024-eprg-littlechild-incentive-regulation.pdf): the 1983 RPI-X proposal; government later chose X = 3 for BT's 1984 cap. [Oftel's 1986 report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7cac6fe5274a2f304ef634/0007.pdf) records price and service-quality monitoring. The fictional cap is a dollar ceiling, not the historical formula.
- [European Commission common charger](https://single-market-economy.ec.europa.eu/sectors/electrical-and-electronic-engineering-industries-eei/radio-equipment-directive-red/one-common-charging-solution-all_en): Directive (EU) 2022/2380 and the 2024 and 2026 dates. Real device power requirements still matter; fictional entrants are a teaching assumption.
- Schumpeter, Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy (1942); [Arrow (1962)](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/rate-and-direction-inventive-activity-economic-and-social-factors/economic-welfare-and-allocation-resources-invention); Gilbert (2006): competing innovation arguments.
- [Cunningham, Ederer and Ma (2021)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/712506): acquired overlapping drug projects were less likely to develop. It is not evidence about charger mergers.
- Williamson, Economies as an Antitrust Defense, AER 58(1), 1968, and OECD, The Standard for Merger Review, 2011: possible savings versus lost-trade tradeoff. Distribution remains a separate question.
