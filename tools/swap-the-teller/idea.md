# Swap the Teller (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-5-narrators-and-points-of-view, grade 3-5. Codes: RL.3.6, RI.3.6, RL.4.6, RL.5.6
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: One mix-up at a county fair (the prize pie goes missing) is told by a first-person kid, a third-person narrator and a grumpy goose, and the student switches tellers at each turn of the story, so they see that who tells a story decides what you learn, what you miss and how you feel about it.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a fairground map with three teller badges: "I" (Jun, age 9), "the narrator", and the goose. "Pick who tells the first part."
  - Core loop: at each part, choose the teller; the same events come out differently (Jun only knows what Jun saw; the narrator sees the whole fair; the goose thinks the pie was a gift). A clue board fills with facts only some tellers include. At each part the student marks "I, me, my" or "he, she, they" telling and answers "What does this teller not know?"
  - The aha or the end state: the student picks who took the pie and why, then reads the fair manager's newsletter article about the day, an informational text with its own view ("the goose is a menace"), and says where their own view matches or differs from the manager's and the tellers'.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): write part five from a teller of your choice.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.3.6: the student's own view of the culprit set against each teller's view.
  - RI.3.6: the fair manager's newsletter: the student's view against the author's.
  - RL.4.6: first-person Jun against the third-person narrator, part by part.
  - RL.5.6: the clue board shows how each teller's view changes how events are described.
- Not like: two-sides-theater (grade 2, voicing two characters); who-said-that (grade 1, who is talking); perspective-camera (grade 6, how an author builds a narrator).
- Art: people (illustrated) for Jun and the fair, an illustrated goose, generated fair scenes, because each teller's view needs its own look.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A missing prize pie at a county fair is told by a first-person kid, a third-person narrator and a grumpy goose; switch tellers to see what each knows, then weigh your own view against theirs and the fair manager's newsletter.
- Flags: none
