## A 5-minute start

1. The **Pencil** case is open. Press **Next clue** and read it together. Ask: "What could that mean?"
2. After all three clues, let the class pick the natural resources they think it came from. More than one is allowed.
3. Press **Follow the trail!** The red string goes from the pencil, to how it was made, back to nature.

## The big idea

Everything we use started as a natural resource: trees, plants, water, soil, rocks or animals. People change those resources to make the things we need. Many things come from more than one resource. A pencil needs trees for the wood and rocks for the dark graphite.

## Talk about it

- What is something in our classroom made from trees? From rocks?
- Which resources can grow back (trees, plants, wool)? Which can't (rocks)?
- Pick something you are wearing. What natural resource might it come from?

## Good to know

- Some cases accept more than one fair answer, and the detective explains why (orange trees are plants too).
- Solved cases get a stamp, so the class can see which ones are left.
- Sound starts on (the Sound button turns it off). There's no score.
- A narrator voice (an old park ranger) reads each new case, each clue as it is pinned up, and the trail as it is followed. **Read** repeats the last thing the detective said, or the newest clue. With Sound off only **Read** speaks.
