## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Walk the street**. Read the first stop: Biscuit wants to cross to the park.
2. Children hold up one finger or two to vote, then tap the choice.
3. A safe choice moves Biscuit on; an unsafe one explains what could happen, and the class tries again.

## The three modes

- **Walk the street:** seven stops: the crosswalk, a bike helmet, buckling up in the car, a school fire drill, a hot stove, the pool and a bottle of medicine. The path across the top shows how far Biscuit has come.
- **Safe or not?:** ten of sixteen cards ("Chase a ball into the street," "Know your grown-up's phone number"). Thumbs up or thumbs down.
- **Crossing practice:** Biscuit waits at the curb. Look left, right and left again (in that order), wait while cars pass, and cross only when the walking person shows. Then keep looking.

## Teaching notes

- Each stop ends with what to do, not with what goes wrong. Keep the tone calm.
- Young children should cross streets with a grown-up. The crossing practice builds the habit, not independence.
- Fire safety: get out, stay out, never hide, never go back for toys. Practice your school's real meeting place.
- Medicine: only from a trusted grown-up. If children find pills, they tell a grown-up and don't touch them.

## Talk about it

- Which stop was hardest? Why?
- What would you tell a younger child about the stove?
- Where is our fire drill meeting place?

## Good to know

- Keys 1 to 3 switch modes.
- Some children have had scary experiences with cars, fires or water. Watch for worry and follow up quietly.
