# Constantinople Defense (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-7-fall-of-rome-and-the-byzantine-empire, grade 7. Codes: D2.His.2.6-8, D2.His.14.6-8
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Guard Constantinople through real sieges from 626 to 1453, spending each era's treasury on the land walls, the fleet and Greek fire, gold for diplomacy, and trade; each siege shows what changed (enemies, weapons, allies) and what lasted (the walls, Roman law, the Church, the harbor), and the fall in 1453 turns out to have many causes, not just cannons.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the city on its peninsula, the triple land walls, the Golden Horn and a treasury chest.
  - Core loop: an era card names the threat (626 Avars and Persians, 717 to 718 Arab siege, 1204 the Fourth Crusade, 1453 the Ottomans); the student splits the budget, runs the siege (shown as a map, not combat), and reads what really happened. Lost land and trade shrink the next era's budget.
  - The aha or the end state: a continuity and change board filled across eras, and a causes web for 1453 (cannons, the 1204 sack, lost lands and trade, a small population) that the student ranks and explains.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "Venice's view" card for 1204.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.6-8: the board classifying what changed and what continued across eight centuries.
  - D2.His.14.6-8: the causes web for the city's long survival and its fall.
- Not like: rome-republic-to-empire (the Republic's fall, a story); house-of-wisdom (knowledge kept and passed on); mongol-messenger (a relay race).
- Art: generated scenes of the city and walls; public domain Byzantine coins and objects (Met open access, ids to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Guard Constantinople through real sieges from 626 to 1453, spending on walls, fleet, diplomacy and trade; sort what changed and what lasted over eight centuries, then weigh the many causes of the city's fall.
- Flags: sensitive (war and religious conflict shown without gore and with care for all sides; fresh review)
