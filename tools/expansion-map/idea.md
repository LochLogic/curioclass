# Expansion Map (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-westward-expansion, grade 8. Codes: D2.His.4.6-8, D2.Geo.7.6-8, D2.His.14.6-8
- Format / family: map / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Slide a year scrubber from 1803 to 1853 and watch the United States add territory while trails, canals, railroads and telegraph lines spread and Native nations' lands shrink; at each event pin, read two or three dated voices (a settler, a Cherokee leader protesting removal, a Mexican family in 1848) with the factors behind each view, and link causes to effects.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an 1803 map with Native nations' lands, the US and other claims, and a year slider.
  - Core loop: move the slider; layers change (Louisiana Purchase, Indian Removal Act and the Trail of Tears, Texas, the Oregon Trail, the Mexican Cession, railroads and telegraph). Each pin opens real voices; a factors card asks what each person had at stake (land, faith, money, law, survival).
  - The aha or the end state: a cause-and-effect chain the student builds from pins (cotton land demand to removal; the telegraph and railroads to faster settlement), and a closing map comparing 1803 and 1853 Native lands.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): follow one nation's land across the scrubber.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.4.6-8: the factors card for each dated voice.
  - D2.Geo.7.6-8: transport and telegraph layers show how new technology changed connections and settlement.
  - D2.His.14.6-8: the cause-and-effect chain built from pins.
- Not like: nations-of-north-america (grade 5, Native nations by region); civil-war-map-room (campaigns); state-quest.
- Art: public domain historical maps and portraits (Library of Congress, to check at spec); land boundaries drawn in code from checked data.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Slide from 1803 to 1853 as US territory, trails, railroads and telegraph lines spread and Native nations' lands shrink; read dated voices at each event, name what shaped each view, and chain causes to effects.
- Flags: sensitive (Native removal and the Mexican-American War told plainly with care; fresh review); needs a data source (land boundaries by year, checked)
