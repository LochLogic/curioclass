# Show, Don't Tell (idea card)

- Module: ela-4-showing-character-through-details, grade 4. Codes: RL.4.3, W.4.3
- Format / family: cards / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Each card holds a telling sentence (Maya was nervous, The house was creepy); the student reads scenes to collect what characters think, say and do, then plays detail cards to rewrite the telling card as a scene that shows it, and a reader who can't see the card guesses the hidden feeling, so they learn that readers know characters from details, not labels.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a face-down telling card and a short scene. "Read the scene. What tells you how Jaylen feels? Collect the clues."
  - Core loop: Read: tap details in a scene and sort them into Thinks, Says, Does, and for setting cards Sees and Hears. Write: draw a telling card, then build a short scene with detail cards (tapping his pencil, "Is it my turn yet?") and some of the student's own sentences, with a time-word strip (later, meanwhile, at last) and an ending line. Telling words like nervous or scared, if used, get caught by a "You told!" stamp.
  - The aha or the end state: the reader character (an illustrated librarian) reads the scene and guesses the hidden feeling from the detail cards played; a mixed signal (smiling and shaking) gets "I'm not sure", so the student sharpens it.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class guesses the feeling from a student's scene.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.4.3: describing a character, a setting or an event in depth from details in the text, sorted by kind.
  - W.4.3: writing a short scene with a situation, dialogue, description, time words, sensory details and an ending.
- Not like: slow-mo-writer (grade 5, slowing a moment); character-choice-story (grade 3, traits from choices); dialogue-director (comic to prose).
- Art: people (illustrated) for scenes and the reader, because faces and gestures are the clues.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Each card holds a telling sentence (Maya was nervous); collect what characters think, say and do in short scenes, rewrite the card as a scene that shows it, and a reader guesses the hidden feeling from your details.
- Flags: none
