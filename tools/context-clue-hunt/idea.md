# Context Clue Hunt (idea card)

- Module: ela-1-word-meanings, grade 1. Codes: L.1.4, L.1.5
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good: each silly word is a case with clues.
- One idea: A silly word (glorp) has replaced a real word in a sentence, and the child collects clues (the nearby words, the picture, the word's ending, what kind of thing it is) to say what it means before the reveal, so they learn that a word's meaning can be worked out from what surrounds it.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a detective notebook and the sentence "Sam glorped the ball over the fence." "What does glorped mean? Find clues."
  - Core loop: tap clue words to collect them (ball, over the fence); look at the word's ending (-ed tells it already happened, -s tells there are many) and its root (glorp, glorps, glorped); pick a meaning from three choices; reveal. Case types vary: category cases ("A blick is an animal that has feathers and swims." The child sorts it: a bird? a fish?), define-it cases (say what it is by its group and what makes it special), real-life cases (where in your home is something cozy?), and shades cases (did she peek, glance or stare? act-out animations show each).
  - The aha or the end state: the case file shows every clue the child used, and a closing case gives no picture at all, only words.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): teacher sentence sets.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - L.1.4: sentence-level context clues, affixes and inflections as clues, finding the root word.
  - L.1.5: sorting into categories, defining by category and key traits, real-life connections, shades of meaning among verbs and adjectives (acted out).
- Not like: Vocabulary Jar (words from read-alouds, used later). Word Lab (grade 2 prefixes and compound words). Context, Roots, Dictionary (grades 6-8).
- Art: generated scenes (silly-word scenes and act-out animations), because the picture is one of the clues.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A silly word replaces a real one in a sentence; collect clues from nearby words, the picture, the word's ending and its group to work out what it means before the reveal. Some cases ask peek, glance or stare?
- Flags: none
