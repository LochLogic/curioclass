# Brain Explorer (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-brain-and-behavior, grade 12. Codes: APA.Biological
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Turn and slice an illustrated brain, open each region through a real person's case (Phineas Gage's frontal lobe, a stroke that takes speech, a split-brain patient), and zoom down to one neuron firing and one synapse to see how a message becomes behavior.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a rotating brain with labeled lobes and a case shelf: "In 1848 an iron rod passed through this man's head. He lived. What changed?"
  - Core loop: open a case; the region it involves lights up; the student predicts what the damage will change, then reads what really happened. Each region card covers structure, function and how we know (lesion cases, imaging, stimulation).
  - Zoom: from region to a neuron; the student fires an action potential, watches it travel and release neurotransmitters, then adds a drug or toxin (caffeine, an SSRI, curare) to see what each changes at the synapse.
  - Systems layer: the nervous system's divisions and the endocrine system shown as a body map, with a stress scene (a near miss on a bike) tracing both.
  - The aha or the end state: a short quiz-by-case where the student names the region and the system involved in new everyday behaviors.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a case-matching relay.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - APA.Biological: brain structures and functions, neural transmission, the nervous and endocrine systems, and the methods used to study them, each taught through cases and the neuron zoom.
- Not like: illusion-lab (perception and memory tasks); train-your-dog (learning); any science-subject body tools (this one ties biology to behavior).
- Art: generated or illustrated anatomical art (layered, medically checked) and drawn-in-code neuron animation, plus people (illustrated) for the case subjects, because a clear, accurate brain is the core of the tool.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Turn and slice an illustrated brain, open each region through a real case like Phineas Gage, then zoom to a single neuron firing and see what drugs do at the synapse.
- Flags: none
