# Research packet: settle-the-valley

Concept: Over fifty years, place farms, homes, a mill and roads in a river valley where people already live, and watch land and people change each other, from cleared forests and worn soil to a flood that forces families to move.

Grade: 4   Standards: D2.Geo.4.3-5, D2.Geo.5.3-5, D2.Geo.8.3-5, D2.Geo.9.3-5

## Standards in plain words

- D2.Geo.4.3-5 asks students to explain how cultural values and technological practices shape how communities modify and adapt to their environments.
- D2.Geo.5.3-5 asks students to explain how cultural and environmental characteristics of places transform over time as populations and land uses evolve.
- D2.Geo.8.3-5 asks students to explain how human settlements and movements depend on natural resources like flowing rivers, fertile soils, and timber.
- D2.Geo.9.3-5 asks students to analyze the effects of environmental events like severe flooding on settlement patterns and human migration.

## Real data

- USGS Hydrology and Land Cover Studies: Deforestation significantly reduces infiltration rates, driving sharp spikes in surface storm runoff and sediment transport.
- USGS Water Science School: Impervious surfaces (paved roads, roofs) inhibit infiltration, causing rapid runoff concentration in stream channels.
- USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service: Unprotected bare soil loses topsoil at orders of magnitude faster rates than forested or vegetated slopes.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming river floods are purely natural events: Fourth graders often view floods as random weather, overlooking how upstream tree clearing and building in natural floodplains worsen flood heights.
- Treating land as empty before settlers arrived: Students frequently overlook that Indigenous peoples actively managed river valleys with sustainable agriculture and fire management for generations.
- Viewing environmental change as instantaneous: Deforestation and soil depletion accumulate gradually across decades before culminating in catastrophic flood vulnerability.

## Sensitivity

- Indigenous displacement: Respectfully acknowledge that "settling" land where people already lived involved displacement of Native nations; avoid portraying the valley as unpopulated wilderness.
- Flood disaster trauma: Frame flooding as an environmental systems consequence rather than depicting gruesome destruction or personal tragedy.

## Similar tools elsewhere

- SimCity / City Skylines: Urban zoning simulations with environmental and disaster mechanics.
- National Geographic Education: Interactive human-environment interaction map activities and floodplain case studies.

## Gaps

- Fourth-grade students can grasp causal chains (cut trees -> soil washes into river -> shallow river overflows easier -> town floods), so Curio Class simulators should clearly visualize this multi-decade progression through simple 5-step visual stages rather than complex hydrodynamic equations.
