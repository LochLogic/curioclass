# Toy Ad Reality Check (idea card)

- Module: arts-3-5-reading-media, grade 3-5. Codes: MA:Re7, MA:Re8, MA:Re9, MA:Cn11
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good
- One idea: A made-up toy commercial makes the Turbo Gecko racer look huge and fast; step through it shot by shot, then open the box on a plain table with a ruler, and match each shot to the trick that made the difference (a low camera, a close-up, sped-up footage, cheering kids, a loud song).
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the 30-second ad on a TV, and next to it a plain table with the real toy still in the box.
  - Core loop: play, pause, step frame by frame; tag each shot with a trick from the case board (low angle, close-up, fast cuts, sound effects, music, crowd, small print). Then the reality test: measure the toy, time how fast it goes, see what the small print said ("batteries not included", "sold separately").
  - The aha or the end state: the case file: each trick, what it made you believe, and the reality. The student rates the ad on two scales, how well it works and how honest it is, and writes one sentence on why those can be different.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes on the most sneaky trick.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MA:Re7: identifying how the shots, sound and words are built to make you feel something.
  - MA:Re8: deciding what the ad wants you to believe.
  - MA:Re9: evaluating the ad with two criteria (effective, honest).
  - MA:Cn11: how ads aimed at kids fit everyday life, and why there are guidelines for them.
- Not like: cereal-ad-questions (K-2: the parts of an ad); viral-video-breakdown (grade 6-8: how a video spreads).
- Art: generated scenes for the ad and the plain table; made-up toys and brands only; music original to Curio Class.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (any claim about rules for children's advertising, checked); overlap risk (cereal-ad-questions)
