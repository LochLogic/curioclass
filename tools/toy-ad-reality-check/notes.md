## What it teaches

Students identify how a commercial creates a claim, test that claim, and support a judgment with evidence.
They separate a feeling about an ad from a measurement of the toy.

## Key skills

- Notice camera angle, framing, playback speed, sound and small print.
- Compare a dramatic shot with an ordinary view of the same toy.
- Measure, time and test before making a claim.
- Explain a rating with evidence rather than guessing a preferred opinion.
- Ask what an advertised price includes.

## Support at home

- Describe an ad you remember. Ask what it wanted you to believe and which detail made that feeling.
- Use a familiar object to show how close-up and low-angle views change apparent size.
- When looking at packaging already at home, find what is included and what needs something extra. Nothing needs to be bought.

## Five-minute start

Tap Play, pause Launch, tap a spot on the picture and choose Low angle. Open Plain view.
Ask whether the first picture proves the toy is large. In The lab, align the ruler with the toy and check it.
Stamp the claim using the measurement. Trade the driver role with a partner.

## Modes

The ad has six shots over 15 seconds. Pause, step back or jump on the timeline. Slow plays at half speed.
Drag a trick onto a frozen frame, or tap a spot then a card. Keyboard users can press Enter on a trick.
Music is a separate seventh card and can pin anywhere, including the honest Night shot.
Sound captions keep the sound clues available with Sound off.

The lab offers five tests in any order. The box also supplies evidence for the claim about everyone owning the toy.
The ruler moves by drag or arrow keys. Tap Check alignment after placing it.
The stopwatch accepts stops within 0.3 seconds of the fixed 1.7-second crossing.
Its timing is repeatable, so it is practice in observing a comparison, not an assessment of reaction skill.
After a close stop, both runs replay together to show the difference.
Try the box-only jump before the separate ramp. Both Stretched and Not in the box fit the canyon claim.
After a wrong stamp, repeat that experiment before choosing again. Other completed experiments are kept.

The verdict asks for the ad's intended message with two supporting claims. Both ratings accept any value from 1 to 5;
each needs evidence. Check the four rules, add the full setup cost, and close an evidence-complete case.
The four detective questions remain available at the end. Ratings are opinions, not graded answers.

## Talk questions

- Can an ad make you want something and still be unfair? Can you defend both ratings?
- Which claim really held up? Does one honest claim make every other claim true?
- What would the jump look like without a close camera or sound effect?
- Why does showing eight racers fail to prove everyone has one?

## Good to know

Turbo Gecko, its ad, its prices and all measurements are fictional teaching data. No purchase advice is intended.
The size is 12 cm. One meter in 1.7 seconds is about 0.59 meters per second; the ad's 0.57 seconds is about three times faster.
With a separate ramp the toy clears 10 cm. The box contains one racer, one 30 cm track piece and a sticker sheet.
It does not contain the loop track set, ramp or batteries. The full pictured setup for one racer costs $65.46,
which is $35.47 more than $29.99. Eight separately sold racers would cost more; this check counts one.
