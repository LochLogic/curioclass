# Toy Ad Reality Check

## One idea
Every shot in a toy ad quietly makes a claim, and a claim can be tested: pause a made-up 15-second ad for the Turbo Gecko racer, pin each trick (a low camera, a sped-up shot, a cheering crowd, small print read too fast) to the claim it makes ("it's huge", "it's super fast"), then take the real toy into the lab with a ruler, a stopwatch and the box, and the case board shows which claims hold up, which are stretched and which need things that are not in the box, so your verdict on the ad rests on evidence, not on a feeling.

## Not like
- cereal-ad-questions (K-2, same subject): the class turns off music, pictures and words and votes on wanting. Here each claim is checked against measurements of the real toy, and the verdict needs evidence.
- viral-video-breakdown (6-8, mystery): traces how reposts changed a video's meaning and credit. Here one ad, and the question is whether what it shows is true of the toy.
- audience-talkback (this band): swaps staging to find what changed a feeling. Here nothing is swapped; tricks are found by pausing and then tested in a lab.
- cartoon-soundtrack-mixer (this band): music changes a cartoon's mood. Here music and sound are two of six tricks, and the case is about truth, not mood.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, media arts, module "Reading Media (Media Arts)".
- Topic line: checking a toy ad's claims
- Driver: students in pairs, or the teacher on a projector with the class calling out tricks. 20 to 30 minutes.
- Standards: MA:Re7 (find how each shot is built: angle, speed, sound, words), MA:Re8 (say what the ad wants you to believe), MA:Re9 (rate the ad on whether it works and whether it is fair, with evidence), MA:Cn11 (ads to children have real rules, and kids meet ads every day).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons: `The ad` (default), `The lab`, `The verdict`. A case board (six claim cards) runs down the side in every mode, filling in as the case goes.

**The ad.** Focus: a TV screen (code frame) playing the ad, with a timeline of its six shots under it.
- `Play`, `Pause`, `Step back a shot`, `Slow` (half speed). Pausing freezes the shot; the trick finder opens on it.
- Trick finder: six trick cards (Content). Drag a card onto the paused picture, or tap the picture where you see the trick and then tap the card. A right match pins it to that shot with a short line ("The camera is down low, looking up. That makes small things look big."). A card that is not in this shot says so and stays in the tray ("No sped-up film here: the background isn't racing past.").
- Each pinned trick fills a claim card on the case board: "Turbo Gecko is huge", "It's super fast", "It jumps a giant canyon", "Everyone has one", "Its eyes glow in the dark", "All this for $29.99". Each claim card shows which trick made it.
- `Plain view` (after a trick is pinned, on that shot): the same moment without the trick (camera at table height, normal speed, no crowd sound), side by side, so the trick's effect is visible.

**The lab.** Focus: a painted lab table seen from above, with the real Turbo Gecko on it, a 30 cm ruler, a 1 m test track and a stopwatch (code). Five tests, any order, each answering one claim:
- `Measure it`: drag the ruler to the racer; it reads 12 cm (a toy you can hold in one hand).
- `Time it`: tap `Go` and stop the stopwatch when the racer crosses the 1 m line (the racer takes 1.7 s; the student's stop time is shown, and any time within 0.3 s counts). Then the ad shot plays beside it: the ad's racer covers the same 1 m in about 0.6 s. The lab card shows both times and "about 3 times as fast in the ad".
- `Jump it`: try the jump with what's in the box (it rolls off the edge) and then with the ramp (sold separately), which clears a 10 cm gap: a pencil case, not a canyon.
- `Lights out`: dim the room; the eyes glow. This claim holds up.
- `Open the box`: the box's contents spill out: the racer, one short track piece and a sticker sheet. No ramp, no loop track, no batteries; the box flap's small print (readable, at normal speed) says so.
- After each test the student marks its claim card `Holds up`, `Stretched` or `Not in the box`, and the evidence chip from the test pins to the card.

**The verdict.** Focus: the case board, big, with the verdict form beside it.
1. `What does the ad want you to believe?`: pick one of three (Content) and pick two claim cards that show it.
2. Two ratings, each a 1 to 5 scale with a reason built from evidence chips (at least one): `Does it make kids want it?` and `Is it fair to kids?`. The tool never gives a "right" rating; it checks that each rating has evidence and shows a reason sentence ("I rated fairness 2 because it looks 3 times faster than it is and the ramp isn't in the box").
3. `The rule book`: four plain-words rules that U.S. toy ads for children are expected to follow (Content); the student ticks which ones the Turbo Gecko ad broke and the board shows the shots.
4. `Cost check`: the price of everything the ad showed (racer, track set, ramp, batteries), added up with the student filling in the total.
- `Detective questions`: a card of four questions to ask any ad, kept on screen at the end.

## Content
**The ad** (15 s, six shots, made up; Turbo Gecko is a 12 cm green lizard racer with flame stickers and glowing eyes):
1. 0 to 3 s, "Launch": the racer on a ramp seen from the floor, filling the screen, a loud guitar chord. Tricks: Low angle, Close-up. Claim: "Turbo Gecko is huge".
2. 3 to 6 s, "Speed": the racer tearing along a track, background streaking, engine roar. Tricks: Sped-up film, Sound effects. Claim: "It's super fast".
3. 6 to 9 s, "Canyon": the racer flies over a gap between two rock-painted boxes in slow motion with a boom. Tricks: Close-up, Sound effects (and a ramp just out of frame, visible in Plain view). Claim: "It jumps a giant canyon".
4. 9 to 11 s, "Crowd": eight racers on a huge loop track, cheering crowd sound. Tricks: Crowd sound, Music. Claim: "Everyone has one".
5. 11 to 13 s, "Night": a dark room, the racer's eyes glowing. Trick: none (Plain view looks the same). Claim: "Its eyes glow in the dark".
6. 13 to 15 s, "Price": "ONLY $29.99!" big, with small print for 1 second, read fast: "Track, ramp and batteries sold separately. Each racer sold separately." Tricks: Small print, Music. Claim: "All this for $29.99".

**Trick cards** (6): Low angle ("The camera looks up, so it looks big"), Close-up ("So close it fills the screen, with nothing to compare its size to"), Sped-up film ("Shown faster than it really goes"), Sound effects ("Roars and booms a toy doesn't make"), Crowd sound ("Cheering makes it feel like everyone wants one"), Small print ("Important facts, tiny and quick").
Music sits on the timeline as a band under all shots (a pumping rock loop, Web Audio, original); its card "Music makes it feel exciting" pins to any shot.

**Lab results**: size 12 cm; 1 m in 1.7 s (0.59 m/s), the ad shows 1 m in about 0.57 s, 3 times faster; jump: none without the ramp, 10 cm with it; eyes glow: yes; box: racer, one 30 cm track piece, sticker sheet.
**Expected marks**: huge: Stretched; super fast: Stretched; canyon: Not in the box (needs the ramp) and Stretched (10 cm); everyone has one: Stretched (the ad shows 8 racers and a loop track, each sold separately); eyes glow: Holds up; $29.99: Not in the box. The board accepts both marks on the canyon.

**What the ad wants you to believe**: "Turbo Gecko is a giant, super-fast racer that does huge stunts right out of the box, and everyone has one" (right); "Turbo Gecko is a small racer with glowing eyes"; "Turbo Gecko is good for learning about lizards".

**Rule book** (plain words, from the guidelines in Sources): 1. Don't make a toy look bigger, faster or able to do more than a child can really get it to do. 2. Say clearly what comes in the box and what is sold separately, in words kids understand. 3. Don't make kids feel everyone else has one. 4. Show the toy the way a child would really play with it. The Turbo Gecko ad breaks 1 (shots 1 to 3), 2 (shot 6), 3 (shot 4) and 4 (shots 2 to 4).

**Cost check**: racer $29.99, track set with loop $19.99, ramp $9.99, 4 AA batteries $5.49; total $65.46, so the ad's whole picture costs $35.47 more than the price it shouts.

**Detective questions**: What does this ad want me to believe? What trick is making me feel that? What would it look like plain? What's not in the box?

## Interactions in detail
- While the ad plays every control stays live: Pause stops at once on the current frame; the timeline jumps to any shot and keeps playing; Slow changes speed at once; Sound off silences the music and effects and shows them as captions ("[engine roar]", "[crowd cheering]") so the sound tricks can still be found.
- Shots are painted frames with code motion (pans, zooms, speed streaks, a glow); Plain view is a second painted frame of the same moment (a table-height photo-like view) with no motion tricks.
- A trick can be pinned to more than one shot; Music pins anywhere. The case board fills in as soon as a shot has one right trick.
- The stopwatch counts in tenths; the racer's crossing time is fixed (1.7 s), so the student's timing skill is what varies.
- Saved on this device (localStorage `toy-ad-reality-check`): pins, lab marks, the verdict and ratings. `New case` clears them.
- Keyboard: Space plays and pauses, comma and period step shots, Tab through trick cards and Enter pins the focused card to the paused shot.
- Sizes: the TV is the largest thing in The ad at 1366x768 and 680x425; the case board collapses to a strip of six claim icons with words at 375 wide, opening as a sheet.

## Voice
Bank voice `science-narrator` (style "Say in a clear, informative way, like a nature documentary narrator"): the case is narrated like a nature film about a wild ad ("Here we see the toy commercial in its natural habitat..."). Not used by any 3-5 arts neighbor or another tool in this band (cereal-ad-questions uses news-reader). `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.mode`.
- welcomeAd (how; entering The ad; stale when Play is tapped): "Here is the Turbo Gecko ad. Tap Play, then Pause wherever something looks too good."
- pinHow (how; paused, nothing pinned; stale when a trick is pinned): "Drag a trick card onto the picture. What is the camera doing?"
- pinRight (event; stale on the next pin): the trick's line from Content.
- pinWrong (event; stale on the next pin): the card's "not here" line.
- adDone (event; all six claims made): "Six claims. Now let's test them. Tap The lab."
- welcomeLab (how; entering The lab; stale when any test is started): "Pick a test. The real toy is on the table."
- testDone (event; after each test; stale when the claim is marked): "Now mark the claim: holds up, stretched, or not in the box."
- labDone (event; all tests marked): "The evidence is in. Tap The verdict."
- welcomeVerdict (how; entering The verdict; stale when an answer is picked): "What did the ad want you to believe? Pick one, then rate it."
- ratingHow (how; a rating with no evidence; stale when a chip is added): "Back it up. Add a piece of evidence to your rating."
- closing (event; verdict done): "Case closed. Ask these four questions about the next ad you see."
