## What it teaches

Students identify visible clues in eighteen real museum works and use them to distinguish six classroom style recipes.
They connect each work with its artist and cultural setting, then test how the same clues change their own picture.

## Key skills

- Look closely and point to evidence in an artwork.
- Distinguish a shared clue from a more specific clue.
- Connect paintings with artists and places where the works were made.
- Compare brushwork, light, pattern and outlines across pictures.
- Experiment with a recipe and explain what changed.

## Support at home

- Look at a picture in your home together. Ask your child to point to a line, a shadow and a small detail.
- Make two pencil drawings of the same object, one with strong outlines and one with short strokes. Compare the effects.
- On a walk, look for light from one side. Talk about which surfaces are bright and which are in shadow.

## A 5-minute start

1. In Look, open Red Fuji. Tap Tells, then a pin, and name what you see. Whole picture returns to the full work.
2. In Match, tap Deal. Select a tell card, then tap the painting. An incorrect tell returns without a penalty.
3. If one tell fits two styles, find another. Choose a style, then an artist. The painting joins the Album.
4. In Make, choose Fruit and tap Pointillism. Compare dots with Quick dabs and Thick wavy paint.

## Modes

- **Look:** eighteen paintings, arranged in six unnamed style columns whose order changes on a new page load.
  Wall labels give the title, attribution, date, material and museum. Style is hidden until Show style or a match.
- **Match:** collect visible tells, then identify the style and artist. Enter on a focused hand card plays it directly;
  a pointer can select and tap the painting or drag the card. Trade is unlimited. There is no score or timer.
- **Make:** four layered scenes, ten independent tell toggles and six exact recipe buttons. Save picture downloads a
  1600 by 1000 PNG. Side by side becomes available when the selected style has a painting in the Album.
- **Album:** eighteen slots, grouped by style, with recipe icons and year ranges. Clear album asks for confirmation.
  The album stays on this device. Storage restrictions may prevent saving, but do not prevent playing.

## Talk about it

- Why can Bold outlines fit two of our recipes? What second tell would help?
- How do dots and short dabs change the same scene differently?
- What connects a Persian painted book page with its original purpose? What connects a Japanese print with making copies?
- Which clues can you point to in both your own picture and a painting in your Album?

## Good to know

These six recipes simplify selected works for close looking. They do not define every work in a tradition or imply
that all Persian paintings, Dutch paintings or Japanese prints look alike. The printed dates describe this lesson's
recipes and examples, not the full history of those traditions. Make models surface effects; it does not reproduce
an artist's materials, process or cultural knowledge. Gold here is a screen texture representing gold leaf.

Match accepts only the selected tells in each card's recipe. Another visible feature can still be present, such as
outdoor light in Red Fuji. The specified "No ... here" feedback is too absolute for those cases; treat it as a recipe
rejection and invite students to point out what they actually see. This wording needs a spec correction.

The win card records the painting, attribution and the tells the student actually spotted. Save picture captures
the scene and recipe at the moment it is tapped, so students can continue experimenting during the PNG export.

The NCAS VA:Re7 goal is practiced by finding and zooming to visual evidence. VA:Cn11 is practiced through the
style-card contexts, artist identification and wall labels: handmade Iranian books, Dutch painting, Japanese
woodblock reproduction and French painting traditions. No sensitive personal information is requested.

Sound controls the chimes and narration; Read reads the current hint, or the opened painting's wall label. Narration
uses the browser voice until lively-host recordings are supplied. Motion off removes movement and keeps the rules.
Escape first zooms out, then closes an opened Look card. Tab reaches all controls, and visible focus is preserved.
