# History or Fiction? (idea card)

- Module: ela-7-fiction-vs-history, grade 7. Codes: RL.7.9
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good: every line of the story is a suspect, and primary sources are the clues.
- One idea: The student investigates a short historical fiction story set during the Great Chicago Fire of 1871, checks each detail against primary sources (newspaper reports, survivor letters, a city map, photographs of the ruins) and stamps it Documented, Altered or Invented, then works out why the author made each change, so they learn how fiction writers use and bend history, and why.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a case board: a five-page story by Curio Class about a boy who carries messages through the burning city, and an evidence drawer of primary sources.
  - Core loop: the student pins a story detail (the fire started in the O'Leary barn; the wind blew from the southwest; the boy meets the mayor on the bridge) and searches the drawer for sources that confirm or contradict it. They stamp it: Documented, Altered (a real fact changed: time, place, who was there) or Invented (a character or conversation that never happened, but could have). For each Altered or Invented stamp they pick or write the reason: to give the reader someone to follow, to compress time, to show what many people felt.
  - The aha or the end state: the twist clue: the famous story of Mrs. O'Leary's cow kicking over a lantern is in the fiction, and the drawer shows it was a newspaper invention its reporter later admitted. The student decides whether the novelist should keep it, and writes a short author's note for the story that tells readers what is real.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second case on another event with strong public-domain sources (chosen at spec).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.7.9: comparing the fictional portrayal with historical accounts of the same time and place, and explaining how and why the author used or altered history.
- Not like: satire-decoder (grade 11, what is said versus meant); memoir-and-biography (6-8, two nonfiction tellings); two-authors-one-event (grade 7, two articles' spin).
- Art: public domain engravings and photographs of the fire and ruins (Library of Congress or Chicago History Museum ids, to be checked at spec); generated scenes for the story.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A historical fiction story about the 1871 Chicago Fire is the case: check each detail against primary sources, stamp it documented, altered or invented, and work out why the author changed history where they did.
- Flags: needs a data source (primary sources and the reporter's later admission about the cow story, confirmed at spec); sensitive (light: deaths in the fire told plainly; the O'Leary family's treatment, including anti-Irish prejudice, told with care)
