# Research packet: playground-rules
Concept: Hang rule signs on a busy playground with no rules and watch the scene replay calmer; give each rule its reason, then decide a new swing rule with illustrated classmates by listening and voting.
Grade: k   Standards: D2.Civ.3.K-2, D2.Civ.7.K-2, D2.Civ.12.K-2, D4.1.K-2, D2.Civ.11.K-2, D4.8.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Civ.3.K-2: "Explain the need for and purposes of rules in various settings inside and outside of school." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain why playground rules exist to protect children from injury and keep play fair.
- D2.Civ.7.K-2: "Apply civic virtues when participating in school settings." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Practice fairness, respect, and turn-taking when playing with classmates.
- D2.Civ.11.K-2: "Explain how people can work together to make decisions in the classroom." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Work cooperatively with peers to establish fair rules for shared equipment.
- D2.Civ.12.K-2: "Identify and explain how rules function in public (classroom and school) settings." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Describe how posted rules guide safe behavior in shared school areas like playgrounds.
- D4.1.K-2: "Construct an argument with reasons." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Provide a clear reason explaining why a specific playground rule is needed.
- D4.8.K-2: "Use listening, consensus-building, and voting procedures to decide on and take action in their classrooms." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Listen to classmates' ideas and vote to choose a shared playground rule.

## Common mistakes
- Believing rules exist solely to spoil fun: Kindergarteners frequently think rules are mean adult commands rather than safety tools designed to keep everyone unharmed (Source: C3 Framework Civics indicators D2.Civ.3.K-2).
- Believing the loudest or fastest person should get equipment: Young students assume rushing or shouting establishes ownership over swings rather than fair turn-taking (Source: California History-Social Science Content Standards K.1).
- Believing voting means everyone gets their individual preference: Students often struggle with the concept that a vote means adopting the majority decision as the collective rule (Source: C3 Framework Dimension 4 Deliberation indicators).
- Thinking safety rules only apply when a supervisor is looking: Children often view rules as compliance tests rather than inherent physical protections (Source: CPSC Public Playground Safety Handbook).

## Sensitivity
- Behavioral and emotional regulation: Young children may feel frustrated or upset when having to wait their turn. The tool should frame waiting and voting positively without shaming characters who need reminders.
- Inclusivity: Ensure playground rules reflect accessible play for all children, including students with physical disabilities or sensory differences.
