# Three Viewpoints Scene

## One idea
Devise a one-minute scene about the late bus being cut from three sides (the student who rides it, the driver, the principal) by placing lines on a six-beat board, taking or passing on a devising partner's "what if" offers, tightening the pacing until the tension curve peaks and turns, and then playing one role out loud while the other two are voiced on cue.

## Not like
- stage-blocker (6-8, stage family): where actors stand and what each seat sees. Here nothing is placed in space; the scene's words, order, viewpoints and timing are devised.
- gallery-critique (9-12, roleplay): a debate about one painting with claims pinned to it. Here the student writes a scene, not an argument, and performs a part.
- character-voice-booth (3-5): records one line in many voices. Here no recording; the student speaks live.
- scene-staging-comparison (this band): watches two productions of an old scene. This tool makes a new scene.
- choreography-board (6-8): revision of dance phrases on a timeline. Here the timeline is beats of a scene with a tension curve.
- morning-show-crew, viral-video-breakdown (this band): media, not theater.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, theater, module "Devising a Scene (Theater)". Topic line: devising a scene from three viewpoints.
- Driver: students, alone or in threes on one laptop (each takes a role in Perform). 20 to 35 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS TH:Cr2 (develop and refine work collaboratively), TH:Pr4 (select and analyze; pacing and character), TH:Pr6 (perform), TH:Cn10 (relate to personal and community experience).
- Header: title, topic line, modes **Devise** (default), **Tighten**, **Perform**, then kit `motion`, `sound`, `read`.

## Screens and modes

### Layout (all modes)
- Top of the stage: the painted rehearsal stage with three painted props on stands, one per character (Art), each with its character's name in code on a tape strip: Maya (a trumpet case), Mr. Ortiz (a driver's cap and key ring on a stool), Ms. Dale (a clipboard and lanyard on a chair). A code spotlight (a soft ellipse) falls on whoever is speaking.
- Under it: the beat board: six columns, one per beat (Content), each holding up to 4 line cards in order. Under the board, the tension curve (one point per beat, 0 to 3) and the time ruler (seconds per beat and total).
- Panel: `hint`, the mode's controls, **Play scene** (becomes **Stop**), **Reset**.

### Devise (explore and build)
- Focus: the beat board.
- Tap an empty slot in a beat: a picker shows that beat's line options for all three characters (two each, plus any unlocked by an offer), each with its character, its estimated seconds and a small tag if it shows that character's need. Tap one to place it. Tap a placed card: **Move up**, **Move down**, **Remove**, **Hear it** (plays it in its character voice).
- Jules, the devising partner (the narrator voice), makes an offer after the 3rd, 6th, 9th and 12th line placed, in order (Content). The offer card appears beside the board with **Yes, and** (unlocks the offered line in its beat's picker and places it in the first free slot of that beat) and **Not this one** (Jules answers "Okay. Keep going." and the offer is set aside; set-aside offers can be reopened from an **Offers** list in the panel).
- Responds: each change redraws the tension curve and the time ruler at once.

### Tighten
- Focus: the tension curve, enlarged to half the stage, with the beat board shrunk under it (cards still tappable).
- The scene checks (Content) as four lights over the curve: **Three sides**, **Rising**, **The turn**, **Pacing**. Each is green with a check or amber with an exclamation and one line saying what to change.
- Controls: per beat, a **Pause before** stepper (0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3 s; default 0.5); the selected card's **Cut**. Cuts and pause changes update the ruler and lights at once.
- **Play scene** plays it with the spotlight moving prop to prop and the playhead on the board.

### Perform
- Focus: the speaking prop and a large cue card in the center: the current line in big type (at least 28 px at 1366x768) with the character's name.
- **Your role**: Maya, Mr. Ortiz or Ms. Dale (three buttons; in a group of three, choose **All three live** and nothing is voiced).
- **Start** runs the scene cue to cue: the other roles' lines are voiced and shown small; at each of your lines the card turns to the accent, shows "Your line" and waits. Say it aloud, then tap **Done** (or Space). The pause before each beat is honored. **Back one line** replays the previous cue. **Stop** ends.
- At the end: the run time (from Start to the last line) and the planned time, with "You took 14 s longer than planned. Pauses or slower lines?" No score.

## Content

### Characters and the issue (made up for Curio Class)
- The district is cutting the late bus that leaves at 5:00 after clubs. Maya Chen, 7th grade, first-chair trumpet; Mr. Ortiz, the late-bus driver for twelve years; Ms. Dale, the principal, who must cut $40,000 from the school's budget.

### Beats
1. Setup (who we are) 2. The news 3. What I need 4. The clash 5. The turn 6. After

### Lines (beat, character, option: text; tension 0 to 3; "need" marks a line that shows that character's need; "turn" marks a line that changes the situation). Seconds are estimated as words / 2.5 + 0.4 until the recordings exist, then the clip length.
- 1 Maya a: "Band ran late again. Mr. Ortiz, you waited for me?" (0). b: "Last one on the late bus. Like always." (0)
- 1 Ortiz a: "Twelve years I've driven this bus. I always wait for the band kids." (0, need). b: "Seat belt, Maya. Long day?" (0)
- 1 Dale a: "Mr. Ortiz, before you go, I need a minute." (1). b: "Good, you're both here. I wish I wasn't." (1)
- 2 Maya a: "Wait. Cut? Then how do I get home after band?" (2). b: "You're joking. Right?" (1). c (offer 3): "..." (a silent beat, 2 s; 1)
- 2 Ortiz a: "Starting when?" (1). b: "So after Friday, this route is just gone." (2)
- 2 Dale a: "The district is cutting the late bus. Friday is the last run." (2). b: "The late bus is done after Friday. I just found out." (2)
- 3 Maya a: "My mom works nights. If there's no bus, there's no band. For me." (3, need). b: "I practice every day. I'm finally first chair." (2, need)
- 3 Ortiz a: "Those kids are my route. Some of them only talk to me." (2, need). b: "The late run is half my hours." (2, need). c (offer 1): "My own daughter plays trombone. I know what band means." (2, need)
- 3 Dale a: "I have to cut forty thousand dollars. Every choice hurts someone." (2, need). b: "If I keep the bus, I lose a teacher's aide." (3, need)
- 4 Maya a: "So band only counts for kids whose parents can drive." (3). b: "Did anyone ask the kids who ride it?" (3)
- 4 Ortiz a: "You cut the bus and you cut the kids who need it most." (3). b: "Nobody asked me either." (2)
- 4 Dale a: "You think I wanted this? Show me the money and I'll keep it." (3). b: "I don't need a speech. I need a plan." (3). c (offer 2): "I rode a late bus for six years. Don't tell me I don't know." (3, need)
- 5 Maya a: "What if we ask? Twenty band kids, one sheet. Who rides and when." (2, turn). b: "Fine. I'll quit band." (3). c (offer 4): "Wait. It's not just me. Half the jazz band rides this bus." (2, turn)
- 5 Ortiz a: "What if the late run only goes Tuesdays and Thursdays? Half the fuel." (2, turn). b: "I could take Maya. Just this week." (1)
- 5 Dale a: "If you can show me the riders, I can take it to the board." (2, turn). b: "It's decided. I'm sorry." (3)
- 6 Maya a: "Tuesday. Same seat." (0). b: "I'll have the sheet by lunch." (1)
- 6 Ortiz a: "Seat belt, Maya." (0). b: "Twelve years. Let's make it thirteen." (0)
- 6 Dale a: "Board meets Monday. Bring the sheet." (1). b: "Go home, both of you. We'll fix what we can." (0)

### Jules's offers (in order)
1. "What if Mr. Ortiz's own daughter is in band too?" unlocks 3 Ortiz c.
2. "What if Ms. Dale rode a late bus herself as a kid?" unlocks 4 Dale c.
3. "What if Maya says nothing at first? Just silence." unlocks 2 Maya c.
4. "What if the turn is Maya finding out it's not just her?" unlocks 5 Maya c.

### Scene checks (the tool's own rules for this exercise, said on screen as "a director's rule of thumb")
- Beat tension = the highest tension among its lines (an empty beat is 0 and is flagged).
- **Three sides**: each character has at least one "need" line somewhere in beats 1 to 4. Amber names who is missing: "We never hear what Ms. Dale needs."
- **Rising**: beat 2 is at least 1, beat 3 at least beat 2, and beat 4 at least beat 3; beat 4 is 3. Amber: "The clash isn't the high point yet."
- **The turn**: beat 5 has a "turn" line, beat 5 has no line with tension 3 placed after its turn line, beat 6's tension is 1 or less, and the pause before beat 5 is 1 to 2 s. Amber names the first failure ("Give the turn a breath: set Pause before beat 5 to 1 or 2 seconds.").
- **Pacing**: no beat longer than 15 s (lines plus its pause), total 30 to 75 s. Amber names the long beat.

## Interactions in detail
- Up to 4 lines per beat; a full beat's empty-slot button is gone and the picker says "This beat is full. Cut a line to add one."
- A line can be placed once; options already placed are dimmed in the picker.
- Play scene and Perform: lines play in board order, each in its character's voice, with the pause before each beat; every control stays live: tapping a card jumps playback to that line at once, **Stop** stops at once, Pause steppers changed mid-play apply from the next beat. Editing the board in Devise or Tighten while playing stops playback first.
- The narrator never speaks during Play scene or Perform; offers that come due while playing wait for Stop.
- Perform with an empty board: Start is disabled and the hint says "Put at least one line in each beat in Devise."
- Saved on this device: the board, pauses, offers answered (localStorage, try/catch). **Reset** asks to confirm.
- Motion off: the spotlight jumps instead of gliding; timing unchanged.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 the six beats, the curve and Play are visible without scrolling; beats become 2 rows of 3 under 900 px wide. Preview 680x425: props strip, board and curve readable. At 375 wide: beats as a vertical list, curve as a small strip above.
