## What it teaches
Students devise a six-beat scene by choosing and ordering dialogue from three viewpoints. They revise its tension and pacing, then perform a role aloud with partners or voiced cues.

## Key skills
- Hear a character's need behind their words.
- Accept and develop a devising partner's offer, or set it aside deliberately.
- Build a clash and a turn through line order and silence.
- Refine pacing by cutting lines and changing pauses.
- Perform a role live and compare the result with a timing plan.

## Support at home
- Pick a small shared problem, such as a noisy room. Say one sentence from each of three people's viewpoints.
- Say the same sentence with a short pause and a longer pause before it. Ask what changed for the listener.
- Make a tiny scene together. Try accepting a partner's suggestion and adding one detail to it.

## A 5-minute start
1. In Devise, tap + in Setup. Choose a line, then hear it with Play scene.
2. Add news, a need and a clash. Ask which viewpoint has not yet been heard.
3. Answer Jules's first offer. An accepted line is added automatically when its beat has space.
4. Add a turn and an after. Switch to Tighten and read the amber checks.
5. In Perform, choose a role and Start. Speak your lines, then tap Done or press Space.

## The modes
- Devise: six beats, up to four lines in each. Select a card to move it up or down, remove it or hear it. A line appears only once.
- Tighten: the enlarged curve shows the highest tension in each beat. Choose a beat in Pause before and use the stepper. Cut a selected line to make space.
- Perform: take Maya, Mr. Ortiz or Ms. Dale. Other roles are voiced if the browser supports speech. All three live waits on every cue. Back one line replays a cue. Stop ends immediately.

## Talk about it
- Who loses something if the bus is cut? Where do we hear each person's need?
- Does accepting a suggestion always improve a scene? What did you keep or set aside, and why?
- Which line changes the situation? What happens if a clash line comes after it?
- How is this fictional issue connected to access to activities in your community?
- Did the live performance take more or less time than planned? What would you revise?

## Good to know
The four checks are a director's rule of thumb for this exercise, not a universal test of good theater. Empty beats are marked separately. A green check is a prompt to listen and revise, not a score.

Timing uses words divided by 2.5, plus 0.4 seconds per line and each beat's pause. The silent offer lasts two seconds. The example passing scene runs 58.4 seconds with 1.5 seconds before the turn and 0.5 seconds before other beats. Actual browser speech and live delivery vary.

This is a fictional school issue. Maya Chen is a seventh-grade first-chair trumpet player. Mr. Ortiz has driven the late bus for twelve years. Ms. Dale must cut $40,000. The scene's proposals are dialogue, not verified transport savings.

Work saves on this device using localStorage when available. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere. Reset asks before clearing the saved board, pauses and offer answers. Motion off removes movement while preserving timing. Read speaks the current instruction; during playback it first stops the scene.

NCAS TH:Cr2 is practiced through building on Jules's offers and revising a shared board. TH:Pr4 is practiced through character needs, line order, tension and pacing. TH:Pr6 is practiced by live performance. TH:Cn10 is practiced by interpreting the school-access issue through the characters' needs and community experience.
