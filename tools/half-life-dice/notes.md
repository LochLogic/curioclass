## A 5-minute start

1. In pairs, one student predicts while the other drives. Pick d6, press Track one, then Roll once several times. Swap roles after five rolls.
2. Compare the tracked die with the crowd. A die has no memory: every live die has a 1 in 6 chance next roll, even after surviving seven rolls. The crowd still follows a regular decay curve.
3. Press Roll to 10, then Average 20 runs. Drag Mark a half-life to exactly 500 nuclei and compare the crossing with the exact value.

## The three modes

- **Roll:** fair dice model nuclei. A 1 means decay, tails for Coin. Gray dice with an x stay out of subsequent rolls. The solid line and circular points are one observed tray; the dashed curve is expected; the dotted line and diamonds are the mean of 20 independently seeded trays. Reset repeats a seed. New seed starts a different tray.
- **Nucleus:** pick N1 to N5, F1 or F2. The outlined product is the student's proposed nucleus. Set A and Z, then Check. Check reports both sums without supplying the correct product. A correct equation names the nuclear change and reveals the energy ladder. Compare energies per event, and the fission and fusion per-nucleon figures on their cards.
- **Dating lab:** pick a sample, drag the ruler pin and press Estimate before Calculate. Move the unplaced pin, then record one Estimate for the sample. Calculate keeps the estimate beside the exact age and reports the percent difference. Count again draws the next seeded count and lists the spread. The count models 100 minutes from 1 g, shown in one second of classroom time. Counting noise samples a seeded Poisson distribution. The square root of the count is a one-standard-deviation counting uncertainty, not a complete archaeological error budget.

## Talk about it

- Does surviving make a nucleus more likely to decay on the next roll? What changes when the whole crowd is smaller?
- Why does averaging runs bring the observed curve closer to the expected curve?
- In a beta equation, why can A stay the same while Z changes?
- Fusion gives less energy per event than this fission, but more per nucleon. How do the different denominators explain that?
- Why does a low count make a carbon date less certain?

## Good to know

- HS-PS1-8: rolling and reading a half-life models decay over time. Balancing products models changes in the nucleus. Comparing the revealed logarithmic ladder and event/per-nucleon values models energy released in decay, fission and fusion.
- Dates use the textbook half-life of 5,730 years and living carbon activity 13.6 dpm/g. NNDC lists carbon-14 at 5,700 ± 30 years. The owner's choice between these remains open; this tool uses 5,730 consistently.
- OpenStax Chemistry 2e, [21.3 Radioactive Decay](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/21-3-radioactive-decay), describes carbon dating as accurate to about 30,000 years and reasonable to about 50,000. Deep core wood is a practice sample beyond that range. These simplified dates do not include calibration or contamination.
- Dead Sea Scrolls fragment and seeds from Tutankhamun's tomb are named OpenStax examples. The leaf, charcoal, mammoth bone and deep core wood are labeled practice samples.
- The scrolls and the seeds are real artifacts; their activity values here are simplified textbook examples. Published radiocarbon studies give a range of dates across many scrolls, and real dates are calibrated. The seeds example fits the tomb's date of about 1323 BCE.
- N3 to N5 touch on cancer care and thyroid treatment, which a student may know through their own family. Invite discussion without asking for personal medical stories.
- [21.4 Transmutation and Nuclear Energy](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/21-4-transmutation-and-nuclear-energy) supplies the fission and fusion reactions. The energies are from OpenStax College Physics 2e, [32.5 Fusion](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/32-5-fusion), 17.59 MeV for D-T, and [32.6 Fission](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/32-6-fission), average U-235 fission 200 MeV, checked by the integrator 2026-10-29. Methane combustion comes from section 5.3; decay data from NNDC NuDat 3; unit conversions from NIST CODATA 2018.
- The displayed equations follow the spec's simplified particle bookkeeping. The drawings show nucleons, not atom size or realistic nuclear geometry.
- Progress means checked cards and dated samples, saved on this device only. Each Clear removes progress only for its own mode. No names, accounts, recordings or network requests.
- Tab reaches all controls. Arrow keys operate steppers, the half-life handle and the dating ruler. Read speaks the current hint. Header Sound starts on; Counter clicks starts off. Motion off removes animation while preserving the roll, averaging and count durations.
