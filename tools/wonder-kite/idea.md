# Wonder Kite (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-1-wonder-kite, grade 1. Codes: D1.1.K-2, D1.2.K-2, D1.3.K-2, D1.4.K-2
- Format / family: showdown / none. Fit: change to builder, because one student alone must pick the big question and tie on the bows; the class version becomes the extra.
- One idea: Pick a big question you care about (Why does our town have a library?), tie small questions to its tail as bows tagged map, clock, rule or coin, and each bow that really helps answer the big one holds a fact and lifts the kite higher.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three kites on the ground, each with a big question (Why does our town have a library? Why do we have a crossing guard? Why do people go to the farmers market?). "Pick the one you want to know most, and tell us why."
  - Core loop: the student says why it matters to them (picture choices), then picks from a pile of small questions. Each has a badge: map (where), clock (when), rule (who decides), coin (who pays). A helpful one ties on as a bow, shows its fact, and the kite rises; an off-topic one (What color is the sky?) drifts away with a hint.
  - The aha or the end state: the kite flies with its bows, and the student sees the small answers add up to an answer to the big question, then chooses which bow helped most.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): class votes on the big question; teacher can write a local one.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D1.1.K-2: the student says why the chosen question is important to them.
  - D1.2.K-2: map, clock, rule and coin badges link the big question to geography, history, civics and economics ideas.
  - D1.3.K-2: each bow carries the facts and concepts its small question uncovers.
  - D1.4.K-2: only bows that connect to the big question lift the kite.
- Not like: inquiry-ladder (grade 4 version with sources and contested words); how-do-you-know (fact vs opinion in sources).
- Art: generated scenes (sky, town, kites), because flight and color carry the reward.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pick a big question you care about (Why does our town have a library?) and tie small questions to the kite's tail as map, clock, rule or coin bows; each bow that helps holds a fact and lifts the kite.
- Flags: none
