## What it teaches

Students write and defend a school app's data policy. They connect collection, retention, access, commercial use, AI review and family rights to a student's daily data trail and to competing institutional priorities.

## Key skills

- Trace data collected through ordinary app use, including information a student may not expect to share.
- Distinguish a legal constraint from an ethical or practical disagreement.
- Weigh privacy, teaching, safety and costs when revising a policy.
- Test a policy change against explicit assumptions and explain why it changes support.
- Compare three drafts and defend the tradeoffs in one of them.

## Support at home

- Talk about a familiar school service. What does it need to know to do its job, and what could it leave unknown?
- Pick a household record, such as an old calendar. Discuss who should see it and when it could be discarded.
- Ask your child to describe a privacy choice that helps one person but creates work for someone else.

## Five-minute start

Open Draft and change Ads from the free targeted-ad version to de-identified use for a discount. The legal flag clears, but the forecast still has only one yes. Call the vote and watch the roll call. Ask why a flag-free draft can still fail.

## Modes

**Draft:** Six clause selectors open the option groups. The painted day strip updates immediately. A lit moment is collected; grey moments say not collected. On a narrow screen, scroll the day strip sideways. Tap a member's plate for their full response. Ask the room shows the best single, flag-free change for members currently leaning no. Read the draft opens all six sections and Counsel's full notes. Show the math exposes points by clause.

On compact screens, a rules line above the paintings shows access, retention and commercial use for the collected records. It labels the AI flag's distribution separately. The full-size cards carry these rules as chips. A clause edit also states whether the collection count changed or the same records now have different rules.

**Compare:** Pin up to three complete drafts, including a draft with a legal flag, and compare their aligned day strips. Load into Draft restores all six choices. Pins last for this page load only; they never leave the device. There is no text entry or saved student work.

## Discussion questions

- Which moments in Jordan's day would you want to keep out of a school app? Why?
- When does a legitimate user of data become a privacy concern?
- What does a counselor's review change about an automated early-warning flag? What does it leave unresolved?
- Why does the most private preset lose the vote? Is a passing vote enough to defend a policy?
- Compare two drafts with the same number of collected moments. Can their privacy consequences differ?

## Good to know

Everything about Linden Valley Unified, its 900 students, Aldermark Learning, Jordan and the council is fictional. Jordan is 12, so the COPPA example concerns children under 13. The district is in California so the SOPIPA example applies. These are selected classroom constraints, not a complete legal compliance test. A flag-free draft is eligible for this model's vote, not a certification that a real policy is lawful. FERPA has exceptions, including a school-official exception for eligible contractors acting for the school under its control. The vendor analyst option is explicitly limited to the district's purposes under contract. The partner-company option supplies no consent or qualifying exception.

The voting model is authored for Curio Class. Members vote yes at 2 points; 3 of 5 passes; any flag blocks a vote. There are 2,304 complete drafts, 576 flag-free drafts, 156 passing flag-free drafts and no unanimous draft. These are model outcomes, not research about what real parents, students or school staff believe. The most private preset gives totals 8, 10, -1, -5, -1 and loses 2 to 3. Privacy and voting success are distinct.

Hall-pass times and lunch purchases are always collected because they are core app functions. Disabling location does not remove the nurse pass record. E2 and E3 both collect the quiz and attendance moment, but review and distribution differ. Family opt-out is a right provided by a clause; the model does not simulate an individual family exercising it. De-identified use provides a discount in the model; no dollar amount or anonymity guarantee is claimed.

The tool makes no network requests. The kit may remember only the Motion setting on this device. There are no points awarded to the player, timers, confetti, or success mascots. Motion off removes movement but keeps the vote's 600 millisecond roll-call pace. Sound uses browser speech because this build does not record narration. Members' quotations are displayed only; the clerk is the sole narrator.
