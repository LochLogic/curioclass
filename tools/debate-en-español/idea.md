# Debate en Español (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-9-12-current-events-and-debate, grade 9-12. Codes: ACTFL.Advanced Low, WRS.Communities
- Format / family: roleplay / spanish. Fit: good: a structured debate on a real local issue in a Spanish-speaking city is a real reason to argue at paragraph length.
- One idea: Take a side on a real, long-running public question in a Spanish-speaking city (should Bogotá extend its Sunday car-free Ciclovía to more streets, should a town limit tourist rentals), read short news-style texts and residents' views in Spanish, then argue in paragraphs with connectors (sin embargo, por lo tanto, aunque) against a made-up rival who answers your points.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a city council livestream with the question, and a dossier: two short articles and four residents' voices (audio and text).
  - Core loop: round 1, opening: the student writes a paragraph stating a position with two reasons from the dossier. Round 2, the rival answers a specific point; the student must rebut it, quoting the rival's words and using a counter connector (Aunque tienes razón en que..., eso no significa que...). Round 3, closing in the future and conditional (Si la ciudad..., habrá...). Each round has a rubric the student scores themself, then sees a model paragraph by a native speaker.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's three paragraphs posted as a public comment to the city's (made-up) forum page, kept on this device only (the screen says so), and a checklist of the time frames and connectors they used.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a projector debate with two teams and the rival as moderator.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Advanced Low: paragraph-length argument across present, past and future, with connectors and rebuttal.
  - WRS.Communities: taking part in a real community's public conversation, reading its local voices, and writing a comment addressed to that community.
- Not like: counterpoint-frames (English ELD argument moves); consejero (personal advice); mundo-hispano (cultural traditions).
- Art: illustrated residents and council (people, illustrated); public domain or licensed city photos (rights checked at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Take a side on a long-running public question in a Spanish-speaking city, read local articles and residents' views in Spanish, and argue in three paragraph rounds against a rival who answers your points.
- Flags: needs a data source (real issue facts checked and kept evergreen, recorded voices); free writing is self-scored with a rubric and a model, no AI grading; light sensitivity (local politics, fresh review).
