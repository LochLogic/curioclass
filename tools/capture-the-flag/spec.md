# Capture the Flag

## One idea
A twelve-tile board of small security challenges inside a made-up freight company, where every flag you find by careful reading (decoding a note, auditing passwords, reading logs, catching a pretext call) unlocks a second step that asks you to patch the hole it came from, and a tile only turns fully green when the hole is fixed, not just found.

## Not like
- data-defenders (grade 5, same family): an escape room where each puzzle is a repair with lots of clues. Here you find a flag string first, then choose the patch, with real-thing tools (decoders, a password meter, logs).
- phishing-spotter (6-8, same family): fast rounds of keep-or-report. Here one deep case at a time, with no clock, and phishing is only one of four categories.
- network-architect (9-12, same wave group): designs a network and tests it. Here the systems already exist and you audit them.
- data-policy-council (9-12): writes privacy policy. Here the work is hands-on finding and fixing.
- api-playground, ai-evaluator (9-12): software and AI; no security.

## Who and how
- Grades 9 to 12, computer science. Module: Cybersecurity (Systems and Security), "Security measures, breaches, social engineering and fixes".
- Topic line: "passwords, encryption, social engineering and logs".
- Driver: students, one laptop each or pairs (pairs work well: one reads the artifact aloud). 30 to 50 minutes (about 3 minutes per tile; the board can span two periods since progress saves).
- Standards (CSTA 2026, as listed): HS-SYS-SE-31, HS-SYS-SE-32, HS-SYS-SE-33. 2017 crosswalk: 3A-NI-05 (how sensitive data can be affected by attacks), 3A-NI-06 (recommend security measures), 3A-NI-07 (tradeoffs between usability and security), 3A-NI-08 (tradeoffs of cybersecurity recommendations), 3B-NI-04 (ways to protect devices and information from unauthorized access).
- tool.json: driver "students", minutes "30-50", modes ["Board", "Toolbox"], grades_label "Grades 9-12". Kit starter with `kit:theme-912` and `kit:security`.

## Screens and modes
Header (kit, 9-12 theme): "Capture the Flag", small line "Larkspur Freight, a made-up company". Mode buttons: **Board** (opens first), **Toolbox**. Then Motion, Sound, Read.

Board view (stage): a 4 by 3 grid. Columns **Passwords**, **Codes**, **People**, **Logs**; rows 100, 200, 300. Tile states, each with an icon and a word: Open (outline), Flag found (amber border, flag icon, "patch it"), Patched (green, shield icon, "patched"). Above the grid: Score, "Flags 5 of 12", "Patched 4 of 12". Panel: `#hint`, a short how-it-works list ("Find the flag. It looks like flag{...}. Then patch the hole."), the scoring line (including the cost of a wrong flag), the ethics line "Larkspur Freight and everyone in it are made up, so you can practice here. Only ever test a real system you own or have written permission to test.", **Clear progress** (confirm Yes/No).

Challenge view (stage, replaces the grid): the brief (2 sentences), the artifact(s) drawn by the security engine, and a **Back to board** button. Panel: **Flag** input (monospace, placeholder `flag{...}`) and **Submit**; **Hint** (shows the hint and halves this tile's flag points; says so on the button: "Hint (half points)"); the **Toolbox** drawer (the same four tools as Toolbox mode, opened on the one this challenge needs, except C100, which opens on Shift so the drawer does not give away its paid hint); after the flag, the **Patch** card: the question, which states what Larkspur needs (the constraint the right pick depends on), and three defensible choices, each with its cost and gain shown after you pick; once patched, a line says what was traded. Under Flag, before the first try: "A wrong flag takes N points off this tile." After a tap copies text into a tool, the tool's answer is brought into view (smoothly only with Motion on) and a line by the artifact says "Copied into Strength." (or the tool's name).

Toolbox view: four tabs. **Shift** (Caesar: a two-ring wheel and a shift stepper 0 to 25, text in, text out), **Keyword** (Vigenere: key field, Encode/Decode), **Base64** (Encode/Decode), **Strength** (password box, readouts: length, pools ("symbol (and space) 33"), "Bits by characters" with "(if every character were picked at random)", "Bits by words or pattern" (random lowercase words split by spaces, or the engine's pattern parts, such as "Winter (word, capital) + 2026 (year) + ! (symbol)"), "Bits used" (the lowest), "time to try every possibility at 10 billion guesses a second" with the line "That rate models a fast offline attack on stolen password hashes. A sign-in page that slows tries is far slower.", and "On a list of 20 very common passwords: yes/no"; a warning line when it is on that list, or when a pattern sets the rating; a bar with a "70 bits" tick and a 128 end label; big times in words, up to "more than a thousand trillion years"). A note under Strength: "Don't type a password you really use. Nothing here is saved or sent."

## Content
Larkspur Freight (larkspur.example) and everyone in it are made up. Scores: a flag is worth 100, 200 or 300; a hint takes half, and each wrong flag (or a wrong Check lines in S100, or a wrong full order in L300) takes a quarter of the tile's value, never below a quarter (so a tile is worth at least 25, 50 or 75); patch +50 on the first right choice. Maximum 3,000. The cost is said before the first try (under Flag, in the board's scoring line, and in the points pill, which shows what the tile is worth now). Wrong tries are saved with the tile, so a reload does not reset them. (Changed at Refine round 1: guessing every option was free.)
Flags are accepted with or without the `flag{ }` wrapper, any letter case, spaces trimmed. Wrong: "Not this one. This tile is now worth N." After 3 misses the Hint button pulses.
Flags name what was found, never the fix, and the right patch is not the longest or always in the same place (Refine round 1).

**Passwords**
- **P100 On the list.** Brief: "An old sign-up form saved passwords in plain text. Which account uses a password from the common list?" Artifact `file` "signup-export.csv": amaya.r Tidepool-Lantern-88; bkoh password1; cdiaz Orchard9Comet!; dfinch violet harbor bicycle; evance Winter2026!; fgrant m0nkeyB@rs. Tool: Strength. Flag `flag{bkoh}`. Hint: "Run each one through the Strength tool and look at the common list line." Strength rates the other rows by their patterns: Tidepool-Lantern-88 36.8, Orchard9Comet! 36.2, violet harbor bicycle 38.8 (words), Winter2026! 26.6, m0nkeyB@rs 28.8. Patch "Drivers sign up on a shared tablet, often in gloves, and the help desk is one person. What stops this at sign-up?": A "Refuse passwords found on a breached list, and say why" (right: cost, a few people pick a second password; gain, no list password gets in with no extra rules, NIST advice); B "Require 12 characters with a capital, a digit and a symbol" (slow to type in gloves, written down, Password123! passes); C "Make everyone choose a new password every 30 days" (resets swamp the help desk; Winter2026! to Spring2026!; NIST advises against forced changes). After: "Traded: a second try for a few people at sign-up, for no common passwords at all. Also: never store passwords in plain text. Systems store salted hashes."
- **P200 Length wins.** Brief: "Four choices for the new admin password, typed from memory at the warehouse console, where no password manager runs. The guesser knows people use words. Find one the meter rates at 70 bits or more." Artifact `note`: Gr4v1ty#7 / bluemoon / kettle orbit maple / kettle orbit maple fog lantern drum. Strength results (computed): Gr4v1ty#7 59.1 by characters (9 characters, pool 95) but 24.3 as a pattern (Gravity, capital, two swaps undone, #, 7), so 24.3, less than a second; bluemoon 37.6 bits (no pattern), about 21 seconds; kettle orbit maple 105.9 by characters but 38.8 by words, so 38.8, about 47 seconds; the six words 205.9 by characters, 77.5 by words, so 77.5, about 700,000 years. When the shown bits are 70 or more the meter prints `flag{77_bits}`. Hint: "Look at the bits by words or pattern line." Patch "Admins type this password from memory at the console. What should its rule be?": A "Let passphrases run to 64 characters or more, with spaces" (right: costs a few seconds of typing; length slows guessing and random words can be remembered; NIST: allow 64 or more, and at least 15 for a password used alone); B "Require 8 characters with a capital, a digit and a symbol" (hard to remember, Gr4v1ty#7 swaps are tried early); C "Use a random 20-character string from a password generator" (strong, but nobody types it from memory: it ends up on a note at the console). (Refine round 1: "at least 64" read as a minimum; NIST asks to allow a maximum of at least 64.)
- **P300 Second step.** Brief: "Someone had cdiaz's password at 3 a.m. Why didn't they get in? Open the row that explains it." Artifact `log` "Sign-in record: cdiaz" (time, device with the place under it, password, second step, result; the place column folded into the device cell at Refine round 1 so the second step is reachable on a phone): Mon 08:02, Laptop L-114 (known, office), correct, not asked (known device), signed in. Mon 12:40, Phone P-22 (known, office), correct, not asked, signed in. Tue 03:02, new device (new place), correct, code sent to cdiaz's phone: denied, blocked. Tue 03:04, new device (new place), correct, code sent: no answer, blocked. Tue 08:05, Laptop L-114 (office), correct, not asked, signed in. Tue 08:06, Laptop L-114 (office), password changed by cdiaz, none, done. Rows expand on tap; the 03:02 row's details read "Second step denied by the account owner. flag{code_denied}". On a narrow stage the copy note adds "Swipe the table sideways to see every column." Hint: "The password was right. What else does the log check?" Patch "Every account needs a second step. Staff carry company phones, and this year has no money for new hardware. Which kind?": A "An app prompt on the company phone that shows a number to match" (right: a tap, nothing to buy; number matching stops approving out of habit); B "A USB security key for every account, plugged in at each sign-in" (strongest, won't work on a fake page, but keys and spares cost money this year lacks); C "A six-digit code sent by text message to each person's phone" (free, but a text can be redirected and a fake page can ask for the code).

## Interactions in detail
- Tiles open in any order. A flag-found tile reopens straight to its Patch card; a patched tile opens read-only with the answers shown.
- Patch: choosing a wrong option shows its reason (its cost and gain) and lets you choose again; +50 only for a right first choice; the tile turns green whenever the right one is chosen, and the "Traded: ..." line appears.
- Hint use is shown on the tile (a small "hint" tag) and halves that flag's points.
- Log artifacts get a small toolbar: sort by any column, filter by account. Row taps expand details where Content gives them.
- Text tools work on any text; Strength runs on each keystroke; nothing typed is stored.
- Progress saves on this device: localStorage `curio-capture-the-flag` holds only tile states, hint use, wrong tries and patch results (no typed text). Clear progress asks Yes/No.
- When all 12 tiles are patched: one line under the score, "Every hole is patched." No celebration beyond that.
- Motion off: no tile flip; states change in place.
- Log tables wider than their card fade at the right edge; open details wrap within the visible part of the card; a long log scrolls inside its own card so the column names stay in view.
- Sizes: board tiles at least 84 x 64 px (on a phone the tile rows shrink, 64 to 110 px, so the board fits the window); at 1200 px of stage or more (and 820 tall) artifact text is 16 px and the panel widens to as much as 560 px; in the challenge view the artifact scrolls inside its own card while Flag and Submit stay visible without page scrolling at every size in STANDARDS.md; at 760 wide the panel goes below with Flag first; at 375 wide the board is 2 columns by category with row labels. Artifact text is monospace 14 px or more (13 px in the 680x425 preview).
- Keyboard: Tab goes modes, header, tiles (arrow keys move within the grid), then in a challenge: artifact spots, Flag, Submit, Hint, Toolbox, Patch.
- `window.__tool = {state, sec, setMode, open(tile), submit(tile, text), patch(tile, choice)}`.
