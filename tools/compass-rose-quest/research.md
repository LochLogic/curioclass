# Research packet: compass-rose-quest
Concept: Follow compass directions and a map key across a park to find treasure, telling natural features from built ones, then make your own room map with a key and clues and watch a parrot try to follow it.
Grade: 1   Standards: D2.Geo.1.K-2, D2.Geo.3.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Geo.1.K-2: "Construct maps, graphs, and other representations of familiar places." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Draw a simple map of a familiar room using geometric shapes, symbols, and a matching key.
- D2.Geo.3.K-2: "Use maps, globes, and other simple geographic models to identify cultural and environmental characteristics of places." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Navigate a park map using cardinal directions (North, South, East, West) and distinguish natural land features from human-built structures.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing "North" with looking straight up at the sky: First graders often point upward toward the ceiling when asked where North is, confusing 2D vertical map orientation with physical altitude (Source: C3 Framework Geography indicators D2.Geo.1.K-2).
- Drawing 3D side views instead of overhead bird's-eye views: When asked to map a bed or table, students often draw side legs and pillows rather than an aerial flat rectangle (Source: California History-Social Science Framework Grade 1).
- Assuming symbols must be photo-realistic drawings: Children often spend excessive time drawing realistic trees rather than simple map icons explained in the key (Source: National Geographic Geography Education Guidelines).
- Classifying human-made plant beds as natural features: Students regularly confuse landscaped park elements (flower gardens, manicured grass, fountains) with wild natural features (Source: California History-Social Science Content Standards 1.2).

## Sensitivity
- Housing and room variation: When asking children to draw a room map, provide options to map the classroom, a playground, or an imaginary clubhouse so children in shared or temporary housing feel comfortable.
- Spatial orientation support: Provide clear visual anchors (color-coded direction arrows) to support students developing spatial awareness.

## Gaps
- Universal standardized elementary icon set: Map symbols for items like playground swings or park bridges differ across school textbook publishers without a single federal standard.
