# Hola Hallway (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-k-12-greetings-and-introductions, grade k-12. Codes: ACTFL.Novice Low, WRS.Communication
- Format / family: showdown / spanish. Fit: good when solo-first: one student greets the hallway alone; the class vote is the class mode.
- One idea: People walk down a school hallway at different times of day, and you greet each one in Spanish, choosing by the clock and by who they are (a friend gets "¿Qué tal?", the principal gets "¿Cómo está usted?"), so you learn that a greeting depends on the time and on the person.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a sunny morning hallway with a wall clock at 8:00. A classmate walks in and waves. "Say hello in Spanish. Tap your greeting, then say it out loud."
  - Core loop: each passer-by carries clues (the clock, the person's role, their mood). The student picks a greeting, a name exchange (Me llamo..., ¿Cómo te llamas?), a feelings answer and a courtesy phrase (por favor, gracias, de nada, con permiso). The character answers in recorded Spanish and reacts: a "¡Hola!" to the principal gets a friendly raised eyebrow, not a buzzer. Optional: record your own line and play it back next to the model (kept on this device only).
  - The aha or the end state: the day runs from buenos días to buenas tardes to buenas noches at the evening concert, and the student sees the tú and usted split mapped on a "who gets which" chart they filled in themselves.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the projector shows each passer-by and the class votes on the greeting before the reveal.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Novice Low: memorized greetings, names, feelings and courtesy phrases in isolated words and set phrases.
  - WRS.Communication: interpersonal exchanges with each character, interpretive listening to their replies, and choosing register (tú or usted).
- Not like: asl-hello-booth (ASL, attention norms); el-restaurante (requests and food, Novice High); calendario-vivo (dates and weather).
- Art: illustrated people from many Spanish-speaking backgrounds (people, illustrated), because the greeting depends on reading the person.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Greet each person walking down a school hallway in Spanish, choosing by the clock and by who they are (friend or principal, tú or usted); hear them answer, and fill in your own chart of which greeting fits whom.
- Flags: needs a data source (recorded native Spanish voices, several regions); mic is optional and shows the on-device line (rule 7), no speech recognition; K-12 span check (gf-04).
