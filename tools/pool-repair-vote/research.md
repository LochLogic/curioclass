# Research packet: pool-repair-vote
Concept: The town pool needs repairs; students hear a grandparent, a swim team parent, a shop owner and a teen lifeguard, follow the plan from problem to hearing to vote, and choose how to pay for it: taxes, entry fees or both.
Grade: 3   Standards: D2.Civ.13.3-5, D2.Civ.10.3-5, D2.Eco.12.3-5

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Civ.13.3-5: "Explain how policies are developed to address public problems." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Follow a community problem from initial discovery through public hearing testimony and town council debate to a formal funding policy vote.
- D2.Civ.10.3-5: "Identify the beliefs, experiences, perspectives, and values that underlie their own and others' points of view about civic issues." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain how personal circumstances (such as a senior on a fixed income, a swim parent, a merchant, or a lifeguard) shape differing viewpoints on town spending.
- D2.Eco.12.3-5: "Explain the ways in which the government pays for the goods and services it provides." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Contrast paying for a public facility through general municipal taxes versus charging direct user entry fees.

## Common mistakes
- Believing town pools and parks are free: Third graders frequently think public facilities cost nothing because there is no cashier at the entrance, missing that local tax dollars pay for water, electricity, chemicals, and lifeguards (Source: National League of Cities Municipal Finance Guide).
- Confusing general taxes with user fees: Students often treat all government payments as taxes, failing to distinguish between general taxes paid by all property owners and user fees paid only by those who enter (Source: C3 Framework Economics Indicators D2.Eco.12.3-5).
- Viewing differing viewpoints as an argument between good and bad people: Children often assume someone who opposes a tax increase dislikes children, missing that senior citizens on fixed pensions must budget carefully (Source: C3 Framework Civics Indicators D2.Civ.10.3-5).
- Thinking public decisions must be all-or-nothing: Students often overlook compromise solutions, such as pairing a small tax allocation with a discounted youth pass (Source: California History-Social Science Framework Grade 3).

## Sensitivity
- Fixed incomes and economic vulnerability: Treat the grandparent's concern about tax increases on a fixed pension with empathy, while also ensuring the lifeguard's concern about keeping admission affordable for low-income kids is respected.
- Democratic deliberative tone: Model polite public hearing decorum, demonstrating that listening to opposing perspectives leads to stronger, fairer community compromises.
