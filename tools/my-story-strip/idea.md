# My Story Strip (idea card)

- Module: ela-k-draw-tell-and-write, grade k. Codes: W.K.1, W.K.2, W.K.3
- Format / family: studio / comic-maker. Fit: good.
- One idea: The child makes a three-panel strip in one of three kinds (what I like and why, all about a thing, what happened), drawing each panel, telling it into the microphone and adding a word or sentence label, so they see that each kind of writing has its own job.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three big doors: "Tell what you like", "Teach about something", "Tell what happened", each with a picture example behind it.
  - Core loop: draw a panel (stamps and a simple brush), record what it says (or skip), then label it from a word bank or by sounding out. Each kind has its own panel prompts: opinion (my topic, my choice, because); informative (what it is, one fact, another fact); narrative (first, then, at the end and how I felt).
  - The aha or the end state: the strip plays back with the child's voice and labels, and the narrator points to the panel that makes it an opinion, a teaching strip or a story.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): print the strip. On screen: recordings never leave this device.
- Standards, one line per code: W.K.1: opinion strip naming a topic and a preference with a reason; W.K.2: informative strip naming a topic and facts about it; W.K.3: narrative strip with events in order and a reaction.
- Not like: Dialogue Director (grade 3 sibling, speech bubbles to quotation marks). First, Next, Last (grade 1 sequencing with transition words). Owl Wants More (adding details when asked).
- Art: drawn in code for the child's canvas and stamps, plus generated example strips behind each door, because the drawing must be the child's own.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pick a strip: what I like and why, all about a thing, or what happened; draw three panels, tell each one aloud, and label it with a word or sentence. Recordings stay on the device.
- Flags: none (microphone: privacy line required)
