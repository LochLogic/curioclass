## A 5-minute start

1. Read the welcome together: an idiom means something different from its words one by one.
2. Tap **Cloudburst Peak**. Look at the left picture (what the words say) and laugh about it. Press **Hear it**.
3. Read the example sentence aloud, then answer the check. The right picture appears: what people really mean.

## The big idea

Idioms are everyday expressions whose meaning can't be worked out word by word. English learners meet them constantly in conversation, books and TV. Seeing the literal picture next to the real meaning makes the gap visible, and often funny, which helps the idiom stick.

## The twelve places

Cloudburst Peak (raining cats and dogs), Bakery Bay (a piece of cake), Iceberg Point (break the ice), Drizzle Hill (under the weather), Bean Farm (spill the beans), Library Village (hit the books), Moonlight Lagoon (once in a blue moon), Pig Meadow (when pigs fly), Horse Ranch (hold your horses), Butterfly Garden (butterflies in my stomach), Hot Springs (in hot water) and Tennis Court (the ball is in your court).

## Talk about it

- Which idioms have a picture that helps you remember the meaning? Which ones don't?
- Does your home language have an idiom like any of these? What does it say word for word?
- Use two idioms in a sentence about your own week.
- Act one out and let a partner guess it.

## Good to know

- Each idiom, its meaning and its example sentence are recorded with a natural voice. Play them as often as you like.
- The real-meaning picture stays blurred until the check is answered, so students think first.
