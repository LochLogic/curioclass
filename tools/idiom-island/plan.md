arc: explore-first
minutes: 40
focus: WIDA.ELD-1
goal: I can explain what an English idiom says word for word, what it really means, and use it in a sentence.
materials: A device per student or pair, with headphones. The tool on the projector. Index cards.
grouping: Pairs on devices, with whole-class talk.
---
## Before class

- Open the tool on the projector to the welcome screen.
- Invite students to bring an idiom from their home language. Make it an invitation, not a requirement.
- Stamps save in each browser, so students should keep the same device.

## Hook (5 min)

1. Say, very seriously: "I'm a little under the weather today, but this lesson will be a piece of cake." Then ask: "Am I standing under a cloud? Am I eating cake?"
2. "English is full of sayings that don't mean what the words say. They're called idioms. Today you explore an island made of them."

## Explore (15 min)

1. On the projector, tap **Cloudburst Peak** together. Look at the left picture, what the words say, and laugh about it. Press **Hear it**.
2. Read the example sentence aloud and answer the check. The real-meaning picture stays blurred until you answer, so think first.
3. Pairs explore on their own devices and earn as many stamps as they can. Push for at least six of the twelve places, like **Bakery Bay**, **Iceberg Point** and **Bean Farm**.
4. Circulate and ask: "What does it say? What does it mean? How did you figure it out?"

## Make sense (8 min)

1. "Which idioms have a picture that helps you remember the meaning? Which don't?"
2. Sort the idioms on the board into two groups: easy to guess from the words (**Library Village**: hit the books) and impossible to guess (**Moonlight Lagoon**: once in a blue moon).
3. Invite home-language idioms: "Does your language have one like these? What does it say word for word?" Translate one together and enjoy how strange it sounds in English.

## Apply (8 min)

1. Charades: one student acts out an idiom literally, like **Horse Ranch** (hold your horses), and the partner guesses it.
2. Each student writes two sentences about their own week, using two idioms correctly.

## Exit check (4 min)

On an index card: pick one idiom and write what the words say, what it really means, and a sentence that uses it. Cards that explain only the literal picture show who needs another round of the checks.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** press **Hear it** and read the sentence aloud more than once. Pair beginners with a stronger reader.
- **Support:** focus on four idioms only, and let students draw the literal and real meanings side by side.
- **Stretch:** collect all twelve stamps and open the **Passport**.
- **Stretch:** find an idiom in a TV show or book this week and explain it to the class.

## Short on time

- Do **Cloudburst Peak** together, then pairs explore three places (10 min).
- Exit card with one idiom (4 min).
