## What it teaches
Students weigh leadership choices across a fictional ultimate season and explain how those choices affect team culture.
They can distinguish a game result from trust, effort, belonging and respect for opponents.

## Key skills
- Consider who is included in practice and competition.
- Respond to a teammate's mistake without losing sight of the whole team.
- Resolve disagreement in a sport where players make their own calls.
- Compare short-term performance priorities with longer-term team culture.
- Use a season's choices and consequences to support a reflection on leadership.

## Support at home
- Ask your teen about a time someone made a new member feel welcome in a group. What did that person do?
- After a game or shared activity, discuss what the group did well beyond the final result.
- Talk through a fictional group-chat disagreement. Compare responding privately, publicly and staying silent.

## A 5-minute start
Open the first practice and introduce the Kestrels, a mixed high school club ultimate team with players of all genders.
The student was just voted captain. Ultimate is self-officiated: players make and settle their own calls under Spirit
of the Game. Read the dilemma, let pairs discuss, then preview a card with one tap and commit it with a second.
Ask the first Talk about it question before pressing Next. Leave Team board off at first so the story leads.

## The season
There is one story, no mode selector, timer, leaderboard or correct-answer badge. Six chapters cover inclusion,
playing a new team, group-chat blame, a contested receiving foul, tired teammates and the final loss.
Four spirit circles show opponents' ratings. Granite's circle appears before the group chat and uses the dials
after Harbor; a later chat choice cannot retroactively change that game.
The transition names the Game 2 loss before Granite's ratings, and Next names the upcoming team chat.

Every season ends with the same results: Harbor 13-9 win, Granite 8-13 loss, Pines 13-12 win, Summit 10-13 loss.
Choices change the fictional culture dials, spirit ratings and epilogue. They do not control sports results.
The four dials start at 5, clamp to 0-10, and describe 0-3 as low, 4-6 as steady and 7-10 as strong.
These are an explicit story model, not a validated assessment of students or a prediction about real teammates.

## Talk questions
1. What does a new player need in the first week?
2. Is winning big disrespectful? Where is the line?
3. Should team conflict be handled in a group chat?
4. Why might a sport with no referees still work?
5. What's the difference between pushing a team and punishing it?
6. What do you say to a teammate whose mistake ended the season?

## Good to know
Tap once to select, twice to commit. Keys 1, 2 and 3 select only; Enter commits. Tab and Enter also work.
Read reads the current instruction, scene and choices, or the consequence, discussion question and spirit ratings.
Sound controls narration and tones. Motion removes movement without changing when students can act.
Start over begins a fresh season. Play again retains only the previous season's ghost bars in page memory.
Nothing about the season is saved or sent. Reload clears the comparison. The shared Motion preference is remembered
on this device by the Curio Class kit. There are no generated people, faces or body parts.
The jersey numbers are fictional visual identifiers, not a teaching statistic.

- [WFDF Rules of Ultimate 2025-2028](https://rules.wfdf.sport/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/WFDF-Rules-of-Ultimate-2025-2028.pdf).
  Rule 17.2.2 returns a contested receiving foul to the thrower. The scene uses this specific case; other contested
  calls do not all have the same outcome. Independently confirmed during cold review, November 3, 2026, against
  [WFDF's pocket-format rules, section 17.2.2](https://rules.wfdf.sport/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/WFDF-Rules-of-Ultimate-2025-2028-Pocket-Format.pdf).

  [WFDF's Ultimate teaching resource, Spirit score sheet](https://wfdf.sport/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/ebookprint_ultimate_final_version-2-1.pdf)
  confirms the 0-4 scale, with 2 meaning Good. The culture-dial-to-rating mapping is this story's invention.
- [USA Ultimate](https://usaultimate.org/), mixed division context.
- Team, people, dialogue, game scores and dial effects are fictional and supplied by the tool spec.
