# Captain's Choices

## One idea
You captain a self-refereed ultimate team through a season whose scores are fixed (2 wins, 2 losses in every playthrough), so the only thing your choices change is the team: four culture dials, the spirit scores opponents give you, and who comes back next year.

## Not like
- rules-referee (6-8 PE, escape): official rules called from a replay booth. Here there are no referees at all; players make and settle their own calls, and the question is how a team behaves, not what the rule says.
- fitness-program-designer (9-12 PE, builder): solo planning with numbers. Captain's Choices is a branching story about people.
- conflict-coach (6-8 SEL, roleplay): practices I-statements in single peer conflicts. Captain's Choices runs a whole season where early choices pay off later, seen through a leader's role.
- safety-street (K-2 health, story): one walk with safe and unsafe choices. Captain's Choices has no right answer per scene; every choice trades one value for another.

## Who and how
- Grade 9-12, Physical Education, module Sportsmanship and Leadership (responsible behavior and teamwork).
- Driver: students (alone or pairs on a laptop), or the teacher on a projector with the class voting each choice and discussing.
- Minutes: 15-25 per playthrough; a second playthrough takes about 10.
- Standards: SHAPE.S3 (develops social skills through movement: leadership, conflict resolution, sportsmanship, inclusion).
- Topic line: "sportsmanship, leadership and team culture".
- Modes (tool.json): [] (one story; the season card at the end).

## Screens and modes
Header (theme-912): title "Captain's Choices" with small line "Sportsmanship, leadership and team culture", **Motion**, **Sound**, **Read**. No mode buttons.

- Stage: the scene. A background painting (Art) with a scene card over it: chapter label ("Game 2 vs Granite"), a scoreboard chip when a game is on, the situation text (3 to 5 sentences), and the people involved as initial avatars (round chips with initials and a jersey color, never faces). The group chat scene (chapter 3) is drawn as a phone chat in code instead of a card.
- Panel: three choice cards (tap one), then after the choice the consequence text, a "Team mood" line in words ("Trust went up. Belonging went down."), the **Talk about it** prompt (a question for the class), and **Next →**.
- Panel footer: **Team board** toggle (off by default): shows the four dials as bars with numbers while playing. **↺ Start over**.
- After a game chapter: the **Spirit circle** card on the stage: the opponents' spirit score for the Kestrels in the five categories (0 to 4 each, with words), its total out of 20, and one line from the opponents' captain.
- End: the **Season card** (stage): record "2 wins, 2 losses. The same in every season you play.", the four dials with words, the four spirit totals as a small line, the epilogue lines (Content), and the last run's dials as faint ghost bars if this is a second playthrough in the same page session. Buttons **Play again: same scores, different team** and **See every choice you made** (a list of the six choices).

## Content

### The team
The Kestrels, a high school club ultimate team in the mixed division (players of all genders on the field together). Ultimate has no referees: players make their own calls, guided by the Spirit of the Game. You were just voted captain.
- **Priya** (PR): third-year, best thrower, high standards.
- **Marcus** (MA): senior, fastest player, quick to blame.
- **Lena** (LE): first-year, quiet, just moved to town.
- **Theo** (TH): sophomore, eager, still drops easy catches.
- **Coach Ruiz** (CR): a volunteer coach who lets captains lead.

### Dials
Trust, Effort, Belonging, Respect (for opponents and the game). Each starts at 5, range 0 to 10 (clamped). Words: 0 to 3 "low", 4 to 6 "steady", 7 to 10 "strong".

### Chapters (choice: effects; consequence)
**1. First practice.** "Twenty players show up. Marcus wants an A team versus B team scrimmage so the starters can 'get real reps.' Lena is standing alone near the cones."
- a) Mix the lines and pair each new player with a veteran. Belonging +2, Trust +1. "Lena ends up next to Priya, who shows her how to call the force. By the end Lena is calling it herself. Marcus mutters that the scrimmage was sloppy."
- b) Let Marcus run the A team; new players can watch and learn. Effort +1, Belonging -2. "The A team looks sharp. Lena spends most of practice on the sideline and leaves early."
- c) Stop and have the team agree on three team rules first. Trust +2, Respect +1, Effort -1. "It takes twenty minutes. The team picks: show up, talk on the field, make fair calls. Less throwing, but everyone spoke."
Talk about it: What does a new player need in the first week?

**2. Game 1 vs Harbor (win, 13-9).** "Halftime, up 10-4. Harbor is a new team with lots of beginners. Your top line wants to stay in and keep throwing long trick shots."
- a) Rotate everyone in and run the plays we practiced. Belonging +2, Effort +1. "Theo gets his first score of the season. Harbor makes a run late, but you win 13-9."
- b) Keep the top line in and score as many as we can. Effort +1, Belonging -1, Respect -1. "You pull away, then ease off late. You win 13-9. Harbor's captain says 'good game' a little stiffly."
- c) Let the team play trick shots and celebrate loudly after each score. Belonging +1, Respect -2. "The bench is loud and having fun. Harbor stops smiling. You win 13-9."
Talk about it: Is winning big disrespectful? Where is the line?

**3. After Game 2 vs Granite (loss, 8-13).** Group chat. Marcus: "Theo dropped 4 easy catches. That's the game." Theo stops replying.
- a) Message Marcus privately to cool it, then post: "We lost as a team. Film session Tuesday." Trust +2, Belonging +1. "Marcus sends a thumbs-up. Theo shows up Tuesday early."
- b) Post: "Drops lose games. Everyone has to step up." Effort +1, Trust -2, Belonging -1. "A few people react with a fire emoji. Theo leaves the chat for a day."
- c) Stay out of it; it will blow over. Trust -1, Belonging -1. "It doesn't blow over. Two more players take sides."
Talk about it: Should team conflict be handled in a group chat?

**4. Game 3 vs Pines (win, 13-12).** "Universe point, 12-12. A Pines defender bumps Priya as she reaches for a catch; the disc hits the ground. Priya calls 'Foul!' The Pines player says 'Contest. I got there first.' In ultimate, a contested call sends the disc back to the last thrower."
- a) Walk over, help them talk it through calmly, accept the contest and restart. Respect +2, Trust +1. "It takes a minute. Play restarts with the disc back. Two passes later, Lena catches the winning score."
- b) Back Priya loudly and tell Pines they always cheat. Respect -3, Trust +1. "Priya feels backed. The Pines sideline goes quiet and cold. You still score the winner."
- c) Tell Priya to drop the call so you look good. Respect +1, Trust -2. "Pines gets the disc. Priya stares at you. You get it back on a turnover and still score the winner."
Talk about it: Why might a sport with no referees still work?

**5. Midweek practice.** "Everyone is tired from tests. Two players skipped without saying why. Marcus says practice is pointless."
- a) Ask the team what's going on, then run a short practice of mini-games. Effort +1, Trust +1, Belonging +1. "You learn one skipper has a new job. The mini-games get loud and competitive in a good way."
- b) Run extra sprints for everyone because two people skipped. Effort +1, Trust -2, Belonging -1. "Everyone runs. Nobody talks on the way to the parking lot."
- c) Cancel practice. Effort -2. "People are relieved. Game day feels a little rusty."
Talk about it: What's the difference between pushing a team and punishing it?

**6. Game 4 vs Summit, playoffs (loss, 10-13).** "Down 10-12, Theo throws the turnover that lets Summit score the final point. Your season is over. The bench is silent."
- a) Huddle up: thank the seniors, name one thing each player did well, then join Summit's spirit circle. Trust +1, Belonging +1, Respect +1. "Theo hears that his defense saved two points. Summit's captain thanks you for a clean game."
- b) Say "losses happen" and head straight to the bus. Trust -1. "The ride home is quiet. Theo sits alone."
- c) Ask Marcus, the senior, to speak first. Trust +1. If Trust is 6 or more after this choice: "Marcus thanks Theo for never giving up on a point." Otherwise: "Marcus says 'we should have won.' Nobody answers."
Talk about it: What do you say to a teammate whose mistake ended the season?

### Spirit score (four spirit circles: after chapter 2 for Game 1, at the start of chapter 3 for Game 2 before the chat opens, after chapter 4 for Game 3, after chapter 6 for Game 4; Game 2 uses the dial rule only)
The opponents score the Kestrels in the five WFDF categories: Rules knowledge and use; Fouls and body contact; Fair-mindedness; Attitude and self-control; Communication. Words: 0 Poor, 1 Not good, 2 Good, 3 Very good, 4 Excellent.
- Start each category at 2 (Good).
- Respect 7 or more after the chapter: Fair-mindedness +1 and Attitude +1. Respect 3 or less: both -1.
- Chapter effects: 2c Attitude -1. 4a Communication +1, Fair-mindedness +1. 4b Attitude -2, Communication -1. 4c Fair-mindedness +1. 6a Communication +1.
- Clamp each category to 0 to 4; total out of 20.
- Opponent captain line: total 14 or more "Great spirit. Thanks for a fun game." 10 to 13 "Good game." 9 or less "That one got heated. Let's talk next time."

### Epilogue lines (Season card; one line per person, then team lines)
- Lena: Belonging 6 or more: "Lena signs up first for next season and brings a friend." Otherwise: "Lena doesn't sign up again. She says she never felt part of the team."
- Theo: Trust 6 or more: "Theo spends the summer throwing with friends and comes back sure-handed." Otherwise: "Theo switches to cross-country. 'Less yelling,' he says."
- Marcus: Respect 6 or more and Trust 5 or more: "Marcus volunteers at the middle school clinic before he graduates." Otherwise: "Marcus graduates. The team remembers his speed more than his words."
- Team, Respect 7 or more: "The league votes the Kestrels its spirit award." Effort 7 or more: "Practice turnout is the best it has been in years." Neither: "Next season starts with a lot to build."
Worked paths (verify agent recomputes): all a's end Trust 10, Effort 7, Belonging 10, Respect 8 (every good epilogue). All b's end Trust 1, Effort 9, Belonging 0, Respect 1 (Lena and Theo leave, turnout line shows: a hard-driving team that wins the same games).

## Interactions in detail
- One choice per chapter; after tapping, the other two cards fade, the chosen one stays, the consequence and Team mood appear. No undo inside a chapter; Start over restarts the season.
- Team mood names every dial that moved, in the order Trust, Effort, Belonging, Respect, with "went up" or "went down". With Team board on, bars ease to new values (instant with Motion off).
- Scores and records never change with choices. The scoreboard chip shows the final score only on the consequence screen.
- Classroom voting: tapping a choice card once selects it with a "Tap again to choose" label (so a teacher can point and discuss first); a second tap commits. Keyboard: 1, 2, 3 select; Enter commits.
- Nothing is saved; the ghost bars of the last run last only while the page is open.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 the scene card and all three choice cards are visible without scrolling; under 900 px tall the choices come first in the panel and the Talk about it prompt folds under a **Talk about it** button. Preview sizes keep scene text at 12 px or more by re-laying out the card, not scaling it.
