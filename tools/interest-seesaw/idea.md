# Interest Seesaw (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-5-interest-seesaw, grade 5. Codes: D2.Eco.9.3-5, D2.Eco.10.3-5, NSPFE.Saving, NSPFE.Managing Credit
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Put $100 of birthday money in a bank, a credit union or a savings bond and watch interest pile up year by year, then borrow $200 for a bike and watch interest pile on; a seesaw tips toward you or away from you, and a peek inside the bank shows your savings lent to a neighbor at a higher rate, which is how the bank pays you.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a kid with an envelope of birthday money, three doors (bank, credit union, savings bond) and a seesaw with a coin pile on each end.
  - Core loop: choose where to save and how many years; the saving side of the seesaw grows. Then borrow for a bike with a monthly payment; the paying side grows by the interest. Change the rate or the time and watch both sides.
  - The aha or the end state: the inside-the-bank view: deposits become a neighbor's car loan, and the gap between the two rates runs the bank. A credit union is owned by its members; a savings bond lends to the government.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "pay it off faster" slider on the bike loan.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.9.3-5: the inside-the-bank view of banks, credit unions and bonds moving money from savers to borrowers.
  - D2.Eco.10.3-5: the rate slider and the yearly interest stack show what an interest rate is.
  - NSPFE.Saving: comparing where to save and how time grows savings.
  - NSPFE.Managing Credit: the bike loan, its total cost and paying it off faster.
- Not like: skateboard-fund-chances (grade 4, risk and insurance); stock-market-1929 (grade 11, borrowing to invest); learn-and-earn (earning, not interest).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the kid, the teller and the neighbor; the seesaw drawn in code because it is a live instrument.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (realistic current savings and loan rates and savings bond terms, dated on screen; real-world rates by default per rule 9)
