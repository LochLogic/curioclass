# Research packet: interest-seesaw
Concept: Put birthday money in a bank, a credit union or a savings bond and watch interest add up, then borrow for a bike and watch interest add on; a seesaw shows when interest works for you and when it works against you.
Grade: 5   Standards: D2.Eco.9.3-5, D2.Eco.10.3-5, NSPFE.Saving, NSPFE.Managing Credit

## Standards in plain words

- D2.Eco.9.3-5: "Describe the role of other financial institutions in an economy." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to describe how banks, credit unions, and government savings programs safeguard money and issue loans.
- D2.Eco.10.3-5: "Explain what interest rates are." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to explain that interest is either money earned on savings or extra money paid back when borrowing.
- NSPFE.Saving: "Explain why financial institutions pay interest to depositors." (https://www.councilforeconed.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-National-Standards-for-Personal-Financial-Education.pdf). The student must be able to explain that institutions pay interest to attract deposits that they can lend to other borrowers.
- NSPFE.Managing Credit: "Credit allows people to purchase and enjoy goods and services today, while agreeing to pay for them in the future, usually with interest." (https://www.councilforeconed.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/2021-National-Standards-for-Personal-Financial-Education.pdf). The student must be able to explain how borrowing adds interest charges to the total cost of buying an item.

## Real data

- US Department of the Treasury (TreasuryDirect) Savings Bond Rates: https://www.treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/ (Public domain). Shows current Series EE fixed rates, 20-year doubling rule, and Series I inflation-adjusted composite rates.
- Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) National Deposit Rates: https://www.fdic.gov/resources/bankers/national-rates/ (Public domain). Historical average annual percentage yields on savings and money market accounts.
- Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED Consumer Credit Database: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TERMCBCCALLNS (Public domain). Data series tracking average interest rates on consumer credit and installment loans.

## Common mistakes

- Treating credit like free gift money: Fifth graders often think taking out a loan is free cash, missing that every dollar borrowed plus interest must be repaid from future earnings (Council for Economic Education).
- Confusing interest earned with interest paid: Students frequently confuse the two directions of interest, forgetting that savers earn interest while borrowers pay interest (FDIC Money Smart).
- Imagining deposited cash sits in an individual drawer: Students often assume their birthday money rests untouched in a bank drawer, unaware that institutions lend deposited funds out to finance mortgages, car loans, and business investments (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis).
- Assuming banks and credit unions are identical: Students assume all financial institutions are for-profit corporations, missing that credit unions are member-owned non-profit cooperatives (NCUA).

## Sensitivity

- Family financial diversity: Students come from households with widely differing levels of savings, credit access, and debt. Keep the scenario centered on a relatable kid context (birthday gift money and saving for a bicycle) without moralizing about poverty or judging families with debt.
- Avoid predatory loan depictions: Do not include payday lenders or high-risk traps; focus cleanly on standard depository savings and installment credit.
- None seen for living culture or religion.

## Similar tools elsewhere

- Practical Money Skills Financial Football / Peter Pig's Money Counter (https://www.practicalmoneyskills.com): Interactive youth games teaching basic earning, saving, and budgeting concepts.
- FDIC Money Smart for Young People (https://www.fdic.gov/resources/consumers/money-smart/): Interactive classroom activities modeling savings and interest calculation.
- Hands on Banking (https://handsonbanking.org): Free simulation modules comparing saving up for a purchase against paying with credit.

## Gaps

- Compounding math for fifth graders: Continuous or fractional daily exponential math is too advanced for ten-year-olds; Curio Class tools should illustrate balance shifts with clean monthly or yearly stepped visual intervals ($100 saved -> $105 after interest; $100 borrowed -> $110 repaid) on the seesaw beam.
