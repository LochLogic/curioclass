## A 5-minute start

1. In **Festival**, ask: "Who made tonight happen?" Tap the **Ferris wheel**. A card lists four jobs. Tap **Welder** to meet the person: what they do, pay, the training most people start with, and the job outlook.
2. Look up: the **Advanced Manufacturing** lantern over the fair is lit. There are 14 lanterns, one for each career cluster.
3. Switch to **Trails** and pick **Kettle corn**. Tap **Next** four times: a farmer, a food scientist, a truck driver, a cook and an accountant. One bag of kettle corn took 4 clusters.

## The three modes

- **Festival:** Maple Bend Harvest Night, a made-up town at dusk. Drag the town sideways (or use the mouse wheel, the arrow keys or the ◀ ▶ buttons). 37 glowing places hold 93 careers. Meeting a career here (open it from a place's card or a trail stop) lights its cluster's lantern. **Still dark** lists the clusters not found yet; tap one to pan to a place that has one. **List** shows all 37 places as a plain list, with a filter for the ones not opened yet. **Start over** clears the lanterns (it asks first).
- **Trails:** five chains of people behind one thing at the fair: the kettle corn, the stage lights, your ticket, a twisted ankle and the new bridge. **Next** and **Back** step through them, and the town pans to each stop: the job's card (title, cluster and one line) sits on the map at its numbered dot, and earlier stops keep their job titles there, so the whole chain reads on the map. The last stop shows how many clusters it took at the top of the map. On a phone the ◀ ▶ arrows hide in Trails (Next and Back pan the town; dragging still works). **Open career** meets the person at any stop.
- **Clusters:** all 14 clusters with how many careers each has here. Tap one for a one-line description of the cluster and its **ways in**: its careers sorted by the education most people start with, from no degree to grad school, with median pay on one shared scale and the job outlook. **See it** jumps to that job at the festival. Opening a job from the ladder doesn't light its lantern; meet it at the festival for that.

## Talk about it

- Pick one thing in your room (a pencil, the lights, your shoes). Which clusters did it take to get here?
- Which cluster surprised you? Which jobs did you not expect to find in it?
- In Clusters, compare Education or Public Service (careers in this tool at every level) with Hospitality (two levels here). The tool shows only a few careers per cluster: what jobs might fill Hospitality's empty columns in real life? Where could you check?
- An outlook of "Decline" does not mean a job is disappearing. What else might it mean?

## Good to know

- The 14 clusters are Advance CTE's Career Clusters framework (2024).
- Typical entry-level education and job outlook (2025-35) come from the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' Occupational Outlook Handbook; every career's sheet links its Handbook page. Some careers sit inside a larger Handbook profile (a game designer inside Web Developers and Digital Designers, a robotics technician inside Electro-mechanical and Mechatronics Technologists and Technicians); the sheet names the profile.
- "Entry-level education" is what most people start with. Many jobs have other ways in, and the "How people get there" line on each sheet says more.
- Median pay is for the exact job, from BLS, May 2025, national. When the Handbook page covers a larger group, the sheet gives that group's pay too. Musicians and actors have hourly pay only, because much of their work is part-time.
- "People doing it" is the Handbook's number of jobs in 2025 for the linked profile, which counts people who work for themselves (a lot of farmers, for example).
- Each cluster's one-line description is a plain-word version of Advance CTE's "The Cluster Defined".
- Each cluster here has 4 to 13 careers; a real cluster has many more. "Careers in this tool: 3 of 5 levels" is about this tool's careers, not the whole cluster.
- All four Manufacturing careers are at the Ferris wheel, so Where? sends you back there until you open one.
- The lanterns, and the careers and places a student opened, are saved in this browser on this device only. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- Maple Bend, its festival and the trails are made up. The town is painted art (FLUX.1-schnell, drawn on a local computer): a land strip and one sprite per place. The sky, stars, string lights, road and every sign and number (FAIR, 72°, 2 : 1, LIBRARY, SALE, $, HARDWARE, HIRING, HOTEL) are code, so the words stay sharp on a projector.
