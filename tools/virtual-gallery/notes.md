## What it teaches

Students can select a coherent portfolio, refine its physical presentation, and communicate its meaning through sequence, labels and a statement. A visitor walk tests whether the exhibition presents the story they intended.

## Key skills

- Choose works for a theme and explain the exceptions.
- Arrange frames with consistent center lines, useful gaps and lighting.
- Predict what a visitor notices first from the doorway.
- Use shared ideas to connect neighboring works.
- Write concise labels and an artist statement for an audience.

## Support at home

- Choose six familiar objects together. Ask which five share an idea and why the sixth might still belong.
- Walk into a familiar room. Compare what each person notices first and what placement contributes.
- Explain a favorite object in one short sentence, then revise the sentence for someone unfamiliar with it.

## A five-minute start

Choose Where I'm From. Open two contrasting works, add them, and star the one that matters most to you. Switch to Hang, choose a tray work, select Back wall, and use Hang on wall. Drag another frame beside it, or use the same keyboard controls. Turn on Light for each. Enter Walk and compare First seen with your star. Return to Select to build a six-to-eight-work show.

## Modes

Select offers 14 works attributed to Jordan Reyes, a fictional high school artist. Titles, materials, dimensions, years and tags are specified content, not historical artworks. Your own images are optional, stay in memory, and need a title and tags. They disappear on reload or closing the page.

Hang uses an 800 by 600 cm room, 320 cm high, with a centered 120 cm door. Each door-side wall is 340 cm wide. Select a tray work, choose a wall, and use Hang on wall, or drag it onto a wall. Arrow keys move a selected hung work 5 cm along its wall; Shift moves 25 cm. Delete or Remove from wall returns it to the tray. Drag the tray to reorder, or use Bring to front and Move later. Frames include a 5 cm mat and 3 cm frame on each edge. Wave Bowl uses a 30 cm shelf. Plan is a second way to place work; short previews and phones use the plan as the main view.

Walk is a visitor camera at 160 cm eye height. Tray order determines stops. Each move takes one second and each stop two seconds, including with Motion off. Stop holds the current camera. Step, Back and order-strip buttons interrupt immediately. The direction toggle begins again at the door. The large wall-label inset stays readable independently of the room's perspective.

Checklist gives live findings, including the measurements behind them. Show me selects a relevant work or wall in Hang. Print show provides a black-on-white exhibition sheet with plan, statement and numbered labels. Clear show asks for confirmation.

## Talk about it

- What makes a work belong besides matching a theme tag?
- Did the doorway reveal your intended anchor? What changed when you moved it?
- Does an equal gap look equal between works of different sizes?
- Which transition changes the meaning of the neighboring works?
- What does a checklist miss about an exhibition's quality?

## Good to know

The stated accessibility guidance places wall-label centers at 137 cm (54 in), within a comfortable band of 122 to 168 cm (48 to 67 in). Seated eye level is approximately 109 to 130 cm (43 to 51 in). Center-line alignment, the 30 cm gap and 15 percent spread thresholds, lighting and the tag-based flow check are practices modeled by this tool, not legal requirements. The wall line can be set from 120 to 170 cm.

Notes use a 25-word limit; statements use 100 words with no sentence over 25 words. Counts split on spaces and sentence-ending punctuation. A short, thoughtful text matters more than simply staying under the limit.

Home has six matching portfolio works, water five, city five, growing four, and night two. Growing and night cannot meet the five-work theme requirement with the supplied portfolio. This is intentional: revise the show premise, or add relevant work of your own. Water needs at least one selection on another strength.

Text and show settings are saved only on this device under virtual-gallery-show. Imported image pixels are never stored. A reload drops imported works referenced by a saved show and explains why. Storage restrictions can make the show last for this visit only. Sound uses the shared Curio Class kit and browser speech; this build has no recorded narration. All images are bundled; there are no network requests.

## Standards and sources

VA:Pr4 is practiced through selecting, comparing and identifying an anchor. VA:Pr5 is practiced through hanging, spacing, lighting and readable text. VA:Pr6 is practiced through arranging a visitor sequence, writing a statement, and revising the show after walking it.
