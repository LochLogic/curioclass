## What it teaches
Students can plan food and meal timing around a fictional athlete's fixed training schedule, then use evidence from a replay to revise their plan. Energy balance, food group variety and timing are separate checks.

## Key skills
- Add serving values across meals, days and a week.
- Compare a plan with energy and food group targets.
- Connect previous-day carbohydrate and meal timing to the fuel model.
- Test a change, interpret its result and revise a plan.

## Support at home
- Look at an ordinary day's meals together and name the food groups represented, without judging foods.
- Discuss how meal timing changes when a family activity happens early or late.
- Notice opportunities to have water available before, during and after an activity. Nothing needs to be bought for this discussion.

## Five-minute start
Open Ana's Typical week. Select a meal and add one serving from the shelf. Tap Energy, low to locate the training days below the tool threshold. Switch to Game day and press Play game day. Compare the finish with a replay after removing the pre-game foods.

## Modes
**Plan the week:** the seven-day board uses the coach's fixed schedule. Select a cell and add from the shelf; tap a food chip to remove a serving. Arrow keys navigate cells. Delete removes the last serving and keeps the cell ready for another arrow or Delete. Tab passes through one selected cell, then reaches the shelf. Four foods fit per slot.

**Game day:** the clock advances one hour every two seconds at 1x and every half second at 4x. It stops at the final whistle. A faint dashed line is the current plan's forecast through 10 pm; the bright line is the replay so far. Edits during replay retain the clock. Select a meal flag to edit, then use the illustrated removal row or the same food shelf. Move meal enables flag dragging, left/right arrow keys on a focused flag, and the Meal time slider. During stays fixed to play.

**Small screens:** the board scrolls inside its frame. Day energy bars stay visible and follow its horizontal position. Week tools opens the week actions in a dialog. The food shelf and check list have their own scroll regions.

The result traces this plan's wake, game-start and finish fuel. After a finished replay, an edit labels the recalculated outcome Forecast until Play tests it again. A strong tank finish does not mean every nutrition check is clear.

## Talk questions
- Ana can finish strongly in the model while five weekly checks remain unresolved. What does the tank leave out?
- Why does adding food after the final whistle leave the game result unchanged?
- Which change helps Marcus without copying an afternoon-game plan?
- What evidence would you use to set a specific goal for the next revision?

## Good to know
All athletes are fictional. No information about students is requested. Save keeps one plan per athlete under `gdf-plans` on this device. The kit also remembers Motion. Clear saved asks before removing the athletes' saved plans. Browser storage can be unavailable; planning still works.

The fuel tank and flag amounts are teaching rules, not measurements or personalized advice. They do not model fluid volume, absorption differences, body composition, fatigue, weather or all nutrients. A meal adds carbohydrate grams times 0.25 across the next hour. During spreads that amount across play. The tank caps at 100 and floors at zero. Awake drain is 0.05 per minute. Play drains are Ana 0.65, Priya 0.55, Marcus 0.28 and Kai 0.85 per minute. Starting fuel uses the previous day's carbohydrate. A final tank at least 30 gives Strong finish, 15 to below 30 gives Running low late, and below 15 gives Out of gas.

Rest energy uses the moderately active table; practice and game energy use the active table. This mapping is the tool's simplification. The build retains the spec's 2020–2025 calorie and food group tables. Food values are representative servings, not brands. Day energy bars use exact sums; the selected-day label uses the sibling's FDA rounding and general Daily Value percentages. Those percentages are not athlete-specific targets. MyPlate group amounts use the active level on every day.

The flags use the specified thresholds: energy under 80% or over 130%, weekly food groups under 80%, added sugars over 10% of calories, sodium over 2,300 mg, missing breakfast on training days, pre-game carbohydrate under 60 g inside 1–4 hours, fat at least 20 g or fiber at least 10 g inside 2 hours, recovery lacking a single meal with 40 g carbohydrate and 15 g protein within 2 hours, and missing pre-game or during drinks. The extra meal and nutrient thresholds are original teaching choices.

NHES.S6 is practiced by identifying a specific gap, choosing changes and comparing a new result. NHES.S7 is practiced by constructing and testing the food plan. Training itself is never edited. No student measurements or personal health data are used.

Health context: these standards can include sensitive personal health decisions. This activity addresses fictional food planning only. Keep discussion on the fictional athlete; do not ask students to disclose their intake, bodies or medical needs. Food access and preferences differ, so compare the model's tradeoffs without judging anyone's meals.
