# Face the Grammar (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-6-12-asl-grammar-and-facial-expression, grade 6-12. Codes: ACTFL.Intermediate Low, WRS.Comparisons
- Format / family: puzzle / asl. Fit: good: each level is a puzzle where the same hands mean different things depending on the face.
- One idea: The same signs play twice with different faces, and you work out what changed: a yes/no question, a wh-question, a topic set up first, a negation or a condition, finding that in ASL the face and head carry the grammar that English puts in word order and voice.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two Sign Strips of the illustrated signer with the same hand panels and different face plates, each with a large face inset. "Same hands. Different meaning. What did the face do?"
  - Core loop: levels of puzzles. Match a strip to its English meaning; pick which face makes a sentence a question; put a sentence in topic-comment order (STORE, I GO) and pick the face for the topic. The marker line over the gloss shows where each face starts and stops, and head nods, shakes and tilts play in the tween. Later levels splice: the student chooses a face plate for each part of a sentence from a short set and watches the result play.
  - The aha or the end state: a short signed conversation at Natural speed where the student must read questions, topics and negations from the face alone (the marker lines hidden), and a comparison card: how English marks a question (word order, rising voice) and how ASL does it (eyebrows, head, eye gaze).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a mirror practice step for each face pattern (camera view, never recorded; the screen says nothing leaves the device).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Intermediate Low: understanding and producing simple questions, statements and negations with correct non-manual markers and topic-comment order.
  - WRS.Comparisons: comparing how ASL and English mark questions, topics and negation.
- Not like: sign-parameters-lab (face as part of a single sign); classifier-scenes (space, not face); asl-story-theater (role shift in narrative).
- Art: graphics only, per docs/agents/ASL-GRAPHICS.md: the shared illustrated signer with the full face plate set (neutral, yes/no, wh, negation, topic, conditional and more; each reviewed against a written description of the marker; people review) and vector Sign Strip hands; the face is the content, so the face inset is large. No video, no photos of people.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: The same signs play twice with different faces; work out what changed (yes/no or wh-question, topic, negation, condition) and learn how ASL carries grammar on the face where English uses word order and voice.
- Flags: sign entries and face plates checked by the fresh blind review (ASL-GRAPHICS.md); sensitive (linguistic accuracy of each marker, fresh review, rule 5); camera mirror shows the on-device line (rule 7); the on-screen practice note (one common form; practice with a fluent Deaf signer or your ASL teacher when you can).
