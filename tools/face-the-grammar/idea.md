# Face the Grammar (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-6-12-asl-grammar-and-facial-expression, grade 6-12. Codes: ACTFL.Intermediate Low, WRS.Comparisons
- Format / family: puzzle / asl. Fit: good: each level is a puzzle where the same hands mean different things depending on the face.
- One idea: The same signs play twice with different faces, and you work out what changed: a yes/no question, a wh-question, a topic set up first, a negation or a condition, finding that in ASL the face and head carry the grammar that English puts in word order and voice.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two video clips of a Deaf signer with the same hand signs. "Same hands. Different meaning. What did the face do?"
  - Core loop: levels of puzzles. Match a clip to its English meaning; pick which face makes a sentence a question; put a sentence in topic-comment order (STORE, I GO) and pick the face for the topic. Later levels splice: the student chooses the face for each part of a sentence from a short set of filmed options and watches the result.
  - The aha or the end state: a short signed conversation at natural speed where the student must read questions, topics and negations from the face alone, and a comparison card: how English marks a question (word order, rising voice) and how ASL does it (eyebrows, head, eye gaze).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a mirror practice step for each face pattern (camera view, never recorded; the screen says nothing leaves the device).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Intermediate Low: understanding and producing simple questions, statements and negations with correct non-manual markers and topic-comment order.
  - WRS.Comparisons: comparing how ASL and English mark questions, topics and negation.
- Not like: sign-parameters-lab (face as part of a single sign); classifier-scenes (space, not face); asl-story-theater (role shift in narrative).
- Art: real signing video by fluent Deaf signers (required; the face is the content).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: The same signs play twice with different faces; work out what changed (yes/no or wh-question, topic, negation, condition) and learn how ASL carries grammar on the face where English uses word order and voice.
- Flags: needs a data source (signing video); sensitive (linguistic accuracy of each marker, fresh review, rule 5); camera mirror shows the on-device line (rule 7).
