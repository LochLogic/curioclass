# Mystery Powders (idea card)

- Module: science-5-properties-and-mixtures, grade 5. Codes: 5-PS1-3, 5-PS1-4
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good
- One idea: A school bakery's labels fell off five white powders (salt, sugar, baking soda, cornstarch, plaster) and a mystery mix; test each with a hand lens, water, vinegar, iodine, a magnet and a conductivity tester to identify them by their properties, and tell plain mixing apart from mixing that makes something new.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a bakery back room with five unlabeled jars, a test tray and a case notebook. A baker named Ana needs the jars sorted before the bake sale.
  - Core loop: run a test on a powder and log the result in a property table (crystal shape, dissolves or not, fizzes with vinegar, turns blue-black with iodine, gets warm and hard with water, conducts in salt water). Each test costs a little of each sample, so planning matters.
  - New substance check: for every mix, the student decides "just mixed" or "something new" from evidence (gas bubbles, a color change, heat, a hard solid) and can try to get the starting materials back (salt from evaporated water, yes; plaster, no).
  - The aha or the end state: the student names each jar and the mystery mix's ingredients, citing the tests in the table; the bake sale goes on.
  - Safety note: a lab-safety card says never taste lab samples and that iodine and plaster are not food.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 5-PS1-3: observing and measuring properties to identify each material.
  - 5-PS1-4: testing whether mixing two substances makes a new one, using evidence.
- Not like: particle-zoom (particles and conservation); melt-or-not (grade 2 reversible changes).
- Art: generated scenes for the bakery and test results (the fizz, the iodine color), and people (illustrated) for Ana.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Identify five unlabeled white powders and a mystery mix by testing them with water, vinegar, iodine, a magnet and a conductivity tester, and decide which mixes make a new substance.
- Flags: none
