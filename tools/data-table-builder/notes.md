## What it teaches
Students can design a table with one row per student and one column per question. They can count the same answers in different charts and choose the column and grouping that answer a question.

## Key skills
- Choose a list, whole number or yes/no answer kind.
- Collect consistent answers and organize them in rows and columns.
- Read counts, typical values and totals without using averages.
- Compare groups and support an answer with the chart's rows.

## Support at home
- Sort a few household objects by a shared feature. Make a paper table and count each choice.
- Ask a small family question with two or three choices. Draw a bar for each choice, including any with zero answers.
- Try drawing the same counts as stacked dots. Talk about what stays the same.

## A 5-minute start
1. Open Sample class. Predict which fruit is most common and tap its guess button.
2. Tap Favorite fruit, then Show me to reveal the chart. Compare Bar graph and Dot plot. The check means the column matches, not that the guess was correct.
3. Tap a bar or stack to find its rows in the table. Sort a column and notice that chart counts stay the same.
4. Try Teeth lost as a Dot plot. One dot stands for one row; the tallest stack is at 8.
5. Switch to Our class. Add Favorite season and Teeth lost before collecting three rows.

## The modes
Sample class has 24 invented student records and six questions. It is labeled made up and cannot be edited.
Our class starts empty. Choose up to six columns, then add up to 40 rows. A new column added after collection asks for explicit answers for existing rows before saving it.
Tap a cell or press Enter to edit it. Arrow keys move between cells. Escape cancels the open card.
The chart changes immediately after adding or editing a row. Column removal and row deletion require confirmation.

## Talk questions
- What does one row describe? What does one column hold?
- Which column answers this question? Do we need to split it into groups?
- Why does sorting rows leave the bar heights unchanged?
- What does a zero slot tell us? What if two choices tie?
- Does the number of yes answers tell the same story when groups are different sizes?

## Good to know
Data is saved only on this device, under dtb-our-class. Nothing is sent anywhere. Enter only choices and counts, never student names or personal details. A teacher can clear the saved data with confirmation.
Use non-sensitive columns and allow students to pass. Tooth loss varies: it is not a contest or health assessment. Jumping jacks are an optional ready column; choose another for students who prefer or need it.
The ADA's primary tooth chart gives 20 baby teeth and shedding ages of about 6 to 12. Room 4's tooth-loss values are fictional.
Question check marks confirm a matching column and split. They do not grade prediction accuracy or prove understanding.
Your own question records a chosen column and asks the student to explain its relevance. It makes no automatic correctness judgment about free text.
Charts, columns and questions are exploratory; there is no score, timer, saved best or reward for guessing every choice.
Narration uses Curio.voice with browser speech during Build. The knowing-guide recording manifest is supplied; no audio was recorded.
At small landscape sizes, main chart controls stay beside the chart. The table scrolls internally. Phone layouts stack table, chart and panel; Add my row is pinned.
On a Sample question, choose a prediction before inspecting a chart. The first column, chart or split tap after a valid guess locks it and reveals the data; Show me also reveals it. Wrong columns show their own counts. Our class charts stay live while rows are collected.
Short previews use a wider question panel and a bottom Build strip. All six custom choices fit together. Multi-column row entry can still need card scrolling, and the phone's stacked layout uses page scrolling for questions.
