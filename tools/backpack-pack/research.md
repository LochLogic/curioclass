# Research packet: backpack-pack
Concept: Pack a camping backpack with only six pockets; each item you add means leaving another behind, and the trip shows which were needs and which were wants.
Grade: k   Standards: D2.Eco.1.K-2

## Standards in plain words
D2.Eco.1.K-2: "Explain how scarcity necessitates decision making." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
What a student must be able to do: Explain why limited space (six pockets) means you cannot pack every item you want, forcing choices between survival needs and optional wants.

## Common mistakes
- Believing strong desire makes an item a need: Kindergarteners often argue that a favorite toy, video game, or candy is a "need" because they want it intensely (Source: Council for Economic Education, EconEdLink Kindergarten Needs and Wants Guide).
- Assuming container space is unlimited: Young children frequently believe a backpack can hold unlimited things if forced inside, failing to recognize physical volume constraints (Source: C3 Framework Dimension 2 Scarcity Indicators).
- Assuming natural water sources outdoors are safe without treatment: Children often believe mountain streams or lake water are pure and drinkable (Source: National Park Service Backcountry Health & Safety Guidelines).
- Believing emergency gear is only needed if bad things happen: Children view raincoats or first aid kits as useless on a sunny morning departure (Source: Leave No Trace Center for Outdoor Ethics Youth Education).

## Sensitivity
- Socioeconomic sensitivity: Children in low-income or housing-insecure families may lack basic household needs in real life. Curio Class tools must keep the context strictly grounded in an adventure/camping simulation rather than judging personal belongings or home situations.
- Physical safety: Outdoor survival guidance must strictly match verified guidance from the National Park Service and American Red Cross.
