# Tug of War Arena (idea card)

- Module: science-3-balanced-and-unbalanced-forces, grade 3. Codes: 3-PS2-1, 3-PS2-2
- Format / family: simulator / forces. Fit: good
- One idea: Add pullers to each side of a tug of war and read the force arrows: equal pulls mean the rope holds still, unequal pulls mean it moves toward the stronger side; then at the playground, time a swing and a ball rolling in a bowl and use the pattern to predict where each will be next.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a field day rope with a flag at the middle line; pullers of different strengths on a bench, each with a force number and arrow. "Make the flag stay still."
  - Core loop: drag pullers on, predict (stays, moves left, moves right), run; arrows add up on a force bar. Challenges: balance three on one side against two, add a puller to win by a little, add a box to the rope (a push and a pull on one object).
  - Pattern station: a playground swing and a marble in a bowl; the student pushes once, records where it is at each tick on a strip chart, sees the back-and-forth repeat, and predicts the position at a future tick before playing it.
  - The aha or the end state: a claim card: "balanced forces, no change in motion; unbalanced, it speeds up toward the bigger force" and "repeating motion can be predicted".
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3-PS2-1: investigation of balanced and unbalanced pulls on the rope and their effect on its motion.
  - 3-PS2-2: the playground station, measuring a swing and a rolling marble and using the pattern to predict future motion.
- Not like: push-the-pumpkin (kindergarten push size and direction); crash-test-lab and curling-stones (later family members with mass and collisions).
- Art: people (illustrated) for a varied team of kids and adults on the rope; force arrows and charts drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Add pullers to a tug of war and read the force arrows to see balanced pulls hold still and unbalanced ones move, then time a swing and a rolling marble and use the pattern to predict their next position.
- Flags: none
