## What it teaches

Students read a signed tile collection as a quadratic expression, factor it by making a consistent rectangle, and complete a square without changing its value. They connect factored form to zeros and square form to a smallest value.

## Key skills

- Read terms, coefficients and constants from signed areas.
- Preserve an expression by adding or removing zero pairs.
- Test every cell against its row and column edge pieces.
- Recognize perfect squares and a difference of squares.
- Complete a square and interpret its remainder and smallest value.

## Support at home

- Arrange twelve small household objects into different rectangles. Say how each pair of side counts gives the same total.
- On scrap paper, sketch a large square and equal strips along two edges. Discuss the corner needed to finish the square.
- Use positive and negative marks on paper to invent pairs whose total is zero. Explain why adding such a pair changes the picture but not the value.

## A five-minute start

In Mat, press x² once and x three times, then add two unit tiles and Flip each one. Read x² + 3x − 2 and vary x length. In Frame, choose the first card, place x² at the corner, share the strips between the edges, and fill the unit corner. Select a tile and tap a cell, drag, or use arrow keys. Turn vertical strips before placing them along the top edge. Press Check after using the whole pile.

## Modes

Mat is free exploration. Solid tiles are positive. Red hatched tiles with a minus glyph are negative. Drop opposite tiles of the same kind onto each other to cancel a zero pair. Hover or focus a term to read its name and coefficient.

Frame offers ten cards. Its frame grows with the arrangement, rather than showing a solved outline. The second x² may extend either edge. Check requires a full, consistent rectangle, the entire pile used, and exact agreement with the original expression. Cards 5 and 6 require two and three zero pairs of x strips. No rectangle tests factor pairs over the integers, including negative pairs.

In a compact view, use the tile list below the diagram to select any tile. In Mat and Frame, the four arrow buttons move it one cell. The first arrow on a loose Frame tile places it at the corner. Flip, Turn and Remove still act on the selected tile. Small diagram tiles are pictures; the list and buttons provide full-size controls. Square uses Split strips for the same guided construction at those sizes.

No rectangle requests a worked check of all integer constant-factor pairs. It does not award success for a binary guess. Compare each pair's sum with the middle coefficient and explain the mismatch. Square is also a guided demonstration, not a scored assessment.

Square offers six cards with even strip counts. Split strips, then Fill the corner. The added positive corner and equal negative units preserve the expression. Outside positive and negative units cancel in pairs, leaving the constant in square form. The number-line slider changes the inset square, separately from the drawing-length slider.

Negative edge pieces represent signed algebraic quantities, not negative physical lengths. The tile model is a formal signed-area rectangle. The separate inset uses an absolute side length and is labelled with its scale.

## Talk about it

Why does the expression stay unchanged when a zero pair is added? Why do all the cells have to agree with both edges? Why can no integer factor pair make x² + x + 1? What makes a square term smallest?

## Good to know

Nothing is saved. Each mode retains its work only for this page session. Read speaks the current action hint. Sound and Motion use the Curio Class kit. No recorded audio is included in this build.

The source of the algebra is CCSS High School Algebra, Seeing Structure in Expressions; OpenStax Elementary Algebra 2e 7.3 and Intermediate Algebra 2e 9.2; and NCTM Illuminations Algebra Tiles. This tool teaches A-SSE.A.1, A-SSE.A.2, A-SSE.B.3a and A-SSE.B.3b. Exponential rewriting and finite geometric series belong in other tools.
