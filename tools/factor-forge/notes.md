## A 10-minute start

1. Open **Smash**. A red-hot number sits on the anvil. Ask: which prime goes into it? Strike it with that **prime hammer**.
2. Each good strike breaks off a prime coin and grows the **factor tree**. A wrong hammer just clangs, and the tool shows why (84 ÷ 5 = 16.8, not a whole number).
3. Keep going until every piece is prime. The tree ends with the prime factorization, like 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 2² × 3 × 7.
4. Switch to **GCF & LCM** when the class is ready.

## GCF & LCM

Students smash two numbers, one after the other. Then the anvil becomes a crucible.

1. Tap a coin from the first number, then the **same prime** from the second. The pair drops into the crucible.
2. When there are no more matches, press **Melt it!**
3. The **GCF** is one coin from each pair multiplied together: the primes both numbers share.
4. The **LCM** is the shared primes once, times every coin left over from either number.
5. A check appears every time: GCF × LCM = the two numbers multiplied.

If a shared prime is left unpaired, the tool says so before it melts.

## Forge Rush

60 seconds to break off as many prime coins as possible. A wrong hammer costs 2 seconds, so divisibility rules pay off. The best score is saved on this device. Keys 2, 3, 5 and 7 also swing the hammers.

## The big idea

Every whole number greater than 1 is either prime or can be broken into primes in exactly one way (apart from order). Prime factorizations make the GCF and LCM visible: the GCF is what two numbers share, and the LCM is the smallest number built from everything either one needs.

## Divisibility rules (the 💡 Hint button uses these)

- **2:** the number is even.
- **3:** the digits add up to a multiple of 3.
- **5:** it ends in 0 or 5.
- **7, 11, 13:** no quick rule here. Just try them.

## Talk about it

- Does it matter which prime you strike first? Try 60 starting with 2, then with 5.
- Why does a prime number turn straight into a coin?
- Two numbers share no primes at all. What is their GCF? Their LCM?
- Where do GCF and LCM show up outside math class? (Splitting things into equal groups; two schedules lining up again.)

## Good to know

- Smash numbers go up to 600 and use primes up to 13. GCF & LCM pairs go up to 180, beyond the standard's "up to 100" and "up to 12", for extra practice with factorizations.
- Students can drive it on their own devices, or the teacher can project it.
