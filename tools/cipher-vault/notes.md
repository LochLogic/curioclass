## What it teaches
An open codebreaker's bench for investigating historical encryption and the structure left in messages. Students can encode and decode with six machines and use counts, coincidence, repeats and guessed words to investigate their output.

## Key skills
- Compare an observed distribution with a reference model.
- Form a key hypothesis and examine its effects on real text.
- Separate a changing rotor state from an initial setting.
- Recognize the conditions required for a one-time pad.
- Explain the limitations of a historical machine or a search instrument.

## Support at home
- Shift an alphabet on two paper strips and exchange a short message.
- Count repeated letters in a paragraph from a book; compare it with a name.
- Make two different sentences with the same length and talk about how a secret key could select either one.

## At the bench
No assigned sequence or required reading. The wheel opens on WKH GLH LV FDVW and shift three reveals THE DIE IS CAST. Machines, tools, case files and practice sheets can be opened in any order. Cases are records, never tasks to mark off. This Curiosity is a second card beside the anchor lesson, not a replacement for its attack-side coverage.

## Useful conversations
Why does sorting a Caesar message's frequency bars remove the evidence of its shift? What changes when a repeated-key cipher is split into columns? What does an Enigma crib exclude? What must remain secret in a pad?

## Good to know
The Manual holds mechanism instructions, mathematical definitions, histories, limitations and source licences. The Leeson case retains the historical phrase REMEMBER DEATH and its actual three-grid variant. Vicksburg uses the museum transcript and regenerated ciphertext. The 1930 Enigma example is excluded because it requires reflector A, while the specified instrument uses B. Four original practice sheets are distinguished from historical records.

All storage stays on this device. Saved partner boxes contain plaintext and keys; this is an instrument for exploring historical ciphers, not secure storage. No cross-device exchange is required. Recorded speech is not included; Read uses the kit's local browser voice. The target bank voice is gravel-ranger.

The original practice prose illustrates ideas rather than guaranteeing an attack: letter counts and repeated gaps suggest possible keys and periods. They cannot guarantee recovery of arbitrary text. Original practice sheets and drawn shapes are CC BY 4.0; code is MIT. Historical public-domain texts, numerical facts and contemporary summaries have their individual sources and rights basis in the Manual.

## Content notes
The Leeson inscription mentions death. The Vicksburg dispatch comes from Confederate officers during a war fought to preserve slavery; its history says so plainly. The Manual credits al-Kindi's work, distinguishes Poe's fictional cryptogram from historical correspondence, and separates Mary's newly recovered letters from her trial evidence and execution. No violent imagery or rewards are used.
