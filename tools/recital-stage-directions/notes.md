## What it teaches

Students name and use the nine stage areas from a dancer's viewpoint. They connect costume, lighting,
shape, level, pathway and timing to the main idea they read in two original recital pieces.

## Key skills

- Keep the performer's left and right when changing viewpoints.
- Place dancers in a called area or formation.
- Choose costumes and lit areas, then observe their effect.
- Select movement clues and use them to support a main idea.
- Compare two pieces and perform a short phrase with timing and focus.

## Support at home

- Face one another and point to your own right. Notice that your right appears on the other person's left.
- Make a three-by-three floor map with paper. Take turns calling an area and walking there.
- Watch a short dance together and name one shape, pathway or timing clue you noticed.

## Five-minute start

Flip From the seats to From the stage. Notice the guide's own hand arrows. Pick Circle, then tap DR.
Flip again. Turn on Calls and try the first three calls. Enter Recital and choose costumes and lights.

## Modes

Stage map allows free placement, including three dancers in one area. Calls remain optional and never lock.
Use the plan or stage tiles. Select a dancer and use arrow keys to move it in screen directions; V flips the view.

In Recital, dress and light Autumn Leaves, then Watch from your seat. Select clues and tap Show me to examine
their moments. Main idea offers three readings. Read Rush Hour too, then Compare the eight traits.
Clues describe these two pieces, not everything ballet or jazz can be.

For Your turn, clear a safe space and mark nine squares with paper. Face the laptop as the audience.
Follow each spoken call, four lead-in counts, and the segment counts. Slower uses 80 beats a minute.
Seated or smaller movements can preserve timing, direction and focus. Students can also call for a partner.

## Talk questions

- Why did stage left move across the screen when the camera changed?
- Which clue best supports your reading of Autumn Leaves or Rush Hour?
- What changed when a dancer moved into an unlit area?
- How do the two pieces use formations and levels differently?

## Good to know

Stage right is the performer's right while facing the audience. Downstage is toward the audience.
Autumn Leaves lasts 20 seconds at 72 beats a minute; Rush Hour lasts about 14 seconds at 104.
Each room phrase has 28 movement counts and 16 lead-in counts, plus spoken instructions.
The music is original synthesis, not a historic recording or a complete style lesson.
Browser narration is a fallback for the specified easy-coach voice; no recordings are included.
Choices are stored only on this device. Start over clears this tool's saved choices.

The ballet history card follows the Library of Congress overview and Gail Grant's ballet dictionary.
The jazz card follows Jacob's Pillow Dance Interactive's account of African American social dance and jazz music.
The activities address grades 3-5 Dance DA:Pr4, DA:Pr6, DA:Re8 and DA:Re9.
