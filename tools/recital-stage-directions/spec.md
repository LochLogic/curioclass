# Recital Stage Directions

## One idea
Stage directions belong to the dancer, not the audience: flip one stage between "From the stage" and "From the seats" and the nine area labels stay glued to the dancer's own left and right, so you watch stage left land on the audience's right every time; then place, costume and light a recital, read two pieces' main ideas from a seat, and dance the areas yourself with the laptop as your audience.

## Not like
- stage-blocker (6-8, stage family): blocks a play from above and checks sightlines from sixteen seats. Here there are no sightlines; the point is whose left is "left", the nine dance areas, and reading a dance from a seat.
- dancers-on-tour (6-8, A3's band): adapts a dance to different venues. Here there is one recital stage.
- levels-and-pathways (K-2, dance family): levels and pathways painted on a studio floor. Here the floor is a proscenium stage with named areas.
- poem-to-movement (this band): builds a phrase from a source. Here the phrases are given; the student places, reads and performs them.
- choreography-board (6-8, dance family): arranges and revises formations on a timeline. Here placement answers a stage manager's call, and the reading is of finished pieces.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, dance, module "Performing and Watching Dance (Dance)".
- Topic line: stage areas and watching a recital
- Driver: students (pairs: one caller, one dancer) or teacher (projector; the class dances Your turn in the room). 15 to 30 minutes. Students move in the room; the screen shows code figures.
- Standards: DA:Pr4 (perform a phrase through the stage areas with timing, focus and use of space), DA:Pr6 (name the stage areas; choose costumes and lights), DA:Re8 (read each piece's main idea from its movement clues), DA:Re9 (compare the two styles' typical moves).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons: `Stage map` (default), `Recital`, `Your turn`.

**Stage map (sandbox).** Focus: the stage view, big and central, with a small top-down plan beside it (code diagram, nine areas in a 3 by 3 grid with "AUDIENCE" along the front edge).
- The view switch, a large two-part toggle right on the stage: `From the seats` (the proscenium view, curtains, the dance engine's stage) and `From the stage` (standing center stage looking out at the empty house). Both views show the nine areas as faint floor tiles with their labels (DR, DC, DL, CR, C, CL, UR, UC, UL) and a full name on hover or focus ("Downstage right"). The two side wings are labeled `Stage right` and `Stage left` in both views; the house edge says "audience's left" and "audience's right" in the seats view.
- A guide figure stands center stage in both views with arrows on its own hands: "my right" and "my left". Flipping turns the camera 180 degrees around the stage (an 800 ms orbit; Motion off: a cut), so the student watches the labels stay with the dancer while the screen's left and right swap.
- Three recital dancers wait in the wings (badges circle, square, triangle). Drag a dancer onto the plan or straight onto the stage view (or tap the dancer, then tap an area). The area lights up under them in both views and on the plan; the panel says "Circle: downstage right".
- `Calls` (optional, in the panel): the stage manager's calls one at a time (Content). Place the dancers, then `Check`: each dancer's area gets a tick or a cross with words ("Square is upstage left. The call was upstage right: that's on the dancer's right."). `Next call`. Calls never lock.
- `Clear stage` returns the dancers to the wings.

**Recital.** Focus: the stage from the seats, framed by the tops of two empty red seat backs in the front row (code shapes). Two pieces (Content): `Autumn Leaves` (ballet) and `Rush Hour` (jazz). For the chosen piece:
1. `Dress and light`: costume chips (two color sets per piece and a neutral set; the dancers' top and bottom colors) and lights: a color (Warm amber, Cool blue, Rose, Clear white) and which areas are lit (tap areas on the plan; unlit areas dim to 25%). Changes show at once on the stage.
2. `Watch from your seat`: the piece plays (24 counts at its tempo, about 15 to 18 s), three dancers in formation, with the student's costumes and lights.
3. `What did you see?`: eight clue cards (four that are in this piece, four from the other piece); tap the ones you saw, then `Show me` replays the moments for every card the student tapped (the dancers freeze on that count with the clue named). Then pick the main idea from three (Content).
- `Compare` (after both pieces are read): a two-column board, `Autumn Leaves` and `Rush Hour`, with a middle strip `Both`. Drag the eight trait cards (Content) to where they belong; each one, once placed, plays a 2-count clip of that trait from its piece (or both pieces side by side for Both). A short card about each style's history sits at the bottom of each column.

**Your turn.** Focus: the stage plan from the dancer's side (stage right on the screen's right, because the student faces the laptop the way a dancer faces the audience), with the current call big over it and the target area glowing. 
1. A set-up card: "Mark nine squares on the floor with paper, three by three. Stand the laptop where the audience would sit, facing you. Your right is stage right." with a small diagram.
2. Pick a phrase (`Leaf Fall`, `Rush Hour`, `Explorer`; Content). `Start`: for each segment the call is spoken and shown, then a 4-count lead-in, then the counts with a count number and the target area lit. Calls name where (area), what (move), how long (counts) and focus (where to look).
3. `Bow` ends the phrase. `Again` repeats; `Slower` plays at 80 beats a minute.

## Content
**Areas** (engine floor units, seen from the seats, x 0 to 100 left to right, z 0 back to 60 front): stage right is x 0 to 33.3 (the audience's left), center x 33.3 to 66.7, stage left x 66.7 to 100; upstage z 0 to 20, center z 20 to 40, downstage z 40 to 60. From the stage the view is mirrored, so stage right is on the screen's right.

**Calls** (Stage map, `Calls`): 1. "Circle, center stage." 2. "Square, downstage right." 3. "Triangle, upstage left." 4. "Two dancers downstage left, one upstage center." 5. "Everyone in a line across center: right, center, left." 6. "Circle downstage center, square and triangle behind her, upstage right and upstage left." 7. "A diagonal from upstage right to downstage left." 8. "Everyone on the audience's right." (answer: stage left areas; the check line says so).

**Pieces** (each 24 counts; three dancers; the dance engine's poses and gaits):
- Autumn Leaves (ballet, 72 beats a minute, 20 s): counts 1-8 the three dancers rise on their toes with arms opening (releve, arms-wide), slow; 9-16 they travel on curved paths from upstage to center, arms flowing (walk gait on tiptoe, smooth), one balances in an arabesque; 17-24 each sinks slowly to the floor and curls (low level), one after the other. Main idea choices: "Leaves drifting down and settling" (right), "A race to the finish", "Waking up in the morning".
- Rush Hour (jazz, 104 beats a minute, 14 s): counts 1-8 sharp walks on straight lines crossing each other downstage, hits on counts 4 and 8 (stamp, point); 9-16 quick kicks and claps in a line at center, accents just before the beat on 12 and 16 (syncopation); 17-24 a big jump together (star-jump), then all three freeze in sharp poses facing different ways. Main idea choices: "A busy city where everyone hurries past each other" (right), "A sleepy afternoon", "Floating in the sea".
- Clue cards: Autumn Leaves: "Smooth, flowing arms", "Curved paths", "Starts high and ends low", "Slow, soft timing". Rush Hour: "Sharp, punchy moves", "Straight paths that cross", "Quick timing with surprise accents", "Ends in a frozen shape". Each card plays its moment from its count.
- Traits for Compare: Autumn Leaves: "Rising onto the toes (releve)", "Long balanced line on one leg (arabesque)", "Arms that flow from shape to shape (port de bras)". Rush Hour: "Sharp hits on the beat", "Accents just off the beat (syncopation)", "Kicks and claps". Both: "Dancers move in formations", "They use high, middle and low levels".
- Style cards: Ballet: "Ballet grew in the royal courts of Italy and France in the 1500s and 1600s. Its steps still have French names." Jazz: "Jazz dance grew from African American social dances and jazz music in the early 1900s, and later moved onto Broadway stages and into dance studios."

**Your turn phrases** (100 beats a minute; segment: call, counts):
- Leaf Fall: "Start upstage left. Rise high and open your arms, eyes up." 8 / "Walk a curved path to center stage, looking at your hands." 8 / "Sink slowly to the floor, eyes down." 8 / "Stand and bow." 4.
- Rush Hour: "Start downstage right. Eight sharp steps straight across to downstage left, looking ahead." 8 / "Turn and walk straight to upstage center, look at the audience." 8 / "Four kicks in place." 8 / "Freeze, then bow." 4.
- Explorer: "Start upstage right. Tiptoe to upstage center, looking around." 8 / "Zigzag to center stage." 8 / "Run to downstage left and reach out to the audience." 8 / "Bow." 4.
Each phrase is 28 counts (16.8 s) plus four 4-count lead-ins (9.6 s) and the spoken calls.

## Interactions in detail
- Dance engine: `Dance.floor('stage')` for From the seats; From the stage uses `Dance.floor('square', {backdrop})` with the painted empty-house image and the x axis mirrored (the tool maps x to 100 - x and faces dancers 180 degrees). Dancers are `Dance.dancer` with badges; pieces are `Dance.phrase` steps on `Dance.clock`, with `Dance.music` tracks (a soft piano-and-pad 3/4 waltz feel for Autumn Leaves at 72, a snappy drum, bass and clap groove for Rush Hour at 104).
- The flip keeps every dancer, light and label where it is on the stage; only the camera moves. A drag in progress during the flip is dropped where it was released, in floor units, never in screen units.
- While a piece or phrase plays every control stays live: a costume or light change shows at once; the view toggle flips at once (the piece keeps playing); Stop stops at once. Clue cards tapped during Watch are counted as tapped (they do not wait for the end).
- Your turn follows "speak now and wait": each call is spoken (or shown with Sound off, held for its estimated length), then the lead-in starts, then the segment's counts; nothing is spoken over counts. `Stop` during a call cuts the call and the phrase.
- Placing a dancer on an occupied area puts both there, side by side (areas hold up to three). A dancer dropped off the stage returns to the wings.
- Saved on this device (localStorage `recital-stage-directions`): costume and light choices, clue answers and the Compare board. `Start over` clears them.
- Keyboard: Tab to a dancer, Enter, then arrow keys pick an area (the arrows follow the current view's screen directions, and the area's full name is announced); V flips the view. Panel before stage in tab order.
- Sizes: both views fit the stage at real pixels with the plan as a small inset (top right on wide screens, under the stage on 375 wide); the view toggle sits on the stage itself so it is always in view; at 680x425 the panel is compact (the step's main buttons only).

## Done when
1. Focus: the stage view is the largest thing at 1366x768, 680x425 and 375 wide, with the view toggle on it and the plan inset visible.
2. Flipping keeps every label with the dancer: "Stage left" is on the screen's right from the seats and on the screen's left from the stage, and the guide's "my left" arrow always points at it.
3. All eight calls check correctly, including call 8 (stage left areas).
4. A costume, light or view change during a piece shows at once and the piece keeps playing.
5. Clue cards replay their moments; both main ideas can be picked and answered.
6. Compare: each trait card plays its clip when placed; Both plays both pieces side by side.
7. Your turn: each call is heard before its lead-in, never over counts; Slower plays at 80.
8. How lines are spoken once in a run of five actions; calls are spoken every time.
9. Choices and answers survive a reload; `Start over` clears them.
