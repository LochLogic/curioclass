# Recital Stage Directions (idea card)

- Module: arts-3-5-performing-and-watching-dance, grade 3-5. Codes: DA:Pr4, DA:Pr6, DA:Re8, DA:Re9
- Format / family: sandbox / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Stage directions are told from the dancer's side, so "stage left" is the audience's right; place dancers for a recital on a nine-area stage, add costumes and lights, then flip to an audience seat and back to read what the staging says, and finally dance the stage areas yourself in a room marked out on the floor.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a stage seen from above with the nine areas, a "flip to the audience" button and three dancers waiting in the wings.
  - Core loop: the stage manager calls "two dancers downstage right, one upstage center"; place them, then flip to the audience view and check. Add costumes and a light color for the piece. Then watch two short recital pieces in different styles from an audience seat and tap the clues (shapes, levels, timing, energy) that tell each one's main idea.
  - The aha or the end state: "Your turn on stage": a set-up card shows how to mark the nine areas with paper on the floor, screen as the audience; the tool calls a short phrase across the areas with timing and focus, and the student performs it.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a dancer and a caller in the class swap roles.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - DA:Pr4: performing a phrase through the stage areas with timing, focus and use of space.
  - DA:Pr6: naming the stage areas and choosing costumes and lights for the recital.
  - DA:Re8: reading each piece's main idea from its movement clues.
  - DA:Re9: comparing the two styles' typical moves and describing how they are alike and different.
- Not like: stage-blocker (grade 6-8 sightlines for a play); dancers-on-tour (grade 6-8 adapting to venues); levels-and-pathways (K-2).
- Art: people (illustrated) for dancers, their own figures; generated scene for the theater and audience view.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Place recital dancers on a nine-area stage, flip to the audience to see why stage left is your right, add costumes and lights, read two pieces' main ideas from an audience seat, then dance the stage areas yourself.
- Flags: sensitive (the two dance styles: a fresh review agent checks them for respect and accuracy, rule 5)
