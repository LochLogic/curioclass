# Allusion Hunter (idea card)

- Module: ela-7-figurative-language-and-allusions, grade 7. Codes: L.7.5
- Format / family: explorer / myths. Fit: good: a field of texts to explore, each allusion a find that opens its source story.
- One idea: The student hunts through original song lyrics, ads, headlines and a short story for allusions (a "Trojan horse" app update, a "David and Goliath" match, a "Romeo" at the bus stop), taps each to open the source story, and explains what the allusion borrows from it in this context, so they learn that an allusion packs a whole story into a few words and that its meaning depends on where it is used.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a field journal and a page of mixed texts, all written by Curio Class: a sports headline, a phone ad, a song verse, a text message.
  - Core loop: the student taps a phrase they think is an allusion; a correct find opens a short illustrated card of the source (Greek myth, a Bible story told as literature, Shakespeare, a fable, history). They then write what the allusion means here: why call an app update a Trojan horse? A decoy card catches phrases that sound special but aren't allusions. A second stage in the journal works with the found words: sorting near-synonyms by connotation (Herculean, huge, tremendous, overwhelming) and completing analogies built from allusions (Achilles' heel is to weakness as Pandora's box is to...).
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes their own headline for a school event using one allusion on purpose, and the tool asks a reader card whether it lands, and what a reader who doesn't know the source would miss.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a class "allusion of the week" wall.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - L.7.5: interpreting literary, biblical and mythological allusions and other figures of speech in context (5a); using analogies between allusions to understand each (5b); and distinguishing connotations among words with similar meanings (5c).
- Not like: myth-museum (grade 4, Greek myths behind everyday words); double-meanings (grade 8, puns, irony and allusion in literary and informational texts); word-thermometer (grade 6, connotation scale).
- Art: public domain artworks for source stories (Met or AIC object ids, to be checked at spec), generated scenes for the modern texts.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Hunt for allusions in original song lyrics, ads, headlines and texts; tap each to open its source story, explain what it borrows here, then write a headline with an allusion of your own.
- Flags: sensitive (light: Bible stories presented as literary sources only, alongside myths and Shakespeare)
