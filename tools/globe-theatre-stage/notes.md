## What it teaches
Students distinguish staging choices required by a play's words, readings those words allow, and details a production adds. They compare fixed excerpts in three invented productions, then build a reading and defend it with linked lines.

## Key skills
- Notice how position, gaze, gesture and delivery change a scene's meaning.
- Use dialogue and stage directions as evidence.
- Distinguish textual requirements from defensible interpretations.
- Explain and reconsider a directing choice.
- Compare Shakespeare with an American dramatist.

## Support at home
- Say one everyday sentence warmly, suspiciously and wearily. Discuss what changed even though the words stayed the same.
- Read a short exchange together. Each person chooses where to pause, then explains that choice using a word in the exchange.
- When watching a play or film, ask what the performers added that a printed script might leave open.

## A five-minute start
Open Hamlet. The initial selected line includes "Where's your father?" Switch A, B and C without changing the line. Follow the dashed sight lines, read the delivery score, and try one version aloud. Choose a production choice, set Requires, Allows or Adds, and tap a script line to pin the evidence. Check my ruling reveals the tool's reading and its reason, without awarding points.

## Modes
Compare keeps one script fixed across three invented productions. Each tab is labeled as made up for Curio Class. Choose one item in the list to rule on it. Setting a ruling turns evidence linking on; turn it off to browse without changing pins. Check stays unavailable until a ruling has a linked line. Reopen my ruling permits revision after a check. Once every production choice in a scene has a ruling and evidence, the license strips show both your distribution and our distribution. These are classifications, not an assessment of evidence quality.

Direct has three decisions per scene. Pick an option or write your own, then link its supporting lines. Switching an option clears that decision's previous links so a new claim needs fresh evidence. Write up to 600 characters in the director's statement. Read my production assembles the choices, line quotations and statement; unfinished parts are explicitly marked. Copy uses plain text.

Allow 30 to 45 minutes for two scenes. Work alone or in pairs; pairs can swap the same laptop. For a class discussion, project one scene and invite competing readings.

## Talk questions
- Which choice would stop being fair if one line were removed?
- Where do a direction and a line of dialogue settle different things?
- Can two productions disagree while both respecting the script?
- Does adding an action differ from contradicting an explicit direction?
- What does the long direction near the end of Trifles settle, and what remains an actor's decision?

## Good to know
The stage, costumes and twelve productions are invented teaching designs, not reconstructions of actual performances. The stage building does not teach architectural history. Tableau sight lines describe each invented interpretation. The hidden Hamlet listeners are shown faintly for the student, not as people the characters necessarily see.

Mr Hale is shown faintly with an outside label when the script places him outside. The five-person cast remains visible for reference. These are line-based snapshots, not a full animated performance of every gesture. On narrow screens the stage and script precede the work panel, so using evidence and rulings requires page scrolling; laptops provide the full side-by-side desk.

The delivery score shows stress, pause, pace and pitch, with a written equivalent. Read speaks instructions, not the script. The build uses browser speech through Curio.voice; no voice recordings were made. Motion off stops dust and makes changes instant. A selected script line supports arrow keys, Home and End; Enter pins a line while linking is on. Gloss buttons also provide a keyboard route to dotted words.

Rulings, evidence and Direct work save per scene under localStorage globe-work when available. They never leave this device. If browser storage is unavailable, the page says to keep it open. Clear my work asks Yes or No and clears all four scenes. The tool makes no network requests. Copy may require the device's normal selection and Copy command where clipboard access is restricted.

## Content notes and standards
Grade 12 ELA, RL.11-12.7: analyze multiple interpretations of a play and how each interprets its source. The student acts on this standard by changing staging while keeping text fixed, ruling with evidence, and defending a production. Shakespeare and the American dramatist Susan Glaspell are both included.

Trifles centers on a suspected murder, a husband strangled, and a dead canary with a wrung neck. Only a closed box is painted. The Shrew speech asserts a husband's rule over a wife. The three productions present sincerity, irony and exhaustion as interpretations rather than endorsing that claim. Do not ask students to disclose personal experiences. Present differing readings with respect and distinguish the play's claims from the classroom's values.
