    Case file: 1936

    In 1936 the Literary Digest mailed about 10 million straw ballots, with names drawn largely from telephone directories and car registration lists, plus its own subscribers. About 2.4 million came back. The Digest predicted Alf Landon would beat Franklin Roosevelt, 57 percent to 43 percent. Roosevelt won with 60.8 percent of the popular vote to Landon's 36.5 percent, and 523 electoral votes to 8. George Gallup, with a much smaller sample chosen to match the voting public, called a Roosevelt win. In 1936, people with phones and cars were better off than most voters, and the people who mailed ballots back were not a random slice either.

    Sources: Squire (1988), Public Opinion Quarterly; Office of the Clerk, US House; National Archives.
