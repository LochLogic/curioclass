# Remix Lab (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-modern-takes-on-classic-stories, grade 8. Codes: RL.8.9
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good: analyze modern stories against old patterns, then write your own.
- One idea: The student reads short modern stories written for Curio Class that quietly draw on old ones (a sixteen-year-old drone racer who flies too high, an underdog robotics team against a champion school, a new kid who keeps outwitting the bullies like a trickster), finds the old pattern under each by matching it to source cards (Icarus, David and Goliath, Anansi), names what was kept and what was made new, then plans a modern story of their own on a pattern they choose, so they learn how modern writers borrow themes, plot patterns and character types and make them new.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a shelf of three modern stories and a deck of pattern cards from myths, folk tales and religious works (each with a short retelling of the source).
  - Core loop: the student reads a modern story and lays pattern cards under it until one fits; then marks pairs (the father's warning and Daedalus' warning, the wax wings and the cheap battery). For each pair they say kept, changed or new, and what the change does for a modern reader. A pattern card can fit more than one story, and one story can hold two patterns.
  - The aha or the end state: the student plans a modern retelling of a pattern of their choice in a beat sheet: what stays (the pattern's core), what becomes new (setting, character, ending), and one line on why their change would matter to a reader now; they write its opening scene.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a gallery of student beat sheets, sorted by pattern.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.8.9: analyzing how each modern story draws on a myth, traditional story or religious work, and how the borrowed material is rendered new.
- Not like: shakespeare-remix (grade 9, a change log between Shakespeare and his source); allusion-hunter (grade 7, short allusions in everyday texts); setting-swap (grade 7, moving one story's setting).
- Art: public domain artworks for the source stories (Met or AIC object ids, to be checked at spec); generated scenes for the modern stories.
- Proposed title: Old Patterns, New Stories (Remix Lab is generic and sits close to shakespeare-remix and game-remix)
- Proposed concept line: Read short modern stories that secretly borrow from myths, folk tales and Bible stories; find the old pattern under each, name what was kept and made new, then plan a modern story on a pattern of your own.
- Flags: sensitive (light: Bible stories presented as literary sources; Anansi and other living traditions get a fresh review per rule 5)
