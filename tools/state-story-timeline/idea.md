# State Story Timeline (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-4-state-history, grade 4. Codes: D2.His.2.3-5, D2.His.3.3-5, D2.His.14.3-5
- Format / family: timeline / timeline-maker. Fit: good as a timeline, but a teacher template alone fails a solo student; it needs real, authored state content by default.
- One idea: Pick your state and walk its story from the Native nations who lived there first, through newcomers and statehood, to today; at each stop ask questions about the people who drove the change, trace causes and effects, and compare a kid's life then with yours.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a U.S. map. "Tap your state." The timeline opens with the Native nations of that land, named as nations, with their homelands and ways of life.
  - Core loop: stops along the line (for example a treaty, a trail or railroad, a gold or farm boom, statehood, a modern change). At each, the student builds questions about who caused it and who it affected, then links cause cards to effect cards.
  - Kid then and now: at three stops a kid's day in that period is compared with the student's.
  - The aha or the end state: a cause-and-effect chain across the whole timeline shows how one change led to the next, including what was lost and gained and for whom.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): teachers add local events and images (the original template).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.3-5: kid-then-and-now stops compare life in each period with today.
  - D2.His.3.3-5: question building about the individuals and groups who made each change or kept things going.
  - D2.His.14.3-5: cause cards linked to effect cards across the timeline.
- Not like: my-life-timeline (sibling engine, personal history); revolution-road (national events); then-and-now-overlay (one Main Street).
- Art: people (illustrated) and public domain (Library of Congress maps and photos, chosen and checked per state).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pick your state and walk its story from the Native nations who lived there first to statehood and today; ask questions about who drove each change, link causes to effects, and compare a kid's life then and now.
- Flags: too big (50 authored state timelines); needs a data source (per-state history and Native nations, sourced); sensitive (Native history, removal and treaties; fresh review agent per rule 5)
