## What it teaches

Children practice four crew roles that make a shared aquarium recount ready for an audience. They can choose clear
pictures, keep music below a speaker's words, match true sentences to pictures, and prepare a room for families.

## Key skills

- Compare a clear picture with blur, darkness, cropping, and glare.
- Listen for words while changing music volume.
- Connect one true sentence to the picture it describes.
- Practice speaking clearly to an audience.
- Rehearse a show and fix a crew's specific problem.

## Support at home

- Tell about something you did together. Take turns saying one true sentence about each part.
- While someone tells a short story, hum quietly and then more loudly. Talk about when the words are easy to hear.
- Arrange seats for a pretend show. Check whether everyone could see the speaker and the picture.

## A 5-minute start

Open Camera and ask the class to choose a take without naming its problem first. Compare the enlarged picture,
then use it. Try Sound at its starting loud level and lower it while a sample plays. Ask which crew should go next.

## The modes

Camera offers three takes of each of six paintings. Any take may be kept and replaced. The class controls the show;
the readiness check asks for all six clear takes. Back slide, Next slide, and the six slots revisit any picture.

Sound starts at music level 5, equal to the model's voice reference. Tap Test it and adjust while listening.
Levels 1 through 5 use gains 0.063, 0.126, 0.251, 0.501, and 1.000, representing -24, -18, -12, -6, and 0 dB.
The listening symbol is an illustrative model: check at 1-3, question at 4, cross at 5. It is not a microphone test.
Browser speech output varies by device, so listen to the actual result before class. Tests stop after six seconds.
The Sound check requires a full test at one unchanged level with Sound on. Changing levels while listening still
works immediately, but tap Test it again at the new level to confirm it. Interrupted or muted tests do not count.
The music is an original D-major eight-bar loop at 72 beats per minute, made in code.

Speaker uses tap a sentence, then tap a picture. A wrong sentence returns to the tray. Six correct matches reveal
the practice card. Let children speak the lines before tapping We practiced.
Children can sit or stand tall in a position comfortable for their bodies while practicing.
These are preparation checks, not proof that a child understood or spoke: matching can be solved by trying pictures,
and We practiced is the teacher or class's own report. Ask children to explain one match and let every speaker rehearse.

Room cycles each setting with one tap. Lights Dim or Off, Screen Down, Chairs Face the screen, Speaker spot Beside
the screen, and Volume Medium pass. The star with a microphone represents the speaker without depicting a person.
Light wash, beam shadow, flipped chairs, speaker position, and volume marks make changes visible.

Show starts with the entrance painting and title. Dress rehearsal names each job's readiness and the first specific
fix. Each row links to that crew. Present works with unfinished jobs. Tap Next slide when the child or teacher is
done speaking. The end slide thanks families with soft synthesized applause. New show asks before clearing the work.

## Talk together

- Which part of this picture would be hardest for a family to see?
- What happened to the words when the music got louder?
- What does each crew contribute that another crew cannot fix for it?
- What did rehearsal help us change before families arrived?
- What true detail from our own class trip could we say aloud with this picture?

## Good to know

This is teacher-led K-2 media arts, serving MA:Pr5 through role practice and revision, MA:Pr6 through rehearsal and
presentation, and MA:Cn10 through a shared-experience recount. If your class has not visited an aquarium, frame this
as a model trip rather than claiming it happened. Connect the crew process to an actual shared class experience aloud.

The six animal sentences follow the sources listed in the spec: NOAA, Monterey Bay Aquarium, Florida Museum, and
Smithsonian Ocean. Jellyfish have a nerve net rather than a brain. Seahorse fathers carry young in a brood pouch.
The pictures are paintings, not photographs from your school. No camera, microphone, upload, or recording is used.
Nothing is saved between visits. Work remains only while this page is open. Kit Motion preference is per device.

Projection contrast is an illustrative model, not a room measurement. For a slide from 0.05 to 1.0, contrast is
(1 + ambient) / (0.05 + ambient). Ambient 0.6, 0.25, and 0.05 give 2.46, 4.17, and 10.5 to 1 respectively.
These round to the spec's 2.5, 4.2, and 10.5. The white overlay is a visual teaching cue, not a calibrated projector.
Dim and Off are passing settings in this model, not AVIXA compliance ratings for an actual room.
BBC R&D White Paper WHP 324, Mike Armstrong (2016), discusses speech intelligibility and audio mixing. It does not
establish this tool's five numeric thresholds. The gain factors are illustrative choices from the brief, not measured
music-to-speech loudness ratios or guarantees that every listener can hear every word.

Named fact references: NOAA Ocean Service, What are jellyfish made of?; Monterey Bay Aquarium, Giant Pacific octopus
and African penguin; NOAA Fisheries, Green Turtle; Florida Museum, Sharks FAQ; Smithsonian Ocean, A Pregnant Seahorse.

Read speaks the current next action or open checklist. Sound switches off speech, chimes, and music; Read still works.
Both dialogs include their own Read button. Read during a sound test stops the test so it can speak clearly.
Motion off removes movement, including water bubbles and light fades, while keeping listening-test timing.
The requested science-narrator / Charon voice is inventoried but not recorded in this Build-only run. Playback uses
the kit's best available browser voice. Keyboard users can Tab and Enter through every job. Arrow keys change slides
in Camera and Show. Sentence cards come before picture targets in keyboard order.
