# Field Trip Slideshow (idea card)

- Module: arts-k-2-presenting-media-together, grade k-2. Codes: MA:Pr5, MA:Pr6, MA:Cn10
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good
- One idea: After a class trip to the aquarium, the child does each crew job in turn (camera, sound, speaker), each with one real skill to get right (hold still and get close, record where it is quiet, speak loud and slow), then sets up the room and presents the slideshow to visiting families.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the aquarium trip as a big illustrated scene and three crew badges: Camera, Sound, Speaker.
  - Core loop: Camera: frame shots of the otters and the jellyfish; a wobbly or far-away shot is easy to see and redo. Sound: choose where to record a sound (next to the loud fountain or in the quiet hall) and hear the difference. Speaker: pick a favorite memory card and record one sentence about it, or read it with the narrator. Put the slides in order.
  - The aha or the end state: presenting day in the library: the child chooses the lights, the volume and where the families sit, then presses play; the families react, and the child taps how it felt and which job they liked best.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a teacher can load photos from a real class trip (kept on the device); each child takes one badge.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MA:Pr5: practicing the basic skill of each crew job (steady close camera, quiet sound, clear speaking).
  - MA:Pr6: setting up and presenting the slideshow to an audience, then sharing how it went.
  - MA:Cn10: choosing a favorite memory from a shared experience to include.
- Not like: sound-and-picture-book (planning one's own three-page story); morning-show-crew (grade 6-8 one story across many media); mini-documentary-crew (grade 9-12).
- Art: generated scenes for the aquarium and library; people (illustrated) for the families and crew.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: After a class trip to the aquarium, take each crew job in turn (camera, sound, speaker), get its one skill right, then set up the room and present your slideshow to visiting families.
- Flags: records audio (on-screen line that recordings and any loaded photos never leave the device); overlap risk (sound-and-picture-book; keep this one about crew jobs and presenting)
