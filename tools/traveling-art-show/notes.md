## What it teaches

Students choose six artworks using one theme checklist, then protect, place and label them for a month in a town library.
They use visible damage to explain why materials need different display conditions, and compare a library with a museum.

## Key skills

- Apply the same selection criteria to several artworks.
- Match a material with the things that can damage it.
- Find a safer display spot and choose suitable protection.
- Make a useful label with a title, artist, material and optional idea sentence.
- Compare how two places care for art.

## Support at home

- Look at an existing picture at home together. Notice whether sunlight reaches it during the day.
- Find a paper object and a ceramic object. Talk about which might bend, fade or break, without testing damage.
- Make a spoken label for a favorite drawing: its title, artist and material.

## A 5-minute start

1. Open Look around. Tap the window wall and watch its sun patch.
2. Tap Pumpkin Patch. Tap its enemy buttons to inspect the material, then tap the window wall to try a month.
3. Try a glass frame. Notice that the sun still fades the drawing. Move it to a shaded wall.
4. Enter Shows, read the Our Town checklist and choose six works.
5. Tap Check my six, then Look around this month. Let pairs debate the placements before opening the show.

## Modes

**Look around:** Explore all ten spots in October, January and June. Tap a work then a spot, or drag it there.
The two-second preview is unscored. Pick protection below the wall. Tap enemy buttons for a short explanation.

**Shows:** Our Town in October, Water All Around in January, Animals We Know in June, then Your show with any listed theme
and month. Choose, Place, Label and Open steps remain tappable once reached. All shows are available using Next show
and Previous show. The crate contains eight finished, signed works. Only Check my six reveals theme fits.
In Label, selecting a material or typing the optional idea updates a full label beside the artwork below the wall.
The label count tracks how many materials have been chosen, without revealing whether they are correct.

Open the show runs 30 modeled days in about eight seconds, with growing damage at days 10, 20 and 30. Tap anywhere to
jump to results. Motion off keeps the same duration but shows the final damage at the end. Fix and run again restores
the artworks and keeps the placements. A wrong material label brings a visitor note, separate from physical damage.

**Library and museum:** Tap each row to highlight and compare who watches, light, air, touching and purpose.

## Talk questions

- Why did the glass frame help at the low wall but fail at the window?
- What changes when the heater comes on in January? What changes in the summer reading crowd?
- Why can the bowl or the heron use a sunny spot when a marker poster cannot?
- Which two works could exchange spots and still be safe? Explain using the materials.
- How does the library do a museum's job with fewer tools?

## Good to know

- The pictures and student artists are made up. The material-care principles come from the sources below.
- This is a deliberately visible one-month teaching model, not a conservation forecast for every real work.
  Light sensitivity varies by pigment, paper, printing process and exposure. A window is modeled at 30,000 lux for
  four hours daily: 3.6 million lux hours in 30 days, versus 400 lux hours daily at 50 lux for eight hours in a gallery.
- Glass frames here include a spacer: glazing never touches pastel. A sleeve rubs pastel even in a safe spot.
  Frames and covers stop hands only in this model. Nothing in the protection menu stops sun, heat or cart bumps.
- The locked case holds one work, stops hands and bumps, and is shaded. Flat works need walls or the case;
  three-dimensional works need shelves, pedestal or case.
- The three shows have 1,560, 768 and 336 safe placements respectively, counting each placement once rather than
  counting different safe protection choices separately. There is no single hidden arrangement.
- Progress, labels and placements stay in this browser on this device under `traveling-art-show`.
  Start show over clears only the current show. Nothing is sent anywhere. The kit also remembers Motion.
- Keyboard: Tab to a work, Enter to pick it up, Tab to a spot, Enter to place. Arrow keys move among spot numbers.
  Material menus use normal keyboard selection. Read speaks the current hint. Narration uses the browser voice;
  the specified curious-librarian recording bank is listed for future recording. No audio was recorded in this build.
- The four arts standards are practiced directly: theme selection and a consistent checklist (VA:Pr4, VA:Re9),
  protection, placement and labels (VA:Pr5), and the library/museum comparison (VA:Pr6).
