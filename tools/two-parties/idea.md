# Two Parties (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-the-early-republic, grade 8. Codes: D2.His.1.6-8, D2.Civ.12.6-8, D2.Civ.1.6-8
- Format / family: cards / none. Fit: good
- One idea: A card game of the 1790s: lead Hamilton's Federalists or Jefferson's Republicans through fights over the national bank, the whiskey tax, the Jay Treaty and the Sedition Act, using newspapers, voters and local societies as influence cards, and judge each law as a fix for a real public problem; it ends with the peaceful handover of 1801.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two party tables, a stack of issue cards and a Congress track.
  - Core loop: an issue card names a real problem (war debt, no national currency, trouble with Britain); play influence cards (a partisan newspaper, a speech, a petition) to push a law through Congress; each law card then shows who it helped and who it hurt, and the student rates it as a fix.
  - The aha or the end state: neither party was in the Constitution, yet they shaped it; the election of 1800 and Marbury v. Madison (1803) close the game, connecting party fights to how power changes hands and to the courts.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two players, one per party.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.1.6-8: the epilogue connects the decade's fights to the election of 1800 and the courts.
  - D2.Civ.12.6-8: rating each law (actual and proposed) as a fix for its public problem.
  - D2.Civ.1.6-8: the influence cards show what citizens, parties, interest groups and the press could and could not do.
- Not like: constitutional-convention (writing the plan); bill-to-law-board-game (grade 4, a bill's path); motion-on-the-floor.
- Art: public domain portraits (Hamilton, Jefferson, Adams; to check at spec) plus generated scenes; newspaper cards from real 1790s papers.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A card game of the 1790s: lead Hamilton's Federalists or Jefferson's Republicans through fights over the bank, a whiskey tax and the Sedition Act, using newspapers and voters, and judge each law as a fix for a real problem.
- Flags: sensitive (both leaders' slaveholding and the Sedition Act's targets named plainly; fresh review)
