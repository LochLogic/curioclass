# Logic Gym (idea card)

- Module: ela-7-evaluating-arguments, grade 7. Codes: RI.7.8
- Format / family: arcade / fallacies. Fit: good for fast judgments on relevance and sufficiency, with a slower "full workout" round so tracing a whole argument is taught too.
- One idea: Every claim is a barbell, and each piece of evidence is a weight plate: the student loads plates that fit the bar (relevant) and decides when there are enough to lift (sufficient), and in quick reps a bad plate slides off or the bar won't budge, so they learn to judge whether an argument's evidence fits and is enough.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a gym floor, a claim on the bar ("Our town's new skate park made the streets safer") and a rack of evidence plates.
  - Core loop: quick reps: the student loads plates or rejects them. An off-topic plate ("Skateboarding is in the Olympics") won't seat on the bar; a single anecdote is a light plate that can't lift a big claim alone. Each rep ends with the student naming the problem: irrelevant, not enough, or sound. Streaks build "strength" for harder claims. The full workout round: a whole letter to the editor; the student traces its claims and reasons, loads each with the evidence given, and judges whether the reasoning connects (does the skate park data come from before or after?).
  - The aha or the end state: the student's training log shows which kind of weakness they spot fastest and which they miss, and a final round asks them to fix a weak argument by choosing the plates it needs.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a class "spot" mode where the room shouts lift or drop.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RI.7.8: tracing the argument and its claims, and judging whether the reasoning is sound and the evidence relevant and sufficient, in reps and the full workout.
- Not like: argument-x-ray (grade 6, tagging an op-ed's skeleton); fallacy-field-guide (grade 10, named fallacies and their lookalikes); podcast-listening-club (6-8, judging claims by ear).
- Art: drawn in code for the gym and plates, with illustrated gym characters (people, illustrated) as spotters.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Every claim is a barbell and each piece of evidence a plate: load only plates that fit the bar and decide when there are enough to lift, in quick reps and a full workout on a whole letter to the editor.
- Flags: none
