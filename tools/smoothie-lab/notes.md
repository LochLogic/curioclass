## A 5-minute start

1. In **Explore**, make 4 scoops of strawberry and 6 of banana. Press **Blend and taste**. That's batch 1.
2. Now make 2 strawberry and 3 banana, and blend. The owner says it tastes exactly like batch 1, and the log marks it "= #1".
3. Ask why. Both simplify to 2 : 3. Equivalent ratios taste the same, even though one batch is twice as big.

## The big idea

A ratio compares amounts: 2 scoops of strawberry for every 3 of banana. Multiply or divide both parts by the same number and you get an equivalent ratio. It is the same recipe in a different size, so it tastes (and looks) the same. On a graph, equivalent ratios line up on one straight line through 0.

## Talk about it

- Before blending: the layers in the jug are each one scoop. How many scoops of each? What fraction is strawberry?
- Two batches are the same color. What do you know about their ratios?
- In **Orders**: what did you multiply by? How do you know?
- Why do all the equal-tasting recipes sit on a straight line that starts at 0?

## Good to know

- **Orders** has seven: scale up, fill to a total, use what's left, simplest form, a party batch, a percent, and a unit rate. Each order asks for a recipe plus one condition.
- Every try lands on the graph: red tastes wrong, gold tastes right but misses the condition, green fills the order. Once the order is right, the ratio table and the line of equal-tasting recipes appear.
- After two wrong tries, the owner gives a hint.
- Smoothie color is mixed from the fruit amounts, so equivalent recipes really are the same color.
- The jug holds 24 scoops. Tap a fruit bowl or a **+** to add a scoop. Adding fruit after blending starts a fresh jug.
- Works for students on their own or teacher-led on a projector.
