# Mundo Hispano (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-6-12-cultures-of-the-spanish-speaking-world, grade 6-12. Codes: WRS.Cultures, ACTFL.Intermediate Mid
- Format / family: map / spanish. Fit: good: the Spanish-speaking world spans continents, and the map shows that range at a glance.
- One idea: Explore every country where Spanish is an official language, plus Spanish-speaking communities in the United States, through short Spanish texts and audio about one living tradition each (a practice, the things it uses, and why it matters to the people who keep it), and find that Spanish is spoken alongside Indigenous and other languages in many places.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a world map with the Spanish-speaking places lit, and a passport journal. "Elige un lugar."
  - Core loop: each place opens a card with a short text in Spanish at Intermediate Mid level, audio by a speaker from that place, photos, and the practice / product / perspective triangle filled in. The student answers questions in Spanish, then fills the "why" side of the triangle from the text. Language layer: which other languages are spoken there (for example, Guaraní alongside Spanish in Paraguay).
  - The aha or the end state: the journal compares two places the student chose and one tradition of their own, with comparison frames in Spanish, and the map shows how varied the Spanish-speaking world is.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): each student presents one place; regional words layer (the same thing called different names in different countries).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - WRS.Cultures: relating practices and products to the perspectives behind them, for real traditions, explained from inside the culture.
  - ACTFL.Intermediate Mid: reading, listening to and writing strings of sentences about traditions and comparisons.
- Not like: ciudad-perdida (directions in a made-up city); deaf-culture-explorer (Deaf culture); el-restaurante (ordering food).
- Art: public domain or licensed photos per place (sources and rights checked at spec); generated scenes only where no real photo fits.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Explore every Spanish-speaking country and US Spanish-speaking communities through short Spanish texts and local voices about one living tradition each; fill in what people do, what they use and why, and compare two places with your own.
- Flags: sensitive (cultural accuracy, Indigenous traditions, stereotype risk: fresh review, rule 5); needs a data source (photos, recorded regional voices, checked facts per place); too big for one pass (about 21 places plus US communities; write all of them, over time, per rule 1).
