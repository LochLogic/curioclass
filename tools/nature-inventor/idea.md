# Nature Inventor (idea card)

- Module: science-1-plant-and-animal-parts, grade 1. Codes: 1-LS1-1
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Meet a person with a problem (a coat that will not stay shut, a cold walk to school, a hard nut to crack), study how an animal or plant part solves the same problem (burr hooks, a polar bear's thick fur, a bird's strong beak), then build a gadget from that idea and test it.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an inventor's shed with a problem board; a child, Sami, needs a way to keep their mitten on. "Look at nature for an idea."
  - Core loop: open a nature book of parts (burr hooks, turtle shell, duck feet, cactus spines, a giraffe's long neck, hedgehog quills, a bird's beak), zoom in to see how each part helps the plant or animal; pick one, then build the gadget from parts (hooks and loops, a shell-shaped helmet, webbed swim gloves); test it on the problem.
  - Real inventors cards: true stories, plainly told: a hook-and-loop fastener came from burrs on a dog's fur; the bumpy front edge of whale flippers inspired fan blades; the kingfisher's beak shaped a quieter train nose.
  - The aha or the end state: a gallery of the student's inventions, each paired with the plant or animal part that inspired it.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 1-LS1-1: every problem is solved by copying how a plant or animal uses its outside parts to survive, then testing the design.
- Not like: who-s-my-baby (parents and young); shade-builder (shade from parts, not from nature).
- Art: generated scenes for the shed and close-up nature photos (burr, beak, fur), people (illustrated) for Sami and the problem owners, because the close-ups are the evidence.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Solve a person's problem by copying a plant or animal part (burr hooks, thick fur, a strong beak); study how the part helps it survive, build a gadget from the idea and test it.
- Flags: none (the real invention stories need a fact check at spec time)
