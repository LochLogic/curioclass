# Voices on the Vote (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-voices-on-the-vote, grade 8. Codes: D2.His.5.6-8, AASL.Include
- Format / family: timeline / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Dated voices from 1789 to 1870 argue about who should vote (property owners only, all white men, women, free Black men); the student places each on a timeline, traces how and why views shifted, then opens a shelf of left-out voices and adds them where they belong, seeing how the story changes when everyone is on the line.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an empty timeline and a stack of quote cards with names and dates hidden.
  - Core loop: read a quote, guess its decade from clues, then place it; the card reveals who said it and why (a property qualification debate, the 1821 New York convention, the Seneca Falls Declaration of 1848, free Black conventions, the 1869 split over the 15th Amendment). A "why did views change?" tag links shifts to causes (new states, wars, movements).
  - The aha or the end state: the left-out shelf (Native voices, Black women such as Frances Harper, working men without property, New Jersey women who voted until 1807); placing them changes the student's summary of how views changed.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): students each add one researched voice.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.5.6-8: placing voices and tagging how and why views changed over time.
  - AASL.Include: adding the left-out voices so the timeline represents diverse perspectives.
- Not like: voices-of-freedom (sources on slavery); reconstruction-amendments (the amendments after 1865); revolution-road (grade 5).
- Art: public domain portraits and documents (Library of Congress, to check at spec).
- Proposed title: Who Gets to Vote? (two grade 8 tools named "Voices..." side by side will be confused)
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: sensitive (racist arguments of the period quoted only as needed, never slurs; fresh review)
