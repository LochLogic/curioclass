# Motion on the Floor (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-motion-on-the-floor, grade 8. Codes: D4.8.6-8, D2.Civ.7.6-8, D2.Civ.11.6-8
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Chair a student club meeting about spending the club's fundraiser money: follow an agenda, take motions, handle an amendment and a vote, and make sure the quiet minority is heard; then watch the same kind of choice made by a city council, a school board and a state legislature, and compare how each decides and why.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a classroom set up for the meeting, a gavel and an agenda.
  - Core loop: illustrated members speak, move, second and amend; the student, as chair, picks the right next step (recognize a speaker, call the question, count the vote) and handles fairness moments (a member keeps interrupting; two members are ignored). A fairness meter and a rules meter both matter.
  - The aha or the end state: three short replays show the same kind of choice at a city council (public comment, open meeting rules), a school board and a state legislature (committees, two votes); a compare board names how each protects the public's voice.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class runs a real meeting with the tool as a guide.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D4.8.6-8: running the meeting with democratic procedures to decide.
  - D2.Civ.7.6-8: the fairness moments apply civic virtues in a school setting.
  - D2.Civ.11.6-8: the compare board of club, council, school board and legislature procedures.
- Not like: agora-assembly (ancient direct democracy); constitutional-convention (writing a plan); class-election (choosing leaders).
- Art: people (illustrated) for members and officials, generated scenes for each room.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Chair a student club meeting with an agenda, motions, an amendment and a fair vote, making sure the minority is heard, then compare how a city council, a school board and a state legislature make the same kind of choice.
- Flags: none
