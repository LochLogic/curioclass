# Planetarium Guide (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-5-presenting-clearly, grade 3-5. Codes: SL.3.4, SL.3.5, SL.3.6, SL.4.4, SL.4.5, SL.4.6, SL.5.4, SL.5.5, SL.5.6
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: The student plans and records a short planetarium talk under a dome of stars, choosing the stops, the facts and a picture or sound for each, then gives it twice for two very different audiences, so they hear that a clear talk has an order, a pace and words that fit who is listening.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a dark dome and a guide's podium. "Tonight's show needs a guide. Pick four stops for your tour." Stops: the Moon, Mars, the Big Dipper, a comet, Saturn's rings.
  - Core loop: order the stops so the tour makes sense, pick two facts and one detail per stop from fact cards, attach a picture or a sound to each, and record each stop's lines. A pace meter uses only the recording's length and pauses against the words in the script (no speech recognition). An on-screen line says the recordings never leave the device.
  - The aha or the end state: the audience switch: kindergartners on field trip day, then visiting scientists. For each, the student swaps lines between a formal and an informal card ("Saturn's rings are made of ice and rock" or "Guess what? Saturn wears a hula hoop of ice!") and re-records, then plays both talks back to back.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a short recorded reading of a public-domain star poem (Jane Taylor's 1806 "The Star") as the show's closing.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - SL.3.4, SL.4.4, SL.5.4: a talk with ordered stops, chosen facts and details, at a pace the meter checks.
  - SL.3.5: the recorded star poem reading at a clear pace, plus the recorded tour.
  - SL.4.5, SL.5.5: pictures and sounds attached to each stop to make the main ideas stick.
  - SL.3.6, SL.4.6, SL.5.6: complete sentences and the formal versus informal swap for each audience.
- Not like: show-and-tell-spotlight (k-2, one object and a listener switch); script-read-through (reading a script aloud); audience-shift-briefing (high school briefing).
- Art: generated scenes for the night sky and planets, illustrated audiences, because the dome has to feel real.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: none (records audio: privacy line required, no browser speech recognition)
