Ripple is a music maker, not a contest: there is no score and nothing to win. Glowing strings stretch across a dark pool; sweep across one and it plays, and every note sends ripples over the water. Kids can draw new strings, pick from 30 instruments and 12 scales, add a beat, record loops on top of each other and save what they made.

**Things to notice together.** Every string is tuned to the scale shown at the top, so any sweep sounds good, which makes it safe to try things. Change the key or the scale and listen to the mood change (minor pentatonic sounds different from major). Tempo (BPM) is how fast the beat goes. Loop layers show how songs are built from parts stacked over a steady beat. Faster sweeps play louder and brighter, the way a real string does when plucked harder.

**How to play.** Move the mouse across a string to pluck it. Drag on empty water to make a new string; click the badge in a string's middle to change its instrument or scale. Space starts the beat, L records a loop layer, and Help lists every key. Setups holds ready-made stages and saves your own.

**Good to know.** It works best with a mouse or trackpad and a keyboard; on a touch screen fingers can drag and pluck strings, but some controls need a mouse. Sound starts after the first click. Recordings save as a video, a WAV sound file, or a zip with each part on its own, to the device's downloads folder; nothing is sent anywhere, and it never uses the camera or microphone. Saved setups stay on the device. The water keeps moving while it plays, and Settings can turn the waves down.
