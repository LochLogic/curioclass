# Puppy Traits (idea card)

- Module: science-3-traits-and-variation, grade 3. Codes: 3-LS3-1, 3-LS3-2, 3-LS4-2
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Meet a litter of puppies and their parents and sort each trait (coat color, ear shape, size, knowing tricks, weight) into "came from parents" or "shaped by food, training and where it lives", then visit wild beach mice and dark-soil mice to see how small differences in a group help some survive.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a family photo of a mother dog, a father dog and six puppies; trait tags under each. "Which traits did the puppies get from their parents?"
  - Core loop: inspect each puppy, compare traits to the parents on a trait table and a bar chart (how many have floppy ears, how many have spots), and see the litter varies. Then follow two siblings who go to different homes for a year: one gets lots of exercise and good food, one does not; one learns tricks. The student marks which changes came from the environment.
  - Plants too: two sunflower seeds from the same plant, one in shade and one in sun, grow to different heights.
  - Wild litter: deer mice on pale sand dunes and on dark forest soil; coat colors vary in each litter, and an owl spots the mismatched ones first. Over generations the student watches which coat color becomes common in each place.
  - The aha or the end state: a sorted trait board with evidence, and an explanation card about why the pale mice do better on pale sand.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3-LS3-1: trait tables and charts from the litter and the sunflowers show inherited traits and variation in a group.
  - 3-LS3-2: the two-homes story and the sun-and-shade sunflowers show traits influenced by the environment.
  - 3-LS4-2: the beach mouse step, explaining how coat color differences give some mice an advantage in surviving.
- Not like: who-s-my-baby (grade 1 like-but-not-exactly-like); life-cycle-wheel (stages, not traits); fossil-clues (environments over deep time).
- Art: generated scenes (real-looking dogs, mice on sand and soil), because the traits must be visible on the animals. Predation is shown as an owl swooping, with no gore.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Meet a puppy litter and sort its traits into those from parents and those shaped by food and training, then watch pale and dark mice on sand and soil to see how small differences help some survive.
- Flags: none (the beach mouse case is based on real studies; the spec checks the facts)
