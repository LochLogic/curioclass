## A 5-minute start

1. Start in **Explore**. The left view is space, looking down on the North Pole with sunlight coming from the right. The right view is a backyard, facing south, at the same moment.
2. Press a phase button, or drag the Moon around its orbit. Ask: "Which half of the Moon is lit? Which half faces Earth?"
3. Drag the green **You** dot around Earth to change the time. Everything above your dashed horizon line is in your sky.

## The big idea

The Sun always lights half of the Moon. The phase is how much of that lit half we see from Earth, and that depends on where the Moon is in its orbit. The Moon also rises about 50 minutes later each day, so a full moon rises at sunset and a first quarter moon is highest at sunset.

## Talk about it

- Why can't we see a new moon, even though it's in the sky all day?
- Play days at 9 pm. What happens to the Moon's shape and its place in the sky night after night?
- A full moon is opposite the Sun. Why don't we get a lunar eclipse every month? (Turn on **Tilt and eclipses** to check.)

## Predict mode

The backyard shows a Moon at some time of day. Students drag the Moon in space to where it must be, then press **Check my Moon**. A correct answer is within about one phase.

## Good to know

- The model is not to scale: the real Moon is about 30 Earth widths away, and its 5° tilt is drawn larger so you can see it.
- The backyard is at mid-northern latitudes. In the Southern Hemisphere the lit side is flipped.
- Keyboard: focus the space view and use the arrow keys to move the Moon (Shift with arrows moves the time).
