# Screen Week
Renamed from: Screen Time Detective

## One idea
Screen time isn't one number: three pretend kids' weeks, laid out hour by hour, show what the screen time pushed out (sleep, moving, homework, people) and when, so the kid with the most hours isn't the one in trouble; then you redesign one kid's week block by block until it fits the sleep and activity guidelines and still keeps the screen time they love.

## Not like
- goal-tracker (6-8 PE, creature): Jordan's activity log and a plant that grows from consistency. Screen Week is a 24-hour data view of whole days, about what screens crowd out, and has no creature or goal sentence.
- misleading-charts (6-8 CS, mystery): judging published charts for tricks. Here the charts are honest; the question is what a week's pattern says.
- pressure-point, stress-toolkit, relationship-spectrum (group siblings): a story, a body test and a placing line. This is the group's only data tool, and nothing is about the student's own life.
- data-table-builder and the data-desk family: real public datasets. Screen Week uses three made-up weeks (labeled pretend) checked against real guidelines, so it does not join that family.
- Title: the overlap pass flagged one of twelve "Detective" titles and suggested "My Screen Week". The weeks are pretend kids', not the student's, so "My" would mislead; "Screen Week" keeps the topic word.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, Health, module Digital Wellness (screen time, online safety and media influence).
- Driver: students on laptops, alone or in pairs. A teacher can project one kid's week for a class discussion.
- Minutes: 15-30.
- Standards: NHES.S2 (analyze influences: how media and devices shape habits), NHES.S3 (access valid information: health guidelines for sleep and activity); NHES.S6 (goal setting) in Redesign.
- Topic line: "digital wellness and screen time".
- Modes (tool.json): ["Investigate", "Redesign"].

## Screens and modes
Header: title "Screen Week" with the topic line under it, mode buttons **Investigate**, **Redesign**, then **Motion**, **Sound**, **Read**. A kid switch under the header in both modes: **Rowan, 12**, **Mia, 13**, **Theo, 11**, each a small card with that kid's painted room corner (Art) as its picture and the name in code. The kids themselves are never drawn: their rooms and things stand in for them. A tag on the stage corner always: "Pretend data".

### Investigate (opens first)
- Focus: the week strip. It is the biggest thing, across the full stage width, centered; the quote card and stat tiles sit in one short row above it, the panel to the right.
- Stage: the week strip, seven rows (Mon to Sun), each a 24-hour bar from 6 am to 6 am, with hour ticks every 3 hours and labels at 6 am, noon, 6 pm, midnight. Blocks are colored and patterned by category with a glyph (Content). Screen blocks also carry a bright outline so all screen time shows at a glance. Lights out and wake are small moon and sun markers.
- Above the strip: the kid's room corner picture (small, left), the kid's quote and three stat tiles for the week: **Screen time** (hours), **Sleep in range** (nights of 7), **Moved 60+ minutes** (days of 7).
- Panel, top to bottom: hint; filter chips (one per category; tapping fades the others), a segmented **All days** | **School nights** | **Weekend**, a toggle **After 9 pm** (shades the strip from 9 pm on and adds "Screen after 9 pm" minutes to each row's end); the chart switch **Week strip** | **Hours by day** | **Sleep vs late screen**; and **Case notes** (3 questions per kid, optional).
- Hours by day: stacked bars per day (same patterns), screen kinds on top, with the day's total screen hours printed above each bar.
- Sleep vs late screen: a scatter, x = screen minutes after 9 pm that day, y = sleep hours that night, seven dots labeled with the day, the age's sleep range shaded with its label ("Ages 6 to 12: 9 to 12 hours" or "Ages 13 to 17: 8 to 10 hours").
- Tapping any block shows its card: day, start and end, category, minutes, and for Theo's homework blocks the pickups.

### Redesign
- Focus: the editable strip, at the same size and place as in Investigate; the checks list is the top of the panel, right beside it, so every edit and its check result are seen together.
- Stage: the same strip, now editable, for the chosen kid (a copy; the Investigate data never changes).
- Panel: the live checks list (Content), then the selected block's editor: category chips, **Start -15** **Start +15**, **End -15** **End +15**, **Clear block** (turns it into Other). Day controls for the selected day: **Lights out** with -15 and +15, **Wake** with -15 and +15. Week controls: **Phone charges outside the bedroom from** a time stepper (Off, then 8:00 pm to 11:00 pm in 15-minute steps), **Copy this day to the other school nights**, **Start over**.
- A "Before and after" strip toggle draws the original week as a faint ghost row under each edited row.

## Content

### Categories (glyph and pattern; never color alone)
Sleep (moon, small dots), School (book, solid gray, locked), Homework (pencil, fine stripes), Moving (sneaker, chevrons), People in person (two heads, solid), Other: meals, chores, getting ready, free time (no glyph, plain). Screen kinds, all with the bright screen outline: Scroll and watch, alone (phone, solid), Games with friends online (controller, wide stripes), Talk: video chat and texting (speech bubble, dots), Watch together (TV, cross-hatch).
Homework done on a laptop counts as Homework, not screen time; a note under the chips says so.

### Guidelines used by the checks
- Sleep per night: ages 6 to 12, 9 to 12 hours; ages 13 to 17, 8 to 10 hours (CDC).
- Moving: at least 60 minutes of physical activity every day, ages 6 to 17 (CDC).
- Screens off at least 30 minutes before bed (CDC sleep tips).
- Screen-free times like dinner, homework and before bed, and choosing apps and shows that teach, inspire creativity or connect you with people (AAP family media plan) are quoted in Redesign's tip line.

### Case notes (answer by tapping; each answer shows the evidence highlighted on the strip)
- Rowan 1: "Rowan says 'I barely use my phone.' When does most of the scrolling happen?" Options: After school / At dinner / In bed after 9 pm. Answer: In bed after 9 pm (highlights the 9:30 to 11:45 blocks).
- Rowan 2: "Tap every night Rowan slept less than 9 hours." Answer: Sun, Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu.
- Rowan 3: "Which one change gives back the most sleep?" Options: Less scrolling after school / Phone outside the bedroom at night / No games on Friday. Answer: Phone outside the bedroom ("about 2 hours a night on school nights").
- Mia 1: "Mia has the second most screen time. Is she short on sleep?" Answer: No: 8.5 to 9.5 hours every night, inside 8 to 10.
- Mia 2: "What kind of screen time is most of Mia's?" Answer: Games with friends and Talk (24 of 30 hours are with people).
- Mia 3: "Which days did Mia miss 60 minutes of moving?" Answer: Fri and Sun.
- Theo 1: "Which night did homework take the least time?" Answer: Thu (40 minutes).
- Theo 2: "What was different that night?" Answer: the phone was in the kitchen (3 pickups, not 35 to 41).
- Theo 3: "Theo has the least screen time. What is his screens' real cost?" Options: Sleep / Homework time / Moving. Answer: Homework time.
Closing line after each kid's three notes: "Same kind of tool, three different stories. Hours alone don't tell you what screens are doing to a day."

### Redesign checks (live, each with ✓ or an open circle and a count)
1. Sleep in range: nights inside the kid's age range, of 7.
2. Moved 60+ minutes: days of 7.
3. Screens off 30+ minutes before lights out: nights of 7.
4. Homework kept: school nights (Mon to Thu, Sun) with at least the kid's homework need (tool's own story numbers: Rowan 60, Mia 60, Theo 40 minutes), of 5.
5. Still them: hours of the favorite screen kind kept, against at least half of the original week's (tool's own rule: Rowan 16.75 of 33.5 h, Mia 9 of 18 h, Theo 3.5 of 7 h).
School blocks are locked. Wake on school days can't be later than 45 minutes before school starts (tool's own rule). Tip line under the checks: "The AAP suggests screen-free times like dinner, homework and before bed, and picking screen time that teaches, makes something or connects you with people."

## Interactions in detail
- Every edit snaps to 15 minutes and re-runs the checks and the stat tiles. Blocks can't overlap: pushing an edge into a neighbor stops at the neighbor. A block shorter than 15 minutes is removed.
- Phone charges outside the bedroom from T: every screen block after T is cut to end at T (blocks starting at or after T are removed); it applies to all seven days and shows a small phone-on-a-charger glyph (code) at T on each row; the control itself shows the painted kitchen charger picture (Art). Off restores the blocks.
- Worked example (verify recomputes): Rowan, phone outside from 9:15 pm, lights out 9:45 pm on Sun to Thu (other days unchanged): sleep 9 h every night (7 of 7), screens off 7 of 7, Still them 17.0 of 16.75 h needed (✓), moving still 1 of 7.
- Any tap (a block, a filter, a stepper, the kid switch) changes the screen at once, also while a voice line is playing; a stale line is dropped.
- Sleep in range counts the ends as inside (9.0 hours is in 9 to 12).
- When all five checks pass: a quiet line "This week fits the guidelines and still sounds like {kid}." No confetti.
- Start over restores the kid's original week. Switching kids keeps each kid's edits for the page session. Nothing is saved; there is no field for the student's own data.
- Keyboard: arrow keys move between blocks in a row and between rows; [ and ] nudge the start, - and = nudge the end; L and Shift+L move lights out.
- Narrow: the strip keeps 24 hours across the width, row labels shorten to M T W T F S S, and block glyphs hide under 20 px wide (pattern and outline stay).
