## What it teaches
Children estimate and measure lengths in inches, feet, centimeters and meters. They choose a useful tool, align its zero, compare units and use a marked tape to solve length stories.

## Key skills
- Make a guess using a one-unit reference, then compare it with a measurement.
- Choose a tool that has the right units and can bend or stand as needed.
- Read from zero and add measured pieces when a tool is too short.
- Compare two ribbons and connect larger units with smaller counts.
- Lay lengths end to end or side by side to model addition and subtraction.

## Support at home
- Before measuring a familiar object, each person makes a guess. Compare the guesses after measuring with a ruler you already have.
- Lay two strips of scrap paper at the same starting point. Talk about the extra part of the longer strip.
- Put two strips end to end, then side by side. Ask what changes and what stays the same.

## A 5-minute start
Open Freight. Invite a guess before anyone chooses a tool. Use the one-unit reference or a previously measured object to explain the guess. Choose a tool, line up its zero with the item and read its other end in the End circle. Type the reading and tap Check.

## Modes
**Freight:** eight untimed jobs. The first five mix ordinary, curved, standing and two-unit objects. A ribbon comparison and two order slips finish the run. The ghost uses the last completed run, or the labeled 32-point Starter ghost. New run abandons unfinished work without saving it.

**Yard:** browse all 36 items, choose a tool and use Show me to check. There are no points. Freight and Yard keep their places when switching modes.

## Talk questions
- Why is the beginning of the wood different from the zero line on this ruler?
- Which tool could measure a curve? Which can stand beside the broom?
- Why does the same bench need more inches than feet?
- Does this story put pieces together, remove a piece or compare two lengths?

## Good to know
This is a model of measurement, not a ruler calibrated to your screen. The item and its units change scale together. When a one-unit square cannot fit, an accurate one-unit segment replaces it. The exact object measurements and order slips are story values.

Use the arrow keys or Nudge buttons to move a tool by a quarter unit. On a curved item, Right arrow follows the path as a keyboard alternative to dragging. At zero and At end provide a keyboard alternative for placing order pieces. Mark and move records the tool length at the join. Read speaks the current instruction. Sound controls narration and chimes; Motion controls movement.

Guess stars are 3 within the larger of 1 unit or 10 percent; 2 within 1 unit or 20 percent; 1 within 2 units or 30 percent. The first matching band wins. A first-choice tool stamp is worth 1 point. Readings earn 2 then 1 point, with two attempts maximum. Ribbon readings earn 1 each. An exact run is 44 points. Stars are feedback on estimates, not a claim of mastery.

The first tool choice alone determines its stamp, even when later tools are tried. Correct readings also require aligned zero, a completed curve trace, the right tape face and enough measured pieces. For order slips, the layout and the story's answer must both agree.

Guesses must be above zero, since every object has a positive length. The required estimate bands are generous on very short objects. Discuss the estimate, rather than treating stars or a win against a weak previous run as proof of mastery. The cold review retains the spec's explicit bands for accepted guesses.

After a ribbon difference is checked, both ribbons and the labeled gap remain on the table. Compare their shared start and the extra part before tapping Next crate. On a second-unit estimate, the tool keeps the first unit's face until Check. Selecting another Yard tool clears the previous revealed answer. Resizing during a drag preserves the measurement and ends that gesture; start a new drag to continue.

Runs are saved only on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere. Storage failures leave the activity usable without saving. Narration falls back to the browser; this Build does not record audio.
