# Fraction Wall Duel (idea card)

- Module: math-4-equivalent-fractions-and-comparing, grade 4. Codes: 4.NF.A
- Format / family: cards / fraction-wall. Fit: good, with a computer rival as the default opponent so one student can play alone.
- One idea: Play fraction cards against a rival on a shared fraction wall; the bigger fraction wins the round, an equal one steals it, and every win must be backed by a reason the wall can show (same pieces, same count, or the half line).
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a fraction wall (halves down to twelfths, plus tenths and hundredths) and a hand of five cards. The rival, Rosa, plays 3/4 face up. "Beat it, or match it to steal."
  - Core loop: the student plays a card; both fractions light up as bars on the wall. To claim the win they pick a reason: rename to the same pieces (3/4 is 9/12, 5/6 is 10/12), same number of pieces (3/5 vs 3/8: fifths are bigger), or the half line (5/8 is past 1/2, 3/7 is not). The wall animates the reason.
  - Steal rule: an equal fraction (2/3 on 4/6) only counts once the student splits each third into two sixths on the wall; the split is the proof that (n x a)/(n x b) is the same amount.
  - Later decks add tenths and hundredths, and trap cards that compare fractions of different wholes (half of a big pizza vs half of a small one), which the rule book voids.
  - The aha or the end state: a fraction's size depends on both numbers, and the same amount can wear many names.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two students on one screen, hands hidden by a pass-the-device screen.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 4.NF.A: equivalent fractions by splitting every piece into n smaller pieces on the wall (4.NF.1); comparing unlike fractions by common denominator, common numerator or the 1/2 benchmark, same whole only, recorded with <, = or > and a stated reason (4.NF.2).
- Not like: fraction-trail (grade 3 fractions as places on a line), how-many-fit (same wall engine, grade 6 division), fraction-kitchen (grade 5 adding unlike fractions with cups).
- Art: drawn in code for the wall (exact lengths); people (illustrated) for Rosa and the card backs.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Play fraction cards against a rival on a shared fraction wall: the bigger fraction wins, an equal one steals, and each win needs a reason the wall can show (common pieces, common count or the half line).
- Flags: none
