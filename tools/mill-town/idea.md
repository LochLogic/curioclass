# Mill Town (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-reform-and-the-industrial-north, grade 8. Codes: D2.His.2.6-8, D2.Civ.14.6-8, D2.Eco.13.6-8
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Be a young woman leaving a New England farm for the Lowell textile mills in the 1830s: branching choices about work, wages, the boardinghouse, a strike over cuts, and the 1840s petitions for a ten-hour day, while a side panel shows each worker making far more cloth than a hand weaver and cloth getting cheaper, so the student weighs who gained from rising productivity and who pushed for reform.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a farm kitchen with a recruiter's handbill and a trunk to pack.
  - Core loop: each chapter is a choice (stay or leave, save or send money home, join the walkout or not, sign the petition, write for a workers' paper); the story branches for the character, with real events and real words from mill workers. The productivity panel updates (yards per worker, price per yard).
  - The aha or the end state: a change and continuity board (factory time replaces farm seasons; families still depend on daughters' wages; later Irish immigrant workers take over the mills), and a means-of-change card comparing the mill workers' tools (strikes, petitions, newspapers) with today's.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes at the strike meeting.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.6-8: the change and continuity board.
  - D2.Civ.14.6-8: the strikes and petitions, compared with present-day ways of changing work.
  - D2.Eco.13.6-8: the productivity panel shows output per worker rising and cloth getting cheaper, and asks who shared the gain.
- Not like: factory-floor (grade 9, the owner's tycoon view); manor-life (medieval work); levers-of-power (modern civic action).
- Art: public domain (mill workers' writings, period engravings and photographs, to check at spec) plus illustrated characters.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Leave the farm for an 1830s Lowell mill: choose how to work, save, strike and petition while machines make each worker far more productive, then weigh who gained and how workers pushed for reform.
- Flags: needs a data source (wages, hours and output figures, checked); sensitive (the mills ran on cotton picked by enslaved people; one plain card)
