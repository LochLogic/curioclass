# Climate on the Move (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-10-climate-on-the-move, grade 10. Codes: D2.Geo.9.9-12, D2.Geo.10.9-12, D2.Geo.12.9-12
- Format / family: timeline / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Four cases on one long timeline (the Little Ice Age, the Dust Bowl, the Sahel droughts and a recent major hurricane) show how slow climate shifts and sudden disasters move people, change farming and trade, and ripple into politics far away.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a timeline from 1300 to today with a temperature and rainfall band underneath and four case markers.
  - Core loop: open a case; place its events (a failed harvest, a migration, a price spike, a new law) on the timeline; a linked map shows where people moved and how trade and land use shifted. Each case pairs data with one family's story.
  - Compare view: slow change (Little Ice Age, Sahel) vs sudden shock (Dust Bowl storms, the hurricane); the student sorts effects into migration, land use and trade, and politics.
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes how one case's effects reached people who never felt the weather themselves (grain prices, refugees, new policies).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): each group adds a local case.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.9.9-12: the slow-change cases trace climate variability's effects on migration, settlement and land use from local to global.
  - D2.Geo.10.9-12: the land use and trade sort shows how environmental change shifted trade and farming patterns.
  - D2.Geo.12.9-12: the sudden-shock cases follow a catastrophe's effects on trade, politics and migration.
- Not like: suburb-boom (land use from policy, grade 11); independence-timeline (political timeline).
- Art: generated scenes for each case and people (illustrated) for the families, with public domain Dust Bowl photographs (Library of Congress FSA, checked), because the human side carries the data.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (paleoclimate and rainfall series, checked); the recent hurricane case needs current figures checked
