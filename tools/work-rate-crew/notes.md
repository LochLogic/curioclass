## What it teaches
Students add work rates, build and solve rational equations, and check proposed roots in the original equation.
They distinguish a root that gives negative story time from a truly extraneous root that makes a denominator zero.

## Key skills
- Combine fractions into one rational expression.
- Translate simultaneous and staggered work into an equation.
- Compare completion times and total crew costs.
- Rewrite a rational expression to explain its limiting value.
- Substitute roots and reject undefined original expressions.

## Support at home
- Invent two people doing the same chore at steady rates. Discuss what each does in one hour before combining them.
- Ask why averaging two workers' times cannot give the time when they work together.
- Try substituting a proposed answer into a fraction. Explain why dividing by zero makes it unusable.

## A 5-minute start
Open Calls. Assign Ana and Ben. Combine their rates into one fraction, predict the time, and press Paint.
Try the office's five-hour guess as an estimate first. The house stops full; the readout preserves the uncapped work.
This guided example is worked practice rather than an independent job check.

## Modes
Calls progresses through an average-time misconception, deadline and cost comparisons, an unknown crew, a late start,
and a quadratic with a root that gives negative sprayer time. In Deadline, calculate and paint all three pairs before
filing the cheapest qualifying quote. All three crews together can also be tested.
For the sprayer roots, students also calculate the sprayer's two times, in root order, separated by a semicolon.

Quote sheet rewrites 4x/(x + 4) as 4 − 16/(x + 4). The graph explores positive partner times from 0.5 to 60 hours.
The formula extends to all x > 0. The boundary x = 4 gives exactly two hours; only 0 < x < 4 gives under two hours.

Office audit contains four checking sheets inside the company's back office. These are practice equations, not jobs.
Sheet 3 needs a verdict for both roots. For an extraneous root, type an actual denominator from the original equation.
For a valid root, type the equal value of the two sides. Enter evidence in root order, separated by semicolons.
Check in the original reveals a worked example. Once shown, that sheet remains worked practice for this page visit
and cannot earn an independent audit filing. Students can still check their reasoning, then try another sheet.

## Talk questions
- What units belong to the reciprocal of a crew's completion time?
- Why does exactly three hours count as meeting a three-hour deadline?
- How does Ben's one-hour delay change the equation?
- Why does the sprayer's smaller algebraic root fail the story while an audit root fails the equation?
- What does subtracting a positive fraction from four tell you about the combined time?

## Good to know
The company, names, houses, prices and rates are made up. Rates are constant; crews work independently and share the
same one-house workload. Cost means combined hourly prices multiplied by actual job duration. There are no material costs.
One second represents one hour in Paint. Every animation lasts at most four seconds. Motion off preserves timing and
uses a progress bar. Crew, call and mode controls interrupt runs immediately.
An unchecked estimate that reaches the full house reveals its exact finish time. The student can finish that call
as worked practice, but cannot use the revealed time to earn an independent job stamp during that page visit.

Finished calls are stored only on this device using localStorage. Clear board removes them. Storage failure does not
prevent play. Sound and Read use the shared kit with browser speech fallback; no voice recordings are bundled.
