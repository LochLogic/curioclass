## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Explore**. Tap the bottom line of the staff: it's **E**. Tap the spaces going up: **F A C E**.
2. Press **═ Lines** for the saying (Every Good Bird Does Fly), then **▭ Spaces**. Switch to **Bass** and try both again.
3. Tap a piano key and watch where the note goes on the staff. **C4** is middle C, on its own little ledger line.
4. Switch to **Note Ninja**, pick **Treble lines**, and let the class call out each note before it reaches the red line.

## The modes

- **Explore:** tap a line or space (or a piano key) to place a note, hear it and see its letter. Up to eleven notes stay on the staff, and **▶ Play** plays them in order. **🔤 Letter names** labels every line and space. **🎯 Note words** asks for a word spelled with note letters (BAG, CAGE, CABBAGE...); any line or space with the right letter counts.
- **Note Ninja:** notes slide toward the ninja. Tap the letter (or press A to G) to slice the note closest to the red line. A note that reaches the line costs a life, and three lives end the round. The notes speed up every eight slices. Five levels: **Treble lines**, **Treble spaces**, **Treble staff** (with middle C, D and G above), **Bass staff** (up to middle C) and the **Grand staff**. Best scores are saved on this device.
- **Count the beats:** pick **2/4**, **3/4** or **4/4** and fill two measures with notes and rests. The green boxes under each measure show which beats are filled, and the counts appear under every note ("1", "2 &", "3 - 4", rests in brackets). A note that doesn't fit is turned away with how many beats are left. **▶ Play** counts in and plays the measures on a drum, with long notes held for their whole length. **🧩 Puzzle** fills both measures and leaves a gap: which note or rest fits?

## The big idea

The five lines and four spaces of the staff each stand for one letter name, A to G, repeating. The **clef** says which is which: the treble clef curls around the G line, and the bass clef's two dots sit around the F line. **Middle C** sits between the two staves on a ledger line.

A **time signature** tells how many beats are in each measure (the top number) and which note gets one beat (a bottom 4 means the quarter note). The notes and rests in each measure must add up to the top number.

## Talk about it

- Why does the treble staff's bottom line (E) have a different name from the bass staff's (G)?
- Which notes are on lines and which are in spaces? Does the pattern ever break?
- In 3/4, what note fills a whole measure by itself?
- Why do rests count, even though you don't hear anything?

## Good to know

- The sayings are a starting point. Many classes make up their own, and reading by landmarks (middle C, treble G, bass F) gets faster than sayings.
- Sharps and flats (the black keys) aren't used yet; the black keys just play their sound.
