## A 5-minute start

1. Students open **Quiz** and sort 36 activity cards: **Love it**, **Maybe** or **Not for me** (buttons, keys 1 2 3, or drag the card right, up or left).
2. They pick up to three **strengths**, then answer five **How do you like to work?** questions.
3. **My matches** shows their three-letter interest code, the interest hexagon and careers ranked by match. Tap any career for pay, training, job counts, what the work is like and a "try it now" idea.
4. **Explore** puts all 83 careers on the hexagon. Pick a cluster to light up its careers; the dashed circle marks where the student landed.

## The six interest types

The cards use John Holland's six interest types, renamed for middle school:

| Type | Friendly name | Likes |
|---|---|---|
| Realistic | Doer | building, fixing, tools, animals, outdoors |
| Investigative | Thinker | science, puzzles, research |
| Artistic | Creator | art, music, writing, design, performing |
| Social | Helper | teaching, caring, coaching |
| Enterprising | Persuader | leading, selling, starting things |
| Conventional | Organizer | details, records, planning |

The hexagon puts types that often go together next to each other (Doer and Thinker, Helper and Persuader), and opposites across.

## How the match works

- **Interests** (most of the score): the student's six card totals are compared with each career's O*NET interest profile.
- **Work style**: outdoors, working with the public, hands-on, routine and teamwork, compared with O*NET work context ratings. "Either" or "A mix" never counts against a career.
- **Strengths**: a small boost when a career uses strengths the student picked.

Results are kept on the device so students can come back; **Start over** clears them for the next student. **Print my results** gives a one-page summary to hand in or take home.

## Talk about it

- Did your top type surprise you? Which card was hardest to sort?
- Pick one match you had never heard of. What would a day look like?
- Find two careers in the same cluster with very different training. Why might someone choose each one?
- Pay is a median. What else matters when you choose a career?

## Good to know

- This is a conversation starter, not a test. Interests change a lot between 11 and 18; encourage students to retake it next year.
- Pay is the national median (half earn more, half less) from May 2025; starting pay is lower and pay varies by state. Musicians and actors show hourly pay because much of their work is part-time.
- "People doing it" counts wage and salary jobs; self-employed workers such as many farmers and musicians are not included.
- Training levels come from O*NET Job Zones; each career also has a plain-language path.
- Interest data: O*NET 30.2 (CC BY 4.0). Pay: BLS OEWS. Clusters: Advance CTE (2024).
