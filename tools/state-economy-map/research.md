# Research packet: state-economy-map
Concept: Pick your state and stack layers of land, resources, farms and industries to find where jobs cluster and why, then follow your state's top products to the places that buy them and see what comes back.
Grade: 4   Standards: D2.Eco.4.3-5, D2.Geo.11.3-5

## Standards in plain words

- D2.Eco.4.3-5: "Explain why individuals and businesses specialize and trade." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to explain how regional resources lead states to specialize in particular products and trade with others for goods they do not make.
- D2.Geo.11.3-5: "Describe how the spatial patterns of economic activities in a place change over time because of interactions with nearby and distant places." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to describe how job clusters locate where land, rivers, and transport links support them, and how connections with outside markets alter local economies.

## Real data

- US Census Bureau State Export and Trade Data: https://www.census.gov/foreign-trade/statistics/state/ (Public domain). Tracks annual state-level exports, top destination countries, and commodity categories.
- USDA Economic Research Service State Fact Sheets: https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/state-fact-sheets/ (Public domain). Comprehensive agricultural commodity rankings and farm receipts for all 50 states.
- US Energy Information Administration State Energy Profiles: https://www.eia.gov/state/ (Public domain). State-by-state energy production, resource reserves, and electricity generation data.
- Bureau of Economic Analysis GDP by State: https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gdp-state (Public domain). Regional economic performance by industry sector.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming states are completely self-sufficient: Fourth graders frequently believe every state produces all its own food, electricity, and manufactured goods, missing how specialization and interstate trade function (NCSS C3 Framework).
- Equating natural resources directly with job counts: Students often think states with forests or oil have workforces made up primarily of lumberjacks or drillers, overlooking manufacturing, logistics, and service occupations (BEA GDP by State).
- Treating trade as a one-way loss of goods: Students often perceive exporting as giving away goods rather than exchanging them for revenue and needed imports (Council for Economic Education).
- Ignoring physical transport geography: Students often assume factories or farms can appear anywhere on a map without considering water supply, rail lines, port access, and soil fertility (NCSS C3 Framework Geography).

## Sensitivity

- State economic disparities: Avoid framing states with lower average incomes as failing; emphasize geographic specializations, natural assets, and diverse economic roles.
- Environmental considerations: Extraction industries (such as coal mining or offshore oil drilling) involve ecological trade-offs. Present data objectively without partisan bias.
- Tribal sovereignty and resources: Note that sovereign Native nations within states manage key timber, mineral, water, and agricultural assets.

## Similar tools elsewhere

- National Geographic MapMaker Interactive (https://mapmaker.nationalgeographic.org): Layer-stacking map viewer showing climate, natural resources, and population densities.
- USDA ERS State Fact Sheet Dashboards (https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/state-fact-sheets/): Interactive state selector summarizing agriculture, top commodities, and demographics.
- Census Business Builder (https://cbb.census.gov): Mapping tool displaying local industry concentrations, employment, and trade.

## Gaps

- Complexity reduction for fourth graders: Official federal databases categorize thousands of technical commodity codes (6-digit HS codes); Curio Class tools should distill these into 3 to 5 clear, tangible commodity categories per state (e.g. citrus, timber, cheese, aircraft parts, computers).
