# State Economy Map (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-4-state-economy-and-resources, grade 4. Codes: D2.Eco.4.3-5, D2.Geo.9.3-5
- Format / family: map / none. Fit: good for specialization and trade; D2.Geo.9.3-5 (effects of catastrophic events on settlement and migration) does not match a jobs-and-resources map.
- One idea: Pick your state and stack map layers of land, resources, farms and industries, find where jobs cluster and why, then follow your state's top products out to the other states and countries that buy them and the goods that come back.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a U.S. map. "Tap your state." It opens with a land layer (mountains, rivers, soil, coast).
  - Core loop: switch on layers one at a time (crops, mines and forests, factories, ports, tech and tourism). A cluster finder asks "Why are so many of these jobs here?" and the student links the cluster to a resource or route (dairy near grass and water, a port city on the coast).
  - Specialize and trade: the state's top products show trade arrows to buyers elsewhere; the student sees why the state makes a lot of a few things and buys the rest.
  - The aha or the end state: a "my state's specialty" card in the student's words: what we make best, why here, and who we trade with.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare two states side by side.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.4.3-5: specialty products and trade arrows explain why people and businesses in the state specialize and trade.
  - MOVE D2.Geo.9.3-5: to settle-the-valley, where a flood or drought year forces families to move or rebuild (catastrophic events on settlement and migration). A better second code here is D2.Geo.11.3-5 (economic patterns changing through ties with near and far places), taught by a then/now toggle on the industry layers.
- Not like: three-towns (grade 2 kinds of community); colonial-trade-routes (historic trade); state-quest (state locations).
- Art: drawn in code (map layers must be data-accurate), with generated icons for industries.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pick your state and stack layers of land, resources, farms and industries to find where jobs cluster and why, then follow your state's top products to the places that buy them and see what comes back.
- Flags: needs a data source (per-state industry and agriculture data, e.g. BLS and USDA, to be confirmed at spec); module code mismatch (Geo.9.3-5)
