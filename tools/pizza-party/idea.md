# Pizza Party (idea card)

- Module: math-1-halves-and-fourths, grade 1. Codes: 1.G.A
- Format / family: builder / fraction-food. Fit: good for equal shares; see MOVE for the rest of the cluster.
- One idea: Cut round pizzas and rectangle sandwiches so two or four guests get fair shares; uneven cuts get a funny complaint, and more guests mean smaller pieces.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a party table, guests arriving, a pizza and a cutter. "Two friends are here. Cut the pizza so it's fair."
  - Core loop: the child drags cut lines; the tool shows each piece's size. Fair shares get a happy bite; an uneven cut gets "Hey, his half is bigger!" Shares are named: halves, fourths, quarters, "half of the sandwich."
  - Rectangles can be cut more ways (two triangles or two rectangles are both halves).
  - The aha or the end state: the same pizza cut for 2 and then for 4 guests; the fourths are smaller, which surprises kids who think 4 is bigger than 2.
  - Rebuild: pieces slide back together into the whole pizza.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): odd guest counts are kept out (thirds are grade 2).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 1.G.A (equal shares part, 1.G.3): partitioning circles and rectangles into two and four equal shares, naming halves, fourths and quarters, and seeing that more shares means smaller shares.
  - MOVE 1.G.A (attributes and composing parts, 1.G.1 and 1.G.2): new tool: a grade 1 shape builder, where you compose pictures from flat shapes and from solids (cubes, cones, cylinders) and a picky robot accepts only true triangles (three sides, closed) whatever their color, size or turn. A party theme cannot teach defining attributes or composing solids well.
- Not like: garden-grid (grade 2 rows, columns and thirds), fraction-trail (grade 3 fractions as numbers), recipe-scaler (grade 4 family sibling).
- Art: generated scenes (party table, glossy food); people (illustrated) for guests; cut lines drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Cut pizzas and sandwiches so two or four guests get fair shares; uneven cuts get a funny complaint, shares get named (halves, fourths, quarters), and more guests mean smaller pieces.
- Flags: too thin for the whole 1.G.A cluster (needs the MOVE above or a new grade 1 shapes tool)
