## A 10-minute start

1. Open **Progressions**. Tap **I**, then **V**, **vi** and **IV**, and press **Play**. Ask: where have you heard this before? (A lot of pop songs.)
2. Watch the **How far from home?** meter while it plays. Which chord pulls hardest back to I?
3. Switch to **Build a chord**. Tap C on the keyboard, then switch between **Major** and **Minor**. Only one note moves. What changes in the mood?
4. Finish with a few rounds of **Home or not?**

## The big idea

A **triad** is three notes stacked in thirds: a root, a third and a fifth. The size of each third decides the chord's quality:

| Quality | Bottom third | Top third | Sounds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major | 4 half steps | 3 half steps | bright, open |
| Minor | 3 | 4 | darker |
| Diminished | 3 | 3 | tense, unstable |
| Augmented | 4 | 4 | dreamy, unsettled |

Build a triad on each note of a major scale and you get seven chords, named with Roman numerals: capitals for major (I, IV, V), lower case for minor (ii, iii, vi), and ° for diminished (vii°). Each chord has a job:

- **Home (tonic):** I, and the softer homes vi and iii. Music rests here.
- **Away (predominant):** IV and ii. They move the music somewhere new.
- **Tension (dominant):** V and vii°. They lean back toward I.

## Cadences in Home or not?

- **Authentic:** V → I. The strongest way home.
- **Plagal:** IV → I. The gentle "Amen" ending.
- **Half:** the phrase stops on V. It sounds like a question.
- **Deceptive:** V → vi. You expect home and get a surprise.

## Talk about it

- Why might a songwriter end a verse on V and save I for the end of the chorus?
- The Pop anthem (I V vi IV) and the Fifties (I vi IV V) use the same four chords. Why do they feel different?
- Try the **Surprise!** preset. How does the deceptive ending make you feel?
- Change the key. The chord names change. What stays the same?

## Good to know

- Seven major keys are included. Note names are spelled the way musicians write them (B♭, not A♯, in F major).
- The minor key and seventh chords are not included yet.
- Playing styles: block chords, broken chords (an arpeggio pattern) or strummed. The bass plays the root an octave lower.
- The sound is made by the browser itself, so the tool works offline.
