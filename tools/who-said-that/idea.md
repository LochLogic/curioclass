# Who Said That? (idea card)

- Module: ela-1-who-is-telling-the-story, grade 1. Codes: RL.1.6, RL.1.9
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good for the narrator code; not for comparing characters (see MOVE line).
- One idea: Lines from a story float down as speech bubbles and caption boxes, and the child drags each to the character who said it or to the narrator's box, so they learn that someone is always telling the story, even when no one is talking.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three characters from The Town Mouse and the Country Mouse and a storyteller's chair. "Someone is telling this story. Who says each line?"
  - Core loop: lines fall; the child sorts them to Town Mouse, Country Mouse or the narrator. Clue tools: quotation marks glow, and "said" tags highlight. Later stories add a first-person narrator ("I"), where the narrator is also a character.
  - The aha or the end state: the story replays with each line in its speaker's voice, and the narrator's voice runs through the whole thing; the child sees how much of the story the narrator tells.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): voice a character's lines aloud.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.1.6: sorting lines to characters or the narrator at points across the story.
  - MOVE RL.1.9: alike-and-different-books (comparing two characters' adventures fits its overlapping hoops, which already teach RL.K.9 and RL.2.9).
- Not like: Two Sides Theater (grade 2 point of view and voices). Dialogue Director (grade 3 quotation marks). Story Map Treasure (plot).
- Art: generated scenes (story pages) and illustrated characters, because each speaker must be recognizable at a glance.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Lines from a story float down as speech bubbles and caption boxes; drag each to the character who said it or to the narrator, and hear the story replay in each speaker's voice.
- Flags: none
