## A 10-minute start

1. Press **▶** (or the space bar). The starter beat is a kick on every beat.
2. In the **Channel rack**, click or drag across the **Closed hat** steps to add hats, and give the **Snare** steps 5 and 13. Right-click a step for a softer ghost note.
3. Open **🗂 Sounds** and press **Use kit** on a kit (808, Trap, Lo-fi...) to change every drum at once.
4. Open the **Piano roll**, pick **Bass**, and click in some notes. Scale lock keeps every row in the key, so nothing sounds wrong.
5. Turn up **Swing**, then **Pump**, and listen to how the groove changes.

The nine **Producer missions** in the left panel walk students from a first beat to an exported track.

## The views

- **Channel rack:** eight drum channels and four synth channels (Bass, Chords, Lead, Arp). Each drum channel has 16 steps, a sound, volume, pan and pitch. **Fill ▾** adds every beat, every eighth, off-beats, random and more, or shifts the pattern. Synth rows show a mini view of their notes.
- **Piano roll:** click to add a note, drag to stretch it, drag a note to move it, click it to remove it. **Chord stamp** (Chords channel) writes a whole chord built from the key with one click. **Oct −/+** shows lower or higher notes. Below the grid: the instrument and its Cutoff, Reso, Attack, Release and Volume knobs.
- **Song:** eight patterns, A to H, in a 16-bar playlist. Patterns stack, so drums can live in A and a melody in B, layered in the same bar. **SONG** mode plays the arrangement; **PAT** loops the pattern being edited.
- **Mixer:** a fader, pan, reverb send, delay send, mute and solo for every channel, with level meters. The master has volume, the **Filter** (a DJ sweep: left muffles the mix, right thins it out) and **Pump** (sidechain: the synths duck on every kick).
- **🗂 Sounds:** the sound library. Click any sound to hear it, pick a channel in **Load into**, and press **Use**. Search covers every sound.

## The sound library

- **214 sounds** in 13 categories: 25 kicks, 20 snares, 13 claps, 16 closed hats, 11 open hats, 11 cymbals, 38 percussion (congas, tabla, cajon, steel drum, kalimba and more), 13 toms, 31 FX (risers, impacts, lasers, scratches, vinyl crackle, game sounds), 16 vocal chops, 10 808 and sub hits, and 10 hits and stabs.
- **Ten kits** set all eight drum channels at once: 808, 909, Boom bap, Trap, House, Lo-fi, Acoustic, Latin, Electro and World.
- **30 instruments** for the synth channels: 6 basses (including 808, acid, wobble and FM), 8 keys, 5 pads, 6 leads, 4 plucks and bells, and the **Sampler**.
- **Your samples:** import WAV or MP3 files, or record up to 10 seconds from the microphone (a beatboxed snare, a clap, a word). Silence at the start is trimmed. A sample can go on a drum channel, or on a synth channel as a **Sampler** that plays it at any pitch from the piano roll (C4 is its own pitch).
- Every sound is synthesized in the browser, so the whole studio is still one small file that works offline.

## Saving and sharing

- The track saves itself on this device as students work, and imported samples are kept on the device too.
- **Save file** downloads a .json project with the samples inside, so it can be opened on another computer with **Open**.
- **⬇ Export WAV** renders the track to an audio file: the arrangement in SONG mode, or four loops of the pattern in PAT mode.
- **Demo tracks** (House, Boom bap, Trap, Synthwave) show how a full track is built. Loading one replaces the current track, and **Undo** brings it back.

## The big idea

Producers build tracks from short loops. A **beat** sets the groove (kick, snare or clap on the backbeat, hats for motion); **harmony** and **melody** sit in a key; **arrangement** turns loops into a song by adding and removing layers over time; and **mixing** balances everything so each part has its own space. Small choices like swing, ghost notes, sidechain pumping and filter sweeps are what make a loop feel alive.

## Talk about it

- What changes when you move the snare off beats 2 and 4?
- Why does a bassline that follows the kick feel tight?
- Listen to the House demo with Pump at zero, then turned up. What does sidechain do to the feel?
- How does your arrangement build energy? Where does it drop out, and why?
- Swap the whole kit. Does the same pattern now feel like a different genre?

## Good to know

- Keyboard: **Space** plays and stops; **Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Y** undo and redo; the letter keys **A W S E D F T G Y H U J K** play the selected synth (**Z** and **X** change octave). With **⏺ Record** armed and the track playing, the notes you play are written into the piano roll, snapped to the nearest step.
- Changing the key moves every synth note with it. Changing the scale doesn't move notes; notes outside the new scale get a red outline.
- Hover over anything to read what it does in the bar along the bottom.
- The microphone needs the browser's permission the first time.
