# Recital Practice Plan

## One idea
Practice is a budget you can hear: spread four weeks of minutes across the passages that are actually hard, then listen to each week's take of every hard passage played back with exactly the slips your plan left in, and judge it on a rubric you wrote before the meter shows what it measured.

## Not like
- theory-gym (9-12, puzzle): names keys and cadences. Nothing here is analysis drill; the music is a set you must get ready.
- music-history-radio (9-12, timeline): one tune across eras. Here the pieces stay put and your playing of them changes week by week.
- beat-lab (9-12, studio): composes and produces a track. Here nothing is composed; you plan, practice and judge performance.
- band-practice (6-8, race) and recorder-concert-rehearsal (3-5, this batch): play along in time. Here you never play along; you plan time and listen critically.
- museum-courtyard-performance and mini-documentary-crew (this band, also tycoon): those spend money and crew time on a venue or a shoot. Here the only currency is practice minutes, and the result is heard.

## Who and how
- Grade 9-12, music, module "Preparing a Recital (Music)".
- Topic line: planning practice for a recital
- Driver: students on laptops, alone or in pairs, with headphones. 30 to 45 minutes (one sitting runs all four weeks); the plan is saved on this device.
- Standards: NCAS MU:Pr4 (select, analyze and interpret repertoire), MU:Pr5 (rehearse, evaluate and refine), MU:Pr6 (present), MU:Re9 (evaluate using criteria), MU:Cn10 (relate personal experience to music).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound`. Kit theme `theme-912`. Modes (`.seg`): `Set` (default), `Rubric`, `Practice`, `Recital`. Practice is enabled once a set of 2 or more pieces is chosen; Recital once week 4 has been practiced.

**Set.** Focus: the program strip (the set in order with its running time), centered and widest on the stage, with the venue picture above it. Top: three venue cards (Content) with their painted picture; tap to choose (`Library at lunch` is preselected). Middle: five piece cards (title, composer, year, length at the chosen tempo, a difficulty bar from the model), each with `Add` / `Remove` and a tempo slider (80% to 110% of the listed tempo, step 5%) that changes its length and difficulty live. Bottom: the program strip: pieces in order (drag, or `Earlier` / `Later`), each with 20 s of spoken introduction added, and the total ("9:35 of 10:00"). The total turns to a warning with words when over 10:00 or under 9:00 ("Over by 0:24: drop a piece or speed one up"). Under the strip: `Venue notes` (the chosen venue's note for each piece in the set) and two text boxes: `Why this set` (500 characters, "Why does this set matter to you?") and, per piece, `Introduction` (200 characters, what you will say before it).

**Rubric.** Focus: the rubric table, filling the stage. Six criteria cards (Content); tap 3 to 5 to use them. Each chosen one becomes a row with four level columns (4 to 1); level 4 and level 1 descriptors are prefilled and editable (120 characters). `Reset rubric`. A note: "Write it before you listen. Then hold yourself to it."

**Practice.** Focus: the week board: one row per practice unit, with its minutes slider and readiness bar side by side, the biggest thing on screen. Week tabs `Week 1` to `Week 4` (only the next unplayed week and earlier ones are open). Top of the panel: `Minutes a day` (30, 45, 60) and `Days a week` (5, 6, 7), and the week's total ("315 minutes this week"). Units: for each piece in the set, `Hard passage` (named, for example "Minuet bars 1 to 8") and `Rest of the piece`; plus `Run the whole set` (one unit for run-throughs). Each unit row: a minutes slider in 15 minute steps (the sliders together can't pass the week's total; the leftover shows), a method toggle `Slow, in chunks` / `Straight through` (hard passages and rest only), and the readiness bar (0 to 100%) with last week's value as a tick mark. `Practice the week` applies the model, moves the bars (0.8 s ease; Motion off: jump) and opens Listen for that week.
- **Listen** (a panel under the board, or a sheet over it at narrow widths). Focus: the piano roll of the take playing, wide across the stage. For each hard passage: `Play take` (this week), `Play week` picker to compare any earlier week, `Stop`. The piano roll scrolls with a playhead; wrong notes show in the warning color with an x, early or late notes as small offsets from their grid line. After a take plays, the rating row: one 1 to 4 button group per rubric criterion. `Show the meter` (enabled after rating every metered criterion of that take) reveals the measured values beside your ratings, with "You heard it the same" or "The meter says 2; you gave 3".
- Every control works while a take plays and acts at once: Stop cuts the sound in the same frame; another `Play take` or week stops the current take and starts the new one; moving a minutes slider never changes a take already made.

**Recital.** Focus: the venue picture with the program card over it. `Begin recital` plays the program in order: for each piece, its introduction text on screen for 3 s, then its hard passage as the week 4 take (8 to 15 s each), then a 1 s pause. About 60 s for four pieces. `Stop`, `Skip piece` (both live). Afterward, the recital sheet: per piece, readiness, the meter values, your rubric ratings for the week 4 take, the venue note, and `Why this set`. `Print sheet` (black on white). `Start over` (confirm; clears weeks, keeps the set and rubric).

## Content
**Pieces** (all public domain, piano; bars as played with repeats; listed tempo; length = bars x beats / tempo; hard passage; model difficulty in minutes for the hard passage and the rest):
1. Minuet in G major, BWV Anh. 114, Christian Petzold (about 1725; long credited to J. S. Bach). 32 bars in 3/4, both halves repeated: 64 bars at quarter = 116, 1:39. Hard passage bars 1 to 8. Difficulty 60 and 80.
2. Prelude in C major, BWV 846, J. S. Bach (1722, The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book 1). 35 bars in 4/4 at quarter = 66, 2:07. Hard passage bars 1 to 4 (keeping the broken chords even). Difficulty 90 and 120.
3. Gymnopédie No. 1, Erik Satie (1888), "Lent et douloureux". 78 bars in 3/4 at quarter = 72, 3:15. Hard passage bars 5 to 12 (the melody over the rocking chords, quiet and even). Difficulty 70 and 100.
4. The Entertainer, Scott Joplin (1902), "Not fast". Intro 4, A 16 (repeated), B 16 (repeated), A 16, C 16 (repeated), interlude 4, D 16 (repeated): 152 bars in 2/4 at quarter = 76, 4:00. Hard passage A section bars 5 to 8 (the syncopated melody with left-hand stride). Difficulty 200 and 260.
5. Night Bus, an original piece written for Curio Class (2026). 32 bars in 4/4 at quarter = 104, 1:14. Hard passage bars 9 to 12. Difficulty 110 and 60.

**Venues** (picture; one line; notes per piece, a guide not a rule):

Prelude "Even and calm; it may blend into the noise." Gymnopédie "Very quiet. In a busy room, the soft melody may get lost." Entertainer "Catchy; people stop to listen." Night Bus "Your own discovery piece; say where the name came from."
- Senior center afternoon: "A digital piano in the community room. A seated audience of about 25 that listens closely." Notes: Minuet "Many listeners learned it as children." Prelude "A quiet room suits it." Gymnopédie "A quiet room is where it works best." Entertainer "Many in this audience know it from the 1973 film The Sting." Night Bus "Tell them a little about how a new piece is made."
- Coffee shop open mic: "A stage keyboard with a sustain pedal, a small speaker and an espresso machine." Notes: Minuet "Light; fine as an opener." Prelude "Needs the sustain pedal working well." Gymnopédie "Quiet; ask for the grinder to wait." Entertainer "Lively and loud enough for the room." Night Bus "Its rhythm fits the room."

**Practice model** (shown on screen as "A simple model, not a law"; its rules in a `How the model works` note):
- Start: hard passages 20% ready, the rest 40% (already read through once).
- A week: each unit's readiness r becomes 1 - (1 - r) x e^(-k x m / d), where m is its minutes, d its difficulty times (tempo factor)^2, and k = 1 for `Slow, in chunks` and 0.4 for `Straight through`. A unit with no minutes that week loses 8%: r x 0.92.
- Tired hands: 60 minutes a day for 7 days gives k x 0.85 that week, so the seventh day adds almost nothing; the note says "Rest days are part of practice."
- `Run the whole set`: each minute adds to a Flow value (0 to 100%, start 0, d = 90). Flow sets the pause between pieces on Recital night (1 s at 100%, up to 4 s at 0%, with "finding the page" shown) and is a line on the recital sheet.
- A take: tempo = target x (0.75 + 0.25 r); each note wrong with probability 0.25 x (1 - r)^1.5, played a semitone or a whole tone off (seeded per week and passage, so a take replays the same); timing wobble standard deviation 60 x (1 - r) ms; dynamics range 0.3 + 0.5 r (velocity spread, so a weak take is flat).

**Rubric criteria** (level 4 / level 1 prefilled; metered criteria and their meter levels 4, 3, 2, 1):
- Right notes ("Every note right." / "Many wrong notes."): wrong notes 0, 1, 2 to 3, 4 or more.
- Steady beat ("The beat never wobbles." / "The beat drifts and stumbles."): wobble under 10 ms, under 20, under 35, 35 or more.
- Tempo ("At the target tempo." / "Much slower than the target."): 97% or more, 92%, 85%, below 85%.
- Dynamics ("Clear louds and softs." / "All one loudness."): range 0.70 or more, 0.60, 0.50, below 0.50.
- Shape ("Phrases rise and fall like speech." / "Every note the same weight."): ears only, no meter.
- Keeps going ("Slips never stop the music." / "Stops after slips."): ears only, no meter.

## Interactions in detail
- Changing the set after week 1 starts asks "Change the set? New pieces start at 20%." Removed pieces keep their readiness if added back.
- Tempo slider: length and difficulty update live; a set that was legal can become illegal, and the strip says so at once.
- Sliders never allow more minutes than the week's total; the leftover shows ("45 minutes not planned"). `Practice the week` works with leftover minutes (they are just not used) but says "45 minutes unplanned" on the board.
- Readiness bars show the number and a word (Rough under 50%, Getting there 50 to 79%, Solid 80 to 89%, Ready 90% and up).
- Takes are made once per week at `Practice the week` and stored; replays are identical. The week 0 take ("First read") exists from the start.
- Extremes: a unit at 100% stays at 100%; a week with 0 minutes for everything lowers every unit 8% and the board says "A week off costs you."
- The meter never shows before your ratings for that take; ratings can be changed afterward, but the first rating stays on the recital sheet as "first ear".
- Audio: Web Audio piano voice (the kit's tone or a small sampled-free synth: two detuned triangles with a fast decay), velocity from the dynamics model. Sound off: takes still play visually on the piano roll with no sound.
- Saving: localStorage `curio-recital-plan` (set, tempos, venue, rubric, weekly minutes and methods, readiness, takes' seeds, ratings, texts). `Start over` clears weeks only.
- Sizes: at 1366 x 768 the week board's rows and `Practice the week` are visible without scrolling for a 4-piece set (9 rows); at 680 x 425 the board shows rows at 36 px with the method toggle as an icon button; at 375 wide Listen opens as a full-width sheet.

## Done when
1. Focus: the program strip in Set, the rubric table in Rubric, the week board in Practice and the piano roll while a take plays are each the largest thing on screen at 1366 x 768 and 680 x 425.
2. The three sets in Content show 9:54, 9:29 and 9:35; a tempo change updates length and the warning at once.
3. Every hard-passage note matches the IMSLP score (pieces 1 to 4) and the spec (Night Bus).
4. Rerunning the model checks in Sources in the tool (same minutes) gives the same readiness values to within 1%.
5. A take at low readiness audibly has wrong notes, a slower tempo and a wobbly beat; at 95% it sounds clean; a replay is identical.
6. While a take plays, Stop, another take or another week act in the same frame; sliders stay usable.
7. The meter stays hidden until the take is rated; the first ratings appear on the recital sheet.
8. The recital lasts about a minute for four pieces and Stop and Skip piece act at once.
9. How lines once each over five weeks of play; setFits, agree and tired every time their event happens; no line names a disabled control.
10. All 5 images load with no people and no lettering; the metronome pendulum swings at the take's tempo (still with Motion off, showing the number).
