## What it teaches

Students can choose a recital program for an audience, allocate a limited practice budget, and evaluate a performance using criteria they wrote before listening. They hear how the same passage changes across four weeks of simulated practice.

## Key skills

- Match repertoire, tempo and introductions to an audience.
- Put practice time where a piece needs it most.
- Compare slow work in chunks with playing straight through.
- Rate notes, beat, tempo, dynamics and musical shape by ear.
- Connect a program to personal experience and explain its meaning.

## Support at home

- Ask your student why one chosen piece matters to them. Listen to the explanation without needing to know the music.
- Together, divide a familiar task into a difficult part and the rest. Discuss which deserves more attention.
- Notice a song's changes in loudness or steadiness together. Compare what each of you heard before discussing a judgment.

## A 5-minute start

1. In Set, choose a venue and add Minuet in G, Prelude in C, Gymnopédie No. 1 and Night Bus. At the listed tempos, the program is 9:35 including four introductions.
2. Open Venue notes & introductions. Write why the program matters to you, then one introduction.
3. In Rubric, choose three to five criteria and edit the descriptions for levels 4 and 1.
4. In Practice, open First read and listen to one passage. Rate it before revealing its meter.
5. Return to Week board, assign minutes and practice the week. Compare the new take with First read.

## Modes and the class period

Set prepares the audience and repertoire choice. Each tempo factor changes length and the square of model difficulty. Twenty seconds per spoken introduction belongs to the planned program, even though the condensed recital displays introductions for three seconds.

Rubric asks students to establish criteria before judging. Shape and Keeps going are deliberately ears-only. Meter levels for the other four use the fixed thresholds in the spec; edited descriptors do not change that numerical meter. A disagreement invites another listen rather than treating the meter as a complete musical judgment.

Listen keeps error marks, timing reference ticks, actual BPM and readiness readouts hidden until the first judgments and meter reveal. A new criterion must be rated before its meter appears. Feedback compares the immutable first ear rating with the meter; revising a rating afterward does not earn first-ear agreement. Hover over a rating's endpoints to review your own descriptions.

Practice runs four weeks within one 30 to 45 minute class period. The daily and weekly time values are a planning model, not time students must wait. Sliders use 15 minute increments and never exceed the available total. Unplanned minutes are unused. Recorded weeks cannot be rewritten. The next week inherits the previous plan for revision.

Recital presents each introduction and week 4 hard passage, not the entire ten-minute program. Run-through Flow changes the pause between pieces. Stop and Skip piece interrupt immediately. The final sheet includes readiness, meter values, first ear ratings, venue notes and the student's reflection. Print sheet is black on white.

The recital's shaded keyboard lights the pitches of the saved take, including its slips. It uses the same timed note events as the audio, and is a presentation aid rather than a play-along task.

## Talk about it

- Which hard passage gained most from your plan? Did any unit need more time?
- What did a change of tempo cost in running time and difficulty?
- Which rating changed after a second listen? Why might a meter miss musical shape?
- How would you explain this set differently in a library and a quiet community room?
- Where does your personal experience enter the choice and presentation of this program?

## Good to know

- The venues, original Night Bus piece, difficulty values and exponential practice model are invented for Curio Class. Readiness is not a diagnosis or forecast for a real musician.
- The actual beat meter reports root mean square timing offsets, in milliseconds. Right notes counts individual played pitches in both hands, including chord tones.
- Takes use seeded note errors, timing and velocity. A saved take never changes when a future plan or target tempo changes. Sound off preserves visual playback; Motion off preserves the timing.
- Sound is a sampled-free piano-like voice made from two detuned triangle oscillators and a fast decay. No real performances or student audio are recorded. Browser narration uses the easy-coach line bank with the best available browser voice; no narration clips were recorded for this build.
- Work is stored only in localStorage under curio-recital-plan. If storage is unavailable, the footer says to keep the page open. Start over confirms before clearing weeks, plans, takes and ratings; it keeps repertoire, rubric, tempos, venue and written texts.
- Musical ornaments in Minuet are omitted in accordance with the explicit right-hand sequence in the spec. Ties in Satie and Joplin are one sustained note, not new attacks.
- An added piece after week 4 has no week 4 take. The recital labels that fact and asks for a new practice cycle, rather than inventing a practiced performance.

## Standards through student action

MU:Pr4: select a set for an audience, compare difficulty, choose tempo and write introductions. MU:Pr5: plan four weeks, compare takes and revise allocations. MU:Pr6: present the condensed recital. MU:Re9: create a rubric, rate takes and compare evidence. MU:Cn10: write why this set matters personally and carry that reflection onto the recital sheet.
