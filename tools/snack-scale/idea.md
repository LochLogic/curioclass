# Snack Scale (idea card)

- Module: math-k-more-less-same, grade k. Codes: K.CC.C
- Format / family: sandbox / balance. Fit: good, with one fix: every snack in a round is identical, so the seesaw tips by how many, not by how heavy. Mixed snacks would teach weight, which is who-wins's job.
- One idea: Pile identical crackers on each side of a seesaw, guess which side will go down, then let go; matching the crackers in pairs shows the same answer, because the unmatched extras are the "more."
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a playground seesaw held level by a squirrel, and a bowl of crackers. "Put crackers on both sides, then tap Let go."
  - Core loop: the child drags crackers onto each side (up to 10 a side), taps a side to guess "more" (or taps "the same"), then taps Let go. The side with more sinks; level means the same. A Match button lines the crackers up in pairs so the unmatched ones glow.
  - Numeral rounds: the squirrel holds up the cards 6 and 9. The child guesses which is more, then the seesaw loads that many crackers to check.
  - The aha or the end state: 7 widely spaced crackers look like more than 8 bunched ones; the seesaw and the pairs both say 8 wins. Spread out does not mean more.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a snack switcher (apples, grapes; always one kind per round); a class vote before Let go.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - K.CC.C: comparing two groups up to 10 as greater, less or equal by matching (the pairs button) and by counting (number tags on request), and comparing two written numerals from 1 to 10 (the card rounds).
- Not like: who-wins (compares weight and length of single objects), balance-the-beam (grade 1 equal sign with numbers on both sides), sheep-count (counting one set).
- Art: generated scenes (playground, squirrel, glossy snacks), because the seesaw is the hook; the tilt is drawn in code so it stays exact.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pile identical crackers on each side of a seesaw, guess which side has more, then let go. Match them in pairs to see the extras, and compare numeral cards up to 10 the same way.
- Flags: overlap risk (who-wins teaches weight; every round's snacks stay identical so the lesson stays number)
