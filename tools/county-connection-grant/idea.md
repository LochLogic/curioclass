# County Connection Grant (idea card)

- Module: computer-science-6-8-who-gets-connected, grade 6-8. Codes: MS-SYS-HW-30, MS-SYS-IM-37, MS-SOC-ET-41
- Format / family: map / none. Fit: good, because households, towers and cable runs sit on a county map.
- One idea: Spend a fixed grant to get a rural county online, matching each household on the map (a dairy family, a grandparent who lives alone, a student with homework, the library, a clinic) to a device and a connection (fiber, fixed wireless, satellite, a cell hotspot) by need, cost, speed and power, then launch a telehealth app and see whose language, bandwidth or device it still leaves out.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a county map with a small town, ranches, a ridge that blocks signal and a budget meter. "Every household needs to get online. You have the grant."
  - Core loop: tap a household to read its needs (video homework, a doctor's video visit, running a farm's sensors, power outages in winter); pick a device and a connection; the map shows coverage, monthly cost and the speed they will really get. Fiber is fast but costs per mile; satellite reaches everyone but has data limits; the ridge blocks wireless for two homes.
  - Stress test: a winter storm knocks out power for a day; homes with battery-backed devices stay online.
  - The twist: the clinic launches a telehealth app that needs steady video, works only in English and needs a new phone; the map lights up who it now leaves out despite being "connected".
  - The aha or the end state: the right system depends on each user's needs, and good design for one community can still leave people out.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-SYS-HW-30: match devices and connections to each household's tasks and limits.
  - MS-SYS-IM-37: see how access differs across the county by distance, cost and terrain.
  - MS-SOC-ET-41: judge the telehealth app's design choices and propose changes so more people can use it.
- Not like: network-architect (grade 9-12 school network design); packet-race (grade 6-8 protocols); where-s-my-phone-from (supply chains).
- Art: generated map scenes in a painted rural style; coverage layers drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (realistic costs, speeds and data caps for each connection type, sourced and dated, rule 9)
