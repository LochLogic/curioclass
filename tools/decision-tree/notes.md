## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Grow**. The Double Booking (Dev) is loaded. Read the setup under the picker out loud: robotics practice and a friend's bowling party at the same time.
2. Ask the class what matters to Dev, and turn on those goal chips (any number). Each chip has an icon, and every leaf shows that icon with an up arrow (helps), a down arrow (hurts) or a flat dash (no change).
3. Tap two or three option cards (or press 1 to 4) to plant them as branches. Each branch shows its Right away leaf.
4. Before moving on, ask: which branch looks best right now? Then press **Play** (or P), or drag the time slider, to grow the leaves for Next week and Next month. Watch the badges at the top of each branch change between Blooming, Even and Wilting.

## The two modes

- **Grow:** four fictional, ordinary dilemmas: The Double Booking (Dev), The Quiz Photo (Kai), The Tablet Fund (Nia), The New Game (Theo). Each has four options (A to D) with one consequence per time band, and each consequence is marked against the four goals. A branch's standing is its ups minus its downs over the goals that are on, counted from Right away up to the slider's stop. Tools:
  - **Graft:** tap two planted branches that could both happen. The right pair grows a fifth option, G, between them. A wrong pair says so; after two misses there is a clue.
  - **Make a call:** with two or more branches planted and before Next month, tap the branch you think will stand best by Next month. When the slider gets there, the panel says "You called it." (ties count) or names the branch that is standing best. No points, nothing stored.
  - **Who it touches:** shows who each consequence lands on.
  - **Choose a branch:** at Next month, pick the branch you would choose. A card shows the choice, its three consequences and a look-back question. Choosing is never graded.
  - **Print tree:** the dilemma, the goals that are on and every planted branch with its leaves and marks up to the current time, black on white.
  - **Clear tree:** pulls up every branch and resets time, keeping the goals.
- **Side by side:** two trees of the same dilemma with every option planted, one for **Goals A** and one for **Goals B**. The line under the slider says which branch is standing best for each set. **Swap** trades the two sets. The graft appears here only if the class found it in Grow this session.

## Talk about it

- Which branch looked best right away? Was it still best by next month? Why might the quick win fade?
- Turn on one goal at a time. Does the best branch change? What does that tell you about why people disagree over the same choice?
- In Side by side, give the two trees different goals. When they pick different branches, is either one wrong?
- How did the graft come about? What made those two options fit together when the others did not?
- Turn on Who it touches. Which consequences land on someone other than the kid who decided?

## Good to know

- Keys: 1 to 4 plant or pull up A to D, 5 the graft once found, G starts or cancels a graft, left and right arrows move time, P plays, W shows who it touches, Escape cancels. Everything also works with Tab and Enter.
- There is no text box anywhere. The dilemmas and kids are fictional; nothing asks about a student's own life, family or classmates, and no feelings are scored. Branches are marked only against the fictional kid's goals. Nothing is saved except the Motion setting shared by Curio Class tools, and nothing is sent anywhere.
- The tool never says a choice is right or wrong. It shows that the option that looks best right away often is not the one standing best later, and that people with different goals can sensibly choose differently. The graft does not always win, on purpose.
- If a dilemma stirs something up for a student, or a student is upset, the teacher's school counselor is the right next step.
- Motion off makes branches and leaves appear at once; Play keeps the same 1.2 second steps. Sound off keeps the tool silent; Read speaks the current hint.
- Standard: CASEL Responsible Decision-Making, which includes identifying solutions for personal and social problems and anticipating and evaluating the consequences of one's actions (CASEL, What Is the CASEL Framework?, casel.org). The tree is a teaching picture, not a research finding. The dilemmas, consequences and goal marks are original content written for Curio Class. The hillside backdrop is generated (FLUX.1-schnell); the tree, leaves, icons and sign are drawn in code.
