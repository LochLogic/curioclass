## What it teaches
Children speak and respond in role as a moon-base crew. They use moon facts to steer a story, revise a choice, and explain how their revision changes the greenhouse ending.

## Key skills
- Speak as someone with a job in an imagined place.
- Listen and respond to another character.
- Mime a prop to make a role clear.
- Use a science fact to make a dramatic choice.
- Revise a scene and explain what changed.

## Support at home
- Pretend a chair is a moon buggy. Take turns being its driver and the radio operator, speaking to each other in role.
- Mime a watering can or a wrench. Ask someone to guess your job from your actions.
- Retell a familiar story, change one choice, and talk about how its ending changes.

## A five-minute start
The teacher is Commander Ada. Read Ada's cream script card aloud in role. Mission Control is the narrator, a separate character. Choose one or more crew jobs and let children mime their props. No real props are needed; a scarf or clipboard can help the teacher enter the role.

Tap Start mission. After each radio message, read Ada's card and allow time for the class to answer. The story never advances itself. At a choice, listen to the crew's spoken answer and tap its button.

## Modes
**Crew:** Six jobs can be shared by table groups or the whole class. Tapping a checked badge repeats its job line. A check assigns a job; it is not a score.
Choose at least one job before entering Mission with either the tab or Start mission.

**Mission:** Eight story beats cover the long night, the greenhouse, batteries, ice, lifting, warmth, dust and the ending. The three meters count story resources, not physical measurements. Each starts at one dot, and three dots means full. Air is part of the dialogue and remains inside the base; it has no separate meter.

Say the crew's line before tapping its choice. Button positions vary between missions. A choice stays committed until Back or Rewind; tapping the other line asks for Rewind rather than silently replacing the story. Repeating the chosen line repeats the radio response without adding dots. There is no score or mastery assessment.

Rewind opens the story map. Tap a past Batteries, Water or Warmth choice on the current path. An unanswered choice is
unavailable until the crew chooses a line again. The resources return to their earlier state, and later nodes fade as
the old story. Try another line and compare the ending. Back also removes a choice when returning to it. New mission
keeps the crew jobs.

## Talk about it
- How can your voice and your pretend prop show your job?
- What did the crew say to Ada? How did she respond?
- Which moon fact helped you choose?
- Rewind one choice. What changed in our story? Why?
- How did the crew act when the doors opened?

## Good to know
This is a 20 to 30 minute, teacher-led K-2 drama. Pause for spoken answers and group mime. Children may mime seated.
TH:Cr3 asks children to revise their dialogue: after Rewind, Ada invites them to say the new line in their own words.
For TH:Re9, choose a prop for your job, say how the crew met each moon problem, and explain which choice mattered most and why.
TH:Cn11 connects Moon science with the drama. NGSS 1-ESS1-1 is context, not a full lesson on observing sky patterns.

Read speaks Mission Control's current message, never Ada's script. On an unanswered choice, Mission Control also voices
the two crew options in their displayed order. Radio again replays that same transmission. On Crew, Read includes the
premise and the next action. Sound controls narration and effects; Read remains available. Motion off removes rover
travel, floating ice and transitions without advancing the story. Keyboard: Right for Next, Left for Back, R for Rewind,
A for Ada's card. Tab and Enter operate every button.

Story progress stays only in memory. The shared kit remembers its Motion preference on this device. No class information is collected or sent. No microphone is used.

The roughly 29.5 Earth-day lunar sunrise cycle gives about 14.75 days each of daylight and night, away from polar exceptions. Lunar gravity is about one sixth of Earth's; mass does not change. Ice exists in permanently shadowed polar craters, and there are no liquid rivers. The Moon has an extremely thin exosphere, not breathable air. A blanket conserves existing warmth; it is not a heat source. The simplified greenhouse presumes a sealed base with heating and air.

The radio compares lunar night with a home freezer. NASA describes near-equatorial night temperatures around minus 133 Celsius, far below an ordinary home freezer. Polar daylight and night differ from the simple two-week pattern used in this fictional base. The crater painting shows patchy ice in shadow, not an underground river or a sunlit glacier. Recovering a big block and carrying it to the base is dramatic shorthand, not a demonstrated lunar mining process.

The first instruction for Start mission, Next, a choice and a rewind leads that moment's radio transmission once.
At the ending, hear the story outcome first, then the invitation to explain and start a new mission or rewind.
Later transmissions repeat the story facts without repeating the instruction. Read map reads the map's instruction
while it is open. Allow the radio to finish and the crew to perform; every tap can still interrupt immediately.
