# Monologue Rehearsal (idea card)

- Module: arts-9-12-acting-a-monologue, grade 9-12. Codes: TH:Pr4, TH:Pr6, TH:Cn10
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Pick a monologue, answer its given circumstances (where am I, who am I talking to, what do I want, what is in my way), split it into beats where the tactic changes, try a vocal and physical choice for each beat, and perform it for an audience you choose, which changes what works.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a rehearsal room and a shelf of monologues (public-domain plays and originals written for Curio Class), each with a line about the character.
  - Core loop: fill the given circumstances, with each answer pinned to the line that proves it. Mark beats and name a tactic for each (to plead, to warn, to tease). Rehearse: record a take, see a simple contour of pace, loudness and pauses per beat, and check whether the tactic changes where the beat does. A coach card offers one physical note per beat.
  - The aha or the end state: choose the audience (an audition panel, a community center, a family room) and the coach says what that audience needs; then perform live or record a final take. A last card: where the character's beliefs meet or clash with the student's own.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a partner plays the silent listener.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - TH:Pr4: using the given circumstances and beats to shape character choices.
  - TH:Pr6: performing the monologue for a chosen audience.
  - TH:Cn10: how the student's beliefs and community shape their reading of the character.
- Not like: character-voice-booth (grade 3-5: one line in many voices, voiceprints); director-s-chair (directing a scene); three-viewpoints-scene (grade 6-8 devising).
- Art: generated scene for the rehearsal room; people (illustrated) for the coach and audiences.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: records audio (on-screen line that takes never leave the device; contour computed on the device; no browser speech recognition); needs a data source (public domain monologues in public domain translations)
