## What it teaches

Students prepare a character using given circumstances and a whole-speech want, divide a monologue into purposeful
beats, and rehearse vocal and physical choices. They use a real listener's responses to revise their acting and
connect the character's beliefs to their own.

## Key skills

- Find evidence in a play for who, where, when and to whom a character speaks.
- Choose an intention and try different tactics, pace, volume, body, stresses and pauses.
- Perform aloud for a specific audience and listen closely to a partner.
- Compare planned changes with felt changes, treating a listener's response as evidence.
- Reflect on a character's beliefs and your own position.

## Support at home

- Read a short familiar passage aloud twice, first to reassure someone and then to challenge them. Ask a family
  member what changed, without telling them the intention first.
- Listen to a story together. Name a moment where the speaker seems to start doing something different.
- Talk about a character's belief you agree or disagree with, and explain your reason.

## Five-minute start

1. In pairs, open a setting and read the speech. Choose this one opens the preparation desk.
2. Answer at least who, where and what the character wants. Check the first four answers with What the play says.
3. Open Score. Add one beat change with a space dot. Choose a different tactic for that beat.
4. Use Rehearse, Start and Next beat while speaking aloud. Space advances; Shift+Space goes back.
5. Choose an audience in Perform, hand the laptop to a listener and perform live. The listener taps words, taps
   Done, names a want and saves. Compare shows the evidence.

## The modes

- **Choose:** Viola, Marullus, Helena or Mrs Hale, with the complete selected passage in a reader.
- **Prepare, Circumstances:** six shared answers, limited to 200 characters each. The first four have facts from
  the play. Want and obstacle are shared with Score.
- **Prepare, Score:** three independent versions. A space dot toggles a change before the following word. Tap a
  word, then Stress or Pause after to toggle its mark. Select a beat row to edit its tactic, pace, volume and body.
  Copy the current version to either other version. Copy replaces the destination score. Clear marks affects only
  the current version. The closest want belongs to that version and supports comparison with the listener.
- **Rehearse:** a large prompter with marks and choices. Start begins an unlimited elapsed timer. Next beat and
  Back change the highlight; selecting another version keeps the beat number when that version has it.
- **Perform:** four audience settings. Hand to partner removes the actor's marks from the displayed script.
  The listener flags up to 30 words, chooses a shuffled want or Something else, and adds a word for the feeling.
- **Compare:** a saved snapshot of the score at performance time, gold listener flags, teal actor bars and pale
  brackets for matches. Select any earlier performance. Connect has three 300-character reflection boxes.

## Talk questions

- Which beat changes did your listener feel? Which surprises are worth keeping?
- Can two different wants produce similar outward choices? What other evidence would help?
- What changed when you played to an audition panel rather than to younger students?
- What does the character believe, and where do you agree or disagree?

## Good to know

This is for grades 9-12, usually pairs on one laptop for 30 to 45 minutes. A student can prepare and rehearse alone;
the listener's evidence comes from a real partner. No model pretends to judge acting. No microphone, camera,
recording, upload or network connection is used. Narration speaks instructions, never the monologues.

Work saves automatically under `curio-monologue-rehearsal` in this browser on this device. It includes plans,
versions, performance snapshots, partner taps and reflections. Shared school devices retain that work until someone
uses Clear all in Compare and confirms. Device storage can be blocked; an on-screen message reports a failed save.

The matching window is two words before through six words after a planned change. Each flag goes to one nearest
planned change; an equally distant tie goes to the earlier mark. Multiple flags can refer to the same change, but
the count includes each planned change once. A match is an approximate listening aid, not proof of a correct choice.
No marks or flags is a useful starting point; this tool awards no grade or points.
The headline describes flags near boundaries, not proof that a tactic read. Broad or random flagging can create
accidental matches; discuss the specific words and repeat the performance with a listener. Agreement on a closest
want also does not prove the listener understood every part of the actor's own wording.
When at least 12 flags put three quarters of possible boundaries inside a matching window, Compare shows a broad-flag
notice and a flag count instead of a matched-change ratio or all-changes narration. This is a tool guard against
indiscriminate sampling, not a psychological threshold. The full listener record remains available in the overlay.
During a take, the actor's modes stay locked until the listener submits both a want and a feeling word.
After Compare, Plan a change in Score or Revise a choice in Score returns to that take's monologue and version.

The on-screen duration assumes 150 spoken words per minute. The texts have 207, 179, 208 and 161 spoken words.
Mrs Hale's two `[...]` cut markers are not spoken words, but remain tokens when placing marks and flags. Shakespeare's
source line breaks are retained; Viola has 25 lines in the downloaded Gutenberg edition. All tactics and wants are
suggestions, not rules or claims about the author's intended performance.

Content note: Trifles concerns a woman held for her husband's death. This selected speech concerns loneliness,
neighbors, regret and solidarity. Teachers may choose a different speech when that subject does not fit a group.
The Shakespeare speeches include romantic confusion, rejection, public anger and shaming. Any student can play any
character; no gender, accent or body type is prescribed. Treat older generalizations about women as character speech,
not facts. Tactic tips are experiments, not rules, and physical choices can be adapted for comfort and access. Discuss the distinction
between a character's beliefs and a student's beliefs when using the Connect prompts.

Keyboard: all controls have visible focus. In Score and the listener's script, Tab enters the words once; arrow
keys move through the words and spaces. Enter activates a word or toggles a space mark. Tab leaves the word list
for the next control. Rehearse uses Space and Shift+Space without scrolling the page. At phone width, Edit beat
opens a bottom sheet; Close beat sheet returns space to the script. Long scripts and preparation panels scroll.

NCAS links: TH:Pr4 is practiced in circumstances and scoring; TH:Pr6 in the audience choice and live performance;
TH:Cn10 in the three Connect reflections. The character belief is the student's interpretation, supported by the text.
