## A 5-minute start

1. Tap **Next pet**. Marmalade walks in. Ask the class: "Cat or dog?" Everyone says it out loud, then tap **Cat**. Marmalade flies onto Kip's memory wall.
2. Tap **Next pet** again (Bear) and tap **Dog**. Kip now remembers two pets.
3. Tap **Mystery pet**. Kip asks the class to guess first ("What will Kip guess? Say it out loud!"), then draws a dotted line to the remembered pet that looks most like the new one and shows its guess.
4. Switch to **Pet Parade** and tap **Start parade**. Ten new pets walk by. Count together how many Kip gets right (with two pets on the wall, it is 6 of 10).

## The two modes

- **Teach:** the class is the teacher. **Next pet** brings the next example, and **Cat** or **Dog** puts it on that shelf of Kip's memory wall. Labels are never checked, so a wrong label teaches Kip wrong. **Mystery pet** brings a new pet for Kip to guess: the dotted look-alike line ends on the one card Kip used, and that card glows. Tap a wall card, then **Forget**, to make Kip forget it. Once every example card is shown, the big button becomes **Pet Parade**. **Start over** empties the wall and the chart.
- **Pet Parade:** the measurement. The same ten pets walk by every time, so runs compare fairly. Each slot fills with a check or an X, the score shows large, and Kip's Chart adds a bar: how many Kip got right, with the number of pets it remembered under the bar.

## Talk about it

- Why did Kip think Pippin was a cat? (Look at the line: Pippin has pointy ears, like Marmalade.)
- What kind of pet should we show Kip next so it stops making that mistake?
- Kip got more right after we showed it more pets. Did we tell Kip any rules? How did it learn?
- What happens if we teach Kip a wrong label on purpose? Try it, run the parade, then use Forget to fix it.
- Chart talk: look at Kip's Chart. Which bar is tallest? How many pets did Kip remember for that run? What do you predict the next bar will be if we show Kip two more pets?

## Good to know

- Parade scores after teaching the first cards in order with the right labels: 1 card: 5 of 10. 2 cards: 6. 3 or 4 cards: 7. 5 to 9 cards: 8. 10 or 11 cards: 9. 12 or more: 10. The parade is 5 cats and 5 dogs.
- Two pets are hard on purpose. **Pippin** is a dog with pointy ears, and Kip gets it right only after it has seen **Ziggy** (card 12), a pointy-eared dog. **Waffles** is a dog with a short nose; **Pudding** (card 10), another short-nosed dog, fixes it first. The lesson: Kip needs an example like the new pet, not just more of the same.
- A wrong label never helps. Labeling Marmalade as a dog lowers the parade score (5 instead of 6 with two cards, 8 instead of 10 with 12 or more cards); labeling Snowball as a dog with five cards drops it from 8 to 6. When a mistake comes from a wrongly labeled card, Kip says "Oops! Kip remembered that pet wrong." Forget that card and it goes back to the front of the deck, so Next pet brings it again for a fresh label.
- Kip's Chart keeps the last 8 runs (4 on a small screen). Stopping a parade early adds no bar.
- Keyboard: Tab moves through the mode buttons, header buttons, panel buttons, then the wall cards. Enter or Space activates. On a selected wall card, Delete forgets it and Escape lets go. Arrow keys move between wall cards.
- Motion off: pets appear in place instead of walking, with the same timing and the same scores.
- Every line is spoken; the "how to" lines are spoken once, then stay in the hint. **Read** speaks the hint again.
- The pets are painted pictures; Kip, the lobby and the chart are drawn in code, so they stay sharp on any projector. Nothing is saved; a reload starts fresh.
