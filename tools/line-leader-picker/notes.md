## What it teaches
People choose the Turn Machine's two instructions. Its chart shows the consequences for who leads, including friends with no turns in a simulated month.

## Key skills
- Choose and edit a two-step rule.
- Compare gold stickers with blue Before outlines.
- Count turns and inspect empty columns.
- Distinguish equal chance from a turn for everyone.
- Make a personal fairness judgement using visible evidence.

## Support at home
- Give toy animals turns in a line. Compare keeping the leader at the front with moving the leader to the back.
- Draw toy names from a jar. Compare replacing the name with leaving drawn names out.
- Discuss a pretend animal who is away for a day. Invite different ideas about fairness.

## Using the workbench
Choose two pictures and Run, One day, or To the end. Change a Step provides a previous month. Is It Fair? provides four rule cards and Bee's absence switch. There is no assigned route, score, level or finish screen.

The evidence cards are optional and available in any order after a month appears. Yes and No record an observation. The small mark shows the rule or chart fact, never a grade for that observation. Fair and Not fair are available without answering the cards and are never graded. Our chart records inspected facts and the last call per rule and attendance setting for this page load. A new month clears on-screen observations and preserves the saved call. The previously recorded month remains in Our chart until another observation or call replaces it.

The Manual opens from the lower left. Motion, Sound and Read are on the wooden power panel. Only the first quiet hint speaks automatically; Read speaks the current readout or explanation. The bank remains friendly-dad, Achird. Recordings are not yet present, so Read uses the browser voice. No audio was recorded in this fix.

## The real material
This is an authored simulation of an imaginary animal class, not a real classroom dataset. Curio Class, Line Leader Picker, spec.md defines the twenty names, twenty-day month and four rules. Fictional definitions and original text: CC BY 4.0. The bundled interpreter in src.html: MIT. Illustrations generated with OpenAI gpt-image, edited for Curio Class, supplied as Curio Class content: CC BY 4.0. No outside dataset or quoted standards wording was added.

A keeps the front friend in place. B rotates the leader to the back; an absent friend at the front also passes to the back. C draws from all present candidates and replaces the name. D removes each drawn name; an absent friend's name remains in the jar for later days. Bee is absent on day 2 only when the switch is on. The rule-level equal-chance readout assumes everyone is present: A no, B/C/D yes. Attendance is a separate situation.

Counts and evidence come from actual simulated days. The interpreter and an independent occupancy calculation in _scratch/line-leader-picker/validate.py agree: Rule C has an expected 20 × (19/20)^20 = 7.169718448 empty columns. Independent sampling of 10,000 months gave 7.171. Individual months differ. These are model results, not empirical classroom claims. The verification script is MIT.

Bee's count readout adds the sick-day line only when Bee has zero turns and would have led in the coupled all-present month. B always passes Bee's day-2 turn. C compares the same uniform day-2 draw in the full jar; later draws use the same sequence. D gives every friend one turn in a full month, including Bee.

## Boundaries
The feelings sentence is a fictional classmate prompt, not a claim that every child feels the same. No health advice, personal questions, saved student data or network requests. Motion off removes travel and pulses while keeping day pacing.

The older spec and Refine contain lesson instructions. The user's Curiosity instruction wins: no forced Who quiz, grade tags, kit header, automatic question chain or answer gate.
