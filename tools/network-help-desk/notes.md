## A 5-minute start

1. Open Calls. Read Grandpa Lou's first words, then choose a question. Watch the matching part of the house appear.
2. Ask another question. Press Make a claim, pick a kind, then a cause, select one or two clue cards that prove it, then Send fix.
   Grandpa Lou needs both the screen clue and cable clue together. A weak clue gets the same reply for every cause, so compare evidence before choosing.
3. Compare a connection call with Leo's permission call or Grandma June's login call.

## The two modes

- Calls: eight fictional callers, in any order. Six questions reveal the connection, router, other devices, screen, login and account. An unfinished call keeps its clues for this page load. A wrong claim adds two minutes to Call time and points back to evidence.
- House: select a device and change its controls. Try Internet, Library site, Family printer and Parent settings. Tap the router to change power and the desktop cable end to unplug it. Drag the tablet or select it on the map and use arrow keys, one small step per press. Reset house restores everything.

## Pairs or projector

In pairs, one student reads the caller cards and clue cards aloud; the other drives. Swap roles on the next call. On a projector, let the class choose questions and ask students to name the clue that proves a claim before sending the fix.

## Three kinds

- Connection: a cable, radio, distance or power breaks the path.
- Logging in: prove who you are using the correct password. The technical word is authentication.
- Permission: an account has already logged in, but its access rights do not include this resource. The technical word is authorization. Leo should open the Student page. Ana can send the file to a family laptop, respecting the guest boundary.

NIST distinguishes identity verification from access rights: [authentication](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/authentication) and [authorization](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/authorization). Compare the actions in calls 6, 7 and 8 rather than treating every refusal as a bad password.

## Talk about it

- Which question changed your mind? Which clue proves the cause?
- Why can a guest use the internet but not the family printer?
- Does a correct password let a student open a teacher page?
- In House, what still works when Caps Lock is on? What still works on Guest?

## Good to know

Call time counts one minute per new question and two per wrong claim. It models help desk effort, not a clock or score. Wi-Fi bars are approximate: walls and distance weaken Wi-Fi; the thresholds are a Curio Class model.

All callers, houses and passwords are pretend. Nothing is sent or recorded. Calls and House reset on reload. Only the kit Motion preference is stored on this device. Sound controls narration and chimes; Read speaks the current hint. Motion off keeps the same fix and ping timing.

Students practice E4-SYS-NT-14 through cable, Wi-Fi and router experiments; E4-SYS-SE-13 through password, account and guest permission comparisons; and E4-SYS-HW-12 through evidence-based hardware troubleshooting and testing a fix.
