# Smoke and Honey (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-6-smoke-and-honey, grade 6. Codes: D2.Eco.8.6-8, D2.Eco.1.6-8
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: In one town, a paper mill's smoke sends neighbors to the clinic at no cost to the mill, while a beekeeper's bees pollinate orchards for free; the student tries a smoke tax, a filter rule or a payment for hives and watches the paper price, mill jobs, clinic visits and the orchard harvest, learning that markets make too much of a harmful thing and too little of a helpful one until the hidden cost or benefit is counted.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a valley town with the mill's chimney, the clinic, the hives and the orchards, and four family cards.
  - Core loop: set a policy and run a year; meters show paper made and its price, mill profit and jobs, clinic visits, honey and fruit. Family cards (a mill worker, a parent with an asthmatic child, the beekeeper, an orchard grower) tell how the year went for them.
  - The aha or the end state: every policy has winners and costs; a closing card shows a real twist: in California, almond growers pay beekeepers to bring their hives, so a benefit can be counted when people bargain.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): set the tax rate as a class vote.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.8.6-8: the hidden costs and benefits shown on the meters, and how each policy changes market outcomes.
  - D2.Eco.1.6-8: family cards show how each choice affects individuals, businesses and the town.
- Not like: border-trade-post (trade policy between countries); concert-ticket-market (grade 12 supply and demand); the-shrinking-lake (environmental change over decades).
- Art: generated scenes of the town and people (illustrated) for the families, because the people bearing the cost must be visible.
