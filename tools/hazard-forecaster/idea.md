# Hazard Forecaster (idea card)

- Module: science-8-natural-hazards-and-human-impact, grade 8. Codes: MS-ESS3-2, MS-ESS3-3, MS-ESS3-4, MS-ESS3-5
- Format / family: data / none. Fit: good for MS-ESS3-2 only
- One idea: Filter real records of earthquakes, hurricanes and wildfires by place, season and size to find where and when each strikes, place a few warning sensors where the patterns point, then test them against years the data held back.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a map of North America with three hazard layers and a question for each ("Where do big earthquakes happen?", "When is hurricane season?", "Where do fires spread most?").
  - Core loop: filter by place, month, size and decade; charts update beside the map. Write a pattern statement for each hazard. Then place a limited number of sensors and warning sirens; the tool replays held-back years and scores how many events were caught early and how much warning time people would have had.
  - The aha or the end state: some hazards can be forecast (hurricane tracks, fire weather) and some only mapped by risk (earthquakes), and warnings plus building choices save lives. The human side: short, factual accounts of how early warnings helped real communities.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare sensor plans.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-ESS3-2: the student analyzes real hazard data to forecast future events and places warning technology to reduce their effects.
  - MOVE MS-ESS3-3, MS-ESS3-4: new tool: "Town Footprint" (grade 8), watch a town's water, land and energy use grow with its population and spending, then design a way to monitor and shrink one impact.
  - MOVE MS-ESS3-5: new tool: "Warming Evidence Desk" (grade 8), ask questions of real records (temperature, carbon dioxide, the sun's output, volcanoes) to sort out which factors explain the rise in global temperature over the past century.
- Not like: climate-dashboard (grade 9 trends); population-dashboard (grade 12 footprints); town-planner-disaster-ready (grade 4 hazards on a budget).
- Art: maps and charts drawn in code; generated scenes for the hazard cards.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Filter real earthquake, hurricane and wildfire records by place, season and size, place a few warning sensors where the patterns point, then test them against years the data held back.
- Flags: needs a data source (USGS earthquake catalog, NOAA hurricane tracks, US wildfire records, checked); sensitive (deadly disasters; accounts stay factual and careful, rule 5)
