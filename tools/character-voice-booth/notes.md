## What it teaches
Students keep a line's words fixed while changing pitch, speed, loudness and pauses to suggest a character or feeling. They listen, compare evidence and revise one vocal choice at a time.

## Key skills
- Make a deliberate voice choice for a character.
- Read changes in a voiceprint and connect them to what you hear.
- Keep three choices steady while changing one.
- Plan, perform, listen and refine a take.
- Describe a partner's performance with evidence.

## Support at home
- Say “Who left this door open?” in two character voices. Ask your child which voice choice changed.
- Take turns guessing a feeling from the same sentence. Explain what you heard, then try again.
- Try a fair test: change only pauses while keeping the other voice choices steady.

## Five-minute start
Open Booth and record My usual voice. Keep the line, choose Tiny mouse and record again. Tap Compare, choose the two takes and tap Play A then B. Name one difference you can hear and see. Without a microphone, open Plan, move a dial and tap Perform it.

## Modes
Booth records up to eight seconds after a silent count-in. The latest My usual voice take sets the baseline; otherwise the first take does. Up to six takes stay in memory. A seventh removes the oldest take that is not the baseline. Change one thing asks for a usual take followed by a take changing one named dial.

Compare overlays two takes on a shared chart. Solid A and dashed B retain their take numbers. Mystery listener shuffles and hides the take identities. Make guesses before Reveal. Its result is temporary.

Plan works with no mic. Five notches per dial reshape a dotted ghost print immediately. Perform it opens a large performance card. After mic access has succeeded in Booth, Record to check overlays a measured take with the plan.

For the live guessing task, the listener looks away from the screen. The performance card keeps the prop and four choices visible for the performer.

Plan uses 2.5 words a second as its usual speed, including when checking a recording. Pitch and loudness use the current baseline. A plan outside the chart's pitch range is labeled at the chart edge.

## Talk questions
- Which change made the character easiest to recognize? Point to the print and listen again.
- Can you change loudness without also changing pitch?
- What would you revise after a partner guessed differently?

## Good to know
Audio stays in this page's memory. There is no upload, download, audio storage or cross-device transfer. Closing the page, Clear all takes and Delete discard the buffers. The microphone track stops after every take. Narration and Read pause during capture.

Pitch is relative speech pitch, not a musical note. These are approximate measurements with tool-chosen thresholds. Speech below 70 Hz, above 700 Hz, very soft speech and unclear or noisy frames may be missed. A take needs at least half a second of voiced frames. Speed assumes you said every word in the chosen line; the tool does not recognize words. Loudness is relative to your baseline and microphone position, not calibrated sound pressure.

The four-word line “Shh. Something is coming.” has only three gaps between words. If you request four pauses, the ghost shows three and labels that limit. Choose a line with at least five words to plan four internal pauses. This is a specification conflict documented in handoff.md.

Use a comfortable voice. Stand at the same mic distance when comparing. Permission, secure browser context and the hosting iframe's microphone permission affect recording. Plan remains available. If the embedded page blocks recording, open the standalone tool full screen.

The activity teaches NCAS TH:Cr2 and TH:Cr3 through planning and revising, TH:Pr4 and TH:Pr5 through repeated vocal choices and fair tests, and TH:Re7 through comparing takes and responding as a mystery listener.
