arc: explore-first
minutes: 50
focus: HS-PS2-4
goal: I can use Newton's law of gravitation and Coulomb's law to compare gravitational and electric fields, and explain why both fall off as one over r squared.
materials: The tool on the projector and on student devices. Calculators.
grouping: Pairs on devices, with whole-class pauses.
---
## Before class

- Open **Twin boards**. It loads the **Opposite pair**.
- Board units are invented: both boards use field = size / r² with the constant 1, so the two pictures compare directly.
- The Real pairs mode uses real constants (NIST and NASA values).

## Hook (5 min)

1. Show the **Opposite pair**: two "m 3" discs on the gravity board with every line pointing in, and a +3 and a −3 on the electric board. "The objects are the same size. Why are the pictures different?"
2. Take ideas, then: "Charge has two signs. Mass has one. Today you find what else is the same and what's different."

## Explore (17 min)

1. Pairs press **Single**, then **Halve distance** and **Double distance**. "What happens to both readouts?" (Twice as far, a quarter of the field.)
2. Pairs press **Like pair**. The probe at the midpoint reads 0.00 on both boards. They press **Release probe** and watch.
3. Pairs turn on **Arrow grid** and **Strength shading** and find where the fields cancel. "Is there such a spot on the Opposite pair electric board?"
4. Pairs switch to **Spread** and drag the distance to 2, then 3. The same 36 lines cover 4, then 9 tiles. They turn on **Show one over r** to compare.

## Make sense (10 min)

1. Write both laws side by side: F = Gm₁m₂ / r² and F = kq₁q₂ / r². "What's the same? What's different?"
2. "Why does doubling the distance cut the lines per tile to a quarter, not a half?" (They spread over an area that grows as r².)
3. "The gravity board always looks like an electric board where every charge is negative. Why can gravity never push?"

## Apply (13 min)

1. Pairs switch to **Real pairs**. Compare the hydrogen atom (electric about 2.27 × 10³⁹ times stronger) with Earth and Moon (gravity wins).
2. Pairs use **Double** and **Halve distance** and notice the ratio line never changes. "Why?"
3. Pairs calculate the force between two 1 kg balls 1 m apart, each with 1 μC, and check the tool.
4. Discuss: "The electric force is about 10³⁹ times gravity in an atom, yet gravity holds the Moon in orbit. Why?" (Matter is almost neutral, so charges cancel; mass only adds up.)

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: "Two charges are 2 m apart. They move to 6 m apart. What happens to the force?" (It becomes 1/9.) Then: "Name one way gravitational and electric forces are alike and one way they differ."

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** stay with **Single** and the readouts, and keep a table of distance and field.
- **Support:** use the keyboard to move the probe in steady 0.25-unit steps.
- **Stretch:** in the **Opposite pair**, why does a perfectly centered probe never land? Nudge it 0.75 units and try again.
- **Stretch:** why can the apple not be moved closer than Earth's surface in **Real pairs**?

## Short on time

- **Halve distance** and **Double distance** in **Twin boards** (8 min).
- **Spread** (6 min), then the exit card (5 min).
