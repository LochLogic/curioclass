# Cooler Challenge (idea card)

- Module: science-6-thermal-energy, grade 6. Codes: MS-PS3-3, MS-PS3-4, MS-PS3-5
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good for MS-PS3-3 and MS-PS3-4
- One idea: Build a lunchbox cooler from real materials and choose what cold packs go inside, then test it in a hot car; the temperature graph shows that heat always moves from warm to cool, that layers only slow it, and that the mass and kind of cold pack change how long lunch stays cold.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an empty lunchbox shell, shelves of materials (foil, foam, cotton, cardboard, a sealed air gap) and cold packs (a frozen water bottle, a gel pack, a steel "ice cube", frozen grapes), each with a mass and a price.
  - Core loop: choose layers (each slows conduction, convection or radiation, shown with arrows) and cold packs within a budget and a thickness limit, then run a four-hour hot-car test; the lunch temperature graphs live. A test bench beside it asks the student to plan fair tests: 200 g against 400 g of frozen water, or frozen water against steel of the same mass, changing one thing at a time.
  - The aha or the end state: insulation never stops heat, it only slows it, and more mass and water's large heat capacity buy time; the student writes which change mattered most, with the graph as evidence.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a class board of minutes cold per dollar.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-PS3-3: design, build, test and improve a device that slows heat transfer, judged by the graph.
  - MS-PS3-4: the test bench has the student plan investigations of energy transferred, type of matter, mass and temperature change.
  - MOVE MS-PS3-5: skate-park-energy (proposed title Half-Pipe Energy), where friction turns the skater's lost kinetic energy into thermal energy in the wheels and ramp.
- Not like: hot-meets-cold (grade 11 final temperatures); hand-warmer-lab (grade 10 reactions); design-challenge-studio (grade 12 open design).
- Art: generated scenes for the lunchbox, materials and the hot car; heat arrows and graphs drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Build a lunchbox cooler from insulation and pick the cold packs inside, then run a hot-car test; the graph shows how layers slow heat and how mass and material change how fast lunch warms.
- Flags: none
