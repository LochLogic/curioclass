## What it teaches
Students locate a fictional smoke by solving a triangle from two azimuth readings and a known baseline. They use the law of cosines to confirm the location, distinguish ambiguous SSA cases, and calculate an area from two sides and their included angle.

## Key skills
- Turn compass directions into angles inside a triangle.
- Pair each side with its opposite angle in the law of sines.
- Use the included angle in the law of cosines.
- Find zero, one, or two locations consistent with a radio reading.
- Convert a triangle's area from square miles to acres.

## Support at home
- Point to two landmarks from one place. Talk about the angle between the two directions.
- Sketch two circles and a ray on scrap paper. Try making zero, one, and two intersections.
- Fold a rectangular sheet in half diagonally. Discuss why a right triangle has half the rectangle's area.

## Five-minute start
1. In Spot, tap a clearing and describe the triangle that appears.
2. Open Show the law of sines. Identify the angle opposite AF.
3. Turn on Tower C check. Follow the circle through the smoke.
4. In Dispatch, choose Smoke 1. Aim both sight handles and calculate the angles.
5. Compare a worked example with a fresh New smoke, without opening Show me.

## Modes
Spot is open exploration with rounded azimuths. A valid smoke snaps slightly to the intersection of those readings, so the diagram and calculations agree. Near a map boundary, the nearest valid intersection from neighboring whole-degree readings keeps the smoke on the map. A smoke on the A-B line has no usable triangle.

Dispatch requires both sights, all three angles, AF, and CF before the engine leaves. The check uses unrounded values and allows 0.05 miles of rounding error. After two misses in one step, Show me fills a worked example. Two rejected checks anywhere in a run, using Show me, or reusing a triangle already explored in Spot during this session makes the whole run guided and earns no saved check mark. Restarting the same guided triangle in this session keeps it guided; choose a fresh case for independent practice. Students can still finish the survey and send the crew. A check mark is a completion convenience, not an assessment score.

Radio compares distances 9, 7, and 6 miles with a 7-mile perpendicular height. The 9-mile circle meets A's forward ray twice; 7 miles makes a tangent; 6 miles has no intersection. Pin each solution after choosing the count. Choosing every count does not finish the map task, and there is no radio score. A wrong count or two missed map pins labels the result a guided comparison. The completed two-spot case marks the farther location that fits the lake clue.

Burn area uses half the product of two sides and the sine of their included angle. Convert the unrounded area before rounding acres. Burns 1, 2, and 3 have acute, obtuse, and right included angles. After two rejected checks or Show me, feedback identifies guided practice. Editing a checked answer clears its result immediately; since the solution has already been exposed, further checks of that case remain guided.

## Talk questions
- Why does B use 250 degrees for its direction back to A?
- Why can a second distance still leave two possible locations?
- What extra information resolves Call 1?
- Why is multiplying the rounded square miles by 640 sometimes slightly different?

## Good to know
All locations, crews, and incidents are fictional. This is a two-dimensional surveying model with equal scales in both directions, not operational fire guidance. No inventor or invention date is claimed. Only completion check marks are stored, locally on this device; Clear marks removes them.

Keyboard: Tab reaches mode and panel controls before the map sights. A and B sights turn one degree with arrows, ten with Shift. In Spot and Radio, focus the map, move its crosshair by 0.1 mile with arrows or 1 mile with Shift, then press Enter. Sound and Motion are kit controls. Read speaks the current hint. No recorded audio is supplied; narration uses the available browser voice.

Sources: OpenStax, Algebra and Trigonometry 2e, sections 10.1 and 10.2; NIST Handbook 44, Appendix C; Common Core G-SRT.D.9 to D.11. Independent numerical calculations are retained in the build scratch folder.

Named references: [OpenStax 10.1, sines, SSA and triangle area](https://openstax.org/books/algebra-and-trigonometry-2e/pages/10-1-non-right-triangles-law-of-sines), [OpenStax 10.2, cosines](https://openstax.org/books/algebra-and-trigonometry-2e/pages/10-2-non-right-triangles-law-of-cosines), [NIST Handbook 44, 2026 Appendix C, area units](https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2025/12/30/appc-26-HB44-20251222.pdf), and [USDA Forest Service, Firefighter Math, Chapter 6, Location](https://www.fs.usda.gov/t-d/pubs/pdf/00511802.pdf). Azimuth is clockwise from north; crossing two lookout readings locates the modeled smoke.

Coverage limit: these activities apply the formulas. They do not derive the area formula or prove the laws, so they do not cover the derivation and proof parts of G-SRT.D.9 and D.10.
