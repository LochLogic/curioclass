# Day in the Life (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-3-communities-around-the-world, grade 3. Codes: D2.Geo.4.3-5, D2.Geo.6.3-5
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Spend one school day with a kid in Kenya, Peru, Japan and Norway, seeing how their family's ways of building, farming, dressing and getting around fit the land and weather, then zoom out to a map of where people live in each country and find out why most live where they do.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: four kids wave from four postcards. "Pick a friend to spend the day with."
  - Core loop: a morning-to-night story in each place: the trip to school, lunch, after-school chores and play. At three moments the student taps a "Why like this?" button (a house built for winter dark in northern Norway, terraces on a steep Andes slope near Cusco, a train under the mountains in Japan) and hears how culture and land shaped that choice.
  - Where people live: each day ends on a population dot map; the student guesses where most people live, then learns why (Norway's people mostly in the milder south and along the coast, Japan's on coastal plains between mountains).
  - The aha or the end state: a comparison chart across all four friends: weather, land, how people adapted, and where they cluster.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): add your own day as a fifth column.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.4.3-5: "Why like this?" moments show culture shaping how people adapt to and change their land.
  - D2.Geo.6.3-5: the dot map shows how environment and culture shape where people live in each country.
- Not like: festival-parade (grade 1 celebrations); pen-pal-parcels (products and trade); region-compare (grade 6 data).
- Art: people (illustrated) and generated scenes, because each friend and place must be specific and true, not generic.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Spend a school day with kids in Kenya, Peru, Japan and Norway, seeing how homes, food and travel fit the land and weather, then find on a population map where most people live in each country and why.
- Flags: sensitive (portraying real cultures without stereotype; fresh review agent per rule 5); needs a data source (population distribution maps, checked facts per place)
