# Dialogue Director (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-narrative-writing, grade 3. Codes: W.3.3, L.3.2
- Format / family: studio / comic-maker. Fit: good.
- One idea: The student makes a short comic story with speech bubbles, then turns it into prose: every bubble becomes a quoted line with the right commas and quotation marks, and every picture becomes narration with actions, thoughts and time words, so they see what a written story has to say in words that a comic shows in pictures.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a director's chair and a four-panel strip ready to fill: pick two characters and a setting from illustrated sets (a lost dog at a farmers' market, a science fair mix-up, a snow day).
  - Core loop: build the strip: pose the characters, type into bubbles, add a title (capitals checked). Then the "prose take": each panel sits beside a blank page; the student writes narration for what the picture showed (who did what, what they felt, then, after that, finally) and moves each bubble in as dialogue. A punctuation helper marks where commas, quotation marks and capitals go, and checks possessives on names (Rosa's dog).
  - The aha or the end state: a listener with eyes covered hears only the prose; if an action from a picture was never written, the listener asks "Wait, what happened?" until the story stands without pictures and reaches an ending.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): print the strip and the prose side by side.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.3.3: the prose take: situation and characters, events in order, dialogue, actions, thoughts and feelings, time words and a closing.
  - L.3.2: commas and quotation marks in dialogue, capitals in the title, possessives in names, checked line by line.
  - MOVE L.3.2 (commas in addresses, spelling patterns and suffixes, checking spelling in a dictionary): to draft-car-wash's Polish station at its grade 3 level.
- Not like: my-story-strip (grade k, draw and tell); two-sides-theater (grade 2, point of view); show-don-t-tell (grade 4, feelings through details).
- Art: people (illustrated) characters in several poses plus generated scene backdrops, because a comic is only as good as its panels.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Make a four-panel comic story, then rewrite it as prose: bubbles become quoted dialogue with correct commas and quotation marks, pictures become narration, and a listener who can't see the panels asks about anything left out.
- Flags: none
