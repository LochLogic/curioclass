arc: model-then-make
minutes: 40
focus: W.2.2
goal: I can write a how-to paragraph with a top bun, fillings and a bottom bun.
materials: The tool on the big screen. One sheet of paper per child, folded into five strips (or a printed burger outline). Crayons.
grouping: Whole class on the carpet for the model, then at tables for writing.
---
## Before class

- Open the tool with **Dogs** picked. Sound is on.
- Fold a paper into five strips and label them: top bun, three fillings, bottom bun. This is your example.
- On the board, list things the class knows how to do: brush your teeth, make a sandwich, wash your hands, feed a pet.

## Hook (4 min)

1. "What do you need to make a hamburger?" Draw it as they answer: bun, meat, lettuce, cheese, bun.
2. "What happens if you forget the top bun? Or put a boot in the middle?" (It falls apart. You can't eat it.)
3. "A paragraph is built just like a burger. Today you will build one."

## Model (12 min)

1. With **Dogs** on screen, read each sentence card together. For each one ask: "Is this the top bun, a filling, the bottom bun, or not about dogs at all?" Click the card, then its place.
2. When a sentence is **Not in this burger**, it turns into a boot. "Why doesn't this one belong?" (It isn't about dogs.)
3. Press **Serve it!** and let the bear react. If it isn't right, fix it together and serve it again.
4. Pick **Plant a seed** and build it the same way. Stop at the fillings: "Does the order matter here?" (Yes. First, next, last. You can't water the seed before you plant it.)
5. Point out the order words: first, next, last. Write them on the board.
6. Open **Write your own** and build one together about washing your hands. Fill the **Top bun**, three fillings and the **Bottom bun** from the class's ideas, then serve it.

## Make (14 min)

1. Each child picks a how-to from the board list (or their own) and writes on the folded paper: top bun first, then three fillings using first, next and last, then the bottom bun.
2. Walk around and ask: "Is every filling about your topic? Read me your top bun."
3. Early finishers draw lettuce, cheese and meat on the strips, and check their work for a boot.

## Share (6 min)

1. Two or three children read their burgers. The class listens: "Which sentence was the top bun? Did anything sound like a boot?"
2. Type one child's paragraph into **Write your own** and press **Serve it!** for the bear.

## Exit check (4 min)

Read a sentence aloud. Children show where it goes: hands on head for a top bun, hands on tummy for a filling, hands on feet for a bottom bun, and a head shake for a boot. Try "First, put toothpaste on the brush." (Tummy.) "Now your teeth are clean and shiny!" (Feet.) "My cat is orange." (Head shake.) "Brushing your teeth is easy." (Head.)

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** give the top bun as a starter ("___ is easy if you follow three steps.") and let children say the fillings to an adult who writes them.
- **Support:** two fillings are enough for a first burger.
- **Stretch:** write a second burger about a topic from **Recess** or **Fall**, with no order words. Does order matter there?
- **Stretch:** a "double burger" with four fillings and a better bottom bun than "That's how you do it."

## Short on time

- Model only **Dogs** and **Plant a seed** (10 min).
- Write one burger together in **Write your own**, with no individual writing (8 min).
- Finish with the head, tummy and feet check (2 min).
