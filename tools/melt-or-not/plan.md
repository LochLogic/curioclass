arc: explore-first
minutes: 40
focus: 2-PS1-4
goal: I can tell if a change from heating or cooling can go back, and use what I saw as my evidence.
materials: The tool on the big screen. One ice cube on a small plate. Optional: a crayon and a cracker for Apply.
grouping: Whole class on the carpet, with turn-and-talk partners.
---
## Before class

- Open the tool so it shows the ice cube. Sound is on; turn the volume up.
- Put an ice cube on a plate where everyone can see it. Don't explain it yet.
- Pick turn-and-talk partners.

## Hook (5 min)

1. Point at the ice cube. "What is happening to our ice cube?" (It's melting.)
2. "When it's all water, could we ever get our ice cube back? How?" Take a few ideas. Don't settle it.
3. "Today we're scientists. We'll change things with heat and cold, then test if the change can go back."

## Explore (15 min)

Every object has four steps, shown by the bar at the top: change it, predict, test, sort.

1. **An ice cube.** Press **Warm it up**. "Can the puddle change back into ice?" Partners talk, then thumbs up for **Can go back**, thumbs down for **Can't go back**. Tap the bin the class picked, then press **Put it in the freezer** to test it.
2. **A slice of bread.** Same steps. Before the test, ask: "If we put the toast in the fridge, will it turn back into bread?" (No. It stays toast.)
3. **A chocolate bar**, **A raw egg** and **A cup of orange juice**. Let partners whisper a prediction before every vote. The juice is cooled first, so its test is to leave it in the sun.
4. When a test doesn't match the vote, the tool says **Surprise!** Stop there: "What did the test show us? Why didn't we expect it?"
5. Keep going with **Next thing**: popcorn, butter, cake batter, clay, paper. For the paper, remind the class that burning is only for grown-ups.

## Make sense (8 min)

1. Look at the **Can go back** bin. "What do these have in common?" (They melted or froze, or just changed shape.)
2. Look at the **Can't go back** bin. "What happened to all of these?" (They were cooked, baked, popped or burned. Something new was made.)
3. Teach the frame for an argument, and model it with chocolate: "I think chocolate **can** go back, because when we cooled it, it turned solid again." The test is the evidence.
4. Partners use the frame for one object each. Call on two or three pairs.

## Apply (7 min)

1. Name an object the tool didn't have. Children stand up if they think the change can go back, and stay sitting if it can't.
2. Use a crayon melted in the sun (can go back: it hardens again, in a new shape), a cracker toasted in a toaster (can't), a snowman melting into a puddle (the water can freeze again, but not into a snowman), and a pancake on a hot pan (can't).
3. After each one: "Who can give me a because?"

## Exit check (5 min)

1. Back to the plate. "Our ice cube is water now. Can it go back? How could we test it?" (Freeze it.) Put the plate in a freezer and check it tomorrow.
2. Each child tells their partner one change that can go back and one that can't, with a because. Listen in on four or five pairs. Those without a because need the frame again tomorrow.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** act the changes out (melt slowly to the floor, freeze stiff). Keep the frame on the board: "I think ___ can / can't go back because ___."
- **Support:** let partners point to the bin on the screen instead of giving a full answer.
- **Stretch:** "The chocolate turned solid, but in a new shape. Did it really go back?" (It is still chocolate, just a new shape.)
- **Stretch:** "Can you think of something that can go back, but only partway?" (The snowman.)

## Short on time

- Hook with the ice cube (2 min), then in the tool test just the ice cube, bread, chocolate and egg (10 min).
- Look at the two bins together (4 min), and end with one thumbs vote on a new object (4 min).
