## A 5-minute start

1. The ice cube is on the plate. Ask: what will happen if we warm it up? Then press **Warm it up**.
2. It melts into a puddle. Now predict: can it change back into ice? Take a class vote and tap a bin.
3. Press **Put it in the freezer** to test it. It freezes again, so it goes in **Can go back**. Then try the bread.

## The big idea

Heating and cooling change things. Some changes **can go back**: melted ice, chocolate and butter turn solid again when they cool, and a clay snake can be rolled back into a ball. Other changes **can't go back**: toast, a cooked egg, a baked cupcake, popped popcorn and burned paper stay changed, no matter how cold they get.

## Talk about it

- What evidence did the test give us? What did we see?
- Look at the **Can go back** bin. What do those changes have in common? (Melting and freezing.)
- Look at **Can't go back**. What happened to all of those? (Cooking, baking, burning.)
- Melted chocolate turns solid again, but in a new shape. Is it still chocolate?

## Good to know

- Each object has four steps: change it, predict, test, and sort. The bar at the top of the panel shows which step you're on.
- You can tap a bin on the picture or the buttons in the panel to predict.
- The tally counts predictions that matched the test. A surprise is fine: that's how science works.
- The examples match the NGSS 2-PS1-4 clarification statement (water and butter at different temperatures, cooking an egg, heating paper).
- Burning paper is only ever done by grown-ups, safely. The tool says so too.
- Sound starts on.
