# Market Day (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-1-goods-and-services, grade 1. Codes: D2.Eco.2.K-2, D2.Eco.3.K-2, D2.Eco.4.K-2
- Format / family: tycoon / market. Fit: change family to none, because the market family engine is built for supply and demand curves at grades 6-12; a grade 1 shopper gains nothing from it and would lose simplicity. Format can stay tycoon (or story).
- One idea: Spend five tokens at a weekend market where every stall is a good or a service, so each choice means giving something else up, and each stall owner shows the skill it took to make or do it and whether it came from here or far away.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a sunny market street, a pouch with five tokens. "Walk to a stall and see what it sells."
  - Core loop: visit stalls (bread, a haircut, apples, a bike fix, bananas, a face painter); the student sorts each as a good (you take it home) or a service (someone does it for you). Buying costs tokens; a "what you gave up" shelf shows the things you can no longer get.
  - Ask the maker: tap a stall owner to hear what they had to learn (the baker learned to knead, the bike fixer learned the tools) and where the product was made: honey from a farm down the road, bananas from far away by truck and ship.
  - The aha or the end state: at home, the day's basket shows benefits and costs: what you got, what you gave up, what came from your town and what came from other places.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): class picks one purchase each round.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.2.K-2: each purchase shows its benefit and the cost of what was given up.
  - D2.Eco.3.K-2: each stall owner tells the skills and knowledge their good or service needs.
  - D2.Eco.4.K-2: local and faraway tags on products, with a small map of where each came from.
- Not like: corner-store (grade 2, making change at your own stand); piggy-bank-planner (saving); teddy-bear-makers (making one product).
- Art: generated scenes and people (illustrated) stall owners, because the human side is the lesson.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: overlap risk (corner-store and piggy-bank-planner share coins and spending; keep this one about goods vs services, trade-offs and who made it)
