# Research packet: market-day
Concept: With five tokens, the class shops a weekend market as consumers; each stall sells a good or offers a service, every choice means giving something else up, and the stall owners explain what they made or did.
Grade: 1   Standards: D2.Eco.2.K-2, D2.Eco.3.K-2, D2.Eco.4.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Eco.2.K-2: "Identify the benefits and costs of making various personal decisions." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain the trade-off and opportunity cost of spending limited tokens at market stalls.
- D2.Eco.3.K-2: "Describe the skills and knowledge required to produce certain goods and services." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Describe the craft skills, tools, and labor that stallholders use to bake goods, weave crafts, or repair items.
- D2.Eco.4.K-2: "Describe the goods and services that people in the local community produce and those that are produced in other communities." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Distinguish between physical goods (apples, pottery) and helpful services (knife sharpening, face painting) at local markets.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing goods with the tools used to provide services: Students often label haircuts or bike tune-ups as "goods" because scissors or wrenches are physical objects, missing that the customer bought the person's time and skill (Source: EconEdLink Basic Economics Concepts).
- Assuming money is inexhaustible: Young children often believe ATM machines or digital cards give out infinite money without realizing funds are limited by earnings (Source: C3 Framework Economics indicators D2.Eco.2.K-2).
- Failing to recognize opportunity cost: First graders often think they can purchase everything if they take their time, not realizing that spending a token on a muffin eliminates the token for the apple (Source: C3 Framework Dimension 2 Scarcity Indicators).
- Assuming individuals are only producers or only consumers: Students struggle to realize that a stallholder who sells honey is also a consumer who buys shoes and groceries (Source: California History-Social Science Content Standards 1.6).

## Sensitivity
- Sensitivity regarding real-life money and shopping: Children have widely differing household income backgrounds. Keep the activity strictly anchored in an equitable classroom token simulation where each child begins with exactly five tokens.
- Respect for local agricultural and craft workers: Emphasize the skill, patience, and dignity involved in farming, baking, and hand-crafting goods.
