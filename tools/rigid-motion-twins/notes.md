## What it teaches

Students map one polygon onto another using slides, turns and flips, then use the sequence as a proof of congruence. They find polygon symmetries and investigate why SSS, SAS and ASA determine a triangle, while SSA and AAA do not prove congruence.

## Key skills

- Describe a move as a function on every point.
- Choose a center, angle or mirror line precisely.
- Connect a motion sequence to matching corners, sides and angles.
- Use a measured unequal length to disprove congruence.
- Distinguish a valid triangle criterion from an ambiguous case.

## Support at home

- Trace an irregular shape on scrap paper, cut it out, and slide, turn or flip it onto the tracing. Talk about what stays the same.
- Look for mirror lines and half-turns in rectangular household objects. Compare a rectangle with a square.
- Make a larger drawing of a triangle. Ask why equal angles alone do not make it the same size.

## Five-minute start

Open Tracks. Drag from any corner of the plaster cast to a grid point. A slide moves every corner by the same amount. Undo and try to land in one move. Read the rule in the move list and the correspondence on the earned proof card.

## Modes

Tracks has ten freely selectable levels. Levels 1 to 8 use the same asymmetric, invented footprint. Level 4 needs a half-grid turn center. Level 5 needs at least two moves because a glide reflection is not a single slide, turn or flip. Level 9 includes five polygons; every new nonidentity symmetry fills the tray, with duplicate motions counted once. All five trays must be complete to earn level 9's two stars. Level 10 requires a pair of unequal corresponding measurements before Not twins can succeed.

Criteria uses exact triangles A(0,0), B(4,0), C(1,3) and D(6,1), E(6,5), F(9,2). Next step slides, turns and flips the source, with given parts marked. Back step retraces them. SSA overlays both solutions for a 40 degree angle, sides AB = 5 and BC = 4. AAA shows a scale factor of two.

## Controls and access

Select Slide, Turn, Flip or Measure. Slide by dragging from a cast vertex, or use the coordinate fields and Apply. For Turn, tap the center, choose the counterclockwise angle and Apply. For Flip, tap two line points, then Apply flip. On Self-twins the center, vertices and edge midpoints are extra snap points. A slanted hexagon mirror can be set using its center and a vertex or midpoint.

Tab to the move buttons or the rock. Arrow keys adjust the slide vector, turn center or first flip point; Shift uses half steps. Alt plus arrows adjusts the second flip point. Enter on the rock applies. Numeric fields and their Apply buttons offer another keyboard route. With Measure selected and the rock focused, left/right chooses a corner, up/down switches footprint and Enter selects. Measure taps also select two corners on one shape, then two on the other. C to D is the changed toe segment in level 10.

Motion off removes movement. Sound controls tones and optional browser narration. Read speaks the current hint. Fixed narration is listed for the gravel-ranger voice, but no audio was recorded in this build. Stars are the only tool work saved, on this device, and Clear progress removes them. ## Talk questions

- Why does a single rule describe every corner of the cast?
- What do a flip and a glide reflection have in common? Why does the latter need two allowed moves?
- Why does knowing which side of DE contains C matter in the SSS construction?
- Which measurements would prove the false twin is impossible to repair?

## Good to know

The fossil shape is a mathematical invention, not an identified dinosaur species. Grid distances are unitless. A landing compares all nine corresponding vertices within 1e-9; there is no proximity score. Symmetry attempts start again from the original polygon. Stars reward a successful sequence, with a second star for the minimum number of allowed moves. Undo removes a move from the current proof, while earned stars remain until cleared.
