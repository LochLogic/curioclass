# Weather Wardrobe (idea card)

- Module: science-k-weather-watch, grade k. Codes: K-ESS2-1, K-ESS3-2
- Format / family: showdown / weather. Fit: change to data, because a solo kindergartner logs weather into a picture calendar and finds patterns, which is a picture-graph data desk and shares the weather engine with climate-postcards; the class vote becomes the extra.
- One idea: Each visit, look out the window, stamp the weather (sun, clouds, rain, snow, wind, hot or cold) and dress a character to match; a month of picture calendar shows patterns, and a storm warning shows why people forecast weather.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a window, a character named Leo in pajamas, and weather stamps. "Look outside. What is the sky doing?"
  - Core loop: stamp today's weather, then dress Leo (coat, boots, sun hat, umbrella); Leo shivers, sweats or smiles. The day goes on the calendar. A student who cannot come back daily plays a real recorded month from a sample city, one day at a time.
  - Pattern step: count stamps in a picture graph ("How many rainy days?") and compare a winter month and a summer month in the same city.
  - Forecast step: a TV forecaster says a big storm is coming; the student picks questions to ask (When? How strong? What should we do?) and helps the family get ready (bring in toys, find flashlights, stay inside). The storm comes and Leo is safe.
  - The aha or the end state: the calendar and graph show more cold, snowy days in winter and more hot, sunny days in summer, and that forecasts help people get ready.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): showdown mode where the class votes on the stamp and Leo's outfit.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - K-ESS2-1: daily stamps and the month graph describe weather patterns over time, from the student's own window and from real recorded months.
  - K-ESS3-2: the forecast step, asking what forecasts are for and preparing for severe weather.
- Not like: climate-postcards (grade 3 climates around the world, same engine); sky-diary (sun and moon patterns, not weather).
- Art: people (illustrated) for Leo, the family and the forecaster; generated scenes for the window in each kind of weather.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Look outside and stamp today's weather, dress a character to match, and watch a picture calendar show patterns over the months; a storm forecast shows how people use forecasts to get ready.
- Flags: needs a data source (real daily weather for a few sample cities, for example NOAA daily summaries); the calendar is saved on the device, and the screen says it never leaves the device.
