## What it teaches

Students diagnose and revise a poster using balance, contrast, emphasis, rhythm and unity. They can explain how a change in position, size, color or type changes the visual message, and compare their repair with the starting layout.

## Key skills

- Notice what attracts attention first and compare that with a poster's purpose.
- Move visual weight to make a layout feel steady, including an asymmetric layout.
- Improve text readability using size, weight and foreground/background contrast.
- Make repeated spaces, type choices, colors and left edges more consistent.
- Revise against a brief and explain what improved in a before-and-after comparison.

## Support at home

- Look at a flyer already at home. Ask what you notice first and whether that is the information you need.
- Fold a scrap of paper into a pretend poster. Move a few small household objects on it to explore a steady layout.
- Find repeated spaces or aligned edges on a food package. Discuss how they help you follow the information.

## A five-minute start

Open Studio on Bake Sale. Tap Squint, look at the numbered eye path, then drag the cupcake across the page while watching the balance seesaw. Tap Squint again to return to a sharp view. Try making one small part larger and explain what changed.

Switch to Fix. Read the client's note, repair the first poster until its goal ticks, and open Before and after. Use the wipe to point out one intentional change.

## Modes

Studio opens any of the ten broken posters with all meters live and no goals. Reset poster reloads that starting layout. Studio edits are not saved.

Fix offers ten freely accessible briefs: two balance, two contrast, two emphasis, two rhythm, one unity and a final mixed brief. Previous, Next poster and the Poster menu never lock. Start over restores the current broken layout. Only earned poster completions are saved on this device, under `design-fixer-solved`; Clear progress removes them. The layout itself is not saved. A Fixed stamp stays for the current attempt after a goal becomes unmet, so a student can keep experimenting after earning it.

## Controls

Tap a part to select it, or choose its name from the part menu above Bigger. The menu also reaches small or overlapping parts. Drag to move it, pull its corner to change width and height, use Bigger or Smaller for proportional changes, or choose a color swatch. Bold and Font apply to text. Bring forward and Send back move the selected part one layer. Guides snap edges and centers within four screen pixels; turn them off for free placement. Selecting a bin, schedule line or star also shows the measured gaps on the poster while Guides is on.

Tab visits the panel before poster elements, then moves through those elements in top-to-bottom reading order. Arrow keys move a selected part by 2 poster units; Shift plus an arrow moves 20. Plus and minus resize by 5 percent. Escape deselects. The wipe is draggable and supports Left, Right, Shift plus arrows, Home and End; Escape closes it.

Squint runs from sharp to a 12-pixel blur with up to 70 percent grayscale. At 3 or higher the three strongest parts receive numbered badges. A tie within 2 percent shares a rank. Editing remains available while squinting.

## Talk questions

- Where should the eye land first for this client's purpose? What changed that order?
- Can a poster be steady without being symmetrical? Use Biscuit as evidence.
- Does a meter's tick always mean the layout communicates well? What does the model miss?
- Compare two repairs of the same poster on neighboring laptops. Which choices support the same intent differently?
- Which revision helped you interpret the message, rather than simply decorating it?

## Good to know

The meters are models, not aesthetic laws or an assessment of artistic worth. Pull is square root of bounding-box area times a CIELAB lightness/chroma term; visual weight uses bounding-box area times that term. Balance is the weighted horizontal center, with an 8 percent tolerance. Empty parts inside an icon are not subtracted from its bounding-box area.

Text contrast uses WCAG sRGB luminance. Large text needs 3:1 and other text needs 4.5:1. The teaching model defines large cap height as 3 percent of the 1414-unit page, or 2.4 percent when bold. Letter shapes keep their natural proportions within each editable text box; the threshold uses the actual fitted cap height. The pull and weight models still use the editable box area. Where parts overlap, contrast samples the shapes and text behind the foreground glyphs, and flags text covered by a later layer. Bring that text forward or move it clear. This is a finite sampling model, not a full glyph-by-glyph accessibility audit.

Rhythm measures edge-to-edge gaps along each repeated set; positive gaps are even when their spread is within 10 percent of the mean. The Talent Show stars must also share a row within four units. Recycling bins must match in width and height within 5 percent of the mean. Unity counts type families, ignores bold variations, excludes black, white and the paper color from color count, and groups text left edges within four units.

At narrow phone sizes the poster and Squint come first, followed by meters and tools. Small previews keep the meters beside the poster; the editor scrolls in its panel. At school-laptop sizes the selected tools and meters fit together. Motion off removes the Squint easing. Sound controls narration and subtle editing tones; Read speaks the current hint, including with Sound off. Narration uses the browser voice until recordings are supplied; the manifest specifies big-sister, Leda.

## Standards and sources

NCAS Visual Arts VA:Cr1 is practiced when choosing what a poster should emphasize; VA:Cr2 through arranging its parts; VA:Cr3 through repeated revision against the brief; VA:Re8 through interpreting the client's intended message and comparing it with the visible hierarchy.
