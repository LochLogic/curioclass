## What it teaches

Students can ask questions that uncover a user's needs, turn that evidence into design choices, and revise an app after watching someone try a task. They compare perspectives and find features that help several users.

## Key skills

- Ask open questions and useful follow-ups.
- Separate a person's experience from an answer suggested by your question.
- Choose features from evidence while working within a limit.
- Observe a task test, find a snag, and try a revised design.
- Include people with different language, reading, memory, and tapping needs.

## Support at home

- Ask someone to tell you about the last time a household task was frustrating. Listen before suggesting a fix.
- Sketch a better reminder or instruction on scrap paper, then ask that person to try it.
- Look for a feature in your home that helps more than one person. Ask who else it might help.

## A 5-minute start

Open Interview and meet Lou. Ask about his last app and the hardest part of using a tablet. Ask the class what evidence belongs in the design. Choose Big text and Big buttons far apart, then run the test. Remove Big buttons and test again to see step 4 change.

## Modes

Interview gives each friend time for four questions and a phone with three feature slots. All three tabs stay open. Select a feature and tap an empty slot, or drag a shelf card to the phone. Tap a placed feature to remove it. Each friend keeps separate work until the page closes. Start over with a friend clears that friend's interview and prototype.

One App for All offers four slots. Test the same task for Lou, Ana, then Kofi. The portrait and quote above the table show who is trying each step and what happens. Needs are real for the test even if you skipped the interviews. Start test drive restarts; any feature or slot change stops the current run. Opening the Needs board stops a running test so the evidence stays still while you read. Motion off removes movement but keeps the four-second test time for each friend.

The task is to sign up to water your plot on Saturday and remember to go. The five steps are Read home, Find plot, Pick Saturday, Tap Save, and Remember. This is a simulated app, with a limited set of needs and features, rather than a coded app or an actual garden service.

## Talk about it

- What did an open question reveal that a yes or no question did not?
- Which note seemed useful until the task test? What changed your mind?
- Can a feature be unused in this task and still be useful for another task?
- Which features helped two friends? Why does including more people improve the design?
- In pairs, swap who asks questions and who takes notes when you change friends.

## Good to know

The only successful four-feature app is Read aloud, Big buttons far apart, English and Spanish switch, and Reminder you set. No three-feature set works for everyone. With five slots, eight different sets would work. Read aloud helps Lou and Ana; Big buttons helps Lou and Kofi. These slot limits belong to the activity, not to real app development.

A leading question suggests its own answer. Wishes and needs deliberately share a yellow note style. A wish is stamped Polite yes only after its matching feature is placed and tested. Blue cards hold background facts. Weather forecast is not needed in this particular task. Never used means unused in this task, not worthless everywhere.

Lou, Ana, Kofi, and Maple Street Garden are made up. Their needs are individual, not claims about everyone of an age or language. Ana's mom reads Spanish best; the snag includes its English meaning. The three existing portraits are retained unchanged. The garden and plants use generated illustrations; the cold review redrew kohlrabi and cleaned all six plant cutouts.

Work stays in the open page and disappears when it closes. Only the shared Motion preference is remembered on this device. The tool collects nothing and works offline. Sound toggles narration and tones; Read speaks the current hint even when Sound is off. Narration uses the browser until recordings are supplied.

## Standards and sources

E5-ALG-IM-03 is practiced by planning, testing, and changing the prototype. E5-SOC-HU-18 is practiced by including different users' needs and testing a shared design. The 2017 crosswalk is 1B-AP-13, 1B-IC-19, and 1B-IC-20.

Interview practice: Nielsen Norman Group, [Interviewing Users](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/interviewing-users/).
Shared accessibility benefits: Angela Glover Blackwell, [The Curb-Cut Effect](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_curb_cut_effect), Winter 2017.
Tap targets: W3C, [Understanding WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).
Iteration: IDEO.org, [The Field Guide to Human-Centered Design](https://www.designkit.org/resources/1.html), 2015.
Standards: [CSTA K-12 Computer Science Standards](https://csteachers.org/k12standards/), 2017 crosswalk.
