## What it teaches

Students turn differences in class results into testable questions, hold other settings steady, repeat measurements,
and use their own means to support a claim. They collect evidence that magnetic and electric forces act across empty space.

## Key skills

- Notice when two tests change more than one thing.
- Plan a comparison that changes only one setting.
- Use three trials and a mean rather than one lucky result.
- Compare ratios and notice a curve that levels off.
- Use an off check and a compass to investigate an invisible field.

## Support at home

- Compare two everyday objects and name all the differences. Discuss which one you would change first in a fair test.
- If a fridge magnet is already at home, compare its pull close to and farther from a steel surface. Keep small magnets away from young children.
- Ask your child why something sticking by touch does not yet show a force acting across empty space.

## A five-minute start

Open Coil. Compare Groups 1 and 2, add Turns, and select that question. Test 10, 20 and 40 turns with one battery,
steel and no gap. Read the three trials and means. The default 10-turn count is too small for the largest ratio:
the student may compare 20 to 40, or collect a stronger fair set at two batteries to compare 10 to 40.

## Modes

Coil starts from four class results, then investigates turns, batteries, core and gap. Each Test produces three trials.
Only rows with the other settings held steady join the active question's chart. Retesting replaces a row.
Static compares Balloon A and B, then investigates rubbing and gap. Four rubbing values, including 40, are needed
to see the leveling off. Numeric chart axes use the actual spacing between settings; Core uses categories.
Field uses an off/on/off/flip sequence and fresh filings. Drag the compass, or focus it
and press arrow keys. A drops an arrow. Notebook evaluates three flawed experiments and shows the fair alternative.
Short Field previews have a Coil settings button. Change the shared turns, batteries or core in that dialog,
then Close to return to mapping. Full laptop layouts keep those settings alongside the paper.
Sprinkle with power on before a flip and again after it. If the first sprinkle comes late, use it as the new
baseline, flip once more, and sprinkle again. Only an observed flip at the same position and coil settings earns
the reversal check. A weak setup gets a hint to strengthen it rather than being credited for a small turn.

## Talk about it

Why can Groups 1 and 2 not isolate turns? Why is aluminum different from steel here? Why do we repeat trials?
What does a reversed needle tell you that unmarked filings do not? Why do scissors near a compass matter?

## Good to know

This is an idealized model, labeled MODEL on the benches. Clip counts, bit counts, core multiplier, trial spread,
gap factors and the 2.5 ohm safety resistor are model choices. They do not predict a particular real apparatus.
The model current is cells times 1.5 volts divided by 2.5 ohms, with wire and battery resistance omitted.
The coil's internal field scales with turns and current. Real lifting force and clip count need not be proportional
to either; the proportional clip rule here is a teaching model. Non-steel coils do make fields, but this model
rounds their clip-lifting counts to zero. A zero count does not establish an absent field.

Real coils run hot if connected straight to a battery. Switch off between tests. This screen is not wiring guidance.
Steel can retain some magnetism. An unpowered steel nail may tug a nearby compass slightly, which the field model omits.
The modeled scissors in Sam's third case illustrate that other steel objects can confound an on-only check.
The Field paper's north is magnetic north, an idealized local horizontal direction. Filings show the coil component's
shape, while the compass includes a one-unit Earth field. This separates shape from direction without adding vector calculations.
Rub saturation is modeled by 1 minus exp of negative rubs divided by 10; real materials, humidity and handling matter.
The damp sponge neutralizes the balloon between the modeled trials.

Results are the only investigation data saved, in localStorage curio-emlab with guarded access. Boards and first answers
last only for this page session. Your results stay on this device. Clear table clears both benches and boards after confirmation.
There is no overall grade or mastery score. A corrected first answer remains labeled tested, fixed or found, fixed.
