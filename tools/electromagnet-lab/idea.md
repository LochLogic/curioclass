# Electromagnet Lab (idea card)

- Module: science-8-electric-and-magnetic-forces, grade 8. Codes: MS-PS2-3, MS-PS2-5
- Format / family: lab / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Build an electromagnet, ask which changes make it pull harder (coil turns, batteries, core, distance), test each one, then map its invisible field with a compass probe and iron filings and compare a charged balloon's pull on bits of paper.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a scrapyard crane lifting a car with an electromagnet, then a bench with wire, a nail, batteries, paper clips and a ruler.
  - Core loop: the student first writes or picks questions on a question board ("Does more turns mean more clips?"). Each test changes one thing and counts the paper clips lifted at a set distance; results build into a table and graph. A static bench does the same with a rubbed balloon: more rubbing and less distance mean more pull.
  - Field bench: drag a compass around the coil to draw arrows, sprinkle filings to see the pattern, and switch the current off to watch it vanish. The student then reviews a sample experiment with a flaw (two things changed at once) and fixes the design.
  - The aha or the end state: the strength of electric and magnetic forces depends on current, turns, core, charge and distance, and fields act across space without touching.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-PS2-3: the question board and the tests have the student ask questions about data on what affects electric and magnetic force strength.
  - MS-PS2-5: the field bench and the flawed-design review give evidence that fields exist between objects that are not touching, and practice evaluating the experiment.
- Not like: field-lines (grade 11 field maps for gravity and charge); circuit-builder (grade 11 circuits); secret-code-signals (grade 4 signals).
- Art: bench and field drawn in code; a generated scene of the scrapyard crane as the opening hook.
- Proposed title: keep

- Flags: none
