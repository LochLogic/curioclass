# Syllable Surgeon (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-multisyllable-decoding, grade 3. Codes: RF.3.3, L.3.4
- Format / family: puzzle / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Long words arrive at a word clinic too tangled to read, and the child operates: snip off the prefix and suffix, split the middle into syllables by their vowel patterns, read each piece, then stitch the word back together and read it whole, so they learn a step-by-step way to read any long word.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a cheerful clinic and a patient word on the table (unbelievable). "This word can't be read aloud yet. Let's operate."
  - Core loop: tools: a prefix clip, a suffix clip, a syllable scalpel. Snip un- and -able, split be-liev, read each part, then stitch and read. A meaning check asks what the prefix and suffix did to the word (-ful, full of; -less, without). Latin suffix ward (-tion, -sion, -ment, -ous, -ible). Rule-breaker ward: irregular words (laugh, through, island) cannot be cut by the rules and go to a "learn whole" chart.
  - Root-family ward (core): a root word sits in the middle of its family (company, companion, accompany; act, action, react); the child snips each family member back to the root, says what the root means, and uses it to work out a new family word in a sentence.
  - The aha or the end state: the child reads a short paragraph packed with long words that seemed impossible at the start.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): teacher word lists.
- Standards, one line per code: RF.3.3: meanings of common prefixes and derivational suffixes; decoding words with Latin suffixes; decoding multisyllable words; reading irregularly spelled grade-level words.
  L.3.4 (parts b and c, taken in from idiom-illustrator): the meaning check shows how a known prefix or suffix changes a word (b); the root-family ward uses a known root to figure out an unknown word with the same root (c).
- Not like: Vowel Team Tag (grade 2 vowel teams). Word Lab (grade 2 meaning, not decoding). Root Detective Cards (grade 4 Greek and Latin roots).
- Art: generated scenes (a friendly, not gory, word clinic with word-patient characters), because the clinic should feel playful.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Long words arrive at a word clinic too tangled to read; snip off prefixes and suffixes, split the middle into syllables, read each piece, then stitch the word back together. Rule-breaker words get learned whole.
- Flags: none
