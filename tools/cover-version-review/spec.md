# Cover Version Review

## One idea
A review is only as good as the moment it points to: two made-up performers sing "Simple Gifts" in very different ways, you measure what each changed line by line on a difference strip (tempo by tapping, loudness, instruments, extras), and every claim in your review is pinned to a line you can flip between A and B in one tap.

## Not like
- gallery-critique (9-12, roleplay): pins spoken claims to places on one painting in a debate. Here claims pin to moments in time across two performances, and the evidence is measured by the student first (tapped tempo, heard instruments).
- character-voice-booth (3-5, lab): the student records their own voice and sees a voiceprint. Nothing is recorded here; two given versions are compared.
- music-history-radio (9-12): one tune through eleven eras, listened to as a tour. Here two versions of one song are measured and argued about.
- band-practice (6-8): timing of the student's own taps against a band. Here taps only measure someone else's tempo.
- folk-song-arranger (this band): makes an arrangement and checks it. This tool judges finished versions.
- scene-staging-comparison (this band): two theater productions and reactions. This is music, and the comparison is measured, not felt.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, music, module "Listening and Reviewing (Music)". Topic line: comparing two cover versions.
- Driver: students (headphones). 20 to 35 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS MU:Re8 (interpret intent and meaning), MU:Re9 (apply criteria to evaluate), MU:Cn10 (relate personal preferences to music).
- Header: title, topic line, modes **Listen** (default), **Measure**, **Review**, then kit `motion`, `sound`, `read`.

## Screens and modes

### Layout (all modes)
- Focus (all modes): the difference strip, full stage width, at least 50% of the stage height at 1366x768: eight columns, one per lyric line (the line's words on top), and two rows of lanes, A (June Avery, "Porch") above and B (Ninth Street Band, "Parade") below. Lanes per version: Tempo, Loudness, Instruments, Extras. A painted venue thumbnail sits at the left of each version's row.
- The playhead runs inside the column of the line playing. The current line's words light up word by word (karaoke) in the column head.
- Panel: `hint`, the mode's controls, **A/B** (flip which version is heard at the same line, from the same point in the line), **Play line**, **Play all** (becomes **Stop**), **Plain tune** (the melody as printed, piano, steady, for reference).

### Listen (explore)
- All lanes are filled with the tool's values from the start, so a student can just play, flip and look.
- Tap a column: selects that line. **Play line** plays it in the current version; **A/B** while it plays switches version at the same position in the line at once.

### Measure (the lab)
- The lanes empty for both versions ("?") and become the student's record.
- Tempo: with a line selected, **Play line** and tap **Tap tempo** (or T) along with the beat; after 4 or more taps the lane shows the measured beats a minute. **Clear taps**.
- Loudness: three buttons **soft**, **medium**, **loud** for the selected line and version.
- Instruments: toggles for the six instruments (icons with names: Voice, Guitar, Trumpet, Trombone, Tuba, Snare) for the selected line and version.
- Extras: chips **held note**, **key change**, **added repeat**, **humming**, **shout-back**, **slows down**, **none**.
- **Check my measurements** compares the record with the tool's values (Content) and marks each cell: a check (match), or "off" with the true value shown under it. Tempo matches within 8% (tool's choice). Nothing is scored; the summary says "18 of 24 tempo, loudness and instrument cells match. Tempo is the one you missed most."

### Review
- Focus: the strip stays on top (shrunk to 40%), the review writer under it.
- The writer builds claims from four pickers: **Version** (A, B, both), **Criterion** (Expression, Fits the words, Steady and in tune, Surprise), **Judgment** (works well, works partly, doesn't work), and **Pin to line** (tap a column). The tool then offers the evidence chips available for that pin from the strip (the student's measured values where they match, or the true values after Check), for example "A line 7: tempo 58, held note". A claim needs a pin and one evidence chip to be added; free text (up to 140 characters) finishes the sentence ("because...").
- Each added claim is a card; its pin button plays that line in A then B (or the named version), and the card flashes its column on the strip.
- **My taste** (Cn10): a closing card with two sliders the student sets (I like slow and quiet ... fast and loud; I like it plain ... dressed up) and a sentence frame "I preferred ___, but ___ did ___ better."
- **Copy review** copies the review as plain text (claims, pins, evidence) to the clipboard. Saved on this device.

## Content

### Lyrics (Joseph Brackett, 1848, public domain), one line per column
1. 'Tis the gift to be simple, 'tis the gift to be free,
2. 'Tis the gift to come down where we ought to be,
3. And when we find ourselves in the place just right,
4. 'Twill be in the valley of love and delight.
5. When true simplicity is gain'd,
6. To bow and to bend we shan't be asham'd,
7. To turn, turn will be our delight,
8. Till by turning, turning we come round right.
- Melody: the 1848 Shaker tune, transcribed by the builder from a public-domain printing (Sources) into `MS.parse` note names, 8 beats per line in quarter-note beats. Plain tune plays it at 96.

- A is in D major, a quiet fingerpicked guitar; tempo within a line holds at the listed value except where "slows down" eases to 80% over the last 2 beats. B is in G major (A flat after the key change), steady, snare on beats 2 and 4.

### Teacher note facts (notes.md)
- A "cover" is a new performance or recording of a song first made by someone else. Simple Gifts is a Shaker song from 1848; Aaron Copland used it in Appalachian Spring (1944), which may be named and linked but is not reproduced.

## Interactions in detail
- A/B is instant: both versions are scheduled as two silent-or-live buses from the same line start; flipping crossfades in 30 ms at the same position (the position maps by beat within the line, so A's slower tempo and B's faster one line up by words, not seconds).
- Every control stays live while anything plays: selecting another column jumps playback there at once, A/B flips at once, Stop stops at once; Tap tempo taps are timed from pointerdown or keydown (e.repeat ignored).
- Tap tempo: bpm = 60 / mean interval of the taps in the current line's playback; fewer than 4 taps shows "Tap at least 4 times." Taps more than 1.6 times the median interval apart start a new count (the student lost the beat). Extreme results under 30 or over 240 are discarded with a note.
- Measure mode with no record: Check is disabled with the hint naming Play line and Tap tempo.
- Review: a claim with a pin but no evidence chip has **Add** disabled; the hint says "Pick an evidence chip." Up to 8 claims; delete with an x.
- Mode switch stops sound. Saved on this device: measurements, claims and taste sliders; **Reset** clears all with a confirm.
- Motion off: the playhead jumps by beat; karaoke highlight still steps word by word.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 the strip, A/B, Play line and the current mode's main controls are visible without scrolling. Preview 680x425: strip with line numbers and lane values, text 10 px or more. At 375 wide: the strip turns vertical (lines as rows, A and B side by side).

## Done when
1. Focus: at 1366x768 and 680x425 the difference strip is the widest, central thing, with both versions' lanes readable.
2. Tap while it plays: A/B flips at the same word within 50 ms; tapping another column jumps there at once; Stop is immediate.
3. A scripted tapper at 58 and at 120 beats a minute (30 ms jitter, held clicks) reads within 8% and Check marks it a match; 3 taps shows "Tap at least 4 times."
4. Check marks each loudness, instrument and tempo cell correctly against the Content table (recompute in Python).
5. A claim cannot be added without a pin and an evidence chip; its pin plays the right line in A then B.
6. Play all lengths match Sources within 1 s for each version.
7. No voice line plays while music plays; how lines speak once in five repeats; claimAdded speaks every time.
8. Copy review gives plain text with every claim's line number and evidence; Reset clears saved work.
