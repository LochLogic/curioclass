# Cover Version Review (idea card)

- Module: arts-6-8-listening-and-reviewing, grade 6-8. Codes: MU:Re8, MU:Re9, MU:Cn10
- Format / family: showdown / none. Fit: change to lab, because the core is a solo listener comparing two versions on a synced difference strip and writing a cited review; the class vote becomes an extra.
- One idea: On the school radio show, two singers perform the same public-domain song very differently; the difference strip lines both versions up in time (tempo, loudness, instruments, key), you mark what each performer changed, and your review must point every claim at a moment you can play.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the radio booth, the song's lyric sheet and two performer cards with a line each about who they are.
  - Core loop: play A and B in sync. The difference strip shows tempo and loudness curves and instrument lanes; the student marks moments ("B drops to just voice here") and says what each change is trying to say. Then pick or write criteria (does the change serve the words? is it performed with control?).
  - The aha or the end state: the review: every sentence links to a timestamp, and a reader can click it to hear the proof. A last line asks which version the student would put on a playlist of their own, and why.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes and compares criteria.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MU:Re8: interpreting how each performer's use of tempo, dynamics and instruments conveys intent.
  - MU:Re9: evaluating both versions with stated criteria, backed by cited moments.
  - MU:Cn10: the student's own playlist choice and how their interests shape it.
- Not like: music-history-radio (eras of one tune); band-practice (playing your part); gallery-critique (grade 9-12 visual art).
- Art: generated scene for the radio booth; people (illustrated) for performers; both performances recorded for Curio Class from a public-domain song.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Two performers sing the same public-domain song very differently; line them up on a difference strip, mark what each changed (tempo, loudness, instruments), and write a review where every claim points at a moment you can play.
- Flags: needs a data source (songs and recordings: public domain song, original performances)
