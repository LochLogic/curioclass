## What it teaches
Students compare two interpretations of the same song using tempo, loudness, instruments and arrangement choices.
They support musical judgments with a playable pin and separate their own preferences from evaluation by criteria.

## Key skills
- Tap a steady beat and estimate beats per minute.
- Listen for instruments, dynamics and changes to a tune.
- Explain how an arrangement changes the effect of the words.
- Support a judgment with a specific musical moment.
- Recognize how personal taste shapes a preference.

## Support at home
- Sing or hum a familiar tune quietly, then brightly. Ask which words feel different and why.
- Tap a beat together and try to keep it steady without counting aloud.
- Compare two ways to perform a song you already know. Name one thing each version does well.

## A five-minute start
Open Listen, press Play line, and use A/B while the line plays. Select line 4 or 7 to hear a clearer contrast.
Open Measure, play a line and tap Tap tempo at least four times. Choose every instrument heard, then Check.
Open Review, pick a criterion, pin the measured line, select an evidence chip and explain why it matters.

## Modes
- Listen shows the tool's values, without scoring.
- Measure begins with unknown cells for both versions. A record belongs to one line and one version.
  In compact windows, the measurement selector shows one complete set of controls at a time.
  Tempo is 60 divided by the mean tap interval. Four taps are required; matching uses an 8 percent tolerance.
  A gap over 1.6 times the median interval begins a fresh count. Estimates outside 30 to 240 bpm are discarded.
  Check compares recorded fields and reveals the true values for that recorded line. Unrecorded lines stay unknown.
- Review accepts up to eight claims. Reliable evidence comes from matching measurements or a checked line.
  Use Both for a pin that plays A and then B. A named-version claim plays that version.
  Taste sliders and the closing sentence connect personal preference to the evaluation.
  Open My taste for the closing card; all of its controls fit together in a dialog.
- Copy review creates plain text with line numbers and evidence. Work saves only on this browser and device.
  Reset asks before clearing it. No microphone is used. Nothing leaves the device.

## Talk questions
- Does the held note help the word "delight" mean something different?
- Can a bright brass arrangement fit words about simplicity? Explain your criterion.
- Which version did something well even if it was not your preference?
- Can "steady" be a criterion without making every intentional slowdown a mistake?

## Good to know
The performers June Avery and Ninth Street Band, and their Porch and Parade arrangements, are fictional.
The voice is an ah/oo formant synthesizer. It does not pronounce words. Lyrics appear on screen only.
Use headphones. The synthesized instruments are simplified timbres, not recordings of real performers.
A cover is a new performance or recording of a song first made by someone else.
Simple Gifts is a Shaker song from 1848, attributed to Joseph Brackett. Aaron Copland used its tune in
[Appalachian Spring (1944)](https://www.loc.gov/collections/aaron-copland/). Copland's arrangement is not reproduced.

## Standards in student actions
- MU:Re8: interpret how tempo, dynamics and instrumentation express the words, using a pinned claim.
- MU:Re9: select an explicit criterion and judgment and support it with measured evidence.
- MU:Cn10: set personal taste sliders and finish the preference sentence while crediting the other version.

## Sources and transcription limits
- [National Core Arts Standards](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/), Music responding and connecting.
- Brackett's 1848 words and melody are public domain. Lyrics follow the supplied specification.
- [John Chambers tune transcription](https://trillian.mit.edu/~jc/music/abc/Contra/NEFR/NEFRT.abc),
  tune 168, "'Tis the Gift to be Simple", provides a pitch reference for the public-domain tune.
- [Robert A. Hudson, Simple Gifts, IMSLP #52769](https://imslp.org/wiki/Simple_Gifts_(Brackett,_Joseph)),
  published 2010, CC BY 3.0, was consulted as a score reference. Its harmony is not reproduced.
  The Internet Archive/Wikimedia copy is catalogued 1848, but its metadata says Finale PrintMusic 2009.
  That catalogue date does not verify an 1848 printing.
- The specification asks for a pre-1930 printing. No such printing was verified during this build.
  The plain tune currently uses an eight-beat-per-line normalization, with pickup words inside each column.
  Verification against an authenticated historical printing remains open and should precede release.
- Tempo and arrangement data, fictional performers and the match tolerance were supplied for Curio Class.
- Illustrations generated with OpenAI gpt-image, edited for Curio Class.

## Build decisions
The A slowdown eases linearly from the listed tempo to 80 percent over the last two beats of lines 4 and 7.
This gives 68.82 seconds for A, within one second of the specification's 69.4-second upper estimate.
B has ten eight-beat lines, eight-beat intro and outro, and a four-beat tag: 50 seconds at 120 bpm.
