# Cell City (idea card)

- Module: science-6-cells-and-body-systems, grade 6. Codes: MS-LS1-1, MS-LS1-2, MS-LS1-3
- Format / family: explorer / cells. Fit: good
- One idea: Check real micrographs to find which samples are made of cells, then zoom from a whole body through organ systems and tissues into one cell, where each organelle's job is shown plainly with a city comparison the student can switch on.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a microscope bench with sample slides, and beside it a body figure with system layers.
  - Core loop: at the bench, view real micrographs (onion skin, cheek cells, pond water with single-celled life, a leaf, cork) next to nonliving samples (sand, a plastic shaving); decide cells or no cells, and one cell or many of different types. Then zoom: digestive system, stomach, stomach lining, one cell, its organelles. In the cell, each part is shown as it really is first; a "city view" switch adds the power plant and city hall overlay, with a note on where the comparison breaks down. A plant cell toggle shows the wall and chloroplasts.
  - The aha or the end state: a body task (run up a flight of stairs) lights up the systems that work together (muscles, lungs, heart, nerves); the student builds a short argument, with evidence from the zoom, that the body is systems made of groups of cells.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a printable sample sheet for a real microscope day.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-LS1-1: the microscope bench is the investigation: living samples show cells, one or many, of different types.
  - MS-LS1-2: the cell view models how each part contributes to the function of the cell as a whole.
  - MS-LS1-3: the stair-run task and the zoom levels support an argument that the body is interacting subsystems made of cell groups.
- Not like: membrane-gatekeeper (grade 9 arcade at the membrane); mitosis-mixer (grade 9 cell division); how-animals-see (built, sense organs).
- Art: public domain or openly licensed micrographs (checked, licenses recorded) so real cells look real, plus generated scenes for the body levels and the city overlay.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Check real micrographs to find which samples are made of cells, then zoom from the body through organs and tissues into one cell, where each organelle's job is shown, with a city overlay you can switch on.
- Flags: needs a data source (licensed micrographs, checked)
