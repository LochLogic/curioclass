# Data Detective (idea card)

- Module: math-8-bivariate-data, grade 8. Codes: 8.SP.A
- Format / family: data / data-desk. Fit: good.
- One idea: Investigate cases with real paired data (arm span vs height, geyser wait time vs eruption length, hours of daylight vs temperature): plot the scatter, read its pattern, fit a line by eye, use its slope and intercept to predict, and crack a two-way table for a yes-or-no question.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a detective's desk with case files and a partner, Inspector Kaya. Case 1: "A footprint was found. How tall was the person?" The data: foot length and height for a sample of adults.
  - Scatter rounds: the student plots the data and names the pattern: positive or negative, linear or not, clusters and outliers. One case has no association at all, and the student must say so.
  - Fit rounds: drag a line by eye through the cloud; a closeness meter shows how far points sit from it. The student writes the line's equation and explains the slope (how many centimeters of height go with each extra centimeter of foot, read from the data) and the intercept (and when it means nothing).
  - Predict rounds: use the line to answer the case, then check against a held-back point.
  - Table rounds: a two-way table (do students with a curfew also have chores?) with row percentages; the student decides whether the two are associated.
  - The aha or the end state: paired data have patterns, a line can summarize a linear one, and the slope tells the story of the case.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): measure the class's arm spans and heights (stays on the device).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 8.SP.A: scatter plots and their patterns, clustering and outliers (8.SP.1); fitting a straight line by eye and judging closeness (8.SP.2); using the line's equation to predict and interpreting slope and intercept (8.SP.3); two-way tables with relative frequencies to judge association in categorical data (8.SP.4).
- Not like: box-plot-builder (same desk engine, grade 6 one variable), correlation-guessing-game (grade 9 correlation and residuals), which-model-wins (grade 9 choosing model types).
- Art: generated scenes (the detective's office, case files); people (illustrated) for Inspector Kaya; all plots drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Solve cases with real paired data (foot length vs height, geyser wait vs eruption): read the scatter's pattern, fit a line by eye, use its slope and intercept to predict, and crack a two-way table.
- Flags: needs a data source (openly licensed paired data sets, for example published body-measurement samples and the public Old Faithful eruption records)
