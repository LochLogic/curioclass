# Behind the Podcast (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-11-behind-the-documentary, grade 11. Codes: D2.His.9.9-12, D2.His.13.9-12, D2.His.17.9-12
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Play an original ten-minute history podcast on the Great Depression, open its show notes, trace each claim to the source it cites, flag sources that don't support the claim, and then weigh the episode's central argument against a historian's article for accuracy.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a podcast player with a waveform, a transcript and a show-notes panel: "Episode 12: Who caused the Great Depression?"
  - Core loop: as the episode plays, claims light up in the transcript; the student opens each one's cited source (a 1932 newspaper, a WPA interview, a Federal Reserve chart, a memoir) and marks it supports, partly supports or doesn't fit (wrong date, wrong place, a quote out of context, a source that can't know).
  - Argument step: the episode's central argument sits beside a short historian's article on the same question; the student checks both against the evidence and marks which claims hold.
  - The aha or the end state: a "corrections" note the student writes for the episode, naming what it got right, what it overstated and which source would fix it.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): students script a 60-second correction segment.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.9.9-12: the claim tracing links each point of the episode to its sources.
  - D2.His.13.9-12: the support ratings critique whether the episode's sources are appropriate for its claims.
  - D2.His.17.9-12: the argument step critiques the episode's central argument against a written history in another medium.
- Not like: missing-voices-archive (gaps in an archive); claim-repair-shop (repairing explanations); stock-market-1929 (living through the crash).
- Art: generated scenes (a studio, period backdrops) and public domain sources (Library of Congress, FSA photos, checked); the narration is an original script with a full transcript, because the episode itself must be ours to plant faults in.
