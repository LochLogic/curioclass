## What it teaches
Children practice sounding out unfamiliar, made-up words across seven phonics patterns: short vowels (CVC), silent e (CVCe), digraphs, blends, vowel teams, diphthongs, r-controlled vowels and word endings. A Words menu picks one pattern or a ready mix. They read aloud to a grown-up or partner who can help mark each attempt.

## Key skills
- Blend letter sounds into one spoken word.
- Notice the difference between short vowels and silent-e patterns.
- Read digraphs (sh, ch, th, wh, ck, ng) and blends (bl, st, -mp, -nd) as one chunk.
- Read vowel teams, diphthongs and r-controlled vowels (ai, ee, oi, ow, ar, ur) as one sound.
- Read a word ending (-s, -ed, -ing, -er, -est, -ly, -ful, -less, -ness, -tion) as one chunk, after the base word.
- Return to words that need another try.

## Support at home
- Say three sounds slowly and ask your child to blend them. Take turns making up a word.
- Write a short made-up word on scrap paper. Ask your child to point to each sound, then blend it.
- Keep practice brief and kind. After a tricky word, try it together and return to it later.

## A five-minute start
Open Explore with a child and a reading partner. Choose the pattern or mix already taught in class from the Words menu. Read a few words aloud, using Next and Back. Flag a word to revisit. Try a 10-word Practice test together, then use Practice these for any Not yet or skipped words.

## Modes
- Explore: stable bank order, Back, Next, Shuffle, Flag and a flagged-word picker. Arrow keys navigate; F flags.
- Practice test: 10, 20 or 30 shuffled words, 20 by default. The pie timer is green for 2 seconds, yellow for 2 more, then red. Red means take your time and try. It never advances a word or sounds an alarm.
- Challenge: 30 shuffled words and a one-minute limit. Right, Not yet and Skip advance immediately. Skip counts as Not yet. A perfect run gets a banner, a short cheer and optional confetti.

Right arrow or R marks Right. Left arrow or N marks Not yet. Skip is available by touch or Tab and Enter. Scoring is a person's judgment, never speech recognition. Unreached Challenge words are reported separately and are not mislabeled as attempted words.

## Talk questions
- Which letters did you blend together?
- How does the final e change what you try for the vowel?
- Which word would you like to read again?

## Good to know
CCSS RF.2.3a is the focus specified for this tool. The bank has exactly 114 CVC, 50 CVCe and 36 letter-pair entries, totaling 200. The owner must approve the supplied bank before shipping; possible real words and sound-alikes are listed in handoff.md. These are made-up reading prompts, not vocabulary definitions.

Decodable nonsense words are a common structured-literacy practice, including for children with dyslexia. This is practice, not a diagnosis or treatment. There is no microphone and no automatic reading assessment.

Flags, last mode, the Words choice and best Challenge scores stay in localStorage on the current device. Best scores are separate for each Words choice. The first visit opens Explore. Later visits restore the last mode, with timed modes waiting for Start. Settings cannot change during a timed run; a mode button can leave it. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Sound controls brief instructions and the perfect-run cheer. The Read button reads instructions only and can be used even with Sound off. No tool voice reads a target word or word list. Browser narration is the approved temporary voice until voice night. Motion off removes confetti but keeps all reading timings. During a word, the page and flourish stay still.

The short flagged and results lists are the spec's exceptions to one target word at a time. Practice these opens only attempted Not yet and skipped words. All returns to the selected kinds. At least one kind must remain selected. Flags are stored by word and displayed only within the current selected kinds.

On a shared computer, tap Reset in the top bar to clear the flags, choices and best scores saved in that browser. In Practice test and Challenge the instructions are spoken once before Start; once a run begins the voice stays quiet so the reader can focus.
