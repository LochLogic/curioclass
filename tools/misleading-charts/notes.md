## What it teaches
Students can measure how a chart exaggerates or hides a comparison, identify six drawing techniques, and redraw a fair comparison from the same data. They distinguish a true numerical claim from a misleading picture and an unsupported conclusion.

## Key skills
- Compare a drawn height or picture area with its underlying numbers.
- Inspect baselines, axis units, time windows and year gaps.
- Divide totals by group size to compare rates.
- Use evidence to rule on a headline and revise a chart.
- Explain what a chart can and cannot establish.

## Support at home
- Find a chart in a flyer or newspaper together. Ask where its axis begins and what its units mean.
- Compare two differently sized groups using an equal-sized share, such as a rate per 100 people.
- Ask what information just outside a chart's time window might change the story.

## A five-minute start
Open Case 1. Tap Ruler, then the two bars. The drawing doubles even though sales grow by just 5 percent. Tap Axis check to see the baseline at 950 bags. Choose a trick and headline ruling, press File it, then turn on the fix switch. Compare the published chart with the printed correction.

## Case Files
Six cases can be opened in any order. Clue tools add findings to the Notebook. Ruler requires two different marks. On the robotics case, measure the first and last points to compare the first and last growth steps. On the two-line case, compare points on the same line; unlike units cannot be divided into a useful growth comparison.

Before a ruling can be accepted, gather diagnostic evidence. Cases 1 and 6 need a ruler comparison and Axis check. Case 2 needs Zoom out and Data sheet. Case 3 needs a ruler comparison. Case 4 needs Axis check and Data sheet. Case 5 needs Per 100 and Data sheet. After a rejected filing, use those diagnostic tools again before another check; an unrelated clue cannot unlock another guess. There are no scores or attempt counts. Tapping a chart mark also readies Ruler.

The correction stays printed when its case is reopened. Only closed case numbers are saved on this device. Reset cases asks for confirmation and clears those saved stamps. The kit also remembers the Motion preference. No data leaves the device.

## Spin Room
Choose one of the six invented data sets. Adjust how it is drawn while its numbers stay fixed. The meter compares drawn effects with numerical effects and explains its comparison below. Fact-checker's view lists the techniques currently active. Make it honest restores every control to the fair setting for that data set. This is an exploratory sandbox, with no score or target to maximize.

Some controls do not apply to every data set. Per 100 uses visits per school day for the library data. Only the smoothie data has a second quantity. Only robotics has unequal year gaps. A one-point time window is not allowed because it cannot show a change.

## Talk questions
- Can the numbers be true while the picture is misleading?
- Why do bar lengths need a zero baseline?
- What does the recycling poster do to the area of the second picture?
- How does accounting for school days change the library story?
- Does a shared rise in smoothie sales and soccer wins establish a cause?
- Which school has the most broken laptops per student? Which has the biggest total?
- Why does equal horizontal spacing distort the robotics club's growth?

## Good to know
Pine Hollow, every publisher and every number are invented. The full library year means the ten-month school year, September through June. The source has no July or August data. A library visit is not proof that a student did or did not read.

The lie factor is the proportional effect shown by the graphic divided by the proportional effect in the data, following Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (1983), chapter 2. Here it is `(picture ratio - 1) / (data ratio - 1)`. It is shown only for comparable changes in the same direction and is hidden when the data ratio is 1. For picture marks that grow in both directions, the tool compares area. One means an honest size comparison; it does not establish that a headline is supported or that one quantity causes another.

The smoothie correction uses separate charts with zero baselines and states that rising together does not show cause. Rate and time-window corrections address context that a single lie factor cannot evaluate.

Each case has the primary technique named in its intended correction. Other critiques can be valid too: the library case also needs attention to school days, and the smoothie chart also starts its left axis above zero. Discuss these secondary findings even though filing uses the case's primary technique. On compact screens, "y" beside a time gap means years.

The chart techniques and causation caution follow Alberto Cairo, *How Charts Lie* (2019). The catalog lists CSTA MS-DAT-DC-23, MS-DAT-DI-25 and MS-DAT-DI-26. In the 2017 crosswalk, this tool practices the transformation part of 2-DA-08, but students do not collect new data. It does not cover 2-DA-09, which requires refining a computational model using data it generated; that code should move to a modeling activity. Rates connect to 7.RP.A. These bar and line charts do not cover the specific dot-plot, histogram and box-plot displays in 6.SP.B.4.

The filing checker offers feedback on fixed choices. Repeating the required clue tools and trying every choice can eventually close a case. A closed stamp records completion, not demonstrated understanding. Ask students to explain the measured difference in their own words. Preventing that shortcut fully needs a change to the specified filing activity; this review does not add an extra quiz.

Against the official 2026 CSTA standards, MS-DAT-DI-26 is the direct match: students change a visualization and compare how it reads. MS-DAT-DC-23 is partial: time-window filtering and rates are present, but sorting and grouping structured records are absent. MS-DAT-DI-25 has a limited relationship/classification exercise in comparing variables and ruling on a chart; this is not a general prediction activity. Do not claim this tool alone fully covers those last two codes.

In the Spin Room, a lie factor describes only the named comparison. For two axes, the meter reports the smoothie comparison and the separate wins comparison; neither can prove a cause. Equal spacing is misleading only when the visible year gaps differ. A short window containing only one-year gaps is still a selected window, but it does not have unequal time steps.

## Access
Use Tab to reach controls before chart marks. Enter or Space selects a chart mark. Focus is visible. Escape closes Data sheet. Sound turns narration and feedback on or off; Read speaks the current hint, including with Sound off. Motion off makes redraws immediate. On short screens, Published item and Correction are separate tabs and the Notebook shows its most recent finding.

While the phone data sheet is open, Tab skips covered controls; the header controls and Close remain available. Closing the sheet restores the clue tools and case folders. Desktop data sheets keep the visible sidebar available.
