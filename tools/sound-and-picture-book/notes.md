## What it teaches

Students plan a beginning, middle and end, combine pictures with sounds, and revise their book. They can explain how a different sound changes what they imagine in the same picture.

## Key skills

- Plan who, where and what happens.
- Organize three parts of a story.
- Arrange pictures and choose background and action sounds.
- Compare a story before and after a change.
- Tell a story aloud while pictures and sounds play.

## Support at home

- Look at one picture together. Make a soft rain sound, then an owl hoot. Ask what might be happening each time.
- Tell a three-part story with a toy. Say what happens at the beginning, middle and end.
- Make a sound for a familiar story, then try a different sound and talk about what changed.

## A five-minute start

Teacher led for grades K-2. Tap Sample book, then Play. Tell Raccoon's Night Walk together. In Swap, tap Snap and Snore to exchange them. Play again. Ask what the raccoon might be hearing now. There is no score or single right story.

## Modes

Plan: tap Who, Where or What happens to open a complete card tray. Choose a card, then a page. Who changes the main animal on every page. If a page holds four things and no animal, remove a thing in Make before choosing Who; nothing is deleted to make space. With no card selected, a page button opens its slots for clearing. Make it opens after all pages have places.

Make: choose a page, Place, Characters or Things. Up to four pieces fit on each page. Tap a piece for poses, Flip and Remove. Drag it to move, use its Size handle to resize, or move it with arrow keys. Delete removes the selected piece. Tap empty paper or press Escape to leave selection. In a short preview, selection tools replace the picture tray. Background and Action open sound trays. Tap a sound's name to assign or remove it; tap Hear to audition. Hear page plays the combination.

Play: tell the story aloud. Each action begins 0.8 seconds after its page. Auto turns every six seconds and stops at The end. Left and Right also turn pages. Start again begins at page one.

Swap: drag matching kinds of sound chips together, or tap two matching chips. Tap two page numbers to move whole pages, including their sounds. Undo swap keeps the ten latest consecutive swaps. Editing a picture or assigning a sound clears that history, so Undo cannot erase the new edit. Another idea offers the sample suggestions that fit the current book. Suggestions that assign new sounds are done in Make, on page two. Matching sounds do not count as a change.

## Talk questions

What does the picture show? What does the sound make you imagine? What changed when only the sound moved? What changed when the whole page moved? Which version tells the story you meant?

## Good to know

The book saves automatically on this device only. A new book asks before replacing it. If storage is unavailable, the book works for this visit. No camera, upload, microphone, recording or network request is used. All animal illustrations are original generated paintings; every environmental sound is synthesized. Motion off removes movement while retaining timing. Sound off keeps the sound labels visible.

The voice manifest requests gravel-ranger / Algenib. This Build contains no recorded narration; the kit uses browser speech. The class supplies the story, and narration waits until each action finishes. Read stops the current sound before speaking. Each choice tray has its own Read button for its current instructions.

Standards: MA:Cr1 is practiced through planning cards; MA:Cr2 through ordering three pages; MA:Cr3 through revising pictures and swaps; MA:Pr4 through combining and playing pictures and sounds. Source: [National Core Arts Standards, Media Arts grades K-2](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/Media%20Arts%20at%20a%20Glance%20-%20new%20copyright%20info.pdf). The general sound-and-image idea follows Michel Chion's Audio-Vision (1994), especially added value: a sound can influence the meaning we perceive in a picture. It does not give every listener the same feeling.
