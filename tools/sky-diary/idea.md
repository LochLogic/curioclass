# Sky Diary (idea card)

- Module: science-1-sun-moon-and-stars, grade 1. Codes: 1-ESS1-1, 1-ESS1-2
- Format / family: explorer / sky. Fit: good
- One idea: Stand in a backyard and speed up the sky over a day, a month and a year, predict where the sun or moon will be next, and compare how many hours of daylight a winter day and a summer day get.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a backyard at dawn with a house, a tree and a compass rose on the ground; a diary page. "Where will the sun be at lunchtime? Tap your guess."
  - Day view: drag the clock; the sun rises in the east, climbs and sets in the west, stars come out and seem to turn at night. The student taps predictions first, then plays time to check.
  - Month view: the moon each night at the same time, its shape changing in a repeating pattern; predict the next shape.
  - Year view: pick a winter day and a summer day; light bars on the diary page show daylight hours (about 9 and 15 hours in the middle of the US, real values computed for a chosen place).
  - The aha or the end state: a diary of stamps with patterns written in: the sun rises in the east every day, the moon's shape repeats each month, summer days are longer than winter days.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): pick your own town to see its sky.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 1-ESS1-1: predicting and checking the sun's path, the moon's shapes and the stars, then naming the pattern.
  - 1-ESS1-2: comparing daylight hours on days at different times of year.
- Not like: shadow-clock (grade 5 shadows explained by Earth's spin and orbit, same engine); moon-phase-model (grade 6 view from space).
- Art: generated scenes for the backyard in different seasons; the sky, sun path and moon drawn in code from real astronomy so positions are true.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Speed up the sky over a backyard for a day, a month and a year, predict where the sun and moon will be next, and compare daylight hours on a winter day and a summer day.
- Flags: none (positions are computed, no data source needed)
