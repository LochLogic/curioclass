# Economy Checkup (idea card)

- Module: cte-6-8-economy-checkup, grade 6-8. Codes: D2.Eco.11.6-8, D2.Eco.12.6-8
- Format / family: data / none. Fit: good, because real data read on a chart is the core.
- One idea: Read the US economy's vital signs (unemployment, inflation and total output) for the last 30 years the way a doctor reads a patient's chart, mark the sick years yourself, then pick any year and see how it hit four neighbors differently: a saver, a borrower with a fixed-rate loan, a retiree on a fixed pension and a job seeker.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a clipboard chart with three lines and a doctor's checklist ("fever: inflation over 4 percent; weakness: unemployment rising; growth: output shrinking").
  - Core loop: scrub through the years; mark years that fail the checkup; then compare with the recession years (2001, 2008 to 2009, 2020) and the high-inflation years (2021 to 2023).
  - Four neighbors: for the chosen year, each neighbor's card shows what happened: the saver's cash bought less in 2022; the fixed-rate borrower paid back in cheaper dollars; the retiree's fixed pension bought fewer groceries; the job seeker faced 10 percent unemployment in 2009.
  - The aha or the end state: the same numbers tell different stories to different people; inflation and unemployment do not hurt everyone the same way.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.11.6-8: read unemployment, inflation and output data to judge the economy's health year by year.
  - D2.Eco.12.6-8: explain how inflation and unemployment affect savers, borrowers, retirees and job seekers differently.
- Not like: economy-dashboard (grade 12 full dashboard and forecasting); interest-rate-dial (rates and borrowing); box-plot-builder (statistics).
- Art: drawn in code (charts) with people (illustrated) for the four neighbors.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (BLS unemployment and CPI, BEA real GDP, via FRED, dated); overlap risk (economy-dashboard, kept apart by grade and the four-neighbor focus)
