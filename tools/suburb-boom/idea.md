# Suburb Boom (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-11-suburb-boom, grade 11. Codes: D2.Geo.4.9-12, D2.Geo.6.9-12, D2.Geo.8.9-12
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Watch one real metro area grow from 1945 to today on aerial images as highways, federal home loans, redlining and zoning rules arrive and decide who can buy where; farms become subdivisions, wetlands are paved and later flood, and the student changes one decision to see the pattern shift.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a 1945 aerial photo of the metro's edge (farms, a creek, a streetcar line) and a policy shelf with four cards: interstate route, FHA loan rules, a 1930s lending map, a large-lot zoning code.
  - Core loop: step forward by decade; each policy card plays as it did, and the map shows subdivisions, highways, white flight, closed streetcars and paved wetlands, matched against the real aerial images. A people panel follows two families (one white, one Black) with the same income and their different options.
  - What-if: change one card (route the highway elsewhere, drop the lending map, allow apartments) and replay; the model's result is labelled a what-if.
  - The aha or the end state: the flood of a recent decade on the paved wetland, and a then-and-now map of home values and race; the student explains how human and physical systems shaped each other.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare a second metro.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.4.9-12: the wetland and flood thread shows human and physical systems acting on each other.
  - D2.Geo.6.9-12: the decade steps evaluate how settlement changed the land and the communities living there.
  - D2.Geo.8.9-12: the policy cards and what-if replays show how loans, highways and zoning shaped city, suburb and rural patterns.
- Not like: climate-on-the-move (climate moving people); layers-and-scales (thematic maps); who-decides (present-day local action).
- Art: public domain aerial photographs (USGS, checked) plus drawn-in-code overlays, and people (illustrated) for the two families, because the real aerials prove the change.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: sensitive (redlining and housing discrimination; fresh review agent); needs a data source (one real metro with 1930s lending maps and USGS aerials; Mapping Inequality license to be checked)
