## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Meet the shapes** and tap the **triangle**. Read the sentences together: "It is flat. It has 3 sides. It has 3 corners."
2. Press **Count with me**. Each side lights up and gets a number, then each corner. Count out loud with it.
3. Switch to **Guess my shape**. Read the first clue, press **Cross out**, and ask: which shapes are left?

## The three modes

- **Meet the shapes:** twelve shapes. Eight are flat (circle, triangle, square, rectangle, rhombus, trapezoid, pentagon, hexagon) and four are solid (cube, sphere, cylinder, cone). Each one comes with four or five sentence frames: flat or solid, sides or faces, corners, same-length sides or rolling, and "It looks like..."
- **Guess my shape:** the tool hides a shape and gives one clue at a time. **Cross out** grays every shape that doesn't fit the clues so far. A wrong guess explains which clue it breaks ("The clue says: It has 3 sides").
- **Partner talk:** a child is the describer. Everyone else closes their eyes while the describer holds **peek**, then the describer uses the empty frames on screen ("It has ___ sides") and the class guesses.

## Language goals

- Students say whole sentences with math words: *side, corner, flat, solid, face, roll, the same length*.
- Partner talk is the real language practice. Let describers point at the frames while they speak. Beginners can answer with one word in the blank; more fluent speakers can add "It looks like..."
- Accept "point" or "vertex" for corner. The tool says "corner" because that is the K-1 word.

## Talk about it

- How is a square like a rectangle? How is it different?
- Which solids can roll? Which can stack? Which can do both?
- Find something in our room that looks like a cylinder.

## Good to know

- Keys 1 to 3 switch modes.
- The shapes are drawn in code, so they stay sharp on any projector.
