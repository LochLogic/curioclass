## What it teaches

Children build and read real one- and two-syllable words by lining up sound jars on a baking tray, hearing the oven blend those sounds into a single word, and reading the finished word on the pastry box. When a jar is swapped or left out, the oven bakes what is on the tray so students hear right away how changing a first, middle, or last sound makes a different word.

## Key skills

- Blend three and four sounds smoothly into a spoken word, including digraphs (sh, ch, th, wh, ck) and consonant blends (fr, st, cl, dr, mp, nd).
- Hear whether a word has a short or long vowel sound and use silent e or vowel teams (ai, ay, ee, ea, oa) to spell long vowels.
- Spot which part of a word (beginning, middle, or end) needs to change when a baked word does not match the customer's order.
- Build two-syllable words layer by layer, checking that every syllable layer holds a vowel sound.
- Choose the ending (-s, -ed, or -ing) that fits a sentence and pick common irregular words (said, was, of, come, one, could) by how they look.

## Support at home

- In the kitchen or on a walk, play "oven blend": say three sounds slowly ("sh... i... p") and let your child blend them together into one word ("ship!"), then trade roles.
- Pick a short word like *cap* or *hop* and ask what happens when a quiet *e* sprinkles on the end (*cape*, *hope*).
- When reading a bedtime story together, pause at a word with *-s*, *-ed*, or *-ing* and cover the ending with your finger so your child can spot the base word first.

## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Counters** on the projector or laptop and tap **Digraph Donuts**. Listen to the customer's order ("One ship, please!").
2. Tap a few jars on the pantry shelf (`s`, `h`, `sh`) so the class hears the difference between two separate sounds and one digraph sound. Put `s`, `i`, `p` on the tray on purpose and press **Bake** so everyone hears the oven bake *sip* and watches the first jar glow to show where the fix belongs.
3. Tap the glowing slot to empty it, drop in `sh`, and press **Bake** again. Have the class read the box aloud during the 3-second pause before the oven says the word.

## The counters and Rush Hour

- **Digraph Donuts:** six three-sound words with `sh`, `ch`, `th`, `wh`, and `ck` (*ship, chop, thin, whip, duck, fish*).
- **Blend Buns:** six four-sound words with beginning and ending blends (*frog, stop, clap, drum, nest, hand*).
- **Magic-e Muffins:** listen to the vowel first and pick the **Short** tin (little muffin) or **Long** tin (tall muffin), then build *cape, pin, hope, cub, kite,* and *tap* using the `e` sprinkle shaker for long vowels.
- **Team Tarts:** vowel teams `ai`, `ay`, `ee`, `ea`, and `oa` (*rain, play, feet, seat, boat, green*). Homophone bakes like *feat* for *feet* get a special explanation from the customer.
- **Two-Layer Cakes:** two-syllable words (*rabbit, napkin, sunset, cupcake, picnic, muffin*). Each cake layer has a marked vowel spot and cannot stack without a vowel jar.
- **Ending Eclairs:** read or listen to a sentence (*"Yesterday, the frog ___."*) and add `-s`, `-ed`, or `-ing` to the baked base eclair (*jumped, digging, waters, pulled, baking, sings*). The oven shows consonant doubling (*digging*) and e-dropping (*baking*) automatically and explains why once.
- **Tricky Treats:** words that cannot be baked by sound (*said, was, of, come, one, could*). Students pick the matching labeled box from three close look-alikes.
- **Rush Hour:** a 90-second mix of customers from every counter with coins and a three-in-a-row streak bell. **Relaxed** is on by default for classroom use and turns customer patience bars off.

## Talk about it

- When we baked *sip* instead of *ship*, which part of the word changed: the start, the middle, or the end?
- Why did the customer ask for *feet* with `ee` instead of `ea` when the picture showed socks?
- How do we know how many layers a cake word like *picnic* or *cupcake* needs?

## Good to know

- Every jar has both a tint and a distinct silhouette (round consonant jars, tall vowel jars, double-wide team jars, short wide ending jars, and a sprinkle shaker for silent e) so color is never the only signal.
- Completed counters and the best Rush Hour score are saved only in this browser on this device (`localStorage`) and never leave the device. **Clear** in the control panel's saved-progress box erases saved progress.
