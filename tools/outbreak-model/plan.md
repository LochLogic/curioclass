arc: explore-first
minutes: 50
focus: F-LE.A.4; F-IF.C
goal: I can solve exponential models for time with logarithms in base 2, 10 and e, read percent growth from an exponential, and explain where a pure exponential model breaks.
materials: The tool on the projector and on student devices. Calculators with ln and log.
grouping: Pairs on devices, with whole-class pauses.
---
## Before class

- Open **Grow** with **Virus X in a city** (10 cases, doubling every 3 days).
- Every story is invented and labeled as a made-up example, not a real forecast.
- Model: N(t) = a e^(kt). For Virus X, k = 0.2310, so growth is 26.0 percent a day.

## Hook (5 min)

1. "Ten people have a new virus, and cases double every 3 days. When will a million people have it?" Take guesses in days.
2. Record the guesses on the board. "Let's find out, and learn to answer exactly."

## Explore (15 min)

1. Press **Play** and watch the **Milestones** list: 100, 1 thousand, 10 thousand. "How far apart are they?" (9.97 days each.)
2. Press **Log**. The curve becomes a straight line. "Why?"
3. Pairs turn on **Compare steady growth**. "How long would a steady line take to reach 1 million?"
4. Pairs switch **Describe growth by** between doubling time, tenfold time and continuous rate. "The curve never moves. What changes?"

## Make sense (12 min)

1. Switch to **Ask when** and press **1 million**. The **Solve it** panel works it three ways and all land on t = 49.83 days.
2. On the board, solve 10 × 2^(t/3) = 1,000,000 with log base 2, then base 10, then ln. "Why must they agree? Where does log 2 come from?"
3. Compare with the class guesses. Press **Check it** to run the clock to that moment.

## Apply (13 min)

1. Pairs turn on **Guess first** and solve two targets for another **Story** on paper before pressing **Show the answer**.
2. Pairs try the Fading rumor, which halves every 5 days. "Why is 'when does it reach 2,100?' answered 'Never'? What does the negative t mean?"
3. Switch to **Hit the ceiling** and pick **United States**. The exponential passes the population; the logistic levels off. "When do they start to disagree, and why?"

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: "A post has 50 shares and the shares double every 4 hours. Write N(t) and solve for when it reaches 100,000 shares, showing the log step. What percent does it grow each hour?" Then: "Name one assumption of the model that breaks first in a real school."

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** start with base 2 only, counting doublings before using logs.
- **Support:** use the table of whole days on either side of each answer.
- **Stretch:** derive D = ln 2 / k and W = ln 10 / k from N(t) = a e^(kt).
- **Stretch:** compare the Virus X ceilings for Denver, Colorado and the World. Why are they so close together in days?

## Short on time

- **Grow** with **Log** (8 min).
- **Ask when** for 1 million (8 min), then the exit card (5 min).
