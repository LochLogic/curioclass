# Reaction Kitchen (idea card)

- Module: science-7-chemical-reactions, grade 7. Codes: MS-PS1-2, MS-PS1-5, MS-PS1-6
- Format / family: lab / molecules. Fit: good
- One idea: Work three kitchen benches: decide whether a new substance formed by comparing properties before and after, weigh a sealed bag to find that mass stays the same while atoms rearrange, then design a hand warmer that runs on rusting iron.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a kitchen counter with three benches, each with a one-line question ("Did something new form?", "Where did the mass go?", "Can you make heat on purpose?").
  - Bench 1: mix pairs (baking soda and vinegar, sugar and water, milk and lemon juice, sugar on a hot pan) and compare property cards before and after (density, melting point, solubility, smell, color). Dissolving sugar fails the test; the others pass. The student sorts each into reaction or not, with the property data as the reason.
  - Bench 2: run baking soda and vinegar in an open cup on a scale (mass drops) and in a sealed bag (mass holds); a molecule view counts every atom before and after so the student sees the gas carried the "missing" mass away.
  - Bench 3: mix iron powder, salt, water and a filler in a pouch; tune the amounts to stay between 40 and 50 C for an hour, test, read the graph, change one thing, test again. A cold pack option uses baking soda and citric acid.
  - The aha or the end state: atoms are rearranged, never lost, and some rearrangements give off or take in heat that people can design with.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-PS1-2: bench 1 has the student analyze property data before and after to decide if a reaction happened.
  - MS-PS1-5: bench 2's sealed-bag test and atom count model conservation of atoms and mass.
  - MS-PS1-6: bench 3 is the design project: build, test and modify a device that releases or absorbs heat by a chemical process.
- Not like: hand-warmer-lab (grade 10, bond energies); molecule-builder (building, not reacting); mystery-powders (grade 5 identifying powders).
- Art: generated scenes for the kitchen and ingredients; atoms, scales and graphs drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Three kitchen benches: test whether baking soda and vinegar made something new, weigh a sealed bag to find mass conserved as atoms rearrange, then design a hand warmer that runs on rusting iron.
- Flags: overlap risk (hand-warmer-lab; keep this one to kitchen materials and simple design trials, no bond energies); watch size (three benches, spec should keep each short)
