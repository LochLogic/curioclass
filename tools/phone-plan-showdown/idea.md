# Phone Plan Showdown (idea card)

- Module: math-7-multi-step-equations-and-inequalities, grade 7. Codes: 7.EE.B
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: Play a skeptical customer at a phone store whose salesperson makes claims ("Plan B is always cheaper"); write the equation or inequality behind each claim, solve it, and say exactly when the claim is true.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a store counter, a salesperson named Marcus with a big smile, and two prepaid plans: $20 a month plus $3 per GB, or $35 a month with 5 GB included and $1 per GB after.
  - Core loop: Marcus makes a claim; the student builds it as an equation or inequality from tiles (20 + 3g < 35), solves it, graphs the solution set on a number line, and answers Marcus in words ("only if I use less than 5 GB").
  - Two kinds of equations: px + q = r and p(x + q) = r, such as a family of 4 each paying (x + 5) dollars for a $72 bill. The student solves once with arithmetic steps and once with algebra, and compares the steps.
  - Fine-print rounds: tax at 6.5%, a 1/4-off first month, a $0.10 per minute call fee; the student converts between forms and estimates first (is the bill near $40 or $60?).
  - Final showdown: the student picks a plan for their real use (or a sample family) and defends it with the inequality.
  - The aha or the end state: an inequality turns "it depends" into an exact answer you can check.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): swap roles, and a partner plays the customer.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 7.EE.B: multi-step problems with whole numbers, fractions, decimals and percents, converted between forms and checked by estimation (7.EE.3); building and solving px + q = r and p(x + q) = r, comparing arithmetic and algebraic solutions, and inequalities px + q > r or < r graphed and interpreted in context (7.EE.4).
- Not like: model-my-world (grade 9 equations fit to data), two-trains (grade 8 systems with two unknowns), balance-puzzle (grade 6 one-step).
- Art: people (illustrated) for Marcus and the customer; generated scene for the store; plans, tiles and number lines drawn in code. Made-up carrier names only.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Play a skeptical customer at a phone store: turn each sales claim into an equation or inequality, solve it, graph the solutions, and say exactly when Plan B really is cheaper.
- Flags: needs a data source (current prepaid plan price ranges, so the numbers stay realistic)
