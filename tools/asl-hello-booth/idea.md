# ASL Hello Booth (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-k-12-greetings-and-introductions-in-asl, grade k-12. Codes: ACTFL.Novice Low, WRS.Communication
- Format / family: showdown / asl. Fit: good when solo-first: one student at the booth works alone, and the whole-class sign-along is the class mode.
- One idea: Deaf signers step up to a welcome booth on video and greet you; you answer each one (hello, your name, how you feel) by picking and then copying the right signs, and you learn the polite ways to get a signer's attention before you start.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a booth at a community festival. A Deaf signer on video waves into view and signs HELLO. A caption says: "Watch first. Then answer in the booth mirror."
  - Core loop: each visitor signs one short exchange (HELLO, NAME YOU WHAT?, HOW YOU?, NICE MEET YOU). The student watches, picks the meaning from picture choices (receptive check), then sees the answer modeled slowly and copies it in an on-screen mirror (camera view, never recorded). Between visitors, an attention moment: a signer is looking away, and the student picks how to get their attention (wave in their line of sight, a light tap on the shoulder, a flick of the lights for a room) or sees why a poor choice (grabbing an arm, waving in their face) lands badly.
  - The aha or the end state: a final visitor signs a whole greeting at natural speed with no captions, and the student follows all of it; the booth guestbook shows every signer met and the name sign or fingerspelled name each shared.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): projector sign-along: the class copies the signer together, then pairs greet each other. A slow / natural speed switch on every clip.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Novice Low: understanding and producing memorized greetings, names (fingerspelled) and feelings in isolated signs and set phrases.
  - WRS.Communication: interpretive (reading the signer's greeting), interpersonal (answering each visitor in turn) and the cultural norms of starting a signed conversation.
- Not like: fingerspell-flash (letters only; here names sit inside a real greeting); asl-family-tree (vocabulary and description, not conversation openers); hola-hallway (spoken Spanish greetings by time of day).
- Art: real signing video by fluent Deaf signers (required; drawings cannot show movement and facial grammar); illustrated festival booth around the video (people, illustrated) for warmth.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Deaf signers greet you at a festival booth on video; read each greeting, answer with hello, your name and how you feel by copying the signs in a mirror, and learn polite ways to get a signer's attention.
- Flags: needs a data source (signing video by fluent Deaf signers, licensed or recorded; see the ASL video question); sensitive (Deaf cultural norms: fresh review, rule 5); camera mirror shows the on-device line (rule 7); K-12 span check (gf-04: a K child and a teen beginner both need full depth, likely two framings).
