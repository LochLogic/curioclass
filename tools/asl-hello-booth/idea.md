# ASL Hello Booth (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-k-12-greetings-and-introductions-in-asl, grade k-12. Codes: ACTFL.Novice Low, WRS.Communication
- Format / family: showdown / asl. Fit: good when solo-first: one student at the booth works alone, and the whole-class sign-along is the class mode.
- One idea: Illustrated Deaf signers step up to a welcome booth and greet you in Sign Strip panels; you answer each one (hello, your name, how you feel) by picking and then copying the right signs, and you learn the polite ways to get a signer's attention before you start.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a booth at a community festival. An illustrated Deaf signer waves into view and signs HELLO in the player. A caption says: "Watch first. Then answer and copy."
  - Core loop: each visitor signs one short exchange (HELLO, NAME YOU WHAT?, HOW YOU?, NICE MEET YOU), with the wh-question or yes/no face shown in the face inset. The student watches, picks the meaning from picture choices (receptive check), then steps through the answer as start, movement and end panels and copies it, with an optional camera mirror beside the panels (camera view, never recorded). Between visitors, an attention moment told as a short comic strip: a signer is looking away, and the student picks how to get their attention (wave in their line of sight, a light tap on the shoulder, a flick of the lights for a room) or sees why a poor choice (grabbing an arm, waving in their face) lands badly.
  - The aha or the end state: a final visitor signs a whole greeting at Natural speed with no captions, and the student follows all of it; the booth guestbook shows every signer met and the name sign or fingerspelled name each shared, as panels.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): projector sign-along: the class copies the signer together, then pairs greet each other. Slow / Natural speed and Left-handed on every sign.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Novice Low: understanding and producing memorized greetings, names (fingerspelled) and feelings in isolated signs and set phrases.
  - WRS.Communication: interpretive (reading the signer's greeting), interpersonal (answering each visitor in turn) and the cultural norms of starting a signed conversation.
- Not like: fingerspell-flash (letters only; here names sit inside a real greeting); asl-family-tree (vocabulary and description, not conversation openers); hola-hallway (spoken Spanish greetings by time of day).
- Art: graphics only, per docs/agents/ASL-GRAPHICS.md: a small cast of illustrated signers (one set of rendered body and face plates each, people review) sharing the vector Sign Strip hands, arrows and face inset; illustrated festival booth (people, illustrated). No video, no photos of people.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Illustrated Deaf signers greet you at a festival booth; read each greeting, answer with hello, your name and how you feel by copying the signs, and learn polite ways to get a signer's attention.
- Flags: sign entries and face markers checked by the fresh blind review (ASL-GRAPHICS.md); sensitive (Deaf cultural norms: fresh review, rule 5); camera mirror shows the on-device line (rule 7); the on-screen practice note (one common form; practice with a fluent Deaf signer or your ASL teacher when you can); K-12 span check (gf-04: a K child and a teen beginner both need full depth, likely two framings).
