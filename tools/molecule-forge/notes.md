## What it teaches
Students model the composition of molecules and extended structures. Their own connections determine formulas, shapes and material patterns.

## Key skills
- Join atoms using their available bonding spots.
- Compare a formula with the connections and three-dimensional model it represents.
- Distinguish isomers and alternative bond drawings of one structure.
- Explore repeated patterns and compare neighbouring atoms.
- Relate bonding patterns to qualitative mechanical and electrical behaviour.

## Support at home
- Look at a pencil and discuss why its graphite can leave a mark.
- Compare a grain of salt with a piece of copper wire already at home, without tasting or heating either.
- Look for the same repeated pattern in a fence or tiled floor and describe what changes at its edges.

## A five-minute start
Open the bench and let its waiting oxygen invite two hydrogen taps. There is no required sequence, assignment or endpoint.

## Bench positions
Forge builds molecular graphs. Crystal grows finite lattice specimens from one or two seed atoms. The field guide is an unrestricted reference book.

## Talk questions
What changed when you moved the connection but kept the atoms? What happens at a crystal edge? Which neighbour counts stay the same inside?

## Good to know
Anchor: MS-PS1-1, simple molecules and extended structures. Also reaches HS-PS2-6 and HS-PS1-1 as informal exploration.
The instrument is not a full teacher lesson plan. No plan.md is supplied, so this build alone does not establish lesson-card takeover.
Shapes settle to computed PubChem models, with explicitly labelled reference/ideal fallbacks for hydrogen, silane and allene. Mechanical and current tests are qualitative; consult the Manual for assumptions.
Shelf data stays on this device. The finished file works offline without fetching art, data or sound.

Cut open follows the inspected atom and keeps its bonded neighbours exposed, so coordination can be seen as well as counted.
