## What it teaches
Students use volume and surface area to design packages with a fixed capacity and less material. They compare a cylinder model with real cans and calculate how efficiently 24 packages fill a case.

## Key skills
- Keep volume fixed while changing a radius or a square base.
- Connect separate top, bottom and wall areas to total material.
- Plot trials and use a curve to find a useful design.
- Explain why real packaging may use more material than a mathematical minimum.
- Compare product volume with case volume and use grams per unit area.

## Support at home
- Compare the shapes of two empty packages already at home. Ask why a maker might choose a tall, wide or stackable shape.
- Look down at a few circular lids arranged in rows. Find the gaps, then compare with square objects placed edge to edge.
- Ask your student to explain why keeping the same volume does not keep the same amount of material.

## Five-minute start
Open Orders and choose O2. Drag Radius and release at five different values. Watch the dots become a curve. Before selecting Reveal best, describe the shape you think would use least material.

## Modes
Orders has six requests: soup, drink, bulk food, a closed gift box, an open seed tray and a drink can with costlier lids. Radius or base side changes while height keeps the volume fixed. The package is a true-scale front view; the viewport zooms out for tall towers. Release the handle or slider to plot a trial. Show curve can reveal the model curve early. Reveal best marks the exact formula-based minimum without moving the student's design; its dimensions and material also appear on the bench. Clear dots affects only the current order. Shapes and dots last for this page session; no work is saved.

This is an explorer, with no mastery score or pass gate. Lowest sampled material (or cost for O6) describes the plotted trials, not a skill rating. A near-minimum trial gets a factual comparison, not achievement praise; trials copied after Reveal best do not trigger that event. Five repeated releases may show the model curve, just as Show curve does, but they do not establish five different designs.

Net opens the flat pieces, sharing one scale. It starts open on screens at least 900 pixels tall; shorter screens start with it closed. Closed cans have two disks and a wall rectangle; closed boxes have two square faces and four wall panels; trays have one square base and four walls. Dashed lines are folds.

For O6, Lid cost weights both the lid and bottom. Actual metal mass continues to use unweighted area. Earlier dots are recalculated for the new price so the graph compares one consistent cost rule.

Increasing that price makes the minimum taller and thinner; decreasing it makes the minimum shorter and wider. A box accepts only finite numbers, rounds to the supported step and clamps to its range. Invalid text restores the last valid price.

Shelf uses four unbranded cans at one outside-size scale. Tap each to compare it with the best closed cylinder of its own model volume. The nominal capacity on a label differs from the volume of a cylinder modeled from outside dimensions. These are simplified cylinders without a detailed rim, wall thickness or neck model.

Case uses the order's current shape. Pack the case places 24 packages in four rows of six. A second tap finishes the drop immediately. For cans, inside dimensions are 8r × 12r × h, so fill is always π ÷ 4, or 78.5%. Square packages fill 100%. This ignores manufacturing tolerances, dividers and case wall thickness. Open trays are treated by their bounding volume.

## Talk questions
- Which trials helped you find the low point? Would you choose different trials next time?
- Why do a tall thin can and a short wide can both use more material?
- What changes when lids and bottoms cost three times as much per square centimeter?
- Why might a drink can be tall and thin? Think of hands, cup holders, machines that sell them, and the lid.
- Why does changing radius not change the fraction of a rectangular case filled by cans?

## Good to know
Grade 10 geometry, Modeling with Geometry. Orders practices G-MG.A.3; Shelf practices G-MG.A.1; Case and the labeled metal-per-area calculation practice G-MG.A.2. No calculus is needed. Reveal best uses exact formulas, not numerical searching.

Lengths show two decimals, Orders areas two decimals, Shelf areas one decimal and percentages one decimal. Calculations retain full precision. At radius 3.84, O2 height is 7.66 cm; the exact minimum has radius 3.837215... and height 7.674430..., displayed 3.84 and 7.67. Rounded displays do not define a new exact shape.

The metal figure, 0.03 g per cm², is a practice number. It does not represent the measured density or thickness of each real can. Case applies the same calculation to every order. For boxes and trays it is labeled a practice metal equivalent, not actual cardboard mass.

Use Tab, arrow keys and Enter to work without a mouse. On the package handle arrows change 0.01 cm; Shift plus arrows change 0.10 cm. Number boxes accept direct entry. Read speaks the current hint. Motion off removes motion, and can packing appears instantly as the spec requests. Narration uses the browser until recordings are provided.

No network requests, accounts, collection or saved designs. The kit remembers only its shared Motion preference on this device.
