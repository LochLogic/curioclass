# Metadata X-Ray (idea card)

- Module: computer-science-6-8-data-metadata-and-you, grade 6-8. Codes: MS-DAT-DC-21, MS-DAT-DC-22, MS-DAT-IM-28
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good, because two dials (precision and how often) change what the data reveals.
- One idea: Flip three everyday records (a phone photo, a step-tracker log and a school app's sign-in record) to see the metadata hiding behind them (time, place, device), turn dials for precision and how often data is recorded, and watch a map of one made-up student's week go from "somewhere in the city" to "their front door at 7:42 a.m.", then decide who should keep which details.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three cards face up (a dog photo, a step count, a "checked in to class" badge) and an X-ray button.
  - Core loop: X-ray each card to reveal its hidden fields. A precision dial changes the location from 2 decimal places (about 1 km) to 5 (about 1 m); a frequency dial changes step data from daily totals to every minute. A map and a timeline beside it redraw with each turn.
  - Who holds it: for each detail, drag it to "stay on my phone", "the app maker", "the school", "my family" or "delete", and see what each holder could now work out.
  - The aha or the end state: more precise and more frequent data answers questions nobody asked, and deciding who holds it matters as much as collecting it.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-DAT-DC-21: turn the precision dial and explain what each level of detail can reveal.
  - MS-DAT-DC-22: X-ray each record and name the metadata stored with it.
  - MS-DAT-IM-28: decide who should hold each detail and defend the choice.
- Not like: data-defenders (grade 5 security); secret-keeper (K sharing); data-policy-council (grade 9-12 privacy law).
- Art: generated scenes (phone, watch, school app) with a map drawn in code over a made-up town.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: none (all data made up; no real location is ever read)
