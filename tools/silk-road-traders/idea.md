# Silk Road Traders (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-6-ancient-india-and-china, grade 6. Codes: D2.His.2.6-8, D2.Geo.7.6-8
- Format / family: cards / silk-road. Fit: good
- One idea: Trade cards along the routes linking Han China, Central Asia and India's kingdoms: each deal moves goods (silk, horses, spices, cotton cloth) and sometimes an idea or skill (Buddhist teachings, ways of growing and weaving), and a map shows ideas spreading faster where camels, oasis towns and monsoon ships make travel easier; at the end the student sorts what the trade changed and what it left the same.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a route map from Chang'an to the Ganges valley with a starting hand of goods.
  - Core loop: move a caravan or ship between markets; trade to fill orders; travel cards (camels, oasis rest, mountain passes, monsoon winds) change speed and cost. Some trades carry an idea card, which lights its spread on the map.
  - The aha or the end state: a sort board: Buddhism reaching China and new crops are change; dynasties ruling, Confucian schooling and village farming are continuity.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two players race to fill orders.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.6-8: the closing sort of the run's events into change and continuity.
  - D2.Geo.7.6-8: travel cards and the spread map show how transport shapes connections and the spread of ideas.
- Not like: salt-and-gold-caravan (a business across one desert); mongol-messenger (a later empire's relay); house-of-wisdom (how knowledge was kept and translated).
- Art: generated scenes for the route and markets; public domain objects (Han and Gupta-era items, Met open access, ids to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Trade cards along the routes linking Han China, Central Asia and India: goods change hands, and ideas like Buddhism ride along faster where travel is easier; then sort what the trade changed and what stayed the same.
- Flags: sensitive (belief systems shown with care; fresh review); needs a data source (dates for each idea's spread, checked); the silk-road family has no siblings, so the family tag may be unneeded
