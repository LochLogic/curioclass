# Evidence Corkboard (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-evidence-corkboard, grade 8. Codes: D2.His.16.6-8, D3.3.6-8, D2.His.12.6-8
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Pin evidence (letters, maps, toll records, shipping prices, a city population table) to a corkboard to answer why the Erie Canal changed New York; the student strings each pin to the claim, tags what each piece cannot show, and lists the sources they would look for next.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: an empty corkboard with the question card in the middle and a box of evidence.
  - Core loop: read a piece; pin it under a reason; string it to the claim; a limits tag asks what it cannot show (a toll record counts boats, not who profited; a booster's letter is one-sided). Weak strings sag until a second piece supports them.
  - The aha or the end state: a finished argument card read off the board, plus a "next sources" list the student writes from the gaps (whose story is missing: canal workers, the Haudenosaunee whose land the canal crossed).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): print the board as a poster.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.16.6-8: the argument card built from the strung pins.
  - D3.3.6-8: evidence from several sources supports each reason, with limits tagged.
  - D2.His.12.6-8: the "next sources" list drawn from the gaps.
- Not like: why-the-pony-express-ended (grade 5, a claim from two sources each, no limits); resource-detective (grade 2); unlabeled-archive (identifying sources). Vary the look: a canal office board, not a detective's den.
- Art: public domain (canal maps, prints and toll reports, Library of Congress and New York State archives, to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (freight costs and population figures before and after 1825, checked)
