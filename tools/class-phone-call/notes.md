## What it teaches

Students practice listening closely to a speaker and asking the exact question word (Who, What, Where, When, Why, or How) that uncovers a missing detail, asking what an unfamiliar word means, building on what the speaker said with an on-topic follow-up question, answering questions about key details from memory, and recounting a short story in order.

## Key skills

- Listening for what kind of information is missing (a person, a thing, a place, a time, a reason, or a way) and choosing the question word that asks for it.
- Asking a speaker to repeat a hard-to-hear part or explain what a new word means.
- Taking turns across table groups by listening while another group holds the phone and waiting for the turn badge to pass.
- Choosing a follow-up question that stays on the speaker's topic instead of jumping to an unrelated thought.
- Recalling key details without looking at notes and putting four story events in the order they happened.

## Support at home

- **Play the "mumble word" game at dinner:** Tell a short two-sentence story about your day, but cough or hum over one important word ("Today I saw a big ___ by the grocery store"). Let your child ask the right question ("What did you see?") to unlock the word, then swap roles.
- **Ask one more question on topic:** When someone in the family shares news, challenge your child to ask one follow-up question about the very same topic before talking about themselves.
- **Take a four-part family message:** After a real phone call with a relative or friend, ask your child to tell another family member what happened first, next, then, and last.

## A 5-minute start

1. Divide the class into up to four table groups (**Owls**, **Foxes**, **Bears**, **Deer**; you can set 2, 3, or 4 groups under **Rules**). Point out the ringing classroom speakerphone and tap **Answer**.
2. Listen to Ranger Sam's first sentence from Pine Hollow Park and review the three phone rules on the **Rules** card: *Listen*, *One at a time*, and *Wait your turn*.
3. When static crackles over a word, have the group shown on the turn badge say a full question out loud, then invite a helper to tap that question card (**Who**, **What**, **Where**, **When**, **Why**, or **How**). Watch the answer fill the yellow message pad and the turn badge pass to the next table.

## How a call works

- **Listen and ask:** Each of the six calls from Pine Hollow Park has three crackly gaps and one new vocabulary word. Tapping a wrong question word makes Ranger Sam explain that he did not say that part yet and replay the crackly sentence while the turn passes. After two misses on the same gap, the right card wiggles once to keep the call moving. **Say that again** replays the last sentence, and the gold **What does it mean?** card appears as soon as Sam uses the call's new word (*crew*, *kid*, *habitat*, *trail*, *owlets*, *tracks*).
- **Tell me more:** After all three gaps are filled, two follow-up question cards appear. Tap a card once to hear it read aloud, and tap it again (or tap **Ask it**) to ask Ranger Sam. One builds on Sam's story and adds a bonus picture to the notepad; the other is off topic, and Sam gently steers the class back.
- **Quiz:** The message pad slides away so the class answers from memory. Sam asks two questions, each with three shuffled picture choices. Tap a picture once to hear it, then tap **This one** (or tap the picture again) to answer.
- **Take a message (Grade 2):** Four picture cards from the call appear shuffled under the pad. Have the class recount the call out loud in order, then tap the cards into slots 1 to 4 and press **Read the message**. Grades K and 1 can press **Skip** to finish the call right after the quiz.

## Talk about it

- How could we tell that the first blank needed **What** and the next one needed **Where**?
- Why didn't **Say that again** fix the static by itself? What did we have to do next?
- In **Tell me more**, why did one question help us learn more about Ranger Sam's news while the other one stopped the story?

## Good to know

- Every sentence, question card, picture choice, and hint is spoken aloud, and every choice is a picture, so a pre-reader or a child playing alone can complete all six calls.
- In Call 3 (*The Turtle Crossing*), Ranger Sam models road safety on purpose: he carries the turtle in the direction it was already walking and reminds children to tell a grown-up rather than stepping into a road themselves.
- Tap **Calls** in the panel at any time to switch between the six park stories, or **Rules** to review the three listening rules and adjust the number of table groups from 2 to 4.
