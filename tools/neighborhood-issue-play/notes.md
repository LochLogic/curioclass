## What it teaches
Students can invent and perform a three-scene play from different neighbors' views of a community issue. They give characters wants and obstacles, design a set and costumes, and reflect on what their play says about their own community.

## Key skills
- Listen to different wants and reasons before choosing characters.
- Create a clash by putting one character in another character's way.
- Organize a story into a problem, a clash and a try at a fix.
- Make set, costume, movement and voice choices that support a character.
- Perform a role and explain the play's connection to a real community.

## Support at home
- On a walk, notice a place neighbors share. Talk about two different things people might want there.
- Take turns saying a made-up character's line with a big or small voice, fast or slowly. Ask what the choice tells you.
- Make up a short scene where two people want different things. Try a fix and ask who still needs help.

## A five-minute start
1. Pick a problem, then tap at least three neighbors. Their words appear on screen, without narration.
2. Tap Make the play and add two characters. Give one the other character as a blocker.
3. Visit Theater. Change the backdrop or add a prop, and dress the characters.
4. Visit Scenes. Starter lines follow your character choices. Edit them, then choose a fix in Scene 3 and tick whom it helps.
5. Tap Stage check and Perform. Choose a role, open Curtain and read aloud. Next line or Space advances. Finish with Bow and a reflection.

## The five steps
Street gathers viewpoints. Characters builds two or three neighbors with wants, blockers, voice sizes, speeds and movements. Theater offers three backdrops, two wings, up to three movable props and two costume pieces per character. Scenes holds three scenes of two to six lines. Perform presents the script at the student's pace, or uses Auto at 1.5 seconds plus 60 milliseconds per character.

The live stage check checks written wants, a chosen character blocker, and a proposed fix with someone named as helped. It checks the plan choices, not the meaning of custom dialogue or the quality of the performance. This limit is also stated on screen. Read Scene 2 to see whether competing wants come through, and Scene 3 to try the fix. A play can be performed with failing plan checks. The six-second preview deliberately gives every puppet only "Okay!" so the class can see a version without an expressed clash. Agreement, internal obstacles and quiet scenes can also be dramatically interesting; this activity practices an interpersonal clash.

## Talk together
- Whose reason did you first overlook? What changed when you heard it?
- Does the blocker make sense for both characters, or have you made someone unfairly bad?
- Who does your fix help? Who might still have a different want?
- What did a voice, movement, costume or set choice tell the audience?
- What does your play say about this neighborhood? What connects to your street?

## Good to know
The three issues are fictional. Park reopening in May is a fictional sign suggestion, not a real repair timetable. Crosswalk lines alone do not establish pedestrian safety; a real town considers local conditions. A play-street suggestion describes an authorized closure, never an invitation to play in an active road.

The restored third issue is neighbors who cannot yet talk to each other after a new family moves in. The family is never the problem. Fern speaks as a member of that family in this scenario, with ideas of her own to share. Greetings and pictures work both ways; an interpreter may help people understand one another. No language, nationality, accent or cultural identity is assigned. Do not ask a student to represent a language or community, or imitate an accent. Welcome suggestions are ideas for a fictional play, not evidence that every newly arrived family wants the same activity. Ask whose voice is still missing. TH:Cn10 and TH:Cn11 support these community connections, handled through listening and reflection.

All six neighbors are painted human paper puppets. The supplied names are retained as fictional character names. The set includes different ages, skin tones and bodies, a walking stick, a wheelchair and a stroller. Appearance does not identify a character's language. The third set is a neighbors' courtyard, replacing the empty-lot selection.

The whole play, including the closing answer, stays in localStorage on this device. Nothing is recorded, uploaded, printed or shared. If storage is unavailable, the tool says to keep the tab open. New play asks before clearing. Changing problems asks before resetting characters and script while retaining theater and costumes.

Motion off removes entrances, bobbing and curtain travel while preserving the same Auto and flat-preview timing. Props support pointer dragging and arrow keys after selection. All controls work by keyboard; reached step tabs support Left, Right, Home and End. Read speaks the current instruction, never the neighbors or the student script. Browser narration is used because no recordings were made in this build.
