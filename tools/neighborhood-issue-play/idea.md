# Neighborhood Issue Play (idea card)

- Module: arts-3-5-plays-about-our-community, grade 3-5. Codes: TH:Cr1, TH:Pr6, TH:Cn10, TH:Cn11
- Format / family: builder / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Walk a made-up neighborhood and hear what neighbors say about a real kind of problem (the park closed for repairs, no crosswalk by the school, a new family who speaks another language), turn what they said into characters who want things, build a paper toy theater (set, costumes, puppets) and perform the short play.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a street map with five neighbors to talk to and four problem cards.
  - Core loop: pick a problem, hear each neighbor (illustrated, each with a different view and a reason). Make two or three characters: what they want, what is in the way, how they move and speak. Sketch the set and costumes on the toy theater. Write or choose lines for three short scenes (problem, clash, a try at a fix).
  - The aha or the end state: the paper puppets perform on the toy theater while the student reads one role aloud; then a printable script and theater to perform for family. A last card asks what the play says about the neighborhood and the student's own street.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): small groups build one play together and perform for the class.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - TH:Cr1: inventing characters, the world and the set and costume ideas.
  - TH:Pr6: performing the play for an audience (family or class) and sharing a reflection.
  - TH:Cn10: connecting the play to the student's own community and experience.
  - TH:Cn11: drawing the play from a community issue and the neighbors' views.
- Not like: three-viewpoints-scene (grade 6-8 devising a scene from perspective cards); stage-blocker (grade 6-8 blocking); act-it-out (K-2 folktale).
- Art: generated scenes for the street; people (illustrated) for neighbors; paper toy theater pieces drawn in a cut-paper style.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Hear neighbors talk about a real kind of problem (a closed park, a missing crosswalk, a new family), turn them into characters who want things, build a paper toy theater with set and costumes, and perform your short play.
- Flags: overlap risk (three-viewpoints-scene; keep this one about building a play from a community and its set); records audio if the student records their role (on-screen line that it never leaves the device)
