# Neighborhood Issue Play

## One idea
A play about your street starts with neighbors who want different things: hear six neighbors on one real kind of problem, turn two or three of them into characters with a want and someone in their way, and the paper toy theater runs a stage check before the curtain opens: if nobody stands in anybody's way, the puppets just agree and the audience yawns, so you see that a play needs a clash.

## Not like
- three-viewpoints-scene (6-8, A3's band): devises one scene from perspective cards. Here the student builds a whole three-scene play (problem, clash, a try at a fix) from a community issue, with set and costumes, and performs it on a toy theater.
- stage-blocker (6-8, builder): places actors for sightlines. Here nothing is about sightlines; the build is characters, wants and a set.
- act-it-out (K-2): acts out a folktale. Here the story is invented from neighbors' views.
- audience-talkback (this band): judges two versions of a finished scene. Here the student makes the play.
- song-form-builder and virtual-gallery (builders): build forms and shows. Here the built thing is a play, tested by the stage check.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, theater, module "Plays About Our Community (Theater)".
- Topic line: making a play about a neighborhood problem
- Driver: students (pairs or small groups on one laptop) or teacher (projector; the class builds one play and reads the parts). 20 to 40 minutes, can span two sessions (saved on this device).
- Standards: TH:Cr1 (invent characters, the world, the set and costume ideas), TH:Pr6 (perform for an audience and reflect), TH:Cn10 (connect the play to your own community), TH:Cn11 (draw the play from a community issue and neighbors' views).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Steps across the top (always tappable once reached): `Street`, `Characters`, `Theater`, `Scenes`, `Perform`.

**Street.** Focus: the painted street (16:9) with six neighbors standing by their doors (paper puppets on sticks). Panel: three problem cards (Content). Tap a problem: a small sign appears on the street where it is (park gate, school corner, empty lot). Tap a neighbor: their view opens as a speech bubble in two lines (what they want, and why), read on screen. A checklist under the problem counts "Heard 0 of 6". `Make the play` is available after hearing at least three.

**Characters.** Focus: the character cards (up to three) in the stage area, each with its puppet. Tap `Add character` and pick a neighbor (2 to 3 characters). Each card has:
- `Wants`: the neighbor's want (from Street), or `Something else` with a short text box (60 characters).
- `Who or what is in the way?`: one of the other characters, or `The rules`, `Time`, `Money`, `Nobody`. Suggested blocker shown in gray from the views (Content).
- `How they talk`: two toggles, Big or Small voice, Fast or Slow.
- `How they move`: Hurry, Shuffle, Strut, Tiptoe (the puppet's entrance and bob in Perform).
- A one-line read-back under the card in plain words: "Officer Gail wants kids out of the street, but Nutmeg is in the way. Talks big and fast. Struts."

**Theater.** Focus: the paper toy theater (the painted proscenium front), centered and as large as fits. Panel: `Backdrop` (Park gate, School corner, Empty lot), `Wings` (two side pieces from: Tree, Lamp post, Fence, Bush), `Props` (up to three: Bench, Traffic cones, Seed packets, Wheelbarrow, Blank sign, Ball; drag to place on the stage floor), `Costumes` (per character, up to two: Hat, Scarf, Glasses, Apron, Sash, Backpack). Every change shows at once inside the theater. A blank sign gets a short text (16 characters) drawn in code.

**Scenes.** Focus: the script, three columns or tabs: `1 The problem`, `2 The clash`, `3 A try at a fix`. Each scene holds 2 to 6 lines. For each line: pick the speaker, then a suggested line (built from that character's want, reason and blocker; Content) or type one (90 characters). Scene 3 also asks `What do they try?` (three fixes for the problem, or type one) and `Who does it help?` (tick characters). `Stage check` sits at the bottom of the panel and runs before Perform:
- "Everyone wants something." (every character has a want)
- "Two wants clash." (at least one character's blocker is another character)
- "Scene 3 tries a fix." (a fix is chosen and helps at least one character)
Each line gets a tick or a cross and a reason in words. If "Two wants clash" fails, a 6-second Flat Preview plays in the theater: the puppets enter, each says "Okay!" in a bubble, and the code audience (rows of round paper heads, no faces) slowly droops and one gives a "Zzz". Then the line under it: "Nobody is in anybody's way, so nothing happens. Give someone a blocker."

**Perform.** Focus: the toy theater, full stage. `My role` chip row (pick the character you will read, or `I'll read them all`). `Curtain` opens the paper curtain (code), puppets enter on their sticks in the way they move (Hurry, Shuffle, Strut, Tiptoe), the speaker's puppet bobs, and the line shows in a bubble with its stage direction from How they talk ("(big and fast)"). `Next line` (or Space) advances; on your own role's lines the bubble is outlined in gold with "Your line! Read it aloud." Between scenes a paper card shows the scene name. After the last line, `Bow` (puppets bow; the code audience claps with a soft Web Audio clap) and the closing card: "What does your play say about this neighborhood? Is there a problem like this on your street?" with a text box (saved). `From the top` restarts.

## Content
**Neighbors** (paper puppets, animals; name; one line about them):
- Mrs. Shell, a tortoise: walks the block every morning, slowly.
- Rufus, a raccoon: runs the corner store.
- Officer Gail, a goose: the school crossing guard.
- Nutmeg, a squirrel: a nine-year-old who loves ball games.
- Councilor Bramble, a beaver: on the town council.
- Fern, a frog: a gardener with a baby in a stroller.

**Problems and views** (want; why; suggested blocker):
A. "The park is closed for repairs until spring."
- Mrs. Shell: wants a bench somewhere for now; "My knees need a place to rest on my walk."; blocker: Time.
- Rufus: wants the park open fast; "Families shop at my store after the park. Sales are down."; blocker: Councilor Bramble.
- Nutmeg: wants a place to play now; "We've been playing ball in the street. It's the only flat place."; blocker: Officer Gail.
- Officer Gail: wants kids out of the street; "Cars come around that corner fast."; blocker: Nutmeg.
- Councilor Bramble: wants the repairs done right, not rushed; "The slide was cracked. Rushing could make it unsafe again."; blocker: Rufus.
- Fern: wants the sandbox open at least; "My little one has nowhere to dig."; blocker: The rules.
Fixes: "A play street on Saturdays, closed to cars." (helps Nutmeg, Officer Gail, Fern); "A borrowed bench outside the library until spring." (Mrs. Shell); "A sign on the gate: Park opens in May, store open today!" (Rufus, Councilor Bramble).
B. "There's no crosswalk by the school."
- Officer Gail: wants a painted crosswalk and a sign; "I can't stop cars where there are no lines."; blocker: Councilor Bramble.
- Nutmeg: wants to walk to school alone; "My mom says no until there's a crosswalk."; blocker: Money.
- Rufus: wants to keep his two parking spots; "A crosswalk takes the spots in front of my store."; blocker: Officer Gail.
- Councilor Bramble: wants a car count first; "The town has to count the cars before it can paint lines."; blocker: Time.
- Mrs. Shell: wants more time to cross; "I'm slow. The cars don't wait."; blocker: The rules.
- Fern: wants slower cars; "A speed bump would help, too."; blocker: Rufus.
Fixes: "Kids and neighbors count the cars for a week, to help the town." (Councilor Bramble, Nutmeg, Officer Gail); "Move the crosswalk one house down, away from the store." (Rufus, Officer Gail); "A sign that says Slow, children crossing." (Fern, Mrs. Shell).
C. "The empty lot: a garden or parking?"
- Fern: wants a community garden; "We could grow food and flowers together."; blocker: Rufus.
- Rufus: wants parking; "Customers drive around the block looking for a spot."; blocker: Fern.
- Nutmeg: wants a small field to play in; "There's nowhere to kick a ball."; blocker: Rufus.
- Councilor Bramble: wants everyone to vote; "It's the town's land. Everyone should have a say."; blocker: Time.
- Mrs. Shell: wants shade trees; "There's no shade on this whole block."; blocker: Money.
- Officer Gail: wants it lit at night; "It's dark there, and people cut through it."; blocker: Money.
Fixes: "Half garden, half parking, with trees between." (Fern, Rufus, Mrs. Shell); "A town vote with a sign-up at the store." (Councilor Bramble, Rufus); "A garden with a lamp and a little patch of grass." (Fern, Nutmeg, Officer Gail).

**Suggested lines** (templates; `<want>`, `<why>`, `<blocker>` from the card, written so they read naturally):
- Scene 1: "<Name>: I want <want>. <why>" and "<Name>: Have you heard? <problem>"
- Scene 2 (speaker A, blocker B): "<A>: <want>, that's what we need!" / "<B>: But <B's want> matters too!" / "<A>: You're in my way, <B>." / "<B>: And you're in mine."
- Scene 2 (blocker is The rules, Time or Money): "<A>: I want <want>, but <the rules say no / there isn't time / there isn't enough money>."
- Scene 3: "<Name>: What if we tried this? <fix>" / "<Helped name>: That could work for me." / "<Not helped name>: It's a start. But what about <their want>?"
Wants are stored in two forms (a noun phrase for templates, "a bench somewhere for now", and the full sentence) so every template reads correctly; the builder checks all 6 neighbors by 3 problems by 4 template slots read cleanly.

## Interactions in detail
- Street views are read on screen; the voice speaks only the instruction lines (Voice), never the neighbors (their words are the student's material, read in their own time).
- A character can be removed (its lines go with it, after a confirm). Changing the problem keeps nothing but costumes and the theater, after a confirm ("A new problem starts a new play.").
- The stage check runs live as the student edits and again when `Perform` is tapped; Perform still opens with a failing check (the student may choose a flat play), but the failing line stays visible in the panel.
- Perform pacing is the student's: `Next line`, or `Auto` (each line stays up 1.5 s plus 60 ms a character). Every control stays live while puppets move: `Next line` during an entrance skips the entrance at once; `Curtain` again closes it; `From the top` stops everything.
- Motion off: puppets appear in place without sliding or bobbing; the speaker is shown by a gold outline instead of the bob; the curtain is open or shut with no slide.
- Saved on this device (localStorage `neighborhood-issue-play`): the whole play (problem, characters, theater, scenes, the closing answer). `New play` clears it after a confirm. Nothing is recorded; there is no print or share.
- Keyboard: steps are tabs; Space or Enter for `Next line`; props move with arrows when selected.
- Sizes: the street and theater fit 16:9 at real pixels; at 680x425 the panel is compact (the current step's main choice first, `Next line` always in view); at 375 wide the theater stacks above the panel and Scenes shows one scene at a time with its tab row.

## Voice

Not used by any 3-5 arts neighbor or another tool in this band (flipbook-studio, 6-8 media arts, also uses it; it is not a close neighbor). `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.step`.

- Checks run (Python, 2026-10-29): suggested blockers that are other neighbors: A has two mutual pairs (Nutmeg and Officer Gail, Rufus and Councilor Bramble), B has three one-way blocks (Officer Gail by Councilor Bramble, Rufus by Officer Gail, Fern by Rufus), C has one mutual pair (Fern and Rufus) and Nutmeg blocked by Rufus. So only 2 or 3 of the 15 possible pairs clash from the suggestions alone; the rest need the student to pick a blocker from the dropdown, which is the point of the stage check. The suggestion line therefore names a neighbor only when that neighbor is in the play, else "Try one of your other characters." Every neighbor is helped by at least one fix in every problem (no one left out in A, B or C). Contrast ratios with the WCAG 2.1 formula.

## Done when
1. Focus: at 1366x768, 680x425 and 375 wide the street, then the theater, are the largest thing in their steps, and `Next line` is always visible in Perform.
2. Two characters with no character blocker fail "Two wants clash" and the Flat Preview plays; setting one blocker makes it pass at once.
3. Every suggested line reads as a full sentence for every neighbor and problem.
4. Perform shows each line with its speaker bobbing (or outlined with Motion off), your role's lines in gold, and `Next line` during an entrance skips it at once.
5. Changing backdrop, wings, props or costumes updates the theater within one frame.
6. The whole play survives a reload; `New play` clears it after a confirm.
7. How lines are spoken once in a run of five edits; checkFail and flat are spoken each time they happen.
8. Nothing is recorded, printed or shared.
