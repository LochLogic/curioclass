## A 5-minute start

1. The rainforest is open. Ask: what living things do you see? Let children come up and tap one at a time.
2. Each tap adds the plant or animal to the field journal, with how many we have seen. Some hide in more than one spot.
3. When the rainforest is done, try the desert, then press **Compare**.

## The big idea

A habitat is a place where plants and animals live. Different habitats have different numbers of kinds of living things. That variety is called **diversity**. A warm, rainy rainforest has many more kinds than a hot, dry desert.

## What lives where

- **Rainforest (14 kinds):** fern, bromeliad, orchid, philodendron, toucan, sloth, red-eyed tree frog, jaguar, blue morpho butterfly, leafcutter ant, howler monkey, scarlet macaw, emerald tree boa, poison dart frog.
- **Desert (7):** saguaro cactus, prickly pear cactus, brittlebush, roadrunner, jackrabbit, horned lizard, desert tortoise.
- **Pond (10):** cattail, water lily, frog, painted turtle, mallard duck, dragonfly, sunfish, water strider, great blue heron, pond snail.
- **Backyard (9):** maple tree, dandelion, sunflower, robin, squirrel, earthworm, ladybug, honeybee, cottontail rabbit.

## Talk about it

- How many **kinds** did we find? How many living things in all? Why are those numbers different? (Three ants are one kind.)
- Which habitat has the most kinds? Why might that be?
- Three water striders skate on the pond. Do they count as three kinds or one?
- What living things would we find in our schoolyard?

## Good to know

- Real habitats hold far more kinds than these: thousands of insects, fungi and tiny living things. The counts here are a classroom model.
- Each habitat's journal stays while the tool is open; reloading starts fresh.
- Names are read aloud with a recorded natural voice. Sound starts on.
- Teacher-led on a projector or smartboard.
