## What it teaches
Students use real earthquake distributions, true-depth sections, seismic arrivals and a published reconstruction to inspect plate tectonics. The instrument has no assigned route or endpoint.

## Key skills
- Read magnitude, depth, dates and positions from real observations.
- Compare a map pattern with a cross-section through it.
- Measure P and S arrivals and interpret a spherical circle intersection.
- Distinguish observations, numerical models and schematic evidence.
- Read plate movement with its reference frame and averaging interval stated.

## Support at home
- Look at a world map together and compare coastline shapes across the Atlantic.
- Pin your town together and compare its nearby earthquake count with another place you know.
- Tonight, compare Japan and the middle of the Atlantic with a depth slice. Talk about what the dots show below the surface.

## A five-minute start
Open directly on the globe. Drag it, press Play, change the depth floor and inspect a dot. Let the student choose the next instrument. There is no quiz, target list or teacher gate.

## The instruments
Slice projects quakes within a 300 km swath onto a great-circle section. Quake distance scales are equal; the sea-floor relief is exaggerated x20 in a separate strip above the true-depth plot.
Stations starts randomly and cycles 20 shallow real epicentres and synthesizes surface-focus iasp91 traces. The channel selector keeps every station's markers accessible. The 26-minute trace has a five-minute ruler; compact views keep the trace beside its two markers.
Wave displays PREM ray paths and iasp91 arrival readouts. Rewind rotates published continental fragments to 200 Ma. Pin searches 1251 cities and measures local catalogue history, plate geometry and the nearest of 1609 NOAA volcano locations.

## Good to know
Content: This real earthquake archive includes 2004 Sumatra, 2011 Tohoku, 2008 Sichuan, 2015 Nepal and 2023 Kahramanmaras. The M8+ catalog and Strike also name real events; some students or families may have lived through these disasters.

The catalogue is the stated M5.5 size fallback: 26230 events in 1970-2025. A quiet area on this map does not establish low hazard. Older magnitudes and event origins can be revised.
Plate speed is the Merdith model's 0-5 Ma average after area-weighted removal of shared global rotation, not a GPS measurement. The correction covers 99.81 percent of Earth's surface on a 5 degree grid. EDGE MOTION is the relative opening or closing normal to the nearest plate edge. Do not use that model to explain Hawaii's hotspot track. Missing small topology areas show unknown; edge distances are model estimates.
Rewind's fossil and mountain regions are schematic evidence on reconstructed crust. They do not claim an organism lived at the dial's date. Rewind is one model, not recovered imagery of ancient Earth.
No casualty counts or images of harm appear. Everything is bundled, works offline and stays on the device. The Manual contains provenance and full limitations.

## Access
All controls have native keyboard support. Globe arrows turn, Shift turns farther, + and - zoom, Space plays, and Q/V/P/F/A switch layers. Enter inspects the nearest filtered earthquake to the centre or places the active instrument there. Motion off stops autonomous visual movement and uses year steps for playback. Read speaks current measurements.
