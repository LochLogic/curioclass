Living Earth / Manual

At the station

Drag the globe to turn it. Press Play to watch real earthquakes appear through time.

For the curious

Drag to spin. Play lets the plate margins emerge from the catalogue. Wheel, pinch or + and − zoom from 1× to 6×. Focus the globe and use arrows to turn 5 degrees, Shift for 20. With no instrument open, Enter inspects the filtered earthquake nearest the centre. Space plays when the globe or Play has focus; on another button it presses that button. Letter shortcuts and [ and ] also work after a button click. The five brass tabs open instruments.

The dots

Each dot is a real earthquake. Bigger dots mean bigger quakes; colour shows how deep they were.

For the curious

Every ComCat earthquake of magnitude 5.5 or greater from 1 January 1970 through 31 December 2025 is bundled: 26,230 events, retrieved 5 November 2026. The M5.0 coordinate pack exceeded the specified 700 KB threshold, so this is the stated M5.5 fallback. Millions of smaller quakes are left out. About 500 ComCat place names contain a question mark where an accent letter was lost at the source; they are kept as supplied. The catalogue is an observation history, never a forecast or a measure of local safety.

Positions are rounded to 0.01 degree, depth to 0.1 km, magnitude to a tenth, origin date to a UTC day. A few negative catalogue depths are clamped to the surface. Early magnitudes include older scales; origin solutions can be revised. Dot diameter encodes magnitude. Shallow quakes are salmon (0–70 km), intermediate quakes amber (70–300 km), deep quakes violet (300–700 km). Tap a dot for its date, place and numerical depth. The event selector lists M8+ events; every smaller bundled event is still tappable.

QUAKES counts the whole sphere under the filters; IN VIEW counts the half facing you. Turn from the Pacific to the Atlantic to compare them. The time fader includes the chosen calendar year; Play moves continuously through the dates. Q, V, P, F and A switch quakes, volcanoes, plate edges, relief and motion arrows. Present-day geography remains fixed during catalogue playback.

Slice

Draw a line across the globe to look below it. Dots in the section show how deep the quakes were.

For the curious

Drag A to B, up to 3,000 km. Earthquakes within 150 km of either side of the shortest great-circle path are projected into the section. Turn the globe and use Set end at centre twice for a keyboard section. Try Japan and the Mid-Atlantic, changing only depth to compare the patterns. Quake depths use equal horizontal and vertical kilometres. The ETOPO sea-floor profile has its own strip above the quake plot, exaggerated ×20. Its sea-level line is separate from the true-depth 0 km line. The 0.5 degree relief grid cannot resolve a narrow trench in detail.

Stations

Tap three places, then Strike to record a quake. Move P to the first wave and S to the second; the longer the wait, the farther away the quake.

For the curious

Place three stations on the globe, or turn it and press Place. Strike starts at a random event, then cycles through 20 geographically spread, shallow M6.5+ catalogue epicentres. Tap A, B or C on the globe, or use the channel selector, to inspect its trace. Move the P and S markers to the packet onsets. Arrow keys nudge a marker 0.5 s; Shift jumps 10 s. Each marker has its own labelled grab handle. A drag on the trace moves the nearer marker; Shift drags S. The horizontal trace is 0 to 26 minutes, with a five-minute ruler.

Circles uses the measured S minus P gaps and the bundled iasp91 arrival table to infer surface distance. Reveal shows the catalogue epicentre and its separation from the three-plane intersection, in kilometres. Coincident or badly placed stations can be ambiguous. If the three measured distances cannot meet in one place, the offset is not known. The mathematical intersection uses normalized three-plane fitting with a 200 km residual limit. A station in a direct-wave shadow has no usable S minus P circle. These are drawn traces, never recordings; the model uses a surface focus even for a catalogue quake whose actual focus was below it. It is an idealized measuring instrument.

Wave

Choose a place and press Fire. Watch waves bend through the Earth; S waves cannot pass through the liquid outer core.

For the curious

Tap a source, then Fire, or use Enter on the globe and the Fire control. Scrub elapsed time, or change receiver angle. Amber P rays and violet S rays follow PREM. The arrival readout separately uses iasp91, both calculated by TauP and bundled as numbers. The outer core is liquid: no direct S ray crosses it. This bundled iasp91 table ends direct S at 99 degrees and direct P at 98 degrees; far-side P returns at 140 degrees. The ring and shadow arc use these same edges. The common textbook shadow edge is about 104 degrees. Diffracted waves bend round the core in the gap; they are not drawn. Weak inner-core and converted arrivals can also exist there.

Ray coordinates are quantized to 0.01 degree, 0.1 km and 0.1 s. Arrival times are sampled every degree and linearly interpolated. P includes Pn and far-side PKP/PKIKP; S includes Sn. Reflections, diffraction and S-to-P conversions are left out. These radial model Earths smooth over the real interior’s variations. The cutaway always takes the source as its top.

Rewind

Turn the deep-time dial or press Play to move the continents into the past. The fossil outlines move with the rocks that hold them.

For the curious

The deep-time dial rotates continental fragments using Merdith and colleagues’ 2021 model, from now to 200 million years ago (Ma). Finite rotations are resolved through the published plate circuit relative to plate 000, sampled every 5 Ma, with quaternion interpolation between samples. These are reconstructed crustal outlines, not ancient coastlines or ancient sea-floor relief. Only fragments with a continuous usable rotation circuit across 0–200 Ma are drawn. Modern dots, volcanoes and relief are withheld.

Fossil belts are broad schematic regions redrawn after the USGS This Dynamic Earth figure, not specimen locations. Glossopteris and Mesosaurus are older evidence carried on the moving crust, not organisms asserted to live at the dial’s date. The dotted pink rock traces mark the broad Appalachian, Scottish and Scandinavian mountain belts. Solid fossil-region outlines mark Glossopteris; dashed outlines mark Mesosaurus, so colour is not the only key. Other reconstructions differ, especially farther back.

Pin and plate motion

Tap a place, or search for a city and press Find. Read the plate speed and whether its nearest edge is opening or closing.

For the curious

Tap any point or search the bundled Natural Earth city list, choose the matching city and press Find. It is a limited gazetteer; a town not listed can still be pinned on the globe. Escape removes the pin. Distances use a spherical radius of 6,371 km. Local earthquake counts always use the whole 1970–2025 M5.5+ archive, independently of the current filters.

Plate identity and nearest edge use resolved present-day Merdith topology. Forty-four polygons could be resolved from this archive; missing small regions say plate not known here. Polyline joins and decimation limit edge precision; two source joins span about 100 km. Within 100 km the readout says boundary area. A model boundary is not a surveyed fault, and these values do not measure hazard.

Speed and heading average the model from 5 Ma to now, with its shared global rotation removed. This is an area-weighted no-net-rotation estimate, not a GPS measurement. A 5 degree equal-angle grid, weighted by surface area, covers 99.81 percent of the globe in resolved plates. Arrows use the same numbers, with length scaled for visibility. EDGE MOTION compares the two plates normal to their nearest shared edge: opening means apart, closing means together. Sliding along an edge can still occur when this number is near zero. The model’s own README explicitly excludes Pacific hotspot-track analysis: do not use these arrows to explain the Hawaiian-Emperor bend. A missing rotation says UNAVAILABLE. Nearest volcano uses all 1,609 NOAA locations; a white triangle does not assert that the volcano is currently erupting.

Things people have found

Deep quakes under Japan tilt inland. Mid-Atlantic quakes stay shallow. Some station layouts give no single intersection.

  • Under Tonga and Fiji, some quakes approach 700 km deep inside the descending Pacific plate. USGS depth notes; IRIS source studies.
  • USGS estimates that the Pacific rim hosts about 81 percent of the world's largest earthquakes. That is a historical summary, not a percentage measured from this filtered display. USGS earthquake belts; FEMA hazard annex.
  • The largest instrumentally recorded earthquake was M9.5 in Chile on 22 May 1960, before this catalogue begins. USGS record; NOAA record.
  • Direct S waves cannot traverse the liquid outer core. Their far-side shadow is evidence for that liquid layer. The USGS and IRIS sources below explain the textbook shadow; this instrument uses the bundled table edges stated above.
  • In 1936, Inge Lehmann used P-wave observations to propose a solid inner core inside the liquid core. University of Copenhagen; American Physical Society.
  • Plates can open an ocean by a few centimetres a year, about the pace of fingernail growth. Compare EDGE MOTION across the Mid-Atlantic Ridge; the model rate varies along it. USGS plate motion; University of Malta research account.
  • Hawaii lies far inside the Pacific plate; its island and seamount chain becomes older toward the northwest. USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory; NOAA island formation.
  • Mesosaurus fossils occur in southern Africa and South America, evidence that those continents were connected. The USGS fossil figure and Freie Universität Berlin source below corroborate the connection.

Power and privacy

Motion stops the slow turn. Read speaks the measurements you can see.

For the curious

Motion off stops automatic rotation and the drum, and plays the archive in year steps. Sound controls switch tones and voice. Read speaks the current measurements. After a first drag, the hint says Press play; after first Play, it says Drag to spin. Opening an instrument keeps that hint silent. After that only Read speaks. Everything used by the instrument is in this one file. No network, account or location request is made. Settings stay on this device; no student work is recorded. Source links open in a new tab only when you choose them.
