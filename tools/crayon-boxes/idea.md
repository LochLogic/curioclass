# Crayon Boxes (idea card)

- Module: math-1-tens-and-ones, grade 1. Codes: 1.NBT.B
- Format / family: tycoon / base-ten. Fit: good, as a supply-room job over a school week: orders arrive each day and the closet's stock changes.
- One idea: Run the school supply closet: pack loose crayons into boxes of ten, fill each table's order as boxes and loose crayons, and compare two classrooms' stock with >, = and <.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a supply closet, a heap of loose crayons, empty ten-crayon boxes, and Mr. Okafor the custodian. "Pack the loose crayons into boxes of ten."
  - Core loop: the child drags crayons into a box; at ten it closes with a click. The shelf label updates (3 boxes and 4 loose, 34). Orders arrive ("Table 2 needs 46") and the child sends boxes and loose crayons. A box can be opened into ten loose ones when needed.
  - Special days: "a box and some more" (11 to 19), and full-box days (30 is 3 boxes and 0 loose).
  - Friday check: two classrooms' shelves side by side (52 and 47); the child compares boxes first, then loose, and picks >, = or <.
  - The aha or the end state: 52 beats 47 because it has more boxes, even though 7 loose is more than 2.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the teacher sets custom orders.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 1.NBT.B: a ten as a box of ten ones, 11 to 19 as a box and some more, decades as full boxes and no loose ones (1.NBT.2), and comparing two-digit numbers by tens then ones with >, = and < (1.NBT.3).
- Not like: ten-frame-spillover (kindergarten teens only), place-value-vault (grade 2 hundreds), elevator-hundred (adding with tens and ones).
- Art: generated scenes (a cozy supply closet), people (illustrated) for Mr. Okafor; crayons and boxes drawn in code so counts are exact.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run the school supply closet: pack loose crayons into boxes of ten, fill table orders as boxes and loose crayons, and on Friday compare two classrooms' stock with >, = and <.
- Flags: none
