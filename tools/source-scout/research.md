# Research packet: source-scout
Concept: For the question Should recess be longer?, scouts choose which sources to gather (a doctor's article, a survey, a toy ad, the principal's letter), weigh who might disagree, then sort each source's lines into fact and opinion to rate its trust.
Grade: 5   Standards: D1.5.3-5, D3.1.3-5, D3.2.3-5

## Standards in plain words

- D1.5.3-5: "Determine the kinds of sources that will be helpful in answering compelling and supporting questions, taking into consideration the different opinions people have about how to answer the questions." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to choose diverse sources (medical advice, school letters, surveys, advertisements) to answer a school policy question while considering conflicting perspectives.
- D3.1.3-5: "Gather relevant information from multiple sources while using the origin, structure, and context to guide the selection." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to gather facts from different document types, evaluating who created each source and why.
- D3.2.3-5: "Use distinctions among fact and opinion to determine the credibility of multiple sources." (https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf). The student must be able to sort verifiable facts from personal opinions to rate how trustworthy each source is.

## Real data

- Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) & SHAPE America: Strategies for Recess in Schools: https://www.cdc.gov/healthyschools/physicalactivity/recess.htm (Public domain). Evidence-based guidance, time recommendations, and physical activity statistics.
- American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) Policy Statement: The Crucial Role of Recess in School: https://www.aap.org (Professional medical policy). Pediatric consensus on cognitive rest and social development.
- National Association of Elementary School Principals (NAESP) School Leadership Reports: https://www.naesp.org (Educational leadership reference). Administrative scheduling considerations and instructional time laws.

## Common mistakes

- Believing numbers make a sentence an automatic fact: Fifth graders frequently assume that any claim with a number is a proven fact, missing that advertisers use statistics deceptively (Stanford History Education Group).
- Discarding all opinions as useless: Students often think opinions have no place in inquiry, overlooking that survey data and stakeholder opinions are essential for understanding community needs (NCSS C3 Framework).
- Treating commercial ads as neutral data: Students frequently trust colorful product advertisements without recognizing that the sponsor's goal is commercial sales rather than objective advice (Media Literacy Education).
- Expecting a single source to decide policy: Students often think a doctor's recommendation alone settles the school schedule, ignoring school board requirements for state instructional minutes and teacher contract rules (NCSS C3 Framework).

## Sensitivity

- School community balance: Present all school stakeholder viewpoints (students, parents, pediatricians, teachers, and principals) fairly and constructively. Avoid villainizing principals or teachers who must enforce state curriculum requirements.
- Medical guidance: Reflect official health consensus from the CDC and AAP (minimum 20 minutes daily recess, no withholding recess for punishment).
- None seen for living culture or religion.

## Similar tools elsewhere

- Stanford History Education Group / Digital Inquiry Group Civic Online Reasoning (https://cor.stanford.edu): Lateral reading activities training students to evaluate online credibility and fact versus opinion.
- News Literacy Project Checkology (https://checkology.org): Interactive sorting activities classifying statements into news, opinion, and sponsored content.
- PBS Media Literacy Classroom Activities: Media analysis modules dissecting advertising claims and scientific evidence.

## Gaps

- Source text length calibration: Authentic medical policy statements and administrative memos are written in adult professional prose; Curio Class spec writers should create leveled, 4-sentence document cards (Doctor's Note, Principal's Memo, Student Survey Result, Playground Ad) with clear claim lines suitable for fifth-grade drag-and-drop sorting.
