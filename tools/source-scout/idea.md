# Source Scout (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-5-source-scout, grade 5. Codes: D1.5.3-5, D3.1.3-5, D3.2.3-5
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Before anyone answers "Should recess be longer?", the student scouts a shelf of eight sources, keeps the four that best cover the question and the people who would disagree, then drags each kept source's sentences into Fact or Opinion and watches its trust meter settle, learning that true facts picked to sell something still need a second source.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a scout's clipboard with the question, an empty four-slot backpack and a shelf of eight sources (a doctor's article, a parent survey, a toy company ad, the principal's letter, a teacher's blog, a kid's opinion poster, a study on recess and focus, a playground injury report).
  - Core loop: tap a source to flip its tag (who made it, when, why); choose four for the backpack. A "who might disagree?" strip (kids, teachers, parents, the principal) lights up for each viewpoint the backpack covers. Then sort each kept source's lines into Fact or Opinion; the trust meter combines the sort with who made it and why.
  - The aha or the end state: the toy ad's one true fact ("kids who play outside move more") still earns low trust because it was chosen to sell; the strongest backpack covers both sides.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second question (Should our school have a garden?) with a new shelf.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D1.5.3-5: choosing which four sources help answer the question, with the disagree strip showing whose views are covered.
  - D3.1.3-5: the flip tags (maker, date, purpose, kind of source) guide which sources go in the backpack.
  - D3.2.3-5: the fact-or-opinion sort feeds each source's trust rating, with a reason line the student writes.
- Not like: made-for-whom (grade 4, infers audience and purpose of state-history sources); why-the-pony-express-ended (builds a claim from evidence); history-article-review (critiques a finished article).
- Art: people (illustrated) for each source's maker and generated scenes for the source objects, because kids judge sources by who stands behind them.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: none
