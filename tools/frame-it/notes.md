## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Shoot**. The harbor is in the viewfinder with the thirds grid on.
2. Drag the picture to aim, slide **Zoom** in a little, and put the lighthouse on one of the four circles where the grid lines cross.
3. Press **Take photo** (or Space). The review draws the thirds grid, the subject, the horizon and any leading lines on your photo, with what worked and what to try.
4. Move to **Assignments** and work through the twelve briefs. Each starts from a view that doesn't pass yet.

## The three modes

- **Shoot**: three large painted scenes (a harbor, a city street and a mountain lake). Zoom from 24 to 85 mm, tilt the camera, switch between landscape and portrait, and turn the grid on or off. The map shows where your frame sits in the whole scene. Every photo is reviewed on subject, rule of thirds, cut-off edges, size in the frame, horizon placement and tilt, leading lines and framing.
- **Assignments**: twelve briefs. Lighthouse on a third, big sky, follow the pier, fill the frame, level a crooked horizon, tracks to the tram, a frame within a frame, the rider on a third, a vertical photo of a tall tree, breaking the rule with a mirror reflection, showing scale with a tiny hiker, and an S-curve path.
- **Light studio**: a 3D still life (vase, apple, egg and wooden block). Drag the sun around the table on the dial, change its height, make it hard or soft, warm or cool, and add a fill card or a bright background. Five looks to make: side light for texture, a silhouette, soft and even, dramatic, and golden hour.

## How the review decides

- The **subject** is what the photo seems to be about: large, whole, and placed with care.
- **Thirds**: the subject's center is within a short distance of a point where the grid lines cross.
- **Horizon**: on a third line is praised, across the middle is flagged (except in the mirror brief), and a tilt over 1° is called crooked.
- **Leading lines**: a line (pier, tracks, path) runs well into the photo and ends at the subject.
- These are guidelines, not laws. The mirror brief is there to show breaking a rule on purpose.

## Talk about it

- Compare two of your photos of the same place. Which feels more alive, and why?
- When would you put a subject dead center on purpose?
- Why does a tiny hiker make mountains look bigger?
- In the light studio, which light makes the apple look most round? Which hides its shape?

## Good to know

- Arrow keys aim, + and - zoom, [ and ] tilt, Space takes the photo.
- **Save photo** downloads a PNG to this device. Nothing is uploaded.
- Assignment stars are saved on this device.
- The light studio needs WebGL, which almost every school browser has.
