## What it teaches
Students design and critique an experiment using assignment, control, placebo, blinding, blocking and paired comparisons. They use a randomization test to judge whether a measured difference could be explained by chance, while distinguishing that question from bias.

## Key skills
- Separate a treatment effect from practice, expectations and group differences.
- Explain why random assignment differs from letting people choose.
- Use a placebo and blinding to reduce systematic differences.
- Read a shuffle distribution and compare bias with spread across repeated studies.
- Revise a methods section in response to a specific criticism.

## Support at home
- Ask your student to explain why testing a skill twice can make an apparent improvement misleading.
- Take a familiar headline about a treatment and ask who chose the groups and who knew the treatment.
- Discuss why a fair test can still produce an uncertain result, without doing an energy drink experiment.

## Five-minute start
Open Story and run the initial design. Press Shuffle check, then Submit to the journal. Read the editor's criticism, revise its outlined row, and run again. The first large apparent benefit combines a small treatment effect with practice, expectation and informed stopwatch timing.

## Modes
Story follows six editable Methods rows. A returned letter exposes one flaw at a time. Published and Pilot both require the editor's prescribed methods checks; Pilot means the shuffle result did not cross the flag threshold. Skipping review after a rejection produces Retracted and a fresh paired replication.

Bench runs 400 new studies of the current design. Pin up to three designs to compare distributions on one common horizontal scale. The mean shows the center, SD shows spread, and Flagged is the share with fewer than 50 of 1,000 shuffled differences at least as far from zero as the observed difference.

## Talk questions
- Why can an extremely unusual shuffle result still come from a biased design?
- Which differences disappear when everyone takes both conditions? Which require random order?
- What does blocking within gamers guarantee? What can still differ by chance?
- Why does random assignment support a causal comparison without making these 40 volunteers representative of everyone?
- Why does computer timing change spread as well as removing the informed stopwatch effect?

## Good to know
The lab, Dr. Mara Quill, The Open Bench Journal, all volunteers and all numerical effects are made up. Volunteers are adults aged 18 to 22. This is a statistics simulation, not advice to consume energy drinks. The American Academy of Pediatrics advises that children and teens should not drink energy drinks.

Negative estimates mean faster with the drink. The model fixes the treatment effect at -10 ms. Person effects have SD 20 ms, session noise SD 8 ms, and stopwatch noise SD 30 ms. Gamer and sleep traits affect baseline time, not the treatment effect. Water reveals treatment; placebo permits blinding. Blocking splits gamers equally; paired random order puts exactly half on each first-day treatment.

Two-group shuffle checks re-deal labels among recorded times, keeping group sizes fixed. Paired checks flip each person's difference independently. This is a teaching randomization model; it does not model caffeine biology, carryover, safety, recruitment or generalization beyond the volunteers. The randomization test does not repair an observational comparison.
