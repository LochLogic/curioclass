# Bowling Lab (idea card)

- Module: science-4-energy-and-motion, grade 4. Codes: 4-PS3-1, 4-PS3-2, 4-PS3-3
- Format / family: lab / none. Fit: good for 4-PS3-1 and 4-PS3-3; 4-PS3-2 moves (see below).
- One idea: Roll a ball from different heights on a ramp into a set of pins, measure its speed with a light gate and how far the pins fly, and find that a faster ball carries more energy; then predict what happens to that energy in a collision (pins move, a crash sound, a little warmth).
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a school-hallway bowling lane with a ramp, height marks, a light gate and a set of pins. "Which height will knock the most pins? Predict first."
  - Core loop: choose the ramp height and ball, predict, roll; the light gate reads the speed and the table logs speed, pins knocked and how far the farthest pin slid. The student graphs speed against pins and distance.
  - Collision questions: before each crash the student picks what will change (the ball slows, pins speed up, a sound, a tiny temperature rise on a heat camera) and checks; a heavier and a lighter ball at the same speed show mass matters too.
  - The aha or the end state: an explanation card from the student's own data: faster means more energy, and in a collision energy moves from the ball into the pins, sound and heat.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 4-PS3-1: the speed and pins data are the evidence that a faster object has more energy.
  - 4-PS3-3: asking questions and predicting energy changes before each collision, then checking.
  - MOVE 4-PS3-2: to contraption-workshop, whose chains carry energy by electric current, light, sound and heat between parts and can show each transfer in full.
- Not like: contraption-workshop (energy conversions in a device); collision-lab (grade 11 momentum); push-the-pumpkin (kindergarten push size).
- Art: generated scenes for the lane and pins, with the light gate, graph and heat camera drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Roll a ball from different ramp heights into bowling pins, measure its speed and how far the pins fly, and predict where the energy goes when ball meets pins.
- Flags: none
