## What it teaches

Students design a brand to a client brief, then refine it using evidence from size, crop, contrast and printing tests.
They learn why an identity needs a system of lockups and why reproduction tests cannot judge personality or intent.

## Key skills

- Connect a symbol, typeface and palette to three descriptive words.
- Compare the same design across physical and digital surfaces.
- Read contrast ratios and distinguish text contrast from non-text contrast.
- Revise a design when a small-size, crop or one-color test exposes a problem.
- Give a partner specific evidence for a critique and a revision.

## Support at home

- Look at a bag or package together. Ask which details would survive if it were printed in one ink.
- Compare a shop sign and its small app or social icon. Notice what stays and what changes.
- Describe a made-up business with three words, then sketch a mark that communicates one of them.

## A 5-minute start

Pick Pocket Moon Bakery. Start the brand, choose a symbol and change a typeface or color.
Open Test and inspect the outlined paper-bag tile. Read its evidence, then use Fix in Design.
Try a two-color heart: its touching colors become a single region in one ink.
Change to a line mark and test again. A container can frame a mark, but cannot separate touching colors inside it.

## Modes

- Brief: six fictional clients, each with an audience, three words and a required surface. An own-business form stores text locally.
- Design: SVG symbol, container, monogram, type, palette and lockup controls. Undo and redo affect the current brand.
- Test: eight surfaces, their measured tests, and student-entered brief-fit judgments. Tap a tile to see the reason and a repair route.
- Sheet: lockups, clear space, measured minimum mark size, colors, ratios, typography and fit notes. Print this sheet, then swap seats.

## Talk questions

What do you notice first? Which choice says one of the brief's words?
Which surface is least forgiving? What would you change first, and what might that change cost elsewhere?
Can a brand pass every reproduction test and still miss the client's intent?

## Good to know

This tool serves grades 9-12 media arts: MA:Cr2.1.HSI, MA:Cr3.1.HSI, VA:Cr3.1.HSI and MA:Re9.1.HSI.
Students develop to a brief, evaluate evidence, revise and critique a partner's work.
The businesses and taglines are fictional. No personal names are requested. Brands stay in this browser's localStorage.
Clear all brands asks before deleting local saved work. Storage may be unavailable in private or restricted browsers.

The 80% silhouette, 30%-80% icon coverage, 92% crop, 1.5:1 gray and 25% clear-space cutoffs are this tool's own conventions.
WCAG supplies the contrast formula and 4.5:1 text, 3:1 large-text and 3:1 non-text thresholds.
Passing a simulation is not proof that a real sign is readable at a particular distance.

All eight specified typefaces are bundled inside the file. The six restored families use locally installed fonts,
converted to Latin WOFF2 subsets. Bebas Neue and Pacifico each have one weight; the other six offer Regular and Bold.
Narration uses Curio Class's queue and browser speech until recordings are supplied; no audio was recorded in this build.
