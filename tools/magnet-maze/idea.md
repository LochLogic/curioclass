# Magnet Maze (idea card)

- Module: science-3-magnets-and-static-electricity, grade 3. Codes: 3-PS2-3, 3-PS2-4
- Format / family: puzzle / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Steer a steel ball through mazes with magnets you place but never touch to the ball, find out how distance, which pole faces and what is in between change the pull, solve static levels with a rubbed balloon, then write your own design problem a magnet can fix and solve it.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a tabletop maze, a steel ball and a tray with two bar magnets. "Get the ball to the star. You can't touch it."
  - Core loop: place and turn magnets; the ball rolls. Each level asks one cause-and-effect question first ("Will it pull from farther away?", "What if we flip the magnet?", "Does it pull through paper? through wood?"), the student predicts, then plays. Some pieces are aluminum and copper, which do not move.
  - Static levels: rub a balloon on a sweater to pick up paper bits or bend a thin stream of water; rub two balloons and see them push apart. A charge meter shows how much rubbing and how close changes the pull.
  - Design level: three short problem stories (a cabinet door that swings open, steel cans mixed with aluminum cans at a recycling center, a toolbox lid that loses its screws). The student writes the problem: what must the fix do (criteria) and what limits it (constraints), then builds a magnet solution and tests it.
  - The aha or the end state: a level map with each question answered, and the student's own design problem with its working fix.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3-PS2-3: each level's question and test about magnetic or electric pulls between objects not touching (distance, orientation, material between).
  - 3-PS2-4: the design level, defining a simple design problem with criteria and constraints and solving it with magnets.
- Not like: tug-of-war-arena (contact forces on a rope); contraption-workshop (energy chains).
- Art: generated scenes for the maze table and the problem stories; field lines and the charge meter drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Steer a steel ball through mazes with magnets you never touch to it, test how distance, poles and materials change the pull, try static levels with a balloon, then define and solve your own magnet design problem.
- Flags: none
