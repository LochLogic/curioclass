## What it teaches

Poet's Workbench makes syllables, stress, rhyme and repeated sounds visible while a writer works.
It supports the sound half of RL.7.4 and the form work of RL.7.5, with an approachable desk for younger writers and room for experienced poets.
It is a Curiosity and a second module card; this build has no teacher lesson plan.

## Key skills

- Hear strong and weak syllables and compare a line with a repeating foot.
- Distinguish a pronunciation from its spelling, including alternate pronunciations.
- Explore perfect rhymes, slant rhymes and the effects of repeated sounds.
- Compare stanza patterns and revise a poem while keeping ownership of its language.

## Support at home

- Say family names together and tap the beats you hear. Notice where your pronunciations differ from the page.
- Read a familiar poem aloud twice, emphasising different words. Talk about what changes.
- Find rhyming sounds during a walk. Invented words are welcome; the dictionary need not know them.

## A few minutes at the desk

The scanned Frost line is ready on open. Type beneath it, tap a word, change a mark, or open a book from the shelf.
There is no sequence to follow. The Manual explains the marks and the keyboard shortcuts when wanted.
K to 2 learners can dictate to an adult and clap the beats. Independent use is tuned for grade 7.

## Papers and controls

Plain, haiku, limerick, common meter and sonnet papers add faint guides. They never grade a poem.
Play taps the caret's line, or the nearest nonempty line above; Shift Play taps the poem. Sounds marks alliteration and assonance.
Alt R opens rhymes; the full-size Stress key or Alt F changes stress or pronunciation at the caret.
The tempo changes the running beat without stopping playback. Four drafts stay in this browser on this device.
Print includes the proof marks. A device-local English voice, if available, enables Speak.
Read uses only the fixed hint recording; this build declares the smooth-narrator line but has no recorded clip.

## Good to know

The full filtered CMU dictionary is bundled. It represents one American English pronunciation set.
Unknown words use spelling estimates and dotted marks. Meter is a closest pattern, rather than a rule about what a poet must do.
Some shelf editions differ from modern editions. Long dashes become commas, with an existing colon or semicolon kept alone.
Content notes: shelf poems include death, grief, war, religious imagery and Hughes's celebration of Black identity. Haiku paper is an English writing convention.
Draft storage may be unavailable or cleared; the desk still works without it.
No text leaves the instrument; links in the Manual open only when a person chooses them.

## Readouts and source checks

Rhyme keys fold CMU IY R into IH R, retaining original displayed phones. Other pre-R pairs were inspected and kept distinct because of accent variation.
CMU primary stress stays strong; secondary stress is flexible and grey. Known hyphenated parts, possessives, contracted -'d and some un-/-able forms use explicit word-building estimates.
Unknown coinages retain dotted spelling estimates. Auto still chooses each line's closest pattern, breaking ties toward the poem's most common foot; it does not force the poem's foot onto a different line.
The match count reports fixed syllables only and separately states flexible syllables. No score, goal, praise or grading is attached.

`_scratch/poets-workbench/fix-facts.json` records the touched poem lines and form observations against Project Gutenberg and the Academy of American Poets.
Both are witnesses to public-domain literary text; no modern site prose is copied. The four form-to-meaning notes are original interpretations labelled "One reading" (Curio Class, CC0).
The Raven's source colon is retained; its independent witness uses a semicolon. Annabel Lee retains its source colon. Older-edition wording is preserved.
Cambridge's pronunciation of here and Merriam-Webster's pronunciation guide were consulted as references only, with no copyrighted prose or audio bundled.

Read visibly restores "Type a line." and announces it to assistive technology. Its smooth-narrator clip remains intentionally absent until the collective Forge pass; it does not use browser speech for student text.
The optional Speak control still accepts only a device-local English voice. `voice/lines.json` remains equal to `allLines()`.
