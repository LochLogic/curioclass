# Checks and Balances (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-congress-the-president-and-the-courts, grade 12. Codes: D2.Civ.4.9-12, D2.Civ.11.9-12
- Format / family: cards / bill-to-law. Fit: good
- One idea: Play one branch in a card game against the other two (computer-played by default): pass, block, sign, veto, override, appoint, confirm and strike down, while a scoreboard tracks not just who wins but what each route to a decision achieved (speed, durability, protection of rights, public support).
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three seats (Congress, the President, the Court) and a policy on the table, for example a national data-privacy rule; the student picks a seat.
  - Core loop: each turn, play an action card from your branch's real powers; the other branches answer with their checks (veto, override, judicial review, confirmation, the power of the purse, executive orders and their limits). Each card cites its constitutional source and one real example.
  - Purposes scoreboard: when the policy ends (law, order, ruling or dead), the board scores the procedure that produced it: how fast, how lasting, how many voices heard, rights protected.
  - The aha or the end state: replay the same policy through a different route (law vs executive order vs court case) and through a state legislature or an international agreement, and compare the scoreboards; a short "how the balance shifted" note covers powers that grew or shrank over time.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): three students or teams play the three seats.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Civ.4.9-12: the cards are the Constitution's powers and limits, with the shifted-balance note on how they changed and are still contested.
  - D2.Civ.11.9-12: the purposes scoreboard evaluates different decision procedures, including state and international ones, by the civic purposes they achieve.
- Not like: bill-to-law-board-game (family sibling, state lawmaking for grade 4); federal-or-state (levels of government); supreme-court-simulator (ruling on rights cases).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the branches' figures and generated scenes of the Capitol, White House and Court, with designed cards, because the card art carries the game's feel.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Play one branch against the other two in a card game of real checks (veto, override, judicial review, confirmation), then score each route to a decision on speed, durability and rights, and replay it another way.
- Flags: none
