# Research packet: school-fix-it-meeting

Concept: The class picks a school problem (a long lunch line, a crowded hallway), surveys classmates and adults on why it matters to them, talks proposals through with turn-taking rules, and votes on a plan to bring to the principal.

Grade: 3   Standards: D1.1.3-5, D2.Civ.9.3-5, D4.8.3-5, AASL.Collaborate

## Standards in plain words

- D1.1.3-5 asks students to explain why compelling questions are important to others, including peers and school staff. School problems affect different members of the building in distinct ways.
- D2.Civ.9.3-5 asks students to use deliberative processes when making decisions or reaching judgments as a group. Discussing solutions requires listening, turn-taking, and evaluating evidence.
- D4.8.3-5 asks students to apply deliberative and democratic procedures to make decisions and take action in their classrooms and schools.
- AASL Collaborate asks learners to work productively with others to broaden perspectives, solve shared problems, and work toward common goals.

## Real data

- CDC Healthy Schools Guideline: At least 20 minutes of seat time for lunch (excluding transit, line waiting, and tray clearing); 10 minutes for breakfast.
- NFPA 101 Life Safety Code (Educational Occupancies): 6 feet (72 inches) minimum unobstructed clear width for exit corridors.
- AASL Standards Framework: Shared Foundation III (Collaborate), emphasizing active listening, turn-taking, and peer consensus.

## Common mistakes

- Conflating line waiting time with lunch period: Third graders frequently assume a 30-minute lunch period means 30 minutes of eating, overlooking that 15 minutes in line leaves only 15 minutes of seat time.
- Voting without deliberation: Students often rush to a show of hands before hearing how proposals affect others (such as cafeteria workers or younger grades).
- Misidentifying authority: Students may assume a classroom vote automatically changes school rules, rather than serving as a formal proposal to be presented to the principal.

## Sensitivity

- Cafeteria and nutrition sensitivities: Avoid shaming students who receive free or reduced-price meals or take longer to eat; lunch line discussions should focus on logistics, schedules, and flow.
- Hallway behavioral dynamics: Avoid framing hallway congestion as purely "bad behavior" by students; highlight architectural limits, locker placement, and staggered release times.

## Similar tools elsewhere

- Responsive Classroom: Morning Meeting and problem-solving class conference protocols.
- iCivics: Classroom deliberation modules and Student Voice initiatives.

## Gaps

- School policies vary widely on cafeteria flow and transition schedules; Curio Class simulations should provide realistic structural choices (staggering grade release times, opening a second salad bar, creating one-way hallway traffic) that fit elementary school operations without requiring complex budget models.
