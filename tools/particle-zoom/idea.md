# Particle Zoom (idea card)

- Module: science-5-matter-is-made-of-particles, grade 5. Codes: 5-PS1-1, 5-PS1-2
- Format / family: explorer / particles. Fit: good
- One idea: Zoom from a kitchen counter into sugar dissolving in tea, air pumped into a balloon, a puddle drying and ice melting to see particles too small to see explain each change, and weigh everything before and after on a scale to find the total weight never changes.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a kitchen counter with a cup of tea, a sugar cube, a balloon pump, a glass of ice and a digital scale. "Where does the sugar go? Zoom in."
  - Core loop: pick a change, watch it at normal size, then zoom with a slider through a magnifier, a microscope and finally a particle view where each kind of particle is a colored ball. The student explains in their own words, then compares with a caption.
  - Scale bench: weigh the cup and the sugar, dissolve, weigh again; melt ice in a sealed bag; mix baking soda and vinegar in a sealed bottle with a balloon on top, then in an open cup. The student graphs before and after and spots that only the open cup lost weight, because gas escaped.
  - The aha or the end state: a claim card: matter is made of particles too small to see, and in melting, dissolving, heating and mixing the total weight stays the same.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 5-PS1-1: the zoom views are the model of particles explaining dissolving, an inflating balloon and evaporation.
  - 5-PS1-2: measuring and graphing weights before and after heating, cooling and mixing show the total weight is conserved.
- Not like: particle-party (grade 7 phase changes and heating curves, same engine); mystery-powders (identifying substances).
- Art: generated scenes for the kitchen; the zoom and particle views drawn in code so particles move accurately.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Zoom from a kitchen counter into dissolving sugar, an inflating balloon and melting ice to see particles too small to see, and weigh each change before and after to find the total weight stays the same.
- Flags: none
