# Eras of Computing (idea card)

- Module: computer-science-6-8-computing-history-and-impact, grade 6-8. Codes: MS-SOC-HI-38, MS-SOC-HI-39, MS-SOC-ET-42
- Format / family: timeline / none. Fit: good, because eras, actors and effects line up in time, and the lessons carry forward to a present choice.
- One idea: Place the inventors, communities, companies and governments who shaped computing across five eras, follow one past technology (the personal computer) through its intended effects and its surprises (new jobs, lost jobs, privacy, e-waste), then use those patterns to argue for or against delivery drones in your own town.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a long timeline wall with five era bands (war-time machines, mainframes, personal computers, the web, phones and AI) and a pile of actor cards.
  - Place: drag each actor card (a government code-breaking team, a university lab, a garage start-up, a hobby club, a standards group) to its era and see what they added.
  - Follow one thread: the personal computer's intended effects (work at home, learning) and surprises (typing pools vanish, data about users piles up, old machines shipped abroad as e-waste) appear one by one; the student sorts each as intended or surprise, helped or hurt, and who.
  - Local debate: a town council asks about drone delivery; the student builds a case using three patterns from the timeline (who gains jobs, whose privacy, where old drones go) and hears two neighbors answer.
  - The aha or the end state: technologies bring planned and unplanned effects, made by many kinds of actors, so judging a new one means asking the old questions.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-SOC-HI-38: place the actors who shaped computing across its eras and say what each added.
  - MS-SOC-HI-39: sort one technology's intended and unintended effects and who they touched.
  - MS-SOC-ET-42: argue for or against a new technology in your community using evidence from the past.
- Not like: inventors-hall (grade 3-5 inventions and inventors); cloud-through-time (grade 9-12 data centers); algorithm-audit (grade 6-8 bias).
- Art: public domain photos for real historical moments where checked (Library of Congress, Smithsonian open access), otherwise generated era scenes.
