## What it teaches

Students describe and sketch graphs from stories, then construct a linear model and explain its rate of change and initial value.

## Key skills

- Read increasing, decreasing and constant pieces in context.
- Distinguish a straight piece from one getting steeper or flatter.
- Build a graph from words and compare its read-back with the story.
- Find a steady rate and a starting value from words, points, tables and graphs.
- Explain each model number with its units.

## Support at home

- Describe a walk with a pause. Ask which parts of a distance-from-home graph would rise, fall or stay flat.
- Think of something saved each week. Ask how the amount already saved differs from the amount added each week.
- Sketch a ball's height during a toss on paper. Discuss the brief turning point, without drawing a flat plateau.

## Five-minute start

Open Match. Read Lucia's morning, pick one graph and choose it. Tag the pieces, then check.
A graph of distance from home is not a picture of the road. Try Sketch's hill story to test that distinction.

## Modes

Match has six core stories, Sketch five and Model six. A result other than Matched adds an unused spare,
up to nine, seven and nine stories respectively. There are no points, timers or confetti.

Match allows one graph pick and two tag checks. Sketch allows two checks, then shows one working shape.
Collinear straight pieces merge before checking. Free draw gives read-backs without marks and preserves the round.
Sketch feedback marks the part whose shape, starting amount or relative rate differs. The read-back names the
student's starting position and compares repeated rises or falls across pauses, without prescribing a correction.
Model accepts decimals, fractions and a leading dollar or minus sign. Its line appears after Check, never while typing.
Each meaning choice locks after one tap. Shown work never becomes a first-try Matched result through extra practice.

## Talk questions

- What quantity is on the vertical axis? Would the same shape tell a different story with another quantity?
- Does going down always mean slowing down? What does a downward distance-from-home piece actually mean?
- Why might the first table row fail to give the starting value?
- What are the units of the rate, and the units of the initial amount?

## Good to know

Grade 8, Common Core 8.F.B.4 and 8.F.B.5. All characters, prices and amounts are made up.
The ball story ignores air resistance. The bottle and candle stories deliberately idealize steady rates.
The unnumbered bent pieces are qualitative sketches, not exact projectile trajectories or measured growth curves.
The gift-card line reaches zero at 80/7 smoothies, about 11.4; the balance pays for eleven whole purchases.
In Model, a negative rate is shown in the equation; meaning cards explain its magnitude and direction in words.
Progress stays in this browser only. Clear progress asks once, then resets marks and spare stories.
The end-of-set notebook names every completed story beside its saved mark.
No account, recording, upload, analytics or network connection is used. Browser narration is the Build fallback.
