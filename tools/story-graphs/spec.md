# Story Graphs

## One idea
A graph is a story told in shape, and the tool reads any graph back to you as a story in words ("the water gets deeper at a steady rate, then stays the same"), so you can test a graph against a story line by line, in both directions; and a story that changes at a steady rate comes down to two numbers, where it starts and how fast it changes.

## Not like
- motion-graph-match (grade 11, graphing): you make position, velocity and acceleration graphs live by driving a cart, in real units. Here nothing moves in real time; you read short life stories (water, money, plants, a ball, Lucia's walks) and match, tag and build graphs by their shape, with no numbers on the sketch axes.
- graph-story-lab (grade 9, graphing): given real function graphs, you scrub a cursor and mark domain, range, maxima and average rate. Here there is no cursor or scene scrub; the student picks, tags and builds whole shapes from words, and the words come back from the graph. No story repeats (no phone battery, tea, hike or parking).
- two-trains (grade 8, graphing): two lines and where they meet. Here one story at a time, and the point is shape (up, down, flat, steady, curving) and the meaning of one line's two numbers.
- ski-slope-designer (grade 8, graphing): slope as steepness of a ski run, similar slope triangles, y = mx + b from a lodge (8.EE.6). Here slope is never felt or proved; it is read as "how fast the story changes", always with its unit and context, alongside the starting value.
- function-machine-factory (grade 8, 8.F.A): tests whether a rule is a function and compares two functions. Here every round is one story, never a comparison, and the question is what the shape and the numbers say about that story.
- data-detective (grade 8, 8.SP): fits a line by eye to noisy real data. Here the data follow an exact steady rule, and the line is built from a description, two points, a table or a graph.
- model-my-world (grade 9): you type a whole equation that must hit every row of a record, then solve with it. Here the form is fixed (y = m x + b), only m and b are typed, the line is shown only after Check, and the work that counts is saying what each number means.

## Who and how
Grade 8, math, Modeling with Functions. Standard 8.F.B (8.F.4 construct a linear model and interpret rate of change and initial value; 8.F.5 describe a graph qualitatively and sketch a graph from a verbal description). Driver: students, alone or in pairs on laptops (tool.json "students"); works on a projector with one student at the keyboard. 20 to 35 minutes. Modes `Match`, `Sketch`, `Model`. Prompts are not personal; no disclosure note needed.
Band 6-8: light theme set on `:root` by the tool (do not link `kit/theme-912.css`). Link `kit:curio` then `kit:graphing`. A student narrator, Lucia, an eighth grader, tells most stories in first person. Playful, never babyish: comic panels, a chime on a right answer, no confetti, no points.

## Standards map
| Code | Student action that teaches it | What they see | Done when check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8.F.5 (describe) | Match: picks the one graph of four that tells Lucia's story, then tags every piece of it: Up, Down or Flat, and Straight, Getting steeper or Getting flatter (straight = linear piece, curving = nonlinear) | a wrong pick shows "This graph says: ..." beside the story; tags checked per piece; then each story sentence lights with its piece | Done when 3 and 4 |
| 8.F.5 (sketch) | Sketch: builds a graph from a new story by placing points and bending pieces, then Check compares each story sentence with what the graph says | a two-column list, Story and Your graph, with a mark per line; a passing graph plays back with each sentence lighting as its piece plays | Done when 5 and 6 |
| 8.F.4 | Model: from a description, two points, a table or a graph, types m and b into the story's own template (g = __ t + __), then picks what each number means in the story | after Check, their line drawn through the story's points with hits and misses, a note naming the error kind; then two sets of four meaning cards | Done when 7 and 8 |

### Match
Focus: the four graph cards, then the one big graph in the tag step.
Layout at 1366x768: stage row 1 is the comic strip (three 4:3 panels, 200x150 each, ink borders, comic gutters) with the story to its right or under it (sentences as plain text, 17 px); row 2 the four graph cards in one row (each about 220x230, same size, same ink color, same axes and labels, piece count equal across the four). Panel: bubble, `Choose this graph` (disabled until a card is selected), round dots, hint. In the tag step the cards are replaced by the chosen graph at about 900x430, the comic strip shrinks to 110 px tall, and the panel shows the tag chips and `Check`, `Next story`. All of this sits above the fold. At 680x425 the comic strip and Lucia's head fold away (story text stays), the cards go 2x2 (about 230x150 each), the panel stays beside the stage compact; the tag step graph fills the stage.
Steps: (1) tap a card, press `Choose this graph`. One pick only. Right: check icon, `pickRight`, the graph grows into the tag step. Wrong: X icon on the pick, its read-back "This graph says: ..." under it and the trap note in the bubble, then the right card gets its check icon and the button becomes `Tag the pieces`. (2) Tag step: numbered piece buttons (44 px circles at each piece's middle); piece 1 is selected. Chips: row 1 `Up`, `Down`, `Flat` (arrow icons); row 2 `Straight`, `Getting steeper`, `Getting flatter` (small curve icons). Tap chips or drag a chip onto a piece. `Flat` sets the piece and disables row 2 for that piece. A piece with its tags complete moves the selection to the next untagged piece. Tags show as badges (icon plus word) beside each piece. `Check` is enabled once every piece is tagged. (3) After tags are right or shown, every story sentence gets the number chip of its piece, and focusing either lights both. `Next story` is enabled.

### Sketch
Focus: the sketch plane.
Layout at 1366x768: stage row 1 the story scene (4:1, 600x150) beside or over the story text (sentences); row 2 the plane, about 900x440: x axis "time", y axis the story's quantity, grid 12 by 8 with no number labels except 0 (and "full" or "empty" where the story names them). Panel: `Check`, piece row (`Piece` with left and right arrows and the piece number), shape buttons `Straight`, `Getting steeper`, `Getting flatter` for the selected piece, `Undo point`, `Clear graph`, `Free draw` (toggle), `Next story`, dots, hint. After Check the scene image folds away and the story area becomes the compare list (Story | Your graph, one row per story part, with a check or X icon). At 680x425 the scene image folds, the plane fills the stage, the panel is compact (Check and shape buttons first).
What the student does: tap the grid to place points; the first point snaps to the x = 0 column at the tapped height, each next point must be to the right of the last (snap to whole grid steps, at most 9 points). Points drag (staying between their neighbors' x) and move by arrow keys. Tap a piece, or use the Piece arrows, to select it; shape buttons bend it (disabled on a flat piece). `Check` (enabled at 2 or more points) runs the checker (Interactions). Pass: chime, `sketchRight`, playback. Second fail: the working sketch from Content appears dashed in ink over the student's faint sketch, `sketchShown`.
Free draw: a blank plane in Lucia's context (time, distance from home). Every change updates a live read-back list ("Lucia moves away from home at a steady speed. Lucia stays in one place. ..."). No Check, no marks. Toggling off returns to the round exactly as left.

### Model
Focus: the representation (text, two points, table or graph) and the equation template under it.
Layout at 1366x768: stage row 1 a story card (1:1 painted object, 120 px, beside the story text); row 2 the representation (a table of four rows, two points as large ordered pairs with words, or a plane about 600x330); row 3 the template in the story's letters, for example `g = [ m ] t + [ b ]`, with 64 px wide number boxes labeled "rate of change (m)" and "initial value (b)" below. Panel: `Check`, the note box, `Next story`, dots, hint. After the numbers are right or shown, the representation shrinks to a 300x180 thumbnail and two columns of four meaning cards appear under the template ("What does 50 mean here?" and "What does 200 mean here?"), each card 44 px or taller. Everything above the fold. At 680x425 the story card's image folds; meaning cards go one column per number, scrolling inside the stage only if needed (the template and Check stay visible).
What the student does: type m and b (decimals, fractions like 3/20, a leading $ or minus sign all accepted), press `Check` or Enter. Then tap one card per number, one try each.

**End of a set (every mode):** after the last round a summary card in the stage: the round dots with words ("Matched 5, partly 1, shown 1"), `Play the set again` (reshuffles and clears this mode's marks) and the other two mode buttons.

## Interactions in detail
- Marks per round: Match "Matched" (right pick and tags right by the second Check), "Partly" (one of the two), "Shown" (neither). Sketch "Matched" (first Check), "Fixed" (second), "Shown". Model "Matched" (numbers on the first Check and both meanings right), "Partly" (anything between), "Shown" (numbers shown after two Checks and both meanings missed). Dots: filled with a check, half filled, ring with a dash; each has a text label for screen readers and on hover. A round not Matched adds the next unused spare of that mode at the end (once per round). Saved on this device (localStorage `curio-story-graphs`, try/catch); `Clear progress` asks once, then resets marks and spares.
- Match tags: a piece is right when its direction and shape both match (Flat needs only Flat). Check 1: each piece gets a check or an X icon; wrong tags stay for editing. Check 2 with any X: the right tags replace the badges, the student's wrong ones show struck through beside them, `tagsShown`. Chips are a palette: they never run out or disappear.
- Sketch checker: read the pieces (direction from the sign of the rise; shape from the button; flat pieces are straight), merge neighbors that are straight, same direction and the same slope (so a steady part drawn with three points still reads as one part), then the part count must equal the story's, each part must match its direction and shape, and the story's extra rules must hold (exact on the grid). Feedback: the compare list, one row per story part; when the counts differ the list shows all the student's parts with the line "Your graph tells 4 parts. The story has 3." Feedback never says what to change. After pass or shown, `Check` is disabled until `Clear graph`, and `Next story` is enabled.
- Sketch edges: a tap left of or on the last point's x is refused, the point flashes, and `backInTime` plays once per round (then the hint only). A 10th point is refused with the hint "Nine points is the most. Drag or undo a point." `Undo point` removes the last point; `Clear graph` removes all (both keep the Check count). Dragging a point never passes a neighbor's x.
- Playback (after a pass, or `Play back`): a dot runs along the sketch, 1.2 s per part (at most 5 s), and each story row lights as its part plays. Any tap during it (a point, a shape, a mode, Next) stops it at once and acts. Motion off: no moving dot; all rows light together; the same lines play.
- Model check: parse both boxes (empty box: Check stays disabled; text that is not a number: the box outline goes red with an X icon and the note "Type a number, like 4, -3.5 or 3/20"). Notes after a wrong Check, first that applies: m and b swapped: "Your two numbers look swapped. Which one is there at the start, before anything changes?"; m with the wrong sign: "Your line goes up, but the story's amount goes down." (or the reverse); m is run over rise: "You divided the change in x by the change in y. Rate of change is change in y over change in x."; m right, b equals the y of the first point or row when its x is not 0: "Your b is the y of the first point, but that point is not at the start. Work back to where x is 0."; otherwise "Your line misses the story's points. Check each number." After any Check the student's line is drawn (dashed) through the story's points with hits ringed and misses showing a gap bar; the right line is never drawn before the numbers are right or shown. Check 2 wrong: the right numbers fill the boxes (marked "shown"), the line is drawn, meaning cards appear.
- Meaning cards: one tap each, no change after. Right: check icon, chime. Wrong: X on the pick, check on the right card, one line in the note area from Content's right card.
- Mode switch: hush voice, stop playback, keep each mode's round and state.
- Keyboard: cards and chips are buttons (arrow keys move within a group, Enter or Space acts). Sketch plane takes focus: arrow keys move a crosshair one grid step, Enter places a point, Backspace = Undo point; placed points use the engine's keyboard nudging. Model: Enter in a box = Check. Focus ring in `--focus`, 44 px targets.

## Heard in order
Grade 8: no line must be heard whole, and each can be cut by the next tap. Order still matters in four places:
- Match pick: `pickRight` or `pickWrong` plays first; the tag step's `tagHow` (first time) queues after it, never before.
- Match Check: `tagsRight`, `tagsWrong` or `tagsShown` plays before `nextHow`.
- Sketch Check: `sketchRight` starts with the playback; `sketchWrong` plays once the compare list is on screen.
- Model: `modelRight` or `modelShown` plays before the meaning cards' result lines; `meaningRight` or `meaningWrong` for m plays before the one for b. `setDone` plays after the last round's result line.
