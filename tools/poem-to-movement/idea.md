# Poem to Movement (idea card)

- Module: arts-3-5-dance-ideas-from-art-and-words, grade 3-5. Codes: DA:Cr1, DA:Cr3, DA:Cn10
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Pick a starting idea (a short poem, a painting, a sound), pull out its action words and qualities, turn them into a movement phrase you draw as a floor map, then let a viewer guess which idea it came from; if the guess is wrong, revise until the dance carries its source.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three idea cards: a short public-domain or original poem, a public-domain painting, and a recorded sound (wind, a train, rain on a roof).
  - Core loop: highlight words or spots in the source; each becomes a movement verb and quality (slow and sinking, quick and scattered). Order four to six moves into a phrase, then draw its floor pathway with symbols (straight, curved, zigzag, turn). The on-screen dancer performs it on that map; the student dances it too.
  - The aha or the end state: the viewer (an illustrated kid) watches and guesses the source from the qualities used; a river poem danced all sharp and fast reads as a storm. The student revises and the viewer tries again. A last card: what does this idea remind you of in your own life?
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a real partner guesses and gives one note instead.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - DA:Cr1: making movement from a chosen stimulus (text, picture, sound).
  - DA:Cr3: revising from feedback, and drawing the phrase's pathway as a floor map with symbols.
  - DA:Cn10: connecting the dance's main idea to the student's own experience.
- Not like: levels-and-pathways (K-2: reading pathways the dancer paints); choreography-board (grade 6-8 revision devices); dance-pattern-puzzle (patterns).
- Art: public domain (Met or AIC painting, object ids to check at spec); people (illustrated) for the dancer and viewer, with their own figures.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pick a poem, painting or sound, turn its words and qualities into a movement phrase, draw its floor map, then let a viewer guess the source and revise until the dance carries it.
- Flags: overlap risk (levels-and-pathways floor drawing; this one uses the map as notation for the student's own phrase)
