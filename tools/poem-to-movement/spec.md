# Poem to Movement

## One idea
A dance can carry where it came from: tap the action words in a poem (or spots in a painting, or moments in a sound), each becomes a move with a quality (slow or quick, light or strong, smooth or sharp, low to high), the dancer performs your phrase along the floor map you draw, and the Viewer, who sees only the qualities, guesses the source; a river poem danced all quick and sharp gets read as a storm, so you revise until the dance says river.

## Not like
- levels-and-pathways (K-2, dance family): a class reads pathways the dancer paints on the floor. Here the floor map is the student's own notation for a phrase they built, and it feeds the Viewer's guess.
- dance-pattern-puzzle (3-5, dance family): find what repeats in move-card patterns. Here there is no pattern to find; moves come from a source's words and the test is whether the qualities carry the source.
- choreography-board (6-8, dance family): revises a phrase with choreographic devices on a timeline. Here revision is driven by one reader's guess, and the devices are the four qualities.
- recital-stage-directions (this band): stage areas and reading two recital pieces. Here the student makes the phrase.
- place-mood-makeover (this band): a mood compass for a picture. Here the reader is a guess between six sources from a dance's qualities, not a mood.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, dance, module "Dance Ideas from Art and Words (Dance)".
- Topic line: making a dance from a poem, a painting or a sound
- Driver: students (pairs: one sets, one dances) or teacher (projector; the class dances the phrase in the room). 15 to 30 minutes. Students move in the room; the screen shows a code figure.
- Standards: DA:Cr1 (make movement from a stimulus), DA:Cr3 (revise from feedback; draw the phrase's pathway as a floor map with symbols), DA:Cn10 (connect the dance's idea to the student's own experience).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Steps (tabs, always tappable once reached): `Source`, `Moves`, `Map`, `Viewer`.

**Source.** Focus: the source, filling the stage. Six source cards in the panel: two poems, two paintings, two sounds (Content). Tap one:
- Poem: the text, large, on a paper card; `Listen` reads it aloud (voice clip). Highlightable words are underlined lightly; tap one to highlight it (gold). 
- Painting: the painting, full stage; tap a spot to highlight it (a ring and its word).
- Sound: a 12-second sound made in code, with a waveform strip (code) and four marked moments; `Listen` plays it with a playhead; tap a moment to highlight it.
Highlight 4 to 6 (the panel counts "3 of 4 to 6"). `Make moves` opens Moves.

**Moves.** Focus: the dancer (code figure) on the studio floor, big, center; the phrase strip of move cards under it. Each highlight became a card: the word, its verb, and four quality toggles: `Speed` (Slow, Quick), `Weight` (Light, Strong), `Flow` (Smooth, Sharp), `Level` (Low, Middle, High), plus `Path` (In place, Straight, Curved, Zigzag). Defaults come from the word's meaning (Content); every toggle can be changed. `Verb` (a small menu) swaps the move's action (13 verbs). Drag cards to reorder. Tap a card to see that move alone; `Play phrase` plays all (4 counts each, 96 beats a minute, a 4-count count-in, so 10 to 15 seconds). `Dance it`: the same play, with each move's name and quality words big under the dancer and the count, for the class to dance along.

**Map.** Focus: the floor map, a top-down square (code diagram) with the front edge labeled "audience". The phrase's pathway is drawn as notation: a start mark (X), each traveling move as its symbol line (straight line, curved line, zigzag line) with an arrow, in-place moves as a small circle with the move number, and a turn as a looped arrow. Two ways to set it: tap a card's `Path` (the map redraws), or `Draw path`: drag one line across the map; it is read as straight, curved and zigzag parts in order and each traveling move takes the next part (moves set to In place stay in place). `Clear map` resets the paths to the cards' defaults. The dancer replays the phrase following the map (small view beside it).

**Viewer.** Focus: the dancer performing the phrase on the studio floor, with the Viewer's seat (an eye icon and a speech bubble, no face) at the front edge. `Show the Viewer` plays the phrase once; then the Viewer's bubble says what it saw and its guess, with the quality summary as four small meters (Speed, Weight, Flow, Level) and the main path symbol: "Mostly quick, strong and sharp, on a zigzag. I think it's the storm." The six source cards show under it, the guess circled; the real source is marked "yours". If the guess is right: "The Viewer read your source." If not, `Revise` returns to Moves with the cards that pulled the guess away outlined (the ones furthest from the source's profile). A closing card when the guess is right: "What does this idea remind you of in your own life?" with a text box (saved).
- `Partner guess` (extra): hides the source and the Viewer; a classmate taps one of six cards, then both guesses are shown.

**Highlights and default moves** (word: verb, Speed, Weight, Flow, Level, Path):
- Wind (poem words): hang: float, slow, light, smooth, high, curved / trembling: shiver, quick, light, sharp, high, in place / passing through: glide, quick, light, smooth, middle, curved / bow down: sink, slow, light, smooth, low, in place / passing by: float, slow, light, smooth, middle, curved.
- Train (poem words): Faster: rush, quick, strong, sharp, middle, straight / charging: stomp, quick, strong, sharp, middle, straight / Fly: leap, quick, light, smooth, high, straight / driving rain: scatter, quick, light, sharp, low, zigzag / wink: freeze, quick, light, sharp, middle, in place / whistle by: rush, quick, strong, sharp, middle, straight.
- River (painting spots): still water: glide, slow, light, smooth, low, curved / a heron wading: reach, slow, light, smooth, middle, in place / drifting mist: float, slow, light, smooth, middle, curved / a low moon: sink, slow, light, smooth, low, in place.
- Storm (painting spots): lightning: leap, quick, strong, sharp, high, zigzag / bending trees: sway, slow, strong, smooth, low, in place / rolling clouds: roll, slow, strong, smooth, high, curved / pouring rain: scatter, quick, strong, sharp, middle, zigzag.
- Rain (sound moments at 1, 4, 7 and 10 s): first drops: shiver, quick, light, sharp, high, in place / patter: scatter, quick, light, sharp, low, zigzag / a gust: glide, quick, light, smooth, middle, zigzag / slow drips: freeze, slow, light, sharp, low, in place.
- Drum (sound moments at 1, 4, 7 and 10 s): BOOM: stomp, slow, strong, sharp, low, straight / silence: freeze, slow, light, smooth, middle, in place / boom boom boom: stomp, quick, strong, sharp, low, straight / fading: melt, slow, light, smooth, low, in place.

**Verbs** (13) and their engine poses (`Dance.poses`, `Dance.dyn`): float (reach-up to arms-wide on tiptoe, suspend), shiver (arms-wide, shake), glide (walk gait, arms-wide, smooth), sink (stand to curl, smooth), rush (run gait), stomp (stamp, sharp), leap (star-jump, sharp), scatter (hop gait, sharp), freeze (holds the previous pose, suspend), reach (point to reach-up, smooth), sway (bend-side left to right, swing), roll (twist with a full turn, smooth), melt (stand to sit, collapse). Weight: strong adds a deeper knee bend (mix 0.4 toward plie) and sharp accents on each count; light puts the dancer on tiptoe (toe 1). Speed: slow does the action once over 4 counts; quick does it once per count. Level: `Dance.level`.

**The Viewer's rule.** Average the phrase's Speed, Weight, Flow and Level over its moves; the main path is the most used traveling path (In place only if nothing travels). Distance to each source = sum of the four differences, plus 0.75 if the main path differs. The guess is the nearest source. If two are equally near: "It could be the wind or the river." (both circled; counts as a miss for the source not named first only if the source is neither). With one move only, the Viewer says "One move is hard to read. Add more." but still guesses.

**Poem texts** (public domain, shown in full): Rossetti, "Who Has Seen the Wind?" (8 lines, from Sing-Song, 1872); Stevenson, "From a Railway Carriage", first stanza (8 lines, from A Child's Garden of Verses, 1885). The builder copies the text from the Project Gutenberg editions in Sources.

## Interactions in detail
- Dance engine: `Dance.floor('studio')` with this tool's own colors (wall #2d2a3e, mirror off-tone #3a3650, floor a gray marley #8b8794 to #a5a1ad, no barre drawn), one dancer with `Dance.dancer` (colors in Look), `Dance.phrase` (one step per card, 4 counts, `level`, `dyn`, `travel` along the move's map part), `Dance.clock(96, {meter: 4, countIn: 4})`, `Dance.counts`, `Dance.readPath` with the floor for `Draw path`, and a gentle `Dance.music` track (a soft pad and wood clicks) under `Play phrase`.
- While the phrase plays, every control stays live: changing a quality of the move that is playing changes it from the next count; reordering cards restarts from the moved card's slot at the next count; Stop stops at once. The Viewer's line waits until the phrase ends (it never talks over the dance).
- A path that runs off the map is clamped to the floor's edge (the engine's `inside` with a pad); the dancer never leaves the floor.
- Fewer than 4 highlights: `Make moves` stays disabled and the hint says "Highlight at least four." More than 6: the seventh tap is refused with "Six is the most. Tap one to remove it."
- Revise outlines the cards whose qualities are furthest from the source profile (the two with the largest distance); the outline names the quality ("Too sharp for a river").
- Motion off: the figure jumps pose to pose once per count (engine rule); the map's arrows still draw.
- Saved on this device (localStorage `poem-to-movement`): each source's highlights, cards, map and the closing sentence. `Start over` clears the current source.
- Keyboard: highlights are buttons in reading order; cards reorder with Alt and arrows; Space plays or stops. Tab order: panel, phrase strip, then source highlights.
- Sizes: the dancer and floor fit at real pixels; at 680x425 the phrase strip sits under the floor in one row (scrolling sideways) and the quality toggles show for the selected card only; at 375 wide the floor stacks above a single selected card with its toggles, and `Play phrase` stays in view.

## Voice
Bank voice `gentle-storyteller` (Vindemiatrix), style "Say gently and expressively, like a grandmother reading a picture book aloud": she reads the poems and guides the steps. Not used by any 3-5 arts neighbor or another tool in this band (act-it-out, K-2 theater, also uses it; not a close neighbor). `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.step + ':' + state.source`.
- welcomeSource (how; entering Source; stale when a card is tapped): "Pick a poem, a painting or a sound to dance."
- highlightHow (how; after a source opens; stale after the first highlight): "Tap four to six words, spots or moments that make you want to move."
- poemWind, poemTrain (event; on Listen; stale when the source changes): the poem text, read in full.
- welcomeMoves (how; entering Moves; stale when a toggle is tapped): "Each word is now a move. Tap Play phrase, then change the speed, weight, flow or level."
- mapHow (how; entering Map; stale when a Path is set or a line is drawn): "Show where the dancer travels. Tap a path on a move, or drag a line across the floor."
- welcomeViewer (how; entering Viewer; stale when Show the Viewer is tapped): "Tap Show the Viewer. The Viewer only sees how you move, not your source."
- guessRight (event; after a right guess; stale on the next play): "The Viewer read your source. What does this idea remind you of?"
- guessWrong (event; after a wrong guess; stale when Revise is tapped): "The Viewer guessed the storm. Tap Revise to change the moves that pulled it there." (built from the guess)

## Sources
- Christina Rossetti, "Who Has Seen the Wind?", Sing-Song: A Nursery Rhyme Book (1872), public domain: Project Gutenberg, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Sing-Song+Rossetti
- Robert Louis Stevenson, "From a Railway Carriage", A Child's Garden of Verses (1885), public domain: Project Gutenberg, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Child%27s+Garden+of+Verses
- Movement qualities (time, weight, flow, space) for children's dance, after Laban: Anne Green Gilbert, Creative Dance for All Ages, 2nd ed. (Human Kinetics, 2015), the dance concepts chapter.
- Floor patterns drawn as pathway notation (straight, curved, zigzag): National Core Arts Standards, Dance, DA:Cr3 grade 3 to 5 ("document a dance phrase using a recognized system, for example a drawing or symbols"), https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/
- Japanese woodblock print and Romantic landscape as styles: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Heilbrunn Timeline of Art History, "Woodblock Prints in the Ukiyo-e Style" and "Romanticism", https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/ (named as styles; both paintings are original).
- The Viewer's profiles and rule are this tool's own (said on screen as "the Viewer's way of seeing").
- Checks run (Python, 2026-10-29): with the default moves, the Viewer guesses every source correctly, with these margins to the runner-up: wind 0.50 (river next), train 1.00 (storm), river 1.00 (wind), storm 0.75 (train), rain 1.25 (storm), drum 0.75 (train). The river's four moves all set to quick, strong, sharp, high and zigzag are read as the storm (distance 0.25); set to quick, light, sharp, low and zigzag they read as rain. The rule first counted In place as a path, which made the rain read weakly (1.88 against 2.13); it now uses the most-used traveling path. Of the 96 phrases where every move is the same, 21 tie between two sources, so the tie line is needed. Phrase length at 96 beats a minute: 4 moves 10.0 s, 5 moves 12.5 s, 6 moves 15.0 s, plus a 2.5 s count-in. Contrast ratios with the WCAG 2.1 formula.

## Done when
1. Focus: in Moves and Viewer the dancer is the largest thing on the stage at 1366x768, 680x425 and 375 wide, and `Play phrase` is visible without scrolling.
2. Each source's default phrase is guessed right; the river danced quick and sharp is guessed as the storm or rain.
3. Changing a quality while the phrase plays changes the dancer from the next count; Stop stops at once.
4. `Draw path` turns a drawn S into curved parts and a drawn zigzag into a zigzag part, and the map symbols match.
5. The Viewer's line never plays during the dance.
6. Ties are named as ties.
7. Motion off: the figure steps pose to pose per count; nothing else breaks.
8. Highlights, moves, map and the closing sentence survive a reload.
9. How lines are spoken once in a run of five plays; guess lines are spoken after every Viewer play.
