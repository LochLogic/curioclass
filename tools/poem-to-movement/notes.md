## What it teaches

Students make a dance phrase from a poem, painting or sound. They document the pathway, test whether a Viewer recognizes the source, and revise movement qualities using that feedback.

## Key skills

- Notice words, image details and sound moments that suggest movement.
- Choose and change speed, weight, flow, level and pathway.
- Read and draw a floor map with symbols for traveling and staying in place.
- Revise a phrase using specific feedback.
- Connect a dance idea to an experience from everyday life.

## Support at home

- Choose four action words in a familiar poem. Make a seated or standing movement for each one.
- Trace a pathway with a finger. Take turns reading the route with slow, quick, light or strong movements.
- Ask what a movement reminds your child of. Try a different quality and discuss how the meaning changes.

## A 5-minute start

1. Choose River. Tap the still water, heron, mist and moon.
2. Tap Make moves, then Play phrase. Try the moves with comfortable space, seated or standing.
3. Change Speed and Flow on one card and watch again.
4. Open Map and read the arrows and numbered circles. Draw a path if useful.
5. Open Viewer and tap Show the Viewer. Revise or add a personal connection.

## The four steps

Source offers two poems, two original paintings and two synthesized sounds. Highlight four to six details. Moves turns each highlight into an editable card. Tap a card to preview it alone, drag cards to reorder, or use Alt plus an arrow while a card has focus. Every card has four counts at 96 beats a minute. The four-count count-in adds 2.5 seconds.

Map shows X for the start, arrows for travel, numbered circles for in-place moves and a loop for a roll. Draw path reads straight, curved and zigzag parts in drawing order. In-place moves keep their place. Clear map restores the default pathways for the current cards.

Viewer waits until playback ends, then compares average Speed, Weight, Flow and Level plus the most frequent traveling path. It circles every tied nearest source. A tie containing the student's source counts as recognized. Revise outlines the two cards whose qualities differ most from the intended source. Partner guess hides the source and model result until a partner chooses. The closing sentence connects the source to the student's own life.

## Talk about it

- Which quality made the biggest difference to the meaning?
- Can two different dances carry the same source?
- What did your floor map show that the move names did not?
- When might you disagree with the Viewer? What did you see instead?

## Good to know

The Viewer is a deliberately simple model, not a judge of dance quality. A wrong guess invites another interpretation. Its numeric meters describe averages: Speed runs slow to quick, Weight light to strong, Flow smooth to sharp and Level low to high. The most-used traveling pathway wins; an equal path count uses Straight, Curved, then Zigzag order.

The tool saves each source's work and personal sentence on this device, using localStorage. Nothing leaves the device. Start over clears only the current source. Sound controls narration, music and source sounds. Read speaks the current hint. Motion off keeps the same timing and shows one pose per count. No recordings were made in this build; narration uses the browser's available voice.

Students may adapt every move to their own comfortable range. Make room before moving. The code dancer is temporary; painted dancer poses are queued for owner review.

## Sources

- Stevenson, [A Child's Garden of Verses, Project Gutenberg ebook 25609](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/25609/pg25609-images.html), first eight lines of From a Railway Carriage. The collection originally appeared in 1885.
- Anne Green Gilbert, Creative Dance for All Ages, second edition, Human Kinetics, 2015.
- [National Core Arts Standards](https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/), Dance: DA:Cr1, DA:Cr3 and DA:Cn10, grades 3-5. Students create from stimuli, revise and document a phrase, then connect its idea to lived experience.
- Paintings are original illustrations using woodblock-print and Romantic landscape conventions, rather than reproductions of historical works.
- Illustrations generated with OpenAI gpt-image, edited for Curio Class.
