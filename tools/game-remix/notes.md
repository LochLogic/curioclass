## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Arcade** and pick **Fetch!** by Maya. Press **▶ Play**, click the stage, and steer the puppy with the arrow keys. Watch each line of the program light up as it runs.
2. Press **Remix this**. In the program card, click the **1** in `change score by 1` (under `when puppy touches ball`) and pick **3**. A tag appears, and **What changed** lists the change.
3. Point at the locked line at the top of the card: "Remix of Fetch! by Maya". Ask: why can't we delete it?

## The three modes

- **Arcade:** three games made by fictional kids (Fetch!, Pumpkin Pop, Snow Bounce) and four classmates' remixes of them. Pick a card, press Play, and every line glows as it runs. **Slow-mo** slows the clock to one third, and **Pause** then **Step** runs one line at a time. The remix cards show their own What changed list.
- **Remix:** your own copy. Every word in a box is a choice: click it and pick another. Add, move or delete lines, add or delete events, swap a sprite's costume, drag a sprite on the stage to set where it starts, change the backdrop, or **Borrow** a sprite (and its code) from another game. Every difference from the game you started from gets a tag in the card and a row in What changed. **Original / My remix** in the run bar plays either one with the same luck, so they can be compared fairly. Undo goes back one change at a time.
- **Share:** a card with the title, the remixer, every credit line, a picture of the game, the changes and a remix code. **Copy code** or **Print card** (one half-letter page). Another student opens it with **Load a code**. **Save on this device** is the only thing that stores anything; **Clear saved** empties it.

## Debugging by comparing

Two classmates' remixes have bugs on purpose:

- **Upside Fetch** by Kai: "Help! My puppy can't go up." What changed has one row. The up arrow now moves the puppy down.
- **Pumpkin Rush** by Jay: "Faster pumpkins! But my score is stuck." What changed has two rows. One is the speed-up Jay wanted; the other set the score change to 0.

Students find the bug by reading What changed, not by reading the whole program. Ask them to fix it without undoing the change Jay meant to make. Most remixes are not bugs, so the list is also useful for showing off a change.

## Talk about it

- What is the difference between remixing a game and copying it?
- Why does the credit line stay even after you change almost everything?
- Upside Fetch had one change. How did What changed help you find the bug faster than reading the whole program?
- Snow Bounce challenge: make it harder with exactly one change. Which change made the biggest difference?

## Good to know

- Nothing leaves the page. Remix codes are just letters and numbers that hold the changes, so sharing works by pasting the code wherever the class already shares work, or by printing the card.
- Names: the remixer box starts with a random nickname and accepts letters, digits, spaces and ' ! - . only. Use a first name or a nickname.
- Keys reach the game only after the stage is clicked (or focused with Tab). Keys 1 to 5 click sprites for keyboard players. Space pauses, S steps, Ctrl+Z undoes.
- A remix family can be three remixers deep. Past that, start a new remix from an original.
- Credit stays with history: a borrowed sprite's credit line stays even after the sprite is removed again, because the remix did build on that game. Talk about it if a student asks why.
- A remix code holds at most 240 characters, names included. Very long titles in capitals take the most room; the title box stops at what still fits and says so.
- Undo goes back one change at a time, exactly as it was made, even moves that cancelled each other out. Start over can be undone too, and turns off when nothing is different from the original.
- Challenges: after Try it, the challenge stays on screen under the hint and turns green with a check when it is met. The tool says how to do each step once, then only shows it; it still says every fix, every challenge met and every failed code load.
- On a projector: play an original, let the class vote on one change, make it, and play again.
