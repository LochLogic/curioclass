# Scene Staging Comparison (idea card)

- Module: arts-6-8-comparing-productions, grade 6-8. Codes: TH:Re7, TH:Re8, TH:Re9, TH:Cn11
- Format / family: showdown / none. Fit: change to explorer, because a solo student inspects two productions layer by layer (set, costume, light, voice) with each production's time-and-place notes; the class vote becomes an extra.
- One idea: Two productions of the same public-domain scene (the rehearsal of the workers' play in A Midsummer Night's Dream, one staged in an Elizabethan-style outdoor playhouse, one set in a 1930s factory town) play side by side; record your reactions moment by moment, then see a classmate's reactions and find out where culture, era and choices made you differ.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two stages, the scene's text, and a reaction track under each.
  - Core loop: watch both; tap reactions at moments (laughed, confused, moved, bored). Open the layers of each production: set, costumes, light, voice, and the designer's note on why (what the time and place meant to its audience).
  - The aha or the end state: only after committing, a classmate's reaction track appears beside the student's; the student finds two points where they differ and explains why (experience, culture, a choice in the staging). Then a verdict on which production served the scene better, with evidence pinned to moments.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class compares reaction tracks.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - TH:Re7: recording personal reactions and comparing them with a peer's.
  - TH:Re8: identifying how cultural perspective and personal experience shape each response.
  - TH:Re9: supporting a verdict with criteria and evidence from the staging.
  - TH:Cn11: researching each production's time and place to understand its choices.
- Not like: director-s-chair (grade 9-12: directing Antigone across five theaters); audience-talkback (grade 3-5 swapping elements); gallery-critique (visual art).
- Art: generated scenes for both productions; people (illustrated) for actors; a public domain image of an Elizabethan playhouse drawing (to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Watch two productions of one public-domain scene side by side, record your reactions moment by moment, then compare them with a classmate's and find how culture, era and staging choices made you see it differently.
- Flags: overlap risk (director-s-chair; this one stays on a different play and on responding, not directing)
