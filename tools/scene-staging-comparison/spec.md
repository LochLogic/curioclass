# Scene Staging Comparison

## One idea
Your reactions are evidence: watch one scene from Chekhov's "A Marriage Proposal" in two productions (an 1890 parlor played straight and a bright bare-stage farce today), tap what you feel moment by moment, then lay your reaction strip beside a classmate's on the same laptop and open the staging layers (light, sound, pace, where they stand, costume) at the moments where you split, to find what made you see it differently.

## Not like
- globe-theatre-stage (ELA 12, explorer): switches line delivery in Shakespeare scenes in one 3D theater. Here the scene is Chekhov, the productions are fixed, and the data is the viewer's own reactions over time and how they differ between people.
- director-s-chair (9-12, stage family): the student directs one scene in five houses. Here the student directs nothing; they watch, react and explain.
- dance-styles-explorer (9-12): dance styles with critique notes stamped to counts. Here the stamps are feelings, recorded live, and compared between two viewers.
- art-passport, orchestra-zoo (K-2 explorers): look closer at artworks and instruments. This is theater for 6-8 and argues from evidence.
- cover-version-review (this band): measures two music versions. This tool records felt reactions and compares people.
- three-viewpoints-scene (this band): makes a new scene; this watches an old one.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, theater, module "Comparing Productions (Theater)". Topic line: why two productions of one scene feel different.
- Driver: students in pairs on one laptop (Viewer 1 and Viewer 2), or alone with the sample viewers. 20 to 35 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS TH:Re7 (perceive and analyze), TH:Re8 (interpret: how culture and experience shape a response), TH:Re9 (evaluate with evidence), TH:Cn11 (relate theater to its time and place).
- Header: title, topic line, modes **Watch** (default), **Compare**, **Layers**, then kit `motion`, `sound`, `read`.

## Screens and modes

### Layout (all modes)
- Stage: two theater frames side by side (stacked under 900 px wide), Production 1 "The Parlor, 1890" on the left and Production 2 "Bare Stage, Today" on the right, each a painted empty set (Art) with code silhouettes of the three characters (flat shapes, one per character, distinct outlines: Lomov tall with a tailcoat shape, Natalya with an apron shape, Chubukov broad with a cane) and a caption bar under the frame showing the current line.
- Under the frames: the reaction strip, ten columns (moments) by two rows (the productions), where reaction stamps appear.
- Panel: `hint`, **Viewer** (Viewer 1, Viewer 2), the mode's controls, **Reset**.

### Watch
- Focus: the production playing (the other frame dims) and the five reaction buttons right under it.
- **Play Production 1**, **Play Production 2** (each becomes **Stop**), **Next moment** and **Previous moment** (step through when stopped).
- Reaction buttons (big, 56 px): **Laughing**, **Leaning in**, **Uneasy**, **Sorry for them**, **Lost**. Each tap stamps the current moment of the playing production for the current viewer (icon and word), up to 3 stamps per moment per viewer per production (a 4th replaces the oldest).
- Playing: the silhouettes move to the moment's positions, the light changes (a code overlay: warm low lamp, or flat white), sound cues play (Content), the caption bar shows the lines with the moment's pauses kept.

### Compare
- Focus: the reaction strips, enlarged to the whole stage: rows for Viewer 1 P1, Viewer 1 P2, Viewer 2 P1, Viewer 2 P2, and, toggled with **Sample viewers**, the three sample viewers' rows (Content).
- **Show splits** highlights the three moments where the two productions got the most different reactions from you, and the three where you and Viewer 2 differed most (rule in Interactions). Tap a highlighted column: jumps to Layers at that moment.
- Under the strip, the opinion builder (TH:Re9): "In moment [pick], Production [pick] made me feel [pick] because of its [layer pick]: [up to 120 characters]." **Add** puts it in the opinion list (up to 6). A sentence needs a moment where the student has a stamp; otherwise Add is disabled.

### Layers
- Focus: one moment, both productions frozen side by side, with five layer tabs over them: **Words** (the same in both, shown once, with each production's pause before it), **Where they stand** (a top-down code diagram per production: the stage outline, three labeled dots, arrows for any move), **Light**, **Sound**, **Costume and props**, plus **Its time** (Cn11: one card per production about when and where it was made).
- **Previous moment** and **Next moment**; the reaction stamps for this moment show under each frame.
- **What made the difference?** chips (Light, Sound, Pace, Where they stand, Costume, My own life, I'm not sure) for the current moment; picks are saved and offered in the opinion builder.

## Content

### The scene (retold for Curio Class in plain words from Anton Chekhov's one-act "A Marriage Proposal", written 1888 to 1889, public domain; the retelling is original)
Characters: Lomov, a nervous neighbor who worries about his heart; Natalya, 25, who runs her father's farm; Chubukov, her father.
1. Lomov arrives in his best coat. Chubukov: "Dressed like that? Are you here to borrow money?" Lomov: "No! I've come to ask... a favor."
2. Lomov: "I want to ask for your daughter's hand." Chubukov: "At last! I'll get her!" He rushes out.
3. Natalya comes in, flour on her apron. Lomov talks around it: "As you know, my family has long owned the Oxen Meadows..."
4. Natalya: "Your meadows? The Oxen Meadows are ours." Lomov: "Ours." Natalya: "Ours!"
5. They shout. Lomov clutches his chest: "My heart is pounding. My foot's gone to sleep."
6. Chubukov returns and takes his daughter's side. Insults fly. Lomov staggers out.
7. Chubukov: "And he came here to propose to you!" Natalya: "Propose? Bring him back! Bring him back!"
8. Lomov returns. They try again, and argue about whose hunting dog is better, Guess or Squeezer.
9. Lomov faints into the chair. Chubukov: "He's dead!" Natalya: "Ivan! Ivan!"
10. Lomov wakes. Chubukov: "Kiss her and be done with it!" They are engaged, and still arguing about the dogs.

### Productions (made up for Curio Class)
- **The Parlor, 1890**: a realistic Russian country-house parlor in the style of the Moscow Art Theatre a decade later (period furniture, a samovar, an oil lamp, ticking clock). Played straight: real anger, real fear for Lomov's heart, long pauses. Light warm and low, from the lamp; it dims a step at moment 9. Sound: a ticking clock under everything, rain against the window from moment 4. Costumes: Lomov in a black tailcoat and white gloves, Natalya in a work apron over a long dress, Chubukov in a dressing gown.
- **Bare Stage, Today**: a bare stage with two folding chairs and a bright, flat, even light; played as fast farce, every quarrel a race; Lomov's faint is a full pratfall. Sound: a live accordion plays a short sting at each insult and a sad slide at the faint. Costumes: matching blue overalls for all three, Lomov's "best coat" a tuxedo T-shirt over his overalls.

- Moment length in play = its pause + its lines at 2.5 words a second + 1 s. Production 1 runs about 70 s, Production 2 about 56 s (Sources). Longer than 30 s because watching the whole scene is the task; Stop and stepping are always live.

### Its time (Cn11 cards)
- P1: "Chekhov called it a joke in one act. Realistic acting, slow and detailed, grew in Russia around 1900 at the Moscow Art Theatre, which staged Chekhov's longer plays. An audience then knew these country neighbors: land and a good dog really mattered."
- P2: "Farce is comedy played fast and big, with physical gags. Playing an old play on a bare stage today shifts the focus to the actors' bodies and timing. Today's audience may not own land, so the meadow fight becomes pure silliness."

### Sample viewers (made up; a stamp row each for both productions)
- Vera, grandparent, grew up in a farming village: P1 Sorry for them at 4, 5, 6 and Laughing at 10; P2 Uneasy at 4 ("people really do fall out over land"), Laughing at 9 and 10.
- Theo, 12, never been to a farm: P1 Lost at 3 and 4, Leaning in at 9; P2 Laughing at 1, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10.
- Amara, has a heart condition in her family: P1 Uneasy at 5 and 9; P2 Uneasy at 9 ("I couldn't laugh at the faint").

## Interactions in detail
- Every control stays live while a production plays: a reaction tap stamps at once; Stop stops at once; Previous and Next jump at once and keep playing from there; switching Viewer mid-play stamps the rest for the new viewer.
- Split rule: for each moment, a production's reaction set is the stamps of one viewer; difference = the number of reaction types in one set but not the other (0 to 5). Ties keep the earlier moment. With no stamps at all, Show splits is disabled and the hint says to watch first.
- Viewer 2 with no stamps: their rows say "Viewer 2 hasn't watched yet. Pass the laptop and pick Viewer 2."
- Mode switch stops playback. Saved on this device: both viewers' stamps, chips and opinions (localStorage, try/catch). **Reset** clears all with a confirm.
- Motion off: silhouettes jump to each moment's positions instead of sliding; the pratfall is a single drop; pauses and timing are kept.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 both frames, the reaction buttons and Play are visible without scrolling. Preview 680x425: frames and strip readable, caption 11 px or more. At 375 wide: one frame at a time with a P1 / P2 switch, reaction buttons under it.

## Done when
1. Focus: in Watch the playing production and the reaction buttons are the biggest, central things; in Compare the strips fill the stage; at 1366x768 and 680x425.
2. Tap while it plays: a reaction stamps on the moment showing at that instant; Next, Previous and Stop act at once; switching Viewer mid-play stamps for the new viewer from then on.
3. Both productions play every moment with the table's pauses, positions, light and sound cues, and Motion off keeps the timing.
4. Show splits highlights the moments the split rule picks (recompute by hand on a test set) and is disabled with no stamps.
5. Layers shows all six tabs for every moment and saves chips; the opinion builder refuses moments with no stamp.
6. Two viewers' stamps stay separate and survive reload; Reset clears them.
7. No narration over a playing production; how lines once in five repeats; bothDone and opinionAdded speak each time they happen.
