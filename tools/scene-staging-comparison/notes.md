## What it teaches
Students compare two fictional productions of the same Chekhov scene and connect their reactions to specific staging decisions. They can explain how a viewer's experiences and a production's historical style shape an interpretation.

## Key skills
- Notice feelings while watching and tie them to a moment.
- Compare light, sound, pace, blocking, costumes and props.
- Listen to another viewer's interpretation without treating difference as error.
- Build an opinion from an observed detail and a recorded reaction.
- Relate realistic acting and farce to a production's time and setting.

## Support at home
- Say the same everyday sentence slowly and then quickly. Ask how the timing changes its meaning.
- Remember a funny scene together. Talk about whether every family member found the same part funny.
- Notice how a lamp changes a room's mood compared with bright overhead light.

## Five-minute start
Press Play Production 1 and tap feelings as the scene unfolds. Stop or step whenever useful.
Play Production 2 and react independently. Open Compare, tap Show splits, and open a marked moment.
In Layers, inspect Words and one other staging layer. Choose a possible reason, then add an opinion in Compare.

## Modes
Watch presents ten moments per production. Captions are silent; sound effects are synthesized.
Reaction buttons work while playing or stopped. Three stamps are kept per viewer, production and moment;
a fourth replaces the oldest. Reaction symbols always have word labels in the comparison and reaction controls.

Compare shows both viewers. The sample viewers are fictional, with different experiences rather than correct answers.
Gold top borders mark production differences; blue edges mark differences between viewers.
Each difference counts distinct reaction types present in only one set. Duplicates do not increase it.
Viewer comparisons combine each viewer's two production sets at a moment. Earlier moments break ties.
The top three moments are highlighted, including zero differences when all remaining moments tie.
Missing data from either viewer is not treated as a disagreement between viewers.

Layers shows the same words, blocking, light, sound, costumes and props, and time context.
The top-down arrows show moves from the preceding moment, rather than precise walking paths.
Personal reason chips are saved per viewer and moment. They are offered in the opinion builder.
An opinion requires that viewer's stamp on the selected production and moment plus a reason, up to 120 characters.
Up to six opinions are saved across the pair.

## Talk questions
- Which production changed your feeling most? Point to one moment and one staging decision.
- Why might Vera and Theo react differently to a land dispute?
- What might Amara notice about the faint that another viewer misses?
- How does a slow pause change a joke? Can a funny scene also feel uncomfortable?
- Which time-context card helps explain a production choice?

## Good to know
The story concerns a marriage proposal, shouting, insults and a faint. A character worries about his heart;
this is fictional drama and some students may find the illness gag uncomfortable. Every reaction is valid.
No correctness scores or competition are attached to feelings. TH:Re7 is practiced by live watching and stamping,
TH:Re8 by comparing viewers and reflecting on personal experience, TH:Re9 by evidence-backed opinions,
and TH:Cn11 by inspecting the time cards and linking them to staging.

The productions are invented. The Parlor is labeled 1890 but deliberately borrows a realistic acting style
associated with the Moscow Art Theatre a decade later; it is not presented as a reconstruction of a real production.
Chekhov wrote the play in 1888 to 1889. Source references are in tool.draft.json and the supplied spec.
The final captions give 77.7 seconds for Production 1 and 63.4 seconds for Production 2 by the specified formula.
Role labels and bracketed action captions are included in the whitespace word count, consistently in code and checks.

Everything works offline. Reactions, reason chips and opinions are saved only in this browser on this device.
If storage is unavailable the tool says so. Reset asks before clearing both viewers' work.
Sound off silences effects; Read explicitly reads the current hint. Read stops a playing scene first.
No narration plays during playback. No voice recording was made during this build; browser speech is the fallback.
Motion off removes transitions, looping gestures and blinking while retaining the full pauses and moment durations.
On phones, use P1 / P2 to view one production at a time. Compare's wide evidence table scrolls horizontally.
