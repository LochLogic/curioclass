## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Meet the feelings**. Tap **Happy**. Bear smiles, the screen turns sunny, and Bear says how happy feels in the body.
2. Ask: when have you felt happy? Where do you feel it in your body? Try two or three more feelings.
3. Switch to **Stories**. Read the first story together, ask how Maya might feel, and tap the weather the class chooses.

## The big idea

Feelings are like weather. Every kind of weather is okay, it can change during the day, and naming it helps. Nine feelings each get a weather: happy is sunny, sad is rainy, angry is a heat wave, scared is thunder and lightning, worried is cloudy, confused is foggy, excited is windy, calm is gentle snow and proud is a rainbow.

## The three modes

- **Meet the feelings:** each feeling with its weather, a body clue ("my face gets hot") and one thing that helps.
- **Stories:** twelve short stories. There is no wrong answer. If the class picks a feeling the story didn't expect, Bear says that feelings can be different, then offers kind words or a good idea.
- **Class forecast:** children come up one at a time and tap their weather. The tally shows counts only, keeps no names and is never saved. **Make the forecast** turns the counts into a report ("Partly sunny, with some rainy").

## Talk about it

- What does angry feel like in your body? What about calm?
- Can you feel two kinds of weather at once? (Yes: excited and worried on the first day of school.)
- What could we say to a friend whose weather is rainy?
- Does weather stay the same all day? Do feelings?

## Good to know

- The class forecast is a check-in, not a survey. If a child picks a stormy feeling, follow up quietly later, the way you usually would.
- Sound and Bear's voice start on. Bear's lines are recorded with a natural voice, so they sound the same in every browser. **Read** replays the last thing Bear said.
- Turn **Motion** off for a calmer screen; the weather stays, it just stops moving.
- Teacher-led on a projector or smartboard. The buttons are big enough for children to tap.
