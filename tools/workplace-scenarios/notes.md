## What it teaches
Students trace how specific workplace choices affect other people later in a day. They can explain why clear communication, shared responsibility and honest follow-up matter, then compare a replay with their first choices.

## Key skills
- Give a teammate useful information early.
- Follow a safety rule while finding a creative alternative.
- Stop and ask for help when a group count is wrong.
- Ask directly for help and respond honestly to a concern.
- Use feedback to name a change for tomorrow.

## Support at home
- Plan a shared chore together. Ask what someone needs to know if you will be late.
- Recall a small choice that helped someone later. Follow its effects together.
- Ask each other one thing to keep and one thing to change after a busy day.

## A five-minute start
Open the Job card together and read the four camp rules. Let a student choose how to handle the late arrival, then advance to craft time. Tap the thread beside the changed opening and follow it back to the morning message. Ask what information Kai needed.

## Modes
**Day:** Eight fictional moments in a junior counselor's camp day. Tap A, B or C, or use keys 1, 2 and 3. Choices lock until a rewind. Next advances at the student's pace. Read speaks the story and all three choices while waiting, or the result after a choice. The Job card has its own Read button.

**Ripple map:** Always available. Unplayed stops stay gray. Each line exists only where an earlier choice changes later words. Tap a line or its matching label to read its two ends. Solid + means helped, dashed minus means cost someone, dotted dot means mixed. Notes from Kai, Dev and Ms. Ortega arrive at the end of the day. Tap a note to bring its words into view and hear it. Read repeats the selected note.

Rewind to here keeps the earlier choices, clears the selected stop and everything after it, and returns to that decision. New day clears the current day. There are no scores, points or winning endings. On a projector, invite a class vote by letter before each choice. Allow 15 to 25 minutes with discussion; a focused solo click-through can be shorter.

## Talk questions
1. Which choice reached someone much later? What exact words changed?
2. What made the short late message less useful than the detailed one?
3. When did a creative idea keep the safety rule intact?
4. How did asking Dev directly differ from complaining to someone else?
5. Rewind one moment. What stayed the same, and which consequences changed?

## Good to know
This is a made-up camp and volunteer role, not a statement about a real camp's staffing or safety policy. The story includes a missing child count, a rule violation and an unkind group-chat message. Discuss effects without asking students to disclose personal experiences.

Only this day's choices and position are stored in this browser on this device. Nothing leaves it. No names, accounts or analytics are used. If local storage is blocked, the tool still works and states that saving is unavailable. Motion off removes movement. Sound off silences narration and tones; Read still speaks on request. Narration uses the browser voice until recordings are supplied.

The six practice tags use the original 2012 Career Ready Practices list: CRP1 responsibility, CRP4 communication, CRP6 creativity, CRP8 problem solving, CRP9 integrity, CRP12 teamwork. Students act on each of these in the eight decisions. These short labels describe the skills practiced here, not the complete wording of each standard. The modernized 2024 list is different; this tool does not present its older numbering as current.
