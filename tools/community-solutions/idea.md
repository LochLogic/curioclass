# Community Solutions (idea card)

- Module: science-5-protecting-earth, grade 5. Codes: 5-ESS3-1
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Read real case files on how actual communities used science to protect a resource, combine two of their ideas into a plan for a fictional river town, and defend it at a town meeting where residents ask hard questions.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a small river town where the creek has turned cloudy and the fish are gone; the student is the newest member of the town's green team.
  - Core loop: at the library desk, open five or six case files from real places (a city that cut its water use, a town that restored a wetland, a coast that rebuilt dunes, a farm region that planted stream buffers). For each, note the problem, the science used, the cost and the measured change. Pick two ideas and combine them into a plan. At the meeting, illustrated residents (a farmer, a shop owner, a kid, an angler) ask questions, and the student answers with facts from the notes.
  - The aha or the end state: a vote, then a "five years later" screen whose results match what the real cases showed; plans backed by data from places where they worked, and that answer the people affected, earn support.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): groups each take one case file and teach it to the others.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 5-ESS3-1: the case files are the information the student obtains, and the plan combines at least two real communities' science-based solutions.
- Not like: town-planner-disaster-ready (grade 4 hazards on a budget); invasive-species-alert (grade 7, managing one ecosystem); beaver-builders (kindergarten footprint).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the residents and generated scenes for the town and the real places, because the human side is the point.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Read real case files on how communities used science to protect water, soil and wildlife, combine two ideas into a plan for a river town, then answer residents' questions at the town meeting.
- Flags: needs a data source (five or six real community case studies with checked outcomes); sensitive if a case involves tribal lands or environmental justice (fresh review agent, rule 5)
