# Sign Parameters Lab (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-k-12-the-five-parameters-of-a-sign, grade k-12. Codes: ACTFL.Novice Mid, WRS.Comparisons
- Format / family: lab / asl. Fit: good: change one variable, observe, record, conclude is exactly how minimal pairs work.
- One idea: Look at a real sign, then change exactly one of its five parameters (handshape, location, movement, palm orientation, face) on a control panel and watch the illustrated signer sign the result, so you discover that one change can turn a sign into a different word, and that some changes make no word at all.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a lab bench with a Sign Strip monitor and five dials, one per parameter. The first sample is a familiar sign. "Change one dial. Predict, then watch."
  - Core loop: the student picks a sample, turns one dial (for example, location from forehead to chin) and predicts whether it becomes a new sign, the same sign or not a sign. The monitor redraws the sign from its edited entry and plays it, with the changed parameter highlighted in the panels (the moved hand, the new handshape inset, the new arrow or the new face). The lab notebook logs each trial: parameter changed, before, after, result. Later samples ask the reverse: watch two signs and name which parameter differs.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's notebook shows that every parameter carries meaning, including the face, and they write a conclusion with a frame: "When I changed ___, the sign changed from ___ to ___." A comparison card shows English minimal pairs (pat / bat) next to ASL ones: both languages build words from small parts.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "spot the parameter" quick round on the projector; Slow / Natural speed; Left-handed.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Novice Mid: recognizing and producing the curated signs in each pair, using the parameters to read and copy them accurately.
  - WRS.Comparisons: comparing how ASL and English build meaning from small contrasting parts (parameters and sounds).
- Not like: fingerspell-flash (handshapes for letters only); face-the-grammar (face as grammar across a sentence, not as a word parameter); classifier-scenes (handshapes as classifiers in space).
- Art: graphics only, per docs/agents/ASL-GRAPHICS.md: the shared illustrated signer with vector Sign Strip hands; every curated sign and every "not a sign" result is a sign entry the engine draws, so each dial change redraws exactly; lab bench and dials drawn in code. No video, no photos of people.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Turn one dial (handshape, location, movement, palm or face) on a real sign and watch an illustrated signer show the result; log whether it became a new word, the same word or no word, and compare with English sound pairs.
- Flags: every curated pair and "not a sign" result checked by the fresh blind review against written references (ASL-GRAPHICS.md); sensitive (linguistic accuracy, fresh review, rule 5); the on-screen practice note (one common form; practice with a fluent Deaf signer or your ASL teacher when you can); K-12 span check (gf-04).
