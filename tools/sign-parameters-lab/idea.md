# Sign Parameters Lab (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-k-12-the-five-parameters-of-a-sign, grade k-12. Codes: ACTFL.Novice Mid, WRS.Comparisons
- Format / family: lab / asl. Fit: good: change one variable, observe, record, conclude is exactly how minimal pairs work.
- One idea: Watch a real sign, then change exactly one of its five parameters (handshape, location, movement, palm orientation, face) on a control panel and see a Deaf signer sign the result, so you discover that one change can turn a sign into a different word, and that some changes make no word at all.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a lab bench with a video monitor and five dials, one per parameter. The first sample is a familiar sign. "Change one dial. Predict, then watch."
  - Core loop: the student picks a sample, turns one dial (for example, location from forehead to chin) and predicts whether it becomes a new sign, the same sign or not a sign. The monitor plays the real result, filmed for every curated pair. The lab notebook logs each trial: parameter changed, before, after, result. Later samples ask the reverse: watch two signs and name which parameter differs.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's notebook shows that every parameter carries meaning, including the face, and they write a conclusion with a frame: "When I changed ___, the sign changed from ___ to ___." A comparison card shows English minimal pairs (pat / bat) next to ASL ones: both languages build words from small parts.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "spot the parameter" quick round on the projector; slow / natural speed.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Novice Mid: recognizing and producing the curated signs in each pair, using the parameters to read and copy them accurately.
  - WRS.Comparisons: comparing how ASL and English build meaning from small contrasting parts (parameters and sounds).
- Not like: fingerspell-flash (handshapes for letters only); face-the-grammar (face as grammar across a sentence, not as a word parameter); classifier-scenes (handshapes as classifiers in space).
- Art: real signing video of every curated sign and every "not a sign" result, by fluent Deaf signers (required); drawn in code for the lab bench and dials.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Turn one dial (handshape, location, movement, palm or face) on a real sign and watch a Deaf signer show the result; log whether it became a new word, the same word or no word, and compare with English sound pairs.
- Flags: needs a data source (filmed minimal pairs and non-sign results, chosen and checked by fluent Deaf signers); sensitive (linguistic accuracy, fresh review, rule 5); K-12 span check (gf-04).
