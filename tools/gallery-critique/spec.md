# Gallery Critique

## One idea
In a panel debate about a real painting, every claim has to point at the picture: pin each statement to the spot that backs it, challenge the ones that can't be pinned, and only what is pinned goes on the record you take away.

## Not like
- art-through-time (6-8, same family and group): many works, linked by history. Here one work at a time, argued in depth.
- art-style-match (3-5, same family and group): names a style from visible tells. Here style is at most one claim among many; the work is interpreted and judged.
- director-s-chair (9-12, story): choices that change a performance's meaning. Here nothing is changed; the painting is fixed and the argument moves.
- dance-styles-explorer (9-12): writes dance critiques with a vocabulary guide. Here critique is spoken by three roles and every sentence must be pinned to evidence; no free vocabulary list.
- virtual-gallery (9-12, same group): presenting a set of works. Here responding to one.
- fair-use-court and other 9-12 roleplays: no rules or verdicts here; the "record" is a critique, not a ruling.

## Who and how
- Grade 9-12, visual arts, module "Critique and Interpretation (Visual Arts)".
- Driver: students (laptops, alone or in threes taking one role each in turn); a teacher can run one work on a projector and let the class vote on pins. 20 to 40 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS VA:Re8 (interpret intent and meaning), VA:Re9 (apply criteria to evaluate artistic work).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound`. Kit theme `theme-912`. Two screens: `Choose` and `Panel`, plus the `Record`.

**Choose.** Three framed works side by side (Content). Under them, three seats: `Critic`, `Curator`, `Artist`, each with its goal in one line (Content "Roles"). Pick a work and a seat, then `Take your seat`. A small `How it works` disclosure lists the four rounds.

**Panel.** Left: the painting large in the art-museum frame (Family engine), zoomable, with evidence spots shown as faint numbered rings only while pinning (Content). Right panel, top to bottom:
1. A round strip: `Describe`, `Analyze`, `Interpret`, `Judge`, with the current round marked. Rounds move only forward, by `Next round` (enabled once every claim in this round is pinned or challenged and the student has added one of their own).
2. The floor: the claims spoken this round, as speech cards with a speaker avatar and name. Each card has two buttons: `Pin` and `Challenge`.
   - `Pin`: the spots appear on the painting; tap the spot (or spots, up to two) that backs it, then `Done`. If the claim is supported by one of the chosen spots, the card is marked "On the record" with a pin icon and the spot numbers. If not: "That spot doesn't show it. Try another, or challenge the claim." Unlimited tries.
   - `Challenge`: three reasons as buttons: `No evidence in the picture`, `Wrong round` (said too early), `A fact that needs a source`. The right reason strikes the claim through with the reason in words; the speaker answers with one line (Content). A wrong reason: "Look again at what kind of claim this is."
3. Your claim: a sentence builder for this round (Content "Frames"), with menus, then `Pin it` (the student pins their own claim the same way; it must match a spot that supports it).
4. In Judge only: the lens selector (Content "Lenses"). Choosing a lens changes which judging claims count as supported (a claim pinned under the wrong lens shows "Not under this lens").
- `Record` (top right) opens the record at any time.

**Record.** The transcript of everything on the record, grouped by round, each line with its speaker and spot numbers, struck claims listed under "Challenged" with reasons. Then the role's product (Content "Roles"): the Critic's review, the Curator's wall label (an editable text area seeded from the pinned Describe and Interpret claims, with live checks: 100 words or fewer, no sentence over 25 words), or the Artist's notes. Buttons: `Print`, `Back to the panel`, `New panel` (back to Choose). Nothing is saved; the page warns on New panel if the label was edited.

**Frames** (the student's own claim; menus in brackets):
- Describe: "I see [a thing from this work's spot list]."
- Analyze: "The [line, color, shape, space, light, pattern] at [spot] [leads my eye to, balances, contrasts with, repeats] [spot]."
- Interpret: "[Spot] suggests [one of four meanings for this work]." Meanings, rain: "people alone in a crowd", "a new, modern city", "a quiet, cool moment", "wealth and comfort". gulf: "danger from every side", "calm in the face of danger", "uncertain rescue", "hard work at sea". bath: "care as daily work", "closeness", "a private moment", "everyday life as worth painting".
- Judge: "Through the lens of [lens], it [succeeds, falls short] because of [spot]."
A student claim is supported when the chosen spot is one the frame's chosen meaning or element lists (the builder writes a small table: meaning or element to spots per work, from the claims above).

**Lenses** (Judge round): `Looks real` (does it convince as a real scene?), `Well designed` (do the parts work together?), `Strong feeling` (does it move the viewer?), `Does a job` (does it teach, record or honor something?). One line under the selector: "A judgment depends on the lens. Say which one you're using."

**Roles:**
- Critic: "Reach a judgment and back it." Product: a review, the pinned claims in order plus the Judge claim and lens, 150 words or fewer.
- Curator: "Write the wall label." Product: an editable label, seeded from pinned Describe and Interpret claims, checked live: 100 words or fewer, no sentence longer than 25 words.
- Artist: "Explain how it was made." Product: notes listing the pinned Analyze claims with their spots.

## Interactions in detail
- Claim order in a round: the two panelists alternate; a claim written for the student's seat is said by "A visitor". All three claims of a round appear at once after a short stagger (400 ms each; instant with Motion off).
- A claim card can be pinned or challenged, not both. Undo: `Unpin` and `Withdraw challenge` on the card until `Next round`.
- Wrong-round claims (said too early) can't be pinned in this round; Pin on one says "This belongs to a later round. Challenge it as Wrong round."
- Flawed claims are never supported by any spot.
- The student's own claim is required once per round; more are allowed (up to three).
- The label text area counts words (split on spaces) and sentences (split on ., ! and ?), with the counts beside it in words; it never blocks printing, only warns.
- Keyboard: claims are buttons in reading order; Pin then arrow keys move between spots, Enter chooses; Escape cancels pinning.
- Sizes: painting and panel side by side down to 760 px wide; in the 680x425 preview the painting fills the left 55% and the floor shows one claim at a time with `Previous` and `Next`. At 375 the painting sits on top at 40% height.

## Done when
1. Every supported claim pins on its listed spots and no other; every flawed claim pins on none and is struck only by its right reason.
2. Wrong-round claims refuse Pin with the right line.
3. Under the Judge lenses, a claim pinned under the wrong lens shows "Not under this lens".
4. Each role's product builds from the record; the Curator's label counts words and long sentences correctly (test a 101-word label and a 26-word sentence).
5. `Next round` stays off until the floor is cleared and one own claim is pinned.
6. How lines once in five pins in a row; pinned and missPin every time, dropped when stale.
7. Painting, floor and Pin are visible without scrolling at every STANDARDS.md size; Print gives a clean black-on-white record.
8. Nothing is stored.
