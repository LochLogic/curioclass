# Fraction Trail (idea card)

- Module: math-3-fractions-as-numbers, grade 3. Codes: 3.NF.A
- Format / family: map / number-line. Fit: good.
- One idea: Hike a trail from the trailhead (0) to the lake (1); split it into equal legs to place campsites at fractions, and find that 2/4 and 1/2 are the same campsite on the ground.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a hiking map, a trail from a trailhead to a lake, a ranger, Ms. Kai. "Split the trail into 4 equal legs. Put a campsite at 3/4."
  - Core loop: the child drags trail markers to split the trail into b equal legs; each leg is 1/b of the way. They place campsites at a/b and see the label grow leg by leg (1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 = 1 at the lake). The trail continues past the lake for 5/4 and 3/1.
  - Equal-camp rounds: two hikers camp at 1/2 and 2/4 and meet at the same spot; the child finds more pairs (3/6, 4/8).
  - Compare rounds: which campsite is farther, 3/8 or 3/4? 2/6 or 5/6? The child places both and picks the sign.
  - Different-trail round: half of a long trail versus half of a short trail; the ranger explains we can only compare fractions of the same whole.
  - The aha or the end state: fractions are places on a line, and different names can mark the same place.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a campsite challenge list for the teacher.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3.NF.A: unit fractions as one equal leg of the trail (3.NF.1), fractions on a number line (3.NF.2), and equivalence, whole numbers as fractions, and comparing with same numerator or same denominator only for the same whole (3.NF.3).
- Not like: pizza-party (grade 1 equal shares), garden-grid (grade 2 shares of shapes), fraction-wall-duel (grade 4 comparing on a fraction wall).
- Art: generated scenes (an illustrated trail map, a lake and campsites), with markers drawn in code so lengths stay exact.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Hike a trail from the trailhead (0) to the lake (1), split it into equal legs and place campsites at fractions; find that 1/2 and 2/4 are the same spot, and compare camps on the same trail.
- Flags: none
