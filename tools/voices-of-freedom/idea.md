# Voices of Freedom (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-slavery-and-sectionalism, grade 8. Codes: D2.His.4.6-8, D2.His.14.6-8, D2.Civ.14.6-8
- Format / family: explorer / primary-sources. Fit: good
- One idea: A quiet reading room of real first-person sources (Frederick Douglass, Harriet Jacobs, William Still's records of people escaping, abolitionist petitions, interviews with formerly enslaved people): the student reads each with plain context, notes what shaped the speaker's view, traces causes and effects of resistance and growing division, and compares the ways people fought slavery with ways people press for change today; no points, timers or game mechanics.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a calm reading room with a shelf of sources grouped by voice (people who were enslaved, people who escaped, people who helped, people who fought it in law and print).
  - Core loop: open a source (portrait, excerpt, plain-language note, when and where); a reflection card asks what shaped this person's view (their situation, faith, law, risk); a timeline strip links each source to events (the Fugitive Slave Act, Dred Scott, John Brown).
  - The aha or the end state: a means-of-change wall (escape, testimony, petitions, the press, armed resistance, the Underground Railroad) compared with today's means; a causes chain toward secession built from the sources.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): teacher notes with discussion prompts.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.4.6-8: the reflection cards on what shaped each voice.
  - D2.His.14.6-8: the timeline strip and causes chain toward the Civil War.
  - D2.Civ.14.6-8: the means-of-change wall, past and present.
- Not like: remembrance-archive (family sibling, grade 10, the Holocaust); reconstruction-amendments (after the war); expansion-map.
- Art: public domain photographs and portraits (Library of Congress, to check at spec); real faces, never illustrated stand-ins.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Read real first-person sources from people who were enslaved, people who escaped and those who fought slavery; note what shaped each voice, trace causes toward the Civil War, and compare their ways of seeking change with today's. No game mechanics.
- Flags: sensitive (slavery; fresh review; excerpts chosen to avoid slurs per rule 6, with dialect transcription handled with care and explained); needs a data source (Library of Congress narratives and texts, checked)
