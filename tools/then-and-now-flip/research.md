# Research packet: then-and-now-flip
Concept: Flip picture pairs (phones, classrooms, toys) to sort then from now, then line up three versions of one thing from Grandma's time to today and compare a long-ago kid's day with yours.
Grade: k   Standards: D2.His.1.K-2, D2.His.2.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.His.1.K-2: "Create a chronological sequence of multiple events." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Place everyday items or routines in order from long ago, to Grandma's time, to today.
- D2.His.2.K-2: "Compare life in the past to life today." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain how tools, schools, toys, and daily chores long ago differ from how children live now.

## Common mistakes
- Collapsing all past eras into a single "long ago": Kindergarteners often assume grandparents lived alongside ancient people or dinosaurs, failing to differentiate between 50 years ago and centuries past (Source: C3 Framework History indicators D2.His.1.K-2).
- Believing children in the past never had fun: Students often assume life before electronics was completely sad or devoid of play, unaware of rich traditional games like tops, tag, and marbles (Source: National Toy Hall of Fame educational materials).
- Assuming older technology was defective: Children view rotary phones or oil lamps as broken versions of modern gadgets rather than ingenious inventions for their time (Source: National Archives Milestone Documents Education Guide).
- Overlooking continuity: Forgetting that basic human needs, love of family, storytelling, and friendship have remained constant across all generations (Source: California History-Social Science Content Standards K.6).

## Sensitivity
- Diverse family structures: Some children do not have living grandparents or live with foster families. Use "Grandma's time" as a relatable temporal anchor for the mid-20th century while ensuring activities accommodate all family constellations.
- Unequal access to modernization: Historical technological adoption was uneven; many rural, Appalachian, and tribal communities lacked residential electricity or telephone access until the mid-to-late 20th century.
