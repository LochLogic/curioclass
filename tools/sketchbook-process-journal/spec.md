# Sketchbook Process Journal

## One idea
The sketchbook keeps receipts: every page you add is tagged with what changed your idea (a research fact, a trait you borrowed from an older tradition, a trait you broke, or your own call), and the idea map at the back draws those threads, so you can see exactly where research and history bent your plan and where your own voice took over.

## Not like
- gallery-critique (9-12, roleplay): argues about one finished painting from evidence. Here nothing is finished; the subject is how your own idea changed.
- virtual-gallery (9-12, builder): chooses and hangs finished works. Here you make the thumbnails, studies and plan that come before any work exists.
- city-skyline-studio (9-12, studio): a construction drawing that obeys perspective rules. The drawing pad here is a plain pencil for loose thumbnails; the tool's work is the map of decisions, not the drawing.
- art-through-time (6-8, timeline) and art-passport (K-2): tour styles of the past. Here one tradition is a tool you borrow from and break, tied to a present-day issue.
- storyboard-studio (9-12, studio): plans shots in order. The order here is the order your thinking changed.

## Who and how
- Grade 9-12, visual arts, module "Artistic Investigation and Tradition (Visual Arts)".
- Topic line: developing an idea from an issue and a tradition
- Driver: students on laptops, alone. 30 to 45 minutes for a first pass; the book is saved on this device so it can grow over several classes.
- Standards: NCAS VA:Cr1 (generate and conceptualize ideas, 1.1 and 1.2 HS), VA:Cn10 (synthesize knowledge and experience to make art), VA:Cn11 (relate artistic ideas to societal, cultural and historical context).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound`. Own light palette (Look), not theme-912. Modes (`.seg`): `Sample book` (default on first load), `My book`, `Idea map`. `Idea map` shows the map of whichever book was open last (a label says "Theo's book" or "Your book").

**Sample book.** Focus: the open sketchbook spread, the biggest thing on screen (at least 60% of the width at 1366x768). A two-page spread on the painted paper texture; left page is the page itself (its picture, its text), right page is its "What changed" tag card. Tabs along the book's edge, one per page (1 Issue, 2 Tradition, 3 Thumbnails, 4 Study, 5 Revision, 6 Revision, 7 Plan), and `Prev page`, `Next page`. Panel: who Theo is (one line), `See the map` (goes to Idea map with this book), `Start my own book` (goes to My book). Nothing can be edited here.

**My book.** Focus: the open spread with the current page's working area (picker, drawing pad or plan form), largest on screen. Panel: page list (tabs), `Add page` (menu: Thumbnails, Study, Revision; Plan once at least one Thumbnails page exists; 12 pages at most), `Delete page` (confirm; never pages 1 and 2), `Save on this device` is automatic (a small "Saved" readout), `Clear book` (confirm).
- Page 1, Issue: three issue cards (Content); tap one to open its picture and three research cards. `Choose this issue`. The research cards stay on this page for later tagging.
- Page 2, Tradition: three tradition cards; tap one to see its two painted examples big (tap a picture to enlarge it) and its five traits. `Choose this tradition`.
- Thumbnails page: four boxes in 4:3, each with the drawing pad (below). One line under each box ("what is this one?", 60 characters).
- Study page: one large drawing box, and a picker "Studying which trait?" (the chosen tradition's five traits).
- Revision page: a picker "Redrawing which thumbnail?" (any earlier thumbnail, shown small beside the pad), then one large drawing box.
- Plan page: one drawing box for a final sketch, plus a form: Title (60 characters), Medium (picker: pencil, ink, watercolor, acrylic, collage, printmaking, digital, other), Size (width and height in cm, number boxes 5 to 300), Palette (pick 3 to 6 swatches from a 24-swatch grid), Statement (400 characters, live count): "What is it about, and what did you take from the tradition?"
- Every page from 3 on has the right-page tag card "What changed on this page?": aspect chips (Subject, Composition, Color, Symbol, Material; pick one or more), then for each picked aspect a "Because" picker: a research card from page 1 (by its short name), `Borrowed: <trait>` (each tradition trait), `Broke from: <trait>` (each trait), or `My own idea`. A note box (140 characters). A page with no tag shows "Not tagged yet" on the map, never an error.
- Drawing pad: `Pencil`, `Dark` (a softer, darker pencil), `Eraser`, `Undo` (20 steps), `Clear box` (confirm). Pointer and pen input with pressure where the device gives it; drawn at real pixel size into a 1200 x 900 backing canvas per box, saved as a compressed image.

**Idea map.** Focus: the map, filling the stage. Columns are the book's pages in order (small page thumbnails across the top). Five lanes are the aspects (Subject, Composition, Color, Symbol, Material). Each tag is a node in its lane at its page's column; a line runs along each lane through its nodes (the thread of that aspect). On the left, a source column lists the research cards and tradition traits used, and a curved link runs from each source to every node it caused. Node marks (shape, letter and color, never color alone): research = square "R", borrowed = triangle "T", broke from = a diamond split by a gap "B", my own idea = circle "O". Under the map, a count line: "Research changed 3 things. The tradition gave you 4. You broke from it 2 times. Your own ideas: 3." Tap a node: a card opens with the page's picture, the aspect, the source and the note, and `Open this page` (to My book, or to that sample page). `Filter` buttons: `All`, `Research`, `Tradition`, `Mine` (Mine shows broke-from and own-idea nodes together: where your voice shows).

## Content
**Issues** (short name, card text, picture):
- City heat (heat-roofs): "Cities run hotter than the land around them."
  - R1 Day and night: "In US cities, daytime temperatures run about 1 to 7°F higher than in outlying areas, and nighttime temperatures about 2 to 5°F higher."
  - R2 Roofs: "On a warm day, ordinary roofing can reach as much as 66°F warmer than the air around it."
  - R3 Trees: "Trees and plants cool streets two ways: shade, and water evaporating from their leaves."
- Plastic in the ocean (plastic-shore):
  - R1 Tons: "In 2010, an estimated 4.8 to 12.7 million metric tons of plastic washed into the ocean from land."
  - R2 Most common: "Plastic is the most common kind of trash found in the ocean and on beaches."
  - R3 Smaller: "Plastic does not rot away. It breaks into smaller and smaller pieces; under 5 mm, they are called microplastics."
- Monarch butterflies (monarch-firs):
  - R1 Decline: "Monarch numbers have been falling steadily since the mid-1990s."
  - R2 Milkweed: "Monarch caterpillars eat only milkweed."
  - R3 Winter: "Eastern monarchs spend the winter packed into fir forests in the mountains of central Mexico."
- Card captions say where each fact is from (short source name, full line in Sources).

**Traditions** (name, place and dates, one-paragraph intro of two sentences, five traits):
- Vanitas still life (the Dutch Republic, 1600s): "Painters filled tables with beautiful things and quiet warnings. Every object reminds the viewer that life and wealth do not last." Traits: T1 "Symbols of death (a skull)", T2 "Symbols of time running out (a snuffed candle, an hourglass)", T3 "Things at the edge of decay (wilting flowers, peeled fruit)", T4 "A dark plain background with one strong window light", T5 "Costly objects shown in loving detail".
- Ukiyo-e woodblock prints (Japan, Edo period, 1600s to 1860s): "Prints of famous places, seasons and city life, carved on wood with one block for each color. Artists sold them in series, like Hokusai's Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji (about 1830 to 1832)." Traits: T1 "Flat areas of color with bold outlines", T2 "A big shape close up, framing a small far view", T3 "A place shown in a season or weather", T4 "Prints made in a series", T5 "Prussian blue, a new imported blue printmakers took up around 1830".
- WPA posters (United States, 1936 to 1943): "During the Great Depression, a government program paid artists to make posters for parks, health, safety and community events. Most were silkscreen prints, so each design could be printed many times." Traits: T1 "Flat layered colors, a few at a time", T2 "One short bold message", T3 "A public topic for everyone", T4 "Simple strong shapes that read from far away", T5 "Made to be printed many times and posted in public".

**Sample book** (Theo Marsh, a made-up grade 11 student; plastic in the ocean + vanitas still life). Page, picture, text on the page, tags (aspect: source, note):
1. Issue: plastic-shore picture and the three research cards; Theo's line "The beach near my aunt's house is half plastic after a storm."
2. Tradition: the two vanitas pictures; Theo's line "Vanitas paintings warn you about things that last too short. Plastic is the opposite: it lasts too long."
3. Thumbnails (sample-thumbs): three thumbnails: a heap of trash on a beach, a skull built of bottles, a table with a fish and a plastic bag. Tags: Subject: R1 Tons ("Tons is too big to draw. One table of it is something people can look at."); Composition: Borrowed T5 ("A table, like the Dutch painters").
4. Study (sample-study): a value study of a bottle and a shell lit from one side. Tags: Composition: Borrowed T4 ("Dark back, one window light. It makes plain trash look precious.").
5. Revision (sample-rev1): the skull replaced by a fish skeleton tangled in a bag. Tags: Symbol: Broke from T1 ("Not a human skull. The warning is about the fish."); Subject: R2 Most common ("The bag, because it is the thing I find most.").
6. Revision (sample-rev2): the snuffed candle swapped for a plastic lighter that will never burn down; bright bottle caps scattered. Tags: Symbol: Broke from T2 ("Their candle burns out. My lighter never goes away."); Color: My own idea ("Bright plastic colors against the dark, so the trash is the loudest thing.").
7. Plan (sample-plan): a color sketch of the final still life. Title "Still Life With Forever", acrylic on flattened cardboard packaging, 60 x 45 cm, palette of 5 swatches (near black #1a1714, bottle green #2e6b4f, bag white #e9ece6, cap red #c8322a, cap yellow #e6b52c). Tags: Material: My own idea ("Painted on a cardboard box from a delivery, so the painting is made of packaging too."); Symbol: R3 Smaller ("Tiny bright specks in the sand at the front: the pieces you can't pick up."). Statement: "A Dutch vanitas warns that beautiful things don't last. My still life warns about the things that last too long. I borrowed the dark table and window light, but swapped the skull for a fish and the candle for a lighter."
- Map counts for the sample, which the build must reproduce: research 3 (R1, R2, R3), borrowed 2 (T5, T4), broke from 2 (T1, T2), own idea 2. Total tags 9.

**Swatch grid** (24): six hues (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet) in four values each, plus the five sample colors shown as Theo's palette. The builder sets the hex values; every swatch has a name for screen readers ("dark blue").

## Interactions in detail
- My book starts with pages 1 and 2 only; `Add page` stays disabled until an issue and a tradition are chosen (its hint says "Choose an issue and a tradition first."). Changing the issue or tradition later is allowed: tags that pointed at the old cards are kept but shown as "(from your old issue)" in gray on the map, never deleted.
- Tags are optional; untagged pages appear on the map as a column with a dotted outline and "Not tagged yet".
- The map lays out at real pixels: columns at least 90 px wide; with more than 8 pages, the map scrolls sideways inside the stage while the source column stays fixed. Links from sources draw behind nodes; overlapping links fan out 4 px apart.
- A node's mark is at least 28 px; tap targets 44 px. Keyboard: Tab walks nodes column by column; Enter opens the card.
- `Filter` dims (25% opacity) nodes and links that don't match, keeping the lanes in place.
- Saving: localStorage `curio-sketchbook-journal` holds issue, tradition, pages (type, drawings as webp data at quality 0.6 downscaled to 800 x 600, lines, tags, plan form). Writes are debounced 1 s after the last stroke. If storage is full or blocked, a line says "This device won't save more. Clear a page or keep this tab open." and nothing is lost on screen.
- `Clear book` asks once, then empties the key and returns to page 1.
- No names are asked for; nothing leaves the device.
- Sizes: at 680 x 425 the spread shows one page at a time (the tag card under a `Tags` toggle); at 375 wide the drawing box is full width with tools in one row above it; the drawing tools never scroll out of view while drawing at 1366 x 768.

## Voice
Bank voice: `clear-teacher` (Iapetus), "Say clearly and kindly, like a patient elementary school teacher", played here as a patient art teacher who asks where ideas came from. The bank lists it for grades 3 to 5; every 9-12 voice is taken by a neighbor (see Open questions). `Curio.voice`, scope = mode. Most text is read on screen.
- welcomeSample (how; Sample book entered; stale once a tab or Next page is tapped): "This is Theo's sketchbook. Tap Next page to see how the idea changed."
- welcomeMine (how; My book entered with no issue chosen; stale once an issue card is tapped): "Tap an issue card, then tap Choose this issue."
- pickTradition (how; issue chosen and no tradition; stale once a tradition card is tapped): "Now open Tradition. Tap a tradition card, then tap Choose this tradition."
- addPage (how; both chosen and only two pages; stale once a page is added; needs `#addPage`): "Tap Add page and pick Thumbnails to start drawing."
- tagHow (how; the first time a drawn page has no tag; stale once an aspect chip is tapped): "Tap what changed on this page, then pick what caused it."
- welcomeMap (how; Idea map entered; stale once a node is tapped): "Each line is one part of the idea. Tap a mark to see what changed it."
- firstBreak (event; each time a Broke from tag is added; stale when the page changes): "You broke from the tradition there. That's where your own voice shows."
- planDone (event; each time the Plan page gets a title, medium, palette and statement for the first time in this book; stale when the mode changes): "Your plan is complete. Check the map to see what shaped it."

## Done when
1. Focus: the open spread in both book modes and the map in Idea map are the largest thing on screen at 1366 x 768 and 680 x 425.
2. The sample book's map shows exactly 9 nodes with the counts in Content, and every node's card opens the right page.
3. In My book, Add page stays disabled (and no line names it) until an issue and a tradition are chosen.
4. Drawing works with mouse, pen and touch, Undo steps back 20 strokes, and drawings survive a reload.
5. A tag with each of the four sources appears on the map with its shape and letter, linked to the right source in the left column; Filter dims the others.
6. Changing the issue after tagging keeps old tags, grayed and labeled, never deleted.
7. With 12 pages the map scrolls sideways and the source column stays put; nothing overlaps at 760 and 375 wide.
8. How lines are spoken once each in five page adds in a row; firstBreak is spoken every time a Broke from tag is added.
9. All 15 images load, show no people and no fake lettering; WPA bands are blank.
