## What it teaches
Students develop an artwork from a present-day issue and an older artistic tradition. They can explain how research, borrowed traits, deliberate breaks and their own decisions changed a plan.

## Key skills
- Turn a research fact into a visual decision.
- Investigate the context and visual traits of an artistic tradition.
- Generate several thumbnails and develop one through studies and revisions.
- Explain the reason for a change in subject, composition, color, symbol or material.
- Connect a final plan to the evidence behind it.

## Support at home
- Look at an ordinary package together. Discuss what an artist could say by reusing it as a surface.
- Ask your student to describe one idea they changed this week and what caused the change.
- Compare two objects at home. How could lighting, arrangement or color change the story they tell?

## A 5-minute start
Open Theo's Sample book. Turn to page 5 and compare the decision to page 3. Open Idea map, tap a mark and use Open this page to connect the explanation to the image. Start My book, choose an issue and a tradition, then add Thumbnails.

## Modes
Sample book is read only. Theo Marsh is a made-up grade 11 student, not a real student whose work was collected.

My book begins with Issue and Tradition. Choose both before adding a working page. Draw four thumbnails, investigate a trait in Study, redraw an earlier thumbnail in Revision, then make a Plan. The book holds 12 pages. Tags are optional: choose an aspect, a cause and a short decision note.

Idea map displays the last book opened. Each lane follows an aspect of the idea. R squares are research, T triangles borrowed traits, B split diamonds deliberate breaks and O circles original decisions. Mine includes B and O. Filters dim other marks without moving them. Select a node for its image and note.

## Talk questions
- Which research fact actually changed what you drew?
- What did you take from a tradition, and how did its context affect your choice?
- Where did breaking a trait make your meaning clearer?
- Does your medium or surface add something to the idea?
- What would you investigate next before making the finished work?

## Good to know
Grades 9 to 12, visual arts. Supports NCAS VA:Cr1 (including 1.1 and 1.2 HS), VA:Cn10 and VA:Cn11 through student investigation, alternatives, revision and contextual decisions. No points or right-answer scoring.

The pad is a plain drawing tool, not a painting program. Pencil and Dark respond to pen pressure when available. Touch and mouse also draw. Undo holds 20 strokes per box while this tab is open. Clear box asks first. On a focused pad, arrow keys make short strokes and Space makes a dot. Text descriptions and decision notes provide a keyboard alternative for explaining a thumbnail.

The book saves automatically after edits settle for one second. Images use 800 by 600 WebP at quality 0.6. Drawings remain on screen when storage fails; the status explains that the device cannot save more. Keep that tab open. Clear book asks once and removes this book's storage key. Pages 1 and 2 cannot be deleted. No names are requested, and nothing leaves the device.

Changing the issue or tradition keeps old tags. The map marks them gray and labels their earlier source. A plan needs a title, medium, 3 to 6 palette colors and a statement. Dimensions range from 5 to 300 cm. Choosing a palette does not change the pad's pencil medium.

Tradition images are original style studies, not copies or historical artifacts. WPA image bands intentionally carry no lettering. The vanitas example includes the conventional skull symbol of mortality. Theo first turns it into a skull made of bottles, then replaces it with a fish skeleton. The two original studies were redrawn during cold review so this change is visible.
