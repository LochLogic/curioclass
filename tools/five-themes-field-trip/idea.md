# Five Themes Field Trip (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-6-five-themes-of-geography, grade 6. Codes: D2.Geo.1.6-8, D2.Geo.2.6-8, D2.Geo.3.6-8
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: change to map, because all three codes ask the student to make and read maps, not only tag photos
- One idea: On a field trip through one river city (New Orleans proposed), the student collects photos at each stop, pins them on a blank base map they build up layer by layer under the five themes, compares real satellite images from decades apart, and adds a chart layer, so the city's patterns (high ground, levees, neighborhoods, port) appear on a map they made.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a nearly blank outline of the city and a trip ticket listing five stops, each tied to a theme.
  - Core loop: at each stop, a photo and a short guide note; the student tags the photo with a theme and places a symbol on the base map (location grid, place details, region boundary drawn by the student, movement arrows for ships and roads, interaction marks for levees and wetlands). A then-and-now satellite pair shows land and water changing.
  - The aha or the end state: the student turns one layer into a chart (land elevation along a line, or wetland area by decade) and writes one sentence linking where people built to what the land is like.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second city with the same toolkit.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.1.6-8: the student constructs the layered map of cultural and environmental features.
  - D2.Geo.2.6-8: then-and-now satellite images and photos explain how the city's location relates to changes in its land and water.
  - D2.Geo.3.6-8: the chart layer and symbol choices represent and analyze a spatial pattern.
- Not like: region-compare (data across world regions, not one place up close); the-shrinking-lake (one change over sixty years); layers-and-scales (grade 9, class breaks and scale).
- Art: public domain (NASA and USGS Landsat images, Library of Congress photos; items to check at spec), because the real images are the evidence.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Take a field trip through one river city: pin photos from five stops onto a base map you build layer by layer with the five themes, compare satellite images decades apart, and chart one pattern to explain where people built.
- Flags: needs a data source (Landsat pairs and city data); sensitive (if New Orleans, hurricane and flood history told with care)
