# Research packet: map-maker
Concept: Map a new town on an island seen only from a satellite photo, placing the port, farms and homes where the land suits them, with key, scale and grid; then a traveler follows your map and shows where it gets her lost.
Grade: 3   Standards: D2.Geo.1.3-5, D2.Geo.2.3-5

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Geo.1.3-5: "Construct maps and other graphic representations of both familiar and unfamiliar places." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Draw a clear map of an unfamiliar island using standard cartographic components including a title, compass rose, map key, grid, and scale.
- D2.Geo.2.3-5: "Use maps, satellite images, photographs, and other representations to explain relationships between the locations of places and regions and their environmental characteristics." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Analyze an island satellite photograph to identify suitable land for human activities, such as placing a port in a sheltered bay and farms on flat arable plains.

## Common mistakes
- Ignoring the map scale: Third graders frequently assume that two points placed near each other on a piece of paper represent a short walking distance, ignoring the scale ratio (Source: National Geographic Map Skills Guide).
- Forgetting that the top of a map is not automatically straight ahead: Students often fail to orient their map with a compass rose, assuming walking forward always corresponds to the top edge of the map (Source: USGS Topographic Map Education).
- Misidentifying grid coordinates by swapping axes: Children frequently reverse horizontal and vertical coordinates (calling A3 when they mean 3A), leading travelers to the wrong quadrant (Source: California History-Social Science Framework Grade 3).
- Placing infrastructure on unsuitable landforms: Students sometimes place farm fields on rocky vertical cliffs or harbors on shallow reef flats, forgetting to evaluate the physical terrain shown in the satellite photo (Source: C3 Framework Geography Indicators D2.Geo.2.3-5).

## Sensitivity
- Environmental sustainability in town planning: Encourage students to consider ecological protection when placing towns and ports, such as avoiding delicate wetlands or clear-cutting critical forest watersheds that stabilize mountain soil.
- Clear cartographic accessibility: Map legends and symbols must use distinct geometric shapes and high-contrast color palettes to assist students with visual impairments or color-vision differences.

## Similar tools elsewhere
- National Geographic Education: "MapMaker Interactive". URL: https://education.nationalgeographic.org/resource/mapmaker/. Core mechanic: Students build maps by adding layers, custom symbols, scale bars, and labels over satellite basemaps.
- USGS: "TopoBuilder". URL: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/national-geospatial-program/national-map. Core mechanic: Custom quadrangle map generator allowing users to configure grids, contour intervals, and legends.
- PBS KIDS: "Mapping Adventures". URL: https://pbskids.org/. Core mechanic: Young players place landmarks on a blank island map and guide a lost animated traveler using compass directions and a key.

## Gaps
- Exact bathymetric depth chart for small fictional island inlets: Standard public satellite imagery indicates water color gradations, but exact depth sounding requires localized sonar survey data.
