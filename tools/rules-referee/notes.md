## What it teaches
Students use slow motion, position, step counts, touches and arrival times to apply twelve real sports rules.
They can explain which instant in a play determines the call.

## Key skills
- Freeze a play at the instant the rule applies.
- Compare player positions when a pass is made.
- Count steps and team touches in sequence.
- Judge contact with a boundary or the net.
- Compare two clocks that have the same start.

## Support at home
- During a game, pause and ask which moment would help an official make a call. Explain your reasons together.
- Draw a line on paper and use small objects as players. Move the attacker after a pretend pass and discuss why the pass time matters.
- Count three pretend team touches together, then discuss why a fourth changes the call.

## A five-minute start
Open a locker, tap Play, and watch one complete replay. Use the scrubber, tenths buttons and sport tool to examine it.
Choose a call. After a wrong call, use the booth tool and move to another frame before trying again.
Read the rule after a correct call. The third correct call in a sport opens its locker.
The booth's three lights show that lock's progress. Open locker returns to the painted symbol reveal.

## Modes
Escape uses four three-call locks and a symbol keypad. The whiteboard supplies the sport order.
Timer is optional and starts Off. Expiry offers Keep going without losing progress.
Replay booth opens any clip, always enables Key moment and keeps no progress.
Escape progress stays in memory in this tab. Reloading clears it. No student information is collected.

## Talk questions
- Why does the passing instant matter more than where the attacker receives the ball?
- Which details made two similar plays need different calls?
- Why would comparing clocks with different starting times be misleading?
- What evidence would you show a teammate who disagrees?

## Good to know
These are simplified fictional replays using the named international rule sets. School leagues may use adapted rules.
The exact token centers carry the given coordinates. Player and ball markers are schematic and deliberately oversized for visibility.
The activity practices applying knowledge of rules, directly supporting SHAPE Standard 2.
It supports discussion around Standard 1 sports skills but does not assess or replace physical motor practice.
The locks record completed learning clips, not a mastery grade. Wrong calls are free and teach through feedback.
Space plays or pauses, Left and Right step one tenth, and A through D choose calls. Keys 1 and 2 switch modes.
With Offside line on and the field focused, Up and Down snap the line between players. Pointer dragging also snaps.
Motion off removes decorative effects; replay movement and timer duration remain, because they teach the rule.
Sound uses browser speech. Read speaks the current hint, or the full rule card after a correct call.
