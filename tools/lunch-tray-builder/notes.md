## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Build a lunch**. Ask a child to choose a food from the shelf. It lands in its group's spot on the tray.
2. Keep going until someone asks, "What's still empty?" Press **Is my tray ready?** to make the empty spots glow.
3. When all five spots are full, the tray celebrates. Ask: which food was your favorite to add?

## The three modes

- **Build a lunch:** 28 foods. Each one lands in its group: fruits, vegetables, grains, protein or dairy. Cookies and chips go on the napkin as "sometimes foods" that don't fill a group. Tap a food again to take it off.
- **Sort it:** one food at a time, ten in a round. The class decides the group, then drags the food to that spot (or taps it). A wrong spot gives a hint ("it is made from milk").
- **Fix the tray:** seven ready-made lunches (pizza day, taco lunch, picnic...) each missing one or two groups. Find what's missing and add it.

## Teaching notes

- Groups follow USDA MyPlate. Corn and peas are vegetables. Beans count as protein here; MyPlate lets them count as a vegetable too, so accept either if a child explains it.
- There are no "good" or "bad" foods in this tool. Sometimes foods are fine sometimes; they just don't fill a food group.
- Be mindful of children's food situations at home and at school. Focus on trying new foods and on what helps our bodies, not on what anyone's family eats.

## Talk about it

- Which food group helps build strong bones?
- Can you name a vegetable that's a root? A leaf? A seed?
- What would you add to the pizza day tray?

## Good to know

- Keys 1 to 3 switch modes.
- Nothing is saved.
