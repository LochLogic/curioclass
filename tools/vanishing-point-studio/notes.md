## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Find it** and pick **Railroad**. Drag along one rail, then the other. The two lines meet at one point: the vanishing point.
2. Press **Show the horizon**. The vanishing point sits on the horizon line, which is the artist's eye level.
3. Switch to **Draw**, choose **Box**, and drag a rectangle below the horizon line. Then drag the red vanishing point and watch the box turn.

## The big idea

In one-point perspective, every edge that runs straight away from you meets at a single vanishing point on the horizon line. Edges that face you stay flat (horizontal) or straight up and down. Where an object sits compared with the horizon decides what you see: below it you see the top, above it the bottom, and to one side you see the side facing the vanishing point.

## Draw tools

- **Box:** drag the front face. The slider sets how deep new boxes go.
- **Road:** drag across the bottom of the page for its width. The center dashes get shorter and closer together as they go back.
- **Room:** drag the far wall of a room or hallway. The floor, ceiling and walls come toward you.
- **Row:** drag up from the ground to make a post. Copies shrink and bunch up toward the vanishing point, like telephone poles.
- **Line:** snaps to the vanishing point, to horizontal, or to vertical.
- **Guides** shows the blue construction lines. Turn it off to see the finished drawing. **Shading** adds value: lighter tops and fronts, darker hatched sides.

## Talk about it

- Why does the box above the horizon show its bottom?
- In the railroad picture, the rails are parallel in real life. Why do they meet on the page?
- What happens to the spaces between the posts as they go back?
- Move the vanishing point low. What kind of view is that? (A worm's-eye view.) High? (A bird's-eye view.)

## Good to know

- **Example street** loads a finished scene to explore. **Save picture** downloads the drawing as a PNG without the guides.
- The drawing is kept in this browser only and comes back next time. **Clear page** starts fresh.
- Every box front sits on the picture plane, so fronts always cover the sides of other boxes. That keeps the drawing tidy; real scenes can have objects at many depths.
- The paintings in Find it were made with an image generator, so their edges may be a little off. If your lines cross in a few places, that is a good thing to talk about.
