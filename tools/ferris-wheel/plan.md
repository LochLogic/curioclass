arc: explore-first
minutes: 50
focus: F-TF.A; F-TF.B; F-TF.C
goal: I can model a Ferris wheel ride with a sinusoid, read amplitude, midline and period from it, and explain radians as arc length on the unit circle.
materials: The tool on the projector and on student devices. Graph paper. A string and a round lid for the radian demo.
grouping: Pairs on devices, with whole-class pauses.
---
## Before class

- Open **Ride** with the defaults: radius 15 m, hub 18 m, one turn in 40 s, boarding at the bottom.
- Note the equation for the defaults: h(t) = 18 + 15 sin(2πt / 40 − π/2).

## Hook (5 min)

1. Press **Play** and let the graph grow for one full turn. "What shape is this graph? Have you seen it anywhere else?"
2. "Anything that repeats can be modeled this way: tides, sound, daylight. Today, a Ferris wheel."

## Explore (15 min)

1. Pairs press **Pause** and drag the car a quarter turn. The graph dot jumps to t = 10 s, h = 18 m. Then they drag the dot to t = 30 s.
2. Pairs change one slider at a time and ride again, recording which part of the graph changed: radius, hub height or time for one turn.
3. Pairs try **Board at** each position and both **Turn** directions, and use **Keep this graph** to compare up to 3 curves.
4. "Why do Bottom-counterclockwise and Bottom-clockwise give the same graph, but Right side does not?"

## Make sense (12 min)

1. On the board, connect each slider to the equation: hub height is the midline, radius is the amplitude, time for one turn is the period.
2. Switch to **Unit Circle**. Press **One radian**: a radius-length rod bends onto the rim. Wrap the string around the lid to show the same thing.
3. Press **Unroll the arc**. "The height is sin θ. Why does about 6.28 of these fit around?"
4. Turn on **Show cosine**. The identity line always reads 1.000. "Why must sin² θ + cos² θ = 1?"

## Apply (13 min)

1. Pairs open **Match** and work through the target graphs. **Check** accepts any wheel within 0.5 m, so equivalent wheels count.
2. Pairs who finish open **Real Wheels** and compare the London Eye with Chicago 1893. "The London Eye moves at about 0.21 m/s. Why can you barely feel it move?"

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: "A wheel has radius 20 m, a hub 25 m high, turns once in 60 s, and you board at the bottom. Write h(t) and give the highest and lowest heights." Then: "Why is 18 + 15 sin(2πt / 40 − π/2) the same as 18 − 15 cos(2πt / 40)?"

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** change only one slider at a time and write the equation after each change.
- **Support:** use **Step π/6** and **Step π/4** to read exact values.
- **Stretch:** turn on **Show sideways distance** and explain the live check x² + (h − D)² = r².
- **Stretch:** "A radian is about 57.3 degrees. Where does that number come from?"

## Short on time

- **Ride** with one slider at a time (10 min).
- **One radian** in **Unit Circle** (5 min), then the exit card (5 min).
