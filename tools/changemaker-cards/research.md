# Research packet: changemaker-cards

Concept: Collect cards of real people who improved their communities; each starts face down with a "before" picture, and asking who, what, why and how flips it, then the album lines up from long ago to today.
Grade: 2
Standards: D2.His.3.K-2, D2.Civ.14.K-2

## Standards in plain words

- D2.His.3.K-2 (Generate questions about individuals and groups who have shaped a significant historical change): Second graders ask who, what, why, and how questions to discover how remarkable historical figures improved society.
- D2.Civ.14.K-2 (Describe how people have tried to improve their communities over time): Second graders learn how individuals and groups worked together across different eras to solve unfair problems and help others.

Card flip interaction flow in Curio Class:
1. Face-down presentation: The card displays a detailed illustration of the community problem ("before").
2. Four inquiry prompts:
   - Who saw this problem? (Reveals the changemaker's identity)
   - What was unfair or needed fixing? (Explains the historical challenge)
   - Why did it matter to the community? (Identifies empathy and human need)
   - How did they take action? (Details the peaceful solution and collaboration)
3. Card flip: All four prompts completed flips the card with a rewarding chime, displaying the changemaker's finished portrait and key achievement.
4. Timeline album: The card slots into the student's album in chronological order from long ago to today.

## Common mistakes

1. Assuming changemakers acted entirely alone, ignoring the large teams, neighbors, and community members who joined them.
2. Believing historical heroes had magical powers rather than using everyday courage, learning, persistence, and cooperation.
3. Thinking historical change happens instantly overnight, rather than through years of patient organizing and hard work.
4. Viewing "before" problems as ancient ancient fairy tales rather than real struggles experienced by real people.
5. Confusing the chronological order of eras (e.g., placing space exploration before the Underground Railroad).

## Sensitivity

- Treat historical injustices (slavery, child labor, segregation, labor exploitation) with historical accuracy and age-appropriate delicacy, emphasizing human dignity, resilience, and positive action rather than graphic trauma.
- Present a diverse collection of changemakers across race, gender, and background.
- Highlight peaceful, civic problem-solving methods: writing, marching, organizing, teaching, and inventing.
