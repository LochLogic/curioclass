# Changemaker Cards (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-2-heroes-and-changemakers, grade 2. Codes: D2.His.3.K-2, D2.Civ.14.K-2
- Format / family: cards / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Collect cards of real people who improved their communities, but each card starts face down with only a "before" picture; ask the right questions (Who? What was wrong? What did they do? What changed?) to flip it, and line the collection up from long ago to today.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a card album with empty slots and one face-down card showing a town before (a dark street, a library that kids couldn't use). "Ask a question to find out who changed this."
  - Core loop: the student picks or builds questions from a who/what/why/how wheel; each good question reveals part of the card (portrait, what they did, how, what changed). Cards include adults and kids, famous and local, across many times (for example a librarian who started bookmobiles, a kid who led a park cleanup, a civil rights leader).
  - The aha or the end state: the full album sits on a timeline, so the student sees people have tried to improve communities over a long time and in many ways (building, speaking up, helping, voting).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a blank card for a local changemaker the student knows.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.3.K-2: generating questions is how each card is unlocked.
  - D2.Civ.14.K-2: the album timeline shows how people tried to improve communities over time.
- Not like: tools-for-change (grade 5 strategies for a local problem); symbol-spotter (holidays and national symbols); storm-helpers (helpers after one storm).
- Art: people (illustrated portraits of real people, checked against photos for respect and likeness), because real faces make the history human.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Collect cards of real people who improved their communities; each starts face down with a "before" picture, and asking who, what, why and how flips it, then the album lines up from long ago to today.
- Flags: sensitive (choice and telling of real people's stories; fresh review agent per rule 5); needs a data source (each person's facts sourced)
