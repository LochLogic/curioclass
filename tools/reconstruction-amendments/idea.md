# Reconstruction Amendments (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-8-reconstruction, grade 8. Codes: D2.Civ.4.6-8, D2.Civ.14.6-8, D2.His.2.6-8, D2.Civ.13.6-8
- Format / family: explorer / founding-docs. Fit: good
- One idea: Open the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments one at a time, each with three panels (what it promised, how it was carried out, how it was undermined), and follow who acted at each step (Congress, presidents, the courts, states, Black citizens themselves), then see the same amendments used again a century later.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three amendment cards on a desk with a timeline from 1865 to 1965 underneath.
  - Core loop: open an amendment; read its text in plain English; step through the panels: carried out (the Freedmen's Bureau, enforcement laws, Black voters and officeholders such as Hiram Revels), undermined (Black Codes, violence, court rulings like Cruikshank and Plessy, the end of federal enforcement in 1877, poll taxes and literacy tests). A branch tag marks which part of government acted, at which level.
  - The aha or the end state: a chart of Black voter registration in one Southern state before and after its new laws; then a change and continuity board ending with Brown v. Board and the Voting Rights Act using the same amendments.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the teacher picks the state for the chart.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Civ.4.6-8: the branch tags show the powers and limits of Congress, presidents, courts and states.
  - D2.Civ.14.6-8: Black citizens organizing, voting and holding office, then later movements using the amendments.
  - D2.His.2.6-8: the change and continuity board from 1865 to 1965.
  - D2.Civ.13.6-8: each amendment as a policy, with its purpose, how it was carried out and its consequences.
- Not like: voices-of-freedom (before the war); premise-inspector (grade 11, reasoning in founding documents); rights-match (grade 5, Bill of Rights). Shares the founding-docs engine.
- Art: public domain (Library of Congress photographs and documents, to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Open the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments: see what each promised, how it was carried out and how Jim Crow undermined it, who acted at each step, and how the same amendments were used again a century later.
- Flags: sensitive (racial violence and Jim Crow told plainly with care; fresh review); needs a data source (voter registration figures, checked)
