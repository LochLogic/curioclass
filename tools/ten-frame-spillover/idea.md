# Ten Frame Spillover (idea card)

- Module: math-k-teen-numbers, grade k. Codes: K.NBT.A
- Format / family: sandbox / ten-frame. Fit: good.
- One idea: Drop ladybugs into a ten frame; when it fills, the extras spill onto a second frame, and every teen number shows up as one full ten and some more ones.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a big leaf with two empty ten frames and a jar of ladybugs. "Tap the jar to drop a ladybug."
  - Core loop: each tap drops one ladybug into the next square. At 10 the frame glows "a full ten!" and the next one spills to the second frame. The screen builds "ten and 4 more, fourteen, 10 + 4 = 14" as you go, and a voice says it.
  - Break-apart mode: 16 ladybugs land scattered on the leaf. The child drags ten into the first frame; the rest go to the second, and the equation writes itself.
  - The aha or the end state: "four-teen" means 4 and a ten; eleven and twelve are the odd names, and the tool says so plainly.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): hide the numerals and say only the words; a mode where the child builds the equation from tiles.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - K.NBT.A: composing 11 to 19 by filling one ten and adding ones, decomposing a scattered teen set into ten ones and some more, and recording each with a drawing (the frames) and an equation (10 + 6 = 16).
- Not like: crayon-boxes (grade 1, tens and ones to 99 as bundles), sheep-count (counting a set), hundred-hop (the number sequence).
- Art: generated scenes (a big leaf and cheerful ladybugs), because the counters fill the screen; frames drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Drop ladybugs into a ten frame; when it fills, the extras spill onto a second frame and the screen says "ten and 4 more, fourteen, 10 + 4 = 14." Then split a scattered 17 into a ten and the rest.
- Flags: none
