## What it teaches
Students compare ways to clean missing, inconsistent and duplicated survey answers. They learn to explain who each choice counts and what a cleaned result still cannot establish.

## Key skills
- Compare at least two fixes before choosing one.
- Distinguish a submission from a student.
- Notice when a cleaning rule removes a whole group.
- Support a report with evidence from the cleaned data.
- Separate a stated preference from future buying behavior.

## Support at home
- Ask your child how two spellings of the same meal might change a tally. Make a small pretend tally together.
- When a survey result comes up, ask who answered and who might be missing.
- Discuss a time a guess filled a gap. What would help readers recognize it as a guess?

## A five-minute start
Open Clean. Try Drop rows with a blank, then Keep the blanks. Compare the Grade 6 row and the lunch chart.
Ask why the students disappeared even though they answered the lunch question. Try another fix on each remaining problem.

## Modes
Clean offers Chart, Rows and Days a week views. The nine fixes always rebuild from the original survey, so their order does not destroy evidence.
Report unlocks after two fixes have been tried on every problem. Students select reasons, a statement of coverage, at least two limits, and their recommendation.
Part 1 describes each cleaning rule; Part 2 and the evidence show the combined result of all three choices.
The first check records five statement matches. A second check supplies any still-wrong statements, visibly labeled Marcus fixed.
Unchanged Marcus-supplied reasons retain that label in later drafts and never earn first-check credit.
Send to Ms. Reyes is a fictional in-page exchange. Nothing is sent to a person or service. Copy report uses the device clipboard when available.

## Talk questions
- Why did removing blank Days also remove answered lunch picks?
- Does reaching Add it mean the cleaning choices were sound?
- What is gained and assumed when three vegetarian spellings are merged?
- Why is a missing answer different from zero days?
- What would a taste test tell the cafeteria that this survey cannot?

## Good to know
Ridgeview, Ms. Reyes, Marcus, all survey rows and replies are fictional. The problems are documented survey-data problems.
The school has 452 students. There are 391 respondents, 61 nonrespondents, 412 submitted rows and 21 extra copies.
All 118 sixth graders used paper forms. Page 2, with Days, was missing. Online Days was required.
Merge assumes Vegetarian, vegetarian and veg name one meal. The menu decision compares the literal Vegetarian category with the least-picked menu lunch.
With spellings separate, the three bars stay separate. With them merged and one extra copy removed, Vegetarian has 53 picks and Beef tacos 48.
Days averages omit blanks and include filled guesses when Fill is chosen. Filled values are explicitly tagged.
The Days caption counts blanks or filled guesses in the retained rows. Dropping write-ins leaves 92 grade 6 rows, rather than 118.
Reports and survey choices are kept only for the current page session. Reload starts again. The kit may remember Sound and Motion preferences.
The planned casual-peer voice is listed in voice/lines.json. This build uses the kit's browser speech fallback; no audio was recorded.

## Standards
