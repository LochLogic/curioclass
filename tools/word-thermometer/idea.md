# Word Thermometer (idea card)

- Module: ela-6-connotation-and-context, grade 6. Codes: L.6.4, L.6.5, RL.6.4, RI.6.4
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good for connotation: sorting near-synonyms on a cool-to-hot scale is a real classification, and dropping the sorted words into a passage tests the sort.
- One idea: The student lines up near-synonyms on a thermometer from warm praise to cold insult (thrifty, frugal, careful, cheap, stingy), then swaps them into a story and a news paragraph and watches the tone of the whole passage change while the facts stay the same, so they learn that words with the same meaning carry different feelings and that writers choose them on purpose.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a tall thermometer, warm (admiring) at the top, cold (insulting) at the bottom, and a bin of five word tiles that all mean "doesn't spend much".
  - Core loop: the student drags tiles onto the scale and justifies each placement with a context card ("You'd call your grandmother this to her face"). Then the passage below picks up a highlighted word from the scale: a narrator describing a shopkeeper, or a news report about a city budget. Swapping "thrifty" for "stingy" shifts a tone gauge and changes how readers see the person. Later sets include figurative phrases ("a heart of stone", "tight-fisted") that must be read in context before they can be placed, and word pairs where the student names the relationship (stingy and generous are opposites; a miser is a kind of stingy person) to place a hard word.
  - The aha or the end state: the student rewrites one passage twice, once to make readers admire a character and once to make them dislike her, changing only five words, and a side-by-side shows the facts never moved.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a class thermometer where everyone places one contested word and sees the spread.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MOVE L.6.4: context-roots-dictionary (ela-6-8-academic-vocabulary-strategies), which teaches context, Greek and Latin roots and the dictionary as its whole loop for grades 7 and 8 and should add a grade 6 level; here context is used, but word-solving strategy is not taught in full.
  - L.6.5: connotation sorting (5c), figurative phrases read in context (5a), and naming word relationships to place a word (5b).
  - RL.6.4: swapping words in the story passage and explaining how each choice changes the tone.
  - RI.6.4: the news paragraph, where word choice changes how readers judge the facts.
- Not like: double-meanings (grade 8, puns, irony and allusions); tone-dial (grade 10, rewriting a scene in five tones); rhetoric-spotter (grade 9, sorting persuasive appeals).
- Art: drawn in code for the thermometer; generated scenes for each passage (the shopkeeper's counter, the city hall budget meeting).
