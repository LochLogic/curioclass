# Factory Floor (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-9-industrial-revolution, grade 9. Codes: D2.His.2.9-12, D2.Eco.13.9-12, D2.Geo.7.9-12
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Run a Lancashire cotton mill from 1760 to 1900, buying machines and training workers, and watch output per worker soar while the town around it changes: who moves in, how they live, and what stays the same.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a cottage spinning wheel in 1760 and a ledger: "Make 100 pounds of yarn. How many hands does it take?"
  - Core loop: each decade, spend profits on capital (spinning jenny, water frame, steam engine, power loom) or human capital (training, a mechanics' school). A productivity meter shows yarn per worker and real wages. Raw cotton arrives from plantations worked by enslaved people in the US South, shown plainly on the supply line.
  - Town layer: every upgrade pulls migrants from farms and from Ireland; the town map grows terraces, a canal and a railway; disease, child labor and the Factory Acts arrive as events with real figures.
  - The aha or the end state: a 1760 vs 1900 split view: output and living standards rose over the long run, but early workers paid the price; continuity cards show what did not change (long hours, the cotton supply's tie to slavery until 1865).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare a Japanese silk mill of the 1890s to see the same technology spread.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.9-12: the decade-by-decade run with change and continuity cards and the 1760 vs 1900 split view.
  - D2.Eco.13.9-12: capital and human-capital choices drive the productivity and real-wage meters, showing why investment raises growth and living standards.
  - D2.Geo.7.9-12: machines spread outward from Lancashire while the town pulls rural and Irish migrants in; a world map shows the technology reaching other countries and moving people there too.
- Not like: mill-town (grade 8, first-person Lowell worker story); baron-or-builder (judging industrialists).
- Art: generated scenes (mill floor, the town growing over decades) and drawn-in-code charts, because the town's change is the point.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run a Lancashire cotton mill from 1760 to 1900; invest in machines and skills, watch output per worker climb, and see the town fill with migrants and the costs paid by workers and enslaved cotton growers.
- Flags: needs a data source (historical productivity and real-wage series, checked); sensitive (child labor, slavery in the cotton supply)
