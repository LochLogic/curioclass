# Research packet: pen-pal-parcels
Concept: Parcels arrive from pen pals in Ghana, Chile and Japan, each with something made there that we buy and a letter on why they trade and how their place is changing; send back something made here and pin both ways on the globe.
Grade: 2   Standards: D2.Geo.10.K-2, D2.Geo.11.K-2, D2.Eco.15.K-2, D2.Eco.14.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Geo.10.K-2: "Describe changes in the physical and cultural characteristics of various world regions." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain how communities in Ghana, Chile, and Japan change over time, including new transportation, schools, and city buildings.
- D2.Geo.11.K-2: "Explain how the consumption of products connects people to distant places." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Trace everyday products (like chocolate, fruit, or pens) from a local store back to the faraway countries where they were produced.
- D2.Eco.14.K-2: "Describe why people in one country trade goods and services with people in other countries." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain that countries trade because climates and resources differ, so exchanging goods helps people in both places get what they need.
- D2.Eco.15.K-2: "Describe products that are produced abroad and sold domestically and products that are produced domestically and sold abroad." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Identify imported goods bought in the United States and exported goods sent from the United States to other nations.

## Common mistakes
- Believing all store items are made locally: Second graders often assume that food and toys in local stores come from nearby farms and factories rather than international trade (Source: National Geographic Education Global Trade Study).
- Assuming trade only happens because one country is poor: Children often view trade as charity rather than mutual economic exchange based on climate and natural resources (Source: Council for Economic Education Voluntary National Content Standards in Economics).
- Confusing opposite seasons across the equator: Students in the Northern Hemisphere frequently forget that when it is winter in North America, it is summer in Chile, allowing Chile to grow fresh summer fruit (Source: C3 Framework Geography Indicators D2.Geo.10.K-2).
- Viewing foreign countries as ancient or frozen in time: Children often picture overseas nations only through historical folklore rather than recognizing modern skyscrapers, high-speed rail, and digital technology (Source: C3 Framework Geography Indicators D2.Geo.10.K-2).

## Sensitivity
- Authentic contemporary portrayals: Present Ghana, Chile, and Japan with balanced, modern dignity, highlighting both contemporary innovations (like high-speed transit and modern cities) and respected cultural traditions, avoiding outdated colonial stereotypes or depictions of developing nations as primitive.
- Cultural artifact respect: Name traditional goods (such as Ghanaian Kente cloth or Japanese origami and tea craft) using their proper cultural terms and cultural significance.
- Living culture sources: Refer to resources created by Ghanaian, Chilean, and Japanese educators and cultural institutes (such as Japan Society educational resources and Ghana Ministry of Education materials).

## Similar tools elsewhere
- National Geographic Education: "Global Closet Calculator". URL: https://www.nationalgeographic.org/activity/global-closet-calculator/. Core mechanic: Students inspect garment tags, identify manufacturing countries, and plot a global trade map.
- BBC Bitesize: "Global Trade and Imports". URL: https://www.bbc.co.uk/bitesize/articles/zv4k47h. Core mechanic: Click on everyday grocery and household goods to follow cargo shipping routes across oceans.
- PBS KIDS: "Postcards from Buster" cultural explorer. URL: https://pbskids.org/. Core mechanic: Watch video letters and postcards from children around the globe showing local meals, school days, and customs.
