# Untangle: a note for grown-ups

## What it teaches

Untangle is a puzzle game about seeing connections in space and planning a move. Your child drags stars until none of their connecting lines cross or crowd each other. The solved sky then lights into an invented picture.

## Key skills

- Spot which connections are tangled and trace where they go.
- Plan a new position before dropping a star.
- Try a different arrangement when a first idea makes more crossings.
- Use the live dashes, crossing rings and count to refine a move.

## Support at home

- Draw a few dots joined by lines on scrap paper. Try drawing the same connections in another arrangement with fewer crossings.
- Make a loop of string on a table and cross it over itself. Take turns pointing out a crossing and finding a way to open it.
- After a sky lights up, ask which move helped most. There is no need to race or finish a particular number of skies.

## The game

There are 20 First Skies, growing from 6 stars to 18, then 550 ladder skies in 22 painted worlds, plus a new sky each day. Every sky is open from the start. The world's last sky reveals another painted constellation. These are invented pictures, not real constellations or traditional sky stories.

Underneath the game is graph theory: points joined by connections. Each supplied puzzle has a crossing-free straight-line arrangement. Many different arrangements win; your child's solution need not look like the hidden picture.

The ladder's predicted difficulty rises from 3 to 80 using the spec's model. Individual skies can feel easier or harder depending on the player. The model does not promise that every player will need more moves on every later sky.

## Stars and controls

Stars depend on moves, never time. Three stars means finishing within the displayed pace, two means within twice that pace, and any other solve earns one. The pace is one move per star for First Skies and the daily, or one per scrambled star for a ladder sky. Verified solutions reach those paces.

Undo removes a kept move and is free. Restart restores the same tangled start. A drag shorter than four pixels returns to its starting point and costs no move. While a star is held, the count is live, so exploring before a drop is welcome. The sky wins only after a drop with no trouble.

Use Tab to focus a star; Enter or Space lifts and drops it. Arrow keys move it, Shift plus an arrow makes a smaller move, and Escape puts it back. C opens the Star chart; W inside the chart opens the world overview. The shell also provides Undo, Restart, Sound, Motion and Scores.

The daily's final reveal shows the six straight beams that created its web. Sound can be switched off. Calm motion keeps the dashes and rings, with still artwork and a short reveal cross-fade.

## Privacy

Everything saves on this device only. There are no accounts or ads, and nothing is sent anywhere. The local high-score board uses three initials, never a name.
