# Trade Island (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-global-trade, grade 12. Codes: D2.Eco.14.9-12, D2.Eco.15.9-12
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Run the economy of one of two islands that can each make fish and cloth at different costs, discover that both gain by specializing and trading even when one island is better at both, then add tariffs, a currency swing and a factory that moves offshore and see who on each island wins and loses.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two islands across a strait, each with workers, boats and looms, and a production chart: "Your island is better at both. Should you trade at all?"
  - Core loop: assign workers to fish or cloth; set a trade deal with the other island (played by the computer); consumption bars show both islands ending with more when each specializes in its lower opportunity cost. Seasons add storms and new tools that shift costs.
  - Policy round: add a tariff, change the exchange rate, or let the cloth factory move to the cheaper island; panels track growth, jobs, wages, workers' rights rules and the reef's health on each island.
  - The aha or the end state: a winners and losers table across both islands (fishers, weavers, shoppers, factory owners), and the student's short case for or against the tariff, with a link to a real trade dispute.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two students run the islands and bargain.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.14.9-12: the specialize-and-trade loop shows comparative advantage at work.
  - D2.Eco.15.9-12: the policy round explains globalization's effects on growth, labor, rights, environment and income distribution on each island.
- Not like: where-s-my-phone-from (real supply-chain data, grade 10); the-buyout (one market's structure); opportunity-cost-game (marginal choices).
- Art: generated scenes (two islands with distinct cultures, harbors, looms) and people (illustrated) workers, because each group's gains and losses need faces.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run one of two islands, find that specializing and trading leaves both better off even when one is better at everything, then add a tariff, a currency swing and a factory move and see who wins and loses.
- Flags: none
