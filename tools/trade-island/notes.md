## What it teaches

Students calculate what an island gives up to produce one good, find mutually beneficial trades, and explain why being better at both jobs does not remove the gains from trade. They then use evidence to argue how a tariff or a factory move affects different groups, jobs, rights and the environment.

## Key skills

- Calculate opportunity cost from production per worker.
- Compare production with consumption after a trade.
- Distinguish a gain for an economy from a gain for every group.
- Trace policy effects across two economies and their different currencies.
- Build an argument with gains, losses and evidence from both places.

## Support at home

- Talk about two household tasks. Could sharing them differently help even when one person is faster at both?
- Pick an item already at home and discuss who might benefit or lose if its import price rose.
- Ask your student to explain why more factory jobs and cleaner water need not move together.

## A five-minute start

Open Islands and commit a Yes or No prediction. Move Pell's workers to find its best outcome with the strait closed. Open Season 2, make a Cost card, then test a deal. Ask what changed in consumption rather than simply what the island produced.

## The three modes

**Islands:** Every island has 100 workers. One full basket needs three fish and one cloth. Pell's best alone is 120 baskets in Seasons 1 and 2, 75 in Storm and 150 with Motor boats. Wren's best alone is 50. Tokens are rounded to the nearest five workers; the numeric counts and model use individual workers. Whole baskets round down. The computer chooses Wren's split to maximize exact baskets and accepts only when whole baskets exceed 50. A deal remains while the worker slider changes, unless Pell cannot cover it. Each season keeps its own choices.

**Policy:** This is a separate money-based model, not a continuation of Islands' 100 workers. Quantities represent seasonal fish crates and cloth bolts; fractional jobs are worker equivalents. Prices clear the two markets. Shoppers' changes are consumer surplus, producer changes are supply-curve areas, owners' changes are profit, and workers' changes are earnings above other work. Owners are Pell residents even if the factory moves. All changes compare with the original open-trade baseline, not the last lever setting. Totals use unrounded values. A stronger shell changes the units' purchasing power, so Growth is not added up in either island when the exchange rate moves. These estimates are teaching assumptions, not national accounts or a forecast.

**Case:** Pin 2 to 5 rows or cards while the chosen policy is on. Include both islands, a gain, a loss, and a Jobs, Wages, Rights or Reef card. Pins retain their values and policy settings when levers move. Write at least 15 words, name Pell and Wren, and refer to a pinned group or measure from each island. Isolated letters, repeated-word padding and an unrelated word list do not meet those checks. The check tests structure, not correctness or persuasiveness. Either stance can pass. Save stores choices, evidence and writing on this device; nothing is sent. Saving rechecks the evidence and text.

## Talk questions

- Why can Pell gain from making less cloth even though it weaves faster?
- Which opportunity cost changes in the storm? Why does that erase a mutually beneficial price?
- Can Pell's total rise while its shoppers lose? Does that settle whether a tariff is justified?
- What changes if stronger labor rules and cleaner dye treatment make moving less profitable?
- Which pinned value supports your conclusion, and which makes your conclusion harder to defend?

D2.Eco.14.9-12 is practiced through costs, specialization and deals. D2.Eco.15.9-12 is practiced through policy levers, distribution, labor conditions and reef outcomes, then a two-island argument. The rights cards are invented model rules, not descriptions of any current country. The real historical cards are comparisons, not claims that the fictional islands depict Cambodia or Switzerland. ## Sources and model limits

- [Ricardo, chapter 7](https://www.econlib.org/library/Ricardo/ricP.html?chapter_num=8): the Portugal and England example, first published in 1817. Cloth requires 90 versus 100 workers, wine 80 versus 120. These are Ricardo's illustrative quantities. Econlib counts the preface in its URL numbering.
- [OpenStax, comparative advantage](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/33-1-absolute-and-comparative-advantage): gains arise from different opportunity costs. Equal costs remove a strictly mutually beneficial price in this model.
- [OpenStax, protectionism](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/34-1-protectionism-an-indirect-subsidy-from-consumers-to-producers): tariffs redistribute gains and impose efficiency costs. The model permits a small large-country tariff to benefit Pell at Wren's expense; both together lose.
- [Flaaen, Hortacsu and Tintelnot, AER](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20190611) and [AEA research highlight](https://www.aeaweb.org/research/washing-machines-2018-tariffs-effect): 2018 washer prices nearly 12% higher, dryers similarly affected, about 1,800 jobs, more than $800,000 annual consumer cost per job. Do not infer that this is the cost of every tariff.
- [ILO, Cambodian factories](https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/new-trade-regime-textiles-and-clothing-how-cambodian-factories-improved): the 1999 agreement, inspections and public findings. [USTR, January 2002 agreement release](https://ustr.gov/archive/Document_Library/Press_Releases/2002/January/US-Cambodian_Textile_Agreement_Links_Increasing_Trade_with_Improving_Workers%27_Rights.html) confirms 6% normal quota growth and the initial bonus up to 14%; the renewal raised that ceiling to 18%. The tool refers to the initial deal, not the renewal or the actual annual awards.
- [CRS IN10230](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN10230): the Swiss franc cap and its removal on 15 January 2015. [Reuters, 15 January 2015](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/currency-market-furore-wipes-%24100-billion-off-swiss-stocks-29387) reports the exporter share-price falls. A stronger currency affects exporters and import buyers differently.
- UNEP, *Sustainability and Circularity in the Textile Value Chain* (2020): textile wastewater and environmental effects. The reef index and its coefficients here are fictional model assumptions.

The factory moves only if its post-settlement Wren profit exceeds its Pell profit by more than one shell. Higher wages and stricter dye treatment change that calculation. Gains and losses are always stated with words and signs, not color alone. No score, best record or claim of mastered economics is stored.
