# Deaf Culture Explorer (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-6-12-deaf-culture-and-community, grade 6-12. Codes: WRS.Cultures, WRS.Communities
- Format / family: explorer / asl. Fit: good: a rich, layered collection to browse, built around Deaf people telling their own story.
- One idea: Walk through a Deaf community center whose rooms open into history, culture and daily life (the first American school for the deaf in 1817, the 1880 Milan congress and the long ban on sign in schools, Deaf President Now in 1988, Deaf arts, everyday etiquette), with each room led by an illustrated Deaf host and captioned comic-strip stories, so the student sees Deaf culture as a culture with a language, not as a disability.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the lobby of a community center, rooms labeled History, Language, Arts, Everyday Life, Today. An illustrated Deaf host signs a welcome in the Sign Strip player, captioned.
  - Core loop: each room holds objects, illustrated scenes and short captioned comic-strip stories. Students open layers (what happened, how people felt, what changed). In each room they answer one "perspective" question in a journal: why the practice makes sense from inside the culture (why eye contact matters, why many Deaf people use a capital D).
  - The aha or the end state: the "Today" room links the past to now: Deaf-owned businesses, Deaf artists, how to find and join local Deaf events respectfully as a learner, and a checklist the student builds of how to be a good guest in Deaf spaces.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a guided tour mode; the journal stays on this device only (the screen says so).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - WRS.Cultures: relating Deaf community practices and products (storytelling, name signs, attention-getting, Deaf arts) to the perspectives behind them.
  - WRS.Communities: learning how to take part in the Deaf community beyond the classroom as a learner, and how to keep learning (events, Deaf-made media).
- Not like: asl-hello-booth (practicing greetings); mundo-hispano (Spanish-speaking cultures); asl-story-theater (performance).
- Art: graphics only, per docs/agents/ASL-GRAPHICS.md: the host and storytellers are illustrated signers (rendered body and face plates, people review) with vector Sign Strip hands; captioned illustrated comics for the signed stories; illustrated historical scenes; public-domain photos only of places, objects and documents (Library of Congress or Gallaudet sources, ids and rights checked at spec); illustrated rooms (people, illustrated). No video, no photos of people.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Explore a Deaf community center whose rooms open into Deaf history, ASL, arts and everyday life, led by an illustrated Deaf host and captioned stories, and build your own guide to joining Deaf spaces respectfully as a learner.
- Flags: sensitive (Deaf history including oralism, identity and cochlear implant views: fresh review, rule 5); needs a data source (photo rights for places and documents; sourced history for the comics); open question whether archival photos of people (for example Deaf President Now) fall under "no photos of people" (ASL-GRAPHICS.md); the on-screen practice note (one common form; practice with a fluent Deaf signer or your ASL teacher when you can); the old concept line claims content "developed with Deaf educators", which is not true yet, so the new line drops it.
