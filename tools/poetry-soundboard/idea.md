# Poetry Soundboard (idea card)

- Module: ela-7-sound-and-form-in-poetry, grade 7. Codes: RL.7.4, RL.7.5
- Format / family: studio / poetry. Fit: good: the student remixes a poem's sounds and form, hears the result, and records their own reading.
- One idea: The student puts a public-domain poem on a soundboard where rhyme, repetition, alliteration and meter each have a channel, mutes or changes one channel at a time and hears what the stanza loses, then rearranges a sonnet's lines and a short soliloquy to see how form carries meaning, so they learn that a poem's sounds and shape are part of what it says.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a mixing board with four channel strips over a stanza from a public-domain poem (for example "The Raven" stanza 1, or "Stopping by Woods" if its status is confirmed), each strip lit where its sound happens.
  - Core loop: the student mutes the rhyme channel (rhyme words swapped for plain synonyms) and hears both versions; mutes alliteration; breaks the meter by adding a syllable. For each change they write what the stanza lost (the hypnotic pull, the knock-knock sound). Form level: the fourteen lines of a Shakespeare sonnet are shuffled; the student rebuilds it and finds the turn, and a soliloquy is compared with the same thoughts written as a conversation.
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes a four-line stanza of their own using two channels on purpose, and records a reading of it. On screen: "Your recording stays on this device. Nothing is uploaded." Playback shows their stressed beats beside the meter they aimed for (they tap the beats, no speech recognition).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a class reading where everyone claps the meter as the stanza plays.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.7.4: hearing and explaining how rhyme, repetition and alliteration shape a stanza's meaning, and connotations of the swapped words.
  - RL.7.5: rebuilding the sonnet, finding its turn, and comparing a soliloquy with a conversation to see how form shapes meaning.
- Not like: poem-drum (K-2, clapping a poem's beat and finding rhymes); double-meanings (grade 8, puns and irony); globe-theatre-stage (grade 12, staging interpretations).
- Art: drawn in code for the soundboard; public domain illustrations for each poem (Met or AIC object ids, to be checked at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Put a poem on a soundboard with channels for rhyme, repetition, alliteration and meter; mute one at a time to hear what the stanza loses, rebuild a shuffled sonnet to find its turn, then write and record a stanza of your own.
- Flags: needs voice production (readings that keep the meter, synthesized voices must be checked at spec); records audio (on-screen line that nothing leaves the device; no speech recognition); needs a data source (confirm public-domain status of each poem)
