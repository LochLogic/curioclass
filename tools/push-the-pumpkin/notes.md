## What it teaches

Children compare how far and which way objects move after pushes and pulls. They plan a fair comparison, then use the ball's path and stop spot to judge whether their own barn design works.

## Key skills

- Make a guess before trying something.
- Change one thing in a comparison.
- Compare stop flags and count fence posts.
- Notice a change in speed or direction.
- Judge a design from what actually happened, then change it.

## Support at home

- Roll a soft ball gently across a clear floor. Compare a small push and a bigger push from the same place.
- Take turns stopping a slowly rolling soft ball with your hands. Talk about the push that stopped it.
- Use a folded piece of cardboard to turn a slowly rolling ball. Say what changed before trying again.

## A five-minute start

Open Push. Tap Go, then Big and Go. Invite children to point to the two flags and say what changed. Try pushing from the other side. Pick Wagon to explore a pull.

## The modes

Push is free exploration. Every push leaves a flag, with up to eight flags held on the page. Clear flags makes room for a new comparison.

Find out holds five investigations. Children predict before testing, choose a fair pumpkin comparison, and use the two stop flags to compare. A wrong prediction is useful evidence, not a lost point. The barn wall remembers their original guesses and what they observed.

Barn holds three design puzzles. First watch a roll with no pieces. Children place a ramp, bale or board, roll again and decide Yes or Not yet. Each try remembers their judgment beside what happened. A faded ball helps compare the working design with a no-piece run.

## Talk together

What stayed the same? Which flag is farther from Start? Where was Jo when it went the other way? Did the ball stop inside the door? Did the buckets actually fall? What did our piece change?

## Good to know

Teacher led, kindergarten science, NGSS K-PS2-1 and K-PS2-2. Sound starts on, after the first tap. Read repeats the current next step. Motion off keeps the same waiting time and shows the result without moving the ball. No student work is saved and no personal information is requested. The Motion preference is remembered on this device.

The distances, relative toy weights and barn outcomes are story values for comparing one change at a time. They are not measured predictions for real pumpkins, wagons, ramps or hay. The tool does not assess friction or combine strength and direction in the five investigations. The design puzzles combine speed and direction.

The faded ball repeats the no-piece path, including its original stop. A ramp placed at Start faces downhill; a ramp farther along faces uphill. The wall keeps each child's first guess beside the observed pictures and stop flags. The small bucket collision compares the same ball and stack, not a universal rule about impact force.

Science sources: NGSS K-PS2 clarification and boundary statements; NASA Glenn Research Center, Newton's Laws of Motion; OpenStax College Physics, Impulse. A ramp can increase speed, a board can turn a rolling ball, and a push opposite the motion can slow or stop it.
