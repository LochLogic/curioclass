# Claim vs Claim (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-argument-and-rebuttal, grade 8. Codes: W.8.1
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good: the student plays a student representative turning a messy group chat into a formal statement for the school board.
- One idea: A class group chat is arguing about a new rule (no phones from first bell to last), and the student, as the class representative, sorts each message into their claim, an opposing claim or neither, then writes a formal paragraph for the school board that states the strongest opposing claim fairly and shows exactly where the two part ways, so they learn to distinguish their claim from the alternatives and to argue in a formal style.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a phone screen full of chat messages from classmates (with grawlixes where tempers flare) and a board meeting invitation: "Two minutes, in writing, by Friday."
  - Core loop: the student picks a side, then sorts each message: supports my claim, opposing claim, or neither (jokes, insults, off-topic). Opposing messages that make the same point are merged into one opposing claim, stated in its strongest form ("Parents need to reach students in an emergency"). A tone translator helps rewrite chat language into formal style; the student does the rewriting, and the tool flags slang, sarcasm and "you guys". The student marks the exact point of disagreement (both sides agree on safety; they disagree on whether the office phone is enough).
  - The aha or the end state: the formal statement is read by an illustrated board member who restates the opposing claim back: if the student's version is a straw man, the board member says so and asks for the fair version. The final statement introduces the claim, names and answers the strongest opposing claim, and keeps a formal style throughout.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the real school's own rule as a topic, with the teacher's chat prompt.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.8.1: introducing a claim, acknowledging and distinguishing it from alternate and opposing claims, organizing reasons and evidence, and keeping a formal style.
- Not like: counterclaim-rally (grade 7, rebutting point by point in a volley); debate-prep-desk (grade 6, strengths and limits of sources); usage-panel (grade 11).
- Art: drawn in code for the phone chat; people (illustrated) for the board members.
