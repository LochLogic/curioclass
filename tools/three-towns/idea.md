# Three Towns (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-2-urban-suburban-and-rural, grade 2. Codes: D2.Geo.8.K-2, D2.Geo.9.K-2
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good, if the sort leads into tracing where each town gets what it needs.
- One idea: Sort homes, jobs and ways to travel into a city, a suburb and a farm town, then follow one breakfast in each to see which parts come from nearby and which come from far away, and why each town's land shapes the work people do there.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: three postcards (tall buildings by a harbor, rows of houses with yards, a farm town in a river valley). "Where does this apartment building belong?"
  - Core loop: drag cards (apartment, ranch house, subway, school bus, tractor, ferry, grain elevator) to a town; each sort explains the clue.
  - Breakfast trace: each town's kid eats breakfast; tap each food to see a line to where it came from (eggs from the farm next door in the farm town, milk trucked in to the city, oranges from far away), and how each family gets there.
  - The aha or the end state: a land-and-work layer shows why the work fits the place: the harbor city has a port and ships, the river valley has flat rich soil and farms, the suburb sits near the city's jobs.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): "Which is our town most like?" vote.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Geo.8.K-2: the breakfast trace compares how families in each kind of community use nearby and faraway places to meet daily needs.
  - D2.Geo.9.K-2: the land-and-work layer connects each place's physical environment (harbor, valley soil) to its jobs.
- Not like: me-on-the-map (levels of place); state-economy-map (grade 4 industries on a real state map); day-in-the-life (other countries).
- Art: generated scenes (three towns in one style), because the contrast between places is the content.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Sort homes, jobs and transport into a city, a suburb and a farm town, then trace one breakfast in each to see what comes from nearby and what from far away, and how each town's land shapes its work.
- Flags: none
