# Time Machine Talk (idea card)

- Module: eld-3-5-then-and-now-language, grade 3-5. Codes: WIDA.ELD-5, WIDA.Narrate, WIDA.Inform
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good: you choose which era of one street to visit, and each trip is a short branching story told in the past.
- One idea: Ride a time machine to the same town street in 1900, 1950 and today; at each stop you describe what you saw in the past tense (people rode in streetcars, there was no...), and your report to the town museum only saves when the verbs and time words are right, so then-and-now language becomes the key to history.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the museum guide beside the time machine and a photo of the street today. "Go back. Look. Come back and tell me what was different."
  - Core loop: at each era the student explores the scene (transport, jobs, school, stores, clothes) and makes choices (ride the streetcar or walk to the general store). Back at the museum they write the report: sequence words (first, then, after that, long ago), past forms (rode, sold, there were), and compare frames (Then..., but now...). Irregular verbs show their present twin so the change is visible (ride / rode, buy / bought). A wrong form makes the machine stall with a hint, never a block on the story.
  - The aha or the end state: the museum shows the three eras side by side with the student's sentences as captions, a then-and-now exhibit in their own words.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): Help Lots / Some / Little; a fourth era chosen by the class.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - WIDA.ELD-5: the language of social studies: community change over time, transport, work and daily life.
  - WIDA.Narrate: recounting the visit in order with past tense and sequence words.
  - WIDA.Inform: describing and comparing how things were and how they are now, with then and now frames.
- Not like: then-what-happened (grade 6-12 comic narration); cause-and-effect-rewind (science causes); mi-día (Spanish present-tense routine).
- Art: public domain street photographs (Library of Congress, ids to check at spec) for the eras, plus illustrated guide (people, illustrated).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Ride a time machine to one town street in 1900, 1950 and today; explore each era, then report what you saw with past-tense verbs and then-and-now frames to build the town museum's exhibit.
- Flags: needs a data source (public domain era photos, ids checked); light sensitivity (who appears in each era's scenes, fresh review); home-language glossary may matter (open question).
