## What it teaches

Students organize a spoken perspective so a listener can follow the claim, reasons, evidence and an opposing view. They give slides a purpose and use rehearsal measurements to choose one revision for their next trial.

## Key skills

- Write signposts that let a listener follow the reasoning.
- Address an opposing view and give it an answer.
- Choose media that add something to the spoken words.
- Measure section pace and interpret listener feedback.
- Compare repeated trials after changing one thing.

## Support at home

- Listen to a short explanation and repeat its main point in your own words. Compare what you heard with what your student intended.
- Ask your student to give the same request in one minute and then two minutes. Discuss what they kept.
- Notice a presentation together and ask what its pictures added to the words.

## A five-minute start

Open the sample, select Other side and inspect its intentionally overloaded slide. Open Thread view and type the talk's point from the signposts alone. Enter Rehearse, press Start and speak. Press Next at each section boundary. In Review, find one section or slide to change, record the change, and run again.

## Modes

Build pairs one section with one slide. Planned time starts at 150 words per minute, rounded to five seconds with a five-second minimum. Editing seconds makes that section's plan manual. Task changes preserve the talk. Club pitch protects Must keep sections from deletion.

Rehearse is a live rehearsal, not playback. The three-second count precedes timing. Space advances, P pauses, and F / L / H / N stamp listener observations. A solo speaker can ignore the listener pad. Script, Notes only and Nothing change what is shown, while pace always uses the script's planned words.

Review marks each section with planned and actual time, script-based pace and listener stamps. It keeps up to eight runs separately for the sample and My talk. Fill in the self-check, record What I changed, compare runs and conclude with a next trial. Fastest and slowest refer to words per minute, not duration.

The opening Review card points to a busy slide shown too briefly, or the fastest section. Write one change there, then use Revise section to return to that part of the current talk. Run comparisons share one seconds scale; a shorter strip means a shorter run. Listener marks include their section-relative seconds and stay distinct even when tapped together.

## Talk questions

- Could someone follow the signposts without seeing the slides?
- Does the Other side receive a clear answer?
- Which slide does a different job from the speaker's voice?
- What happened after changing just one thing?

## Good to know

SL.11-12.4 is practiced through the run sheet, task registers, thread and listener test, opposing-view check and delivery trials. SL.11-12.5 is practiced through the six slide kinds, purpose selection, slide readings and measured screen time.

Busy means over 20 slide words. Reading means over 60 percent of slide tokens also appear in the script. These are teaching heuristics, not universal presentation rules. Slow is below 110, steady 110 to 170 inclusive, fast above 170 words per minute. The default 150 is labeled a common rule of thumb because the named NCVS source was not plainly located at build.

Reading uses the exact matching fraction before rounding its display. A fraction just over 60 percent is labeled "over 60 percent" when whole-percent rounding would obscure the threshold.

The Other side structure check reads section roles and link words, not the script's meaning. The sample answers its objection within the same card, so its structure reminder remains until an Answer card or a following “but” link is present. Discuss whether the actual spoken answer is convincing even when the structural check passes.

Pace measures the script's words divided by the time in that section. It cannot tell whether a speaker actually said every word. It does not recognize speech or count spoken fillers. Fillers per minute come only from listener taps; the self-check is a separate estimate. Timing uses the instant of each tap and excludes Pause.

Microphone access is optional and starts only when the student turns Listen for pauses on. It measures RMS loudness in the current frame, calibrates room noise during the run's first two seconds and counts continuous quiet of at least two seconds. Quiet-time share excludes calibration and paused time. A quiet room and quiet speech can be difficult to distinguish. Permission refusal leaves the rest of the tool usable. The stream stops on completion, abandonment, mode change, page hide or turning it off. No recording, transcription or audio upload occurs.

Talks, numeric runs, listener tests, self-checks and notes stay in localStorage under speech-coach:v1. A banner reports storage failure. Clear My talk and Reset sample each ask Yes / No. Run notes save automatically; Save run notes explicitly saves again. Local browser data can still be cleared by the device owner.

The sample discusses sleep calmly and factually. It is a made-up board comment, not individualized health advice. Sleep needs vary. Its closing image has an original caption about the resting harvester. The two historic museum paintings depict people; they are supplied public-domain art, not generated people.
