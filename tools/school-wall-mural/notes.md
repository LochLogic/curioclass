## What it teaches

Students turn community wishes into weighted criteria, select a mural, and revise their choice using evidence about its public setting. They can explain why a high-scoring design may still need changes before presentation.

## Key skills

- Listen for different community priorities.
- Build a rubric and explain how weights change a decision.
- Compare details at different distances and at passing speed.
- Use a simple model to investigate paint fading and revisions.
- Defend a choice with evidence and name unresolved concerns.

## Support at home

- On a walk, compare a sign up close and from farther away. Ask which details survive the distance.
- Ask two family members what they would want from a public artwork. Discuss which wishes might conflict.
- Look at a sun-faded outdoor surface. Talk about what you would test before choosing a material.

## A 5-minute start

1. In Listen, open two neighbors' cards and add their wishes.
2. In Judge, change one weight. Name what moved and why.
3. Inspect a sketch and choose it. In Street test, open Across the street.
4. Ask whether the rubric included everything the wall needs.

## Modes

- **Listen:** six fictional neighbors connect a place and a wish to one or two criteria. Added criteria begin at weight 2.
- **Judge:** criterion scores are 1 to 4. Weights are 0 to 3. The weighted total is the sum of weight times score. All-zero weights tie every sketch. Equal weights sets included criteria to 2.
- **Street test:** inspect the chosen sketch at 3, 18 and 9 meters; ride past for 1.7 seconds; compare modeled pigment fading over five summers. Revisions change the rubric scores everywhere and have Undo controls.
- **Board:** answer questions about the chosen sketch's two lowest criterion scores and the comparison with alternatives. Locked cards name missing evidence. Leave unanswered preserves the concern in the reply. There is no vote or win state.

An answer can have evidence and still describe a weakness. The board keeps concerns about criterion scores of 1 or 2 visible even when the student cites a source. Changing the rubric restarts the presentation so its claims match the current decision.

Open **Changes** to see the applied revisions and their Undo buttons. Close Changes to return to the rubric or street controls. Applying a revision pins the mural being tested, so it stays on the wall even if the leader changes.

## Talk about it

- Whose priorities had the most influence? Who might disagree with your weights?
- When could a less readable historical scene be worth keeping?
- Is a proposed timeline panel evidence that history is already represented?
- What would you ask a real paint manufacturer before approving outdoor paint?

## Standards in student actions

VA:Pr4: select and compare work in Judge. VA:Pr5: prepare the work for a space through distance and paint revisions. VA:Pr6: defend meaning and presentation in Board. VA:Re9: construct and apply weighted criteria. VA:Cn10: connect community wishes and local stories to an artwork choice.

## Good to know

This is a fictional Willow Street. The landmarks are real within its story, not claims about an actual neighborhood. A student's weights, choice, banner and revisions save only on the current device. Reset clears them. Browser narration stands in for the clear-teacher recording; no audio was recorded during this build.

Visual angle is `2 atan(size / (2 distance))`, converted to arcminutes. The 15 arcminute glance guide and three focal-shape guide are this tool's rules of thumb. Blur follows the spec's continuous formula below 15 arcminutes. They do not assess an individual's eyesight.

The paint view is labeled **a simple model, not a lab test**. Each summer removes 1%, 4% or 15% of remaining color strength for grades I, II or III. Listed pigments have equal weight in image saturation; swatches mix toward primer gray. It does not predict actual years of service. Product formulation, medium, coating and exposure affect real paint performance. The grades are those assigned to the fictional sketches; check an actual brand before purchase. PY74 stays II in the model.

The spec's equal-weight totals 20, 19, 18 and 15 are unweighted sums. At the specified Equal weights setting of 2, the totals are 40, 38, 36 and 30, with the same ranking.

The street paintings illustrate the model; the readout supplies the assumed detail dimensions. They are not scale drawings from which to measure a bird's eye or a window.
