# Tile the Floor (idea card)

- Module: math-3-area, grade 3. Codes: 3.MD.C (also proposed: 3.G.A, equal-area part, moved here from shape-family-tree)
- Format / family: puzzle / area. Fit: good.
- One idea: Cover each room's floor with square tiles until counting gets slow, then find the shortcut (rows times columns), and split odd-shaped rooms into rectangles you can add.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a house plan seen from above and a tiler, Mr. Ito. "Cover the kitchen with tiles. No gaps, no overlaps."
  - Core loop: early rooms are tiled one square at a time and counted (square inches for a doll house, square feet for a room, square meters for a hall). Larger rooms make tiling slow, and the tool offers a row of tiles: tile one row, count the rows, multiply. The child checks it matches counting.
  - Split rooms: an L-shaped room splits into two rectangles; a 6 by 7 room splits as 6 by 5 plus 6 by 2 to show a times (b + c).
  - Painters job: four painters share one floor; the child splits it into four parts with the same number of tiles, even when the parts are different shapes, and names each part 1/4 of the floor.
  - The aha or the end state: area is the number of unit squares, and multiplying side lengths counts them all at once.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): design your dream room and find its area.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3.MD.C: area as unit squares (3.MD.5), counting square units of different sizes (3.MD.6), and relating area to multiplication and addition, including the distributive split and L-shaped rooms (3.MD.7).
  - 3.G.A (equal areas, 3.G.2, if moved here): splitting a floor into parts with equal area and naming each part as a unit fraction of the whole.
- Not like: fence-builder (perimeter), area-model-builder (grade 4 multiplication by place value), garden-grid (grade 2 rows and columns without area).
- Art: generated scenes (cutaway home interiors), tiles drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Cover rooms with square tiles until counting gets slow, then shortcut with rows times columns; split L-shaped rooms into rectangles, and split a floor into four equal-area parts for four painters.
- Flags: none
