# Drop Zone (idea card)

- Module: science-5-gravity, grade 5. Codes: 5-PS2-1
- Format / family: lab / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Drop an apple at kids' schools in Alaska, Ecuador, Australia and Antarctica, record which way each falls, then zoom out to the whole Earth to see every "down" points to Earth's center, and use that evidence to argue that gravity pulls things down toward the center.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a schoolyard in Alaska with a kid holding an apple and a plumb line. "Drop it. Which way does it go?"
  - Core loop: at each place, drop objects (an apple, a ball, a feather in a vacuum tube) and record the direction with the plumb line; the kid on the other side of the world says their down is "down" too.
  - Zoom out: pull back to space; all the plumb lines and fall paths from around the globe are shown at once, and they all point to Earth's center. The student is asked: why don't people in Australia fall off?
  - Moon and Mars extra: drop the same apple on the Moon and on Mars; it falls more slowly, but still toward that body's center.
  - The aha or the end state: an argument card where the student states the claim and attaches evidence from at least three places.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 5-PS2-1: the drops around the globe and the zoomed-out view are the evidence for the argument that Earth's gravity pulls objects down toward its center.
- Not like: planet-builder (grade 6 gravity and orbits); push-the-pumpkin (pushes); shadow-clock (Earth's spin and orbit).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the kids at each school; generated scenes for each place; the globe and plumb lines drawn in code.
- Proposed title: Which Way Is Down? (a clearer name for the aha, and "Drop Zone" is a common name for games and rides)
- Proposed concept line: Drop an apple at schools in Alaska, Ecuador, Australia and Antarctica, record which way it falls, then zoom out to see every down points to Earth's center and argue that gravity pulls toward it.
- Flags: none
