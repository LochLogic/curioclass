# Historians Disagree (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-7-historians-disagree, grade 7. Codes: D2.His.17.6-8, D2.His.9.6-8, D4.4.6-8
- Format / family: explorer / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Two short secondary accounts explain why the Mongol Empire grew so fast, one stressing horse archers and organization, the other a run of wet years that fed the grasslands (from tree-ring research); the student names each central argument, sorts the kinds of sources each relied on (chronicles, letters, archaeology, tree rings), and judges which argument holds up better and why.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two articles side by side, each with a footnote drawer.
  - Core loop: highlight each article's central claim and restate it; open the footnotes and sort each source into a kind (chronicle written by the conquerors, a victim's chronicle, a traveler's letter, archaeology, tree rings); a credibility card asks who wrote it, when, and what it can and cannot show.
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes a verdict; the two arguments can both be partly right, and a source's kind shapes what it can prove (tree rings show rain, not decisions).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a third article that combines both.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.17.6-8: naming and comparing the two central arguments.
  - D2.His.9.6-8: sorting the kinds of sources each account used.
  - D4.4.6-8: the credibility cards and the written verdict.
- Not like: mongol-messenger (same empire, a relay race); panel-of-experts (four fields, one question); unlabeled-archive (identifying primary sources).
- Art: public domain illustrated manuscript pages of the Mongols (to check at spec); the tree-ring chart drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: keep
- Flags: needs a data source (the tree-ring study and the chronicles each account cites, checked); overlap risk (topic shared with mongol-messenger, different skill)
