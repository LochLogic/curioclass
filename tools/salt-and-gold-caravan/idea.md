# Salt and Gold Caravan (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-7-medieval-africa, grade 7. Codes: D2.His.1.6-8, D2.Eco.14.6-8, D2.Geo.11.6-8
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Run a trading house that sends camel caravans from the Saharan salt mines at Taghaza to Timbuktu and the gold fields of Mali; salt is scarce in the south and gold in the north, so the desert itself makes the trade pay, while every barrier (distance, water, tolls at Mali's markets, raiders) eats into profit.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a ledger, a map from the salt mines to the Niger River, and a few camels for hire.
  - Core loop: each season, buy salt, hire guides and camels, choose a route and wells, pay tolls and trade at Timbuktu; ledgers show cost per load and profit. Guides explain Mali's rule over the gold trade.
  - The aha or the end state: the student sees why trade crossed the desert at all (the land produces different things), and an epilogue follows Mansa Musa's journey to Mecca in 1324, which carried so much gold that observers in Cairo reported its value falling.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two rival trading houses.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.1.6-8: the epilogue connects the caravan trade to Mali's rise and to Cairo and Europe.
  - D2.Eco.14.6-8: barriers (distance, water, tolls, raids) and how they shape what gets traded and where.
  - D2.Geo.11.6-8: each region's land (salt deposits, gold fields, farms on the Niger) drives the trade pattern.
- Not like: silk-road-traders (ideas spreading by card trade); cocoa-chain (a modern supply chain); border-trade-post (tariff policy).
- Art: generated scenes for the desert, mines and Timbuktu; public domain Catalan Atlas detail of Mansa Musa (to check at spec).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run caravans from the Saharan salt mines to Timbuktu and Mali's gold fields; learn why salt and gold were worth crossing a desert for, what every barrier cost, and how Mali's gold reached Cairo and beyond.
- Flags: sensitive (the trans-Saharan trade also carried enslaved people; named plainly with care; fresh review)
