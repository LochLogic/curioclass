# Predator-Prey Island (idea card)

- Module: science-7-ecosystems-and-interactions, grade 7. Codes: MS-LS2-1, MS-LS2-2
- Format / family: simulator / ecosystem. Fit: good
- One idea: Run the real wolves and moose of Isle Royale, a Lake Superior island studied every winter since the late 1950s, test which change (a hard winter, a food shortage, disease, a new wolf) explains each rise and crash in the real counts, then predict the same kinds of interactions in other ecosystems.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the island map and the real count graph, faint, with sliders for starting wolves, moose, winter snow depth and balsam fir food.
  - Core loop: run the model and lay its curve over the real counts; pick a year where they differ and try a cause until the model matches. Food and snow change how many moose survive and calve; moose numbers change wolf numbers. Beavers compete with moose for aspen, and foxes and ravens feed at wolf kills.
  - Pattern step: three short cases from other ecosystems (lynx and hare in northern forests, cleaner fish and their reef clients, two barnacle species sharing a rock); the student predicts the interaction type and the shape of its graph, then checks.
  - The aha or the end state: populations rise and fall with resources and with each other, and the same kinds of interaction (predation, competition, mutual benefit) repeat across very different ecosystems. The human side: the researchers who count from small planes each winter, and the wolf reintroduction after the population fell to a couple of animals.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-LS2-1: the student analyzes the real counts and model runs for the effect of food and other resources on moose and wolves.
  - MS-LS2-2: the island's three interaction types and the outside cases let the student explain and predict patterns across ecosystems.
- Not like: carrying-capacity (grade 9, one population leveling off); food-web-wobble (grade 5 matter loops); energy-pyramid (energy per level).
- Art: generated scenes of the island through the seasons and the animals; graphs drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run Isle Royale's real wolves and moose, test what explains each rise and crash in decades of winter counts, then predict competition, predation and partnership patterns in other ecosystems.
- Flags: needs a data source (Isle Royale annual wolf and moose counts from the long-term study, checked, plus the three outside cases)
