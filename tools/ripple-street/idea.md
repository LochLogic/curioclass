# Ripple Street (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-3-ripple-street, grade 3. Codes: D2.Eco.1.3-5, D2.Eco.2.3-5, D2.Eco.8.3-5
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Make one kid's Saturday choices on Ripple Street (rake leaves for pay or go to the park, plant flowers, play loud music), weigh the costs and benefits to the kid, notice the rewards and fines that nudge each choice, and watch the ripples reach the neighbors.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: Ripple Street at 8 a.m., Ava stretching on her porch. "It's Saturday. What should Ava do first?"
  - Core loop: each choice opens a two-column card (what Ava gets, what she gives up), then ripples spread along the street: flowers cheer Mrs. Lin and bees visit her garden (external benefit); loud music wakes the baby next door (external cost).
  - Nudges: some choices come with incentives: $5 for raking (positive), a library fine for a late book (negative), a sticker from the block club for flowers. The student predicts whether each nudge changes Ava's mind, then sees.
  - The aha or the end state: the day's map shows Ava's own tally next to the street's ripples, so the student sees a choice can be good for you and costly for others, or the reverse.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): class votes Ava's choices.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.1.3-5: two-column cards compare benefits and costs of each choice.
  - D2.Eco.2.3-5: rewards and fines are tagged positive or negative incentives and tested.
  - D2.Eco.8.3-5: ripples show external benefits and costs to neighbors.
- Not like: smoke-and-honey (grade 6 externalities and policy); piggy-bank-planner (saving).
- Art: generated scenes and people (illustrated), because the ripples must be easy to see on one street.
