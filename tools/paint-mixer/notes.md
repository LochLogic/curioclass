## A 5-minute start

1. Open **Mix**. Tap **Red**, then **Yellow**. The dabs swirl together in the dish and make orange.
2. Ask the class to predict before every dab: "What will happen if we add blue?"
3. Work down the **Discoveries** list: orange, green, violet, a tertiary color, a tint, a shade, brown and gray. Each one ticks itself off.
4. Press **Save this swatch** to keep a color and its recipe in the swatch book.

## The four modes

- **Mix**: free mixing with red, yellow, blue, white and black. The card under the dish names the color and says what kind it is (primary, secondary, tertiary, tint, shade or tone) with its recipe.
- **Color wheel**: the 12-color wheel, every color mixed from the three primaries. Turn on **P, S, T** labels, **Warm and cool**, **Complements** (tap a color to find its opposite; the middle shows them mixed) and **Tints and shades** rings. Tap any color to mix it in the dish.
- **Tints and shades**: pick a color to see its value ladder, from shades (add black) to tints (add white), and a landscape painted only in that color. Tap a step to mix it.
- **Match**: mix to match a target color, then **Check my mix** to see your color beside the target and earn up to three stars. **Hint** suggests the single dab that gets closest. Four levels: secondary, tertiary, tints and shades, tricky.

## Why it acts like real paint

Most color mixers on screens add light, so blue and yellow make gray. This one models paint: each color has a reflectance curve across the spectrum, and paints mix the way pigments do (Kubelka-Munk theory). That gives the results students see with real paint:

- Blue and yellow make green.
- **Red is stronger than yellow.** One red and one yellow lean red-orange; it takes more yellow to make a true orange. "A little dark paint goes a long way" is a real studio lesson.
- Purples come out dull, as they do with school paint. Artists often buy a violet paint for that reason.
- All three primaries make brown, and so do any two complements.
- A little black darkens a lot; white needs more dabs to lighten.

## Talk about it

- Which colors can't be mixed from other paints? Why do we call them primary?
- Where on the wheel is a tertiary color? Why does its name have two parts?
- Why do you think artists put complementary colors next to each other?
- Look at the one-color landscape. How did the artist show near and far?

## Good to know

- Keys 1 to 5 add paints, Backspace undoes a dab, and C cleans the dish.
- The dish holds 16 dabs.
- Screen colors are approximate. Real paints differ by brand, so encourage students to test the same recipes with real paint and compare.
