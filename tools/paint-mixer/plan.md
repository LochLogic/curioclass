arc: explore-first
minutes: 45
focus: VA:Cr2
goal: I can mix primary colors into secondary and tertiary colors, and make tints and shades of a color.
materials: The tool on the projector and on student devices. Real red, yellow, blue, white and black paint, brushes and paper for Apply.
grouping: Pairs on devices, then the same pairs at paint tables.
---
## Before class

- Open **Mix** on the projector with a clean dish.
- Set out real paint for Apply, a little of each color per pair.
- Screen colors are close but not exact, and real paints vary by brand. That is part of the lesson.

## Hook (5 min)

1. "If you only had three paints, could you make every color in this room?" Take guesses about which three.
2. "Artists started with just a few colors. Today you discover how they made the rest."

## Explore (15 min)

1. On the projector, tap **Red**, then **Yellow**. The dabs swirl into orange. "What will happen if we add blue?" Predict first, then try it.
2. Pairs work through the **Discoveries** list: orange, green, violet, a tertiary color, a tint, a shade, brown and gray. Each one ticks itself off.
3. Pairs save their favorite with **Save this swatch**, which keeps the recipe too.
4. Point out a surprise: one red and one yellow lean red-orange. "Why does it take more yellow?" (Red is a stronger paint.)

## Make sense (8 min)

1. Open the **Color wheel** and turn on the **P, S, T** labels. "Which colors can't be mixed from others?" (The primaries.) "Why does a tertiary color's name have two parts?" (It's a primary mixed with a secondary.)
2. Turn on **Complements**. Tap red and find its opposite. "What happens when complements mix?" (Brown or gray.)
3. Open **Tints and shades** and pick a color. Read the ladder from shade (add black) to tint (add white). Look at the one-color landscape: "How did the artist show near and far?"

## Apply (12 min)

1. Pairs move to real paint. They mix the same three things: orange, green and one tint. They paint a swatch of each on paper and write the recipe under it.
2. "Was real paint the same as the tool? What was different?"
3. Pairs who finish try a shade, and notice that a little black darkens a lot.

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: "How do you make green? How do you make light blue? Name one pair of complements." (Blue and yellow. Blue and white. For example, red and green.) Cards that mix up tints and shades show who needs the ladder again.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** stick to the secondary colors first, with the **Color wheel** on screen as a guide.
- **Support:** let pairs use **Hint** in **Match** for the single best dab.
- **Stretch:** **Match** on the tricky level, going for three stars.
- **Stretch:** "Why do you think artists put complementary colors next to each other?"

## Short on time

- **Mix** the three secondary colors together on the projector (7 min).
- Look at the **Color wheel** with **P, S, T** labels (5 min), then the exit card (4 min).
