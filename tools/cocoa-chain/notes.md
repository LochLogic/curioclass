## What it teaches

Students trace goods and money through a chocolate supply chain across Ghana, the Netherlands and the United States.
They explain how the 2023 rains in West Africa affected different people, using dated harvest and price evidence.

## Key skills

- Trace a product between countries and explain what each stop needs.
- Compare effects on farmers, buyers, dockworkers, factory workers and shoppers.
- Separate a price per bag from a world price per tonne.
- Build a cause-and-effect explanation and choose evidence that supports each step.

## Support at home

- Pick a food already at home. Look at its origin label together and imagine its journey to the kitchen.
- Ask how bad weather far away might change a familiar product nearby.
- Talk about the difference between a higher price and earning more overall when there is less to sell.

## A five-minute start

Open Follow the bar and tap the red thread. Meet the farm family, answer their money question with a map pin,
then use Next stop to follow the bags to the shelf. Bring the 2023 rains and compare the farm with the store.
The narrator is optional. Read speaks the current hint and the stop card.

## Modes

**Follow the bar:** six painted stops, a shared Before/After switch, needs links and worker reflections.
Map pins answer an unanswered needs question; after that they navigate. Back, Next stop and dollar segments also navigate.
The numbered route legend names the stops. More opens the full worker card. Hard facts is available at the Farm.
The country row records reflection participation, not correctness. Any Helped, Hurt or Both choice reveals evidence.

**Explain it:** place six of eight sentences in causal order and match four number chips to the relevant sentences.
Drag, or tap a tile and a step. Use Tab and Enter for the same operations. Filled slots swap their sentences.
Check reports one problem at a time. A complete explanation can be read as a paragraph. Clear reshuffles the tray.

## Talk questions

- Which parts of the chain need another country? What travels back in the other direction?
- Why can farmers get a higher price per bag and still face a difficult harvest?
- Why does the farmers’ price chip not support the statement about a world price per tonne?
- What do both dollar studies agree about? Why do their percentages differ?
- Which evidence connects rain in Ghana with a change on a shelf in the United States?

## Good to know

Content note: the Farm Hard facts card names child labor, dangerous tools and heavy loads plainly.
This supports discussion of different groups’ circumstances within nations under D2.Eco.15.3-5.
It does not ask students for personal or family disclosures. No illustration shows a child working.
The Quarshie and cacao-origin text credits Ghanaian farmers and South American cacao use.
Sensitivity review of people, places, names and the historical text remains a cold-review task.

All workers and this particular route are illustrative. Real cocoa takes many routes. The Netherlands factory is
near Amsterdam, the Ghana port is Takoradi, and the candy maker and store are in the United States.
The period spans the 2023 rains through late 2024 cocoa prices and 2025 chocolate price decisions.
It is a historical comparison, not a current-price dashboard or a single-date snapshot.

The 14-to-11 bags and 10-to-8 containers illustrate fewer goods. They do not measure a particular household or ship.
The $1.00 and about $1.10 shelf tags are calculated examples, not measured brand prices.
16 bags per trade tonne follows COCOBOD’s price convention; 64 kg gross bags are not 64 kg net cocoa.
The dollar bars show shares of retail value, not profits. They use older studies and do not change with the rains.
Exact cents are retained to one decimal so the parts add up. At $4, the 2015 maker and store total is 317.6 cents.
No student work is saved. The shared kit remembers only Motion on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

## Cold review source clarifications

- The GH¢1,308 price began in September 2023, rather than the spec's October label.
  [COCOBOD's September 12, 2023 release](https://citinewsroom.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PRESS-RELEASE-12TH-SEPT.-2023.docx.pdf)
  already names that price; [Graphic Online, September 11](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/govt-increases-cocoa-price-bag-up-from-gh-800-to-gh-1-308-tome-now-gh-20-943-from-gh-12-800.html)
  dates the season opening. The [April 5, 2024 COCOBOD release](https://cocobod.gh/news/review-of-the-producer-price-of-cocoa-for-the-202324-cocoa-season)
  confirms the later GH¢2,070 price. The spec file is unchanged.
- The port's lower export earnings refer to the first half of 2024 versus the first half of 2023,
  rather than every possible period or accounting series.
  [Bank of Ghana July 2024 data, reported by MyJoyOnline](https://www.myjoyonline.com/ghanas-cocoa-crisis-deepens-2024-half-year-revenues-crash-by-nearly-700-million/).
- The US maker's approximately 10 percent or higher action was announced in 2025, with a mix of list-price
  changes and smaller packs. It is not a measured rise for every bar or store.
  [July 2025 company remarks](https://hershey.gcs-web.com/static-files/96d3bd2f-5d2c-41cf-bbac-57ab132ecac1)
  and [AP's July 23 report quoting the company](https://apnews.com/article/f597cd39672a05a3cac3b170a5541431).
- The farm scale, buying system, plantain-leaf processing, Takoradi/Tema ports and mostly raw-bean exports
  are also documented in [ICCO's Ghana country report, 2023, pages 6-9](https://www.icco.org/wp-content/uploads/3-FEASIBILITY-STUDY-ON-AFRICA-COCOA-EXCHANGE__Annex_GHANA.pdf).
- Netherlands bean-import leadership is by volume: [CBS, April 2026](https://www.cbs.nl/en-gb/news/2026/14/the-netherlands-is-the-world-s-leading-exporter-of-cocoa-products).
  [ECA's historical quarterly table](https://www.eurococoa.com/wp-content/uploads/WEBSITE-REPORT-WESTERN-STATS-Q1-2025-1.pdf)
  supports the late-2024 four-year grinding low.
- Ghana's hazardous-work minimum age is 18: [ILO Child Labour Observatory](https://webapps.ilo.org/clodashboard/section/2F).
  The 2018-19 child-labor figure remains a dated NORC estimate, not today's census.

The needs questions and the explanation provide guided practice. A student can find the right answers by
trying pins and using Check's first-error feedback. They cannot establish independent mastery; do not use
the six-link count or the finished paragraph as an assessment score. Removing that route requires a spec decision.
At phone widths and short preview heights, the full-text explanation choices still require scrolling.

## Unresolved harvest series

The model retains the spec's 655,808-to-530,873 tonne series, also printed in USDA FAS report GH2025-0048 in 2025.
A [newer COCOBOD sector-reform release](https://cocobod.gh/news/press-release-on-cocoa-sector-reforms-for-financial-viability-and-long-term-sustainability)
instead gives 432,145 tonnes as actual 2023/24 production. These official figures are not reconciled here.
The 19 percent decline and illustrative 14-to-11 bags therefore belong to the specified USDA series;
they should not be taught as a universally agreed final harvest figure. Resolving this changes the spec's worked model.
