# Headline Writer (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-main-idea-and-key-details, grade 3. Codes: RI.3.2
- Format / family: studio / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: The child is the headline writer at a kid newspaper: they read a short news story, write a headline that tells its main idea, then choose the three details that belong in the story's first lines because they prove the headline, so they learn that a main idea is what the key details add up to.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a newsroom desk and an editor (Ms. Delgado): "This story goes to print in five minutes and it has no headline."
  - Core loop: read the story; write a headline (free typing, with a word-count limit). The editor asks: does it tell what the whole story is about, not just one part? Then the child drags three details into a "proves it" box; a detail that does not support the headline bounces back. The child recounts the story in two sentences for the radio.
  - The aha or the end state: the paper prints with the child's headline beside two other headlines (one too narrow, one too broad), and the child explains in a sentence why theirs fits.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): compare headlines from several students.
- Standards, one line per code: RI.3.2: determining the main idea (the headline), recounting key details (the radio recount) and explaining how they support the main idea (the proves-it box).
- Not like: Main Idea Umbrella (grade 2 main topic per paragraph). Main Idea Pinboard (grades 3-5 quoting evidence, does not carry RI.3.2). Fact Fusion (grade 5 sources).
- Art: generated scenes (a newsroom and news photos) and people (illustrated) for the editor, because a real-looking front page makes the job feel real.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Be the headline writer at a kid newspaper: read a short news story, write a headline that tells its main idea, then pick the three details that prove it before the paper goes to print.
- Flags: needs a data source (short kid news stories: either original stories about real events, with every fact sourced, or clearly invented local news); overlap risk (main-idea-pinboard, mild)
