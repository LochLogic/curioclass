# Dramatic Irony Theater (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-dialogue-and-dramatic-irony, grade 8. Codes: RL.8.3, RL.8.6
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good: a short play the student watches and controls, with a choice of what the audience learns and when.
- One idea: The student runs a short stage play as its director and decides when the audience learns the secret (before the scene, halfway, or never), and the same dialogue turns from suspense to comedy to plain surprise depending on that choice, while each line is tagged for what it does (moves the action, reveals a character, forces a decision), so they learn how the gap between what characters and audience know creates suspense or humor.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a small theater, two original one-act scenes by Curio Class: a suspense scene (a babysitter alone in a house where the audience knows the phone line is cut by a storm, not a person) and a comedy (two friends each secretly planning the same surprise party for the other).
  - Core loop: the student places the "reveal" card: show the audience the secret before the scene, midway or not at all. The scene plays with illustrated actors and speech bubbles; an audience meter shows suspense and laughter rising or falling. The student pauses on lines and tags what each does: propels action, reveals character, provokes a decision. They predict how the tension resolves before the last page.
  - The aha or the end state: the same scene run three ways side by side on the meter, and the student explains which lines became funny or tense only because the audience knew more than the character.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): two students read parts aloud while the class holds the secret.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.8.3: tagging how lines of dialogue and incidents propel the action, reveal character and provoke decisions.
  - RL.8.6: changing what the audience knows and explaining how the gap creates suspense or humor.
- Not like: perspective-camera (grade 6, narrator's point of view); tension-meter (grade 9, suspense from structure); globe-theatre-stage (grade 12, interpretations of Shakespeare scenes).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the actors on a stage set, with a different palette per scene.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Direct a short play and decide when the audience learns the secret; the same lines turn tense, funny or merely surprising, and you tag how each line moves the action or reveals a character.
- Flags: none
