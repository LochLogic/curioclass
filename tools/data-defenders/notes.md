## A 5-minute start

1. Read the hallway together: three locked doors at Cedar Hollow Elementary, each guarding one promise about data. Tap the **Records Room**.
2. In **Who needs what**, ask: does the bus driver need the health notes? Tap boxes to take folders away, then press **Check lock**. The lock says how many boxes give too much access and how many people can't do their job.
3. Switch to **Pick the password**, let the class choose one, then test all four on the meter. Ask why the long, silly one wins. When both symbols light up, the room is secured: C for Confidentiality.

## The mode

- **Escape:** three rooms in any order (two puzzles in the first two, three in the Server Closet), then the exit door.
  - **Records Room (C, Confidentiality):** give each person only the folders their job needs (least privilege), then pick the strongest password for the records account.
  - **Grade Book Room (I, Integrity):** add up each student's scores and compare with the seal saved with them (a checksum) to find the changed card, then read the change log to find the odd row and pick the first fix.
  - **Server Closet (A, Availability):** find the fewest servers that keep the site up on report card night, then pick the backup plan that loses the least work when the disk breaks, then in the **Network closet** sort parts into wired and wireless and predict which devices stay online when the Wi-Fi access point breaks.
  - **The exit:** sort six short stories into C, I or A. Drag a card or tap it, then tap a letter. Wrong cards slide back.
- **Clue** gives two hints per puzzle, one press at a time.
- **Team timer** is Off by default. 20 or 30 minutes counts down; at zero it says "Time!" and nothing locks, so teams can finish.

## Talk about it

- Why shouldn't everyone at school be able to open every folder, even if they are all nice people?
- What makes a password strong: being long, or being weird? Why is the school's name a bad idea?
- The seal didn't stop the change. What did it do instead?
- If a backup lives on the same server as the work, what happens when that server breaks?
- When the Wi-Fi goes down, which devices keep working? Why might a school wire some devices with cables?
- Which of the three promises matters most for the nurse's notes? For the lunch menu? For the website on report card night?

## Good to know

- Cedar Hollow, its people, scores and website are made up. Every web address in the tool is a reserved test name, and nothing a student does is saved or sent anywhere.
- The answers: C1, each person keeps only their job's folder, and Jordan's dad keeps only the public folder (lunch menu and bus times); C2, "purple tiger skates home"; I1, Cy (79 adds up, the seal says 59); I2, row 5 (2:14 a.m. from an unknown laptop; row 3 makes the same change in the daytime from her own laptop), then "Put the real scores back from Monday's backup, and change Ms. Ortiz's password"; A1, 3 servers; A2, plan C (back up every night to a server in another building); A3, wired: network cable, office desktop, switch, printer; wireless: Wi-Fi access point, Wi-Fi signal, Room 12 laptop, library tablet; online with the access point down: office desktop, nurse desktop, printer.
- After two wrong checks in C1 and the Network closet, "!" badges mark what still needs work.
- **Read** speaks the current hint and clue aloud. **Motion** off turns the door swing into a gentle outline flash.
- Works on a projector, a laptop or a phone. On small screens, and always in the Server Closet, a room's puzzles sit on tabs.
