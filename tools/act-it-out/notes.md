## What it teaches

Children use their bodies, faces, and voices to inhabit a character, make choices in a story, and perform for another group.
They can show how changing a costume or a voice changes the character they imagine.

## Key skills

- Invent a character's movement, expression, and voice.
- Listen for a story cue and join at the right moment.
- Choose between two ways to play a scene.
- Trade roles and try another character's part.
- Combine costume pieces and explain an imaginary character.

## Support at home

- Choose an imaginary hat. Put it on with pretend movements and show how its owner walks.
- Tell a familiar story together. Trade characters and tell it again with different voices.
- Make a frozen picture of a story moment. Ask a family member what your face and posture tell them.

## A 5-minute start

1. Make two groups, Sun side and Moon side. A child alone can try both parts in turn.
2. Tap Billy Goats. Read or listen to one beat, then tap Next when the class is ready.
3. Mime putting on the costumes. The peg toys show the outfit; the children supply the acting.
4. At the choice, let children try both voices before you tap one. Then tap Next.
5. Tap Freeze to make a still picture. Tap Unfreeze to move again.

## Stories

Billy Goats grows from tiny tiptoes to a large goat's steps. Gingerbread Boy changes the chasing character and the way he runs.
Big Turnip grows from a seed to a group effort, with a tiny mouse helping at the end. The bear is an optional invented addition.
Use Swap during a story to trade the roles. At The end, Swap and play again restarts with the opposite roles.
Back restores the costumes given by that earlier beat, including replacement hats and noses.

There is no score, timer, correct acting style, or automatic advance. Let children invent their interpretations.
For Kindergarten, model a single motion and invite an answer. In grade 1, ask for a distinct face and voice.
In grade 2, compare two interpretations and have the group choose one intentionally, then perform the same scene with roles exchanged.

## Trunk

Tap a costume piece to put it on, and tap it again to remove it. Each place holds one piece.
The wardrobe scrolls vertically and groups pieces by head, face, body, feet, and tail.
Backdrop cycles through six places. Surprise me picks three or four pieces in different places.
Ask cycles through five character questions. Take it all off empties the puppet.
The eight sound buttons are gentle synthesized effects; children can make their own sounds too.
Each costume tap names the piece being put on or taken off. After Ask, act out the answer, then tap Ask for another question.

## Talk questions

- How can we tell the tiny goat and the big goat apart just by how they move?
- What changed when the troll used a squeaky voice?
- How did your body or face change when you traded parts?
- What do you imagine about the character you dressed? Show us before telling us.
- How could we make the pulling scene show everyone working together?

## Good to know

This is teacher-led, grades K-2, for a projector or smartboard. All acting can be done seated.
For the river and bridge scenes, mime actions in your own space. There is no need for physical bumping, jumping, or contact.
The Gingerbread Boy keeps the traditional ending: the fox gobbles him up, told in one gentle sentence with a soft Snap sound.
The goats' story describes a bump and splash without injury. Choose another story if it better fits the group today.

Read repeats the current story line and the next-action hint, or the current Trunk question.
Sound controls narration and sound effects. Motion off removes swaying and flying; it does not advance the story faster.
Right arrow is Next, Left arrow is Back, F is Freeze, and S is Swap. Enter or Space presses a focused button.
All story progress and outfits live only in memory. The shared kit remembers the Motion preference on the device.
The file makes no network requests. No recording, microphone, camera, account, or saved student information is used.
