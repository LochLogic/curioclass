# Home Front Mobilizer (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-11-world-war-ii-and-the-home-front, grade 11. Codes: D2.His.4.9-12, D2.His.10.9-12
- Format / family: tycoon / none. Fit: good for mobilization; the incarceration story is not a game lever (rule 5), it is told through evidence inside the town
- One idea: Run one West Coast city's war production from 1942 to 1945 (retool plants, hire, ration, sell bonds) while the official record celebrates it, then open what that record left out: Japanese American neighbors removed by Executive Order 9066, Black workers shut out until protest, women laid off at war's end.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the city in December 1941, a car plant, a cannery, a shipyard and the downtown blocks, including a Japanese American business street.
  - Core loop: each quarter, retool plants, recruit workers, set ration rules and run bond drives; production and morale meters respond. Official sources (posters, newsreels, government photos) report each quarter.
  - February 1942: the Japanese American street empties by order; the student cannot stop or speed it. A family's story follows them to an incarceration camp, with their own words.
  - The record drawer: compare official sources with suppressed or overlooked ones (Dorothea Lange's impounded photos, Black workers' letters, braceros' records) and mark what each kind of evidence can and cannot show.
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes how a worker, a factory owner, a Japanese American family and a government photographer each saw the same years, and what the official record would have hidden.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.4.9-12: four perspectives with the factors that shaped them (race, class, wartime fear, patriotism).
  - D2.His.10.9-12: the record drawer detects limits of propaganda and official photos against suppressed and personal sources.
- Not like: factory-floor (19th-century mill); remembrance-archive (Holocaust sources); suburb-boom (postwar growth).
- Art: public domain photographs (Lange and Adams at the Library of Congress and National Archives, checked) and generated city scenes, because real photos are the evidence being compared.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run a West Coast city's war production from 1942 to 1945, then open what the official record left out: Japanese American incarceration, Black workers shut out until protest, women laid off at war's end.
- Flags: sensitive (incarceration, never gamified; fresh review agent per rule 5)
