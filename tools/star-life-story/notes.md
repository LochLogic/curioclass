## What it teaches

Planned tool, not yet playable. Students will relate a star's initial mass to its lifetime and fate,
explain fusion in the Sun, and connect elements and Big Bang evidence to their cosmic origins.

## Key skills

- Compare stellar lifetimes on a shared logarithmic scale.
- Distinguish a phase's duration from its equal-width place in a phase strip.
- Follow how successive fusion fuels build heavier nuclei.
- Use independent evidence to explain a hot, dense early universe.
- Trace common body elements to their main cosmic sources.

## Support at home

- Ask your child why a star with more fuel can have a shorter life. Let them explain how quickly it uses fuel.
- Discuss the difference between sunlight leaving the Sun and reaching Earth, about eight minutes later.
- Name oxygen, carbon and hydrogen together. Ask whether all three have the same cosmic origin.

## Five-minute start, once built

Scrub the Sun's phase strip. Pin its life with Compare, then select the 40 solar mass card.
Explain why the equal-width phase strip and logarithmic life line show the same life in different ways.

## Planned modes

Life Line compares stellar mass, lifetime, core fusion and remnants. Arrange is optional practice.
Sun connects fusion to energy, stellar zones and the light-travel time to Earth.
Origins explores three Big Bang clues and a periodic table of main element sources.

## Talk questions

- Why do late burning phases disappear on an age scale even when they matter physically?
- What changes when the core reaches iron?
- Which elements in a human body have a Big Bang origin, and which were made later?
- Why is 0.03 percent a constant-power estimate rather than an exact solar history?
