## What it teaches

Students practice recognizing and reading familiar and irregularly spelled words aloud. A teacher, caregiver or reading partner helps them judge each reading and revisit words that need more practice.

## Key skills

- Read common words accurately and fluently.
- Notice words that need another try.
- Read again at a comfortable pace.
- Build confidence with a manageable set of words.

## Support at home

- Choose three familiar words from a book. Find and read them together on another page.
- Write a few practice words on scrap paper. Read them together, then revisit one the next day.
- If a word is hard, say it for your child, notice its spelling together, and invite another try without rushing.

## A 5-minute start

1. Choose one or more levels. Level 1 holds the most common words; each level is alphabetical.
2. Tap Start in Explore. Ask the child to read aloud. Use Next and Back freely. Flag useful words to revisit.
3. Try a ten-word Practice test. Mark Right or Not yet together. The pie timer is only a quiet pace cue.
4. At the end, tap Practice these to return to the words needing another try.

## The modes

- **Picker:** choose one or several sets, including across Dolch and Fry. Picks remain selected when switching tabs. Mix shuffles the combined selection in Explore. Overlapping words appear once, using the first selected set in the displayed list order as their label.
- **Explore:** no score or timer. Back and Next move through the words. Shuffle changes the order. Flagged opens a list of flagged words in this reading deck; tap one to jump to it. Sets returns to the picker.
- **Practice test:** choose 10, 20, 30, or the whole selected set. The words shuffle. The pie timer stays green for four seconds, yellow for four seconds, then red with “Take your time.” It never moves on or makes a sound. Right and Not yet move on immediately. Skip is recorded separately and included in review. End finishes early and includes unread words in review.
- **Challenge:** 30 shuffled words, up to one minute. Right, Not yet and Skip move on. Skip counts as Not yet. The score is human marked, not measured by a microphone. The best score is saved for each exact combination of sets. Only 30 Right within one minute earns the large banner, short kit chime and optional confetti.

## Talk about it

- Which word would you like to read again?
- Where have you seen that word in a book?
- Which part of this word matches the sounds you expected? Which part needs remembering?

## Good to know

CCSS RF.2.3f is the main focus: recognize and read grade-appropriate irregularly spelled words. With suitable list choices, this practice also supports RF.1.3g and RF.K.3c. Fry hundreds are frequency groups, not grade levels. The lists include regular words as well as irregular words; familiar-word practice is not a complete phonics lesson.

Arrow keys move in Explore. In scored modes, Right arrow or R marks Right; Left arrow or N marks Not yet. F toggles a flag. Tab and Enter operate every control. The reading display shows one word at a time; the requested flag picker and end review deliberately list several.

Sound and the speaker button beside the instructions speak instructions only, never the displayed word or the review list. There is no automatic speech assessment and no recording. Practice has no total time limit. Motion off removes celebration movement while keeping every timer unchanged.

Picks, flags, mode and best scores remain on this device in localStorage, where available. No information is sent anywhere. The file works offline. Sound uses the browser's available English voice until the planned voice recording session.

Content is embedded unchanged from the teacher-supplied words.json: six Dolch sets and ten Fry hundreds. Artwork is limited to quiet parchment and one still top-bar quill, as requested.

On a shared computer, tap Reset in the top bar to clear the flags, choices and best scores saved in that browser. In Practice test and Challenge the instructions are spoken once before Start; once a run begins the voice stays quiet so the reader can focus.
