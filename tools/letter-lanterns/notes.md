## What it teaches

An open village for connecting print with speech, exploring letter shapes, comparing similar words and hearing whole-word banners. Every object is available without a prescribed path.

## Key skills

- Connect a printed letter with an anchor word and, where available, its isolated sound.
- Explore big and small manuscript forms on a ruled slate.
- Compare the letter that changes between two words.
- Hear short and long vowels in simple word pairs.
- Recognize ten familiar whole-word banners.

## Support at home

- Find one of the village's letters on a package and say a word that begins with it.
- Draw a big letter in the air and notice where its strokes start.
- Say pan and pin together and listen for the sound that changes.

## A 5-minute start

Tap any lantern. Choose another letter from the tray, try its slate, or open Wordbook. Follow a child's interests: there are no required rounds or earned objects. In Wordbook, small lanterns speak letter models; a closed window reveals its word when tapped. Banners always speak whole words.

## The three views

**Lanterns:** choose any enabled letter. Trace and Write it yourself are optional. On a vowel, Two glows offers a small short-sound flame and a tall long-sound flame. Both are freely playable.

**Street:** browse four streets. Tap a shop to explore its sign. Read the street gives a short pause before modeling each visible word. Stop and arrows act immediately. The string shows touched letters where there is room; compact landscape and phone layouts keep the shop view instead.

**Wordbook:** choose any sign, twin pair, vowel pair or banner. The lanterns above a sign model its letters. Twin windows begin closed, even for a previously opened shop, and a tap reveals either word. There is no hidden answer, score or mastery rating.

## Settings and privacy

Settings can hide letters and banners, light all enabled lanterns, and clear the saved village. Glow records an exploratory touch only, never reading ability. Saved choices stay on this device; no microphone is used and nothing is transmitted. A damaged save starts quietly with the default village.

## Talk about it

- What changes when pan becomes pin?
- Where do the tails of p and g sit on the slate?
- What do you hear in no and not?

## Good to know

The slate shows one ball-and-stick style with single-story a and g. It is not a judgement of all handwriting styles. Tab reaches the controls. On the slate, Space begins ink, arrow keys move it, and Space lifts it. Shift makes smaller moves. Back to lantern and Next stroke are available before drawing.

This supports exploration related to RF.K.3a-d and L.K.1a. It does not assess those standards. C and g use hard-sound examples; x is modeled at the end of ox, and q uses quail. Long u uses unicorn. Box and fox contain three letters but four speech sounds. US English examples do not represent every dialect.

Isolated sounds use recordings. Where an isolated recording is unavailable, the model says an anchor word instead of letting a browser read a bare letter as its sound. Whole words use the available voice. A spoken response is not measured.
