# Federal or State? (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-the-constitution-and-federalism, grade 12. Codes: D2.Civ.4.9-12, D2.Civ.1.9-12
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Sort real powers (print money, license drivers, run schools, regulate a reservation casino, sign a climate treaty) into federal, state, local, tribal, shared or international bins, then replay the powers whose line moved over time and decide where today's contested ones belong.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: six bins across a map that zooms from city hall to the world, and a stack of power cards.
  - Core loop: drop each card in a bin; feedback quotes the source of that power (an article of the Constitution, the Tenth Amendment, a state constitution, a treaty or tribal sovereignty) and a short real example.
  - Moving line round: a slider replays cards whose bin changed over time (voting rules after the 15th, 19th and 26th Amendments and the Voting Rights Act, education after federal funding, marijuana, Real ID, gaming on tribal land) with the cases or laws that moved them.
  - The aha or the end state: three contested cards today; the student places each, then sees the strongest argument for each side and writes one sentence defending their choice.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a timed whole-class sort.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Civ.4.9-12: the moving line round shows powers and limits that changed over time and are still contested.
  - D2.Civ.1.9-12: the six bins distinguish the powers of local, state, tribal, national and international institutions.
- Not like: checks-and-balances (branches, not levels); build-a-government (designing a system); bill-to-law-board-game (state lawmaking, grade 4).
- Art: drawn in code (bins and zooming map) with illustrated example scenes on each card, because the cards need quick, clear pictures.
- Proposed title: Who Has the Power? (the sort now covers local, tribal and international too)
- Proposed concept line: Sort real powers into federal, state, local, tribal, shared or international bins, replay the ones whose line moved over time, and decide where today's contested powers belong.
- Flags: sensitive (tribal sovereignty facts; fresh review agent per rule 5)
