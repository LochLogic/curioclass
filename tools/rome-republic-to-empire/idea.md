# Rome: Republic to Empire (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-6-ancient-rome, grade 6. Codes: D2.His.2.6-8, D2.Civ.5.6-8, D2.His.14.6-8
- Format / family: story / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Play one senator's family from 133 BCE to 27 BCE through real turning points (the Gracchi's land reform, Marius's paid army, Sulla's march on Rome, Caesar crossing the Rubicon, Augustus's settlement); the senator's choices change their own fate, while a government chart shows the Republic's offices still standing as real power slides to one man.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the Senate house and a government chart (two consuls, the Senate, assemblies, tribunes) with power meters.
  - Core loop: at each turning point, read what happened and choose (back reform, side with a general, stay silent, flee); the story branches for the family, and the chart's meters drain from the Senate and assemblies toward generals and finally one ruler, though the offices keep their names.
  - The aha or the end state: a sort board of what changed and what continued under Augustus, then a cause-and-effect web of why the Republic fell (land, armies loyal to generals, violence in politics, wealth from conquest), compared with how the US Constitution spreads power.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes on the senator's choices.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.6-8: the closing sort of change and continuity from Republic to Empire.
  - D2.Civ.5.6-8: the government chart, its structure and functions, compared with the US system at the end.
  - D2.His.14.6-8: the cause-and-effect web built from the story's turning points.
- Not like: agora-assembly (one vote in Athens); constantinople-defense (the eastern empire later); two-parties (early US politics).
- Art: public domain Roman portrait busts and coins (Met open access, ids to check at spec) plus generated scenes, because real faces of Caesar and Augustus exist.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Play a senator's family from 133 to 27 BCE through real turning points as power slides from Senate to generals to Augustus; sort what changed and what lasted, and map the causes of the Republic's fall.
- Flags: none
