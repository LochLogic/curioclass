arc: explore-first
minutes: 50
focus: A-APR.B; A-APR.C
goal: I can connect the zeros, multiplicities and leading coefficient of a polynomial to its graph, and use the remainder theorem to find factors.
materials: The tool on the projector and on student devices. Graph paper for the exit check.
grouping: Pairs on devices, with whole-class pauses.
---
## Before class

- Open **Build** on the projector. The default track is P(x) = −0.2(x + 4)(x − 4), one hill 32 m tall.
- One grid square is 10 m each way, so the track is true to scale.
- Decide whether pairs will try **Commissions** today or save it for homework.

## Hook (5 min)

1. Show the default track. "The park says a coaster is just a polynomial. Where are the zeros? Where are the stations?"
2. "Today you design tracks by choosing where they touch the ground, and you find out what the equation has to look like."

## Explore (17 min)

1. Pairs drag the right footing from 4 to 2 and watch both forms of the equation change.
2. Pairs set the zero at x = 2 to multiplicity **2**, then **3**. For each, they record how the track meets the ground: crosses, touches and turns back, or crosses flat.
3. Pairs press **Flip track** and change a with the slider. "What changes, and what stays the same?" They read the end-behavior sentence each time.
4. Pairs build a degree 4 track with two hills and one tunnel, then press **Ride** and listen for the tone at each ground contact.
5. Pairs who want to see the algebra press **Show the multiplication**.

## Make sense (10 min)

1. "Why does a zero of multiplicity 2 never let the track cross?" Push for the sign argument: a squared factor is never negative, so the sign doesn't change there.
2. "Two tracks have the same zeros but different a. What stays the same?" (The zeros and the crossing behavior. The height and the direction change.)
3. "How can you tell the end behavior from the factored form alone?" (The degree and the sign of a.)

## Apply (13 min)

1. Switch to **Survey**. The plan comes in standard form only, and the track hides in fog.
2. Pairs move the stake and press **Survey here**. Synthetic division gives the remainder, and a post that tall is planted at c. A remainder of 0 locks a footing.
3. Ask: "Why does the remainder equal the height of the track at c?" Try c = 0: "What do you notice about the constant term?"
4. Pairs use **Check for a double footing** when a zero might repeat, and keep going until the fog lifts.
5. Pairs who finish try Plan 9: "It has only one footing. Where did the other two zeros go?"

## Exit check (5 min)

On graph paper, sketch y = (x + 3)(x − 1)²(x − 4) with the zeros labeled and the right end behavior. Then: "P(2) = 0 for some polynomial P. What do you know about its factors?" Sketches that cross at x = 1 show who needs **Build** again.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** start with degree 2 and 3 tracks, with **Show the multiplication** open.
- **Support:** give pairs a table with columns for zero, multiplicity and "crosses or touches."
- **Stretch:** **Commissions** C5 (Big Three): "Why must the tunnel zeros sit close together?"
- **Stretch:** match a ghost track in the blueprints (B1 to B4) by reading its zeros and a.

## Short on time

- **Build** with multiplicity 1, 2 and 3 at one zero (10 min).
- One **Survey** plan as a class (10 min), then the exit sketch (5 min).
