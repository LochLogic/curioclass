## What it teaches

Students use a station's temperature and rainfall evidence to predict its biome. They compare a simplified key with real vegetation, then test how stable and changed conditions affect recovery after disturbance.

## Key skills

- Read monthly rain bars and a temperature line with different units.
- Apply a branching key and check each numerical comparison.
- Compare a model prediction with observed vegetation.
- Distinguish recovery after disturbance from recovery under a changed climate.
- Explain what climate averages leave out.

## Support at home

- Notice whether local plants are mostly grasses, shrubs or trees. Discuss how rain and temperature might help explain the pattern.
- Look for regrowth in an already disturbed place from a public path. Ask which plants arrived first.
- Talk about how an average can hide unusual dry or wet years. No screen or purchase is needed.

## A five-minute start

Open Read. Choose Kasama and notice that its year starts at July, so southern summer sits in the middle.
Press Place on chart. Enter Key and use the displayed numbers, rather than guessing from the station name.
Press Reveal. In Change, press Fire and Play. Rewind to the green shoots.

## Modes

Read compares 13 fixed WMO 1991 to 2020 station normals. Temperature is the mean of the twelve monthly means, not a day-weighted mean.
Rain is their sum. The two graph axes have different units. All rain plots use 0 to 400 mm.

Key follows the student's answers, including mistaken comparisons. Check my answers identifies arithmetic or logic errors.
The tally counts a station as right only when every comparison is right and the predicted biome matches the real biome.
Re-keying replaces that station's record rather than adding another attempt.

Change begins locked until the station's key is complete. Disturbance resets vegetation, not climate.
Years are approximate. Play spans each succession story in about eight seconds.
Climate sliders change all twelve months uniformly. They are what-if experiments, not forecasts.

## Talk questions

- Why is Dodge City's key result a forest when its real vegetation is prairie?
- How can a system recover after a disturbance while its conditions remain similar?
- Which changed condition makes Berlin recover toward grassland in this model?
- Does a sharp threshold in a key imply a sharp boundary in nature?
- What evidence would you need about soils, seeds, grazing and fire?

## Good to know

HS-LS2-6 is taught through the student's own comparison of stable-climate recovery and changed-climate recovery.
Dodge City is the deliberate exception: dry years, runoff, hot winds, fire and grazing favor prairie despite its mean climate.
The biome key uses only climate. It is not a complete ecosystem model or a forecast.
Succession times are rounded approximations from several studies. Disturbance intensity and local seed supply can stall recovery.
At zero climate offsets the actual station biome returns, including prairie at Dodge City.
A nonzero climate change uses the computed key destination. This makes model assumptions explicit.

Progress saves only in localStorage under biome-atlas. Clear my progress and Reset clear it.
No student identity, analytics or external requests are used. Every image and datum is bundled.
Narration uses the curious-librarian line direction. No audio was recorded during this build; the kit uses browser speech.
Sources are listed in tool.draft.json and the full source spec.

## Review source limits

The biome names are the spec's teaching categories, not observations of vegetation inside each weather-station enclosure.
A 2026-11-03 point query of the WWF layer hosted by [UNEP-WCMC](https://data-gis.unep-wcmc.org/server/rest/services/Hosted/Terrestrial_Ecoregions_of_the_World/FeatureServer/0)
places Niamey in West Sudanian savanna, rather than the spec's Sahelian acacia savanna.
Kredarica falls in the broad Dinaric Mountains mixed forests polygon; this regional map cannot confirm alpine tundra at the station's elevation.
The supplied teaching categories are retained pending correction of the spec. Do not present the 12 matches as twelve independently verified local vegetation observations.

Real biomes on the map stay unknown until Reveal for that station. Wrong comparison paths are explained separately from Dodge City's deliberate model miss.
Recovery labels show the next approximate milestone and identify the completed same-climate recovery.

Size: Higher resolution succession cutouts keep vegetation readable on a full-screen landscape. Thirty paintings remain bundled offline; the bundle may exceed 6 MB.
