# Source Sorter (idea card)

- Module: ela-6-research-and-citing-sources, grade 6. Codes: W.6.7, W.6.8
- Format / family: sort / research. Fit: good for credibility and for quote versus paraphrase, with a short research arc around it so W.6.7 is real (a question that gets refocused).
- One idea: The student answers a research question by sorting a shelf of sources into use, check and skip with a reason for each, then turns the keepers into notes as quotes or paraphrases while a copy meter shows how close each paraphrase sits to the source's words, so they learn to judge a source from its own clues, take notes without plagiarizing, and credit every source.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a question card, "Why are there fewer monarch butterflies?", and a cart of ten sources: a wildlife agency page, a scientist's interview, a school club blog, a product ad for a garden spray, an encyclopedia entry, a 1998 article, a forum post.
  - Core loop: each card shows author, date, purpose and what evidence it gives. The student sorts it into Use, Check or Skip and taps the clue that decided it (old date, selling something, no author). From Use cards they take notes: a quote must be exact and in quotation marks; a paraphrase is checked by a copy meter that highlights runs of the source's own words. The tool builds a citation line for each note from the card's details.
  - The aha or the end state: halfway through, the sources point to one cause the question was too broad for (milkweed loss), and the student refocuses the question; the end state is a short answer paragraph where every sentence carries a note tag and a sorted source list.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second question shelf (why do some towns have more lightning strikes?).
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.6.7: a short research project from several sources, with the question refocused when the sources call for it.
  - W.6.8: judging credibility from the source's clues, quoting and paraphrasing without plagiarism, and listing basic bibliographic information.
- Not like: fact-fusion (grade 5, combining three trusted sources into one table); fake-or-fact-lab (6-8, lateral reading to check made-up pages from outside); research-notebook (grade 9, threads from every sentence back to source words).
- Art: generated scenes (milkweed fields, a monarch on a car windshield), drawn in code for the cart and source cards.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Answer a research question by sorting a cart of sources into use, check or skip from their own clues, then take notes as quotes or paraphrases while a copy meter catches borrowed words; the citation builds itself.
- Flags: needs a data source (monarch decline facts, checked at spec); overlap risk (fake-or-fact-lab on credibility; keep this one on clues inside the source)
