# Viral Video Breakdown (idea card)

- Module: arts-6-8-analyzing-viral-media, grade 6-8. Codes: MA:Re7, MA:Re8, MA:Re9, MA:Cn11
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good
- One idea: A made-up 40-second video of a kid helping at her grandfather's bakery went viral, but by the fifth repost it means something else and her name is gone; take the original apart shot by shot, then trace each repost to find who changed the meaning, how, and what that owes her.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the video with its view count, and a repost chain of five copies on a made-up platform.
  - Core loop: in the original, tag framing, music, captions and cuts and what each makes a viewer feel. Then step through the reposts: a new caption, a cropped frame, a sped-up clip with a joke sound, a stitch with a reaction. Comments (written for Curio Class) show how each audience read it.
  - The aha or the end state: the case board: what the maker meant, what each repost made it mean, and where her credit was lost. The student judges the original and one repost with criteria (clear, fair to the people in it, effective), and answers the fairness questions: may you repost it, must you credit her, what if it mocks her grandfather.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes on the most misleading repost.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MA:Re7: analyzing how framing, music, captions and cuts steer viewers.
  - MA:Re8: interpreting the maker's intent and how audiences read it differently.
  - MA:Re9: evaluating the original and a repost with stated criteria.
  - MA:Cn11: the ethics of reposting, credit and fair use, in everyday media life.
- Not like: toy-ad-reality-check (grade 3-5 ad tricks); fair-use-review (library: deciding fair use cases); morning-show-crew (making media).
- Art: generated scenes for the video's frames; people (illustrated) for the family; made-up platform with no real brand look.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: A made-up video of a kid at her grandfather's bakery went viral; take it apart shot by shot, then trace five reposts to find who changed its meaning and dropped her credit, and judge what reposting owes the maker.
- Flags: overlap risk (fair-use-review; keep fair use here to the reposting questions and link out)
