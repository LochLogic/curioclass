## What it teaches

Students analyze how shot size, camera angle, sound and editing shape a video's meaning. They use evidence from a chain of fictional reposts to judge credit, context, permission and money.

## Key skills

- Identify a shot's size, angle, sound layers and story job.
- Compare frames, captions and sound at the same film moment.
- Trace a copy to an earlier source using inherited details.
- Support a claim with two relevant findings.
- Explain a fairness judgment while considering the maker's perspective.

## Support at home

- Describe a familiar scene from far away, then close up. Talk about what each view makes you notice.
- Retell the same family story with two different openings. Discuss how the opening changes the feeling.
- When sharing a story someone told you, name who told it and ask whether they want it passed on.

## A five-minute start

Press Play in The video. Jump to the oven and label its Size, Angle, Sound and Job. Check shot reveals Nina's notes without a score. Open The reposts, select R1 and R2, and compare the first frames and the end card. Save a second finding with Captions before making a claim.

## Modes

The video: eight painted shots total 20 seconds. Voice-over is shown in captions and is never spoken. Music, the quiet street, oven and crust are synthesized. Check shot compares all four labels, including the complete set of sound layers. Graphic and n/a apply to the end card.

The thumbnail notes badge means the maker's comparison was opened. It does not award correctness. The summary covers the film controls while open; a visible labeling control returns to the film.

The reposts: cards run in posting order. Tap two cards or use A and B. Compare uses one original-time clock, so R5's faster clip still shows the same shot. Each clue tool creates one finding per distinct pair and tool. Draw arrow starts with a repost, then its earlier source. Arrows are hypotheses, not automatic proof. Open Make a claim, then Choose findings. Select two proving chips, tap Back to claim, then Make the claim.

Use the claims as coached investigation, not as an independent assessment. A student can eventually discover a passing card by trying the finite choices with the required findings attached. Ask pairs to point to the inherited detail and explain why it supports their card. The tool does not grade their explanation.

The verdict: Nina's statement is one perspective, not an answer key. Credit and meaning remain unknown until the case includes an arrow and relevant findings. Permission and money are given case facts. Choose Fair, Not fair or It depends and enter a reason for each repost. The final chips have no correct answer; the closing card reflects the selected obligations. Copy includes unfinished calls as an honest draft.

## Talk about it

- What did the sound make you assume before you read the caption?
- Which inherited detail is stronger evidence than a matching music track?
- Can a repost give credit and still change the story unfairly?
- Would asking first change your call? Why?

## Good to know

All characters, account roles, captions, posting times and view counts are fictional. No account is linked. The bakery was never in danger, and Grandpa Teo has always been a baker. This is ethical analysis, not a legal fair-use determination.

Work is saved only in this browser on this device using localStorage. Reset asks before clearing it. If storage is blocked, the tool works in the open tab. Copy uses the clipboard when available and selects a read-only text box otherwise; use the device's Copy command. Editing invalidates the last copied message. The tool works offline and sends no data.

Motion off removes decorative movement. Content playback keeps its timing. Read stops content playback before speaking the current next-action hint. Fixed narration uses the browser voice until the curious-librarian recordings are supplied; no speech is recorded in this build.
