# Viral Video Breakdown

## One idea
A made-up 20-second video of 13-year-old Nina's grandfather baking at 5 a.m. went viral: take it apart shot by shot to see how it makes you feel what it does, then line up five reposts frame by frame, by sound and by time to prove who cut her name off and who turned it into a story it never was, and decide what each one owed her.

## Not like
- storyboard-studio (9-12): plans shots for a film you will make. Here the shots are read from a finished video, and the case is about what happens after posting.
- frame-it (6-8): composes photos. Here shot size, angle and sound are labeled in someone else's edit.
- morning-show-crew (this band): plans how a message spreads on purpose. Here the spread happened without the maker and changed her message.
- fact-check family tools (fake-or-fact-lab and others): judge whether a claim is true. Here the question is the chain of copies, credit and meaning, and the evidence is in the clips themselves.
- cover-version-review (this band): compares two performances. Here six versions of one video form a family tree.

## Who and how
- Grade 6-8, media arts, module "Analyzing Viral Media (Media Arts)". Topic line: taking apart a viral video and tracing its reposts.
- Driver: students (pairs). 25 to 40 minutes.
- Standards: NCAS MA:Re7 (perceive and analyze media), MA:Re8 (interpret intent and meaning), MA:Re9 (evaluate with criteria), MA:Cn11 (media in society: ethics, credit, context).
- Header: title, topic line, modes **The video** (default), **The reposts**, **The verdict**, then kit `motion`, `sound`, `read`.

## Screens and modes

### The video (breakdown)
- Focus: the player (a phone-shaped 9:16 frame, the original is 9:16) in the center, with the shot strip under it: eight thumbnails, one per shot, with their lengths.
- **Play** (becomes **Stop**), **Previous shot**, **Next shot**. Tap a thumbnail to jump there.
- Per shot, a label card beside the player: **Shot size** (wide, medium, close, extreme close), **Angle** (eye level, high, low, overhead), **Sound** (ambient, voice-over, music, sound effect; more than one allowed), **Job** (sets the place, shows the work, shows time passing, makes you feel, gives credit). The student taps choices; **Check shot** compares with the maker's notes (Content) and shows Nina's note for that shot. Wrong picks are shown with the right one, no score.
- After all eight are checked: the "how it works" summary: the shots' sizes as a bar from wide to extreme close over time, and the line "It starts wide in the dark, moves in close to the work, and ends on her name."

### The reposts (the case)
- Focus: the case board: six video cards in a row by posting time on a timeline (original plus five reposts), each a small phone frame with its account name, time after the original, caption and view count (Content). Under it, the family tree area where the student draws "copied from" arrows.
- Clue tools in the panel, used on any two cards: **Compare** (both play side by side, frame locked to the same moment in the original's time, with a difference overlay that outlines what's missing or added: crop edges, watermark, end card, logo, stickers), **Sound** (both audio waveforms drawn one over the other; matching music shows matching shapes), **Captions** (both captions side by side with changed words highlighted).
- **Draw arrow**: tap a repost, then the card it was copied from. An arrow to a later card is refused ("It can't copy something posted after it."). Each repost takes one arrow.
- Three case questions in the panel: **Who first dropped Nina's credit?**, **Who first changed what the video means?**, **Where did the flour ad get its copy?** Pick a card for each, then attach at least two clues (each Compare, Sound or Captions finding the student opens is saved as a clue chip). **Make the claim** checks it: right card and the clues that prove it, or a nudge naming which clue to look at ("Compare the crop of these two.").

### The verdict
- Focus: a table of the five reposts by four questions: **Gave credit?**, **Kept the meaning?**, **Asked first?**, **Made money from it?** The first two fill from the case (the student's arrows and findings); the last two from facts in Content.
- For each repost the student picks **Fair**, **Not fair** or **It depends** and one reason chip or a short line (100 characters). Nina's own statement (Content) is shown first, as one perspective, not the answer.
- A closing card: "A repost owes the maker: ___" with chips (credit, the original meaning, asking first, a share of any money, nothing) the student selects; **Copy** copies the verdict text. No score.

## Interactions in detail
- Every control stays live during play: jumping shots, Stop and the label buttons act at once. In Compare, both clips share one clock; scrubbing one moves both; a sped-up repost (R5) is mapped by the original's time so the same shot lines up.
- The difference overlay is drawn in code from the known edits (crop rectangle, watermark box, sticker box, logo box, end card), never by image analysis.
- Clue chips are created only when a tool is opened on a pair that shows that difference; the same chip is not duplicated.
- Arrows: one per repost; tapping a repost with an arrow lets you redraw it. The family tree layout redraws at once.
- Make the claim with fewer than two clues is disabled; the hint names the tools.
- Saved on this device: labels, arrows, clues, claims and verdict (localStorage, try/catch). **Reset** asks to confirm.
- Motion off: playback still runs (it is the content) but transitions between modes and arrow drawing are instant.
- Sizes: at 1366x768 the player and shot strip (The video) and the six cards with the clue tools (The reposts) are visible without scrolling. Preview 680x425: the phone player and strip readable. At 375 wide: one card at a time in The reposts with a timeline scrubber above.

## Done when
1. Focus: in The video the phone player is the biggest, central thing with the shot strip under it; in The reposts the six cards and tree are; at 1366x768 and 680x425.
2. Every shot's Check shot result matches the Content table.
3. Tap while it plays: jumping shots and Stop act at once; in Compare, scrubbing one clip moves both and R5 lines up by shot despite its speed.
4. Compare, Sound and Captions show the right differences for every pair named in the answers, and create the right clue chips.
5. An arrow to a later card is refused; the right tree (R1 and R4 from the original, R2 from R1, R3 from R2, R5 from R3) can be drawn.
6. Each of the three claims is accepted only with the right card and two proving clues; wrong ones get a specific nudge.
7. The verdict table fills credit and meaning from the case, shows Nina's statement first, and Copy gives the text.
8. No line plays during a clip; how lines once in five repeats; claimRight and arrowRefused speak every time.
