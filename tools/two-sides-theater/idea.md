# Two Sides Theater (idea card)

- Module: ela-2-story-structure-and-point-of-view, grade 2. Codes: RL.2.5, RL.2.6
- Format / family: roleplay / none. Fit: good.
- One idea: The child puts on Goldilocks and the Three Bears in a little theater, first ordering the scenes so the beginning sets up the story and the ending closes it, then voicing the same scene twice, once as Baby Bear and once as Goldilocks, so they hear how each character sees what happened.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a curtain and a director's cue sheet with empty slots. "Which scene opens the show? Which one ends it?"
  - Core loop: arrange scene cards; the theater plays them and asks what the opening tells us (who, where) and how the ending wraps things up. Then the two-sides scene: the child reads Baby Bear's lines and Goldilocks's lines about the same broken chair, with voice choices (sad, scared, cross) and records each.
  - The aha or the end state: the two recordings play back to back; the child picks which lines show each character's side and sees the same event told two ways.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second tale (Jack and the Beanstalk, the giant's kitchen scene told by Jack and by the giant). On screen: recordings never leave this device.
- Standards, one line per code: RL.2.5: ordering scenes and saying how the beginning introduces and the ending concludes the action; RL.2.6: noticing each character's point of view and voicing each one differently when reading dialogue aloud.
- Not like: Who Said That? (grade 1, narrator versus character). Story Stage (kindergarten retell). Story Building Blocks (grade 3 chapters, scenes and stanzas).
- Art: generated scenes (a theater and backdrops) and illustrated characters, because the actors must look ready to perform.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Put on Goldilocks and the Three Bears: order the scenes so the opening sets up the story and the ending closes it, then voice the same scene as Baby Bear and as Goldilocks and hear the two sides.
- Flags: none (the old line's wolf retelling of the Three Little Pigs sits close to a well-known copyrighted picture book, so this card uses a different tale)
