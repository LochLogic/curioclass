# Transformation Dock (idea card)

- Module: math-8-transformations, grade 8. Codes: 8.G.A
- Format / family: studio / transformations. Fit: good.
- One idea: Choreograph shape dancers on a gridded stage with slides, flips, turns and zooms set to an original beat; the routine replays as a list of coordinate rules, proves when two dancers are congruent or similar, and tiles triangles across the floor to show why a triangle's angles add to 180 degrees.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a stage floor with a coordinate grid, a triangle dancer, a beat track and a choreographer, Kemi. "Eight counts. Slide, flip, turn. Make it land on the mark."
  - Move rounds: the student adds moves to a timeline (translate by (3, -2), reflect over the y-axis, rotate 90 degrees about the origin, dilate by 2 from the origin); the shape dances, and its coordinates update. Measuring tools check that lengths, angles and parallel sides survive every rigid move.
  - Match rounds: a dancer must land exactly on a partner. If only slides, flips and turns are needed, the two are congruent; if a zoom is needed, they are similar. The routine itself is the proof.
  - Rule rounds: predict where (2, 5) goes under each move before it plays; write the rule (x, y) to (-y, x).
  - Floor-pattern rounds: copies of one triangle slide and turn to tile the floor; the three angles meet on a straight line (180 degrees). Parallel stage lines cut by a diagonal show equal angles; a dilated triangle keeps its angles (angle-angle similarity).
  - The aha or the end state: rigid moves keep size and shape, zooms keep only shape, and a sequence of moves is an argument.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): perform a routine on the projector; classmates call out the rules.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 8.G.A: properties preserved by rotations, reflections and translations (8.G.1); congruence as a sequence of rigid moves (8.G.2); effects of moves and dilations on coordinates (8.G.3); similarity as moves plus a dilation (8.G.4); angle sum, exterior angles, parallel lines cut by a transversal and angle-angle similarity, argued with tiled triangles (8.G.5).
- Not like: rigid-motion-twins (grade 10 fewest-move proofs), mirror-studio (grade 4 symmetry), image-transformer (grade 12 matrices).
- Art: generated scenes (stage, lights); people (illustrated) for Kemi; dancers as shapes drawn in code on the grid. Original music only.
- Proposed title: Transformation Dock (applied in the 2026-10-27 audit)
- Proposed concept line (applied in the 2026-10-27 audit, docs/agents/audit/clashes-k8.md K13; it replaces the One idea above where they differ): An L-shaped cargo tile must land on its outline; slide, flip, turn and resize it, replay the route as coordinate rules, decide which docks are congruent or only similar, then tile triangles to see why angles sum to 180.
- Flags: none
