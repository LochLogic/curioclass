# ASL Story Theater (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-9-12-asl-storytelling-and-narrative, grade 9-12. Codes: ACTFL.Intermediate High, WRS.Communication
- Format / family: studio / asl (a storyboard and comic-strip studio). Fit: good: the student plans a signed story on a storyboard and rehearses it against the plan.
- One idea: Read a Deaf storyteller's short story told as an illustrated comic strip with role shift, then plan your own: a storyboard maps each character to a spot in signing space and a body turn, and you rehearse against it (optionally recording on this device) to check that every shift lands where you set it.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a small theater stage. An illustrated Deaf storyteller tells a short two-character story as a comic strip in the Sign Strip player. "Watch how the body turns for each character."
  - Core loop: step 1, read: the model story plays panel by panel, with the body turned left or right at each role shift, gaze lines, the space map showing where each character lives, and pause marks between scenes. Step 2, plan: the student picks a story frame (a mix-up at a bus stop, a lost dog) and builds a storyboard from the same panel parts: each character gets a side, a height of eye gaze and a short line in gloss, and the storyboard plays back as a comic. Step 3, rehearse: the student signs the story beside their storyboard, with an optional camera recording that stays on this device and never leaves it (the screen says so). Step 4, review: a self-check against the storyboard (did I shift for every line? did my pacing pause between scenes?), with the recording if one was made.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's storyboard plays as a finished comic, side by side with the model story, showing what fluent role shift and pacing look like next to theirs.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): plan-only mode with no camera (the default when no camera is present); a peer feedback checklist for class sharing; printable storyboard.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Intermediate High: telling a connected signed narrative across time frames with role shift and pacing.
  - WRS.Communication: presentational signing of a story, and interpretive watching of Deaf storytellers.
- Not like: classifier-scenes (one scene with classifiers, not a narrative); face-the-grammar (sentence grammar); deaf-culture-explorer (culture, not performance).
- Art: graphics only, per docs/agents/ASL-GRAPHICS.md: the storyteller is the shared illustrated signer with front, back and turned-left / turned-right body plates for role shift and face plates (people review), vector Sign Strip hands, arrows and space map; comic-strip panel frames and illustrated stage (drawn in code). No video of signers, no photos of people.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Read a Deaf storyteller's comic-strip story to see role shift, then plan your own short signed story on a storyboard that sets each character's place and gaze, rehearse it, and check it against your plan.
- Flags: story panels checked by the fresh blind review (ASL-GRAPHICS.md); sensitive (ASL storytelling tradition, fresh review, rule 5); camera recording shows the on-device line (rule 7); the on-screen practice note (one common form; practice with a fluent Deaf signer or your ASL teacher when you can).
