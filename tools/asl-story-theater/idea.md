# ASL Story Theater (idea card)

- Module: world-languages-9-12-asl-storytelling-and-narrative, grade 9-12. Codes: ACTFL.Intermediate High, WRS.Communication
- Format / family: studio / asl. Fit: good: the student plans, rehearses and records a signed story with real tools.
- One idea: Watch Deaf storytellers tell a short story with role shift, then plan and record your own: a storyboard maps each character to a spot in signing space and a body shift, and your recording plays back beside the plan so you can check that every shift lands where you set it.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a small theater stage. A Deaf storyteller (video) tells a 60-second story with two characters. "Watch how the body turns for each character."
  - Core loop: step 1, watch: the model story replays with markers at each role shift, eye-gaze change and pause. Step 2, plan: the student picks a story frame (a mix-up at a bus stop, a lost dog) and builds a storyboard: each character gets a side, a height of eye gaze and a short line in gloss. Step 3, rehearse and record with the camera (video stays on this device and never leaves it; the screen says so). Step 4, review: the recording plays beside the storyboard with a self-check (did I shift for every line? did my pacing pause between scenes?).
  - The aha or the end state: the student's own story plays as a finished piece, and a side-by-side with the Deaf storyteller shows what fluent pacing and role shift look like next to theirs.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): plan-only mode with no camera; a peer feedback checklist for class sharing.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - ACTFL.Intermediate High: telling a connected signed narrative across time frames with role shift and pacing.
  - WRS.Communication: presentational signing of a story, and interpretive watching of Deaf storytellers.
- Not like: classifier-scenes (one scene with classifiers, not a narrative); face-the-grammar (sentence grammar); deaf-culture-explorer (culture, not performance).
- Art: real signing video by fluent Deaf storytellers (required); illustrated stage (drawn in code).
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Watch Deaf storytellers use role shift, then plan your own short signed story on a storyboard that sets each character's place and gaze, record it on this device, and check it against your plan.
- Flags: needs a data source (Deaf storyteller video); sensitive (ASL storytelling tradition, fresh review, rule 5); camera recording shows the on-device line (rule 7).
