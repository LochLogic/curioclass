# Campaign Manager (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-elections-parties-and-media, grade 12. Codes: D2.Civ.2.9-12, D2.Civ.10.9-12
- Format / family: tycoon / election. Fit: good
- One idea: Run a fictional presidential campaign (budget, messages, polling, ads, ground game, the Electoral College map), then run the same race under other rules (a national popular vote, ranked-choice voting, Australia-style compulsory voting) and see how each changes who campaigns where and which citizens matter.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a fictional candidate with three issue positions, a budget, a blank calendar to November, and a US map with real electoral votes.
  - Core loop: each week, spend on ads, rallies and field offices; pick messages for voter groups whose interests differ (retirees, students, farmers, union members, small business owners); polls respond with realistic noise; interest groups and news coverage react.
  - Rules switch: replay under the other systems and watch the map, the spending and the voters courted change.
  - The aha or the end state: a turnout panel with real US turnout over time (who could vote, who did) and the student's note on which system best fits a theory of democracy they choose (majority rule, broad participation, protecting minorities).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): rival campaigns on one map.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Civ.2.9-12: the turnout history, the theory choice and the other countries' rules analyze citizens' role over time and alternative models.
  - D2.Civ.10.9-12: the voter groups with different interests show how personal interests shape political choices and appeals.
- Not like: class-election (family sibling, a real class vote for grade 3); who-decides (local action); checks-and-balances (governing, not campaigning).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the candidate, staff and voters, and drawn-in-code maps and polls, because voters should look like real people.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Run a fictional presidential campaign through ads, polls and the Electoral College, then replay it under a popular vote, ranked-choice and compulsory voting to see which citizens each system makes matter.
- Flags: sensitive (partisan balance: fictional candidates and parties only, reviewed by a fresh agent)
