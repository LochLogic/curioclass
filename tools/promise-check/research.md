# Research packet: promise-check
Concept: A class president race and a town mayor race run side by side; check each promise against what that office can really do, help write an honest speech, vote by secret ballot, and compare how each place decides.
Grade: 3   Standards: D2.Civ.1.3-5, D2.Civ.11.3-5, D2.Civ.2.3-5, D2.Civ.7.3-5

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Civ.1.3-5: "Distinguish the responsibilities and powers of government officials at various levels and branches of government and in different times and places." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain the real powers and limitations of a town mayor compared to what a school class president can decide.
- D2.Civ.11.3-5: "Compare procedures for making decisions in a variety of settings, including classroom, school, government, and/or society." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Compare how a classroom elects a class officer using secret ballots with how a town's registered voters elect a mayor in a municipal election.
- D2.Civ.2.3-5: "Explain how a democracy relies on people’s responsible participation, and draw implications for how individuals should participate." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain why voters must evaluate candidate promises carefully and vote honestly to keep democracy functioning well.
- D2.Civ.7.3-5: "Apply civic virtues and democratic principles in school settings." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Practice fair play, honesty, and respect for all students during a school campaign and election.

## Common mistakes
- Believing elected leaders can do whatever they want: Third graders frequently imagine mayors and class presidents rule like monarchs, not realizing authority is constrained by laws, school policies, and budgets (Source: National League of Cities Cities 101).
- Falling for impossible promises: Students often vote for candidates who promise wild perks (such as "no homework forever" or "free cotton candy daily"), overlooking whether the office can actually deliver (Source: iCivics Cast Your Vote Guide).
- Confusing public hand-raising with private secret voting: Children regularly miss why secret ballots exist, not realizing anonymous voting prevents peer pressure and bullying (Source: National Constitution Center Australian Ballot History).
- Believing leadership is a popularity contest: Students often think an election is about who has the most friends rather than who possesses responsible ideas, integrity, and public virtue (Source: C3 Framework Civics Indicators D2.Civ.7.3-5).

## Sensitivity
- Fostering civic trust without cynicism: Teach students to fact-check campaign promises critically without becoming jaded or cynical about government service and democratic institutions.
- Fair school playground culture: Ensure class election scenarios celebrate constructive service and fairness, avoiding popularity dynamics that leave students feeling excluded.
- Equal voting dignity: Highlight that whether a vote is cast in a small classroom or a grand city hall, every single citizen's vote carries equal weight.
