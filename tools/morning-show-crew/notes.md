## What it teaches

Students adapt one story into a video, poster, site post and spoken announcement while keeping its essential facts consistent. They plan distribution within a time budget and use audience results to revise that plan.

## Key skills

- Keep the same essential facts across different media.
- Combine pictures, headlines and supporting words for a purpose.
- Budget production time and repeated distribution.
- Notice audiences a familiar channel can miss.
- Separate hearing about a story from receiving its complete message.

## Support at home

- Choose a family event and say the same four essential facts as a spoken invitation and a note. Check that the time, place and next action agree.
- Discuss who could miss an announcement made in only one place, and how you could include them.
- Notice a repeated message during the week. Talk about what stayed consistent and what changed between versions.

## Modes

Play segment loops a two-second title plus four three-second shots. Changes to played slots wait for the next loop. Poster Big changes the selected fact line's emphasis. Site adapts the same story to a school news card. Announcement chips append sentences; Undo sentence removes the last. Hear it previews the current take with browser speech.

The week: tap an unrun day's cell to buy or cancel its job directly. Buying the first English announcement includes writing and the first read. Reads cost time again each day. The video edit takes four hours using spare quarter-hour blocks across days. Its final editing block reserves half an hour for the first airing. Once edited, it airs on subsequent days when half an hour fits; tap its cell to pause that day's edit or airing. A blocked purchase leaves the plan intact. Spanish reads require that day's English read. Spanish posters require poster design; Spanish site posts require a published site post. Captions require a started video edit.

Cells on completed days are locked; Undo day reopens the last day with the plan intact. Map dots are symbolic shares, not individual people. ## Talk questions

- Why can the late bus miss a homeroom-only plan, even when the other students hear it often?
- What changed when you moved a persistent poster or site job earlier?
- Which group did your plan miss? What did reaching that group cost?
- How can a version be heard widely and still fail to deliver the right message?
- Why are families counted as households while the audition estimate counts students only?

## Good to know

Everything is a made-up school and a simplified planning model, not evidence about actual language learners or school audiences. The student groups are disjoint: 410 on time, 60 late bus and 70 Spanish-speaking students learning English, totaling 540. There are 400 family households and 45 teachers. Summing all five rows gives 985 mixed audience units, not 985 students or people.

Every day has three crew hours, for twelve hours Monday through Thursday. Quarter-hour units are exact. Reach rates below are expected meetings per member per active day. They are teaching assumptions written for this tool.

| Job | Crew hours | On time | Late bus | Learning English | Households | Teachers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Announcement | Write 0.5 once, read 0.25 per day | .45 | 0 | .10 | 0 | .35 |
| Spanish announcement | 0.25 per day | 0 | 0 | .35 | 0 | 0 |
| Video | Edit 4, air 0.5 per day | .50 | 0 | .20 | 0 | .30 |
| Captions, on aired video | 1 once | .10 | 0 | .15 | 0 | .05 |
| Poster, per printed copy | Design 2 once, print 0.25 each | .08 | .12 | .08 | .01 | .05 |
| Spanish poster, one copy | 1 once | 0 | .02 | .10 | .02 | 0 |
| Site | 1 once | .02 | .06 | .01 | .15 | .08 |
| Spanish site | 1 once | 0 | 0 | .03 | .12 | 0 |

The model accumulates expected meetings m. Heard share = 1 - exp(-m). Got-it share = 1 - exp(-v) * (1 + v), where v includes only complete, matching versions at the time each day ran. This is a Poisson two-meeting model. It describes an assumption, not a measured threshold for learning or persuasion. Caption and translation increments inherit the original medium's validity. Wrong facts cannot be repaired by repetition. There is no score, badge for a winning plan, or saved best.

Counts round each audience row to the nearest whole unit, then sum rounded student rows for the audition estimate. Auditions are an explicitly fictional guess of 8% of those students. Media breakdown counts expected meetings, not unique recipients. Families cannot contribute to the audition count.

Announcement time = counted words / 2.5. The 30-word greeting, correct facts and sign-off gives 12 seconds. Browser speech is a preview; its actual duration may differ from that planning estimate. Repeating sentences can push the bar past 30 seconds but never adds distribution reach.

Visual shorthand: the clock carries When with hands at 3:15 and a code-drawn Friday label; the music room carries Where; the sign-up door carries How with its room and Thursday deadline written on the sheet. What needs explicit words, including no experience needed. A wrong textual fact overrides a supporting image. Overlays follow the image crop on every medium.
