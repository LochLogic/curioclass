## What it teaches
Students combine music, effects and moving pictures into a timed score. They can explain how tempo, loudness and instrument sound change the mood of the same cartoon.

## Key skills
- Match musical choices to an intended story mood.
- Place sound effects at picture events on a timeline.
- Compare the same frames with different scores.
- Explain a composition using speed, loudness and instrument sound.
- Revise a scene after listening to the whole work.

## Support at home
- Tell a short story twice, once with a gentle humming sound and once with a quick bouncy hum. Ask what changed about the story.
- Use quiet taps, a rustling cloth or a hum to accompany a made-up animal adventure. Take turns choosing the mood.
- Ask your child which instrument they would choose for rain, a surprise or a warm ending, and why.

## A five-minute start
1. Tap Play silent in Theater. Watch what actually happens before deciding on a mood.
2. Tap Hear it three ways. The six-second storm repeats with Funny, Scary and Sad scores.
3. Tap Start scoring. Choose a scene block and a preset, then change one musical choice.
4. Add an effect at the lightning or the kitten reveal. Tap Play to check its timing.
5. Enter Mood Swap and compare your story with the other scores.

## Modes
Theater introduces live silent-film accompaniment. Score it gives each six-second scene one cue with Feel, Tempo, Loudness, Instrument and Style. The Effects panel contains ten synthesized effects. Tap a sound then tap the effects track, or drag it into place. Rain can last three to six seconds. Mood Swap changes only the sound while the same cartoon continues.

## Talk questions
- What did the tuba change about the storm? Name something you heard, not just the mood word.
- Could a slow, soft cue still feel creepy? Compare feel and instrument as well as tempo.
- Which effect needs to land exactly on the picture? Which can start earlier or continue longer?
- How did the kitten reveal change your music for the final scene?

## Standards in student actions

## Good to know
Space plays or stops outside text fields and select menus. Number keys 1 to 4 select scene blocks. Tab reaches the Music, Effects and Story panels before timeline controls. Select an effect and use arrows to move it in quarter-second steps; Delete removes it. With a shelf sound selected, Enter on the effects track places it at the playhead. The ruler can be dragged or moved with arrow keys.

The starting score is silent. Choosing a music setting or preset creates that scene's cue. Clear score clears cues, effects, intended mood and both writing responses. Nothing leaves the device. Storage can be unavailable in some private browser modes; keep that tab open in that case.

While stopped, each music edit plays a short two-note sample using the chosen tempo, loudness, instrument and style. Play scene hears the whole cue with the picture. Crowded effect labels spread across two lanes so each remains reachable; their pins and duration strokes stay at the exact times. Crowded labels show their start time followed by length, and selecting a chip also gives both in the hint. An effect keeps its chosen length when moved; playback clips only the part after the film ends.

Motion off keeps all 24 seconds and the timed poses, replaces the bright lightning flash with gray, and freezes ambient grain and rain streak motion. Headphones help pairs compare timbres. All sound is synthesized in the browser. Fixed narration currently uses the browser voice fallback, with the Zephyr recording manifest prepared.
