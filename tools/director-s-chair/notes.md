## What it teaches

Students direct the same eight-beat scene in five theater traditions, then explain how stage shape, masks,
movement, power and the relationship to an audience change an interpretation. They support a director's note
with evidence from productions they have staged.

## Key skills

- Connect historical theater conventions to practical staging choices.
- Notice who is visible and where an audience's attention goes from different seats.
- Compare the effect of the same words in different theatrical traditions.
- Revise blocking and describe the consequence of a directing decision.
- Justify an interpretation using a particular moment in a production.

## Support at home

- Tell the same short story to someone beside you and then to someone across the room. Discuss what changes.
- Retell an everyday event once privately and once as if addressing a crowd. Ask which version invites sympathy.
- Discuss a performance you remember. Name one choice in movement, position or setting that shaped its meaning.

## A five-minute start

Choose Greek theater, read its three house rules and tap Begin. Choose an entrance for the Guard, then use Seat
to watch the same moment from three places. Move to beats 5 and 6 and complete the other two choices. Read the
program note, return to Tour and try a different tradition.

## The modes

Tour has five houses in historical order. Any order of visits is allowed. Arrival shows a painted venue, rules
and cast. Scene offers three pairs of directing choices, eight fixed text beats and three seats. Play scene
runs the full scene with two seconds per beat plus movement time; it does not narrate the play text. The longer
Noh bridge crosses are deliberate. Next beat finishes the current cross and moves on; Seat and Stop remain live.

Adjust enlarges the ground plan. Select an actor and drag, use arrow keys in the focused plan to move 0.25 meters,
or use Q and E to turn by 45 degrees. The turn buttons provide the same action. Notes about a house convention
are descriptive and never prevent a move. These exploratory edits last for the current visit; saved productions
keep the three directing choices, as specified, rather than free adjustments. Room-source light controls appear
inside Adjust in the realist house only.

In compact Adjust layouts, controls sit above the ground plan. Crowded initials and sitting/kneeling labels
move into linked callouts; their lines end at the original blocking positions. The selected seat stays named
in the Seat button and on the plan. This changes label placement, not the actors or the sightline calculation.

Compare unlocks after two complete productions. Beats 1, 4, 6 and 8 are shown from each house's front seat.
Write up to 600 characters about which theater made the Guard most sympathetic and support that interpretation
with visible staging evidence. Save on this device keeps choices and the note in this browser only. Clear removes
that saved record and the current choices. No work leaves the device.

## Talk questions

- Where did the Guard seem most afraid, and which visible decision made you think so?
- When did the audience become a confidant, a council or an unseen observer?
- How does a mask shift expression from a face to a whole body?
- Which seat changed your reading of power? Point to the moment rather than only naming the tradition.
- What did you borrow from a house's conventions to form your directing concept?

## Good to know

This is a staging comparison, not a reconstruction of historical performances of Antigone in these traditions.
The Noh and commedia casts are explicit adaptations of the same scene. Dates, customs and architecture are
introduced through house rules. Stage dimensions, actor counts in the drawings, blocking, focus weights and
notices are the tool's own simplification and interpretation. The eye is a rough guide to emphasis, not a score
for a correct artistic choice. Hidden figures appear as dashed outlines for explanation.

Notice headings name the earlier beat when a directing choice carries forward. A hidden Guard may still be
drawn as a dashed explanatory figure; use Seat to inspect an unobstructed view. The commedia copying option
can hide the zanni behind the Capitano from the front of the crowd, even though its supplied notice describes
the comic intention. Ask students to compare the intention with the sightline evidence.

The scene concerns a forbidden burial and a ruler's threat of death. There are no graphic depictions. This
supports NCAS TH:Cn11 through cultural and historical context, TH:Cr2 through choices constrained by that
context, TH:Re7 and TH:Re8 through watching and interpreting the productions, and TH:Re9 through the student's
evidence-based director's note.

The Guard's report is from Sophocles, Antigone, F. Storr's translation, Loeb Classical Library (1912), public
domain, Project Gutenberg ebook 31. Cuts are marked [...]. Two long dashes in the translation are commas here.
Creon exits at beat 7. Speaker labels change with the house; the words do not. Narration describes controls,
never performs the play. Fixed lines use browser speech because recording was excluded from this build.
