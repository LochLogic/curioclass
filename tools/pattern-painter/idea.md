# Pattern Painter (idea card)

- Module: math-3-patterns-and-two-step-problems, grade 3. Codes: 3.OA.D
- Format / family: mystery / none. Fit: good, if both kinds of case are real detective work: pattern cases (who painted this, and what rule did they follow?) and receipt cases (is the witness's answer reasonable?).
- One idea: Someone keeps painting squares on the town's giant multiplication-table mural; find the rule they followed and prove why it always works, and check two-step story claims by estimating to catch the ones that can't be true.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a town square with a 10 by 10 multiplication mural and Detective Rue. "Overnight, someone painted squares. What rule did they follow?"
  - Pattern cases: painted squares show a rule (every even product, every multiple of 5, the square numbers, the 4s column is double the 2s). The child predicts the next painted square, paints it, then picks or completes the reason ("4 times a number is always even because 4 = 2 x 2").
  - Receipt cases: a witness says "I bought 3 packs of 8 markers and gave away 9, so I have 33 left." The child estimates first (24 minus about 10 is about 14), flags the claim, then solves with a letter for the unknown (3 x 8 - 9 = m).
  - The aha or the end state: patterns in the table are not luck; each one has a reason, and estimating catches a wrong answer before you calculate.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a case maker for the teacher.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 3.OA.D: two-step problems with all four operations, a letter for the unknown and an estimation check (3.OA.8), and finding and explaining patterns in the multiplication and addition tables (3.OA.9).
- Not like: lost-and-found (grade 2, add and subtract only), rule-machine (grade 4 patterns from a given rule), multiplication-dungeon (fluency).
- Art: generated scenes (a colorful town square mural), people (illustrated) for Detective Rue and witnesses.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line (applied in the 2026-10-27 audit, docs/agents/audit/clashes-k8.md K10; it replaces the One idea above where they differ): Color the town's multiplication-table mural to make patterns pop (even products, multiples of 5, square numbers); predict the next square and say why the rule always works, then estimate first to check whether a two-step answer can be right.
- Flags: none
