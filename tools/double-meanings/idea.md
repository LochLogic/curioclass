# Double Meanings (idea card)

- Module: ela-8-puns-irony-and-allusion, grade 8. Codes: RL.8.4, RI.8.4, L.8.5
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good: sorting lines by how they carry two meanings, then flattening them to prove it.
- One idea: The student sorts lines from stories, speeches and ads into pun, verbal irony, analogy or allusion, explains both meanings of each, then swaps the key word for a plain one and watches the line fall flat, so they learn that a double meaning lives in one chosen word and that it shapes both the meaning and the tone.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: four bins (Pun, Verbal irony, Analogy, Allusion) and a stack of line cards from public-domain stories and speeches (Twain, Wilde, Lincoln) and original ads by Curio Class.
  - Core loop: the student sorts a card, taps the word that does the double work, and writes the surface meaning and the hidden one. Then the "flatten" button swaps that word for a plain synonym; a tone gauge drops and the second meaning disappears, and the student says what was lost. Hard cards fit two bins (an allusion used ironically) and the student defends a choice. Informational cards (a scientist's analogy, a speech's allusion to the Declaration) show how these devices work in nonfiction too.
  - The aha or the end state: the student writes their own line for a school poster that carries a double meaning on purpose, then flattens it themselves and checks that the gauge drops.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a "groan or grin" vote on student puns.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - RL.8.4: in the story lines, the meaning of figurative and connotative words and how puns, analogies and allusions shape meaning and tone.
  - RI.8.4: the same in speeches and informational lines, including analogies and allusions to other texts.
  - L.8.5: interpreting puns and verbal irony in context (5a), using analogies and word relationships to understand words (5b), and the connotations exposed by the flatten swap (5c).
- Not like: allusion-hunter (grade 7, hunting allusions in everyday texts); word-thermometer (grade 6, connotation scale); satire-decoder (grade 11, a whole satire's two cases).
- Art: drawn in code for the bins and gauge; public domain portraits of the quoted authors (museum ids to be checked at spec).
