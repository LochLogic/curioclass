# Stop-Motion Clay Shoot

## One idea
Stop-motion only looks smooth when the one thing you meant to move moves a little and nothing else changes at all: set up a tabletop studio (tape the tripod or not, lamp or window light, a remote or your finger), shoot a clay snail and a clay bird frame by frame, and the Jump Finder lays each frame over the one before and points at exactly what jumped (the whole set from a bumped camera, the light from a passing cloud, a hand left in the shot, or a move bigger than half the figure), so you fix the studio, reshoot the frame, and premiere a film that plays clean.

## Not like
- flipbook-studio (6-8): draws frames and tunes timing by holding drawings. Here nothing is drawn; you pose real-looking clay on a set, and the lesson is controlling everything that should not change.
- storyboard-studio (9-12): plans camera shots, angles and cuts. Here the camera never moves (that is the point); the storyboard is four simple beats.
- cartoon-soundtrack-mixer (this band): scores a finished cartoon. Here you make the pictures; the premiere's music is one choice of three.
- near-and-far (3-5): arranges a still picture for depth. Here the set is a stage for motion over time.
- recorder-concert-rehearsal (this band): also finds what went wrong and fixes it, but by ear, naming a player, a bar and a note. Here it is by eye: the Jump Finder's difference view shows what changed between two frames, and the fix is in the studio set-up.
- poem-to-movement and recital-stage-directions (this band): code dancers on a floor. Here painted clay cutouts on a tabletop set.

## Who and how
- Grade 3-5, media arts, module "Stop-Motion Animation (Media Arts)".
- Topic line: shooting and fixing a stop-motion
- Driver: students in pairs (one poses, one runs the camera, then swap) or the teacher on a projector for Fix the rough cut. 20 to 40 minutes. No webcam: the "camera" is the screen's view of the painted set.
- Standards: MA:Cr1 (plan the beats on a storyboard), MA:Cr2 (set up the studio and shoot), MA:Cr3 (find and fix what jumps), MA:Pr5 (camera, lighting and posing skills), MA:Pr6 (premiere the film with a title and music).

## Screens and modes
Header: title, topic line, `Read`, `Motion`, `Sound` (kit). Mode buttons: `Rough cut` (default), `Studio`, `Premiere`.

**Rough cut (puzzle, first).** Focus: the film window, a 16:9 viewer, with the frame strip under it. A made-up 36-frame clip by "the after-school club" (Content) plays at 12 frames a second, on twos, and stutters four times.
- `Play` and `Stop`; drag the strip or use the arrow keys to step one frame.
- `Onion skin` (on by default while stepped, off while playing): the frame before shows at 35% under the current one.
- `Jump Finder`: a difference view. The current frame minus the one before is shown as bright where something changed and dark where nothing did. A clean frame lights only the figure; a bumped frame lights every edge of the set; a flicker frame lights everything evenly; a hand frame lights a hand shape at the edge.
- Tag a frame: tap `Something jumped here` and pick a cause from four cards (`The camera got bumped`, `The light changed`, `A hand is in the shot`, `The move was too big`). Right: the frame gets a cause icon on the strip and a one-line why ("Everything moved, even the table. That's the camera."). Wrong: "Look at the Jump Finder. What lit up?" and the frame stays open.
- Fix each tagged frame: the fix for its cause (Content) is a button (`Line it back up`, `Even out the light`, `Reshoot without the hand`, `Add an in-between`); `Add an in-between` opens the posing view with both neighbor frames as onion skins and the student poses the halfway frame. When all four are fixed, `Play` runs smooth and a card says what each fix teaches about the studio, then `Go to the studio`.

**Studio (sandbox).** Focus: the camera view of the set, big, with the frame strip under it and a small studio panel beside it.
1. `Plan`: pick a story (Content) and see its four beats as storyboard cards (start, something happens, action, end), each with a thumbnail made from the figures' poses. Cards can be reordered or swapped from a short list of beats.
2. `Set up`: a painted top view of the tabletop studio with three choices, each a toggle: `Tape the tripod down` (off: each snap has a 1 in 8 chance of a small bump), `Light` (`Window` with clouds outside, or `Lamp` with the blinds closed: Window changes brightness frame to frame), `Remote shutter` (off: a snap within 1.5 s of letting go of a figure catches the hand at the edge of the frame). The tool never says which choices are right; the Jump Finder shows it.
3. `Shoot`: drag a figure on the set (it moves in small floor steps; a long drag is allowed); tap a pose on the pose bar (snail: 4; bird: 6) to change its pose; tap `Snap` (or Space) to add a frame. Onion skin shows the last frame. Props (strawberry, flower, pebble, leaf) can be placed. Up to 48 frames.
4. `Check`: the Jump Finder runs over every frame pair and marks the strip with cause icons; tapping a mark opens that frame with its fix buttons (or `Reshoot`, which deletes the frame and returns to the pose before it).
- `Play` (12 frames a second, each frame shown twice, so 24 frames last 4 s) with a `Speed` choice: `On twos` (smooth enough, the usual) or `On ones` (each frame once: twice as fast, so you need twice as many frames for the same move).

**Premiere.** Focus: a little cinema, painted, with the film on its screen. Choose a title card (three painted title boards, with the student's title typed on the blank board), music (Web Audio, three original short loops: `Tiptoe` pizzicato, `Adventure` march, `Silly` kazoo-and-tuba; or none), and an end card. `Premiere` plays title, film and end card with music; the painted audience is rows of cinema seats with clay-figure plush toys in them. Saved on this device.

## Content
**Rough cut** (36 frames, on twos, 6 s; Moss the clay snail crawls to a strawberry): frame 9 bumped (the whole set shifts 3 units right and 1 unit down, and stays shifted until fixed: fix `Line it back up` moves frames 9 to 36 back); frame 16 flicker (brightness +22%: fix `Even out the light` matches it to its neighbors; the card says "Next time: a lamp and the blinds closed"); frame 23 hand (a hand silhouette in the lower right, code: fix `Reshoot without the hand`; card: "Use the remote, or step back before you snap"); frames 29 to 30 too big (Moss moves 14 units in one frame, more than half his own 18-unit length: fix `Add an in-between`; card: "Small moves, about the width of a pea in real clay").

**Figures** (painted cutouts, one per pose):
- Moss, a clay snail (green body, orange shell): stretch, squash, peek (eyes up), tuck (in the shell).
- Wren, a clay bird (blue with a yellow beak): stand, crouch, hop (in the air), wings up, wings down, look back.

**Stories** (four beats each):
1. The Strawberry: Moss crawls toward a strawberry / Wren lands next to it / both stretch for it / Moss tucks in his shell, Wren takes a peck.
2. The Leaf: a leaf lands on Moss / Moss peeks out / Wren hops over / Wren lifts the leaf off.
3. The Race: Moss and Wren line up / Wren hops ahead and looks back / Moss keeps crawling / Moss wins while Wren is busy with a flower.

**Fix cards** (shown after each fix, and in Studio checks): bumped: "Tape the tripod's feet down, and don't lean on the table." Light: "Close the blinds and use one lamp, so clouds can't change your light." Hand: "Use the remote, or take your hands back and wait a second before you snap." Too big: "Move a little at a time. If the figure doesn't overlap its onion skin at all, the move is too big."

**Jump Finder rules** (between two frames): camera bump = the set's anchor points (table edge, backdrop corner) moved more than 1 unit; light change = average brightness changed more than 8%; hand = the hand layer is in the frame; too big = a figure moved more than half its own length (Moss 18 units long, Wren 12).

## Interactions in detail
- Units: the set is 100 units wide; figures move in floor units, never screen pixels, so the rules hold at every size.
- Bumps in Studio are random but seeded per snap and saved, so Play and Check agree; a bump shifts the camera, so later frames stay shifted until `Line it back up`.
- Window light: brightness follows a slow cloud wander (each frame plus or minus up to 12%), so most frames on Window trip the 8% rule within a few snaps.
- While Play runs every control stays live: Stop stops at once, Speed changes the next frame, Onion skin and Jump Finder can be toggled, and tapping the strip jumps there and keeps playing from it.
- Hand rule: with Remote off, `Snap` within 1.5 s of releasing a drag or pose change adds the hand silhouette (code, gray, from the bottom right edge); the panel never blocks the snap.
- Saved on this device (localStorage `stop-motion-clay-shoot`): Rough cut progress, the student's frames, studio choices, title and music. `Start over` clears them, with a confirm.
- Keyboard: Tab to a figure, arrow keys move it 1 unit (Shift: 5), number keys 1 to 6 pick a pose, Space snaps, comma and period step frames.
- Sizes: the camera view is the largest thing at 1366x768 and 680x425; the frame strip scrolls sideways and shows 8 thumbnails at 375 wide, with the studio panel under the view.

## Look
- An animation light box: paper #f4f1f6 (cool lilac white), panels #e6e0ea, ink #2a1d1a, muted #615a66, accent (`--brand`) #a63f53 clay rose (buttons with white text). Jump Finder view is its own dark frame (#141414) with bright lime #b6f23a for change.
- Cause icons on the strip: a tripod (bump), a sun with a cloud (light), a hand (hand), a stretched arrow (too big), each with a word in its tooltip and the list under the strip, never color alone.
- Contrast: white on rose 6.07:1; rose on paper 5.43:1; rose on panels 4.69:1; ink on paper 14.55:1; muted on paper 5.93:1; ink on panels 12.58:1.
- Different from neighbors: flipbook-studio red on cream paper, paint-mixer purple, traveling-art-show brick on warm cream (this band: brick is orange-red on cream, this is pink-red on cool lilac), storyboard-studio coral on near black.

## Voice
Bank voice `casual-peer` (style "Say casually and naturally, like a friendly teenager explaining something"): an older student who runs the after-school animation club. Not used by any 3-5 arts neighbor or another tool in this band. `Curio.voice` with `scope: () => state.mode`.
- welcomeCut (how; entering Rough cut; stale when Play or the strip is touched): "Our club's film keeps jumping. Tap Play, then step through it with the arrows."
- finderHow (how; after the first full play; stale when Jump Finder is turned on): "Turn on Jump Finder. It lights up whatever changed since the last frame."
- tagHow (how; Jump Finder on, nothing tagged; stale when a frame is tagged): "Find a frame where too much lit up, and tap Something jumped here."
- causeRight (event; stale on the next tag): the frame's why line from Content.
- causeWrong (event; stale when the frame is tagged again): "Look at the Jump Finder. What lit up?"
- cutDone (event; all four fixed): "Clean! Now go make your own. Tap Go to the studio."
- welcomeStudio (how; entering Studio; stale when a story is picked): "Pick a story first. Four beats, start to end."
- setupHow (how; after Plan; stale when any studio choice is touched or Shoot is tapped): "Set up your studio. Your choices show up later in the Jump Finder."
- shootHow (how; Shoot open, no frames; stale on the first Snap): "Move Moss a tiny bit, then tap Snap. Then do it again."
- checkHow (how; 12 or more frames and no Check yet; stale when Check is tapped): "Tap Check to see if anything jumped."
- checkClean / checkMarks (event; after Check): "No jumps. That's a clean shoot." / "Some frames jumped. Tap a mark to see why."
- premiere (event; Premiere starts): "Lights down. Here we go."

## Sources
- How stop-motion works (pose, photograph, repeat; 12 frames a second shot "on twos" is common in hobby and school work), and the common problems of camera bumps, light flicker from daylight, hands in frame and moves that are too large: Aardman Animations, "Animate It!" and education resources, https://www.aardman.com/ ; Susannah Shaw, Stop Motion: Craft Skills for Model Animation, 2nd ed. (Focal Press, 2008), chapters on camera, lighting and animating.
- Taping the tripod, using a remote shutter and using artificial light with windows covered: Shaw (above); Into Film (UK film education charity), stop-motion animation teaching guides, https://www.intofilm.org/
- Standards: National Core Arts Standards, Media Arts, MA:Cr1, Cr2, Cr3, Pr5, Pr6 grade 3 to 5, https://www.nationalartsstandards.org/
- Checks run (Python, 2026-10-29): at 12 frames a second on twos, 24 frames last 4.0 s and the 48-frame cap lasts 8.0 s; crossing the 100-unit set in 4 s on twos takes about 4.2 units a frame, well under the too-big rule (half a figure: 9 for Moss, 6 for Wren), so a smooth crossing is possible within the cap. With an untaped tripod at 1 in 8 per snap, a 24-frame shoot has a 4% chance of no bump and 3 bumps on average, so the lesson shows up in almost every shoot. The "half its own length" too-big rule and the 8% light rule are the tool's teaching rules, chosen to match what the onion skin and the eye show, not published thresholds. Contrast ratios with the WCAG 2.1 formula.

## Done when
1. Focus: the camera view is the largest thing at 1366x768, 680x425 and 375 wide, with the strip under it.
2. Rough cut: each of the four planted problems lights the Jump Finder as described, takes its right cause, and its fix makes Play run smooth.
3. Studio: with tripod untaped, Window light and Remote off, Check finds bumps, light changes and hands in a 24-frame shoot; with all three fixed, only too-big moves can appear.
4. Check and Play agree on every frame after a reload (seeded bumps saved).
5. Every control stays live while Play runs; Stop stops at once.
6. Premiere plays title, film and end card with the chosen loop, and nothing ever uses a camera or uploads.
7. How lines are spoken once in a run of five actions; event lines when they happen.
8. Frames and choices survive a reload; `Start over` clears them.
