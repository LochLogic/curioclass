# Jump Line (idea card)

- Module: math-2-adding-and-subtracting-to-1000, grade 2. Codes: 2.NBT.B
- Format / family: sandbox / number-line. Fit: good.
- One idea: Draw your own jumps on an open number line to solve a sum or difference, then compare your path with three other kids' paths to the same answer and say why each one works.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a long empty line on a park path, a grasshopper at the start. "Solve 46 + 38. Drag to make jumps of any size."
  - Core loop: the child drags jumps (+30, +4, +4) and labels them; the grasshopper hops along. Subtraction runs backward or as "how far from 38 to 46?" Bigger numbers come later (270 + 50 crosses 300; 600 - 235).
  - Path gallery: three illustrated classmates show their paths (one jumps to the next ten first, one jumps a hundred then backs up). The child picks which path is right and finishes a sentence: "It works because...".
  - Mental lane: +10 and +100 from any number, said fast.
  - The aha or the end state: different jump paths land on the same number, and the friendly numbers (tens and hundreds) are what make paths short.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): add up to four two-digit numbers in one path.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 2.NBT.B: fluent add and subtract within 100 (2.NBT.5), adding up to four two-digit numbers (2.NBT.6), adding and subtracting within 1000 with a drawn model and composing hundreds (2.NBT.7), adding 10 or 100 in your head (2.NBT.8), and explaining why a strategy works (2.NBT.9).
- Not like: elevator-hundred (grade 1, hundred-chart building), roller-coaster-rounding (grade 3 rounding on a line), place-value-vault (reading and comparing numbers).
- Art: people (illustrated) for the classmates whose paths you compare; the line drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Solve sums and differences to 1000 by drawing your own jumps on an open number line, then compare your path with three classmates' paths to the same answer and explain why each one works.
- Flags: none
