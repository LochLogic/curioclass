# Fast or Slow? (idea card)

- Module: science-2-changing-landforms, grade 2. Codes: 2-ESS1-1, 2-ESS2-1
- Format / family: timeline / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Watch real Earth changes as short clips and photos (a landslide, a volcano erupting, a river carving a canyon, a beach slowly washing away), place each on a timeline from seconds to millions of years, then protect a hillside home and a beach house by comparing ways to slow wind and water.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a long timeline with picture stops (a blink, a day, a year, a grandparent's life, a very, very long time); a stack of event cards. "How long did this change take?"
  - Core loop: open a card (a clip, a before-and-after photo, a short read-aloud from a park ranger or a scientist), use two or more clues from different sources, and place it on the timeline; the tool shows the real time and asks which clue helped.
  - Protect step: a hillside home and a beach house face wind and water. Try solutions (plant grass, a sand fence, a stone wall, a ditch, a windbreak of trees), run a storm and a sped-up year, and compare how much land each one saved and what it costs or blocks.
  - The aha or the end state: the full timeline shows some changes happen in minutes and some take longer than people have lived, and a comparison card says which solution worked best where.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - 2-ESS1-1: placing events using clues from several sources (clips, photos, a ranger's account) as evidence that Earth changes quickly or slowly.
  - 2-ESS2-1: the protect step, comparing several solutions that slow or stop wind or water from changing the land.
- Not like: erosion-table (grade 4 measuring rates of weathering and erosion); island-maker (shapes of land, not change over time); earth-s-calendar (deep time at grade 6).
- Art: generated scenes and public domain photos for real events (for example US Geological Survey photos, which are public domain, checked per photo), because real places make the time scales believable.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Place real Earth changes (a landslide, an eruption, a canyon, a shrinking beach) on a timeline from seconds to millions of years using clues from clips and photos, then compare ways to protect land from wind and water.
- Flags: needs a data source (public domain photos and clips of real events, USGS or NPS)
