# Fed Chair (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-money-and-the-federal-reserve, grade 12. Codes: D2.Eco.9.9-12, D2.Eco.12.9-12
- Format / family: simulator / none. Fit: good for D2.Eco.12; D2.Eco.9 does not fit (see below)
- One idea: Take the chair in four real moments (1979 inflation, the 2008 crisis, 2020 lockdowns, the 2022 price surge), set interest rates while Congress picks fiscal policy beside you, and watch inflation and unemployment respond over the following years, then compare with what really happened.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: the Federal Reserve boardroom, a rate dial, and the 1979 dashboard: inflation above 11 percent, a worried public.
  - Core loop: each meeting, set the rate (and, where it applies, asset purchases); a calibrated model moves inflation, unemployment and loans with realistic lags. A fiscal panel shows Congress's choices (tax cuts, relief checks, spending) for that era, which the student can recommend.
  - Bank layer: short scenes show how a bank, a family's mortgage and a small business feel each rate change.
  - The aha or the end state: the student's path next to the real one (Volcker's high rates and the deep 1982 recession, near-zero rates in 2008 and 2020, the 2022 hikes), and a short evaluation of which mix of monetary and fiscal policy fit each economic condition.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class sits as the committee and votes.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.12.9-12: the four eras and the fiscal panel evaluate monetary and fiscal policy choices across different conditions.
  - MOVE D2.Eco.9.9-12: the C3 indicator is "the roles of institutions such as clearly defined property rights and the rule of law in a market economy", not central banking; new tool: two farming villages, one with registered land titles and fair courts and one without, where the student decides whether to plant an orchard, lend or start a shop, and sees why secure rights and contracts change investment.
- Not like: economy-dashboard (reading real data); stock-market-1929 (one crash as an investor); inflation-wallet (a family through hyperinflation).
- Art: generated scenes (boardroom, a bank, a family's kitchen table) and drawn-in-code charts, because the people affected make the rate real.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Chair the Fed in four real moments from 1979 to 2022, set interest rates as Congress picks fiscal policy, watch inflation and jobs respond with real lags, then compare your path with history.
- Flags: needs a data source (historical rates and indicators, calibrated model checked)
