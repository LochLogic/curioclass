## What it teaches

Students draw and refine an animation by changing the spacing of drawings and the time each drawing stays visible. They can compare the feel of the same artwork under different timing choices.

## Key skills

- Draw a sequence of small changes using onion-skin guides.
- Compare even spacing with slow-in and slow-out spacing.
- Change holds and playback speed to express an intention.
- Revise a media artwork after watching its motion.
- Produce a timed paper flipbook from editable vector drawings.

## Support at home

- Draw a dot in the corners of a few scraps of paper. Move it a little each time, then flip the stack.
- Watch someone blink. Compare a long pause with several quick blinks, and talk about the different feeling.
- Roll a ball gently across a table with a partner catching it. Discuss where it starts slow, moves quickly and stops.

## A 5-minute start

Open Starters and choose Real bounce. Press Play. Switch to Time and try On twos, then Hold the key on a floor drawing. Ask: what feels heavier now? Stop, load Even bounce and compare. On a narrow or short screen, Tools or Timing opens the additional controls.

## The modes

Draw stores strokes as editable vectors. Pencil widths are 3, 6 and 12 units on a 960 by 720 drawing page. Eraser removes entire strokes. Copy page makes a new editable drawing. Each page has up to 50 undo steps. Blue shows preceding drawings, green shows following drawings. Onion skin is absent in Time, Flip and print.

Time changes holds, never the artwork. Drag a hold handle or use minus and plus on a focused thumbnail. Presets set holds, while the 6, 8, 12 and 24 frames-per-second settings change speed without changing holds. Each dot marks the center of a drawing's stroke bounds. Blank drawings break the chart's line. The chart measures drawing centers, not physical speed between every possible pair of shapes.

Flip plays the book using the same timing. Loop repeats until Stop. Ping-pong plays forward and back. Step and Back advance a single held frame. A tick sounds only when a drawing begins. Motion off removes the page-corner lift and idle light animation but keeps playback timing.

Print pages creates eight frames per sheet, with repeated drawings for their holds, black strokes, cutting lines, frame numbers and a staple margin. Print is a paper-making activity. The tool offers no GIF export or sharing code.

## Talk about it

- What changed when you doubled the holds but kept the drawings?
- Which drawing needs more time to make a ball feel heavy?
- How can four drawings suggest a calm blink or a surprised blink?
- What do close chart dots tell you? When can a drawing's center be misleading?
- What would you revise to make your intention clearer?

## Good to know

The book saves automatically on this device only. It never leaves the device. Local storage can be unavailable or full; the current book remains usable until the page closes. New book and replacement starters ask before discarding artwork. The limit is 48 drawings and 400 strokes per page. Editable holds range from 1 to 6 frames. The Blink starter retains the spec's explicit 8-frame first hold until edited, so its original 11-frame, 0.9-second timing is preserved.

Keyboard: Tab reaches every control. Left and Right step drawings; minus and plus change the focused drawing's hold. A focused hold slider also takes Left, Right, Home and End. Alt + Left or Right reorders a focused thumbnail. Drawing itself uses a mouse, pen or touch. Erasing ignores pen pressure.

## Sources and standards

Timing and spacing explanations follow Richard Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit (2001), and Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, The Illusion of Life (1981), chapter 3.
