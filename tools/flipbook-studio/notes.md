## What it teaches

Students draw and refine an animation by changing the spacing of drawings and the time each drawing stays visible. They can compare the feel of the same artwork under different timing choices.

## Key skills

- Draw a sequence of small changes using onion-skin guides.
- Compare even spacing with slow-in and slow-out spacing.
- Change holds and playback speed to express an intention.
- Revise a media artwork after watching its motion.
- Produce a timed paper flipbook from editable vector drawings.

## Support at home

- Draw a dot in the corners of a few scraps of paper. Move it a little each time, then flip the stack.
- Watch someone blink. Compare a long pause with several quick blinks, and talk about the different feeling.
- Drop a ball and let it bounce. Watch where it seems to hang at the top and where it rushes at the floor, then compare with Real bounce.

## A 5-minute start

Open Starters and choose Real bounce. Press Play. Switch to Time, tap 24 first, then On twos to see film's twos: 12 new drawings per second. At the starter's 12 per second, On twos gives only 6 new drawings per second. Select a floor drawing and try Hold the key. Ask: what feels heavier now? Stop, load Even bounce and compare. On a narrow or short screen, Tools or Timing opens the additional controls.

Try a bowling ball using only timing: load Real bounce, tap On twos, then give the top drawings 1 frame and the floor drawings 4.

Plan a jump before drawing: make the start, the top and the landing first (your key drawings). Add the drawings in between with onion skin, closer together near the top. Watch it, then revise the plan's timing for the feeling you want. Key drawings mark where a move starts, turns and ends.

## The modes

Draw stores strokes as editable vectors. Pencil widths are 3, 6 and 12 units on a 960 by 720 drawing page. Eraser removes entire strokes. Copy page makes a new editable drawing. Each page has up to 50 undo steps. Solid blue shows preceding drawings; dashed green shows following drawings, so direction remains readable in grayscale. Onion skin is absent in Time, Flip and print.

Time changes holds, never the artwork. Drag a hold handle or use minus and plus on a focused thumbnail. Presets set holds, while the 6, 8, 12 and 24 frames-per-second settings change speed without changing holds. Each dot marks the center of a drawing's stroke bounds. Blank drawings break the chart's line. Compare dot gaps as speed only with equal holds: a longer hold adds time without moving a dot. The chart measures drawing centers, which can shift when a shape changes.

Flip plays the book using the same timing. Loop repeats until Stop. Ping-pong plays forward and back. Step and Back advance a single held frame. A tick sounds only when a drawing begins. Motion off removes the page-corner lift and idle light animation but keeps playback timing.

Print pages creates eight frames per sheet, with repeated drawings for their holds, black strokes, cutting lines, frame numbers and a staple margin. Print is a paper-making activity. The tool offers no GIF export or sharing code.

## Talk about it

- What changed when you doubled the holds but kept the drawings?
- Which drawing needs more time to make a ball feel heavy?
- How can four drawings suggest a calm blink or a surprised blink?
- With equal holds, what do close chart dots tell you? What changes if one hold is much longer? When can a drawing's center be misleading?
- What would you revise to make your intention clearer?

## Good to know

The book saves automatically on this device only. It never leaves the device. Local storage can be unavailable or full; the current book remains usable until the page closes. New book and replacement starters ask before discarding artwork. The limit is 48 drawings and 400 strokes per page. Editable holds range from 1 to 6 frames. The Blink starter opens with an 8-frame hold on its first drawing (a calm blink). Editing it switches to the 1 to 6 frame range. Its original 11-frame, 0.9-second timing is preserved until then. That starter hold has eight visible cells and is labeled. A standard 48-page book held six frames uses 288 frames; retaining Blink's eight-frame opener with 47 six-frame drawings uses 290 frames and needs 37 print sheets.

Keyboard: Tab reaches every control. Left and Right step drawings; minus and plus change the focused drawing's hold. A focused hold slider also takes Left, Right, Home and End. Alt + Left or Right reorders a focused thumbnail. Drawing itself uses a mouse, pen or touch. Erasing ignores pen pressure.

## Sources and standards

NCAS Media Arts MA:Cr2.1.6-8, MA:Cr3.1.6-8 and MA:Pr5.1.6-8 are practiced through planning key drawings, original drawing, iterative refinement for intent and production using animation tools. Timing, spacing and key drawings follow Richard Williams, The Animator's Survival Kit (2001), including the section "Keys, extremes and breakdowns", and Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, The Illusion of Life (1981), chapter 3. Live cross-checks: [Dave Burgess, Timing, Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/timing-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/) and [Kevin Koch, Slow In and Slow Out, Animation Mentor](https://www.animationmentor.com/blog/slow-in-and-slow-out-the-12-basic-principles-of-animation/). Twenty-four frames per second is a common film rate, not the only rate. Twos produces 12 drawings per second at 24 fps; at this tool's default 12 fps it produces 6 drawings per second. The starter books and activity prompts were written for this tool. Illustrations generated with OpenAI gpt-image, edited for Curio Class. Narration uses browser speech; no voice recordings were made.
