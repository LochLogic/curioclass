# Debate Prep Desk (idea card)

- Module: ela-6-argument-writing, grade 6. Codes: W.6.1, D3.4.6-8, D4.1.6-8
- Format / family: builder / evidence. Fit: good; the thing built is a full argument essay, tested against a coach's practice objections.
- One idea: The student picks a real kid question (should school start later?), pulls evidence from a shelf of sources of mixed strength, writes a claim with reasons, and before the "debate" a coach throws practice objections that expose the strengths and limits of both sides, so they learn that an argument is built from reasons and evidence and is honest about where it is weak.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a prep desk with three issue folders (later school start, year-round school, phones at lunch). Each folder has six short sources: a sleep study summary, a survey of students, a principal's letter, a bus company memo, a blog opinion, a news article.
  - Core loop: the student picks a side, writes a claim, and drags source cards under each reason; each card shows who wrote it and what it actually measured. The coach (scripted, not a chatbot) presses: "That blog is one parent's opinion. What does it prove?" "Your survey asked 40 students at one school." The student marks each piece of support strong or limited and writes one line on the other side's best point and its limit.
  - The aha or the end state: the essay builder turns the outline into paragraphs with an introduction, transitions (because, as a result, however), a formal-style check that flags slang, and a conclusion. A strengths-and-limits table sits beside it: the student can see their side is not flawless and that saying so makes the essay stronger.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): print both sides' tables for a short class debate.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - W.6.1: writing the full argument: claim, organized reasons, relevant evidence from credible sources, transitions, formal style and a conclusion.
  - D3.4.6-8: building the claim and the counterclaim and naming the strengths and limitations of each in the table.
  - D4.1.6-8: the essay draws on several sources and acknowledges the limits of its own argument.
- Not like: counterclaim-rally (grade 7, a live back-and-forth where each rebuttal answers the last point); claim-vs-claim (grade 8, formal style and distinguishing opposing claims); argument-builder-pro (grade 9, building both sides for a fair fight).
- Art: generated scenes for each issue (a dark bus stop at 6:45 am, a summer classroom), people (illustrated) for the coach.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Pick a real kid question (should school start later?), build a claim with reasons and evidence from sources of mixed strength, then face a coach's practice objections and write an essay that names its own limits.
- Flags: needs a data source (real school-start and sleep research summaries, cited and checked at spec)
