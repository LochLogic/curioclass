# Columbian Exchange (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-7-age-of-exploration, grade 7. Codes: D2.His.14.6-8, D2.Geo.7.6-8, D2.Geo.11.6-8
- Format / family: sort / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Sort crops, animals and diseases by where they began (the Americas or Afro-Eurasia), then send each across the Atlantic and watch three linked panels change on both sides: diets (potatoes in Europe, maize in Africa, chilies in Asia), populations (Indigenous peoples devastated by smallpox and measles) and plantations (sugar, and the forced transport of enslaved Africans).
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: two continents facing each other across the ocean and a pile of picture cards (potato, horse, smallpox, cacao, wheat, sugarcane, maize, cattle, tomato, malaria).
  - Core loop: sort each card to its home side, then drag it across on a ship; the panels update with a short plain fact and a source. Some cards ask why the land made it spread (sugar needs hot, wet land; potatoes grow in cold soil).
  - The aha or the end state: the student builds a causes and effects chain from one card (sugarcane to plantations to the slave trade; potatoes to population growth in Europe).
  - Class mode or extras (optional): "trace your dinner" for one meal.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.14.6-8: the causes and effects chains, each with more than one link.
  - D2.Geo.7.6-8: ships and routes carry crops, animals and germs, and the panels show the spread.
  - D2.Geo.11.6-8: the land each crop needs shapes where it was grown and traded.
- Not like: colonial-trade-routes (grade 5 triangular trade); ship-s-log (one voyage); silk-road-traders (overland trade).
- Art: public domain botanical and natural history prints (to check at spec) plus people (illustrated) for the effects panels.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Sort crops, animals and diseases by where they began, then send them across the Atlantic and watch diets, populations and plantations change on both sides, from potatoes in Europe to smallpox in the Americas and sugar's forced labor.
- Flags: sensitive (population collapse and slavery told plainly with care; fresh review); needs a data source (population estimates, which vary widely)
