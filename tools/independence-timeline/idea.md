# Independence Timeline (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-10-decolonization-and-independence, grade 10. Codes: D2.His.2.9-12, D2.Civ.14.9-12, D2.Geo.5.9-12
- Format / family: timeline / none. Fit: good
- One idea: Lay out eight independence stories from 1945 to 1975 (India and Pakistan, Indonesia, Ghana, Algeria, Kenya, Vietnam, Egypt, Mozambique) on one timeline with a linked map, compare the paths people took (negotiation, mass nonviolence, armed struggle), and see what changed and what stayed after the flag went up.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a 1945 world map mostly colored by empire, and an empty timeline running to 1975: "How did 50 new nations appear in 30 years?"
  - Core loop: drag each country's event cards (first movement, key protest or war, the handover, the first years after) onto the timeline; the map recolors as each nation forms. Each card has a short source in a leader's or ordinary person's words (Nkrumah, Gandhi, a Kenyan Land and Freedom Army fighter, an Algerian woman in the war).
  - Path tags: the student tags each story's means of change, and a compare view lines the paths up by cost, speed and outcome.
  - The aha or the end state: a "before and after" panel for each country shows continuities (borders drawn by empires, export economies, languages of rule, Partition's displacement) next to changes, and the student writes which path's result surprised them.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): each group presents one country.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.His.2.9-12: the before and after panels and the timeline show change and continuity across the era.
  - D2.Civ.14.9-12: the path tags compare historical means of changing societies, from boycotts to armed struggle to negotiation.
  - D2.Geo.5.9-12: the map shows how colonial and new national decisions reshaped borders, languages and land use (Partition, plantation economies).
- Not like: scramble-for-africa (how empire arrived, grade 9 explorer); movement-strategy-room (US civil rights choices).
- Art: people (illustrated) leaders and ordinary participants, plus public domain photos where checked, because the human stories carry each path.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Place eight independence stories from India to Mozambique on one timeline and map, tag how each was won (talks, nonviolence, armed struggle), and compare what changed and what stayed after independence.
- Flags: sensitive (Partition violence, colonial wars and torture in Algeria and Kenya; fresh review agent per rule 5)
