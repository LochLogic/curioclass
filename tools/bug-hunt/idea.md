# Bug Hunt (idea card)

- Module: science-8-evolution-and-natural-selection, grade 8. Codes: MS-LS4-1, MS-LS4-2, MS-LS4-3, MS-LS4-4, MS-LS4-6
- Format / family: arcade / none. Fit: good for MS-LS4-4 and MS-LS4-6
- One idea: Be a bird snapping up moths on tree bark; the moths you miss breed, the colors in the population shift round by round, and when the bark turns sooty or clean again the student graphs the change the way it really happened to England's peppered moths.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a pale, lichen-covered tree trunk with moths of slightly different shades, and a 30-second timer.
  - Core loop: tap moths to eat them; when time runs out, the survivors breed, passing on their shade with a little variation and a rare new dark form. A histogram of moth colors updates every generation. After a few rounds, the bark darkens with soot; later, clean-air laws let the lichen return. A line graph tracks the share of dark moths across all generations.
  - The aha or the end state: no single moth changed color; the population did, because moths varied and the ones that blended in survived to breed. The student explains it in three sentences: variation, survival, inheritance. The real story card covers the dark form's rise in industrial Britain in the 1800s and its fall after clean-air laws.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a pooled class histogram.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - MS-LS4-4: the student's explanation ties genetic variation to who survives and reproduces.
  - MS-LS4-6: the histograms and the line graph are the math representation of a trait increasing and decreasing over generations.
  - MOVE MS-LS4-1, MS-LS4-2, MS-LS4-3: new tool: "Fossil and Bone Bench" (grade 8), order fossils through rock layers to find patterns of change, compare limb bones (a whale flipper, a bat wing, a human arm), and compare early embryos across species.
- Not like: membrane-gatekeeper (grade 9 arcade); tree-of-life (grade 9 shared ancestry); gene-pool (heredity and breeding).
- Art: generated scenes of real-looking bark and moths, because blending in only works if they look real; graphs drawn in code.
- Proposed title: keep
- Proposed concept line: Be a bird hunting moths on tree bark; the moths you miss breed, the bark turns sooty or clean, and a graph of moth colors across generations shows natural selection as it happened to England's peppered moths.
- Flags: needs a data source (peppered moth history, checked)
