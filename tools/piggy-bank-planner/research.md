# Research packet: piggy-bank-planner
Concept: Save for a kite in the shop window: each week choose to spend, fill the piggy bank or visit the bank that keeps money safe and lends it out, and see why saving gets you more.
Grade: 2   Standards: D2.Eco.5.K-2, D2.Eco.9.K-2, D2.Eco.10.K-2

## Standards in plain words
- D2.Eco.5.K-2: "Identify prices of products in a local market." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Look at items in a store (like the kite in the shop window) and identify what they cost in dollars and cents.
- D2.Eco.9.K-2: "Describe the role of banks in an economy." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain how banks protect deposited money, make loans to borrowers, and pay interest to savers.
- D2.Eco.10.K-2: "Explain why people save." (National Council for the Social Studies, C3 Framework, https://www.socialstudies.org/system/files/2022/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.2.pdf)
  What a student must be able to do: Explain why setting aside money instead of spending it right away lets someone accumulate enough to buy a larger goal later.

## Common mistakes
- Believing banks give out free money: Young children frequently imagine banks hand out money like gifts or candy rather than storing deposits that belong to account holders (Source: NEFE Early Childhood Financial Education Research).
- Thinking saving means never spending: Second graders often view saving as permanent loss of spending rather than delaying spending to buy a bigger item later (Source: Council for Economic Education National Standards for Personal Financial Education).
- Believing money in a piggy bank grows on its own: Children often think money placed in a home jar multiplies without realizing that only interest-bearing bank accounts pay extra money (Source: CFPB Comparing Places to Save Money Guide).
- Confusing ATMs with infinite money machines: Children regularly believe automated teller machines print or create cash rather than deducting from a pre-funded bank balance (Source: St. Louis Fed Page One Economics: Banking Basics).

## Sensitivity
- Family economic diversity: Second graders come from households with very different financial circumstances, including unbanked or underbanked families and families facing poverty. The tool must focus on personal choice mechanics (wants versus saving goals) using classroom play money without moralizing family finances or assuming all families have bank accounts.
- Commercial brand neutrality: Avoid referencing real retail banks or specific commercial toys; use generic civic terms like "Community Bank" and "Town Toy Shop".

## Similar tools elsewhere
- U.S. Mint: "Counting with Coins" grocery game. URL: https://www.usmint.gov/learn/kids/games/counting-with-coins. Core mechanic: Children add coin values to buy items in a grocery store setting.
- Practical Money Skills: "Peter Pig's Money Counter". URL: https://www.practicalmoneyskills.com/play/peter_pigs_money_counter. Core mechanic: Players sort coins, count totals, and choose between spending in a store or saving in a piggy bank.
- EconEdLink: "Savings Match Game". URL: https://www.econedlink.org/. Core mechanic: Drag and drop terms like interest, deposit, and savings goal onto matching definitions.

## Gaps
