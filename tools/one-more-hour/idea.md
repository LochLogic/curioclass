# Opportunity Cost Game (idea card)

- Module: social-studies-12-scarcity-and-choice, grade 12. Codes: D2.Eco.1.9-12, D2.Eco.2.9-12
- Format / family: arcade / none. Fit: change to simulator, because rapid arcade choices can't teach marginal reasoning or policy incentives, which need the student to weigh one more unit at a time
- One idea: Spend a senior's Saturday one hour at a time (one more shift, one more hour of study, one more hour of sleep) and watch marginal benefit fall and marginal cost rise, then apply the same reasoning to a town's real-style policy question where incentives hit different groups differently.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: a clock with 14 waking hours, a pay rate, a test on Monday and friends texting; "Where does the next hour go?"
  - Core loop: place each hour; a chart plots the benefit of the last hour against what it cost (lost pay, lost study, lost rest), and the student finds where to stop. Surprises (overtime pay, a canceled shift) change the incentives.
  - Policy round: the town council weighs a fare-free bus, a sugary-drink tax or a congestion fee; the student sees how each changes the choices of riders, shop owners, drivers and low-income families, then argues for or against using marginal benefits and costs.
  - The aha or the end state: a one-paragraph argument the tool checks for a margin, an incentive and the groups who gain or lose.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): the class votes, then sees who in town wins and loses.
- Standards, one line per code:
  - D2.Eco.1.9-12: the policy round shows incentives shaping choices, with costs and benefits for different groups.
  - D2.Eco.2.9-12: the hour-by-hour chart and the final argument use marginal benefits and costs.
- Not like: market-day (grade 1 choices); concert-ticket-market (supply and demand); the-buyout (market structure).
- Art: generated scenes (the student's day, the town) and people (illustrated) townspeople, because the groups affected need faces.
- Proposed title: One More Hour (the old title names a lesson, not the play)
- Proposed concept line: Spend a Saturday one hour at a time and watch the benefit of each extra hour fall as its cost rises, then use the same marginal reasoning to argue a town policy that changes incentives for different groups.
- Flags: none (module description also names economic systems, which carry no code here)
