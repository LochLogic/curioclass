arc: explore-first
minutes: 45
focus: NHES.S1; NHES.S7
goal: I can explain how germs spread, even before anyone feels sick, and show which healthy habits stop them.
materials: The tool on the projector, and on student devices if you have them. Optional: glow lotion and a black light for real handwashing.
grouping: Whole class for predictions, then pairs on devices.
---
## Before class

- Open **Classroom outbreak**. Germ vision starts on.
- Draw a run table on the board with three columns: habit, number sick, and our prediction.
- Plan a trip to the sink if you want students to practice the five steps for real.

## Hook (5 min)

1. "Raise your hand if you've ever caught a cold from someone. Did you know they were sick when you caught it?"
2. Introduce the class on screen: 24 students, and one (the dashed circle) came to school with cold germs but feels fine.
3. Students predict how many of the 24 will catch the cold in two weeks. Write the predictions on the board.

## Explore (15 min)

1. Press **Start 2 weeks** with no habits on. Watch the coughs (green puffs), the shared door handle and the crayon bin. Record the number sick (usually about 20).
2. Pairs choose one habit to test: wash hands, **Cover coughs** (into elbow), **Stay home when sick**, or wipe shared things. Then **Run again**. The same student brings the same germs, so the test is fair.
3. Pairs add their result to the board. Then try two habits together.
4. Last run: turn on all four. (It usually stops at the first student.)

## Make sense (10 min)

1. "Why didn't anyone know the first student was sick?" (People can spread germs the day before they feel sick.)
2. Read the board. "Which habit helped the most? Which is easiest to do?"
3. "Washing sometimes barely helped. Why?" (One missed wash before snack is enough.)
4. "Coughing into your hand blocks the spray. Why is the elbow better?" (Your hands carry the germs to everything you touch.)
5. Remind the class this is a simple model of one classroom, not a medical prediction.

## Apply (10 min)

1. Switch to **Wash up**. A volunteer does the five steps: wet, soap, scrub, rinse, dry. Scrubbing counts real seconds toward 20. Use **Turn hand over** for the back.
2. The black light shows what was missed. "Where do germs hide?" (Thumbs, between fingers, under nails.)
3. Try **water only** to see how much soap matters.
4. If you can, go to the sink: everyone practices the five steps while humming for 20 seconds.

## Exit check (5 min)

On a card: name two ways germs spread in a classroom, and the one habit you'll use most, with a reason. Cards that don't mention spreading before feeling sick show what to revisit.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** run the simulation as a whole class, and have students vote on which habit to try next.
- **Support:** give a frame: "Germs spread by ___. I will ___ because ___."
- **Stretch:** "Why does staying home when you're sick protect other people more than it protects you?"
- **Stretch:** test the flu shot option and compare it with the other habits.

## Short on time

- One run with no habits, then one with all four (8 min).
- **Wash up** with one volunteer (5 min), then the exit card (5 min).
