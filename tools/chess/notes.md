# Chess: a note for grown-ups

Chess is the classic look-ahead game. Every move asks the same question: "If I go here, what can they do back?"
That habit of picturing the next move, and the one after, is the thinking this game is built on.

What a child practises without being told:

- Planning a few steps ahead and checking a plan before acting.
- Seeing patterns: a fork (one piece attacks two), a pin, a back-rank mate.
- Spatial reasoning: lines, diagonals, the L of a knight, coordinates like e4.
- Losing kindly and trying again. A wrong move is never punished; the game shows what was off and puts the piece back.

How it plays:

- Play the computer: seven puppets, from Mouse to Lion. All are available from the start. Takebacks are allowed and cost a star.
- Play a friend on one device. Flip the board manually or switch on "Turn the board each move".
- Puzzles: nine short acts of five, mate in one, mate in two and win a piece, from the Lichess puzzle database (CC0).
- Today's puzzle draws from the same 45 checked puzzles. Daily progress belongs to the puzzle, even if the device date changes.
- Hints show legal destinations and can be switched off. A Clue points to the piece and counts as a try.
- Puzzle scoring counts the first run. Restart, reload and Undo preserve the tally. Finished acts return as Encores for fun.

The ladder was measured against Stockfish 19 during development. Lion plays at about 2250 on a fast computer:
its engine-match estimate was 2245, with a 95 percent interval of 2176 to 2314. These are comparison-engine estimates,
not human FIDE ratings. The full table and procedure are in codex-report.md. On a very slow computer a time cap may
stop a search before its node budget, making a puppet a little weaker. A half-budget stress test measured Lion at 2197.

Draws are explained in plain words: stalemate, the same position three times, fifty moves with no capture or pawn move,
or not enough pieces left to checkmate. The game declares repetition and fifty-move draws automatically, so children do
not need to know how to claim them. Checkmate on the hundredth half-move takes priority.

Nothing leaves the device. There are no accounts, ads or chat. Initials on the score board are three letters only.
A good next step for a keen child is a local chess club or a school chess program.
