# Character Choice Story (idea card)

- Module: ela-3-characters-and-their-choices, grade 3. Codes: RL.3.3
- Format / family: story / choose-path. Fit: good, with one change: the child chooses what the character would do, not what they themselves would do, so the choice is a reading of the character.
- One idea: The child reads a short story about a character with clear traits and wants, and at each fork predicts what this character would really do from the clues so far; then the story shows what the character did and how that action caused the next event, so the child sees that characters' traits and motives drive the plot.
- How it plays (solo student by default):
  - First screen: Kai, who wants to win the town kite contest and hates to lose. "You know Kai a little. What will Kai do next?"
  - Core loop: read a page, collect trait and want clues in a character card (wants to win; brags; is a loyal friend). At a fork, pick the action Kai would take and point to the clue that says so. The story plays what Kai does, and a cause line links the action to what happens next. Some forks test a change: after a hard moment, Kai's feelings shift and a new trait appears.
  - The aha or the end state: the child plays one "out of character" path (what if Kai had not bragged?) and sees how the whole chain of events changes, then writes one sentence: Kai's choice to ___ caused ___.
  - Class mode or extras (optional): a second story with a quieter character.
- Standards, one line per code: RL.3.3: describing a character's traits, motivations and feelings from clues, and explaining how their actions cause the sequence of events (the cause line and the out-of-character path).
- Not like: Two Sides Theater (grade 2 point of view). Story Building Blocks (grade 3 structure). Plot Rollercoaster (grade 6 plot arc).
- Art: generated scenes (story pages) and people (illustrated) for the characters, because faces carry feelings.
- Proposed title: Stay in Character
- Proposed concept line: Read a short story and, at each fork, choose what the character would really do from the clues so far; the story then shows how that choice caused what happened next, and one out-of-character path shows the plot fall apart.
- Flags: none
