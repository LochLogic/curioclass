## How it works

Three gateways. Grades 6 to 12 have no first gateway: the read starts at Gateway 2. Grades 3 to 5 have all three.
A course must at least partly pass the earlier gateways to be read on Gateway 3. In Gateway 2, a zero on any one
indicator stops the course before Gateway 3, whatever the total. So a course cannot make up for a missing piece
(say, no research teaching) with strength elsewhere.

Points: Gateway 1 has 44 (grades 3 to 5 only), Gateway 2 has 56 (50 or more passes), Gateway 3 has 14 (12 or more
passes).

## How this differs from the math rubric

- There is no focus line like the 65 percent of major work. The door is text quality and knowledge building.
- Texts are judged before teaching is. A unit with good lessons on a weak or badly placed text fails early.
- The same skill is often two indicators: one for teaching it, one for practising it (words, sentences, the
  writing process, research, discussion). A plan must show both.
- For grades 3 to 5 the reading of words and fluency are must-pass. A grade 3 to 5 ELA course cannot be planned
  as "grade 6, easier".

## What this adds to the plan

Proposed rows for docs/ROADMAP.md, "Gaps found, and how each closes". Each is tried first on the ELA pilot:

1. A text record for every anchor: a measure, what makes it hard, and the task, in one place.
2. A question set for every anchor, with the share of questions that need the text counted.
3. A written discussion routine.
4. Each taught skill shown twice in a unit plan: where it is taught and where it is practised.
5. The year's share of informational and literary text, counted.
6. For grades 3 to 5: a foundational skills sequence (long words, word parts, fluency) before any spine.
7. The companion criteria for multilingual learners, read and summarised.
