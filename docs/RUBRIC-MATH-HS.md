## How it works

Three gateways, in order, as in K to 8: focus and coherence, then rigor and the eight practices, then supports. Any
gateway 1 indicator scored zero stops a series before gateway 3. The difference is the unit of review: the reviewer
reads the whole series (for us Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II together), and asks for each standard whether the
series as a whole covers every part of it. Each course must still carry its own share.

- **Statistics and probability:** S-ID.A.2, S-ID.C.7 and S-IC.A.1.
- **Applying grades 6 to 8 at high school level:** ratio and proportion; percents and unit conversion with compound

## Begun parts and parent codes

These conventions stand as the 2026-11-15 fix reports settled them.

- **Begun parts.** When a course teaches part of a code another course owns, it records the code family, the parts
  taught and the retained product or check that holds the evidence, and names the later owner, who completes the
  code. Ownership never changes. Begun codes are named in the frame's "What students learn" prose, never in the
  Owned standards cell; no tilde marks, and code lists use semicolons (Algebra I fixes, expert 2 and 7; Geometry
  fixes, expert 4).
- **Distributed parent codes.** A parent code whose lettered parts are taught in different units closes only when
  each part has its own named evidence; together they close the parent. F-IF.C.7 is the worked case: distributed
  family evidence closes it, and part d is assessed in Algebra II unit 2 only (Algebra II fixes, settled table).
- A check that lists a parent code says which parts it samples.

5. The rigor balance per unit, with independent work for each aspect.

7. A check that no day only reteaches grade 6 to 8 content.
8. The K to 8 support gaps (family note and supplies per unit, extensions, accommodations, reading-level supports,
   "what to do next" after each check), carried into high school.
