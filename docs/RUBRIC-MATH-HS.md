## How it works

Three gateways, in order, as in K to 8: focus and coherence, then rigor and the eight practices, then supports. Any
gateway 1 indicator scored zero stops a series before gateway 3. The difference is the unit of review: the reviewer
reads the whole series (for us Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II together), and asks for each standard whether the
series as a whole covers every part of it. Each course must still carry its own share.

## The grade 12 courses: Precalculus, Statistics and Calculus

Both sources are written for the standards without a (+) mark, so focus rows 1a to 1c have nothing to measure in
these three courses. In their place (Cory, 2026-11-17, Desk card q-electives-frames-1, L49):

- **The owned-outcome account** stands in for 1a to 1c. Every code a course owns (its (+) codes and its
  data/standards/curio-math-electives.txt outcomes) is taught in full in one named unit and assessed on that unit's
  end-of-unit assessment; the course-level checks sample codes again and never claim all of them.
- **One cap: review and readiness take at most 15 percent of unit minutes.** Counted: review days and part-days in
  the rows' load notes, retrieval tasks outside them, readiness checks (ten minutes each) and daily-opening minutes
  spent on earlier courses' rows, each minute once. Not counted: check days, respond days and time on the course's
  own earlier units. Each unit plan lists its count; the scope and sequence sums them and states the course share
  against the cap. No unit has its own share.
- **The modeling cycle stays whole:** no step (assumptions, model, computation, interpretation, validation,
  revision, report) is cut to save time.

## Multilingual learners

EdReports scores support for multilingual learners as its own report, beside the content score, but ties each
indicator to a content indicator so a reviewer can see where in the course the support is or is not. Our summary of
what it asks, for high school math:

1. **Full part in grade-level work.** For each content indicator on focus, coherence, rigor and the practices
   (1b, 2a to 2l), the student still learning English gets supports that let them take part fully in that same work,
   not a simpler task. Supports raise access while keeping the thinking demand: speaking, listening, reading and
   writing all used; help to understand the whole task (what it asks, how to take part, how to show the answer); and
   supports specific to the lesson, not one generic line at the top of a chapter. It is the largest part of the score.
2. **Language planned over time.** Language goals that grow across lessons, units and the course toward independence,
   the way content goals do, set out in a scope and sequence (its own or inside the content one), balanced across the
   four domains, spiralled with rising precision, and stated at the lesson level beside the content goal. Lesson-level
   language goals carry the most points here.
3. **Teacher guidance.** The approach and the research behind it explained; how to use the supports; drawing
   attention to how language works in math talk and writing; matching support to each student's current language
   (light, moderate or heavy, adjusted as the teacher learns more, not a fixed ladder); scaffolds that work in
   different program models; flexible grouping for fair participation; drawing on the student's home language; and
   supports offered fairly.
4. **Assessment.** Accommodations that let the student show grade-level understanding without changing what is
   assessed, with the classroom supports kept during assessments; a formative plan for language alongside content,
   tied to the lesson and unit language goals; and guidance on gathering and using that language and content
   evidence to improve teaching.

What this asks of our plans: the English learner hook stays inside each day and keeps the task's demand (criterion 1);
the unit plan states a language goal per section and the scope and sequence shows how language goals grow (criterion
2); teacher notes say how to adjust support by student and use home language (criterion 3); and each check names its
language accommodations and what language evidence the teacher reads (criterion 4).

## Begun parts and parent codes

These conventions stand as the 2026-11-15 fix reports settled them.

- **Begun parts.** When a course teaches part of a code another course owns, it records the code family, the parts
  taught and the retained product or check that holds the evidence, and names the later owner, who completes the
  code. Ownership never changes. Begun codes are named in the frame's "What students learn" prose, never in the
  Owned standards cell; no tilde marks, and code lists use semicolons (Algebra I fixes, expert 2 and 7; Geometry
  fixes, expert 4).
- **Distributed parent codes.** A parent code whose lettered parts are taught in different units closes only when
  each part has its own named evidence; together they close the parent. F-IF.C.7 is the worked case: distributed
  family evidence closes it, and part d is assessed in Algebra II unit 2 only (Algebra II fixes, settled table).
- A check that lists a parent code says which parts it samples.
