# Funding map: the larger money

Written 2026-11-27 from one wide web search (Cory asked whether a grant could push Curio Class further and faster).
PROSPECTS.md holds the small and local prospects and CONTACTS.md the people to call; this file holds the larger
routes, what gates them, and what to research or reach next. Several sources were grant aggregator sites whose dates
and amounts disagreed: check each funder's own page before acting on a line here.

## The short answer

There is real potential, at a scale well above the $10,000 philanthropy in the current plan. Two gates come before
almost all of it, and neither is about the curriculum:

1. **A legal home.** Large funders pay a nonprofit, a district or a fiscal sponsor, not an individual. The CNDC
   inquiry (CONTACTS.md, item 2) unlocks most of this file; the longer path is in LEGAL-HOME.md.
2. **Classroom evidence.** Big education funders want proof teachers used the materials with students. A small honest
   pilot (a few teachers, teacher-reported results, no student tracking) changes which doors open.

## The comparison to cite

England's **Oak National Academy**: publicly funded, openly licensed, 10,000+ lessons, an AI lesson assistant with an
automated quality checker (paper with MIT Open Learning, 2025). About GBP 43M for curriculum plus GBP 2M for AI. It
proves a government will fund this model at national scale. Curio Class is the US-shaped version: every state's
standards, free, no logins, no tracking.

## The angles (one pitch each)

- **States:** standards-aligned, free, adaptable to each state.
- **English-speaking nations:** Oak shows the model; Curio Class could be adapted to other national curricula.
- **Underserved and rural communities:** no cost, no devices required for print days, no accounts.
- **US government:** AI literacy, rural priority, "returning education to the states."
- **AI research:** a large, public test of whether AI-built curriculum with human sign-off can meet a stated bar.
- **AI public relations:** "AI built a free, quality-checked curriculum for every child."

The no-tracking promise needs care with research funders, who usually want student data. Teacher-reported evidence
squares it.
