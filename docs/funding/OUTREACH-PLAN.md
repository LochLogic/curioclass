# Outreach plan: what we know, what we do first, how we reach out

Written 2026-11-27 at Cory's request, while all outreach is on hold (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-27). Three parts: the
baseline anyone reaching out must know about Curio Class and its maker, the readiness list (what to do to be most
successful), and the path to reaching out, with tone and wording. Prospects stay in PROSPECTS.md, people in
CONTACTS.md, the legal home in LEGAL-HOME.md, budget lines in COSTS.md.

**The standing rule (Cory, 2026-11-27): Curio Class is not built yet, so no outreach puts it in a position where it is
expected to perform.** We build relationships, learn and stay visible. We don't promise pilots, results, dates, reach
numbers or deliverables until a course is finished and we choose to.

## Part 1. The baseline: what we need to know about ourselves

### What Curio Class is, in one breath

A free, open K-12 curriculum and classroom toolkit, in development: full courses (lessons, printables, checks and
interactive tools) that a teacher or parent can run from the first day to the last, with no account, no ads and no
student data. Content is CC BY 4.0 and code is MIT, so anyone may use, adapt, translate and reprint it, including a
ministry of education, without asking us.

### How it is made (the AI question comes up in every serious conversation)

AI drafts the plans, lessons and tools. Separate AI readers check each piece for teaching, accessibility, play and
sensitivity, a person reads and signs off, and nothing reaches a classroom page without that sign-off. The plain
version: "AI does the drafting and first checks; a person decides what's good enough." Some funders reject
AI-written applications (Emergent Ventures does), so applications themselves are written by Cory, with AI for
proofreading only, wherever a funder asks for that.

### What we offer, and what we don't

- **Offer:** free, open courses as they are finished; the source files; permission to adapt, translate and reprint
  under CC BY; honest notes on what's finished and what isn't; our build process if it helps others.
- **Don't offer (yet):** delivery dates, pilots with targets, a version for another country's curriculum, training,
  support contracts, user numbers, or anything that needs a team.

### What we want from a conversation (in order)

1. Advice and an honest read from people who have done this.
2. A relationship that can grow when a course is finished.
3. Only later, and only once there's a legal home: funding for finishing, adapting or translating.

## Part 2. The readiness list: what to do to be most successful

3. **Find a board and a few advisors (Cory).** The IRS form asks about governance; plan 3 or more directors with at

   include teachers who read courses.
4. **Refresh the one-pager.** ONE-PAGER.md is from 2026-10-28: tools-first, an old count, and a $10,000 ask that

5. **Write the honest status line once and reuse it.** "In development. Science for kindergarten to grade 3 is
   finished; the rest of the curriculum is planned and being written." Updated at each sign-off.
6. **Refresh COSTS.md** for the curriculum-first build (it still counts tools), and add what an adaptation for

9. **Evidence without performance.** No pilots with targets now. When a course is finished: teachers try it and

International work follows the same stages. With another country, stage 3 starts with people already working there
(the ministry's curriculum unit through its development partners or local educators), never a cold pitch to a
minister.

### Tone rules

- **Plain, warm and short.** A few contractions, one idea per paragraph, no hype, no closing salutation, no em
  dashes (the paste-ready rules).
- **Say what's finished and what isn't,** every time, in the first or second paragraph.
- **Ask small.** Advice, a conversation, a read. Never "partner with us" in a first email.
- **Partner-first with other nations.** Curio Class is a starting point local educators could adapt, if it's
  useful; they know their classrooms and their curriculum. We never "bring" a curriculum, "fix" a system or
  "solve" anything.
- **Honest about AI and about scale:** one person, AI drafting, a person signing off.
- **Credit others.** Name the open programs we learn from or adapt (Illustrative Mathematics, Oak) where it's true.

### Words to use and words to avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "in development", "finished so far", "planned" | "launching", "rolling out", "scale", "reach millions" |
| "free and open; you can adapt it without asking us" | "our product", "our platform", "license our content" |
| "I'd value your view" / "what would make this useful?" | "partnership opportunity", "we'd love to bring" |
| "a starting point your teachers could adapt" | "a solution for your schools", "transform", "close the learning gap" |
| "when a course is finished" | any date, target, pilot size or number of students |
| "AI drafts; a person signs off" | "AI-powered", "automated curriculum" |
| "we collect no student data" | "safe", "compliant", "approved" (unless true and sourced) |

## Part 4. English-speaking nations

Filled from the 2026-11-27 country research (West Africa; East and Southern Africa; the Caribbean, Pacific and Asia;
and the donors and partner organizations that fund curriculum there). Prospects and partners from it go in
PROSPECTS.md, "English-speaking nations"; this part holds only what changes the plan above.

**What changes the plan:**

1. **Go through the partners ministries already trust.** In every country researched, the ministry door opens
   through a development partner: a UNICEF education section, the GPE grant agent, or a regional body like the OECS,
   SPC EQAP or the Commonwealth of Learning. We don't cold-email ministers. Stage 1 (Listen) abroad means reading
   their plans and talking to those partners and to peers like Learning Equality.
2. **Lead with open channels, not asks.** The first international step is making Curio Class easy to find where
   ministries and their writers already look. That means a Kolibri channel and contributions to the Global Digital
   Library, African Storybook and StoryWeaver, which are all CC BY. These take no money and promise nothing, so they
   fit stage 2 (Be findable) once a band is finished.
3. **Offer source material, never "our curriculum".** The offer is: free to adapt, translate, rebrand and print under
   your own name, written by your own teachers. Ministries will own and brand anything they use. We defer to national
   curricula, local languages, local authors and local publishers. We say plainly that Curio Class is US-built,
   unfinished and untested abroad.
4. **The adaptation guide matters most here** (Part 2, item 8). Partners will also ask for a mapping to their
   curriculum (the OECS would want one to its K-6 OHPC), print-ready files, teacher guides, and examples swapped to
   local names, places, currency and metric units. Our K-3 science is the first candidate, because the OECS OHPC and
   the early-grade reforms in Sierra Leone, Uganda and Rwanda all start there.
5. **Funding comes last and through others.** Nobody funds installing an outside curriculum, and donor money goes to
   governments. If money ever comes, it will be a small adaptation grant led by a local partner, or a role as free
   source material inside a plan a ministry already has funding for. We never bid on tenders as one person, and we
   don't ask a ministry to fund development.
6. **The legal home helps here too.** Partners and UN agencies contract with registered organizations, and Kenya
   requires a local entity or agent. This supports Part 2, item 1 (form the nonprofit now).

**Shortlist for early relationships** (detail in PROSPECTS.md): the OECS Commission, Sierra Leone, Uganda, Liberia and
Rwanda, with Learning Equality (Kolibri) and the Global Digital Library as the first open channels. The Commonwealth
of Learning is a peer across all three regions.

**Sample wording, a regional body or development partner (stage 3 only):**

> Curio Class is a free, openly licensed K-12 curriculum we are writing in Colorado. Kindergarten to grade 3 science
> is finished; the rest is still being written. Everything is CC BY, so a ministry or its writers could adapt,
> translate, rebrand and print any of it under their own name. We'd like to understand how open material reaches
> your teachers today and whether anything we make could be useful raw material for that work. We're not asking for
> funding or a commitment.

**Avoid abroad:** "bring", "give", "deliver" or "roll out" a curriculum; "transform"; "proven"; "pilot in your
schools"; any count of students reached; any date.
