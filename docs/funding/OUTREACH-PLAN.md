# Outreach plan: who we are, what we do first, and every shot at funding

First written 2026-11-27 while outreach was on hold. Rebuilt the same day when Cory lifted the hold and asked for
three things: a "fund the build" track first, deliverable-focused options kept, and "a way to hit every single
stretch of a shot at funding" (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-27, "Funding goes aggressive"). Five parts: the baseline, the
readiness list, the three paths with their tone, English-speaking nations, and the queue. Prospect facts stay in
PROSPECTS.md, people in CONTACTS.md, the legal home in LEGAL-HOME.md, and budget lines in COSTS.md. Cory's bio
material is in drafts/corpus/ (git-ignored, because this repository is public).

**The standing rule (Cory, 2026-11-27, reframed): promise only what we control.** We commit to outputs we make, such
as finished courses, a build schedule we set with margin, and submitting to a review. We never promise outcomes we
don't control: student results, adoption, reach numbers, or a pilot's findings. The earlier "don't make us perform"
tone keeps its value. It now applies to outcomes, and it still governs the relationship work (path 3) and every
international conversation.

**Tailoring (Cory, 2026-11-27): "omission isnt lying, its tailoring."** Every application leads with what that
funder values and leaves out what doesn't help it. The one line we hold: nothing we write says or implies something
untrue (for example "nonprofit" before filing, "piloted" before a pilot, "used in schools" before it is).

## Part 1. The baseline: what we need to know about ourselves

### What Curio Class is, in one breath

A free, open K-12 curriculum and classroom toolkit, in development: full courses (lessons, printables, checks and
interactive tools) that a teacher or parent can run from the first day to the last, with no account, no ads and no
student data. Content is CC BY 4.0 and code is MIT, so anyone may use, adapt, translate and reprint it, including a
ministry of education, without asking us.

### How it is made (the AI question comes up in every serious conversation)

AI drafts the plans, lessons and tools. Separate AI readers check each piece for teaching, accessibility, play and
sensitivity, a person reads and signs off, and nothing reaches a classroom page without that sign-off. The plain
version: "AI does the drafting and first checks; a person decides what's good enough." For a build funder this is a
strength: it is why one person can finish 118 courses at a cost no publisher can match. Some funders reject
AI-written applications (Emergent Ventures does), so for those Cory writes the application and AI only proofreads.

### What we offer, by path

- **Build funders (path 1):** a named piece of work finished with their money, on a schedule we set with margin, plus
  a short public report of what it bought. Everything stays free and openly licensed.
- **Deliverable funders, reviewers and buyers (path 2):** the finished course or band as the deliverable: complete,
  printable, free, built to the state review bar, and submitted for review when they ask. An evidence plan that
  measures and shares what teachers find. No promised student gains.
- **Partners and peers (path 3):** the open files, permission to adapt and rebrand, honest notes on what's finished,
  and our build process if it helps them.
- **Never offered:** promised student outcomes, user counts, training or support contracts, or anything that needs a
  team we don't have.

## Part 3. Three paths, each with its own tone

Every prospect sits on at least one path, and many sit on two. The queue (Part 5) works all three at once.

### Path 1. Fund the build (the lead path, open now)

**Who:** funders who pay for making something: individual grants (Emergent Ventures, Awesome Foundation),
open-source funds (FLOSS/fund, GitHub Secure Open Source Fund, Mozilla Incubator), compute and in-kind credits (AWS,
Claude for Open Source), small family and community foundations, service clubs, local donors, the "someone who
wouldn't think twice" donor, and any funder that takes cold proposals (Stand Together, after the sponsor).

**The ask:** a named amount for named work. "$5,000 is a year of AI compute; $16,000 is the whole year-one build."
After the COSTS refresh: "$X finishes K-5 science and ELA." We show what's already finished as the proof that the
money turns into courses.

**Tone:** a confident builder. Concrete line items, finished work linked, a build schedule we set with margin.
Short and direct; no hedging about being early, because early is the point of a build grant.

> Curio Class (curioclass.org) is a free, open K-12 curriculum: full courses with lessons, printables and offline
> tools, no accounts and no student data. Kindergarten to grade 3 science is finished and free to use today, and all
> 118 courses have a full-year plan. I build it with AI drafting and a person signing off every page, which is why
> it costs a fraction of a commercial curriculum. $[amount] pays for [named work] by [date we set]. Everything stays
> free and openly licensed, and I'll publish what the money bought.

### Path 2. Fund the deliverable (the end product as the focus)

**Who:** funders, reviewers and buyers who care about the finished thing: state review and adoption lists
(Louisiana, Indiana, Oklahoma, Oregon, Texas, Virginia, Tennessee), EdReports, Cambridge endorsement, national
listings abroad (India's DIKSHA, Singapore's SLS, BC's Focused Education), state OER funds (Washington OSPI,
Wisconsin WISELearn through a district), research and development money (NSF STEM K-12 with a university lead or
the LLC, IES, EIR and FIPSE when they return), prizes (Yidan, WISE, Reimagine Education, Tällberg nomination, Tools
Competition), Accelerate, and ministries through their development partners.

**The reframe:** here the deliverable is the focus, and that's fine, because a deliverable is an output we control.
We name the finished course or band, show it, and commit to finishing the next one on our own schedule. Where a
funder asks for outcomes, we offer an evidence plan (what we'll measure and share, through teachers, with no student
data), never a promised result. Where a funder wants proof of impact at scale (Yidan, the Tools Competition data
rules), we say what's true now and enter only when the facts fit.

**Tone:** a careful publisher. Specific about scope, alignment and quality; precise about what's finished; honest
that outside review hasn't happened yet. Lead with the reviewers' own criteria (our ADOPTION-BAR mirrors them).

> [Band] of Curio Class is finished: [N] days of lessons, printables and checks, aligned to [standards], free under
> CC BY, and built to the same criteria your reviewers use. We'd like to submit it for [review / listing]. The rest
> of the curriculum is written to the same bar, and the next band, [name], is scheduled for [date we set].

### Path 3. Relationships and visibility (the periphery, kept)

**Who:** peers (Oak National Academy, African Storybook, Siyavula, Learning Equality), researchers, teachers, state
OER offices, development partners abroad, plus no-obligation listings and sign-ups (OER Commons, MERLOT, the state
OER hubs, the UNESCO OER Dynamic Coalition, the White House pledge, Kiwix, the Digital Public Goods registry).

**Why it stays:** this is where warm introductions, advisors, co-applicants and the university lead for NSF come
from, and where funders first hear the name. It feeds paths 1 and 2. It keeps the listening tone of the first plan:
ask small, promise nothing, learn.

**Tone:** warm, curious, plain. Say what's finished. Ask for a view, never a partnership in a first email.

> I'm Cory, and I'm building Curio Class (curioclass.org), a free, open K-12 curriculum: science for kindergarten to
> grade 3 is finished, and the rest is planned and being written. It's CC BY, so anyone can adapt it. You've done
> what I'm trying to learn, and I'd value 20 minutes of your honest view on what you wish you'd known early.

### Tone rules for all three paths

- **Hit hard (Cory, 2026-11-27).** Open with the size of the ambition and the size of the work. The ambition is
  global and fully philanthropic: a complete K-12 curriculum, free forever, built to reach millions of children in
  every country that wants it. State it as the goal we're building toward, then back it with hard numbers from
  SCALE.md (billions of AI tokens processed, thousands of lesson days, 118 full-year courses, every page signed off
  by a person). Facts stay true and dated; the goal is labeled a goal.
- **Never self-disqualify.** Don't tell a funder why we might not fit (age, size, missing thresholds). Make the
  case for why they should back us. If a form asks directly, answer the question truthfully and briefly, then
  turn to the case.
- **Plain, warm and clear.** A few contractions, one idea per paragraph, no closing salutation, no em dashes (the
  paste-ready rules).
- **Say what's finished and what isn't** in the first or second paragraph.
- **Promise outputs, never outcomes.** Dates are ours to set; give margin.
- **Tailor every send** to what that reader values (the tailoring rule above).
- **Partner-first with other nations** (Part 4). We never "bring" a curriculum, "fix" a system or "solve" anything.
- **Honest about AI and about scale:** one person, AI drafting, a person signing off.
- **Credit others.** Name the open programs we learn from or adapt where it's true.

### Words to use and words to avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "finished", "in development", "planned", "scheduled for [date we set]"; "built to reach millions of children worldwide" (a goal) | "launching", "rolling out", "reaches millions" or "used by" (as a fact, until counted) |
| "free and open; you can adapt it without asking us" | "our product", "our platform", "license our content" |
| "$X finishes [named work]" | "help us grow", "support our mission" with no named work |
| "built to the criteria your reviewers use" | "meets", "approved", "endorsed" (until true) |
| "what we'll measure and share" | "will raise scores", "close the learning gap", "proven" |
| "AI drafts; a person signs off" | "AI-powered", "automated curriculum" |
| "we collect no student data" | "safe", "compliant" (unless true and sourced) |

### Hard questions, short answers

| They ask | We say |
|---|---|

| How many users? | We don't count users; no logins and no tracking, by design. We can share teacher notes |
| Is it AI-made? | AI drafts and does first checks; a person reads and signs off every piece |
| Who reviews it? | Our written review stages, the maker's sign-off and [advisors, once named]; outside review is the next step |
| Why free? | Teachers shouldn't have to pay or hand over student data for good material |
| What will our money buy? | [Named work], by [date], with a public note when it's done |
| What if you stop? | It's all open and public; anyone can carry it on |

| Are you a nonprofit? | Fiscally sponsored by [CNDC] (true only once signed); our own nonprofit is being formed (true only once filed) |

## Part 4. English-speaking nations

From the 2026-11-27 country research. Prospects and partners are in PROSPECTS.md, "English-speaking nations"; this
part holds only what changes the plan.

1. **Go through the partners ministries already trust.** In every country researched, the ministry door opens
   through a development partner: a UNICEF education section, the GPE grant agent, or a regional body like the OECS,
   SPC EQAP or the Commonwealth of Learning. We don't cold-email ministers.
2. **Lead with open channels.** A Kolibri channel and contributions to the Global Digital Library, African
   Storybook and StoryWeaver (all CC BY). These cost nothing, promise nothing, and put us where ministries' writers
   look.
3. **Offer source material, never "our curriculum".** Free to adapt, translate, rebrand and print under their own
   name, written by their own teachers. We defer to national curricula, local languages, authors and publishers,
   and say plainly that Curio Class is US-built, unfinished and untested abroad.
4. **The adaptation guide matters most here** (Part 2, item 8): curriculum mapping (the OECS would want one to its
   K-6 OHPC), print-ready files, teacher guides, local names, places, currency and metric units. K-3 science is the
   first candidate.
5. **Money comes through others** (path 2 abroad): nobody funds installing an outside curriculum, and donor money goes
   to governments. The realistic money is a small adaptation grant led by a local partner, or a role as free source
   material in a plan a ministry already has funding for. We never bid on tenders as one person.
6. **A registered organization helps here too.** Partners and UN agencies contract with registered organizations.

**Shortlist:** the OECS Commission, Sierra Leone, Uganda, Liberia and Rwanda, with Learning Equality (Kolibri) and the
Global Digital Library as the first open channels. The Commonwealth of Learning is a peer across all three regions.

> Curio Class is a free, openly licensed K-12 curriculum we are writing in Colorado. Kindergarten to grade 3 science
> is finished; the rest is still being written. Everything is CC BY, so a ministry or its writers could adapt,
> translate, rebrand and print any of it under their own name. We'd like to understand how open material reaches
> your teachers today and whether anything we make could be useful raw material for that work.

**Avoid abroad:** "bring", "give", "deliver" or "roll out" a curriculum; "transform"; "proven"; "pilot in your
schools"; any count of students reached.
