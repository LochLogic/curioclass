# A legal home: fiscal sponsor, nonprofit, and the LLC

Researched 2026-11-27 on primary pages (irs.gov, sos.state.co.us, tax.colorado.gov, sam.gov, nsf.gov, ies.ed.gov) after
Cory asked about forming a nonprofit and whether LeMay Ventures LLC can help. This is information, not legal advice:
items marked **CPA** or **attorney** need a professional before acting. The choice of path is on the Review Desk
(q-funding-legal-home-1).

## The short answer

Use CNDC as the bridge now, form a Colorado nonprofit in parallel, and keep Curio Class out of LeMay Ventures LLC. The
LLC helps only where a for-profit is the applicant on purpose (AWS credits; SBIR if a paid product line ever exists).

## Forming a Colorado nonprofit with 501(c)(3) status

| Step | Government fee | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Articles of incorporation, Colorado SOS (purpose and dissolution clauses written to IRS standards) | $50 | Same day online | https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/info_center/fees/business.html |
| Registered agent (Cory, at a Colorado street address) | $0 | Same day | same |
| Board, bylaws, conflict-of-interest policy (IRS sample in the Form 1023 instructions, Appendix A) | $0 | 1 to 4 weeks | https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023 |
| EIN | $0 | Minutes | https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number |
| Form 1023 (likely) or 1023-EZ | $600 or $275 | 80% decided within 191 days (1023) or 22 days (EZ) | https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/form-1023-and-1023-ez-amount-of-user-fee, https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/wheres-my-application-for-tax-exempt-status |
| Colorado charitable solicitation registration (before soliciting, unless exempt) | $10, then $10 a year | Up to 10 days | https://www.sos.state.co.us/pubs/info_center/fees/charitable.html |
| Colorado sales tax exemption (DR 0715, needs the IRS letter) | $0 | After the determination letter | https://tax.colorado.gov/DR0715 |
| Colorado periodic report | $25 a year | Yearly | SOS fee schedule |
| Full SAM.gov registration (needed for any federal award; a UEI alone cannot apply) | $0, renew yearly | About 10 business days | https://sam.gov/content/entity-registration |

**Total: about $685 in government fees with Form 1023, plus about $35 a year; about 7 to 8 months to a determination
letter.** Notes:

- **Board.** Colorado allows one director (C.R.S. 7-128-103; confirm on leg.colorado.gov), but the IRS asks about
  insider deals, and any deal with a business a director controls over 35 percent (LeMay Ventures) needs a vote by
  directors without a conflict. Plan on 3 or more directors, at least 2 independent.
- **Form 1023, not the EZ.** The EZ needs receipts at or under $50,000 in each of the next 3 years, which the grant
  goals would pass, and it bars LLCs and successors to a for-profit.
- **27-month rule.** File within 27 months of incorporating and the exemption reaches back to the incorporation date.
- **Public charity.** Curio Class would claim publicly supported status (509(a)(1), 170(b)(1)(A)(vi)): a third of
  support from the public or government over 5 years. **CPA** to confirm.
- **Annual filing.** Form 990-N while gross receipts are normally $50,000 or less; 990-EZ under $200,000.
- **Solicitation exemption.** C.R.S. 6-16-104(6)(c) exempts organizations under $25,000 a year (government and
  501(c)(3) grants not counted) with no paid solicitor; its wording is ambiguous. **Attorney** or the SOS Charities
  Program to confirm, including whether a CNDC-sponsored project registers itself.

## Fiscal sponsor, own nonprofit, or both

| Path | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| CNDC only | A 501(c)(3) umbrella right away; no board to build; compliance handled | About 10 percent fee (14 on government money); CNDC controls the funds; its Colorado-focus rule |
| Own nonprofit only | Full control; can register in SAM and apply directly | About 7 months to the letter; needs an independent board and yearly filings |
| **Both, in sequence (recommended)** | Money can flow now; the nonprofit takes over once its letter arrives | Ask CNDC for its spin-off terms in writing first |

## What LeMay Ventures LLC can and cannot do

| Route | LLC eligible? | Amount | When | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NSF STEM K-12 (25-545) | Yes, "U.S.-based commercial organizations, including small businesses" | $350k to $750k R&D | Rolling | https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/stem-k-12-nsf-stem-k-12/nsf25-545/solicitation |
| AWS Education Equity Initiative | Yes, "for-profit social enterprises" | Up to $10k AWS credits | Rolling | https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/our-impact/education-equity-initiative/ |
| IES From Seedlings to Scale (84.305J) | Yes | About 12 awards | 2026-12-11; weak fit, letter of intent date passed | https://ies.ed.gov/funding/grants/2027/seedlings-scale-grants-program-84-305j |
| NSF SBIR/STTR (26-510), Learning and Cognition Technologies | Small businesses only; the PI must be employed 51 percent or more by the company | Phase I up to $305k | Invited pitch first; deadlines 2027-03-04, 2027-07-07 | https://seedfund.nsf.gov/topics/learning-cognition-technologies/ |
| ED/IES SBIR FY2027 | Small businesses | FY2026: $250k Phase I, $1M Phase II | Release expected late 2026 or early 2027 | https://ies.ed.gov/funding/research/programs/small-business-innovation-research-sbir/solicitation-information |

SBIR scores "commercial potential", so a free CC BY and MIT product would need a real paid line (district services,
hosting, training) to compete: a weak fit today. The LLC **cannot** take tax-deductible gifts, and most foundations
will not grant to it (a private foundation grant to a for-profit needs "expenditure responsibility", legal but rare).
Its UEI (N6WPEQQNUSP5) is not full SAM registration.

**Structures checked:**

1. **LLC as a paid vendor to the nonprofit:** allowed, but the LLC is a "disqualified person" under the excess benefit
   rules (IRC 4958): fair market value, comparability data, a vote by directors without a conflict, and disclosure on
   Form 1023 Part V. https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/intermediate-sanctions
2. **A new single-member LLC for Curio Class, wholly owned by the nonprofit later:** gifts to it count as gifts to the
   charity (IRS Notice 2012-52). Useful for liability or a brand, only once the nonprofit exists.
   https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-12-52.pdf
3. **Converting LeMay Ventures LLC:** not recommended. An LLC can be a 501(c)(3) only if every member is a 501(c)(3) or
   a government (Notice 2021-56), and conversion would drag in the consulting business and make the nonprofit a
   for-profit successor. https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-21-56.pdf
4. **L3C or nonprofit LLC:** Colorado's SOS has no L3C form; a public benefit corporation is for-profit and cannot take
   deductible gifts. **Attorney** to confirm.
5. **The Curio Class name, domain and work:** whoever owns them today (Cory personally, if built outside the LLC:
   **attorney**) assigns them to the nonprofit in writing, approved by the independent board. CC BY and MIT already let
   anyone use the work, so the transfer matters most for the name, the domain and future grant-funded work. Don't route
   it through LeMay Ventures.

**Why keep them apart:** consulting liability could reach Curio Class assets; one SAM registration would mix both
businesses; federal audit rules would cover the whole LLC; and the LLC sells to governments Curio Class may also
approach (a conflict to disclose).
