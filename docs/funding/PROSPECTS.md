# Funding prospects

Research pass 2026-10-28. Every entry was found through web search, often on aggregator sites; confirm the program,
amount and deadline on the funder's own page before writing to them. Status column: who has been contacted and when.

## What the research says first

1. **Most big foundations do not take cold emails.** Gates, Lumina and the Horace W. Goldsmith Foundation say they
   do not accept unsolicited proposals or letters of inquiry; Walton's status is mixed (sources disagree). A cold email
   to them will not get read. They are reached through invited programs or warm introductions.
2. **Most grants need a 501(c)(3) or a fiscal sponsor.** A fiscal sponsor (an existing nonprofit that holds the money
   for a fee of about 5 to 15 percent) unlocks most of the list below. Setting one up is step one.
3. **The fastest money goes to individuals:** a few programs fund a person and an idea directly, with short forms
   and quick answers. Start there.
4. **"Who uses it" is the first question.** Before the bigger asks, collect proof of reach without tracking:
   Cloudflare's request counts and a handful of teacher quotes.

## The "someone who wouldn't think twice" donor

That donor exists, but the research is clear that they are reached through people, not cold email. Routes:

- **Warm introductions:** family teachers, school administrators they know, the derby community, LinkedIn
  connections in tech. One forwarded email from someone they trust beats a hundred cold ones.
- **Show, don't tell:** the strongest pitch is a link to one great tool plus the bench page showing the 721 to go.
- **Public posts** where tech philanthropists read (a Show HN style launch post, education subreddits) create the
  inbound version of the same thing.
