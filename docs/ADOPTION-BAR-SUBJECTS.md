# The adoption bar for the other subjects

**Status:** second draft, 2026-11-11. Revised once after the curriculum expert's cold read
(docs/reviews/bar-subjects-2026-11-11.md). Not yet read by Cory. Nothing here is a rule until he has read it.

**What this page is for.** Every one of the 105 courses is to be finished to the adoption bar (docs/FOUNDATION.md,
section 4; docs/ADOPTION-BAR.md). Math has a rubric file; English language arts and science rubrics are drafted
separately. This page writes the bar for every other subject: one shared core, then a short part per subject.

**What we may never say.** That Curio Class was reviewed by, meets, or is endorsed by any body named here. This is a
bar we hold ourselves to. Most instruments are all rights reserved, so nothing is quoted. Every criterion is in our
own words.

**What this page is not.** An approach page. Layer 3 asks for one per subject before a spine, and none exists (see
"Before a spine in this subject"). A subject's part moves to its own docs/RUBRIC-*.md file once it is firm.

## The pass rule

**Proposed, waiting for Cory's yes.** No course has been read against it yet, so it may prove too hard or too easy.
It copies the shape of the instruments read: alignment, accuracy and feasibility are a door that stops a review, and
the rest is scored.

- **The door: C1, C2, C3 and C14, each passed in full.** A course that fails one is not read further until fixed.
- **C4 to C13, C15, C16:** each is marked pass, part pass or fail. A finished course has no fail and no more than
  three part passes.
- **Pass:** no "fails if" condition is found in the sample. **Part pass:** a condition is found in one sampled unit
  only, and the fix is local. **Fail:** it is found in two or more units, or is built into the course's design.
- **The sample.** The reviewer reads the whole spine and coverage table, then three whole units picked by the
  reviewer, never by the builder, one from each third of the year: every lesson, page, tool and check in them. A
  course of four units or fewer is read whole. Below, "three units" means this sample.
- The subject part is read the same way: no criterion failed, and the part pass count above covers core and subject
  criteria together.
- **Beyond the three units,** the reviewer reads: for C1, the whole coverage table, tracing ten standards of the
  reviewer's choice to lesson and check item; for C4, the progression page; for C8 and C9, one cumulative check;
  for C13, the approach page; for C3 and C16, all public copy for the course and the logic model.

**Where our promises cost us marks, by choice.** Curio Class will not build some things rubrics ask for
(FOUNDATION, section 3): data dashboards (Texas names one as an example), adapting to each student and plugging
into a learning management system (Ohio, narrative items), accessible-format files lodged with a national centre
(Oregon), and professional learning tied to the materials (Ohio, scored). Our answers are the class record sheet,
score codes, open pages, print and teacher pages.

**Where the instruments conflict on values.** Oklahoma's arts rubric checks materials against state law. Maryland,
the federal health tool and the SEL registry ask for equity content in terms that pull the other way. No stance
passes all of them. C12 follows FOUNDATION promise 7, a middle line, and Curio Class does not claim to satisfy any
state's values criteria. Said once here, so it is not re-opened per course (open choice 5).

**Acceptability is local.** A national site cannot settle what suits each community, so each course page says what
a school may leave out (open choice 3).

## The subject parts

Each part adds to the core. Unless a part says otherwise, it covers four band courses. Tags: **[I]** from the instrument, **[S]** from the standards, **[O]** ours. Full
titles, links, licences and how much was read are in the sources table. "Fails if" lines work as in the core.

### 1. Social studies

Fourteen courses, kindergarten to grade 12, including the named high school courses.

**Rests on.** EdReports and AIR criteria (2024) and Louisiana's tool (2026-27), read in full by the writer;
Maryland's rubric (2025), read in full by the expert; Massachusetts read in part.

1. [I] Inquiry and knowledge together: units are framed by questions and big ideas, and content is taught directly
   and in order so facts, ideas and words pile up. History runs in time order unless the spine gives a reason.
2. [I] Students work with real sources, primary and secondary, at a reading level right for the grade. Each source
   has a stated difficulty and a reason for its place in the year.
3. [I] Where an event has more than one account, the sources carry more than one.
4. [I] The discipline's skills are taught, not assumed: who made a source and why, its setting, checking one source
   against another, order in time, cause and effect, reading a map or data table, weighing cost against benefit.
5. [I] Students write and talk from evidence: claim, evidence, reasoning, and the other side's claim. Writing is
   taught directly. Short research tasks recur.
6. [I] Subject words are taught and used.
7. [I] Checks test knowledge and skills together and include a task done from sources.
8. [I, in two of three instruments] Students apply what they concluded to a real problem near them. Maryland puts
   this in the category that must be met; Louisiana does not ask it. How often is open choice 6.

**Fails if:** coverage without depth; sources are textbook summaries only; one telling of a contested event;
questions answerable without the sources; skills practised on content the course does not teach.

**Unsupported.** Content is left to states. Economics, geography and psychology have no rubric of their own.
Maryland's value-laden items are left out on purpose (see the values paragraph).

**Standards.** data/standards/c3-indicators.txt (2013) is skills only. No content standard is loaded, and
psychology cites a standard with no file.

**Spine states:** the content standard and each unit's question. **Unit plan fills:** the source list
with reasons. **Confidence:** likely.

### 2. Arts: visual arts, music, theater, dance, media arts

Twenty courses: five disciplines, four bands.

**Rests on.** Texas fine arts rubric (2025) and California's five categories, read in full by the writer;
Oklahoma's rubric (2024; four gateways, the fourth on state law), read in full by the expert.

1. [I] All four artistic processes are in each course: create; perform, present or produce; respond; connect.
2. [I] Technique is taught directly, in steps, and gets harder.
3. [I] The discipline's words are taught and used in making and responding.
4. [I] Students make, adapt or extend their own work and show it, with some choice in what they make or study.
5. [I] Students are taught to read, judge and respond to other artists' work.
6. [I] Students give and get feedback, and reflect to refine their work.
7. [I] Works and traditions come from many times and cultures, with history and setting taught.
8. [I] Assessment ranges across the year: performances and finished works scored with a guide, self-assessment, and
   work kept over time.
9. [I] Pacing fits real arts schedules (rotations, short weeks). Lessons list equipment and safe use of space.

**Fails if:** prompts to express with no technique taught; no performing or showing; response work without the
discipline's words; one culture's canon; a plan that needs a daily period.

**Unsupported.** No rubric treats the five disciplines apart or says how to choose works. A screen cannot teach a
performing art, so the performing is in the room.

**Standards.** data/standards/ncas-performance-standards.txt (2014; a national revision is under way, no date). The
standards are by grade to grade 8; our courses are bands.

**Spine states:** which grade's standards fall in which year of the band. **Unit plan fills:** the
works studied and the showing. **Confidence:** likely for the instruments, thin for the evidence.

### 3. Health

**Rests on.** The federal Health Education Curriculum Analysis Tool, 2021. The writer read the overview and the
first halves of chapters 4 and 5; the expert read both chapters in full. Nobody has read the topic modules, where
coverage is scored.

1. [I] Each unit names the healthy behaviours it is for, and every lesson ties to one.
2. [I] Facts are basic, accurate and useful for a decision. Facts alone fail.
3. [I] Each skill is taught in five steps: why it matters, the steps, a model, practice on real situations,
   feedback.
4. [I] Lessons work on beliefs, on what peers really do, and on social pressure.
5. [I] Content fits the age; skills come back across topics and bands; a topic gets enough time. A one-off lesson
   fails.
6. [I] Units link students to family and trusted adults, and learning reaches past the classroom.
7. [I] The health, medical and scientific content is checked by a named person able to judge it, with the date. A
   health curriculum specialist counts. An agent's read does not.
8. [I] Objectives cover knowing, valuing and doing, and can be measured. Assessment is of more than one kind and
   includes students assessing themselves.
9. [I] Materials are inclusive: they fit students' cultures and the conditions they live in, avoid blame, and guide
   the teacher on their own bias.
10. [I, modules unread] A band coverage map shows which topics and healthy behaviour outcomes in
    data/standards/hecat-topics.txt each band teaches, and where.

**Fails if:** scare tactics; fact recall as the end check; skills named and never rehearsed; outdated figures; no
coverage map; a sensitive topic taught with no note on what a school may leave out.

**Unsupported.** The tool counts features and does not show a curriculum works. It assumes a teacher leads, in a
classroom or online. No instrument says which sensitive topics a national site carries (open choice 3).

**Standards.** data/standards/nhes-2024.txt (SHAPE America, 2024). The tool is keyed to the 2007 edition. Skills map
across; the first standard was reworded.

**Spine states:** the coverage map. **Unit plan fills:** the behaviour outcome and the skill steps.
**Confidence:** likely, until the writer has read the second half of chapter 5 and one topic module.

### 4. Physical education

**Rests on.** The federal Physical Education Curriculum Analysis Tool, 2019 (preliminary analyses and directions
read; grade tables not read) and Oregon's 2025 criteria, read in full by the writer only.

1. [I, federal tool] For every standard in every grade of the band there is a stated outcome, content that teaches
   it, and an assessment of it.
2. [I, Oregon] Motor skills are built in order across grades.
3. [O] Lessons are planned so most of the class is moving most of the time.
4. [I] Fitness and safety content is accurate.
5. [I, Oregon] Lessons work in a gym, a field or a small room, and adapt for weather, class size and little
   equipment.
6. [I, Oregon] Every lesson has an adaptation for disabilities and for a range of skill.
7. [I, Oregon] Assessment is of skill, knowledge and conduct, and reports growth against the student's own earlier
   work, not rank.
8. [O, from memory] No exercise as punishment and no elimination games. Oregon requires the profession's
   appropriate practice guidelines; nobody here has read them.

**Fails if:** a year of games with no outcomes; long lines and waiting. Ours: grading on dress or attendance;
fitness tests used as grades.

**Unsupported.** A screen cannot give movement time. An open site honestly offers plans, task cards, assessments
and knowledge content.

**Standards.** data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt (four standards). The federal tool is keyed to the earlier five. The
method carries over; the tables do not.

**Spine states:** the outcome for each standard by grade. **Unit plan fills:** space, equipment and
the adaptation. **Confidence:** likely.

### 5. Computer science

**Rests on.** Ohio's rubric (April 2025): gateway 1 read in full by the writer, all 24 pages by the expert.

1. [S] Coverage spans every concept area, not programming alone: systems, data, algorithms, society.
2. [S] The practices (working together, testing and fixing, abstraction, explaining) are taught inside the content.
3. [I] Programming ideas are ordered, and each band says what the one before taught.
4. [I] Students meet real, current uses and cases of computing.
5. [O] Every unit has unplugged work. Ohio asks about offline versions and gives them no points.
6. [I] Technology is built into the learning, not added on: each tool has a purpose in the lesson, gives feedback,
   and does not distract. This is where Ohio's first gateway puts most of its points.
7. [I] Tools run in a common browser on school devices, with notes for technical staff. [O] Nothing to install.
8. [I] Assessment includes projects and code, with rubrics.
9. [I] Teacher pages assume a teacher not trained in the subject and explain the content at adult level.

**Fails if:** block puzzles with no concepts named; nothing on data or society; a tool that needs an install or an
account; no help for the non-specialist.

**Unsupported.** No national materials rubric. No page may claim coding improves general thinking.

**Standards.** data/standards/csta-2026-ids.txt: the 196 foundational codes of the 2026 standards, with no header and
no wording. The 135 high school specialty standards are absent.

**Spine states:** the concept areas per unit. **Unit plan fills:** the unplugged task.
**Confidence:** likely.

### 6. World languages: Spanish, American Sign Language

Two courses today, each "all levels".

**Rests on.** Texas languages rubric (2026), all 22 pages read by the writer only. It covers American Sign Language.

1. [I] Each level names a target proficiency level, and objectives are written as what the learner can do.
2. [I] Function, context, text type and control get harder in a stated order, and earlier ones come back.
3. [I] Units are themes from real life. Words and grammar serve a communicative task, not lists.
4. [I] Students take in and produce the target language almost all the time, with input they can understand and
   supports that fade.
5. [I] Authentic texts from several communities carry products, practices and perspectives.
6. [I] Each mode (interpretive, interpersonal, presentational) is taught, practised in every unit and assessed by
   performance. Each unit ends with one linked task across the three.
7. [I] Interpersonal tasks have a real information gap. A scripted dialogue does not count.
8. [I] Supports hold the communicative goal steady, and heritage speakers have a path.
9. [I] American Sign Language: handshape, location, movement, palm orientation, non-manual markers, use of space
   and classifiers, each taught directly and practised in every unit. [O] Materials are video.

**Fails if:** word lists and conjugation drills; instructions in English; textbook-made texts only; no unrehearsed
two-way task; one course for all levels.

**Unsupported.** A no-account site cannot give two-way practice, so it happens between people in the room.

**Standards.** data/standards/actfl-wrs.txt (2015 standards, 2024 level names). The 2026 can-do statements and any
American Sign Language standards are not loaded.

**Spine states:** the target level. **Unit plan fills:** the three-mode end task.
**Confidence:** likely.

### 7. Career and technical education. Ours in part, untested

Career Exploration (grades 6 to 8), Career Pathways (grades 9 to 12).

**Rests on.** Texas career and technical rubric (2025), all 7 pages read by the writer only. It is the one materials
rubric found. *To firm it:* a second state's rubric, and the 2024 career-ready practices wording.

1. [I] Knowledge and skills are tied to a named field, taught directly, and grow harder.
2. [I] Academic, technical and employability skills are taught together.
3. [I] Students study career clusters and programs of study with real labour market figures.
4. [I] Students build their own plan: interests, goals, the training each path needs.
5. [I] Students practise career tasks: a resume, an interview, a portfolio.
6. [I] Learning is hands-on: labs, simulations or projects with the field's own texts and tools, safety taught.
7. [I] Students solve real problems from a field and defend a choice with data.
8. [I] Assessment includes demonstrations and a portfolio.

**Fails if:** career quizzes with no plan made; no real data; no applied task; employability as a poster.

**Unsupported.** Quality lies mostly outside materials (equipment, employers, work-based learning). The course page
says what a school adds.

**Standards.** data/standards/cte-career-clusters-2024.txt: cluster names and twelve practices by number only. It
fits exploration courses and would not fit a technical one.

**Spine states:** the fields studied. **Unit plan fills:** the applied task and its data source.
**Confidence:** thin.

### 8. Personal finance, grades 9 to 12

**Rests on.** The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's review tool (2015), read in part by the writer only.

1. [S] All six topic areas of the 2021 standards are covered at grade 12 level.
2. [I] Lessons end in decisions and skills practised on real choices.
3. [I] Figures, rules and rates are current, each with a date and a source.
4. [I] Opinion is marked, and choices are shown with more than one reasonable answer.
5. [I] No branded product or provider; authors and funders named.
6. [I] People of varied ages, incomes and households are shown without judgement.
7. [I] A teacher with no finance training can teach it with modest preparation.
8. [O] The course page states that the course has not been studied.

**Fails if:** stale rates; a sponsor's logo; advice presented as fact; vocabulary tests as the end check.

**Unsupported.** The bar for this course is the design side. The evidence side needs studies of the course itself
and is stated as unmet on the course page. No page may promise changed behaviour.

**Standards.** data/standards/nspfe-2021.txt. The tool's content lists predate it, so criterion 1 is checked against
the file.

**Spine states:** the topic per unit. **Unit plan fills:** the decision practised.
**Confidence:** likely.

### 9. Social and emotional learning

**Rests on.** The SEL registry's criteria paper (2020): design criteria read in full by the writer and the expert.

1. [I] All five competency areas are covered.
2. [I] There is a sequence for each grade, or clear guidance for running a band over several years. One lesson set
   for a whole band earns the registry's lower design rating.
3. [I] It is for every student, in school hours.
4. [I] Sequenced: activities connect and build.
5. [I] Active: managing oneself and getting on with others are practised by doing, such as acting out a hard
   moment or rehearsing a calming habit. The other areas are practised by reflection or talk.
6. [I] Focused and explicit: set time, and each lesson names its skill.
7. [I] Guidance for the adult, and a way to carry the skill beyond the lesson.
8. [O] The course page states that no study stands behind the course.

**Fails if:** posters and discussion with no practice; two of five areas; a band taught the same way every year.

**Unsupported.** The registry tests evidence first. The bar for these courses is the design half; the evidence half
is stated as unmet on the course page.

**Standards.** data/standards/casel-5.txt, the same framework.

**Spine states:** the year-by-year sequence. **Unit plan fills:** the practice by doing.
**Confidence:** likely for design.

### 10. English language development. Ours in part, untested

Supports inside other courses are C11.

**Rests on.** California's criteria map for specialized ELD, grades 4 to 8, skimmed by search; the WIDA PRIME web
page. *To firm it:* read the California map end to end and open the WIDA rubric file.

1. [I] Every lesson is tied to a named language standard.
2. [I] Language is taught through grade-level content and texts, with scaffolds, not simplified texts.
3. [I] Forms of English are taught directly: words, sentence structure, word parts, how texts are built.
4. [O] Each unit plans structured talk, with frames.
5. [I] Work is set at more than one proficiency level, with notes for newcomers and long-term English learners.
6. [O] The home language is used as a help.
7. [I] Progress checks are tied to the language standard, with more than one point of entry and exit.
8. [O] The course is for a protected period and does not stand in for English language arts.

**Fails if:** word lists without content; one level for all; no speaking; ELD used as remedial reading.

**Unsupported.** No national scored rubric exists. Placement needs a proficiency score, which the school supplies.

**Standards.** data/standards/wida-2020.txt. California's criteria are keyed to its own ELD standards.

**Spine states:** entry and exit points. **Unit plan fills:** the levels and the talk routine.
**Confidence:** thin.

### 11. Library and research skills. Ours, untested

**Rests on.** No materials instrument exists. Built from the Empire State continuum (introduction page only), the
national school library standards (not re-opened) and one trial of lateral reading known through a press notice.
*To firm it:* open the continuum's grade lists and the trial itself.

1. [O] An inquiry cycle is taught in every band: ask, find, judge, use, share, reflect.
2. [O] Each grade has named priority skills, each with a check.
3. [O, one trial] Judging a source is taught as leaving the page to see what others say about it, not as a

4. [O] Skills are practised on real tasks from the grade's other courses, named in the spine.
5. [O] Citing, fair use and honest use of others' work are taught at each band.
6. [O] Students make a product from their research and share it.

**Fails if:** a one-off orientation; a website checklist; skills with no task to use them on.

**Unsupported.** Everything beyond the trial. No evidence for the elementary grades.

**Standards.** data/standards/aasl-2018.txt: the codes match the second edition (2025); the label is stale.

**Spine states:** the host tasks from other courses. **Unit plan fills:** the check per skill.
**Confidence:** thin.

## Open choices for Cory

In the expert's order, by how much each changes what courses teach.

1. **Which content standards does social studies teach?** Options: one state's standards for kindergarten to grade
   12; one state for K to 8 and another source for high school; C3 only. *Recommend:* one state's set throughout,
   C3 kept for skills, with psychology and economics on their national subject standards. *Changes:* every social
   studies spine.
2. **How are the language courses split, and how is American Sign Language made?** Options: levels 1 to 4 for each
   language; fewer levels; keep one course. Sign language needs video, which may mean people on camera.
   *Recommend:* split into levels, load sign language standards, and decide the video source before any spine.
   *Changes:* the course count rises above 105.
3. **Which sensitive health topics does Curio Class carry, and who checks accuracy?** Options: all the tool's topics with a
   leave-out path; a reduced set; none. *Recommend:* all, each sensitive unit able to be left out without breaking the
   course, with one paid read per band by a health curriculum specialist. *Changes:* the health coverage map and a cost.
4. **Are SEL and library skills courses or strands?** Options: stand-alone courses with a year-by-year sequence;
   strands inside health, English language arts and social studies. The registry accepts both for SEL.
   *Recommend:* keep them as courses, held to the whole core at the days their spine states, with a sequence per
   grade. *Changes:* the course count if strands are chosen.
5. **What is our stance where state instruments conflict on values?** Options: FOUNDATION promise 7 governs and the
   page says so once; write to one state's criteria. *Recommend:* the first. *Changes:* C12 and the sensitivity
   read stay as they are.
6. **Is informed action required in social studies?** Options: once a year in every course, sized small; in every
   unit; optional. *Recommend:* once a year per course. *Changes:* one task per social studies course.
7. **Is the pass rule right?** Options: as proposed; stricter; looser. *Recommend:* accept, then test on the first finished course.
   *Changes:* what "finished" means for every course here.
8. **Do we confirm that no professional learning is offered?** Options: none; a short free guide
   per subject. *Recommend:* none, stated as a promise that costs marks. *Changes:* nothing built.
9. **Do we accept the library part as our own bar?** Options: yes, named as ours; wait for an instrument. *Recommend:* yes. *Changes:* four courses can proceed.
10. **Do we accept the design half as the bar for SEL and personal finance?** Options: yes, evidence half stated as
    unmet on each course page; no courses until a study exists. *Recommend:* yes. *Changes:* wording on
    five course pages.
11. **Are family notes written in Spanish too?** Texas, Ohio, Maryland and the SEL registry each ask for family
    materials beyond English. Options: English only; English and Spanish; later. *Recommend:* decide once for all
    105 courses, not per subject. *Changes:* one more page per unit everywhere.
