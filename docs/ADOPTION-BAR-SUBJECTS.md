# The adoption bar for the other subjects

**Status:** second draft, 2026-11-11. Revised once after the curriculum expert's cold read
(docs/reviews/bar-subjects-2026-11-11.md). The owner's answers were applied on 2026-11-11 (Desk card
q-bar-subjects; see "Decided" and "Still open with Cory" near the end). Not read by the expert since revision.

**Core subjects come first.** This is a soft goal (Cory, 2026-11-11; docs/agents/decisions/BASELINE.md rule 24).
Work on the subjects on this page waits behind math, English language arts, science and social studies unless it
is complementary, such as a shared rule or a standards fix that serves both. Social studies is core. Every course
is still finished to the bar; this sets the order, not the scope.

**What this page is for.** Every one of the 105 courses is to be finished to the adoption bar (docs/FOUNDATION.md,
section 4; docs/ADOPTION-BAR.md). Math has a rubric file; English language arts and science rubrics are drafted
separately. This page writes the bar for every other subject: one shared core, then a short part per subject.

**What we may never say.** That Curio Class was reviewed by, meets, or is endorsed by any body named here. This is a
bar we hold ourselves to. Most instruments are all rights reserved, so nothing is quoted. Every criterion is in our
own words.

**What this page is not.** An approach page. Layer 3 asks for one per subject before a scope and sequence, and none exists (see
"Before a scope and sequence in this subject"). A subject's part moves to its own docs/RUBRIC-*.md file once it is firm.

## The pass rule

**Accepted for now (Cory, 2026-11-11), to be tested on the first finished course.** No course has been read
against it yet, so it may prove too hard or too easy.
It copies the shape of the instruments read: alignment, accuracy and feasibility are a door that stops a review, and
the rest is scored.

- **The door: C1, C2, C3 and C14, each passed in full.** A course that fails one is not read further until fixed.
- **C4 to C13, C15, C16:** each is marked pass, part pass or fail. A finished course has no fail and no more than
  three part passes.
- **Pass:** no "fails if" condition is found in the sample. **Part pass:** a condition is found in one sampled unit
  only, and the fix is local. **Fail:** it is found in two or more units, or is built into the course's design.
- **The sample.** The reviewer reads the whole scope and sequence and coverage table, then three whole units picked by the
  reviewer, never by the builder, one from each third of the year: every lesson, page, tool and check in them. A
  course of four units or fewer is read whole. Below, "three units" means this sample.
- The subject part is read the same way: no criterion failed, and the part pass count above covers core and subject
  criteria together.
- **A small course is read the same way.** SEL and library skills courses may be smaller than other courses by
  the nature of the subject (decided 2026-11-11). They are "held to the whole core at the size its scope and sequence states":
  every criterion from C1 to C16 and every criterion of the subject part still applies, and what is measured is
  the number of units and days the scope and sequence itself sets out, not the size of a math or science course. Being small
  waives no criterion.
- **Beyond the three units,** the reviewer reads: for C1, the whole coverage table, tracing ten standards of the
  reviewer's choice to lesson and check item; for C4, the progression page; for C8 and C9, one cumulative check;
  for C13, the approach page; for C3 and C16, all public copy for the course and the logic model.

**Where our promises cost us marks, by choice.** Curio Class will not build some things rubrics ask for
(FOUNDATION, section 3): data dashboards (Texas names one as an example), adapting to each student and plugging
into a learning management system (Ohio, narrative items), accessible-format files lodged with a national centre
(Oregon), and professional learning tied to the materials (Ohio, scored). Our answers are the class record sheet,
score codes, open pages, print and teacher pages. Professional learning: none for now (decided 2026-11-11). It is
a future area of growth, not a closed door.

**Where the instruments conflict on values.** Oklahoma's arts rubric checks materials against state law. Maryland,
the federal health tool and the SEL registry ask for equity content in terms that pull the other way. No stance
passes all of them. C12 follows FOUNDATION promise 7, a middle line, and Curio Class does not claim to satisfy any
state's values criteria. Said once here, so it is not re-opened per course. Cory's values rule (2026-11-12) now
settles these conflicts in order: the higher court, then the evidence, then dignity and openness, then the age of
the student (see "Still open with Cory", b, and social studies item 11). FOUNDATION's promises still bind.

**Acceptability is local.** A national site cannot settle what suits each community, so each course page says what
a school may leave out. For health this is decided: all sensitive topics are carried, and each sensitive unit can
be left out without breaking the course.

## The subject parts

Each part adds to the core. Part 6, world languages, is shelved (2026-11-11) and is kept for a later pass. Unless a part says otherwise, it covers four band courses. Tags: **[I]** from the instrument, **[S]** from the standards, **[O]** ours. Full

### 1. Social studies

Sixteen courses, kindergarten to grade 12, in Massachusetts's course order (redrawn 2026-11-13, Desk card
q-ss-course-list): one course per grade from kindergarten to grade 5 (one row, `ss-k5`), World Geography and
Ancient Civilizations I and II (6, 7), Civics (8), World History I and II (9, 10), US History I and II (11, 12),
US Government and Politics and Economics with personal finance (half years, 11 to 12). Fifteen are core.
Psychology is not core and waits for the after-the-core stage. The list is data/courses.csv; the reasons are in
docs/courses/social-studies/STANDARD.md, section 2.

**Rests on.** EdReports and AIR criteria (2024) and Louisiana's tool (2026-27), read in full by the writer;
Maryland's rubric (2025), read in full by the expert; Massachusetts read in part. The standard: Cory's option (d)
(DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13; paper: docs/reviews/social-studies-content-standard-2026-11-12.md), made
usable in docs/courses/social-studies/STANDARD.md. **No base text** (pre-work P2,
docs/reviews/social-studies-open-material-2026-11-13.md): every complete open program is NonCommercial, ShareAlike
or all rights reserved, so every social studies day is Curio Class's own, written with references.

Items 1 to 8 come from the instruments. Items 9 to 14 come from the decisions on the standard. Each says the
layer it is read at: scope and sequence (S), unit plan and section plan (U), day (D).

1. [I] **The inquiry arc with knowledge.** Each unit is framed by a compelling question, with supporting questions
   for its sections, and runs the C3 arc: ask and plan, use the tools of civics, economics, geography and
   history, weigh sources and use evidence, then state a conclusion and act on it. Content is taught directly and
   in order inside the arc, so facts, ideas and words pile up. History runs in time order unless the scope and
   sequence gives a reason. (S, U)
2. [I] **Real sources at the right level.** Students work with primary and secondary sources at a reading level
   right for the grade. Each source has a stated difficulty, a reason for its place in the year, and a rights
   line. (U, D)
3. [I] Where an event has more than one account, the sources carry more than one. (U)
4. [I] The discipline's skills are taught, not assumed: who made a source and why, its setting, checking one source
   against another, order in time, cause and effect, reading a map or data table, weighing cost against benefit.
   (U, D)
5. [I] **Claims from evidence.** Students write and talk from evidence: claim, evidence, reasoning, and the other
   side's claim. Writing is taught directly. Short research tasks recur. (U, D)
6. [I] Subject words are taught and used. (U, D)
7. [I] Checks test knowledge and skills together. Every end-of-unit assessment includes a task done from sources
   the student has not seen; exit tickets ask for a skill used on content, not a fact alone. (U, D)
8. [I, in two of three instruments] **Informed action.** Students apply what they concluded to a real problem near
   them. Maryland puts this in the category that must be met; Louisiana does not ask it. How often (decided
   2026-11-11): once a year per course, and up to three times a year if the evidence shows a need for more. The
   action is one a student can take safely at that age, and the course never tells a student which side to
   take. (S, U)
9. [S] **Content against the anchor.** Each unit cites the Massachusetts 2018 codes it teaches
   (data/standards/ma-hss-2018.txt), and every anchor standard of the course is taught in full (C1). Skills cite
   C3 (data/standards/c3-indicators.txt). Grade 3 cites Curio Class's own year in place of the anchor's (item 13).
   News and media literacy is placed: grade 8 carries 8.T7, the high school courses carry NML.T1 to T5. (S, U)
10. [O] **The check states.** Each unit plan is compared with California, Tennessee and New York, and for grades 9
    to 12 with the college-level course frameworks; the Educating for American Democracy Roadmap, the economics
    content standards (CEE 2010) and Geography for Life (2012) are references. Anything a strong set treats as
    essential that the anchor leaves out or teaches thinly is added inside the year's days, with its source
    written down. One comparison table per unit. Additions never push the year past its stated days (C1, C2).
    Check-set names never appear in student, teacher or family copy. (U)
11. [O] **The values rule** (Cory, 2026-11-12), in order: (a) where a court has ruled, side with the higher
    court; (b) facts and backed sources decide claims, the discipline's evidence first; (c) people are taught
    with dignity, respect, kindness and openness, and we do not hide humanity: LGBTQ people appear where history
    includes them; (d) grade level and life experience set how and when a subject is taught. Where scholarly
    bodies split on a claim, rule (b) is applied by a named reader of the discipline, not by a vote of
    organisations. (U, D)
12. [O] **Sensitive topics told plainly and at the right age.** Hard history (slavery, the removal and treatment of
    Native nations, genocide and the Holocaust, war, segregation and the fight for civil rights) is taught with
    its facts and its human cost, never softened until false (C12). Native nations are named by their own names
    and shown in the present as well as the past. The section plan names each sensitive topic and gives the
    teacher a note on how to handle it; the fresh-agent sensitivity read covers every unit. (U, D)
13. [O] **The own-state method.** No state's own history is taught as if it were every student's. The anchor's
    Massachusetts year (grade 3) is replaced by Curio Class's own year, Communities Then and Now
    (docs/courses/social-studies/grade-3-outline.md), held to the same reads. Where the anchor teaches its own
    state's government (8.T6), the course teaches "your state" as a method: its founding, government, peoples,
    land and economy, with worked examples from several states and a link to each state's own standards. (S, U, D)
14. [O] **Our own days, from open pieces.** With no base text, days are written by Curio Class. Open pieces (CC BY
    or public domain, per docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md) may be adapted with a credit line; NonCommercial material
    (Massachusetts's Investigating History units, the New York toolkit and others) is reference only and is never
    copied. (D)

**Fails if** (by item):

- 1: coverage without depth; a unit has no compelling question, or its question is answered without the unit's
  content; skills practised on content the course does not teach.
- 2, 3: sources are textbook summaries only; a source has no stated difficulty or reason; one telling of a
  contested event.
- 5, 7: questions or end-of-unit tasks answerable without the sources; no task from an unseen source.
- 8: a course has no informed action in its year; the action tells the student what to think, or is unsafe for
  the age.
- 9: a unit cites no anchor codes; an anchor standard of the course has no lesson and no check item.
- 10: a unit plan has no comparison table; an addition has no source; additions push the year past its days.
- 11: a claim rests on no backed source; a ruling of a higher court is contradicted; a group is left out of a
  history that includes it.
- 12: a hard fact is softened until it is false; content is taught below or above the age set by rule (d); a
  sensitive topic has no teacher note; no sensitivity read on record.
- 13: Massachusetts-specific content is taught as if it were every student's; a "your state" day has examples
  from one state only, or no link to the student's own state standards.
- 14: a day carries wording, tasks or images from reference-only material; an adapted day has no credit.

**Unsupported.** Economics and geography have no rubric of their own; they are read against the CEE content
standards (2010) and Geography for Life (2012) as references. Psychology's standard is the APA high school
standards (2022 revision), loaded by hand when psychology's turn comes (apa.org blocks automated reading).
Maryland's value-laden items are left out on purpose; the values rule (item 11) governs instead.

**Standards.** data/standards/c3-indicators.txt (2013) for skills; data/standards/ma-hss-2018.txt (Massachusetts
2018, ids and our own words, credit in the header) for content. Not loaded: the framework's practice standards
(C3 carries the skills) and its literacy standards (the history and social studies reading and writing standards, RH and WHST, are not
in the ELA records; loading them is open, see docs/courses/social-studies/STANDARD.md). C3 publisher source confirmed:
NCSS, 2013; the revised 2017 PDF was read.

**Scope and sequence states:** the anchor codes and C3 indicators per unit, each unit's compelling question,
where informed action falls, and where the own-state method is used. **Unit plan fills:** the comparison table
with the check states and every addition's source. **Section plan fills:** the source list with difficulty,
reasons and rights; the sensitive topics and their teacher notes. **Confidence:** likely.

### 2. Arts: visual arts, music, theater, dance, media arts

Twenty courses: five disciplines, four bands.

**Rests on.** Texas fine arts rubric (2025) and California's five categories, read in full by the writer;
Oklahoma's rubric (2024; four gateways, the fourth on state law), read in full by the expert.

1. [I] All four artistic processes are in each course: create; perform, present or produce; respond; connect.
2. [I] Technique is taught directly, in steps, and gets harder.
3. [I] The discipline's words are taught and used in making and responding.
4. [I] Students make, adapt or extend their own work and show it, with some choice in what they make or study.
5. [I] Students are taught to read, judge and respond to other artists' work.
6. [I] Students give and get feedback, and reflect to refine their work.
7. [I] Works and traditions come from many times and cultures, with history and setting taught.
8. [I] Assessment ranges across the year: performances and finished works scored with a guide, self-assessment, and
   work kept over time.
9. [I] Pacing fits real arts schedules (rotations, short weeks). Lessons list equipment and safe use of space.

**Fails if:** prompts to express with no technique taught; no performing or showing; response work without the
discipline's words; one culture's canon; a plan that needs a daily period.

**Unsupported.** No rubric treats the five disciplines apart or says how to choose works. A screen cannot teach a
performing art, so the performing is in the room.

**Standards.** data/standards/ncas-performance-standards.txt (2014; a national revision is under way, no date). The
standards are by grade to grade 8; our courses are bands.

**Scope and sequence states:** which grade's standards fall in which year of the band. **Section plan fills:** the
works studied and the showing. **Confidence:** likely for the instruments, thin for the evidence.

### 3. Health

**Rests on.** The federal Health Education Curriculum Analysis Tool, 2021. The writer read the overview and the
first halves of chapters 4 and 5; the expert read both chapters in full. Nobody has read the topic modules, where
coverage is scored.

1. [I] Each unit names the healthy behaviours it is for, and every lesson ties to one.
2. [I] Facts are basic, accurate and useful for a decision. Facts alone fail.
3. [I] Each skill is taught in five steps: why it matters, the steps, a model, practice on real situations,
   feedback.
4. [I] Lessons work on beliefs, on what peers really do, and on social pressure.
5. [I] Content fits the age; skills come back across topics and bands; a topic gets enough time. A one-off lesson
   fails.
6. [I] Units link students to family and trusted adults, and learning reaches past the classroom.
7. [I] The health, medical and scientific content is checked by a named person able to judge it, with the date. A
   health curriculum specialist counts. An agent's read does not. The plan stays as proposed: one paid read per
   band by a health curriculum specialist. The cost is confirmed with Cory before anything is spent.
8. [I] Objectives cover knowing, valuing and doing, and can be measured. Assessment is of more than one kind and
   includes students assessing themselves.
9. [I] Materials are inclusive: they fit students' cultures and the conditions they live in, avoid blame, and guide
   the teacher on their own bias.
10. [I, topics unread] A band coverage map shows which topics and healthy behaviour outcomes in
    data/standards/hecat-topics.txt each band teaches, and where.

**Fails if:** scare tactics; fact recall as the end-of-unit assessment; skills named and never rehearsed; outdated figures; no
coverage map; a sensitive topic taught with no note on what a school may leave out.

**Unsupported.** The tool counts features and does not show a curriculum works. It assumes a teacher leads, in a
classroom or online. No instrument says which sensitive topics a national site carries. Decided 2026-11-11:
Curio Class carries all of the tool's sensitive topics, and each sensitive unit can be left out without breaking
the course.

**Standards.** data/standards/nhes-2024.txt (SHAPE America, 2024). The tool is keyed to the 2007 edition. Skills map
across; the first standard was reworded. Decided 2026-11-11 (card q-standards-currency): the 2024 standards are
kept, with a written mapping to the older-keyed review tool.

**Scope and sequence states:** the coverage map. **Section plan fills:** the behaviour outcome and the skill steps.
**Confidence:** likely, until the writer has read the second half of chapter 5 and one topic module.

### 4. Physical education

**Rests on.** The federal Physical Education Curriculum Analysis Tool, 2019 (preliminary analyses and directions
read; grade tables not read) and Oregon's 2025 criteria, read in full by the writer only.

1. [I, federal tool] For every standard in every grade of the band there is a stated outcome, content that teaches
   it, and an assessment of it.
2. [I, Oregon] Motor skills are built in order across grades.
3. [O] Lessons are planned so most of the class is moving most of the time.
4. [I] Fitness and safety content is accurate.
5. [I, Oregon] Lessons work in a gym, a field or a small room, and adapt for weather, class size and little
   equipment.
6. [I, Oregon] Every lesson has an adaptation for disabilities and for a range of skill.
7. [I, Oregon] Assessment is of skill, knowledge and conduct, and reports growth against the student's own earlier
   work, not rank.
8. [O, from memory] No exercise as punishment and no elimination games. Oregon requires the profession's
   appropriate practice guidelines; nobody here has read them.

**Fails if:** a year of games with no outcomes; long lines and waiting. Ours: grading on dress or attendance;
fitness tests used as grades.

**Unsupported.** A screen cannot give movement time. An open site honestly offers plans, task cards, assessments
and knowledge content.

**Standards.** data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt (four standards). The federal tool is keyed to the earlier five. The
method carries over; the tables do not. Decided 2026-11-11 (card q-standards-currency): the 2024 standards are
kept, with a written mapping to the older-keyed review tool.

**Scope and sequence states:** the outcome for each standard by grade. **Section plan fills:** space, equipment and
the adaptation. **Confidence:** likely.

### 5. Computer science

**Rests on.** Ohio's rubric (April 2025): gateway 1 read in full by the writer, all 24 pages by the expert.

1. [S] Coverage spans every concept area, not programming alone: systems, data, algorithms, society.
2. [S] The practices (working together, testing and fixing, abstraction, explaining) are taught inside the content.
3. [I] Programming ideas are ordered, and each band says what the one before taught.
4. [I] Students meet real, current uses and cases of computing.
5. [O] Every unit has unplugged work. Ohio asks about offline versions and gives them no points.
6. [I] Technology is built into the learning, not added on: each tool has a purpose in the lesson, gives feedback,
   and does not distract. This is where Ohio's first gateway puts most of its points.
7. [I] Tools run in a common browser on school devices, with notes for technical staff. [O] Nothing to install.
8. [I] Assessment includes projects and code, with rubrics.
9. [I] Teacher pages assume a teacher not trained in the subject and explain the content at adult level.

**Fails if:** block puzzles with no concepts named; nothing on data or society; a tool that needs an install or an
account; no help for the non-specialist.

**Unsupported.** No national materials rubric. No page may claim coding improves general thinking.

**Standards.** data/standards/csta-2026-ids.txt: the 196 foundational codes of the 2026 standards, with no header and
no wording. The 135 high school specialty standards are absent. Decided 2026-11-11 (card q-standards-currency):
the 135 Specialty standards are loaded. The high school course teaches the Foundational set and claims a
Specialty standard only where a topic already goes that far.

**Scope and sequence states:** the concept areas per unit. **Section plan fills:** the unplugged task.
**Confidence:** likely.

### 6. World languages: Spanish, American Sign Language. Shelved, 2026-11-11

**Shelved for a later pass (Cory, 2026-11-11), American Sign Language included.** No scope and sequence is written, no
standards are loaded and no video is planned for these courses now. The part below is kept as written so the
later pass starts from it. What is owed when it is unshelved is listed under "Before a scope and sequence in this subject".

Two courses today, each "all levels".

**Rests on.** Texas languages rubric (2026), all 22 pages read by the writer only. It covers American Sign Language.

1. [I] Each level names a target proficiency level, and objectives are written as what the learner can do.
2. [I] Function, context, text type and control get harder in a stated order, and earlier ones come back.
3. [I] Units are themes from real life. Words and grammar serve a communicative task, not lists.
4. [I] Students take in and produce the target language almost all the time, with input they can understand and
   supports that fade.
5. [I] Authentic texts from several communities carry products, practices and perspectives.
6. [I] Each mode (interpretive, interpersonal, presentational) is taught, practised in every unit and assessed by
   performance. Each unit ends with one linked task across the three.
7. [I] Interpersonal tasks have a real information gap. A scripted dialogue does not count.
8. [I] Supports hold the communicative goal steady, and heritage speakers have a path.
9. [I] American Sign Language: handshape, location, movement, palm orientation, non-manual markers, use of space
   and classifiers, each taught directly and practised in every unit. [O] Materials are video.

**Fails if:** word lists and conjugation drills; instructions in English; textbook-made texts only; no unrehearsed
two-way task; one course for all levels.

**Unsupported.** A no-account site cannot give two-way practice, so it happens between people in the room.

**Standards.** data/standards/actfl-wrs.txt (2015 standards, 2024 level names). The 2026 can-do statements and any

### 8. Personal finance, grades 9 to 12

## Still open with Cory

**Both are now settled** (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12 and 2026-11-13). The text below is kept as the record of the
question.

- **(a), settled:** option (d). C3 for skills, Massachusetts 2018 as the content anchor in Massachusetts's course
  order, checked unit by unit against California, Tennessee and New York, with Curio Class's own year in place of
  the anchor's own-state year. Made usable in docs/courses/social-studies/STANDARD.md; applied in social studies
  items 9, 10 and 13.
- **(b), settled:** the values rule, in order: the higher court, then facts and backed sources, then dignity,
  respect and openness, then grade level and life experience. Applied in social studies item 11.

**(a) Which content standard does social studies teach?** Cory's words: "1 needs a better option. this may
require a unification of content standards or possibly a "high watermark" method." He did not accept one state's
set as the only option. He asked for a unified set, or a high watermark method: where sources differ, take the
more demanding expectation. Three options are to be worked up:

- **One state's set.** The second draft's proposal: one state's standards from kindergarten to grade 12, C3 kept
  for skills, psychology and economics on their national subject standards. To work up: which states are
  candidates and why, the reuse terms of each, and how well each fits the fourteen courses.
- **A unified set built from several sources.** To work up: which sources go in, the rule for merging them and
  for what is left out, who checks the result, what it costs to build and keep current, and how a school in any
  one state would trace a course back to its own standards.
- **A high watermark.** To work up: which sources are compared, what "more demanding" means for content
  (earlier grade, more depth, more topics), how to stop the sum from growing past what a year can teach (C1 and
  C2 still apply), and the same tracing question.

For each option the work-up also says what it would change in the fourteen scopes and sequences. It must be worked up and
brought back before anything is chosen. **No social studies scope and sequence starts before it is settled.**

**(b) What is our stance where state instruments conflict on values?** Cory's words: "5 is another place where i
feel a "high watermark" may be best (follow the higher quality or rigor)." He leans to a high watermark here too.
Said plainly: rigor can be ranked, so where two instruments ask for more or less of the same thing, the higher
one can be followed. A values conflict is not always a matter of higher or lower. Two instruments can ask for
opposite things, and neither is the more rigorous. So the rule needs working out alongside (a): what "higher"
means, which conflicts it can settle, and what governs the ones it cannot. Until then FOUNDATION's promises still
bind, promise 7 among them, and C12 and the sensitivity read stay as they are.
