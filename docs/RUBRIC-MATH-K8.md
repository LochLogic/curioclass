## How it works

Three gateways, in order. A course must at least partly pass the first to be read on the second, and must fully
pass both to be read on the third. Any indicator scored zero in the first gateway stops the course before the
third. So focus and coherence are not one factor among many: they are the door.

## What each part asks, and where Curio Class answers it

| Part | In our words | Where we answer it today |
|---|---|---|
| **Gateway 1, focus** (1a to 1d) | Assessments test this grade's content; each assessment says which standards it tests; assessments let students show the whole of each standard; every student gets a lot of work on grade-level problems, enough for the whole of each standard | The teaching test and the unaided check (SPEC.md); the unit and course checks ("What a finished course holds", item 4). **Gap:** our checks must name the standard for each item |
| **Gateway 1, coherence** (1e to 1h) | Most of the year is major work; supporting work is used to practise major work; problems link clusters and domains; the materials say what earlier grades taught and what later grades need | The scope and sequence (67 percent major work; line plots placed to practise fractions; the builds-on and sets-up columns). **Gap:** units need problems that link units on purpose |
| **Gateway 2, rigor** (2a to 2d) | Understanding, skill and fluency, and real applications are each built where the standards call for them, and the three are balanced over the year | The teaching test covers understanding; worksheets and generators cover fluency. **Gap:** nothing checks the balance per unit or per course |
| **Gateway 2, the eight practices** (2e to 2l) | Each of the eight Standards for Mathematical Practice is developed on purpose, tied to grade content | **Gap:** not in our plan at all. No spec, unit or ledger column names a practice |
| **Gateway 3, teacher supports** (3a to 3g) | Notes for the teacher on how to run each piece; explanations that teach the teacher the maths and how it links across grades; a year plan with standards; a note for families; the approach explained with its research base; a list of supplies; an assessment system across the year with guidance on what to do with the results | Teacher pages that teach the teacher (Track 7); the scope and sequence; the cumulative checks it names (a start-of-year check and four more). **Gaps:** a family note, a supplies list per unit, the approach written down with sources, "what to do next" after each check |
| **Gateway 3, student supports** (3j to 3q) | Supports for students with disabilities and other special populations; extensions for students ready for more depth; varied ways to work and show learning; grouping ideas; accommodations on assessments; a range of people shown, with guidance on drawing on students' backgrounds; supports for different reading levels; manipulatives that are true to the maths and tied to written methods | The three hooks (English learners, access); the sensitivity read. **Gaps:** extensions for advanced students, assessment accommodations, reading-level supports on word problems, grouping notes |
| **Gateway 3, design** (3r to 3u) | Technology used to engage with the standards; ways to work together; a look that helps thinking and does not distract; guidance for the teacher on using the technology | Our strongest part: the tools, the kit, the lesson plans |

## What this adds to the plan

Six gaps the earlier reviews did not name. Each is now a row in docs/ROADMAP.md, "Gaps found, and how each
closes", and each is tried first on the pilot unit:

1. The eight mathematical practices.
2. The balance of understanding, fluency and application.
3. Assessment items labelled with their standard, and "what to do next" guidance.
4. Extensions for students ready for more.
5. A family note and a supplies list per unit.
6. Assessment accommodations and reading-level supports.
