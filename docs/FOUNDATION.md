# Curio Class: the foundation (layer 1)

Gathered 2026-11-11 (Cory). This is the one home for what Curio Class is for, who it serves, what it promises and
what it assumes. Every layer above it (the approach to teaching, the progressions, the spines, the unit plans, the
tools) is built on this page and may not contradict it. Until today these lines were spread over docs/VISION.md,
docs/ROADMAP.md, docs/ADOPTION-BAR.md and docs/agents/decisions/BASELINE.md; those files keep their detail and now
point here. Where one of them disagrees with this page, this page wins and the other file is fixed.

It changes rarely, and only with Cory. The layers and the order of work are in docs/ROADMAP.md ("The layers").

## 1. What it is for

A free, complete, open K-12 classroom toolkit: interactive tools, printables, lessons and units for every standard,
so a teacher can run a full course without paying for curriculum or software (VISION, Mission).

**The end goal.** A teacher can run any course on Curio Class from the first day to the last, free, with no account,
and the course would stand up to a state adoption review. A course is a grade of a subject or a high school course.
Depth comes first: one course is finished before the next is started (ROADMAP, "The path from here").

**We own the full course** (Cory, 2026-11-11). Curio Class writes its own courses. Well-reviewed open courses are
used as evidence for order, time and anchors, and a lesson may be adapted where its licence allows and the credit
is given. We do not stitch two programs together: each hangs together through its own routines and pictures, and a
mix is weaker than either. A tool built to a clear job in our unit will also fit the same lesson in a course a
teacher already uses, and that is welcome, but it is not the plan.

## 2. Who it is for

| Who | What that means for the work |
|---|---|
| **A classroom teacher** running a course | The main reader of every spine, unit plan and teacher page. Teacher pages teach the teacher |
| **A parent teaching one child at home** (named 2026-11-11) | Every unit carries a short "teaching one child" note; answer keys explain, not only list; discussion is written so a parent can hold it; sample pieces show what grade-level work looks like |
| **A student alone** | The site is public and a child may open any tool with no adult present. Every tool stands alone: defaults work for a solo user and teacher toggles are extras (BASELINE rule 4) |

## 3. The promises

These hold even where a review, a district system or a convenience would reward breaking them.

1. **Free for good.** No paywall, ever (ROADMAP, Goal).
2. **No accounts, no ads, no tracking, no student analytics** (VISION, "What we will not do"). A tool cannot
   remember a student from one day to the next and there is no live teacher dashboard. We chose that.
3. **Results live with the student and the teacher.** A score code the student shows, and printable record sheets.
   The score code records only what a tool can mark (ROADMAP, "What a finished course holds", item 4).
4. **Every tool works offline as a single file.** Where a tool stores anything, it says on screen that it never
   leaves the device (BASELINE rule 7).
5. **Paper is a first-class product.** Worksheets, units and printables matter as much as screens. Printables read in
   black and white.
6. **Honest words.** We say taught, practiced or shown, per the ledger, never more. Practice tools are not
   diagnoses and score codes are not proof. We never say Curio Class was reviewed by, meets or is endorsed by
   anyone. Public copy names no companies (BASELINE rule 15).
7. **Classroom-safe, and plain about hard facts.** History and science are not softened; they are told with care
   for the people involved, and a fresh agent checks sensitive content before it goes live (BASELINE rules 5, 6, 8).
8. **We do not copy a branded product** in name, look or text, and we respect every licence: read and link, adapt
   only where the licence allows.

## 4. The bar

State adoption is the bar we build to (Cory, 2026-11-10; BASELINE rule 20). A full course should meet the substance
of what state and national curriculum reviews ask for. We do not pursue formal adoption now. The bar applies to
courses and the shelves that feed them; Curiosities and Recess are outside it. The bar never outranks the promises
above. What it asks and what we took from it: docs/ADOPTION-BAR.md. The rubrics we read ourselves against are filed
as docs/RUBRIC-*.md.

**Quality over speed, and over saved work** (BASELINE rules 1, 2, 14). Each piece is really good before the next
wave grows. An existing spec or tool carries no weight against what a unit needs: if the unit needs something
different, the old work loses.

## 5. What we assume about the room

Decided 2026-11-11 (Cory) unless marked.

1. **Grades 3 to 12: one device per student on a tool day.** The courses are designed that way and say so plainly.
   Every tool day still carries one line for a single projector, because some rooms have nothing else and a reviewer
   will ask.
2. **Kindergarten to grade 2: not yet decided.** Open question for Cory when the first K to 2 course comes up.
3. **The physical world is written into the lesson plan.** A tool does not replace rolling the marble, using a
   protractor or reading a whole book. Hands-on work, materials and their cost are part of the unit, with a path for
   a teacher who has nothing (docs/reviews/science-4-spine-2026-11-11.md, finding 7).
4. **A teacher judges open work.** A tool marks what has a right answer. For writing, argument and discussion, the
   tool gives the work a structure so it is quick to read, and print carries the rest: a rubric, sample pieces at
   each score level, and a look-for sheet. Our job is to make that judging fast and consistent (BASELINE rule 21
   already says a tool cannot mark writing or talk).
5. **Time.** A spine states its own year and period length and gives a shorter path where ordinary schools have less
   time than a full program assumes.

## 6. What a tool is for

Decided 2026-11-11 (Cory), from the discussion recorded in docs/agents/decisions/DESK-ANSWERS.md.

1. **A tool is the end piece of instruction, a means to a defined end.** The end is defined in a unit plan: what
   each day is for, where a tool is used and for what job. **No tool is built or refined until a unit plan names
   its job.**
2. **A tool can be whatever the unit needs.** The unit plan asks, for each key moment, what the ideal instrument for
   that idea would be. Sometimes that is a tool nobody has specced; sometimes it is a marble and a ramp. The limits
   are the promises and assumptions on this page and the cost of doing a tool well, not what software can do.
3. **Key tools and practice tools.** A key tool is one the unit cannot run without; key tools are built first and
   hold the course up. Practice tools are lighter, built from shared templates over the long term, and still correct
   and still meet the three hooks. Lighter never means lower quality. A course is runnable when its key tools are
   live and print covers the rest. "Key" is a mark given by the unit plan; the roles of BASELINE rule 21 (lesson,
   see why, practice) still say what a tool does.
4. **Curiosities and Recess** are play and wonder. They claim no standard and are outside the bar (VISION).

## 7. How the work is checked

1. **The curriculum expert reads cold, often** (docs/agents/EXPERT.md). Cory's attention and a teacher's are the
   scarce things, so frequent cold reads by the expert, which teaches itself from outside sources first, are the
   main review at every layer. That has to be good enough, and we say so.
2. **Human reads are rare and aimed.** A working teacher reads the pilot unit of a course (ROADMAP, "What a finished
   course holds", item 7). The first is Cory's mom, a grade 2 teacher.
3. **Lower layers are solid enough to build on, and expected to be revised** by what is built on them. No layer is
   declared finished in the abstract.
4. **What goes to Cory:** anything that changes what a course teaches or who it is for, anything this page cannot
   settle, and anything that needs money, accounts, outside people or a live deploy (BASELINE, "What goes to Cory").
