## 1. Crank-Up Cardboard Crane

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build | 60-120 min | Indoors | Low | Nearby | 1 |

**Materials:** a shoebox or cereal box, 2 cardboard tubes, a pencil or wooden spoon for an axle, string (about 4 feet), a paper cup, a paper clip, tape, scissors, coins or small toys to lift

**Nice to have:** a spool, a bottle cap for a crank handle, a long ruler for the arm

### The big idea
A toy city is under construction, and the workers need a crane. Real cranes lift steel beams with a winch, a drum that winds up a rope. Build a crane with a crank you turn to lift a heavy load.

### Goals
- You build a crane that lifts a cup of coins by turning a crank.
- You test how much your crane can lift before it tips.

### Objectives
- I can build a winch that turns circles into lifting.
- I can find out why a crane tips and make it steady.
- I can test and record how many coins my crane lifts.

### Get ready
Clear a table. Empty the box. Gather 20 coins or small toys for loads. Poke holes only with a grown-up nearby.

### Steps
1. Make the tower. Stand a cardboard tube up inside the box and tape it at the bottom.
2. Make the arm. Tape the second tube (or a ruler) across the top of the tower, sticking out one side.
3. Make the winch (15 minutes). Push a pencil through both sides of the box, near the bottom. It should spin easily.
4. Tape one end of the string to the pencil. Run the string up and over the end of the arm.
5. Make the hook. Tie a paper clip to the string end. Hang the paper cup from it.
6. Turn the pencil. The string winds up and the cup rises!
7. Load test. Add 1 coin at a time. Write down the number when the crane tips or the arm bends.
8. Make it stronger. Add weight to the box as a counterweight, a heavy thing on the far side. Test again.

### Make it yours
- Build a crane that spins on a jar lid to turn left and right.
- Add a crank handle so the pencil is easier to turn.
- Set a mission: move 10 blocks from the floor to a "rooftop" shelf.

### Stuck?
- The pencil won't turn: make the holes a little bigger.
- The string slips: wrap tape around the pencil first, so it grips.
- The crane tips over: put a can or books in the back of the box.

### Show it off
Run a "construction site" demo. Lift a load onto a book tower while the family watches.

### Clean up
Put coins back where they belong. Keep the crane on a shelf or recycle the box.

### For the grown-up
This builds early engineering: a winch is a wheel and axle, and a counterweight is about balance. Your child may need help poking holes in thick cardboard, so do that part or check in. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you stop your crane from tipping over?"

## 2. Five-Step Chain Reaction Machine

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build | 60-120 min | Indoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** books, dominoes or blocks, a marble or small ball, cardboard tubes, a toy car, string, tape, paper cups, a ruler, a cardboard ramp

**Nice to have:** a bell, a pulley-like spool, a timer

### The big idea
One push starts it all. A ball rolls, a book falls, a car zooms, a cup flips, and a bell rings! Build a chain reaction machine where each step sets off the next one. Five steps, one push, no help.

### Goals
- You build a machine with at least 5 steps.
- Your machine runs from start to finish with one push.

### Objectives
- I can plan a chain of causes and effects.
- I can fix the one step that breaks the chain.
- I can use ramps, falls and pushes to move energy along.

### Get ready
Pick a floor spot about 6 feet long, away from walkways. Gather your parts in a box.

### Steps
1. Plan on paper (10 minutes). Draw 5 boxes in a row. Write one step in each. Example: domino falls, ball rolls, car zooms, cup flips, bell rings.
2. Pick an ending. What should happen last? A flag goes up? A toy drops into a cup?
3. Build step 1 and step 2. Test them together 3 times.
4. Add step 3. Test steps 1 to 3 together.
5. Add steps 4 and 5. Each step must touch or push the next one.
6. Run the whole thing. Where did it stop? That's your weak link.
7. Fix the weak link. Move it closer, make the ramp steeper, or add tape.
8. Run it 3 times in a row with no fixes. That's a real machine!

### Make it yours
- Add a step that goes up, like a pulley that lifts a cup.
- Give the machine a silly job, like "the Morning Wake-Up Machine."
- Add more steps. Can you get to 10?

### Stuck?
- The ball rolls off the ramp: tape paper walls on the sides.
- Dominoes don't fall: put them closer, about a finger apart.
- The chain stops between steps: move the pieces closer and test just those two.

### Show it off
Invite the family to "press start." Count down from 5 and let them push the first piece.

### Clean up
Take it apart and put each piece back where it came from. Check under furniture for marbles.

### For the grown-up
This builds cause and effect, energy ideas, and real engineering patience (testing one link at a time). Keep marbles away from very young siblings. **Ask at dinner:** "Which step failed the most, and how did you fix it?"

## 3. Secret Life of a Lost Sock

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1 |

**Materials:** paper, a pencil, markers, a stapler or tape, one sock without a partner

**Nice to have:** a sock puppet you make while you write, a timer

### The big idea
Socks go into the wash in pairs. They come out alone. Where do the lost ones go? Write the story from the sock's point of view. That means the sock tells it as "I." Maybe it ran away to join a circus!

### Goals
- You write a story told by a lost sock, at least 1 page long.
- You make it into a small book with pictures.

### Objectives
- I can write from a character's point of view.
- I can use details to make a made-up world feel real.
- I can build a story with a problem and an ending.

### Get ready
Find a lonely sock. Set it on the table where you can see it. Fold 2 sheets of paper in half for a small book.

### Steps
1. Name your sock. Give it a personality. Is it brave, grumpy, or dreamy?
2. Brainstorm (10 minutes). Where did it go? Under the dryer? A secret Sock City? Out the window on a bird?
3. Pick one idea. Write 3 things that happen there.
4. Write the beginning. Start with "I." Example: "I was spinning in the dryer when a crack opened up."
5. Write the middle. Add a problem. Maybe the sock wants to get home but there is a guard.
6. Write the ending. Does it go home? Does it stay? Does it find its missing partner?
7. Add sock words. Use words like stretchy, stinky, fuzzy and toe-hole.
8. Draw pictures for each page. Make a cover with a title.

### Make it yours
- Write the same story from the washing machine's point of view.
- Make the sock world a whole country with its own king.
- Write a "missing sock" poster as the last page.

### Stuck?
- No idea where it went: list 5 wild places, then pick the funniest.
- The story is short: add what the sock sees, hears and smells.
- You keep writing "he" or "she": go back and change it to "I."

### Show it off
Read your story to the family using the sock as a puppet.

### Clean up
Put the sock back in the drawer. Maybe its partner will show up!

### For the grown-up
This builds point of view, a key reading and writing skill in grade 4, plus describing words and story structure. **Ask at dinner:** "Where did your sock go, and would you want to visit?"

## 4. Invent a Sport

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Move | 60-90 min | Either | Low | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** a soft ball or rolled-up socks, a laundry basket or bucket, a pillow, tape or chalk, paper, a pencil

**Nice to have:** a timer, a whistle, a frisbee or paper plate

### The big idea
Someone invented soccer. Someone invented basketball. Today, you invent a brand-new sport! Pick your gear, make the rules and play the first game ever.

### Goals
- You create a new sport with at least 5 rules.
- You play it and change the rules to make it better.

### Objectives
- I can write fair rules that everyone can follow.
- I can test a game and fix what is not fun.
- I can explain how to score points.

### Get ready
Find a safe space. A yard, a park with a grown-up, or a cleared room with soft gear only. Gather 3 or 4 pieces of gear.

### Steps
1. Mix up two sports. Example: bowling plus basketball becomes "Bowl-ketball."
2. Pick your gear. Use 2 or 3 things, like a sock ball and a basket.
3. Set up the field. Mark the start, the goal and the edges with tape or chalk.
4. Write 5 rules (10 minutes). How do you score? What's not allowed? How does a turn end? How do you win?
5. Play alone first. Try 3 rounds. Is it too hard? Too easy?
6. Change one rule. Play again.
7. Make it work for 2 or more players. Who goes first? Do players take turns?
8. Name your sport and design a team logo.

### Make it yours
- Add a silly rule, like "every point must be scored while hopping."
- Make it a team sport with a pass rule.
- Write a short announcer script for a big game.

### Stuck?
- The game is boring: add a time limit or a bonus point zone.
- The game is too hard: move the goal closer.
- Players argue: write the rule more clearly on the rule sheet.

### Show it off
Teach the family your sport after dinner. Hold the first official match.

### Clean up
Peel up tape or wash away chalk. Put the gear back.

### For the grown-up
This builds writing clear rules, fairness, and lots of movement. Indoors, use only soft balls and keep away from glass and lamps. If your child plays in a park, go with them. **Ask at dinner:** "Which rule did you change, and why?"

## 5. Design Your Own Country

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story | 60-120 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1 |

**Materials:** 3 or 4 sheets of paper, a pencil, colored pencils or markers, a ruler, tape

**Nice to have:** a world map or atlas to look at, a folder

### The big idea
You just discovered a new land, and it's yours to plan. What is it called? What do people eat? What are the laws? Design a whole country from its flag to its favorite food.

### Goals
- You make a flag, a map and a fact page for your country.
- You write at least 3 laws and one holiday.

### Objectives
- I can draw a map with a key, a box that explains the symbols.
- I can explain why my laws are fair.
- I can describe a place with real details.

### Get ready
Clear a table. Label 3 sheets: "Map," "Flag" and "Fact Page."

### Steps
1. Name your country. Try a mash-up: "Pancakia" or "Waveland."
2. Draw the map (20 minutes). Draw the coast first. Add mountains, rivers, forests and 3 cities.
3. Make a key in the corner. A triangle means mountains. A dot means a city.
4. Design the flag. Pick 2 or 3 colors. Write what each color means.
5. Write 3 laws. Example: "Everyone gets one free nap a day."
6. Fill in the fact page. Capital city, number of people, money name, national animal, favorite food.
7. Invent one special day. What do people do to celebrate?
8. Put it all in a folder and give it a cover.

### Make it yours
- Write the national song, just 4 lines long.
- Design money for your country with a portrait of your pet.
- Make a travel poster that invites tourists.

### Stuck?
- The map looks empty: add roads, bridges and an island.
- Can't think of laws: think of a rule you wish your house had.
- The flag looks plain: add a symbol, like a star, a leaf or an animal.

### Show it off
Give a "welcome to my country" tour. Teach the family your national greeting.

### Clean up
Put the pencils away. Keep your country in its folder.

### For the grown-up
This builds social studies ideas (maps, laws, government, culture) and writing, in a playful way. **Ask at dinner:** "What is the most important law in your country, and why?"

## 6. Mystery Goo Lab

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 60-90 min | Indoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** cornstarch (about 2 cups), water, a bowl, a spoon, a measuring cup, dish soap, a baking tray, food coloring if you have it, a towel

**Nice to have:** a magnifying glass, small toys to hide, an apron

### The big idea
Is it a solid or a liquid? Punch it and it's hard. Hold it and it drips through your fingers! Mix up two mystery goos and run tests to find out how they behave.

### Goals
- You make 2 kinds of goo from kitchen things.
- You run 5 tests and write what each goo did.

### Objectives
- I can measure and mix ingredients carefully.
- I can test how a material acts under different forces.
- I can describe a material that acts like a solid and a liquid.

### Get ready
Work on a tray or at the kitchen table on a towel. Wear old clothes.

### Steps
1. Goo 1: put 1 cup of cornstarch in a bowl. Add half a cup of water slowly, stirring.
2. Stir until it's hard to stir fast but easy to stir slow. Add a drop of food coloring.
3. Run 5 tests. Punch it fast. Pour it slowly. Roll a ball in your palm, then stop. Drop a toy on top. Squeeze it.
4. Write what happened for each test in one sentence.
5. Goo 2: mix 1 cup of cornstarch with half a cup of dish soap. Knead it into a soft dough.
6. Run the same 5 tests on Goo 2. Which goo is stranger?
7. Make your own goo recipe. Change one amount and test again.

### Make it yours
- Give each goo a monster name and a backstory.
- Hide a small toy in Goo 1. Rescue it without a spoon.
- Make a "goo museum" with labels for each sample.

### Stuck?
- Goo 1 is too runny: add 1 spoon of cornstarch at a time.
- Goo 1 is too dry: add 1 spoon of water.
- Goo 2 is sticky: add more cornstarch and knead.

### Show it off
Put on a goo show. Let a grown-up punch Goo 1 and feel it go hard.

### Clean up
Throw goo in the trash, not the sink. It can clog pipes. Wipe the tray and wash hands.

### For the grown-up
Cornstarch and water is a "non-Newtonian fluid," a classic science surprise. This uses only kitchen items, no borax or other chemicals. Please make sure goo goes in the trash, not down the drain. **Ask at dinner:** "When did the goo act like a solid?"

## 7. Spaghetti Tower Challenge

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1+ |

**Materials:** 20 sticks of dry spaghetti, tape, string (about 3 feet), scissors, a ruler or tape measure, a small ball of foil (the size of a large grape) for the top

**Nice to have:** a timer, mini marshmallows or gumdrops if you have them

### The big idea
Engineers race to build the tallest tower using spaghetti. The catch? It has to hold a "jewel" on top without falling. You get 20 sticks, some tape and some string. Ready, set, build!

### Goals
- You build a free-standing tower that holds a foil ball on top.
- You beat your own height record at least once.

### Objectives
- I can use triangles to make a strong shape.
- I can test a design and change it after a fail.
- I can measure and record heights.

### Get ready
Clear a table. Count out 20 spaghetti sticks. Cut 10 pieces of tape. Roll the foil ball.

### Steps
1. Try a shape. Tape 3 sticks into a triangle. Press on it. Now tape 4 into a square. Which is stronger?
2. Sketch 2 tower plans (10 minutes). Use triangles!
3. Build round one in 18 minutes. Use a timer if you have one.
4. Put the foil ball on top. Let go. Does it stand?
5. Measure from the table to the top. Write it down.
6. Look at what broke or bent. Write one fix.
7. Build round two with your fix. Can you go taller?
8. Try string as "cables" to pull the tower straight.

### Make it yours
- Build a bridge instead and see how many coins it holds.
- Build a tower that leans like a famous one but still stands.
- Challenge someone at home. Who builds tallest in 10 minutes?

### Stuck?
- Sticks snap: tape them in pairs to make them stronger.
- The tower leans: make the base wider.
- The top wobbles: add a triangle brace near the top.

### Show it off
Line up all your towers from shortest to tallest. Give the family a tour.

### Clean up
Sweep broken spaghetti into the trash. Roll up leftover string.

### For the grown-up
This is a classic engineering challenge that teaches shapes, stability and trying again. Dry pasta is sharp when it snaps, so it's best to sweep up rather than walk barefoot. **Ask at dinner:** "Why do you think triangles worked better than squares?"

## 8. Cardboard Loom Weaving

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Art | 60-120 min | Indoors | Low | Nearby | 1 |

**Materials:** a piece of cardboard about the size of a sheet of paper, a ruler, a pencil, scissors, yarn or string, strips of old cloth or ribbon, tape, a plastic fork or a paper clip as a needle

**Nice to have:** a large plastic needle, a stick to hang it on

### The big idea
People have woven cloth for thousands of years. Your loom will be a piece of cardboard. Weave over, under, over, under and watch a pattern grow. Make a tiny rug, a bookmark or a wall hanging.

### Goals
- You build a cardboard loom.
- You weave a piece with at least 3 colors or textures.

### Objectives
- I can follow an over-under pattern.
- I can plan stripes and color patterns.
- I can finish my weaving so it doesn't come apart.

### Get ready
Mark dots every half inch along the top and bottom of the cardboard. Cut a short slit at each dot, about half an inch deep.

### Steps
1. Warp it. The warp is the set of strings that go up and down. Hook yarn in the first slit and tape the end on the back.
2. Run the yarn down to the bottom slit, then back up to the next top slit. Keep going across.
3. Tape the end on the back. Keep the warp tight, not stretched.
4. Weave row one. Tie yarn to your fork or paper clip. Go over, under, over, under.
5. Row two goes the opposite way: under, over, under, over.
6. Push each row down gently with your fingers.
7. Change colors or materials (30 minutes). Try cloth strips, ribbon, or yarn. Make stripes.
8. Finish. Flip the loom. Cut the warp strings in the middle on the back. Tie pairs together in knots.

### Make it yours
- Weave in a feather or a long leaf for nature texture.
- Make a tiny rug for a toy or doll house.
- Make a pattern: 3 rows blue, 1 row white, repeat.

### Stuck?
- The sides pull in: leave a little loop at the end of each row.
- You lost your pattern: look at the row below. Do the opposite.
- The warp slips out: tape it tighter on the back.

### Show it off
Hang it on a stick by the door so the family sees it first thing.

### Clean up
Wind leftover yarn into a ball. Put scissors away.

### For the grown-up
Weaving builds patterns, counting, focus and fine motor skills. It's also great for math: patterns and alternating sequences. Stay nearby for scissors. **Ask at dinner:** "What pattern did you weave, and what would you try next?"

## 9. Frozen Fruit Pop Shop

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | 60-90 min | Indoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** fruit you have (bananas, berries, canned peaches or pineapple), yogurt or juice, small paper cups, spoons, a fork for mashing, a bowl, a measuring cup, foil, paper, markers

**Nice to have:** a blender (grown-up runs it), honey, wooden craft sticks

### The big idea
Your frozen pop shop opens tonight! Invent 3 new flavors. Give them wild names. Mix, pour, layer and freeze. After dinner, your customers line up.

### Goals
- You invent and make 3 different frozen pop flavors.
- You make a shop menu with names and descriptions.

### Objectives
- I can measure and mix ingredients.
- I can write a recipe so someone else could make it.
- I can describe food with tasty words.

### Get ready
Wash your hands. Ask which fruit you may use. Set out cups, spoons and a bowl. Clear a spot in the freezer.

### Steps
1. Plan 3 flavors (10 minutes). Example: "Banana Moon" (banana and yogurt), "Purple Storm" (berries and juice), "Sunset Stripes" (two layers).
2. Mash flavor 1. Put half a banana and half a cup of yogurt in a bowl. Mash with a fork.
3. Fill a cup about three-quarters full. Spoon it in.
4. Make flavor 2 the same way with different fruit.
5. Try layers for flavor 3. Pour one layer, freeze 30 minutes, then pour the next.
6. Cover each cup with foil. Poke a spoon or stick through the middle. The foil holds it up.
7. Write recipe cards. List what went in and how much.
8. Make your shop menu. Give each pop a name, a price in pretend money, and a 1-line description.

### Make it yours
- Make a "mystery flavor." Customers guess what's inside.
- Add a crunch layer at the bottom, like crushed cereal.
- Make tiny "sample" pops in an ice cube tray.

### Stuck?
- Pops are icy: add more yogurt or mashed banana.
- The stick falls over: use the foil cover to hold it straight.
- Not enough fruit: use juice with a few pieces of fruit inside.

### Show it off
After dinner, open the shop. Read the menu and take orders.

### Clean up
Wash bowls, spoons and the fork. Wipe the counter. Put fruit away.

### For the grown-up
This builds measuring, recipe writing and descriptive language. Pops need about 4 hours to freeze, so start after lunch. Please check allergies. If you use a blender, you run it. **Ask at dinner:** "Which flavor would sell the most, and why?"

## 10. Pizza Box Solar Oven

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 90-120 min | Outdoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** a pizza box or a shoebox with a lid, foil, plastic wrap, black paper, tape, a ruler or stick to prop the lid, a plate, a food to heat (crackers with cheese, or a tortilla with cheese)

**Nice to have:** an oven thermometer, chocolate pieces, a stopwatch

### The big idea
The sun is a giant heater, 93 million miles away. Can you trap its heat to cook a snack? Build a solar oven, an oven powered by sunlight, and melt cheese with no fire and no plug.

### Goals
- You build a solar oven from a box.
- You melt cheese or chocolate using only sunlight.

### Objectives
- I can explain how foil reflects light, or bounces it.
- I can explain why black paper gets hot.
- I can measure changes over time.

### Get ready
Pick a sunny day, best between 11 in the morning and 2 in the afternoon. Clear a table. A grown-up cuts the lid flap.

### Steps
1. A grown-up cuts a flap in the lid. Leave about 1 inch of lid on 3 sides. Fold the flap up on the 4th side.
2. Cover the inside of the flap with foil, shiny side out. Smooth it flat.
3. Line the inside bottom of the box with black paper.
4. Tape plastic wrap over the hole in the lid. Seal the edges tight. This traps the warm air.
5. Put your snack on a plate inside the box.
6. Set the oven in the sun. Prop the flap with a stick so it shines light into the box.
7. Check every 10 minutes (40 minutes). Draw what you see each time.
8. Turn the box as the sun moves, so the flap catches the most light.

### Make it yours
- Build two ovens: one with foil, one without. Which gets hotter?
- Try melting different things: cheese, chocolate, a crayon in foil.
- Give your oven a name and make a sign for "Sun Kitchen."

### Stuck?
- Nothing melts: wait longer, or check that the flap points at the sun.
- Heat escapes: tape any gaps in the plastic wrap.
- No sun: try again another day. Clouds block the heat.

### Show it off
Serve your sun-cooked snack to the family. Tell them how far the heat traveled.

### Clean up
Throw away food scraps. Fold the box to save it, or recycle it.

### For the grown-up
This builds understanding of light, heat, reflection and absorption. Please cut the box flap yourself. Solar ovens get warm, not dangerously hot, but use an oven mitt to remove the plate. Check allergies for any food. **Ask at dinner:** "Why did the black paper help?"

## 11. Detective Case File for the Family

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Puzzle | 60-120 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1+ |

**Materials:** paper, envelopes or folded paper, a pencil, markers, tape, small objects to use as clues (a cookie crumb, a sock, a note)

**Nice to have:** a magnifying glass, a flashlight, sticky notes

### The big idea
Something has gone missing from your house! You know who did it, because you planned it. Build a mystery case for your family to solve, with clues, suspects and a twist.

### Goals
- You create a mystery with at least 3 suspects and 5 clues.
- Your family solves the case using your clues.

### Objectives
- I can plan a story where clues point to an answer.
- I can write clues that are fair but not too easy.
- I can make "red herrings," fake clues that trick the reader.

### Get ready
Pick the "crime." Example: "Who stole the last cookie?" Write the real answer on a paper. Fold it and hide it.

### Steps
1. Pick 3 suspects. They can be stuffed animals, pets or family members. Give each a name and a reason to want the cookie.
2. Plan 5 clues (15 minutes). 3 point to the real thief. 2 are red herrings.
3. Make the clues. A footprint drawing. A crumb trail. A torn note. A sock left behind.
4. Write a "witness statement" for each suspect. Example: "Mr. Fluff says he was napping all day."
5. Hide the clues around one room. Write a clue list so you don't forget where.
6. Make the case file. Put a cover sheet in an envelope with "CASE 001."

## 25. Learn to Juggle Scarves

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Move | 45-75 min | Either | Low | On your own | 1 |

**Materials:** 3 light scarves, or 3 plastic grocery bags, or 3 thin dish towels

**Nice to have:** 3 small balls or rolled socks for the next level, a timer

### The big idea
Jugglers make it look like magic. The secret? Start with scarves! They float slowly, so you have time to catch them. Learn to juggle 3 in one session.

### Goals
- You juggle 3 scarves for at least 10 throws.
- You invent your own juggling trick.

### Objectives
- I can throw and catch with both hands.
- I can follow a pattern of throws in order.
- I can practice a hard skill until it gets easier.

### Get ready
Find a space with room above your head. Move breakable things away.

### Steps
1. One scarf. Hold it by the middle. Toss it across your body, up past your other shoulder. Catch it with the other hand.
2. Do it 10 times. Toss left to right, then right to left.
3. Two scarves. Hold one in each hand. Toss the right one. When it's at the top, toss the left one.
4. Catch them in order. Practice 10 times.
5. Three scarves (20 minutes). Hold 2 in your right hand, 1 in your left.
6. Toss right. Toss left. Toss right again. Keep going!
7. Count your throws. Write your high score.

- Juggle to music and keep the beat.

- Make a juggling show with a costume and a silly name.

- Scarves go everywhere: throw higher and slower.
- You drop a lot: everyone does! Stop, breathe and try 3 more.
- You lose the order: say "right, left, right" out loud.

### Show it off
Put on a juggling show for the family with 3 scarves.

### Clean up
Fold the scarves and put them back.

### For the grown-up
Juggling builds coordination, focus and practice habits, and it is good for both sides of the brain. **Ask at dinner:** "What's your juggling high score?"

## 27. Rain Stick Storm Song

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Music | 60-90 min | Indoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** a long cardboard tube (paper towel or wrapping paper tube), foil (about 2 feet), dry rice or dry beans (about half a cup), paper, tape, scissors, markers

**Nice to have:** colored paper, yarn, a second tube

### The big idea
Tip it up and hear the rain! A rain stick is an old instrument that sounds like a storm. Build one, then use it to play a song about a storm that starts small and gets wild.

### Goals
- You build a rain stick that makes a rain sound.
- You play a "storm song" with a beginning, middle and end.

### Objectives
- I can build an instrument and change its sound.
- I can play soft and loud, slow and fast.
- I can plan a song with parts that change.

### Get ready
Clear a table. Trace a tube end on paper twice. Cut two circles a little bigger than the end.

### Steps
1. Tape one paper circle over one end of the tube. Tape it all around, tightly.
2. Crunch the foil into a long, twisty snake. Then coil it like a spring.
3. Slide the foil spring into the tube. It slows down the falling rice.
4. Pour in a handful of rice. Cover the open end with your hand. Tip it slowly. Listen!
5. Add or remove rice until you like the sound. Then tape the second end on.
6. Decorate. Wrap it in paper and draw stripes, raindrops or lightning.
7. Write a storm song (15 minutes). Part 1: a few drops (tip slow). Part 2: a downpour (tip fast and shake). Part 3: it fades away.
8. Add other sounds. Tap fingers for drops. Clap for thunder.

### Make it yours
- Make a second rain stick with beans. Compare the sounds.
- Write words for your storm song.
- Make a family storm band: one person claps, one snaps, you play the rain stick.

### Stuck?
- Rice falls too fast: add more foil to slow it down.
- The sound is too quiet: add a little more rice.
- The ends pop off: use more tape, wrapped around the sides.

### Show it off
Turn off the lights. Play your storm song for the family.

### Clean up
Sweep up spilled rice. Put leftover rice back in the bag.

### For the grown-up
This builds music (dynamics, tempo and form) and simple sound science. Keep rice and beans away from small children who might choke. **Ask at dinner:** "How did you make your storm get louder?"

## 30. Egg Carton Seed Game

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Game | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1+ |

**Materials:** an egg carton (12 cups), 48 small things like dry beans or buttons or pebbles, 2 small bowls, markers

**Nice to have:** a partner (a sibling, a friend or a grown-up after work), paint to decorate

### The big idea
This game is thousands of years old. People played it in the dirt with seeds and stones. It's all about counting and planning ahead. Build the board, learn the game, then invent your own twist.

### Goals
- You build a game board and play at least 3 games.
- You invent a new rule and test it.

### Objectives
- I can count and plan ahead to win.
- I can follow and explain game rules.
- I can change a rule and see how it changes the game.

### Get ready
Open the egg carton. Set a bowl at each end. These are the "stores." Put 4 beans in each cup.

### Steps
1. Sit with the carton between you and your partner. Each player owns the 6 cups on their side. The bowl on your right is yours.
2. On your turn, pick up all the beans from one of your cups.
3. Drop one bean in each cup going right, around the board, like a circle. Drop one in your store when you pass it. Skip the other player's store.
4. If your last bean lands in your store, take another turn!
5. Does your last bean land in an empty cup on your side? Take it and all the beans across from it. Put them in your store.
6. The game ends when one side is empty. The other player puts their leftover beans in their store.
7. Count your store. Most beans wins!
8. Invent a twist (15 minutes). Example: "If your last bean lands on 3 beans, you get 2 more turns." Test it.

### Make it yours
- Decorate the carton like a treasure chest.
- Play solo against yourself, switching sides.
- Use 5 beans in each cup to see if the game changes.

### Stuck?
- Rules confuse you: play one turn at a time, slowly, saying each step.
- No partner: be both players. Try to make the best move for each side.
- Beans roll: use flat things like buttons or pasta.

### Show it off
Teach the game to the family. Hold a tournament with a paper medal.

### Clean up
Pour the beans into a bag. Close the carton.

### For the grown-up
This is a version of an ancient "sowing" or "count and capture" game played around the world, especially in Africa. It builds counting, strategy and planning ahead. Keep beans away from small children. **Ask at dinner:** "What's your best strategy to win?"
