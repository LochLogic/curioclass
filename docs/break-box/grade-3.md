## 1. Spoon Catapult

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build | 60-90 min | Either | Low | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** 7 pencils, 6 rubber bands, a plastic spoon, tape, scrap paper, a cup or bowl for a target, a few books

**Nice to have:** pom-poms, a tape measure, craft sticks instead of pencils

### The big idea
A grumpy dragon guards a castle across the room. Your only weapon is a spoon! Build a catapult that flings paper boulders over the castle wall. How far can you make one fly?

### Goals
- You build a catapult that really works.
- You land a shot in a target cup from across the room.

### Objectives
- I can build a lever that flings things.
- I can change one part and test if it goes farther.
- I can measure and compare my shots.

### Get ready
Clear a space on the floor about 3 big steps long. Find 7 pencils and 6 rubber bands. Crumple 10 paper balls. Make each one the size of a grape.

### Steps
1. Stack 5 pencils in a bundle. Wrap a rubber band tight around each end.
2. Put 2 more pencils together. Wrap a rubber band around one end only. Now they open like a mouth.
3. Slide the bundle between the 2 open pencils. Push it close to the wrapped end.
4. Cross a rubber band over the middle in an X. This holds it all together.
5. Tape the spoon to the top pencil. The spoon bowl goes at the far end, facing up.
6. Load a paper ball. Hold the base down. Push the spoon down and let go!
7. Make it better (20 minutes). Slide the bundle closer to the end, then farther. Add a rubber band. Change only one thing each time. Which shot flew the farthest?
8. Stack books into a castle wall. Set the target cup behind it. How many of 10 shots land in the cup?

### Make it yours
- Build a second catapult and hold a catapult battle with a family member.
- Make a moving target: a toy on a string that someone pulls slowly across the floor.
- Invent a scoring board: 1 point for over the wall, 5 points for the cup.

### Stuck?
- The spoon falls off: wrap more tape around the spoon handle, tight and flat.
- Shots go sideways: push the spoon down straight, not at a slant.
- Shots are short: move the bundle closer to the open end of the arm.

### Show it off
Give your family a "siege show" when they get home. Let each person try one shot at the castle.

### Clean up
Pick up every paper ball. Keep the catapult in a box for next time.

### For the grown-up
This builds early ideas about levers and force, plus fair testing (change one thing at a time). Paper balls and pom-poms only: no hard objects, and never aim at faces or pets. **Ask at dinner:** "What one change made your catapult shoot the farthest?"

## 2. Invent a Card Game

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Game | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1+ |

**Materials:** 8 sheets of paper or old cereal box cardboard, scissors, markers or crayons, a pencil

**Nice to have:** index cards, a deck of playing cards to borrow ideas from

### The big idea
Every card game was made up by someone. Today that someone is you! Make your own deck, with your own pictures and your own rules. Then teach it to your family.

### Goals
- You make a deck of at least 24 cards.
- You write rules so clear that someone else can play.

### Objectives
- I can write rules in the right order.
- I can test my game and fix what does not work.
- I can explain my game to a new player.

### Get ready
Fold each sheet of paper in half 3 times. Unfold it. Cut along the folds. Each sheet makes 8 cards. Make 24 to 32 cards.

### Steps
1. Pick a theme. It could be space aliens, snacks, sea monsters or anything you love.
2. Make 4 groups of cards. Give each group a color or a symbol. Example: 6 red rocket cards, 6 blue planet cards.
3. Number the cards in each group, 1 to 6.
4. Add 2 to 4 special cards. Maybe a "Black Hole" card that makes someone lose a turn. You decide what they do.
5. Choose how to win (10 minutes). Collect sets? Get rid of all your cards? Have the highest number? Write it at the top of a rules page.
6. Write the rules in 5 to 8 short lines. Start with "Deal 5 cards to each player."
7. Play it alone first. Play two hands yourself. Fix any rule that feels broken or boring.
8. Give your game a name and draw a box or envelope for it.

### Make it yours
- Add a "trade" rule so players swap cards with each other.
- Make a solo version where you play against a timer or the deck.
- Draw a tiny story on each special card, like "The alien steals your snack!"

### Stuck?
- The game ends too fast: deal more cards or make the goal harder.
- The game goes on forever: add a card that ends the round.
- You can't think of a rule: borrow one from a game you know. Then change it a little.

### Show it off
Teach your game to the family at dinner. You are the boss of the rules!

### Clean up
Put the cards and the rules page in an envelope. Throw away the paper scraps.

### For the grown-up
Inventing a game builds writing, sequencing and number sense, and testing it builds patience with fixing mistakes. Let your child explain the rules without help, even if it takes a while. **Ask at dinner:** "What rule did you change after you tested it?"

## 3. Write and Perform a Puppet Play

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pretend | 60-120 min | Indoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** old socks or paper lunch bags, paper, markers, scissors, glue or tape, a table and a blanket for a stage

**Nice to have:** yarn for hair, buttons for eyes (glue them on tight), a flashlight for a spotlight

### The big idea
Puppets can say things people never would. A sock can be a pirate. A paper bag can be a grumpy king. Write a short play, make the cast, and put on a real show.

### Goals
- You make 2 or 3 puppets with their own voices.
- You write a play with a beginning, a problem and an ending.
- You perform it for an audience.

### Objectives
- I can write a story with a problem and a fix.
- I can write lines for each character to say.
- I can use my voice to show how a character feels.

### Get ready
Tip a table on its side, or drape a blanket over two chairs. Kneel behind it. That is your stage. Put all your puppet supplies on a tray.

### Steps
1. Make 2 or 3 characters (20 minutes). Give each one a name and one big feeling. Maybe a brave mouse and a nervous dragon.
2. Pick a problem. The cake is missing. The king lost his crown. A storm is coming.
3. Plan 3 scenes on paper. Scene 1: meet the characters. Scene 2: the problem gets worse. Scene 3: they fix it.
4. Write the lines (20 minutes). Put the name, then what they say. Example: "MOUSE: Who ate my cheese?"
5. Give each puppet a special voice. Squeaky, deep, slow or fast. Practice each one.
6. Rehearse the whole play twice. Read from your script. Keep puppets above the stage edge.
7. Make a ticket for each person in the audience.

### Make it yours
- Add a sound effects scene: knock on the table for a door, crinkle paper for fire.
- Let the audience vote on the ending, and write two endings to choose from.
- Make one puppet a narrator who talks to the audience.

### Stuck?
- Puppets won't stay on your hand: tape a loop of paper inside the sock or bag.
- You forget your lines: tape the script to the back of the stage.
- Too many puppets for two hands: have one character "leave" before the next one comes in.

### Show it off
Hand out tickets and perform for the family after dinner. Take a bow with your puppets.

### Clean up
Put the puppets and script in a bag. Fold the blanket. Pick up the scraps.

### For the grown-up
This builds story structure, writing dialogue and speaking up in front of people. Scissors are used, so stay nearby. Socks you don't mind losing are best. **Ask at dinner:** "Which puppet was the most fun to be, and why?"

## 4. Cipher Wheel Secret Codes

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Puzzle | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** a cereal box or thin cardboard, a bowl about 6 inches wide, a small cup about 4 inches wide, a pencil, a ruler, scissors, a paper fastener or a pushpin and an eraser

**Nice to have:** colored markers, an envelope

### The big idea
Spies send messages no one else can read. You need a cipher wheel. Spin it, and every letter turns into a different letter. Only someone with the same key can crack it!

### Goals
- You build a cipher wheel that turns.
- You send a secret message and someone decodes it.

### Objectives
- I can use a key to change letters into code.
- I can decode a message by working backward.
- I can make my own secret key.

### Get ready
Open the cereal box flat. Trace the big bowl to make one circle. Trace the small cup to make a smaller circle. Cut both out. Ask a grown-up for help with thick cardboard.

### Steps
1. Put the small circle on top of the big one. Push a paper fastener through both centers. Or poke a hole with a pushpin into an eraser below.
2. Write the 26 letters around the edge of the big circle. Space them evenly, like clock numbers. Start A at the top.
3. Write the 26 letters around the small circle too. Line them up with the big letters.
4. Pick a key. Turn the small wheel so small A sits under big D. Your key is "D."
5. Write a short message, like "MEET ME AT THE COUCH."
6. Code it. Find each letter on the big wheel. Write the small letter under it.
7. Give the coded note and the key to someone. Can they crack it? (15 minutes)
8. Now try a new key. Write a mystery for someone to solve.

### Make it yours
- Make a secret spy name and sign every message with it.
- Hide coded clues around the house that lead to a treasure.
- Use symbols instead of letters on the small wheel, like stars and moons.

### Stuck?
- Letters don't line up: draw lines from the center like pizza slices first, 26 slices.
- The wheel won't turn: make the hole a little bigger.
- Decoding is confusing: go the other way. Find the small letter, write the big one.

### Show it off
Leave a coded note at each family member's plate. Give them the key after dinner.

### Clean up
Recycle the box scraps. Keep your wheel in an envelope marked "TOP SECRET."

### For the grown-up
A cipher wheel uses the same idea as early real codes. It builds spelling, careful matching and logic. The pushpin is sharp, so stay nearby. **Ask at dinner:** "How would someone crack your code without the key?"

## 5. Our House Newspaper

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story | 60-120 min | Indoors | Low | On your own | 1+ |

**Materials:** 2 big sheets of paper (or 4 regular sheets taped together), a pencil, markers, a ruler, scissors, tape

**Nice to have:** old newspapers to look at, junk mail pictures to cut out

### The big idea
Big news happens at your house every day. The cat knocked over a cup. Someone burned the toast. A new sock went missing! You are the reporter. Make the first edition of your home newspaper.

### Goals
- You make a newspaper with at least 4 stories.
- You interview someone and quote what they said.

### Objectives
- I can write a news story that tells who, what, when and where.
- I can write a headline that grabs a reader.
- I can ask questions and write down answers.

### Get ready
Fold your big paper in half like a real newspaper. Draw lines with the ruler to make boxes for stories. Leave a strip at the top for the name.

### Steps
1. Name your paper. Try "The Kitchen Times" or "The Daily Hallway." Write it big across the top.
2. Hunt for news (15 minutes). Walk the house. What changed? What broke? What is new?
3. Write the top story. Answer who, what, when and where. Keep it to 5 sentences.
4. Write a headline. Use strong words, like "MYSTERY CRUMBS FOUND ON COUCH!"
5. Interview someone. Ask 3 questions. Write their exact words in quotes. A pet counts too, if you make up the answers.
6. Add 2 more parts. Pick from: weather from the window, a comic, a pet report, a fake ad, a recipe, a sports page about the family.
7. Draw a picture for the top story. Write a short caption under it.

### Make it yours
- Write a "from the future" edition: news from your house in the year 2100.
- Add a puzzle corner with a maze or a word hunt.
- Make an advice column. Answer a letter from a worried stuffed animal.

### Stuck?
- Nothing happened today: report on something small as if it were huge.
- Your stories don't fit: write smaller or make page 2.
- No one to interview: interview yourself about your break.

### Show it off
Deliver the paper to a grown-up's chair. Read the headline out loud like a news reader.

### Clean up
Put the markers away and recycle scraps. Keep each edition in a folder.

### For the grown-up
This is real writing practice dressed up as play: main idea, details, headlines and quotes. Let your child report on the household's funny moments, and be a good sport if you are in the news. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the biggest story in your newspaper today?"

## 6. Hallway Solar System

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** paper, markers or crayons, scissors, tape, a long string or yarn (about 10 feet), a ruler, a big plate and a coin to trace

**Nice to have:** a tape measure, a flashlight, a ball for the Sun

### The big idea
Space is HUGE. The planets are far, far apart. Turn your hallway into the solar system. Walk from the Sun to Neptune and feel how big space really is.

### Goals
- You make the Sun and all 8 planets.
- You lay them out in the right order with real spacing ideas.

### Objectives
- I can name the 8 planets in order from the Sun.
- I can tell which planets are big and which are small.
- I can use a scale to show long distances in a small space.

### Get ready
Find the longest hall or room in your home. Clear the floor. Lay the string down the middle, end to end.

### Steps
1. Make the Sun. Trace the big plate and color it yellow and orange. Tape it at one end of the string.
2. Make the planets (20 minutes). Small ones first: Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars. Make each about the size of a coin.
3. Make the giants. Jupiter and Saturn are big, about the size of your hand. Uranus and Neptune are smaller, about the size of a cookie.
4. Color each one. Mars is red. Jupiter has stripes. Saturn needs rings. Earth gets blue and green.
5. Use this spacing in footsteps from the Sun: Mercury 1, Venus 2, Earth 3, Mars 4, Jupiter 13, Saturn 24, Uranus 48, Neptune 75. Use small baby steps if your hall is short.
6. Tape each planet by the string at its spot. Notice the first 4 are crowded. The rest are very far!
7. Make a sign for each planet with one fun fact. Example: "A day on Venus is longer than its year."

### Make it yours
- Add a dwarf planet or a comet far past Neptune.
- Make an alien for one planet and tell what it eats.
- Give a "space tour." Shine a flashlight Sun and walk visitors down the hall.

### Stuck?
- Hall too short: use baby steps, or go around a corner.
- Can't remember the order: say "My Very Eager Mother Just Served Us Noodles."
- Planets keep falling: tape them to the floor, not the wall.

### Show it off
Give your family a guided space walk. Stop at each planet and read the sign.

### Clean up
Peel the tape slowly. Stack the planets in order in a folder.

### For the grown-up
This builds science vocabulary, order, and a feel for scale, which is hard for kids (and adults). Use painter's tape on walls or floors. **Ask at dinner:** "Which planet would you visit, and what would you pack?"

## 7. Junk Drawer Wind Chime

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Music | 60-90 min | Either | Low | Nearby | 1 |

**Materials:** a clothes hanger or a sturdy stick, string, scissors, 6 to 8 metal things that ring (old spoons, forks, keys, jar lids, metal cookie cutters, bottle caps with holes), tape

**Nice to have:** beads, a ruler, a nail for a grown-up to poke holes in lids

### The big idea
Some things clink. Some things ring. Some things go bong! Hunt your home for old metal things that make cool sounds. Then build a wind chime that plays its own song.

### Goals
- You build a wind chime that rings when it moves.
- You put the sounds in an order you like, from low to high.

### Objectives
- I can tell high sounds from low sounds.
- I can test how length and size change a sound.
- I can plan a pattern for my chime.

### Get ready
Ask a grown-up which old metal things you may use. Put them in a pile on a towel. Cut 8 pieces of string, each as long as your arm.

### Steps
1. Sound test (10 minutes). Tap each item with a spoon. Is it high, low, short or long?
2. Line them up from lowest sound to highest.
3. Tie a string to each one. Tie it tight with a double knot. For lids, a grown-up can poke a hole.
4. Tie the strings to the hanger or stick. Leave about 2 finger-widths between each.
5. Try different lengths. Make some strings long and some short. Things need to bump each other to ring.
6. Add a "clapper" in the middle. Hang a big spoon or key a little lower. It hits the rest when wind blows.
7. Hold it up and blow, or shake it gently. Move pieces until it sounds good to you.
8. Hang it by a window, on a porch, or on a door handle.

### Make it yours
- Make it play a pattern: low, high, low, high, all the way across.
- Build a "silly chime" that only uses things from the kitchen drawer.
- Hang beads above each piece so the string looks like a necklace.

### Stuck?
- Pieces slip out of knots: wrap tape around the knot.
- It doesn't ring: hang pieces closer together so they touch.
- The hanger tips: move a heavy piece to the other side to balance it.

### Show it off
Hang it where the family will hear it. Play them a "wind song" by blowing on it.

### Clean up
Put the spoon and leftover string away. Sweep up any bits.

### For the grown-up
This builds listening skills and early ideas about sound (pitch, size and vibration), plus balance. Check which metal items can be used. If a lid needs a hole, you make it with a nail and hammer. **Ask at dinner:** "Which piece made the lowest sound, and why do you think so?"

## 8. Jar Terrarium

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 60-90 min | Either | Medium | Nearby | 1 |

**Materials:** a big clear jar or plastic bottle with the top cut off, small pebbles, soil, a spoon, small plants or moss or seeds, water in a cup, newspaper to work on

**Nice to have:** a small toy for a tiny world, a spray bottle, activated charcoal from a fish store

### The big idea
What if you could hold a whole tiny world in your hands? A terrarium is a mini garden in a jar. It makes its own rain! Build one, and give it a tiny story too.

### Goals
- You build a living mini garden in a jar.
- You see water form on the jar walls like rain.

### Objectives
- I can build layers that help plants grow.
- I can tell what plants need to live.
- I can watch and write down what changes.

### Get ready
Lay newspaper on a table or work outside. Wash the jar. With a grown-up, go to a yard or park to dig a little soil. Find moss, small weeds or tiny plants.

### Steps
1. Make the bottom layer. Put pebbles in the jar about 1 inch deep. This catches extra water.
2. Add soil. Spoon in soil about 2 inches deep. Pat it flat.
3. Plan your world (10 minutes). Where do the plants go? Will there be a hill, a path, a pond of pebbles?
4. Plant. Poke a hole with your finger. Set the plant in. Press soil around it.
5. Add your extras: a flat rock, a twig log, a small toy.
6. Water a little. Use about 3 spoons of water. Do not flood it.
7. Close the lid or cover it with plastic wrap. Set it in bright light, but not hot sun.
8. Draw your terrarium. Check it tomorrow. Do you see drops on the glass? That is the water cycle!

### Make it yours
- Make it a dinosaur land or a fairy village with a tiny sign.
- Plant seeds from the kitchen, like a bean, and watch it sprout.
- Make two jars. Put one in light and one in a darker spot. Which grows better?

### Stuck?
- No plants outside: use seeds, or a cutting from a houseplant (ask first).
- The glass gets very foggy: open the lid for an hour.
- Plants droop: check if the soil is too dry or too wet.

### Show it off
Give your world a name. Show it to the family and tell them who lives there.

### Clean up
Shake the newspaper into the trash or yard. Wash the spoon and your hands.

### For the grown-up
This builds life science ideas: what plants need, layers, and the water cycle. Go with your child to collect soil and plants, and have them wash hands after. If you cut a bottle, do the cutting yourself. **Ask at dinner:** "Where does the water in the jar go, and where does it come back?"

## 9. Toy Zipline

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build | 60-90 min | Either | Low | Nearby | 1 |

**Materials:** string or yarn (about 12 feet), a small toy figure or stuffed animal, a paper cup, a pipe cleaner or paper clip, tape, a straw, scissors, 2 sturdy anchor spots (a chair and a doorknob)

**Nice to have:** a clothes hanger hook, a small basket, a timer

### The big idea
A small toy is stuck on the far side of the room. The only way out? A zipline! Build a ride that zooms your toy safely from up high to down low.

### Goals
- You build a zipline that carries a toy from start to end.
- You make the ride faster or slower on purpose.

### Objectives
- I can test how a slope changes speed.
- I can build a carrier that stays on the line.
- I can solve problems when my design fails.

### Get ready
Pick a start spot that is high, like a chair back. Pick an end spot that is low, like a table leg. Clear the space under the line.

### Steps
1. Thread the straw onto the string. This is your slider.
2. Tie the string high at the start. Pull it tight and tie it low at the end.
3. Build a seat (15 minutes). Tape a paper cup under the straw. Or make a hook from a paper clip.
4. Put your toy in. Let it go! Did it get to the end?
5. Test the slope. Move the low end lower. Is the ride faster? Move it higher. Is it slower?
6. Make a safe landing. Put a pillow at the end so your toy doesn't crash.
7. Design a second carrier. Try a basket, a pipe cleaner seat, or a cup with a seat belt. Which is fastest?
8. Time 3 rides. Count "one-banana, two-banana." Write the best time.

### Make it yours
- Make it a rescue mission: deliver a "medicine" bead across the canyon.
- Add a brake: a rubber band near the end that slows it down.
- Build a two-way line with a string to pull the carrier back up.

### Stuck?
- The toy stops in the middle: make the slope steeper or pull the string tighter.
- The cup tips over: tape a coin to the bottom for weight.
- The string sags: tie to something heavier, or ask a grown-up to hold one end.

### Show it off
Run a "zipline park" with tickets. Let each family member pick a toy to ride.

### Clean up
Untie the string and wind it around a piece of cardboard. Put the toys away.

### For the grown-up
This builds early physics (slope, gravity, friction) and problem solving. Keep the line below head height and out of walkways so no one trips or walks into it. Outdoors, tie between a fence and a chair. **Ask at dinner:** "What made your zipline faster?"

## 10. No-Cook Lunch Menu

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | 60-90 min | Indoors | Medium | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** bread or tortillas or crackers, 2 or 3 fillings you have (cheese, sliced meat, hummus, cream cheese), fruit and vegetables that need no knife (grapes, bananas, baby carrots), a butter knife, plates, paper and markers

**Nice to have:** cookie cutters, toothpicks, a small apron

### The big idea
Welcome to your own restaurant! Today you are the chef. Plan a menu, make lunch with no stove, and serve it to a customer. Five stars or bust!

### Goals
- You make a real menu with prices and pictures.
- You make and serve a lunch with no cooking.

### Objectives
- I can plan a meal with different food groups.
- I can follow my own steps to make food.
- I can use a butter knife safely.

### Get ready
Wash your hands. Ask a grown-up what food you may use. Clear a counter. Lay out a cutting board and plates.

### Steps
1. Name your restaurant. Try "Cafe Crunch" or "The Hungry Bear."
2. Make the menu (15 minutes). Fold paper in half. Write 3 mains, 2 sides and 1 drink. Draw each one.
3. Give each item a fun name, like "Rolling Tortilla Log" or "Rainbow Fruit Cup."
4. Add prices in pretend money. Use 1 to 5 coins.
5. Make your food. Spread, roll, stack and slice with the butter knife. Use cookie cutters for fun shapes.
6. Plate it nicely. Put each food in its own spot. Add a napkin.
7. Take an order. Hand your customer the menu. Write their choice on a pad.
8. Serve it! Ask, "How is everything?"

### Make it yours
- Make a theme lunch: a pirate lunch, a space lunch, or an "all round foods" lunch.
- Make a "mystery plate" the customer must eat without knowing the name first.
- Invent a new sandwich and name it after yourself.

### Stuck?
- Bread tears when you spread: use softer spread, or let the butter warm up.
- Not sure what to make: pick one bread, one filling, one fruit. Done!
- No customer yet: serve lunch to a row of stuffed animals, then eat it yourself.

### Show it off
Serve a grown-up at lunch or after work. Ask them to leave a star rating on your menu.

### Clean up
Wash the plates and knife. Wipe the counter. A real chef leaves a clean kitchen.

### For the grown-up
This builds planning, reading a recipe they wrote themselves, and kitchen safety. A butter knife only; please cut anything hard or round, like grapes for little ones or whole apples. Check allergies before your child serves anyone. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the best seller at your restaurant?"

## 11. String Telephone

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Science | 45-75 min | Either | Low | Nearby | 2+ |

8. Make a cover with a title, like "The Girl Who Ate a Whole Volcano."

### Make it yours
- Write a tall tale about your pet or a stuffed animal.
- Tell it out loud in a grand old storyteller voice.
- Make a tall tale "team-up." You meet another tall tale hero and have a contest.

### Stuck?
- Can't think of a trait: what do people say about you? Start there.
- It's not funny yet: make it even bigger! Then bigger again.
- Writing is slow: draw first, then write one sentence under each picture.

### Show it off
Read your tall tale out loud to the family. Ask, "Is it true?" and keep a straight face.

### Clean up
Put the book on the shelf with real books. Put pens away.

### For the grown-up
Tall tales are a real story type kids study in school. This builds exaggeration, describing words and story order. If you have a family story that grows each time it's told, share it first. **Ask at dinner:** "What was the biggest thing you did in your tall tale?"

## 23. Nature Paintbrushes

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Art | 60-90 min | Either | High | Nearby | 1 |

**Materials:** 5 or 6 sticks about as long as a pencil, rubber bands or string, things from outside (leaves, pine needles, grass, feathers, flower heads), paint or watercolors, paper, a cup of water, an old towel

**Nice to have:** a big piece of cardboard to paint on, an old shirt to wear

### The big idea
Who needs a store brush? Nature has hundreds of them! Make brushes from leaves, grass and pine needles. Then see what wild marks each one makes.

### Goals
- You make at least 5 different nature brushes.
- You paint a picture using marks from every brush.

### Objectives
- I can build a tool from natural things.
- I can compare the marks different tools make.
- I can plan a picture using texture.

### Get ready
Go outside with a bag. Collect sticks and brush stuff. Ask a grown-up before you pick any flowers. On a balcony or indoors? Use a dish brush, string, a cotton ball and kitchen herbs.

### Steps
1. Make brush one. Bunch some grass at the end of a stick. Wrap a rubber band tight around it.
2. Make 4 more. Try pine needles, a big leaf, a feather, a flower head. Each stick gets one kind.
3. Put a little paint on a plate. Put each color in its own puddle.
4. Test page (15 minutes). Make one line and one dot with each brush. Write the brush name under it.
5. Which made the best grass? Best rain? Best fur? Write a word next to each test.
6. Plan a picture. A wild jungle? A storm at sea? A furry monster?
7. Paint it. Use at least 4 brushes. Let the brush shape the picture.
8. Lay it flat to dry for 20 minutes.

### Make it yours
- Paint only with your brushes and your fingers. No store brushes.
- Make one giant brush with a long branch and paint on cardboard outside.
- Use the brushes to paint rocks for a garden path.

### Stuck?
- The brush falls apart: wrap the rubber band 3 times, or use string.
- Paint is too thin: use less water.
- Marks all look the same: press harder, twist the brush, or drag it sideways.

### Show it off
Hang your painting with the test page next to it. Ask the family to guess which brush made each mark.

### Clean up
Rinse the brushes. Put the nature bits back outside. Wash the plate and your hands.

### For the grown-up
This builds art skills (texture, line, planning) and lets kids be inventors of their own tools. Stay nearby for outdoor collecting, and check that plants are safe to touch. Paint can stain, so an old shirt helps. **Ask at dinner:** "Which nature brush surprised you the most?"

### The big idea

### Goals
- You draw a giant maze with one true path.
- Someone tries your maze and you time them.

### Objectives
- I can plan a path and hide it with dead ends.
- I can test my maze to make sure it works.
- I can tell a story with a map.

### Get ready
Tape 4 sheets together into one big square. Clear a big table or floor spot.

### Steps

5. Add story traps. A cat in a dead end. A sticky spill. A secret tunnel that jumps across.

### Clean up

### For the grown-up

### For the grown-up

| Kind | Time | Where | Mess | Grown-up | Players |

**Materials:** 3 or 4 paper cups, a flashlight, a pushpin or a sharp pencil, a pencil, paper, tape

### The big idea
Long ago, people saw pictures in the stars. A hunter! A bear! A swan! Make a cup that shines stars on your ceiling. Then invent your own star pictures and tell their stories.

### Goals
- You make 3 cups that shine star patterns on the ceiling.
- You invent one new star picture and tell its story.

### Objectives
- I can name a star picture, called a constellation.

- I can tell how light makes the dots on the ceiling.

Put the pushpin back in its box. Stack the cups. Recycle scraps.

### For the grown-up

## 30. Dragon Egg Nursery

| Pretend | 60-90 min | Indoors | Low | Nearby | 1+ |

**Materials:** a ball or a rolled-up sock for the egg, a shoebox or basket, old towels or scarves, paper, markers, tape, a small notebook, a flashlight

**Nice to have:** foil to make the egg shiny, a stuffed animal to be the baby dragon, a timer

### The big idea
You found a strange, warm egg under your bed. It is a dragon egg! The note next to it says: "Keep it safe until it hatches." You are now the dragon keeper. Build a nursery and get ready for the baby.

### Goals
- You build a cozy nest and a nursery for your dragon egg.
- You keep a care log and "hatch" your dragon at the end.

### Objectives
- I can plan what a baby animal needs to be safe and healthy.
- I can write a log with times and notes.
- I can invent a creature with its own needs and powers.

### Get ready
Pick your egg. Wrap a ball or sock in foil or color paper for it. Clear a quiet corner for the nursery.

### Steps
1. Decide your dragon kind. Fire, ice, forest, ocean or cloud? Write it on the egg.
2. Build the nest (15 minutes). Line the box with towels. Fire dragons like it warm. Ice dragons like it cool. Pick a good spot.
3. Make a care chart. What does a baby dragon need? Warmth, quiet, a song? Write 5 things.

### Make it yours
- Draw a "baby book" page with the dragon's size, color and first sound.
- Invent a dragon language with 5 words, like "skree" for hungry.
- Make it twins: two eggs that need different things.

### Stuck?
- The egg rolls away: put it in a sock ring or a cup.
- You run out of care ideas: think about how you take care of a pet or a baby.
- Waiting is boring: make a dragon toy for the baby while you wait.

### Show it off
Hold a "hatching party" when the family gets home. Introduce your baby dragon and read from your log.

### Clean up
Fold the towels. Keep the nest box for your dragon's bed.

### For the grown-up
This builds caring thinking, writing a log with times, and inventing a creature with rules that make sense. It's a gentle, imaginative one for a quiet afternoon. **Ask at dinner:** "What does your baby dragon need most to be happy?"
