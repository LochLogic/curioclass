# Lens: science

Pre-work P5a (docs/PRODUCTION-PATH.md), written 2026-11-13. The expert reads this before any science progression,
course frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day. It is a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is the science
rubric P5b adds. The standard is the Next Generation Science Standards (data/standards/ngss-pes.txt). Grades 6 to
8 adapt OpenSciEd Middle School (CC BY 4.0) as the base text, in its 18-unit order (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13); K to
5 and 9 to 12 are Curio Class's own, with OpenSciEd elementary and high school as reference only.

## What good teaching of science looks like

**Three dimensions, used together.** Each performance expectation joins a core idea, a science or engineering
practice, and a crosscutting concept (patterns, cause and effect, scale, systems, energy and matter, structure and
function, stability and change). Students learn the idea by doing the practice and noticing the concept. Telling
the idea, then "doing a practice" on something unrelated, is one dimension at a time. A performance expectation is
an end point the unit reaches, not a lesson title.

**A phenomenon drives the unit.** Each unit opens on something real students can observe and cannot yet explain,
or a design problem they cannot yet solve. The class builds a question board from what it notices and wonders, and
the unit answers those questions. A good anchor is specific, puzzling at the grade and explainable with the unit's
core ideas, not a topic heading. A local phenomenon may replace it (the science-4 swap rule).

**The storyline.** Each lesson answers a question the last one raised and ends on one that needs the next. Students
can say why they are doing today's work. Content arrives when it is needed; reading, teacher explanation and
simulations all have a place once the need is felt.

**Models are built and revised.** An initial model early, revised as evidence arrives, and a class consensus model.
A model shows mechanism (what we cannot see), not just labelled parts. A model that does not change after an
investigation means the investigation did not matter.

**Argument from evidence.** Claims are backed by data the class collected or read, with reasoning tied to a core
idea. Engineering units weigh designs against criteria and constraints.

**Investigations are planned and fair.** Students help choose what to vary, hold steady and measure. Data are
recorded, graphed and read for patterns before conclusions. A no-kit data path or simulation gives real data where
a class cannot investigate, said plainly to be weaker (the three materials tiers).

**Transfer at the end.** The end-of-unit task is a phenomenon or problem the class has not seen.

**Safety.** Every investigation sheet names hazards and controls for the grade. Household substitutes only where
safe; heat, flame, sharp tools or chemicals only with adult steps; eye protection where anything can splash or
fly. A year safety sheet as well. Living things handled humanely.

**Plain facts.** BASELINE rule 5 holds: evolution, climate change and the age of the Earth are taught as the
evidence shows, without "some say" softening. Values rule (b), facts and backed sources decide claims, applies.

## Errors reviewers catch

**Progression**
- A core idea is owned by two grades at the same depth, or a K-2 to 3-5 to middle school handoff skips a band.
- A grade assumes a model (particles, energy transfer, food webs) that no earlier grade builds.
- A practice (planning investigations, using math, models) grows in no grade, or is only "named" across the band.
- A crosscutting concept is listed everywhere and developed nowhere.

**Course frame**
- A performance expectation for the grade has no unit, or a unit claims one it only touches.
- Grades 6 to 8: a unit departs from OpenSciEd's order with no recorded reason, or the redraw drops a topic the old
  order taught (the check DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13 requires).
- High school: a course claims band expectations with no plan for which course owns which.
- No unit names its anchor phenomenon or design problem.

**Scope and sequence**
- A unit's anchor is a topic ("Energy") not a phenomenon.
- A model is used before the unit that builds it.
- Engineering design expectations appear in no unit.
- Equipment is assumed with no substitute or no-kit path.

**Unit**
- The question board's questions are not the questions the lessons answer.
- No initial model, or no point where the model is revised.
- Lessons in an order that only the teacher can explain; a lesson has no "why are we doing this" link.
- The end-of-unit task reuses the anchor or a lesson's phenomenon, so it tests memory, not transfer.
- The assessment tests the core idea alone, with no practice or crosscutting concept in the items.
- Content above the grade band (the standards' assessment boundaries), or below it.

**Day**
- The day's investigation answers no question the class asked.
- A practice in name only ("students model" with a labelling worksheet).
- Data with no pattern to find, or conclusions given before data are read.
- A hazard with no control on the student sheet, or a household substitute that is not safe.
- The day leaves no written evidence the teacher can act on (APPROACH principle 8).
