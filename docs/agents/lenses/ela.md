## What good teaching of ELA looks like

**The text is the unit.** In ELA a unit is built on a text or a set of texts, not on a standard. Standards are
practised through the reading, writing and talk the texts call for.

**Complex text, and the staircase.** Every student reads grade-level complex text regularly, with support, not
only text at their "level". Complexity has three legs: a measure, a qualitative judgment (meaning, structure,
language, knowledge needed) and the reader and task. Demand climbs across the year and the grades toward college
and career text. Supports come before, during and after reading and match what makes this text hard. A simpler
rewrite read in its place is not a support.

**Building knowledge through topic sets.** What a reader knows shapes what they understand. Units gather texts on
one specific topic (not a broad theme like "courage") so knowledge and words pile up, reaching across a year into
history, science, the arts and literature.

**Close reading with text-dependent questions.** Questions can only be answered from the text, and move from what
it says, to how it says it, to what it means beside other texts. Hard parts are reread on purpose.

**Writing from sources.** Most writing draws on what students read. Writing is taught directly, with models, the
process (plan, draft, revise, edit) and sentence work drawn from the anchor texts. Short on-demand pieces and
longer process pieces both appear, and short research projects recur.

**Foundational skills, K to 2 above all.** Reading words is taught explicitly and systematically: phonological
awareness, letter-sound correspondences in a planned order, blending and decoding, then spelling patterns,
syllables and word parts, with decodable text that matches what has been taught, then fluency practice. Students
are never taught to guess a word from a picture or context in place of reading it. Daily read-alouds of rich text
build knowledge and vocabulary while decoding catches up. Grades 3 to 5 still teach long words and fluency.

**Speaking and listening.** Discussion is a taught routine: norms, roles, sentence frames that fade, students
answering each other with evidence. Talk comes before writing and helps it.

**Vocabulary.** Words that carry across texts and subjects (general academic words) are taught from the texts,
met before, during and after reading, and come back later. Word-learning strategies (context, word parts,
reference) are taught. Domain words come with the topic.

## Errors reviewers catch

**Progression**
- A reading or writing skill climbs nowhere: the same task at the same text demand in two grades.
- K to 2: a phonics element is used in decodable text before the grade that teaches it, or a pattern is never
  taught.
- Grade 3 assumes foundational skills grade 2 does not finish, with no grade 3 phonics and fluency plan.
- Topic sets do not connect across grades, or repeat the same topic at the same depth.
- The writing types (argument, informative, narrative) are not balanced across the grades.

**Course frame**
- A unit is named by a skill ("main idea") or a broad theme, not by a specific topic and its texts.
- The year's anchor texts are not on file, or have no measure, no qualitative reason and no reason for their place.
- The year's texts lean to one era, one tradition or one kind (all literary, all excerpts).
- No long text read whole across the year.

**Scope and sequence**
- Text demand does not climb across the year, with no stated reason.
- Research appears once or never; writing through the full process appears once or never.
- Discussion is formal too rarely to be "often".
- K to 2: the phonics scope and sequence is missing, or runs apart from the decodable texts.

**Unit**
- Fewer than 80 percent of questions and tasks need the text.
- A support replaces the text (a summary read in its place) or has no teacher note.
- No word list from the texts, or words taught once and never met again.
- The end task does not join reading with writing or speaking on the unit's topic, or has no scoring guidance.
- A text with sensitive content has no teacher note (BASELINE rule 8) or no sensitivity read.

**Day**
- The day opens with a long activity before the text; reading is not the main work.
- A question answerable from general knowledge or the picture alone.
- Writing asked for with no model and no sentence work that day or before.
- K to 2: a decodable text with patterns not yet taught, or a cue to guess from the picture.
- The exit ticket is not about the day's text.
