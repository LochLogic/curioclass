# Lens: physical education

Pre-work, 2026-11-16. The expert reads this before any PE course frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day. It is
a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is the PE part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md. The standard is SHAPE
America, 2024 (data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt, four standards), reviewed with CDC's PECAT 2019, which is keyed to
the earlier five (docs/standards-notes/pe-2014-to-2024.md). The grade-outcome rule and the appropriate practice
points are in docs/courses/physical-education/STANDARD.md. The approach page is
docs/courses/physical-education/APPROACH.md (approved 2026-11-21).

## What good teaching of PE looks like

**Outcomes, not a year of games.** Every unit serves stated grade outcomes for the standards, has content that
teaches them, and checks them. A game is a setting for a skill, not the lesson.

**Skills are built in order.** A skill is learned as a pattern with a few key cues, then used in changing
conditions, then combined and used in games, dance and routines. Skills come back across the year and the years.
A young child is not asked for a mature overhand throw before the parts are taught.

**Most of the class moving most of the time.** No lines, no waiting for a turn, enough equipment for pairs or small
groups, quick set-ups. At least half of the lesson is moderate to vigorous activity. Talk is short and given while
students are ready to move.

**A safe place to try.** Students can fail and try again without being watched by everyone. No exercise as
punishment. No elimination games where the weakest sit first. No games with people as targets. No public team
picking. Competition is small-sided, and students can choose how competitive a setting they play in.

**Every body included.** Each lesson adapts for disabilities, for a range of skill and fitness, and for short-term
injury. Activities are not split by gender, and the language is gender neutral. Activities come from many
cultures.

**Fitness is learned, not judged.** Students learn what fitness is and how to build it. Fitness measures are for
personal goals, done privately, never graded and never posted.

**Assessment shows growth.** Skill, knowledge and conduct are checked against cues and outcomes and reported as
growth from the student's own earlier work, not as a rank. Grades never rest on dress, attendance, effort alone or
a fitness score.

**It works in a real space.** Plans fit a gym, a field or a small room, a class of thirty and little equipment,
and say what to do on a wet day.

## Errors reviewers catch

### Scope and sequence

- A year of games or activities with no outcomes stated.
- A grade with no outcome for one of the four standards.
- A span indicator placed in no year.
- Skills out of developmental order (a complex combination before its parts).
- Dance or fitness taught once and never again.

### Unit

- No cues for a skill, or more than two or three cues in one lesson.
- Game play before the skill is taught.
- A check that ranks students, or a fitness test used as a grade.
- No adaptation for disabilities or a range of skill.
- Equipment the room cannot have, with no low-equipment option.
- Indicator wording copied from SHAPE America (ids and our own words only).

### Day

- Lines and waiting; one ball for a whole class.
- Elimination, human targets, public team picks, exercise as punishment.
- Static stretching before a warm-up.
- Long talk before moving.
- A student told to sit out, with no role.
- A lesson with no closure and no evidence.
- Unsafe space: too many students for the room, hard surfaces with no plan, heat with no water break.

### Across subjects

- PE and health both teaching, or neither teaching, the physical activity outcomes.
- PE dance and the dance course teaching the same thing in the same year.

## What this lens does not cover

- Individual adapted PE plans: the school's specialists.
- Medical clearance and conditions (asthma, heart, injury): the school nurse and families.
- State fitness tests and state requirements: the school.
