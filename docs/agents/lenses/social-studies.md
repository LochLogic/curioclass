# Lens: social studies

Pre-work P5a (docs/PRODUCTION-PATH.md), 2026-11-13. The expert reads this before any social studies progression,
course frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day. It is a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is the social studies
part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md (and P5b). The standard (docs/courses/social-studies/STANDARD.md): C3 for
skills, Massachusetts 2018 as content anchor, checked against California, Tennessee and New York, under the values rule.

## What good teaching of social studies looks like

**The inquiry arc runs every unit.** C3 sets four moves: a compelling question no lookup settles; the
discipline's concepts and tools (civics, economics, geography, history); sources and evidence; communicating
conclusions and, where it fits, taking informed action. A unit question is honest only if the unit's sources can
answer it and more than one answer can be defended. Supporting questions break it into day-sized parts.

**Inquiry rests on taught content.** Students cannot weigh evidence about something they know nothing about.
Knowledge is taught directly and in order. History runs in time order unless the scope and sequence gives a reason.

**Sources are read as evidence.** Students ask who made a source, when, for whom and why (sourcing), what was
happening around it (context), and whether other sources agree (corroboration). Primary and secondary sources both
appear, at a reading level right for the grade. Where an event has more than one account, the unit carries more
than one.

**Claims come with evidence and reasoning.** A claim answers the question, evidence comes from named sources, and
reasoning says why the evidence supports it. Older students answer a counterclaim. It is taught and modelled, and
grows from a spoken "I think, because" in kindergarten to a sourced argument in high school.

**Historical thinking has named parts.** Causation (causes have kinds and weights). Continuity and change (what
changed, for whom, what stayed). Perspective (explain past views without adopting them). Context (an act is judged
against its time and its alternatives). Chronology.

**Geography and economics are ways of reasoning.** Geography asks where, why there, and so what, read from maps,
data and images. Economics asks how people choose under scarcity: costs and benefits, incentives, trade, markets
and government.

**Civics builds dispositions as well as knowledge.** How government works, rights with responsibilities, how to
deliberate with people who disagree, and how to take informed action. Action is a student choice informed by
evidence, never a cause the course picks (the adoption bar sets it at once a year per course).

**The values rule, in order** (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12). (a) Where a court has ruled, side with the higher court.
(b) Facts and backed sources decide claims, the discipline's evidence first. (c) People are taught with dignity,
respect, kindness and openness; we do not hide humanity, and LGBTQ people appear where history includes them.
(d) Grade level and life experience set how and when a subject is taught.

**Sensitive and hard history.** BASELINE rule 5: facts stay plain, with the human side; a hard fact is never
softened until it is false. Rule 8: teacher notes name where the standards touch a sensitive topic. Native and
hard-history content in a course needs a qualified person's read (FOUNDATION section 7) and a fresh-agent
sensitivity read (REFINE.md). The grade 3 outline shows the pattern: Native nations in the present tense as well
as the past, from their own materials, sacred or restricted knowledge left out; slavery named plainly, with
resistance and lives built; contested origin stories taught from the record and from the people concerned today.
No role play or simulation that puts a child in a victim's or perpetrator's place.

## Errors reviewers catch

**Progression**
- A C3 skill (sourcing, corroboration, claim and evidence) is owned by no grade, or by two grades at the same depth.
- A grade assumes a skill (reading a timeline, a map grid, a source's purpose) that no earlier grade hands on.
- Chronology breaks across grades with no stated reason (grade 7 ends about 500, grade 9 must pick up there).
- Geography or economics appears only as content, with no growing reasoning skill across the grades.

**Course frame**
- An anchor standard in the course's Massachusetts topic range has no unit.
- The replaced grade 3 year, or "your state" in grade 8, keeps Massachusetts-only content.
- No unit lists what the check set (California, Tennessee, New York) adds, or an addition has no recorded source.

**Scope and sequence**
- A unit question can be answered without the unit's sources, or has only one defensible answer.
- History out of time order with no reason given.
- A contested event, and the unit gives one telling only.
- Writing from sources appears once a year or never builds from short to long.

**Unit**
- No compelling question, or supporting questions that do not add up to it.
- Sources are textbook summaries only; no primary source, or no source with a stated reading level and reason.
- A sensitive topic carries no teacher note (rule 8), or Native or hard-history content is not flagged for the
  sensitivity read.
- The end-of-unit assessment tests recall only, with no task done from sources.
- A values-rule miss: a claim decided by opinion where a court ruling or backed sources settle it; a group present
  in the history left out; content above what the grade's age suits.

**Day**
- The day's source has no sourcing step (who, when, why) before students use it.
- A question on the student page can be answered without reading the source.
- A claim is asked for with no model of claim, evidence and reasoning earlier in the unit.
- A hard fact softened, a slur printed (rule 6), or a role play that puts a child in a victim's or perpetrator's place.
- The exit ticket checks a fact when the goal was a skill, or a skill when the goal was a fact.
