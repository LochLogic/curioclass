# Lens: English language development

Pre-work for ELD (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md, the ELD row), 2026-11-16. The expert reads this before any ELD
course frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day. It is a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is part 10 of
ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md. The standard: docs/courses/eld/STANDARD.md (WIDA 2020, ids and our own words only). The
approach: docs/courses/eld/APPROACH.md. Building waits until C11 is proven in the core courses. English learner
supports inside other courses are C11, read by those courses' lenses; this lens covers the ELD course.

## What good teaching of ELD looks like

**Language through content.** Students learn English by using it on things worth knowing: the grade's science,
social studies, math and stories. The content is the grade's own, made reachable with help, not swapped for easier
material. ELD time is not remedial reading and not an ELA lesson repeated.

**A clear language goal.** Each lesson names the key language use (narrate, inform, explain, argue) and the forms
that carry it: words, sentence patterns, linking words, how a text is put together. The goal is about language, on
content.

**Forms taught directly.** The teacher shows the form in a real text, names it, explains how it works and why the
writer used it, and the class tries it. Taking one sentence apart and rebuilding it is a strong routine.

**Talk comes first, and often.** Students speak more than the teacher. Structured talk with a named routine and
frames happens every lesson, before writing. Oral language gets real time, especially for younger students and
newcomers.

**Levels, not one size.** The same task is set at more than one proficiency level. Help (frames, word banks,
pictures, talk in the home language) is planned per level and withdrawn on a named day. Newcomers, long-term English
learners and English learners with disabilities each get notes.

**The home language is a resource.** Cognates, comparing how two languages do the same thing, and thinking or
drafting in the home language first all speed English. A student's language and culture are strengths.

**Language need or learning need.** A teacher should be able to tell a gap in English from a gap in content or a
learning disability. The teacher page helps with that and never assumes a disability from slow progress in English.

## Errors reviewers catch

**Course frame and scope and sequence**
- Units built on word lists or grammar drills with no content behind them.
- One level for all students, or levels that change the content rather than the help.
- A WIDA id used for a standard, cluster or key language use the file does not hold.
- No entry and exit points stated, or a course that stands in for ELA.
- The ELA pairing missing for an LA unit, or the host core unit missing for an MA, SC or SS unit.

**Unit**
- No structured talk planned, or talk with no frames.
- The end-of-unit assessment tests content knowledge, not the language goal, or has one level only.
- The error register lists errors with no reason or remedy, or treats home language transfer as a fault in the
  student.
- No notes for newcomers, long-term English learners or English learners with disabilities.

**Day**
- A simplified text replaces the grade's text with no reason.
- The teacher talks most of the lesson.
- A frame that is never withdrawn, or withdrawn with no day named.
- An exit ticket a student can pass without using the day's form.
- WIDA wording copied into a page (ids and our own words only), or a product or service name in public copy.
- A home language treated as a problem, or a student's accent or dialect corrected as an error of meaning.
