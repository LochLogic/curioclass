# Lens: library and research skills

Subject pre-work, 2026-11-16, in the shape of the core lenses (P5a, docs/PRODUCTION-PATH.md). The expert reads this
before any library frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day. It is a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is part 11
of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md (ours, untested). The standard is in docs/courses/library/STANDARD.md: AASL second
edition, with the Empire State continuum as the grade check set.

## What good teaching of library and research skills looks like

**An inquiry cycle every year, at growing depth.** Ask, find, judge, use, share, reflect (the continuum's connect,
wonder, investigate, construct, express, reflect). Young students do the whole cycle with a lot of help on a small
question; older students do it alone on a hard one.

**Skills are taught on real work.** Every unit hosts a task from another course the same grade is doing (a science
question, a social studies source, an ELA research piece). Research skills taught on nothing are forgotten.

**Judging a source means leaving it.** Students learn to open new tabs and ask what others say about a site or claim
before reading it closely (lateral reading), to find the original (go upstream), and to skip the first result when
it is an ad (click restraint). A checklist run on the page itself is the old method and fails on polished fakes.

**Questions drive searching.** Students turn a topic into questions, pick key words, try, look at what came back and
change the search. Library catalogues, databases, reference works and the open web are each taught for what they are
good at.

**Honest use of others' work, every band.** Saying where an idea came from (K to 2), a simple list of sources (3 to 5),
paraphrase, quotation and one citation style (6 to 8), copyright, licences, fair use and AI-made text (9 to 12).

**Making and sharing.** Research ends in a product for a real audience, and students judge their own process.

## Errors reviewers catch

**Progression and frame**
- A one-off orientation or "library skills week" instead of a year of spaced units (bar fails if).
- A grade with no named priority skills, or a skill owned twice at the same depth, or a skill the continuum places
  two or more grades later taught with no reason.
- Lateral reading owned by both library and social studies with no line saying who teaches it first.

**Scope and sequence and unit**
- No host task from another course named.
- Source judging taught as a website checklist (domain endings, a nice design, an about page) without lateral moves.
- A citation unit with no research to cite.
- The end-of-unit assessment is recall of terms, with no search, judgement or citation actually done.
- AASL competency text copied, or a claim of AASL alignment.

**Day**
- A live web page relied on with no dated capture or teacher swap note.
- Students sent to a paid database or a library they may not have, with no free path (no library access is assumed;
  Cory, 2026-11-11).
- A product, platform or search company named on a student page.
- A claim that the method raises scores in general (one field study supports lateral reading in high school only).

## What this lens does not cover

The scored bar. Reading for pleasure and literature (ELA). Id checks (scripts/standards.py). Whether a source may be
adapted (BASE-TEXTS.md and the scout).
