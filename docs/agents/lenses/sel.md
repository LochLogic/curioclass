course frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day. It is a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is part 9 of

## What good teaching of SEL looks like

**Skills, taught like skills.** A good session names one skill, gives it steps and a reason, models it, and has
every student practise it. Programs that work in studies share four features: a connected sequence, active
practice, a set focus of time on the skills, and explicit skill goals (SAFE). A session of talk about values with no
rehearsal is the common failure.

**Practice by doing.** Self-management and relationship skills are rehearsed: a calming routine done with the body,
an I-statement said to a partner, a disagreement worked through on a scene card. Self-awareness, social awareness
and decision-making grow through reflection and talk about invented cases.

**A sequence by grade, with depth that grows.** Each grade year differs, and each sub-skill comes back at more
depth: a picture-book moment in kindergarten, a named strategy with reasons in grades 3 to 5, a choice under real
pressures in 6 to 8, a plan the student designs and judges in high school.

**The skill leaves the session.** The teacher uses the skill's name and cue in the rest of the day, and the family
note gives one thing to try at home. A skill used only in SEL time does not carry.

**A safe room, not a confessional.** Cases are invented. Sharing is a choice, and making one up is always allowed.
Checks score a skill, never a feeling, a trait or a home life. No surveys, no screening, no data kept (FOUNDATION
promise 2).

**SEL is not therapy.** Plain body and thinking skills, with no clinical words used of a child and no claim to
treat anything. A disclosure of harm or risk follows health's steps (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student
tells you something"): the school's reporting route and counselor, named in every unit's teacher notes. Mental health topics, substances and sexual health are health's.

**The values rule, in order** (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12, as social studies applies it). (a) Where a court has
ruled, side with the higher court. (b) Facts and backed sources decide claims. (c) Dignity, respect, kindness and
openness; families of every kind appear where families appear. (d) Grade level and life experience set how and
when. In SEL this means: fairness and bias taught as noticing and checking one's own snap judgments on invented
cases, never as guilt or innocence assigned to a group; unfair norms of the past taught from settled facts; student
voice in class and school life, with no cause picked by the course.

## Errors reviewers catch

**Course frame and grade sequence**
- A grade year that repeats another year of its band with the same cases and the same depth.
- A sub-skill missing from a grade, or met once with no return.
- Minutes a week not stated, or a short path that drops units rather than sessions.
- No "carry it out" cue or family activity for a unit.
- A unit a school could not leave out without breaking later units.

**Scope and sequence**
- Self-management or relationship skills met only by discussion, with no rehearsal.
- A skill's name or steps changed between units of one year.
- A topic that belongs to health (substances, sexual health, suicide) taught as SEL content rather than pointed to.
- A sub-skill taught above the grade's age (systems and unfair norms before grade 5 beyond playground fairness).

**Unit**
- No end-of-unit assessment of the skill, or one that scores a feeling, a trait or a disclosure.
- A sensitive topic (family, identity, loss, mental health) with no teacher note and no reporting-route line.
- Growth mindset taught as "believe and your grades rise" rather than practice and help.
- Calming routines with religious or spiritual framing.

**Day**
- A prompt that needs a personal disclosure to answer ("tell about a time your family...") with no invented option.
- A role play that casts a child as victim or perpetrator, or uses a real classmate.
- An activity that sorts or ranks students by identity or circumstance.
- Clinical labels for a child or a feeling ("that is anxiety", "you are depressed").
- A product, program, app or registry name, or a claim of being rated or evidence-based.
- The exit ticket asks how a student feels instead of what the skill's step is.
