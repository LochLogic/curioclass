## Where it comes from

The Guidelines for Assessment and Instruction in Statistics Education (GAISE): the College Report (2016) and the
Pre-K-12 Report II (2020), both published by the American Statistical Association. Their text is not ours: nothing
below is copied, nothing is quoted, and no Curio Class page names them or claims to follow, meet or be endorsed by
them. What follows is our own reading, used to check ourselves.

## What good teaching with data looks like, in one paragraph

Statistics is taught as a way of answering questions when data vary. Students pose a question that data can answer,
plan how to get or choose the data, analyse it, and say what the answer means and how far it reaches; they go round
that cycle many times, not once at the end of the year. Every idea is met first in a real data set with a context
and a purpose. Understanding comes before procedure: why a method works and when it fails matters more than its
formula. Students do the work in class, in pairs and groups, with simulation and technology used to see ideas
(sampling variation, randomisation, the sampling distribution) and to handle real data sizes. Assessment checks
reasoning in context, and is used to steer the teaching as well as to grade it.

## Checks for a read

**The investigative cycle**
- Each unit names where students pose a question, collect or consider data, analyse it, and interpret it; a unit
  that only analyses supplied numbers says so and why.
- Questions are statistical (they expect variability), not single-answer lookups.
- The year's investigation runs the whole cycle, with its scope of inference stated (frame's Investigation bullet).

**Variability and context**
- Variability is the thread: in data, in samples, between groups, from chance; no summary is taught without its
  spread.
- Every data set has a real context, a free source and an "as of" date, or is labelled invented for practice.
- More than one variable is looked at together often (two-way tables, scatterplots, grouped comparisons, a third
  variable that changes the story).

**Concepts before recipes**
- A procedure's conditions and its meaning are taught and assessed, not only its steps.
- Inference rests on random sampling or random assignment, and each conclusion says which one it rests on
  (generalising to a population, or cause and effect, or neither).
- Simulation and randomisation come before or beside the formula for sampling distributions, intervals and tests.
- A p-value and a confidence level are read correctly in context, with what they do not say.

**Active learning and technology**
- Students produce and argue from data in class; a day is not mostly the teacher computing.
- Technology is used to explore and to handle real data, with the paper path the frame names; no owned outcome
  depends on one product.

**Assessment**
- Items ask for interpretation in context, a choice of method with its reason, and a judgement of a study's design,
  not only a computed number.
- Reports and projects are scored after a draft, written feedback and a revision.
- End-of-unit results feed a named next step (the respond day).

**Data ethics and privacy**
- No class collects students' own sensitive data (money, health, body, home location, identity); public or supplied
  data stand in (frame's Real data and Sensitivity bullets).
