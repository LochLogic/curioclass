# Lens: arts

Pre-work, 2026-11-16. The expert reads this before any arts course frame, scope and sequence, unit plan or day
in visual arts, music, theater, dance or media arts. It is a way of seeing, not a bar: the bar is the arts part
of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md. The standard and the band-year rule are in docs/courses/arts/STANDARD.md
(National Core Arts Standards, 2014, ids only). The approach page is docs/courses/arts/APPROACH.md (approved 2026-11-20).

## What good teaching of the arts looks like

**Four processes, every course.** The standards sort the arts into creating, performing (presenting in visual
arts, producing in media arts), responding and connecting. A good year moves through all four, and most units
touch three. A year of only making, or only appreciation, is not an arts course.

**Technique is taught, not hoped for.** Expression grows from skill. The teacher names a technique, shows it in
steps, and has students practise it with feedback before they use it in their own work. The steps get harder
across the units and across the years: a K to 2 line becomes a contour drawing becomes a gesture study.

**The discipline's words do work.** Each discipline has its own words (value, tempo, blocking, levels, framing).
They are taught on the day they are needed, then used when making and when talking about work. A response with no
discipline words is an opinion, not arts learning.

**Students make their own work and show it.** Copying a model is a step, not the end. Each unit leaves room for
choice in what is made or studied, and ends with the work shown or performed to someone. The showing can be
small: the class, a partner class, a family evening, a hallway wall.

**Critique is a routine, not a free-for-all.** Feedback follows a set protocol: describe what you see or hear,
ask, then suggest, tied to the unit's look-fors. Students revise after feedback and say what they changed.

**Works from many times and places, with their setting.** Each year draws works from more than one culture and
period, and from living artists as well as old ones. A work is taught with when, where, by whom and for what,
not as a picture on a slide. No single tradition is the default.

**Assessment watches growth.** Kept work over time (a portfolio, a recording log) shows growth better than a
single score. Finished work and performances are scored with a guide that names the technique and the words.
Students assess themselves against the same guide.

**The room matters.** Arts happen in real rooms with real limits: one or two sessions a week, a cart instead of
a studio, thirty students and one set of drums. Good plans fit the session, list the equipment, set up and clean
up inside the time, and say how to use the space safely.

**The performing is in the room.** A screen can show and explain. It cannot rehearse a scene or a dance with a
class. Plans put the doing with people and the tool in a supporting role.

## Errors reviewers catch

### Scope and sequence

- A grade's ids placed in the wrong year of the band, or a high school level tied to a grade (STANDARD.md 3).
- A year with one artistic process missing.
- Works studied all from one tradition or all from one century.
- Technique that does not get harder across units or years.
- A plan that needs a daily period in K to 8.
- No showing in a unit.

### Unit

- A making task with no technique taught first ("express your feelings in paint").
- Response work with no discipline words, or with words that were never taught.
- A work studied with no setting: no time, place, maker or purpose.
- Critique with no protocol, or feedback that is praise only.
- No revision after feedback.
- An end-of-unit assessment that is a vocabulary quiz instead of a making, showing or response task.
- A unit that copies the coalition's enduring understandings, essential questions or cornerstone tasks (they
  need permission; we write our own).

### Day

- Set-up and clean-up that do not fit the session.
- Equipment the room may not have, with no low-cost option.
- Unsafe use of space (dance and theater: room to move, floor, footwear; visual arts: blades, heat, fumes).
- A student who must perform alone with no other way to take part.
- The word "respond" used for the artistic process on a respond day: a respond day acts on a check (GLOSSARY).
- A tool asked to judge a finished work or a performance.

### Across the arts

- The five disciplines planned as one: each has its own techniques, words and works.
- A dance unit that double-teaches what PE teaches in its dance units, or a music unit that teaches fractions
  instead of leaning on math (STANDARD.md 6).

## What this lens does not cover

- Whether a particular work is suitable for a grade or community: the course page's local note and the values
  rule apply.
- Instrument and voice pedagogy at depth (a qualified music teacher's call).
- Copyright of the works studied: the open material scout and each source's own terms.
