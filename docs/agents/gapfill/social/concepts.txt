# social gap fill: one tool per new module (module_id|title|format|family|concept)
social-studies-1-wonder-kite|Wonder Kite|showdown||The class picks a big question it cares about (Why does our town have a library?) and says why; each small question, tagged with a map, clock, rule or coin badge, becomes a bow that holds one fact and lifts the kite.
social-studies-2-how-do-you-know|How Do You Know?|showdown||A puppet reads lines from a picture book and a toy poster about the zoo; the class calls each line a fact or an opinion, asks the puppet how it knows, and picks which source would help answer the class question.
social-studies-1-teddy-bear-makers|Teddy Bear Makers|builder||Put the steps of making a teddy bear in order, give each step its tool or machine and the worker with the right skill, and count what one bear costs to make; then the class quizzes the bear maker about the explanation.
social-studies-2-storm-helpers|Storm Helpers|story||After a big storm the class walks a street to see who is affected and how (no power, a flooded road, a closed school), meets the people already helping, then picks a way kids can help and makes a poster about it.
social-studies-2-pen-pal-parcels|Pen Pal Parcels|explorer||Parcels arrive from pen pals in Ghana, Chile and Japan, each with something made there that we buy and a note on how their place changed this year; the class sends back something made here and pins each parcel on the globe.
social-studies-1-old-objects-as-sources|Capsule From 1965|explorer||Open the time capsule a class buried under the school in 1965 and pull out a photo, a letter, a lunch ticket and a toy catalog page; the class finds who made each, when and where, asks its own questions, and compares what kids liked then with now.
social-studies-2-the-last-carousel-ride|The Last Carousel Ride|mystery||The town carousel stopped running in 1975; a rider and the owner's grandson remember why in different ways, and an old newspaper adds a clue. The class compares the tellings, lists possible reasons and picks the most likely.
library-k-2-many-voices-book|Many Voices Book|studio||Each child adds a page to one class book: a greeting from home, a favorite food, a game they play. The class reads it together, finds what is the same and different, and decides how to order the pages so everyone is in.
social-studies-4-inquiry-ladder|Inquiry Ladder|builder||A big question about the past (Was the railroad good for our town?) sits at the top; students climb by adding supporting questions drawn from old photos, timetables and letters, mark words people disagree about, and see each rung help.
social-studies-5-source-scout|Source Scout|sort||For the question Should recess be longer?, scouts choose which sources to gather (a doctor's article, a survey, a toy ad, the principal's letter), weigh who might disagree, then sort each source's lines into fact and opinion to rate its trust.
social-studies-4-made-for-whom|Made for Whom?|explorer||Three sources from the state's past open one at a time (a settler's diary, a railroad poster selling land, a newspaper editorial); students infer each one's audience and purpose, spot how the maker's view shaped it, and rank which helps most.
social-studies-5-why-the-pony-express-ended|Why the Pony Express Ended|mystery||Evidence cards from a rider's letter, a telegraph company notice, a cost ledger and a newspaper ask why the Pony Express closed after only 18 months; students build a claim, back it with evidence from two or more sources, and defend it.
social-studies-5-history-article-review|History Article Review|explorer||Students read short kid-magazine articles about the Revolution, write each one's main claim in a sentence, then check its argument and explanation (enough evidence? steps in order? voices missing?) and give a reviewer's rating with reasons.
social-studies-3-school-fix-it-meeting|School Fix-It Meeting|showdown||The class picks a school problem (a long lunch line, a crowded hallway), surveys classmates and adults on why it matters to them, talks proposals through with turn-taking rules, and votes on a plan to bring to the principal.
social-studies-5-tools-for-change|Tools for Change|cards||Cards show ways people have changed things, from petitions and boycotts to letters and voting, each with a real past and present example; students pick strategies for a local problem, predict results, and turn the plan into a letter or poster.
social-studies-3-pool-repair-vote|Pool Repair Vote|roleplay||The town pool needs repairs; students hear a grandparent, a swim team parent, a shop owner and a teen lifeguard, follow the plan from problem to hearing to vote, and choose how to pay for it: taxes, entry fees or both.
social-studies-3-ripple-street|Ripple Street|story||Make one kid's Saturday choices on Ripple Street (rake leaves for pay or go to the park, plant flowers, play loud music) and see the costs and benefits to the kid, the rewards and fines that nudge each choice, and the ripples on neighbors.
social-studies-4-barter-bay|Barter Bay|simulator||Fishers, bakers and potters on Barter Bay trade by swapping until shells become money and trading speeds up; then the class adds too many shells, too few, or closes the cannery to see inflation, deflation and unemployment.
social-studies-4-learn-and-earn|Learn and Earn|tycoon||Run a kid's bike repair business for a year: spend weeks learning new skills or buying better tools, and watch how many bikes you fix and what you earn compared with a friend who never trains.
social-studies-5-interest-seesaw|Interest Seesaw|simulator||Put birthday money in a bank, a credit union or a savings bond and watch interest add up, then borrow for a bike and watch interest add on; a seesaw shows when interest works for you and when it works against you.
social-studies-4-bike-fund-chances|Bike Fund Chances|cards||Save toward a bike with chance cards each month: keep money in a jar or buy shares in the class juice company that may grow or shrink; then lock, insure or risk the bike as rain, flat tires and a theft come up.
social-studies-5-cocoa-chain|Cocoa Chain|simulator||Follow cocoa from a farm in Ghana through a port and a factory to a chocolate bar here; when drought hits the farms, students explain step by step, with prices and numbers, who is affected in each country and why.
library-3-5-windows-and-mirrors-shelf|Windows and Mirrors Shelf|sort||Students sort book blurbs into mirrors (lives like mine) and windows (lives different from mine), notice which kind their own reading lacks, then build a stack across genres and formats with both and say what they hope to find.
social-studies-7-panel-of-experts|Panel of Experts|explorer||One big question (Was the Black Death a turning point?) goes to a radio panel: a historian, an economist, a geographer and a political scientist. Students mark where the experts agree, each field's key ideas, and the smaller questions each would ask.
library-6-8-rabbit-hole-notebook|Rabbit Hole Notebook|explorer||Start from a question you are curious about and follow short linked sources; each one raises new questions, and the notebook draws how the big question and the smaller ones reshape each other until you choose where to stop.
social-studies-7-historians-disagree|Historians Disagree|explorer||Two short secondary accounts explain why the Mongol Empire grew so fast; students name each central argument, sort the kinds of sources each relied on (chronicles, letters, archaeology), and judge which argument holds up better.
social-studies-8-evidence-corkboard|Evidence Corkboard|mystery||Pin evidence from letters, maps and toll records to answer why the Erie Canal changed New York; students string each pin to the claim, tag what each piece cannot show, and list the sources they would look for next.
social-studies-7-unlabeled-archive|Unlabeled Archive|puzzle||An archive box holds a map fragment, a letter, a coin and a woodcut with no labels; students use dated reference sources to work out who made each, when, where and for whom, then decide which help answer the archivist's question.
social-studies-8-voices-on-the-vote|Voices on the Vote|timeline||Dated voices from 1789 to 1870 argue about who should vote: property owners, all white men, women, free Black men. Students place each on a timeline, trace how and why views shifted, and notice whose voices were left out.
social-studies-6-museum-label-writer|Museum Label Writer|studio||Write the label for a Mesopotamian clay tablet that explains how writing began, in order and with details; fix sample labels that jump around, then rewrite yours for a kids' trail card, an audio guide and the museum website.
social-studies-8-levers-of-power|Levers of Power|simulator||A dangerous crossing near school is also a state road problem and a worldwide traffic safety issue; students try levers (petition, testify, vote, write the paper, organize) through the roles citizens play and judge what each can change.
social-studies-8-motion-on-the-floor|Motion on the Floor|roleplay||Run a student club meeting with an agenda, motions, amendments and a vote while respecting the minority, then compare how the same choice would be made by a city council, a jury and a family.
social-studies-8-shaping-our-week|Shaping Our Week|explorer||Follow a family through one week and tap each group that shapes it: the city council's bus routes, the company where a parent works, that parent's union, the food bank, the youth soccer league; remove one and see what changes.
social-studies-6-smoke-and-honey|Smoke and Honey|simulator||A paper mill sells cheap paper while its smoke sends neighbors to the clinic, and a beekeeper's hives help nearby orchards for free; students try a tax, a filter rule or a subsidy and watch families, the business and the town.
social-studies-6-border-trade-post|Border Trade Post|simulator||Two neighboring countries swap goods at a border post: first by barter, then with money, then under a tariff on imported bikes; students tally time lost, trades made and who gains or pays: shoppers, bike makers, workers and the other country.
cte-6-8-snow-season-prices|Snow Season Prices|tycoon|market|Run the shops of a ski town through one winter: a big snow, a new rental shop and hotels short of workers move the prices of lift tickets, rentals and wages; students play buyers and sellers and explain each change.
cte-6-8-economy-checkup|Economy Checkup|data||Read real unemployment, inflation and output data from the last 30 years the way a doctor reads a chart, then see how each high-inflation or job-loss year hit four neighbors differently: a saver, a borrower, a retiree and a job seeker.
cte-6-8-interest-rate-dial|Interest Rate Dial|simulator||Turn one interest rate dial and watch four people respond: a saver's account grows faster or slower, a car buyer's payment changes, a credit card balance swells, and a cafe owner decides whether to borrow to expand.
cte-6-8-startup-shares|Startup Shares|simulator||Spread a pretend $1,000 across five made-up young companies with new ideas, or a basket of all of them, and fast-forward ten years: some inventions flop, some take off, and the basket shows how spreading out changes risk.
cte-6-8-e-bike-buyer|E-Bike Buyer|story||Choose an e-bike from ads, reviews and a friend's advice, add up the true cost (charger, lock, helmet, repairs), then decide on a warranty, insurance or a repair fund as crashes, thefts and flat tires come up.
social-studies-6-the-shrinking-lake|The Shrinking Lake|timeline||Slide through sixty years of Lake Chad as it shrinks with drought and irrigation and the population around it grows: herders, farmers and fishers move, land use changes, and four countries choose whether to fight over it or share it.
library-9-12-enduring-questions|Enduring Questions|explorer||Turn something you wonder about (Should voting be required?) into a question that reflects an enduring issue such as liberty versus order, then read short expert positions and map where they agree and disagree before choosing supporting questions.
library-9-12-source-portfolio|Source Portfolio|builder|research|Assemble a portfolio of sources for a contested question that covers a range of views; each source card shows origin, authority and context, and the portfolio scores how well sources corroborate each other and how experts cite and review them.
social-studies-11-missing-voices-archive|Missing Voices Archive|explorer||A town archive on the 1918 flu holds the mayor's notices and the newspaper but few words from nurses, immigrant families or Black neighborhoods; students map whose voices are missing, how that limits the story, and which sources could fill gaps.
social-studies-11-behind-the-documentary|Behind the Documentary|explorer||A short documentary script on the Great Depression comes with its source list; students trace each claim to its sources, flag sources that don't fit the claim, then compare the film's central argument with a historian's article for accuracy.
social-studies-10-claim-repair-shop|Claim Repair Shop|puzzle||Sample arguments and explanations about why World War I began arrive broken: evidence that contradicts itself, steps out of order, claims too vague; students critique each for credibility and reasoning, then repair the claim with stronger evidence.
social-studies-12-causes-web|Causes Web|builder||Pick a problem that is local and global at once (high rents, plastic waste) and build a web of causes from economics, geography, civics and history cards, then test whether the explanation holds up as a timeline, a chain or a loop.
social-studies-12-jury-room|Jury Room|roleplay||Twelve jurors decide a civil case about a damaged delivery; students take seats, follow deliberation rules (listen, cite evidence, check bias), reach a verdict, then compare jury deliberation with a town hall and a consensus circle.
social-studies-9-layers-and-scales|Layers and Scales|map||Make thematic maps from real data (languages, rainfall, income) at world, country and county scale, change the class breaks and watch patterns appear or vanish, then pair the map with satellite images and photos to explain a place's politics and economy.
social-studies-11-suburb-boom|Suburb Boom|simulator||Watch a metro area grow from 1945 to today on aerial images as highways, home loans and zoning rules arrive and decide who can buy where; farms become subdivisions, wetlands are paved and later flood, and students change one decision to see the pattern shift.
social-studies-10-climate-on-the-move|Climate on the Move|timeline||Four cases on a timeline (the Little Ice Age, the Dust Bowl, Sahel droughts and a recent hurricane) show how long-term climate change and sudden disasters move people, shift farming and trade, and reshape politics far away.
