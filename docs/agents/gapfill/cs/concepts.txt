# CS gap fill: one tool per new module (module_id|Tool Title|format|family|concept)
computer-science-k-2-computing-then-and-now|Attic Gadget Box|timeline||The class opens a grandparent's attic box (a rotary phone, a paper map, a cassette player) and slides each gadget to the device that does its job today, then meets the people, from many places and abilities, who designed the new one to solve a problem.
computer-science-k-2-asking-with-data|Question Jar|sort||The class pulls questions from a jar and sorts them: can we find out by counting, measuring or asking, or is it an opinion? Then they pick how to collect, numbers or words, see who the plan leaves out, and reword the question.
computer-science-k-2-devices-at-home-school-and-work|Who Uses This Device?|explorer||The class visits a street fair of grown-ups at work (a baker, a vet, a bus driver, an artist, a coach) and taps each one to see the device they use and what it helps them do; second graders match a hobby to a computing job.
computer-science-k-2-using-tech-well|Tablet Buddy|story||The class guides a small classroom pet through a school day with the shared class tablet, choosing at each turn (gentle hands, headphones, its own login, a break after a long video), and sees how each choice helps or hurts the pet and its friends.
computer-science-k-2-fair-rules-for-turns|Line Leader Picker|simulator||The class writes the rule for choosing the line leader (ABC order, draw a name, draw and put it back, take turns around the circle), runs a pretend month of school days, and counts who led often and who never got a turn.
computer-science-k-2-signs-and-changing-values|Morning Meeting Board|showdown||The class reads the picture signs on the morning board (weather, lunch choice, a quiet hand), names each box, and predicts which values will change by lunch, by tomorrow or never; a time slider jumps ahead and reveals the board.
computer-science-k-2-what-the-code-does|Tap-to-Start Puppets|sandbox||Puppets on a little stage each hold a short program that starts when you tap them; the class says what each block will make happen, watches it run, and second graders retell how the show was built and who helped.
computer-science-3-5-computing-inventions-and-inventors|Inventors Hall|timeline||Students walk an inventors' hall and place each exhibit on a timeline (a punch-card loom, an early compiler, a talking calculator, a game cartridge), reading what the old way could not do, why people built the new one and whose ideas were left out.
computer-science-3-5-computing-on-the-job|Job Shadow Day|story||Students shadow four workers through a day (a vet, a farmer, an animator, a bus dispatcher), choose how each uses a computer at key moments, see how they learned a new tool, and watch them share work with a teammate to solve the day's problem.
computer-science-3-5-new-tech-yes-or-no|Family Tech Vote|roleplay||A family votes on a new gadget (a smart speaker, a fitness watch, a doorbell camera); students take each family member's seat, flip cards for what it helps, what data it collects, what it costs the planet and who it leaves out, then explain the choice.
computer-science-3-5-fair-surveys|Fair Survey Kit|data||Students plan a survey about a new playground (who to ask, number or word answers, an opt-out box), then the answers arrive with blanks, misspellings and an age of 400, and they check, fix and sort them in a table before counting.
computer-science-3-5-why-data-varies|Marble Ramp Trials|lab||Students time a marble down a cardboard ramp again and again; the times never match, so they hunt for causes (a late tap, a bump, a typo) and mark outliers, then change the ramp's height and surface to see how two attributes work together.
computer-science-3-5-code-as-a-team|Team Code Relay|builder||Two or three students each build one part of a small game (movement, score, sound) on their own side of the screen, then snap the parts together; when the joined game breaks, they read each part, leave comments, compare two fixes and trade feedback.
computer-science-3-5-plan-it-before-you-code-it|Adventure Outline Cards|story||Students outline a choose-your-own-adventure as numbered steps with a repeat and an if-then fork, written in words at grade 4 and redrawn as an index-card diagram with a coin counter at grade 5; a reader token walks every path to find dead ends.
computer-science-3-5-algorithms-that-help-some-more|Three Route Rules|simulator||A walking app can pick routes three ways (fastest, fewest street crossings, no stairs); students run each rule for four travelers, a kid, a wheelchair user, a parent with a stroller and a jogger, and see who each rule helps and who it lets down.
computer-science-3-5-hardware-and-software-together|Inside the Keypress|explorer||Press a key or snap a photo and follow the signal from keyboard to memory to the app's instructions to the screen; swap the app (drawing, typing, a game) to see the same hardware do a new job, then find the step that left the screen blank.
computer-science-6-8-data-metadata-and-you|Metadata X-Ray|simulator||Students flip a phone photo, a step-tracker log and a school app record to reveal their metadata (time, place, device), turn dials for precision and how often data is recorded, and decide who should keep which details and where that data lives.
computer-science-6-8-cleaning-data-honestly|Lunch Survey Cleanup|data|data-desk|A school lunch survey arrives with blanks, mixed spellings and doubled rows; students pick a fix for each problem (drop, reformat or keep), watch the results and the students left out of the data change, then write a short report on their choices and limits.
computer-science-6-8-design-for-every-user|Accessibility Test Team|builder||A team of three takes turns testing a club sign-up app as different users (a screen reader user, a student new to English, someone with a shaky hand), logs what failed, agrees on fixes with every voice counted, and retests to see who it now serves.
computer-science-6-8-who-gets-connected|County Connection Grant|map||Students spend a grant to get a rural county online, matching each household (a farm family, a grandparent, a student, the library) to a device and a connection by need, cost and battery; then a new telehealth app shows whose language, bandwidth or device it leaves out.
computer-science-6-8-choosing-ai-or-not|AI Fit Check|sort||Students sort a stack of jobs (total a bill, enforce game rules, suggest a song, group lost-and-found photos) into steps, rules or a data-driven model, then run a pretend AI helper on two of them, check its answers and weigh its energy cost.
computer-science-6-8-code-you-did-not-write|AI Code Inspector|puzzle||An AI helper writes a quiz program that looks right; students look up each function in the reference docs (two do not exist), run test inputs, log every change in a fix log, and add credits for the AI help and a borrowed sound.
computer-science-6-8-computing-in-industry|Industry Device Tour|explorer||Students tour a dairy farm, a hospital imaging room and a car plant, open each machine to see its input, processing, storage and output, then match each worker's problem to the thinking move they use: break it down, spot a pattern or leave out details.
computer-science-6-8-computing-history-and-impact|Eras of Computing|timeline||Students place inventors, communities, companies and governments on a timeline of computing eras, trace one past technology's intended and surprise effects (jobs, privacy, e-waste), then use those lessons to argue for or against delivery drones in their own town.
computer-science-9-12-emerging-technology-review|Emerging Tech Pitch|studio||Students pick a local problem (smoke alerts, late buses, library access), compare how an emerging technology (drones, on-device AI, satellite internet) works against the established fix, sketch a concept, and run an impact check on energy, cost and who gets left out.
computer-science-9-12-a-technology-s-long-story|Cloud Through Time|timeline||Students follow one technology, the data center, from room-sized mainframes to today's AI server farms, link each step to the social and economic forces behind it, and tag each era with its energy and water use and the towns and workers who carry the cost.
computer-science-9-12-minds-and-machines|Dice in the Chatbot|lab||Students send the same prompt to a rule-based chatbot and to a tiny next-word predictor, turn the randomness dial and watch the answers change, then sort claims about understanding, feeling and responsibility into a debate backed by what they measured.
computer-science-9-12-reviewing-ai-written-code|Pull Request Review|mystery||Students review an AI-written pull request for a club website: run it against the requirements and tests, find the hidden bug and the license clash in a pasted snippet, then approve it, request changes or rewrite it with proper credit.
computer-science-9-12-design-test-and-refine|Sprint Board|builder||A team of four builds a bus-alert app for a client: they write a design doc and assign roles on a sprint board, then user tests and a spec checklist return issues, and each sprint they choose fixes weighing correctness, accessibility and the client's values.
computer-science-9-12-data-dictionaries-and-validation|Catalog Column Rules|data|data-desk|Students write the data dictionary for a library's checkout dataset (name, type, allowed values, links between columns), then try three ways to check new rows (rules at entry, a cleaning script, a librarian's review) and compare what each catches and misses.
computer-science-9-12-computing-teams-and-careers|Computing Team Stories|explorer||Students pick their interests and goals, then meet five computing teams (a game studio, a public health data team, a climate modeling group, a newsroom graphics desk, a prosthetics startup), see how each mix of people cracked one problem, and sketch their own next steps.
computer-science-9-12-device-limits-and-optimization|Coin Cell Greenhouse|simulator||Students program a simulated microcontroller to water a greenhouse on a coin battery; its slow sensor, 2 KB of memory and sleep mode set hard limits, so they refactor the loop into procedures and smarter control flow until it lasts a month.
