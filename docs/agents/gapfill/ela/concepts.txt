# module_id|title|format|family|concept
ela-k-2-pictures-and-words-together|Windblown Pages|showdown|text-features|A gust blows the pictures off story and fact book pages; the class matches each picture, caption and diagram back to its words, says whether the author or the illustrator told each part, and names what the picture shows that the words do not.
ela-k-2-comparing-two-books|Alike and Different Books|sort||Two books land side by side: a bear storybook and a bear fact book, or two Cinderellas from different lands. The class drops picture cards into two overlapping hoops to show what is the same, what is different, and which book tells a story.
ela-k-2-question-words-and-key-details|Question Cube|showdown||After a short read-aloud, a giant question cube rolls who, what, where, when, why or how; teams answer and tap the sentence or picture that proves it. Later rolls ask how a character met a big problem or how two facts connect.
ela-k-2-reasons-an-author-gives|Because Bridge|builder|evidence|An author's point stands on one riverbank; the class drags reasons from the text into place as bridge planks and reads the point aloud with because. A true fact that does not support the point makes the bridge wobble until it is swapped out.
ela-k-2-poems-and-songs-aloud|Poem Drum|studio|poetry|First the class decides whether a page is a poem or a story; then the poem scrolls while a drum taps its beat, kids clap along and tap the rhymes, repeated lines and feeling words, and they record a reading in time with the drum.
ela-k-2-new-words-from-read-alouds|Vocabulary Jar|showdown||During a read-aloud, kids tap any word they wonder about; the class uses the picture and nearby words to work it out, drops it in the class jar, and the jar sparkles each time someone uses that word to describe something later.
ela-k-2-class-research-projects|Big Question Chart|showdown|research|The class picks a question (How do frogs grow?), reads picture books and recalls what they have seen, and pins each fact to a big chart under the part of the answer it helps; the chart becomes a shared report the class reads back together.
ela-k-2-adding-details-and-fixing-sentences|Puppet Wants More|studio||A short class story looks thin; a puppet keeps asking (What color? Where? How did it feel?), and each detail the class adds grows into the picture. A last polish fixes capitals and end marks before the puppet reads the better story aloud.
ela-k-2-show-and-tell|Show and Tell Spotlight|showdown||A child picks an object card, adds a quick drawing, and tells about it in full sentences while detail stars light up for color, size and use; a switch changes the listener from a best friend to the principal so kids hear how talk changes.
ela-k-2-listening-and-asking-questions|Class Phone Call|roleplay||A friendly caller phones the class with a short story or news (a lost kite, a new baby goat); the class takes turns asking the caller questions to learn more, then answers the caller's quiz about the key details.
eld-k-2-choosing-and-giving-reasons|I Choose Because|showdown|eld-talk|Two pictures offer a choice (a pet fish or a hamster, a reading corner or a block corner); learners pick one and say why with frames (I choose ___ because ___), then answer a friend's choice with I agree or I disagree because.
ela-3-5-revising-and-editing|Draft Car Wash|builder||A rough draft rolls through a car wash with stations for add, cut, move and polish; students pick changes for a named reader and purpose, fix capitals, commas and spelling at the last station, and compare the clean draft with the original.
ela-3-5-listening-for-main-points-and-reasons|Radio Hour Notes|sort||A short kids' radio show plays a field trip report and a guest arguing for a longer recess; students pause to note the main points, then sort the guest's claims into backed by reasons, backed by evidence, or not backed at all.
ela-3-5-presenting-clearly|Planetarium Guide|studio||Students plan a short planetarium talk: order the stops, attach a picture or sound to each, rehearse with a pace meter, then switch the audience from kindergartners to visiting scientists and hear how their word choice and formality should change.
ela-3-5-readers-theater-and-fluency|Script Read-Through|showdown|read-aloud|Small groups take parts in a short script and rehearse with a scrolling prompter that marks pace and pauses; tapping a tricky long word splits it into syllables and word parts, and each group performs and replays its recording.
ela-3-5-main-idea-with-text-evidence|Main Idea Pinboard|explorer|evidence|Students read a short article or fable, write the main idea or central message on a card, then pin the exact sentences that prove it; a meter shows how many key details the card covers and flags pins that only repeat the idea.
ela-3-5-academic-and-subject-words|Glossary Makers|builder|word-lab|Students build the glossary for a short science or history passage: for each bold word they gather clues from the sentence, word parts and a picture, write a kid definition with a synonym and an opposite, then link two facts with however or similarly.
ela-3-5-pictures-panels-and-performances|Mood in the Pictures|explorer||A story scene appears as plain text, as illustrated comic panels and as a read-aloud with music; students mark what each picture or sound adds (a mood, a hidden clue, a feeling) and where a version follows or changes the words.
ela-3-5-diagrams-and-how-things-happen|How It Works Diagrams|puzzle|text-features|A short text explains how something happens (a canal lock, a bill becoming law, a volcano erupting); students drag each sentence onto the matching part of a diagram, map or timeline, and the animation runs only when every step and cause is in place.
ela-3-5-narrators-and-points-of-view|Swap the Teller|story||One mix-up at a county fair is told by a first-person kid, a third-person narrator and a grumpy goose; students switch tellers, see which details each one includes or leaves out, then decide whether their own view matches the teller's.
ela-3-5-comparing-texts-side-by-side|Side by Side Reader|sort||Two texts open side by side (two books in a series, two articles on one topic, a story told in chapters and one told in letters); students drag shared details to the middle and differences to each side, then name what each structure does.
ela-4-showing-character-through-details|Show, Don't Tell|cards||Each card holds a telling sentence (Maya was nervous); students read a scene to collect what a character thinks, says and does, rewrite the card to show the feeling instead, and the class guesses the hidden feeling from their version.
ela-3-notes-and-categories|Note Card Shuffle|sort|research|Students pick a question about an animal or an invention, read three short kid pages, jot facts on note cards in their own words, and sort the cards into the project's categories; an empty or crowded category shows what to look up next.
ela-6-8-revising-for-purpose-and-audience|Draft Versus Draft|showdown||Two revisions of the same draft go head to head; the class votes for the one that better fits the stated reader and purpose, and the tool marks each change as a cut, add, move or reword so students can name what made it stronger.
ela-6-8-theme-and-textual-evidence|Theme Evidence Ladder|puzzle|evidence|Students propose a theme for a short public-domain story, then rank quoted passages from strongest to weakest support; the tool shows which passages also back a rival theme, and a poem on the same subject tests whether the theme holds across genres.
ela-6-8-analysis-with-evidence-from-nonfiction|Claim, Quote, Comment|builder|evidence|Students build an analysis paragraph about an article: a claim, a quotation or paraphrase that supports it, and their own explanation; the tool flags a quote dropped in with no explanation or one that does not actually prove the claim.
ela-6-8-text-audio-and-film-versions|Page, Audio, Screen|explorer||A book club must recommend one version of a scene or news story (text, audiobook or film storyboard); members mark what each version adds, cuts or changes, then pick the medium that best serves a purpose, from explaining a process to building suspense.
ela-6-8-listening-to-speakers-and-discussing|Podcast Listening Club|sort||Students listen to a short student podcast where two hosts argue about a cashless cafeteria; they tag each claim as supported, unsupported or off topic, then discussion cards ask them to build on, question or challenge a classmate's tags.
ela-6-8-presenting-claims-and-findings|Docent Tour Talk|studio||Students prepare a two-minute museum tour of one exhibit: order their points, choose one image or sound per stop, rehearse against pace and eye-contact checks, then redo the opening for a curator, a family and a group of second graders.
ela-6-8-academic-vocabulary-strategies|Context, Roots, Dictionary|puzzle|word-lab|Students meet academic words (hypothesis, perspective, significant) inside short science, history and math passages; they try context first, then a Greek or Latin root, then the dictionary entry, and the tool tracks which strategy cracked each word.
ela-8-puns-irony-and-allusion|Double Meanings|sort||Students sort lines from stories, speeches and ads into pun, verbal irony, analogy or allusion, explain both meanings, then swap one key word for a plain one and watch the tone and meaning fall flat.
ela-6-8-commas-dashes-and-ellipses|Pause and Break|puzzle||A reading voice pauses exactly where the punctuation tells it to; students add or move commas, parentheses, dashes and ellipses until the sentence sounds the way the writer meant, including quotations with words left out.
ela-6-8-structure-in-informative-writing|Explainer Sections|builder||Students take apart a short explainer, such as how a vaccine trial works, to see what each section does for the reader, then build their own by choosing an organizing pattern and writing headings and transitions that fit it.
ela-6-8-narrative-point-of-view-and-structure|Blizzard Scenes|studio||Students write a short scene about a class trip stranded by a blizzard, then retell it from another character's point of view and in a different order; the versions sit side by side with questions about what each choice reveals or hides.
ela-6-8-how-authors-present-people-and-ideas|Memoir and Biography|explorer||Students read a memoir excerpt and a biography of the same inventor; they tag where each introduces her, which anecdotes it uses and what each author wants readers to think, then compare how the two connect the same events.
eld-6-12-telling-stories-and-experiences|Then What Happened?|builder||Learners narrate a comic strip or a personal moment one panel at a time, choosing time words and past-tense verbs from frames that fade as they go; a listener nods along and asks then what? whenever a step is missing.
ela-9-12-revising-and-editing-for-style|Editors in the Margin|studio||Students revise a short essay by answering margin comments from three editors: one for purpose and audience, one for sentence variety, one for hyphens and spelling; accepted changes are tracked, and a before-and-after read shows which edits mattered.
ela-9-12-developing-themes-with-evidence|Theme Ledger|data|evidence|Students follow two themes through a public-domain story, logging each passage that develops them in a two-column ledger; a chart shows where the themes rise and cross, and they draft an analysis paragraph from their strongest entries.
ela-9-12-central-ideas-and-their-development|Idea Threads|explorer||Students pull a colored thread through a speech or essay, attaching it to each sentence that develops a central idea; a second thread for another idea shows where the two twist together, and every knot must be backed by a quoted line.
ela-10-one-subject-in-many-media|Portrait in Four Media|explorer|research|A scientist's life is told four ways: an encyclopedia entry, an interview transcript, a documentary storyboard and a photo archive; students mark what each account emphasizes, check facts across them, and assemble one accurate profile.
ela-9-presenting-findings-to-an-audience|Audience Shift Briefing|simulator||Students turn a research finding into a three-minute briefing, then rebuild it for a city board, a neighborhood meeting and a middle school class, choosing evidence, visuals and register for each while audience reaction cards respond.
ela-9-12-key-terms-and-academic-vocabulary|Key Term Tracker|explorer|word-lab|Students follow one loaded word (faction, liberty, nature) through a founding essay or a novel, collecting each place the author defines, narrows or bends it; context and a usage dictionary help with unfamiliar words, and the term's meaning card grows.
ela-9-12-narrative-scenes-and-pacing|Pacing Strip|lab||Students write a short scene, then tag each sentence as action, dialogue, description or reflection; a pacing strip shows where the scene rushes or drags, and they revise until the turning point gets the space it needs.
