# Recess: the third shape

Cory, 2026-11-07: fully sold. Roadmap Track 9. Research: docs/agents/research/GAMES-RESEARCH.md (25 concepts,
scores, sources).

**Name: Recess (Cory, 2026-11-07).** **Tone (Cory, 2026-11-07):** a treat from the teacher. When kids are fully done,
they go to Recess and play. The games stay mentally stimulating with educational undertones, but kids should not
feel they are still in class. Standards sit underneath, loosely, never so rigidly that the fun is lost. The design
rules below were written before this and are being reworked with Cory; treat them as a draft.

## What a Curio Class game is

A real game where the skill is the mechanic: you get better at the game only by getting better at the skill. Not a
quiz with a game skin, not a lesson with a reward screen. Where the other two shapes sit:

| Shape | What it is | Score or finish? |
|---|---|---|
| Tool | A lesson shaped around a standard | Often a goal |
| Curiosity | An instrument shaped around a real thing; the standard is a side effect | No score, no finish (light touch, rule 19) |
| Game | A game loop whose core move is the skill | Yes: levels and par |

Original games only. A genre's mechanic (match-3, Sokoban, tower defense, programming puzzles) is fine; a specific
game's name, characters, art or levels never are.

## What the research says (short)

- When the skill is the core move, children learn more and choose to play far longer than when a quiz sits between
  moves (Habgood and Ainsworth 2011).
- Building the skill in is not enough alone: a game that scaffolds forever never moves players to real notation, and
  gains do not transfer (DragonBox studies). So every world ends with a "show the math" card in standard notation.
- Side rewards hurt: coins off the learning path made more players quit (Refraction). One currency only: par.
- Classroom casual play works best with 30 to 90 second levels, fixed rules, instant feedback and progress saved
  without accounts.

## Design rules for every game

- The skill is the verb. If the game still works with the skill removed, redesign it.
- One currency: par (moves, parts or turns). No coins, gems, loot, lives, streaks or forced timers.
- No luck in the core rule; every outcome traces back to the player's reasoning.
- Undo and instant restart on every level; dead ends are flagged.
- Difficulty is labeled honestly.
- After a fail, show what was off and by how much.
- 15 to 30 hand-made levels per game, then an endless or daily mode.
- Every world ends with a "show the math" card and one level that uses standard notation directly.
- Mute by default or one tap away; honor reduced motion; Chromebook and phone friendly.

## First six (from the research pass)

1. **Fraction Splitter** (grades 3-6): split a water flow into halves and thirds so each garden gets an exact
   fraction.
2. **Factor Crusher** (grades 4-7): numbers drop in; break each into a factor pair; primes shatter.
3. **Number Line Launcher** (grades 2-8): land a probe exactly on a fraction, decimal, percent or negative.
4. **Robot Route** (grades K-8, computer science): queue commands, loops and functions for a robot to run.
5. **Graph Shaper** (grades 8-11): set slope and intercept, later parabola values, to route a marble through rings.
6. **Bond Builder** (grades 7-10, chemistry): join atoms until every bonding slot is filled.

Runners-up: Dial Lock, Balance Box, Logic Gates. Ruled out: Untangle and plain sliding-block puzzles (fun, but they
teach too little curriculum); deck building (draws feel like gambling in class); immune-system tower defense (mostly
generic tower defense).
