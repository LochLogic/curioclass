## Verdict

Day 22 is correct mathematics, keyed right, in the plan's shape, and the fix-it on error G (the sign turned round)
is the right error on the right day. The biggest risk is that the day does not make students choose: none of the
ten pairs is one where renaming to the same numerator is the quicker way, so "pick a way" collapses into "find a
common denominator unless the numerators already match", and day 21's new move is never practised. The one change
that matters most is finding 2 (swap one pair for 3/4 and 6/10); a close second is finding 1, because as built the
display's answers screen shows sets B and C answers while students are still working on them.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The display shows all ten answers on one screen, but the day marks set by set

- **What is wrong.** The teacher page says "After each set, show its answers on the display ... fix any wrong sign
  before starting the next set." The display builds one "Check your answers" screen holding all ten pairs with their
  answers. Showing it after set A puts the answers to sets B and C on the wall before students start them.
- **Evidence.** d022-display.html: 14 screens, one of them "Check your answers" with all ten `<li>` answers;
  d022.md, "26 min. Ten pairs in three short sets"; LESSON-DAY.md, the display row ("a check your answers screen
  only where a part names a partner or self check": one screen per `Check:` line).
- **Cost.** The day's whole design (short sets marked at once, the plan's "checked against the key every third
  pair") breaks: the teacher either shows nothing until the end or gives away 7 answers.
- **Change.** Day: split the 26-minute part into three parts, each with its own `Check:` line, so the display builds
  three answer screens: "8 min. Set A", "9 min. Set B", "11 min. Set C" (minutes as settled below, with 2 taken from
  Fix it). Process: when a part names its pairs in sets, the display builds one check screen per set, or the gate
  fails a part that says "after each set" with one check screen.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 4 finding on the answers screen, day 77 finding 3).
  **CHEAP:** missed.

### 2. The pairs never make the student choose the same-count way

- **What is wrong.** The goal is "I can pick a way". Day 21 taught renaming to the same numerator (2/5 = 4/10
  against 4/12). Of day 22's ten pairs, two already share a numerator (3/8 and 3/10, 3/6 and 3/4), and every other
  pair is quickest by a common denominator. Not one pair is quicker by renaming a numerator. Only the worked example
  (3/5 and 6/8) is. So the "two ways" choice is never faced alone, the Pull it together question "Which pair was
  quickest with the same count?" can only be answered with pairs that needed no renaming, and a student who ignores
  day 21 entirely loses nothing.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s03-P22-3 to P22-12; u03-4.md key moment 2 ("choosing between the two ways"); the
  Progressions for the Common Core State Standards, Number and Operations, Fractions, 3 to 5, on comparing by common
  numerators as well as denominators (PDF page 150 (144) as the plan cites it; from memory, not reopened today).
  Illustrative Mathematics grade 4, unit 2, builds its comparing practice so different pairs favour different ways
  (from memory, not reopened today).
- **Cost.** Fluency in choosing is the day's reason to exist (it is the unit's only fluency day). Students reach day
  23's "pick a way and prove it" having practised one way.
- **Change.** Replace s03-P22-10 (5/8 and 7/12, a second twenty-fourths pair; the day already has 9/12 and 6/8)
  with s03-P22-10v2: "3/4 __ 6/10". Key ">: 6/8 > 6/10 (eighths are bigger than tenths), or 15/20 > 12/20". One cut
  by the numerator way, two by the denominator way, so it rewards looking first. Wrong row: "<", D, "6 and 10 are
  bigger". Pull it together's expected answer becomes "3/4 and 6/10 needed one cut to the same count; 7/8 and 9/10
  needed fortieths." This also removes 5/8 = 15/24 from the taught set, which settles the cheap read's worry about
  exit part (a) (see "Settled").
- **Confidence.** Likely (the rows are firm; how much one pair moves the habit is a judgment until a taught run).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.
