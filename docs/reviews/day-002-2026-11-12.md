## Verdict

Day 2 is correct mathematics in a good shape: one worked search on tiles, four tables where students predict
before they check, the register's errors A and C fixed on purpose, and a planned question that makes students say
why the turn ends the search. The biggest risk is the stop itself: the rule the students hold ("when the pairs
turn round, put a star; stop there") only stops once a turned pair appears, which for 44, 46 or a prime is 11 to
47 rows away, while the exit key, day 3 and day 5 all use a different stop ("7 × 7 is past 48"). The one change
that matters most is finding 1: teach "when rows × rows is more than the number, stop" on day 2, on the tiles, and
put it on the rows table page.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The stop students are given cannot end a search with a gap, and the stretch later uses another one

- **What is wrong.** The rows table page says "When the pairs turn round, put a star in the turn column. Stop
  there." The worked search stops at 6 rows of 4 (a turned pair), and the 60 key runs to "10 rows of 6 is the
  turn". So the rule the student uses is "stop when a pair I already have comes back". That works on today's
  tables only because each has a near partner. It fails as soon as there is a gap: on the challenge, 44 turns at
  11 rows, 46 at 23, and 41, 43 and 47 only at 41, 43 and 47 rows, past the page's 10 rows; on day 3, 44 and 66
  turn at 11 rows and 75 at 15. Meanwhile the rest of the stretch uses the square stop: the exit key's reason is
  "8 × 8 = 64 is already more than 50", day 3 reads back "8 × 8 is past 50" and asks "We stopped 48 at 7. Why?"
  expecting "7 × 7 is past 48", day 5 says "up to 30 the turn comes by 5 (6 × 6 = 36)", and the opener promises
  "never needs more than 10 tries: 10 × 10 is 100". Day 2, the day that owns "say how I know I am done", only
  hints at it: the teacher line "once the rows outnumber the squares in each row" (which, read strictly, stops 24
  at 5 rows, not 6) and step 7, "7 rows or more would need fewer than 4 in each row", which is about 24 alone.
- **Evidence.** rows-table.md line 3; s01-K2-1 steps 6 and 7; s01-T2-4 answer; s01-X2 answer and samples; d003.md
  "E2 back" and "Pull it together"; d005.md "Chart"; opener "The search in order, and the turn". The Progressions
  describe the search as going on until the pairs reverse (docs/courses/math-3-5-progression.md, stretch 1, Prog.
  p. 45 (39)); the reversal comes when the number tried passes its would-be partner, which can be seen on a row
  that does not fit. From memory, not reopened today.
- **Cost.** A student who does the challenge or day 3's practice by the rule on the page tries 15 to 47 rows, or
  stops at 10 because the table ends, and learns nothing about why. E2 rewards a reason ("8 × 8 is too big") the
  day never taught, so the record sheet reads "no reason" for students who followed the page.
- **Change.** Teach one stop, the square one, on the tiles, and keep the turned pair as the picture of why it
  works. Wording in "Settled by the expert": K2-1's steps 5 to 7, the teacher's "Why stop" line, the Pull it
  together expected answer, the rows table page's stop sentence, and the T2 keys' stop rows. The key for 60 then
  stops after 8 rows ("8 × 8 = 64 is more than 60"), which also gives back about 2 minutes of the 20 (the cheap
  read's CHECK 4).
- **Confidence.** Firm (the rows themselves, and the next three days' wording).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read saw that the 60 key
  disagrees with the teacher line, CHECK 2, but not the gap numbers, the 10-row page, or that days 3 and 5 use the
  square stop).
