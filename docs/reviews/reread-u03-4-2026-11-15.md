## 2. New findings

Most serious first. Confidence: firm (two sources or the files themselves agree), likely, or judgment.

**N1. A free English Secret History exists; the sheets say none does. Origin: unit.** sources-42d "Their own
history", the opener, u03-4.md field 1 and sources.csv 42-BG5 all say there is no free English text. The row's own
linked page (Columbia Asia for Educators, "The Myth of Chinggis") names Igor de Rachewiltz's translation, and that
translation, shortened by John C. Street, is open access: Western Washington University CEDAR, Books and Monographs 4,
December 2015, https://cedar.wwu.edu/cedarbooks/4, licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Why it matters: the sensitivity read's
first finding (only outsiders speak) was closed on the ground that no free insider text could be checked; for the
Mongols that ground is false, and a teacher who knows the edition will distrust the sheet. Fix: Cory rules on whether a
short attributed excerpt under NC-ND fits Curio Class's licence (a rights question, so his). If yes, check one short
passage (Chinggis's rise or Ogodei's own reckoning of his rule) against the PDF, add it as a sourced row and print it
on the option d sheet; if no, reword the limit to "a free translation exists under a licence that does not let us
reprint it". Confidence: firm (CEDAR listing and the Columbia page).

**N2. The read-aloud substitute fits only one of the translators' words. Origin: section.** The opener ("Translators'
words"), d042, d044 and d045 say: read the translator's word once, explain it, then say "the people of Mali" in its
place. The same paragraph names Browne's "Tartars", and the packet header lists "Negrolands" and "slaves". Read as
written, a teacher reading Source E says "the people of Mali" for the Mongols, and "the slaves of the Massufa" or
"millet imported from the Negrolands" has no fitting substitute. Day 45 also says "read sources aloud on request,
without explanation" and then asks for an explanation of each word. Fix: one substitute per word in the opener and
u03-4.md field 9 ("blacks", "negroes": the people of Mali; "Negrolands": the lands of Mali; "Tartars": the Mongols;
"slaves": enslaved people), the days pointing to it; on day 45, "a one-line word explanation is not coaching".
Confidence: firm (the files).

**N3. Two different texts are both "Source F". Origin: unit.** sources-42d prints "Source F, the letter" (Mongke to
Louis IX, Rockhill pp. 248 to 249); sources-44 prints "Source F" (Mongke speaking to Rubruck, pp. 235 to 236); one
sources.csv row, 44F, covers both. The claim frame asks students to cite "(Source ...)"; "Source F" in a claim cannot
be traced to one text, and day 43's "Source F shows a court of many faiths" points at a header line, not either quote.
Fix: give the letter its own letter (for example Source J, row 42J in sources.csv) and update d043, d044 and the
opener's mentions. Confidence: firm.

**N4. X44-v2's sample uses a detail the note does not hold. Origin: unit.** The note prints riders, a one-in-five
cloth toll and a shrine; the meets sample opens "The riders and the death penalty are the sword". The death penalty is
Polo's (Source D), not the note's. A teacher marking against the sample credits outside detail on an item whose
look-fors say "a reason from the note". Fix: "The riders guarding the road are the sword; one bale in five is trade,
taxed; the shrine is faith." Confidence: firm.

**N5. X46-v2's sample pairs an entry the timeline does not hold. Origin: unit.** The exit is "from the timeline only";
materials.csv adds only Mali and the Yuan for unit 3, yet the sample pairs "Kilwa under Sultan Hasan in the 1330s"
with the Yuan and cites Source C. A student cannot do what the sample does, and a teacher may mark down students who
stay on the timeline. Fix: write the sample from two entries the class timeline holds by day 46 (units 1 and 2 entries
from days 15 and 30, Mali, the Yuan), avoiding the review's pairing. Confidence: firm.

**N6. The component-to-move table leaves some A2 misses with no group. Origin: unit.** s03 field 5 and the opener
give a move for A2 links only for option b (K) and option d (D); option a's links have none. A2's look-fors "two named
sources" and "evidence kept apart from inference" have no row either. Day 46 places students only from this table, so
those students drift to "pairs from the written move" with no written move. Fix: add rows, for example A2 links
(option a): C, trace the route both ways; A2 sources or evidence and inference: F, trace each half of a statement to
its source. Confidence: likely.

**N7. X43-v2's sample says more than the ceramics show. Origin: unit.** "The ceramics show R's port traded with
another region, but local people built the port." Imported ceramics cannot show who built the port; the sample models
the overclaim the section teaches students to avoid (evidence apart from inference), from the other side. Fix: "...
traded with another region; they do not show who built it." Confidence: likely.

**N8. W44's model claim has no Silk Roads consequence. Origin: unit.** Look-for 6 asks for "a Silk Roads consequence,
with conquest's harms kept". The sample's only candidate is paper money that "let people buy and sell across the
realm", which is inside the Yuan. Scored as written, the model earns 1 there, and feedback points students the wrong
way. Fix: add one sentence from the 44 card: merchants, envoys and missionaries crossed the Silk Roads more safely
under Mongol rule, but the conquests that made the roads safe had destroyed cities. Confidence: likely.

**N9. P43-2's sample fails its own culture look-for. Origin: unit.** Look-for 1 asks for "a named contributor beyond
the ruler"; the sample's culture line is "mosques and the sultan's alms", the sultan being the ruler, and Source C
names no mosque at Kilwa (Source I's mosques are Mombasa's). Fix: "Kilwa: its people built a coral-stone mosque (sheet
gloss) and spoke Swahili, an African language with Arabic words (fact card)". Confidence: firm.

**N10. "His grandson Hulegu" now points at the wrong man. Origin: section.** On the option d card the inserted line
"Rubruck met one, a goldsmith from Paris, at Karakorum." sits between Chinggis and "His grandson Hulegu sacked Baghdad
in 1258", so "his" reads as the goldsmith's or Rubruck's. Fix: "Chinggis's grandson Hulegu". Confidence: firm.

**N11. The Tarikh al-Sudan line says more than its linked page. Origin: section.** sources-42a: "chronicles, among
them the Tarikh al-Sudan, kept in family libraries today." The 42-BG2 page (Tombouctou Manuscripts Project) names the
Ta'rikh al-Sudan and Ta'rikh al-Fattash and says personal libraries persist "up until today"; it does not say this
chronicle is kept in them. Fix: "Timbuktu's scholars later wrote chronicles, among them the Tarikh al-Sudan, and
Timbuktu's families still keep libraries of manuscripts today." Confidence: firm.
