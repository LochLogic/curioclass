## Section 1.1 (days 1 to 6): A better, narrowly

- **A measures every goal.** E5 asks the pick the goal map names; B's E5 (B1) does not, and its alternate key is
  garbled (B2). A's retrieval uses a paper strip so misses can feed the owed day 2 group; B's choral version cannot (B3).
- **A is more faithful.** It keeps counting collections, gives student goals per day, and keeps counting up on day 12.
- **Against A:** the exit routine does not fit 6 minutes (A2), every stem holds two asks (A1), and a 9 + 2 card breaks
  the within-10 limit (A3). B's split stems, its 5 + 5 timing (marking in the exit, the six in Synthesis) and its step
  boxes (day 3) and back arrow (day 4) are the better teaching design, and they matter later. If B's E5 asked the pick
  and its retrieval went on paper, B would win this section too.

## Section 1.2 (days 7 to 10): B better

- **A misses an owed slot** (A6: day 7 retrieval misses never reach day 8), and its day 9 groups of six break the
  two-groups-of-15 rule (A8). B puts the day 7 group into day 8 practice and counts turns for day 9 gathering (two
  groups of 15, 20 seconds a child, 5 minutes, both at once).
- **B's exits are better made.** E7 uses stripes and plain shapes so a second rule is real and color-blind safe, with
  both keys (3, 1, 2 or 3 and 3) correct. All four exits are split into one ask each. A's three two-ask stems repeat A1.
- Both day 10 decks are fully correct; B's (eight pairs with total and difference, including a "same" case) practices
  matching harder than A's re-run of the day 6 fact cards.

## Section 1.3 (days 11 to 13): B better

## Section 1.4 (days 14 to 16): B clearly better

- **A leaks the C1 check** (A10). Its day 14 wrong example is 16 counted up from 12, answer 4, on the day half the
  class is observed on 16 - 12 and the day before the other half is. B fences C1 in every warm-up pool and deck ("no

- **B splits D2 and E15 into one ask at a time** ((a) to (g) for D2, three parts for E15), with A and B wrong answers
  read from the marks. A's E15 is three asks in one stem in an 8-minute slot (A13). Both day 16 timings match the unit
  plan's 60 minutes.

## Overall: B better

1. **Checks are safer and sounder in B.** No reserved fact leaks. Spoken stems are mostly one ask each. The record
   format (step boxes) is taught on day 3 and carried to C1. A's worst faults sit on the unit check itself.
2. **B counts turns for about 30 children correctly everywhere.** A's daily exit routine is 2.5 minutes over on 13
   days, and its day 9 grouping breaks the unit's rule.
3. **A wins on presentation and some faithfulness:** student-word goals each day, the kept counting-collections
   activity, a section 1.1 E5 that measures the goal. These are cheaper to fix in B than A's check faults are to fix
   in A.
