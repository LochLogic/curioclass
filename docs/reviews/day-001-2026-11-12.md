## Verdict

Day 1 is correct mathematics and a sound first lesson: tiles in hand, a rectangle tied to its multiplication,
a number of rows that does or does not "come out equal", and the turn asked as a real question, with the search
held back for day 2 and a clean two-sided hand-off to it. The biggest risk is the pictures: the printed and
projected rectangles label their sides "3 tiles" and "4 tiles" (and "1 tile" beside a row of 12) on a day whose
whole language is "rows" and "in each row", and the one picture that carries the idea of a factor, 12 tiles in
5 short rows, is drawn nowhere. The one change that matters most is finding 1 with finding 2: label the figures
in rows, and put the short-row picture on the board and the display. After that, the day mostly needs the
practical set-up a first day needs (grid paper size, pairs, what to collect).

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The figures label sides in tiles, not in rows

- **What is wrong.** K1-1's figure (`rect l=4 w=3 u=tiles grid`) prints "4 tiles" on the top and bottom and "3
  tiles" on both sides; K1-3's prints "12 tiles" twice and "1 tile" on each end of the long row. The day teaches
  "3 rows of 4", writes "rows" and "in each row" labelled on the board, and the frame under the figure reads
  "__ rows of __ is __ tiles". A grade 4 child reading "3 tiles" on the side of a 12-tile rectangle, or "1 tile"
  beside a row of 12, is told the opposite of what the words say. Every side is labelled twice, so the figure is
  also busier than it needs to be.
- **Evidence.** d001-student.pdf side 1; d001-display.html (same SVG); items.csv K1-1 and K1-3 `figure` cells;
  d001.md "Before class". The opener's model: "a factor is a number of rows that comes out equal".
- **Cost.** The first picture of the year teaches a length reading (sides in tiles) when the unit is built on a
  rows reading; the frame and the figure disagree on the first line a student fills in.
- **Change.** Process: a `rows` flag on `rect` that labels one short side "3 rows" and one long side "4 in each
  row", once each. Until it exists, drop `u=tiles` from both figure cells so the sides read "3" and "4" (see
  Settled). Teacher page, Example 1, adds nothing: the steps already say "3 rows, 4 tiles in each row".
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 81 finding 1, a picture of the wrong model).
  **CHEAP:** missed.

- **Evidence.** d001-display.html (Example 2 screens: text only); d001-student.pdf side 1; days.csv day 1
  "tiles under the camera"; DEFAULTS.md "No document camera"; X1v2's third wrong answer.
- **Cost.** In a room with no camera, most students never see the short rows the exit then asks about.
- **Change.** Day: "Before class" adds "Draw 12 dots dealt into 5 rows (3, 3, 2, 2, 2) on the board and cover

- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 80 finding 7, day 82 finding 5: the display
  drops the picture the steps need). **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read noted the turned 4 by 3 is on no screen,

### 3. Grid paper has no size, and the day's longest rectangles need one

- **What is wrong.** The materials say "2 sheets of grid paper" and "a quarter sheet of grid paper" with no size.
  1 by 18 needs 18 squares in a line; on 1-inch grid paper (common in grade 3 and 4 rooms) a letter sheet is 8
  by 10 squares, and a quarter sheet is 4 by 5, so neither 1 by 18 nor the exit's 1 by 10 fits. On centimetre
  grid paper a sheet is about 20 by 26 and a quarter sheet about 10 by 13, so both fit.
- **Evidence.** d001.md header; opener "Supplies for the unit" (no size); IM grade 4 unit 1, lessons 1 and 2, use
  centimetre grid paper (read today, sources below).
- **Cost.** A teacher who grabs the inch grid paper on the shelf loses the practice part to students taping
  sheets together, and the exit cannot be done as written.
- **Change.** Header and opener: "centimetre grid paper (1 cm squares)", and "3 sheets" per student (agreeing
  with the cheap read's count). The quarter sheet for the exit becomes "a quarter sheet of centimetre grid paper,
  long side across".
- **Confidence.** Firm (the arithmetic).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read counted sheets, not size).

## What is right and should not be touched

- The order of the three examples: a rectangle, then a count of rows that works and one that does not, then the
  long thin rectangle that students forget. "Deal the tiles one to each row in turn" is a sound definition of
  "comes out equal" for grade 4.
- The turn question asked of a real, turned rectangle, with the expected first answer ("yes, it has 4 rows now").
- Holding back the search in order (twice stated), so day 2's worked search has a need to answer.
- The four practice numbers: 18 (three rectangles), 14 (two), 16 (a square), 15 (odd, no 2 rows), and 15 left
  open on purpose. The challenge (exactly two rectangles, one a square) is a good, same-standard stretch.
- The hand-off with day 2: C, the short last row and A each have their action in d002 "E1 back", and the readiness
  reteach slot is named on both sides.
- No warm-up on day 1, and the readiness sheet framed as "not a test, a blank is fine".
