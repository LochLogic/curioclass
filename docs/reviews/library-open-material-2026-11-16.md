# Open library and research skills material for Curio Class: is there a base text we may adapt?

Read 2026-11-16 (subject pre-work, the library counterpart of P2). Research notes, not legal advice. Each licence was
read on the publisher's own page that day unless marked "search only" (seen in a search summary, page not opened).
The bar is docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md: CC BY 4.0, public domain or the UK Open Government Licence v3.0 is a base; NC,
SA, ND or unclear is reference only.

## The short answer

- **A real partial base for grades 6 to 12:** 47 Civic Online Reasoning lessons and assessments (Digital Inquiry Group,
  formerly the Stanford History Education Group) are CC BY 4.0, among them the lateral reading lessons the bar's
  criterion 3 rests on. The rest of that curriculum is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 and stays reference.
- **A second partial base for grades 9 to 12 and teacher background:** Mike Caulfield, Web Literacy for Student
  Fact-Checkers (2017), CC BY 4.0. Its examples and tools are dated.
- **Nothing for K to 5.** No openly licensed elementary library or information literacy curriculum was found. The
  Empire State continuum (no licence stated) is the grade check set, not a base.
- **Verdict:** K to 5 our own; 6 to 12 our own with these two partial bases, taken item by item. Adopting them is
  Cory's call.

## Traps

- The Civic Online Reasoning page says the licences cover files reached by PDF or shared-drive link; anything else
  on the site is the authors' copyright. Credit names the group only in the credit line; no claim that it reviewed
  or endorsed Curio Class.
- Lessons built on live web pages go stale. Every adapted task uses a page we describe or a dated capture made our
  own, with a note for the teacher to swap in a current case.
- The evidence for lateral reading is one district field study in high school (Journal of Educational Psychology,
  2022, from memory, not re-read) and earlier classroom pilots. No page may claim more.
