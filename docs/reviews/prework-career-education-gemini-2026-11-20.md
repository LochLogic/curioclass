### 1. The NCDA 2024 learner-goals file is still unloaded, lacks an indicator-level ownership and exclusion rule, and the gate note leaves all 98 cluster/practice ids unanchored

- **Where:** `STANDARD.md` sections 2 and 3 (lines 44 to 54 and 79 to 91); `scripts/progression.py` (`STD_FILES`, `ANCHOR`, `ANCHOR_SKIP`, `codes()`).
- **What is wrong:** Three linked gaps block stage 2:
  1. **Learner-goals file still missing:** `STANDARD.md` section 2 states that loading the 2024 NCDA guidelines file still awaits a read of NCDA's terms page. Until either the NCDA own-words file or Curio Class's fallback goals file is in `data/standards/`, stage 2 has no learner-goal codes to own.
  2. **No indicator-level ownership or exclusion rule for NCDA 2024:** Section 3 says only "The learner goals, once loaded, are owned the same way." That does not tell a progression writer how to divide roughly 150+ CD and ALL indicators across Knowledge Acquisition (K), Application (A), and Reflection (R) between Exploration (grade 8) and Pathways (grade 10), what to do with adult or postsecondary indicators in NCDA (which spans youth and adults), or how the gate handles the 4 SEL-domain goals (about 75 to 93 indicators) if the full framework is loaded.
  3. **Single-file `ANCHOR` leaves 98 Advance CTE ids unanchored:** Lines 89 to 91 state: "`cte` in STD_FILES with this file and the learner-goals file, and in ANCHOR with the learner-goals file". In `scripts/progression.py` (lines 68 to 70), `ANCHOR` holds one filename per subject. If `ANCHOR` holds only the learner-goals file and `cte-career-clusters-2024.txt` sits only in `STD_FILES`, the gate will never verify that any cluster, sub-cluster, or practice is owned or excluded with a reason.
- **Cost:** Stage 2 cannot pass or would be written inconsistently by two writers; adult or SEL indicators in NCDA will fail the anchor check, while dropped clusters or practices will pass silently.
- **Fix (replace the last sentence of the "Ownership" paragraph and the "What the gate needs" paragraph in `STANDARD.md` section 3; ours, untested; scope parts go to Question 2 for Cory):**

> **Learner-goal ownership (pending NCDA terms read and Cory's call).** Once the 2024 career development guidelines file (or our own fallback goals file) is loaded in `data/standards/`, career education owns the seven goals in the Career Development (CD1 to CD5) and Academic and Lifelong Learning (ALL1 to ALL2) domains. Exploration (grade 8) owns the Knowledge Acquisition (K) indicators and introductory Application (A) indicators; Pathways (grade 10) owns the upper Application (A) and Reflection (R) indicators and carries Exploration's owned indicators as `~` where applied at higher demand. Indicators in the Social Emotional Learning domain (SEL1 to SEL4, carried as leans on the SEL courses) and indicators that apply only to adult or postsecondary workers are listed in `data/progressions/cte-excluded.csv` with their reasons (or omitted from the loaded file and noted in its header).

- **Confidence:** Firm.

### 2. Pathways claims ownership of 58 sub-clusters that individual students do not all study (C1 clash), and the grade 8 to grade 10 handoff has no skip-grade rule

- **Where:** `STANDARD.md` section 3 (lines 61, 67 to 70, 79 to 86); `docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md` C1 ("Any standard lacks a lesson, practice or a check item"); `docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md` rule 19 ("Always cover the whole standard"); `scripts/progression.py` line 36 (skip-grade handoff note).
- **What is wrong:** Two ownership and handoff faults sit in `STANDARD.md` section 3:
  1. **Whole-standard ownership vs student choice:** Pathways lets each student pick "two or three clusters" (line 61 and 68 to 69), yet lines 83 to 84 say Pathways "owns the other 58 sub-clusters, each met through its cluster's field tasks and data sheet, so no sub-cluster is excluded." Under C1 and `UNIT-PLAN.md` rule 19, an owned standard must have dedicated teaching, practice, and a check item covering every part of the standard. A generic task template plus 14 cluster data sheets does not give each of the 58 sub-clusters its own lesson and check item, and an individual student taking Pathways will never encounter roughly 45 to 50 of the 58 sub-clusters the course claims to own.

Every Pathways cluster or sub-cluster row names its parent cluster row in Exploration in `assumes`, and its `notes` state the skip-grade reason: "Skips grade 9 on purpose: grade 9 has no required career education course; Pathways is taught in grade 9 or 10 and sits at grade 10 for the gate."

- **Confidence:** Firm that C1 and the skip-grade handoff rule are breached; Judgment call on whether to exclude unchosen sub-clusters in `cte-excluded.csv` vs keep them owned with a disclosure (see Question 1 for Cory).

### 3. Pacing clashes with `CALENDAR.md` (36 weeks vs 32 weeks), and the short-path arithmetic does not add up

- **Where:** `APPROACH.md` "Pacing" (lines 89 to 94) and "The kinds of day" (line 17); `docs/courses/CALENDAR.md` (lines 11 to 22); `docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md` C2 ("The days do not add up to the stated year").
- **What is wrong:** Two arithmetic and calendar faults:
  1. **36 weeks vs 32 weeks:** `APPROACH.md` sets default pacing to "36 weeks, three sessions a week of 45 minutes, so 108 sessions or 81 hours a year; a school at two sessions a week runs the short path, 72 sessions or 54 hours." Every yearlong Curio Class course runs on the 32-week (160-school-day) calendar in `docs/courses/CALENDAR.md` (which `scripts/frame.py` uses to map course days onto school days and weeks; `sel/STANDARD.md` uses 32 weeks: 64 sessions at 2/week and 32 at 1/week). At 32 weeks, 3 sessions/week is **96 sessions (72 hours)** and 2 sessions/week is **64 sessions (48 hours)**.
  2. **Broken short-path subtraction:** `APPROACH.md` line 91 says the short path cuts 36 sessions (from 108 to 72, or 32 sessions under the 32-week calendar) by dropping "the second field task in each unit." Yet `APPROACH.md` line 17 requires only "at least one field task day" per unit, so a unit may not have a second field task at all. Even if a course has 6 to 8 units and every unit has a second field task of 1 or 2 sessions, dropping it removes only 6 to 16 sessions, nowhere near 32 or 36 sessions.
- **Cost:** Frame writers cannot pass `scripts/frame.py` calendar checks, and the short path fails C2 because its dropped sessions do not equal the stated session reduction.
- **Fix (replace `APPROACH.md` "Pacing", lines 89 to 94; ours, untested; calendar choice is Question 3 for Cory):**

> Settled 2026-11-16 (Cory): each course is one year at two or three sessions a week. Default time on the Curio Class 32-week calendar (`docs/courses/CALENDAR.md`), ours and untested: 32 weeks, three sessions a week of 45 minutes, so 96 sessions or 72 hours a year. A school at two sessions a week runs the short path: 64 sessions or 48 hours, dropping 32 named sessions across the year (each unit drops its second field task and its extra explore or skill practice sessions, named by day in the scope and sequence, while keeping at least one field task day, the plan day, the assessment day, and the respond day in every unit). No national body publishes a time figure for these courses that we found (Advance CTE, the Association for Career and Technical Education, and NCDA searched 2026-11-20; Advance CTE and ACTE's middle grades design principles recommend dedicated instructional time with flexible delivery models, and NCDA's stages are not tied to an age), so none is cited, and the course page says so. Each frame may change these numbers with a reason.

- **Confidence:** Firm.
