# Strategic review of the whole project, 2026-11-11

A cold read by a curriculum program strategist with no earlier knowledge of the project. Not an edit. This file
is the only thing written. Phase 1 was written from the foundation, the bar, the rubric file, the status table
and an inventory, before the roadmap, the handoff or any plan was opened. Phase 2 checks it against the
project's own path.

## 1. Verdict

1. The direction is right. The re-founding of 2026-11-11 (layers, unit plan before tool, key and light tools,
   print as a full path, checks written first) is sound design and better than what came before it.
2. The path is not yet solid, because nothing below it has touched the ground. There is no lesson, no student
   page, no played tool, no teacher's note and no student result anywhere in the repo. Every size, minute and
   cost in the plans is a guess, and the files say so.
3. Three things matter most:
   - **Build the pilot unit now, not after 30 more unit plans.** The plan with sizes and dates for 105 courses
     waits on it, and so does the template every other plan is written to.
   - **Put a human and a machine into the checking.** Today an AI writes, an AI reads cold, and an AI reworks the
     keys. No qualified person and no script stands behind any fact, key or licence.
   - **Stop spending below the proof.** Spec fixing, eight more spines, bars for parked subjects and item-level
     owner decisions are all running ahead of the first finished unit.

## 3. Phase 1: my independent plan

Written before I opened the roadmap, the handoff, the unit plan template or any plan.

### What the inventory told me

- 105 courses, 56 core. Three have a spine. One has any unit plan. None has a lesson, print, a check with a key
  or a teacher page. That last line of `COURSES-STATUS.md` is the true state of the project.
- Grade 4 math is 43 units, 18 stretches and 150 days in `data/units.csv`. If that is typical, the core is
  about 2,400 units and 8,000 teaching days. All 105 courses are on the order of 10,000 teaching days and
  100,000 keyed items.
- 817 tool folders, 764 specs, 241 built files, 83 live. All were made before the foundation.
- The git log I could see starts 2026-10-22: 725 commits in 21 days. The foundation is one day old. The pace is
  very high and the work of the last two days is almost all documents about documents.

### The phases, in order

**Phase A. One unit to the bottom (the vertical slice).** One five-day unit: plan, five lesson days, print,
checks with keys, one tool, teacher page, family note. Produces: the first thing a teacher can use, and the
first real numbers. Gate to leave: the owner has opened it; a working teacher of that grade band has read it;
at least one day has been taught to at least one child; every key reproduced by something other than its
author; the unit's cost written down.

**Phase B. Unit economics.** From phase A, record for each artifact: tokens, wall hours, owner minutes, defects
found per read. Produces: cost per lesson day, per unit, per key tool. Gate: a one-page model that multiplies
out to one course and to 56, with the owner's hours as a named constraint.

**Phase C. One stretch on major work.** A full run of 10 to 15 days on the most important idea of the grade,
including a readiness check, an end check and a respond day. Produces: proof the method holds across units and
that the template is stable. Gate: template frozen as version 1; defect rate per day falling.

**Phase D. One whole course.** Grade 4 math, all 150 days. Built in stretches, each released when done, not held
for the year. Gate: a whole-course read against the rubric file; a human content read of every check; an
accessibility pass; two teachers' notes on file.

**Phase E. One unit each in the other three core subjects**, in parallel with phase D. Produces: the three other
cost models and the three other templates. Gate: each subject has a rubric file, an approach page and one
finished unit before any second spine in that subject.

**Phase F. Scale by band.** Grades 3 to 5 math first (the progression is shared), then outward. Courses in
parallel only up to the owner's reading capacity. Gate per course: the finished-course list.

**Phase G. Packaging.** State alignment documents, accessibility statements, version stamps, a teacher guide per
course. Starts at the end of phase D so it is designed on a real course.

### Critical path and what runs beside it

Critical path: lesson template, pilot unit, human read, cost model, stretch, course. Nothing else shortens the
date of the first finished course.

Parallel and safe: rubric files and approach pages for the other core subjects; the ELA text rights work, which
is slow and has outside dependencies; the item-checking script; accessibility tooling. Not safe to run in
parallel: more spines, more unit plans and more specs, because each is shaped by a template that phase A will
change.

### Owner decisions and when

Before phase A: who the teacher reader is; whether any money is spent on human review. After phase B: how many
courses a season holds. Before phase E: the social studies content standard; the K to 2 device assumption.
Before phase F: the triage of old tools; the order of courses.

### Quality at scale

- Machine checks where an answer is computable: every math key, every standard code, every day count.
- Cold AI reads on every unit, and a second cold read on a one-in-five sample to measure the first.
- A qualified human on a sample of every course and on every item of a course's summative checks.
- A real teacher on one unit per course at least, and on every course's first stretch for the first five courses.
- A defect ledger: what was found, by which reader, at which layer. The rate per reader tells you which read to
  keep paying for.

### Triage of pre-foundation work

Decide by demand, not by inspection. A tool earns course work only when a unit asks for it. Everything live stays
live as a free-standing shelf with honest labels. Everything unbuilt is frozen. Count the three bins after the
first stretch and again after the first course.

### Biggest risks and how each is retired early

| Risk | Retired by |
|---|---|
| A unit costs far more than hoped | Phase A cost sheet, in week one |
| Plans read well and teach badly | One taught day, phase A |
| AI readers share the writer's blind spots | A human sample and the defect ledger |
| The owner becomes the bottleneck | Decision rules; a count of owner minutes per unit |
| ELA text rights block the subject | Start the rights list now |
| Old tools pull the design | The demand rule, stated before the count |

### What "done" means for one course

Every day on the spine has a teachable lesson and its print. Every standard is taught and checked in full. Every
key has been reproduced. Every key tool is live and played. The course has a teacher guide, pacing options and
family notes. A rubric read is on file with findings closed. A qualified human has read the checks. Two teachers
have used at least one stretch. Public pages claim only what the ledger shows.

## 4. Phase 2: the comparison

### Where the project's path matches mine

- Prove one course before fixing or scaling (ROADMAP, "Changed after a cold review").
- Count what ships, not what specs say (the four-column measure).
- A written definition of a finished course, with a gap table that closes only on evidence.
- A cost for one tool and one unit recorded before scale (ROADMAP step 4, "Done when").
- A logic model, and no claim of results.
- Tool building paused.

### Where it differs and the project is right

- **Checks at the stretch, not the unit.** I would have put a check on every unit. The expert showed that this
  took about a fifth of the year (`docs/reviews/math-4-spine-2026-11-11.md`). The project's rule is better.
- **Key and light tools, with print as the fallback path.** I assumed a course needed its tools to be runnable.
  FOUNDATION section 6 makes a course runnable with few tools. That shortens the critical path more than
  anything I proposed.
- **Checks written before days, with a lettered error register.** I had assessment later. Writing the end check
  first is what caught the missing parts of standards (UNIT-PLAN.md, "What the expert read changed", 3).
- **Triage by demand.** I had this, but the project's wording is sharper: "an existing spec or tool carries no
  weight against what the unit needs".
- **"Always cover the whole standard"** as a standing rule. Correct, and it is exactly what a review counts.

### Where it differs and the project is at risk

**Risk 1. The pilot sits behind 30 unit plans and a key-tool ranking it does not need.**
Evidence: ROADMAP step table, 4d then 4e then 4f. HANDOFF: "Then 4e ... and 4f (build pilot unit 11.1 and have it
read)". `COURSES-STATUS.md`: "The plan with sizes and dates is written after the grade 4 math pilot unit". Yet
`s11.md` is written, its key tool was ruled on ("Community Plot Planner stands"), and only its expert read is
owed. Cost if it lands: the remaining 30 plans come to roughly 86,000 words plus reads. If the pilot changes the
fields, all 43 are reworked. The whole 105-course plan stays unsized for that time. Recommendation: 1 and 2
above. Cory acts. Done: unit 11.1 is built before stretch 5 is planned.

**Risk 2. The method's weight, measured from the files.**
Six stretch sheets hold 14,960 words. Eleven unit plans hold 19,612. `TOOL-ASKS.md` holds 2,768. That is about
37,300 words for 13 units, or 2,900 words a unit, before any lesson. Two expert reads add 9,000. One course at
this rate is about 125,000 words of plans and 30,000 of reviews. The 56 core courses are about 7 million words
of plans. At a careful reading speed that is over eight hours per course for a person, or about 470 hours for
the core, against the 10 to 20 hours a week the roadmap gives Cory. So the plan layer will never be read by a
person. That can be acceptable, but only if it is treated as a machine contract: structured, script-checked and
sampled. Today it is prose with word caps. The caps moved from 1,500 to 1,000 to 1,250 in one evening, and
`u03-4.md` is still over. The owner was asked to rule on word counts three times in a day. That is the
template becoming the product. Where to cut without lowering the bar: (a) move check items, exit questions and
errors into data and generate the tables; (b) write supports, the "two rooms" field and the family note once per
stretch unless a unit differs; (c) drop caps in favour of "no field restates another"; (d) plan a stretch only
when the stretch before it is built, so plans stop running ahead. Who: Claude proposes after the pilot; Cory
says yes once.

**Risk 3. No evidence from a classroom, and the first reader is the wrong grade.**
Evidence: HANDOFF, "No plan, check or tool here has been tried with a student or read by a working teacher".
FOUNDATION 7.2 names a grade 2 teacher as the reader of a grade 4 unit. `docs/pilots/` does not exist.
`unit-plans-4d-2026-11-11.md` says days 3, 4 and 7 hold more than fifty minutes. `s03.md` guesses 25 to 28
minutes for seven items. Cost: every time figure in a 150-day spine may be off in the same direction, and that
is found only after the year is written. Smallest real test: recommendation 5. One taught day beats ten more
cold reads.

**Risk 4. Verification debt.**
What rests on unchecked agent reports: the source search and the bar for other subjects (HANDOFF says so); the
standards currency check; the catalog read, which its own spot check put at 80 percent; every answer key; every
"confirmed" page pointer; all three expert reads of the spines. In `s03.md`, seven of eleven errors are "from
memory". `verified.txt` has no entries, so the Verified column is zero. The expert and the writer are the same
kind of model with the same training, so a cold read removes anchoring but not shared error. FOUNDATION 7.1
says this "has to be good enough". I disagree for four things: mathematical correctness of summative checks,
science safety, text rights, and sensitive or community content. BASELINE rule 5 ("We don't wait on outside human
reviewers") was set on 2026-10-27, two weeks before the bar. It should be asked again. Recommendations 4 and 7.

**Risk 5. The old catalog is still drawing work, and the triage is undecided.**
Evidence: FOUNDATION's open question waits for "the grade 4 math unit plans". Meanwhile the git log shows spec
fix batches 1 and 2 landing on 2026-11-11, including eleven grade 6 to 8 ELA specs for a course with no unit
plan. BASELINE rule 22 still says major work "gets two or three tools", and nine second tools were approved.
But the plans say otherwise: `s03.md`, "No unit has a key tool; the stretch runs on print", for 15 days of the
grade's most important work. Two rules now pull in opposite directions. Sunk-cost risk: 431 specs were written
in the three days before the foundation. Loss risk: 83 live tools that work alone are worth keeping whatever the
courses ask for. Recommendation 6. Also apply the same question to the other shelves: 1,008 worksheets and the
Word Wall were also made before the foundation, and no rule says how a lesson day uses or replaces them.

**Risk 6. Layers are being built on layers that are not solid.**
- The approach pages are "second draft" and Cory has not read them (HANDOFF, layer 3), yet 17 plan files follow
  them.
- The science and ELA spines were drafted before any science or ELA rubric file or approach page existed
  (`COURSES-STATUS.md` shows "no" and "shared page only" for both). The approach review found the shared core
  was "partly a math page in disguise" and contradicted the science spine.
- Stretches 3, 9, 10 and 11 have had no expert read in their current form, and 11 is the pilot.
- Sara's list holds eight more math spines and two progression pages, all to be "corrected by the grade 4 pilot".
This is the project's own layer rule being bent. `DESK-ANSWERS.md` says so: "This bends 'one course finished
before the next is started' on purpose". The cost is rework and, worse, a growing pile of drafts that each need
an owner's yes.

**Risk 7. The first non-math proof has no date and no trigger.**
ROADMAP: "Grade 6 ELA follows the same path", and science and social studies "wait their turn". The ELA review
says three of six text sets do not exist and the shelf has almost no informational text. That is a rights and
sourcing problem with long lead time, and it is the real critical path for 13 courses. Social studies is rightly
blocked on the content standard, and that question is rightly near the top of Sara's list. But it is a scope
decision for 14 courses and should be worked up by the Claude session with the expert, then decided by Cory,
not left as one person's side task. Recommendation 8.

**Risk 8. Owner decisions: too many small, too few large.**
About 55 answers were given on 2026-11-11 across eight cards. Many could have been settled by rule (stretch 10
number sizes, pattern terms under 1,000, the facts check counts). The roadmap heading says "Decisions, kept
few". Decided by default instead: that no human expert is required; that 105 courses proceed with two people;
that the pilot waits; that the licence fix waits; that the parked subjects got a 7,500-word bar on the same day
the core-first rule was written. Recommendation 9.

**Risk 9. Sara's list is ordered well and sized badly.**
`NEXT-SPINES.md` has ten pieces: five expert reads, a standards policy paper, three or more rubric files, two
approach pages, a text plan with licence checks, two progression pages, five stretches of plans, eight spines.
That is months of steering, and nearly all of it is draft layers under an unproven template. What should stay:
piece 1 (the reads on the pilot's critical path) and her own "Sara's read" as a person, which is the only human
judgment in the loop. What should come off: stretches 4 to 8 (collision risk with the main session, which
HANDOFF admits); the eight spines until the pilot is read; licence judgments on texts; and any piece where she
is the only reviewer of her own draft, a gap the roadmap already named.

**Risk 10. Legal and public-repo points.**
- `LICENSE-CONTENT.md` covers "data" with CC BY 4.0 while `data/standards/ccss-*.txt` carry full text under the
  Common Core public licence. The file headers are correct; the licence page is not. Deferred in HANDOFF.
- The rubric files summarise all-rights-reserved criteria in a public repo. The approach is careful (own words,
  links, no quotes). Keep summaries short and structural. A long indicator-by-indicator paraphrase can still be
  a derivative work. Have a person compare `RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md` with the source once.
- "Public copy names no companies" holds on the site, but the public repo names review bodies and open courses
  throughout. That is fine as citation. Make sure no generated site page pulls from those docs.
- Adapting any lesson from an open course needs attribution in the lesson itself, not only in a sources field.
  No lesson exists yet, so set the rule in the lesson-day template.
- ELA texts: "free legal copy" and "licence" are on Sara's list as a check. That is a rights decision and
  should have a second reader.
- `docs/US Curriculum Design Blueprint.pdf` is cited as a source. I did not open it. If it contains copied
  third-party text, it should not sit in a public repo.

**Risk 11. What is missing from the plan.**
- **A lesson-day definition.** The largest layer has no template (recommendation 3).
- **Assessment validation.** No item has been tried. Marks such as "32 of 36" are "ours, untested". There is no
  plan to check timing, difficulty or whether the two forms of a check are equal.
- **Accessibility conformance.** The plan has hooks and "an automated access check". Reviews ask for conformance
  to a named level, for tools and for print files. No level is named.
- **Versioning and errata.** A live site that changes mid-year breaks a teacher's course. No rule says how a
  unit is versioned, frozen for a school year, or corrected.
- **A course-level teacher guide and pacing options.** The spine promises "a shorter path"; no artifact holds it.
- **Print volume.** Paper is a first-class product, and a print-led course has a real cost per student per year.
  Nobody is counting pages.
- **State alignment.** Deferred as "the crosswalk, later". Most states do not use the Common Core as written.
  For "any state", one worked crosswalk on the first finished course is part of the proof, not a later track.
- **Spanish.** Family notes are bilingual. Student materials are not addressed.
- **High school math and K to 2.** No rubric file for high school math is on anyone's list. K to 2 lacks its
  device decision and, for ELA, a phonics scope, which is specialist work.
- **Device and browser testing** on the machines schools own.
- **Capacity.** No budget of tokens, money or owner hours exists. The usage limit already stopped four agents
  mid-run (HANDOFF). A plan that is firm on scope needs a stated capacity so the date can be honest.
