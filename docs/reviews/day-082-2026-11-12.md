## Verdict

Day 82 is correct mathematics in a sound fluency shape: three short worked sentences, sixteen mixed items in four
checked sets, a judged task and a three-item exit on new numbers, with the one rule ("the bigger amount stands
alone") said once and left on the board, and a real tie to place value at the end. The biggest risk is in the
marking: students self-mark a fluency count against keys that print only one order of the factors, so a child who
writes the equally right 35 = 5 × 7 untics it, and the teacher marks the exit slips from keys that print one form
only. The one change that matters most is to make every key accept both factor orders and both sides of the equals
sign (finding 1); after that, relabel the exit's 16 + 8 = 24 (finding 2) so it reaches a response on day 83.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The keys mark right equations wrong on a self-marked fluency count

- **What is wrong.** The key and the check screens print "35 = 7 × 5, or 7 × 5 = 35", never 35 = 5 × 7 or
  5 × 7 = 35, for every sentence-to-equation item. The three exit keys print one form only: "14 = 2 × 7",
  "10 × 6 = 60", "16 = 2 × 8". The teacher page says "Either side of the equals sign is fine" but is silent on
  factor order. 4.OA.A.1 reads 35 = 5 × 7 as both "5 times as many as 7" and "7 times as many as 5", so either
  order is a right equation for either sentence; IM grade 4 unit 5 lesson 3 writes comparison equations with the
  factors in both orders.
- **Evidence.** d082-key.html, d082-display.html check screens; items.csv answer cells; the standard text
  (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt, 4.OA.A.1).
- **Cost.** Students tick "right first try" from the screen; a child who writes 45 = 9 × 5 untics a right answer,
  fixes it into the key's order and learns that order matters when it does not. The fluency target is read from
  those ticks. On the slip, a teacher marking from the key may put a letter on the record for 2 × 7 = 14.
- **Change.** Every sentence-to-equation answer cell on day 82 ends "(the two factors in either order)", and the
  exit cells gain their second form, as under "Settled". The teacher page's rule line becomes: "Either side of the
  equals sign, and either order of the two factors, is fine." Process: plan.py, for a row whose answer is a
  multiplication equation, prints the accept line itself (the factors commute, the sides swap), so later days do
  not retype it.
- **Confidence.** Firm (the standard and one open course agree).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (s11 plan read: self-marked keys lacked accept lists).

### 2. The exit's 16 + 8 = 24 carries error B, and B's wording does not fit day 82

- **What is wrong.** wrongs.csv gives `s10-X82c` the wrong answer "16 + 8 = 24, or 16 × 2 = 32" as one row,
  error B. 16 + 8 is an addition of the two lengths, not a multiplication: it is D's "counts both amounts
  together". Day 83 answers B with "who has more?" and names only 16 × 2 = 32, so the 16 + 8 students reach no
  response. Separately, the key prints B's register text, "when the smaller amount or the number of times is
  unknown", beside 48 × 6 = 288 and 50 × 10 = 500, where nothing is unknown: on day 82 B is "multiplies the bigger
  amount again".
- **Evidence.** wrongs.csv s10-X82c; d082 "Tomorrow"; d083 "5 min. Readiness item 2, or day 82's exit back";
  errors.csv B and D; d082-key.html.
- **Cost.** One wrong answer on the slip goes to the wrong group or none; the key tells the teacher a story about
  an unknown that is not there.
- **Change.** Split the row: `16 + 8 = 24` error D, note "both lengths added"; `16 × 2 = 32` error B. "Tomorrow"
  adds: "16 + 8 = 24 (error D, both lengths added) joins day 83's A group: the strips side by side." errors.csv B
  wording as under "Settled".
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 91: wrong-answer rows wrong; s11 plan: wrong answers
  with no letter).

### 3. The student page has no room for the sentences it asks for

- **What is wrong.** On the rendered PDF each answer cell is half the width and about 0.4 inches tall. Half the

  nine-year-old's hand). "Tick it if right first try" has no place for the tick, and the fix in a second colour
  has no room beside the first try. The written task's box is 0.9 inches for six strips; the challenge has no room
  ("on lined paper" is on the teacher page only). Meanwhile the bottom 2.5 inches of side 2 are empty.
- **Evidence.** build/lessons/math-4/u10-1/d082-student.pdf, both sides.
- **Cost.** Students cram or skip the second half of each sentence, which is the part (a) work; the teacher

- **Change.** Each set table gets three columns: the item (40 percent), "Write it" (50 percent, rows 3em tall),
  and a narrow "✓" column. The written task gets the empty space: two `{sketch}` boxes stacked, or a taller box if
  lesson.py offers one. Add "Challenge: use lined paper." to the challenge line. Re-run the side count.
- **Confidence.** Firm (seen on the page).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 89 finding 2, day 92 layout).

## What is right and should not be touched

- The shape: three worked sentences, four short mixed sets each checked against the key before the next, ticks for
  a right first try, fixes in a second colour, "a student compares only with their own last set". It is fluency
  built with no timer, as the unit plan asks.
- The mix: both directions, counted and measured, "times as much/long/tall", ten times a two-digit number in set 3
  and a one-digit number times a multiple of 10 in set 4. All inside the numbers rule.
- "We do not multiply 48 by 6: the 48 is already the whale", and "The equation has no feet in it; the sentence
  tells what each number counts." Exactly the B guard and the MP.4 point (Progressions margin, via the progression
  page).
- The planned question "A ten is how many times as much as a one?", the unit's one tie to 4.NBT.A.1.
- The exit: three items on new numbers, both sentence orders and one measured sentence, with signs of A, B and C.
- Day 82 to day 83 for A and B is real: day 83's second part names E82's errors and acts on them; C is sent back on
  the slips with "Which is bigger?", which is honest.
