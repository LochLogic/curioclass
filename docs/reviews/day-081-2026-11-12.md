## Verdict

Day 81 is correct arithmetic, well paced, and squarely on 4.OA.A.1's "interpret 35 = 5 × 7" clause; a teacher
could run it with no prep. The biggest risk is that it never shows a comparison: every picture the class sees
(the cut strips, both worked examples) is equal groups, two bars of 12 with no single strip to compare them
with, so students can finish the day reading "12 is 3 times as many as 4" as grade 3's "3 groups of 4 is 12".
The one change that matters most is to put the one-copy strip into the notice task and both examples (finding 1);
next is to turn the fix round so it practises the day's direction, equation to sentence (finding 2).

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The day's pictures show equal groups, not a comparison

- **What is wrong.** A multiplicative comparison has two amounts: the smaller one and the larger, measured in
  copies of it. The unit opener's own strip picture says so ("One strip for the smaller amount. Under it ... a
  row of equal copies"), and the unit plan names it as what the teacher page must explain ("How a comparison
  differs from grade 3's equal groups: two amounts, one measured in copies of the other"). Day 81 does neither.
  The notice task lays 3 strips of 4 beside 4 strips of 3 (two arrays of 12); the figures for Example 1
  (`strip 3_fours:4*3=12 4_threes:3*4=12`) and Example 2 draw two bars of 12 and 42 with no 4-strip, 3-strip,
  7-strip or 6-strip on its own. The display screens confirm it. Only the fix's right picture has a "One copy"
  bar. The teacher page never says what makes 12 = 3 × 4 a comparison rather than equal groups, and the planned
  question's expected answers ("12 is 3 fours and 4 threes") are equal-groups language.
- **Evidence.** opener.md "The strip picture"; u10-1.md field 1; d081-display.html. Progressions, Operations and
  Algebraic Thinking, PDF page 44 (38): the tape diagram for a multiplicative compare shows a smaller tape and
  the larger as copies of it. Illustrative Mathematics grade 4, unit 5, lesson 2 (read today): the smaller amount
  is drawn as its own unit beside the copies, and the synthesis says outright that one equation can show equal
  groups or a comparison.
- **Cost.** The day's goal is to read an equation as a comparison. Without the second amount in sight, the two
  sentences are only the commutative property said with new words, and unit 10.2 (smaller amount unknown, the
  number of times unknown) needs exactly the picture this day leaves out.
- **Change.** Notice task: each partner also cuts one extra strip of their size and lays it above their row:
  "the one copy". Example figures and one step, as under "Settled by the expert". Add one sentence to "Tie the
  strips to 12 = 3 × 4": "Equal groups is one amount. A comparison is two amounts: the 12 and one 4. The 12 is
  measured in 4s." Pull it together expects, last: "12 is 3 times as many as 4, and 4 times as many as 3,
  because the 4 and the 3 can swap jobs." The exit key accepts the copies only when the one-copy strip is drawn
  too. Pay for the words by cutting the "If day 80's error rides here" line to "If day 80's error rides here, a
  pair counts its copies aloud."
- **Confidence.** Firm (the unit's own model, two outside sources).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read (against the opener's strip picture). **REPEAT:** yes (day 92 finding
  13: a picture that is not the model the unit carries).

### 2. "Fix it" practises day 82's direction, not day 81's

- **What is wrong.** Ben reads a sentence and writes an equation (3 = 4 × 12); the student writes "Right
  equation". That is sentence to equation, the part (b) that day 82 teaches, and the row is coded part b. Day 81
  teaches equation to sentence, and the slip's sign of error C is in that direction ("7 is 4 times as many as
  28"). So the one wrong example on the day fixes an error in a form students have not been taught yet, and does
  not model the form the slip tests. The source is the register: errors.csv sets C's example in the writing
  direction and gives it to day 81. Day 82 shows the same example again in its "exit back" part, where it fits.
- **Evidence.** items.csv `s10-F81-1` (part b); errors.csv stretch 10 C; d082.md "Day 81's exit back"; u10-1.md
  field 1 (day 81 is MP.2 and interpret, day 82 is MP.4 and represent).
- **Cost.** Six minutes spent on a skill one day early; students who swap roles when reading see no model of
  the fix in reading form before the slip.
- **Change.** Retire `s10-F81-1` from day 81 and add `s10-F81-2` (rows under "Settled by the expert"): Ben reads
  12 = 4 × 3 and says "3 is 4 times as many as 12". Student page: "What went wrong" stays; "Right equation"
  becomes the two sentence frames. Process: errors.csv lets a letter carry one example per direction, or the
  plan check warns when a fix row's part differs from every other row's part on its day.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process (the register set the example's direction). **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new.

### 3. The readiness reteach disagrees with the unit opener

- **What is wrong.** The opener's reteach (readiness item 1): pairs, 15 counters into 3 rows, then 24 into 3

  where the opener says 30 per pair.
- **Evidence.** opener.md "The reteach" and "Supplies"; items.csv `s10-R1`; d081.md header and first two parts.
- **Cost.** A teacher who read the opener the night before (rule 5 assumes it) finds a different reteach on the

- **Change.** Day 81 follows the opener: "Students who missed readiness item 1 ... deal 15 counters into 3 rows,
  then 24, as in the unit opener." Whole-class branch: "the warm-up and these 5 minutes are the reteach; the
  warm-up sums move to day 82's warm-up." Materials: "per pair, 30 counters (only if the reteach runs)".
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 91 finding 2: the day and the unit's other pieces
  disagree).

### 7. Small wording repairs

- **Check line in Read it both ways.** "Is the number that stands alone the bigger amount?" is about an equation,
  but students are checking sentences. Say: "Does each sentence start with the bigger amount?" (it then matches
  the six equations' check).
- **Grid paper size.** A 3 by 4 rectangle on quarter-inch or centimeter grid is too small for nine-year-olds to cut
  into strips. Say "1-inch or 2-centimeter grid paper" in the header and the notice task.
- **Print list.** "the readiness results from day 79" is not printed; move it to "Before class".
- **Strip sheet lines.** The sheet's boxes end with "Equation with a letter" and "Answer in a sentence". Tell
  students: "Write the equation on the letter line; leave the answer line blank today."
- **"Read the first two aloud first: the product sits on either side."** Say: "Read equations 1 and 2 aloud
  first: the product can be on either side of the equals sign."
- **Check screen at 12 minutes.** The display shows all six answers; the teacher page has partners check one
  equation. Say: "At 12 minutes, partners check one equation against the strips; then show the answers screen."
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 89 finding 9, day 91 finding 14, day 93 finding 11).
