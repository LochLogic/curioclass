## Verdict

The day's design is sound for its standard: a spoken sourcing model, then four sources of four kinds in an organizer,
a share that sorts limits by maker, time and purpose, a judged exit, a careful content note, and the right teacher
lines on Cumming and on the cartoon as an argument. It cannot be taught today because no source has its text or
image, so the probe can judge the day's frame but not its reading load or whether the exit can be answered from the
cuts. The biggest risk once the sources exist is the exit: it asks students to repeat, from the same four sources,
what they just wrote in the organizer, so it measures recall of the organizer rather than sourcing on their own. The
one change that matters most is a short unseen source on the exit ticket.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The exit reuses the four sources students just finished in the organizer, so it does not measure sourcing alone

- **What is wrong.** s05-X80 asks for two details of service from "today's sources", their kinds and one limit.
  Students spent 20 minutes writing exactly that for B to E in pairs (s05-P80-2: maker, service, detail, limit), and
  the share read limits aloud. The organizer is collected before the exit, but the source sheet stays out, so a
  student can rebuild a pair's answer from memory. Days 78 and 82 in the same section put an unseen source on the
  exit (78E, 82E and 82F); day 80 does not. LESSON-DAY's "What changes by subject" reads rule 3 for social studies as
  "an unseen source", and the lens asks that the exit check a skill used on content.
- **Why it matters.** Day 82's warm-up and the record sheet's look-for ticks will read a recalled pair answer as an
  individual's skill. The standard (MA.USI.T5.5) is "use" sources; the day's only individual evidence of use is
  this ticket.
- **Fix.** Add a short unseen source to the ticket, about 100 words, public domain, not reserved for day 89 (Gooding
  and Taylor are). The sensitivity read's finding 2 asks for a Black soldier's own wartime letter on day 80; put that
  letter on the ticket as Source F, so it does both jobs. Stem: "Read Source F. Who wrote it, when and for whom? Give
  one detail of service it shows, and one thing it cannot tell us. Then name one of today's sources B to E that shows
  something F cannot." Look-fors: maker, date and audience; a detail of service; a limit tied to maker, time or
  purpose; a second source named for what it adds. Candidate letters (each to be checked against a transcription
  and logged in sources.csv): Lewis Douglass, sergeant major of the 54th Massachusetts and the son of Source A's
  author, to Amelia Loguen after Fort Wagner, July 1863; Spotswood Rice to his children, September 1864. Update the
  unit plan's day 80 exit row, the section plan's Day 80 source list and E80, items.csv s05-X80 and wrongs.csv.
- **Confidence.** Likely (the section's own days 78 and 82 and LESSON-DAY's subject rule agree; the bar only
  requires unseen sources on the end-of-unit assessment).
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN unit.

### 2. The repair rule cuts the day's only sourcing model in half

- **What is wrong.** If more than a third of day 79's tickets show error D, the day takes the warm-up "and the
  example's first five minutes", leaving five minutes for the Douglass model: reading A aloud (about 120 words),
  six steps, and filling the frame. "Model every step in the remaining five minutes" cannot be done. The rule ("the
  next day's warm-up and first five minutes repair it") is the math day's, in the section plan field 3 and the
  unit plan, from UNIT-PLAN.md and LESSON-DAY. In math the first minutes after a warm-up are often a review; here
  they are the sourcing model the whole day stands on.
- **Why it matters.** A class that confused Gettysburg and Vicksburg loses the model it needs for the organizer and
  exit, which are on a different standard (T5.5) from the repair (T5.4).
- **Fix.** Template: in a subject whose next day teaches a different standard, the repair is the warm-up plus a
  small group, never the next day's model. In d080: "If more than a third of day 79's tickets show error D, run the
  sort in the warm-up and take a small group of those students during Pairs; the worked example keeps its 10
  minutes." The same change in d080's own Tomorrow for day 82 (finding 4).
- **Confidence.** Firm (the minutes do not fit; the rule's source is the shared template).
- LAYER process; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN template.

### 3. Error letters and source letters share A to I, and the day's prose cannot be read without guessing which

- **What is wrong.** The error register uses A to I; every source sheet uses A to G. On day 80: "Address B with day
  79's E" (error B, Source 79E); "E argues for voting rights ... For E or H, separate observation from inference"
  (Source E, then error E and error H in the next sentence); "Flag E for inferred feelings" in Tomorrow;
  "Accept oral reasoning to distinguish language gaps from E or H". The gate also cannot tell them apart: day 82
  names "H" (its Lost Cause error), so the check that day 82 picks up day 80's H passes when it does not (finding 4).
- **Why it matters.** A teacher reading the night before has to work out each letter; a teacher who reads "E" as
  Source E flags the wrong students.
- **Fix.** Template (one rule line in LESSON-DAY, social studies): in prose, an error is always written "error E",
  a source always "Source E" (or "79E" for another day's), and the gate fails a bare capital letter in the day's
  prose. In d080 apply it throughout.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- LAYER process; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed (the cheap read caught the H meaning clash, not the
  collision with source letters); ORIGIN template.

### 6. Source C's maker and the troops' name are not what the sheet and the day say

- **What is wrong.** (a) The sheet and sources.csv give Source C as "an antislavery newspaper's report". The famous
  passage ("Colonel Montgomery and his gallant band of 300 black soldiers, under the guidance of a black woman")
  was, to my knowledge, a correspondent's dispatch first printed in the Wisconsin State Journal in June 1863 and
  reprinted in the Commonwealth on July 10 inside F. B. Sanborn's article on Tubman, which is where she is named.
  So the maker, date and audience a student must write in the organizer depend on which part is cut. (b) The day
  puts "United States Colored Troops names Black Union regiments" right after the Combahee sentence. The soldiers on
  the raid were the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers, renamed the 34th United States Colored Troops only in 1864 (the
  sensitivity read's NPS source agrees). Students will write "USCT at the Combahee".
- **Why it matters.** This is the day that teaches sourcing; the source with the weakest provenance is the one
  carrying the fourth look-for.
- **Fix.** sources.csv 80C: cite the Commonwealth article by author (Sanborn) and note the reprinted dispatch, then
  cut a passage that includes both the dispatch's opening and Sanborn's sentence naming Tubman, so the organizer row
  can read "a Boston editor in July 1863, reprinting a soldier-correspondent's report". In d080 replace the USCT
  sentence with: "The soldiers were the 2nd South Carolina Volunteers, most of them men who had escaped slavery;
  in 1864 the regiment became the 34th United States Colored Troops, the army's name for its Black regiments."
- **Confidence.** (a) Likely, from memory; check the July 10 issue before cutting. (b) Firm.
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed (the cheap read said the phrase was checked against a reprint,
  not that the maker is wrong); ORIGIN unit.

### 8. The model names a limit the other sources answer, and the day never asks students to see it

- **What is wrong.** The worked example ends on Source A's limit: a call to serve cannot show who joined or how they
  were treated. C (Black soldiers on the Combahee) and D (a Black infantry company in 1865) answer exactly that, and E
  raises the treatment question. The day never asks it. Corroboration, the lens's third sourcing move, is absent on
  the section's main sourcing day.
- **Why it matters.** Students learn "every source has a limit" as a list, not that a limit is a question another
  source can answer, which is what day 82 needs.
- **Fix.** In Share, replace "Within the organizer, discuss what a photograph cannot tell you. Expect motives." with
  "**Ask.** Which of B to E answers something Source A cannot show? Expect: C and D show Black men did serve; E asks
  what their service should earn. What can the photograph still not tell you? Expect: why they joined or what they
  felt." This also gives the section plan's planned question its **Ask.** (finding 12).
- **Confidence.** Likely (the lens and the SHEG sourcing, context, corroboration sequence; from memory).
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN day.
