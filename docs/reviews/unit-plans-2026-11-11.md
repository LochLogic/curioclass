# Review: the unit plan template and its three trial plans

Reviewed 2026-11-11 by the curriculum expert, cold. Under review: docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md and the trial plans
docs/courses/math-4/u03-1.md, u09-1.md and u11-1.md (roadmap step 4c). Read first: FOUNDATION, ROADMAP ("The
layers"), APPROACH, APPROACH-MATH, APPROACH-SOURCES, RUBRIC-MATH-K8, the grade 4 math spine, the grades 3 to 5
progression, the grade 4 science and grade 6 ELA spines, the five earlier reviews, and the three tool specs the
plans cite. Outside reading is listed under Sources. Nothing here has been tried with children, and this review
says nothing about results.

## Verdict

The fields are close to right and the three plans are honest, careful and mathematically correct: I reworked
every readiness item, end-check item and exit question (33 in all) and found no wrong key. The biggest risk is
structural, not mathematical: the stretch has no page of its own, so each unit plan carries a slice of
stretch-level material (readiness, a share of the end check, where it sits, sources), which is why the plans
burst the cap and why the pilot cut check items to fit, and in all three plans a check item repeats a worked
example the class has already seen. The one change that matters most: add a one-page stretch sheet, written
before any unit plan of the stretch, that holds the readiness check and the end check whole, and add the rule
that no check item reuses the numbers or story of anything taught.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. A stretch sheet is needed, and it should be written first

- **What is wrong.** Readiness, the end check, the error letters, "where it sits" and the sources belong to the
  stretch. Split across unit plans they cannot be balanced or read whole. Unit 11.1 cut two end-check items to
  meet a word cap, against the template's own rule 1. Units 3.1 and 9.1 own items "by share of days", which
  spreads items by time and not by the parts of the standard.
- **Evidence.** The ELA spine already decided that "a small stretch record owns the text set, the word list,
  the culminating task and the checks" (ela-6-spine.md, "The unit of record"). The science spine puts its
  first-ideas probe, phenomenon, class model and end task on the stretch. ROADMAP names layer 6 "Stretch and
  unit plan". The two open courses read put this material one level above the lesson span: a module overview
  and a one to two page topic overview in EngageNY grade 4; a unit entry of about 700 words plus about 370
  words of front matter per lesson in Illustrative Mathematics K-5. The rubric's evidence guide asks that
  assessments let students show the full intent of each standard (indicator 1c) and judges the assessment
  system as a whole (3g).
- **Cost.** A weak end check copied into every stretch; plans that drop items to fit a page.
- **Change.** Fix a stretch sheet with these fields, in this order. One page of prose (about 700 words); the two
  check tables do not count toward it.
  1. **Header.** Course; stretch and days; its units with days; weight (major, supporting, additional); days by
     kind, summed from the units.
  2. **Standards, limits, and what is carried.** Each standard with its parts listed by verb. The limits. What
     is carried through the whole stretch, filled by the subject page (math: the model, the day it is tied to
     the written form, the day it is withdrawn; science: the phenomenon or design problem, the question board
     and the class model; ELA: the text set, the word list, the main piece of writing).
  3. **Where it sits.** Builds on, with the handoff marked strong or weak; leads to; where it comes back.
  4. **Readiness check, whole.** When it is given; each item with its code, its key and the unit it serves;
     for each gap, the ten-minute reteach, when those ten minutes happen, and the earlier tool or page.
  5. **End check, whole.** A table: number; code and practice; the part of the standard (the verb) it tests;
     the item; the answer; wrong answers with their error letter; the unit that owns it. Every part listed in
     field 2 has at least one item before any part has two. For each teacher-judged item: the look-fors and
     two sample answers, one that meets them and one that does not. One line on accommodations (read aloud,
     large print, a scribe, an oral answer).
  6. **Error register.** Errors lettered once for the stretch, each with what it shows and its source or
     "from memory". Unit plans use these letters.
  7. **The respond half.** How the end check is marked inside the period, and what each error group does.
  8. **Tools and supplies across the stretch.** Each tool with its day, mark and job; the supplies list.
  9. **Family note,** once per stretch.
  10. **Sources and what is still to make.**
- A stretch with one unit (2, 5, 11, 14, 18) has one merged document: the stretch fields, then unit fields 3 to
  11.
- **Confidence.** Firm that the checks need one home (three sources agree). The exact field list is a judgment
  call. An adoption reviewer would mark down an end check that misses parts of a standard; the rest is design.

### 2. In all three plans a check item repeats something already taught

- **What is wrong.** End-check item 3.1b (Kai, 2/5 and 4/10) is also the wrong example for error D, used in the
  day 12 talk. Exit question E12 (3/4 and 5/6) is the Jo question asked aloud earlier the same day. Item 9.1c
  (6,285 + 947 = 15,755) is the day 73 wrong example and the day 73 planned question, word for word. Item 2 of
  11.1 (40 feet of fence, 12 feet long) is the day 90 wrong example for error D and one of the tool's four
  unaided items; the paper E90 (56 square inches, 7 wide) repeats another of them.
- **Evidence.** Reasoning from what "unaided" is for: a remembered answer is not evidence of the skill. From
  memory, this is standard assessment practice; no source was opened for it.
- **Cost.** The teacher reads recall as understanding and skips the response the class needs.
- **Change.** New template rule: "No end-check item or exit question reuses the numbers or the story of a
  worked example, a wrong example, a planned question or a tool item in the same stretch. Same error, new
  numbers." Replacements:
  - 3.1b: "Rosa says 3/4 and 9/12 are different amounts, because 9 and 12 are bigger than 3 and 4. Is Rosa
    right? Explain with a drawing." (Not right; each fourth is cut into 3.)
  - E12: see finding 5.
  - 9.1c: "Dev added 7,468 + 853 and got 15,998. Without adding, how can you tell Dev is wrong? Then find the
    sum." (About 7,500 + 900 is 8,400; the sum is 8,321.) Tag it "4.NBT.B.4; uses 4.NBT.A.3".
  - 11.1 item 2: "Mia has 50 feet of fence for a rectangular pen. The pen is 16 feet long. How wide is it?
    Show your steps." (9 feet. Wrong: 34 or 18, error D.)
- **Confidence.** Firm that the items repeat (read in the plans). Likely on the cost.

### 3. Unit 11.1: day 92 and part of the end check are grade 3 work, and the end check misses parts of the standard

- **What is wrong.** Day 92's main work (every pen with 36 feet of fence, then every pen with area 36) is
  3.MD.D.8, "rectangles with the same perimeter and different areas or with the same area and different
  perimeters". The plan's own readiness item 4 tests it as something students already have. Meanwhile the day's
  goal is choosing the measure and its exit question (E92) is a "3 times as long" problem; the day teaches
  neither. In the end check, item 4(c) is again 3.MD.D.8, items 4(a) and 4(b) carry the cue words "cover" and
  "go around", and nothing tests perimeter with multi-digit sides, which is the link to major work that
  justifies the unit's place (4.NBT.B.4).
- **Evidence.** The standards text for 3.MD.D.8. Illustrative Mathematics teaches same-perimeter and same-area
  rectangles in grade 3 unit 7, lessons 11 and 12, and has no such lesson in grade 4. EngageNY grade 4 module
  3 gives its second and third lessons to "times as long as wide" problems and multi-step problems. The
  Progressions suggest a both-measures problem for grade 4 (area 60, perimeter 32, find the sides) and say the
  numbers "can now be any of the numbers used in Grade 4" for perimeter (PDF page 112 (106)).
- **Cost.** One of five days repeats grade 3; a reviewer reading indicators 1a and 1c would note below-grade
  items and a missing part. A student is tested on comparison problems nobody taught.
- **Change.**
  - Day 92 row: "Opens: task, 10 min: 36 feet of fence, which pen gives a dog the most room? Then teaching
    first: one 'times as long' problem and one two-step problem (find a side, then the other measure). By
    hand: six mixed problems with no cue words, two of them 'times as long'; written task. All pens with area
    36 moves to the extension."
  - End check, six items in thirty minutes: item 1 as now; item 2 as in finding 2; item 3 as now; new item 4,
    "A rectangle is 318 m long and 147 m wide. Find its perimeter." (930 m; 465 is error B); new item 5,
    teacher judged, MP.4, "A sandbox is 5 feet wide. It is 4 times as long as it is wide. (a) A cover lies
    flat on top of it. How big is the cover? (b) A wooden border runs along every side. How long is the
    border?" (100 square feet; 50 feet. Wrong: length 9, swapped, 25, "100 feet"); item 6, MP.3, the old 4(c)
    re-aimed at the formula, "A garden is 8 feet by 5 feet. Give the sides of a different rectangle with the
    same perimeter. Explain how you know without adding four sides." (For example 10 by 3; length plus width
    is still 13.)
  - Practices: the Progressions name MP.2 (thinking of the situation, then writing its equation) and MP.8
    (formulas as summaries of repeated calculations) for this standard (PDF pages 111 to 112 (105 to 106)).
    Replace "MP.1, make sense: day 90, the notes" with "MP.2: day 90, writing the equation for each note". The
    tool's notes are one-step and do not build MP.1.
  - Field 1, replace "To apply is to use a formula with meaning" with "To apply is to write the equation for
    this rectangle, with its numbers and one unknown, and solve it; not to recall a formula and fill it in
    (Progressions)."
- **Confidence.** Firm on the grade 3 overlap and the missing items. Likely on the day 92 redesign.

### 4. Unit 9.1: two days for adding, four for subtracting

- **What is wrong.** The writer's doubt is right. Adding has three days; subtracting has three, of which the
  last is half end check, so subtracting across zeros has no day of its own.
- **Evidence.** The Progressions: the extension to 1,000,000 "does not have to take a long time" (PDF page 70
  (64)). EngageNY grade 4 module 1: two addition lessons (one is word problems) and four subtraction lessons,
  one on zeros. Illustrative Mathematics grade 4 unit 4: four lessons for both operations together, with a
  whole lesson on zeros (lesson 21) and a return in unit 9. Fishtank splits three and three.
- **Cost.** The hardest case in the stretch is taught in a rush on the way to the check.
- **Change.** Unit 9.1 becomes days 72 to 73; unit 9.2 becomes days 74 to 77. Day 72 as now. Day 73 joins the
  present days 73 and 74 (numbers of different lengths, several carries, to six digits, an estimate first,
  errors found and fixed, the written task). Its exit: "Estimate first, then write in columns and add: 286,947
  + 53,268." (340,215; about 340,000. Wrong: 819,627, error B; 239,105, error A.) Day 74: subtracting with one
  ungrouping, expanded form beside the algorithm. Day 75: several ungroupings, to six digits. Day 76: across
  zeros. Day 77: mixed, then check and respond. Stretch balance: understanding 3, fluency 2, check 1.
- **Confidence.** Firm (three sources agree). Goes to the project owner: it changes two rows of the spine.

### 5. Unit 3.1: day 12 carries too much, and E12 cannot be done by hand in five minutes

- **What is wrong.** Day 12 has an opening task, new content (cuts into 3, 5 and 10; fractions past 1), a
  fifteen-minute tool or print block, a sort of six pairs, the unit's only written argument, and an exit with
  three parts, on the day the strips are taken away. E12 asks a student to draw fourths and sixths on one
  freehand line and see a difference of one twelfth.
- **Evidence.** Illustrative Mathematics gives explaining equivalence its own lesson (unit 2, lesson 9) after
  two lessons of making equivalents (lessons 7 and 8), and does its non-foldable cuts on printed number lines.
  The arithmetic of E12: 3/4 is 9/12 and 5/6 is 10/12.
- **Change.** Move fractions past 1 wholly to day 11 (it already includes 5/4 and 3/2). Day 12 keeps cuts into
  3, 5 and 10 and the argument; drop the six-pair sort to four pairs. E12 becomes: "The line from 0 to 1 is
  marked in twelfths. Mark 2/3 and 4/5 as well as you can. Are they the same amount? Then write another name
  for 2/3." (No: 2/3 is 8/12 and 4/5 falls between 9/12 and 10/12. 4/6 or 8/12. Error B gives "yes".) Using
  2/3 and 4/5 keeps the add-2 trap with numbers not used in the talk.
- **Confidence.** Likely.

### 6. Readiness: the check for stretch 3 misses its most direct prerequisite, and no plan says where the reteach minutes come from

- **What is wrong.** The writer is right that no item tests 3.NF.A.3b, and that two of four items serve a unit
  a week away. Also, a check given in the first ten minutes of day 1 cannot shape day 1, and "ten minutes
  before day 11" names no slot in a day that is already 5 + 50 + 5.
- **Evidence.** The standards text for 3.NF.A.3a and b. Illustrative Mathematics re-opens grade 3 equivalence
  on strips before its equivalence lessons (unit 2, lesson 4). Fishtank gives a pre-unit assessment before the
  unit starts.
- **Change.** Stretch 3 readiness: (1) place 3/4 and 5/4 on a line marked 0, 1, 2; (2) new, 3.NF.A.3b, "The
  top strip is cut in halves and one half is shaded. The strip below is cut in fourths. Shade the same amount.
  1/2 = __/4"; (3) circle the larger of 1/3 and 1/8; (4) write 3 as a fraction and 1 as fourths. The pizza
  question moves to the opening of unit 3.4 (day 21, "the same whole"). Template rule: "At least half the
  readiness items serve the first unit. An item for a later unit is asked at the opening of that unit. The
  reteach takes the next day's warm-up and first five minutes, for the whole class if more than a third missed
  the item, otherwise for a small group while the rest do the warm-up." Better still, and for the owner: give
  the readiness check in the last ten minutes of the day before the stretch opens, so it is marked before day
  1.
- **Confidence.** Firm on the missing item. Judgment call on the timing.

### 7. The respond half of the last day has no marking step

- **What is wrong.** Day 93 runs "respond, 30 min, in groups by error letter" straight after a thirty-minute
  check that includes a teacher-judged item. Nobody can mark thirty papers in no time.
- **Change.** Stretch sheet field 7: "Students mark the short items against the key on the screen, pen down,
  five minutes. Hands up by item sets the groups. Teacher-judged items are read after class and answered in
  the next day's first five minutes." For one child: go over the missed items together.
- **Confidence.** Likely. A working teacher would raise it; a rubric reviewer would not.

### 8. The template bakes math into shared rules

- **Evidence.** science-4-spine.md ("How science differs", "The checks"); ela-6-spine.md ("The units inside

- **Change.** In UNIT-PLAN.md mark each rule and field line as shared or "math fills". Shared wording: rule 7,
  "Every day has a kind, in the subject page's words"; rule 8, "What is carried is named, with what happens to

### 9. Four smaller gaps a rubric reviewer would look for

- **No line for the mathematics the teacher needs.** Indicator 3b asks for adult-level explanations. Add to

- **No accommodations on checks, and no reading-load line.** Indicator 3n, and gap 6 in ROADMAP. Add both to

- **Connections.** Indicator 1g looks for problems that link clusters. Widen "the major work it practises" to

**The four awkward points, settled.**

1. **The last day of a stretch.** Its kind is "check". Write "Check (end check 30, respond 30)". The spine's
   balance table gains a check column.
2. **"Practice tool" against "practise with feedback".** Three word sets collide: the marks (key, practice),
   the jobs (show why, practise with feedback, check unaided) and the roles (lesson, see why, practice).
   Rename the mark to "light tool", which is FOUNDATION's own description. Write "(key)" or "(light)".
3. **A lesson tool does more than one job.** One job per use, not per tool. A day row names the part used, its
   job and its minutes, at most two parts a day: "Community Plot Planner (key): the five notes, show why, 20
   min; on your own, check unaided, 5 min."
4. **No home for the assembled checks.** The stretch sheet (finding 1).

**The cap.** Count words, not pages; a page depends on the screen. The comparison is useful: the open courses
spend 700 to 2,000 words on a unit of 16 to 22 days and keep exit items and errors in the lessons, so a
Curio Class unit plan is really a topic overview plus lesson front matter. With the stretch material moved out
(about 450 to 550 words a plan: readiness, where it sits, sources, the family note, the end-check table), the
three plans come to about 1,400 to 1,500 words. Fix: unit plan 1,500 words; stretch sheet 700 words of prose
plus its two tables; a one-unit stretch 2,500 words in one document. When over, cut in this order: sentences
that restate a template rule; "Not in this unit" lines that repeat field 1; the reasons behind a spec judgment
beyond one sentence; supports that repeat the course-wide ones. Never cut: check items, exit questions,
errors, day rows. Judgment call.

**The field list to fix (unit plan).** Header. 1 Standards and practices (share, limits, connections,
strategies or algorithm, practices, what the teacher page must explain). 2 Where it sits (one or two lines;
the readiness items it uses and where its results go). 3 Checks, written first (owned end-check numbers; exit
questions in full). 4 Expected errors and first ideas. 5 What is carried, and instruments. 6 Days. 7 Talk and
writing. 8 Practice that returns. 9 Supports and extension (with check accommodations and reading load). 10
Two rooms (one child; one projector). 11 Not in this unit, and still to make.

## 2. The three plans as teaching

**What holds in all three.** Every key is right. Every number is inside the limits: denominators from the list
with no hundredths in 3.1; sums under 1,000,000 in 9.1; in 11.1 every area is a product within 100 or a
one-digit number times a multiple of 10, and fractions appear in perimeter only. No decimals are computed.
Every day teaches first except days 12 and 92, whose tasks are 8 and 10 minutes and aim at an idea. No timer
appears. Each model has a tie day and a withdrawal day. The order of 3.1 (strips, number line, argument, then
the rule in 3.2) matches all three open courses, each of which gives three model lessons before the rule.
Expanded form beside the algorithm is the bridge Illustrative Mathematics uses (unit 4, lesson 18).

## 3. The tool calls

- **Unit 3.1.** Paper strips and printed lines are the right instrument for days 10 and 11. The judgment of
  Fraction Wall Duel (partly fits) is fair: its blank-card rounds do the splitting, inside a comparing game on
  a wall that stops at 1. The strip and line splitter is not worth a separate tool. Paper does day 12
  (Illustrative Mathematics does these cuts on printed lines). What is worth having is the same thing as a
  mode: the wall's split tool set over a number line from 0 to 2, where the student picks n, writes the new
  name, then sees the cut. That serves 3.1, 3.2, tenths to hundredths in stretch 7 and grade 5. Judgment call.
- **Unit 9.1.** The judgment of Road Trip Logbook (does not fit) is fair, and the spec shows more than the
  plan says: every trip sum is grade 3 size (386 + 457) and every evening includes subtracting from 2,000. Do
  not build an adding-only ledger. Ask for one shared column practice template with settings (add, subtract or
  both; number of digits; marks by column or by answer; a right-first-try count), which also carries the
  warm-up to day 150 and grade 3. Paper with a key does the job until then. Likely.
- **Unit 11.1.** The instruments are right. The four gaps: (1) no worked example first: real, and against the
  approach's own rule that a lesson tool shows a model before it asks; (2) the 30 by 20 lot: not a gap, since
  the tool states 600 and never asks for it; keep it that way; (3) every note names its measure: real but
  small, and choosing is day 92's work; one unaided item could drop the cue; (4) whole feet and real-world
  only: true by design, and paper carries the rest. Two gaps the plan missed: (5) the spec writes perimeter
  only as 2 x (5 + ?) = 30, while the Progressions say the four-side form prevents the usual error (PDF page
  111 (105)); the fence stage should write 5 + ? + 5 + ? = 30 first; (6) its unaided items now duplicate the
  plan's checks (finding 2); one side must change.
- **The first three key tools.** Community Plot Planner: yes, the pilot plan supports it. Fraction Wall Duel:
  not yet shown. Its home is units 3.3 and 3.4, which have no plan, and it has no number line although the
  spine names the number line as the one model for stretches 3 to 7, and it cannot show a fraction past 1.
  Hold the decision until those two plans exist. Road Trip Logbook: no. Stretch 9 is six days of work the
  Progressions call quick, all three open courses run it on paper, and the tool cannot be used until the
  stretch's last day. Re-mark it a light tool. A better third is likely to be the area model tool for stretch
  12, which continues the pilot's rectangle, but that waits for its plans. Likely; goes to the project owner
  under step 4e.
- **A wording problem under all three.** FOUNDATION defines a key tool as one "the unit cannot run without".
  Every plan must also give a paper and projector path, so no tool can meet that. Proposed wording: "A key
  tool is the one a day is planned around: it does the day's main job in a way print cannot (feedback on each
  try, help that fades as the student succeeds, many cases fast), and the paper path is the fallback."
