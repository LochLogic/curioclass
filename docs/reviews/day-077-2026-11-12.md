## Verdict

The check itself is sound: six items, three a part, two across zeros, each keyed or judged with look-fors and
samples, in fair grade 4 numbers, and a no-prep teacher can give it. The respond half is where the day breaks: the
groups are set by error but the day sets them by hands up, the signs for items 3 and 6 are never shown to the
students who must find them, and the display puts one group's task on the screen at a time while five groups work
at once. The one change that matters most is finding 1 with finding 2: students find their own wrong answer on one
screen that lists all five groups, with the sums for items 3 and 6 shown at the marking.

## On the question asked: can any end-check item show error E?

Yes, item 1 and only item 1. `s09-E1v2` (version 2, 2026-11-12) passes into the hundred thousands, and a student
who stops at the longer number's last digit writes 22,335 (E); s09.md section 5 records that version 1 could not.
Item 2 stays inside six digits (415,263), items 3 to 6 cannot. Two weaknesses remain, and neither needs new
numbers. (a) The printed grid gives the new place a narrow, odd-looking cell squeezed between a wide sign cell and
the digits, so it reads as a margin; a child may write the 1 in the sign cell or not at all for layout reasons,
which blurs E. (b) "11,225, every carry lost" is listed as A only, but it also drops the last carry; a child who
loses every carry usually writes the last column whole, 111,225. **The right fix**: keep the numbers; print the
grid with equal columns so the new place looks like any other place (finding 5); add the 111,225 row and the D
row; and, for the stretch, give Check 2's `C2.1` a sum that passes into a new place, so E has a second cold chance
(today `C2.1`, 275,684 + 98,537 = 374,221, cannot show E). That last part is day 78's call, listed under shared.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The groups cannot be set the way the day says

- **What is wrong.** "Hands up sets the groups." The groups are by error, not by item: a miss on item 1 can be A,
  D or E; on item 5, B, F or I. A raised hand for "item 1" does not say which group. Two group signs sit on items
  the students cannot mark: "Dev's 15,998 let stand on 3" (B) and "Rosa's 6,532 let stand on 6" (F to H), while the
  answers screen shows 3 and 6 only as "read by your teacher". And a wrong answer on no list (two errors, a fact
  slip) has no group at all.
- **Why it matters.** The first five of the fifteen group minutes go to the teacher reading papers one by one; the
  students the B and F groups were written for (those who accepted a wrong answer) never join them.
- **Fix (day).** Groups paragraph: "Students find their wrong answer on the groups screen and go to that group; a
  student in two goes to the subtracting one. A wrong answer on no list: bring it to me." Answers screen: show the
  sums for 3 and 6 (finding 3), so a student who wrote 15,998 or 6,532 can see it. **Fix (process).** DEFAULTS.md,
  the respond half: "Hands up by item sets the groups; on a check whose groups are by error, students match their
  wrong answer to the group list on the screen."
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 2. The display shows one group's task at a time, to five groups at once

- **What is wrong.** The check-day display (from the day 93 read's fix) gives one screen per group, A and D, E, B,
  F to H, I, then one per challenge. Groups work at the same time on one projector, so four of the five cannot see
  their task while the fifth's is up. The teacher page says "Tasks are on the display".
- **Why it matters.** The task text is the only instruction a group has; the teacher is sitting with one group.
- **Fix (process).** The check-day build puts every group on one screen (here about 120 words, which fits at
  projector size) and the two challenges on one more; the one-screen-per-group form stays for a group that is
  taught from the front.
- **Confidence.** Firm (seen in d077-display.html, nine screens).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new (a side effect of day 93 finding 8). **CHEAP:** missed.

### 3. The answers screen speaks a different numbering from the page the student holds

- **What is wrong.** The student page numbers the items 1 to 6, the teacher page says "show the answers to 1, 2, 4
  and 5", and the display labels them "9.1a, 9.1b, 9.1c, 9.2a, 9.2b, 9.2c". A nine-year-old marking item 4 has to
  know it is "9.2a".
- **Why it matters.** Self-marking is the evidence the groups come from; a mismatch puts ticks on the wrong items.
- **Fix.** Display (build): label by the student page's number ("1. 122,335 ... 3. The sum is 8,321; your reason is
  read by your teacher ... 6. The difference is 5,468; your adding is read by your teacher"); keep "9.1a" for the
  key and the record sheet only. Gate: the answers screen's labels must be the numbers on the student page.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** script. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 4. The mixed set has nowhere to be worked, and the print list misses grid paper

- **What is wrong.** practice-d077's teacher text says the set is worked "on grid paper or the place value columns
  sheet"; the print list has no grid paper (only large-square, on request), and gives the columns sheet one per two
  students, which the groups need later. The opener's supply list puts grid paper per student on every day. The
  cheap read found the small boxes on the sheet; the supply gap is a separate one.
- **Why it matters.** A teacher who preps from the print list has ten six-digit problems and no paper for them.
- **Fix.** Print list: "grid paper, 1 sheet per student, for the mixed set". Student line on the sheet: "Work on grid
  paper. Write each answer on its line."
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 84 finding 7, opener and day supplies drift).
  **CHEAP:** missed.

### 5. Item 1's grid and wrong-answer rows blur error E and miss D

- **What is wrong.** See "On the question asked". The new-place cell is narrow and unlike the others (the first,
  sign column is the widest). "11,225 (A, every carry lost)" is also an E answer, and the common form, 111,225, is
  not listed. The A and D group names "two digits in one column", but no row gives D's answer on item 1 or 2, so the
  key cannot name it.
- **Why it matters.** Item 1 is the only cold evidence on E in the stretch; record sheet letters drive day 79.
- **Fix.** Rows in "Settled by the expert". Build: digit grids print equal columns, the sign in a narrow first cell.
- **Confidence.** Likely (that a narrow cell changes what children write is reasoning, not a source).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 93 finding 12, a missing wrong-answer row). **CHEAP:** missed.
