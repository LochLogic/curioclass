## Gaps

1. Grade 2 is the only course that shows a range of people: eight names from many family backgrounds, plus a real
   textile tradition handled with a sensitivity plan. No gap.
2. Grade 3 names one person (Mira) and its unit 5 notes say story contexts "use a range of names". Thin, but it
   states the intent. Flag, not a gap by the rule.
3. Kindergarten, grades 1, 5, 6, 7, 8, Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II name no people at all. Their problems are
   objects, quantities and measurements (paper pieces, strips, tiles, tanks, packets). They are also mostly one kind
   of setting: school and classroom (K, 1, 5 to 8), with generic work and measurement scenes above that. None
   leans on one culture; none shows any.
4. No course except grade 2 has a named cultural context across its year.
5. Precalculus, Calculus and Statistics have no unit plans, so nothing can be tallied. Their frames carry the
   sentence; the plans must list people and places when written.

## Changes

None to unit plans or frames.

- Frames: every frame in scope (K, 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8, Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Precalculus, Calculus,
  Statistics) already carries "Word problems use a range of names and settings; the unit plan lists the people and
  places its problems show." (grade 3's has an added clause about family money, body weight and home life). Nothing to
  add.
- Unit plans: the rule is to swap or add only where a plan already names its contexts. The plans name context
  types (for example "garden rows, bus seats", "recipes and paint mixtures") but, outside grade 2 and one name in
  grade 3, list no people and no places. Inventing lists would break the "do not invent" rule, so no edits were made.
  Numbers, answers, skill ids, standards and minutes are untouched.

## Flags (for a person or the day-writing step)

1. The frames promise "the unit plan lists the people and places its problems show", but 82 of the 89 unit plans
   checked have no such list. Either each plan gets a short people-and-places line when its days are written, or the
   frame sentence is softened. Recommended: a line per unit plan filled during day writing, then tallied again.
2. Grade 2's name set is a good model: eight short names across backgrounds, none paired with a stereotype. Reuse the
   pattern, with fresh names per course so a student does not meet the same eight names for thirteen years.
3. Cultural contexts: only grade 2 has one. For days, candidates should be everyday and respectful (foods, games,
   crafts, measurement traditions) with the sensitivity read, never sacred or ceremonial material, and never a
   stereotype. Placing one in each band (K to 2, 3 to 5, 6 to 8, 9 to 12) is a reasonable target.
4. Setting spread: add rural, coastal, tribal-nation and big-city scenes beside the school and shop scenes when days
   are written. Algebra II's Denver and Seattle daylight table and harbor record are the only real places named.
5. Math-grade-4 was not tallied (barred for this job). Its coverage should be tallied separately.
6. Precalculus, Calculus and Statistics: tally after their unit plans exist.
