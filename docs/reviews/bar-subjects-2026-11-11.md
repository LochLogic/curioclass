# Cold review: the adoption bar for the other subjects

**Date:** 2026-11-11. **Reviewed:** docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md, first draft of the same day.
**Reviewer:** the curriculum expert (docs/agents/EXPERT.md), reading cold. Nothing but this file was written or changed.

## Verdict

The draft is honest about what was read and is a sound first map, but it is not yet a bar: the shared core can be
passed by a weak course because it names artifacts and gives no pass rule, no numbers and no "fails if" lines, which
makes it softer than the math rubric. The door (C1 to C3) cannot be opened today for social studies, computer
science, American Sign Language, psychology or health content, because the standards or the topic map it checks
against are not on file, and the draft does not list those gaps as work owed. Fix the pass rule, turn the missing
standards into named tasks, and put four more choices to the owner before any spine in these subjects is drafted.

## Findings, heaviest first

### 1. The shared core has no pass rule and can be passed by a weak course

**Place.** "How it is used"; "So for Curio Class: criteria C1 to C3 below are the door"; the whole C1 to C16 table.

**What is wrong.** The draft says a criterion passes "when the named evidence exists and can be opened". That is a
test of presence, not of quality. C7 passes if a unit plan field is filled in. C12 passes if a sensitivity read
happened. C13 passes if an approach page exists. Nothing says how many units a reviewer samples, what a fail looks
like, or what happens to a course that passes the door and fails five of C4 to C16. The math rubric
(docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md) has a numeric door (65 percent of time on major work, 75 percent in kindergarten to grade
2), three ways of counting, and an indicator table with gaps. The instruments I read all score: Ohio is 44 points on
a 0 to 2 scale with stated bands per gateway; Oklahoma is 95 points; Maryland has a gate category and rated
categories; HECAT scores 0 to 4 per area. C1's "little time goes to content from outside the course" has no number
at all.

**Smallest fix.** Three additions, no new criteria. (a) A pass rule in "How it is used": C1 to C3 in full; every one
of C4 to C16 at least a part pass and no more than a stated few at part pass, the model the draft itself reports for
Massachusetts. (b) A sample rule: the reviewer reads the spine, plus a stated number of whole units chosen by the
reviewer, not by the builder. (c) A fifth column, "Fails if", one line each, written from the "would mark down"
habit the subject parts already have. For C1, a number: the share of days on the course's own standards, set per
subject when its spine form is known.

**Evidence.** Ohio rubric pages 3 to 4 (scoring rules); RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md. **Cost if ignored.** "Finished to the
bar" would mean "the folders are full". **Confidence:** firm.

### 2. The door cannot be opened for many courses, and the draft treats that as a note, not as work owed

**Place.** C1; the "Standards match" lines in parts 1, 3, 4, 5, 6; open choices 2 and 10.

**What is wrong.** C1 needs "a named edition of the standards, loaded in data/standards/". By the draft's own
account and by docs/reviews/standards-currency-2026-11-11.md: social studies has no content standard; computer
science has codes with no wording; American Sign Language has no standards; psychology cites a standard with no
file; the 2026 can-do statements are not loaded; physical education needs a five-to-four map. Health has a
different hole: the loaded standards are skill standards, and nothing says which of the tool's topics and healthy
behaviour outcomes each band must cover, which is where HECAT does its real coverage scoring (the topic modules,
read by neither the writer nor me). Only choice 2 raises any of this with the owner. The rest sits in "Standards
match" paragraphs, where it reads as a caveat. Under the owner's rule that all 105 courses are finished to the bar,
a caveat is a hidden exemption.

**Smallest fix.** Add a short section, "Owed before the door can be checked", one row per subject: what file or map
is missing, who can supply it (agent, or the owner), and which courses are blocked. Say in one line that a course
blocked this way is not exempt and is not finished. Move the standards-currency fixes into that list by reference.

**Cost if ignored.** Spines drafted against nothing, then redone. **Confidence:** firm.

### 3. The core leaves out things the instruments score, and the "Seen in" column cannot be trusted

**Place.** C1 to C16; the "Seen in" column; "Where our promises cost us marks".

**What is wrong.** Missing from the core, and scored or asked in instruments I read in full:

- Students judging and tracking their own learning. Scored in Ohio's assessment criterion, asked in HECAT's student
  assessment area, in Oklahoma's assessment gateway, and in Maryland. C8 and C9 are all teacher-side.
- Reading load of the student pages and help for students who read below grade. Asked in Ohio and in the math
  rubric, and docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md already has a reading load field. C10 does not name it.
- Preliminary screens that end a review. HECAT says a low score on accuracy, acceptability, feasibility or
  affordability should remove a curriculum from further consideration; PECAT says the same for a bought curriculum.
  C3 and C14 carry the content but C14 is not in the door.
- Professional learning tied to the materials. Scored in Ohio (teacher supports). Curio Class offers none. That is a
  fair choice, but it belongs in "Where our promises cost us marks" and it is not there.
- Telling a content gap from a language gap for English learners (Ohio, student supports). C11 does not ask it.

Overlaps: C8 and C9 split one thing a reviewer reads together; C5 and C13 both rest on the teacher page; C3 holds
three tests (accuracy, no promotion, authorship) that fail for different reasons.

The "Seen in" column: the writer read Ohio's second gateway by headings only, and the column shows it. Ohio asks
directly for what C11, C12 and C13 describe, and OH is not listed on those rows. Maryland, Oklahoma and CASEL are
not in the key at all for the core. A reader will take the column as a count of support. It is not one.

**Smallest fix.** Add self-assessment to C8, reading load to C10, the language-or-content note to C11; add
professional learning to the promises paragraph; split C3 into three lettered tests under one number; cut the "Seen
in" column, or replace it with "read in full for this row" marks only.

**Confidence:** firm on Ohio, HECAT, Oklahoma and Maryland, which I read in full; likely on the rest.

### 4. Social studies: the first open choice is a false either-or, and informed action is not optional in the instrument that gates on it

**Place.** Part 1, criteria 1, 5, 9; open choice 1.

**What is wrong.** Choice 1 sets Maryland (inquiry) against Louisiana (knowledge). Maryland's rubric does not make
that choice. It screens standards coverage before the rubric is opened, and its first category must be met for the
review to go on. That category asks for inquiry together with explicit teaching, a knowledge-building focus, source
work, writing, talk, and units that build toward students taking informed action. So "knowledge first with
questions framing each unit" is already inside Maryland's gate, and the recommendation is sound, but it is not a
fork for the owner. The real fork is criterion 9: the draft makes informed action a per-unit choice, while Maryland
puts it in the category that must be met. The draft does say two instruments require it; the recommendation then
quietly drops it.

The part is also thinner than the instrument on skills. Maryland names the historian's habits (who made a source
and why, its setting, checking one source against another, order in time), asks for a stated reason for each
source's difficulty and place in the year, short research tasks, claims with counterclaims, and writing taught
directly. Criterion 5 lists four skills and omits those.

**Smallest fix.** Rewrite choice 1 as "informed action: required in every course at least once a year, or
optional", with the recommendation to require it once per course, sized small. Add to criterion 5 the four source
habits in our own words. Add to criterion 3 that each source has a stated difficulty and a reason for its place.

**What differs from the instrument, on purpose or not.** Maryland also carries value-laden items (accounts that
push back on the usual telling, teachers examining their own stance). The draft leaves these out without saying
so. See finding 6.

**Confidence:** firm on what Maryland asks; judgment call on the recommendation.

### 5. Health is marked "firm" but is missing a whole scored area, its coverage map, and the hardest owner decision

**Place.** Part 3; C3; C12; open choice 9.

**What is wrong.** Four things.

- HECAT chapter 5 scores eight areas. One of them is about materials being culturally responsive and inclusive, six
  items, including the conditions people live in and teacher guidance on their own bias. The draft has no health
  criterion for it and leans on C12, which is thinner. The same chapter also asks for student self-assessment, more
  than one kind of assessment, and learning that reaches past the classroom. The writer read pages 1 to 5 of 10;
  these sit in the second half.
- Coverage. No criterion ties a health course to the tool's topics and behaviour outcomes by grade span. Criterion 1
  says each unit names behaviours; it does not say which ones the band owes.
- The "acceptability" gap. The draft is right that the tool leaves sensitive topics to the local community. It then
  puts nothing in that place. An open national site has to decide, once, which sensitive topics (sexual health,
  drugs, violence, mental health, body image) its health courses carry, how, and what a school can leave out
  without breaking the course. That is the owner's call under FOUNDATION section 7 and it is not among the open
  choices.
- Wording. C3 says "a medically qualified read". HECAT's accuracy analysis asks for reviewers able to judge health,
  medical and scientific accuracy and records who they were; a health curriculum specialist counts. Criterion 8's
  wording is right and C3's is stricter than the source. Also, "written for teacher-led classes" is slightly too
  flat: the feasibility pages speak of classroom or online delivery.

**Smallest fix.** Add two criteria (the inclusive area in our own words; a band coverage map against
data/standards/hecat-topics.txt). Change C3 to match criterion 8. Add the sensitive-topics choice for the owner.
Lower confidence to "likely" until the second half of chapter 5 and one topic module have been read by the writer.

**Confidence:** firm (chapters 4 and 5 read in full). The topic modules are unread by anyone on the project.

### 6. The instruments disagree on values, and the draft takes a middle line without saying so

**Place.** C12; part 2 criterion 7; part 1.

**What is wrong.** Oklahoma's arts rubric has a fourth gateway that checks materials against state law. Maryland,
HECAT and CASEL each ask for equity content in terms that pull the other way. No single stance passes all of them.
C12 ("a range of people, no stereotypes, no shaming, hard facts told plainly") is a reasonable middle, and it
follows FOUNDATION promise 7, but the draft presents it as "seen in" six instruments, as if it were common ground.

**Smallest fix.** One paragraph under the core: FOUNDATION promise 7 governs; state instruments conflict here;
Curio Class does not claim to satisfy any state's values criteria; the conflict is listed once so no reviewer
re-opens it per course. Put the stance itself to the owner (see below).

**Confidence:** firm that the conflict exists (Oklahoma and Maryland read in full); judgment call on the fix.

### 7. Some subject criteria are ours, not the instrument's, and are not marked

**Place.** Part 4 criteria 3, 7, 8 and the "mark down" line; part 5 criteria 1, 2, 5, 6; part 2 criterion 8; part 9
criterion 5.

**What is wrong.**

- Physical education. PECAT's standards analysis checks three things per standard per grade (outcome, content,
  assessment), which criterion 1 states correctly. A word search of all 92 pages found no criterion on moving time,
  on growth against rank, on dress or attendance grading, on fitness tests as grades, on exercise as punishment or
  on elimination games. PECAT's acceptability page only points elsewhere for appropriate practices. So criteria 3,
  7 and 8 and most of the "mark down" line rest on Oregon (read by the writer, not by me) or on memory. Only
  criterion 8 is marked.
- Computer science. Ohio supports criteria 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8. Criterion 5 ("every unit has unplugged work") is
  stronger than Ohio, where offline versions are a narrative item with no points. "Nothing to install" is ours;
  Ohio asks whether add-ons are needed and for guidance to technical staff. Criteria 1 and 2 come from the
  standards and the framework, not the rubric. Ohio's first gateway also gives most of its points to technology
  being built into the learning, which the part does not mention. The draft's statement that Ohio's adaptive and
  learning-management items are narrative is correct.
- Arts. Oklahoma confirms the four processes, student choice, flexible pacing and supplies. Criterion 8 (end tasks
  are performances or finished works) is narrower than Oklahoma's assessment gateway, which asks for a range across
  the year, including self-assessment and work kept over time.
- SEL. The part matches CASEL's design criteria accurately. Criterion 5's examples sit close to the source's own;
  reword them. CASEL also names teaching SEL inside academic lessons as an accepted approach, which bears on open
  choice 7.

**Smallest fix.** Mark each criterion with one of three tags: from the instrument, from the standards, ours. That
is the language of UNIT-PLAN rule 16. Widen arts criterion 8. Reword SEL criterion 5.

**Confidence:** firm for computer science, arts and SEL; likely for physical education (word search, not a full
read, and Oregon unread by me).

### 8. A spine writer could not start from this page tomorrow

**Place.** The whole of "The subject parts".

**What is wrong.** The page is a bar, which is what it set out to be. But UNIT-PLAN's subject slots (the kinds of
day, what is carried forward, the form of daily evidence) are not filled for any of these subjects, and no approach
page exists for them. A spine writer would still not know: the content standard (social studies); the target
proficiency level per language level; which health topics fall in which band; which grade's arts standards fall in
which band year (the draft says the spine must say, not how to decide); how many days a light-shelf course has;
which unit plan field each subject criterion lands in.

**Smallest fix.** Do not grow this page. Add to each part two lines, "The spine must state" and "The unit plan must
fill", and one line at the top saying an approach page per subject is owed before a spine, as layer 3 requires.

**Confidence:** firm.

### 9. Thin parts speak in the same voice as firm ones

**Place.** Parts 7, 10, 11.

**What is wrong.** The "Unsupported" and "Confidence" lines are honest. Library rests on an introduction page and a
press notice; ELD on a skim of criteria for grades 4 to 8; career education on one rubric. But the criteria in
those parts read exactly like the ones in health. A reviewer holding a course to library criterion 3 will not see
that it is ours.

**Smallest fix.** Head each thin part "Ours, untested" and say what would firm it (open the continuum's grade
lists; read the California map end to end; open the WIDA rubric file).

**Confidence:** firm.

### 10. Length: about a third can go

**Place.** Whole page.

**What to cut or move.** (a) The sources table repeats every "Rests on" paragraph; keep the table, cut "Rests on"
to one line. (b) The "Seen in" column (finding 3). (c) The evidence sentences in "Unsupported" repeat the search
report; keep one line and the link. (d) "A reviewer would mark down" becomes the "fails if" text. (e) "How the
published rubrics work" can be three lines. (f) FOUNDATION files rubrics as docs/RUBRIC-*.md. Once a subject's part
is firm, move it to its own rubric file in the math model and leave the core here.

**Confidence:** judgment call.

## The firm rule: all 105 courses

Nothing in the draft exempts a course, and it says so. Three places lean on the rule and should be worded with
care:

- Open choice 7's second path (SEL and library as strands) and choice 8 (split languages into levels) change the
  course count. The draft flags 7 as the owner's call and should flag 8 the same way.
- "Only the design half can be met" (SEL) and "a new course scores nothing there" (finance) are honest. They should
  be written as "the bar for this course is the design half; the evidence half is stated as unmet on the course
  page", so no one reads them as "cannot be finished".
- Finding 2: an unopenable door must be a task list, not a caveat.

Claims that go past what can be shown: "this page copies them" (the three habits) for instruments read in part;
the "Seen in" column; health "Confidence: firm".

- "What we may never say", and the own-words rule.

- The SEL part's reading of the registry: grade-by-grade sequence, practice by doing, design reviewed only after

## Corrections of fact

1. Open choice 11, "One state asks": more than one instrument asks for family materials in a language beyond
   English. Ohio lists it as a look-for, CASEL lists translation among its equity strategies, and Maryland asks for
   home-language communication. The choice still stands as one decision for all subjects.
2. Open choice 1: Maryland does not put inquiry in place of knowledge; its gate category asks for both (finding 4).
3. C3, "a medically qualified read": the tool asks for reviewers able to judge health, medical and scientific
   accuracy, and gives non-medical examples (finding 5).
4. Health, "written for teacher-led classes": the feasibility pages allow for online delivery.
5. Health "Rests on": chapter 5 has eight scored areas; the draft's criteria reflect the first half.
6. Computer science criterion 5 is presented as resting on Ohio; Ohio gives unplugged versions no points.
7. Oklahoma's arts rubric has four gateways, the fourth on state law; the search report's account should be checked
   against this before the draft cites it.
8. Sources table, HECAT: the chapter files are at .../healthy-youth/hecat/pdfs/hecat_chapter-04.pdf and
   hecat_chapter-05.pdf; the listed landing page alone does not show how to reach them.

## What to put to the owner, in order of how much each changes what courses teach

1. **Social studies content standard** (draft choice 2). Which state's, whether one state serves kindergarten to
   grade 12, and what psychology and economics stand on. Recommendation in the draft is sound. Nothing in social
   studies can start without it.
2. **World languages: levels, and American Sign Language** (draft choice 8, widened). Splitting into levels changes
   the course count. American Sign Language needs standards on file and materials on video, which may need people
   on camera; under FOUNDATION 7.4 that goes to the owner. Missing from the draft.
3. **Health: sensitive topics** (missing). Which topics the health courses carry, how, and what a school may leave
   out. With it, who the qualified reader is and what it costs (draft choice 9).
4. **SEL and library: courses or strands** (draft choice 7), together with year-by-year SEL (draft choice 4). The
   registry accepts SEL taught inside academic lessons, so the strand path is not a lesser one.
5. **The values stance where state instruments conflict** (missing; finding 6). Recommend: promise 7 governs and
   the page says so once.
6. **Informed action: required once per course or optional** (draft choice 1, reframed).
7. **The pass rule for C4 to C16** (missing; finding 1). It decides what "finished" means for every course here.
8. **Re-keying dated tools** (draft choice 10). Sound as recommended.
9. **Professional learning: none offered** (missing). Confirm as a promise that costs marks.
10. **ELD last** (draft choice 6). Order only. Sound.
11. **Library as our own bar** (draft choice 5). Sound.
12. **SEL design half only** (draft choice 3). Mostly wording. Sound.
13. **Family notes in Spanish** (draft choice 11). One decision for all subjects.

Smaller ones to settle without the owner unless they grow: arts bands against grade-by-grade standards; the high
school computer science standards set; what an open site honestly offers for physical education.
