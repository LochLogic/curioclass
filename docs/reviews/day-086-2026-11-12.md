## Verdict

Day 86 is correct mathematics and a well-built day: the two examples share 8 and 6 on purpose, the eight cards
mix every unknown place with three additive problems, card 7 (49 and 7) is a planned trap, and the hand-off from
day 85 is real. The biggest risk is in the day's one decision rule: "Is the bigger amount copies of the smaller,
or the smaller and some extra?" is a question about the numbers, and on card 7 the honest answer is "both"
(49 is 7 copies of 7), while the kind is set by what the story says or asks. The one change that matters most is
to make the story's words the test (finding 1); after that, draw the two examples on one scale (finding 2) and
let the exit test this day's goal, not unit 10.3's second step (finding 3).

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The sort's decision rule asks about the numbers, not the story

- **What is wrong.** The sort part tells the teacher: "Is the bigger amount copies of the smaller, or the
  smaller and some extra?" The student page prints only "Copies or extra?". Any two amounts can be seen both
  ways: on card 7, 49 is 7 copies of 7 and also 7 with 42 extra; on cards 4 and 8 (number of times unknown) the
  student cannot see copies until after solving. What makes card 7 "more than" is the question ("how many
  more"); what makes card 4 "times as many" is the question ("how many times as much"). The day's own watch-for
  on card 7 uses the right test ("Does it ask how many more, or how many times?"), but only as a rescue after a
  wrong sort.
- **Evidence.** 4.OA.A.2 asks students to distinguish the two kinds of comparison as situations; the
  Progressions (OA, p. 44 (38), cited in s10.md) and Illustrative Mathematics grade 4 unit 5 section A both set
  the kind from the story's relationship (from memory for the exact wording; the page pointer is in s10.md).
  The growth-spurt spec avoids the trap: none of its eight Which one? pairs is a multiple pair in a "more than"
  story.
- **Cost.** The day's goal is "decide what kind of comparison a problem is". A careful student who applies the
  stated rule to card 7 sorts it "copies" and is marked wrong for reasoning correctly about the numbers; a
  student who learns the rule as stated has learned to look at the numbers, which is the key-word habit (B)
  in a new form.
- **Change.** Teacher page, sort part, replace the second sentence with: "For each card: who has more? What
  does the story say or ask: times as many (copies), or more than (extra)?" Student page sort line: "Who has
  more? Does the story say times or more? Stuck? Use your strip sheet." When the key is read at 14 minutes, add
  one sentence on card 7: "49 is 7 copies of 7, and also 7 and 42 extra. The question decides: it asks how many
  more." The example frames keep "Copies or extra?" since there the story states the kind. Confidence: firm.
- Tags: LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new.

### 2. The two examples' strips are drawn on different scales, so 48 and 14 look the same length

- **What is wrong.** `strip()` in scripts/lesson.py scales each figure so its longest bar fills 640 units. In
  Example 1 Owen's 48 is 642 units long and Nora's 8 is 107; in Example 2 Owen's 14 is 640 units long and Nora's
  8 is 366. On the display, one after the other, Owen's bar is the same length in both, and Nora's 8 changes
  size by more than three times.
- **Evidence.** d086-key.html and d086-display.html SVGs (rect widths 107 x 6, then 366 + 274); lesson.py
  lines 486 to 494 (`top = max(widths)`, `span = 640`).
- **Cost.** The day's planned question is "Both problems use 8 and 6. Why are the answers so different?" The
  pictures, which are the day's model, show no difference in length. The point the strips exist to make (copies
  grow fast, an extra piece does not) is lost on exactly the pair built to make it.
- **Change.** In lesson.py, let a figure cell carry a fixed unit (for instance `scale=48`, meaning "draw as if
  the longest bar were 48"), and give both K86-1 and K86-2 `scale=48`, so Nora's 8 is the same width in both and
  Owen's 14 is under a third of Owen's 48. A gate note: two `example` rows of one day whose figures share a name
  and a first value should share a scale. Confidence: firm.
- Tags: LAYER process; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (a figure that does not show what the day is about, as day
  89's plain outline).

### 3. The exit chains two steps, which this unit does not teach

- **What is wrong.** E86 asks for the melon (24 = 4 × m) and then the squash, which is 4 more than the melon, so
  the second answer needs the first. That is a two-step problem, unit 10.3's content. The unit opener's limits
  say "One step"; no practice card on day 86 chains two comparisons (only the optional challenge does); the
  wrong answer "melon only" is filed as error F, which the register puts on day 87. The record sheet ticks day
  86 only when both answers are right (opener, "How the unit is checked").
- **Evidence.** opener.md "Limits"; u10-2.md field 1; items.csv s10-X86; wrongs.csv "melon only, F";
  errors.csv F, day 87; record.md line 17.
- **Cost.** A student who sorts and solves both kinds well but does not yet chain gets no tick on the day's
  goal, and the day 86 column on the record sheet then measures day 87's skill. The F sign tells day 87 nothing
  it does not already plan for.
- **Change.** Keep the paired 4 (it is a good trap for error A) and make both answers one step from the given
  pumpkin. New row `s10-X86v2` (rule 8): "A pumpkin weighs 24 pounds. That is 4 times as much as a melon weighs. A
  squash weighs 4 pounds more than the pumpkin. How much does the melon weigh? How much does the squash weigh?"
  Answer "Melon 6 pounds; squash 28 pounds", compute `24/4 | 24+4`, value `6 | 28`. Wrongs: melon 96 (`24*4`,
  B); melon 20 (`24-4`, A); squash 96 (`24*4`, A, the other way round: copies for extra); squash 10 (`24/4+4`,
  note: added 4 to the melon, reread whose weight; no letter, note it). The numbers 24, 4, 6, 28 are on no taught
  stretch 10 row as a set. Then: d086 "Tomorrow" loses the F sentence and names these signs; d087 "Day 86's exit
  back" says "redraw the pumpkin as 4 copies of the melon, then the squash as the pumpkin plus a piece of 4";
  u10-2.md's generated exits rebuild. Confidence: likely (one could argue the chain is a fair preview; it should
  then not decide the day's tick).
- Tags: LAYER process (the plan set the exit, and no gate compares an exit with the unit's limits); CATCH
  expert; REPEAT yes (exits that test something other than the day's goal, as E92 and day 91's exit).

### 7. The student page's room to write is too small where it is needed

- **What is wrong.** On side 2 the four solve boxes are about 0.8 inch tall for an equation, a sentence and, if

  many as __": blanks two characters wide, with no place for the noun ("times as many shells as"), so the frame
  cannot hold the meets sample's own sentence.
- **Evidence.** d086-student.pdf, both sides, looked at as images.
- **Cost.** Students squeeze or skip the sentence answer, which the day asks for on every card, and the frame

- **Change.** Spread the spare height over the four card boxes (about 1.1 inch each). Write the frames as

- Tags: LAYER process (the "__" frame is the stretch 10 convention); CATCH cheap read; REPEAT yes (day 89's
