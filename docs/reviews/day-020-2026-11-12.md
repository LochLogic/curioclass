## Verdict

Day 20 is correct mathematics and a sound shape: a task that lets pairs find fifteenths before they are told, two
worked examples on stacked lines, error A's "right sign from wrong names" fixed on purpose, and a closing question
that makes MP.7 real ("is 15 the only number?"). The biggest risk is in the edges, not the core: the two branches
that respond to day 19's tickets point at a board line that cannot show what they say, and the side 1 frames print
"____/15 4/5", which a grade 4 student reads as a mixed number. The one change that matters most is the cheap
read's and mine together: make Example 2 a pair where the bigger numbers lose (5/8 and 7/12 in twenty-fourths), so
the day's examples stop rehearsing error D and also show a common multiple smaller than the product, which the exit
needs.

- **Evidence.** d020-student.pdf side 1, both frames. The same double-space pattern is in d011, d013, d014, d015

  4/5." (and the same for Example 2). Process: `lesson.py build` keeps a run of two or more spaces in a student
  frame as a visible gap, or `lesson.py check` fails a rendered line where a number is followed by a space and a

  three expected answers in order. That is the productive struggle the standard's "creating common denominators"

  sign alone would let through.

  common multiple is asked, as the grade 4 limits require (math-3-5-progression.md).

  item and the exit are pairs where the bigger numbers lose.
