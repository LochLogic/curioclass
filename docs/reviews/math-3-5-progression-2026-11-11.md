# Review: the grades 3 to 5 math progression (docs/courses/math-3-5-progression.md)

Curriculum expert, cold read, 2026-11-11. Reviewed: `docs/courses/math-3-5-progression.md` (first draft), against
`docs/courses/math-4-spine.md`, `data/sequence.csv`, `data/modules.csv`, the standards file, the official
standards PDF and the 2023 Progressions. Page pointers below are "PDF page (printed page)", the draft's own style.

## Verdict

The draft is sound and can carry fields 2 and 4 of the unit plans once about a dozen corrections are made: nearly
every standard code, quote and page pointer checked out, and 31 of its 33 findings are right as written. The biggest
risk is in the "Limits" and "Hard here" lines, which unit writers will copy without rereading the sources: one limit
is wrong (stretch 16, angles), one handoff rests on words the grade 3 standard does not contain (stretch 16, "square
corners"), two limits are missing (the order of operations footnote, and the "multiply by 1" argument in stretch 3),
and both "from memory" lines fail verification. The one change that matters most: fix stretch 16 before any unit
plan is written from it, because as written it would cut grade 4 angle work that the Progressions place in grade 4
and would test a readiness item on a term grade 3 never teaches.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Stretch 16 limit is wrong: grade 4 does reason about angles on a line and at a point (firm)

**What is wrong.** Line 246: "Supplementary and vertical angles are grade 7 (7.G.B.5)." A unit writer will read
this as "no problems about angles on a straight line or where two lines cross".

**Evidence.** 4.MD.C.7 asks for unknown angles "on a diagram" by adding and subtracting. The Progressions' grade 4
text, Prog. pp. 113 to 115 (107 to 109), has students use angle additivity to find the angles formed by a pair of
intersecting lines and the parts of a right angle or a straight angle, and it introduces the words complementary,
supplementary and adjacent there. 7.G.B.5 (repo standards file, line 280) is about using those facts "in a
multi-step problem to write and solve simple equations". So the grade 7 step is the named facts as tools in
equations, not the diagrams.

**Cost.** Unit 16 would leave out the richest 4.MD.C.7 problems (parts of 90, 180 and 360 degrees), and a reviewer
checking 4.MD.C.7 for full depth would mark it thin.

**Change.** Replace the limits line with: "Whole-number degrees. Unknown angles are found by adding and
subtracting parts, including the parts of a right angle (90), a straight line (180) and a full turn (360), and the
angles where two lines cross (4.MD.C.7; Prog. pp. 113 to 115 (107 to 109)). The words complementary and
supplementary may be used but are not assessed. Using these facts as rules in multi-step equations is grade 7
(7.G.B.5)."

### 2. Stretch 16 handoff: "square corners" is not in 3.G.A.1 (firm)

**What is wrong.** Line 243 says grade 3 hands over "right angles as 'square corners' (3.G.A.1)". 3.G.A.1 names
no angles at all; it lists shape categories. "Square corners" is grade 1 language in the Progressions (Prog. p. 125
(119)). The only grade 3 hook is the grade 3 opening in the standards PDF, which says students classify shapes "by
their sides and angles", and 3.MD.C.7 rectangles.

**Cost.** Readiness item 1 ("picks out right angles in a rectangle (3.G.A.1)") is coded to a standard that does
not teach it. The real handoff is weak, and the draft hides that.

**Change.** Replace with: "Grade 3 hands it: shapes sorted by their sides and angles, informally (3.G.A.1 and the
grade 3 opening, Std. p. 21); 'square corner' is grade 1 and 2 language (Prog. p. 125 (119)), and 'right angle' may
be new as a term. A fraction of a circle is grade 2 (2.G.A.3). Angle measure is new. This is a weak handoff: test
it." Recode readiness item 1 as "(informal; no grade 3 code)". Item 3 ("reads a ruler scale from a start other
than zero") is not what 2.MD.A.4 or 3.MD.B.4 say; recode it as 2.MD.B.6 (number line) or mark it "new skill, taught
in the unit, not a prerequisite".

### 3. A footnote the draft missed: order of operations arrives in grade 3 (firm)

**What is wrong.** The draft cites footnote 2 on Std. p. 23 but not footnote 3 on the same page, attached to
3.OA.D.8: problems are limited to whole numbers with whole-number answers, and students should know the
conventional order of operations when there are no parentheses.

**Cost.** Stretches 10, 13 and 14 all write equations such as 4 × 8 + 3 (stretch 13 readiness item 3). A unit
writer needs to know this is a grade 3 expectation to check, not a grade 5 topic to avoid. Parentheses and nested
grouping are grade 5 (5.OA.A.1).

**Change.** Add to the stretch 10 and stretch 14 "Grade 3 hands it" lines: "Order of operations without
parentheses is expected from grade 3 (Std. p. 23, footnote 3); evaluating expressions with grouping symbols is
grade 5 (5.OA.A.1)." Add one readiness item to stretch 14: "Computes 20 − 3 × 4 correctly."

### 4. Stretch 3 is missing two limits a unit writer will trip on (likely)

**What is missing.**
(a) Prog. p. 150 (144) says explaining equivalence as "multiplying by 1" in the form n/n is not a valid argument
in grade 4, because fraction times fraction is grade 5. This is the most common way teachers explain 4.NF.A.1.
(b) The denominator list (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 100) limits the fractions students are given. The Progressions'
own comparing example reaches a common denominator of 96 beside a margin note restating the limit (same pages). So
denominators met in working may fall outside the list.

**Cost.** (a) A lesson that teaches a rule the grade cannot justify; (b) a writer either bans 5/8 against 7/12 or
believes the course has broken a limit.

**Change.** Add to stretch 3 limits: "Explain equivalence by cutting each part into n smaller parts, not by
'multiplying by 1' as n/n; that argument needs grade 5 (Prog. p. 150 (144)). The denominator list limits the
fractions given in tasks; a common denominator found while working may be outside it (same page)." Also add to
"Hard here": "Comparing to 1 by how far each fraction is from 1 (Prog. p. 150 (144), margin)."

### 5. Both "from memory" lines fail; replace with sourced lines (firm that they are not in the Progressions)

**Stretch 6, line 159.** "Multiplying both numerator and denominator by the whole number" appears nowhere in the
Progressions pages for grades 3 to 5 or the fractions overview. It is a real classroom error (from memory, mine as
well), but it cannot stay as a Progressions claim. Replace with: "Hard here: seeing 3 × 2/5 as 6 × 1/5
(4.NF.B.4b); a whole number times a mixed number, done with the distributive property (Prog. p. 154 (148));
'times as much' when the quantity is not a count (Prog. p. 48 (42) and p. 144 (138)). Teaching note, not from the
Progressions: watch for students multiplying both top and bottom, a carry-over from stretch 3."

**Stretch 17, line 255.** The Progressions say nothing about parallelograms and symmetry; Prog. pp. 132 to 133
(126 to 127) only describe folding, grids and drawing the other half. The mathematics is true (a parallelogram
that is not a rectangle or rhombus has no line of symmetry). Replace with: "Hard here (teaching note, not from the
Progressions): a slanted parallelogram looks symmetric but fails the fold test in 4.G.A.3; have students fold or
use a mirror before they answer."

Then change the closing line of section 5 to say no statement rests on memory, with the two teaching notes marked
as such.

### 6. Stretch 15 "Hard here" points to the wrong page and the wrong grade (firm)

"The smaller the unit, the larger the number" is not on Prog. p. 110 (104). It is the grade 2 idea in 2.MD.A.2
(Prog. p. 103 (97); also the fractions overview, p. 141 (135)). Page 110 (104) says the two-column tables reinforce
place value and repeated reasoning, and its margin frames 4.MD.A.1 as "times as much".

**Change.** "Hard here: the smaller the unit, the more of them it takes, first met in grade 2 (2.MD.A.2; Prog. p.
103 (97)). Two-column tables show the 'times as much' link between units (Prog. p. 110 (104))." Add 2.MD.A.2 to
"Grade 3 hands it" and to finding A5.

### 7. Finding D4 quotes the wrong words (firm)

"May be reached" is on Prog. p. 41 (35) and is about how fluency grows number by number. The support for D4 is on
p. 37 (31): reaching fluency can take a long time and extra time and support must be given where needed.

**Change.** D4 evidence: "The Progressions say reaching fact fluency may take much time and that extra time and
support are essential where needed (Prog. p. 37 (31))." The finding itself stands.

### 8. "No fluency requirement" for multiplication needs one more clause (firm)

Lines 210 and 341 are right that 4.NBT.B.5 does not say "fluently". But the grade 4 opening (Std. p. 27, critical
area 1) names "understanding and fluency with multi-digit multiplication" and "efficient procedures". The draft
already quotes this in A9, so it contradicts itself mildly.

**Change.** Line 341: "Grade 4 has one fluency standard, 4.NBT.B.4. Multiplication (4.NBT.B.5) has no 'fluently'
in the standard, but the grade opening asks for growing fluency and efficient procedures (Std. p. 27): build
efficiency, do not time it. Factor pairs and division carry no fluency requirement."

### 9. Footnote attachments (firm, small)

- Line 59: "No conversions. No 'times as much' problems (Std. p. 25, footnotes 6 and 7)." Footnote 6 excludes
  compound units and finding the volume of a container; "no conversions" comes from the standard's own "given in
  the same units". Footnote 7 applies to the one-step problems of 3.MD.A.2 only. Reword: "One-step problems 'given
  in the same units' (3.MD.A.2). No compound units such as cubic centimeters (footnote 6). No 'times as much'
  problems (footnote 7)."
- Line 140: footnote 4 on Std. p. 31 hangs on 4.NF.C.5, not 4.NF.B.3. Content is right; write "(Std. p. 31,
  footnote 4, attached to 4.NF.C.5)".
- Line 191: footnote 7 is about measurement problems; the stronger source is the Progressions bullet already
  cited. Keep both, Progressions first.

## The four owner items: recommended decisions

### C1. Area and perimeter on day 89, before multiplication (day 94) and division (day 106): keep the order (likely)

- **What the Progressions say.** Prog. p. 112 (106): the numbers "can now be any of the numbers used in Grade 4".
  The margin codes it cites are 4.NBT.4, 4.NF.3d and 4.OA.4, not 4.NBT.5 or 4.NBT.6, and its own examples are
  small (an area of 80 with a side of 5; an area of 60 with a perimeter of 32). "Can" is permission. So the
  Progressions tie grade 4 area to factor pairs within 100 (stretch 1), multi-digit addition (stretch 9) and
  like-denominator fractions (stretch 4), all taught before day 89. The draft's C1 reads the sentence as a
  requirement for large products; it is not.
- **What open courses do.** Illustrative Mathematics grade 4 (scope and sequence opened today) does not teach
  4.MD.A.3 as one block: rectangles with a given area carry factor pairs in unit 1, perimeter problems sit in unit
  5 before multi-digit multiplication, and area problems are threaded through unit 6 with multiplication and
  division. EngageNY grade 4 Module 3 opens with area and perimeter before multi-digit multiplication (from
  memory; the site refused my request today, so do not rest on it). Neither puts large-number area before
  multiplication; both use area early with small numbers.
- **Recommendation.** Keep day 89. The formula work (why A = l × w, the three perimeter forms, unknown sides,
  same area and different perimeter) is the content of the unit and needs no large numbers. Write this rule into
  stretch 11 limits: "Days 89 to 93: areas are products within 100, or a one-digit number times a multiple of 10.
  Unknown-side area problems are factor-pair problems (4.OA.B.4). Perimeter may use multi-digit addition
  (4.NBT.B.4) and like-denominator fractions (4.NF.B.3d). Area with larger sides returns in units 12 and 13 as the
  context for partial products and for division, and in stretch 14 problems." Then make sure the spine's "Where it
  comes back" names 4.MD.A.3 in stretches 12 to 14; without that return the standard is half taught.
- **Reviewer view.** A reviewer would not mark the order down. A reviewer would mark down a course whose only
  4.MD.A.3 items have grade 3 numbers, so the return is the part that must not be lost.

### D4. Times tables: check first (firm)

Keep the spine's label "grade 3 maintenance" (a reviewer wants to see that facts are not re-taught as grade 4
content), and add: "Days 1 to 6 include a facts check; students below the mark get the grade 3 fact tools and print
during warm-ups, and the check repeats before day 94." The Progressions are explicit that fluency takes time and
support (Prog. p. 37 (31)). A reviewer treats this as support for students below grade level, not as off-grade
teaching, as long as it lives in warm-ups and not in lesson days.

### E1. Decimals in money and metric problems: a rule a unit writer can follow (firm)

Sources agree: 4.MD.A.2 allows "simple fractions or decimals"; Prog. p. 89 (83) says decimal notation may be used
in grade 4 measurement but computing in it waits for grade 5; Prog. p. 154 (148) has grade 4 adding tenths and
hundredths as fractions, with dimes and cents; decimal arithmetic is 5.NBT.B.7.

Proposed rule, for the limits table and stretches 7 and 15:

1. **May appear:** decimals to hundredths as given quantities (2.5 km, $3.75, 0.4 L).
2. **May be done with them:** read, write, compare, place on a number line; rename as a fraction with denominator
   10 or 100; rename in a smaller unit as a whole number ($3.75 is 375 cents; 2.5 km is 2,500 m).
3. **Computing:** every sum, difference, product or quotient is done on whole numbers in the smaller unit, or on
   fractions with denominators 10 and 100. The answer may then be written back as a decimal.
4. **Never in grade 4:** lining up decimal points in a written algorithm; a decimal times or divided by anything;
   converting a smaller unit to a larger one where the answer is a decimal (that is 5.MD.A.1).
5. **Test for a task:** if a student could only get the answer by computing in decimal notation, the task is
   grade 5.

A reviewer marks above-grade assessed items; this rule keeps them out.

### E2. Three days on rounding to any place: keep three, re-aim the third (judgment call)

The standard says "any place" and state tests ask it; the Progressions say rounding past the leftmost one or two
places "need not be practiced" (Prog. p. 69 (63), margin) and that rounding to a middle place is the harder case
(Prog. p. 68 (62)). So two days of teaching is enough, but rounding only earns its place when used. Recommended:
day 1 rounding on the number line to the leftmost place; day 2 any place, including a middle place and the
"round up into a new place" case; day 3 rounding used to estimate and check sums and differences, which is what
4.OA.A.3 asks for and what stretch 9 begins. If the owner prefers the draft's option (two days, one to stretch
13), that is also defensible; no reviewer would mark either down.

   handoffs the same way. Add a one-word tag per stretch. Weak: stretch 7 (nothing from grade 3), stretch 16
   (finding 2), stretch 10 (comparison language is new; the base is grade 2 additive comparison), stretch 8
   (base is grade 2), stretch 15 (base is grade 2 length, and customary units are thin in grade 3), stretch 13

2. **Grade 2 and earlier as the true source** (firm; the draft did not read the K to 2 pages). Add: equal shares

   measurement model (2.MD.B.6) under stretches 3, 5 and 16; rows and columns in a rectangle (2.G.A.2) under

   work leans on length models and number lines more than circles (Prog. p. 146 (140)), which should shape the

4. **The practice standards are absent** (firm that they are absent; a reviewer would look for them in unit plans,

5. **Same-whole word problems** in stretch 4 (Prog. p. 153 (147)): add to "Hard here": "Problems where the two
   fractions refer to different wholes cannot be added; include one."
6. **Writing the plus sign in mixed numbers** early (2 + 3/4), Prog. p. 151 (145) margin: add to stretch 4.

## As a working document

- **Finding a handoff in under a minute: yes.** Section 2 has one heading per stretch and the same fields in the
  same order. Do not change that. Put section 2 first.
- **Readiness checks in ten minutes: mostly not, as written.** Stretch 3 and stretch 15 have five items; several
  items across the page are "says why" or "explains", and stretch 15 item 1 needs rulers and item 2 ("has a feel
  for a gram") cannot be tested on paper. A fourth grader does about one short written item a minute plus setup.
  Change: cap every stretch at four items; mark each W (written, markable) or T (teacher listens or watches); at
  most one T per check; rewrite untestable items as tasks. Examples: stretch 15 item 2 becomes "Circles the better
  unit: a paperclip weighs about 1 gram or 1 kilogram"; stretch 3 item 4 becomes "Two pizzas of different sizes are
  drawn; is half of one equal to half of the other? Why?"; stretch 11 item 4 becomes "Draws two rectangles with
  perimeter 12 and different areas". Items that test earlier stretches of this year (for example stretch 5 items
  3 and 4) should be labelled "this year" so the reteach points to the right unit, not to a grade 3 tool.
- **Readable by a teacher: yes for section 2, no for sections 1 and 3.** Section 2 is plain. The strand tables in
  section 1 are dense with codes and repeat section 2.
- **What to cut to reach 5,000 words** (taste, not a reviewer matter): move section 3 (findings, about a quarter
  of the page) to its own file once the fixes are applied, since it is a one-time list and unit writers do not need
  it; cut D1 and D2 now; shrink section 1 to a single table of "limits by grade" per strand; keep section 4 but
  drop its table rows that repeat the spine's fluency plan.
