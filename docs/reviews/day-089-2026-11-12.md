## Verdict

Day 89 is correct mathematics in a sound order: tiles make each measure a count, the count becomes a formula,
the long form of the perimeter comes before 2 × (l + w), the two register errors are fixed on purpose, and the
planned question asks for the reason (MP.8) rather than a rule. The biggest risk is in what the day hands on and
what it prints: "Tomorrow" sends two responses to day 90 that day 90 does not contain, the student page gives a
two-inch blank for three equations while a third of side 1 stays empty, and the display shows a plain outline
on the one day whose whole point is squares inside and unit sides around. The one change that matters most is
to make the hand-off real on both sides (finding 1); findings 2 and 3 are the same kind of gap the day 90 read
found, so they are template and build fixes, not day fixes.

### 5. The edge-squares error is misnamed, unregistered and has no sign on the slip

- **What is wrong.** The teacher page says "Watch for a student who counts the corner tiles instead of unit sides
  on the edge (they get 12, not 16)". The 12 comes from counting every square that touches the edge (5 + 5 + 1 +
  1), with each corner square counted once; "corner tiles" names the wrong thing, and a teacher looking at
  corners will not see it. The error is real and common on exactly this day's model, but it is not in the error
  register, has no wrong-answer rows, and "Tomorrow" seats "students who counted corner tiles" with no way to
  find them from the slip. The student frame invites it: "Perimeter: count around the edge."
- **Evidence.** From memory: counting squares along the edge instead of unit lengths is a long-noted grade 3 and
  4 perimeter error. The Progressions, PDF page 108 (102), make the matching teaching move (mark and label the
  unit lengths around the edge, separately from the squares). My own count of the day's rectangles.
- **Cost.** The one error the tile model itself causes goes unwatched and unrecorded; on the slip an 18 (8 by 3
  counted by edge squares) is marked plain wrong with no response.
- **Change.** Teacher page: "Watch for a student who counts the squares along the edge instead of the unit sides
  (12, not 16: a corner square has two sides on the edge). Have them mark each unit side with a tick and count the
  ticks." Student frame: "Perimeter: count the unit sides around the edge." Register and rows as under "Settled by
  the expert".
- **Confidence.** Likely (one source for the teaching move, memory for the error).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert.

### 6. Twenty minutes of whole-class modelling on one rectangle, and one minute of student talk

  planned question asks "is it always the same?".
- **Evidence.** IM grade 4, unit 5, lesson 16 (read today): its perimeter work runs as 10 and 15 minute activities

  and the first expected answer becomes "it worked for every pair's". Keep "Three rectangles" alone, as now. The

- **Confidence.** A judgment call; real minutes come from the taught run.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert.
