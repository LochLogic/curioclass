# Safety read: science-grade-1 (course finish, 2026-11-22)

Kind: safety. Target: every hands-on day of science-grade-1 (COURSE-RUN stage 7). Read-only; this report is the only file written.

## What I studied

- NSTA position statement, Safety and School Science Instruction, https://www.nsta.org/nstas-official-positions/safety-and-school-science-instruction (checked 2026-11-22). Taught: one teacher should not supervise more than 24 students doing science activities; eye protection, gloves and aprons supplied even where law does not require them; no food or drink where hazards are used; send families a safety acknowledgment form; cut any activity that cannot be done safely.
- NSTA, Safety in Elementary Science (PDF), https://static.nsta.org/pdfs/SafetyInElementaryScience.pdf (opened and read in full, 2026-11-22). Taught: rubber bands and other projectiles mean impact goggles or safety glasses (ANSI/ISEA Z87.1) for all occupants of the room; younger learners (4 to 6) need more safety instruction; hazard analysis written into each lesson; never taste a plant, check plant allergies, no poisonous or thorny plants; teacher demonstrations need a rehearsal and a safety review; extension cords covered against tripping; other hazards to analyze include falls, bright light, loud noise, choking and sharp objects; handwashing steps.
- NSTA, Eye Protection and Safer Practices FAQ, https://www.nsta.org/eye-protection-and-safer-practices-faq (checked 2026-11-22). Taught: eye protection is required when elastic materials under stress (springs, rubber) or projectiles are used; eyewear must meet Z87.1 (Z87 impact, Z87+ high impact); shared eyewear is cleaned after each use with soap and water and air dried; it applies at the elementary level, to everyone in the room, during set-up, work and take-down.
- NSTA safety topic page, https://www.nsta.org/topics/safety (checked 2026-11-22). Taught: the elementary resources set, including a sample elementary safety acknowledgment form (that PDF did not render as text, so nothing here rests on it).
- NASA, eclipse safety, https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/safety/ (checked 2026-11-22). Taught: it is never safe to look directly at the bright sun without special eye protection; sunglasses are not safe; optical devices without a solar filter cause instant severe injury. Supports the shadow-stick and moon days' "never look at the sun" and "no binoculars".
- America's Poison Centers, button batteries, https://www.poison.org/battery (checked 2026-11-22). Taught: a swallowed button battery can burn the esophagus in as little as 2 hours; batteries in the nose or ear also injure young children. Supports the course's "AA or AAA only, never button cells, compartment secured".
- American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology, latex allergy, https://acaai.org/allergies/allergic-conditions/latex-allergy/ (checked 2026-11-22). Taught: avoidance is the treatment; rubber toys and balloons contain latex. Supports the non-latex band rule on days 28 to 31.
- U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission battery page, https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Batteries: refused automated reads (403); the poison center page above was tried instead. No finding rests on the refused page.
- A chemical society's elementary safety guide (ACS, Safety in the Elementary School Science Classroom) returned no readable text. No chemical in this course needs a safety data sheet beyond school art chalk and modeling clay, both common nontoxic art supplies; no SDS was opened, and no finding rests on one.
- docs/FOUNDATION.md section 5 (room assumptions: K to 2 one shared device with a paper path; the physical world is written into the plan with a path for a teacher who has nothing).
- docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md row S5 (the door): every hands-on day lists materials per group and has a Safety and set-up section; a hazard in the materials is named with its control; a stand-in is no less safe; a course safety page covers the room's rules.
- courses/science-kindergarten/safety.md (section headings only), as the course safety page model the grade 1 course would follow.

## Verdict

Fail, on the door: the day-level safety writing is careful and mostly sound (sun, batteries, latex, scissors, lamps, string and mouths are all named with controls), but the course has no course safety page, which S5 requires. Fix finding 1 and the three major findings (2 to 4) and the course passes with fixes; the rest are minor.
