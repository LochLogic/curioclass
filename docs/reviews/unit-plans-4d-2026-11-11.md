# Cold read: grade 4 math stretch sheets and unit plans, stretches 1 to 3 (step 4d)

2026-11-11. Written by the curriculum expert agent (docs/agents/EXPERT.md). This is one reader's
judgment of plans on paper. Nothing here was tried with children, and no working teacher has read it.

## Verdict

These plans are close to usable: every answer key is right, every part of each standard has a check item
before any part has two, and the numbers stay inside the grade's limits. Three things hold them back from
a lesson writer's desk: several days carry more than fifty minutes can hold (days 3, 4 and 7 most of all),
the no-reuse rule still leaks through the tools' own numbers, and stretch 3 teaches the common-denominator
procedure before benchmarks and the same whole, which is the reverse of both open courses. The word caps
are wrong, not the plans: the caps count content the page itself says must never be cut.

## Findings, in order of weight

### 1. Three days are overloaded, and the day rows hide it (firm)

The first review's overload fault has come back in a new place. The day rows give minutes for the opening
and the tool but none for the by-hand work, so no row can be added up to fifty.

- **s02, day 7.** Five minutes of answers, twelve of teaching, fifteen on the tool, four rules of eight
  terms each with reasons, four wrong examples, and a three-part exit. It carries three parts of the
  standard in one day, against rule 8's limit of two. Illustrative Mathematics gives this work four
  lessons. Smallest fix: tapes 1 and 5 only, ten minutes; two rules by hand; wrong example A in the
  teaching, C and E by hand, D moved to day 8.
- **u01-1, day 3.** A 36-fact check, eight minutes of teaching, fifteen on the tool, eight numbers from 40
  to 100 searched by hand, a wrong example, a written task and a two-part exit. Smallest fix: four numbers,
  not eight. Drop Rush shift 1: its cards use 12, 20, 18, 30, 28 and 40, the numbers of days 1 and 2. Keep
  cards 4a and 4b, with "write fits or no before you tap Pack", because Pack does the dividing for the
  student and the day's goal is "with no tiles".
- **u01-2, day 4.** Two tool parts for 22 minutes, six fence questions, three wrong examples, a five-part
  exit, and parts (b) and (c). Smallest fix: one tool part (Fill Line, ten minutes), four fence questions,
  and E4 cut to the fill-in plus one "is it a multiple" question.
- **All day tables.** Add minutes to the by-hand column so each row sums to fifty.

Evidence: UNIT-PLAN.md rules 8 and 13; the Illustrative Mathematics unit 6 lesson list (four lessons in
section A against two teaching days here).

### 2. Stretch 3 teaches the procedure before the sense-making (likely; the owner's call)

Units 3.3 and 3.4 should swap their first halves. Both open courses teach benchmarks before common
denominators: Illustrative Mathematics puts "Relate Fractions to Benchmarks" at lesson 6 and "Ways to
Compare Fractions" at lesson 12, ahead of lessons 13 and 15 on equivalent fractions and common
denominators. EngageNY module 5 topic C compares to 0, 1/2 and 1 first, then same numerators, then related
units, then the general method. The Progressions list common denominators first and then say "Students
also reason using benchmarks such as 1/2 and 1", but that is the order of an explanation, not a teaching
order.

My reasons for benchmarks and the same whole first:

- The same whole is the condition for any comparison. As planned, students compare for four days before
  they meet it.
- Benchmark reasoning needs only grade 3 number-line sense plus the names for 1/2 from unit 3.2. It builds
  a sense of size and goes straight at error D (bigger numbers mean a bigger fraction).
- Once a procedure is known, students use it for everything. Item 3.4a already has to say "without renaming
  both fractions" to stop that.
- Day 17's own example (5/12 against 1/2, rename one) is the natural bridge from benchmarks to renaming.

Smallest fix: move day rows 21 and 22 ahead of rows 17 to 20. Unit 3.3 becomes: the same whole and 1/2; 1
and closer to 1; rename one; rename both. Unit 3.4 becomes: common numerators; mixed fluency; choose a way
(the Duel day); check. Four days each, the choose-a-way day still last, no check item changes. An adoption
reviewer would not mark down either order. The spine's unit titles change, so this goes to the owner.

### 3. The no-reuse rule still leaks, mostly through the tools (firm)

End checks and exit questions are clean against each other and against the taught examples. What is left:

- **s03, item 3.1b** (3/4 = 9/12) is the same as Fraction Wall Duel's blank card "3/4 by 3" and its first
  item on your own. The writer flagged it. Fix on the spec side, as u03-4 says.
- **s03, family note** names "one half or three fifths", the fact item 3.4a needs, and E20(b) works the
  same pair. Fix: a family note with no numbers from any check, and change E20(b)'s second fraction.
- **s02, items 2 and 5** both test "add 5", which was also taught on tape 5 and by hand. Fix: item 2
  becomes "Start at 12. Add 11: 12, 23, 34, 45, 56."
- **s02, item 6.** The remembered answer "even" was taught twice (tape 7 and wrong example D). Fix: "Start
  at 4. The rule is multiply by 3: 4, 12, 36, 108, 324." The key then accepts even and multiples of 4.
- **u01-2, field 11** says the tool's on-your-own numbers (48, 54, 84, 58, 37, 51) are kept out of every
  taught example. That is not true: the spec's own Rush cards use 48 and 84 (3a, 4a), 54 (3b, 5c), 51 (2b)
  and 37 (2a). Only 58 is clean. Fix the sentence, and ask the spec to change its on-your-own numbers.
- **No sheet lists the reserved numbers in one place.** u03-4 says day sheets must keep check numbers out;
  the stretch 1 plans do not. Fix: one "reserved numbers" line on each stretch sheet.

Evidence: UNIT-PLAN.md rule 4; the three specs.

### 4. Stretch 1 never checks an odd composite that needs a real search (likely)

Error F is "odd, or not in my tables, means prime". The checks test it with 33 (3 × 11), 99 (9 × 11, which
is in a table learned to 12) and 95 (ends in 5). The hard cases (51, 57, 69, 77, 87, 91, 93) are all used
up by the tool and the taught examples. Smallest fix: have the spec replace the second variant of Rush card
2c (77, 79, 93) so 77 and 93 are free; item 4 swaps 33 for 93; E5 swaps 95 for 77. Item 5 keeps 99, with
"not in her times tables" changed to "she cannot think of a fact for it".

### 5. Fraction Wall Duel serves one day in fifteen, and the stretch's real need is unowned (likely)

The conditional verdict in u03-4 is sound: key for day 23 only if the eight gaps close, otherwise light. I
checked all eight against the spec and they hold. One the writer missed: with no deck picker, the one deck
played is deck 1, which has no hundredths, though item 3.4a uses 48/100.

The writer is right that a number line splitter is the stretch's larger need, but "needs" is too strong:
printed lines carry days 11 to 15, as they do in both open courses. The strongest case for a tool is
tenths into hundredths on day 15 (and again in stretch 7) and predict-then-see across days 11 to 15. This
is a stronger view than the first review took, because units 3.2 to 3.4 now exist to show the pattern.
u03-1 still says a separate splitter "is not asked for", still gives readiness on day 10, and still says
its fields wait for a yes. Fix u03-1 to match s03.

### 6. Loose or ambiguous items (firm; none has a wrong key)

- **s01, item 6** (Maya, 35) does not say whether 6 was tried. Fix: "She has tried 1 to 6 and found 1 × 35
  and 5 × 7."
- **s01, item 2.** The wrong answer "no 1 or 52" is labelled A, but it is a different idea (a number is a
  factor and a multiple of itself). Give it its own note.
- **s02, item 6.** The key should also accept "multiples of 5" as written today.
- **s02, written task** asks for the 30th shape while the stretch's limit says the farthest step is the
  10th. Fix the limit: a repeating pattern may ask for a step that is a multiple of its length.
- **s03, item 3.1a** needs eighteen hand-drawn marks. Fix: cut each third into 2 (4/3 = 8/6; wrong answers
  4/6, 6/5, 7/6 or 9/6 keep their letters).
- **u01-1, E2** (75) on day 2 tests day 3's skill, pairs outside the tables with no help. Use 28, 32 or 50.
- **u01-1, day 1.** Wrong example A is fixed by the ordered search, which is taught on day 2. Move it.
- **Multiplication table** for students below the facts mark: it must stop at 9 × 9 or 10 × 10. A 12 × 12
  table gives away items 4 and 5.

## The word caps

The plans are not padded in the bad sense. About 100 to 200 words a plan could go under the page's own cut
order, mostly tool gap lists and restated rules, but the gap lists have nowhere else to live.

The caps are wrong because they count protected content. Exit questions, the errors table and the day rows
run 590 to 690 words in every unit plan, and UNIT-PLAN.md says those are never cut. On a stretch sheet the
error register (220 to 290 words) and the look-fors and sample answers (140 to 190) sit outside the two
exempt tables.

Recommended caps, counting only what may be cut:

- **Unit plan: 1,000 words, not counting fields 3, 4 and 6.** The five new plans run about 890 to 1,110 on
  that count, so two need a light trim.
- **Stretch sheet: 800 words, not counting fields 4, 5 (whole, with look-fors and samples) and 6.** s01 is
  about 680. s03 is about 980 and can lose 150 from fields 7, 8 and 10.
- **One-unit stretch: the two added, 1,800.**
- Give tool gap lists a home that does not count: one tool-asks file per course.

Confidence: judgment call. The numbers come from five plans and two sheets.
