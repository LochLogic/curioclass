## Verdict

Day 21 is correct mathematics and teaches the right idea in the right way: one tenth against one twelfth on two printed
lines, then "make the counts match and compare the size of the parts", then the same pair done both ways and "which was
quicker?". The biggest risk is that the 20-minute practice is never checked by anyone against anything: the partner check
has no answers to check against, its `Check:` line does not reach the display, and Pull it together looks only at pairs 1
and 6, so a pair of students who share error D agree with each other and go home wrong. The one change that matters most
is finding 1: show the six signs and renamed fractions on the display at 14 minutes and let partners mark against them.
