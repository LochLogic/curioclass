## 2. The (A) kinds and the read across the other unit plans

Scope of the read: 137 unit plans (15 social studies courses in data/plans/frames.csv, 138 units, less us-history-i
unit 5), their s*.md files and days.csv. Done by scripts over the files plus targeted reads; no full re-read.
Firm = the evidence line shows the fault; likely = the fault fits but one condition was not checked.

### K1. A register letter holds two or more misconceptions (#5)

Read: every field 6 register row whose error cell joins two claims with a semicolon (cells cut at "right:" or
"Correct:", so the correct-answer clause is not counted). **119 of 137 units** have at least one such letter (529 of
1,101 rows by the first pass). Sample evidence:
- world-history-ii s04 B: "Trenches prevented casualties; total war means everyone fought."
- world-geography-and-ancient-civilizations-i s09 B: "Latitude is east-west; every city is a capital."
- civics-government-and-civic-life s03 A: "The President makes all laws; agencies and courts are one body; Roman offices equal modern branches."
- world-history-i s08 B: "Every expulsion occurred in 1492; conversion meant consent; missions were peaceful."
- us-history-i s03 C: "Worcester ordered removal; every nation took one voluntary march under Jackson."

Units without one: civics s08, s09; grade 2 s04, s05; grade 4 s01; grade 5 s07, s08, s09; kindergarten s01, s02;
us-history-i s07; us-history-ii s04, s05, s11, s12; world geography I s01, s02; world-history-i s05.
Limits: a semicolon pair can share one repair (then no split is needed), and "or" joins (as in s05's H) were not
counted. Of the flagged rows I read in the scan output (about 60, one per unit), most hold misconceptions that need
different repairs; a few pairs share one (world geography II s11 C: "Debased coins always cause proportional
inflation; more coins mean more real wealth"). This is the pattern Codex wrote; it is the kind the measure turns on.

- us-government-and-politics s02 (day 12 seminar record, an Understanding day).

Likely (an Application day after teaching, collected and scored with no return or revision named):
- world-history-i s02 (day 25 product), s03 (days 42 to 44), s05 (E68, E71), s06 (day 87 claim), s08 (day 122 table).
- us-government-and-politics s01 (day 4), s04 (days 29 to 31).

Firm 14, likely 12.

### K3. An end item that a day exit rehearses, with no unseen stimulus (#3)

Read: word overlap between each end item and each exit prompt in the same unit (threshold 0.6), then reading the hits.
Most hits were sample answers, not prompts. Likely: world-history-ii s05, end 4a "Annotate Ethiopia's 1935 invasion and
one enforcement failure" against the day 68 exit "Name one failure of collective security in Ethiopia and one in
Spain", whose key gives the 4a meets sample. Possible: world geography I s04 3b against the day 44 exit (but 3b carries
a new caption). Many units state "These independent exit prompts are reserved", which guards against this.
Limit: paraphrased repeats escape a word-overlap test.

### K4. A day exit answered from today's taught sources, not a new one (#21)

Read: exit prompts naming "today's" or "the day's" sources without "new", "fresh", "unseen" or "another"; elementary
rows dropped where the exit is an observation. Likely:
- social-studies-grade-5 s05 day 63: "Use today's annotated clauses to connect all six principles to evidence."
- world-history-ii s03 day 29: "Using today's ledger extract, how did the trader profit..."
- world-history-ii s04 day 49: "Which detail in today's document or testimony contradicts it?"
- world-history-ii s06 day 75: "Using today's named account, explain one effect of occupation..."
- world-history-ii s10 E138: "Name one achievement and one limit of the gacaca courts, from today's account."
- world-history-ii s11 E146: "From today's briefs, give a qualified judgment each on mitigation, habitat and farm output."
Limit: exits that reuse a source without the word "today's" are missed.

### K5. An exit that lets a named part of the goal or standard go unmeasured (#2, #26, #31, #34, #41)

Read, for the two forms a grep can test: (a) exits coded D3.4.9-12 that never ask for significance; (b) revise-from-
feedback days whose exit does not name the feedback. Found none: world-history-i s10 E146 asks for a "significant"
claim; us-history-i s09 E150 says "Name a revision responding to written feedback". The other forms ("a limit or
obstacle", "two details" from four kinds, half a goal) need a reading of each exit against its goal and were not read.
Count 0, and the least sure kind.

### K6. The feedback spec does not cover a scored criterion (#30)

Read: every sentence that says what written feedback names, against whether the unit scores D3.3 (inconsistency).
No other unit specifies feedback that way. Possible: us-history-i s09 returns written feedback on the empire report
with no stated focus at all. Count 0 firm.

### K7. A wrong answer filed under a letter whose register text does not describe it (#15, #28, #38)

Read: for each tagged wrong answer, word overlap with its letters' register text; 64 units had at least one row with
no overlap. Eight were read by hand: two real (social-studies-grade-3 s05, "only adult men faced hardships (G)" where G
is about reconstructions generalizing; economics s06, "both are public purchases (D)" where D is about Congress and the
Fed), five fit on reading, one partial. Multi-letter tags like s05's "(E, H)" are common (world-history-ii s04 tags
"(E, G, H)" on one answer). Estimate 15 to 25 units; only grade 3 s05 counted.

### K8. A day coded to a content standard while it teaches inquiry skills (#36)

Read: days.csv goals about memory, interpretation, historians or sourcing whose codes hold no C3 indicator. Eight hits;
all but one teach their content standard. Possible: us-history-ii unit 5 day 63, "how present views shaped later memory,
to the 1995 museum exhibit dispute", coded MA.USII.T3.7 only (the skill sits in the note as "Practised"). Count 0 firm.

### K9. Content out of time order with no reason given (#40)

Read: days.csv goals and notes about memory, remembrance and monuments, and their placement. The one later-memory case
(us-history-ii day 63) has its reason in s05.md Limits: "signpost the later museum dispute as a memory case". Count 0.
Limit: other kinds of out-of-order content were not searched.

### K10. A group or scholarship the days need is missing from the planned sensitivity read (#9, #47)

Read: units whose body or day list names Native nations or peoples, against the "Sensitive topics" reads line and the
days flagged for the read. 57 units carry Native content; most name the nations' own pages in the read. Likely gaps
(the s05 pattern: a read that names only another community):
- civics-government-and-civic-life s02: day 17's grievance "names Native peoples with a slur"; read is "(Black historians' scholarship studied first)" and only day 16 is flagged.
- us-history-i s08: a voter card for "a Native woman without United States citizenship"; read names "(Black communities, for lynching and the civil rights content)".
- us-history-ii s01: key "rail links expanded markets while taking Native nations' lands (for example the Lakota nations')"; Sensitive topics name no Native content.
- world-history-ii s03: "missionary conversion of Indigenous peoples" in the Americas; the read studies Congolese, Ovaherero and Nama voices only.
Possible (one sentence of Native content, unflagged): us-government-and-politics s02 (Medicine Creek worked example in unit 3's readiness check), grade 3 s06, grade 5 s05, grade 5 s09, world-history-i s03, s06, s10, world-history-ii s10.
The scholarship half (#47: a flagged day asks for scholarship the Sensitivity study does not list) was not read across
units. Count 4.

### K11. The next day's warm-up is booked twice: exit repair and a planned retrieval (#45)

Read: days.csv rows whose note puts a retrieval task or readiness re-ask on a day right after a teaching day. 70 units
have one; 19 of those also state the "next day's warm-up" repair rule in their own text (for example world-history-i
unit 2 days 17, 18, 19, 23). The rule itself is the template's (day-079 #3 gave it ORIGIN template), so this is better
fixed once there: say where a repair goes when the next warm-up is taken. Not counted per unit.

### K12. A wrong fact in a unit plan key (#10)

No grep can find this. s05.md says "Unsourced teaching choices are from memory, untried", and most registers cite
"anticipated error from memory"; other keys may carry the same risk. Not counted.

**Result: Off (121 of 138, 88 percent).** The driver is K1, the register-letter pattern Codex wrote across nearly every

(scoring product days off the sitting) used in most of world history I and II.

## 4. How sure

- K1: firm on the count of compound letters (a plain text test); likely on "needs a change", since a pair that shares one repair could stand. Even at half the rate it is Off.
- K2: firm on the 14 Understanding-day cases (day kinds checked in days.csv); likely on the 12 Application-day cases, where I did not check whether a return day exists in the section plans.
- K4, K10: likely; each hit was read, but the grep misses exits and Native content worded differently.
- K3, K5, K6, K8, K9: low recall. Each read tested one or two forms of the fault; zero found does not mean zero present.
- K7: estimate only (heuristic with a high false-positive rate, eight read by hand).
- K11 and K12: not counted per unit; K11 is better treated as a template line, K12 cannot be read by grep.
- The A/B sort is mine where reports gave only "unit": #26, #28, #34 and #41 could be argued B (the planner could have written a tighter item from loose plan text). Moving them changes no count, since their kinds found no or few other units.
