## What I studied

Reached and read (2026-11-18):

1. https://communicationfirst.org/words-we-use-plain-language-version/ (community's own: people who use AAC). Name
   what a person needs or does, not a label; avoid "nonverbal", "special needs", "deficit", "behaviors". Taught me to
   check how the AAC child (Mina) and the children with aids are written.
2. https://communicationfirst.org/best-practices-for-online-meetings/ (community's own). Give extra time, embrace
   silence, never expect an answer on the spot, do not talk over, treat as equal partners. Taught me to check pass
   lines, "ask the six" calls and who holds ideas and roles in the pictures.
3. https://schoolhouseconnection.org/article/on-being-noticed-feeling-safe-and-finding-strength-in-community (young
   people's own account of growing up without a stable home). Stigma, not wanting pity or judgment, being invisible
   in school. Taught me to check who plays the child with less, and home questions.
4. https://education.ohio.gov/Topics/Student-Supports/Homeless-Youth/Student-Supports/Supporting-Young-Children-Experiencing-Homelessnes
   (state education guidance). Eligibility rests on homeless status not paperwork; families document in alternative
   ways; support early literacy at home. It gives no classroom-practice list, so it only confirms that a child may be
   without an address, supplies or a stable adult; I took the rest of the practice from sources 3, 5 and 6.
5. https://www.splcenter.org/learning-for-justice/frameworks/social-justice-standards (education style guide,
   identity and diversity anchors). Children should be comfortable with people like and unlike them without pride
   denying others' dignity. Taught me to check that difference is shown as ordinary and that no group is only helped.
6. https://www.nctsn.org/what-is-child-trauma/trauma-types/complex-trauma/effects (child trauma network). Trauma in
   young children shows as checking out, easy overwhelm, intense reaction to triggers, or rigid compliance. Taught
   me to check the fable's holding scene, traps that set a child up, and grouping by mistake.

## Verdict

Pass with fixes. The two units are carefully built on the points the frame names: no child reports on home or money,
wrong answers are voiced by puppets, groups are private, pointing and aids count, and unit 2 never asks a chore at
home in the class plan. The serious faults are cross-day: the children shown with a disability are only ever blocked
or helped, three exit cards use pictured children as the mistake, and a few home prompts and one staged trap break
the units' own rules.
