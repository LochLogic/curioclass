## Verdict

Day 74 is sound mathematics in a good shape: one worked difference in expanded form beside the columns, two done
both ways in pairs, six with one ungrouping each in a different place, both register errors fixed on purpose, and
a planned question that makes "the top number keeps its value" the point of the day. The biggest risk is that the
evidence cannot separate the day's two errors where it matters: pair problem 2 gives the same wrong answer for F
and for G, and the exit tells the student which place to ungroup, so day 75's F-or-G branch rests on thin data.
The one change that matters most is to make every item tell F from G (finding 1, with the exit fix the cheap read
already found), and to give the teacher the column picture on the display instead of a 32-cell table to draw
during a 10-minute teaching block (finding 3).

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Pair problem 2 cannot tell error F from error G

- **What is wrong.** In 7,563 − 2,138 the ones are 3 and 8, which differ by 5. Taking the smaller from the larger
  gives 8 − 3 = 5; making 13 ones and leaving the tens alone gives 13 − 8 = 5, then 6 − 3 = 3. Both errors give
  5,435. wrongs.csv has only an F row for it, so the key says 5,435 is F. A pair that writes 5,435 may have done
  either, and the teacher's response is different for each (ask "can I take 8 from 2?" for F; ask what the tens
  became for G). Problem 2 is the second half of the "both ways" block, the place where the teacher is told to
  watch for exactly this.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s09-P74-2, wrongs.csv s09-P74-2 (one row, F); worked by hand. A check over every
  stretch 9 single-ungrouping difference finds the same clash in s09-P75-11 (8,436 − 3,281) and s09-P75-12
  (6,952 − 4,617), day 75's warm-up, and in s09-P72-12 (806 − 452).
- **Cost.** A teacher reading a 5,435 sends a G student to "can I take 8 from 2?", which they already know.
- **Change.** Day: replace P74-2 with s09-P74-2v2, 7,562 − 2,138 = 5,424 (one ten ungrouped); F gives 5,436, G
  gives 5,434, both rows added. Process: `plan.py check` fails a subtraction row on a day whose register names
  both F and G when the F and G values are equal (the column with the single ungrouping has digits 5 apart), and
  fails a row on such a day that keys only one of the two.
- **Confidence.** Firm (arithmetic).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** script. **REPEAT:** yes (same kind as the s10-X85 row: an item that could not show
  the error it was meant to). **CHEAP:** missed.

### 2. The table uses "Left" for two things and never names its halves

- **What is wrong.** Side 1 tells the student "Left: expanded form. Right: columns." The table's last row is also
  called "Left" (what is left after taking away). The header row prints Th H T O twice with nothing over the two
  halves, so the only cue to which half is expanded form is the word "Left", which is also a row. A grade 4
  reader meets "Left" twice in three lines with two meanings.
- **Evidence.** d074-student.pdf side 1 (rendered); d074.md student page.
- **Cost.** Students write the difference into the left half's cells, or the expanded form into the bottom row;
  the teacher spends the first minutes of the teaching block sorting it out, three times (example, 1, 2).
- **Change.** Rename the row "Left" to "Answer" in all three tables. Replace the frame line with: "Th is
  thousands, H is hundreds, T is tens, O is ones. First four boxes: expanded form. Last four: columns."
- **Confidence.** Likely.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 3. The display has no column picture, so the teacher draws a 32-cell table while teaching

- **What is wrong.** The display's example screens are prose steps only: no columns, no crossed-out 7, no small
  1, no expanded-form half. The teacher page says students fill the side 1 table "as you go, not ahead of you",
  and the header says "no document camera: draw the columns on the board". So the teacher must draw both halves
  (eight columns, four rows) on the board, inside the 10-minute teaching block, and the class has nothing on the
  screen showing where the 6 and the small 1 go. The same holds for the fixes: "their working beside the right
  working" is two prose lines, not two worked columns, on a day whose errors are about marks in columns.
- **Evidence.** d074-display.html; d074.md "Teaching first" and "Fix it"; DEFAULTS.md "No document camera ... the
  display page carries every figure and step"; LESSON-DAY.md, display row ("the question with its labelled
  figure"). The figure grammar has `rect` and `strip`, no column layout.
- **Cost.** Two to three minutes of the teaching block lost to drawing; a G error (the 1 written, nothing traded)
  is shown in words, not as the very marks the student copies.
- **Change.** Day, now: "Before class" adds "Draw the example table on the board, top two rows filled." (Fund the
  words by cutting "Ask what the 9 hundreds and 2 tens became, as numbers" to "Ask what 9 hundreds 2 tens became.")
  Process: add a column figure to lesson.py (`cols 9372-4156 ungroup=T`, drawing place names, the crossed-out
  digit, the new digit above and the small 1, revealed on the step that first says "cross out"), and a two-column
  form of it for a wrong answer to fix. Stretch 9 days 72 to 77 all need it.
- **Confidence.** Firm on the gap; likely on the minutes.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (LESSON-DAY F1, display without figures; d080 and d082
  display rows). **CHEAP:** missed.

### 4. Readiness items are marked during the pair block, though nothing uses them until tomorrow

- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read asked where the results
  go, not when they are marked).

### 5. The key prints lines that do not fit the row

- **Evidence.** d074-key.html; d072-key.html and d075-key.html carry the same line; scripts/lesson.py `ACCEPT`;

- **Cost.** Small: a teacher reads past a wrong-sounding line; on a subtraction day "either order" invites the

- **Change.** Process: lesson.py prints the accept line only when a day has an equation row (a letter or "?" in
  a stem). errors.csv G "error" becomes the general form, "Writes the small 1 in the place that needed more, but
  leaves the next place as it was"; the example stays in the "example" cell.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.
