## Verdict

Day 87 is correct mathematics in a sound order: one story worked as two equations and then one, practice with
the letter named and an estimate beside every answer, three finished answers judged by estimate, and the three
register errors fixed on purpose. The biggest risk is that the second key moment, judging answers by estimate,
has nothing to look at: the three stories are on neither the student page nor the display, and the promised
answers screen for the six problems is not built either. The one change that matters most is to make the
display carry every practice row a whole-class part names (findings 1 and 2); after that, the exit must ask for
what it marks (finding 3).

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The judge-it round has no stories to judge

- **What is wrong.** "Judge it by estimate" shows "one finished answer at a time" (P87-7 to 9: Ola's beads,
  Gus's van, Ivy's markers). The student page prints only "Ola ____ Gus ____ Ivy ____". The display has no
  screen for any of the three: `screens()` in lesson.py returns nothing for a practice row, so the first thing a
  class sees is the planned question "How did you know 847 was wrong?" with no van and no 847 before it.
- **Evidence.** build/lessons/math-4/u10-3/d087-display.html and d087-student.pdf; lesson.py lines 727 to 747.
  u10-3.md field 5 names this as key moment 2 of the unit.
- **Cost.** The teacher reads three stories aloud from the teacher page and students estimate from memory of a
  spoken three-sentence story, or the five minutes collapse. The estimate (part h) is taught nowhere else.
- **Change.** lesson.py: a practice row named in a part that is not "Alone" (here the whole-class "Judge it")
  gets a screen of its own: the stem, then "Makes sense, or far off?", and its answer on the next screen. Day:
  add "Read each aloud as it shows." If the script change waits, print the three stems on side 2 above the
  judge line (about 70 words; side 2 has about an inch spare and the student count would be about 320 of 350;
  re-run the side count). Process: the gate fails a practice row that is on neither the student page nor a
  display screen.
- **Confidence.** Firm (seen in both built files and the builder).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** script. **REPEAT:** yes (days 091 and 092: the display said the wrong things
  or did not fit the day).

## What is right and should not be touched

- The order: two equations, then one, for the same story; practice; judge by estimate before anyone computes;
  the three register errors fixed beside right working; written task; exit. It is the unit plan's two key
  moments, in its order.
- "A good estimate uses easy numbers from the story, not the answer, and only needs to catch an answer double or
  ten times off." That is the right bar for 4.OA.A.3 at this point, and the exit's "partly" sample ("68 is about
  70") marks exactly the shortcut it rules out.
- One of the three judged answers is right (Ola), and one is too small (Ivy): students cannot pass by calling
  everything far off.
- All one-equation forms multiply or divide first, so no parentheses are needed; order of operations without
  parentheses is grade 3 content (Std. p. 23, footnote 3, per the progression page), and the H fix teaches it.
- Half the problems carry a "times as many" step (1, 2, 3), tying 4.OA.A.3 back to the stretch's comparison work;
  problem 2's divide-first comparison with the B watch ("who is taller?") is well chosen.
- The challenge (write a story for n = 6 × 20 − 45) is on the same standard and judged by sound look-fors.
- The teacher page is plain and quick to read; "Watch for" and "If many" sit in the part they belong to.
