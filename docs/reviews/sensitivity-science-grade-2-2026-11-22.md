# Sensitivity read: science-grade-2, course finish (2026-11-22)

Kind: sensitivity. Target: science-grade-2 (course finish, COURSE-RUN stage 7). Read-only; this report is the only file written.

## Verdict

Pass with fixes. The course keeps disasters calm and empty of people, puts every heated or food item behind sealed displays and photographs, and offers a pass or a photograph route throughout; no finding blocks teaching. The study step fell short of the brief (see "What I studied"), so the disaster and family-engagement judgments rest on five opened pages plus the course's own safeguards, and the lead should decide whether to accept that or run a second pass in a browser.

## What I studied

Opened and read this session:

- NCTSN, Disasters page, https://www.nctsn.org/what-is-child-trauma/trauma-types/disasters : disasters bring displacement, loss of home, hardship and loss of community supports, so a classroom flood image can touch a real loss; the network sorts its guides by hazard (flood, landslide, earthquake), which matches how unit 2.1 sorts fast changes.
- Ready.gov, Floods, https://www.ready.gov/floods : the national emergency body's own tone for families and children is plain and action-first ("Never play in flood water", family plan, grown-ups handle cleanup). It names the fear ("Floods can be scary") and moves straight to what helpers and families do. This supports the course's "stress helpers and safety" rule.
- Ready.gov, Kids, https://www.ready.gov/kids : children's preparedness material is built on games and preparing, not on aftermath.
- USGS, 1980 Cataclysmic Eruption, https://www.usgs.gov/volcanoes/mount-st-helens/1980-cataclysmic-eruption : opened to check the day 12 caption; it describes the sequence over most of one day (8:32 a.m. start, nine-hour main phase). It states no casualty count.
- NWS, Flood safety, https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood : the "Turn around, don't drown" message; it points to flood survivor stories.

## Findings

### Medium

**M1. Day 4 picture: the child who uses a wheelchair only watches.** File: courses/science-grade-2/u01-1/grounds-pictures-day4.md. Quote: "A girl in a wheelchair watches from the paved path." The boy kneels at the water; the wheelchair user is the one child placed as an observer. A child who uses a chair, sitting in the room, would notice. Fix: "A girl in a wheelchair holds a clipboard at the edge of the paved path and points her pencil at the stream; she is part of the group looking." Do not mention the chair in the read-aloud.

**M2. No plan for a child who starts to tell a real disaster story.** Files: courses/science-grade-2/u02-1/opener.md, d011.md, d012.md. Quotes: "do not ask for personal accounts, and let any child pass" (opener); "thank anyone who starts" (d012). The first stops adults from asking; the second does not say what happens next. A child who has lived through a flood may start talking mid-lesson. Fix, one short block in the opener's Safety section: "If a child starts to tell about a real flood, slide or storm, thank them, say 'That sounds important. Let's talk after class,' and keep the lesson moving. Afterward, listen, and tell the school counselor or follow the school's own steps. Do not ask other children to share and do not ask for details." Add once that the class studies how land changes, not what happened to people.

**M3. "No one in any card is hurt" about a real, deadly event.** File: courses/science-grade-2/u02-1/d012.md, Safety and set-up (the opener says "no one is hurt in any card"). Pair 1 is real photographs of the 1980 Mount St. Helens eruption, in which people died (I could not cite a count; see "Could not settle"). The pictures show no person, which is true, but "no one is hurt" reads as a claim about the event, and BASELINE rule 5 says not to soften. Fix: "No card shows a hurt person." Add to day 12 Words and supports: "If a child asks whether anyone was hurt in the eruption, answer plainly and briefly: yes, some people were. Scientists and helpers now watch volcanoes so people can get away in time. Then return to the pictures."

### Low

**L1. Marsh Hill card 3 sets a liking against flood mud.** Files: u02-1/marsh-hill-day13.md, and the Ask in d013.md and key-marsh-hill-day13.md. Quote: "I like the muddy field. It looks like fun." Day 13 is a flood; for a child whose yard or school was flooded, mud is not fun, and the card makes that child's loss the example of "only a liking". Fix: "I like the green field. It looks nice." Update the Ask ("Why is card 3, 'I like the green field,' not evidence?") and the key. It must still be a liking, not a "when".

**L2. Family note for unit 2.1 does not mention disaster pictures.** File: u02-1/family.md. Quote: "like a flood cutting a creek bank". A family that has lost a home to a flood should read that before the child brings a flood picture home, with a way to say so. Fix, after the first paragraph, in English and Spanish: "Some of our pictures show land changed by a flood or a slide. No one is shown hurt, and no child is asked to tell about a real event. If your family has lived through one and you would like your child to sit out those days, tell the teacher."

**L3. Unit 4 family note and day 43 at-home step disagree about cooking.** Files: u04-1/family.md ("Nothing needs to be cooked or heated at home") and u04-2/d043.md ("A grown-up cooks an egg the day before"). The day 43 step also asks a family for an egg and a stove. Fix: lead with the swap: "Draw an egg before and after cooking. If you are cooking one anyway, look at it cooled and wash hands. Not needed."

**L4. Soft food as a stand-in (days 57, 74, 76).** Files: u05-2/d057.md, u07-2/d074.md, u07-2/d076.md. Quote: "Any soft food stands in for the noodle ... do not eat it". Families short on food, and families for whom wasting bread or noodles is a real wrong, may feel pressed. Fix: put the non-food option first: "A strip of cloth, a sponge or a folded paper towel stands in. If you use food, use a scrap you would otherwise throw away, and wash hands after." I read only the Swap lines; I did not check the in-class use of food on those days beyond the materials lists.

**L5. Spanish family notes in units 1 and 2.1 lack accents** (for example "como", "dia", "inundacion", "orilla"), while unit 4.1's note has them. Files: u01-1/family.md, u02-1/family.md. Hand to the Spanish read; meaning is kept.

**L6. Photograph route for a child with an allergy (unit 7).** File: u07-1/schoolyard-photographs-days-66-70.md. The line "say aloud that a photograph is weaker than looking" sits on the record, not on the child, which is good. Add: "Offer the photograph pair to any group that wants to compare it with the outdoor sheet, so the route is not only for the one child."

### Checked and fine
