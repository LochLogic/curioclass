# Sensitivity read: science-grade-1 (course finish), 2026-11-22

Kind: sensitivity. Target: science-grade-1, all eight units, at the course finish (docs/COURSE-RUN.md stage 7). Read-only: this report is the only file written.

## What I studied

Study came before opening any day. Communities and topics: adoptive, foster and separated families (units 3 and 7, frame column 10); families' home lives and optional home participation (units 1, 6, 8); weather harm (unit 2's storm hook); fear of the dark (units 1 and 5); loud sound (unit 4); emergency signals (unit 6).

1. Adoptees United, About (adoptee-led), https://adopteesunited.org/about/ . The community's own voice: an adoptee-rights group whose board covers domestic, intercountry, transracial and former foster youth adoptees. Its own terms are "adoptee", "birth family" and "original birth records". It sees adoptees as a varied group, not one story.
2. Foster Youth in Action, Our Work (led by current and former foster youth), https://www.fosteryouthaction.org/our-work . The community's own voice: the youth-voted People's Agenda puts youth agency, rights and dignity first. It reminded me that a child in care is a person with a voice, not a case to be handled around.
3. Center for Adoption Support and Education, Topical Brief No. 9, Adoption at School (2024), https://adoptionsupport.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/C.A.S.E.-Topical-Brief-No.-9-Adoption-at-School.pdf . This is the key school guidance. It names the hard assignments: family trees, genetics and baby pictures. Adoptees say they get their hardest questions at school. With a young child, "their teacher's silence can be interpreted as disapproval or shame", so teachers should calmly confirm that adoptive families are real and permanent.
4. National Council For Adoption, Additional Resources for Seeking School-Based Support (2022), https://adoptioncouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Resources-for-School-Supports.pdf . It points to inclusive versions of family-tree and other family assignments and to adoption-positive language, and it groups foster care with adoption for school support.
5. National Child Traumatic Stress Network, Teacher Guidelines for Helping Students after a Tornado, https://www.nctsn.org/sites/default/files/resources/teachers_guidelines_talking_students_after_tornadoes.pdf . Families recover at different speeds, and some lose a home, a pet or a loved one. After a storm, children may be more sensitive to wind and loud noises, may "play" the event, and do better with fewer needless reminders.
6. CDC, Helping Children Cope with a Disaster, https://www.cdc.gov/children-and-school-preparedness/before-during-after/helping-children-cope.html . Reactions by age. Answer truthfully in terms a child can follow, limit repeated reminders, and keep routines. A child who lived through a disaster can be upset again by reminders.
7. Ready.gov Kids, https://www.ready.gov/kids . Preparedness for children stays calm and practical ("everyone in the family can help prepare"). The page gives no talk guidance of its own.

Refused automated reads (study sources only, so no taught fact rests on them): https://www.fosterclub.com/real-stories (403); https://www.naeyc.org/resources/topics/family-engagement (403), with https://headstart.gov/family-engagement tried as the one other source (also 403). For a school family-engagement body's guidance I therefore relied on the adoption-competent school guidance (items 3 and 4).

What the sources ask outsiders not to do: assign family-tree, baby-photo or "who do you look like" work; treat birth or biological ties as what makes a "real" family; meet a child's own mention of adoption or foster care with silence or a change of subject that reads as shame; probe; replay damaging weather to a child who lived through it. Where members differ: adoptees vary on terms ("birth family" against "first family") and on how open they want to be. The course avoids human comparison altogether, which sidesteps that disagreement.

## Verdict

Pass with fixes. The course already keeps every comparison to plants and animals, never asks a family for anything (unit 8's reports come from the class or any willing adult), and its unit 3 move, "a caption supplies the parent; nobody works out a parent from looks", fits the adoption guidance well. The one real gap is that no teacher note says what to do when a child brings up their own family, and a few small wordings should change.

## Findings

### Medium

**M1. Units 3 and 7 have no note on what to do when a child brings up their own family.** The limits say what the teacher must not do. u03-1/opener.md: "no child is compared with anyone in their family. Only plants and animals are compared." u07-1/opener.md: "No child is asked about their own family and no human family is compared." In a parent-and-young unit, six-year-olds will say on their own "I look like my mom", "I don't look like my dad" or "I'm adopted". The C.A.S.E. brief warns that silence or a quick change of subject can read as shame to a young child. Fix: add one bullet to the ideas list in both openers (u03-1 and u07-1), and the same sentence to s03 and s07 field 10:

> **If a child talks about their own family.** Listen, then say one warm line: "Families are made in many ways, and every family is a real family." Ask nothing more, compare no one, and go back to the plant or animal on the page. Tell the child privately that they can come and talk to you any time.

This note does not open human comparison. It keeps the existing rule and gives the teacher words for the moment the rule does not cover.

### Low

**L1. Unit 7 exit distractor: "hiding from its parent".** u07-1/cards-exits-56-61.md, the penguin reason cards: "it cannot keep itself warm yet; it is hiding from its parent; it is waiting for a game." A wrong answer about a young animal hiding from its own parent is needless for a child who has had to fear a caregiver, and the other two cards test the same thing. Fix: replace it with "it is looking for a stick" (or any neutral wrong reason), and update the matching key line in the unit 7 key if it quotes the card.

**L2. Unit 2's storm hook needs a no-storm path.** u02-1/d010.md hook: "Show the photograph, trunks standing; say some trees do fall in storms." The fallen tree's root end is already covered, which is good. NCTSN says that after a storm children can be more sensitive to wind, and that needless reminders should be cut. Fix: add to d010's "Before class": "If your community has had a damaging storm recently, show the photograph without the storm words: say 'in a strong wind' and leave out 'some trees do fall in storms'. If a child tells a storm memory, thank them, say they are safe at school now, and go back to the tower." Put the same sentence in s02 field 10 next to "Sensitivity checks storm imagery".

**L3. Unit 7 family note: the Spanish says more than the English.** u07-1/family.md: the Spanish adds "Los animales lo hacen de maneras distintas" and "no se visita ningún nido", which the English lacks. Neither line is harmful, but families who share a household should get the same note in both languages. Fix: add to the English "Animals do it in different ways" after the pattern sentence, and ", and no nest is visited" after "never at children's own families." (The Spanish read can confirm the wording.)

**L4. Name the comfort option for loud sound in unit 4.** NCTSN lists sensitivity to loud noises among children's common reactions after frightening events. Unit 4 keeps every sound at classroom level and lets any child pass, which already covers most of this. Fix (optional, one clause in the u04-1 opener's safety lines): "A child who dislikes a sound may cover their ears or watch from farther away and still answer." This matches unit 5's "sit by the door" option.

### Checked and sound (no change)

- Unit 3 never draws children (cards-d23: "no children drawn"). Parents are given by captions, never read from looks. "Another adult" of the same kind shows that adults vary too. Error B ("a different patch means it cannot be a cat") teaches that a difference does not take you out of your kind, a good message for every child.
- Unit 7 says "the adult" and "the grown-up" throughout. It uses "mother" only where the species' care really is by the mother (alligator, sea otter, bat, elephant seal), which keeps the facts plain (BASELINE rule 5). page-fawn: "A fawn that looks alone is normal. Do not say the adult left because she does not care". This is careful framing for children with a caregiver away. The duck danger episode shows predation without death.
- Family notes for units 1, 3, 7 and 8 all end "There is nothing to send back to school"; the unit 1 moon look is optional. The unit 8 opener ("Families are not asked") goes further than the frame. No "At home" line asks about family, home or evenings.
- Unit 5's dark room: half dim, never dark, door seats open to anyone, exit lit. Unit 1: calm night pictures "some children fear the dark".
- Unit 6: no pattern copies a real emergency signal (stated on every hands-on day).
- Unit 2's design problems are general and "No child is asked to be the example in a picture"; nothing is worn on a body.
- Cards with people show many skin tones and abilities, with no names.
