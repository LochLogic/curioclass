## Findings, most serious first

**1. C7 is missed: the approach says nothing about who holds the model or when it is withdrawn. (Firm.)**
Where: APPROACH, "Kinds of day" and "Practice that returns". Wrong: the five steps are named, but the page never says what
is carried through a unit (shared rule 12; section field 5) or the day the model is taken away, so a section writer has
nothing to follow and the shared core row fails ("No day is named for withdrawing the model"). Why it matters: a rehearsal
skill taught with the teacher's model on every day is not a skill the student owns, and a unit check taken with the steps
card in hand measures nothing. Fix: add a section "What is carried" to APPROACH with these words:
"What is carried in a health unit is the skill's step card: the steps of the unit's one skill, in short words. The teacher
holds the model on the first fluency day (says each step aloud on a scenario). On the second fluency day pairs hold the
card and the teacher only prompts. The card is withdrawn on the first application day, and the section plan names the day.
The unit check is always taken without the card. A skill returns in a later unit with the card for one day, then without
it. K to 2 use the same steps as three or four pictured words said aloud."

**2. No list of what a health day never does; scare tactics, blame and body focus are not barred on the approved page. (Firm.)**
Where: APPROACH. Wrong: the first "Fails if" of the bar (scare tactics), the lens's day-level rules (no shock images, no
blame, no weighing or calorie counting, no good and bad foods, no method details for suicide or self-harm, no medical
advice) and criterion 9's "avoid blame" live in the lens, which says it is "a way of seeing, not a bar", and in STANDARD
5.7 for sensitive units only. A section writer who reads only the approach page will not see them. Why it matters: these are
the rules that protect a student, and non-sensitive units (tobacco, food, safety) are where scare images and "bad food" talk
usually appear. Fix: add a section to APPROACH, "What a health day never does": "No scare tactics, shock images or worst
cases offered as the usual outcome. No blame of a person, a body or a family type for an illness. No weighing, no calorie
counting by students, no 'good' or 'bad' foods; talk of foods that help and foods to have less often. No method
detail on suicide or self-harm, and no lesson on them without a help line and an adult plan. No medical advice and no claim
that a product or practice treats anything. No task asks a student about their own drug use, sexual activity, diet,
weight, mental health, abuse or family. Each unit's teacher page states which of these bites in that unit."

**3. Two map cells contradict the sensitive-unit rule, so some sensitive content is not removable. (Firm.)**
Where: STANDARD section 4 table against section 5.1. (a) MEH, 3 to 5, reads "1 to 9", which includes MEH 6, the outcome
section 5.1 calls suicide and self-harm and places in 6 to 12 only; the cell has no "(s)". K to 2 correctly leaves it out.
(b) Violence prevention, 6 to 8, reads "1 to 7; 4, 8, 9, 10 in a sensitive unit (s)": "1 to 7" includes V4, which is also
listed as sensitive. Why it matters: rule 1 says no sensitive content in a non-sensitive unit; as drawn, a planner would
put MEH 6 in a grade 3 to 5 mental health unit that a school cannot leave out, or V4 in the non-sensitive violence unit.
Fix: MEH, 3 to 5: "1 to 5, 7 to 9" (MEH 6 not taught; a grade 3 to 5 unit teaches getting help for sad or worried feelings
under MEH 5 or 7 only if the outcome fits, see below). V, 6 to 8: "1 to 3, 5 to 7; 4, 8, 9, 10 in sensitive units (s)". Also
add to section 4 a one-line key: "A number listed twice in a cell means it is taught only in the sensitive unit." I cannot
check which MEH outcome is help-seeking without the wording (finding 10), so the planner confirms against the appendix
before fixing "5, 7". Same check for PHW, 6 to 8, "All but 12": the page gives no reason for dropping PHW 12 and none for
where it goes; add the reason or put it in 9 to 12 only with a note.

**4. The one-semester high school course cannot hold the map as drawn, and "years within a band" does not fit it; no per-grade time anywhere. (Firm on the mismatch, likely on the numbers.)**
Where: STANDARD 4 ("Each year of a band touches all eight standards"; 9 to 12 column "All" for nine topics); APPROACH
"Pacing". Wrong: Cory settled high school health as one semester, but section 4 describes a band of years and gives the 9 to
12 column every outcome of every topic with skills returning "at more depth". A semester is about 80 days; it cannot teach
all 81 outcomes plus sensitive units plus the eight standards twice over. For K to 8 the page says "often one session a
week", and "each band frame states its sessions". Neither the shared bar (C2: minutes per lesson, days add up) nor the
progression writers can start without a number per grade, and nothing says how a three-grade band splits into grades
(which topics go in which grade; the rule is only "each indicator claimed in at least one year"). Fix: (a) Add to STANDARD
4: "Bands K to 2, 3 to 5 and 6 to 8 are three grade years each; the scope and sequence names the units of each grade and
claims each topic at least in alternate years. Band 9 to 12 is one course of one semester: it teaches in full the outcomes
not already met by grade 8 and revisits the rest in a short practice unit." (b) Mark each 9 to 12 outcome that 6 to 8 already
teaches as "revisit" in the map (settled below). (c) Set default time, ours and untested: K to 5 about 30 sessions a year
of 30 minutes; 6 to 8 about 45 sessions a year of 45 minutes; 9 to 12 about 80 days of 45 minutes. Each band frame may change
these with a reason. If the 9 to 12 map cannot fit even then, the planner reports back instead of dropping outcomes
quietly (For Cory, last item).

**5. Two things the approach asks of students conflict with the lens and could harm. (Firm.)**
Where: APPROACH Application ("the student's own life: a goal plan") and Fluency ("scripted rehearsals in pairs: a refusal").
Wrong: (a) the lens bars any task asking a student about their own diet or weight, and SHAPE's goal-setting indicators
(standard 6) invite a personal goal; in a food unit that becomes a diet goal. APPROACH's line "personal plans stay with the
student" protects disclosure, not content. (b) Pair rehearsal of refusal in sexual health, body safety or dating violence
units could have one child playing a pressuring person. Why it matters: a goal on eating or body shape can start or feed a
disorder; role-play of pressure in these topics can embarrass or re-trigger. Fix: add under "Supports": "A personal goal is
chosen from a short, safe list (sleep, water, handwashing, time outside, screen breaks, a kind act, asking for help) and
never about weight, body shape, food restriction or calories; a character's goal is always an option. In sensitive
units, rehearsal uses written scripts of the refusal line only. The teacher or a card plays the pressure; no student plays
a person who pressures, abuses or threatens."

**6. No plan for a student who tells the teacher something, and no teacher bias note. (Firm on bias, likely on disclosure.)**
Where: APPROACH "Supports". Wrong: criterion 9 asks for guidance on the teacher's own bias and the lens says "teachers get a
note"; APPROACH has neither. Health lessons are where children tell adults about harm, and a parent at home teaching one child
has no school office. The lens wants a "help line and adult plan" for suicide and self-harm lessons, but the plan is not
written for the other sensitive units. Why it matters: a missing plan makes the lesson unsafe and the leave-out note
dishonest. Fix: add two bullets: "Every unit's teacher page has a short note on the teacher's own views and
assumptions the topic may touch (for example family structure, food, bodies, faith) and one way to keep them out of
the room." and "Every sensitive unit's teacher page says what to do if a student tells you something: listen, do not promise
to keep it secret, follow the school's duty to report, and tell the student who the next adult is. For a parent at home it names
the help lines for abuse and for a crisis (the national child abuse hotline and the national crisis line, 988) and says that
a child's disclosure is not handled alone." Public copy names the lines by number and purpose only.

**7. Supports: C10 is part met and C11 is missed. (Firm.)**
Where: APPROACH "Supports". Wrong: it has varied scenarios, take part without sharing, and where to find help. It has none
of the three hooks (unaided check, English learner support, access), no extension on the same standard, no reading load and no
guidance for disabilities, and does not distinguish a language gap from a content gap. Health has special load: medical words
taught in depth, refusal scripts that need frames, role play that needs a way in for a child who does not speak or
cannot move. Fix: add: "English learners: each unit teaches six to ten health words in depth, with correct body part names
from the start, and gives sentence frames for each of its skill steps (for example 'I won't do that. I'm going to ...'). A key line says: if a
student says the right idea in another language or points, the gap is language; if a student gives a wrong idea, the gap is
content. Access: any rehearsal can be done in writing, by pointing at a card, by a tool, or with an adult; no student must
speak aloud. Reading load: students read at most a short scenario alone; the rest is read aloud or pictured. Extension: the
same skill on a harder or less familiar scenario, or a skill returned in another topic, never the next grade's standard."

**8. C13 and C16 are part met: no sources on the approach page, one uncited research claim, no evidence statement. (Firm.)**
Where: APPROACH whole page, especially "Understanding" ("what peers really do (norms, often lower than students guess)").
Wrong: a research claim stands with no source, and the lens itself says norm misperception rests on memory. There is no
"Sources" section stating the HECAT chapters 4 and 5 read (expert, in full) and what was not read. There is no statement that
naming behaviours a unit is for is an aim, not a result, and no pointer to the course's one-page logic model. Why it
matters: "research cited that nobody read" and "any claim of effect" are fails in C13 and C16. Fix: (a) rewrite the clause:
"what peers really do (a unit uses a current national survey figure for its age group, with its source and year; that
students tend to overestimate risk behaviour is from memory and not yet read)". (b) Add "Sources and what was read":
HECAT 2021 chapters 4 and 5 (the bar's reading, in full), Appendix 3 and 5, Sexual Health module in part (STANDARD 3);
everything else from memory and marked. (c) Add: "Curio Class names the behaviours a unit is for. It does not claim students
will change them, and it has not been tried with children. Each course has a one-page logic model (C16)."

**9. Criterion 7's reader is a paragraph, not yet a job. (Likely.)**

**11. "Valuing" has no place, and the unit plan's marking steps do not fit health. (Likely.)**

page covers doing (the skill) and knowing (the facts day) and ends an understanding day in "a decision or a belief", but never

**12. Family link: no rule that every unit sends something home, and the sensitive-unit note differs from unit field 9. (Likely.)**
