# Review: grade 6 ELA year spine (docs/courses/ela-6-spine.md)

Curriculum expert, cold read, 2026-11-11. Read first: EXPERT.md, ROADMAP "The path from here" through "Gaps found", ADOPTION-BAR.md, the grade 6 standard text, sequence.csv, modules.csv, the two shape files, LIBRARY-PLAN.md, the three library lists, the math review, and the spine. I also measured the on-file texts in library/ (word counts and a rough Flesch-Kincaid grade, my own script, good to about one grade either way). Outside sources are listed at the end with what each was used for and what could not be read.

## Verdict

The spine is sound in its design: topic stretches built on text sets, reading and writing taught through the texts, argument read before it is written, discussion all year, a real writing calendar, and an honest list of what is missing. The biggest risk is the texts, not the order: three of six text sets do not exist, the grade 6 shelf holds no informational text except four memoirs, the year has one full-length book, and the plan for "read whole" does not fit the minutes. The one change that matters most is to settle the text plan before any unit is written: a named, verified, licensed set for each stretch, with a stated reading load per day, drawing on United States government works and open licences for the modern and wider voices the public domain shelf cannot give.

## The rubric to file

There was no ELA rubric on file. The right one is the EdReports Core Content Review Criteria v2.0, English Language Arts, Grades 6 to 12, with its Evidence Guide v2.0 (updated 7/2025). I read the evidence guide in full text (extracted from the PDF). Links are in Sources. Both are marked "© EdReports, All Rights Reserved", so the file should be a summary in our own words with links, as RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md is. Suggested name: docs/RUBRIC-ELA-6-12.md. EdReports also publishes a companion for multilingual learners (MLL ELA 6 to 12, v2.0); file a summary of that beside it for the English learner hook. I did not read the companion.

What the spine should know about it:

- Grades 6 to 12 are reviewed on Gateway 2 (texts, questions, tasks: indicators 2a to 2v) and Gateway 3 (teacher and student supports). There is no foundational skills gateway at this band.
- The 55 informational to 45 literary balance (2a) and the 35, 35, 30 writing mix (2o) are "narrative only": reported, not scored. The earlier tool scored the writing mix, which is why CommonLit 360 lost a point there. The spine treats both as hard lines; they are not.
- Texts by authors of varied backgrounds and teacher guidance on hard topics (2e) is also narrative evidence only. It will be written about in any report, but it carries no points.
- Scored, and where this spine is most exposed: 2a anchor texts that are well made, rich and engaging; 2b a complexity analysis and a placement reason for every anchor; 2c scaffolds before, during and after each anchor, matched to what makes it hard; 2d text sets on a topic; 2f pacing that fits a school year; 2k to 2n vocabulary and sentence work taught inside the texts; 2s and 2t research; 2u and 2v checks with next steps and a culminating task per unit.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The texts: half the year has none, the shelf has no informational text, and range is narrow

**What is at risk.** Stretches 3, 5 and 6 (70 of 142 unit days) have no text on file. Of the 157 grade 6 pieces in stories-pd.csv, none is informational except four memoirs; 13 are Hardy Boys and Nancy Drew; 106 are from the United States or England. About 19 are by authors of color (Zitkala-Sa 8, Hughes 4, J. W. Johnson 2, Eastman, Tagore, Mukerji, Ozaki 2). Nothing a sixth grader reads in the anchors was written in their lifetime.

**Two corrections to the spine first.** (a) It says every text is pre-1930. Not so: speeches.csv holds FDR, Kennedy, Johnson, Reagan and the Apollo 11 transcript, several tagged for grade 6, and stories-pd.csv holds 1950s stories by Bradbury and Asimov (never renewed). The spine overlooked these. (b) It says three of six stretches need texts. With second texts counted, all six do.

**Evidence.** Evidence guide 2a, 2d, 2e. The Publishers' Criteria ask for works that span "many genres, cultures, and eras" and, in grades 6 to 12, for literary nonfiction built on informational structures (arguments, science, journalism) more than nonfiction told as story. The spine's nonfiction is mostly story-shaped (journals, eyewitness accounts, memoir). Against that: old texts are not a mark against a course in themselves. EL Education grade 6 teaches Zitkala-Sa's "The Land of Red Apples" and "The Cutting of My Long Hair", an 1892 conference speech and the 1928 Meriam Report in its boarding schools module, and lists retold Greek myths at 870L to 1170L (its curriculum map). Appendix B's grade 6 to 8 examples include texts already on file (Little Women, Tom Sawyer, Paul Revere's Ride, O Captain, Jabberwocky, I, Too; from memory). Confidence: firm on the gap, likely on what a reviewer would write.

**Cost.** A teacher cannot start stretch 3. A reviewer's narrative would say the year is one era and largely one tradition, and a class would feel it.

**Change: a text plan per stretch, hosted texts first.** Candidates I verified exist and are public domain are marked (v). "From memory" means check before use.

- **The Conversation**: CC BY-ND 4.0. Text may not be edited, and online republishing must carry their page-view counter. That counter conflicts with the no-tracking promise, so use it in print only, or link.

### 2. Volume of reading: "read whole" does not fit, and there is one long book

**What is wrong.** My counts of the on-file texts, at 150 words a minute (my assumption for a mid-range sixth grader):

| Text | Words | Reading time | Days the spine gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colum, Adventures of Odysseus | 54,200 | 6.0 hours | 14 literature days of 35 minutes: 8.2 hours for everything |
| Church, Story of the Odyssey | 45,100 | 5.0 hours | same |
| The Call of the Wild | 31,800 | 3.5 hours | 15 days: fits, about 14 minutes a day |
| Treasure Island | 68,400 | 7.6 hours | does not fit 24 days |
| Keller, The Story of My Life | 36,800 | 4.1 hours | 7 days: excerpts only |

"One hero cycle read whole" is not possible in class. The anchor reading for the year comes to roughly 100,000 words (my estimate), about 11 of 125 hours. The spine has one long-form text by the evidence guide's definition (full-length books, novels, plays).

**Evidence.** Publishers' Criteria: "several longer texts each year". EL Education has four anchor books and Fishtank six; both have students read anchor chapters outside the lesson (EL: chapters pre-read at home plus 20 minutes on other nights; Fishtank: 30 to 40 minutes of reading beyond its 60-minute lesson). CommonLit 360 has one novel and still met expectations, so one is a floor a reviewer accepts, not a failure. Evidence guide 2a asks for an independent reading system with teacher guidance and tracking; the spine has the log and book talks, which is right. Confidence: firm on the arithmetic, likely on the comparison.

**Change.**
- Each stretch states its reading load: words per day, in class and at home, and a path for a class that cannot count on homework (shared reading with the audio, fewer episodes).
- Stretch 2: pick about 18,000 words of connected episodes and call it that. Do not say "whole".
- Choose The Call of the Wild over Treasure Island on length alone. Plan the novel by chapter (seven chapters), with the lens moving as the book moves, so units 4.2 and 4.3 do not stall the reading for eight days.
- Read one more text whole: Zitkala-Sa's "School Days" in stretch 7.
- Name one on-topic long book per stretch from the shelf for independent reading (Kingsley's Heroes, Twenty Thousand Leagues, White Fang, Indian Boyhood, Daddy-Long-Legs), so volume builds knowledge too.
- A judgment call: replace three 10-minute reading openers with one 20-minute sustained block a week with teacher conferences. Ten-minute bursts lose minutes to settling and do not build stamina; the EL Education report was criticised on stamina for the same reason. Reasonable reviewers differ.

### 3. The checks: two collide with the end checks, and one period is not enough

**What is wrong.** The math lesson (checks at the stretch, respond days) transferred well in principle. It was misapplied in two ways. (a) Three cumulative checks fall straight after an end-of-stretch check: days 52 then 53, 126 then 127, 146 then 149. That is the same class tested twice in two or three days on overlapping standards. In math the stretches are short and many; here there are six, so the two kinds of check land on each other. (b) Twenty minutes of cold reading plus thirty of writing from the text does not fit a 50-minute period that also has to be handed out and collected. The spine doubts this itself.

**Evidence.** Fishtank gives two assessment days per unit (10 of 145). EL Education gives six assessments per module, each a lesson. Evidence guide 2u and 2v ask for next steps and for a culminating task per unit that joins reading, writing and speaking. Confidence: firm on the collision, likely on the time.

**Change.** Where a cumulative check follows a stretch, it is that stretch's end check. Each cumulative check takes two periods (reading and questions; then writing from the same texts), then a respond day. Stretches 2 and 5 keep their half-period end checks. Total: 16 days, against 15 now, with no double testing. The year no longer ends on a test. Add a one-minute oral reading check for each student in the first two weeks, during independent reading, with no extra day (finding 6). The start-of-year check stays.

### 4. Stretch 5: the debate asks half the class to defend child labor, and the lead text is too hard

**What is wrong.** "The argument over child labor, about 1900 to 1920" is settled. A debate (unit 5.9) needs a question with two defensible sides; here one side is indefensible, and the written argument can only agree with the sources. The Kelley speech (v: 22 July 1905, Philadelphia) is a double argument, child labor in the service of votes for women, and is used on advanced high school rhetoric exams (from memory). It is the wrong first text for RI.6.8.

**Evidence.** RI.6.8 and SL.6.3 ask students to tell supported claims from unsupported ones, which needs texts that contain both. W.6.1 needs a claim a reader could reject. Confidence: likely.

**Change.** Keep the topic; it is right for this age and gives the year its history. Keep the 1900s texts for tracing arguments, and include one text from the other side (a mill owner's statement from the 1916 congressional hearings; government work, from memory). Move the written argument and the debate to a live question the same knowledge serves, with current government sources: for example "Should twelve-year-olds be allowed to work for pay, and at what?" (farm work rules, child performers, recent state law changes). For unit 5.4 use a recorded speech so SL.6.3 is real listening: an FDR fireside chat on the 1938 wages and hours law (audio at the FDR Library, from memory), or, from the shelf today, Kennedy's moon speech, which has public domain film. If the texts cannot be verified, the fallback is to pair stretches 5 and 6 on one topic (research the earth's hazards, then argue "should people rebuild where disaster strikes"), which also gives a second 46-day knowledge run like stretches 3 and 4.

### 5. The six thin standards: one is misread, and four have honest answers

- **RI.6.7 is not thin.** The spine says it needs audio or video. It does not. It asks students to integrate information in different formats, "visually, quantitatively", with words. Hine's photographs beside a table and an article is the standard in full. EL Education assesses RI.6.7 with a Zitkala-Sa chapter and photographs. Remove it from the thin list. Firm.
- **RL.6.7.** Use LibriVox readings (public domain) for the novel and the myth, and a class performance of the play scene as the "live version". No teacher has to hunt for media. A synthetic voice is a weak partner for this standard; a human reading is the point. Likely.
- **RL.6.10, drama.** One scene is thin on paper, but the three outside programs I checked have no play in grade 6 either. Write the stretch 2 scene as a labeled Curio Class retelling (the library plan's route 5) and have it performed. If days are ever added, read one short published play whole after the last check. Candidates to verify: Tagore's The Post Office (1914 translation), Stuart Walker's Six Who Pass While the Lentils Boil (1917). Judgment call.
- **W.6.6.** Two days at day 121 cannot build "three pages in a single sitting". Run it all year: every process piece is typed where the school has devices, with a stamina ladder (one page by stretch 3, two by stretch 5, three in stretch 6). Each unit says plainly "needs a device with a word processor on these days" and gives a paper path that does not claim the standard. Collaboration is peer comments in whatever the school uses. A no-account writing pad that saves to the student's own device would help; nothing needs hosting. Evidence guide 2q asks for "multiple" chances to publish with technology. Likely.
- **SL.6.5.** "Graphics, images" and "visual displays" count. A map, a timeline or a printed photograph in a talk meets it. Add a second home: a two-minute expedition talk with a map in stretch 3. Firm.
- **W.6.8, digital sources.** Make two or three of the six to eight research sources live pages on stable government sites, listed on one Curio Class page: a fenced set. Students judge real authors, dates and addresses without open search. Include one weak source on purpose (a 1906 rumor-filled news report, an advertisement), or credibility has nothing to bite on. Search terms are grade 7 (W.7.8), so none are needed. Likely.

### 6. Complexity is misjudged in one direction, and supports for struggling readers are unplanned

**What is wrong.** The spine says the year "opens on text every student can enter" and climbs to the top of the band. My measure of the on-file texts says otherwise:

| Text | Flesch-Kincaid (band 6 to 8 is 6.51 to 10.34) | Words per sentence | Known Lexile (band is 925L to 1185L) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colum, Odysseus | 8.7 | 23.5 | |
| Hawthorne, Gorgon's Head; Minotaur | 8.9; 9.8 | 20 to 23 | |
| Kingsley, Perseus | 9.3 | 25.6 | |
| Baldwin, Old Greek Stories II | 7.4 | 20.6 | |
| The Call of the Wild | 7.9 | 18.7 | 1120L (1080L on one list) |
| Keller, Story of My Life | 9.2 | 20.6 | 1150L (one retailer, unverified) |
| Zitkala-Sa, School Days | 7.1 | 16.2 | 900L and 1040L for two chapters (EL Education map) |
| Reagan, Challenger address | 6.2 | 13.8 | |

The myths are in the middle to upper band, with long, old-fashioned sentences. The year opens on its hardest prose, not its easiest. A reviewer will not fault the complexity: EL Education's anchors are 680L, 850L and 740L, below the band, and it met expectations on qualitative grounds. The risk here is the reverse, access (2c), and the spine plans no scaffolds and nothing for students with gaps in word reading.

**Evidence.** Appendix A supplement (band ranges, confirmed). Evidence guide 2b (order need not climb by Lexile; every anchor needs an analysis and a placement reason) and 2c. What Works Clearinghouse guide for grades 4 to 9: teach multisyllable word reading, build fluency on purpose, use comprehension routines, give practice with stretch text (strong, strong, strong, moderate). The adolescent literacy guide: explicit vocabulary and comprehension teaching (strong), extended discussion of text (moderate), intensive help from specialists for struggling readers (strong). Confidence: firm.

**Change.**
- Drop the claim that the texts climb. Say each anchor sits in the band and why, in its text record. Write the records for the stretch 2 and 4 anchors now; they are the cheapest evidence the course can produce.
- Open stretch 2 with a Baldwin story, then Colum. Easier to harder inside a set is what the Student Achievement Partners text set guidance asks for.
- Every anchor gets scaffolds before, during and after, matched to what makes it hard (for these texts: sentence length, old vocabulary, unfamiliar names). The Enrich step's word help is the "during". Add a "long sentence" routine: one sentence a day taken apart and rebuilt. This also serves English learners and is the sentence work indicator 2m asks for.
- Fluency inside the course: partner rereading of a short anchor passage weekly, the play scene, the poems, and the read-aloud in 7.6 moved earlier and made routine. A morphology strand in the vocabulary opener (finding 7) is the multisyllable word work.
- Audio beside the text for access, with the rule already in the spine for checks.
- Say plainly on the course page that this is a core course, not an intervention, and that the one-minute reading check in week 1 is there to flag students who need more than the course can give.
- English learner supports per stretch, not only per unit: the stretch's word list with Word Wall cards, sentence frames for the seminar and for each writing type, a home-language note where StoryWeaver has a parallel text.
- L.6.1e (variations from standard English) needs care. The Call of the Wild writes French-Canadian speech in dialect, and many students speak a home variety of English. Teach it as choosing language for an audience, never as correcting how a family talks.

### 7. Language and vocabulary: mostly right, with three fixes

**What is right.** Openers on a sentence from the anchor text are what 2m describes (exemplar sentences from core texts). Wit and Wisdom gives 15 minutes of every lesson to vocabulary or style and conventions, so the time is in range.

**What to change.**
- Ten whole language days stand apart from the reading. Unit 2.4 stops the myth for two days of pronouns; unit 6.5 is a lone spelling day. Writing Next found grammar taught in isolation had a negative effect on writing, and sentence combining a positive one (from memory). Move pronouns into the revise days of 2.8, working on the students' own drafts; fold spelling into the editing step of the report; name sentence combining and sentence imitation as the opener's method. Likely.
- About 20 words a stretch is 120 a year. That is thin. Common guidance is nearer 400 words a year taught on purpose (Beck and McKeown, from memory). Raise to 8 to 10 words a week from the texts, with the returns the spine already plans. Likely.
- Start Greek and Latin roots (L.6.4b) in stretch 2, not stretch 3. The myths are their natural home: odyssey, mentor, titanic, echo, panic, chronic. Teach figurative language (L.6.5a names personification) at first meeting in the myths too, not first on day 61. Likely.

### 8. Structure, unit of record, pilot

**Six topics or fewer.** EL Education and Wit and Wisdom run four modules, Fishtank five units of 27 to 32 days, CommonLit six of five to seven weeks. The spine's 18 to 24 days is shorter than all of them, and its 35-minute lesson gives each topic about 14 hours against roughly 27 to 32 in Fishtank. Fewer and longer would build more knowledge. But six lets each writing type go through the full process twice, and stretches 3 and 4 already work as one 48-day run (polar journeys, then the Klondike). My call: keep six, say on the spine that 3 and 4 are a pair with one word list carried through, and treat pairing 5 and 6 as the fallback in finding 4. Judgment call.

**55 units.** Four are one day long (4.5, 6.3, 6.5, 6.9). Merge to about 45: 6.3 into 6.4, 6.5 into 6.7, 4.5 into 4.2, and drop the end-check units that the cumulative checks replace.

**Unit of record.** Keep the small units as rows in data/units.csv, so "unit days" means the same thing in every subject and work ships in small pieces. Add a stretch column and a small stretch record that owns the text set, the word list, the culminating task and the checks. Two rules: an ELA unit's days count as live only when its stretch's text set is live, and the public course page offers the stretch, not the unit, as the thing a teacher picks up. A reviewer reads "unit" as the stretch (2d, 2v). Likely.

**Pilot.** Unit 2.5 is a fair choice of standard: summary has some of the strongest evidence in writing research. As drafted it has two problems. It cannot be read without the eleven days before it, and it leans on Theme Evidence Ladder, a band tool whose spec is not fixed. Make the pilot a five-day slice that stands alone on one connected episode of about 6,000 words (Colum's Cyclops through Circe): read for plot, find the theme, write the summary, a discussion, an end check on a new short myth. Build it on the two fixed-spec tools (Plot Rollercoaster, Summary Squeeze) and add the Ladder when its grade 6 path is fixed. It must ship with what an ELA unit is really made of: the text record, the question set, the word list, the scaffolds. Likely.

### 9. Sensitivity in the named texts

A grade 6 teacher would raise these on first read. Each needs the fresh sensitivity read the spine promises, and a teacher note.

- **The Call of the Wild.** Beyond animal violence: the "Yeehats", an invented Native people shown as savages and killed in the last chapter; "half-breed" used of two main characters; the law of "club and fang". Many schools teach it in grade 7 or 8. Keep it only if the read passes it with a note; the fallback on file is Gay-Neck (Mukerji, 37,900 words, Newbery 1928, an Indian author), which I have not read closely.
- **Henson.** The title's period term; "Esquimo"; Peary's treatment of the Inughuit. Accuracy: historians dispute that the party reached the Pole, and the unit must say so.
- **Scott and Shackleton.** The deaths of Scott's party, one man walking out to die; dogs and a cat killed.
- **Keller.** Chapter 2's account of a Black servant's child she ruled over; period words for disability; Sullivan's letters describe physical struggle. Her "Frost King" chapter is a ready-made text on plagiarism for W.6.8.
- **Colum.** The killing of the suitors; enslaved people in the household.
- **Mother Jones, Hine.** Injured children; a partisan voice, which is fine if taught as a voice.
- **Topics.** Some students have lived through a disaster, and some work. Both stretches need a line for the teacher.
- **Zitkala-Sa.** Hard history, but taught in grade 6 by a well-reviewed program. Do not hold it back; do not use it as a cold-read check text.

### 10. Year size and daily shape

150 days and 50 minutes are defensible. Fishtank plans 145 days of 60 minutes and leaves the rest of 180 open on purpose. EL Education lessons are 45 minutes. Wit and Wisdom is reported at 90. Keep both numbers. Confidence: firm.

The shape needs one honest sentence the spine lacks: at 45 to 60 minutes, the outside programs get their reading done outside the lesson. This course must either do the same or read less. That is finding 2.

### 11. Smaller points

- Publishers' Criteria: 80 to 90 percent of the reading standards need text-dependent work. The daily exit question should always be about the text.
- The culminating task of stretch 2 is a story. Add a short evidence paragraph on theme so the stretch also ends on analysis.
- The reading shelf's "short story" tag covers whole novels in chapter sets (Verne, Doyle, the Hardy Boys). Fix the kinds before counts are quoted.
- Reagan's Challenger address (647 words, on file, about grade 6) and the Apollo 11 transcript are ready cold-read texts for checks.
- Outside sixth-grade programs put a first literary argument before the full argument, as this spine does (EL Education modules 3 and 4).

## The five departures from sequence.csv

All five are right.

1. **Discussion first and all year.** Right. The adolescent literacy guide recommends extended discussion of text; indicators 2i and 2j ask for it "frequently"; EL Education's first unit ends in a graded text discussion. Firm.
2. **Tracing an argument before writing one, with a small literary argument first.** Right, and the same order as EL Education. The writing guide recommends joining reading and writing (moderate). Firm.
3. **Connotation into the novel.** Right; EL Education assesses word choice and point of view together in its first novel. Begin figurative language earlier (finding 7). Likely.
4. **Pronouns in stretch 2, sentence variety later.** Right. It does show the module holds two topics; leave the module as it is. Likely.
5. **Band modules woven in.** Right, and unavoidable.

sequence.csv should be reordered for grade 6 to match once the spine is approved, or carry a note that the spine governs.

## What is right and should not be touched

- Topic stretches with text sets, and the plain statement that the texts, questions and writing are the course and the tools are a tenth of it.
- The topics. Greek myth and the hero's journey are grade 6 topics in EL Education and Wit and Wisdom; Wit and Wisdom also teaches Shackleton in grade 6; CommonLit runs an ocean research unit. Disasters fit middle school earth science.
- The link from stretch 3 to stretch 4. It is the best knowledge-building move in the draft.
- Keller beside Sullivan for RI.6.9. It is the standard's own example.
- The writing calendar: thirteen pieces, two process pieces of each type, three research projects, on-demand writing inside every check. The mix is inside what the criteria describe.
- "The limits of grade 6". Each limit is correct to the standards.
- "Where earlier work comes back", and the daily written exit question.
