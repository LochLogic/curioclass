# Review: grade 4 science year spine (docs/courses/science-4-spine.md)

Curriculum expert, cold read, 2026-11-11. Read first: EXPERT.md, ROADMAP "The path from here" through "Gaps found", ADOPTION-BAR.md, BASELINE 20 to 22, the spine, the shape file, the science,4 rows of sequence.csv and modules.csv, ngss-pes.txt, and the math spine review for house style. Outside sources are listed at the end with what each was used for and what could not be read. Four things the drafting agent could not read were read for this review: the grade 4 evidence statements (all 14), the EQuIP rubric, the EdReports K-5 science evidence guide, and OpenSciEd unit 4.2.

## Verdict

The spine is sound in its bones: the year size matches the fullest open program almost to the minute, energy first is right, the three design problems sit where the standards themselves tie engineering to content, the limits column is accurate, and the end tasks use new phenomena, which is what the rubric scores. The biggest risk is the unit that holds life science and light: it gets 15 percent of the year where four outside programs give it 21 to 33 percent, its anchor is a scene and not a phenomenon, and its end task has no plant in it, so 4-LS1-1 is not met. The one change that matters most is to move energy resources (4-ESS3-1) up beside the energy and device stretch, where all four outside programs put it, and spend the days that frees, plus a few from the Earth block, on a 21-day life and light stretch with a second anchor for plants (full revised sequence in finding 3).

## The rubric question, answered

File two, as with math.

- **EdReports Science K-5 Core Content Review Criteria v2.0 and its Evidence Guide v2.0 (updated 07/2025).** This is the course-level adoption rubric. Three gateways: designed for NGSS (34 points: phenomena and problems drive learning, three-dimensional learning and assessment), coherence and scope (34 points: every core idea, practice and crosscutting concept, accuracy, nothing outside the band, nature of science, tasks that grow harder), teacher and student supports. Licence: copyright EdReports.org, all rights reserved, so summarise in our own words and link, exactly as docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md does. Suggested name: docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE-K5.md.
- **EQuIP Rubric for Lessons and Units: Science, version 3.0 (2016).** This is the unit-level tool, the right one for reading a pilot stretch. Three categories: three-dimensional design, instructional supports, monitoring student progress; category one must score 2 of 3 or the review stops. Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported; educators may use or adapt, with attribution and a new title if changed. It can be filed more fully than the EdReports tools.

Three facts from the evidence guide change how the spine should read. (1) Practices are scored at two grain sizes: every practice the grade's own expectations name must be used many times in different contexts within the grade (2b.i), while all eight practices and all seven crosscutting concepts are owed across grades 3 to 5, not inside grade 4 (2b.ii, 2c). (2) Counting is by element (the bulleted statements under each practice, core idea and concept), not by name. (3) Time is not scored: indicator 3i is narrative only and asks for pacing, a reason for the order, and guidance on what to cut with the trade-offs named. Confidence: firm.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Energy resources sit 60 days from the ideas they need, and make stretch 8 two topics joined by "then"

**What is at risk.** 4-ESS3-1 is five days of "read, combine, write" at the end of a stretch anchored on a flood. The flood does not drive it, so it is a research report bolted on. This is the clearest place where the spine treats a performance expectation as an assignment.

**Evidence.** All four programs read place it with energy: OpenSciEd unit 4.2 (electricity) carries 4-PS3-2, 4-PS3-4, 4-PS4-3, 4-ESS3-1 and two engineering expectations; Amplify Science puts the natural resources core idea in Energy Conversions; Mystery Science ends its energy unit with it; FOSS puts it in its Energy module. The core idea text ties electric current to "produced to begin with" from motion, which is the bridge from a circuit to a power source. The evidence guide (1e) marks down learning that a phenomenon bookends but does not drive. Confidence: firm on what programs do, likely on the move.

**Cost.** Students write about power plants two months after their last circuit, and stretch 8 loses focus as the year's capstone design.

**Change.** Move it to the end of stretch 2 as lesson set 2.7, four days, driven by a question the device work raises: "Our device ran on a battery or a stretched band. Where does the current in the classroom wall come from, and what does each source cost the land?" The spaced return to energy that the spine wanted is kept by Checks 2 to 4 and by the life stretch (light as energy) and the hazard stretch (energy of moving water).

### 2. Life science and light are thin, the anchor is not a phenomenon, and plants are not assessed

**What is wrong.** Stretch 4 has 16 days for three expectations. 4-LS1-1 alone asks for plants and animals, internal and external structures, four functions, structures working together as a system, and an argument that weighs evidence; it gets five days (4.4) shared between the human body and plants. The anchor (hawk, owl and mouse in a field) is a picture, cannot be observed or tested, and gives away its own answer in its last clause. The end task (a bat or a mole) has no plant, and claims "light and seeing" for animals that mostly do not use sight.

**Evidence.** Share of content time for the unit holding 4-LS1-1, 4-LS1-2 and 4-PS4-2: spine 17 percent (16 of 95 days); OpenSciEd 24 percent (1,170 of 4,970 minutes); Amplify Science 25 percent (one of four equal units); Mystery Science 21 percent (4 of 19 lessons); FOSS a third (one of three modules). The evidence statement for 4-LS1-1 requires plants, a system claim, and judging whether evidence is relevant and sufficient. The statement for 4-LS1-2 includes memories guiding action, which the ruler-drop test does not reach. The statement for 4-PS4-2 asks students to change the light path (mirrors, barriers) and say what happens. OpenSciEd re-anchors on plants part way through its unit. Confidence: firm.

**Cost.** A reviewer scoring life science core ideas (2a.ii) finds one of two expectations half taught and not assessed.

**Change.** 21 days, two anchors, in the same module (no split):

- **Anchor A (replaces the field scene):** "A cat crosses the yard at night. Its eyes shine in a flashlight or a photo flash, it walks where we would stumble, and it bolts at a sound we did not hear. Can it see in complete darkness?" It is observable (a photo or clip, or a pet at home), puzzling (many children think eyes glow by themselves and that cats see with no light), testable with the shoebox the spine already has, and it drives all three expectations. FOSS, Amplify Science, Mystery Science and OpenSciEd all anchor this content on vision in low light or a night animal.
- **Anchor B (new, for plants):** "A plant cannot walk to water, run from what eats it or carry its young. Yet a cut white flower stood in coloured water has coloured petals by morning, and one dandelion seeds a field." Celery or a white flower works in any season and costs a dollar.
- **Lesson sets:** first models 2; light, the dark box and paths with mirrors 4; sense, process, remember, respond 4 (add a learned-signal activity for memory); animal structures as a system 4; plant structures 5; end task and revision 2.
- **End task:** one animal and one plant not studied, each with a data card, plus a short separate item on seeing an object round a corner with a mirror.

### 4. Electric current is one station among four, and the contraption repeats stretch 1

**What is at risk.** Three of the core idea statements under 4-PS3 are about electric current, and the evidence statement for 4-PS3-4 has students name the starting and ending form of energy and the part that converts it. In the spine, current is one of four stations in 2.2. A chain-reaction contraption is mostly objects hitting objects, which is stretch 1 again, and a fair test with one variable changed is hard to run on a many-step machine.

**Evidence.** OpenSciEd gives electricity a whole unit and FOSS opens its Energy module with circuits; Mystery Science does use a chain-reaction machine for 4-PS3-4, so the contraption has precedent. The assessment boundary allows stored energy to motion, light or sound. Confidence: likely; reasonable reviewers differ on the device.

**Change.** Keep the clean-up signal. Reword it as a signal device, not a contraption: "Turn stored energy into a sound or a light the whole room notices, from one release. You may use a raised weight, a stretched band or a battery." Criteria the class can measure: noticed from the back wall in five of five trials. Chain-reaction steps become the extension. Give circuits three hands-on days (2.3). Materials note: a single LED will not light on one 1.5 volt cell and dollar-store flashlights are now LED, so specify two cells in series with an LED, or small holiday-string bulbs (from memory; test before printing).

### 5. Three-dimensional on paper; three fixes make it real

What is sound: each lesson set title carries a practice verb; every practice the grade 4 expectations name is placed; the added structure and function (stretch 4), scale (6, 7) and measuring (1, 5) match the grade 3 to 5 elements in Appendices F and G. What is not:

- **The added "computational thinking: a grid of 1s and 0s" is content, not the practice.** The grade 3 to 5 elements of that practice are about measuring, graphing and organising data. The grid is the core idea of 4-PS4-3. Claim "organise simple data to show a pattern" there, or drop the claim. Likely.
- **Stability and change fits stretch 6 best** (things that look stable change over long times) and stretch 5 (change at different rates). Its placement on stretch 8 is weak. Likely.
- **Grade-level practices appear once.** Argument only in stretch 4, analysing data only in 7, obtaining information only in 8. The guide wants repeated use in varied contexts. Add: argument in 6 (which account do the layers support) and 8 (which plan is better); data in 1 and 5 (already done, so name it); obtaining information in 2 (research before design, which the engineering core idea asks for). Firm.
- **Crosscutting concepts are listed, not used.** Add a "used again" column and one prompt per stretch that makes students use the concept as a tool (for example, "name the parts of this system and what would fail if one were removed"). Energy and matter should also be named in stretches 3 and 4. Likely.
- **Three limits to tighten.** Stretch 1: ramp height is only a way to get speed; stored energy of position is outside the core idea (evidence statement note). Also soften "not forces": the core idea says contact forces transfer energy, so one sentence linking to grade 3 forces is wanted. Check 4's "energy from the sun to a lit classroom" must run through a solar panel, wind or water; the sun's energy in food and fuel is grade 5 (5-PS3-1). Stretch 4's reason "so that it follows the wave work" should say light carries energy, since light as a wave is outside the boundary. Firm.

### 6. Checking: good design, heavy load, and four mismatches

The system is the spine's best feature: new phenomena, three dimensions named on the scoring guide, respond days, "if many missed this, do that". Against the evidence statements:

- **Load.** Twelve check days are 11 percent, close to Amplify Science (2 of 22 days per unit). But a five-minute exit question every day adds about 450 minutes, a tenth of the year, and science has a better daily record: the notebook entry and the revised model. Change: one short written check per lesson set (36), tied to that set's three-dimensional target; no daily exit question. Likely.
- **Stretch 1** never asks students to ask questions, which is the practice of 4-PS3-3. Add "write two questions the class could test".
- **Stretch 2** is a group product. Add an individual energy trace of a device the student did not build.
- **Stretch 4** has no plant and does not test 4-PS4-2 (finding 2).
- **Engineering.** 3-5-ETS1-1 and 3-5-ETS1-3 are assessed only in stretch 2. Assess both again in stretch 8 with less help, which is what indicator 2h (tasks grow harder) looks for.
- **Stretch 6** should include one case of bent or broken layers; the core idea and evidence statement name earth forces such as earthquakes. Add a day's work pushing the clay stack.
- **Stretch 7** should have students put map data into a table or chart first (evidence statement).

### 7. Investigations and materials

Workable and mostly safe. Specific fixes:

- **Cost.** My tally for eight groups is 110 to 140 dollars, not 60 (batteries, bulbs, flashlights, mirrors, trays and clay times eight). 60 holds for stations or four groups. Print a range and mark what a school already owns. Judgment call; untested either way.
- **Safety gaps.** Blowing sand through a straw needs eye protection and a box or tray lid, and straws are never shared. Freezer bottle: plastic only, never glass. Foil across a cell with no bulb heats up: seconds only. Acrylic mirrors, not glass. Pulse after exercise needs an alternative for students who cannot jump. Wet sand never goes down a sink. Bread and pasta layers raise allergy and "do not eat" issues; prefer clay. Indicator 3h wants safety guidance across the materials, so add a one-page year safety sheet as well as per-sheet notes.
- **Things that may not work as written.** Dark and light paper under an LED lamp barely warms; it needs sun or an old-style bulb. A baking tray makes messy reflected ripples; a larger tub with single gentle dips, or a rope with a ribbon tied on, shows the point more cleanly.
- **A teacher with nothing.** The spine has no answer, and many teachers pay for supplies themselves. Give every investigation three tiers: (A) group sets; (B) one demonstration set, which for the whole year is about 25 dollars; (C) no materials: Curio Class publishes, for each core investigation, a photo or video run with its real data table, and the tool, so the class still predicts, reads data and explains. The evidence guide (1c) accepts video and demonstration as a direct presentation. Name the eight or so investigations whose expectation says "make observations", "test" or "design" as first-hand core, and say plainly that tier C weakens those. Stretches 1, 3, 5 and 6 can run at zero cost from the schoolyard, the recycling bin and the gym closet.

### 8. Year size: defensible, with one inconsistency

108 sessions of 45 minutes is 4,860 minutes. OpenSciEd's grade 4 plans 4,970 minutes (55 lessons, from its teacher handbook); Amplify Science plans 5,280 with assessment days; Mystery Science 19 hours of core lessons, about 57 with extras. A reviewer would accept it; time is narrative only. Confidence: firm.

The inconsistency: the math spine counts 150 teaching days in a 180-day year. Three sessions a week over those 30 weeks is 90, not 108, and 27 minutes a day over 150 days is 67.5 hours, not 81. So 108 is the size of a full program, and 90 is the ordinary school. Change: say so, and specify both paths in full by marking each lesson set core or extended. In the 90-day path keep every revision day (iteration is worth 4 points under indicator 1g) and cut one day each from 1.2, 1.3, 2.3, 2.5, 2.7, 3.2, 4.2, 4.3, 4.4, 4.5, 5.2, 5.3, 6.2, 7.2 and 8.3, plus the three catch-up days, with the trade-off stated for each. Add a note for schools that teach science in blocks alternating with social studies (29 percent of grade 4 to 6 classes teach it only some weeks): each stretch already stands as a block. Likely.

### 9. Omissions and smaller points

- **Nature of science and engineering connections** are scored (2f). The standards page gives the placements: findings rest on patterns (stretches 3, 7); science assumes consistent patterns in nature (6); scientists and engineers work in teams, science affects everyday life (2); science and engineering depend on each other (3, resources); engineers improve technologies to cut risk (8). Add a column. Firm.
- **Core idea elements with no home yet:** phones and computers decode digitised information (stretch 3 needs a reading); rainfall shapes land and what lives there; living things change their surroundings (5); communicating with peers improves designs (2, 8).
- **One "watch for" line per stretch.** Common ideas to expect: energy gets used up; eyes send out sight; a wave carries the water along; rocks do not change; shells were washed uphill. ADOPTION-BAR asks for this on teacher pages; the spine is where it starts.
- **Stretch 3 needs one question that spans both halves**, for example "How can something get across a room when nothing travels across?"
- **Stretch 5's anchor is erosion only.** Weathering (5.3) needs its own observation, and the two must never be blurred: the EdReports report on Amplify Science marked exactly that as an accuracy error.
- **A rule for swapping in a local phenomenon:** it must be observable, need the same core idea to explain, and not be explained on day 1.
- **Rock layers and deep time** can be sensitive for some families. The standard stays with relative order; say so on the teacher page.
- **For the ledger:** many of these expectations have verbs a screen cannot carry (make observations, test, design). Under BASELINE 21 most science tools should take the role "see why" or "practice", and "taught in full" should come from the stretch.
- **Before wiring the standard text:** the NGSS file is marked all rights reserved by Achieve, with the core idea text reprinted by permission of the National Academy of Sciences. Check the terms first.

## What is right and should not be touched

- **Energy first, then devices, then waves.** OpenSciEd runs collisions then electricity; FOSS and Mystery Science build the same way. The bowling anchor is also one of FOSS's.
- **Engineering inside content, three design problems, each asking more.** The standards link defining problems to 4-PS3-4, optimising to 4-PS4-3 and developing solutions to 4-ESS3-2; the spine follows that exactly. Every problem ties to a science core idea, which indicator 1b requires.
- **Erosion, then layers, then maps, then hazards**, and reusing the erosion tray for the flood.
- **The cork on water**: straight from the core idea, cheap, and children predict it wrong.
- **The downspout**: local, free, and the grass beside it is a built-in comparison.
- **End tasks on unseen phenomena, three dimensions named, respond days, first-ideas probes kept and compared.**
- **The limits column**, the fair test taught early, the honest "what is missing" list, and the line that a tool does not replace rolling the marble.
- **The correction of sequence.csv prerequisites** to the standards' own articulation.

## What I could not judge

- Whether any day count is right for real children; only a pilot and a grade 3 to 5 teacher can say.
- The engineering design evidence statements (the 3-5-ETS1 files returned an error); the Framework chapters; Appendix E; the EdReports multilingual learner guide. Of the evidence guide I read indicators 1a to 1e, 1g, 1i to 1k, 2a to 2c, 2e to 2h and 3f to 3i, not the rest.
- OpenSciEd at lesson level: unit pages and the handbook were read, not the teacher editions; no EdReports report on its elementary units was found. Amplify Science is known only through its EdReports report. FOSS session counts were not found; its shares are by module. The Mystery Science guide is the 2019 edition.
- The materials cost and the bulb and lamp notes rest on my own tally and memory.
- State variations of NGSS, and whether any tool actually works as its spec says.
