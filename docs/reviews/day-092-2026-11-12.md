## Verdict

The mathematics is right, the minutes add to 60 and match the plan, and the shape (a real task, two worked
examples, six problems with no "covers" or "around", a written reason, an exit) would teach the goal to a grade 4
class. The biggest risk is the evidence: the exit says "the whole patio floor" and "along all four edges", so it
hands students the very choice the day teaches, and two of the six problems do the same in softer words. The one
change that matters most is to reword the exit (and the flag problem) so the situation, not a word about edges or
floors, tells the measure; after that, mend the two hand-offs, which promise things the neighbouring days do not do.

### 2. The exit gives away the measure, so it does not test the day's goal

- **What is wrong.** The goal is "I can decide which measure a real problem needs", with no cue words. The exit
  (`s11-X92`) says "paints the whole patio floor. How much floor is that?" and "a string of lights along all four
  edges". The teacher's own question all day is "inside or edge?"; the exit answers it in the stem. Problem 5
  ("Gold trim is sewn along each side") and problem 1 ("How many tiles fill the floor?", "fill" being "covers")
  do the same in practice. The plan's definition of a cue word is only "covers" and "around" (s11 U9, opener
  "Words"), so the writer kept the letter of the rule. End check item 5 (day 93) has the same pattern ("lies flat
  on top", "runs along all four sides"): shared, for the day 93 reader.
- **Why it matters.** The E92 column of the record sheet will show near-full marks on choosing the measure and
  hide the students who still choose by a word. The research case against keyword strategies (Karp, Bush and
  Dougherty, "13 Rules That Expire", 2014; the What Works Clearinghouse guide on problem solving in grades 4 to 8,
  2012, which pushes reasoning from the situation and representations; both from memory) is about exactly this:
  the word does the work, and it fails on the next problem without it.
- **Fix.** Reword the exit and problem 5 so the object in the story carries the measure (Settled, rows 1 to 3).
  Process: in s11 U9 and the opener, define a cue word as "any word that names the inside or the edge (covers,
  fills, around, edge, side, border of, whole floor)"; the situation (fence, tape, paint, trim, wire) is not a
  cue. Send end item 5's wording to the day 93 read.
- **Tags.** LAYER: process. CATCH: expert.
- **Confidence.** Likely (strong reasoning against the day's own goal; the outside sources are from memory).
