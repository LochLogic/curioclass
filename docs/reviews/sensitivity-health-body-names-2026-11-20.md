# Sensitivity read: kindergarten body names (k-phw-body-names), 2026-11-20

**Verdict: Pass with changes.** The row is short, plain and keeps private parts to one sentence, which meets "very minimal". The placement is sound: a school that drops body safety or sexual health still teaches the words, and a child is never left without them. Three gaps need closing before it is safe to hand to a wide range of communities: the family note for this row is promised but not defined, the teacher has no guidance for questions or disclosures in this non-removable row, and two phrases are loose enough to be taught differently by different teachers. Nothing here implies pictures, and nothing is alarming in tone.

## Findings

1. **Confidence: Firm. The family note for this row does not exist in the rules.** Section 5 rule 5 describes a family note only for sensitive units (outline, opt out). The row says "a family note gives the words ahead of the day", but a non-removable row has no stated note content.
   Current (row): "a family note gives the words ahead of the day."
   Proposed: "A short family note (English and Spanish) goes home before this day: it lists the body part words taught, including the correct names for private parts, says they are said once and plainly with no pictures, and says families may preview the day and may keep a child out for that part."
   Also add to STANDARD section 5 rule 5, after "never removes them": "The family note for that row lists the words, says there are no pictures, and offers preview."

2. **Confidence: Firm. No guidance for questions or disclosures in a row that cannot be removed.** Disclosure handling and the duty to report sit only in the removable k-v-body-safety. A school that removes that unit leaves a teacher of this row with none, and this is the row where a child may first use a word and say something.
   Current: "Kept short: no more about private parts here."
   Proposed: "Kept short: no more about private parts here. Answer a child's question in one calm, plain sentence and move on. If a child says anything about being touched or hurt, listen, do not promise secrecy, do not question for details, and follow the school's duty to report."

3. **Confidence: Likely. "ours to keep safe" is vague and slightly ambiguous.** A five-year-old may hear "ours" as shared with others.
   Current: "(covered by a swimsuit, ours to keep safe)"
   Proposed: "(the parts a swimsuit covers; they belong to you, and a trusted adult helps keep you safe)"

4. **Confidence: Likely. "said once, plainly" leaves the actual words to each teacher.** Teachers may avoid naming them or add detail. Name the set, and say how.
   Proposed addition: "Say the words in the same calm voice as 'elbow': penis, vulva, breasts, bottom. No pictures, diagrams, body outlines or pointing; words only. Do not add what the parts do."
   Confidence is Judgment call on the exact word list (see For Cory).

5. **Confidence: Firm. State "no pictures" in the row.** The row never says words only, and the hands_on and model fields are empty, so nothing forbids a body outline. Covered by finding 4's wording; also add to the day's teacher note "No body diagram on any private part."

6. **Confidence: Likely. Child-facing line on telling.** The row calls "private" "the first word body safety uses", which links this day to a sensitive topic and may worry families reading the row. The child needs the telling line, but it need not name body safety.
   Current: "the first word body safety uses."
   Proposed: "Private means just for you. If someone makes you feel unsafe about your body, tell a trusted adult until one helps." Drop the body safety phrase from family-facing text; keep it in teacher notes only.

7. **Confidence: Firm. Housekeeping.** The notes still say "Pending(docs/reviews/sensitivity-health-body-names-2026-11-20.md)". Remove it once these changes are applied. The words list also omits brain, which the notes name; add it.

8. **Confidence: Judgment call. Consistency with the removal rule.** Rule 1 says no sensitive content sits in a non-sensitive unit; rule 5 eases it for body names. That is acceptable, but rule 5 should say once that the easing covers one plain sentence of names and the idea of private, nothing more, so the exception cannot grow.

## For Cory

1. **Opt-out of this one day's private-part names.** Options: (a) offer preview only, no opt-out, since these are plain correct names; (b) offer preview and a quiet opt-out for that part. Recommend (b): it costs little, and preview alone can read as no choice in some communities.
2. **Which words are named.** Options: (a) penis, vulva, breasts, bottom (some schools omit breasts at K); (b) only "private parts" with the names left to families. Recommend (a): a child with no correct word is the harm this row exists to prevent. Say so in the family note so families can see it first.
