# Expert read: social studies content standard options (2026-11-12)

Cold read of social-studies-content-standard-2026-11-12.md (NEXT-SPINES item 5) by the curriculum expert, run per
docs/agents/EXPERT.md. Nothing in the repo was edited.

## Verdict

The paper is sound in method and its headline facts check out: the Fordham 2021 grades, the Massachusetts permission
line, the Tennessee, Virginia, Alabama and Oklahoma dates, and the repo counts are all right, and option (d) is the
right shape. The biggest risk is that the course-by-course fit to Massachusetts is wrong in several places, so the
recommendation ("close to the cost of one state") understates what it does to the course list: under a
Massachusetts anchor about 8 of 14 courses change, not 1. The one change that matters most: put the real fork in
front of Cory first, which is "keep our course order (anchor California or Tennessee) or take Massachusetts's
order (anchor Massachusetts)", because the anchor choice is a course-list choice.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The Massachusetts fit to our courses is wrong in several rows (serious; firm)

What is wrong. Read against the framework itself (2018 PDF, pages 17 to 19 and 117 to 119):
- MA US History I runs 1763 to the end of World War I; US History II starts with 1920s economics and the Depression.
  Our split is 1877. So our grade 11 (Gilded Age, Progressive Era, US as a world power) draws on US I, not "US II";
  our grade 8 takes only the first half of US I. The table says grade 11 = "US History II".
- MA grade 7 is Ancient Civilizations II (South and East Asia, Oceania, Europe, Greece and Rome). Medieval to 1800 is
  high school World History I (c. 600 to 1800). Our grade 7 (medieval to early modern) would carry high school
  standards in grade 7, and our grade 9 to 10 course (it starts at revolutions, c. 1750) maps to World History II
  only. The table says 9 to 10 = "World History I and II", which would teach World I twice.
- MA grade 6 and 7 are two years of geography and ancient civilizations; our grade 6 holds one year of it.
- MA grade 5 runs to the Civil War and the modern civil rights movement; ours stops at the Constitution.
- MA grade 3 is "Massachusetts, Home to Many Different People"; grade 4 is North American geography. Ours: grade 3
  local, grade 4 state history and state government. "Our grade 4 and 5 topics would retag with little movement"
  is not right.

Cost. A teacher or reviewer tracing our grade 7 or 11 to MA codes finds high school standards in middle school, or
a course pulling from two MA courses. The recommendation's cost claim rests on this table.

Fix. Rewrite the "What it means for each high school course" table and the K to 5 paragraph with the rows above.
Retitle it "What it means for each course" and add grades 3, 4, 5, 6 and 7. In (d)'s notes and the
recommendation, replace "at close to the cost of one state" with "at close to the cost of one state if we take
Massachusetts's course order; about 8 of 14 course outlines change (grades 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 to 10, 11)".

### 2. The recommendation hides the real decision, and its licence argument cuts against itself (serious; likely)

What is wrong. Our course list is California's order almost exactly (grade 4 state history, 5 early US, 6 ancient,
7 medieval and early modern, 8 US growth and conflict, 10 modern world, 11 twentieth-century US, 12 government and
economics). So the anchor choice is really: keep our order and anchor to California (or Tennessee 2024), or take
Massachusetts's order and anchor to it. The paper puts this last, as "Decide whether grades 7 and 8 keep their
current order". Second, the recommendation picks Massachusetts mainly for its licence, but caution 3 says licence
"matters less than it looks" because we only need codes, our own short paraphrase and a link. Both cannot carry
the weight.

Fix. Make the first line of "If we had to choose" the fork, stated plainly:
- Path 1: Massachusetts anchor, Massachusetts course order. Terms are certain; about 8 course outlines are redrawn.
  No social studies scope and sequence is written yet, so the cost is topic lists and retagging, not finished work.
- Path 2: California anchor (or Tennessee 2024), our course order kept. Needs a written permission request to
  CDE (or Tennessee terms confirmed) if we go beyond codes, paraphrase and link.
Then say which and why. My read (judgment call): Path 1. Massachusetts's terms are known, the set is the newest of
the exemplary five that is in force and unrevised, it is a coherent K-12 design reviewers know, and its grade 8
civics year matches what Fordham asks states to add. Path 2 is fully defensible if Cory values the current list.
Rewrite caution 3 to say licence decides between equally good anchors, not between a better and a worse fit.

### 3. A national site and "state history" grades: missing from every option (serious; firm)

What is wrong. Most states give a grade (usually 4, sometimes 3 or 7) to their own state's history and
government. Our grade 4 has "State History" and "State Government". Massachusetts grade 3 names Massachusetts
people and places; California grade 4 is California. Any one-state anchor hands every other state's child a
study of Massachusetts or California. The paper does not raise it.

Cost. A teacher in Ohio cannot use a Massachusetts grade 3 unit as written; the course looks local to another state.

Fix. Add a short section "State and local study" under every option: the anchor's state-study standards are
taught as a method ("your state": its founding, government, peoples, economy) with the anchor's codes for the
skills and structure, and a link to each state's own standards for content. Flag to Cory as a scope question
(it changes what grade 3 or 4 teaches).

### 4. "Earlier grade" is not a rigor ranking (moderate; likely)

What is wrong. The high-watermark note says earlier grade and more depth "can be ranked". Depth can. Earlier is a
sequencing choice, not more rigor: the Constitution in grade 3 versus grade 5 says nothing about how well it is
learned, and can be less suited to the age.

Fix. Change to: "Of the three, only depth can be ranked. Earlier grade is a sequencing choice the anchor settles;
more topics only adds." This strengthens the paper's own conclusion that (c) collapses into (d).

### 5. Three reference points a curriculum lead would name are missing (moderate; likely)

What is wrong and fix:
- **New York.** Fordham gives it A- in US history (B+ civics), and it is the only other strong set with possibly
  open terms. Its high school order (9 to 10 global history, 11 US, 12 government and economics) matches ours.
  Move it from "Not checked" to the check set and confirm terms on NYSED's own page (a library guide is not enough).
- **The College Board AP course frameworks** (US History, World History, US Government, Macro and Micro Economics,
  Psychology). They are the working high-watermark for high school content in most districts. Read and link only;
  name them as a check for grades 9 to 12, never in public copy (BASELINE rule 15).
- **Educating for American Democracy Roadmap (2021),** a federally funded national history and civics guide built
  by about 300 scholars. Not standards, but the nearest national content reference for (b) or (d). Terms not
  checked. Add it to the check set as a reference.
Also name the editions the paper left open: APA National Standards for High School Psychology Curricula (2022
revision, from memory; confirm) and Geography for Life, 2nd edition (2012, from memory; confirm).

### 6. The LGBTQ case overstates the Florida side (moderate; firm)

What is wrong. "Florida ... restrict classroom instruction on related topics" is broadly right, but the March 2024
settlement of the challenge to Florida's Parental Rights in Education law clarified that it does not bar
discussing LGBTQ people; it restricts formal instruction on sexual orientation and gender identity. So the
conflict is real but narrower: naming a person's historical role where it is significant (the paper's own C12
step) likely sits inside both.

Fix. Reword the cell: "require it in social studies. Florida restricts formal classroom instruction on sexual
orientation and gender identity (a 2024 settlement clarified that discussing LGBTQ people is not barred)." Keep
the "No" verdict: the rule still cannot rank a requirement against a restriction.

### 7. Small facts to tighten (minor; firm unless marked)

- California: permission is needed for any portion; the per-copy fee applies to full reprints. Write "written
  permission to reproduce any part (a fee for full reprints)". California grade 8 runs to about 1914, not 1877;
  say "close", not "match".
- Alabama: adopted 12 December 2024 (confirmed); classroom year not confirmed on an official page. Add "in force
  year not confirmed".
- District of Columbia: OSSE adopted the new set 22 June 2023; "in force 2024-25" is not on that page. Mark
  unconfirmed or cite the source.
- Oklahoma: add that a new draft was under way in 2026 that drops the election and COVID lines. It strengthens the
  point that state sets move.

### 8. "Criticised from both sides" has one source (minor; firm)

Fix. Either cite a critique from the other direction or write "criticised, for one by the National Association of
Scholars".

## What is right and should not be touched

- Fordham 2021 Table ES-1 grades are exact for every state in the table, including New York B+ / A-, Texas B+ / C+,
  Oklahoma B+ / B+. The 2006 world history A list (eight states) is exact.
- The Massachusetts permission line is quoted correctly in substance (page 2 of the 2018 PDF).
- Repo counts are right and the correction of the brief's "135" to 123 social studies topics is clear: 131 topics,
  123 with C3 codes, 262 of 283 indicators cited (283 across all subjects).
- Option (d) as a concept, and the finding that a capped high watermark becomes (d).
- The values section: the three-step order (evidence, promise 7, C12, then local leave-out) is sound and fits
  ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS. The Florida and Oklahoma cases are real, current and fairly described.
- Plain words throughout; no em dashes; "Curio Class" in full; quotes short.

## What it could not judge

- Whether codes plus our paraphrase plus a link needs a state's permission. That is a legal question; the paper is
  right to say so.
- Tennessee, Alabama, DC and New York reuse terms (not read here either).
- Whether Massachusetts is thin in any one course; that needs the unit-level check (d) already plans.

## Anything Cory must know

- The anchor decision is a course-list decision. Under Massachusetts, about 8 of 14 course outlines change; under
  California, almost none do but a permission request may be needed.
- Every one-state option needs a plan for state-history grades on a national site.
- Nothing here changes the values order; it can go to Cory as written, with the Florida wording fixed.
