# Arts: open material scout

Read 2026-11-16 (pre-work for the arts). Research notes, not legal advice. Every licence read on the publisher's
own page unless marked "search only". The bar is docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md: CC BY 4.0, CC BY 3.0 or public domain (or
CC0) can be a base; NC, SA, ND or unclear terms are reference only.

## The short answer

**No openly licensed K to 12 arts curriculum fits as a base, in any of the five disciplines.** None was found
under CC BY or in the public domain. The arts are written fresh, under docs/courses/arts/STANDARD.md and the
approach page.

What is open is **material to study, not plans to follow**: museum images released as CC0, public domain sound
recordings, and one music theory text under CC BY. These serve "the works studied" and, for high school music,
some theory content.

## Candidates: works to study (images and recordings)

These are not curricula. They are where a unit's works studied can come from, when the work itself is old enough
or released.

| Source | Terms (as read) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Art Institute of Chicago open access images | CC0 for public domain works (its open access page, search only) | Visual arts works studied |
| The Metropolitan Museum of Art open access | CC0 for public domain works (search only; the page returned an error) | Visual arts works studied |
| Smithsonian open access | CC0 for released items (search only; the page refused the fetch) | Visual arts, music, media arts |
| Library of Congress sound recordings (National Jukebox and others) | Recordings first published before 1923 entered the US public domain in 2022 (search only) | Music works studied, dance music |
| Library of Congress photographs and films | Many items have no known restrictions; each item states its own rights | Media arts, theater, visual arts |

Each image or recording used is checked on its own item page at the time it is used: an institution can release
some items and not others.

## What the scout does not settle

- Dance and theater have no open recordings of performances at scale. Units in those disciplines describe works
  in our own words and point to them; they do not embed.
- Living artists' works are not open. A unit that studies one names it and links to the artist's or museum's own
  page, never copies it.
