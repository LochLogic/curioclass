## Verdict

Day 4 is correct mathematics in a sound order (one fact read three ways, the count past ten times, fence,
three register-placed fixes), and a no-prep teacher could run it. The biggest risk is that the day teaches one
move, "ten times, then a fact from the table", and then asks five items, the exit among them, that the move
cannot finish: 79 in 3s, 88 and 92 in 4s, 69 in 3s and the challenge all need a second ten (3 × 20, 4 × 20),
which no example shows. A student who learned exactly what was taught gets stuck at 4 × 13 on the exit and
writes "no", which the teacher reads as error E and answers on day 5 with a number (56) that needs only one ten.
The one change that matters most is finding 1: add "take more tens" to Example 2 and to the paper path.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The day teaches one ten; five items, the exit among them, need two

- **What is wrong.** Example 2 models 96 = 6 × 10 + 6 × 6: one long jump, then a table fact. The paper path says
  "one long jump for ten cartons, then one jump a carton". Day 3 taught "start from ten times" on 48 (also one
  ten plus a table fact). But on day 4: T4-4 is 79 in 3s (49 left after 30, so 16 more jumps), P4-2 is 88 in 4s
  (48 left, 4 × 12, past the 9 by 9 table), P4-3 is 69 in 3s (39 left, 3 × 13), the challenge needs 3 × 24 to
  3 × 26, and the exit X4b is 92 in 4s (52 left, 4 × 13). The only place a second ten appears is a "Watch for"
  line ("Ask 'What is 4 × 20?'") and the keys. The students handed a 9 by 9 table are exactly the ones who will
  stall at 4 × 13.
- **Evidence.** items.csv rows above; d004.md "Teaching first" and the paper path; d003.md example. Grade 3 hands
  over one-digit times multiples of 10 (3.NBT.A.3; docs/courses/math-3-5-progression.md, stretch 11 line), so
  3 × 20 and 4 × 20 are fair to use; they are just not shown. Illustrative Mathematics grade 4, unit 1, lesson 5
  (read today) keeps its multiples within or near the tables, so this day goes further than that course, which
  the 1 to 100 limit of 4.OA.B.4 calls for, but it then has to teach the extra move. Same kind as day 86 finding 3
  (an exit that asks for a step the unit does not teach).
- **Cost.** The exit's "no" on 92 cannot tell "thinks the 4s stop at 40" from "knows they go on but has no way to
  reach 92". Day 5's E branch fences 56 (40 + 16), which needs one ten, so it does not fix the second case. On
  side 1, 79 in 3s is 17 jumps in a half-inch box; the order eats the 10 minutes.
- **Change.** Example 2 gets two more steps as a new row, `s01-K4-2v3` (see "Settled by the expert"): "Is 96 a
  multiple of 3 too? One ten of 3s is only 30, and 66 is left: past the table. Take more tens: 3 × 20 = 60, 3 × 30
  = 90." and "90 and 6 more is 3 × 2. So 3 × 32 = 96: 96 is a multiple of 3 and of 6." Paper path: "one long
  jump for each ten cartons, then one jump a carton". Watch-for: "Ask 'What is 4 × 20?' (for 69: 'What is
  3 × 20?')". Pay for the words: replace the two-sentence "When Fill Line exists ... same 10 minutes" with "When
  Fill Line exists, students write yes or no first, then run each order.", and "Draw the jumps on the board ...
  so the board carries it." with "The display has no number line yet: draw the jumps on the board as the steps
  come." Rerun the count. Day 5 (its reader's call): W5-4 should need two tens, for example "Is 84 a multiple of
  3?" (3 × 28; 84 is taught on day 4, so allowed).
- **Confidence.** Firm (the rows themselves; the arithmetic of what one ten reaches).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes. **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read saw only that 79 does not
  fit its box, and proposed moving X4b away from the 4 × 20 route, the opposite of this fix).
