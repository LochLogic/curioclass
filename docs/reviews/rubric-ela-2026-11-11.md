## The expert's read

Read on 2026-11-11 by a fresh agent. I downloaded the four files linked in the draft and extracted their text.
I read both criteria files whole (15 and 11 pages). In the grades 6 to 12 evidence guide I read indicators 2a
to 2g in full (PDF pages 4 to 32) and the scoring lists for 2h to 2v. In the grades 3 to 5 evidence guide I read
the scoring lists for Gateway 1 and checked the figures for 2a, 2f, 2g and 2o. I also opened the publisher's
tools page to check the multilingual learner files. Nothing from the source is quoted below.

### Verdict

The page is sound in its structure and almost all of its numbers, and it is safe on the licence. The biggest
risk is three statements that are wrong or overstated about how the gateways work, plus one missing number: the
evidence guide sets an 80 percent line for questions and tasks that need the text, and the draft says only
"most". The one change that matters most is to fix the gateway rule (a course must fully pass Gateway 2, 50 of
56, to be read on Gateway 3) and to add a short "what the reviewers count" block from the evidence guide, so
the pilot read has real lines to read against.

### Findings, most serious first

**F1. The gateway rule is wrong. Gateway 3 needs a full pass of Gateway 2, not a partial one.**
- What is wrong: "How it works" says a course must at least partly pass the earlier gateways to be read on
  Gateway 3. Both criteria files say otherwise. A partial pass of Gateway 1 is enough to go on to Gateway 2. To be
  read on Gateway 3, a course must fully pass every gateway that applies to it. For grades 6 to 12 that means 50
  or more of 56 on Gateway 2.
- Evidence: 6-12 criteria, PDF pages 3, 4 and 8; 3-5 criteria, PDF pages 3, 7 and 11.
- Cost: the pilot read would treat a 28 to 49 score as good enough to move on. It is not. The real margin is six
  points, with no zero anywhere.
- Change: rewrite the sentence. "A partial pass of Gateway 1 lets a course go on to Gateway 2. Only a full pass of
  every gateway that applies (Gateway 2: 50 of 56) lets it go on to Gateway 3." The math page words the same rule
  correctly; copy its shape.
- Confidence: firm.

**F2. The 80 percent line is missing, and the page says ELA has no line like it.**
- What is wrong: the 2g row says "most questions and tasks". The evidence guide for both grade bands makes that a
  number: reviewers ask whether 80 percent or more of questions, tasks and assignments need the text. The
  section "How this differs from the math rubric" says there is no line like the math 65 percent. There is one,
  and it is this.
- Evidence: 6-12 evidence guide, PDF pages 31 to 32 (indicator 2g, guiding question and evidence collection);
  3-5 evidence guide, indicator 2g, same figure. 2g is a four-point indicator.
- Cost: plan row 2 says the share will be "counted" but names no target. The pilot's question set could be built
  and read with no pass line.
- Change: add to the header, beside the 55 and 45 figure: "The 80 percent line is real (evidence guide, 2g).
  Reviewers count every question, task and assignment, and also count the ones tied to no text." Change the 2g
  row to say 80 percent. Change the first bullet of "How this differs" to: "The counted line is different: 80
  percent of questions and tasks must need the text. There is no share-of-the-year line like the math 65
  percent."
- Confidence: firm.

**F3. Gateway 1 for grades 3 to 5 is "if it applies", not always. The must-pass claim is overstated.**
- What is wrong: the draft says grades 3 to 5 "have all three" gateways, and that word reading and fluency are
  must-pass for any grade 3 to 5 course. The criteria say Gateway 1 is used only for programs designed to teach
  foundational skills. A program not designed for that starts at Gateway 2. Where Gateway 1 does apply, the
  draft is right that the word reading part (24 points) and the fluency part (8 points) must each be fully passed
  to reach Gateway 3. The draft also leaves out the pass lines.
- Evidence: 3-5 criteria, PDF page 2 (the two figures and their footnotes), pages 4 to 7.
- Cost: plan row 6 ("a foundational skills sequence before any spine") rests on the overstated reading. It may
  still be the right call, because the grade 3 to 5 standards themselves include foundational skills (RF.3.3,
  RF.3.4 and their grade 4 and 5 twins), so a course that claims every standard must teach them. But that is a
  different reason, and whether Curio Class grades 3 to 5 ELA claims to be a full program with foundational
  skills is a scope call.
- Change: reword "How it works" to: "Grades 3 to 5 have a first gateway on foundational skills, used only when
  the program is designed to teach them." Add the pass lines: Gateway 1 has 44 points, 38 or more passes; word
  reading 22 of 24; fluency 6 of 8. Reword the fourth bullet of "How this differs" and plan row 6 to give the
  standards as the reason. Send the scope question to Cory (see "For Cory").
- Confidence: firm on the text; a judgment call on what Curio Class should claim.

**F4. The 2a row presents words-only items as scored, and misses what is scored.**
- What is wrong: the row opens with "students read a lot, and a range of kinds ... with a plan for independent
  reading the teacher can track". In the evidence guide those three things (the balance of kinds, the mix of
  whole texts and excerpts and of long and short texts, and the independent reading system) are reported in
  words only. The one scored point of 2a is the quality of the anchor texts: well made, rich in content, and
  engaging for the grade.
- Evidence: 6-12 evidence guide, PDF pages 5 and 8 to 9.
- Cost: the pilot read might mark the pilot down for volume on a five-day slice, or pass it on volume, when the
  scored question is whether Colum's Cyclops through Circe is a text worth a sixth grader's week.
- Change: split the row's sentence. Scored: anchor texts are well made, rich and engaging. Words only: the 55 and
  45 balance, whole texts and excerpts, long and short texts, and the independent reading system with tracking
  and teacher guidance. Also add what reviewers count for volume (see F6).
- Confidence: firm.

**F5. "Texts are judged before teaching is ... fails early" is not how the tool works.**
- What is wrong: texts (2a to 2e, 14 points) and teaching (2f to 2v, 42 points) sit in the same gateway and are
  added into one total of 56. There is no order between them. What is true: a zero on any scored text indicator
  stops the course, and with a pass line of 50 a course can drop only six points in all, so two half scores on
  the four-point text indicators use most of the margin.
- Evidence: 6-12 criteria, PDF pages 4 to 7.
- Cost: a reader plans around a gate that does not exist, and misses the real constraint, which is the six-point
  margin.
- Change: replace the bullet with: "Texts are a quarter of Gateway 2 (14 of 56). A zero on any scored indicator
  stops the course, and the whole gateway can lose only six points. So a weak or badly placed anchor cannot be
  made up for by good lessons."
- Confidence: firm.

**F6. The page needs a short "what the reviewers count" block. Here is what to add for 2a to 2g.**
- What is at risk: the draft read only 2a in the evidence guide. The pilot read needs the working detail for
  each indicator it scores. One or two lines each, in our own words:
  - **2a.** Counts are made on anchor texts only; texts marked optional or supplemental do not count. Reviewers
    note the share of whole texts and excerpts, the share of long and short texts, whether a reason is given for
    using an excerpt, which kinds of text are missing, and whether informational texts come from sources that can
    be trusted. The guide defines long texts as full-length books, novels and plays.
  - **2b.** Three checks per anchor: a measure inside the grade band, a human judgment of what makes it hard, and
    the reader and the task. A fourth thing is required for full marks: a written reason why this text is here,
    in this grade and at this point. Order does not have to climb by measure.
  - **2c.** Supports must come before, during and after reading, must match what the text record says is hard,
    and each needs a note telling the teacher how to run it. A support with no teacher note does not earn full
    marks.
  - **2d.** The topic must be specific and tight; a broad or vague theme does not count. Texts should show
    different sides of one topic over a long stretch, and across the year the sets should reach into history,
    science, the arts and literature. A set tied by theme must still build knowledge.
  - **2e.** Words only. Reviewers describe who is in the texts and who wrote them, and look for teacher guidance
    on topics that may touch students' own lives.
  - **2f.** Reviewers look for the core path set out plainly, for when and how any extra materials are used, and
    for a schedule with guidance at lesson level that fits a year of about 180 days with room for tests and
    breaks.
  - **2g.** The 80 percent count (F2). Also: tasks that send students back to reread the hard parts, and more
    than one way to answer (speaking as well as writing).
- Evidence: 6-12 evidence guide, PDF pages 5 to 32.
- Cost: without these, the pilot's text record would lack a placement reason, its scaffolds would lack teacher
  notes, and its question set would lack a count. Each of those is a half score.
- Change: add the block above under the table, headed "What the reviewers count (grades 6 to 12 evidence
  guide)". Update "How much was read" to say 2a to 2g were read in full and 2h to 2v in their scoring lists.
- Confidence: firm.

**F7. The table gives points for some rows and not others, and the rows from 2h to 2v fold away details the
pilot read will need.**
- What is missing: point values. In Gateway 2 the four-point indicators are 2a, 2b, 2d, 2f, 2g, 2u and 2v. All
  the others that are scored are two points (2c, 2h to 2t). 2e is words only. In Gateway 3: 3a, 3b, 3e, 3g are
  two points each, 3c and 3f one each, 3d words only; 3j and 3k two each; 3l to 3o and 3r to 3u words only. In
  Gateway 1 (grades 3 to 5): 1g, 1h, 1j, 1k, 1l, 1n are four each; 1i and 1m are not scored in this band; 1o
  and 1p two each, 1q and 1r four each; 1s is not scored in this band, 1t and 1u four each. The three-cueing
  indicator (1h) is all or nothing: four or zero.
- Details from the scoring lists that the rows should carry, one line each:
  - 2h: three kinds of analysis, matching the three groups of reading standards (key ideas, craft and
    structure, bringing ideas together), within a text and across texts.
  - 2i: the discussion routine needs teacher guidance on leading, watching and giving feedback, not only the
    routine itself. 2j: students must use evidence and respond to each other's views.
  - 2k and 2l: words are met more than once, before, during and after reading and in later texts, and earlier
    words come back in practice.
  - 2m: model sentences come from the anchor texts. 2n: students also fit their language to the reader and the
    purpose.
  - 2o: the 35, 35, 30 mix of writing types is words only. 2p: model texts or sample student pieces, and
    guidance for the teacher on feedback. 2q: plan, draft, revise and edit many times, and use technology to
    produce and publish.
  - 2t: for grades 6 to 8 the scoring list names search terms, judging sources, quoting and paraphrasing, and a
    standard citation form. Some of that is grade 7 and 8 content. The grade 6 spine rightly leaves out search
    terms. The pilot read should score against the grade 6 standards and say so.
  - 2v: a culminating task in every unit, on the unit's topic, with guidance for the teacher on scoring it.
- Small accuracy points in the same table: the Gateway 1 word recognition row says "common regular and irregular
  words". The scored indicators there (1o to 1r) are about word parts, syllables and long words in running text,
  with checks. Drop "common regular and irregular words". The fluency row mixes in the grade 6 plan; Gateway 1
  does not apply to grade 6, so move that sentence out or mark it "not scored for grade 6".
- Grades 3 to 5 differences worth one line: the balance figure is 50 and 50; 2i and 2j are about speaking and
  listening on texts read or read aloud; the writing mix figure is 30, 35, 35.
- Evidence: both criteria files; 6-12 evidence guide scoring lists, PDF pages 33 to 91; 3-5 evidence guide,
  Gateway 1 scoring lists.
- Cost: the read cannot weigh a miss without the points, and the folded rows hide the half-score traps.
- Change: add the points to each row label, and one short clause per item above.
- Confidence: firm.

### Settled by the expert

1. **Which indicators the pilot read scores.** Reason: a five-day slice on one anchor can only be judged on what
   five days can show. File: docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md, new section "The pilot read".
   - Scored in full: 2b, 2c, 2g, 2h, 2i, 2j, 2k, 2l, 2r, 2u; 3a, 3j, 3k; and 3n in words.
   - Scored in part, with a note: 2a (the quality of the one anchor only), 2d (how the slice fits stretch 2's
     set), 2f (five days of pacing), 2m and 2n (only if the slice includes its openers).
   - Not scored on the pilot, marked "stretch or course level": 2e, 2o, 2p, 2q, 2s, 2t, 2v, 3b to 3g, 3l, 3m, 3o,
     3r to 3u.
2. **How the 80 percent is counted on the pilot.** Count every question, task and assignment in the five days,
   the exit questions and the end check items included. One that can be answered without the text counts
   against. Target 80 percent or more, and the count is written on the question set. Reason: it follows the
   guide's own count. File: the pilot's question set; plan row 2.
3. **One piece of evidence writing inside the pilot.** The pilot's written work is the summary. Add one short
   written answer on the theme that cites the text (RL.6.2 with RL.6.1), on day 2 or day 3, as an exit
   question or as the first lines of the evidence paragraph unit 2.4 already plans. Reason: without it the slice
   has nothing for 2r, and a summary alone is not analysis. It stays inside the pilot's named standards and
   moves no day. File: docs/courses/ela-6-spine.md, "The pilot".
4. **The text record uses four headings for what makes a text hard:** meaning, structure, language, and what the
   reader needs to know already. Reason: these are the standards' own Appendix A headings, which the guide
   follows. File: the pilot's text record; plan row 1.
