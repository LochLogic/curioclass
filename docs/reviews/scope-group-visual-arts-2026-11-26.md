## Findings

### 1. The printmaking ladder stalls at grade 8 and restarts at Level 2 (scope)

- **Where.** Grade 3: foam-plate printing in steps, one color (3 to 5 days 4, 5). Grade 8: two-color relief printing
  with registration and a planned risk (6 to 8 days 134 to 136). Level 2: "Foam-plate printing in named steps (draw
  with a ballpoint, ink with a brayer, burnish with a spoon, pull)" taught as new (9 to 12 day 169, unit 7 day 3).
  Level 3: "a two-color foam-plate print with registration" as one of the series media (9 to 12 day 332).
- **Why it matters.** A student who took the band courses meets the grade 3 one-color print again two levels into
  high school, then the grade 8 two-color print at Advanced. The lens and bar item 2 ask that technique get harder
  across the years; here it goes back. The blade rule (linocut barred until the safety read, Cory 2026-11-26, Desk
  q-frames-arts-1) is not the cause: the 9 to 12 frame already allows collagraph.
- **Fix.** Day 169 note: name the grade 3 and grade 8 prints as known, keep the one-color step card as the reteach
  for a student new to visual arts, and teach collagraph (built-up card and texture plate, no blade) as the Level 2
  step. Day 332: the print group's step becomes a reduction or multi-plate collagraph print with registration, named
  as one step past grade 8. No unit, row or day count moves.

### 2. Washes are taught as new three times (scope)

- **Where.** Grade 5: flat, graded and wet-into-wet washes with a step card (3 to 5 days 132, 133). Grade 7: "show
  washes in steps: a flat wash and a graded wash" (6 to 8 day 66), wet into wet and dry brush on day 67, no return
  named; day 66 retrieves only the grade 6 rows. Level 2 day 170 "value in layered washes"; Level 3 day 332 "layered
  glazing in ink wash or watercolor", both after grade 7's Fan Kuan layering (6 to 8 day 67).
- **Why it matters.** Grade 7 spends most of a demonstration on grade 5's first steps; the layered-ink step is
  claimed new at grade 7, Level 2 and Level 3. A writer building these days cannot tell what each adds.
- **Fix.** 6 to 8 day 66 note: grade 5's three washes come back as a five-minute strip (reteach for a student new to
  the band); the new step is dry brush and controlled layers. 9 to 12 days 170 and 332 notes name the step each
  adds over grade 7 (Level 2: value planned in three layers over a dried ground; Level 3: glazing at a larger scale
  over a full series). Wording only.

### 3. Contour drawing is never taught before the bridge "re-teaches" it (scope)

- **Where.** No K to 8 day names contour (searched all three files). The 9 to 12 bridge teaches "blind contour,
  modified contour, two-minute gesture" (day 5) as "grade 8 work taught again" (scope unit 1; STANDARD 3.2 says the
  bridge teaches the grade 8 techniques Level 1 needs). The lens's own model is "a K to 2 line becomes a contour
  drawing becomes a gesture study"; 6 to 8 goes from value (days 2 to 10) to gesture (days 148, 149) without it.
  The color wheel mixed from three primaries (9 to 12 day 8) is in the same position: grade 5 mixes colors (3 to 5
  day 134) and grade 6 names color schemes, but no day builds the wheel.
- **Why it matters.** The bridge's day budget (12 full, 6 short) was sized as review; two of its techniques are
  first teachings, and a student coming from grade 8 never had contour.
- **Fix.** 6 to 8 day 7 (still life from observation) adds blind and modified contour as its first named steps,
  drilled on day 10; the bridge's day 5 note then says "taught in grade 6, retaught here". If the lead prefers no
  6 to 8 change, the 9 to 12 scope's unit 1 text says contour and the full color wheel are first taught here, not
  retaught. No row moves.

### 4. The critique routine has four names and its rules sit in one band each (scope)

- **Where.** K to 2: "critique card", describe, ask, suggest, with turn-taking pictures (days 15, 150). Grade 3 uses
  describe, ask, suggest on day 13, then "Show the partner talk card" as a new card on day 28. Grade 4: "Show the
  critique protocol ... the critique-protocol model begins" (day 89). 6 to 8: "Critique protocol returns from grade 5
  as review" (day 11). 9 to 12: "the critique routine (describe, ask, suggest)" taught in the bridge (day 10).
  APPROACH names it "the describe, ask, suggest critique routine". "Praise alone is not feedback" appears only in
  6 to 8 (day 11); "Critique of the work, never the person" only in 9 to 12 (six notes).
- **Why it matters.** One routine runs K to 12; four names make a teacher think grade 3 and grade 4 teach a new
  protocol, and the two rules that keep critique safe and useful reach only some grades. Smaller drift of the same
  kind: the work card's fields (3 to 5 "who, where, when, what for" with a separate setting card; 9 to 12 "maker,
  place, date, purpose, rights line"), and the value scale (10 steps in 6 to 8 day 2, 9 in 9 to 12 day 2).
- **Fix.** One name in all four scopes and day notes, "the critique routine (describe, ask, suggest)", the
  APPROACH words. Each band's first critique day says what the band adds: K to 2 turn-taking pictures; grade 3 the
  suggestion tied to a look-for (3 to 5 day 28 becomes "the critique routine's partner talk", not a new card);
  grade 4 roles and the artist's planned change (day 89); 6 to 8 criteria; 9 to 12 a stated vision and standards.
  Both rules go on each band's first critique day (K to 2 day 15, 3 to 5 day 13, 6 to 8 day 11, 9 to 12 day 10).
  The work card keeps one field list (maker, place, date, purpose; the rights line from grade 6); one value scale
  length (pick 9, matching the bridge). Wording only.

### 5. Leave-out is handled four ways, and 3 to 5 turns it into a time cut (scope)

- **Where.** Each frame's Leave-out bullet (STANDARD 5) names units a school or family may leave out for its
  community's reasons, with the ids not claimed named on the course page and family note. K to 2 scope names both
  frame units (8, 12). 3 to 5 scope reads its frame units (4, 8, 12) as "If a year runs short past the shorter path,
  this is the unit a school leaves out (frame)" (scope lines for units 4, 8, 12 and the 10-days-short bullet: "the
  year's leave-out unit (4, 8 or 12) is the frame's last resort"); the frame says no such thing. 6 to 8 scope and
  days name none of the frame's four (units 5, 8, 11, 12). 9 to 12 scope names unit 15 only, not 14 and 5.
- **Why it matters.** The 3 to 5 reading would let a short year drop each year's whole showing unit and four ids,
  a cut no rule allows (the shorter path keeps every id). The missing names in 6 to 8 and 9 to 12 mean a writer of
  those unit plans may not write the family note and the "ids not claimed" line the standard asks for.
- **Fix.** Each scope's unit Sensitivity line names the frame's leave-out units and the ids a school would not
  claim if it leaves one out. 3 to 5: delete the time-cut sentences (four places); the shorter path's fold-ins stay
  the only short-year route. If the lead wants a last-resort time cut, it is a Cory call (it drops ids), not a scope
  line.

### 6. The 9 to 12 two-levels section says "pending" after Cory decided (scope)

- **Where.** 9 to 12 scope, "Two levels in one room (pending Cory's pick, frame For the lead 4)" and For the lead 4.
  Cory decided 2026-11-26 (Desk q-frames-arts-1, DESK-ANSWERS "after-core frame calls"; frame For the lead 4):
  Levels 1 and 2 may share a room, each keeping its own units and ids, sharing the opening look and critique days.
- **Why it matters.** A Desk answer applied in the frame and not in the scope; a unit-plan writer reads "pending".
- **Fix.** Retitle the section "Two levels in one room (Cory, 2026-11-26, Desk q-frames-arts-1)", drop "pending",
  and have the unit plans mark the shared days the frame asks for. The Level 2 with Level 3 pairing goes to finding 7.

### 7. Levels 2 and 3 in one room was never asked (Cory)

- **Where.** The same 9 to 12 section also pairs Level 2 with Level 3 (units 7 and 12, 8 and 13, 9 and 14, 10 and 15),
  which line up almost exactly by level day. Cory's answer names Levels 1 and 2 only.
- **Options.** (a) Levels 2 and 3 may share by the same rule (own units and ids, shared opening look and critique);
  (b) only Levels 1 and 2, and the scope drops the 2 and 3 pairing. **My pick: (a)**: the days already align, and a
  small high school is as likely to combine its second- and third-year students as its first and second.

### 8. Works that return across bands are named unevenly (scope)

- **Where.** The El-Salahi return is named (9 to 12 day 24: "A maker met in Visual Arts, grades 3 to 5, back with a
  different work"), and the 6 to 8 WPA posters (day 9) name the grade 4 parks poster. Others are not: the Migration
  Series, grade 3 (3 to 5 days 53, 54, panels of travel, work and home, "not violence") returns as Level 1's hook
  (9 to 12 day 13, "panels of violence") with no word of grade 3; Lawrence also opens grade 6 (6 to 8 day 1). Van Gogh
  grade 4 (3 to 5 day 65) and Level 1 (9 to 12 days 1, 7); Jaune Quick-to-See Smith grade 7 (6 to 8 day 104) and
  Level 3 (9 to 12 day 363); Dürer grade 1 (K to 2 day 83) and Level 1 (day 1); Monet grade 2 (K to 2 day 129) and
  Level 4 (unit 16); Rivera grade 5 (3 to 5 day 129) and Level 3 (unit 15); a Persian or Mughal painting grade 7
  (6 to 8 day 86) and Level 2 (day 280). Inside 9 to 12 the Migration Series returns are named (day 329).
- **Why it matters.** The repeats are defensible (different works or new tasks), but only when the day says so;
  the Migration Series is the same series, and the Level 1 hook shows the violent panels grade 3 kept back, which
  the family note should say.
- **Fix.** Each later day's note gets the El-Salahi form: "met in <course>, back with <different work or new
  task>". 9 to 12 day 13 adds that grade 3 saw chosen panels without violence and Level 1 adds the panels of
  violence, with its sensitivity line. Wording only.

### 9. Two task forms return without the step named (scope)

- **Where.** The group mural grid: grade 4 shows "the shared color plan and the grid that joins each member's
  section" with a step card and a grid drill (3 to 5 days 87, 88); grade 8 shows "the panel-and-grid method in steps
  ... squaring a thumbnail onto panels, edge marks where panels join, a shared color plan" as "the year 8 form
  technique, taught here in steps" (6 to 8 day 168). Inside 3 to 5: grade 3 draws a map with a key of the school
  (day 41) and grade 5 draws "the map or sketch set" of places that matter and labels it after a grounds walk (days
  168, 171), with no reference to grade 3.
- **Why it matters.** Both are reasonable returns, but as written a grade 8 or grade 5 teacher reteaches from zero
  and the step up is not visible.
- **Fix.** 6 to 8 day 168 note: grade 4's grid returns; the new steps are edge marks across panels and squaring to
  mural scale. 3 to 5 day 168 note: grade 3's map with a key returns; the new step is labels in art and design words
  for what matters. Wording only.

### 10. The portfolio leaves school each year, but 9 to 12 needs it two years later (scope)

- **Where.** 3 to 5 and 6 to 8 send the portfolio home on each year's last respond day; 6 to 8 still says "The band
  ends with a show of the band's kept work" (scope unit 12, Why here) though the year 6 and 7 folders went home.
  9 to 12 needs the Level 1 portfolio on Level 2 days 161, 203 and the Level 2 mid-year check (day 237, an open
  problem "from a different piece of the student's own Level 1 portfolio"), and the Level 1 reaction on Level 3 day
  328. The scope states no rule that the portfolio stays at school between levels, and days 203 and 237 have no path
  for a placed student (day 329 has one).
- **Why it matters.** A check item that rests on work the student may have taken home, or never made, cannot be
  given as written.
- **Fix.** 9 to 12 scope, unit shape bullet: the portfolio (or a photo record of each piece) stays at school across
  levels; a placed student uses the brought work of the placement route on days 203 and 237. 6 to 8 unit 12 "Why
  here": "a show of the year's kept work, with the band's earlier pieces as photographs".

### 11. Long writing jumps at Level 4 without a ramp (scope)

- **Where.** The longest writing before Level 4 is a paragraph: grade 8's 17-minute argued paragraph (6 to 8 day
  191), Level 3's competing-readings paragraph (9 to 12 day 413); labels run 50 words (Levels 1 and 2, days 134,
  306), 80 (Level 3, day 452), then 60 (Level 4, day 608). Level 4 unit 17 asks for a 1,500 to 2,000 word essay
  (day 547).
- **Why it matters.** The essay days scaffold it in sections, so it can work, but the jump from one paragraph to a
  four-page essay is not ramped by the scopes, and the label lengths do not climb.
- **Fix.** Level 3 day 413 asks for the competing readings and the defended one as a multi-paragraph argument of
  about 500 words; Level 4 labels at 80 words, matching Level 3. Wording only.

### 12. K to 8 courses meet on different weekdays (scope)

- **Where.** K to 2 and 6 to 8 put course days on each week's third and fifth school days (160 x y + ceil(2.5n));
  3 to 5 on the second and fourth ("Tuesday and Thursday"). Weeks agree, so frame.py and CALENDAR.md pass.
- **Why it matters.** calendar.csv places the 3 to 5 days, and their same-year leans (SL.3.1, math 4.G.A.3), one
  weekday off the other bands' convention; a school reading the three courses side by side sees two patterns.
- **Fix.** 3 to 5 adopts the same formula as K to 2 and 6 to 8 (third and fifth days) at its next edit; the lead
  regenerates calendar.csv. No week moves.

## What holds

- Seams: every band-start retrieval names real earlier teaching; the frames' "Picked up from" tables match the days.
- Calendar: all check weeks match CALENDAR.md (K to 5 weeks 17 or 18 and 25 or 26, 6 to 8 weeks 17 and 32, 9 to 12
  weeks 16 and 32); the K to 5 year-end checks before the showing unit and the 6 to 8 checks after it follow
  rules 1 and 3.
- Load: two 40-minute sessions K to 8, five 45-minute sessions 9 to 12; check sittings ramp from talk at the
  teacher's table (K to 2) to 15 written minutes read aloud (3 to 5), 27 (6 to 8) and 38 (9 to 12); every course
  has a once-a-week or 80-day path and a 10-days-short path that keep every id.
- Safety is one rule set: blades by an adult only K to 12, non-toxic labels, no powdered clay, damp-wiped dust, no
  heat below grade 6, no aerosol in 9 to 12.
- Sacred and ceremonial works are "studied by looking only" and never copied in all four; hard history is told in
  one plain sentence at K to 5 and in full at 9 to 12; the 18-and-over family note rule is in the 9 to 12 frame.
- Cultural range holds across the bands, with Native North American, African and Asian works in every band.
- Cross-subject leans (SL.3.1, 4.G.A.3, 7.G.A.1, W.6.1, W.8.1, W.11-12.1, ss6-paleolithic-neolithic) are each placed
  after the other subject teaches them or carry a bridge.
