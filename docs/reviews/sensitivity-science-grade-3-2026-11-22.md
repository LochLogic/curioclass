# Sensitivity read: science-grade-3 (course finish)

Kind: sensitivity. Target: science-grade-3, all six units (days 1 to 119 as written). Date: 2026-11-22. Read-only; this report is the only file edited.

## Verdict

Pass with fixes. The course handles its sensitive topics with care: hunting and death are told plainly and without images, no child is asked about a family or a disaster, and deep time is stated from evidence. The fixes are a response line for a child who becomes upset in the weather-harm days, a picture sequence that assumes a yard and a house, and the Arctic places shown with no people in them.

## What I studied

Several pages were thin or would not open; I say so rather than claim more than they gave.

1. North Slope Borough, Iñupiat History, Language and Culture (https://www.north-slope.org/departments/inupiat-history-language-culture/), the community's own government. Taught: uses "Iñupiat" for the people; frames the people as living, with language and traditions passed on; the page spells the town "Utqiagvik" in its links; it states no rules for outsiders.
2. North Slope Borough School District, Iñupiaq Education (https://www.nsbsd.org/en-US/inupiaq-education-c8d869a3), the community's own schools. Taught: instruction is built on "Iñupiaq values, culture, history, language and world view" and an Iñupiat-centered orientation; Iñupiaq people run the framework, so outsiders should not present the place as only a climate.
3. National Park Service, Iñupiat Heritage Center (https://www.nps.gov/nebe/learn/historyculture/inupiat-heritage-center.htm), the center is in Utqiagvik, opened 1999. Taught: Iñupiat people still practice subsistence whaling today; the people are present, not past.
4. Ready.gov kids (https://www.ready.gov/kids). Taught: preparedness is framed as something a whole family does together, in games and a character series; the page gave no guidance on tone or fear.
5. National Child Traumatic Stress Network, disasters overview (https://www.nctsn.org/what-is-child-trauma/trauma-types/disasters). Taught: disasters can bring displacement, loss of home and belongings, school changes and loss of loved ones; most children are resilient; knowing what to do helps families feel in control. The parent-guidelines page and its PDF did not open (a page with no body text, then a 404).
6. National Weather Service, lightning rods (https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-rods). Taught: a rod does not stop a strike; it gives a path and works only in a complete system. Day 111 matches this.
7. ILRU disaster policies and procedures PDF (https://www.ilru.org/uploads/legacy/resources/emergency_prep/Disaster_Policies_and_Procedures.pdf). Taught only its link titles (disability disaster safety, emergency preparedness and recovery); the body did not read. It supports the point that preparedness plans differ by person.
8. Family engagement: the National Network of Partnership Schools (https://nnps.jhucsos.com/). Taught: welcome all families as partners and customize to the local context. The NAEYC position page returned a 403 and was not tried again.

Not opened: the NSTA evolution position (404), the National Weather Service children's pages (404 or navigation only), the Red Cross disability page (403). Because of this, I could not study a national science teachers' body's guidance on teaching Earth history to families with differing beliefs. See "Could not settle."

Community note: the town's own sources use "Iñupiat" for the people and "Iñupiaq" for the language. I did not find a page by the community stating the town's spelling, so the form "Utqiagvik" is as the course already has it.

## Topics found

Families and children's home lives (family notes, "at home" parts, goat and animal "family" cards); animals dying and hunting (units 3 and 4); deep time and change in living things (day 82); fire, flood and storm harm (days 92, 108 to 112); Arctic and world places (days 59, 82, 105, 107); disability and access (day 112); food, allergies and living things in class (units 2 to 3).

## Findings

### Medium

**M1. No line for a child who becomes upset or starts to tell about a flood, fire or storm (u06-3 opener, days 92, 108, 109, 111, 112).** The course says three times that no one is asked ("Voluntary sharing. Some children may have lived through a flood, fire or storm. No one is asked to tell about it. Examples stay general."). That protects the child who stays quiet. It does not help the teacher when a child volunteers a story or gets upset: the NCTSN overview lists loss of home, displacement and loss of loved ones as what some families have lived. Fix: add to the u06-3 opener, and one sentence in the "Say first" paragraph of day 108 and the day 92 set-up: "If a child starts to tell about a flood, fire or storm they lived through, thank them, say 'that sounds hard,' do not ask for details, and keep the lesson on the paper example. If a child seems upset, let them step out with a helper or sit with you, and tell the school counselor or the family the same day. No one acts out a storm." The section plan says no reenactments; the day text does not.

**M2. The forecast picture sequence assumes a yard, a house and the means to prepare (courses/science-grade-3/u06-3/forecast-sequence-108.md).** Quoted: "A family carries a chair and a flower pot from the yard into the house." and "A person closes a window shutter and ties a gate." A child in an apartment, a shared home, a motel or a shelter sees no one like their own home. Day 108's reading also says "Tie down or bring in loose things from outside," with no word that people get ready in different ways. Fix: change the sequence to two or three kinds of homes, for example "A woman in an apartment moves a plant in from the balcony. A man on a porch ties a chair to the rail. A neighbor checks on an older neighbor." Add to day 108 after "People can get ready so it hurts less": "People get ready in different ways, and neighbors, schools and the weather service help." The limit box in days 109 to 112 already teaches that getting ready has a cost; this line names it for households too.

**M3. The Arctic places have no people in them (u06-2 cards-cairo-utqiagvik-105.md; u04-1 tundra-drawings.md; u05-1 hook, day 82).** Quoted: "Utqiagvik is a town on the Arctic coast of northern Alaska, in the United States. It is near the top of the map. The sea beside it is ice for much of the year." The section plan (s06.md) names borough and Iñupiat sources, but nothing in the day or card tells a child that people live there. The class meets Utqiagvik only as cold, ice and 8 months below freezing, which is the common error of showing the North as empty. The community's own pages and the NPS page show Iñupiat people living in Utqiagvik today, with a heritage center and subsistence whaling. Fix: add one line to the atlas card: "Iñupiat people live in Utqiagvik. Their language is Iñupiaq." and a teacher line in day 105: "Say that people live in Utqiagvik and in every place on these cards. The card tells only the weather." Do not add whaling or other detail without a source row; the NPS page supports "Iñupiat people still practice subsistence whaling today."

### Low

**L1. Cairo is only a desert (cards-cairo-utqiagvik-105.md).** "Cairo is a big city in Egypt. It is beside a river. The land around it is desert." Fix: "Many people live in Cairo." No number without a source row. Manaus ("a city in Brazil ... in a green forest") can take the same line; keep "Brazil" and "city," which are already fair.

**L2. "At home" parts assume a parent in 56 days ("The parent reads ...", "the parent draws ...").** Children live with grandparents, guardians, foster families and older siblings. Fix: "the parent" becomes "the adult" in the at-home parts of days; the family notes already say "your child" and are fine. This applies to all 56 uses; no separate pass is needed, fix a day when next edited (AGENTS.md rule).

**L3. Day 82: the answer to "how old is it" is short and gives a teacher nothing for a family who sees Earth's age differently.** Quoted: "If a child asks how old it is, or whether living things change over ages, say what the evidence shows: the trees grew when this land was warm. No one is asked what a family believes." Fix: add "If a child says their family sees it differently, say: 'People bring different ideas. In science class we look at what the rocks and fossils show.' Do not argue, ask the child to defend a belief or ask about the family." I could not open a science teachers' body's guidance, so this wording is mine; see "Could not settle."

**L4. Class mealworms: the days say they are "never handled" and "never released," and the frame says "kept, returned or released only as local rules allow."** I found no line in the days I scanned for what happens to the mealworms when the unit ends. Fix: one line in the u03-1 opener or the last day of unit 3: "At the end of the unit an adult returns the mealworms to the supplier or keeps them; they are not released outdoors and not thrown away alive."

**L5. UK spellings remain in 59 files (for example "colour," "centimetres," "favour").** AGENTS.md says fix them when next edited; no separate pass.

## What is already right (keep)

- Hunting, predators and death: "Hunting shown without graphic images" holds; the beetle death card is "photograph only"; "An animal that dies is told plainly, with no picture of it" (u05-2 opener); the musk ox is "not certain to die."
- Families: "Non-human families only. No child is asked about family resemblance" (u03-2 opener); goat, cat and fox examples carry the parents.
- Fire: "Do not ask anyone about a fire they know," "Sharing is voluntary," and "Fire is part of many places" (day 92).
- Weather harm on paper only: "Paper only: no flood is made" (day 109); lightning "no story and no storm watching" (day 111); day 108's warning is from another place and "not an account of damage."
- Access: the day 112 product card builds a step-free entrance for a wheelchair, shown as a design need.
- Bodies: "No people's weight or bodies" is kept; the weight cards are small metal weights.
- Allergy checks, mold checks and "may pass" rules recur on each hands-on day.
- Family notes: any answer in any language; nothing to buy; nothing to send back; "No one is asked to share a story from home."

## The three worst

1. M1: no response line when a child volunteers or is upset in the fire, flood and storm days.
2. M2: the forecast picture assumes a yard, a house and a radio.
3. M3: Utqiagvik shown as ice and numbers with no people in it.

## Could not settle

- Whether "Utqiaġvik" (with the Iñupiaq letter) should replace "Utqiagvik" in student text. The borough's own links use the plain form; I did not find a community page stating a preference. For the lead, and for Cory only if he wants the community's own spelling taught.
- Wording for a family who sees Earth's age differently (L3). The national science teachers' body's page did not open, so L3 rests on my own judgment. The lead may want an outside read before the grade is called finished, or leave day 82 as is.
- NCTSN's own guide for adults and the NWS children's pages did not open, so M1's wording rests on the overview page and on ordinary practice. The lead may want one more source before the line is used in a public teacher note.
