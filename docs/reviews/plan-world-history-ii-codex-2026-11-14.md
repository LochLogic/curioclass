## Unit 1: States, rights and revolutions

### 1. Moderate: The retrieval key changes the five source checks handed on by grade 9

**Location:** `s01.md`, field 4, day 1 retrieval row, line 74.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

The key calls “maker, date, audience, purpose and context” the five checks. The progression and World History I scope and sequence instead hand on **origin, authority, structure, context and corroborative value**. Maker and date are components of origin; audience and purpose do not replace authority or structure.

This could mark a student’s correct grade 9 retrieval as incomplete while overlooking missing judgments about access to knowledge and source form. The published [C3 framework](https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.pdf), printed page 54, also supports the inherited set.

**Exact fix:** Replace the prompt’s opening with:

> “Apply the five source checks to the hook’s declaration: origin, authority, structure, context and corroborative value. Write a supporting question and explain what evidence could answer it.”

Replace the key with:

> “Origin: maker, date and place. Authority: how the maker could know. Structure: the source’s form and organization. Context: circumstances surrounding its creation. Corroborative value: what independent evidence can confirm or challenge it.”

Keep audience and purpose as additional sourcing questions.

## Unit 2: The Industrial Revolution

### 2. Moderate: Wealth inequality is claimed, but the evidence principally measures income

**Location:** `s02.md`, field 5, item 2 and packet P1 to P3; field 5 daily evidence, day 19.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

P1 compares wage indices, prices and occupational counts. It correctly warns that these do not directly measure wealth ownership. However, item 2 claims coverage of wealth inequality, and its meeting sample discusses buying power and income gains without explaining ownership of assets. The teaching evidence has the same gap.

Students could therefore receive credit for treating wages and wealth as interchangeable. The anchor explicitly includes wealth distribution. See the [Massachusetts framework](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf), printed page 151.

**Exact fix:** In day 19’s evidence, explicitly distinguish income received over time from wealth held in assets minus debts. In the assessment:

- Add a clearly labelled constructed net-asset distribution row to P1.
- Add a sourced historical statement about land or factory ownership to P3.
- Replace item 2’s bare requirement to address “inequality” with “distinguish unequal income gains from unequal ownership of wealth.”
- Require that distinction in the meeting sample and scoring criteria.

Do not present the constructed row as evidence of England’s actual historical distribution.

## Unit 4: The First World War and its peace

### 4. Moderate: The assessment needs a genuinely shorter response form

**Location:** `s04.md`, field 5, assessment design and items 3 to 5, beginning at line 80.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** judgment call.

The arithmetic totals 50 minutes, but grouping the questions under five numbers does not reduce their response demands. Item 3 gives ten minutes for nine substantive subparts and an additional connection across four categories. Item 5 gives eight minutes for four different critiques involving evidence limitations, source suitability, historical accuracy across media and an expert appraisal.

The meeting samples across the assessment contain about 600 words before map labels. Those samples are illustrative, but the plan supplies no substantially shorter model demonstrating how every criterion fits. The risk is that writing speed displaces historical reasoning.

**Exact fix:** Replace the separate prose responses with these specified forms:

- Make items 3a to 3i one labelled table, followed by one connecting explanation. Provide a complete meeting response of approximately 100 to 120 words altogether.
- Make item 4 and 4a to 4b one integrated treaty evaluation, with separate rubric entries for negotiation, institutions, cultural effects and environmental evidence.
- Replace four separate item 5 responses with one approximately 80-word critique requiring an evidence limitation, an appropriate testing source, a comparison across media and use of the expert appraisal.

Keep all criteria visible. Recalculate the time from the resulting student packet, including reading and annotations. Renumbering the existing prompts alone does not resolve this finding.

## Unit 5: Revolution, depression and dictators

### 5. Serious: The compressed standards inventory omits required content

**Location:** `s05.md`, field 2, MA.WHII.T4.6 and T4.9; field 5, items 1b and 3; daily evidence for days 60 and 64 to 67.  
**LAYER:** plan, with a process cause. **Confidence:** firm.

The official framework includes Lenin’s personality cult within T4.6. It also directs students to analyze specific methods of repression under T4.9, including Nazi treatment of art, the Enabling Act, the Night of the Long Knives, and paramilitary and youth organizations. These details are absent from the local summaries and are not secured by the unit’s teaching or assessment prompts.

This is consequential because the skeleton presents the shortened summaries as the whole standards. A section writer following this plan could omit required learning while the gate still passes. See the [Massachusetts framework](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf), printed page 155.

**Exact fix:**

1. In field 2, add Lenin’s personality cult to T4.6’s required content.
2. Add a dated Lenin memorial or propaganda example to day 60. Extend item 1b’s change-and-continuity explanation to address how commemorating Lenin helped legitimize party authority.
3. In field 2, explicitly list the missing T4.9 requirements.
4. On days 64 to 66, identify concrete examples of political murder, censorship, propaganda, paramilitary organization and youth mobilization. On day 66, explicitly include the Enabling Act, the Night of the Long Knives and Nazi judgments about art.
5. Replace part of item 3’s repetitive general description with a compact evidence-to-method table covering these examples. Retain the comparative explanation and persecution analysis.

These are additions within standards already assigned to the unit, not new course scope.

### 6. Minor: A sentence can reverse von Mises’s stated position

**Location:** `s05.md`, field 5, item 2d meeting sample, line 85.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

The phrase “von Mises opposed credit-driven distortion and obstructed market adjustment” can be read as saying that he obstructed adjustment. His linked argument criticizes policies that obstruct market adjustment. See [his 1931 address](https://mises.org/online-book/causes-economic-crisis-and-other-essays-and-after-great-depression/3-causes-economic-crisis-address-1931/iv-there-way-out).

**Exact fix:**

Old:

> “von Mises opposed credit-driven distortion and obstructed market adjustment”

New:

> “von Mises opposed credit expansion and policies that obstructed market adjustment”

## Unit 6: The Second World War and the Holocaust

### 7. Moderate: Two expressly taught goals have no specified assessment evidence

**Location:** `s06.md`, field 5; days 75 and 77.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** likely.

Day 75 retains the substantial addition on occupation, forced labour and sexual slavery in Asia and the Pacific. Day 77 includes Jewish life before the war. The assessment addresses Holocaust roots, persecution and broad war consequences, but it does not explicitly assess either of these goals. Unlike surrounding units, this plan supplies no daily evidence prompts that close those gaps.

The issue is not that section plans remain unwritten. It is that the unit contract does not identify what evidence those writers must collect for these goals.

**Exact fix:** Add two entries under field 5’s evidence for section writers:

> **E75:** “Using today’s named account, explain one effect of occupation, forced labour or sexual slavery on civilian lives. Identify what the source establishes and one limitation.”

The key must name a specific harm, use a source detail and distinguish the account’s scope from a claim about every occupied community.

> **E77:** “Use one detail from the prewar account to describe Jewish life beyond persecution. Explain what Nazi policy threatened to destroy.”

The key must recognize varied lives, institutions or aspirations before persecution. Require nongraphic sources and do not ask students to imagine themselves as victims.

## Unit 7: The Cold War

### 8. Moderate: The nuclear-material consequence is assessed without the promised evidence

**Location:** `s07.md`, field 5, packet S and item 7; E104.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

The plan explicitly leaves material-proliferation examples unverified. Packet S supplies a nuclear agreement, then correctly explains that the agreement cannot establish the security of every weapon or material. Item 7 nevertheless asks students to evaluate weapons risks following dissolution.

Students need at least one documented material-control case, rather than only evidence that a treaty cannot settle the question. A usable source exists in [GAO-02-426, Appendix I](https://www.gao.gov/assets/a234393.html), which documents thefts and recoveries in former Soviet states.

**Exact fix:** Replace S’s final planning sentences with a short, attributed case. For example:

> “A 2002 government investigation records that highly enriched uranium was stolen from naval fuel storage near Murmansk in 1993 and recovered before leaving the area. This documents a material-control failure and recovery. It does not show that a complete nuclear weapon was stolen or that every facility was insecure.”

Use a different documented case in teaching. Require item 7 to distinguish complete weapons, weapons-usable material and an agreement about disarmament.

## Unit 9: A connected world since 1945

**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

Item 6 deliberately labels its paired political descriptions as constructed transfer material. That is honest, but those descriptions cannot establish how an actual European party presents its aims. **Exact fix:** Replace the generic descriptions in item 6 with short, attributed factual summaries of selected 2024 party positions. Name the party, document, date and location of each selection. Traceable starting documents include the [Rassemblement National 2024 programme](https://rassemblementnational.fr/documents/202411-programme-europeennes.pdf) and [Alternative für Deutschland’s 2024 programme page](https://www.afd.de/europawahlprogramm2024/). Verify selected pages before writing summaries.

Retain the [European Parliament’s constitutive-session table](https://results.elections.europa.eu/en/seats-political-group-country/2024-2029/) and its explicit snapshot date.

Then check the packet against its existing 600-word ceiling and minute allocation. Keep the distinction between publication year and observation year, and preserve the separation from teaching and portfolio sources.
