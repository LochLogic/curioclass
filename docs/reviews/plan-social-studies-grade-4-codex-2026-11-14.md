## Course verdict

The course has a coherent sequence, explicit teaching of inquiry skills, useful handoffs and careful treatment of living Native communities and hard history. It needs revision before lesson writing because assessment coverage has several gaps and the final two units demand substantially more work than their assessment minutes reasonably allow. Preserve the course’s content and day totals while making assessment evidence more explicit and distributing construction work across existing days.

## Unit 1: North America on the map

### 1. Moderate: the direction item does not distinguish error B

**Location:** [s01.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s01.md:76), field 5, Q1; field 6, error B.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

Error B is believing that the top of every page is north. The readiness map explicitly has north pointing up, and Q1 asks which ocean lies north of mainland Canada. A student can answer “Arctic Ocean” without demonstrating that they follow a map’s orientation.

This leaves the teacher without dependable evidence for the error the response plan proposes to address.

**Exact fix:** replace Q1’s ocean-direction question with a direction question on its measurement inset:

> The north arrow points right. P is directly above Q on the page. In which cardinal direction is P from Q? Explain using the arrow.

The answer is **west**. Keep the existing distance calculation. Replace the corresponding wrong answer with **“north, because P is higher on the page” (B)**. Specify this orientation in the packet description and provide a separate north-up locator where needed.

The ruler-distance and approximate conversion answers are correct.

## Unit 4: Voyages and conquest

### 2. Serious: day 64 locates the encounters at the camp without supporting evidence

**Location:** [s04.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s04.md:205), day list; `days.csv`, day 64, `goal`.  
**LAYER:** process. **Confidence:** firm.

The goal says students study evidence of meetings with Native peoples **at L’Anse aux Meadows**. Q2 and its key correctly explain that the camp does not establish a meeting at that exact site.

A lesson writer following the authoritative day goal could teach the unsupported location. [Parks Canada distinguishes the camp evidence from encounters elsewhere in the region.](https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/nl/meadows/culture/sites)

**Exact fix:**

- **Old:** “The Norse voyages and the evidence of their meetings with Native peoples at L’Anse aux Meadows.”
- **New:** “The Norse voyages, the camp at L’Anse aux Meadows, and the evidence and limits concerning encounters elsewhere along the North American Atlantic coast.”

Change the CSV goal first, then synchronize the plan’s mechanical day list. Retain Q2’s existing caution.

## Unit 5: How the United States grew

### 3. Serious: the end assessment omits the acquisition through war with Spain

**Location:** [s05.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s05.md:82), field 5, Q2 and reserved packet A.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

The assessment checks Louisiana, Oregon, Alaska, Mexico’s former lands and Hawaiian annexation. Puerto Rico appears in teaching and daily evidence, but no required final item checks the acquisition associated with the war with Spain.

This leaves one named acquisition pathway in the [original Massachusetts standard, printed page 63](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf), outside the assessment’s claimed whole-standard coverage.

**Exact fix:** add a Puerto Rico inset and dated record to packet A. Extend Q2’s compact location/date/method response grid with:

> Locate Puerto Rico. Give the year and explain how it came under United States control.

**Answer:** 1898; war with Spain followed by the Treaty of Paris. Distinguish territorial acquisition from statehood. The [Library of Congress treaty account](https://guides.loc.gov/world-of-1898/treaty-of-paris) supplies a traceable reference.

Use a short grid entry rather than another paragraph, and include it in Q2’s scoring criteria and timing.

## Unit 6: The regions: states, land and resources

### 5. Serious: both assessments require five constructed maps

**Location:** [s06.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s06.md:101), field 5, M4a to M4e and F3a to F3e; field 7.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** likely, based on the written workload.

The mid-unit assessment requires five thematic maps, potentially a sixth familiar-place inset, plus location, chronology and statehood tasks. The end assessment repeats five map constructions and allows **13 minutes** for them, approximately 2.6 minutes per map, before the remaining inquiry, economics and capital work.

Blank boundaries save tracing time, but students must still consult atlases, locate numerous features and construct functioning conventions. The unit plan has expanded the CSV’s “small drawn map” into five maps. This makes completion speed compete with the geography being assessed.

**Exact fix:** make the independent maps completed on days 114 to 118 an explicitly scored assessment portfolio. Record the familiar-place construction on day 113. Each map must follow modelling with different material and retain individual evidence for its own standard.

Replace the five-map blocks on days 120 and 131 with **one fresh small construction task each**, plus brief unseen interpretation tasks where needed. Every affected standard row must identify its portfolio evidence and final transfer evidence.

Replace field 7’s allocations with this starting design:

- **Day 120:** orientation 3, locating 9, chronology and statehood 6, one map 12, theme interpretation 5, marking 5.
- **Day 131:** orientation 3, locating and chronology 5, one map 10, inquiry and argument 10, economics 4, capital 3, marking 5.

These total 40 minutes each. Their final packets still need a timing check.

### 6. Serious: European exploration has disappeared from the Southwest map requirement

**Location:** [s06.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s06.md:29), fields 2 and 5, day 117 and Southwest map criteria.  
**LAYER:** plan and process. **Confidence:** firm.

The original standard includes **European exploration and pioneer settlements**. The local paraphrase substitutes early European settlement. The tasks then locate Spanish settlements but no exploration route.

A settlement symbol does not supply the missing exploration information. A writer following this plan would omit part of [MA.4.T4d.5, printed page 66](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf).

**Exact fix:** restore exploration in the local standard paraphrase, progression description, day 117 goal and map criteria.

Add a dated exploration-route layer. Suitable distinct selections are:

- Arizona: Anza’s **1774 exploratory route**, supported by the [National Park Service account](https://www.nps.gov/tuma/learn/historyculture/juan-bautista-de-anza-ii.htm).
- New Mexico: the local portion of the **1776 Domínguez–Escalante expedition**, supported by the [National Park Service account](https://home.nps.gov/dino/learn/historyculture/the-dominguez-and-escalante-expedition.htm).

Keep exploration routes visually distinct from forced-removal routes and settlement symbols. Apply finding 5’s portfolio change so this correction does not further overload the assessment day.

## Unit 7: The regions: peoples and hazards

### 8. Serious: most cultural groups receive recognition tasks rather than descriptive assessment

**Location:** [s07.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s07.md:88), field 5, C1 to C5 and scoring guidance.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

Students write about one group per region and underline the supplied contributions of the remaining groups. Underlining recognizes information already stated. It does not demonstrate the descriptive understanding claimed for every named group.

The earlier regional records could supply stronger evidence, but the plan does not explicitly score them as the assessment component carrying that coverage.

**Exact fix:** designate the individual regional records from days 138 to 143 as scored performance evidence. For every required group, require an independently expressed, source-linked description of a cultural practice, institution or contribution. Retain separate results by group.

Replace final C1 to C5’s twenty underlining requirements with a short unseen comparison of two regional examples. Update the assessment rows to name both the performance evidence and final transfer task. This retains the descriptive demand of the [regional standards](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf).

### 9. Serious: the final assessment is too large for 30 minutes

**Location:** [s07.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s07.md:215), field 7 and all final assessment tasks.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** likely, based on the written workload.

The final task requires five cultural descriptions and twenty markings, four hazard explanations, movement and continuity analysis, analysis of two catastrophes and their distant effects, policy development, an international comparison and a two-source argument considering another emphasis. Source orientation must also fit inside the 30 minutes.

The four-minute argument allocation is especially narrow after students have processed the preceding material. The likely result is incomplete evidence caused by writing and reading load.

**Exact fix:** apply finding 8’s performance-evidence change and use this bounded final design:

- Cultural transfer comparison: 5 minutes.
- Four regional hazard responses: 6 minutes.
- Combined movement and catastrophe task: 7 minutes.
- Combined policy and international comparison: 6 minutes.
- Short sourced argument: 6 minutes.

Retain the existing five-minute review and five-minute marking blocks. Use compact diagrams and response grids, and score the proposal’s existing evidence without making students reproduce it. Rewrite prompts and samples to match these bounded outputs before checking the packet’s timing.

### 10. Moderate: several historical assessment statements still lack matched sources

**Location:** [s07.md](/S:/CurioClass/docs/courses/social-studies-grade-4/s07.md:107), regional strips, historical exits and field 10.  
**LAYER:** plan. **Confidence:** firm.

Field 10 honestly marks the regional examples as “from memory pending verification.” However, the source list does not yet connect several specific statements to an identifiable supporting passage. Examples include Somali farming in Maine, Hmong markets, Vietnamese fishing communities and several Western contributions.

These are assessment-answer dependencies. A later writer should not have to infer which source establishes each statement.

**Exact fix:** add a source identifier and passage or page locator to every historical strip and exit statement. Record community, place and date alongside the claim.

Two verified starting points are the [Liberation Farms account](https://realmaine.com/member/liberation-farms/) for Somali farming and markets in Maine, and [California’s Allensworth record](https://ohp.parks.ca.gov/ListedResources/Detail/1047) for the town’s founding. Keep remaining claims marked **from memory** until their matched evidence is recorded.

The constructed H, M, S and P records are clearly labelled. I found no invented archival attribution in those cases.
