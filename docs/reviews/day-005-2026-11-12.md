## Verdict

Day 5 is correct mathematics in a sound order: a task where students discover the one-rectangle numbers, the
definition by exactly two factors, 1 settled by counting its factors, two worked searches (one prime, one composite
that looks prime), three sorts, a decade, the chart and the register's F and G fixed on purpose. The biggest risk is
that the day's goal says "prove it", and teaches that a prime needs a search, but after Example 1 no student ever
writes the search for a prime: the sorts, the decade and the exit accept the bare word "prime", so a student who
calls every odd number prime gets every prime right and is caught by one exit number (77). The one change that
matters most is finding 1: the exit becomes a table where a prime carries the last number tried, and the sorts ask
for it too.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. "Prove it" is half taught: no student writes the search for a prime

- **What is wrong.** The teacher page says "'prime' needs a search to the turn", and Example 1 prints the frame
  "I tried __ to __ and found no pair". After that, nothing asks for it. The sorts give "Prime: ____" with no room
  for the search, the decade asks only for rings, and the exit (s01-X5) asks for a pair for each composite and the
  bare word for each prime. On the exit, 59 and 73 are primes and 34 is even, so error F (odd means prime) is
  detected by 77 alone; a student who guessed "prime" for 59 and 73 is recorded as right on E5. The same holds on
  the end check (item 4: 71 and 53 primes, only 93 catches F).
- **Evidence.** items.csv s01-P5-2 to P5-5, s01-X5, s01-E4; d005-student.pdf side 1; d005-exit.html. IM grade 4
  unit 1 lesson 3 (read today) has students justify prime by finding every rectangle, not by look. The
  Progressions (OA, the 4.OA.B.4 paragraph) frame prime as the result of finding all factor pairs (from the saved
  copy as summarised in math-3-5-progression.md; not reopened). Same kind as day 84 finding 1 and day 85 finding 1
  (an exit that cannot show the error the day is built on).
- **Cost.** E5 says "fine" for students whose rule is still "odd and not in my tables"; they meet 91 or 87 on a
  later day and call it prime. The goal's second half is never evidenced.
- **Change.** See "Settled by the expert": exit `s01-X5v2` as a four-row table (number, prime or composite, the
  proof pair or the last number tried); the sort instruction adds "For each prime, write the last number you
  tried."; Example 1's frame gets its answer on the teacher page ("2 to 5"). Day 6's "Tomorrow" sign and the
  record sheet's E5 tick follow: a prime with no number tried is "s"-like, not a tick (write it as x with the note
  "no search").
- **Confidence.** Firm (the rows themselves; IM lesson 3).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 2. The look-fors reward the definition that lets 1 in

- **What is wrong.** The day teaches "Prime means exactly two factors, so 1 is neither", and Pia's fix turns on it.
  But the written task's third look-for (s01-W5) and its "meets" sample say "a prime has only 1 and itself as
  factors", and so do the challenge's sample and end-check item 5 (s01-E5v2, look-for 3 and its sample). For 1,
  "only 1 and itself" is true, so the phrase the key rewards is the one that makes 1 prime (error G).
- **Evidence.** items.csv s01-W5, s01-C5-1, s01-E5v2 `steps` and `samples`; d005.md "Teaching first". IM lesson 3
  uses "one factor pair" and then needs a separate talk to exclude 1, which is the trap this day avoids by counting
  factors. Same kind as day 83 finding 1 and day 93 finding 3 (the key prints a definition that contradicts the
  day).
- **Cost.** A teacher marking Ben's letter ticks the loose definition the day spent ten minutes replacing; a careful
  student who writes "exactly two factors" matches no sample word for word.
- **Change.** Look-for 3 in s01-W5 and s01-E5v2 becomes "says a prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself"; the
  "meets" samples end "A prime has exactly two factors, 1 and itself." Same wording in C5-1's "meets" if it names
  prime.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes. **CHEAP:** missed.

## What is right and should not be touched

- Prime defined by counting factors, "exactly two", with 1 settled by its one factor: more precise than "one factor
  pair", and it is what makes Pia's fix work.
- The opening task ("only one rectangle?") lets students find the primes before the word, and 1 sits on the list
  on purpose so the "Is 1 prime?" question has something to bite on.
- Example 2 (51, odd and not in the tables) is the register's F in the worked example itself; the "from ten
  times" step (30 + 21) links to day 4's fence and to multiplication.
- The even-number Ask (MP.8) with two right expected answers, one from pairs and one from rows.
- Fixes F and G on the day the register places them, with the right working beside the wrong.
- The hand-offs: day 4's three exit branches are each picked up; day 5's Tomorrow lands in day 6's F and G group
  and its "pencils down" hand-back.
- The numbers stay clear of the reserve (no 77, 93, 99, 71, 53 taught).
