## Verdict

The mathematics is right, the order (two worked perimeters, five fresh errors fixed, mixed practice, pull
together, exit) is a sound fluency day, and unlike day 90 it can be taught today on paper, because every piece it
leans on exists. The biggest risk is the generator sheet, which holds 22 of the 60 minutes: about a third of the
sheets it makes hand students a fact from the end check turned round, many put rectangles in silly units (a
classroom floor 10 centimeters long), and it prints 17 items and "30 minutes" against a day that plans 12 items
in 18. The one change that matters most: make the reserve list and the generator work on fact triples (the two
sides and the result, in any order), and cut the day 91 sheet to the twelve problems the day plans.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The generator sheet reuses the unit's facts, end check included

- **What is wrong.** RESERVED_S11 stores the numbers each item states, and `reservedHit` refuses a problem only
  when all of its stated numbers sit inside one reserved set. A fact turned round passes: the day 90 note "48
  square feet, 6 wide" reserves {48, 6}, so "8 yards long and 6 yards wide" (answer 48) is allowed. In a scan of
  1,000 sheets, 954 hold at least one fact already used in the unit, and 362 hold an end check fact: end item 6's
  11 by 8 = 88 (164 sheets), end item 1's 28 = 4 × 7 (121), end item 5's 4 × 5 = 20 (91). The hand-written board
  list in sheet-d091.md has the same fault: problem 1 is Lee's plot from day 90 (8 × 6 = 48) and problem 6 is
  Abebe's (4 by 8, 32), while the page says "None uses the unit's lesson or check numbers". The board list is
  also not in items.csv, so no gate sees it (LESSON-DAY rule 1).
- **Evidence.** measure-shapes.js lines 336 to 339 and 447; my scan (scratchpad script, reproducible with
  Gen.make over seeds T0 to T999); LESSON-DAY.md rule 3; the "known gaps" list in LESSON-DAY.md names this for the
  gate but not for the generator, whose sheet promises the opposite.
- **Cost.** A child meets 11 × 8 = 88 on Thursday's sheet and the same rectangle on the end check; the check
  then measures memory, not the standard. The sheet's own promise to the teacher is false.
- **Change.** (a) `plan.py reserve` writes, for each stretch item, the fact triple: for area the two sides and the
  area, for a missing side the same three numbers, for a perimeter the two sides and the perimeter. (b) The
  generator builds the same triple for every problem and fix it makes and refuses any match, in any order.
  (c) The six board problems become rows (`check` practice, `path` board, day 91) and sheet-d091.md names them by
  id, so the reuse gate covers them; their new numbers are under "Settled by the expert". (d) A check in
  check_generators.mjs fails the formulas skill if any sheet in its run holds a reserved triple.
- **Confidence.** Firm (read in the code, measured on 1,000 sheets).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** script.

### 2. The day, the sheet, the plan and the generator disagree about what the sheet is

- **What is wrong.** The day gives the sheet 18 minutes alone for "twelve mixed problems" and runs its own five
  fixes (F91 rows) before it. The generator prints those twelve plus five more fixes (items 13 to 17), heads the
  sheet "Time: 30 minutes", tells students "Sketch a rectangle if it helps" while the day says every answer is a
  sketch, and sheet-d091.md says "The fixes at the end are the same five errors the lesson just named". The plan
  (s11.md, days.csv) meant the sheet's fixes to be the day's five fixes; the writer made them rows instead, which
  is the better choice (they are in the key and on the display), but nothing was cut from the sheet. Even twelve
  is a lot: three three-digit perimeters added twice, two mixed-number perimeters, four missing sides, each with a
  sketch, an equation and a unit, is about 90 seconds a problem for a nine-year-old.
- **Cost.** A teacher hands out a sheet that says 30 minutes and has 17 items, in an 18-minute block, after ten
  minutes of fixes; most students stop around item 8, and the pairs' check compares half-done sheets.
- **Change.** The formulas skill gets a field `fixes` (on, off; default on for the Closet), and the day 91 link in
  sheet-d091.md sets it off, so the sheet holds the twelve. The sheet's `minutes` becomes 18 for this skill, and
  its directions say "Sketch each rectangle and label its sides. Write an equation, then the answer with its
  unit." Day 91 adds one line to the sheet block: "Most students finish 8 to 10 in 18 minutes. That is fine: the
  pairs check what is done, and the rest go home or into tomorrow's spare minutes." verifyFormulas counts 12 or
  17 by the field.
- **Confidence.** Firm on the mismatch (read in the built sheet and the code); the 90 seconds is a judgment call
  for the taught run.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read.

### 3. The generator puts real things in impossible units

- **What is wrong.** fxProblem picks the unit and the thing apart. Six sample sheets gave: a classroom floor 10
  centimeters by 5; a tile that covers 99 square meters; a field 406 inches by 35; a card 7 3/4 feet long; a garden
  bed of 45 square centimeters. Fixes say "A rug 12 meters by 9 meters". EDGE_THINGS uses "a pen", the word the day
  90 read changed for new English learners.
- **Cost.** A careful child stops to wonder whether a classroom can be 10 centimeters long; the day 90 read
  already replaced a 5 by 9 inch "poster" for being unreal. Sense-making with units is the day's goal (MP.6), and
  the sheet teaches the opposite.
- **Change.** Each thing carries its own units and size range: field (feet, yards, meters; 30 to 420), classroom
  floor, patio, hall (feet, meters; 6 to 60), rug, tablecloth, mural (feet; 3 to 12), poster, card, tile, picture
  (inches, centimeters; 2 to 30), garden bed (feet; 2 to 12). A bare "rectangle" takes any unit. "A pen" becomes "a
  goat pen" in EDGE_THINGS. Add a check that every story's unit is one its thing allows.
- **Confidence.** Firm (seen on generated sheets).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read.

- The worked examples: length plus width first, "half the fence", then the sum added to itself, with the four-side
  check after. This is the spine's rule for multi-digit sides and the Progressions' guard against error B.

  simplest form is asked (grade 4 limit).

  ask ("Does the floor cover, or go around?"). Fixes before practice is the right order.
- The exit asks the three parts the day teaches (area with a multiple of 10, big perimeter, fraction perimeter),
  with new numbers and three different answers (240, 766, 11), none the answer of a taught item.
