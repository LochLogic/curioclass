## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Unit 2 does not teach its owned accuracy standard in full

**Severity:** Major  
**LAYER:** standard, progression, frame  
**REPEAT:** new  
**Confidence:** firm

**Location:** Frame, Devices bullet and Unit 2, line 176; `g5-first-lateral-check`; `aasl-words.txt`, AASL.IV.B.3.

**Problem:** The frame treats finding a page’s maker and purpose through an external card as complete evidence for AASL.IV.B.3. The actual competency requires systematic assessment of information’s validity and accuracy. The own-words gloss has narrowed that requirement, and the progression and assessment follow the narrower gloss. [AASL learner framework, second edition](https://standards.aasl.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/250918-AASL-framework-for-learners.pdf).

Unit 2’s accuracy and verification work remains merely a “Candidate” in Check set adds. Unit 3 later marks factual differences without requiring students to resolve them.

**Why it matters:** A student could identify a credible maker and an informative purpose, accept an incorrect statement, and still pass the owned standard.

**Exact fix:**

1. Replace the AASL.IV.B.3 gloss with an own-words phrase that includes checking information, for example:

   > Check important information against other evidence before deciding whether to use a source.

2. Retain Unit 2’s maker-and-purpose lateral check, then require students to test one important factual statement against supplied independent evidence and mark it **supported, contradicted or unresolved**, with a reason.
3. Put that complete sequence in the show-me, practice, inquiry and unseen assessment cases. Include a credible-looking source containing an incorrect statement.
4. Update `g5-first-lateral-check`, its skill card, the Devices claim and the Check set read account accordingly.

Keep grade 6’s additional upstream tracing and contextual evaluation. No standard needs to move between grades.

### 2. Units 2 and 3 do not explicitly complete the promised host inquiry

**Severity:** Major  
**LAYER:** frame  
**REPEAT:** yes, incomplete inquiry cycles in the grade 3 independent read, finding 2, and grade 4 independent read, finding 1  
**Confidence:** likely

**Location:** Units and Inquiry arc bullets; Units 2 and 3, lines 176 and 177.

**Problem:** Every unit promises a full inquiry cycle on its host question. Unit 2’s detailed inquiry selects and checks sources, then shares them. It does not require students to use those sources to answer the sea-turtle or host question. Unit 3 produces notes, an outline and a bibliography, but never explicitly requires the resulting answer or conclusion.

The library approach requires a complete cycle with a shared product in each unit.

**Why it matters:** Writers can follow the detailed sequences faithfully while stopping at research preparation.

**Exact fix:** Amend the existing inquiry sessions:

- **Unit 2:** After selecting and checking sources, students use short excerpts to make a two-sentence answer to the inquiry question, linking each sentence to its source. They share that answer and explain one source decision.
- **Unit 3:** Students turn the organized notes into a brief answer stating what the sources agree on, what remains different or uncertain, and which evidence supports the answer. They share it with the bibliography attached.

Name these as the units’ products. Use brief supplied excerpts and compact templates so the work remains inside the existing sessions.

### 3. Unit 4’s group score does not establish each student’s audience-sharing outcome

**Severity:** Moderate  
**LAYER:** frame  
**REPEAT:** yes, insufficient individual evidence in a group task, grade 3 independent read, finding 3  
**Confidence:** likely

**Location:** Unit 4, line 178; Host tasks and Sensitivity reads bullets.

**Problem:** AASL.I.C.4 is owned and “checked alone,” but its evidence is the shared product’s rubric. The individual sitting checks what group research added and a choice of format for a hypothetical audience. Neither proves that each student actually shared research with the real audience.

The detailed route also restricts the audience to school, despite promising a course that runs at home.

**Why it matters:** A student can receive the group’s sharing score without contributing to the presentation. A home teacher must invent the participation and audience arrangement.

**Exact fix:**

> Use groups of three and a compact product template. On the share day, every student communicates one identifiable, source-backed part of the answer. The teacher records an individual sharing result beside the group-product result. Audience questions may be answered jointly, with contributions attributed.

Keep the share to approximately 20 minutes by limiting each group’s prepared presentation to about 90 seconds, leaving time for brief questions.

Add the home route:

> At home, the teaching adult researches a second subquestion and contributes findings to the pooling task. The child presents their own sourced contribution to that adult as the family audience and answers a question about it. The same individual criteria apply.

Clarify that the school audience restriction describes the classroom route. This uses the existing family audience and requires no additional outside participant.

### 4. The continuum comparison overstates three treatments

**Severity:** Moderate  
**LAYER:** frame, progression  
**REPEAT:** yes, incomplete check-set substitutions in the grade 3 and grade 4 independent reads  
**Confidence:** firm

**Location:** Check set read, lines 103 to 120; Units 1 and 3; course-wide self-check description.

**Problem:** Direct inspection of the stored continuum’s grade 5 column shows three distinctions the frame does not preserve:

| Grade 5 priority | What the frame provides |
|---|---|
| Navigating within selected websites and databases, table 0, row 18 | Changing queries and choosing results, with no explicit internal-navigation task |
| Choosing note-taking strategies according to purpose, row 21 | Taking notes and arranging an outline or mind map, without choosing a strategy for a reason |
| Reflecting on new understanding and the effectiveness of the product, presentation and inquiry process, row 36 | Rating research steps and naming a future change |

**Why it matters:** The comparison says these priorities are met, so later writers have no clear instruction to supply the missing actions.

**Exact fix:**

1. **Navigation:** Replace one Unit 1 practice search case with a printed menu or category-navigation case. Model finding the relevant page within a selected source, and include one fresh example in the assessment.
2. **Purposeful notes:** In Unit 3, model choosing between two familiar note layouts for different purposes. Require a layout choice and brief reason during practice and inquiry, and organized notes in the assessment. Reduce its four-source bibliography to three sources, retaining print, video, web and the unnamed-source explanation, to make room.
3. **Reflection:** Replace the step-only self-check with prompts covering what was learned, whether the answer or presentation communicated its evidence clearly, and one process improvement.
4. Record the treatments in the relevant progression notes and Check set read account.

These changes preserve the existing rows and ownership.

### 5. The year-end check’s workload is not supported by its component timings

**Severity:** Moderate  
**LAYER:** frame  
**REPEAT:** yes, period-capacity concerns in the earlier library frame reads; response allocation also repeats the grades 1 to 12 review, m1  
**Confidence:** likely

**Location:** Unit assessment timings, lines 175 to 177; year-end check, line 185.

**Problem:** The year-end check allows 27 minutes for a question judgment, query change, three justified source choices, a lateral check, notes comparing two passages and a three-source bibliography. Unit 2 alone budgets 25 minutes for three source choices and two lateral checks. Unit 3 budgets another 25 minutes for notes and a four-source bibliography.

Shorter cases may reduce the time, but the frame gives no component allocation establishing that the cumulative sitting fits. Its first respond session also has no explicit division between Unit 4’s deferred reteach and cumulative-check gaps.

**Why it matters:** Completion speed can displace the intended evidence, while Unit 4’s reteach competes with cumulative response.

**Exact fix:** Make the cumulative check a smaller sample:

| Component | Planning allocation |
|---|---:|
| One question judgment and one query change | 5 minutes |
| One justified source choice from three and one lateral accuracy check | 8 minutes |
| Notes comparing two brief passages and one bibliography entry | 12 minutes |
| Rereading and completion | 2 minutes |
| **Total** | **27 minutes** |

Retain the fuller unit assessment results as unscored records. Treat these allocations as provisional until the actual pages are tried.

Specify week 29 as **12 minutes for Unit 4’s missed rubric steps, 12 minutes for cumulative-check gaps, and five minutes for a fresh try**. Keep the yearly reflection in week 30.
