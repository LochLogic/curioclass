## 1. Do grades 3 to 5 ELA claim to teach foundational skills?

- **Why it is asked.** The rubric on file has a first gateway for grades 3 to 5 (reading long words, word parts,
  reading fluency). It is used only when a program is designed to teach those skills. The standards of those
  grades include them (RF.3.3, RF.3.4 and their grade 4 and 5 twins).
- **Choices.** (a) Yes: grades 3 to 5 teach them, the first gateway applies in full, and each of those spines
  starts from a taught sequence. (b) No: Curio Class says plainly that word reading is left to another program,
  and does not claim every standard for those grades.
- **The expert's pick:** (a) yes.
- **Where:** docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md, "What this adds to the plan", row 6. Report: docs/reviews/rubric-ela-2026-11-11.md.

## 2. New wording for the shared approach page on modelling in reading and writing

- **Why it is asked.** docs/APPROACH.md, principle 3, says that for reading and writing "we rely on modelling,
  which is common practice with weaker evidence". The federal guides rate it higher than that: strong for
  writing in elementary grades and in grades 6 to 12, strong for reading in kindergarten to grade 3 and, in an
  older guide, grades 4 to 12. Each rating is for the whole practice, with modelling as one part. Nobody may
  edit that page from Sara's list.
- **Choices.** (a) Take the expert's replacement wording for principle 3 and for the "thin or contested" list,
  given word for word in the report, finding F4. (b) Leave the shared page as it is and let the ELA page carry
  the correction.
- **The expert's pick:** (a).
- **Where:** docs/reviews/approach-ela-2026-11-11.md, F4. The ELA page already says it under "Modelled reading
  and writing".

## 3. May the text record, question set and word list live in data, outside the word cap?

- **Choices.** (a) They become rows in data/plans/, shown as generated tables that do not count toward the cap,
