Line numbers are file lines (frontmatter included). Source support was judged against each row's status text; no page was re-fetched. ## Must-fix (4)

**M1. A colonist's slur is printed for children.**
File: u05-2/cards-day95-accounts.md line 28.
Text: "Exact words for the teacher to show: 'where the Saluages dwelt there the ground is exceeding fat & fertill.'"
Problem: "Saluages" is the old spelling of "savages". d095.md line 28 says "He used a harsh old word for the people; we do not repeat it," so the card contradicts its own day. "Show" puts it in front of children.
Fix: print only "the ground is exceeding fat & fertill" (as d095 line 28 already does) and drop "where the Saluages dwelt". The plain summary beside it already says "where the people lived".

**M2. Virginia table says slavery was legal in 1619 and says Africans "arrived".**
File: u05-6/cards-days111-112.md lines 22 to 27 (table row "Virginia (Jamestown) | 1607 | South | Legal. Africans arrived in 1619."); u05-6/opener.md line 16 ("Africans arrived in Virginia in 1619, and slavery was legal there").
Problem: row U5AE says slavery "was not yet a law in Virginia then and was recognized in Virginia law in 1662", and says to write that people were brought against their will, never "workers who came". "Arrived" is the exact framing the row forbids, and "Legal" beside 1619 is wrong.
Fix: "Slavery: 1619, about twenty Africans were brought against their will (captives taken from a ship). In 1662 Virginia law recognized slavery." d111.md line 28 has the right words; copy them. Also add "taken from their homes" there, as the row asks.

**M3. Pembroke route states more than the row supports.**
Files: u05-7/d116.md line 84 (student page: "the schooner Pembroke sailed from Salem. It carried wood, horses, rice and fish out. It came back from Dominica"), line 27 ("Draw an arrow from Salem to Dominica and a second one back"); u05-7/cards-days113-116.md line 79; item s05-P116c ("Which way did the ship sail first, from Salem? South.").
Problem: U5S (checked 2026-11-21) records only that the Pembroke returned to Salem from Dominica; "the page does not state the outbound port." The outbound leg, its start at Salem and its end at Dominica are not supported, and P116c grades the direction of that unsupported leg. Dominica itself is supported.
Fix: write "In 1764 the schooner Pembroke came back to Salem from Dominica, a Caribbean island, with sugar, coffee and molasses. It had carried wood, horses, rice and fish out." Trace one arrow, Dominica to Salem; draw the outbound leg dashed and label it "route not stated". Change P116c to the return direction (north) and P116d is fine.

**M4. A list that sets enslaved people among goods.**
File: u05-7/d114.md line 97 (student page: "Fish. Lumber. Sugar. Rum. Enslaved Africans."); item s05-P114c ("Sort the list into goods and people carried by force"); cards-days113-116.md line 43.
Problem: people are printed as the fifth item in a column of commodities, and children must file them. Even with a correct answer, the page shows people as an item. The teacher line (d114 line 17, "never goods or cargo") is right; the page undoes it.
Fix: print only "Fish. Lumber. Sugar. Rum." with the question "Which are goods?", and give people a separate prompt: "Who was carried across the sea by force? Write who they were." Re-key P114c to match. Also see S3 on d115.

## Should-fix (11)

**S1. d115 W115a route-card sort (asked about).**
File: u05-7/d115.md lines 22, 68; cards-days113-116.md line 55.
Text: "one ship carries tools to a harbor and brings back rope; a second ship carries people against their will."
Judgment: acceptable in purpose (error T, mixing people with goods), and "against their will" is the right plain phrase, but the parallel build (tools, rope, people) invites a column of three things. Not acceptable as a sort of "three loads".
Fix: "A ship carried tools to a harbor and brought back rope. A different ship carried people who had been taken from their homes and forced aboard. Which of these are goods? Which are people?" Add "people are never cargo or goods" to the teacher line, and use the stop card line already on d114.

**S2. Seloy card may misname a leader as a town.**
Files: u05-3/cards-days97-99.md lines 20, 25; u05-3/d097.md lines 24, 27; item s05-P97e; u05-8/packet-day120.md line 55; u05-1/d090.md line 25.
Problem: row U5A reads "Seloy's town" and "Seloy's council house", and cuts "any Timucua leader's decision at Seloy". The cards call it "Seloy, a Timucua town" and Card 2 says "Name: not in our record". "Seloy's" reads as a person's name. Person look first at the museum page. If Seloy is the leader, Card 2 has a name and P97e is wrong.
Fix: use the row's own words ("Seloy's town") until a person confirms; if Seloy is a person, rewrite Card 2 and P97e.

**S3. Card B4 shows only the Timucua attack.**
File: u05-8/packet-day120.md line 55; key-day120.md line 22.
Text: "Relations worsened, and in 1566 the Timucua attacked the fort."
Problem: the card leaves out that the Spanish made the council house into a fort (same row, taught on d097). A child meets a one-sided picture on the end check, with the Timucua as the aggressors.
Fix: "The Spanish made a Timucua council house into a fort. Relations worsened, and in 1566 the Timucua attacked it."

**S4. Card B (Virginia Company) and Card A over-claim.**
File: u05-3/cards-days97-99.md lines 45 to 46.
Problem: Card A says "Powhatan, led by the Pamunkey and five other core tribes"; U5C says the Pamunkey were one of six core tribes, not that they led. Card B says the company "paid for the James Fort"; U5L says it "founded" James Fort. d098 line 30 has the correct versions.
Fix: Card A "Powhatan; Wahunsenaca led about thirty groups; the Pamunkey were one of six core tribes." Card B "founded James Fort in 1607."

**S5. d099 Label 2 frames 1622 in the company's voice.**
File: u05-3/d099.md line 97; cards-days97-99.md line 76.
Text: "An official report on the 1622 attack, to reassure investors and migrants."
Problem: U5O says "official response to the 1622 attack." Printing "the attack" with no maker in the label states the colonists' view as fact, on a lesson about two sides. "Report" is also not the row's word.
Fix: "An official response to the 1622 fighting, written to reassure investors and migrants. It is the company's side." Name Waterhouse as author.

**S6. Wahunsenaca spelling overrides the nation's own.**
File: u05-3/d098.md line 30; opener.md; sources.csv U5C note.
Problem: the Pamunkey page spells the name Wahunsonacock. The unit says "we write it as the card does." Rule 2 asks for respect for the nation's own names.
Fix: use the Pamunkey spelling, with "also called Powhatan", or print both once.

**S7. Native-nation facts rest on Wikipedia.**
Rows U5K (Samoset "an Abenaki sagamore"), U5AC (Metacom), U5AA (praying towns, 20 percent figure), U5Y (the harvest gathering, and "Wampanoag descendants say it was first set up for the colonists alone").
Problem: none is a nation's own site, museum, government, university or Britannica. U5Y is the sharpest case: the card and d118 line 76 and d118 credit call it "from Wampanoag descendants" and a "source" with a maker, but the maker is a Wikipedia article. Children are taught maker, when and why, and this one is mislabelled.
Fix: re-source from the Pilgrim Hall pages (it has Samoset, King Philip's War and harvest pages) or a Wampanoag tribal page; until then label these "a modern reference page" and remove "Wampanoag descendants" as the maker in d118 and cards-days100-103.md line 105.

**S8. Wabanaki card misattributes its maker and states today unsupported.**
File: u05-4/d103.md lines 94 to 116; cards-days100-103.md line 109; item s05-P103h.
Text: "Made by the Sipayik Tribal Government... The Abenaki and Wabanaki nations live today."
Problem: U5G is an essay by Harald Prins on the Sipayik site; the row says to label it a modern account. Nothing in the row says the Abenaki and Wabanaki live today. Samoset as "an Abenaki sagamore" matches U5K only.
Fix: "A modern essay on the Sipayik Tribal Government website." Add a row for a nation's own page that supports "live today", or cut the sentence.

**S9. Compact numbers and dates.**
Files: u05-4/cards-exits-100-104.md line 21 ("Forty-one men signed in all"); cards-days100-103.md lines 33 and 58; d101 line 102; key-exits-100-104.md line 14.
Problem: U5P says forty-one names, not "men". The key then builds "women or children did not sign" on it (the row cut that claim). Also the Mayflower anchored near Cape Cod on 21 November (U5L), but the Compact was "signed at Cape Cod on 11 November": children see the signing ten days before the arrival (the two sources use different calendars).
Fix: "forty-one names"; the E101 answer "Native nations, or any group not on the signer list"; give both dates one calendar, or say "in November 1620" and drop the day.

**S10. Servants and enslaved people.**
Files: u05-5/cards-days106-110.md line 91 ("held for life and could be sold") and line 97 ("Eli is held for life and is sold"); u05-6/cards-days111-112.md line 31 ("They were enslaved for life"); u05-7/cards-exits-113-116.md line 21 and cards-days113-116.md line 83; u05-8/packet-day120.md line 22; cards-day121-fresh.md line 28.
Problem: U5R does not record "held for life" or "could be sold". Eli's card shows a person as sold, while the student pages (d109) wisely do not. Also "enslaved workers" (A3 and item s05-Q3), "three workers" (day 121 Group D), "workers who could not leave and were never paid" (E114 card) and "goods and workers gathered at ports" (d116) name enslaved people as workers.
Fix: drop "could be sold" and "is sold" from the cards; either add a source row for "for life" or phrase as "the law let enslavers hold people against their will". Write "an enslaved person" or "people who were enslaved", "three people who worked there", "people who were held against their will and never paid".

**S11. d104 and item s05-P104c claim each colony's "first" nation.**
File: u05-4/d104.md line 28; item s05-P104c ("St. Augustine: the Timucua. Jamestown: the Powhatan nations. Plymouth: the Wampanoag.").
