### 6. Day 79: the exit tests the 1862 land laws, which the day no longer teaches (origin section)

The cheap read's minutes fix moved the Homestead and Pacific Railway Acts entirely to the exit: Sources D and E are
uncovered and read aloud for the first time in the last ten minutes, and X79 then asks students to "explain how one
1862 land or railroad law extended federal power, and whose land it was". Nothing in the day teaches what "federal
power" means here, and the X79 key's place for Davis ("signed in Richmond") is in no source either. An exit that is a
student's first meeting with a topic measures cold reading, not the day's teaching, so the next day's repair cannot
tell a teaching gap from a reading gap. A teacher will also see the "I can" promise (whose land the laws gave away)
taught by nobody. **Fix:** in the section plan, give the land laws a taught slot (take 5 minutes from Teaching first's
battle facts card, which the organizer covers again, and read Sources D and E there with the "Indian titles" gloss),
then make the exit's law part an application to a fresh short clause (for example the Railway Act's land-grant
section) or drop it from X79 and let end item 4 carry it. Change the X79 sample's "signed in Richmond" to a place a
source gives, or accept "across the Confederacy". Confidence: likely.

### 7. Day 79 and 80: error K's repair asks students to name nations no student source names (origin unit)

errors.csv K, the section plan's error table, the opener and days 79 and 80 all repair "empty land" by reading
"Indian titles" and naming the nations. No source on the day 79 sheet names a nation; only the teacher's Words and
supports does ("the Lakota and Cheyenne"), and that list is partial: the Union Pacific line ran across Pawnee,
Lakota, Cheyenne and Arapaho lands among others, and the Central Pacific across Washoe, Paiute and Shoshone lands
(from memory; to check). So the repair becomes the teacher telling, and a student cannot test the claim from
evidence. This sits beside the open finding that the Native nations content has had no sensitivity read; it is a
distinct fault, in the evidence. **Fix:** add one short checked source or a facts line to the day 79 sheet that names
the nations whose land the 1862 grants crossed, with its own source line (a treaty or a federal map of land
cessions), and change the error K repair to "read Source E's 'Indian titles' beside that line and name two nations".
Run the pending sensitivity read on it. Confidence: likely.

### 1. Day 78: the "neutral" exit read hands students the obstacle, and misstates the source line (origin day)

The exit step says to read Source E "neutrally; its first lines say Conkling's meeting was full of Union men who
disliked the Proclamation". The passage's first lines say nothing of the kind; the source line says Lincoln wrote to
"those among them who disliked" it, not that the meeting was full of them. Said aloud, the teacher's line also gives
away the obstacle look-for (the X78 sample's obstacle is exactly this), so the exit no longer shows who can find an
obstacle in a source. **Fix:** "Read its source line and passage aloud, neutrally. Do not explain them." Confidence:
firm (the sheet and the key disagree with the line).

### 2. Day 78: the Conkling passage does not match the printed text, and sources.csv says it has no dashes (origin unit)

The sheet prints "Why should they do anything for us if we will do nothing for them? If they stake their lives for
us they must be prompted by the strongest motive, even the promise of freedom. And the promise, being made, must be
kept." From memory, the Collected Works text reads "do any thing for us, if we will", "for us, they must", a dash
before "even the promise of freedom", and "And the promise being made, must be kept." sources.csv 78E says "no dashes
in the passage", which would then be wrong. Students are taught that wording matters in sources. **Fix:** recheck
the four sentences against Basler vol. 6 (or the American Presidency Project transcript), print them as written with
the dash shown as a comma and the sheet saying so, and correct the 78E note. Confidence: likely (from memory; the
check settles it).

it as written; the gloss can stay. Confidence: likely (from memory).

### 4. Day 79: "Lee asked Davis to let him enter Maryland" (origin day)

Source B is Lee's own proposal, and the organizer asks for Lee's decision. Lee wrote on September 3 and, from memory,
wrote again on September 4 that he would proceed unless Davis disapproved; his army began crossing the Potomac that
day, before any answer. "Asked to let him" turns Lee's decision into Davis's, which is the confusion error D
targets. **Fix:** "Lee told Davis the time was right to enter Maryland, and began crossing before Davis could reply."
Confidence: judgment call (the battlefields.org page for 79B calls it a request).
