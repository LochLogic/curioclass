## Verdict

The history on the teacher page is right in what it says, and the day is honestly marked not ready: four of its
five sources are placeholders, so it cannot be taught or its exit given. The biggest risk is in the plan files, not
the prose: the unit's repair for error C ("shade the Proclamation's areas and add Fortress Monroe") would mark as
covered a fort the Proclamation itself excepted, and the exit's fourth look-for ("a limit or an obstacle") lets a
student who still believes error C pass it, so the repair can never fire on evidence. The one change that matters
most is to split that look-for and teach the reach with the excepted places named, Fortress Monroe among them.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The error C repair puts Fortress Monroe inside the Proclamation's reach; it was excepted

- **What is wrong.** errors.csv (unit 5, C, `if_many`), s05.md field 6, u05-2.md field 4, the opener's error table
  and d078 Tomorrow all say: shade the Proclamation's areas on the outline map, then "add Fortress Monroe". The
  Proclamation excepted Elizabeth City County, Virginia, by name, and Fortress Monroe is in it. People who reached the
  fort were held under Butler's policy and the Confiscation Acts, not freed by the Proclamation. A teacher shading
  "Virginia" on a state-level 1863 outline map will shade the fort, and the repair will teach a wrong fact on the
  very day it is meant to fix one.
- **Why it matters.** It is a key error in a reteach (C3a), and it hides the best example the section has of the
  gap between legal declaration and actual freedom, which is the day's own point.
- **Fix.** errors.csv C `if_many` and the same line in s05.md, u05-2.md field 4, the opener and d078 Tomorrow:
  "Shade the Proclamation's areas on the day 79 outline map; leave the excepted Virginia counties round Norfolk and
  the loyal slave states unshaded; mark Fortress Monroe there and ask why people were already free of their
  enslavers at a fort the Proclamation left out." Add to the opener's first teacher bullet: "The excepted Virginia
  counties included Elizabeth City County, where Fortress Monroe stands." The day 79 outline map (still to make)
  must show the Norfolk-Hampton exception as an inset.
- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT yes (a wrong fact in a key or reteach). CHEAP missed. ORIGIN unit. Confidence firm.

### 5. The section plan does not have the teacher explain why the Proclamation stopped where it did

- **What is wrong.** u05-2.md field 1 says the teacher page must explain "why the Proclamation freed no one at once
  in Kentucky or Maryland and still changed the war". d078 says only that it "did not apply" there. The why is
  missing: Lincoln issued it as commander in chief, as a war measure against states in rebellion, which is why it
  could not reach loyal states, and keeping the border states loyal was itself an obstacle.
- **Why it matters.** Without the reason, "not Kentucky" is a fact to memorise and the link the exit asks for

  (finding 6) without new minutes.
- **Fix.** d078 Pairs, after "or named exceptions": "Lincoln issued it as commander in chief, as a war measure
  against states in rebellion. His war powers did not reach loyal states, and he needed Kentucky, Maryland and
  Missouri to stay in the Union." (about 35 words; the page is at 741).
- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT new. CHEAP missed. ORIGIN day. Confidence firm.
