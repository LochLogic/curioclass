## Verdict

Day 9 is a short, sound assessment day: every key is right, every part of 4.OA.C.5 has an item and each item is
coded honestly to the parts it tests, and a teacher can run the hour from 495 words. The biggest risk is that the
assessment cannot see the unit's second key moment: item 4 asks for steps 1 to 6 in a row, so day 8's goal (a
later step without drawing) and error B are never tested, yet the respond group teaches the jump method for it.
The most useful change is to make item 4 ask for one later step (E4v2, settled below); next, open item 2's
accept list so students self-mark it fairly, and judge item 5 on each student's own item 4 numbers.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Item 4 cannot show day 8's goal or error B, but the respond group teaches the jump for it

- **What is wrong.** Day 8's goal is "find a later step without drawing them all", the section plan's key moment
  2, and error B ("draws or counts every step to reach a later one") has its own "if many" action. Item 4 asks
  for steps 1 to 6, each one jump after the last, so adding 5 five times is the efficient and right way; no
  student needs a later step. The B row ("draws all six steps and miscounts, or runs out of time") describes a
  slip, not B: drawing is never wrong on this item. The A and B group then teaches "Say step 6 in words first:
  6 + 5 × 5 = 31", a move item 4 never asked for.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s02-E4 and wrongs.csv; s02.md U5 key moment 2 and U6 day 8 goal; d009.md Groups.
- **Cost.** The record sheet's End 4 column cannot tell a student who still draws every step from one who jumps,
  so Check 1 (day 42) and unit 13 ("100th shape") start with no evidence on the move they build on.
- **Change.** Settled below: E4v2, "Step 1 is one square sitting on a row of 5 squares. The rule: each step adds
  one more row of 5 underneath. Draw steps 2 and 3. Write the number of squares in steps 1 to 4. How many
  squares are in step 9?" Key: 6, 11, 16, 21; step 9 is 6 + 8 × 5 = 46 (or 1 + 9 × 5 = 46). Compute `1+5 |
  1+2*5 | 1+3*5 | 1+4*5 | 6+8*5`, value `6 | 11 | 16 | 21 | 46`. Wrongs: "5, 10, 15, 20; step 9: 45" (A, the
  one on top left out); "51" (no letter: 9 jumps counted from step 1, not 8); "all nine steps drawn" (B). Student
  page: "Steps 1 to 4: ___ ___ ___ ___ Step 9: ______". Group A and B: "Say step 9 in words first, 'the first
  shape and how many jumps?': 6 + 8 × 5 = 46." Day version up (rule 8). Item 5 still reads "your numbers for item
  4" and its key holds.
- **Confidence.** Likely (the standard does not demand a later step; the unit's own goal and register do).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 6 finding 1, an error the end-of-unit assessment
  never tests). **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read judged item 4's A and B apart; they are apart only as a slip).

### 2. Item 2 is self-marked against a closed list, and three of its four features stop at 100

- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (s11 plan, "self-marked keys lacked accept lists"; day 6
  finding 7). **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read checked the four features hold on the five numbers).
