## Verdict

The day is not teachable as written, and the reason sits above the day: its central task is to draw a map, and the
student has no map, no outline and no locations to draw from (the fact cards give names, not places), so the map
part is a memory test of African and Eurasian geography. The second risk is that the day's checks do not measure the
day: the warm-up treats a foreign traveller as a "court source", and the exit is the worked example with its nouns
swapped, answerable without any source. The one change that matters most is to put a located reference (our outline
maps, or until then a printed locator sketch per option) in students' hands before day 42 runs; the sourcing,
facts and care work on the sheets is otherwise strong and should be kept.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The map task has nothing to map from

What is wrong: P42-2 asks for "a dated map of your option with a key, a scale and its routes" with "centres and
routes placed". The outline maps are "still to draw" (materials.csv; u03-4 field 11). The section plan's stand-in
(field 5) is "students draw on squared paper from the fact card's coordinates and towns", but no fact card has a
coordinate, and the cards place towns only by modern country ("Koumbi Saleh ... in today's Mauritania"). An option a
student cannot set Walata, Timbuktu, Gao and Djenne relative to the Niger and the Sahara; an option b student cannot
set Sofala a fortnight south of Kilwa on a coastline they have never been shown; an option d student must draw
Eurasia from memory. The day's patch ("schematic sketches ... not to scale") answers the scale look-for, not the
placing one. The cheap read saw the scale word (its finding 2), not that nothing can be placed.

Why it matters: the standard's verb is "map"; 25 of the day's 50 minutes and A2's map component rest on it. Students
will copy whatever map they find on a phone, which brings in error B (modern borders) the day warns against, or will
draw boxes that cannot be marked for "places". A teacher with no prep has nothing to hand out.

Fix: before day 42 ships, give each option sheet a located reference. Best: the three outline maps (art step).
Until they exist: add to each option sheet a small printed locator sketch, or a "where it lies" line per town in
relative terms (Walata: southern edge of the Sahara, west of Timbuktu; Timbuktu: on the Niger's northern bend; Gao:
downriver, east; Kilwa: island town on the coast of today's Tanzania; Sofala: about 1,000 miles south; Karakorum,
Khanbaliq, Baghdad, Samarkand for option d). Change the u03-4 field 5 fallback to name what is actually printed, and
P42-2's look-for "centres and routes placed" to "placed as the sheet's locator shows".
Confidence: firm (materials.csv, field 5 and the cards disagree on the page). ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert;
REPEAT yes (day 79 finding 7, the 1863 outline map); CHEAP missed.

### 2. Source A is taught as a "court source"; it is a foreign visitor's account

What is wrong: s03-P42-1's stem is "Sort three statements about a ruler of Mali into what a court source shows and
what it cannot show". The day's Ask follows it: "Which statement needs evidence from people outside the court?" But
Source A is Ibn Battuta, a Moroccan judge writing for readers at home, which the same day stresses ("Identify maker,
date and audience: outsiders addressing readers at home"). P42-2's sample answer says "the court source shows a ruler
with armed guards and gold" too. A student who has just been told Ibn Battuta was an outsider can fairly answer the
Ask with "Ibn Battuta was outside the court, so his account shows support", which is error L passing through the
door the warm-up was meant to close.

Why it matters: the warm-up is the repair of error L. The real point (the ceremony is the court's self-display,
recorded by a visitor who saw the display, not the subjects' views) is sharper than the one printed, and the
mislabel teaches a wrong sourcing habit on the day the unit asks for maker, date and audience.

Fix: P42-1 stem "Sort three statements into what a visitor's account of a court ceremony shows and what it cannot
show about the ruler's subjects." Day 42 Ask: "Which statement would need the voices of ordinary people of Mali?
Expect the support claim: the visitor saw a display the court staged." P42-2 sample: "Ibn Battuta's account shows
...". Opener error L line: "A court ceremony, or a visitor's account of one, proves consent."
Confidence: firm. ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (day 80 finding 6, a maker misdescribed);
CHEAP missed.

### 3. The exit is the worked example with the nouns swapped, and uses no source

What is wrong: K42-1: "at a mountain-pass market a king's deputy takes a share of every load of copper ... a map
label and a two-sentence explanation of its politics and its economy." X42: "a ruler taxes a river crossing that
inland farmers and miners supply. Mark the connection on the map, and explain what it meant for the ruler's power
and for the economy." Same structure, same look-fors (who collects, who works), 30 minutes apart; the K42 sample
answer can be copied with "pass" changed to "crossing". Neither uses a source, while the day's goal is "explain its
politics and economy using sources" (Side 1 goal line; P42-2 look-fors 3 to 5).

Why it matters: LESSON-DAY "What changes by subject" sets "a new situation" on a check; a surface swap is not one.
The exit measures recall of the model, not whether a student can draw politics and economy out of evidence, so the
record sheet's day 42 column and day 43's repair (error I) rest on weak evidence. The lens names it: an exit that
checks the wrong thing.

Fix (settled below): keep X42 invented and reserved, but change what it asks: a three-line invented traveller's
note ("At the ford the king's men take one bar of salt in ten; the carriers say the salt comes from pans twenty
days north") printed on the ticket, and ask: mark producer, carrier and collector on a sketch, and say one thing the
note shows about power, one about the economy, and one thing it cannot show. Look-fors: arrow from producer through
carrier to collector; power and economy each tied to a words-in-the-note detail; one limit.
Confidence: likely (one rule line plus a side-by-side reading). ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes
(day 78 finding 3, day 80 finding 1); CHEAP missed.

### 4. The X41 hand-back drops error A, and the warm-up is coded to a standard it does not teach

What is wrong: s03-X41's wrongs are error A ("He was a dynasty, not an empire.") and error L. handbacks.csv routes
X41 to day 42 with error L only; nothing anywhere repairs A. The section plan says "MA.WHI.T3.1. The words for power
return in day 42's warm-up and day 44's discussion", and P42-1 is coded MA.WHI.T3.1, but P42-1 uses no word for
power at all: it is a sourcing task (shows and cannot show).

Why it matters: a third of the class writing "dynasty, not empire" on day 41 gets no repair before A1 on day 45, and
the coverage trace credits T3.1 practice that does not happen.

Fix: handbacks.csv 41 to 42 gains error A; P42-1 gets one line that names the words ("The ruler is the brother of
the last mansa and rules many peoples: which two words for power fit?") or the plan drops the claim and recodes
P42-1 to D2.His (sourcing). The day's warm-up adds: "If many showed error A, apply dynasty and empire to Mansa
Sulayman together (Musa's brother; many peoples)."
Confidence: firm. ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (day 82 finding 1, coded to what it does not
teach); CHEAP missed.

### 5. The exit stem asks for a map that does not exist and a market it does not name

What is wrong: X42 says "Mark the connection on the map". No map is printed on the ticket (a quarter page); the day
patches with "Students draw their own schematic in the blank space". The first look-for is "connection marked from
producers to crossing to market", but the stem names no market.

Why it matters: a student who marks producers to the crossing has done what the stem asks and misses look-for 1;
the teacher marks a fault the item caused. A quarter page leaves no room for a sketch plus two explanations.

Fix: stem "Sketch the connection" (or print the tiny schematic, see finding 3), name the market in the stem or drop
it from the look-for, and print X42 as a half-page ticket with a sketch box (LESSON-DAY: a teacher-judged ticket item
gets a tall box).
Confidence: firm. ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed (the cheap read's finding 3 is the
arrow not being modelled, a different fault).

### 6. The warm-up's statements live only in the day frame, and the page gives one reason line for three

What is wrong: P42-1's row says "Sort three statements" without the statements; the writer supplied them in the
student frame (writer's note: "The warm-up row omits its three statements"). The student page then gives one
"Shows: ___ Cannot show: ___" line and one "Reason:" line, while the look-fors are "each statement sorted | the
reason for each given".

Why it matters: the key, display and reuse gate cannot see text that is not in the row (rule 1, facts in data), and
the page cannot hold three reasons, so a teacher marking the look-for "the reason for each" finds no place for it.

Fix: put the three statements in P42-1's stem (with the wording settled below) and give the student page a
three-row sort: statement, shows or cannot show, reason.
Confidence: firm. ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed.

### 7. The worked example's look-for asks for a date the case does not give

What is wrong: K42-1's first look-for is "a dated label at the right place", and its answer says "the pass, the
market and the deputy, dated". The case gives no date, so the day tells students to write "date not supplied". The
model then shows a label without the one feature (a date) the day's Ask names first ("Expect years, places, a
key ...").

Why it matters: the worked example is meant to show what a trustworthy label looks like; it shows the opposite and
the key marks against it.

Fix: give the invented case a date ("about 1300, invented") in the stem; drop the "date not supplied" line from the
day.
Confidence: firm. ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed.

### 8. Source C's cut removes the one line that shows Kilwa's people were African

What is wrong: Gibb's text runs "... to Kulwa, which is a large town on the coast. The majority of its inhabitants
are Zanj ... I was told by a merchant ...". The sheet cuts the second sentence (its wording, "jet-black in colour,
and with tattoo marks", is a fair reason to cut). But the day and day 43 lean on error K ("Africans built and governed
these African states") with only the teacher's word for it; the visitor's own statement that most of the town's
people were East Africans is the evidence the repair needs.

Why it matters: the unit scores students on resting claims on sources; error K's repair is the one claim here left
without a source on the sheet.

Fix (settled below): restore "The majority of its inhabitants are Zanj ..." and close the cut there, with a gloss
(*Zanj*, Arabic writers' name for East African peoples, per the sensitivity read's finding 10).
Confidence: likely. ORIGIN: section. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (day 78 finding 4, a cut that drops what
the day needs); CHEAP missed.

### 9. The option d card says Chinggis's armies "took north China"

What is wrong: they took the Jin capital (1215) and much of the north, but the Jin state fell in 1234, seven years
after Chinggis died (1227). The cheap read checked the card's dates and found none wrong; the dates are right, the
claim is too large.

Why it matters: option d students build their Chinggis-to-Khubilai layer from this card; error H (one period) is
the unit's own trap.

Fix (settled below): "his armies took much of north China (the Jin state fell in 1234, after his death)".
Confidence: likely (from memory; standard chronology). ORIGIN: section. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (day 78
finding 1, a fact error in teacher copy); CHEAP missed.

### 10. The worked example models no source, so the sourced half of the product is met first alone

What is wrong: K42-1 is invented and sourceless. P42-2's look-fors 3 to 5 ask politics and economy "explained from a
named source" and "evidence kept apart from inference". No part of the day models taking one detail from Source A or
B, C or D and saying what it shows and cannot show about politics or economy; the frame on Side 2 ("This source shows
__, but it cannot show __ because __") is printed but never modelled today.

Why it matters: rubric C7 fails if "a new skill is first met in independent work". The sourcing frame was taught on
days 35, 36 and 40, so this is a shortfall, not a gap: but the move from a source detail to a political or economic
claim on a map label is new here.

Fix: in the worked example's last two minutes, model one label from a sheet (Source D: "Paper money: the Kaan forces
it on all; shows his power over trade; cannot show how ordinary sellers felt"), and add it as a second K42-1 sample.
Confidence: a judgment call. ORIGIN: unit. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed.

### 11. "Trade gains never make up for conquest" decides day 44's question for the students

What is wrong: the teacher page says it as fact during Teaching first. The opener's rule is that harms and gains "are
true and stay separate" (error D), and day 44 asks students to weigh trade, faith and the sword with a counterclaim.

Why it matters: a moral verdict given by the teacher on day 42 narrows the discussion it is meant to open; error D's
repair is to record both, not to weigh them for the student.

Fix (settled below): "Gains from trade do not erase the killing; both are recorded."
Confidence: a judgment call. ORIGIN: day. LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed.

### 12. The gates cannot see a social studies exit that copies its worked example

What is wrong: LESSON-DAY rule 3 and its gates check that an exit's numbers are new. A social studies exit is new by
its situation, and nothing checks that: X42 passes with the same structure, actors and look-fors as K42-1.

Why it matters: the same fault was found by hand on days 78 and 80 and here; a math-shaped rule fits social studies
badly, so every later social studies section relies on the expert to catch it.

Fix: add to CHEAP-READ.md and the day-writer brief one check line: "For a judged exit, compare its stem and look-fors
with the day's worked example; same structure with nouns swapped fails." A gate note (look-for text overlap above a
threshold between an exit and a same-day example) can follow.
Confidence: likely. ORIGIN: template. LAYER process; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (day 82 finding 6 and day 79 finding
3, math-shaped gates); CHEAP missed.
