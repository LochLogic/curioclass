## Verdict per day

- **Day 1 (Does air weigh anything?): sound idea, fragile as written.** The hook is the right phenomenon and the order (predict, record, then explain) is right. But the whole unit hangs on a 3 g gain read off a whole-gram kitchen scale from a "playground ball", with no branch if the class's own reading shows no gain, and the class cannot see the scale. At 670 of 675 words it has no room for the fixes.
- **Day 2 (Same pieces, new thing, and soil to dry): the weakest science day.** It is a readiness reteach, a block rebuild and a soil-drying set-up. Almost no minute is about air or particles, and the soil numbers are invented when the class could weigh the soil. Three differentiated tasks in 10 minutes will not run as written.
- **Day 3 (Squeezed air, and a first shadow): good core, overloaded.** The syringe evidence and the "same dots, closer together" idea are right. The worked example gives the answer before the squeeze, and the 10-minute shadow reading cannot work for 30 children at one stick at one clock time.
- **Day 4 (Revise the air drawing, and weigh a bean pot): overloaded.** Four jobs (repair, revise, weigh a pot, exit) with one shared scale for six groups, a 5-minute opening that holds a writing task plus up to four competing repairs, and a hand-off line to 1.2 that contradicts the 1.2 plan.

What is right and should not be touched: the predict-record-then-explain order on day 1; same-ball-before-and-after as the fair test; "a dot is a part too small to see, never a grain you could pick up"; dots kept in count and changed in spacing; the first drawing never erased; log what was *added*, not the running reading (every key and wrong row for this is correct); the age-appropriate, no-name particle language; tickets that carry one named error each.
