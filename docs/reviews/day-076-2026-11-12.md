## Verdict

Day 76 is correct arithmetic in a sound shape: rename across the zeros in words first, subtract, add back, with
Kai's own check as the clue that he made the zeros 10. The biggest risk is that both worked examples have only
zeros that pass one on, so the day teaches "a zero becomes 9, not 10" as a rule, while the exit (40,060), practice
3, 5 and 6, and the end check's item 9.2b (600,305) each hold a zero that is the place that needed ones and must
become 10; a child who learned the day's slogan gets the exit wrong by exactly 1, and no key or watch list knows
that answer. The one change that matters most is finding 1: make Example 1 a number where one zero passes on and
one receives (500 − 263), say the rule so it is true, and add the "every zero made 9" wrong answers.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The examples teach "a zero becomes 9", and the exit needs a zero that becomes 10

- **What is wrong.** In 402 and 6,005 every zero sits between the place that gives and the ones, so each zero
  "passed one on and kept 9" (the steps' own words, the teacher's "That is why the zero becomes 9, not 10", the
  Ask, and the written task "why the zeros in 6,005 become 9s"). But when the ones digit is itself 0, that zero
  is the place that needed ones, and it becomes 10: 100,000 is 9, 9, 9, 9, 10; 60,500 ends in 9 tens, 10 ones;
  407,020 ends in 11 tens, 10 ones; the exit's 40,060 is 3 ten thousands, 9 thousands, 9 hundreds, 15 tens, 10
  ones. No example, step, Ask or watch line shows a zero that becomes 10, and the exit is exactly that case. The
  natural wrong answer of a child who learned the slogan, every zero made 9, is missing from wrongs.csv and the
  register: 22,675 on the exit (one short), 53,816 on problem 5 (99,999 − 46,183), 32,653 on 3, 248,655 on 6.
  The teacher reads 22,675 as a slip, or as nothing.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s09-K76-1, K76-2 steps; d076.md "Teaching first", "Pull it together"; s09-W76; wrongs.csv
  s09-X76 and P76 rows; errors.csv letter I (one form only). The Progressions, NBT, PDF page 66 (printed 60),
  shows "decomposing where needed first" with a place that both gives and receives (425 as 3 hundreds, 11 tens,
  15 ones): the method is "where needed", not "zeros become 9". Same kind as the s11 plan's "what item 6 tests was
  taught on no day" and "Error C lacked the commonest wrong answer" in the expert ledger.
- **Cost.** The day's own rule makes the exit wrong for the students who listened best, by an amount the key

- **Change.** Example 1 becomes 500 − 263 (row `s09-K76-1v2`, see "Settled by the expert"): the tens zero passes
