## Verdict

The mathematics of day 11 is right and the day is shaped well: one strip laid on the line, the planned question at
the end, the two fixes aimed at the two errors the unit expects here (C and K), and the explanation the standard asks
for in the frame. The biggest risk is physical: the second half of the day (Example 2, questions 8 and 9, Max's fix,
20 minutes) tells students to lay their strips on the line page's 0 to 2 lines, but those lines are drawn at half the
strips' scale, so two strips end to end are twice as long as the line, and the method in the worked steps (fold a
"1/4 strip", fold to cut fifths) does not match the strips the students hold. The one change that matters most is
finding 1: print the 0 to 2 lines with their marks and cut by marking middles, and tie the 0 to 1 work to the
eighths and tenths strips already in the envelope.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The 0 to 2 lines are drawn at half the strips' scale, so "lay two whole strips end to end" cannot be done

- **What is wrong.** On the strip sheet every strip is 680 of 700 units on a 7.1 inch drawing, about 6.9 inches.
  On number-lines.md the 0 to 1 lines run the same 6.9 inches (they match), but the 0 to 2 lines put 1 at x = 350,
  so one whole is about 3.45 inches. Side 2 of the student page opens "Past 1. Lay two whole strips end to end."
  Two strips are 13.8 inches; the line is 6.9. Example 2 step 1 says "Lay the 1/2 strip from 0 three times": the
  strip's half is 3.45 inches, the line's half is 1.72. The same holds for question 8 (fifths to 2), question 9
  (fourths to 2), Max's fix, and the reteach for readiness items 1 and 4 ("lay the one-fourth strip from 0 five
  times", which needs a line past 1). Nothing on the page or the teacher page says the strips will not fit.
- **Why it matters.** It is 20 of the day's 40 lesson minutes, and the part the plan calls "all the work past 1 is
  here". A teacher with no prep finds out in front of the class. Students will either lay strips that run off the
  line (and take the whole strip as the whole, error K, the very error the half-hour attacks) or stop.
- **Evidence.** The two SVGs in courses/math-4/u03-1/strip-sheet.md and number-lines.md (measured). The Progressions
  place the whole as the length from 0 to 1 on any line (math-3-5-progression.md, unit 3, Prog. p. 141 (135)),
  so the 0 to 2 line can be any scale as long as the student works from its printed marks; that is the way through.
- **Fix.** Keep lines 1 to 3 (0 to 1) blank for laying strips. Print lines 4 to 6 (0 to 2) with their marks: line 4
  in halves (Example 2), line 5 in fifths (question 8), line 6 in fourths (question 9 and Max). Number every line.
  On side 2 replace "Lay two whole strips end to end." with "Past 1. Use lines 4 to 6. The marks are printed. To cut
  each part in 2, mark its middle." In "Past 1, alone" say which line each question uses. Reword Example 2 steps 1
  and 2 (Settled table). The board demonstration keeps the large strips: the teacher's board line can be any length.
  For the reteach, use a 0 to 1 line and count four fourths to 1, then lay the strip once more past the line's end.
- **Confidence.** Firm (measured). **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 80 finding 1, "the strip
  sheet does not fit the day that uses it most"). **CHEAP:** missed (it counted lines, not their scale).

### 2. The worked steps fold a strip the students do not have, and folding cannot cut fifths into tenths

- **What is wrong.** The strip sheet gives each student whole-length strips with their parts printed (a fourths
  strip, an eighths strip, a fifths strip, a tenths strip). There is no piece "1/4 long" unless a student cuts one
  off. Example 1's steps say "Lay the 1/4 strip ... Fold the strip in half and mark the fold"; folding the fourths
  strip in half marks 1/2, not 1/8. "Cut and rename" says "mark it with the matching strip, then fold to cut each
  part in 2": that works for fourths (fold three times) but not for fifths (questions 5 and 8), since no fold of a
  whole strip halves each fifth. Other rooms and At home fall back to "rule tenths every inch" for the same reason.
- **Why it matters.** Students lose minutes folding, get uneven marks, and the 7 minutes for three items the cheap
  read called tight become impossible. The tool to hand is better: the eighths strip laid on the same line puts every
  new mark in place, and the tenths strip does the same for fifths. Laying the finer strip on the coarser marks is
  also the cleanest picture of "each part cut into 2".
- **Fix.** Rewrite Example 1 steps 1 and 2 and "Cut and rename" to lay the matching strip, mark its parts, then lay
  the eighths (or tenths) strip on the same line and mark its parts (Settled table). Keep folding for the teacher's
  large strip at the board, where folding a fourths strip in half again makes eighths. Add to Before class: "Check
  every envelope holds the fourths, fifths, eighths and tenths strips." The cheap read's "every other fourth" wording
  fix is folded into this rewrite.
- **Confidence.** Firm. **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed (it found only the
  "every other" wording and the fifths and tenths missing from day 10's list).

### 3. Ana's fix can be read as right, and neither fix shows the student a line

- **What is wrong.** F11-1's stem: "She counted the marks from 0: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. She named the point 6/6." If a
  reader takes "from 0" to start at the first mark after 0, the sixth mark is 1 and 6/6 is right. The error is
  only visible in a picture, and there is none: the row's figure is `| line d=6 pt=5/6`, the right picture only, so
  the student page prints nothing and the display's "Their working" column is text. Max's fix (F11-2) is clear in
  words but also prints no line, and the teacher page says "Cover the line past 1 with a sheet of paper" with no line
  in front of the students (the line page's three 0 to 2 lines are taken by Example 2, question 8 and question 9).
- **Why it matters.** A fix-it works when the student sees the wrong working and finds the slip; here an able reader
  can defend Ana. The At home Say ("Where would 6/6 be?") depends on a line the child does not have.
- **Fix.** Stem: "Ana marked sixths on a line from 0 to 1. She counted every mark from the mark at 0 to her point: 1,
  2, 3, 4, 5, 6. She named the point 6/6." Give both rows a first figure the student sees: F11-1 `line d=6 blank=5/6
  | line d=6 pt=5/6`; F11-2 `line to=2 d=4 blank=5/4 | line to=2 d=4 pt=5/4`; print `[[fig:s03-F11-1]]` and
  `[[fig:s03-F11-2]]` on the student page. If side 1 then runs to a third side, move Ana's fix to the top of side 2
  (it is still taught before Past 1, so the order holds).
- **Confidence.** Likely. **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 81 finding 5, day 85 finding
  7: a fix whose picture does not show the wrong working). **CHEAP:** missed (it found the missing line-page lines
  for the fixes, not the ambiguous stem or the missing pictures).

- **Confidence.** Firm. **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** script. **REPEAT:** yes (day 6 finding 6, "end-check number 80
