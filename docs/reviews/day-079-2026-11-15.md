## Verdict

The history on the page is mostly right and the day has a sound shape (offices, a modelled source-limit move, a
mapped decision, an unseen-source exit), but it cannot be taught: three sources, the photograph, the map and the
facts card's losses are still placeholders, as its own Before class says. The biggest risk is in the unit's design,
not the day's prose: Component M, a scored part of the end-of-unit assessment, is collected from 15 minutes of
first-day practice with no feedback or revision, and a third of it is the worked example the teacher modelled ten
minutes before. The one change that matters most is to move Component M's scoring off day 79 (or give it a
revision pass after day 80's feedback) and use the freed time to teach the 1862 land laws, which the exit tests
but the day gives about a minute.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Component M is scored from first-encounter practice, and its Grant cell is the worked example

- **What is wrong.** s05.md item 4 and s05-EM make the day 79 organizer (s05-P79-2) a scored component of the day 89
  assessment, "completed alone and collected", "do not return them for revision". It is done in the 15 minutes after
  students first meet Davis, Lee, Grant and the three battles. Its Grant row (sample: "Grant took Vicksburg") is
  exactly K79-1, modelled minutes before; covering side 1 does not stop a student writing what the teacher just said.
  The unit plan's own meets example names "Grant's campaign of attrition" (1864), which day 79 never teaches.
- **Why it matters.** An adoption reviewer reads summative evidence as evidence of learning after instruction and
  feedback; this measures same-day recall of a model. A third of the component scores copying, and a student who
  had a weak first day carries it into the unit grade with no way back. The bar's assessment indicators (rubric,
  social studies assessment and claim items) ask that the end-of-unit task be done from sources after teaching.
- **Fix.** In s05.md item 4, s05-EM and handbacks.csv (79 to 89): either (a) score the organizer only after it comes
  back on day 80 with the exit's look-fors and one revision in a colour pen, or (b) drop Component M and widen
  item 4 on day 89 to all three leaders and all three battles from a short unseen source. In either case the Grant
  cell must ask for a decision other than the Vicksburg move (for example, taking overall command in March 1864) or
  accept Vicksburg only with an effect not stated in the model. Strike "campaign of attrition" from the meets
  example unless a day teaches it.
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN unit. Confidence: likely.

### 2. The Native nations on this day had no sensitivity read, and the 1862 home front leaves out the Dakota

- **What is wrong.** Day 79 teaches federal power over "land taken from Native nations, including the Lakota and
  Cheyenne". The unit's sensitivity line (s05.md "Sensitive topics", and the read actually run) names only Black
  communities; the sensitivity read of 5.2 says so and does not touch Native content. The lens requires Native
  content to get the sensitivity agent's read from the community's own published voice. The day also speaks of
  Native nations only in the past tense, and for 1862 it skips the war on the home front that most directly joins
  land and federal power that year: the United States and Dakota War in Minnesota (August to September 1862), the
  mass execution of 38 Dakota men at Mankato on December 26, 1862, and the expulsion that followed (from memory,
  firm on the dates). Homestead claims in 1862 to 1863 went mainly into Minnesota, Kansas and Nebraska, so the
  Pawnee, Dakota, Osage and others are as much "whose land" as the Lakota and Cheyenne.
- **Why it matters.** Values rule (c) and the lens: a group present in the history left out, and Native nations shown
  only as a past loss. A teacher with no background reads one sentence and moves on.
- **Fix.** Add Native nations to s05.md's sensitivity reads and run the read on day 79 (and the unit's other Native
  content) before printing. Pending that read, change the Teaching first sentence to: "Both laws handed out land the
  United States had taken or was taking from Native nations, among them the Dakota, Pawnee, Lakota and Cheyenne,
  who are still nations today. In Minnesota that August the Dakota went to war over broken treaty payments; the
  army defeated them, 38 Dakota men were hanged that December and the rest were expelled." Record the facts in
  sources.csv (an NPS or Minnesota Historical Society page) before printing, and widen s05-X79's sample beyond the
  Lakota and Cheyenne.
- LAYER process; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN unit. Confidence: likely (firm on the facts, a
  judgment call on the exact sentence, which the Native-community read should settle).

### 3. The C repair borrows five minutes from a part that has none, and the rule that sends it there is math's

- **What is wrong.** If a third of day 78's tickets show C, the day takes "Teaching first's first five minutes" and
  says "If repairing C, shorten reporting, keeping explanations and readings". Teaching first has no reporting; it is
  all explanation and reading. So the repair halves the only part that teaches offices, battles, the photograph and
  both land laws, and the teacher is not told what to cut. The "warm-up and first five minutes" repair is the
  template's handback rule (LESSON-DAY Tomorrow, handbacks.csv `minutes`), built for math days whose first part is
  a short review; a social studies day's first part is new content.
- **Why it matters.** On a repair day the exit's land question has had no teaching at all.
- **Fix.** Template: let a social studies handback take its repair minutes from the warm-up plus a named part (the
  section plan says which), not "the first five minutes". Day: "If repairing C, take the five minutes from the
  Alone part (12 minutes is enough for the organizer) and skip Source G's discussion; keep every other line of
  Teaching first."
- LAYER process; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN template. Confidence: likely.

### 4. Collecting side 2 for day 89 takes side 1, the worked-example notes, with it

- **What is wrong.** The day prints the Component M organizer on side 2 of the student page and says "Collect student

### 5. Vicksburg alone did not give the Union the river

- **What is wrong.** K79-1, s05-P79-2 and the unit plan's item 4 key say Union control of the Mississippi followed
  Vicksburg. Port Hudson, Louisiana, the last Confederate stronghold on the river, surrendered on July 9, 1863, five
  days later; only then did the Union hold the whole river (from memory, firm).
- **Why it matters.** The day's own lasting-effect claim is the one fact in the example a reader can mark wrong.
- **Fix.** K79-1 answer and P79-2 sample: "Lasting: with Port Hudson's surrender five days later, the Union held the
  whole Mississippi and split the Confederacy for the rest of the war." Same clause in the s05 item 4 key. The
  teacher page's "lasting division through Union river control" can stay.
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN unit. Confidence: firm.

### 6. The Homestead Act cut leaves out the clause that ties it to the war

- **What is wrong.** The Source D placeholder asks for "the clause entitling a head of a family, or a person aged
  twenty-one". The same section requires a citizen or a person who has filed a declaration of intention to become
  one, and one "who has never borne arms against the United States Government or given aid and comfort to its
  enemies" (from memory, firm on the substance; check the exact words against the transcript).
- **Why it matters.** The day's goal is the acts "on the home front". The loyalty clause is the line that makes the
  Homestead Act a war measure (it shut out Confederates) and the declarant clause shows who was invited in
  (immigrants). Without it, D reads as a land law that could sit in any decade.
- **Fix.** Change the placeholder to: "[to cut and check: section 1, from 'any person who is the head of a family' to
  'given aid and comfort to its enemies', about 70 words, against the 12 Stat. 392 transcript]", gloss *borne arms
  against*, and add one Teaching first clause: "it shut out anyone who had fought for the Confederacy."
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN section. Confidence: likely.

### 7. The 1863 outline map cannot carry the jobs the days give it

- **What is wrong.** The map spec (sources-79.md side 2, section plan field 5, sources.csv 79-MAP) is "the Union, the
  Confederacy and the border states in 1863". Day 78's and day 79's C repair asks the teacher to "shade covered areas
  and exceptions on the completed map": the exceptions are parishes of Louisiana and counties of Virginia, which a
  state outline does not show. The spec also does not name West Virginia, a state from June 20, 1863, whose counties
  were among the Proclamation's exceptions. "The completed map" is not defined (the teacher's copy, or a student's
  marked sheet?).
- **Why it matters.** A teacher repairing C on a state outline will shade all of Louisiana and Virginia, teaching the
  error the repair exists to fix.
- **Fix.** Map spec: add West Virginia and a hatched area for the excepted Louisiana parishes (New Orleans and its
  surroundings) and the excepted Virginia counties (Norfolk area and the Eastern Shore), all unlabelled; day 79 and
  day 78: "on your own display copy of the map".
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN section. Confidence: firm on the facts.

### 8. Source G does no work

- **What is wrong.** No item, organizer row or exit uses the Antietam photograph, and the page's only point about it
  is that it "records Lincoln's visit, not everyone's motives". It costs a content note and a minute of the most
  crowded part.
- **Why it matters.** It is either a lost minute or a lost teaching point: Lincoln went to Antietam in October 1862
  to press McClellan to pursue Lee, and removed him in November when he did not (from memory, firm). That is
  Antietam's other lasting effect and a leader's decision students could map.
- **Fix.** Keep G and add one sentence: "Lincoln came to press McClellan to follow Lee into Virginia; when he did not,
  Lincoln removed him in November." Or cut G and give its minute to the land laws. I settle on keeping it (below).
- LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed; ORIGIN section. Confidence: likely.
