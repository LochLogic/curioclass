## Verdict

Day 10 is correct mathematics and a sound first day: fold, count both the parts in the whole and the shaded
parts, see the length stay put, with error A fixed on purpose and the standard's "number and size of the parts"
said in full. The biggest risk is that the whole day happens in the hands and none of it on paper, while the
exit asks for a drawn cut on a printed strip and the key ticks the name alone, so a child who doubles both
numbers by rule (the 3.1b wrong answer "you times by 2") gets the same tick as one who understood. The one change
that matters most is finding 1: two Fold and record items get a printed strip to draw the cut on, and the exit's
tick needs the drawn cut as well as 4/6.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Nothing is drawn all day, but the exit asks for a drawing and the key ticks the name alone

- **What is wrong.** Every move on day 10 is a fold of a paper strip; the student page has no strip picture and no
  sketch box anywhere (both sides are about half empty). The exit then gives a printed thirds strip and asks the
  student to shade 2/3, draw cuts and name it: a translation from folding to drawing that is met first on the
  ticket. The key's answer is "2/3 = 4/6" only, so "4/6" written beside an untouched strip gets a tick. The unit
  assessment's own 3.1b counts "you times by 2, with no drawing or reason" as not meeting the standard; day 10's
  record cannot see that child.
- **Evidence.** d010-student.pdf, both sides; d010-exit.html (strip 2.53 in, 0 of 3 shaded); items.csv s03-X10
  answer cell; s03.md field 5, item 3.1b's wrong answers. 4.NF.A.1 asks students to explain "by using visual
  fraction models"; the Progressions for Number and Operations, Fractions, treat the drawn partition as the
  explanation (from memory, not reopened today).
- **Cost.** E10 overstates what the class knows on the first day of the unit, and day 11's reteach branches are
  set from it. Children who learnt "double both" at home pass silently, which is what the family note asks parents
  not to teach.
- **Change.** Day: Fold and record items 2 and 3 each get a blank strip figure to draw on after folding (see
  "Settled by the expert": `fstrip 0/5 lab=none` and `fstrip 0/6 lab=none`, stems end "Draw your cuts."). Exit:
  the answer cell becomes "2/3 = 4/6, with every third cut (6 parts) and 4 shaded", and a wrongs row "4/6 with no
  cuts drawn" (no letter; note "the name without the picture: ask the student to draw it on day 11"). Tomorrow
  adds one line: "Name with no cuts: these students draw their cuts first in day 11's warm-up." Teacher page,
  Fold and record: "After each fold, draw the cut on the printed strip, then write the name."
- **Confidence.** Firm (the rows and the page; 3.1b is the unit's own bar).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 4 finding 1 and day 76 finding 1: the exit needs a move
  the day does not practise). **CHEAP:** missed (its CHECK 5a looked at the factor given, not at what the tick
  accepts).

### 2. The fix-it pictures never show the wrong answer, so the proof the teacher is told to give is not on the screen

- **Evidence.** items.csv s03-F10-1 and s03-F10-2 `figure`; d010-display.html (Their working: text only);

- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 2 finding 2, day 5 finding 5, day 85 finding 7: a fix
  picture that does not show what it claims). **CHEAP:** missed (it checked that the figures draw their cells,

### 3. The day's hardest physical move, cutting each third into 2 by folding, is not told

- **What is wrong.** K10-2 step 2 says "Fold or mark the middle of every third". Folding a strip so that each of
  three parts is halved is not obvious to an adult with a 2-foot strip at the front, and 25 children copying will
  fold the strip in half (one crease, through the middle third). Fold and record 3 then asks for sixths into
  twelfths, parts about 1.2 in wide on the printed sixths strip, which are too small to fold cleanly. "Other
  rooms" has the roll-into-an-S trick for making thirds, but not for halving them.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s03-K10-2 `steps`; strip-sheet.md (strips 7.1 in long); d010.md Other rooms.
- **Cost.** Minutes lost in Teaching first, and wrong creases that give 3/6 or 2/4-looking strips on the very
  example that sets error A.
- **Change.** K10-2 step 2 becomes: "Fold the thirds strip on its two lines, like a Z. Fold the stack in half and
  open it: every third has a crease in its middle, the shaded one too." (Steps only; numbers unchanged, so the id
  stays.) Teacher page, Fold and record, after the error A line: "Sixths into twelfths is too small to fold well:
  mark the middle of each sixth with a pencil, then lay it on the twelfths strip to check."
- **Confidence.** Likely (from handling; a taught run settles it).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

## What is right and should not be touched

- The examples count two things every time, the parts in the whole and the shaded parts, and the step "Lay it on a
  strip that is not folded" shows the length staying put. That is the standard's own explanation.
- "Do not give a rule about multiplying; section 3.2 writes it", with "times as many parts" kept as the language.
- The two fixes are the two halves of error A. "6/6 would be the whole strip" is the right check.
- The readiness branch: item 2 is today's work and its students sit where they see the strips; items 1, 3, 4 wait
  for day 11, which picks them up.
- The challenge leaves tenths and thirds out, so day 12's opening task ("Can 1/2 be written as tenths? As
  thirds?") stays fresh. (I considered a 1/4 search instead; 2/8 = 3/12 is X16's pair, so no.)
- At home: 31 minutes, a real Swap for folding without a sheet, the Say tied to the length check.
