# Review: grade 4 math year spine (docs/courses/math-4-spine.md)

Curriculum expert, cold read, 2026-11-11. Read first: EXPERT.md, ROADMAP "The path from here" through "Gaps found", RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md, BASELINE 20 to 22, the grade 4 standard text, the focus marks, sequence.csv, the shape file, the spine and its rows in units.csv. Outside sources are listed at the end with what each was used for and what could not be read.

## Verdict

The spine is sound in its main line: the order is, almost stretch for stretch, the order of Illustrative Mathematics grade 4 (a well-reviewed open curriculum), the 150-day year is inside the range of the outside programs, and 67 percent on major work clears the 65 percent line, which I can now confirm is real. The biggest risk is not the order but the structure around it: 42 small units each with two checks put roughly one day in five into checking, there is no reteach or flex time, and the last major work (division) ends on day 132 with nothing but geometry after it. The one change that matters most is to move the readiness and end checks from the unit to the stretch, and use the time that frees to finish multiplication and division about 15 days sooner and give 4.OA.A.3 and geometry what they need (full revised sequence in finding 3).

## The 65 percent question, answered

The EdReports evidence guide (K-8 math, v2.0, updated 07/2025) was read for this review. Indicator 1e sets the line at 65 percent for grades 3 to 8 (75 percent for K to 2). It calls the figure a guideline, sends borderline cases (60 to 64 percent) to staff, and has reviewers count three ways: by units or chapters, by lessons, and by time, with assessments included and with supporting work that is tied to major work counted on the major side. The same 65 to about 85 percent range is in the K-8 Publishers' Criteria and in the Student Achievement Partners grade 4 focus document. So the note in RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md and the spine ("not confirmed yet") can be closed: confirmed. Confidence: firm.

What that means for this spine: by days it is 100 of 150 (67 percent) on the strict count, about 105 if the two "Year Together" days and the major share of the checks are counted, and near 79 percent if line plots, measurement and area and perimeter are counted as supporting work tied to major work. By the 42 units it is 28 of 41. By the 16 stretches alone it is 9 of 16 (56 percent), which only passes if the supporting stretches visibly do major work. That is one more reason the supporting units must carry their link to major work on the page (indicator 1f), not only in the spine's rationale.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Unit checks take about a fifth of the year, and there is no time to act on them

**What is at risk.** Each of 42 units carries a ten-minute readiness check and a half-day end check. That is about 21 days of end checks and about 7 hours of readiness checks: roughly 28 of the 144 unit days, plus 4 check days, so about 32 of 150 days (21 percent) are checking. A 2-day unit (there are seven) keeps about 1.3 days of teaching; a 3-day unit (fourteen) keeps about 2.3. There are 88 checks in 150 days and not one day set aside to reteach after any of them. The spine also never says how long a "day" is.

**Evidence.** Illustrative Mathematics counts 2 assessment days inside each of 9 units (18 of 137 to 149 days, about 12 percent) and checks daily with a five-minute cool-down. Eureka Math (2015) has 27 assessment days in 180 (15 percent), and those include a day to give each assessment back and remediate. Evidence guide indicator 3g asks for an assessment system that gives guidance on next steps; a check with no room to respond is hard to defend. Confidence: firm on the arithmetic and the comparison; a judgment call on the best fix.

**Cost.** The day counts overstate teaching time by about a fifth, so every "is five days enough" judgment is really "is four days enough". A real teacher will drop the checks first.

**Change.** Preferred: move both checks up one level. One readiness check at the start of each stretch (ten minutes, on the grade 3 or earlier grade 4 standards it builds on), a five-minute exit question each day, and one end check on the last day of each stretch, taking half the period, with the other half used to respond. Add one "respond" day after each of the first three cumulative checks. State the period length on the spine (60 minutes is what both outside programs assume). This changes the rule in ROADMAP item 4 ("in every unit") to "in every stretch", or, more simply, renames the stretch as the unit and today's units as sections; that is a site-wide call for Cory. Minimum, if the rule stays: units of 3 days or fewer get a ten-minute end check, not a half day.

### 2. 4.OA.A.3 is taught before the things it needs

**What is wrong.** Unit 10.3 (days 90 to 93) is "Multistep problems, remainders and estimates". The standard asks for multistep problems with all four operations, including interpreting remainders. On day 90 the class has not met division with remainders (first taught in 4.NBT.B.6, day 121) or multi-digit multiplication (day 109). The only return is unit 14.3, two days, shared with 4.NBT.B.6.

**Evidence.** The Operations and Algebraic Thinking progression treats interpreting remainders as work on division situations that leave a remainder. Illustrative Mathematics tags 4.OA.A.3 in units 5, 6 and 9, with the weight in unit 6 beside multiplication and division. Indicator 1d asks for the full intent of each standard. Confidence: firm.

**Cost.** Either unit 10.3 quietly teaches remainders early and badly, or the largest application standard of the grade gets its full form for two days.

**Change.** Keep 10.1 and 10.2. Cut 10.3 to two days of two-step comparison problems with a letter for the unknown and an estimate check, no remainders. After division, add a four-day stretch, "Multistep problems with all four operations" (4.OA.A.3 in full, using 4.NBT.B.4 to 6). Net cost in days: zero.

### 3. Multiplication and division finish too late, and measurement comes before the computing it uses

**What is at risk.** The heaviest computing of the grade ends on day 132 of 150. If 150 teaching days sit inside a 180-day calendar, that is past most spring test windows, and only geometry follows, so multiplication and division get almost no spaced practice inside the year. Measurement conversions (day 94) come before multi-digit multiplication (day 109), so conversions by 12, 16, 60 or 1,000 and the "four operations" word problems of 4.MD.A.2 must stay artificially small.

**Evidence.** In Illustrative Mathematics the multiplication and division unit ends near day 105 to 111 of 137 to 149, followed by geometry and a 12-day closing unit that returns to fractions and whole-number operations. Eureka Math finishes multiplication and division by about day 75 and puts a 20-day measurement module after it, as application. Illustrative Mathematics does put conversion before the multiplication unit, so the present order is not wrong; it limits the numbers. Test timing is from memory. Confidence: likely on the lateness, a judgment call on moving measurement.

**Change: the full revised sequence (150 days).**

### 4. Geometry is thin, and angles are measured before rays are drawn

**What is wrong.** Angles and geometry get 15 days, about 12 after the unit checks. Stretch 15 defines an angle by its two rays, but points, lines and rays (4.G.A.1) are first drawn in unit 16.1, after it. The stated reason for the order ("needs angle measure to sort shapes") is true of 4.G.A.2 only.

**Evidence.** Illustrative Mathematics: 15 to 16 days on angles plus 7 to 10 on shapes, and its angle unit opens with a section on points, lines, segments and rays before the size of angles. Eureka Math: 20 days, lines and angles first (order from memory). Confidence: firm on the order, likely on the count.

**Change.** As in the table: Lines and Angles, 12 days (lines, rays, parallel and perpendicular 3; turns and degrees 3; protractor 3; angles add 3), then Shapes and Symmetry, 4 days. Protractor work is the place to add a day if one turns up. Module boundaries do not move; only which unit points at which part.

### 5. The spine omits several things its own plan says it will show

- **The eight mathematical practices.** ROADMAP's gap table says the spine shows all eight over the year. It names none. The evidence guide scores each practice (2e onward) and asks that assessments name the practices they assess (1b, 1c). Change: one column, one or two practices per stretch, all eight covered. Firm.
- **Balance of understanding, fluency and application.** ROADMAP says the spine totals them per stretch. Absent. Change: three small columns of days per stretch. Firm.
- **The limits in the standards.** The grade 4 footnotes (denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 100; whole numbers to 1,000,000; no general unlike-denominator addition; line plots in halves, quarters and eighths; factors within 100; conversions larger to smaller only) appear nowhere on the spine, and the footnotes are also missing from data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt (searched). Indicator 1a marks down above-grade assessment items. Change: a "limits" column, and add the footnotes to the standards file. The stretch 8 rationale ("reads the same on both sides of the decimal point") should say plainly that place value in decimals is grade 5 (5.NBT.A.1) and is not assessed here. Firm.
- **Grade 3 prerequisites by code.** "Builds on" names topics. Indicator 1h wants the content named, and each stretch's readiness check needs codes to draw from. Change: add them (for example 3.NF.A.1 to 3, 3.OA.C.7, 3.NBT.A.2, 3.MD.C.7). Stretch 16's "builds on area" should read 3.G.A.1. Likely.
- **Fluency.** The one required fluency is 4.NBT.B.4, confirmed in the focus document and the evidence guide (2b). The plan starts its warm-up on day 76. Change: run the add-and-subtract warm-up from day 1 in the grade 3 range (within 1,000), widen it at stretch 9, and label the times-table warm-up as grade 3 maintenance so a reviewer does not read it as below-grade teaching (Publishers' Criteria on previous-grade review). Likely.

### 6. Fractions go silent for 86 days

**What is at risk.** Fraction teaching ends on day 64 and the year ends on day 150. The only planned returns are "a few items" on Checks 2 and 3.

**Evidence.** Illustrative Mathematics tags fraction standards in its measurement unit, its shapes unit and the first section of its closing unit. Spaced practice is a well-supported recommendation (Institute of Education Sciences guide on organizing instruction, from memory). Confidence: likely.

**Change.** Add a short "where it comes back" row per major stretch. Concretely: two warm-ups a week on fractions and decimals from day 65; fraction and decimal quantities written into measurement (4.MD.A.2 already asks for them) and fractional side lengths into perimeter and symmetry problems; The Year Together opens with fractions. Name the number line as the model carried through stretches 3 to 7 (What Works Clearinghouse fractions guide, recommendation 2, moderate evidence).

### 7. The four checks: placement is fine, the system is incomplete

Placement is reasonable (in the revision: days 42, 78, 118, 149). What a reviewer would expect and not find:

- **A start-of-year check on grade 3 major work.** The spine itself says grade 3 is weak underneath. Added on day 1 above.
- **More than generator items.** Indicator 1c asks for a range of item types and for chances to show the full intent of standards and practices. A check built only from generators will be short computation. Change: each cumulative check is two parts, a redrawable generator part and two fixed explain-or-model tasks with a scoring guide.
- **Standard and practice named per item** (1b), already on the gap list for unit checks; say it for these too.
- **Time and guidance to respond** (3g): the respond days, and "if many missed this, do that" on every key.
- **A check on the last day of school** cannot change anything. Moved to day 149.
- **Count.** ROADMAP's gap table says "all five cumulative checks"; the spine has four. With the start-of-year check there are five. Make the two files agree.

Confidence: likely.

### 8. Smaller points a teacher or curriculum lead would raise

- "16 stretches" but the table runs to 17; "144 days in units" leaves out unit 17.1 while "42 units" counts it.
- Two days to pull the year together is very short beside 12 in Illustrative Mathematics. If the year ever grows past 150, the first extra days belong there and in geometry. Judgment call.
- 13 tools of about 30 minutes are roughly 7 hours of a 150-hour year. The spine says every day is a gap day; it should also say that most days will be print lessons, so nobody reads the tool column as the course.
- The teacher reader is a grade 2 teacher. Day counts for fractions and division should also be read by someone who has taught grades 3 to 5. Judgment call.

## What is right and should not be touched

- **Fractions in the first half.** This is the Illustrative Mathematics order, and the fractions progression says equivalence is the base of comparison, addition and decimals. Eureka Math does the reverse and is also well reviewed, so this is a real choice, made defensibly. Keep it.
- **Factors and multiples first, 6 to 7 days.** Matches Illustrative Mathematics unit 1 and serves equivalence.
- **Fraction times whole number before any grade 4 multiplication.** It needs only grade 3 equal groups (fractions progression); Illustrative Mathematics teaches it even before fraction addition. Not a problem.
- **Multiplicative comparison before multi-digit multiplication, and after fractions.** Same as Illustrative Mathematics.
- **Decimals straight after fractions, then place value.** Same junction as Illustrative Mathematics unit 4.
- **Line plots placed to practise fraction addition**, and the module left unsplit.
- **Area and perimeter before multiplication**, as the pilot, at 5 days.
- **Partial quotients and area models, not the standard algorithms, for 4.NBT.B.5 and B.6.** Correct to the standards.
- **150 days and 100 major days.** Illustrative Mathematics totals 137 to 149; Eureka Math has 151 lesson days in 180.
- **The "sets up" column and the plain list of what is missing.**
