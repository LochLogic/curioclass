# Review: the approach to teaching (docs/APPROACH.md and docs/APPROACH-MATH.md)

Date: 2026-11-11. Reviewer: the curriculum expert (docs/agents/EXPERT.md), run cold.
Scope: sourcing, soundness as an approach, and fitness as a working document. Nothing here says Curio Class
was reviewed by, meets or is endorsed by anyone.

Page numbers below are PDF page indexes in the files I downloaded, not printed page numbers.

## Verdict

The approach is sound and unusually honest: of 34 load-bearing claims I checked against the sources, 27 are
confirmed as written, and none is invented. The biggest risk is that the "shared" core is partly a math page in
disguise (daily exit question, daily warm-up from generators, understanding/fluency/use day marks, the Common
Core math introduction as the role of standards "in every subject"), and three of those already contradict the
approved grade 4 science spine. The one change that matters most: move the math-shaped promises and unit plan
items out of the shared core into the math page, and state the shared version as a job ("every day ends with
evidence the teacher can act on") that each subject page fills in its own way.

### Corrections with replacement wording

1. **Sinha and Kapur (principle 3).** Replace the sentence with: "A 2021 review of 53 studies found that trying a
   problem before being taught helped older students understand ideas, on average. For grades 2 to 5 the few
   studies pointed the other way: teaching first did better. In every comparison both groups were taught; only
   the order changed. We read summaries of this review, not the paper." (firm on direction, likely on detail)
2. **Software (principle 10 and the math page).** Replace "thin" with: "The National Mathematics Advisory Panel
   found that practice and tutorial software has generally helped in studies, and that one large national study
   found no gain when schools used it in the ordinary way. So we do not count on software to teach by itself."
   (firm)
3. **Tutoring ("The two rooms").** "Tutoring one student or a small group has unusually good evidence ... Few
   of those studies were of parents, and the gains were smaller for them." (firm)
4. **PhET.** "The study was done with college students." (firm)
5. **Worked examples (principle 2).** Add: "The rated evidence is for math and science problems. For reading and
   writing we rely on modelling, which is common practice with weaker evidence." (firm)
6. **Evidence level names.** "strong, moderate, or minimal (the oldest guide says low)". (firm)
7. **Concrete materials (principle 11 and math page).** Add one sentence: "The same guide warns that an idea
   learned only with objects can stay stuck to them, so the objects are faded out on a named day." (firm)

### What is missing, and whether it matters

- **Pashler recommendation 5a, rated Low** (pre-questions before teaching). It bears directly on opening
  questions and the readiness check. Add one line to "thin or contested". Matters. (firm)
- **NMAP findings 11, 25 and 27**, in a source already read: automatic recall needs enough practice; regular
  formative assessment helps in the elementary grades; explicit teaching for students with math difficulties has
  consistently positive effects. They strengthen principles 2, 4 and 6 and the fluency paragraph. Matters. (firm)
- **Concreteness fading** (Fyfe, McNeil, Son, Goldstone 2014) and the **manipulatives meta-analysis**
  (Carbonneau, Marley, Selig 2013: small to moderate gains, larger for retention than transfer, from memory and
  secondary summaries). These are the direct evidence for "concrete, then pictures, then symbols", which now
  rests on indirect sources. Matters for the math page. (likely)
- **Virtual manipulatives.** The tools are on-screen manipulatives, so this is the claim closest to the product,
  and the page admits it read nothing. It should stay flagged as the top gap until one review is read. Matters.
  (firm)
- **Hattie and Timperley 2007.** Useful for one thing: tool feedback should be about the task and the next
  step, not about the child. One sentence in principle 6 or "What a tool is for". Matters a little. (likely)
- **Cognitive load theory.** Already carried by Clark et al. and Deans for Impact. A separate source is not
  needed. (judgment call)
- **Learning trajectories (Clements and Sarama).** Matters for K to 2 math, not for the grade 4 pilot. Park it.
  (judgment call)
- **Nothing was read on parents teaching their own child.** FOUNDATION names that parent as an audience. The
  page should say so in "thin or contested". (firm)

## Part 2. Findings on the approach, most serious first

### F1. The shared core is partly math in disguise, and conflicts with the approved science spine

- **What is wrong.** Principle 6 promises "a daily exit question". The grade 4 science spine says "There is no
  daily exit question", on the advice of its own review (line 91). Principle 4 promises a five-minute warm-up
  every day from printable generators; the science spine has none. Principle 5 and unit plan item 4 mark each
  day as understanding, fluency or use; that is the math rigor triad from the Publishers' Criteria. Science sorts
  days as hands-on, tool, print and end task; ELA counts days by strand. "The role of the standards" cites only
  the Common Core math document for every subject. Principle 11 leans on Rosenshine ("hands-on after the
  basics") and principle 3 on "explore first only where it helps", while the science spine opens every stretch
  with a phenomenon.
- **Evidence.** docs/courses/science-4-spine.md; docs/reviews/science-4-spine-2026-11-11.md;
  docs/courses/ela-6-spine.md; Publishers' Criteria p3.
- **Cost.** A science or ELA unit plan written to the shared checklist would either break its spine or carry
  empty fields. A reviewer reading both documents would see the contradiction.
- **Change.** In the shared core keep the job and drop the format:
  - Principle 4: "Every unit plans where earlier work is practised again from memory. How is set by the subject
    page."
  - Principle 5: rename "Know it, do it, use it" and say "each subject page names the kinds of day it balances".
  - Principle 6: "Every day leaves evidence the teacher can act on. In math this is an exit question; in science
    it is the notebook and the class model, with a short written check per lesson set."
  - Role of standards: one sentence per standards family, each citing its own document.
  - Move the warm-up, exit question and understanding/fluency/use marks to the math page.
- **Confidence.** Firm. An adoption reviewer would mark this down.

**What the science page must add or override.** Phenomenon or design problem first is the rule, not the
exception, with sense-making and teaching following (source: A Framework for K-12 Science Education; NGSS
appendices; EQuIP rubric). Three dimensions in every end task. Investigation days and materials tiers. No daily
exit question. Worked examples apply to calculation and data work, not to building an explanation. Models are
revised, so "the model is withdrawn" does not carry over.

**What the ELA page must add or override.** The text is the unit: text complexity, text sets and knowledge
building, volume of reading, the writing process and the writing calendar, vocabulary taught from the texts,
foundational skills in the lower grades, the daily question always about the text. "Model then fade" becomes
modelled reading and writing, with weaker evidence than math worked examples. "Fluency" means reading fluency,
a different thing.

### F2. Principle 3 draws a grade line the evidence cannot hold

- **What is wrong.** "From about grade 6, a unit plan may open a concept with a problem" turns a thin moderator
  result into a rule. Illustrative Mathematics K-5 puts the task first in grades K to 5 and is widely adopted.
  The rule would also forbid what the grade 4 science spine does every stretch.
- **Evidence.** Sinha and Kapur 2021 (not verified at source); IM K-5 design principles; NMAP finding 23.
- **Cost.** Grade 4 math lessons could become all telling, losing the short "what do you notice" openings that
  cost five minutes and reveal prior knowledge. Or the rule gets quietly ignored.
- **Change.** Replace the grade line with a condition: "A lesson may open with a short task (ten minutes at
  most) when students already have what they need to attempt it and the idea is a concept, not a procedure. The
  teaching follows the same day. In grades 2 to 5 teaching first is the default for anything new." This is the
  recommended decision on drafter flag (a).
- **Confidence.** Likely. Reasonable reviewers differ on the default; none would defend a hard line at grade 6.

### F3. "At Curio Class" lines are written in the present tense about things not built

- **What is wrong.** "Every check lists the common wrong answers", "a five-minute warm-up opens each day". No
  unit exists yet. The opening disclaimer covers it once, but EdReports indicator 3e looks for how the approaches
  are "implemented in lessons and activities", and FOUNDATION promises honest words.
- **Evidence.** EdReports Math K-8 v2.0 evidence guide, indicator 3e (p71 to 72); docs/FOUNDATION.md.
- **Cost.** A teacher reads a promise and finds no lesson behind it.
- **Change.** Write these lines as rules for unit plans ("A unit plan must ..."), and after the pilot add one
  real pointer per principle ("see grade 4 unit 11.1, day 3").
- **Confidence.** Firm.

### F4. Indicator 3e: likely full credit on paper, with three risks

Indicator 3e asks for three things: the approach explained, research-based strategies included and referenced,
and the role of the standards included and referenced. All three are present. Risks: (1) the page must sit in
the teacher-facing materials, not only in the repo; (2) reviewers look for lesson-level examples (F3); (3) many
citations are to summaries, which is acceptable, but the role-of-standards part is math only (F1). I do not
predict a score; no reviewer has seen this. Confidence: likely.

### F5. The fluency paragraph stops at an open decision that the sources can close (drafter flag c)

- **What is at risk.** The page is right that "no time pressure" cannot be sourced. But it leaves the timer
  open, and a timer is a product decision with a known downside for anxious students (contested, from memory)
  and for the no-win-states feel of the tools.
- **Evidence.** WWC 2021 rec 6 (timed work as one part of taught small-group help); NMAP finding 11; Publishers'
  Criteria p11.
- **Change (recommended decision).** No countdown timer shown to the student in any tool. Fluency is built by
  short, spaced, mixed practice with immediate feedback. A teacher or parent may run a one-to-three-minute paper
  sprint on facts already taught, where the student compares only with their own last score, never graded or
  posted. Say plainly: "The evidence supports short timed practice inside taught small-group help. It does not
  tell us whether a whole class needs a clock." This changes what tools do, so it goes to Cory.
- **Confidence.** Judgment call.

### F6. SPEC.md "Explore first, then do" (drafter flag b)

- **What is at risk.** The SPEC rule is about a tool's first minute (click around before the task). Beside
  principle 3 it reads as a discovery stance, and BASELINE 21 requires a model in every lesson tool.
- **Change (recommended decision).** Rename the SPEC rule "Try it, see it, do it" and add one line: "The opening
  try is under a minute and withholds nothing; the model follows at once." No change of substance. It is a
  change to SPEC.md, so the caller files it for Cory.
- **Confidence.** Likely.

### F7. Small-group help (drafter flag d)

- **Judgment.** The honest statement is right and should stay. An adoption reviewer does not expect a core
  program to be an intervention program (from memory of the EdReports criteria on supports), but does expect
  the core to say what a teacher does for students who are behind.
- **Change (recommended decision).** Do not build a program. Do three small things: each unit plan lists, per
  readiness gap, the earlier-grade tool or page and a ten-minute reteach; the math page adds NMAP finding 27 and
  points teachers to the WWC 2021 guide by name; the page says who Curio Class cannot serve alone (students two
  or more years behind). The grade 4 spine already admits "no reteach time beyond the four respond days"; the
  approach should repeat that limit.
- **Confidence.** Likely.

### F8. Real-world contexts (drafter flag e)

- **Judgment.** The claim is confirmed but narrower than it reads. The panel was judging a teaching approach
  built on real-world problems, from few studies. The standards still require application in the major work.
- **Change.** Replace "they are used for application days, not as a cure-all" with: "Contexts are how students
  learn to apply math, which the standards require. We do not expect a context to teach the idea by itself."
- **Confidence.** Firm on the source, judgment call on wording.

### F9. Twelve principles: right content, two too many, order could teach better

- **Judgment.** Nothing is missing that an adoption reviewer would look for. Principles 1 and 9 say the same
  thing from two sides (prior knowledge). Principles 10 and 11 are one decision (which medium carries the idea).
  Merging gives ten. A clearer order follows a unit: plan (what students know; knowledge builds; know, do, use),
  teach (clear steps and models; when to open with a task; talk and writing; screen or hands), practise, check,
  everyone (grade-level work with supports), motivation.
- **Cost of not changing.** None to students. A teacher is less likely to finish a twelve-item page.
- **Confidence.** Judgment call. This is taste, not a mark-down.

### F10. Conflicts with FOUNDATION, BASELINE 20 to 23, the teaching test

I found no contradiction with FOUNDATION or BASELINE 20 to 23. Two tensions to name: BASELINE 21 requires a
model in every lesson tool, and principle 3 allows a problem-first opening (resolved by F2 and F6); unit plan
item 10 asks for a "teaching one child" note, which FOUNDATION supports but no source backs (say so). The math
page agrees with the grade 4 math spine (number line carried through, strategies before algorithms, times
tables as maintenance). Confidence: likely; I did not re-read every spine line in this run.

## Part 3. As a working document

### F11. It is about twice the length the roadmap asked for

- **What is wrong.** ROADMAP describes "a shared core of about two pages and a page for math". The shared page
  runs near 2,800 words before sources; the math page near 1,300.
- **Change.** Keep the principles; cut the evidence prose.
  - In each principle keep "What we do" and one "Why" sentence with the rating. Move the study detail (counts,
    effect sizes, who was studied) to docs/APPROACH-SOURCES.md.
  - Cut "The two rooms" to four sentences and move Bloom to sources.
  - Fold "What a tool is for" into the merged screen-or-hands principle.
  - Keep "Where the evidence is thin or contested" whole. It is the most valuable section.
  - On the math page cut the eight practices list (the standards have it) and the focus, coherence, rigor
    definitions to two sentences.
  - Target: 1,500 words shared, 900 math.
- **Confidence.** Firm that it is over the stated length; judgment call on the cuts.

### F12. A teacher can read it; a parent mostly cannot

The words are plain. The barriers are length, federal guide names in running text, and terms like
"interleaving" used without a gloss in places. Change: add a ten-line box at the top, "What this means in your
room", written for the parent teaching one child. Confidence: judgment call.

The ten shared and six math items cover the right ground. Problems: items 2, 4 and 5 are math-shaped (F1);

## What is right and should not be touched

- "Where the evidence is thin or contested", and the sentence "We wanted to say fluency is best built without
  time pressure. We could not source that, so we do not say it."
- Evidence ratings given in the guides' own words, and the read status on every source.
- Calling Clark, Kirschner and Sweller an argued position, and UDL a design framework.
- Black and Wiliam used without an effect size; Bloom cut down to size.
- The three jobs of a tool (show why, practise with feedback, check unaided).
- On the math page: strategies before standard algorithms by grade, the number line as the carried picture,
  "fluently never means with blocks", wrong worked examples only after the right one, grade-level work for
  students who are behind, and the plain admission about small-group help and on-screen manipulatives.

## What I could not judge

- The Sinha and Kapur paper itself (blocked). Needs the full text, mainly the grade 2 to 5 study count.
- Seven sources listed under Part 1 as not verified.
- Whether a reviewer gives 3e full credit. Needs a built unit and the page in its teacher-facing form.
- Whether teachers and parents can read it. Needs two of each to read it cold.
- Whether the proposed fields fit in two pages. Needs the three pilot units.
- I did not re-read docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md or the last third of the science and ELA spines in this run.

## For the Review Desk (caller files)

Three findings change what a course or tool does and need Cory: F1 (move math-shaped promises out of the shared
core), F5 (no student-facing timer; optional paper sprints), F6 (rename the SPEC.md rule).
