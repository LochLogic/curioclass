### Verdict

The page is sound and honest. Every evidence rating and every figure it states is confirmed at source, and no
claim is invented. The biggest risk is that it looked in the wrong guides for grade 6: it missed the federal
guide written for grades 4 to 12, so it undersells discussion, vocabulary and strategy teaching, and it stretches
two other ratings (text sets, volume of reading) past what those guides studied. The one change that matters
most: add the 2008 adolescent literacy guide, restate those five paragraphs with the wording below, and move
the text record, question set and word list into data rows so a unit plan can hold them inside its word cap.

### Findings, most serious first

**F1. The page missed the federal guide for grades 4 to 12, so three paragraphs rest on the wrong grades.**

- *What is wrong.* "Improving Adolescent Literacy" (Kamil and others, IES practice guide, 2008) covers upper
  elementary, middle and high school classrooms. The spine review already used it. This page does not list it.
  So the page says discussion is "rated minimal, and that for kindergarten to grade 3", rests vocabulary on the
  English learner guide alone, and says the like finding for strategy teaching in grades 4 to 9 is only for
  students with reading difficulties.
- *Evidence.* I read the checklist (PDF 15 to 16), the level-of-evidence paragraph of all five recommendations
  (PDF 17, 22, 27, 32, 37) and step 4 of recommendation 2 (PDF 24), of 65 pages. Recommendation 1, explicit
  vocabulary teaching in regular lessons with repeated meetings in several settings: strong, students in upper
  elementary, middle and high school; the panel warns the effects are on word learning and only some studies
  show a gain in comprehension. Recommendation 2, direct and explicit teaching of comprehension strategies,
  with the teacher modelling by thinking aloud and then guided practice: strong, on five randomized studies.
  Recommendation 3, extended discussion of what a text means: moderate, on four small studies and one large
  correlational one. Recommendation 5, intensive help from specialists for students who struggle: strong. Two
  limits: this guide is the oldest of the set, its panel chose the studies and did not run a full search, and
  its lowest level is called "low", not "minimal".
- *Cost.* The pilot's day 4 is a discussion and its word list is a core piece. The page tells a teacher these
  rest on almost nothing for grade 6. A reviewer checking the research base sees K to 3 and intervention guides
  cited for a grade 6 core course.
- *Change.* Add the guide to the Sources table. Replace three passages:
  - Talk: "Extended discussion of what a text means is rated moderate for grades 4 to 12, on a small set of
    studies (WWC 2008, recommendation 3). For kindergarten to grade 3 it is rated minimal, because it has
    rarely been tested there (WWC 2010, recommendation 3). The standards also require it."
  - Vocabulary, after the Appendix A sentence: "Teaching words directly in regular lessons, with repeated
    meetings in several settings, is rated strong for grades 4 to 12; the studies show word learning, and only
    some show better comprehension (WWC 2008, recommendation 1). Teaching a small set of academic words in depth
    is rated strong for English learners (shared page, principle 9)."
  - Modelled reading, third bullet: "Reading: teaching comprehension strategies directly, with a model and a
    planned handover, is rated strong for kindergarten to grade 3 (WWC 2010, recommendation 1) and, in an older
    guide with a looser search, for grades 4 to 12 (WWC 2008, recommendation 2). For grades 4 to 9 a newer
    guide rates the like practice strong for students with reading difficulties (WWC 2022, recommendation 3)."
  - Thin list: change "Discussion. Minimal, and for younger grades." to "Discussion. Moderate for grades 4 to
    12, on few studies; minimal for younger grades."
- *Confidence.* Firm.

  words is rated strong" sits under "Text sets that build knowledge". Part A of that recommendation is brief
  background before one passage, inside small-group help. It says nothing about a set of texts on one topic.
- *Evidence.* WWC 2022, PDF 28 to 29: three to five minutes each for world and word knowledge before reading.

- *Change.* Replace the sentence with: "For students with reading difficulties in grades 4 to 9, a few minutes

- *Confidence.* Firm.

**F3. Volume of reading: one rating is stretched, one citation is not in the source, and the gap can be closed.**

- *What is wrong.* Claims 7 and 8 in the table. And the page says "we opened no rated source" on independent
  silent reading, when a public domain federal report speaks to it directly.
- *Evidence.* National Reading Panel, Reports of the Subgroups, 2000 (public domain, 449 pages). I read PDF 192
  (3-3) and PDF 216 (3-28) and the paragraph on its search. It found only 14 studies that tried to raise the
  amount of independent silent reading (among them sustained silent reading programs), mostly with no gain. It
  concluded there is "not adequate evidence" that encouraging students to read more raises reading achievement,
  and that this does not show such programs cannot work. It also says hundreds of correlational studies find
  that the best readers read the most. It found clear evidence for guided repeated oral reading. The report
  uses no strong, moderate or minimal scale. It is 26 years old; I read no later review.
- *Cost.* The spine's weekly 20-minute silent reading block is the practice this report could not support. The
  spine already lists the block under "Least sure". The page should say why.
- *Change.* Replace the paragraph's evidence sentences with: "The publishers' criteria ask for several longer
  texts a year and for daily reading of the student's own choice. The best readers read the most, but that is a
  correlation. The National Reading Panel (2000) found too few studies, mostly showing no gain, to say that
  setting aside time for silent reading raises achievement; it did find clear support for guided oral
  rereading. We read no later review." Add to the rule: "Independent reading time always has a teacher
  conference and a log, and it never replaces a reading day." Move the K to 3 connected-text rating to the
  fluency paragraph, with its limit (mostly one-on-one, read aloud with feedback).
- *Confidence.* Firm on what the report says; likely on what later research adds.

**F6. The grammar gap can be closed today, and the spine's "from memory" mark can come off.**

- *Evidence.* Writing Next (Graham and Perin, Carnegie Corporation, 2007; free to read, all rights reserved;
  77 pages). I read the sentence-combining section (PDF 26) and "A Note About Grammar Instruction" (PDF 29).
  It is a meta-analysis of studies in grades 4 to 12, with writing quality as the outcome. Teaching parts of
  speech and sentence structure as a subject of its own had a small negative effect, across the range of
  ability. Sentence combining had a moderate positive effect (0.50), mostly measured against that kind of
  grammar teaching. One study found that grammar taught for use inside writing had a strong positive effect.
  The report's own conclusion is that teaching grammar matters and that the method should change. Limits: the
  outcome is the quality of writing, not a test of conventions (L.6.1, L.6.2); it is a summary, and I did not
  read the studies. Appendix A (PDF 29 to 30) adds that students return to the same grammar topics as their
  writing grows; the page did not read those pages either.
- *Change.* Replace "We opened no source on grammar taught apart from writing; the spine's line on it is from
  memory and stays marked." with: "In a review of studies in grades 4 to 12, grammar taught as a subject of its
  own did not improve writing, and sentence combining did (Writing Next, 2007; the outcome was writing quality,
  not a test of conventions)." Add to the rule: "Sentence combining is a named method." Strike "Grammar apart
  from writing. Nothing opened." from the thin list. In the Sources table, change the Writing Next row to
  "Read in part (sentence combining; the note on grammar)". In the limit on recommendation 3, say "mostly
  students at risk, in pairs or small groups; two studies on sentences".
- *Confidence.* Firm on what the report says; likely as a guide to teaching conventions.
