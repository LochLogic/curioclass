## Verdict

Day 72 is correct mathematics in a sound shape: expanded form beside the algorithm on the same sum, under place
names, with "One what?" at every small 1, is the right way to tie 4.NBT.B.4 to place value, and a no-prep teacher
can run it. The biggest risk, and the writers' own question, is the exit: yes, E72 duplicates Example 1. It has
the same carry pattern (ones and hundreds carry, tens do not) and asks for the very carry that Example 1, its
frame, the closing Ask, the register's error C example and the family note all rehearse, and its wording names
the answer's column; a student can answer "one thousand" by copying side 1 or echoing the question. The change
that matters most is finding 1: a new exit with a carry pattern the day did not model, asking for a small 1 by
where it came from, not where it sits.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. E72 rehearses Example 1, and its question gives away the answer

- **What is wrong.** Example 1 (3,746 + 2,538) carries from the ones and the hundreds, not the tens. E72 (4,658 +
  2,724) does exactly the same. The exit then asks for "the small 1 above the thousands column", which is the
  one carry the day drills hardest: Example 1's step 5 ("12 hundreds is 1 thousand ... One what? One thousand"),
  side 1's frame "____ hundreds is 1 thousand and ____ hundreds", Pull it together ("Then ask the same of the 12
  hundreds"), the register's C example (errors.csv, "the small 1 over the thousands called 'a one'") and the
  family note ("Over the thousands it would be one thousand"). The unit plan says the opposite: u09-1.md section
  3 reads "E72 asks the worth of the small 1 over the thousands, not the tens, so the answer is not the one the
  day's sum rehearses". That is only true of the 14 ones; the day's sum rehearses both carries. On top of that,
  "above the thousands column" puts the answer in the question, so register error C's second sign ("one
  hundred", named by the column it came from) can hardly appear, though day 73 is built to act on it. Example
  2's frame, "The small 1 over the hundreds is one ____", teaches the same echo.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s09-K72-1 steps, s09-X72; d072.md student page side 1; errors.csv row C; family.md;
  u09-1.md section 3. LESSON-DAY.md "What day 90 showed" item 7 (the exit's answer was the worked example's) and
  rule 3. Same kind as day 85 finding 2 and day 92 finding 2. Illustrative Mathematics grade 4 unit 4 lesson 18
  sets expanded form beside the algorithm and has students compare them (read today); its cool-down is behind a
  sign-in, so I could not see how it checks.
- **Cost.** E72 is the only evidence on the day's goal ("say what each small 1 is worth"), and day 73's opening 5
  minutes are spent on what it shows. As written it reports "fine" for students who copied a pattern from side 1,
  and the C branch on day 73 almost never fires.
- **Change.** New exit `s09-X72v2` (see "Settled by the expert"): "Add 4,563 + 2,871 with the standard algorithm.
  Circle the small 1 you wrote when you added the tens. What is it worth?" Carries over the hundreds and the
  thousands, none from the ones: a pattern neither example uses. The question names where the 1 came from (the
  tens), so error C's "one ten" is now the visible wrong answer, and "one hundred" needs the trade (13 tens is 1
  hundred and 3 tens). Example 2's frame becomes "____ tens is 1 ________ and ____ tens." (16, hundred, 6), so
  side 1 rehearses the trade, not the echo. u09-1.md section 3's sentence becomes "E72 asks about the small 1
  made from the tens, in a sum whose carries fall where neither worked example's do." Process: `plan.py check`
  notes an exit on a 4.NBT.B.4 day whose carry (or ungrouping) places are the same set as a worked example's on
  that day.
- **Confidence.** Firm (the rows themselves; LESSON-DAY.md's own day 90 lesson).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes. **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read saw the column name
  in the question, not the duplication of Example 1 or the plan's wrong claim; its fix, "name every small 1",
  keeps the same sum and pattern).

### 2. The column sheet cannot hold what the day sends to it

- **What is wrong.** The day sends eight sums to the column sheet: five in Columns alone, two right sums in Fix
  it ("Add it right on your column sheet") and the challenge ("Use your column sheet"). The sheet has seven
  blocks (three on side 1, four on side 2), and each block has two number rows, so the challenge's three addends
  have no row. "Under each sum, students write what each small 1 is worth", but each block ends at the sum row
  and the next block starts about a quarter inch below: there is no line to write on.
- **Evidence.** columns.md, rendered (7 blocks of header, dashed row, two number rows, sum row); d072.md Columns
  alone, Fix it, Challenge. Same kind as day 84 finding 6 and day 86 finding 6.
- **Cost.** Fast finishers reach the challenge and stall; the worths, the day's goal, end up squeezed in a margin
  or skipped, and the teacher cannot read them.
- **Change.** Fix it: drop "Add it right on your column sheet"; the right sum is Example 1's, already on side 1,
  so the line becomes "Say what went wrong. Show the step on Example 1." (Right sum line stays.) That frees two
  blocks. Columns alone: each cell of the 7 to 11 table on the student page gets "Small 1s worth: ______" under
  the sum, and the teacher line reads "Beside each sum on the student page, students write what each small 1 is
  worth." Challenge: "Use grid paper." (grid paper is already the stand-in in the materials line).
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes. **CHEAP:** missed (it saw items 7 to 11 had no place on
  the student page, not that the sheet itself has none, nor the block count or the third addend).

### 3. Side 1's expanded-form line holds a fifth of what the steps write

- **What is wrong.** Each example gives one line of about 2.4 inches, "Expanded form, place by place: ____".
  The teacher page says students fill the frames as the teacher goes, and the steps write two expanded forms and
  then the place-by-place sum ("3,000 + 700 + 40 + 6, and 2,000 + 500 + 30 + 8 ... 5,000 + 1,200 + 70 + 14").
  The rendered side 1 has no spare room under either example.
- **Evidence.** d072-student.pdf side 1; s09-K72-1 and K72-2 steps.
- **Cost.** Half the day's model, the expanded form, cannot be copied onto the page students keep; they write
  it tiny or not at all.
- **Change.** Ask only for the place-by-place line, in four blanks: "Place by place: ______ + ______ + ______ +
  ______" (5,000 + 1,200 + 70 + 14; 5,000 + 1,100 + 150 + 13). The two numbers' expanded forms stay on the board
  and the display.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes. **CHEAP:** missed (it measured items 5, 6, 12 and 13,
  not side 1).
