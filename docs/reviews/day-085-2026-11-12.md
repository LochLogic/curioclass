## Verdict

Day 85 is correct mathematics in the right place: it adds the third position of the unknown (Table 2's "number
of times"), keeps the "who has more?" habit before any numbers, and its discussion (every copy drawn beside one
long strip marked "n times") is the best idea in the unit for moving students off drawing every copy. The biggest
risk is the exit: it does not ask for the sentence answer, so the role swap (error C) that "Tomorrow" and day 86
both act on can hardly show on it, and its answer, 4, is the worked example's answer. The one change that matters
most is to make the exit ask for a sentence and to give the example a different answer (finding 1 and 2, both
settled below); then lengthen the answer lines so the sentences the day asks for have room.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The exit cannot show the error that tomorrow is built on

- **What is wrong.** "Tomorrow" leads with: on the slip, "Jon read 4 times as many" (error C), and day 86's first
  three minutes act on it. But the exit asks only "Write an equation with a letter, and solve." A student answers
  "n × 5 = 20, n = 4" and never writes who read more. The question itself already names Pat first ("Pat read how
  many times as many..."), so a swap in the equation is unlikely too. The sign day 86 waits for will almost never
  appear, and day 86's C branch runs on nothing.
- **Evidence.** d085.md "Tomorrow"; s10-X85 stem; d086.md "Day 85's exit back"; LESSON-DAY.md section 7 ("naming
  the sign of each error on both exit paths"). Day 84's exit asks for strips; day 85's asks for neither strips nor
  a sentence, though the whole day practises both.
- **Cost.** C is the error IM and the register tie to this lesson (IM lesson 3, the register's C row); the day
  teaches the sentence answer for 50 minutes and then does not look at it.
- **Change.** Exit stem ends "Write an equation with a letter. Solve it, and answer in a sentence." (new id, rule
  8: s10-X85v2; answer gains "Pat read 4 times as many pages as Jon"). Process: when a "Tomorrow" names a sign,
  the exit row must have a wrongs.csv row for it that the stem can produce; a sentence-shaped wrong answer needs a
  stem that asks for a sentence (a check a script can make: a quoted wrong answer on an exit whose stem lacks
  "sentence").
- **Confidence.** Firm (the stem and the hand-off, read side by side).
- **Tags.** LAYER process. CATCH cheap read. REPEAT yes (day 89 finding 5: an error with no sign on the slip).

### 2. The exit's answer is the worked example's answer

- **What is wrong.** s10-K85-1 is 36 ÷ 9 = 4 times; s10-X85 is 20 ÷ 5 = 4 times, the same part (d2). A student
  who copies "4 times" from the example's frame on side 1 gets the slip right.
- **Evidence.** items.csv. The day 90 read found the same thing (exit answer 9 equal to the example's) and
  LESSON-DAY.md "What day 90 showed" item 7 says each such finding "is now a rule or a gate"; the lesson-day
  review asked plan.py to "report (not fail) an exit whose answer repeats a taught answer for the same part".
  plan.py's no-reuse check (lines 272 to 283) compares stem numbers only, so it was not built.
- **Cost.** The day's only evidence on its goal can be passed by copying.
- **Change.** Keep the exit's numbers (20 and 5 collide with nothing) and give the example another answer.
  Stretch 10's fact space for d2 is nearly used up (see "Shared"); 36 and 4, answer 9, is the one choice that
  collides with no exit, readiness or end-check item, and it makes the discussion's "9 times" question point back
  at the example's long strip. Settled below. Process: build the report the day 90 read asked for.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **Tags.** LAYER process. CATCH script. REPEAT yes (lesson-day read, day 90).

### 3. The answer lines are too short for the sentences the page asks for

- **What is wrong.** Every problem says "Answer in a sentence", and the page prints "Answer: ______" about two
  inches long beside the equation line. "The tall jar holds 6 times as many buttons as the small jar" needs a full
  line. Side 2 leaves its bottom third empty. The second frame under "Words and supports" ("The long strip is __
  copies of the short strip") is not on the student page, though LESSON-DAY.md says such frames are printed there.
- **Evidence.** d085-student.pdf, both sides; d085.md lines 145 to 183; LESSON-DAY.md "The student page", second
  bullet. Day 84 prints a line twice as long.
- **Cost.** Students write "6" or "6 times", which is exactly the habit (no sentence, so no check of who has more)
  that lets error C hide.
- **Change.** Each problem gets two lines: "Equation: ________________" and, under it, "Answer in a sentence:
  ________________________________________" (full width). After the example's frame add "The long strip is ____
  copies of the short strip." Rebuild and confirm two sides; side 2's empty third pays for it.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **Tags.** LAYER process (the template has no rule that a line fits the answer it asks for; days 87 and 88 have
  the same short lines). CATCH cheap read. REPEAT yes (day 89 finding 2, day 90 finding 10).

### 4. Six problems in 18 minutes, in blocks, with no must-do set

- **What is wrong.** Day 84 gave 25 minutes to six problems of two kinds. Day 85 gives 18 minutes to six of three
  kinds, after only 5 minutes of teaching the new kind, and runs them in blocks: 1 and 2 larger unknown, 3 and 4
  smaller, 5 and 6 number of times. In blocks a student copies the move from the problem above and never decides
  which kind it is, and a slow student never reaches the new kind, the day's goal. "Shorter or longer" says
  nothing about this part.
- **Evidence.** d085.md "Six problems"; d084.md "25 min. Six problems" (mixed on purpose: "1, 3 and 5 ... 2, 4 and
  6"); days.csv rows 84 and 85. Mixed practice of problem kinds helps students pick the method (Rohrer and Taylor,
  2007, and Rohrer's later classroom trials: from memory, not in APPROACH-SOURCES.md; Pashler et al. 2007 covers
  spacing and worked examples, not mixing kinds, so it is not cited for this).
- **Cost.** Some students leave without having solved a "how many times" problem alone; the rest solved them by
  pattern.
- **Change.** Reorder so problems 1 to 3 are one of each kind and 4 to 6 again: 1 Rosa (P85-2), 2 Pia (P85-4),
  3 jars (P85-6), 4 whale (P85-5), 5 puppy (P85-3), 6 tower (P85-7). Teacher page: "Problems 1 to 3 are one of
  each kind; everyone finishes those. 4 to 6 as time allows; problem 6 is drawn for all in the discussion."
  Renumber the watch line ("252 or 648 on problems 2 and 4") and the fix-it line ("problem 3's answer"). Settled
  below.
- **Confidence.** Likely (the minutes, read against day 84); the mixing is a judgment call.
- **Tags.** LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT new.

## What is right and should not be touched

- The "who has more?" question before any numbers, in the slips-back branch, the practice and the fix.
- The discussion: every copy beside one long strip marked "n times", with "which would you draw for 9 times?". It
  is how the stretch moves students off drawing every copy before day 86 makes drawing optional.
- The fix (Ann and Raj): the swap is the right error for this day, and the right picture is clear.
- Pairs writing a problem for another pair, with the "hide the times" note: real MP.4 work, and it asks students
  to make the very situation the day teaches.
- The challenge (3 times 3 times is 9 times): on the same standard, and a door to grade 5 scaling.
- The warm-up two-step problem, which sets up readiness item 3 and day 87.

## What I could not judge

- Whether 18 minutes holds for six strip problems with real grade 4 students; only a taught run can say.
- The large-print page and the display in a browser (I read them as text and SVG).
- IM grade 4 unit 5 lessons 3 and 4 were not reopened for this read; their use rests on s10.md's source notes.
