# Sensitivity read: science-kindergarten (course finish, stage 7)

Date: 2026-11-22. Kind: sensitivity. Target: the whole course (unit plans s01 to s06, the frame's "Sensitive topics and teacher notes" column, every day, card and family note in courses/science-kindergarten/u01-1 to u06-2). Read-only: nothing but this report was edited.

## Verdict

Pass with fixes. The course is calm, never asks a child about a home, a family, a coat, a meal or a bin, and keeps storm pictures small and invented. Nine small fixes below (none blocks use) tighten the places where the room itself could still expose a child's circumstances.

## Topics found

Severe weather and young children who may have lived through it (unit 2.2, plus the weather cards in the mid-year check); coats and clothing at recess (2.1); families, homes and houses (4.2, 4.3, 6.2 meadow map); the town water source (4.2); food, eating, lunch and pets (4.1, 4.3, 6.2); land clearing and logging (4.3, 6.1); litter, recycling and waste (6.1, 6.2); heat, sun and shade at recess (5.1, 5.2); bodies and strength in pushing and pulling (1.1, 1.2); skin tone in pictures (all card sets); family notes in English and Spanish (every unit). No cultural, Native, faith, hard-history, health or disability content is taught (the unit plans say so, and the scan agreed).

## What I studied

Opened and read on 2026-11-22:

1. After a Crisis: Helping Young Children Heal, NCTSN (https://www.nctsn.org/resources/after-crisis-helping-young-children-heal). Only the landing page opened: a 2005 fact sheet for caregivers of young children; the PDF text was not read. It confirms the audience the course writes for.
2. Talking to Children About Disasters, American Academy of Pediatrics (https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Pages/Talking-to-children-about-disasters.aspx). Taught: simple, concrete explanations; limit graphic images and sounds; ask what a child already knows; do not promise safety that is not true; name helpers.
3. Natural Disasters: Brief Facts and Tips, NASP (https://www.nasponline.org/resources-and-publications/resources-and-podcasts/school-safety-and-crisis/natural-disaster-resources/natural-disasters-brief-facts-and-tips). Taught: stay calm, acknowledge and normalize feelings, build coping and peer support; young children may regress, speak less or show more anxiety; recovery can take months or years. It says nothing about forced disclosure, so that rule rests on the frame and the AAP framing.
4. Disasters, NCTSN (https://www.nctsn.org/what-is-child-trauma/trauma-types/disasters). Taught: disasters can bring loss of home and belongings, displacement, school change and economic hardship, so a classroom may hold such a child; most children are resilient; knowing what to do helps families feel in control.
5. Lightning safety overview, National Weather Service (https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-safety-overview). Taught: "When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors"; substantial buildings and hard-topped vehicles are safe, sheds and rain shelters are not; stay in 30 minutes after the last thunder or lightning. The page does not mention trees.
6. Ready Kids, Ready.gov (https://www.ready.gov/kids). Taught: preparedness is framed as an upbeat family activity with Spanish and educator resources. It gives no framing advice for the youngest children.
7. Lightning safety page, National Weather Service (https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning), for context: it notes a "Flash, Dash Inside" slogan made for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing community.

Refused or missing: NAEYC equity statement (403), Head Start family engagement page (403), National PTA standards (404), CDC children's mental health page (404). Each got one other source tried; none was retried or worked around.

No voice from children or families who have lived a disaster was opened (the plan says those accounts are "still to select"); see "Could not settle".

## What is right (keep)

- Weather pictures are held to "a cloud, a clock, a bolt kept small, never a damaged place or a frightened person" (u03-1/cards-check-mid.md; the unit 2.2 cards follow it). The read-aloud draws no frightening weather and never says a storm came. This matches the AAP and NASP advice to limit graphic content.
- "If a child speaks of a frightening storm, thank them quietly and go on" is on days 15, 16, 17 and 19 and in the opener. It matches the no-forced-retelling posture.
- "Possible, never will come," and the lightning wording (go into the substantial school building; the school's plan sets how long to stay) match the weather service page.
- Coat hooks are invented drawings or photographs with names covered and no child in view; "Ask no child about their own coat, home or family" (u02-1/cards-coat-hooks.md). The snack picture is the teacher's own, "never a child's lunch" (u04-1/cards-eats.md). The people card is an adult with no face detail that "stands for people, not for any child". Litter: "No one is blamed... No child is asked about home waste or recycling," and "recycling depends on the place" (u06-1/opener.md). Family notes say "any answer is a good answer" and "nothing to send back to school."
- Every card set says pictures show many skin tones, hair and abilities, and the days say pointing and any home language count.

## Findings

None high. Four medium, five low.

### Medium

**M1. "Wait until thunder" is printed as an action card and teaches the wrong rule.** Files: u02-2/cards-assessment-forecast.md (Before the storm, card two: "a child outside with a hand at an ear; 'wait until thunder'"); u03-2/cards-check-mid.md (Page A action cards: "plan the picnic indoors first, wait until thunder, go into the school building, stand under a tree"). The weather service says thunder means you are already within striking distance, so go in right away. If the card is a distractor, say so in the key; as printed it reads as a plan. Fix: change the caption to "stay out until thunder, then go in" with a key line "Thunder is the signal to go in at once; the planned indoor play before the afternoon is the better plan." If it is meant to be the right second step (day 15: "if thunder is heard, we go in"), reword it "go in when thunder is heard".

**M2. A child with no coat at morning recess has no cover on days 10 to 12.** Files: u02-1/d010.md, d011.md, d012.md, family.md (the posted unit question "why do we need our coats at morning recess"). The frame says "No child is asked about a home that lacks heat or coats" and the cards honor it, but the days send children outdoors in the morning and never say what the teacher does for a cold child or a child in no coat. Fix: add to the Safety line of days 10 and 11: "Keep a few spare coats in the room for any child; never remark on whose coat is whose or who forgot one." In the family note, change the question to "why does the playground feel cooler in the morning than after lunch" or add "if a coat is hard to find, tell the teacher; the room has spares."

**M3. A real town water source can expose a family's water situation.** Files: u04-2/d037.md, map-pips-town.md, opener.md. The printed town map is used when the teacher has read the water report. Some families haul water, use a well, or live under a boil notice; the day already says "No home, address or family is asked about", which is right. Fix: add to the teacher page: "If the town has an active water advisory, or you cannot say its source in a calm sentence, use Pip's town. Never ask where a child's home water comes from. If a child says theirs is different: 'Water can come from different places; today we follow the school's.'"

**M4. "Less harm" and a lone worker with a saw can read as blame toward families in tree-cutting or factory work.** Files: u04-3/d040.md ("star plan 1 and say 'less harm'"), u04-3/plans-pips-town.md, u06-1/chains-journeys.md chains 1 and 2. The course says "without blame" and both plans build the same six houses, which is good. But "less harm" is a verdict, and the only person shown in chain 1 is the cutter. A child whose parent works in timber, a mill, a factory or on a farm could hear their family named as the harm. Fix: in d040 replace "less harm" with "more living things left in place" (the next phrase already says it) and add "People need homes and people need trees. Both plans are people trying to meet a need." In chain 1 add the spoken line "People also plant new trees." In chain 2 keep "a factory can make the air hazy" and add "and many people work to make it cleaner."

### Low

**L1. Family-structure assumption.** u04-3/cards-assessment-model.md: the people card is "two adults and a child". Draw it as a group of different ages (for example a grandparent, an adult and a child) so no child sees a pattern they are not in.

**L2. Pets.** u04-1/d033.md and d034.md use "pictures of a pet"; keep it as pictures and avoid "your pet" anywhere. No change needed.

**L3. At-home walks assume a safe neighborhood.** u06-1/family.md ("on a walk, look at a patch of ground") and u04-1/d032.md ("go outside with a grown-up"). Add "or look out a window or at a picture."

**L4. Shade and slide access.** u05-1/opener.md and d044.md assume the school has a slide and a tree. Add: "If your playground has no shade or slide, use a bench, a wall or the building's edge; never ask which children live near a park."

**L5. Eye protection for everyone.** u01-1/d001 to d004 and u01-2: add "Fitted eye protection goes over glasses; a child who cannot wear it directs a partner from behind the line; never single out the child."

## Could not settle

The notes meet the course's own rules and I found no line that breaks them, but I cannot cite a family-engagement body for them. Browser check (guidance pages, no fact depends on them, so no sources.csv row): https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/equity/overview and https://headstart.gov/family-engagement.
2. **Voices of children and families who lived a disaster.** s02.md says two voluntary public accounts are "still to select". I did not find or open one. The practice (no disclosure, thank and move on) is consistent with what I read; the lead decides whether the plan's study step needs them named.
3. **NCTSN After a Crisis PDF** text was not read (landing page only).
4. The accuracy of the town water report line and the Denver and Seattle thunder-day counts was outside this read; the unit plans give their sources.

## Three worst

1. M1: "wait until thunder" printed as a plan card against the weather service rule (two files).
2. M2: no teacher line for a child with no coat on the outdoor looks (days 10 to 12).
3. M4: "less harm" and the lone saw and factory worker may read as blame (days 40 and 56).
