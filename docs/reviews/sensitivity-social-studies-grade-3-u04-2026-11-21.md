# Sensitivity read: social-studies-grade-3 unit 4 (days 72 to 89)

Date: 2026-11-21. Report only; no file was edited. Scope: courses/social-studies-grade-3/u04-1 to u04-5, items.csv rows s04-*, sources.csv rows U4A to U4AB. Line numbers are file lines (frontmatter included). Source support was judged against the "status" text in sources.csv; no web page was re-fetched.

## Must-fix (5)

**M1. A made-up Native nation (Sandbar Camp).**
File: u04-5/cards-redo-day89.md line 47 (items s04-F89f, F89g, F89h; key-day89.md lines 17 to 19).
Text: "Each summer the people of a made-up nation net fish at a river mouth. They camp on a sandbar there. In fall they move inland."
Problem: a made-up nation with a seasonal round is an invented Native nation (rule 1). Labelling it made up does not cure it, and the seasonal fishing camp mirrors real nations' pages used in the unit.
Fix: make it a made-up fishing village or a made-up summer fish camp run by a made-up town, not a "nation". Keep the chain (river mouth, netting, summer).

**M2. A made-up "people" with a homeland and lands-held-today legend (Pine Lake).**
File: u04-2/cards-exits-75-76.md lines 26 to 37 (item s04-E75b; key-exits-75-76.md line 12; d075.md line 62).
Text: "A made-up map of one people near Pine Lake." Legend: "= homeland ... [#] = lands held today".
Problem: the legend words only make sense for a nation, so this is an invented Native people with a homeland.
Fix: call it a made-up town or park near Pine Lake and use a made-up key ("the town's land", "the town hall"). The T-error (shading taken as lands held today) can still be taught. Record A on day 88 (Pine Landing and Gull Bay) already does this correctly; copy that pattern. Related, should-fix: u04-5/cards-redo-day89.md lines 32 to 43, River Bend Map, uses "homeland" and "lands held today" on a made-up map with no owner named. Same fix.

**M3. Eastern Band page credited with a fact the sources row does not support.**
Files: u04-3/cards-day78-cherokee.md line 14; u04-4/d085.md line 47 ("What do both cards agree on? Expect: the Trail of Tears."); u04-4/cards-day85-events.md lines 18 to 19.
Text: "After the Trail of Tears, some Cherokee people remained in the East." Credit: "the Eastern Band's own Government page."
Problem: row U4L records only that the Eastern Band is "made up of those who remained and rebuilt in the Qualla Boundary." It does not record the Trail of Tears. Day 85 builds a lesson on two separate makers agreeing about removal, so the Eastern Band card must really say it. The Cherokee Nation page (U4K) does support the removal facts.
Fix: either add a row to sources.csv from an Eastern Band page that states it (and cite it), or reword card 2 to "some Cherokee people remained in the East and rebuilt in North Carolina" and rebuild day 85's agree/differ task so it does not claim the Eastern Band page names the Trail of Tears.

**M4. Penobscot "homeland along the river" and shaded map have no source row.**
Files: u04-2/d075.md line 28 ("shading marks the homeland along the river, as the Nation's page puts it"); u04-2/blank-day75-penobscot-map.md lines 14 to 22; u04-2/blank-day75-legend.md line 14; u04-3/d077.md line 25 ("so its homeland lies along them"); u04-3/cards-day82-compare.md line 12 ("so the homeland lies along it"); u04-1/d072.md line 28.
Problem: U4A supports only that the Penobscots have relied on Maine's rivers for food, drinking water and travel for thousands of years. It does not say where the homeland lies, and U4D explicitly cut any boundary. The map then shows a single [#] square at Indian Island, which row U4D supports only as the address of the offices, yet the legend calls the square "a place the nation holds today". For a child, one dot plus one strip of shading can read as "the Penobscot homeland is a strip, and today's land is one square", which understates both (the Nation's trust lands in the Mattamiscontis area appear in row U4C).
Fix: say only what the pages say ("the Penobscots have relied on the rivers of Maine"); drop "as the Nation's page puts it" unless a row is added for the Nation's own map; label the square "the Nation's offices" (not "lands held") or add a row that supports lands held; add a line that the shading and square are a teaching sketch and the Nation's own map is the authority.

**M5. Place-names claim that row U4B cut.**
Files: u04-4/d084.md line 22 (G repair: "The Penobscot Nation shares its place names, its page says."; O repair: "The Nation shares them now."); u04-4/key-exits-83-85.md line 12 ("The Nation shares Penobscot place names and their meanings in its school lessons"); u04-4/d083.md line 59.
Problem: U4B says "CUT: that the Nation publishes place names (the page does not say so)" and permits only "students study maps with place names in Penobscot and explore their meanings." The teacher repairs and key state a broader, unsupported claim in the Nation's name.
Fix: use the row's wording: "In the Nation's Homeland unit, students study maps with place names in Penobscot and learn what they mean."

## Should-fix (14)

**S1. "Long ago" framing for living nations.** u04-2/cards-day76-chains.md line 12 ("long ago members had two homes"; row says "historically"), u04-2/d076.md line 25 ("the page tells of long ago"), u04-3/cards-day79-menominee.md line 12 ("Long ago the Menominee lived across..."; row says "once"), u04-3/d079.md line 25, u04-3/cards-exits-77-82.md line 21 ("Long ago, the Choctaw homeland was in Mississippi"), u04-3/cards-day78-cherokee.md line 21 (Map 1 "long ago"). Fix: use "in earlier times" or "once" as the pages do, and in each place follow with a present-tense line (the Tribe lives and governs today). Choctaw: row U4AA does not name Mississippi as "homeland"; say "The Choctaw people were forced from their land, much of it in what is now Mississippi" only if a row supports it.

**S2. Past tense about a living nation's practices.** u04-3/cards-day82-compare.md line 14 ("The people harvested wild rice in the fall. The homeland lay across..."), u04-1/cards-day74-sources.md line 12 ("what the Menominee did through the year"), u04-4/cards-day83-contribution.md line 18 ("in the fall they had wild rice"; lower-case "Menominee tribe"), u04-3/cards-day81-makah.md line 14 (Deah paragraph all past tense), u04-3/d082.md line 56 frame "were the same". Fix: attribute and place in time ("Menominee historic preservation officer David Grignon says, in a 2014 interview, that in the fall the Menominee had wild rice"), and add that the Makah Tribe's home is Neah Bay today. Use the full name "Menominee Indian Tribe of Wisconsin" at least once on the card.

**S3. Narragansett "bay" has no support.** u04-2/blank-day75-new-england-outline.md line 25 ("A nation around a bay in Rhode Island"), u04-2/d076.md line 31 ("a second strip for the bay"). Card 2 on day 75 says "a nation in Rhode Island": U4F has no such line (U4G, used only on day 88, does); cite or reword.

**S4. Navajo river facts beyond the row.** u04-3/cards-day80-navajo.md line 14: "begins in southern Colorado" and "a network of ditches and pipes" are not in row U4T (which says tributary of the Colorado, narrow farming valley below Navajo Dam, network of irrigation facilities, 110,630 acres). Also "in the Southwest" (line 12, d080 line 25) is not in U4S. The card never says the Irrigation Project is the Navajo Nation's, so the chain "river, irrigation, fields" is not tied to the Nation by any row. **S5. Makah "from cedar" not in row.** u04-3/cards-day81-makah.md line 14 "People made canoes from cedar." Row U4U says canoes for halibut and salmon fishing only; cedar appears in U4W (the basket). Fix: drop "from cedar". Also "Washington" and "Neah Bay offices" rest on a row that records only the Tribal Council address; add "Washington" to the row or cut it.

**S6. Stream card overstates.** u04-3/cards-exits-77-82.md line 14: "it has rebuilt the outlets of three lakes". Fix: "The Nation is rebuilding lake outlets" and say "more than twenty stream connectivity projects are finished" as the row does. Matches key-exits-77-82 line 12.

**S7. Aquinnah "lands the Tribe holds today".** u04-5/cards-exits-86-87.md lines 39 to 43 and key-exits-86-87.md line 13. Row U4J says only that the common lands include the Gay Head Cliffs, Herring Creek and Lobsterville; "holds" is not stated. The key's "whole island is the homeland" rests on row U4I ("lived ... throughout the island"). Fix: label "common lands, named on its page" and keep "the island" as where ancestors have lived.

**S8. Weak-card prompt teaches a stereotype.** u04-4/d083.md line 28 ("Nations gave corn"), repeated as error G in d084/d085, key-day87-review.md line 15, key-day88.md line 19 ("Corn and beans, from every nation"). The sentence the children are told to fix is itself the "Natives gave corn" trope. The correction (name one nation, one thing, one source) is good. Fix: use a neutral weak card, such as "Nations shared things", and describe G in the keys as "every nation shared the same thing".

**S9. "Indian children" in student copy.** u04-5/packet-day88.md line 41 ("tutoring, counseling and workshops for Indian children"). It follows the source's own word but is a child-facing blanket term. Fix: "Native children" or "children of tribal nations". Also the row says this is the Johnson O'Malley program open to students of any federally recognized tribe; Q5 asks for it as "a contribution of the Narragansett people", which may be a stretch (a federal program run by the tribe's office). Consider a different record B contribution, or say "the Education Department runs a tutoring program".

**S10. Removal events as a sorting game.** u04-4/cards-day85-events.md line 14 ("Many Cherokee people died on the Trail of Tears.") is a card children lay "before or after" others. Fix: tell the teacher to read events 1 to 3 aloud once and set them out already in order; sort only events 4 to 6 against them. The "any child may pass" line (d085 line 28) is good; also add "no child is asked to read these cards aloud".

**S11. **S12. Treaty record gives only the preamble with no safeguard.** u04-5/packet-day88.md lines 63 to 76. The 1854 preamble and "seven treaties" (U4O) are accurate and fine for a sources question, but a third grader will take this as "an agreement between equals". Also the quote uses the period spelling "Menomonee tribe of Indians"; add a read-aloud note that the paper uses the government's old spelling and wording, and that the tribe's name today is Menominee.

**S13. Fix: capitalize Native; rewrite card 4 to name the three nations on the other cards ("The Menominee and Makah cards show two nations' uses of plants"); replace "ancient" with "earlier".

**S14. Made-up unnamed "people" in Salt Marsh and Herder cards.** u04-5/cards-day87-review.md line 28 ("people gather reeds ... weave mats"); u04-3/cards-exits-77-82.md line 57 ("herders keep sheep. In summer they place camps"). Both are labelled made up, but each mimics practices on real nations' pages (mats; seasonal camps; sheep herding is tied to the Navajo Nation in many teachers' minds). Fix: put each in a made-up town with a made-up name and a plainly non-Native setting (a basket-weaving club at a made-up marsh town; a made-up dairy farm with summer pasture).
