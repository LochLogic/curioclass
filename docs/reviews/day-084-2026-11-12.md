## Verdict

Day 84 is correct mathematics, in a sound shape for a no-prep teacher: one story with the unknown moved, six
problems, the register's error B fixed on purpose, a planned question that attacks "times means multiply", and a
clean hand-off to day 85's first three minutes. The biggest risk is that every "smaller amount missing" item on
the day, and the exit, uses one sentence form ("N. That is k times as many as ..."), the larger-unknown items use
another, and the teacher is told to teach that form as a rule; a student can then sort by the shape of the
sentence, which is the key-word habit in new clothes, and the exit cannot tell the two apart. The one change that
matters most is finding 1: reword three practice items and the exit so the comparison comes first in both kinds,
break the 1-3-5 / 2-4-6 alternation, and replace the "number before 'that is'" rule with "who is times as many as
whom".

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The sentence form gives the operation away, and the exit tests the form

- **What is wrong.** On day 84 every smaller-unknown item (Example 2, Fix it, practice 2, 4, 6, the exit) reads "X
  has N. That is k times as many as Y has", and every larger-unknown item reads "Y has a. X has k times as many as
  Y". The practice alternates strictly (1, 3, 5 larger; 2, 4, 6 smaller). The teacher page then teaches the
  pattern "the number before 'that is' belongs to the longer strip". A student can get all six and the exit right
  by "if it says 'That is', divide", without ever asking who has more. Only the challenge (comparison first,
  "Ana's kite string is 5 times as long as Tim's. Ana's string is 35 feet long") breaks the pattern, and most
  students will not reach it. The same two forms run through day 85 (P85-4, P85-5), day 86 (P86-3, X86) and the
  end check (E4 "It is 4 times as tall as a bush"), so stretch 10's evidence on 4.OA.A.2 part d1 cannot separate
  understanding from sentence-sorting.
- **Evidence.** items.csv, every s10 row with "That is" or "It is". Illustrative Mathematics grade 4, unit 5,
  lesson 3 (read today) mixes forms on purpose: one smaller-unknown problem states the comparison before the
  amount, and its synthesis rotates the unknown through all three places. The Progressions, Operations and
  Algebraic Thinking, warn against key-word strategies for word problems (from memory; not reopened today). The
  unit opener's own thesis: "a student draws first and decides the operation from the picture, not from a word".
  Same kind of defect as the expert ledger's E92 row (an exit that used the taught cue words) and day 92's
  finding 2.
- **Cost.** The day's goal ("when the bigger or the smaller amount is missing") is met on paper by students who
  have swapped one cue for another; they meet "Ana's string is 5 times as long as Tim's" on a state test and
  multiply. The teacher's E84 record says "fine" when it is not.
- **Change.** Items (see "Settled by the expert"): three practice items and the exit get the comparison first, in
  both kinds, and the order stops alternating. New order: 1 larger (Lin, as now), 2 smaller (rope, as now), 3
  smaller, comparison first (Zoe), 4 larger (sunflower, as now), 5 larger, comparison first (train), 6 smaller,
  comparison first (bus). Teacher page, "Teaching first", replace the last two sentences with: "Do not teach a
  word rule. Ask who is 'times as many as' whom: that one gets the longer strip, wherever the sentence puts it."
  "Six problems" paragraph: "1, 4 and 5 have the larger amount missing; 2, 3 and 6 the smaller. Three of them say
  the comparison first." Shorter path stays "problems 1 to 4" (still two of each). The unit opener's line "'That
  is 3 times as many as ...' is taught as a pattern on day 84" becomes "Both orders are met on day 84: 'Zoe picked
  5 times as many as Ben. Zoe picked 45' and '... 45. That is 5 times as many as Ben picked'". For the stretch:
  day 85, day 86 and end-check item 4 each need at least one comparison-first item (shared note below).
- **Confidence.** Firm (the rows themselves, and IM lesson 3).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes.

- One story, the unknown moved, the two pictures side by side: the clearest possible case that "times" does not
  decide the operation. The planned question ("The problem says 'times'. Do we multiply?") is real MP.3.
- Fix it is the register's error B example, as days.csv and errors.csv place it, with "Max cannot have 24: that is
  more than Jo" as the reason. That is the right check, and it uses the strips.
- "Who has more?" before any number, asked while walking, with the right signs listed for each error.
- The reread step: "Reread the story with 56 in it." Students learn to check by meaning.
- Hand-off with day 85's first three minutes: complete, with the exit's real wrong answers (63, 18, 24).
- Numbers stay inside the one-digit facts plus one multiple of 10 (7 × 50), as the stretch's rule allows.
