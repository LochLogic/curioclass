## Verdict

Day 6 is sound mathematics in the right check-day shape: six items that cover all four parts of 4.OA.B.4 with
new numbers, earlier marked work kept off desks until pencils go down, a respond half built from the register,
and a clean hand-off to day 7 (s02.md's day 7 row opens with items 5 and 6). The biggest risk is that the check
cannot see two things the stretch teaches and the day then acts on: no item asks about 1, so error G (taught
with a wrong example on day 5, and given a group today) has no evidence anywhere in the stretch, and item 4
gives a prime no place to show its search, so a guess and a proof look the same on the record. The one change
that matters most is finding 1 with finding 2: one reworded item 4 (`s01-E4v2`, below) that adds 1 to the
list and asks for the numbers tested on each prime.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Error G is never tested: no exit or end-check item asks about 1 or 2

- **What is wrong.** The register's error G ("calls 1 prime; calls 2 composite") is taught on day 5 (F5-2, the
  sort with "Neither"), and today's F and G group writes the factors of 1 to 4. But no exit and no end-check
  item contains 1 or 2: E4 is 71, 93, 94, 53; E5 is 59, 77, 34, 73; E5v2 is 99. In wrongs.csv, G appears only on
  taught rows (F5-2, P5-3, P5-4, P5-6, T5-5), never on an X or E row. So the record sheet can never show G, no
  student can be sent to the G task on evidence, and the standard's range, "1-100", is checked without its one
  special case.
- **Evidence.** wrongs.csv tally by letter (s01 rows); 4.OA.B.4 text in data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt;
  Illustrative Mathematics grade 4, unit 1, lesson 3 (read today) states in a synthesis that 1 is neither prime
  nor composite. Same kind as day 85 finding 1 (the exit cannot show the error the next day is built on).
- **Cost.** A student who still thinks 1 is prime leaves stretch 1 unrecorded; the check claims part (d) whole.
- **Change.** Item 4 becomes `s01-E4v2` with five numbers, 71, 93, 1, 94, 53 (see "Settled"), the table gains
  a row, and wrongs rows "1 prime" (G) and "1 composite" (G) are added. Side 2 has about an inch free below item
  6, enough for one row. Process: `plan.py check` warns when a registered error has no wrong-answer row on any
  exit or end-check item of its stretch.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** script. **REPEAT:** yes.

### 2. Item 4 gives a prime nowhere to show its search, and its column invites "1 × 71"

- **What is wrong.** The table's third column is "Factor pair that proves it" on every row, primes included.
  A student who calls 71 prime has an empty box or writes "1 × 71", which the key's own wrongs row says "proves
  nothing". The stem asks for proof of composites only, so a prime is a one-word answer, and the unit's thesis
  ("prime needs a search to the turn that finds none", d005; "the chart makes prime a result", opener) is not
  tested. "Show your work" heads the page but item 4 has no room for the 93 search either. The cheap read noted
  that "for each composite number" hints that composites exist; that is the smaller half of this.
- **Evidence.** d006-student.pdf side 2; wrongs row "1 × 94 given as the proof"; d005 frame "__ is prime: I
  tried __ to __ and found no pair".
- **Cost.** A student who calls every odd number past the tables prime, except 93, scores full marks on the
  primes; the record cannot tell a search from a guess.
- **Change.** In `s01-E4v2`: "Prove each one. For a composite, write a factor pair. For a prime, write the
  numbers you tested." Table heads: "Number | Prime, composite or neither? | Proof". Key for a prime: "tested 2,
  3, 5 and 7" (or any search in order to the turn). A prime with nothing tested is recorded x with the note "a
  look, not a search: ask what they tested".
- **Confidence.** Likely (judgment on how strict the record is; the gap itself is firm).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes.

### 3. Four groups work at once, and the display shows one group's task at a time

- **What is wrong.** "Tasks are on the display." The display builds one 30 point, 90-percent-high screen per
  group and one for the challenge, so three groups and the sieve students need four screens at the same time on
  one projector. This is the side effect of day 93 finding 8's fix (one screen per group).
- **Evidence.** d006-display.html (five `screen` blocks, `.screen { min-height: 90vh; font-size: 30pt }`);
  DEFAULTS.md "The respond half".
- **Cost.** Groups wait while the teacher scrolls; the first minutes of 15 go to "what do we do?".
- **Change.** Day: "Before class" adds "Write the three group tasks on a covered part of the board." (the "No
  projector" line already has this). Process: on a check day the display adds one screen with all group tasks
  together at a smaller size, shown during the groups; the per-group screens stay for explaining.
- **Confidence.** Likely.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new.

### 4. The record sheet is first printed on day 6, after five days of recording on it

- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 88 finding 5, record sheet print).

### 5. The D and E sort puts 9 in both lists, and nobody tells the teacher

- **What is wrong.** "Sort 1, 3, 9, 18, 27 and 54 into 'factor of 9' and 'multiple of 9'." 9 is both. A sort
  into two piles reads as one place each; a group of students who mix up the words will argue, and a no-prep
  teacher may call either answer wrong. It is the same fact the key notes for E2 ("a number is a factor and a
  multiple of itself").
- **Evidence.** errors.csv row D, `if_many`; d006 D and E line; opener table; wrongs note on s01-E2.
- **Cost.** The one group built to fix factor and multiple swapped gets a puzzle with no stated answer.
- **Change.** Register D `if_many`, and so the opener and d006: add "9 goes in both: 1 × 9 = 9 says it." 
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process (the register's text is copied to the opener and the day). **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new.

### 6. The F and G test set has no prime, so "the search finds nothing" is never seen

- **What is wrong.** "Test 2, 3, 5 and 7 in order on 39 and 57. Call a number prime only when the search finds
  nothing." Both are composite (3 × 13, 3 × 19); the register's set (39, 57, 95) is all composite too. The
  students in this group are the ones who call a number prime by its look; they practise finding a factor but
  never finish a search that comes up empty.
- **Evidence.** errors.csv row F; d006 F and G line.
- **Cost.** Half of the move (prime as a finished search) is skipped for the group that needs it.
- **Change.** Register F: "39, 57, 95 and 97"; d006: "on 39, 57 and 97". 97 is prime (7 × 13 = 91, 7 × 14 =
  98) and is on no stretch 1 row. To keep the group's load (the cheap read's point), the G task line is done
  only by students who called 1 prime or 2 composite, once finding 1 makes that visible.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new.

### 7. Item 6's look-fors miss a right reason

- **What is wrong.** A student may answer "Yes. Any number from 6 on needs a partner smaller than 6, and she
  has tried those" (6 × 6 = 36 is already past 35). That is correct, arguably stronger, and hits neither
  look-for 2 ("7 × 5 is 5 × 7 turned") nor 3 ("every pair after that repeats one she has") as worded.
- **Evidence.** s01-E6 `steps` and `samples`.
- **Cost.** A teacher ticking look-fors literally marks a right argument down; it goes on the record.
- **Change.** Look-for 2: "says 7 goes with 5, a pair she has (or that any number past 5 needs a partner she
  has already tried)".
- **Confidence.** Likely.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (day 88 finding 4, look-fors mark down a right answer).

### 8. The answers screen speaks of letters, and item 3 shows one working

- **What is wrong.** The screen students mark from opens "Any letter is right. An equation is right in any order
  if it says the same thing." Stretch 1 has no letters; this is stretch 10's sentence printed on every check
  day, on day 6 of the school year. Item 3 asks "Show how you know", and the screen shows one form (4 × 19 =
  76); a child who counted on from 4 × 10 = 40 or fenced 76 differently sees a mismatch.
- **Evidence.** d006-display.html; day 88 finding 8 (the fix that added the sentence).
- **Cost.** A confusing first line, and right work circled as wrong in pen.
- **Change.** Process: lesson.py prints "Any letter is right" only when a check item's key holds a letter. Item
  3's displayed answer adds "Any working that lands on 76 for 4, or shows 76 between two 6s, is right."
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes.
