## Verdict

The day's teaching move is right: a short model of a change in meaning against a change in words, then a long quiet
writing block with conferences, and it names slavery plainly and refuses to balance a Lost Cause counterclaim. It is
not teachable as written, for reasons that mostly sit above the day: the P1 packet and the model draft pair do not
exist, the model is set on the packet the unit says must stay apart from teaching, and the feedback students revise
from is specified three different ways. The one change that matters most is to settle, at unit level, what the day 77
feedback names (including the inconsistency W81 scores) and to supply the model pair on South Carolina's declaration.

## Findings, most serious first (missed by the cheap and sensitivity reads)

### 1. The model draft pair is built on the P1 packet, against the unit's separation rule

- **Wrong.** "What revision is" says "Keep the comparison within P1" and has the teacher prepare the pair "from a
  returned draft and an evidence-checked revision". The unit plan says "Teach with South Carolina's declaration and
  different Lincoln passages" and "Selected passages stay separate from teaching and exits"; sources.csv P1-MS says
  "never in teaching or exits". The cheap read's fix ("built on the P1 sources") would make this worse.
- **Why it matters.** A model revision on the exact question and sources students are revising hands them a
  replacement claim, which the day itself forbids ("Do not supply a replacement claim"), and it weakens P1 as the
  day 89 component. Using a classmate's real draft, even unnamed, is identifiable in a small class.
- **Fix.** Section plan Day 81 names the model's source as 75-SC (already in sources.csv) and lists "model draft pair"
  under Materials and Print; write it as a small section piece (about 80 words before, 80 after: a claim and a
  counterclaim on South Carolina's declaration, plus one spelling-only edit), and change the day's line to "The pair
  uses South Carolina's declaration, not your packet." Drop "from a returned draft".
- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT new. CHEAP missed. ORIGIN section.

### 2. The feedback students revise from is specified three ways, and W81 scores a part none of them asks for

- **Wrong.** Opener: feedback "on two things: the claim's precision and the counterclaim", plus one question. Day 81
  Before class: feedback "must question an unsupported claim, a counterclaim and an evidence limit". Unit plan field
  5: the same three. W81's look-for "D3.3: the inconsistency the feedback named is dealt with" needs feedback that
  names an inconsistency, which no version asks for. And the day's rule arrives the night before day 81, after the
  feedback was written and returned.
- **Why it matters.** A teacher following the opener writes feedback that makes one W81 look-for unscorable, and
  P81-1 ("copy the one sentence of your claim that it questions") cannot be answered if the feedback questioned the
  counterclaim only.
- **Fix.** Unit plan field 5 (the feedback sentence) and the opener's "Before the section" both read: "Feedback on
  draft 1 names four things: one unsupported claim, a counterclaim to strengthen, an evidence limit, and one
  inconsistency across the packet's sources to deal with." Day 81 Before class drops the "Feedback must ..." sentence
  and says only "Check that each draft's feedback names its four things." P81-1 stem: "Copy the one sentence the
  feedback questions (claim or counterclaim)".
- LAYER day. CATCH cheap read. REPEAT yes (handoff). CHEAP missed. ORIGIN unit.

### 3. The day that owns D3.4 never asks for significance

- **Wrong.** D3.4.9-12 is "claims and counterclaims for precision and significance" (data/standards/c3-indicators.txt;
  C3's own wording is precision, significance and knowledge conveyed). X81 asks how evidence changed "precision and

  day, yet the day 89 component (s05 field 5, D3.4 row) scores "precision, historical significance and knowledge".
- **Why it matters.** Students are scored on day 89 for a part of the standard the revision day never names.
- **Fix.** items.csv s05-W81 first look-for: "D3.4 claim: more precise about who, when and what, says why it matters

  to why the war came." Add "why it matters" to the X81 look-fors. Day page line in Words and supports: "Significance
  means why the claim matters to the question."
- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT yes (coverage). CHEAP missed. ORIGIN unit.

### 4. P1-FRE cites the volume of Freehling that ends in 1854

- **Wrong.** sources.csv P1-FRE: *The Road to Disunion*, vol. 1 (1990). Volume 1, *Secessionists at Bay*, covers 1776
  to 1854; volume 2, *Secessionists Triumphant*, 1854 to 1861 (Oxford University Press, 2007), is the one on the
  secession crisis the P1 question asks about. From memory, firm on the volume spans.
- **Why it matters.** A paraphrase "checked against the book" from volume 1 cannot speak to 1860 to 1861, so the
  packet's second interpretation would not meet the election counterclaim the day revises around.
- **Fix.** P1-FRE citation to "William W. Freehling, The Road to Disunion, vol. 2, Secessionists Triumphant, 1854-1861
  (Oxford University Press, 2007)", date 2007; volume 1 may be added as background only.
- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT new. CHEAP missed. ORIGIN unit.

### 5. P1-MAP's citation names a record that holds electoral votes, not returns by state

- **Wrong.** "drawn by Curio Class from the returns as reported to Congress, 1861". Congress counted the electoral
  votes (February 13, 1861: Lincoln 180, Breckinridge 72, Bell 39, Douglas 12). Popular returns by state are state
  records. Lincoln was not on the ballot in ten slave states, which the map must show, since it is the packet's
  evidence for "sectional power". From memory, firm.
- **Why it matters.** Day 81 tells the teacher to "check P1-MAP's returns" against a record that does not hold them;
  a map without the "not on the ballot" states invites the misreading that Southern voters rejected Lincoln in a
  contest he was in.
- **Fix.** Citation: "drawn by Curio Class from the electoral count of February 13, 1861 and the states' official
  returns"; sources.csv note: "show the ten states where Lincoln was not on the ballot".
- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT new. CHEAP missed. ORIGIN unit.
