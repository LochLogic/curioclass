## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The second half of the day cannot tell the right reason from error D, and may teach D

- **What is wrong.** For two fractions each one part short of 1, the one with bigger numbers is always the larger.
  So D gives the right sign on Example 2 (4/5, 5/6), the planned question (5/6, 7/8), Rio's fix, One part short 3
  and 4 (3/4, 2/3; 1/2, 99/100), Dee's claim (3/4, 5/6) and exit (b) (7/8, 11/12). The rule said in Pull it
  together, "more parts in the whole means each part is smaller", is easy to hear as "bigger bottom, bigger
  fraction". Only the first half (Each side of 1) and exit (a) can show D. The day's only item where D fails in the
  one-part setting is Challenge 1 (5/4 and 13/12: D says 5/4 < 13/12, the truth is 5/4 > 13/12), and most
  students will not reach it.
- **Evidence.** Reasoning, checked against every row above. The unit plan names D as the error to watch all unit
  (errors.csv row D, from the Progressions, PDF page 149 (143), and the federal fractions guide, roadblock 2.2).
  From memory: the Progressions present the one-part-short comparison as reasoning about the missing piece, not a
  rule about denominators.
- **What it costs.** A student who answers by big numbers gets the whole second half right, Dee's claim right
  without the look-fors, and part (b) of the exit right; the teacher reads "secure" and day 19 starts on a hidden D.
- **Change.** In items.csv, change s03-C18-1's `check` to `practice` (keep the id; numbers unchanged) and print it on
  side 2 as One part short 5, with "Extra: ______ and ______" under it. Rename the block "One part short, or one
  part more". Give it 2 more minutes (One part short 6 to 8) and take them from Dee's claim (10 to 8). Add a third
  **Ask.** to Pull it together: "Someone says the fraction with bigger numbers is always closer to 1. Test it on 5/4
  and 13/12." Expect: "no: 13/12 is closer, and it is the smaller one; it has the smaller extra part". In the
  Watch-for line of One part short add: "a right sign on 3 and 4 is not enough; ask what each is missing. Item 5
  shows who is using bigger numbers (D)". s03-C18-2 stays the challenge. Confidence: likely.
- Tags: LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT yes (day 84 finding 1: items whose form lets the wrong method score);
  CHEAP missed (the cheap read saw only that exit (b) cannot show D).

  built page and the code).

### 3. Ivy's right working leads with renaming, which this section rules out

- **What is wrong.** The opener says common denominators "are section 3.4's, and nothing here may lean on them",
  and the exit asks "How do you know without renaming?". F18-2's right working (steps, answer cell and the teacher
  prose "then 2/3 = 4/6 on the line") settles Ivy by renaming to sixths and comparing 4/6 with 5/6, a
  same-denominator comparison. The day's own method, "2/3 is one third short of 1 and 5/6 only one sixth short",
  comes last as "Or".
- **Evidence.** opener.md, "The math, for the adult" and "Limits"; items.csv s03-F18-2 `steps` and `answer`.
- **What it costs.** On the day that builds the missing-part reasoning, the fix models the other way, the one day 19
  teaches. J students in particular see the denominator trick again.
- **Change.** s03-F18-2 `steps`: "Ivy's working: thirds are bigger pieces, so 2/3 > 5/6.|Right working: 2/3 is 2
  parts of size one third; it is one third short of 1. 5/6 is 5 parts of size one sixth; it is one sixth short. A
  sixth is smaller than a third, so 5/6 is closer to 1, and 2/3 < 5/6." `answer`: "Not right: 2/3 is 1/3 short of 1
  and 5/6 only 1/6 short, so 2/3 < 5/6". Keep the figure (4/6 straight under 2/3 is a fair picture check). Teacher
  prose, Fix it: "Ivy ... (J): say each as 'how many parts, of what size', then shade both missing parts on the
  board line: 1/3 is longer than 1/6." This also makes the row two steps, which sidesteps finding 2 for this row.
  Confidence: a judgment call (2/3 = 4/6 on a line is section 3.1 work, but comparing in sixths is 3.4's method).
- Tags: LAYER day; CATCH expert; REPEAT new; CHEAP missed.
