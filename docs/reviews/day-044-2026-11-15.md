## Verdict

Day 44 has the right shape: the minutes add to 50 and match days.csv, the hand-off in from day 43 and out to days 45
and 46 hold in substance, the sources are real and quoted exactly, and the feedback-before-scoring chain is sound.
The biggest risk is the evidence itself: each source was cut so it tells one side (Mongke only tolerant, Polo's money
only enforced, Ibn Battuta only praising), so the three board columns tilt before students weigh anything, and the
claim students write in 15 minutes has only a limit frame and a model answer that fails its own counterclaim
look-for. The one change that matters most: restore the two cut lines that join faith to the sword (Mongke's letter)
and money to exchange (Polo), and give the claim a weighing frame with a corrected model.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Source F is cut to show the Khan's tolerance only; the same visit records faith used as a mandate to conquer

Source F stops at "so he gives to men divers ways". Rockhill's next pages (248 to 250) give Mongke's letter to Louis
IX from the same stay: "The commandment of the eternal God is, in Heaven there is only one eternal God, and on Earth
there is only one lord, Chingis Chan", followed by the demand that Louis submit or face war. As cut, F is the only
Mongol "faith" evidence and it reads as religious tolerance, so the faith column for the Mongols becomes "they let
people worship freely". That is a weak and partly misleading answer to "won mainly by trade, faith or the sword": the
richer and truer reading is that the khans claimed Heaven's mandate for conquest and ruled many faiths after it. Rockhill
p. 235 also has Mongke telling Rubruck "your interpreter translated badly", and p. 248 has Rubruck writing the letter
down "as well as I could understand through an interpreter": a ready-made transmission limit the claim's limit
look-for needs. Fix: add a second short excerpt to sources-44.md (Settled 1), and one "Expect" line on the teacher page:
"Source F's letter joins faith and the sword: Heaven's one lord on earth, and war for those who refuse."
Confidence: firm (Rockhill 1900, pp. 235 and 248 to 250, checked).

- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT yes (a source cut drops the line the day needs, day-078 finding 4, day-079 finding 6).
  CHEAP missed (the sensitivity read glossed "one God" as Tengri; neither read used the letter). **ORIGIN section.**

### 2. Ibn al-Athir is framed as an outsider; he wrote as a Muslim mourning his own world

sources-42d.md says "Both writers were outsiders writing for readers at home"; opener.md heads its list "Outsiders,
all of them" and puts Ibn al-Athir there. Browne's p. 427 shows the opposite: he opens by calling the event "the
death-blow of Islam and the Muslims" and shrinking from recording it. He was a historian of the attacked Muslim lands,
writing from reports of people who fled, with a victim's horror (he calls the Mongols "accursed miscreants" a page
later). On day 44 the claim must "name a limit", and the W44 model's limit is "both writers were outsiders"; a
student who applies the sheet's frame to Source E names the wrong limit. The right limits are second-hand reports and
a hostile, grieving perspective, not distance. Fix: sources-42d header and opener (Settled 6). The day's own line
("Ibn al-Athir heard from people fleeing events") is right and stays.
Confidence: firm (Browne 1906 scan, pp. 427 to 429).

- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT yes (a maker described wrongly on the sheet, day-080 finding 6). CHEAP missed (the
  sensitivity read praised "every traveller framed as an outsider"). **ORIGIN section.**

### 3. The W44 model answer fails two of its own look-fors

s03-W44's meeting sample ends "Counterclaim: Mali's ruler also had armed guards. Limit: both writers were outsiders."
The look-fors are "a counterclaim answered with evidence" and "a limit of the sources named". The sample names a
counterclaim and never answers it, and its limit is generic (and, per finding 2, wrong for Ibn al-Athir). The sample is
what the teacher marks against that evening and what feedback points students toward; scored as written, a claim that
only names a counterclaim gets the point. Fix: rewrite the sample (Settled 2).
Confidence: likely (the row and its look-fors).

- LAYER day. CATCH cheap read. REPEAT yes (look-fors and samples that let a wrong answer pass, day-078 finding 2).

### 4. Source D's cut drops Polo's own line on exchange, so the day's trade inference rests on text students do not have

The teacher page says "Source D supports an inference about currency helping exchange under coercion", and W44 asks
for "a Silk Roads consequence". The sheet's ellipsis removes the sentence that carries the exchange half: "And indeed
everybody takes them readily, for wheresoever a person may go throughout the Great Kaan's dominions he shall find these
pieces of paper current, and shall be able to transact all sales and purchases of goods by means of them just as well
as if they were coins of pure gold" (Yule 1903, book 2, ch. 24, checked). As cut, D shows only compulsion and a
merchant restriction, so the cheap read rightly found the sample's "helped merchants" unsupported. The better fix is
to restore the sentence (Settled 3): then D shows both sides, coercion and exchange, which is exactly what error D's
two columns need.
Confidence: firm (Gutenberg 10636, checked).

- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT yes (a cut that drops the day's needed line, as finding 1). CHEAP missed (cheap

### 7. The question asks how power was won; most sources show how it was held, and the day never says so

Sources A, D, F and G show power displayed, enforced or kept (a court, a currency, a creed, justice). Only E shows
power won. A student weighing "won mainly by" from these sources will either force display into "won" or default to
the sword because only E fits. The best answers make the distinction (the Mongols won by the sword and kept power by
money, tribute and Heaven's mandate; Mali's rulers kept it by trade taxes, Islam and strict justice), and the W44
look-for "Chinggis to Khubilai: conquest to Yuan government" already assumes it. Fix: one follow-up after the Ask:
"**Ask.** Does this detail show power won, or power kept? Expect: E shows winning; A, D, F and G mostly show keeping;
a strong claim can say the answer changes over time."
Confidence: judgment call.

- LAYER day. CATCH expert. REPEAT new. CHEAP missed. **ORIGIN day.**
