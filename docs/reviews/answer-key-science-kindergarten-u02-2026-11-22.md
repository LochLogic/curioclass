## Findings

1. **Key text is truncated in both keys (fix first).** Sentences are cut mid-word, so a teacher cannot read them. In key-assessment-weather.md: "A day square for 4 September 2024 with t: cloudy, rainy and windy are true for this day."; "Six rain squares in a row with date nume: six."; "Two rows of rain squares. September: six: September six, October two." In key-assessment-forecast.md: "Denver: ele: 2, 6, 8, 9, 12, 14, 15, 21, 22, 29 and 30 June 2023. The teacher says the total, eleven, since it is over ten.) Seattle: an empty row. (Teacher key: no days with thunder reported in June." (unbalanced parentheses). Fix: rewrite each line whole, for example "September 4: cloudy, rainy and windy are true; sunny is not." and "Denver June 2023: eleven squares (2, 6, 8, 9, 12, 14, 15, 21, 22, 29, 30). The teacher says the total, eleven, because it is over ten. Seattle: an empty row, no days with thunder reported." The answers themselves are right.

2. **W1a: the layout gives the answer away.** The card clips the three true squares to the day square and leaves sunny loose beside it, so a child can touch the clipped ones without reading any record, and the item cannot show that the child can describe the day. Fix: put all four sky squares loose in a row beside the day square, and put the record on the day square (cloud drawing, rain drops, wind lines), or have the child match each loose square to what the day square shows.

3. **W1a: sunny is arguably also true, so "sunny is wrong" is not safe.** The archive for 4 September 2024 shows few or scattered cloud all morning (09:53: few, few, scattered) and broken cloud by 15:53. The key's convention (greatest layer at 3 p.m.) is never told to the child, so a child who touches sunny as well has a fair reading. Fix: either mark touching sunny as "shown with help", not a miss, or name the time in the stem ("in the afternoon"). Rain (thunderstorm with rain at 16:32 onward) and 20 knots of wind (16:32) are confirmed.

4. **W2: the morning sky panels do not match the record for 16 and 17 September.** Cards show the morning "sunny" on all three days. The archive at 09:53 shows few, few, scattered (16th) and few, scattered, scattered (17th); only 18 September is clear (few, few). Under the key's own convention scattered is neither sunny nor cloudy. The answer is unaffected: temperatures 74/82, 79/74 and 67/83 are confirmed, so the afternoon is warmer on 16 and 18 and cooler on 17. Fix: draw the 16 and 17 mornings as part sun, part cloud, or drop the sky pictures from the W2 cards so only the warmth marks carry the item.

5. **F1: step 2 and the key disagree.** Item step: "the question is one the page can answer." Key and plan say a valid question the page cannot answer is kept on the not-on-this-page card and marked shown, never an error. Fix: change step 2 to "the question is about what may come, not what already happened". Also the key lists "how strong" as answerable, but source 19D says the page shows no strength or percentages. Cut "how strong" from the key's list.

6. **F1 and F2a: the first-page clock is unspecified.** The card says only "a clock". If it shows an afternoon time, F2a is given away; if a morning time, it misleads. Fix: say on the card that the F1 clock has no hands set (or a plain question mark), so the two F2a clocks are the only timed ones.

7. **F2a: a step asks for speech the stem never asks for.** Step 2, "says could come, not will come", has no prompt; the stem is "Touch when the storms could come." Day 19 already hears one reason per child, so say so in the key: record "could" when the child offers it, and treat "will come" as error D, but do not mark a silent correct touch down.

8. **W3b: steps read as all required, the key allows either.** The steps list laying squares side by side, pointing to leftovers and giving a reason; the key says the child may say it "in words, signs or a picture" instead. Fix: write steps 1 and 2 as "lays the squares side by side and points to the leftovers, or says it in words, signs or a picture".

9. **F3a: the wrong card shows a child standing outside with a hand at the ear.** It is a fair distractor, but the picture models standing outside near a storm. Consider drawing the child indoors at a window so no card shows that act.
