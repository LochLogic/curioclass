## Unit 1: The colonies and their neighbors

### F1. Serious: the research choice disappears in the assessment

**Location:** `s01.md`, field 5, Q2, Q2’s look-fors and sample, and E8. **LAYER: plan. Confidence: firm.**

Day 8 permits research on Penn, Williams or Winthrop. E8 instead supplies only Williams sources, and Q2 asks students to check “one Williams finding in your day 8 research record.” A student who followed the advertised Penn or Winthrop choice cannot supply the assumed record.

**Exact fix:** preserve the choice.

- Replace E8’s opening, “From two short Williams sources,” with “From two short sources about your chosen founder.”
- Replace Q2’s second sentence with: “State one sourced finding about the founder you researched on day 8, name its source, and explain which founding purpose it illustrates.”
- Keep the Massachusetts Bay and Rhode Island comparison from unseen card C.
- Change the look-fors and meeting sample to accept documented findings about any of the three offered founders.
- Add equivalent two-source research sets for Penn and Winthrop to field 10’s production list.

The standard permits researching one founder; it does not require everyone to choose Williams. [Massachusetts framework, printed page 72](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf).

## Unit 2: Slavery in the colonies

### F2. Moderate: the assessment’s decisive source text remains unspecified

**Location:** `s02.md`, field 5, “Withheld packet specification,” and field 10, “Exact assessment extracts remain to select.” **LAYER: plan. Confidence: firm.**

The questions and keys are substantial, but A and B remain selections to be chosen, while M’s numerous context panels remain descriptions of material to write. Consequently, the reader cannot establish that the selected evidence supports every keyed answer or that the complete read-aloud packet fits the 34-minute assessment.

This is more than unfinished page layout. Selecting the passages determines what students can infer.

**Exact fix:** put the actual short renderings of A and B and the complete wording of M’s context panels in field 5. Give each its source, passage locator and word count. Then check Q1 through Q8 against those exact texts and retain the 500-word ceiling. Map artwork and page layout may remain production tasks.

### F3. Moderate: the generated goal retains an inaccurate century-wide claim

**Location:** `days.csv`, day 18; repeated in `s02.md`, the frame and progression wording. **LAYER: plan and process. Confidence: firm.**

“Slavery legal in every colony through the 1700s” invites the reading that this remained true throughout the century. The plan’s teacher explanation correctly qualifies the claim, but the generated goal does not. Northern abolition measures began before 1800. [Library of Congress, “Moves toward Freedom in Northern States”](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/civil-rights-act/prologue.html?loclr=fbloc).

**Exact fix:** replace the day 18 goal with:

> In the late colonial period, slavery was legal in all thirteen colonies; ownership was widespread but uneven, including among many early leaders.

Make the corresponding qualification in the frame, progression and local MA.5.T1.5 paraphrase, then regenerate mechanical text.

### F4. Minor: the sample places a 1795 carpenter in a colony

**Location:** `s02.md`, field 5, Q5 meeting sample. **LAYER: plan. Confidence: firm.**

The packet correctly identifies Daniel’s payroll as an early republic example. The sample nevertheless says his skill “helped build the colony.” The cited record concerns construction of the President’s House in 1795. [Payroll history and record description](https://www.whitehousehistory.org/building-the-presidents-house-with-enslaved-labor).

**Exact fix:** replace “helped build the colony” with “helped build the President’s House in the new national capital.” Keep the explicit distinction between this later example and colonial history.

## Unit 4: Independence and a first government

### F5. Moderate: E51 adds unnecessary reading to a three-minute exit ticket

**Location:** `s04.md`, field 5, daily evidence directions and E51. **LAYER: plan. Confidence: judgment call.**

Every exit receives three minutes. E51 asks students to read eight profiles, choose an unfamiliar pair, and compare each person’s action and effect. The eight-person survey belongs in the lesson. Repeating it at the exit makes reading and selection consume the time intended to reveal comparison.

**Exact fix:** replace E51’s opening with:

The unit’s distinction between the Articles’ congressional adoption in 1777 and completed ratification in 1781 should remain.

## Unit 5: The Constitution

### F6. Serious: constitutional responsibilities were lost from the standard

**Location:** `s05.md`, field 2, MA.5.T3.1; field 5, Q1 and day 63 evidence; `data/standards/ma-hss-2018.txt`, MA.5.T3.1. **LAYER: plan and process. Confidence: firm.**

The official standard includes individual rights **and responsibilities**. The local paraphrase, teaching evidence and assessment list only rights. This is a missing requirement rather than a missing own-word line. [Massachusetts framework, printed page 75](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf).

**Exact fix:**

- Replace “individual rights” with “individual rights and responsibilities” in the local standard and field 2.
- Use Article VI’s oath, already included in packet P, as an explicit responsibility.
- Add to day 63’s evidence and Q1: “What responsibility does Article VI place on the officeholders it names?”
- Key: they must support the Constitution through the required oath or affirmation.
- Add that component to the scoring look-fors and meeting sample.

The supporting provision is available in the [Constitution transcript, Article VI](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript).

### F7. Serious: the final assessment checks too little of the Bill of Rights

**Location:** `s05.md`, field 5, Q6, Q7 and scoring. **LAYER: plan. Confidence: firm.**

Day 69 explicitly teaches a protection in each amendment. The final assessment checks one First Amendment freedom and the Fourth Amendment investigation. It provides no final evidence for the remaining protections, although the plan claims the whole standard.

The broader reading and explanation requirement and the investigation of one amendment are distinct parts of MA.5.T3.6. [Massachusetts framework, printed page 75](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf).

**Exact fix:** retain Q7’s investigation and replace Q6’s single-freedom task with a short Bill of Rights case chart. With the text available, students explain a relevant protection, retained right or reserved power for each amendment. Include the Ninth and Tenth accurately, rather than calling every amendment a new individual freedom. Supply the cases, answers, errors and scored samples in field 5. The [Bill of Rights transcript](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/bill-of-rights-transcript) provides the text.

## Unit 6: The new republic grows

### F9. Moderate: the error register treats a correct geographic statement as wrong

**Location:** `s06.md`, field 6, error C. **LAYER: plan. Confidence: firm.**

“Latitude runs east and west” is presented as an error. Latitude lines do run east and west; latitude measures position north or south of the equator. The current wording could cause a teacher to correct accurate reasoning. [National Ocean Service explanation](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/latitude.html).

**Exact fix:** replace that clause with:

> Latitude measures how far east or west a place lies.

Add the correction:

> Latitude measures position north or south of the equator, although its lines run east and west. Longitude measures position east or west of the prime meridian.

Keep the separate route and territorial-boundary misconceptions.

The plan’s correction of the cotton gin’s California attribution is sound. The remaining upstream copies should be synchronized, as listed below.

## Unit 7: Removal, slavery’s spread, and the fight against it

### F10. Serious: a regional contrast substitutes for state-by-state tracing

**Location:** `s07.md`, field 2, MA.5.T5.1; field 5, Q3 and map specification; day 105; corresponding progression and local standard. **LAYER: plan and process. Confidence: firm.**

The official standard requires state-by-state tracing of Northern abolition. The local paraphrase drops that requirement, and Q3 compares New York with Mississippi. Unit 2’s Pennsylvania and Massachusetts examples do not complete the missing sequence. [Massachusetts framework, printed page 76](https://www.doe.mass.edu/frameworks/hss/2018-12.pdf).

**Exact fix:**

- Restore “state by state” to the local standard, progression and field 2.
- On day 105, use a dated Northern abolition map and timeline, retrieving Pennsylvania and Massachusetts and adding the other relevant jurisdictions.
- Distinguish an initial gradual measure from the final end of bondage.
- Replace Q3’s opening with: “Use the dated state captions to trace Northern abolition in sequence. Identify where gradual measures left people unfree, then compare that pattern with slavery’s western expansion.”
- Retain the internal-trade record, 1808 distinction and family-separation question.
- Add the map’s actual jurisdiction captions and the corresponding answer sequence before section writing.

Use supplied dates rather than requiring a memorized list. The [National Park Service’s “Northern Unfreedom”](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/chapter-1-race-slavery-and-freedom-northern-unfreedom.htm) explains why initial abolition measures must not be confused with immediate freedom.
