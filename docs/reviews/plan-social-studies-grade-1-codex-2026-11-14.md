## Course verdict

**Revise before lesson writing.** The course has a coherent sequence, thoughtful privacy protections, labelled inventions, and substantial attention to evidence and present-day Native nations. Its largest weakness is the collection of individual assessment evidence: several plans require extensive explanations from early readers without a credible classroom recording schedule. Preserve the content and handoffs while reducing simultaneous assessment demands and making the paper and oral evidence paths explicit.

For every finding below, **REPEAT is unassessed** because earlier review reports were deliberately not consulted. Workload conclusions are planning judgments, not measured classroom results.

## Unit 1: Our groups

### 1. Serious: Daily evidence lacks the required classroom collection method

**Location:** `s01.md`, field 5, daily exit tickets. The same problem recurs in units 2 through 7.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** likely.

Several exits require multiple generated responses. Day 5 asks for a rule, a duty, their reasons, a needed fact and a concept. The plan does not specify a brief response every child can record or a rotation for hearing explanations. A teacher could follow the table and still obtain evidence from only the children who speak publicly.

**Exact fix:** Give each exit two explicitly separated parts: a paper response for every child and an oral prompt for a named rotating group during supported work. For day 5, use:

- **Paper:** “Mark the rule that helps everyone join the puppet activity.”
- **Oral:** “Explain its reason. Suggest a helpful duty, then identify one fact and one important word our supporting question needs.”

Record the component evidence separately. Apply this method to the other teaching-day exits in units 1 through 7, retaining their existing content and codes. Unit 8 provides the clearest existing model.

### 2. Moderate: English learner support has no usable sentence frame

**Location:** `s01.md`, fields 2 and 8.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm against adoption-bar C11.

Pictures, actions and familiar-language responses are useful, but the first unit supplies no frame for the explanations it requires. Its language demands include membership, contribution and purpose.

**Exact fix:** Add to field 8:

> Rehearse “A member ___. This helps the group ___.” For rules, use “The rule is ___. It helps because ___.” Model with a teaching example, then accept the child’s own explanation in speech, pointing with explanation, or a familiar language.

## Unit 2: Clues from the past

### 3. Moderate: Source reasoning has language support but no sentence frame

**Location:** `s02.md`, fields 2 and 8.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm against adoption-bar C11.

The plan appropriately separates English grammar from time understanding, but it does not scaffold the different language needed to explain why a source helps answer a question.

**Exact fix:** Add:

> For source choice, rehearse “This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___.” For time, rehearse “Yesterday we ___. Today we ___. Tomorrow we will ___.” Use different teaching examples from the assessment records.

Finding 1 also applies to days 11 through 17. Calendar placement and source-kind responses can provide the paper component; explanations need the listening rotation.

The school photographs are traceable catalogue items, including the uncertain date of about 1899. Their use as evidence about another school, rather than proof of the student’s own school history, is appropriately bounded. [Dog classroom photograph](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2001703662/), [posed classroom photograph](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/96525653/).

## Unit 3: Leaders

### 4. Serious: Day 30 combines too much individual explanation and observation

**Location:** `s03.md`, field 5, assessment allocation and items 1 through 4.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** likely.

The assessment requires analysis of three cases, several leadership responsibilities, a comparative argument, an ordered historical explanation, two historical questions and a partner exchange. Ten minutes for fifteen pairs gives the teacher only 40 seconds per pair, before allowing for recording, missed exchanges or communication support. Circulating during the individual work period does not resolve the competing need to support dictation.

**Exact fix:** Distribute the assessment components within the existing teaching days:

1. Collect item 1 after teaching on day 26, using the reserved everyday and fictional cases.
2. Collect ordered-explanation evidence after teaching on day 27, using a separate fresh case.
3. Observe eight pairs on day 28 and seven on day 29 for the explanation-question exchange.
4. Keep the unseen Addams source, historical questions and one claim task on day 30.

Update field 5 to identify which observations satisfy each part. Reserve day 31’s opening for exceptional missing evidence, within its existing five minutes.

### 5. Moderate: The Addams key claims a result the supplied passage does not establish

**Location:** `s03.md`, field 5, item 3.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm.

The student passage says Addams worked to make streets cleaner and play places safer. The sample answer concludes that people had safer places. That moves from an effort to an achieved result without supplying the evidence, weakening the source-limit distinction taught elsewhere.

**Old:** “People had help and safer places.”

**New:** “The group offered help, and Addams worked for cleaner streets and safer play places.”

Alternatively, add a verified result to the student passage before assessing it. The named [National Park Service account](https://www.nps.gov/people/jane-addams.htm) is traceable.

Finding 1 also applies to this unit’s daily exits.

## Unit 4: Choosing together

### 6. Serious: Four assessment rows conceal a much larger response load

**Location:** `s04.md`, field 5, items 1 through 4 and the day 40 allocation.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** likely.

The rows collectively require a tally result, two kinds of vote, two disagreement examples, several fairness explanations, two elected offices and duties, government functions, goods, services, taxation, citizenship, eligibility, terms and a reasoned re-election judgment. Fifteen minutes of individual work and ten minutes of circulating explanations do not credibly accommodate this, particularly for children needing dictation.

**Exact fix:** Assign summative evidence explicitly:

- Day 35: elected offices and duties.
- Day 36: government functions, goods, services and collective payment.
- Day 37: eligibility and fixed terms.
- Day 38: independent re-election reasoning.
- Day 40: the unseen tally and rule card, election distinction, fairness and one brief new judgment.

Use fresh assessment cards after teaching. State that recorded earlier evidence satisfies the corresponding parts, rather than requiring every part again on day 40.

Finding 1 also applies to the daily exits.

## Unit 5: Maps and places

### 7. Serious: Day 54 repeats evidence that could already be collected

**Location:** `s05.md`, field 5, assessment allocation and items 1 through 6.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** likely.

The plan gathers some earlier model evidence, but still assigns six substantial tasks to day 54: representation, multiple geographic models, five feature definitions, directions, city locations, capitals, historical explanation, a local-map explanation and a written claim. The teacher must also collect dictated responses and unheard explanations.

**Exact fix:** Make the earlier evidence assignments explicit:

- Day 45: representation and key use.
- Days 46, 50 and 51: geographic representations and feature explanations.
- Day 48: capital explanation.
- Day 52: local-map construction and explanation.
- Day 54: the fresh Reed map, direction transfer and one integrated explanation of growth.

Retain the unseen source task while removing redundant reassessment. Add a part-by-part collection schedule to field 5.

### 8. Moderate: The final direction task omits south

**Location:** `s05.md`, field 5, item 3.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm.

Item 3 elicits west, east and north. Drawing four direction labels in item 5 does not independently establish using south to describe a relationship. South appears in a daily exit, but the end-of-unit task does not directly check it.

**Exact fix:** After “What is north of Reed?” add:

> “Which direction is Reed from the fields?”

Add **“south”** to the key. The supplied arrangement already supports this answer.

Finding 1 also applies to the daily exits.

## Unit 6: One country, many people

### 9. Moderate: The observation rotation does not identify how every meaning is verified

**Location:** `s06.md`, field 5, opening allocation and items 3 through 5.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** likely.

The plan requires meanings and evidence for four symbols, two texts and two songs. Listening to six children on each of five days provides approximately one listening turn per child, while some children may need explanation checks across several components. The paper-response format is not defined well enough to show how it distinguishes recognizing a symbol from explaining its meaning.

**Exact fix:** Specify a paper evidence format for each component, such as linking a symbol to a meaning picture and marking the supporting detail. State that ambiguous marks require an explanation. Add a child-by-component record, allocate the existing listening turns by missing component, and use days 66 and 67 for unresolved evidence. Do not mark an explanation secure from a name or tune match alone.

Finding 1 also applies to the nonassessment daily exits.

Preserve optional patriotic participation. The distinction between studying the Pledge and compelled participation is supported by [Barnette](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep319/usrep319624/usrep319624.pdf).

## Unit 7: Helping our community

### 10. Moderate: Citizenship background needs a current, qualified source line

**Location:** `s07.md`, fields 2 and 10.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm.

The mechanical paraphrase states birth citizenship without qualification, while field 10 says a blocked brochure leaves the anchor paraphrase controlling the draft. The limits already correctly recognize citizenship at birth abroad. A legal explanation should have a checked current source, including the 2026 ruling, rather than rely on an older curricular paraphrase.

**Exact fix:** Add this teacher-background line:

> As of November 14, 2026, most children born in the United States are citizens at birth, including children whose parents are temporarily or unlawfully present. Some children born abroad are also citizens at birth. Others become citizens later. Narrow exceptions, including certain children of foreign diplomats, are teacher background and are not assessed here.

Link [Trump v. Barbara, decided June 30, 2026](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-365_4hdj.pdf) and the [federal citizenship explanation](https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/guides/chapter3.pdf). Replace “anchor paraphrase controls this draft” with the dated verification. Preserve all privacy protections.

### 11. Minor: The sample answer adds a detail absent from the student source

**Location:** `s07.md`, field 5, item 2.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm.

The sample says Titcomb planned routes, but the supplied student passage says she planned a book wagon. Route planning is supported by the adult source, but children cannot cite information they were not given.

**Old:** “Titcomb planned routes so families could borrow books near home.”

**New:** “Titcomb planned a book wagon so families could borrow books near home.”

The historical account is traceable and supports the broader episode. [Kathryn Miller’s 2024 account](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/bookmobiles-big-data-how-library-scientists-make-information-accessible-all).

Finding 1 also applies here. For the assessment, explicitly assign citizenship evidence to day 69 and the history, fiction, news and experience evaluations to days 75 and 76. This leaves day 79’s fresh sources and transfer tasks a more credible share of the lesson.

## Unit 8: Earning, buying and saving

### 12. Minor: The oral-evidence frequency is overstated

**Location:** `s08.md`, field 5, daily exit introduction.  
**LAYER:** plan. **REPEAT:** unassessed. **Confidence:** firm.

Six children per course lesson requires five lessons to hear thirty children. This course generally meets three times weekly, so that is not once weekly per child.

**Old:** “about once weekly per child.”

**New:** “once every five course lessons, usually about every one and a half to two school weeks.”

Keep the separate paper and spoken records. This unit’s explicit response method and distributed assessment are useful models for the earlier units. Its savings-account explanation is supported by the named [federal guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/money-as-you-grow/school-age-children-preteens/explore-saving/).
