## Verdict

Day 13 is correct mathematics in a sound shape: the rule is written under a line the student has just cut, each
number is tied to a count, and the two errors the day exists for (A, the top kept; F, "twice as much") each get a
named fix. The biggest risk is that both worked examples cut by the same number as their numerator (2/5 cut into 2,
3/2 cut into 3), so "multiply both by the top number" fits both models and the closing question ("which number is
the cut?" on (3 × 3)/(3 × 2)) cannot be answered by looking. The one change that matters most is finding 1: make
Example 2 cut each half into 5 (3/2 = 15/10) and ask the closing question on it.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. Both worked examples cut by their own numerator, so the rule they model is ambiguous

- **What is wrong.** Example 1 cuts 2/5 into 2: (2 × 2)/(2 × 5). Example 2 cuts 3/2 into 3: (3 × 3)/(3 × 2). In both,
  the multiplier equals the top number. A student who infers "multiply both numbers by the numerator" gets both
  examples right, and the two models never show the cut as a number of its own. The day's goal is exactly that
  distinction ("point to the cut each number counts", opener), and the pull-together **Ask** ("In 3/2 = (3 × 3)/(3 ×
  2), which number tells the cut? Which numbers count parts?") is set on the one equation where three of the four
  numbers are 3: the expected answer "the 3 in front" cannot be told from "the 3 on top" by sight. The practice
  breaks the pattern (1/2 cut into 3 and 5; 5/3 cut into 2), but only after the models have been copied into the
  frames.
- **Evidence.** items.csv, s03-K13-1 and s03-K13-2. Illustrative Mathematics grade 4, unit 2, lesson 10 (read today,
  https://im.kendallhunt.com/k5/teachers/grade-4/unit-2/lesson-10/lesson.html): its worked method varies the
  multiplier (2, 3, 4, 5, 10) on a base whose numerator is 1, so the multiplier never coincides with the top number.
  Variation that keeps the feature to be learned apart from the features that do not matter is standard worked-example
  advice (from memory; not reopened today).
- **What it costs.** The day's understanding goal is to name the cut; a student can finish the examples and the
  closing talk without ever separating cut from count. Day 16's written task (Mo: "what does each 2 mean?") meets the
  same ambiguity.
- **Change.** Example 2 becomes 3/2 cut into 5: the line from 0 to 2 in halves, point at 3/2, "Cut each half into 5
  equal parts", key 3/2 = (5 × 3)/(5 × 2) = 15/10. New id (rule 8), for example s03-K13-2v2, steps rewritten with 5 ×
  2 = 10 and 5 × 3 = 15; wrongs: A 3/10, B 8/7, K 15/20. Not 12/8: that is day 11's Example 2 (s03-K11-2). 15/10 is
  on no exit or check item (only practice P16-9, which it helps). Student frame "Each half was cut into ____. 3/2 =
  (____ × 3)/(____ × 2) = ____/____" stands. Pull-together **Ask** becomes "In 3/2 = (5 × 3)/(5 × 2), which number
  tells the cut? Which numbers count parts?" Expect: "the 5 is the cut; 10 is the parts in one whole, 15 the parts
  up to the point." Example 1 stays (2/5 = 4/10 is fixed by the plan and the register), but its teacher line adds:
  "The cut is the number in front, not the top number: here both happen to be 2."
- **Confidence.** Likely (one source read today, strong reasoning).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 2. The display never shows the cut, and the figure language cannot

- **What is wrong.** The examples' steps say "Cut each fifth into 2 equal parts. Draw the new marks" and "Write the
  new name below the point: 4/10", but the display keeps the uncut fifths line on every screen (screens 4 to 11; the
  same for Example 2, screens 13 to 20). There is no 4/10, no tenths ticks. The teacher page says "You cut the board
  line; the display shows each step", so the cut, the whole point of the day, lives only on the board. The figure
  kinds cannot do better: a `line` with a second group (`/ d=5 sub=2 pt=4/10`) grows on the display, but the student
  page prints the whole figure, so the cut and the answer would be printed for the student. The same gap affects every
  "cut it yourself" example in unit 3 (days 11, 13, 14).
- **Evidence.** build/lessons/math-4/u03-2/d013-display.html, screens 4 to 20; lesson.py `step_fig` (a multi-line
  figure grows on the display; the student page draws it whole). DEFAULTS.md: "the display page carries every
  figure and step".
- **What it costs.** In a room where the board is far or small, students copy cuts they cannot see; the display, the
  only clean picture in the room, shows the before and never the after.
- **Change.** Process: let a line group be marked display-only, for example a leading `then` (`line d=5 pt=2/5 /
  then d=5 sub=2 pt=4/10`): drawn on the display from the step that names drawing, and on the key, never on the
  student page or the ticket. Then K13-1 takes `line d=5 pt=2/5 / then d=5 sub=2 pt=4/10` and K13-2 (as in finding
  1) `line to=2 d=2 pt=3/2 / then d=2 sub=5 pt=15/10`. Until it is built, the teacher page keeps the board line, which
  it already has.
- **Confidence.** Firm (seen on the built page and in the code).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (display dropped the picture its step points at, day 80
  finding 7 and day 82 finding 5). **CHEAP:** missed.
