## Verdict

Day 75 is correct mathematics in a sensible fluency-day shape: one worked difference with an estimate and an
adding check, two sets with the place names withdrawn between them, the register's B and H fixed on purpose, and a
two-sided hand-off with days 74 and 76. The biggest risk on paper is one the cheap read already found: the display
has no answer screen for either set, so the two "check your own" moments the day promises do not exist. The
biggest risk it missed is in the teaching itself: the worked example alternates ungrouping and subtracting column
by column, which is the habit the Progressions and the day's own error register name as where H and F come from,
while the H remedy ("ungroup everything first") is never shown until day 76 uses it as a fix; finding 1 rewrites the
example's steps so the day teaches the method it later asks for.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The worked example teaches the alternating habit that the day's own remedy for H undoes

- **What is wrong.** K75-1's steps go column by column: "Ones: can I take 6 from 3? No. Ungroup 1 ten ... 13 − 6
  = 7. Tens: can I take 5 from 0? No. Ungroup ..." Ungrouping and subtracting alternate in every column. That is
  the only method the day models. Yet the register's remedy for H is "Ungroup everything first, then subtract;
  check by adding", the opener says renaming before any column "keeps the student from switching between
  ungrouping and subtracting, which is where H and I start", F's remedy is also "ungroup everything first", and day
  76's response to E75 (H or F) is "H's wrong example again, ungrouping everything first". So the day that
  introduces H teaches the switching that causes it, and students first meet "ungroup first" as a correction.
- **Evidence.** Progressions for the CCSS, Number and Operations in Base Ten, compiled edition (CC BY), PDF page
  66 (printed 60): all necessary decomposing done first, then the subtractions, "highlights the two major steps"
  and helps inhibit taking the smaller top digit from the larger; when students alternate, they may forget to
  decompose after they have just subtracted. Read today. errors.csv rows F and H; opener.md "Why this model";
  d076.md "E75 back". The opener's own line that both orders are the standard algorithm is right; the problem is
  which one the one worked example on the day shows.
- **Cost.** The class watches five minutes of alternating, then practises it on six differences. The students who
  make H on E75 are then told to do something they were never shown. On a fluency day the modelled habit is the
  one that sticks.
- **Change.** Rewrite K75-1's `steps` cell to ungroup first, then subtract (text in "Settled by the expert"). Same
  numbers, same id (rule 8 is about numbers; the day's version goes up). Teacher page, Teaching first, replace
  "Say the point once: ..." with: "Say the point once: find every ungrouping first, write the new top number, then
  subtract. The estimate catches a big mistake, and adding back catches a small one." Student page strapline:
  "Estimate. Line up the ones. Ungroup where you must, right to left. Then subtract. Add back to check." The
  example frame's first blank row becomes "New top" (finding 5). Nia's fix then reads as a lapse in a method the
  class has seen. A student who already alternates correctly is not marked wrong: say so in one clause ("either
  order is right if the add-back works").
- **Confidence.** Firm (the Progressions page, read today, and the unit's own register and opener agree).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 2. A right estimate can be "fixed" into a wrong one at the self-check

- **What is wrong.** Students check their own sets against the answers and "fix in pen". The P75 answers give one
  estimate each, and they mix roundings: P75-1 to 3 to the nearest thousand, P75-4 to 6 to the nearest ten
  thousand (245,817 − 129,563 "about 250,000 − 130,000 = 120,000"). A student who rounds P75-4 to thousands
  (246,000 − 130,000 = 116,000) has a better estimate than the key's and will cross it out. Day 73's rows say "(any
  close estimate)"; day 75's do not, and neither does the teacher page.
- **Evidence.** items.csv s09-P75-1 to 6 against s09-P73-2, 4 and 7; d075.md Set 1 and Set 2.
- **Cost.** Students learn there is one right estimate, which is the opposite of what 4.NBT.A.3 estimating is for,
  and the "estimate first" habit the day builds looks like a second sum to get right.
- **Change.** Each P75 answer adds "(any close estimate)" after its estimate, as day 73 does, so the screen (once
  the cheap read's display fix lands) prints it. Set 1, after "Each: estimate, subtract, add back.": "Any close
  estimate is right; it only has to catch a big mistake."
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.
