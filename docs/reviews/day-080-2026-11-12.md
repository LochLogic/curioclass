## Verdict

Day 80 is right mathematics and the right first day: copies built with cubes, "times as many" set beside "more
than" on the same 3-strip, the two register errors (A and D) fixed on purpose, and a planned question that asks
what each number counts. The biggest risk is the paper, not the teaching: the strip sheet the day sends every
drawing to has too few frames, a different drawing rule and rows too short for item 3, the exit slip asks for
two strips with no room to draw them, and items 4 to 6 print as 1 to 3. The one change that matters most is to
make the day and its strip sheet agree (finding 1); findings 2, 3, 4 and 7 are the same template and build gaps
the unit 11.1 reads found, so they are process fixes.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The strip sheet does not fit the day that uses it most

- **What is wrong.** Three clashes on the day's 25-minute core:
  - *Too few frames.* The sheet has six frames (three a side). Day 80 sends seven drawings to it (items 1 to 6
    and the challenge), and the warm-up has students write their counts by threes and fours "on the back of the
    strip sheet", which spends a side of frames before the work starts.
  - *Two drawing rules.* The teacher page says items 1 to 3 are drawn "a square for each cube"; the sheet's own
    instructions say "Use one box for each copy of the smaller strip".
  - *Rows too short.* A frame row is ten boxes. Item 3, "4 times as many as 4", needs 16 squares in the copies
    row under the square-per-cube rule. It cannot be drawn on the sheet.
  The square case 4 × 4 is also the one comparison where the planned question ("Where is the 4? Where is the
  3?") and error C (swapped roles) cannot be seen, since both numbers are 4.
- **Evidence.** strip-sheet-student.pdf, both sides; d080.md warm-up and "Build, draw, say"; `s10-P80-3`.
- **Cost.** Students run out of frames by item 4 and squeeze the rest into margins; on item 3 they either stop
  or switch to boxes with no one having told them to, in the middle of the day whose point is a clean picture.
- **Change.** Day: the warm-up counts go on the student page (side 2 has a third of a page empty): add a line at
  the top of side 2, "Warm-up. Count by 3s to 30. Count by 4s to 40." with two full-width lines, and the teacher
  page says "on side 2" instead of "on the back of the strip sheet". The rule switches at item 3, while the cubes
  are still out: items 1 and 2 drawn a square for each cube; from item 3 on, each copy a box with its number
  inside (this is the bridge from cubes to boxes, done with the cubes still in hand). Item 3 becomes a non-square
  that needs the box rule (see the table). The challenge gets a `{sketch}` box on side 2, so the sheet's six
  frames hold items 1 to 6 exactly. Process: a blank sheet's `leans_on` days are checked against it in the
  cheap read: frames needed against frames printed, and the largest drawing against a row.
- **Confidence.** Firm (seen on both pages).
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 91 finding 2, day and sheet disagree).

- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** cheap read. **REPEAT:** yes (day 89 finding 1, day 91 finding 5, day 92
  findings 1 and 6).

- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** script. **REPEAT:** yes (day 90 finding 10, day 91 finding 9, day 89

### 5. Lin's wrong answer describes the right picture

- **What is wrong.** `s10-F80-2` reads: "She drew a 3-strip, then 4 more 3-strips. She got 15." That is also an
  exact description of the right strip picture from Example 1: Pia's 3-strip on top, 4 copies under it, five
  3-strips on the page. Lin's error is that the first strip is in the same row (or that she counted both rows);
  the stem says neither. The teacher page says "in one row", and the display's figure shows one row, but the
  student page prints the stem with no figure.
- **Evidence.** d080-student.pdf side 1; the display's "Lin's row" figure; Example 1 step 6.
- **Cost.** A careful student who did Example 1 right sees nothing wrong with Lin's drawing, only with her sum,
  and learns the wrong lesson ("don't count the top one") rather than "the first strip is copy one".
- **Change.** Stem: "Lin drew 4 times as many as 3. In one row, she drew a 3-strip, then 4 more 3-strips. She
  got 15." (Same numbers, so the id stays; the day's version goes up.) Also consider a different name: IM grade
  4, unit 5, lesson 2 has a "Lin" building times-as-many with cubes; the day is credited "informed by", so a
  name of our own keeps rule 4 clean. A judgment call, not a defect.
- **Confidence.** Firm on the ambiguity; judgment call on the name.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new.

## What is right and should not be touched

- The order: examples built, six comparisons built then drawn, the two register errors fixed beside right
  working, the planned question at the two builds, the exit. A and D are the two days.csv names for day 80.
- "4 times as many" and "4 more" built from the same 3-strip and left side by side. This is the contrast the
  Progressions (OA, PDF page 44 (printed 38)) and IM unit 5 lessons 1 and 2 both centre, and it is the whole day.
- "In the bottom row, the first strip is copy one." This is the teaching move against D and the opener's "the 7 is
  the first copy" said in a child's words.
- Saying once that "times" also means occasions, and allowing a home language first: the unit is mostly
  language.
- The cubes going away after the day, and the switch from squares to boxes inside the day (finding 1 only moves
  where it happens).
- The readiness branch ("nothing moves today") and the pairing of students who missed item 1.
- "Tomorrow"'s error hand-off: day 81's first five minutes carry it exactly.
