## Ladder

What the grade gap caused. Grade 2 taught the shape of what the five days ask, and the days name the review correctly in most places: opinion with because (day 98 retrieval), topic, two facts and an ending (day 16), -tion and -sion (day 42, grade 2 day 65), irregular plurals children, teeth, mice, geese (grade 2 day 54), narrator and point of view (day 138, from day 133 and day 84), and the four-box plan (day 138 from day 98). The gaps:

- Day 75 tests men and women as irregular plurals. Grade 3 teaches foot and leaf on day 57 and child, woman, mouse only on day 136. The plan block had used mice and teeth, which grade 2 did teach. The change in the split moved the item off a taught case onto an untaught one.
- Day 98 teaches since and therefore as new on an Application day, with the four-box chart as new and formal discussion roles restated. Grade 2's link words were because and so. The exit tests only because.
- Day 42's plan card is first used on that day (ledger: new on day 42). Grade 2 day 106 had a three-box spoken plan card; day 42 does not name it as review.
- Day 15 (incoming to day 16) says title and key are both a review and new today.
- Day 75's exit reuses a grade 3 case (foot and feet, days 57 and 59), not a lower grade's case. Day 42 reuses grade 2's -tion/-sion words as practice (direction and collection were the grade 2 day 65 dictation words), which is acceptable review.

Did the grade use the lower grades well. Mostly yes. Review lines name the earlier day and use the same words ("possessive with one owner", "link word", "narrator", "closing line"). Day 98's "closing line" and day 138's "close" are two names for the same part (the four look-fors on day 138 say "close", the four boxes on day 98 say "closing line"; the unit plan says "closing sentence"). Day 16 calls it "ending" (the grade 2 word) and "closing" (the grade 3 word) in the same block. Day 75 misses the ladder only on men and women and on the foot and feet exit.
