## Verdict

Day 23 is correct mathematics on a well-chosen set (two of the six number cards reward error D with the right
sign, the rest catch it, and the exit's 2/5 and 3/8 are both under 1/2, so the benchmark cannot settle it). The
biggest risk is the trap teaching: the rule the teacher is told to give ("a trap is only a trap when the story
names two different things") is wrong, and it fires on the day's own ribbon story and on the exit's two walkers,
producing exactly the "cannot tell" over-reach the day's Tomorrow warns about. The one change that matters most
is finding 1: replace that rule with "cannot tell only when the two fractions are of wholes that may not be the
same size", and say it the same way in the keys, the warning and the pull-together.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. The trap rule the day teaches is wrong, and the day's own story and exit break it

- **What is wrong.** The cards part says "Warn pairs that a trap is only a trap when the story names two different
  things", and the pull-together expects "only when the wholes are different things". The ribbon story names two
  different things (Ivy's ribbon, Max's ribbon), yet both fractions are of one meter, so they compare. The exit
  names two people on one trail. A student who learns the stated rule writes "cannot tell" on the ribbons and on
  the exit. Two keys add a second slip: P23-4 and P23-8 say "1/2 > 1/3 is true only of the same whole", which
  makes the number comparison conditional; P23-11's key says it rightly ("4/6 > 3/5 is true, but the
  sandwiches are different wholes"). And "Read both aloud once. The ribbons are past 1; the sandwiches are
  different wholes." reads as words to say to the class, which hands over the story's point before anyone works.
- **Evidence.** d023.md, cards part and pull-together; items.csv P23-4, P23-8, P23-10, P23-11, X23. The error
  register's H: "the fraction is taken as an amount of stuff, not a number" (Progressions, Number and Operations,
  Fractions, PDF page 148 (142), as cited in errors.csv; not reopened today). The standard: comparisons are valid
  only when the two fractions refer to the same whole, and a measure in meters shares its whole, the meter (from
  memory). The opener's own warning: "watch for 'cannot tell' on a story where the whole is the same".
- **Cost.** The day plants the over-reach it then has to undo; E23's "cannot tell" answers become partly the
  day's fault, and day 24 spends a group on them.
- **Change.** See "Settled by the expert": new warning, new pull-together answer, two key cells reworded, and the
  stories line turned into a teacher note.
- **Confidence.** Firm (the day's own items show it).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** yes (a taught rule that over-applies: day 84 finding 1, the
  sort's decision rule in the unit 10 reads). **CHEAP:** missed (the cheap read found that the traps are given
  away, a different defect).

### 2. The card key cannot be cut out as the day says (the writers' change, judged)

- **What is wrong.** "Stack the pairs' keys and cut out the eight card rows." The built key is four pages. The
  card rows run P23-2 to P23-6 on page 2 and P23-7 to P23-9 on page 3, so each card key is two strips from two
  pages. Page 3 also holds the stories and the exit's answer, and page 2 the written task's samples, so "the rest
  stays hidden" means the teacher destroys or keeps 13 sets of leftovers. Each strip carries the id, the standard
  code and a 25-word error definition ("error D, bigger numbers, bigger fraction (the same-numerator case too:
  ...)"), which is a lot for a grade 4 partner to read while saying "yes" or "try again". A class of 26 prints 14
  keys of 4 pages for one 25-minute part.
- **Evidence.** build/lessons/math-4/u03-4/d023-key.html printed to PDF: 4 pages, card rows split at the page 2 to
  3 break. d023.md "Before class" and the print line.
- **Cost.** About 10 minutes of cutting the night before, about 50 sheets, and a real risk that the exit's answer
  reaches a pair.
- **Change.** Keep the idea (a partner holding the key is the right paper stand-in for the tool's feedback); drop
  the cut-out. Process: the build makes a card key for a day whose part names one: one half page, "Card | Sign |
  One proof | Also right", generated from the rows' answer cells, two to a sheet. Until it is built: print the
  teacher key once and write the eight signs and one proof each on the board's covered side for pairs to check
  at the end of each card. The print line becomes "card key, 1 per pair (half page)".
- **Confidence.** Firm on the layout; likely on the cost.
- **LAYER:** process. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.

### 3. The partner routine leaves half of every page blank and checks only the sign

- **What is wrong.** Partner A proves card 1 "in the row" while B holds the key; they swap for card 2. So each
  student writes four of the eight cards, and the collected page has four empty rows the teacher cannot tell from
  "not done". B says "yes" or "try again" against a key that gives one way, though the day's whole point is that
  any complete way is right; a valid same-count proof of card 4 meets a key showing only 4/5 = 8/10 or 40/60. And
  D gives the right sign on card 1 (5/10 and 7/12) and card 6 (6/8 and 8/10), so a "yes" on the sign alone
  passes the very error the day is built against.
- **Evidence.** d023.md cards part; items.csv answers for P23-2, P23-3, P23-6 (one way only); hand check of D on
  each card.
- **Cost.** The day's goal ("prove my answer") is checked by a partner who is told to check something else.
- **Change.** Cards part, after "Swap for card 2, and so on": "On your partner's cards, write the sign only. B
  checks the sign, then the proof: are the new names equal to the old ones, and does the sign follow? Another
  complete way is right too. A right sign with no proof is 'try again'." Student page instruction: "Prove your
  cards. On your partner's cards, write the sign." The card key (finding 2) lists every way in the answer cell.
- **Confidence.** Firm.
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new. **CHEAP:** missed.
