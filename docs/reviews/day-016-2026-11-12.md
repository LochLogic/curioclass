## Verdict

Day 16 is a sound fluency day with correct keys, a well-chosen fix-it (Lena's 6/4 and 9/6 is exactly the case
where one multiplier fails) and a good mix of traps in the eight pairs. The biggest risk is mathematical: the day
teaches "the names with smaller numbers are not the same, so they are not equal", which is false in general (12/8
and 18/12 group to 6/4 and 9/6, two different names for one amount), and it contradicts the unit's day 10 idea that
one amount has many names; the keys come out right only because every not-equal pair happens to be grouped as far
as it goes. The one change that matters most is finding 1: "not equal" is shown by putting both names in the same
parts and comparing the counts (the example's own check step, "2 is 10/5, not 11/5"), and one pair is added where
grouping once leaves two different names that are still equal.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. "Different names, so not equal" is taught as a rule, and it is false

- **What is wrong.** The pull-together's expected answer to "How do you know two fractions are not equal?" is
  "their names with smaller numbers are not the same". The display and Words frame is "The names are __ and __, so
  they are not equal." The keys of P16-3 and P16-9 say "two different names", and the example's 24/12 step says
  "The names are not the same, so not equal." But a fraction has many names with smaller numbers: 12/8 grouped in
  twos is 6/4, 18/12 grouped in twos is 9/6, two different names, and both are 3/2. The rule only works when both
  are grouped as far as they go, which the section has chosen not to teach as "simplest form". On today's pairs a
  student who groups 20/12 in twos (10/6) and 15/10 in fives (3/2) gets "not equal" for the wrong reason, and the
  key marks it right. Students leave with a rule that will fail them on day 19 onward (comparing) and in grade 5.
- **Evidence.** d016.md "Pull it together" and "Words and supports"; s03-P16-3, s03-P16-9, s03-K16-1 step 3. The
  unit's own day 10 goal ("one amount can have more than one fraction name"). Progressions limit, no simplest form
  (via docs/courses/math-3-5-progression.md, not reopened). The example already holds the right move: "Check: 2
  is 10/5, not 11/5", which puts both in the same parts.
- **Cost.** The "not equal" half of the day's goal ("say how I know") is taught with a wrong reason, and the
  written record cannot tell a right reason from a lucky one.
- **Change.** Teach "not equal" as: put both names in the same size parts and compare the counts (or mark both on
  one line). Pull together, second Ask, expect first: "put them in the same parts: 3/6 and 4/6 are different
  counts"; then "only one number changed". Frame: "In the same parts they are __ and __, so they are not equal."
  Walk-and-ask: "Can you group both to smaller numbers? If the names differ, can you put them in the same parts?"
  Keys and K16-1 step 3 as in "Settled by the expert". Add one pair where grouping once leaves two different names
  that are equal (12/8 and 18/12: twos give 6/4 and 9/6, Lena's pair; group again, both 3/2), so the day meets the
  trap on purpose. Errors.csv E's "if many" ("give each fraction a name with smaller numbers, then compare the
  names") gets "in the same parts" added.
- **Confidence.** Firm (a counterexample settles it).
- **LAYER:** day. **CATCH:** expert. **REPEAT:** new.
