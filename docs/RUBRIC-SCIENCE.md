## How it works

Three gateways, in order, the same in every band. A course must meet or partly meet Gateway 1 to be read on
Gateway 2, and must meet both to be read on Gateway 3. In Gateway 2, two indicators are a door of their own:
scientific accuracy (2d) and keeping to the band's content (2e). A zero on either stops the course before Gateway
3, whatever the total. Within an indicator, full points need every one of its conditions.

Points and pass lines (the "meets" line):

| Gateway | K-5 | 6-8 | High school |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Designed for the standards: phenomena drive learning (1a to 1e, 16) and three-dimensional learning and assessment (1f to 1k, 18) | 34, meets at 29 | 34, meets at 29 | 34, meets at 29 |
| 2. Coherence and scope (2a to 2h) | 34, meets at 29 | 34, meets at 29 | 32, meets at 27 |
| 3. Teacher and student supports (3a to 3i, 14; 3j to 3q, 4; 3r to 3u in words) | 18, meets at 16 | 18, meets at 16 | 18, meets at 16 |

Partly meets is 17 or more in Gateways 1 and 2 (16 for high school Gateway 2) and 9 or more in Gateway 3.

## The unit read (EQuIP)

The unit-level tool, used to read a scope and sequence's unit plans and every sampled unit. Three categories,
each rated 0 to 3:

- **I. Three-dimensional design.** For a lesson: the phenomenon or problem drives the work; several elements of
  each dimension are built; students use them together to make sense of things. For a unit, also: the lessons
  fit together toward one set of goals; links to other science domains where they help; links to math and ELA
  at the grade's level.
- **II. Instructional supports.** Real and relevant to students; students say, test and revise their own ideas;
  it builds on what they learned before in all three dimensions; it is accurate; it guides the teacher on
  students who need more or less help. For a unit, also: the teacher is helped to hold the storyline together,
  and supports fade over the unit.
- **III. Monitoring progress.** Tasks give direct, observable evidence of three-dimensional work; checks are built
  into the teaching; scoring guidance is aligned; tasks are fair to every student. For a unit, also: a joined-up
  system of checks before, during and after, and more than one chance to show each goal.

Category I is the door: a unit rated 0 or 1 is not read further. The overall rating comes from the sum of the
three: 8 or 9 is a model of the design, 6 or 7 is one if improved, 3 to 5 needs revision, 0 to 2 is not ready.

## How it is scored and what passes

**The sample.** As the pass rule in ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md sets it: the whole scope and sequence and coverage
table, then three whole units picked by the reviewer, never the builder, one from each third of the year. A
course of four units or fewer is read whole. For S11 the reviewer also traces ten standards of their choice to
lesson set and check item.

**A course passes when all of these hold** (item-level choices made here, to be tested on the first finished
course, the same way the other subjects' pass rule is):

1. **Gateway 1 meets** (29 of 34) and **Gateway 2 meets** (29 of 34; high school 27 of 32), with 2d and 2e at
   full marks, not just above zero. Accuracy is a door in every Curio Class subject (C3 in the shared core).
2. **Gateway 3 meets** (16 of 18), with 3h (safety) at full marks.
3. **Every sampled unit is rated 8 or 9 on EQuIP,** no category below 2. A unit at 6 or 7 is fixed before the
   course is called finished.
4. **S5 and S10 pass in full.** They are doors: a course that fails either is not read further until fixed.
5. **S1 to S4 and S6 to S13:** no fail, and no more than two part passes. Pass, part pass and fail mean what
   they mean in the shared pass rule (part pass: found in one sampled unit only, and the fix is local).
6. The shared core of ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md (C1 to C16) is not read again for science: where it asks for
   something this page does not (family notes, the logic model, the access check on tools, English learner
   supports), the reviewer reads those core rows too and the part pass count above covers them together.

**A layer is read against the rows it owns.** A scope and sequence is read on Gateway 1 at plan level, 2a to 2c,
2e, 2h, S1, S6 (costs), S7 and S11. A unit plan is read on EQuIP and S1 to S4, S6, S9 and S13. A day is read on
S2, S3, S5, S6, S8, S9 and S10.

## How the bands differ

- **K-5 and 6-8** use the same indicators and points. Core ideas are scored by discipline (physical, life, earth
  and space, engineering) and the nature of science has its own indicator (2f).
- **High school** scores the core ideas, practices and crosscutting concepts the course claims, at 8 points each,
  and has no 2f (the nature of science is read inside 2b and 2c). Gateway 2 is 32 points.
- **Curio Class's courses** (data/courses.csv): one elementary course read by grade (science-k5), one middle
  school course (science-ms), and the high school courses. Biology (grade 9), Chemistry (grade 10) and Physics
  (grade 11) are core, and the high school Earth and space expectations are folded into those three (Cory,
  2026-11-13, Desk card q-hs-science-core, following NGSS Appendix K's modified domains model). Earth and Space
  and Environmental Science are electives. There is no core science course in grade 12.
- **Source by band:** 6-8 has a base text; K-5 and 9-12 are written from scratch with references, so they carry
  more risk on S1, S4 and S10 and get the longer read.

## How this differs from the math and ELA rubrics

- Phenomena and three-dimensional work are the first gateway. A course that teaches every core idea well by
  telling does not reach Gateway 2.
- Safety is scored (3h) and is a door here (S5). Math and ELA have nothing like it.
- Counting is by element, not by standard name, and some practices are owed in every grade.
- The daily evidence is the notebook and the class model, not a daily exit question; the exit ticket closes each
  section (approach review 2026-11-11, science 4 scope and sequence).
- Time is not scored, as in ELA, but pacing, a reason for the order and what to cut are read in words (3i).
