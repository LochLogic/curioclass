# Course frame: Physical Education, grades 3 to 5

Course `physical-education-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `pe-3-5`), grades 3, 4 and 5, one band course in three
school years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the pattern of the fixed K to 2 pilot
(docs/reviews/frame-physical-education-grades-k-to-2-fixes-2026-11-25.md); the scope and sequence sets the days
later, one year at a time. Each year's part of this file (its units and its two course-level checks) is that year's
frame, as the subject page's "one frame per grade year" asks.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 24: 8 in grade 3 (units 1 to 8), 8 in grade 4 (units 9 to 16), 8 in grade 5 (units 17 to 24). Each
  year is whole on its own: its own units, end-of-unit assessments and mid-year and year-end checks.
- **Days.** 240: 80 a year for three years. Each year: 74 days in units and 6 for course-level checks and respond
  days (two check days and one respond day at mid-year, the same at year-end); grade 3 74 + 6, grade 4 74 + 6,
  grade 5 74 + 6. One session of about 35 minutes, two or three sessions a week (2.5 on average), over the 32 weeks
  of the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md): the PE dose APPROACH.md "Pacing" sets for grades 3 to 5 (80
  sessions of 35 minutes, Cory 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-pe-1; elementary PE 2 to 3 sessions a week, Cory 2026-11-16).
  This frame keeps that default: 17 owned or begun rows a year at about four sessions a row fit 74 unit days. The 35
  minutes hold the instant activity, warm-up, focus, cool-down and closure, and the set-up, clean-up and collecting
  of equipment (about 3 minutes, done by students in their station groups; about 4 on mat and net days). Each unit's
  days include its end-of-unit assessment. No unit is over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. The course
  says openly that 2 to 3 sessions a week is below the 150 minutes a week SHAPE America's guidelines set for
  elementary school (shapeamerica.org/standards/guidelines); the course page, teacher pages and family notes offer
  short active breaks and home activity. On the even spread the calendar uses, course day d of a year falls on about
  school day 2d of that year, so a unit's weeks below are course day times two, divided by five.
- **Standard.** SHAPE America National Physical Education Standards, 2024 (data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt): all
  57 span-5 learning indicators (SHAPE.S1.5.1 to S1.5.22, S2.5.1 to S2.5.18, S3.5.1 to S3.5.7, S4.5.1 to S4.5.10)
  are owned in this band, each by the one progression row that owns it (data/progressions/physical-education.csv,
  grades 3, 4 and 5, 48 rows). A row's `~` codes are named in What students learn: "begun toward" where the owner is
  later in the band, "used in new settings" where it is earlier. Ids and our own words only; no indicator sentence
  is quoted (STANDARD.md 2). Check set: CDC's PECAT 2019 grade 3, 4 and 5 tables, keyed to the 2013 five standards
  and mapped through docs/standards-notes/pe-2014-to-2024.md, read for placement only (outcome stated, content
  teaches it, assessment checks it). The tables' grade 4 starts are kept: passing with the hands, dribbling in
  combination, overhead volleying and object handling joined to traveling are not expected before grade 4 (units
  10 and 12). Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) and the appropriate practice points (STANDARD.md 4) hold in every
  unit.
- **Grade outcomes.** Bar criterion 1 asks for an outcome for every standard in every grade (STANDARD.md 3 rule
  2). In our words; the scope and sequence copies them and adds the indicators each year works on.
  - Grade 3. S1: throws, rolls and catches in several tasks, jumps and lands and balances in place, does body rolls
    on a mat, kicks and dribbles with the feet, bounces a ball on the move, strikes with long and short tools, and
    jumps rope in several tasks (S1.5.3, S1.5.6 to S1.5.12, S1.5.16, S1.5.18 to S1.5.20; units 2, 3, 5, 6, 7), with
    four-count dance phrases begun (unit 8). S2: uses movement ideas and tactics to stay safe, says when a warm-up
    and cool-down are needed, names a skill's key parts, matches activities to the health-related fitness parts and
    describes the benefits of activity (S2.5.1, S2.5.7, S2.5.11, S2.5.13, S2.5.15; units 1, 2, 4). S3: acts kindly in
    a group and stays safe with few reminders (S3.5.3, S3.5.4; units 1, 5). S4: describes how a favorite activity
    lets them express themselves and explains the health benefits of moving, names the calming method that works
    best during activity and finds a way to be active outside class (S4.5.1, S4.5.3, S4.5.8, S4.5.9; units 4, 6, 7).
    Checked by skill tasks, knowledge tasks (shown, said or a one-line written answer) and conduct marks.
  - Grade 4. S1: links traveling chains, shifts weight from feet to hands, performs an in-place sequence and a dance
    sequence, volleys and bumps, serves, passes and gathers, and combines moving with sending to a target (S1.5.1,
    S1.5.2, S1.5.4, S1.5.5, S1.5.13 to S1.5.15, S1.5.17, S1.5.21; units 10, 12, 13, 14, 15). S2: explains how to
    attack, says why a cue helps an object skill, describes skill-related fitness, finds a pulse and describes body
    changes, and uses movement ideas in dance, gymnastics, rhythms and individual activities (S2.5.2, S2.5.4, S2.5.6,
    S2.5.10, S2.5.12, S2.5.14; units 9, 10, 11, 15). S3: explains how others see a situation, decides roles with a
    group and describes activities from cultures around the world (S3.5.1, S3.5.2, S3.5.7; units 11, 16). S4:
    describes how favorite activities connect them with others, spots a group difficulty, gives reasons for the
    activities they pick and names a strength and where to practice (S4.5.2, S4.5.4, S4.5.5, S4.5.7; units 11, 14).
    Checked the same way.
  - Grade 5. S1: shows water safety skills on dry land (and simple swimming only in a school's own aquatics
    program), and uses the band's skills in invasion, net and wall, and striking and fielding games and in an
    eight-part routine (S1.5.22; units 18, 19, 21, 22, 23). S2: explains how to defend, solves a game problem by
    testing a change, describes FITT, sets a fitness goal, names technology that helps and its limits, explains how
    food and drink affect activity, and shows understanding of water safety and basic swimming (S2.5.3, S2.5.5,
    S2.5.8, S2.5.9, S2.5.16 to S2.5.18; units 17 to 20, 23). S3: works through a disagreement and makes fair
    decisions that fit the activity's customs (S3.5.5, S3.5.6; units 18, 21). S4: sets a visible goal for four to
    six weeks and reviews what PE has meant to them (S4.5.6, S4.5.10; units 17, 24). Checked the same way.
- **Base text.** None. Physical education is Curio Class's own. Cue words, task cards, game cards and knowledge cards
  are ours; the appropriate practice guidelines (2009) and PECAT are read into our words, never quoted. Each cultural
  game or dance is named only after its own source (the community's own organization or a public folklife archive)
  says it is open to be taught (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day": Understanding (a skill
  shown and named in two or three cues, then tried at once, first try watched by the teacher); Fluency (many tries
  with feedback on one cue: stations, partner tasks, task cards, no lines); Application (the skill in a changing
  setting: a chain or routine, a small-sided game of two or three a side, a dance, a circuit, a tactic in play);
  Check; Respond. Fluency is about two fifths of each unit's teaching days in grades 3 and 4 and about a third in
  grade 5, where the game units are mostly Application: a skill is still learned by many tries, and grade 5 uses the
  band's skills in games. No game is played before its skill has been taught and practiced (lens, "Game play before
  the skill is taught").
- **The C3 column** holds, for PE, the unit's cues and the settings it moves through (alone, partner, station,
  sequence, small-sided game), as science's holds its practices (For the lead 3).
- **Assessment.** Each unit's first skill day has a baseline watch of the unit's main cue in rotating groups (met,
  partly, not yet); its end-of-unit assessment repeats that cue as the skill task, adds a knowledge task (shown by
  moving, said to the teacher or a one-line written exit answer on a half sheet, picture-led; from grade 3 a child
  may write or say it) and marks conduct once, against the S3 behavior the unit's Ends names (recorded during
  practice, with no extra rotation). Growth is the change against the child's own baseline, never a rank. Units of
  10 days or fewer end with one check day; longer units with two, so the teacher sees every child (six groups of
  five, three to four minutes a group while the others work at stations). A one-day check holds one skill task
  watched in the rotation and a short knowledge task answered by the same group in the same visit; where a unit
  names a second skill task, it is the closure watch of the unit's last Fluency day, carried as a mark. No score
  comes from a day a skill is first taught. Every unit here lists one respond day after its check. Students judge
  and track their own work (bar C8): from grade 3 each child keeps a private skill sheet with their own baseline and
  check marks per cue.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** The count starts from grade 3, with PE K to 2 as the course before (its grade 2 units
  15 to 22). A row assumed from the year before, in this course or in K to 2 grade 2, opens as a retrieval task
  inside the instant activity of the first skill day that needs it (Seams gives each row's unit). Six assumed rows
  were last taught two or more grades back, so each gets a readiness check of ten minutes before its unit opens, its
  results shaping day 1: g1-heart-muscle (K to 2 unit 12, grade 1) for unit 4, given in the last ten minutes of unit
  3's last day (day 32); g2-foods-energy (K to 2 unit 18, grade 2) for unit 20, on unit 19's last day (grade 5 day
  36); g1-world-games (K to 2 unit 14, grade 1) for unit 16, on unit 15's last day (grade 4 day 73); g3-strike-tools
  (unit 6, grade 3) for unit 21, on the mid-year respond day (grade 5 day 45); g3-rope (unit 7, grade 3) for unit
  22, on unit 21's last day (grade 5 day 54); g2-water-skills (K to 2 unit 22, grade 2) for unit 23, on the year-end
  respond day (grade 5 day 65). No unit 1 readiness check is needed: unit 1's rows assume grade 2 rows only. Day 1
  of every unit stays clean for the hook and the question. A student who joins in grade 4 or 5 meets the same
  retrieval tasks, and the teacher re-teaches the cue on the spot where a child has never met it.
- **Space, weather and equipment.** Every unit runs in a gym, on a field or in a cleared room, with a low-equipment
  version (rolled socks, paper-plate paddles, tape lines, string ropes, a rope between two chairs for a net) and a
  wet-weather version named in the unit plan (bar criterion 5). Class of thirty: stations in groups of four or five,
  one item a pair or better; small-sided games of two or three a side on several small courts at once, so nobody
  waits. Outdoor days in warm weather carry a water break and shade; the unit plan names them (APPROACH.md, "Safe
  space").
- **Required access.** Two things the low-equipment path cannot replace: a soft surface for rolls, feet-to-hands
  shifts and routine floor work (units 3, 13, 14, 22: mats from the school's stock, or carpet; on a hard floor with
  none, rolls and hands-down shifts are not done and the unit says which standing task replaces each), and real child
  life jackets for unit 23's fastening task (borrowed, or the K to 2 band's four). Each unit's last column names what
  it needs, the safe substitute and the purchase if none is available.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30, counting reusable items once, assuming the K to
  2 band's items are in the closet (soft balls, beanbags, cones, scarves, short paddles, plastic bats, ropes, four
  child life jackets): shown in each unit's last column. Across the band about $390 new, plus mats from the school's
  stock. A school with an ordinary PE closet spends nothing. The low-equipment path's listed buys are provisional,
  so the path is not promised at no cost (For Cory).
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared teacher device is optional: it shows cue pictures, plays the music
  track for dance and rope days (units 7, 8, 15, 16, 22) and runs a visible timer for circuit stations. No owned code
  needs a device: SHAPE.S2.5.16 (unit 17) asks students to name technology and what it can and cannot show, taught
  from picture cards of a step counter, a timer and a skill video, with the teacher's watch as the one real timer. A
  teacher with no device claps or drums the beat and holds up the printed cue card, and every child meets every
  part in full.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs from printed cue cards, picture task cards, game cards and the teacher's record
  sheet; each child's skill sheet, goal card and fitness log are paper and private. No tool is needed, no student
  account exists, and no tool judges movement, ranks students or collects fitness data (APPROACH.md, "What a tool may
  and may not do").
- **Reading load.** Task, game and cue cards are picture-led with a few words a line, read aloud on the first day a
  card is used; a one-line exit answer may be said instead of written. Each unit names its movement words (the rows'
  `words`), taught with a picture or demonstration and a frame ("My plant foot goes ___ the ball."). A child slow to
  say a cue may show it; the check marks movement and understanding apart.
- **Supports.** Every lesson, closure and check task has an adaptation for disabilities and a range of skill
  (equipment size, distance, speed, rules, roles). A child with a short-term injury or a medical condition follows
  the nurse's and family's guidance, with a moving role for the parts that are fine or a seated helping role (cue
  watcher, score keeper of their own count, game official in unit 21). Groups and teams are made by the teacher,
  quickly and privately. No exercise as punishment, no elimination, no human targets, no public team picks, no
  activity split by gender; every small-sided game offers a more and a less competitive court the child chooses
  (STANDARD.md 4, "Competition").
- **Water safety.** Dry land only, for every school: unit 23 teaches the reach and throw rescues to a target,
  fastening a life jacket and the buddy rule, and shows floating, a front and a back kick and two strokes lying on
  the floor. In-water tasks belong to a school's own aquatics program with a qualified lifeguard on site and the
  school's swim policy; no child is made to enter water (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Swaps and leave-outs.** A school or family may swap a named cultural game or dance (units 8, 15, 16) for one
  whose source says it is open to be taught, or an outdoor activity that does not fit its place (STANDARD.md 5). A
  unit left out takes its owned codes off the course page's claim; no other unit claims them.
- **Shorter path.** The 64-session path each year (APPROACH.md, "Pacing", short paths 60 to 64) drops 16 of the
  year's 80 days, all of them Fluency days (about 30 of the 74 unit days in grades 3 and 4, about 25 in grade 5): at
  most 3 in any unit, never a unit's last Fluency day before its check, and never a check, respond, baseline,
  readiness or first-teaching day; both course-level checks and every respond day stay. Days are dropped by practice
  load, not evenly; in grade 5, where Fluency is smaller, the short path may take one Application game day in a
  unit of 9 or more days instead. The scope and sequence names the 16 days a year and confirms each unit keeps at
  least one Fluency day and every owned outcome stays taught and checked.
- **Logic model.** One page a year in the scope and sequence (inputs: 80 sessions of 35 minutes, the cue and game
  cards and the equipment list; activities: the units; outcomes: the grade outcomes per standard above); the course
  page says the course has not been tried with children and claims no effect on fitness or health (APPROACH.md,
  "Sources").
- **Sensitive ground and teacher notes.** Every PE teacher page carries health's disclosure steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"), and changing clothes follows the school's
  policy with no one changing in front of others (Cory, 2026-11-21). No weighing, no body measurements, no body,
  calorie or diet talk; fitness words are habits, never scores; pulse counts, fitness logs and goals are private and
  never compared, posted or graded.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside human reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14). Units with cultural content (8, 15, 16) name the
  communities whose games and dances they use and, before the read, the agent studies that community's own
  published sources on the activity and on whether it is open to be taught. Each pick was spot-checked at a source
  on 2026-11-25 (the unit's last column); the agent's read confirms it is open to be taught before a unit names it.
  Units with emotion, body, fitness-data or food content (4, 6, 9, 11, 17, 20, 24) and disability adaptations in
  every unit name their sources in the last column.
- **Safety read.** The safety agent reads, before they go live, every unit with striking tools or flying objects
  (units 2, 6, 12, 19, 21), mats and floor work (units 3, 13, 14, 22), poles (unit 16) and water (unit 23), against
  these free guides: the Nova Scotia Department of Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades
  Primary-12" (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf;
  opened 2026-11-25 for the K to 2 frame); SPHEReS ("School Physical Activity, Health and Education Resource for
  Safety", https://myspheres.ca/en/), the University of Alberta's online guidelines that replaced the print Alberta
  guidelines (opened 2026-11-25 for the K to 2 frame); and for water CDC's "Preventing Drowning" page
  (https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html, opened 2026-11-25, dated 2025-09-29). The safety agent reads
  the sections each unit needs (striking, mats and gymnastics, net games, water). No student sign-off form. Answer
  keys get the answer-key agent's read.
