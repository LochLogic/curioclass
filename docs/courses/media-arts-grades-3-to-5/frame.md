# Course frame: Media Arts, grades 3 to 5

Course `media-arts-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `media-arts-3-5`), a band course for grades 3, 4 and 5, run as three years one after another (year 3, year 4, year 5). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape as Media Arts, grades K to 2, so a
  technique and a thread come back one step harder a year later in the same place: a reading-and-media-life unit, an
  ideas-and-crew unit, a making-and-judging unit, and a screening unit that ends the year with the class showing.
  Each unit joins two progression threads, and every unit makes, studies another maker's media work and shows (bar
  criteria 4 and 5). Media arts here means pictures, sound and moving pictures framed, put in order, captured, edited
  and shown. In grades 3 to 5 the paper media tools of K to 2 grow into crew work: a card viewfinder at shot sizes
  and angles, flip books and paper zoetrope strips, paper cut-out stop-motion, a drawn shot strip that is cut and
  reordered as an edit, live sound effects, and a class newscast. A shared school device is one way in, never the
  only one.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 5 minutes, trays and any shared device out), the
  teaching and making, and clean-up, hand-back of devices and collecting work (about 4 minutes in a paper or device
  session, 6 in a cutting and gluing session); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days
  include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25
  days) and one respond day after it. Unit days follow the rows' load: the ideas-and-crew unit is longest each year
  (18 days) because it owns five ids, the crew roles and a new motion technique; the reading unit (14 in years 3 and
  4, 16 in year 5, where the advertising history adds a study) sets up the tool and space rules and the reading
  routine; the making-and-judging unit (16, 14, 12) makes one ordered piece and judges it; the screening unit (12, 14,
  14) owns five ids but reuses frames and sounds kept from earlier units as material, and year 4's public showing and
  year 5's newscast need two more days than year 3's class screening. These are course days: the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2,
  rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): no separate media arts weekly minimum was found in the visual and
  media arts association's elementary checklist or its general PreK to 12 program standards (the second-model read,
  at source, 2026-11-25; the elementary 50-minute figure is for visual arts and is not borrowed). This course gives 80
  minutes a week, 40 on the once-a-week path; no numerical comparison is claimed (For the lead item 3). The course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a media arts
  teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look or listen at a work that carries the unit's response and its
  connecting sentence; one making session with the critique; one revision session (kept cards finished, rotating
  look-fors marked); the check; one respond session after every mark is in. A screening unit (4, 8, 12) runs
  demonstration, making with the critique, revision, the showing (check 1), the written and talk check (check 2),
  respond: six sessions. Fluency days fold into the openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one.
  Sessions on that path: year 3, units of 7, 9, 6 and 6; year 4, units of 7, 9, 6 and 6; year 5, units of 8, 9, 5
  and 6; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond session. In every year the screening unit's six
  sessions run in weeks 25 to 30, the year-end check session in week 31 and its respond session in week 32 (as in
  media arts 6 to 8, one week before the full path's week 32, since a once-a-week year has one session in week
  32). Year 5's unit 11 (five sessions) has one check
  session for its written item and kept cards, after demonstration, making with the critique and revision; the full
  path's two check days are not carried into it. The mid-year check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (years 3 and
  4 week 17, year 5 week 18) and their respond sessions one week later (weeks 18 and 19); the year-end check sessions
  fall in week 31 and their respond sessions in week 32. No assessment is
  ever done in a respond session; the rotating look-fors fall on the last two making sessions, fifteen students a
  session. A merged day never drops a critique, a revision or a respond session; the scope and sequence marks which
  days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, media arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 3 claims only the 18 grade 3 ids (`.3`, `.3a` and so on), year 4 only the 18 grade 4
  ids, year 5 only the 18 grade 5 ids: 54 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its
  own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, producing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at
  least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and
  anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  words-file lines of its ids, and each id's action against the national standard's own text where a words line may
  have drifted (For the lead item 1), the nine criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the
  lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add.
  Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done
  2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence still states its id count (18) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Media arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain films, photographs and prints from library, archive and museum collections (the Library of Congress,
  the National Archives, museums' open access), and, for living or recent makers, a description in our own words with
  a link to the maker's, museum's or film board's own page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work
  named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped
  work must keep the unit's ids met and the year's range. Each year's works include living makers and more than one
  region and period (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). Across the band they come from Africa (Niger, Ethiopia,
  Ghana), the Americas (Abenaki and Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) makers, Zapotec Juchitán, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, the
  United States), Asia (Japan, China, Iran) and Europe (France, Spain, Belgium); none repeats a work or a maker the
  K to 2 course studies, and each is studied once in the media arts line, K to 12 (the later bands check against this
  one). Two works also meet the visual arts 3 to 5 frame's picks by form only, not by work: none is shared. Media remix uses only public domain or openly licensed sources, named on the piece.
  Curio Class writes every step card, work card, invented media card set, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Framing: shot sizes (wide, medium, close) and angles (high,
  low, eye level) with the card viewfinder (3), angle and light for effect, front and side light (4), the cut: shot
  order, the cut-in close-up and continuity on a drawn or captured shot strip (5). Order and motion: a 12-frame loop
  in a flip book or paper zoetrope strip, with spacing for slow and fast (3), paper cut-out stop-motion with
  replacement parts (move a little, capture, move again) (4), object stop-motion with stand-in objects and wash
  animation (5). Sound: live sound effects made on cue from found objects (3), a three-layer sound plan (voice,
  effect, music) on a sound chart (4), voice-over and an interview for a newscast (5). Arranging: a purposeful order
  with movement and force (3), balance and contrast (4), emphasis for clarity (5). Presenting: a narrated photo-story
  shown in a class screening with roles (3), a coordinated piece at a public school showing (4), a newscast shown in
  two formats compared (5).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills of a technique with feedback (a "wide, medium,
  close" and "high, low, eye level" call with the viewfinder, a four-frame spacing drill, a sound-on-cue drill, a
  shot-order card drill); in grades 3 to 5 it is mostly the five-minute opener and one or two fluency days a unit.
  Application: making with some choice; the second making day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest),
  and the next day's revision is application too. Check: the self-check and the written, talk and table items of the
  unit's evidence line; a kept piece is scored after the session, and a screening is scored where it happens.
  Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet". A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application,
  fluency, application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in five kinds: a look-for on the guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept piece after the session; a kept card or sheet made on the
  unit's last making days (a storyboard, a role record, a revision log, a screening-plan card, a source log), scored
  with the piece; a written item, one or two short sentences on an unseen public domain, openly licensed or Curio
  Class original piece (stills on paper or the teacher screen), read aloud to any student who asks; talk items at
  the teacher's table, at most one a student on a check day; rotating look-fors; and, in units 4, 8 and 12, look-fors
  marked at the screening. Rotating look-fors: every tool, space, ethics or role item that shows a student using
  something (MA:Cn11.1.3b, 4b and 5b; MA:Pr5.1.3a and 3c; MA:Pr5.1.4a and 4c) is never a check-day item; it is seen
  and marked on a class list over the unit's last three making days, ten students a day for about a minute and a half
  each while the class works. A talk-and-written check day's 40 minutes: set-up 4; the written item for the whole
  class at once 10; six groups of five at the teacher's table for about three minutes each (18), one talk item a
  student, while the others do their self-check on the guide (5) and then a quiet drawing card or label and finish
  work on paper; clean-up 3; 5 minutes of slack for the turns between groups and a student who needs longer. That is
  35 minutes planned. No device is out but the one on the teacher's table, which faces the room; a second adult,
  where there is one, watches the room, and the teacher watches one table, never a room and a table at once. A screening check day (the first of the two check days of units 4, 8
  and 12): set-up 5; six groups of five show in turn at the one screen, stage or box for about four minutes each (24)
  while the rest are the audience and the teacher marks each student's look-fors at the screening place; each
  student's self-check 5; clean-up 4. No talk items are heard that day; they come on the unit's second check day. The
  piece's draft, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision come on
  earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a
  baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two
  marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the portfolio folder (paper, or a file on the
  school's device) with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** The count starts from the band's order (3, then 4, then 5). Year 3's rows assume the
  grade 2 rows of Media Arts, grades K to 2, last used one grade back where a student took it, and every year 4 and
  year 5 unit assumes the year before's rows in the same threads, also one grade back, so each unit opens with a
  retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question.
  Media Arts, grades K to 2 is not a core course, so no unit assumes it was taken (lesson 32): each year 3 retrieval
  task carries its ten-minute reteach of the grade 2 technique as written (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline
  look"), and the same reteach serves a student new to the band in year 4 or 5, or a mixed class that meets year 5
  before year 4. Before the first critique (unit 1), the teacher models describe, ask, suggest on a Curio Class
  example with the unit's look-for and the class practices it with the card; unit 2 retrieves it, and unit 3 extends
  it with named criteria. The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Arts are specials in K to 5 and do not count against the classroom teacher's stagger
  (CALENDAR.md rule 1). Mid-year: week 17 in years 3 and 4 (course days 33 and 34 of the year), week 18 in year 5
  (days 35 and 36), each after the year's second unit. Year-end: week 32 in every year (days 63 and 64 of the year),
  after the screening unit, whose showing, in weeks 26 to 31 (year 3) or 25 to 31 (years 4 and 5), is its own
  end-of-unit assessment (lead structure pass 2026-11-26, the media arts group read's finding 1, option (a); the
  earlier placement before the screening unit by CALENDAR.md stagger tool c is withdrawn, since rule 1 settles the
  stagger). Clashes with other after-core courses are left for the lead's calendar pass (For the lead item 2).
- **Recording and kept work.** No student is recorded, photographed or filmed without the family's yes; a face or a
  voice is never required (hands, the work, a shadow or the sound alone will do); a student who does not want to be
  recorded is observed live and the teacher's mark stands. Media in the room points at things, places and work,
  never at a student who has not said yes. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device and goes
  nowhere else (APPROACH "Kept work"); no student posts or shares media of others online, and no student account is
  used (the rows' teacher notes).
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 12 (students present as reporters in a newscast; the
  printed news-sheet format and off-camera roles keep every id met, and the unit studies a nineteenth-century
  Shanghai picture-news page chosen free of the period's stereotypes). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the
  course page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, markers, card from cereal boxes, found objects for
  sound; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One
  student at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, every crew task has a doubled version
  (parent and student, two roles each), and the showing is to the family. The teacher needs no media arts training:
  each unit's teacher page says what to look and listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as six
  flashlights and brass fasteners counted once, printing of step cards, work cards and invented media cards), all
  optional beside the free path: year 3 $10, $15, $10, $10 ($45); year 4 $10, $15, $10, $15 ($50); year 5 $15, $10,
  $10, $15 ($50). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. Where a school has them, one shared school device (a camera, a sound recorder or a
  free frame-capture program) per group of five is one way in; one teacher screen may show a work, a step or a
  playback. Every day runs from the printed step card, work card and paper media tools without them, and no student
  account is used. The owned codes that name tools, capture, editing or online life (MA:Pr5.1.3a to 3c, 4a to 4c and
  5a to 5c; MA:Cn11.1.3b, 4b and 5b on tools and spaces; MA:Cn11.1.3a and MA:Cn10.1.4b on online behavior and online
  spaces; MA:Pr6.1.3a on distributing) are met in full on paper: in this course a media tool is anything that frames,
  captures, orders, plays or shows pictures and sound, so a paper-path student captures by drawing what the
  viewfinder frames, animates in a drawn frame strip flipped as a flip book, edits by cutting and reordering a drawn
  shot strip with scissors and tape, records a sound as a sound score performed the same way twice, and meets online
  behavior and online spaces on printed invented screens Curio Class writes (no student goes online in class).
  Shared devices are school-approved; each is wiped and handed back by the device card's steps.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, card, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  frame-sequence viewer that plays drawn frames in order, a shot-strip editor, a work viewer) has a printed version
  that does the same job (the flip book, the paper zoetrope strip, the cut-and-tape shot strip) and needs no student
  account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards are picture-led with short words, read aloud on the first day they are used; a
  student reads at most one short card alone (APPROACH "Supports"). English learners: each unit teaches its six words
  in depth, with a picture, a gesture or a sound and a frame ("I see ___ because ___." "The close-up makes us feel
  ___."), a student may show the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student made apart from what
  they said.
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against three free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov), the World Health Organization's
  "Make Listening Safe" pages on safe headphone volume (who.int), and the web accessibility guidelines' rule on
  flashing, success criterion 2.3.1 "Three Flashes or Below Threshold" (w3.org). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH
  "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): no filming, photographing or recording a student without
  the family's yes; no flashing light in any piece, screen, loop or disc, no strobe, and no loop or disc drawn with
  alternating light and dark frames; screen breaks, with no student at a screen for more than about ten minutes at a
  stretch; headphones, where used, at low volume, and no loud sound in a student's ear; flashlights are low-power and
  never shone in eyes, and no laser pointers; device cords taped down or none; card cut by an adult with a blade,
  students use blunt-tip scissors; supplies labeled non-toxic; hands washed after glue, ink and found objects; no
  glass, sharp metal or food as a sound or stop-motion object. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls
  in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (a scroll held
  by a Buddhist temple in Kyoto, Japan), unit 2 (a Nigerien filmmaker), unit 4 (an Abenaki filmmaker), unit 5 (an
  Ethiopian photographer), unit 7 (a photograph of a Zapotec woman of Juchitán, Mexico; a national park on Shoshone and
  other peoples' homelands), unit 8 (a Kanien'kehá:ka artist of Kahnawà:ke), unit 9 (Ghanaian poster painters; old
  trade cards, many of which mock African American, Chinese American, Irish American and Native people), unit 10
  (Chinese ink-wash animation), unit 1 also (a Chilean animator's film of a family parted), unit 11 (an Iranian filmmaker) and unit 12 (a Shanghai picture-news magazine; a Mexican printmaker).
  Every task that ties media to a student's own interests, knowledge or experience (units 4, 8 and 12) offers an
  invented, public or class-shared option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Online behavior is studied on
  invented cases only. No task asks what a family watches, buys, owns or posts, and home media is never judged. Each
  section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its
  teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, work
  cards, paper media tools, found objects, an optional shared device. Activities: technique taught in steps, works
  studied with their setting, crew making with a critique and a revision, a showing or screening each unit. Outputs: a
  portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per student on the main
  look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 18 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried
  with children, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").
