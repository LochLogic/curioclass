- **E154, 1.OA.A.1.** On separate panels a tag sorter starts with one tag. Rin adds an unseen group. The sorter now holds one packet of ten and nine loose tags. Keep the ten marks visible on the packet. Provide an empty change space. Read “How many tags did Rin add?” Shown. Marks 18 in the change space or in 1 + □ = 19. Not yet seen. Nineteen (C), copies the ending collection as the change.
  - Shape: reconstruct a missing change from a grouped ending picture. Save the mark before giving the removal slip. Practice acts out changes with counters.
- **E154b, 1.OA.A.1.** Show two strips with nine pins drawn on each. In the next picture Rin has taken some pins off. Just one pin is still on the strips. “How many pins did Rin take off?” Shown. Seventeen, entered in the picture or in 18 − □ = 1. The eighteen starting pins include the one still attached. Not yet seen. Answers one and points to the ending pin as the number removed (C).
  - Shape: recover a removal from equal starting strips and a single pin left behind. Reserve both pin pictures.
- **E155, 1.OA.A.1.** The whole is 18 cards. Two have clips, three other cards have bands and all the rest have a dot. Show the two marked groups beside an unmeasured, empty dot-card space inside the boundary for the whole. Read “How many cards have a dot?” Shown. Marks 13 in the dot-card space. Not yet seen. Fourteen (A), counts the known five as the first new count through eighteen.
  - Shape: combine two clues about cards without dots to find a missing part.
- **E155 equations, 1.OA.A.1.** Save the count. “What addition puts the cards back together?” Accept 2 + 3 + 13 = 18 or 5 + 13 = 18. Then ask “What subtraction checks your answer?” Accept 18 − 5 = 13 or 18 − 13 = 5, including dictated equations. Writing 5 + 13 = 17 after counting the held five as a new count is A.
  - Shape: rebuild and check the same whole with two number sentences. Reserve the fixed whole and both marked groups. Practice uncovers one part on a supplied bar.
- **E156, 1.OA.A.1.** Rin checks two bead sleeves marked Pine and Elm. Both hide the same number of beads, from one to eight in each. Pine shows twelve beads outside its sleeve and Elm shows one. Give squared paper and the two name labels. “Draw Pine's and Elm's bars to show how many more beads Pine has.” Shown. Both labeled bars start together. Equal hidden lengths and the first exposed bead match. Eleven beads stick out on Pine's bar. Accept any equal hidden lengths in range or matching brackets for the hidden parts. Not yet seen. Thirteen (D), adds the two exposed groups. Twelve (A), counts the starting one as the first new count through twelve. Save the drawing. “What number sentence shows the extra beads?” Accept 1 + 11 = 12 or 12 − 1 = 11 for the exposed groups, or a true equation using the child's whole bars. Then ask “How many fewer beads does Elm have?” Eleven. Accept a point to the same gap.
  - Shape: construct bars from equal hidden starts and separate exposed groups. Reserve the sleeves and quantities. Practice interprets supplied bars.
- **E157, 1.NBT.A.1.** Show nine ten-sticks with unit marks and 28 loose squares. Read “How many squares are on this card?” Shown. Marks the picture, for example by circling two new tens, and writes 118 on it. Not yet seen. Thirty-seven (E), counts nine sticks as nine single squares. One hundred nine (E), counts one stick as one square. Say the written result to a partner after both papers are marked. Give immediate private feedback without retaining a product or spoken tally by name.
  - Shape: produce a numeral from sticks and loose squares that can be regrouped. Reserve this collection. Practice alternates loose collections and completed bundles.
- **E157 count, 1.NBT.A.1.** On a separate take-home strip, 97 is followed by eight empty hooks. “Count on from 97, touching one hook for each number.” Partners take turns. Each partner checks for 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 103, 104, 105. Each child can point and mark just the last number, 105, before the partner key opens. Partners check against a covered key after both have marked. Describe a skipped number by pointing to the first skipped hook. The register has no letter for skipping a counting word.
  - Shape: continue from a given start across 100 on a finite strip. Reserve this start, stop and layout. Practice uses different count runs.

## Day 154

**Application.** I can find what changed in a story.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return both E153 slips. Mix a known addition and subtraction. Say “Invented for practice. Rin sets out seven tiles, adds some, and now has twelve.” Show three spaces for before, change and after. “How many did Rin add?” Five. Accept a drawing. Recall the box for a missing amount on a separate example before children use it in equations. Route a miss to change practice.
- **10 min. Opening.** Rebuild section 2.1's before, move and end panels with fresh counters. Name the start, the change and the end. Show C. Draw added pieces outside the covered start. Say how the number sentence names equal amounts and the box marks the unknown.
- **35 min. Practice.** Ten for result unknown. Rin sets out eight tiles and adds five. Move two of the five to fill ten. Three remain, so there are thirteen. Show the B repair here, then try a fresh joining card. Also act out Rin taking three from fourteen, leaving eleven. Fifteen for change unknown. Act out joining from six to ten and removing from ten to six, both changes of four. Use ten minutes for a recurring A. Children who missed retrieval mark before, change and after while acting. Check those pictures in the last five minutes. Ten for start unknown. Rin added three and ended with eight, or took three away and ended with eight. The starts were five and eleven. Partners draw each story and put the box where its unknown belongs.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Ask “What changed?” Connect one acted story to its situation equation and a solution equation.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E154 tag slip, then E154b pin slip. Shape: recover a joining change from the ending packet and a removal from the starting strips. Reserve both stories.

## Day 155

**Application.** I can find a missing part and explain my equation.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return both E154 slips. Mixed facts and a tens sketch.
- **10 min. Opening.** Show a whole of twelve with eight visible. Revisit part and whole. Compare 8 + □ = 12 with 12 − 8 = 4. Show the A contrast on separate stock.
- **35 min. Practice.** Ten for total unknown, such as 3 + 8 = 11. Use this block for C when needed, with the joining picture before a task that asks for the fixed whole. Fifteen for one part unknown with a supplied open-and-cover bar. Ten for both parts unknown: split six, including zero and six. Keep the whole labeled and check the parts against it.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Reopen parts and check the whole. Ask two children to show which number stayed fixed. Everyone else points on paper.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E155. Shape: combine two clues about excluded cards. Reserve the clipped and banded groups from bar practice.

## Day 156

**Application.** I can show the difference with labeled bars.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return E155. Mixed facts and a story sketch.
- **10 min. Opening.** Match cubes and draw the two labeled bars from the D example. Attach the box to the unmatched length. Read both 6 + □ = 11 and 11 − 6 = 5 against the bars. The extra length stays five when the wording changes to fewer.
- **35 min. Practice.** Fifteen for difference from supplied bars, including ten for A if needed. Ten each for larger and smaller unknown. Use both wordings: “Eight is two fewer than what number?” Ten. “Eight is two more than what number?” Six. Reverse the wording using the same quantities and relation. Work in pairs with supported discussion and a drawn mark from each child.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Ask “Where is the extra part?” Trace the common start and gap. Cover the shared bar.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E156. Shape: generate bars for a fresh pair of sleeves with equal hidden starts. Practice supplied bars remain covered.

## Day 157

**Fluency.** I can connect counts, pictures and numerals to 120.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return E156. Mixed facts and a tens picture.
- **10 min. Opening.** Echo supplied starts of 87 and 99, then continue across the next decade or 100. Revoice numeral and ten-stick with visible units. Show E.
- **35 min. Practice.** Partners count loose collections, bundle by tens and match completed tens drawings to numerals. Include counting from 114 through 120. Embed ten for D and a separate ten for A if necessary. Switch roles with immediate feedback.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Partners read a numeral from their practice pile, then link its label to that count. Both partners get a turn.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E157 collection and count strip. Shape: label a mixed collection, then continue a count on empty hooks. Use three minutes for private marks, one minute for the two partners to say their numeral and one to uncover the keys. Sheets go home. As partners check, each child drops one anonymous slip. Mark it for a stick counted as one only if the child's first marks or explanation show that count. Tally and discard the slips for the 158 repair.

## 9. Supports and extension

On 154 review change as what joins or leaves, Kindergarten 4.3, with a number sentence that uses an equals sign for the same amount. The unknown is the amount to find. The before, move and end panels and the box from Grade 1 section 2.1, the unknown from 2.4 and the number sentence from 5.1 are planned only. Show each with pieces before using it. On 155 review part, one group within the whole, and whole, every piece in the collection (Kindergarten 5.1). On 156 a label tells which bar holds which group, and difference is what sticks out after matching. Section 1.2's label and section 2.3's difference plans are planned only. Review more and fewer (Kindergarten 2.2) with matched pieces. Say “___ has ___ fewer than ___.” On 157 review numeral as the written form of a number, Kindergarten 2.4. A ten-stick holds ten visible ones. Read aloud and offer tactile bars, large marks, pointing or neutral dictation. Extend on 156 by rewording a practice comparison and keeping the same labeled gap.
