## Day 158

**Understanding.** I can explain why different clues name one number.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mixed facts. Retrieve 82 with eight tens worth 80 and two ones worth 2. E157 papers went home.
- **10 min. Opening.** A clue is a hint the number has to fit. Show F. Trade ten loose ones for a ten-stick and connect the new grouping to the unchanged amount.
- **20 min. Practice.** On a 0 to 99 paper chart, show how the two-digit numbers in one row have the same tens digit. Shade for one clue at a time. Include four tens with zero ones, 40. If E157 showed E, use the first ten minutes to open and rebuild a stick. Invite anyone who wants that practice. Use the other ten for a distinct 82 retrieval miss: build a fresh two-digit number, match each digit to its value and shade its place on the chart. Include a number with zero loose ones in either route. Children who have the idea continue chart work. Keep the exact groups, candidates and arrangements for R1, R2, all exits and the last response off practice and repair cards.
- **15 min. R1.** Hide the shared drawing. Everyone marks a trade before hearing the same objection. A partner may read it aloud. A circle around ten loose ones linked by an arrow to the missing stick can give the child's whole reason. Keep each child's own response. Hear the six scheduled turns.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** “What stayed the same in the trade?” Draw a mark from loose pieces to a new stick on separate stock.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E158. Shape: fix the faulty second picture with an exchange mark. Return the slip on 159.

## Day 160

**Application.** I can write a riddle and explain my learning.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return E159. Mix familiar facts and a tens sketch.
- **15 min. Riddle writing.** Choose a private card from the practice deck. Each child writes, draws or dictates two clues for their own numeral. For example, 51 has five tens and one one. Keep the answer on the back and the reserved cards out of the deck.
- **10 min. Try a partner's riddle.** Each partner gets a turn reading or showing clues and a turn testing the other riddle. Check the private numeral after both clues have been tried.
- **5 min. R2 revision.** Return feedback to every child. File the revision with the original.
- **10 min. Record closure.** Children do familiar paper puzzles. With feedback and earlier judgments already in the folders, allow twenty seconds per child to check the revised part and close the record. Carry any missing part forward as missing.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E160. Shape: author two clues from the reserved unlabeled picture. Keep the original before any discussion. The riddle is unscored.
- **10 min. Partner testing and handoff.** Five minutes for the paired repairs in field 4. Three for a fresh unscored card: show three sticks and twenty-two loose squares beside five sticks and two loose squares. Lay out the loose squares in two rows of ten and two more. “Show how the loose squares make the two missing sticks.” Both pictures show 52. Writing 322 by joining the counts is F. Save the exchange marks and leave the pictures visible. Show 54. “How does 54 fit these pictures?” The tens fit, but 54 has two extra ones. Accepting 54 after checking only its tens is G. Shape: match two trades across the pictures, then test a candidate. In the last two minutes, pairs pick up their slips from the table folders. File the handoff prepared during record closure.

## 8. Practice that returns

Use both signs within ten and an earlier story or place-value idea in each warm-up. The place-value standard sets no fluency target. Give immediate feedback and private retries. Review Kindergarten 6.2 ten ones, ten separate things, and further ones, the ones beside those ten. Review Kindergarten 1.1 Partner turns. Grade 1 sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3, 4.5, 5.2 and 5.4 are planned only. Rebuild the chart and show how to check a supplied clue on a practice picture. Show the units and exchange afresh. Demonstrate filing a dated original and its revision with a spare folder. Keep riddle products separate from the retained fluency evidence.

## 9. Supports and extension

On 158, point to tens as joined groups of ten and ones as single pieces. Call one written number mark a digit. Show which group each digit counts after the trade. Review zero from Kindergarten 2.4 as none, here no loose ones. Show a trade by crossing out ten singles and joining a stick of the same amount. Name a clue as a hint the number must fit. Use “Both clues fit because ___.” Read aloud and offer high contrast, tactile strips, a pointed mark or neutral dictation. Extend by finding a second clue pair for a practice number within 99.

## 10. Two rooms
