## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we show what is happening in a story and find what is missing?"
- **Hook, day 17:** Act out four paper books on a shelf, two arriving, then one leaving. Ask what changed and how a drawing could remember it. Connect to the compelling question. This adapts the library opening in field 10.
- **Supporting questions:** Where is the missing change (day 20)? How can a whole help find a part (day 24)? Where is the difference in a drawing (day 30)? Does an equation tell the same story (day 35)?
- **Closing claim:** "I can show the known and missing quantities, solve, and explain why my equation matches." The Story booklet and Equation strips support this claim; day 39 checks it.
- **Teaching:** Model actions before expressions, then connect every number to its quantity. Compare a situation equation with a solution equation. Step back as children choose representations on days 31 to 35. Teacher pages explain why subtraction finds an unknown addend and why equality compares quantities on both sides.

<!-- skel -->
- **~1.OA.A.1:** (begun here; unit 6 completes it (day 38 note)) Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems involving situations of adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing, with unknowns in all positions, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.D.7:** Understand the meaning of the equal sign, and determine if equations involving addition and subtraction are true or false. For example, which of the following equations are true and which are false? 6 = 6, 7 = 8 – 1, 5 + 2 = 2 + 5, 4 + 1 = 5 + 2. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.D.8:** Determine the unknown whole number in an addition or subtraction equation relating three whole numbers. For example, determine the unknown number that makes the equation true in each of the equations 8 + ? = 11, 5 = _ – 3, 6 + 6 = _. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.B.4:** Understand subtraction as an unknown-addend problem. For example, subtract 10 – 8 by finding the number that makes 10 when added to 8. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve taught counting and facts for five minutes daily, without a timer. Center days repeat known routines and offer checking after an attempt. Never introduce a new strategy during fluency practice.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all text aloud. Pair pictures with part, whole, change, difference and same amount. Offer "I know __; I need __." Use large tactile mats and distinguish groups by shape as well as color. Accept pointing and dictation without selecting the operation. Extend by making a different story for the same equation and explaining what changes.
