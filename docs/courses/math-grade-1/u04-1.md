# Section 4.1: Counting and calculating in tens

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-grade-1/4/s/4.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-grade-1 u04-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Read with [the unit plan](s04.md) and [course defaults](DEFAULTS.md). Grade 1 Math, Unit 4, Numbers to 99. **Days 69 to 73.** Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.NBT.B.2, B.2a and B.2c.** "Understand" the two digits of whole-ten numerals. Ten ones "can be thought of" as one bundle. Numerals 10 through 90 in steps of ten "refer to" one through nine tens and zero ones.
- **1.NBT.C.5, day 71.** "Mentally find" one more or fewer ten from a whole-ten start and "explain" the change. Stay below 100.
- **1.NBT.C.6, days 71 to 73.** "Subtract" a multiple of ten from another in 10 to 90, "relate" the drawing to the equation and "explain" the difference, including zero. Use place value and related addition. Keep taught 1.OA.C.6 strategies in day 73 practice.
- **MP.7, day 72.** Match the number of crossed-out towers to the tens removed. Explain why the counted unit stays ten even when the result is zero. The teacher connects unit counts to object counts before asking for a numeral or equation.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read each stem aloud. Everyone makes a picture mark. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen, and apply a letter only where the work or explanation shows that error. Use invented counts and tell children so. Hold the evidence objects, configurations, missing parts, wrong answers and drawings out of all teaching, cards, practice and repairs.

**Unit assessment items owned:** field 5 rows 2 (P decade) and 5 (P differences and Fresh trace).

- **P decade.** See unit plan row 2. Shown: eight drawn tens, zero leftover ones for 80. Wrong: eight ones for the eight rods (A).
  Shape: independently draw a decade and explain the zero. Practice joins supplied labels and existing groups.
- **P differences.** See row 5. Positive panel, 90 - 30 = 60. Wrong: six ones from counting rods as singles (A). Zero panel, 50 - 50 = 0. Wrong: keeps fifty after subtracting all fifty (F).
  Shape: independently generate both removal pictures with equations and reasons. Practice interprets annotated removals and matches related addition.
- **Fresh subtraction trace.** See row 5. 80 - 30 = 50, five tens. Wrong: five ones from counting rods as singles (A).
  Shape: make and justify a new whole-ten removal record. Accept related addition supported by the drawing. Hold each subtraction family in both directions.

**E69, 1.NBT.B.2c.** Fresh scatter of 70 ungrouped squares with a blank total tag. "How could you group this page so someone can count it?" Key: seven groups of ten, total 70. Wrong: 7 from counting each group as one one (A), or 61 from counting the last group as one one (A).
Shape: independently organize fixed picture marks into a countable record. Practice moves and recounts loose pieces.

**E70, 1.NBT.B.2c.** Nine towers with ten visible cells on each, unlabeled. "What number belongs on this picture's tag?" Mark the quantity on the tag. Key: 90, ninety, nine tens and zero ones. Wrong: 9 from counting each tower as one one (A). A child who identifies nine tens but says "nineteen" in place of "ninety" shows G.
Shape: generate a missing numeral from an unfamiliar tower image. Practice joins supplied representations, with no matching choices printed on the exit.

**E71, 1.NBT.C.5.** Show six tens on a stock card with an empty change panel. "How would this picture change if one ten arrived?" Mark the arrival and its resulting label or tell the label for neutral recording. Key: add one entire tower, seven tens, 70. Wrong: 61 from adding one loose mark (E).
Shape: predict one unseen arrival from an initial picture. Practice moves a tower, marks its already visible position, then undoes that move. The less direction stays in practice.

**E72, 1.NBT.C.6.** Give one storage record to each child. Form A begins at 90 with four tens to go; form B begins at 30 with all three tens to go. "What is left in this tray?" Draw the remainder on paper. Keys: A five tens (50), B zero tens (0). Wrong: A five ones from counting each remaining ten as one one (A); B still three tens after all are removed (F).
Shape: complete one remainder record, with positive and zero cases across the class. Practice interprets rods with removals already marked and links these to equations. Protect both families and their inverses.

**E73, 1.NBT.C.6.** One strip labeled 60 and a blank remainder tag. "Four whole tens tore away. What belongs on the new tag?" Key: 20, two tens; 60 - 40 = 20. Wrong: 2 from counting each remaining ten as one one (A); 56 from removing four single tickets (E).
Shape: reconstruct the unseen remainder in a new one-scene context. Practice checks supplied equations. Reserve the strip, removal and resulting tag.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Rebuild it from loose pieces before connecting the tower to the numeral on day 69. Show a ten as one complete group and say tens for more than one group. ## Day 69

**Understanding.** I can count a collection in tens. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Hook.** Put forty loose paper squares on the class mat. Ask how to avoid losing count, then ask the unit's question about the two digits.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** On the retrieval picture of twelve squares, circle ten and mark the two further ones. Log a miss for day 70. Use the close of this part for a familiar within-ten fact if time remains.
- **10 min. Unit 3 centers.** Return all closing papers and E67. Repair for five, then retry for five. If a group was lost, Rin has three paper brushes, three pens and eight chalk sticks, fourteen in all. Mark each group once. Print and say "Invented for practice."
- **10 min. Model.** Make four connecting-cube towers, draw the marked sticks and tie them to 40. Unfold the separate sticks in A to check what each is worth.
- **20 min. Group and compare.** Pairs count a movable collection by ones and again by tens. Both keep a tally of which pieces were counted.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Point from each bundle to its numeral contribution. Say the total in tens and in ones.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E69. Hear children 25 to 30.

## Day 71

## Day 72

**Understanding.** I can subtract tens, even when none remain. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Revisit counting up from a held quantity, tracking only the new counts.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return E71 and repair E using new material.
- **10 min. Tens removal.** Interpret marked removals for 50 - 20 = 30 and the F contrast. Label the number of tens removed and the value of what stays.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E72. Hear children 13 to 18, one assigned form each.

## Day 73

**Fluency.** I can practice tens and familiar facts. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Recall a taught double and one way to check it.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 72 "What unit did you remove?" Day 73 "What does the zero tell us?" In the annotated subtraction work, trace each equation part to its rods with a pencil line. Look for the removed and remaining units in their own places.

## 8. Practice that returns

Use the retrieval named on each day with a brief mix from the earlier within-ten warm-up pool. Day 73 revisits section 3.5 Partner rounds with a counters check and retry and the earlier fill-ten example from section 3.4. Both are planned only, as are section 3.3 Day 52 Rin's paper pins, unit 2 section 2.1 Rin's badge scenes, and unit 3 section 3.6 Paper exit with the rotating six heard. Partners work untimed and retry from a picture cue.

## 9. Supports and extension

Say "One ten is ten ones" as the child unfolds a group. Pair decade words with drawings; offer "__ tens are __ ones." The teacher reads all words. Enlarge cell lines, supply tactile group boundaries, or mark the child's indicated place. For extension, show the same positive tens difference with a removal and a related addition.

## 10. Two rooms

One child and an adult alternate roles, keep the child's paper and revisit one correction before the next attempt. Project one practice picture if available. Children mark their own sheets.
