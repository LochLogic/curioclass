# Section 6.2: Measure with copies of one unit

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-grade-1/6/s/6.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-grade-1 u06-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 1 Math. Unit 6, Length to 120 units (days 111 to 128), see [s06.md). **Days 115 to 117.** Days by kind: understanding 3. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.MD.A.2.** “Express” a length as a whole number of copies of a length unit and “understand” that the count measures the length only when identical units cover it end to end. MP.5, day 115: pick repeatable lengths and test the fit.
- **Limits.** Use whole-number spans. On 117 compare two correct measures of the same object with different units as exploration.
- **Teacher explanation.** Count a cube's side length once. A loose unit has two ends and covers the space between them. Leaving a space or covering space twice breaks the count's link to the length. The unit size belongs with its count.

## 2. Where it sits

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned.** Measurement record M on 127, [unit field 5](s06.md). F on 128 corroborates this evidence. IDs are planned Data ids. Read stems aloud. Give each child a fresh independent paper picture. Outlines are plain, ends visible and lengths drawn to scale.

- **E115 (s06-X115), 1.MD.A.2, MP.5.** Give an unmarked six-unit rectangle, extra identical units, a double-length piece and a piece longer than the rectangle. “What length label belongs on this rectangle?” Children leave a mark at each boundary of the units used. Key: 6 units, six equal lengths touching and spanning both ends. Wrong: 5 equal lengths with open spaces (C). 7 equal lengths laid over each other (D). 5 using four singles and one double (E).
  - Shape: select a consistent unit from mixed stock and make a missing label checkable. Practice extends a row begun by the teacher on other spans. Data id: s06-X115.
- **E116 (s06-X116), 1.MD.A.2.** Make four plain, randomly placed records of the same five-unit span. Use five equal units touching, four separated units, six overlapping units, and a touching mixed row (three singles and one double). “What makes one record reliable?” Child marks the reliable row's matching units and touching ends. Key: the five equal units span it exactly, each touching the next. Wrong: marks four spaced pieces as reliable (C). Marks six overlapping pieces as reliable (D). Marks the mixed row as reliable because the ends touch (E).
  - Shape: justify one valid arrangement in an audit of four full records of one object. Practice repairs one joint or one unequal piece at a time on other objects. The evidence is the marked feature, so a choice by elimination alone is insufficient. Data id: s06-X116.
- **E117a (s06-X117a), 1.MD.A.2.** Use two plain outlines of one rectangle, covered by either eight small equal units or four equal units each twice as long. Withhold totals. “What count goes on the small-piece row?” Key: 8 small units, traced once each. Wrong: 9 after a repeated touch (G). 7 after substituting a double for two small units and counting all pieces together (E).
  - Shape: label a fixed completed small-unit row independently. Practice handles and replaces units on an open strip. Data id: s06-X117a.
- **E117b (s06-X117b), 1.MD.A.2.** Use the same picture on a separate slip. “How can the second row measure that very same length?” Key: the four large units cover the same span. Matching two smaller lengths to one larger length explains how four pairs cover the eight small lengths. Wrong: a combined row of three large and two small, all called large units (E). Four large lengths drawn with open spaces still called a measure (C). Treat this evidence as exploratory.
  - Shape: explain two labels for one unchanged length by marking related pieces on an unfamiliar double-row picture. Practice overlays a single larger unit on two small pieces at a time. Reserve the complete eight-to-four layout. Data id: s06-X117b.

**M on 127, 1.MD.A.2.** Lay and draw concurrently during the eight-minute group. Give each child one private minute to show their own span and explain one of their paper marks. Read the faulty records as separate paper items, outside that spoken turn. Use the child papers for the other marks.

- **M-length (s06-AM-length).** Give extra pieces all of one length. “How long is this rectangle in these units?” Key: 9 units; nine touching copies and a drawing with matching boundaries. Wrong: 8 copies spaced out across the rectangle (C).
  - Shape: independently construct a measurement from identical stock on an unmarked nine-unit rectangle. Reserve the stock and rectangle. Practice repairs or finishes a started row. Data id: s06-AM-length.
- **M-space (s06-AM-space).** Show eight equal pieces with a one-unit space between the fourth and fifth on the nine-unit span. “What does this row show about the measure?” Key: exposed space between equal pieces. Mark one space. Wrong: the count is a valid measure because all pieces are equal (C).
  - Shape: diagnose exposed surface in one stand-alone record. Reserve the complete record. Use loose joints on different objects in practice. Data id: s06-AM-space.
- **M-overlap (s06-AM-overlap).** Show ten equal pieces with two half-unit overlaps spanning nine units. “What does this row show about where the pieces lie?” Key: two pieces cover some of the same length. Mark the doubled coverage. Wrong: accepting the count because the pieces touch (D).
  - Shape: diagnose doubled coverage in a single static record. Practice slides a live joint. Reserve this overlap picture. Data id: s06-AM-overlap.
- **M-mixed (s06-AM-mixed).** Show seven singles and one double, all touching across the nine-unit span. “What do you notice about using these pieces together?” Key: the pieces vary in length even though the ends touch. Mark two unequal pieces. Wrong: accepting the count as one unit size (E).
  - Shape: diagnose unequal lengths in a single touching record. Practice exchanges one loose piece. Reserve the pictured row. Data id: s06-AM-mixed.

Present each M validity picture separately without stating whether it measures the span. Keep joints and unit sizes visible. The child marks the feature that supports their judgment.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Use 6.1's fixed reference to show what stays constant. On 115 tie a row of touching cube side lengths to its numeral. On 116 release the joints to children. On 117 compare sizes while the object stays fixed. Pass individual boundary drawings to 118.

Identical repeatable pieces with traceable endpoints fit this paper work. Large adult-precut tabs replace cubes or same-size clips. Cut a second stock exactly double the first for 117. Check each printed span with the actual tab and recut if scaling changes. Paper and a pencil carry every task. Keep long rows on tables.

## Day 115

**Understanding.** I can measure with equal units.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return both E114 slips privately. Mix familiar facts and use the priority marked method from 6.1 if needed.
- **10 min. Model.** Teacher begins a bookmark with two joined cubes, repeats to ten touching side lengths and writes 10. Children point to one length along the edge and draw a boundary.
- **25 min. Practice.** Pairs complete partly covered classroom strips with matching cubes or paper tabs. Exchange measurer and checker. Reserve the first five for E114 repair.
- **10 min. Explain.** MP.5: exchange one piece and see whether the row still has one repeatable length. Show the change on the paper sketch.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear children 13 to 18. Others mark one usable piece on their sketch.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E115, independent. Return on 116.

## Day 116

**Understanding.** I can tell why units must touch.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E115 privately. Begin a repair with the correct joint, then use a folded-paper puppet for a contrast if needed.
- **10 min. Model.** Teacher repairs one exposed joint on a paper runner and traces how the pieces cover it.
- **25 min. Practice.** Pairs correct broken joints on different strips, mark where the correction happened and swap roles. First five holds E115 repair.
- **10 min. Contrast.** Slide two overlapping tabs apart to touch. Exchange unequal tabs for a single stock and remeasure. Children mark the altered joint or replacement.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear children 19 to 24. Everyone indicates one unit's coverage in their practice picture.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E116, independent. Return on 117.

## Day 117

**Understanding.** I can explain why one length has two counts.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E116. Rehearse one missed joint or stock decision when routed.
- **10 min. Revisit.** Teacher names the length unit in a complete row. Pairs fold a spare strip exactly in half and align its two ends to check the halves.
- **25 min. Practice.** Use one size at a time on a runner. Mark the first count on a practice slip, clear the runner, then measure with the second stock. First five holds E116 repair.
- **10 min. Compare.** A runner takes ten small units or five doubles. Overlay one double on two small pieces to explain the count change and preserve the same endpoints. Children sketch that local match.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear children 25 to 30. Peers mark what stayed fixed.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E117a and E117b, separate independent slips. Return both on 118.

## 7. Talk and writing

Ask “What does one unit cover?” on 116 and “What stayed the same?” on 117. On 117 children sketch one match between a large unit and small units. Look for two small pieces meeting with the same endpoints as the large one.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix untimed within-ten facts with an earlier place-value idea. Each day use kindergarten 3.2's common starts and kindergarten 1.1's Partner turns: one child handles or counts, one watches, then switch. On 116 demonstrate the correct piece arrangement before a puppet contrasts it. The puppet routine from grade 1 section 1.1 is planned only, so show how it works. No owned standard sets a fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

On 115 show a length unit as one length to repeat and end to end as the ends touching. On 116 review overlap from kindergarten 3.2, where one piece covers part of another. Name uncovered space and call it a gap in a measurement. “Each ___ covers ___.” Read aloud and offer blunt, raised joints and large tabs; a scribe records exactly what a child directs. Extend by checking that two different starts for a row, each at an object endpoint and working inward, give the same measure.

## 10. Two rooms

One child measures a paper runner with large tabs and marks the joints. One projector magnifies a practice joint while everyone handles their own tabs. Demonstrate the joint even if some children have used strips in science.

## 11. Not in this section, and still to make
