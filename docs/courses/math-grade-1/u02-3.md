Grade 1 Math. Unit 2, Story problems (days 17 to 39): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 27 to 32.** Days by kind: understanding 1, fluency 1, application 4, check 0. A plan, not a lesson. The tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems” here with an unknown difference. Day 31 also returns to taught action and part-whole structures. Unit 6 completes story coverage.
- **Connection.** Use 1.MD.C.4 to read the day 29 display, up to three categories, with one mark per choice. Use 1.OA.B.4 on days 30 and 31 to link the same difference to addition and subtraction.
- **MP.4.** On day 27 keep both quantities visible while pairing. On day 31 trace the extra group to its place in both equations.
- **Teacher explanation.** Match exactly one object in each row per pair. The same unpaired objects answer how many more and how many fewer. Preserve group identities and start points. Spacing by itself tells nothing about how many.

## 3. Checks, written first

Follow [DEFAULTS.md](DEFAULTS.md). Print and say “invented for practice” on each fictional story or data card. Read each stem aloud once, with a repeat on request. All children mark individual paper. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Attach an error letter when the marks or words show that reasoning. Reserve every exit and check case and its number relationship across models, repairs, cards and practice.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Section 2.1 owns S. Keep the originals and written feedback. S30 is revised for four minutes on day 31 and returns for O. S31 is revised for five minutes on day 32.

- **S30a.** A pretend class has twelve brushes and eight pencils. Use one paper page with blank row guides. “How many more brushes are there?” Key: four extra brushes after eight pairs. Wrong: marks twelve as extra (E).
  Shape: Independently mark the first direction on the reserved page. Practice repairs supplied matches on other pictures.
- **S30b.** On the same page, “How many fewer pencils are there?” Key: four fewer, shown by the same extra brush marks. Wrong: gives twelve by circling the larger group (E).
  Shape: Reverse the comparison wording on the same page. Keep a two-direction independent page out of practice.
- **S30c.** “What addition shows that difference?” Key: 8 + 4 = 12 or equivalent with four identified. Wrong: 8 + 12 = 20, the brush count put in the difference place (E).
  Shape: Generate the addition relation from a child-made comparison row. Practice traces a supplied addition card.
- **S30d.** “What subtraction shows that difference?” Key: 12 − 8 = 4 or equivalent with four identified. Wrong: twelve rung as the difference beside 12 − 8, the larger count given as the answer (E).
  Shape: Generate the matching subtraction relation on the same page. Practice supplies one relation or points to the marks first.
- **S31a.** Lee has eleven flags and puts up three more. “How many flags are up now?” Key: 11 + 3 = 14, draw and circle fourteen as the result. Wrong: thirteen, counting eleven as a new step (H).
  Shape: Independent add-to result page. Practice acts a supplied move with a role label.
- **S31b.** Lee has sixteen flags and takes seven down. “How many flags are still up?” Key: 16 − 7 = 9, nine stay. Wrong: twenty-three after adding the seven (I).
  Shape: Independent take-from result page. Practice repairs a provided picture or moves paper pieces.
- **S31c.** Lee has eight flags and puts up some more. Now there are fourteen. “How many flags did Lee put up?” Key: 8 + 6 = 14, six join. Wrong: fourteen circled as the change (C).
  Shape: Independent add-to change page with a child-generated marking and equation. Keep the reserved flag set out of practice.
- **S31d.** Lee has seventeen flags and takes some down. Nine are still up. “How many flags came down?” Key: 17 − 8 = 9, eight leave. Wrong: nine circled as the change (C).
  Shape: Independent take-from change page. Use a visible action and a movable change tag for practice. Across S31, each paper page has space for the child's picture and equation showing the answer.

**Exit questions**

- **E27 (1.OA.A.1).** Print thirteen triangles and one square unpaired. “Which triangles have no partner?” Key: after one match, mark the other twelve triangles. Any one triangle may be paired to the square. Wrong: circles all thirteen (E).
  Shape: Independently find and mark unmatched marks on an unpaired print. Practice fixes a double match on a model.
- **E28 (1.OA.A.1).** In a pretend classroom there are eleven brushes and two covers. Give unlined drawing space. “What drawing shows how many more brushes?” Key: two labeled rows or groups, two pairs, nine brushes marked as extra. Pictured labels suffice. Wrong: eleven by circling all brushes (E), or ten by counting two through eleven as new steps (H).
  Shape: Generate a labeled matching drawing from speech. Practice repairs a supplied drawing with a misplaced label.
- **E29 (1.OA.A.1).** A pretend paint display shows twenty red choices, two blue choices and three green choices. “How many fewer blue choices than red choices?” Key: mark eighteen unmatched red choices after two red and blue pairs to show eighteen fewer. Wrong: rings all twenty red choices as the difference (E).
  Shape: Independently choose two of three columns and mark their gap. Practice exposes the two relevant columns or repairs captions.
- **E30 (1.OA.A.1).** Print eighteen paper leaves and ten paper flowers in matched rows with the extras unmarked. “What plus and minus equations show this difference?” Key: mark eight extra leaves, 10 + 8 = 18 and 18 − 10 = 8 (accept equivalent orientations). Wrong: eighteen as the difference (E), or nine by counting ten as the first new step (H).
  Shape: Generate both equations from a supplied matching drawing. Practice provides one relation and points to its marks before finding the other.
- **E31 (1.OA.A.1).** On the first card Nia has ten paper cups and adds ten. The second card shows twenty spoons and ten forks side by side. Give joining and comparing picture labels beside each card. Read both labels for each card. “Which label fits each picture?” Children put the selected label's mark by its pictured clue: the group that arrives, or the extras with no partner. Key: joining for Nia's cups, marked by the ten arriving cups; comparing for spoons and forks, marked by the ten extra spoons. Wrong: rings all twenty spoons as the comparing clue (E). Record unsupported label choices descriptively.
  Shape: Identify the action and the side-by-side comparison using a reusable label bank, with independent choices. Practice transforms a single scene or links a supplied equation to a structure.
- **E32 (1.OA.A.1).** A new game card shows twenty triangles and twelve circles. “What fact tells the difference?” Key: mark eight extras, with 12 + 8 = 20 or 20 − 12 = 8 or equivalent. Wrong: twenty after marking all triangles (E), or nine after including twelve as a new step (H).
  Shape: From a static game picture, mark the extra and generate its fact. Practice fixes pairing on movable towers or checks a supplied fact card.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Personal frames remain available as sketches take over. Launch matching rows on day 27 and tie the extra to written addition that day. Day 30 links the same marks to subtraction. Matching rows become optional on day 35.
- **Day 27 instrument.** Ideal: two aligned rows of identical-size pieces with room for a one-to-one link. Drawn row guides and paper pieces fit. Spacing changes can be tested using the same pieces.
- **Day 30 instrument.** Ideal: a gap visible alongside both equations. A matched drawing fits. Ringed extra marks remain the same whichever operation is read.
- **No-kit path.** Replace counters and cube towers with same-size patterned paper marks in rows or columns, and write cards and record sheets by hand. Keep a drawn ten-frame for individual access. Digital tool: None.

## 6. Days

Use fifteen simultaneous pairs, with both roles and at least one marked paper from each child. Each five-minute Synthesis hears six named children at 45 seconds each on the preceding work. Record names and help. Every child then marks the exit in parallel. Collect all papers and return feedback in the next named slot. A day can run with hand-drawn cards throughout.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 28: “Which marks have partners?” Day 30: “Where is the difference in both equations?” Mark a practice comparison by labeling both rows and ringing the extras. Look for one link per pair and both quantities preserved.

## 8. Practice that returns

Review its one-to-one matching with visible extras. Revisit partner checking daily and trace group labels from day 29.

- **Fluency target.** The owned standards set no fluency target. Day 32 is taught comparisons and small facts only, with partner feedback and no timing.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use the familiar more and fewer terms and teach difference as how many are extra after matching. Frame: “There are __ fewer __.” Offer large or tactile rows, picture labels, a sketch area or a child-directed scribe. Read paper labels aloud. Keep the independent grouping decision with the child. Extend by turning the same rows around and restating the difference.
