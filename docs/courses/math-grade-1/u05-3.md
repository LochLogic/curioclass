## 1. Standards and practices

**1.NBT.C.4.** "Add within 100," "relate the strategy to a written method" and "explain the reasoning used." Use two addends. Combine like units in either order or add on in parts. Use 1.NBT.B.2 on day 104 to account for each stick and single. At 100 the model shows ten tens and no loose ones. MP.3, day 106: use the pieces or a drawing to answer an objection to changing the order.

The teacher page explains why changing order keeps both addends, why the trade preserves value, and why an equals sign can only connect equal quantities. Write separate true steps when an intermediate amount differs from the total.

**E104, 1.NBT.C.4.** Show separate mats numbered 39 and 25 with one blank destination mat. "What would the joined groups look like on one mat?" The child's record shows what happens to the source pieces. Key: five existing tens and fourteen ones become six tens and four ones, 64. Trace exactly ten singles into the new stick. Wrong: 54 after discarding ten (A), or 74 after retaining the ten singles and their replacement (B).
Shape: produce the combined mat and show the exchange through marks. Practice checks or corrects a supplied final mat against physical pieces.

**E105, 1.NBT.C.4.** Read that one child combines like units and another starts at 46 and adds 28 in parts. Give one empty space for both paths. "How can your picture show why both ways work?" Key: 40 + 20 = 60, 6 + 8 = 14, 60 + 14 = 74. Also 46 + 20 = 66, then 66 + 8 = 74. Mark the same two tens and eight ones from 28 in both methods. The like-unit path combines tens and ones in separate groups, while adding on starts with all of 46. Wrong on like units: marks 64 by dropping ten (A). Wrong on adding on: moves two singles, 46 + 2 = 48 and 48 + 8 = 56, instead of adding two tens (C).
Shape: use one personally marked picture to trace both methods and show how the same units move in different orders. A shared answer gives no comparison evidence. Practice acts out methods with loose pieces and checks moves aloud, without a twin-path picture card.

**E106, 1.NBT.C.4.** Read the record for 57 + 16: 50 + 10 = 60, 7 + 6 = 13, 60 + 13 = 73. Give a blank picture space. "Where does the 13 go in your drawing?" Key: thirteen singles, ten becoming a marked stick that joins six old tens, three remaining loose. Wrong: marks six sticks and three singles, 63, after losing the full group (A), or seven sticks and thirteen loose ones, 83, after counting that group twice (B).
Shape: interpret and locate an intermediate amount from a stated record, with no supplied diagram. Practice argues about the order of physical moves. Copying 73 gives no evidence about the 13.

**E107, 1.NBT.C.4.** Give an empty mat for 85 + 15. "What number sentences show your way of putting these together?" Use the drawing to locate amounts in the record. Key: 80 + 10 = 90, 5 + 5 = 10, 90 + 10 = 100. Accept other true linked steps with ten tens and zero singles. Wrong: 85 + 15 = 90 = 100 (E), or nine sticks without singles, 90, after dropping ten (A).
Shape: generate a full endpoint record on an empty mat. Practice audits one step in a supplied record, including the endpoint example.

## Day 104

**Understanding.** I can add two two-digit numbers, including a new ten. Base-ten drawing (light), Model, show why, 10 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix known facts with a tens-and-ones picture.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E103, trace the trade.
- **10 min. Model.** Rebuild the columns. Join 27 and 35 physically. Draw the added strips and singles and compare the two contrasts.
- **25 min. Practice.** Check completed mats, including 25 + 35 = 60 with six tens. Each partner checks a different mat and marks one correction.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Point to the traded group in the finished picture.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E104. Children 7 to 12 explain one exchange.

## Day 105

**Understanding.** I can compare place-value methods. Base-ten drawing (light), Compare, show why, 20 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix known facts and a place-value idea.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E104, move the exchanged group once.
- **10 min. Model.** Reorder pieces in 75 + 18 = 93. Contrast adding two whole tens with adding two singles using C's practice case.
- **20 min. Compare.** Each pair has one set of pieces. Both partners take a turn with tens first, ones first and adding on. Allow three minutes per method per child, plus two to compare the resulting groups.
- **5 min. Center rehearsal.** Teach the choice of checking a caption or checking a physical trade.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Identify what stays when the moves are reordered.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E105. Children 13 to 18 explain one link between the paths. Everyone marks both paths.

## Day 106

**Application.** I can explain what each equation records. Base-ten drawing (light), Compare, practice with feedback, 20 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix known facts with a joining or parts picture.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E105, check both paths for kept tens and ones.
- **5 min. Launch.** Name Two methods and show the original pieces for 38 + 24. Take down shared worked drawings.
- **20 min. Compare.** Pairs physically move tens first and ones first in turn, with personal sketches as needed. Both arrive at six tens and two ones, 62.
- **10 min. Objections.** One partner asks whether moving the ones first changes the total. The other points to the same pieces and explains. Switch, with five minutes per direction including time to sketch.

## Day 107

**Application.** I can write equations showing my addition reasoning. Base-ten drawing (light), Practice, practice with feedback, 25 min.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

Use tens, ones, equal and method with visible pieces. "Both ways use __ tens and __ ones." Read prompts aloud. Offer raised cells, big paper pieces and neutral dictation or directed marks for practice and evidence. Extend by asking whether a different order works on the same pair, using the same units as proof.
