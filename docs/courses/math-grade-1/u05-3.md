## 1. Standards and practices

**1.NBT.C.4.** "Add within 100," "relate the strategy to a written method" and "explain the reasoning used." Use two addends. Combine like units in either order or add on in parts. Use 1.NBT.B.2 on day 104 to account for each stick and single. At 100 the model shows ten tens and no loose ones. MP.3, day 106: use the pieces or a drawing to answer an objection to changing the order.

The teacher page explains why changing order keeps both addends, why the trade preserves value, and why an equals sign can only connect equal quantities. Write separate true steps when an intermediate amount differs from the total.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 5.4 owns P1 to P5 and F. Use the named teaching cases for practice. Reserve the check pairs in either order and save each new retry for its day. Read every stem aloud. Everyone marks paper. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

**E104, 1.NBT.C.4.** Show separate mats labeled 29 and 57 and a blank mat for the total. "What would the two groups look like together?" Key: seven existing tens and sixteen loose ones become eight tens and six ones, 86. The child traces ten ones into the new stick. Wrong: 76 after discarding ten (A), or 96 after keeping the traded ten twice (B).
Shape: draw the joined groups and show which ten moved. Practice checks a supplied final mat against loose pieces. Data id: `s05-X104`.

**E105, 1.NBT.C.4.** Read that one child combines like units and another starts at 46 and adds 28 in parts. Give one empty space for both paths. "How did the two children use the same pieces in different ways?" Key: 40 + 20 = 60, 6 + 8 = 14, 60 + 14 = 74. Also 46 + 20 = 66, then 66 + 8 = 74. Mark the same two tens and eight ones from 28 in both methods. The like-unit path combines tens and ones in separate groups, while adding on starts with all of 46. Wrong on like units: marks 64 by dropping ten (A). Wrong on adding on: moves two singles, 46 + 2 = 48 and 48 + 8 = 56, instead of adding two tens (C).
Shape: trace both methods on one picture. Look for what stays the same and where each child starts. Practice acts out the moves with loose pieces. A shared total alone leaves the comparison open. Data id: `s05-X105`.

**E106, 1.NBT.C.4.** Read the three lines for 57 + 16 aloud: 50 + 10 = 60, 7 + 6 = 13, 60 + 13 = 73. Give a drawing space beside each line. "What is happening to the pieces in this child's number sentences?" Key: the first line joins six old tens, the second collects thirteen ones, and the last joins those amounts. Ten of the thirteen ones become the seventh ten, leaving three loose ones. Wrong: marks six sticks and three singles, 63, after dropping that ten (A), or seven sticks and thirteen singles, 83, after counting that ten twice (B).
Shape: match the amounts in a supplied record to one's own drawings. Practice puts number sentences to moves made with pieces. Copying the printed total gives no evidence about the steps. Data id: `s05-X106`.

**E107, 1.NBT.C.4.** Give an empty mat for 85 + 15. "What number sentences show your way of putting these together?" The child's record must show what came from 85 and what came from 15. Ten finished sticks alone do not show those steps. Key: 80 + 10 = 90, 5 + 5 = 10, 90 + 10 = 100. Accept other true linked steps with ten tens and zero singles. Wrong: 85 + 15 = 90 = 100 (E), or nine sticks without singles, 90, after dropping ten (A).
Shape: generate a full endpoint record on an empty mat. Practice audits one step in a supplied record, including the endpoint example. Data id: `s05-X107`.

## Day 105

**Understanding.** I can tell how two ways of adding use the same tens and ones. Base-ten drawing (light), Compare, show why, 17 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix known facts and a place-value idea.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E104, move the exchanged group once.
- **10 min. Model.** For 75 + 18, pool the units: 70 + 10 = 80, 5 + 8 = 13, 80 + 13 = 93. Then add on: start with all of 75, add the stick to reach 85 and the eight ones to reach 93. Say "add on" for this way. Point to the same stick and eight ones in both methods. Use C's contrast if the stick is counted as one.
- **17 min. Compare.** Each partner tries all three methods, two and a half minutes per method. Use the last two minutes to compare the starting groups and the totals.
- **5 min. Center rehearsal.** Teach the choice of checking a caption or checking a physical trade.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Identify what stays when the moves are reordered.
- **8 min. Exit ticket.** E105. Children 13 to 18 explain one link between the paths. Everyone marks both paths.

- **s05-X105, Shape:** Trace a like-unit method and an adding-on method on one record.
- In Compare, use tens first, ones first and adding on with the same pieces.

## Day 106

**Application.** I can tell what each number sentence shows about my pieces. Base-ten drawing (light), Compare, practice with feedback, 20 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix known facts with a joining or parts picture.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E105, check both paths for kept tens and ones.
- **5 min. Launch.** Name Two methods and show the original pieces for 38 + 24. Take down shared worked drawings.
- **20 min. Compare.** Partners build 38 + 24 in both orders. One records 30 + 20 = 50, 8 + 4 = 12, 50 + 12 = 62. The other starts with the twelve loose ones. Each points from the moves to the matching line in their own record. Switch.
- **10 min. Objections.** One partner asks whether moving the ones first changes the total. The other points to the same pieces and explains. Switch, with five minutes per direction including time to sketch.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Locate all twelve starting loose ones in the two methods.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E106. Children 19 to 24 explain where ten of the thirteen ones go.

- **s05-X106, Shape:** Translate a supplied three-line record into drawings, one for each line.

**Shared shape.** Exit turns a printed three-line method into the child's own drawings. Practice writes statements for moves already made with pieces.

## Day 107

**Application.** I can write number sentences that show how I added. Base-ten drawing (light), Practice, practice with feedback, 22 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix known facts and equality.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E106, recover or remove the extra group.
- **10 min. Model.** Audit the supplied steps for 64 + 36 in E's contrast. Use 64 + 30 = 94 and 94 + 6 = 100. Point to the four ones in 94 and the last six from 36 as they form the tenth stick. Clear the pieces and cover the record before E107.
- **22 min. Practice.** Use 65 + 12 = 77. Each partner supplies one move and writes the other's step beside a supplied picture. Before changing partners, have each child show on paper why their step works. A labeled arrow from pieces to an equation can carry the reason. Use 65 + 10 = 75, 75 + 2 = 77 as the check record.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Identify the amounts connected by an equals sign.
- **8 min. Exit ticket.** E107. Children 25 to 30 explain one equality.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 104, "Where is the traded group now?" Day 106, "Why do the methods agree?" Day 107, "Which amounts does this equals sign connect?" In day 107's caption audit, look for a written step that matches the units and has the same value on both sides.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix familiar within-ten addition and subtraction with the day's named review. Return to earlier like-unit grouping from section 5.1 and the marked exchange from section 5.2. No standard here sets a fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use tens, ones, equal and method with visible pieces. "Both ways use __ tens and __ ones." Read prompts aloud. Offer raised cells, big paper pieces and neutral dictation or directed marks for practice and evidence. Extend by asking whether a different order works on the same pair, using the same units as proof.

## 10. Two rooms
