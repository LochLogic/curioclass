Grade 1 Math. Unit 3, Adding and subtracting within 20 (days 40 to 68), [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 58 to 63.** Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 1, application 1, check 0. A plan, not a lesson. Tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.C.6.** “Add and subtract” within twenty. “Use strategies” to make a ten, use a known double or difference, remove to ten, remove from ten, and count up to subtract. Mental fluency is within ten.
- **1.OA.A.2.** “Solve word problems” with three whole-number addends and a symbol for an unknown total at most twenty. **1.OA.B.3.** “Apply properties” by choosing and regrouping a useful pair and preserving every group.
- **Connection.** 1.OA.C.5, “Relate counting” to addition and subtraction on day 63. The record keeps independent forward counting in S63. Day 63 establishes counting up as a choice alongside direct removal for this unit. S64 on day 64 supplies the first scored subtraction count-up in the unit.
- **Practice.** MP.8, day 59: one fewer in one addend and one more in the other preserve the total. Predict the next sum from the pattern.
- **Teacher explanation.** A mover changes groups with no change in the whole. A removal changes the whole exactly once per piece. To count up for a difference, hold the smaller number, make one mark per new count, and stop at the larger number.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read every question aloud. Everyone marks individual paper. Say and print “invented for practice” on fictional scenes. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Describe unsupported wrong numerals without an error letter.

- **E58 (1.OA.C.6, 1.OA.B.3).** Put seven plain buttons and four striped buttons in two unmarked groups on a picture for one total label. Give a ten-frame icon and a double icon, with room to show the corresponding move. “Which way would help you find the number for this label?” Key: mark either route with a valid move and record eleven. Move three from four beside seven and keep one, or split seven into four and three to show 4 + 4 + 3 = 11. Wrong: eight by copying the double of four without the extra three (H), or fourteen by keeping all four after moving three (C).  
  Shape: choose a method from an unworked pair and show why it suits the quantities. Practice compares partial methods already marked.
- **E59 (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6, MP.8).** Rin has eight, six and two paper shapes on three mats. A curved arrow shows one shape sliding from the first to the second mat. “Which groups would you add first to predict how many shapes Rin has after the slide?” Key: marks eight and two as a useful pair before the move, for (8 + 2) + 6 = 16, and tracks the mover, so 7 + 7 + 2 is still sixteen. Accept the two sevens first if the move and conserved total are visible. Wrong: stops at ten, leaving off six (D), or fourteen, leaving off two (D).  
  Shape: choose an order to predict a total across one pictured transfer. Practice groups trains and reads static number rows, with no slide card.
- **E60 (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6).** Rin's score slip shows four, two and six paper tiles in three turns. “How can one pair help you find Rin's whole score?” Key: ring four and six on the slip and mark (4 + 6) + 2 = 12 or an equivalent arrangement using the pair. Wrong: ten after omitting two (D), or eight after omitting four (D).  
  Shape: reconstruct one score from a pictured turn record. Practice uses familiar partner cards and no three-turn score slips.
- **E61 (1.OA.C.6).** Give a folded picture showing nineteen at the start and a removal target of eleven. The first move is hidden. After the fold, the drawing shows two further pieces to remove and a blank space for what remains. Read the cue “Two more to take.” Ask “How would you finish this take away record?” Key: infer nine removed to reach ten, mark two more removed and show eight remaining, 19 − 9 − 2 = 8. Wrong: takes eleven again from ten, crosses every remaining piece and still has one demanded (G).  
  Shape: recover a hidden first move and finish a reach-ten record. Practice uses visible moves and physically separated removal pieces.
- **E62 (1.OA.C.6).** Show a marked ten-strip beside four loose marks. “How can this ten help you show what stays when seven come off fourteen?” Key: mark seven total removals, either four and then three, 14 − 4 − 3 = 7, or seven from the ten, (10 − 7) + 4 = 7. Wrong: two-stage marks remove four and then a fresh seven, leaving three (G).  
  Shape: independently choose where to remove from a given ten-and-ones picture. Practice compares worked approaches on paper and traces a changed quantity.
- **E63 (1.OA.C.6, 1.OA.C.5).** Give eleven dots with nine already crossed out. Beside them show a held group of nine and an unmarked space for new counts. “How do the leftover dots match the counts up from nine?” Key: link the two dots one to one to the new counts ten and eleven, showing a difference of two. Wrong: adds a third count by counting nine as a new step (A).  
  Shape: connect two representations of the same difference using correspondence across pictures. Partners compare separate loose-piece and spoken methods, without a link diagram.

**Unit assessment item owned.** S63 in the day 68 [unit record](s03.md#5-end-of-unit-assessment-whole).

- **S63 (1.OA.C.5, 1.OA.C.6).** On day 63, “Show how to count on for twelve plus three.” Key: hold twelve, mark thirteen, fourteen and fifteen as the new counts, total fifteen. Wrong: counts twelve as one of the three steps and ends at fourteen (A). Written feedback after class and three minutes to revise on day 64.  
  Shape: independently build a forward-count record from a stated addend and three added units. Practice uses loose pieces to count up to a known endpoint for a difference.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

Show the correct case first. Each next teaching day's Practice return holds five minutes for the preceding exit. If more than a third miss, use the five for the most common repair and a fresh retry. With fewer, work with that group while peers retry on paper.

| Letter | Right and wrong together | Response |
|---|---|---|
| H | Day 58, 5 + 5 = 10. Add one to get 5 + 6 = 11. Contrast keeping ten. | Put one changed mark beside the known fact and retry. |
| C | Day 58, retrieve the day 56 move in 6 + 5. Move four and keep one for eleven. Contrast keeping all five for fifteen. | Track where each piece lands and retry. |
| D | Day 59, show nine plus three plus seven as nineteen. Contrast twelve with the last seven missing. | Touch all three groups once, then retry. |
| G | Day 61, eleven minus six removes one and then five, leaving five. Contrast removing one and then the whole six, leaving four. | Partition the removal into two labeled piles before moving and retry. |
| B | Day 62, one ten-strip and two ones mean twelve. Contrast counting the full strip as one and getting three. | Open the strip, touch its ten marks and retry. |
| A | Day 63, show three new counts from thirteen to sixteen: fourteen, fifteen, sixteen. Contrast recording thirteen as an extra fourth count. | Hold the starting group, mark one dot per new count and retry. Day 64 uses its separate S63 return for that retained item. |

## Day 63

**Understanding.** I can show how removing and counting up give the same difference. Children hold loose pieces and drawing slips.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix taught within-ten facts and revisit a known take away picture.
- **10 min. Model.** Pieces (light), show why. For 15 − 13, remove thirteen to see two left. On a reset picture, hold thirteen and say fourteen, fifteen while marking two new steps. Connect each to the added ones in 13 + 2 = 15. Use A's separate example.
- **6 min. Strategy folder.** S63 counts on for addition independently. Give written feedback after class.
- **19 min. Practice return.** First five for E62. For fourteen, partners compare removal and count-up methods on separate surfaces, then swap roles. Use loose pieces (light) for practice with feedback. Give no proficiency mark for today's first use of counting up to subtract.
- **10 min. Discuss.** Locate the stopping quantity on a supplied removal case and read the corresponding addition aloud to a partner.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear six show which number tells them to stop.

## 9. Supports and extension

Point to “start,” “difference” and “remain” as children work. Say that a count up makes one new mark per number said after the held start. Offer “I started at __. I stopped at __.” Read aloud, offer tactile outlines or large print, and allow children to point for neutral recording. Explain two ways to find the same difference within twenty.
