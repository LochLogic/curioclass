Grade 1 Math. Unit 3, Adding and subtracting within 20 (days 40 to 68), [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 58 to 63.** Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 1, application 1, check 0. A plan, not a lesson. Tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.C.6.** “Add and subtract” within twenty. “Use strategies” to make a ten, use a known double or difference, remove to ten, remove from ten, and count up to subtract. Mental fluency is within ten.
- **1.OA.A.2.** “Solve word problems” with three whole-number addends and a symbol for an unknown total at most twenty. **1.OA.B.3.** “Apply properties” by choosing and regrouping a useful pair and preserving every group.
- **Connection.** 1.OA.C.5, “Relate counting” to addition and subtraction on day 63. The record keeps independent forward counting in S63. Day 63 reviews unit 1's count-up method and extends it to teen differences. S64 on day 64 supplies the scored teen count-up.
- **Practice.** MP.8, day 59: one fewer in one addend and one more in the other preserve the total. Predict the next sum from the pattern.
- **Teacher explanation.** A mover changes groups with no change in the whole. A removal changes the whole exactly once per piece. To count up for a difference, hold the smaller number, make one mark per new count, and stop at the larger number.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read every question aloud. Everyone marks individual paper. Say and print “invented for practice” on fictional scenes. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Describe unsupported wrong numerals without an error letter.

- **E58 (1.OA.C.6, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.A.2).** Rin has six plain buttons, two striped ones and five dotted ones. Give one total label and two familiar picture cues, a frame and equal rows. “Which way helps you find how many buttons Rin has?” Key: thirteen. For a ten, move four dotted buttons to the six, leaving one dotted and two striped. For a double, move one striped button to the five, giving six and six with one left. Accept either choice with its marks. Wrong: moves four but still adds all five dotted buttons and both striped ones to ten, giving seventeen (C).  
  Shape: Choose between a ten and a double in an unworked three-group story. Practice compares two supplied records. Keep the three button groups and method-choice sheet out of practice.
- **E59 (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6, MP.8).** Rin has eight, six and two paper shapes on three mats. A curved arrow shows one shape sliding from the first to the second mat. “How could you add the groups to find Rin's total after the slide?” Children can point, circle or draw the groups they would add first. Key: the two sevens give fourteen, then two more give sixteen. Also accept eight and two before the slide, then six, if the picture shows that moving one did not change the total. Another valid order with all three groups also works. Wrong: stops at ten, leaving off six (D), or fourteen, leaving off two (D).  
  Shape: choose an order to predict a total across one pictured transfer. Practice groups trains and reads static number rows, with no slide card.
- **E60 (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6).** Rin's score slip shows four, two and six paper tiles in three turns. “How can one pair help you find Rin's whole score?” Key: ring four and six on the slip and mark (4 + 6) + 2 = 12 or an equivalent arrangement using the pair. Wrong: ten after omitting two (D), or eight after omitting four (D).  
  Shape: reconstruct one score from a pictured turn record. Practice uses familiar partner cards and no three-turn score slips.
- **E61 (1.OA.C.6).** Cover the first removal. Show ten dots on the mat and two answer boxes labeled Already gone and Left at the end. Read both labels. “Rin will take away 19 dots in all. Ten are on the mat now, and 10 still need to go. How many are already gone?” Key: 9. Nine gone and ten still to go make 19. Write 9 in the Already gone box. Wrong: Writes 10 in Already gone by copying the number of dots still on the mat (F).  
  Shape: Recover the covered removal from the whole removal and the part still to go. Practice begins with a visible start and physically separates what goes. Reserve the folded record.
- **E61-left (1.OA.C.6).** Same strip, read next. “Rin takes away the 10 on the mat. How many are left at the end?” Key: 0. Cross out each of the ten on the mat once and write 0 in the Left at the end box. Wrong: Tries to take all 19 again from the ten on the mat and runs out (G).  
  Shape: Show the second removal on the same strip, so the two parts make the whole 19. Practice begins with a visible start and physically separates what goes. Reserve the folded record.
- **E62 (1.OA.C.6).** Show a marked ten-strip beside four loose marks. “How can this ten show what stays when 7 come off 14?” Key: mark seven total removals, either four and then three, 14 − 4 − 3 = 7, or seven from the ten, (10 − 7) + 4 = 7. Wrong: two-stage marks remove four and then a fresh seven, leaving three (G).  
  Shape: independently choose where to remove from a given ten-and-ones picture. Practice compares worked approaches on paper and traces a changed quantity.
- **E63 (1.OA.C.6, 1.OA.C.5).** Give seventeen dots on one panel and a count-up strip starting at sixteen on the other. Read “Seventeen take away sixteen.” “How could both pictures show what is left?” Key: cross off sixteen dots, leaving one. Hold sixteen, mark seventeen as the one new count, and link it to the remaining dot. Wrong: marks two steps by counting sixteen itself (A).  
  Shape: Build both records and match the leftover to the new count. Practice compares two completed methods on separate surfaces. Reserve this pair of panels.

**Unit assessment item owned.** S63 in the day 68 [unit record](s03.md#5-end-of-unit-assessment-whole).

- **S63 (1.OA.C.5, 1.OA.C.6).** On day 63, “Show how to count on for twelve plus three.” Key: hold twelve, mark thirteen, fourteen and fifteen as the new counts, total fifteen. Wrong: counts twelve as one of the three steps and ends at fourteen (A). Written feedback after class and three minutes to revise on day 64.  
  Shape: independently build a forward-count record from a stated addend and three added units. Practice uses loose pieces to count up to a known endpoint for a difference.

Show the correct case first. Each next teaching day's Practice return holds five minutes for the preceding exit. If more than a third miss, use the five for the most common repair and a fresh retry. With fewer, work with that group while peers retry on paper.

## 6. Days

Fifteen pairs work at once and swap roles. Start this section with children 19 to 24. Move to the next six each day, returning to children 1 to 6 after 25 to 30. In Synthesis or Close, six turns of 45 seconds leave 30 seconds to switch. The other 24 mark their own answer to the same question. Record who was heard and any help. Check those marks after class and return them with each child's exit in the next named return. Use the child's next 45-second turn for any explanation still needed. A later answer has its own date. ## 9. Supports and extension

Point to “start,” “difference” and “remain” as children work. Say that a count up makes one new mark per number said after the held start. Offer “I started at __. I stopped at __.” Read aloud, offer tactile outlines or large print, and allow children to point for neutral recording. Explain two ways to find the same difference within twenty.
