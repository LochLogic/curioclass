# Section 6.4: Where does the unknown belong?

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-grade-1/6/s/6.4), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-grade-1 u06-4.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 1 Math. Unit 6, Length to 120 units (days 111 to 128), see [s06.md](s06.md). **Days 121 to 125.** Days by kind: understanding 1, application 4. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems.” Cover all three unknown positions in each story type. Children use the labels joining, take away, parts and comparing. For comparing, use both more and fewer with each of the three unknown quantities. Keep descriptive evidence for start unknown and opposite-language comparisons.
- **Connection.** On 125, 1.MD.A.1 “Order” and “compare” return in retained record L from section 6.1.
- **MP.4, day 122.** Match equal units first, then draw bars with aligned starts, quantity labels and the unknown. Check an equation against those quantities.
- **Teacher explanation.** A situation equation follows the story's quantities and places the unknown where it belongs. To find a removing story's start, a related addition can restore the amount taken away. In comparisons the words more and fewer tell how quantities relate. The unknown's place determines a useful operation. Use paper pieces, then drawings, then equations. All story quantities and answers stay within 20.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E124 and open the priority repair if needed. For I, locate the quantity that arrived. For J, mark the missing part inside the whole.
- **10 min. Model.** Eight is two fewer than ten. Match the bars and mark which length is larger. Then show a strip of twelve that is two longer than a covered strip. Cover the difference along the long bar to find the shorter length, ten. Tape diagram (light), show why, ten minutes.
- **15 min. Practice.** Children cover difference, larger and smaller in other matched models; use more for the larger amount and fewer for the smaller amount. Keep new opposite-language work descriptive. First five continues any E124 repair. Partners practice telling the relation both ways. In the last two minutes, children 1 to 16 hear the two L stems and begin their own drawings. The first speaker has a comparison to show.
- **16 min. Length record.** Hear children 1 to 8 and 9 to 16 in two eight-minute groups, one minute each, while each group draws L concurrently. Others use familiar practice. Give early L written feedback after class.
- **9 min. Revision.** Return S1 to S3 and S6 to S9 with written feedback. Keep before marks alongside revisions.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E125, independent. Give one minute to pair the units and mark the difference. Then hear 13 to 18 on the two directions, up to 30 seconds each plus 10 to note and move. Other children mark or dictate how the quantities relate. Return E125 and early L on 126.

**Shared shape.** Exit gives both directions of one relationship on unbracketed rows. Practice covers one label at a time on already matched models. The separate length record uses its own fixed spans.

- `s06-X125` How would you tell a partner this same difference using ‘more’ and ‘fewer’?
  - Shape: Pair two reserved rows and explain the same comparison in both directions. Practice solves one covered quantity at a time.
- `s06-AL-order` How would you arrange these from shortest to longest?
  - Shape: independently arrange three unsegmented strips and leave a trace. Reserve X/Y/Z together and the trace task. Practice orders classroom objects with a table edge and a partner.
- `s06-AL-compare` How does this strip tell you which is longer?
  - Shape: make two comparisons with one supplied fixed strip and explain the inference from one's own record. Practice carries one reference on other objects and gets partner feedback. Use new strips V and W, not X and Y from L-order, so the order item does not give the answer. Reserve V, W and the fixed strip and keep their lengths unprinted.

## 7. Talk and writing

Ask “What changed?” on 121 and “Which bar holds the unknown?” on 122. Children mark a bar and connect it to an equation. Look for aligned starts, relevant quantity labels and an unknown placed over the intended length.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix untimed within-ten facts with one earlier story meaning. On 121 reuse Rin from grade 1 section 2.1 in new single-actor scenes with classroom strips, without recalling the badge cases. On 122 use section 2.3's Check a supplied match. Both are planned only in the ledger, so demonstrate the action and correction. On 123 distinguish a situation equation from a related solution equation, using the purpose of each; grade 1 section 2.4 wording is planned only and gets a concrete demonstration. No owned standard sets a fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Review whole from kindergarten 5.1, every piece in the collection. Review unknown as the number to find and difference as how much more or fewer. Kindergarten's story problem row in 4.2 is planned only. Show a new number story before asking children to use the word. Name a tape diagram as bars that show amounts. Show a tag as a little paper label naming an amount or piece. Grade 1 section 4.1 has tag at planned only; demonstrate it here. “___ has ___ fewer than ___.” Read aloud, offer raised pieces or neutral dictation and keep directions accessible without naming the operation. Children mark their own paper. Extend by matching two true equations to one story, within 20.
