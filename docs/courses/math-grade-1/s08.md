## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do we know now about numbers and stories?"
- **Hook, day 151:** Show two familiar addition cards. Which feels easy now, and what helps with the other? Children mark a blank frame, then hear the compelling question. Adapt the public opening described in field 10.
- **Supporting questions:** How can addition help subtraction (152)? What does the unknown mean in this story (154)? How do both bars show the difference (156)? Can different clues describe the same number (158)?
- **Closing claim:** "I can test every clue and explain why my answer works." Riddle records R1 and R2 establish this on 160; the earlier records retain the year's number evidence.
- **Teaching:** Revisit one familiar strategy when the response record calls for it, then release practice. On 154 to 156, contrast situation and solution equations. On 158 model a tens exchange; on 159 step back while children test candidates. Teacher pages explain preserved quantity, unknown positions and why reversing subtraction changes its meaning.

<!-- skel -->
- **1.OA.C.6:** Add and subtract within 20, demonstrating fluency for addition and subtraction within 10. Use strategies such as counting on; making ten (e.g., 8 + 6 = 8 + 2 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14); decomposing a number leading to a ten (e.g., 13 – 4 = 13 – 3 – 1 = 10 – 1 = 9); using the relationship between addition and subtraction (e.g., knowing that 8 + 4 = 12, one knows 12 – 8 = 4); and creating equivalent but easier or known sums (e.g., adding 6 + 7 by creating the known equivalent 6 + 6 + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13). (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.B.3:** Apply properties of operations as strategies to add and subtract. Examples: If 8 + 3 = 11 is known, then 3 + 8 = 11 is also known. (Commutative property of addition.) To add 2 + 6 + 4, the second two numbers can be added to make a ten, so 2 + 6 + 4 = 2 + 10 = 12. (Associative property of addition.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.A.1:** Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems involving situations of adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing, with unknowns in all positions, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.A.1:** Count to 120, starting at any number less than 120. In this range, read and write numerals and represent a number of objects with a written numeral. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.B.2:** Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones. Understand the following as special cases: Parts, by verb: c (1.NBT.B.2c) The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones).

## U7. Talk and writing

## U8. Practice that returns

Mix both operations within 10 and one earlier story or place-value idea in every five-minute warm-up. Use short repeated games and feedback, without a timer. Keep all three review arcs even when the response record changes practice choices.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all text aloud. Pair whole, part, difference, tens, ones and clue with pictures. Offer "Both clues fit because __." Use large or tactile paper pieces, pointing, neutral dictation and high contrast. Extend by finding a second equivalent clue set, within 99; do not increase arithmetic difficulty.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, use thirty to forty minutes: shorten partner practice, keep the exit and preserve R1 and R2 feedback on later days. On 160 retain revision, closure and final response. One shared display may show prompts while every child marks paper; no device is required.
