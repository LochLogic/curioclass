## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.NBT.B.2.** "Understand" the value of a digit in each place. Work with quantities below 100, including unfilled places.
- **1.NBT.B.2b and B.2c.** Review that teens "are composed of" a ten and one to nine further ones, starting on day 74. Whole-ten numerals "refer to" their tens and zero ones.
- **MP.7, day 77.** Match each part of a sum to the pieces it counts. The teacher page must explain why changing from pieces to a word, drawing or expression preserves the amount and why the same digit has a different value when its position changes. Use a physically grouped quantity before its picture and numeral.

## 2. Where it sits

## 3. Checks, written first

Read aloud. Each child marks a private paper picture. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A bare wrong number needs working or a reason before it takes an error letter. Put "Invented for practice" on invented picture contexts and say it aloud. Hold each case, artwork, foil and response format away from models and cards.

**Unit assessment items owned:** field 5 rows 1 (P digit values) and 7 (P teen).

- **P digit values.** See row 1. 64 shows six tens worth 60 and four ones worth 4. Wrong: draws four tens and six ones after reversing the digits (B).
  Shape: independently draw the quantity from a bare numeral and annotate both contributions. Practice changes a partly built display physically.
- **P teen.** See row 7. Use a packing record labeled 17, with two singles already visible and room for other marks. Provide the 17 = 10 + __ line. The full drawing shows one ten and seven ones: draw the ten and five more singles, then use 7 on the line. Wrong: draws seven tens and one one (B).
  Shape: restore the missing parts and complete the equation on one partial record. Reserve the two visible singles, missing ten and missing five, and the feedback pictures. Keep kindergarten's seventeen-pennant partition and foldout and the unit 3 hidden bundle off this packet.

**E74, 1.NBT.B.2.** Scatter 43 pictured squares without rows or labels. "How can you show the tens and leftover ones here?" Key: group four tens and leave three singles clearly separate. Wrong: marks those groups as three tens and four ones (B); treats each full group and each single as one one, giving seven ones (A).
Shape: organize fixed loose marks into a readable grouping. Practice builds first from supplied quantities or compares existing arrangements.

**E75, 1.NBT.B.2.** Print 26 above two blank callout boxes, each linked to its digit. "What amount does each digit stand for?" Child marks each amount in its callout. Key: two tens worth 20 and six ones worth 6. Wrong: two ones and six tens (B); draws two rods but says they are worth two ones (A).
Shape: annotate both contributions of a bare numeral. Practice changes a physical build and updates a supplied partial label.

**E76, 1.NBT.B.2.** Show eight towers in two clusters and two singles at one side. "What number is missing from this collection's tag?" Key: 82, eight tens and two ones. Wrong: 28 from reversing the places (B); 10 from counting the towers and singles all as ones (A).
Shape: recover a missing whole label from an unfamiliar grouped display. Practice interprets the parts of records with totals already supplied by the teacher.

**E77, 1.NBT.B.2.** Show five marked ten-sticks and nine squares. "What addition expression tells what this drawing holds?" Mark the two quantities beneath the drawing with their sum expression. Key: 50 + 9 or 9 + 50, total 59. Wrong: 5 + 9, five rods counted as five ones (A); 90 + 5, nine singles moved to the tens place (B).
Shape: translate a fresh drawing to an unsupplied expression. Practice pairs pictures with already printed expressions or generates marks from a supplied numeral.

**E78, 1.NBT.B.2.** Use two parallel forms, one per child. Both ask, "Which label fits this amount?" Form A shows seven tens and four singles and offers 74, 47, 11. Form B shows an empty two-place mat and is read with "Six tens and one one," with labels 16, 7, 61. Mark the chosen label by linking its digits to the matching units or marks. Keys: A 74, B 61. Wrong on A: 47 from reversal (B), or 11 from counting the rods as ones (A). Wrong on B: 16 from reversal (B), or 7 from treating six tens as six ones (A).
Shape: recognize a label by positive quantity correspondence in a picture form or a heard form. Practice repairs labels in completed records with the pieces available and uses no choice bank.

## 6. Days

Fifteen pairs work at once with one turn each before swapping. At exit, six children have 45 seconds each alongside private paper marking, with 30 seconds to cue and collect. Record who was heard and help given. Return all thirty papers in the next Return and repair. Hear one reason or interpretation each.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 74 "Where do the leftover ones go?" Day 75 "When did that digit's value change?" On day 77, draw lines from the supplied expression to the two quantities it describes. Look for links to the whole-ten amount and the loose-ones amount.

## 8. Practice that returns

Use the named daily retrieval with a familiar within-ten warm-up fact. Days 74 to 76 revisit teens from unit 3, section 3.2, with the parts visible. No owned standard sets a fluency target. Day 78 uses taught writing and partner checking in untimed rounds; both children attempt before checking with a card.

## 9. Supports and extension

On day 75 show that a digit is one written mark in a number. The tens place tells how many tens; the ones place tells how many ones. Use the terms orally, with the printed words read aloud. Offer "The __ stands for __ tens." Read all number words aloud. Provide enlarged or tactile columns, numeral cards or neutral scribing at exits. Extend by keeping the same digit in the two different positions on two models and describing its value in each.

## 10. Two rooms

At home one partner moves a loose piece and the other checks the prepared tag. Use drawn strips and pencil marks. With one projector, show a practice build and keep each child's recording and exit on paper.
