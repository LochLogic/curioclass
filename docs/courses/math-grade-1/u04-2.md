Read with [the unit plan](s04.md) and [course defaults](DEFAULTS.md). Grade 1 Math, Unit 4, Numbers to 99. **Days 74 to 78.** Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.NBT.B.2.** "Understand" the value of a digit in each place. Work with quantities below 100, including unfilled places.
- **1.NBT.B.2b and B.2c.** Review that teens "are composed of" a ten and one to nine further ones, starting on day 74. Whole-ten numerals "refer to" their tens and zero ones.
- **MP.7, day 77.** Match each part of a sum to the pieces it counts. The teacher page must explain why changing from pieces to a word, drawing or expression preserves the amount and why the same digit has a different value when its position changes. Use a physically grouped quantity before its picture and numeral.

## 2. Where it sits

Receive E73 from section 4.1, then extend whole tens to quantities with leftover ones. E78 returns on day 79 before children change the tens while keeping the ones. The Place-value record gathers this section's evidence during days 89 to 91.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read aloud. Each child marks a private paper picture. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A bare wrong number needs working or a reason before it takes an error letter. Put "Invented for practice" on invented picture contexts and say it aloud. Keep the exit and packet numbers, pictures, choices and missing parts off the practice cards. For partner-built sets use the worked quantities in field 6. During repair use a practice example and return individual papers privately.

- **P digit values.** Show the numeral alone. Ask “How would you draw what each digit in sixty-four is worth?” Key: 64 shows six tens worth 60 and four ones worth 4. Wrong: draws four tens and six ones after reversing the digits (B).
  Shape: generate and mark both digit contributions in one drawing from a bare numeral. Data: s04-A89digit.
- **P teen.** On a packing record labeled 17, show a torn ten-stick with eight cells still visible and blank space beside it. “How would you finish this drawing of seventeen?” Key: restore two cells to the stick and draw seven loose ones. Then read the equation line separately. “What goes in the empty box in seventeen equals ten plus box?” Key: 7. Wrong: swaps the places, drawing seven tens and one one or putting 1 in the box for the loose ones (B). Accept another complete drawing with one ten and seven ones. Judge the drawing and equation separately.
  Shape: restore the ten and show the further ones in the drawing. Data: s04-A89teen.
  Shape: complete the separately read equation with the further ones. Data: s04-A89teen-equation.

When more than a third miss, repair one priority letter for five minutes and retry on a new case for five. ## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Review kindergarten u06-2 further ones, the ones beside those ten. Unit 3 section 3.2 Day 47 fourteen as a tower and four and Full frame to tower to drawn ten-stick are planned only. Rebuild a ten with spare pieces before using the drawings. Move from bundles to columns on day 75 and tie the children's written numerals to their grouped drawings on day 77. On day 78 give the child the first attempt before offering pieces as feedback.

Ideal: a mat where a digit sits directly above its unit column and each ten shows its ten parts. Paper columns, marked strips and large loose squares fit. No key tool or digital spec. Keep loose pieces out of mouths. During the last turn an adult counts them back in, or use drawn pieces throughout. Substitute drawn strips and dots for cubes, draw the mat on a scrap sheet, and cut or draw slips in place of reusable cards.

## 6. Days

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 74 "Where do the leftover ones go?" Day 75 "When did that digit's value change?" On day 77, draw lines from the supplied expression to the two quantities it describes. Look for links to the whole-ten amount and the loose-ones amount.

## 8. Practice that returns

In each five-minute retrieval, mix familiar within-ten facts with the named review. Include a known whole ten with zero loose ones in each day's matching work. Days 74 to 76 revisit teens from unit 3, section 3.2, with the parts visible. No owned standard sets a fluency target. Day 78 uses taught writing and partner checking in untimed rounds; both children attempt before checking with a card.

## 9. Supports and extension

On day 75 name each written mark from 0 through 9 a digit. For example, the first 2 in 22 stands for twenty and the second 2 stands for two. The tens place tells how many tens; the ones place tells how many ones. Use the terms orally, with the printed words read aloud. Revisit the word expression from kindergarten section 4.4 (its day 78) when the two parts are written as an addition. Offer "The __ stands for __ tens." Read all number words aloud. Provide enlarged or tactile columns, numeral cards or neutral scribing at exits. Extend by keeping the same digit in the two different positions on two models and describing its value in each.

## 10. Two rooms

At home one partner moves a loose piece and the other checks the prepared tag. Use drawn strips and pencil marks. With one projector, show a practice build and keep each child's recording and exit on paper.
