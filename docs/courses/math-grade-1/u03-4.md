# Section 3.4: Make a helpful pair and keep every part

Grade 1 Math. Unit 3, Adding and subtracting within 20 (days 40 to 68), [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 54 to 57.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 0, application 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson. Tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.A.2.** “Solve word problems” with three whole-number addends, totals at most twenty and a box for the unknown total.
- **1.OA.B.3.** “Apply properties” by choosing an order and a pair that help. Grouping and moving addends preserve every piece. Model circling and then brackets for the first pair before children mark one. Keep the property names on the teacher page.
- **1.OA.C.6.** “Use strategies” to make a ten within twenty. “Demonstrate fluency” within ten through accurate recall or a mental method, without time pressure.
- **1.NBT.B.2a and 1.NBT.B.2b.** Recover the day 53 evidence that ten “can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones” and teens “are composed of a ten” and further ones.
- **Practices.** MP.4 on day 54, link each group of objects to a drawn group and its term. MP.7 on day 55, use a pair of ten to find the sum and trace each group in the rearrangement.
- **Teacher explanation.** Pairing is a choice about where to start adding. A split moves only part of an addend to the ten. Taking away starts with the quantity named first, so switching the numbers can change what the action can do.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read each question aloud. Every child marks an individual picture for each exit. Say and print “invented for practice” on fictional scenes. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Use letters only when the marks show the error.

- **E54 (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6).** Rin lays out eight, three and two paper leaves on separate pictured mats for a display. Give a blank sum strip beside the three mats. “What sum would you write to find all Rin's leaves using a pair that makes ten?” Key: mark the eight and two as the first pair and include the three in the sum, as in (8 + 2) + 3 = 13. Accept the pair in either order. Wrong: ten after omitting three (D), or eleven after omitting two (D).  
  Shape: read separate mats and create a total equation with a helpful pair from scratch. Practice joins trains physically and repairs a supplied group label.
- **E55 (1.OA.A.2, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.C.6).** Give mats with four, five and six paper stars and a blank rearrangement mat with one large space and one small space. “How would you move these stars to make a ten and keep the whole group?” Key: send four and six to the large space, five to the other, and show fifteen with the marks or the sum (4 + 6) + 5 = 15. Wrong: shows only ten by leaving off five (D), or only eleven by leaving off four (D).  
  Shape: produce a conserving rearrangement from a before picture and show the useful order. Practice matches each movable bundle across two supplied arrangements.
- **E56 (1.OA.C.6, 1.OA.B.3, 1.OA.A.2).** Rin has two trays with ten spaces each. One holds six dots, the other eight. There are six loose dots. “How could Rin split the loose dots to fill both trays?” Key: put four in the first and two in the second. The filled trays hold twenty altogether. Wrong: sends four to the first tray and still tries to put all six in the second (C).  
  Shape: Infer one split from two different gaps. Practice traces one completed transfer. Reserve both unfinished trays and the loose group.

**Unit assessment items owned.** P54, P55, M2 and the second roster half of M1. See [unit field 5](s03.md#5-end-of-unit-assessment-whole) for full items, look-fors and samples. Day 57 has no separate exit.

- **T53a, retained from section 3.3.** Ten pictured ones equal one ten. Wrong: labels the stick as one one (B).  
  Shape: revise the original many-ones-to-one-ten drawing, with its date. Use a prebuilt strip for practice.
- **T53b, retained from section 3.3.** Eleven is one ten and one further one, 10 + 1 = 11. Wrong: counts the stick as one, giving two (B).  
  Shape: return to the original spoken-numeral-to-drawing page. Practice reads supplied teen pictures.
- **P54.** Give four round marks and five striped ones. “Why do five and four make the same total as four and five?” Key: the same two groups appear in either order, so both show nine. Accept arrows mapping every piece across the swap. Wrong: gets eight after counting the held five as the first of the four new steps (A).  
  Shape: explain a specified reversal with the original groups, on a dedicated slip. Practice handles three groups on trains.
- **P55.** Read “six plus three plus four.” “How could you put a ten first?” Key: (6 + 4) + 3 = 13, with every group shown. Wrong: ten after dropping three (D).  
  Shape: select an order from a bare three-number expression and record the groups. Model joined trains and supplied quantities.
- **M1.** Privately read each fact and ask “How many?” without objects or supplied steps. In order: 4 + 4 = 8, 0 + 5 = 5, 6 + 1 = 7, 9 − 4 = 5, 5 − 0 = 5 and 7 − 7 = 0. A wrong numeral alone gets a descriptive note.  
  Shape: for each of the six brief asks, answer a bare fact mentally, then mark a blank paper panel. Keep these cases and the observation strip out of centers and practice decks. Paper revisions leave the oral record intact.
- **M2.** On the blank make-ten slip read “nine plus six.” Ask “How could a ten help you find the sum?” Key: move one and retain five, 9 + 1 + 5 = 10 + 5 = 15. Wrong: leaves all six beside ten for sixteen (C).  
  Shape: construct the whole make-ten record, picture and equation, on an unworked slip. Practice traces already completed moves.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Keep the day 47 ten as one unit. On day 54, trace grouped cubes into drawings and then into the sum. On day 56, connect a moved part and its leftover to equivalent expressions. Section 3.5 withdraws the shared double frame on day 58.
- **Day 54, join three groups.** Ideal instrument: three visibly distinct trains whose pieces remain countable after joining. Connecting cubes and paper trains (light) fit. Use linked trains during Model for show why and during Practice return for practice with feedback.
- **Day 56, split a group.** Ideal instrument: a double ten-frame and loose counters so the transferred part remains visible. Paper (light) fits for show why during Model and for practice with feedback during Practice return. Use the check papers unaided. No key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Use folded ten-mark paper strips in place of towers, scrap paper squares in place of cubes or counters, pencil grids in place of frames, and hand-drawn picture slips.

## 6. Days

Fifteen pairs work at once and swap roles. Start this section with children 25 to 30. Move to the next six each day, returning to children 1 to 6 after 25 to 30. In Synthesis or Close, six turns of 45 seconds leave 30 seconds to switch. The other 24 mark their own answer to the same question. Record who was heard and any help. Check those marks after class and return them with each child's exit in the next named return. Use the child's next 45-second turn for any explanation still needed. A later answer has its own date. In each teaching day's five-minute retrieval, use a familiar addition and subtraction alongside the named earlier idea. Draw from worked practice only. Keep field 3 cards and their number combinations out of models, repairs and decks. Digital tool: None.

## Day 54

**Application.** I can use a helpful pair in a three-group story. Children hold three paper or cube trains and a drawing mat.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix taught within-ten facts and read a displayed teen drawing.
- **4 min. T53 return.** Revise the dated two parts for two minutes each, using written feedback.
- **4 min. Properties folder.** P54 independently. Write feedback after class.
- **10 min. Model.** Paper trains (light), show why. In an invented scene Rin puts out five round clips, five striped clips and three square tags. Join the two fives, then add three for thirteen. Draw the five, five and three. Circle the first two groups and write (5 + 5) + 3 = □. Say “Add the two inside this mark first.” Fill the box with thirteen.
- **12 min. Practice return.** First five for E53, using field 4 B as needed. In seven minutes, use trains (light) for practice with feedback. Fix a supplied misplaced group label, then mark where that group's term belongs.
- **15 min. Mental observation.** M1 roster 16 to 30, one minute per child. Budget about 30 seconds for six brief responses, 15 for a strategy note and 15 for recording and changeover. Move on gently if evidence stays incomplete. Follow the printed M1 order. Ask about one method per sign as time allows. Record only what the child shows. Children mark their own blank panels at their seats afterward, with a folded copy of the facts, including the first minute of Synthesis for the last child. Hear that child's roster turn later in the same five minutes. Peers independently trace group labels on the just-taught paper task. Give written M1 feedback after class.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear six trace a term to the group it names.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E54 on paper.

## 9. Supports and extension

Name a “helpful pair” as two groups that are easy to add first. Point to the pair, say “move” and “remain,” and offer “I moved __. __ stayed.” Read all text aloud. Offer raised outlines or large shapes and a neutral scribe. The observation stays mental before paper marks. Extend by finding two useful first pairs for a sum within twenty and showing both totals.

## 10. Two rooms

At home, use five minutes for retrieval, fifteen for work or the dated revisions, five for discussion and five for the exit or the retained slips. One child needs one brief M1 turn across days 53 and 54. With one projector, display practice cases while children move or mark their own paper.
