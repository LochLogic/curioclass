## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do the two digits of a number tell us?"
- **Hook, day 69:** Offer forty loose paper squares. How could someone count them without starting over? Ask the compelling question. Field 10 names the opening source.
- **Supporting questions:** What does one tower count (day 70)? Which digit changes with another ten (day 79)? Which place decides a comparison (day 82)? Can different bundles show equal amounts (day 88)?
- **Closing claim:** "I can explain digits, compare quantities, change a number by ten mentally within 99, and subtract whole tens." The Place-value record and Fresh traces establish this on day 92.
- **Teaching:** Model unitizing beside numerals; contrast teen and decade words. Step back on days 85 and 88. Teacher pages explain conserved quantity, zero ones and why tens decide before ones.


- **Practices:** MP.7, day 72: Tens removal links crossed-out towers to equations, including zero differences. MP.6, day 85: Order the cards requires reading each symbol statement aloud and identifying its decisive digit.
- **Fluency and balance:** No owned standard sets a fluency target; 1.NBT.C.5 requires mental reasoning. Keep thirteen understanding, seven fluency, three application and one check day. Fluency days practice taught routines with feedback and retries, without time pressure.
- **Focus:** One major-work unit, 100 percent; 23 of 23 teaching lessons, 100 percent; 1,300 of 1,300 instructional minutes, 100 percent. Exclude day 92, sixty observation minutes and twenty unit 3 response minutes. No supporting cluster is assigned.
- **Limits:** Quantities stay below 100; ten-more prompts start below 90. Ten more from starting numbers 90 to 99 waits for unit 6 (field 3). Subtraction removes whole tens from whole tens, including zero differences. No other two-digit subtraction, three-digit extension, number line or standard algorithm. Unit 5 owns general addition within 100. Day 79 adds one or two whole tens to a two-digit number whose total stays below 100, as repeated ten more, to build 1.NBT.C.5; the method is practice only here and is assessed in unit 5 (1.NBT.C.4).
- **Carried:** Keep unit 3 towers and ten-sticks. Tie towers to numerals on day 69, drawings to subtraction equations on day 72, and ones to written numerals on day 77. Withdraw blocks for mental work on day 80; drawings follow the mental answer. Remove shared models on day 85; restore them for exchanges on day 87, then withdraw shared help again on day 89. Individual models remain available except during mental evidence. Pass towers and drawings to unit 5. 1.NBT.B.2a is practiced on days 69 and 87 (ten ones as one ten, exchanging a ten); 1.NBT.B.2b on days 74 to 76 (a teen as a ten and ones); both were first taught in unit 3, days 47 to 51.

**Collection and scoring.** Reserve these combinations from examples, games and exits. The Place-value record is made once per child, in three groups of about ten, a third of the class each: with thirty children, children 1 to 10 on day 89, 11 to 20 on day 90, and 21 to 30 on day 91. Each group has ten minutes to begin its individual paper packet, followed by twenty minutes of teacher rotation while paper work continues. Each packet needs twenty working minutes, including the first ten; early finishers practice other numbers. Within each twenty-minute rotation hear all seven explanations from each child, about two minutes per child. Planning estimates are fifteen seconds for general digit values, ten each for a ten, a teen and a decade, twenty-five for comparisons, twenty for mental changes and thirty for subtraction. A part the child already shows clearly on paper is heard in one sentence. Do not hurry a child; record any unheard component as missing.

Read all prompts aloud. Supply blank drawing mats and the stated pictures, never completed solutions. The mental number is given only at the individual turn, with objects and completed drawings covered. Record answers and reasoning before the child draws a confirming picture. Give written feedback after each production day; groups revise on the next day for five minutes, including day 92 for the last group. Score these earlier-day products on day 92, after revision. Retain the original mental observation; supported revision cannot replace it. Nothing here is first taught on its production day.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 72, "What unit did you remove?" On day 85, "Which place decides?" On day 88, "What changed without changing the total?" Judge the Place-value record with field 5's look-fors.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve mixed within-10 addition and subtraction for five minutes daily, with one earlier story or taught place-value idea. Use unit 3's ten-frame only to repair arithmetic. Keep taught partner routines throughout fluency days; no races or timed sheets. Unit 4's observation records mental-ten reasoning, not arithmetic fluency.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all prompts and number words aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair ten, ones, digit and equal with drawings. Offer "The tens are __; the ones are __." Use shape as well as color, enlarged tactile mats, movable numeral cards and neutral dictation. Assessment help preserves mental independence. Extend on the same standards by finding several equal tens-and-ones forms below 100 and explaining why each is equal.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, use five minutes retrieval, twenty modeling and paper work, five discussion and five exit. Hear one complete closing observation, not three repetitions; keep later written feedback and revision before day 92 scoring. Day 92 retains both fresh traces. With one projector, show a picture while children work on paper; no device is required.
