## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do the two digits of a number tell us?"
- **Hook, day 69:** Offer forty loose paper squares. How could someone count them without starting over? Ask the compelling question. Field 10 names the opening source.
- **Supporting questions:** What does one tower count (day 70)? Which digit changes with another ten (day 79)? Which place decides a comparison (day 82)? Can different bundles show equal amounts (day 88)?
- **Closing claim:** "I can explain digits, compare quantities, change a number by ten mentally within 99, and subtract whole tens." The Place-value record and Fresh traces establish this on day 92.
- **Teaching:** Model unitizing beside numerals; contrast teen and decade words. Step back on days 85 and 88. Teacher pages explain conserved quantity, zero ones and why tens decide before ones.

<!-- skel -->
- **1.NBT.B.2:** Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones. Understand the following as special cases: Parts, by verb: a (1.NBT.B.2a) 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones - called a “ten.”; b (1.NBT.B.2b) The numbers from 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones.; c (1.NBT.B.2c) The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones).
- **1.NBT.C.6:** Subtract multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 from multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 (positive or zero differences), using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method and explain the reasoning used. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.C.5:** Given a two-digit number, mentally find 10 more or 10 less than the number, without having to count; explain the reasoning used. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.B.3:** Compare two two-digit numbers based on meanings of the tens and ones digits, recording the results of comparisons with the symbols >, =, and <. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve mixed within-10 addition and subtraction for five minutes daily, with one earlier story or taught place-value idea. Use unit 3's ten-frame only to repair arithmetic. ## U9. Supports and extension

Read all prompts and number words aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair ten, ones, digit and equal with drawings. Offer "The tens are __; the ones are __." Use shape as well as color, enlarged tactile mats, movable numeral cards and neutral dictation. Assessment help preserves mental independence. Extend on the same standards by finding several equal tens-and-ones forms below 100 and explaining why each is equal.
