## 1. Standards and practices

**1.NBT.C.4.** "Add within 100," "relate the strategy to a written method," "explain the reasoning used" and "compose a ten." Add a one-digit number to a two-digit number, reaching or crossing a decade. Revisit no-exchange additions, including whole tens. Use 1.NBT.B.2 on day 100 to show the value of the new stick. MP.7, day 102: use the ones total to decide whether to exchange before counting the whole amount.

The teacher page shows ten visible ones becoming a single unit worth ten. The circled group and its replacement represent the same pieces. Splitting the added ones changes how they are grouped and preserves their total.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Keep section 5.4's P1 to P5 and F cases and reversals out of practice, models and repairs. Each child marks a paper picture for every item. Read aloud. Use one response region per item. Record shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Read any judged exit filed as practice and carry any changed data stem back here verbatim. Unit 6 holds 47 + 6 and 43 + 8.

**E100, 1.NBT.C.4.** Give 37 + 6 with a blank mat. "What does 37 plus 6 look like after a trade?" The single drawing traces the added ones, circles the available ten and shows the replacement. Key: thirteen ones, one new ten and three loose ones, four tens and three ones in all, 43. Wrong: 33 after discarding the circled ten (A), or 16 from reading the three old sticks as singles along with thirteen loose ones (D).
Shape: independently generate the complete exchange trace on blank paper. Practice moves real pieces through supplied frame stages.

**E101, 1.NBT.C.4.** Give a 69 mat with one loose square shown starting to move from a group of four. Read that first move as a statement. "How does this move help you show 69 plus 4?" Children annotate the partial picture and show true steps in the same record. Key: 69 + 1 = 70, then 70 + 3 = 73. The new stick accounts for the moved one and the nine already there. Wrong: 63 after losing those ten (A), or 83 after retaining their ten singles alongside the new strip (B).
Shape: reconstruct the split and finish the record from a started move. Practice matches whole diagrams to already completed records.

**E102a, 1.NBT.C.4.** Show five loose ones next to four marked sticks, and an adjoining area for four added ones. "Will 45 plus 4 need a new ten?" Mark the prediction before testing. Keep it visible as the child annotates the picture with the supporting count in the same panel. Key: no, 5 + 4 = 9 ones, 45 + 4 = 49. Wrong: yes, counts the old sticks as four more singles and uses thirteen ones (D).
Shape: commit to a prediction before marking evidence for a below-ten case. Practice uses movable pieces and varying starting numbers; avoid two fixed parallel predictions.

**E102b, 1.NBT.C.4.** Reveal the next panel separately, swapping in five added ones. "Will 45 plus 5 need a new ten?" Keep this panel's initial prediction and its eventual proof visible. Key: yes, 5 + 5 = 10 ones, 45 + 5 = 50. Wrong: marks 40 after discarding ten ones (A), or 60 from keeping them as well as the new stick (B).
Shape: give an independent prediction about the boundary on the same start, then annotate its proof. Practice checks different starts physically and uses no fixed same-start pair. Both panels go to everyone. Keep the retired 49-square scatter out.

**E103, 1.NBT.C.4.** Read 72 + 9. Give three unlabeled pictures showing seven tens and one one, eight tens and one one, and nine tens and one one. "Which picture can you mark to show how you added?" Label the total and show the join and trade on the chosen picture. Key: 2 + 9 = 11 ones, one new ten and one loose one, 81. Wrong: 71 after dropping ten (A), or 91 from counting the ten twice (B).
Shape: solve and annotate an unlabeled picture choice with the exchange link. Movement cards use a peer check and no picture choices. A selection alone gives no method evidence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Review kindergarten section 6.2 day 108 **ten ones** and **further ones**. Keep every one visible before making the trade. Grade 1 section 3.2's frame, tower and drawn stick, and section 4.5's trade and twenty-two case, are planned only. Demonstrate with separate marks before using the strategy.

Ideal instrument: two drawn ten-frames, each with ten visible cells, beside a marked ten-stick whose cells match the traded group. The frames and base-ten drawings (light) fit day 100's concrete-to-equation link. Use existing tens outside the frames and put only loose ones into the frames. Withdraw shared frames on day 101 and retain children's own diagrams. No key tool is needed.

Adult-precut strips and large square paper pieces replace cubes. Draw cells, columns, cards or marks if supplies are absent. Keep loose pieces out of mouths and count them back in during the last practice turn.

## Day 100

**Understanding.** I can make a new ten when adding ones. Double ten-frame (light), Model, show why, 10 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Familiar facts and a place-value picture.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E99, label units or split an equality. Restore the shared towers.
- **10 min. Model.** Build 26 + 8. Put the six loose ones and eight added ones across two frames. Say and point to the label "make a new ten" as ten ones become one stick. Draw the trade and record 26 + 4 = 30, then 30 + 4 = 34.
- **20 min. Practice.** Both partners move pieces and verify supplied stages. Show A and D.
- **5 min. Explain.** Children point to the group that becomes the new stick in their own drawing.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Trace the same ten once on the practice mat.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E100. Children 13 to 18 explain one trade.

## Day 102

**Understanding.** I can predict when the ones make a ten. Base-ten drawing (light), Practice, practice with feedback, 25 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Familiar facts and one earlier story label.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E101, move the same group once.
- **10 min. Model.** Use loose pieces to contrast 32 + 6 = 38, 26 + 4 = 30 and 44 + 8 = 52. Reveal the ones groups first.
- **25 min. Practice.** Physically sort cases with different starts by whether they fill a group of ten. Each partner predicts on a different case and then checks the other's with pieces. Rehearse known card roles in the final five minutes.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Revisit what happens when the ten is exactly full.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E102a and E102b. Children 25 to 30 explain only the exact-ten panel. Everyone marks both.

## Day 103

**Fluency.** I can practice the additions I know. Base-ten drawing (light), Rounds, practice with feedback, 25 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Familiar facts and a place-value idea.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E102a and E102b, confirm the threshold and correct the recorded units.
- **5 min. Recall.** Revisit day 102's roles and demonstrate the planned-only partner rounds of section 3.5 again.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 100, "Where did the new ten come from?" Day 102, "What happens if the loose ones will just fill a ten?" Day 101, mark a completed record's trade and point from a true equation to the new stick. Look for that group accounted for once.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix familiar within-ten addition and subtraction with the day's named retrieval. Use kindergarten section 1.1 **Partner turns**: one child handles or counts, one watches, then exchange roles in simultaneous pairs. On day 100 rebuild section 4.5's planned-only twenty-two example with a marked rod before applying the trade to addition. No standard here sets a fluency target. Day 103 keeps the same untimed try, check and retry sequence.

## 9. Supports and extension

Show exchange, ones and bundle beside a picture. "These ten ones become __. The amount stays __." Enlarge or raise cell lines, read all prompts aloud and use neutral scribing or a directed mark. Extend with two different additions reaching the same total, one with an exchange and one without, using the taught moves.

## 10. Two rooms

Use 25 minutes at a table, with the grown-up taking one checking turn and the child making all paper marks. A single screen can show the practice trade. Draw two empty grids of ten when frames are missing.
