The teacher page shows ten visible ones becoming a single unit worth ten. The circled group and its replacement represent the same pieces. Splitting the added ones changes how they are grouped and preserves their total.

**E100, 1.NBT.C.4.** Give 37 + 6 with a blank mat. "What does 37 plus 6 look like after a trade?" The single drawing traces the added ones, circles the available ten and shows the replacement. Key: thirteen ones, one new ten and three loose ones, four tens and three ones in all, 43. Wrong: 33 after discarding the circled ten (A), or 16 from reading the three old sticks as singles along with thirteen loose ones (D).
Shape: independently generate the complete exchange trace on blank paper. Practice moves real pieces through supplied frame stages. Data id: `s05-X100`.

**E101, 1.NBT.C.4.** Show 39 with one loose square starting to move from a group of nine. "How does this move help you show 39 plus 9?" Key: 39 + 1 = 40, then 40 + 8 = 48. Nine old ones and the moved one form a ten. The other eight are still to be added. Wrong: 38 after losing the filled ten (A), or 58 after keeping its ten singles beside its new stick (B).
Shape: reconstruct an addend from a started drawing and record the rest. Practice follows a partner's whole physical move. Keep partly started split pictures off practice cards. Data id: `s05-X101`.

**E102a, 1.NBT.C.4.** Show a 45 start picture and four separate ones. Keep the starting pieces covered until the prediction is marked. "Will 45 plus 4 need a new ten?" Mark the prediction before testing. Keep it visible as the child annotates the picture with the supporting count in the same panel. Key: no, 5 + 4 = 9 ones, 45 + 4 = 49. Wrong: yes, counts the old sticks as four more singles and uses thirteen ones (D).
Shape: commit to a prediction before marking evidence for a below-ten case. Practice uses movable pieces and varying starting numbers; avoid two fixed parallel predictions. Data id: `s05-X102a`.

**E102b, 1.NBT.C.4.** Reveal the next panel separately, swapping in five added ones. "Will 45 plus 5 need a new ten?" Save the first yes or no before the child marks the pieces. Key: yes, 5 + 5 = 10 ones, 45 + 5 = 50. Wrong: "No. I crossed out the ten loose ones, so I still have just four tens," with 40 shown (A). A child who predicts yes but draws 60 by keeping the ten loose ones and the new stick has a correct prediction and a counting error (B).
Shape: predict at exactly ten from the same start, then mark the proof. Practice tests different starts with movable pieces. Everyone gets both panels. Keep the earlier 49-square scatter out. Data id: `s05-X102b`.

**E103, 1.NBT.C.4.** Read 72 + 9. Offer a blank drawing space or a starter mat showing only the nine new ones. "How did you put 72 and 9 together?" Key: 2 + 9 = 11 ones. One new ten joins seven tens, leaving one loose one, 81. Accept a split such as 72 + 8 = 80, 80 + 1 = 81. Wrong: 71 after dropping the traded ten (A), or 91 after counting it twice (B).
Shape: choose a starting picture and make the missing steps. Neither choice shows the result. Practice checks a partner's card with pieces. Data id: `s05-X103`.

## Day 100

**Understanding.** I can make a new ten when adding ones. Double ten-frame and base-ten drawing (light), Model, show why, 10 min together.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Familiar facts and a place-value picture.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E99. Use labeled units for D or separate true equations for E. Everyone needing the retry gets 83 + 6 = 89, saved from section 5.1. Restore shared towers.
- **10 min. Model.** Build 26 + 8. Put the six loose ones and eight added ones across two frames. Say and point to the label "make a new ten" as ten ones become one stick. Say "trade" for this move: ten loose ones go and one stick comes, the same amount, like unit 4's exchange. Draw the trade and record 26 + 4 = 30, then 30 + 4 = 34.
- **20 min. Practice.** Both partners move pieces and verify supplied stages. Show A and D.
- **5 min. Explain.** Children point to the group that becomes the new stick in their own drawing.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Return to day 96's question. Each partner says "make a new ten" and points to the ten singles that became one stick. The amount stayed the same.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E100. Children 13 to 18 explain one trade.

- **s05-X100, Shape:** Generate an entire exchange trace on a blank mat.
- In Model, reconnect the earlier make-ten idea to the two frames before adding the loose ones.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 100, "Where did the new ten come from?" Day 102, "What happens if the loose ones will just fill a ten?" On day 101, label a partner's trade with a number sentence. Look for those ten ones accounted for once in the writing.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix familiar within-ten addition and subtraction with the day's named retrieval. Use kindergarten section 1.1 **Partner turns**: one child handles or counts, one watches, then exchange roles in simultaneous pairs. On day 100 rebuild section 4.5's planned-only twenty-two example with a marked rod before applying the trade to addition. No standard here sets a fluency target. Day 103 keeps the same untimed try, check and retry sequence.

## 9. Supports and extension

Show exchange, ones and bundle beside a picture. "These ten ones become __. The amount stays __." Enlarge or raise cell lines, read all prompts aloud and use neutral scribing or a directed mark. Extend with two different additions reaching the same total, one with an exchange and one without, using the taught moves.

## 10. Two rooms

Use 25 minutes at a table, with the grown-up taking one checking turn and the child making all paper marks. A single screen can show the practice trade. Draw two empty grids of ten when frames are missing.
