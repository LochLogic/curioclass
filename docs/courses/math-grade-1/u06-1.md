# Section 6.1: Compare lengths from the same start

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-grade-1/6/s/6.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-grade-1 u06-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 1 Math. Unit 6, Length to 120 units (days 111 to 128), see [s06.md](s06.md). **Days 111 to 114.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.MD.A.1.** “Order” three objects by length and “compare” two indirectly using a third. MP.5, day 113: choose a reference that stays the same length while it moves.
- **Connections.** Day 114 maintains 1.NBT.C.4 “Add within 100” and “relate the strategy to a written method,” and 1.OA.D.7 “determine” whether equations are true or false within 20. Keep the unit 5 and unit 2 evidence separate.
- **Strategies and limits.** Align one end. For separate objects use one unchanged reference, either matching an object or falling between the two lengths. On 114 put like units together and compose a new ten where needed. Use concrete pieces before drawing and writing. Compare the quantities on both sides of an equation.
- **Teacher explanation.** A shifted start changes where a tip falls. A common start makes the farther tip evidence of greater length. In an indirect comparison, both judgments refer to the same span.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned.** Length record L on 125 and 126, [unit field 5](s06.md). L and exit IDs below are planned Data ids for the data job. Read each quoted stem aloud. Every child marks a paper picture. Keep first marks independent and attach any later explanation separately.

- **E111 (s06-X111), 1.MD.A.1.** Give movable, unsegmented pictures P and R, four and six equal hidden lengths. Print R with its start at 0, P at 3, so P's tip initially lies farther right. Provide a blank baseline. “Which strip is longer when you line them up?” Key: align starts in the paper record, mark R. Wrong: P marked from the original far tips (A).
  - Shape: reconstruct a common start from two misleading frames and mark the farther tip. Practice orders three physical objects against a table edge. Keep P and R and their offsets out of practice. Data id: s06-X111.
- **E112 (s06-X112), 1.MD.A.1.** Show two framed impressions of the same rigid strip, aligned with each card at its own start. Use hidden lengths P = 5, reference = 9 and Q = 12. Offset P's frame ten of those lengths to the right so its far tip still looks farther right. “Which card is longer?” Child circles the card and can trace the two aligned tips as evidence of that judgment. Key: Q, since P is shorter than the fixed strip and Q is longer than it. Wrong: P because its tip is farther right on the page (A). “Same” after changing the reference length to fit each card (B).
  - Shape: infer from two fixed traces at different positions on paper. Practice carries a reference to physical objects. Reserve both impressions. Data id: s06-X112.
- **E113 (s06-X113), 1.MD.A.1.** Draw J and K six and ten equal hidden lengths in separate frames. Give each child a pencil. Set out a stiff blank tab longer than either card and a loose loop of yarn. “How could you use one of these to tell which card is longer?” Have the child leave the marks or trace that supports the comparison. Accept yarn held at two fixed marks without stretching. Key: one unchanged span matching J, matching K, or strictly between their lengths, with the starts aligned when applied. The marks must show K is longer: K passes a span equal to J, J falls short of a span equal to K, or the span falls between them. Wrong: two different spans made by moving the second mark between cards (B).
  - Shape: choose a reference and use its marks to settle which card is longer. Practice tests already marked references and notices which evidence they give. Reserve this exact pair and the blank reference for the exit. Keep J and K in separate places. Data id: s06-X113.
- **E114a (s06-X114a), 1.NBT.C.4.** Supply a blank picture mat. “How would your drawing help someone check 47 + 6?” Key: seven and six ones make thirteen. Trade ten ones for a new ten, so four old tens, one new ten and three ones give 53. Show which seven ones belonged to 47 and which six arrived. For example, move three of the six to 47 to reach 50, then add the other three for 53. A linked trade also works. A copied five-tens, three-ones picture alone leaves the method open. Wrong: 17 after using four ones for 40 (K). 54 after counting one incoming piece twice (G).
  - Shape: generate an exchange and link it to writing. Practice checks supplied captions. Reserve both source groups and the four old tens beside thirteen ones. Data id: s06-X114a.
- **E114b (s06-X114b), 1.OA.D.7.** Set 2 + 6 + 9 = 18 − 3 + 2 above a blank mat. Read each side aloud. “Do both sides show the same amount?” Key: yes. The left side makes eight and then seventeen. The right starts at eighteen, drops to fifteen and ends at seventeen. Accept counters, marks or two written steps that account for each complete side. Wrong: “False, 17 should come next” (L).
  - Shape: judge a previously unseen complete equality from both whole quantities. Practice repairs a supplied false chain and tests other expressions. Reserve the two complete sides as a pair. Data id: s06-X114b.

**L on 125 and 126, 1.MD.A.1.** Use identical-width, unnumbered strips X, Y and Z of seven, three and five units. Label only their names. Before ordering, place X, Y and Z with starts at zero, seven and three of the hidden units, respectively. Their tips fall at seven, ten and eight, respectively. For L-compare use two new strips, V and W, of eight and four units, and a fixed strip of six, so the order answer does not settle the comparison. All children work on their own mats simultaneously. Each has one private minute including a single spoken reason, with the other evidence on paper.

- **L-order (s06-AL-order).** “How would you arrange these from shortest to longest?” Key: aligned starts, Y, Z, X. Wrong: X first because its staggered far tip lies farthest left (A).
  - Shape: independently arrange three unsegmented strips and leave a trace. Reserve X/Y/Z together and the trace task. Practice orders classroom objects with a table edge and a partner. Data id: s06-AL-order.
- **L-compare (s06-AL-compare).** With V and W separate, give the fixed strip. “How does this strip tell you which is longer?” Key: V passes the fixed strip, which passes W. A meeting reason is “V goes past this strip, and W stops before it.” Wrong: “They're equal” after trimming the reference separately for each target (B).
  - Shape: make two comparisons with one supplied fixed strip and explain the inference from one's own record. Practice carries one reference on other objects and gets partner feedback. Use new strips V and W, not X and Y from L-order, so the order item does not give the answer. Reserve V, W and the fixed strip and keep their lengths unprinted. Data id: s06-AL-compare.

## Day 111

**Understanding.** I can line up ends to compare lengths.

- **5 min. Hook.** Three staggered classroom objects. Children move one object or point to a start. Ask “How can we say how long something is?”
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Align two objects of different widths and mark the one that reaches farther. Mix familiar facts.
- **10 min. Model.** Teacher points to a label, a picture or word that names something, then aligns the paper label and envelope at the near end, draws the line and contrasts the two tip positions. Give pairs a paper sketch.
- **20 min. Practice.** Fifteen pairs order three classroom objects. Mover and checker switch after each round. Review order as putting things in a row by length. Name the shortest and longest after lining up the starts. Have partners tell how the lengths compare using longer, shorter or the same.
- **10 min. Centers.** Return unit 5 originals, F, closure marks and private feedback slip. Teach up to two five-minute priority groups. Within the ten minutes hear names 19 to 24 on one familiar link. Six turns of 30 seconds plus 10 to move and log take four minutes. Peers use paired paper cues during the turns. Keep outstanding P5 links open for later private explanation.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Everyone marks a common-start practice sketch. Check all the sketches. The six private center turns were on unit 5. A common-start sketch gives paper evidence for this day. Date an unheard child's later explanation separately.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E111, independent. Return on 112.

## Day 114

**Fluency.** I can check sums and tell when two amounts are equal.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E113. Rehearse 8 = 10 − 2. Point to each side of the equals sign and show that both have the same amount, eight.
- **5 min. Recall.** Return marked unit 5 drawings. If B is widespread, use this and the first five of Practice for the fixed-reference repair.
- **30 min. Practice.** Have pairs use the check records for 22 + 5 = 27, 52 + 30 = 82, 24 + 13 = 37 and 26 + 8 = 34. Review an equation as a number sentence with an equals sign and the same amount on each side. In 26 + 8, say that in a trade ten loose ones go and one stick comes, the same amount. ## 7. Talk and writing

These standards set no new fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Review longer and shorter from kindergarten 3.2. Name an end as where a strip starts or stops. Of three strips, the shortest has the least length and the longest has the most. “This extends past ___.” Offer raised starts, large paper tabs and neutral dictation. Read all stems aloud. Extend by asking whether a reference longer than both objects always settles the comparison, with paper examples.

## 10. Two rooms

One child carries a precut strip between two table edges and leaves both traces. Use one projector to magnify practice ends. Children keep their own exit pieces.
