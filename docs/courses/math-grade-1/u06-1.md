# Section 6.1: Compare lengths from the same start

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-grade-1/6/s/6.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-grade-1 u06-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 1 Math. Unit 6, Length to 120 units (days 111 to 128), see [s06.md](s06.md). **Days 111 to 114.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.MD.A.1.** “Order” three objects by length and “compare” two indirectly using a third. MP.5, day 113: choose a reference that stays the same length while it moves.
- **Connections.** Day 114 maintains 1.NBT.C.4 “Add within 100” and “relate the strategy to a written method,” and 1.OA.D.7 “determine” whether equations are true or false within 20. Keep the unit 5 and unit 2 evidence separate.
- **Strategies and limits.** Align one end. For separate objects use one unchanged reference, either matching an object or falling between the two lengths. On 114 put like units together and compose a new ten where needed. Use concrete pieces before drawing and writing. Compare the quantities on both sides of an equation.
- **Teacher explanation.** A shifted start changes where a tip falls. A common start makes the farther tip evidence of greater length. In an indirect comparison, both judgments refer to the same span.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned.** Length record L on 125 and 126, [unit field 5](s06.md). L and exit IDs below are planned Data ids for the data job. Read each quoted stem aloud. Every child marks a paper picture. Keep first marks independent and attach any later explanation separately.

- **E111 (s06-X111), 1.MD.A.1.** Give movable, unsegmented pictures P and R, four and six equal hidden lengths. Print R with its start at 0, P at 3, so P's tip initially lies farther right. Provide a blank baseline. “Which strip is longer when you line them up?” Key: align starts in the paper record, mark R. Wrong: P marked from the original far tips (A).
  - Shape: reconstruct a common start from two misleading frames and mark the farther tip. Practice orders three physical objects against a table edge. Keep P and R and their offsets out of practice. Data id: s06-X111.
- **E112 (s06-X112), 1.MD.A.1.** Show two framed impressions of the same rigid strip, aligned with each card at its own start. Use hidden lengths P = 5, reference = 9 and Q = 12. Offset P's frame ten of those lengths to the right so its far tip still looks farther right. “Which card is longer?” Child circles the card and can trace the two aligned tips as evidence of that judgment. Key: Q, since P is shorter than the fixed strip and Q is longer than it. Wrong: P because its tip is farther right on the page (A). “Same” after changing the reference length to fit each card (B).
  - Shape: infer from two fixed traces at different positions on paper. Practice carries a reference to physical objects. Reserve both impressions. Data id: s06-X112.
- **E113 (s06-X113), 1.MD.A.1.** Draw J and K six and ten equal hidden lengths in separate frames. Supply equal-looking blank paper cards and pencil. “Which marks could you carry from one card to the other?” On paper mark the two ends of a reference span. Key: one unchanged span matching J, matching K, or strictly between their lengths, with the starts aligned when applied. A span equal to J shows K passes the second mark. Wrong: two different spans made by moving the second mark between cards (B).
  - Shape: recognize and preserve a useful span by marking a portable record. Practice tests already marked references and notices which evidence they give. Hold both card lengths together and the blank reference. Data id: s06-X113.
- **E114a (s06-X114a), 1.NBT.C.4.** Supply a blank picture mat. “How would your drawing help someone check 47 + 6?” Key: seven and six ones make thirteen. Trade ten ones for a new ten, so four old tens, one new ten and three ones give 53. Match the picture to a written step such as 40 + 13 = 53. Wrong: 17 after using four ones for 40 (K). 54 after counting one incoming piece twice (G).
  - Shape: generate an exchange and link it to writing. Practice checks supplied captions. Reserve both source groups and the four old tens beside thirteen ones. Data id: s06-X114a.
- **E114b (s06-X114b), 1.OA.D.7.** Set 8 + 5 = 14 − 1 above a blank picture mat. “Do both sides show the same amount?” Key: yes, thirteen on each side. A child can mark both quantities on the mat. Wrong: “False, 13 should come next” (L).
  - Shape: judge a previously unseen complete equality from both whole quantities. Practice repairs a supplied false chain and tests other expressions. Reserve the two complete sides as a pair. Data id: s06-X114b.

**L on 125 and 126, 1.MD.A.1.** Use identical-width, unnumbered strips X, Y and Z of seven, three and five units. Label only their names. Before ordering, place X, Y and Z with starts at zero, seven and three of the hidden units, respectively. Their tips fall at seven, ten and eight, respectively. All children work on their own mats simultaneously. Each has one private minute including a single spoken reason, with the other evidence on paper.

- **L-order (s06-AL-order).** “How would you arrange these from shortest to longest?” Key: aligned starts, Y, Z, X. Wrong: X first because its staggered far tip lies farthest left (A).
  - Shape: independently arrange three unsegmented strips and leave a trace. Reserve X/Y/Z together and the trace task. Practice orders classroom objects with a table edge and a partner. Data id: s06-AL-order.
- **L-compare (s06-AL-compare).** With X and Y separate, give a fourth strip equal to Z. “How does this strip tell you which is longer?” Key: X passes the fixed fourth strip, which passes Y. A meeting reason is “X goes past this strip, and Y stops before it.” Wrong: “They're equal” after trimming the reference separately for each target (B).
  - Shape: make two comparisons with one supplied fourth strip and explain the inference from one's own record. Practice carries one reference on other objects and gets partner feedback. Reserve the exact four strips and keep their lengths unprinted. Data id: s06-AL-compare.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Carry the common-start line from 111 to 112, then let children choose the fixed length on 113. On 114 bring back children's marked unit 5 tens-and-ones drawings. Keep the 58-plus-17 packing record and 48-plus-5 drawing closed in the private folder during practice and exits. The 48-plus-5 drawing would show the same merged ones as E114a. Use other marked drawings and carry them to 120. Put shared examples away for each exit.

A rigid strip that keeps two marks fits the indirect-comparison job. Precut paper replaces taut string and makes the endpoints traceable. Number cards can be handwritten. The base-ten drawing (light) fits the day 114 feedback job by showing which ten changed form. Bundled paper strips and loose squares replace connecting cubes. Paper pieces and a pencil give a path for every task. Adults cut pieces and keep long strips on tables.

## Day 111

**Understanding.** I can line up ends to compare lengths.

- **5 min. Hook.** Three staggered classroom objects. Children move one object or point to a start. Ask “How can we say how long something is?”
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Align two objects of different widths and mark the one that reaches farther. Mix familiar facts.
- **10 min. Model.** Teacher aligns a label and envelope at the near end, draws the line and contrasts the two tip positions. Give pairs a paper sketch.
- **20 min. Practice.** Fifteen pairs order three classroom objects. Mover and checker switch after each round. Name a comparison as a statement about which is longer, shorter or the same.
- **10 min. Centers.** Return unit 5 originals, F, closure marks and private feedback slip. Teach up to two five-minute priority groups. Within their turns hear names 19 to 24 on one familiar link, allowing 30 seconds and 10 to note each. Others use paired paper cues. Keep outstanding P5 links open for later private explanation.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Everyone marks a common-start practice sketch. Log which of names 19 to 24 were heard in Centers and carry absences.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E111, independent. Return on 112.

## Day 112

**Understanding.** I can use one strip to compare two separate lengths.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E111 privately and use the common-start cue as needed.
- **10 min. Model.** Teacher aligns the same fixed reference with a shelf edge and a tray edge in separate places. Sketch each end relation. Either use a span between the two lengths or copy one of them first.
- **25 min. Practice.** Pairs carry a marked reference between table stations and find items longer than a desk edge. Keep the first ten available for alignment and width repair. On a class E111 repair use five at opening and five here for the main move. If it differs from the retrieval repair, use five more practice minutes for it.
- **10 min. Centers.** Five for one unit 5 priority move, five for individual retry and a private link. Unit 5 A/B: 76 + 8 = 84. C: 32 + 40 = 72. D: 93 + 4 = 97. E: 65 + 19 = 84. While all work, hear up to six short links at 30 seconds plus 10 to log per name. Carry unresolved links.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear names 25 to 30 on the fixed reference. Peers trace the fixed span. Keep these observation notes separate from unit 5 center links.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E112, independent. Return on 113.

## Day 113

**Understanding.** I can choose something to carry a length.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E112 and mark a fixed span in the repair if needed.
- **10 min. Model.** Test an already marked stiff tab and a slack string. Show the effect of a moved mark, then keep the corrected reference fixed.
- **25 min. Practice.** Partners test supplied references on separate shelves. Some references reach past both and need replacing. Release the decision to the children and switch handlers. First five holds the E112 repair if routed.
- **10 min. Rehearsal.** Use handwritten cards to recall checking and retrying a familiar addition caption before day 114.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear names 1 to 6 choosing a useful span. Others mark which supplied span stayed fixed.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E113, independent. Return on 114.

## Day 114

**Fluency.** I can check sums and tell when two amounts are equal.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Return E113. Rehearse 8 = 10 − 2 by marking eight on both sides.
- **5 min. Recall.** Return marked unit 5 drawings. If B is widespread, use this and the first five of Practice for the fixed-reference repair.
- **30 min. Practice.** In pairs check supplied captions 22 + 5 = 27, 52 + 30 = 82, 24 + 13 = 37 and 26 + 8 = 34. Base-ten drawing (light), practice with feedback, up to 30 minutes. Use bundled strips or drawn tens and dots. Keep marked drawings for 120.
- **10 min. Retry.** Match complete sides using pictures. Use addition and subtraction within 20, a numeral on either side and then expressions on both sides. Repair a false chain and retry with a fresh complete equality.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear names 7 to 12 on one caption or one equality. Peers mark a matching picture.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E114a and E114b on separate slips, independent. Return on 115.

## 7. Talk and writing

Ask “Where must the ends meet?” on 111 and “What stayed fixed?” on 112. On 113 children trace a supplied reference that worked and show why its two existing marks stayed fixed. Look for a common start and one span held unchanged.

## 8. Practice that returns

Use untimed mixed facts within 10 and an earlier story or place-value idea in retrieval. Days 111 to 113 use kindergarten 3.2's Aligned side strips and corner overlay for common starts and kindergarten 1.1's Partner turns: one child handles or counts, one watches, then switch. Day 114 uses the book inventory label audit from grade 1 section 5.4 and equality work from 2.4. The 26 + 8 trade recalls section 5.2. Those ledger entries are planned only. Show a practice caption and rebuild equal quantities before independent turns. Use the private filing routine of 5.4, planned only, with a sample folder first. These standards set no new fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Review longer and shorter from kindergarten 3.2. Name end as where a strip starts or stops and comparison as what a statement tells about two lengths. “This extends past ___.” Offer raised starts, large paper tabs and neutral dictation. Read all stems aloud. Extend by asking whether a reference longer than both objects always settles the comparison, with paper examples.

## 10. Two rooms

One child carries a precut strip between two table edges and leaves both traces. Use one projector to magnify practice ends. Children keep their own exit pieces.
