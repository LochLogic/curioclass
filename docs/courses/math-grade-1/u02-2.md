Grade 1 Math. Unit 2, Story problems (days 17 to 39): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 22 to 26.** Days by kind: understanding 1, fluency 1, application 3, check 0. A plan, not a lesson. The tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems” with the whole, one part or both parts missing. Zero can be a part. Unit 6 completes story coverage.
- **1.OA.B.4.** “Understand subtraction as an unknown-addend problem.” On day 24, the pieces left after taking away a known part are the same pieces that rebuild the whole when added back.
- **1.OA.D.7.** “Understand” the equal sign in both orientations (days 22 and 23). **1.OA.D.8.** “Determine” the unknown whole number (day 24).
- **Connection.** 1.OA.C.5 on days 22 and 24: use forward counts to combine or rebuild. MP.4 on day 25: show two parts inside one boundary and mark the quantity the story asks for.
- **Teacher explanation.** A part-whole story describes groups already inside the collection. The whole stays fixed during a split. Reversing addends retains both parts, while reversing equality retains the quantities on its two sides.

## 3. Checks, written first

Follow [DEFAULTS.md](DEFAULTS.md). Print and say “invented for practice” on each fictional story or data card. Read each stem aloud once, with a repeat on request. All children mark individual paper. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Attach an error letter when the marks or words show that reasoning. Reserve every exit and check case and its number relationship across models, repairs, cards and practice.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Section 2.1 owns S. Make its first dated contribution on day 25. Read one ask per page. All children mark paper. Retain the original, give written feedback, and revise in day 26's five minutes.

- **S25a.** Six round paper tiles and five square paper tiles are on a mat. “How many tiles are on the mat?” Use marks to show the total. Key: eleven, 6 + 5 = 11 or equivalent. Wrong: ten, counting six first (H).
  Shape: Independently supply a whole from the two pictured groups. Keep these tiles and numbers out of the gathered and outlined practice mats.
- **S25b.** Twelve paper shapes include seven circles. The rest are triangles. “How many triangles are there?” Use one marked picture and an equation. Key: five, 7 + 5 = 12 or 12 − 7 = 5. Wrong: nineteen triangles from adding twelve and seven (D).
  Shape: Independently show the whole-minus-known group by category. Practice uses covers and supplied outlines, with different facts.
- **S25c.** Eight paper shapes belong to two groups. “How could you split this group?” Key: any two nonnegative parts totaling eight, such as four and four or eight and zero. Wrong: six and four (G).
  Shape: Reserve the open split on this booklet page. Practice moves one piece at a time between marked trays with a different total.

**Exit questions**

- **E22 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.D.7).** An imagined tank has five striped fish and four spotted fish. Provide a paper tank and an empty equation line. “What equation shows the whole tank?” Children mark groups on the tank picture and record equality. Key: nine, 5 + 4 = 9, 4 + 5 = 9 or either with sides reversed. Wrong: eight, counting five as the first new step (H).
  Shape: Generate a whole and equation from two described groups. Practice traces the boundary of a pictured whole or rearranges supplied labels.
- **E23 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.D.7).** There are ten fish in another imagined tank. Each is striped or spotted. Give a tank outline with room for marks. “What two parts could make this whole?” Key: any one striped and spotted assignment totaling ten, including a zero part. Six striped and four spotted is one valid split. Wrong: five and six (G).
  Shape: Invent a labeled split for a fresh whole. Practice moves a piece from one part of a supplied split to the other.
- **E24 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.B.4).** Thirteen paper shapes are circles or triangles. Four are circles. Print the four circles and a vacant space for triangles, with the whole marked as thirteen. “How many triangles belong here?” Key: nine triangles, since 4 + 9 = 13. Wrong: seventeen from adding the known whole and part (D), or ten marks from counting four as the first added step (H).
  Shape: Infer the unpictured category in a static collection. Practice uncovers or rebuilds a collection with pieces already present.
- **E25 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.B.4).** Fourteen paper shapes include five circles. The rest are squares. “Which drawing shows this story?” Children indicate their choice by marking the known group on one mat. Provide three equally sized mats. One shows five circles and nine squares together. One shows the same fourteen shapes in a before panel with five circles leaving in an after panel. One shows five circles in a mat with nineteen squares. Key: mark the first drawing and identify the five known circles inside its unchanged whole of fourteen. Wrong: chooses the nineteen-square drawing after adding fourteen and five (D).
  Shape: Recognize one static whole with both categories inside it. Practice annotates an already-chosen drawing. Shuffle position across copies and keep all three art styles alike.
- **E26 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.B.4).** A card shows two of nine counters beside a paper cover and the printed relation 9 − 2 = □. “What addition shows the covered part?” Key: 2 + 7 = 9 or equivalent with seven marked as hidden. Wrong: eleven hidden after adding nine and two (D), or eight hidden after including two as the first step (H).
  Shape: Generate an addition equation from a printed subtraction relation on a static picture. Practice starts with a spoken fact and checks by moving pieces back, using different counts.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Children use the ten-frame from section 2.1 and review Two labeled part mats from kindergarten u05-1. Every piece goes in exactly one part of the same whole. On day 22 connect the two parts to a total in an equation. On day 24 tie the hidden part to both operations. From day 25 children can use a labeled sketch. Keep personal frames for access. Withdraw the shared frame on day 33.
- **Day 22 instrument.** Ideal: one boundary with room for two different patterns. A marked paper mat fits. Its outline holds both groups in view when the labels move.
- **Day 24 instrument.** Ideal: a whole with one part concealed and a way to verify it. Paper counters and a folded cover fit. The same pieces reappear when the cover lifts.
- **No-kit path.** Use drawn frames, patterned paper squares instead of counters, a folded sheet as the cover, and handwritten cards or recording sheets. Digital tool: None.

## 6. Days

Use fifteen simultaneous pairs, with both roles and at least one marked paper from each child. Each five-minute Synthesis hears six named children at 45 seconds each on the preceding work. Record names and help. Every child then marks the exit in parallel. Collect all papers and return feedback in the next named slot. A day can run with hand-drawn cards throughout.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 23: “What stayed the same?” Day 24: “Where is the part you know?” On day 24 outline the whole and mark the visible part on a sketch. Look for the correct known part and the hidden part within one fixed total.

## 9. Supports and extension

Read each stem and picture label aloud. Trace each part and the whole, using “The whole is __. I see __.” Offer tactile patterns, large boundaries and child-directed marking or dictation on paper. A child may use two different color or texture marks for the parts. Record help. Extend the fixed-total split by explaining what changes when one part loses one piece.
