## 1. Standards and practices

**1.NBT.C.4.** "Add within 100," "relate the strategy to a written method" and "explain the reasoning used." Show two-digit plus one-digit, two-digit plus whole tens, and two-digit pairs, with and without an exchange where possible. Use two addends and show 100 as ten tens. Use 1.NBT.B.2 on day 108 to identify the formed unit. MP.7, day 109: match the parts of a book inventory to the contribution of each place.

The teacher page distinguishes finding a sum, representing the quantities and explaining the written steps. Accept place value, properties or a valid related addition/subtraction method. The closing window uses methods already taught.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment item owned:** field 5's day 110 row, P1 to P5 and F. Read each prompt aloud. On the working page give each child space to use a picture, a written method and a reason together. Arrows, labels or neutral dictation can show why a step works. Judge each of those links from the child's own work. On P1 and P4 the total is 57 in both cases. Check which tens and ones came from each addend on each sheet. Save all six pairs, their reversals and the completed records for assessment.

**E108, 1.NBT.C.4.** Show just the number cards 49 and 23. "How did you put these two groups together?" Give blank paper for the child's first move and the finish. One solution moves one of the 23 to 49: 49 + 1 = 50, 23 = 1 + 22, 50 + 22 = 72. Also accept joining like units, 40 + 20 = 60, 9 + 3 = 12, 60 + 12 = 72. Wrong: 62 after dropping the new ten (A), or 82 after keeping its ten loose ones too (B).
Shape: choose a first move and explain how it accounts for the rest. Practice checks supplied steps. Show neither the old 49-square scatter nor unit 4's six-tens/twelve-ones choice picture. Data id: `s05-X108`.

**E109, 1.NBT.C.4.** Say "Invented for practice." Read, "A child counts 38 books on one shelf and 29 on another. How many books are on the two shelves together?" Give an outline of two shelves with the two source labels only. Mark the shelves and record the method in one space. Key: 30 + 20 = 50, 8 + 9 = 17, 50 + 17 = 67 books. Marks link the new strip to ten of the seventeen loose ones. Wrong: 57 after dropping the ten (A), or 77 after counting it twice (B).
Shape: find the total for two shelves from the story and show the method on an empty shelf record. Practice checks and updates an existing cart label. Keep the shelf layout off the cart cards. Data id: `s05-X109`.

Day 110 uses F and the retained Addition record, with no extra exit.

**P1, 1.NBT.C.4, day 108.** "How would you show your way to add 54 and 3?" Key: 4 + 3 = 7, 54 + 3 = 57. Five tens unchanged. Wrong: 12, counting the five sticks as singles (D).
Shape: independently document an unchanged tens group beside a written singles method. Keep this blank independent panel off practice cards; practice verifies completed labels. Data id: `s05-AP1`.

**P2, 1.NBT.C.4, day 108.** "How would you show your way to add 36 and 20?" Key: 30 + 20 = 50, 50 + 6 = 56. Preserve six ones. Wrong: 38 from adding two singles (C).
Shape: independently mark the addition of whole tens and the preservation of ones in a blank record. Practice starts with supplied pieces and checks their movement. Data id: `s05-AP2`.

**P3, 1.NBT.C.4, day 108.** "How would you show your way to add 48 and 5?" Key: 48 + 2 = 50, 50 + 3 = 53. Circle ten of the thirteen ones for the trade. Wrong: 43 after losing the filled ten (A).
Shape: independently generate a written single-digit trade with a drawn trace of ten. Keep the blank trade record and its circled completed version off cards; practice repairs already supplied stages. Data id: `s05-AP3`.

**P4, 1.NBT.C.4, day 108.** "How would you show your way to add 23 and 34?" Key: 20 + 30 = 50, 3 + 4 = 7, 50 + 7 = 57. Both like-unit groups accounted for. Wrong: 12 from counting each strip as one (D).
Shape: record both like-unit groups with no exchange on an independent page. Practice sorts supplies or checks a completed drawing; keep packet facsimiles out. Data id: `s05-AP4`.

**P5, 1.NBT.C.4, day 108, with one private oral link on day 108 or 109.** "How would you show your way to add 56 and 27?" On the paper key, 50 + 20 = 70, 6 + 7 = 13, 70 + 13 = 83. Seven old sticks join one new stick and three ones remain. Wrong: 73 after dropping ten (A), 93 after leaving both copies (B), or 56 + 27 = 70 = 83 (E).
Shape: independently generate the full two-digit exchange record for a later private link. Keep the blank packet and independent record format off practice paper. Data id: `s05-AP5`.

**P5-link, 1.NBT.C.4, private evidence for P5.** In the child's one oral turn ask, "How does your writing show the ten you made?" Accept any link that locates the trade in the child-made drawing and links it to a true step. Sample: "These ten ones in my circle are part of the 13 in 70 + 13. They make the eighth stick. The three dots stay loose." Sample for adding on: "I used four of the seven to get to eighty. The rest are the three that make eighty-three," with 56 + 20 = 76, 76 + 4 = 80 and 80 + 3 = 83. Wrong: says the writing counts the new stick and also keeps all thirteen loose ones (B).
Shape: explain one exchange-to-equation link in the original P5 record. No interview rehearsal or practice packet. Data id: `s05-AP5-link`.

**F, 1.NBT.C.4, day 110.** Say "Invented for practice." Give a blank packing label. Show a card for a closed box marked 58 squares and a separate picture of one ten-strip and seven singles arriving. Read the card and name the arriving group as 17. "How would you fill in the new count label so another child can check it?" Key: 50 + 10 = 60, 8 + 7 = 15, 60 + 15 = 75. One new ten joins six tens and five ones stay loose. Accept any true linked record with the same model-to-writing reasoning. Another sample: "68 plus 2 gets to 70, then 5 more gets to 75," with 58 + 10 = 68 and the two moved ones accounted for. Wrong: 65 after dropping ten (A), or 58 + 17 = 60 = 75 (E).
Shape: use a box label and a pictured delivery to make a packing record from scratch. The child chooses the steps and accounts for the new ten. Supply no partial sums or worked display. Data id: `s05-AF`.

Keep shared models covered throughout independent work. In practice, replace towers and singles with adult-precut strips and squares, or draw strips and dots. Draw the two shelves when art is missing and handwrite cards when needed. Keep loose pieces out of mouths and count them during the last practice turn. No key tool is needed.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 108, "Which pieces changed form?" Day 109, "What does your total count?" On day 109 annotate a checked inventory label. Look for units kept apart in the steps, with a link from the quantities or the traded group to the written method.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix familiar within-ten addition and subtraction with the day's named retrieval. Repeat taught card rounds on day 108, with untimed feedback and retries. The familiar paper work on day 110 reinforces matching models and captions. No standard here sets a fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Review exchange, equals and altogether with pictures. "My new ten is __ in my picture and __ in my writing." Read aloud, enlarge or raise picture cells, and accept a neutral scribe, directed mark or signed link. Use the same support during the record and F. Extend by comparing two taught-type additions with the same total, one with an exchange.

## 10. Two rooms

For one child on day 108, allow two minutes for directions, eighteen for the record and one for the P5 explanation. Use the remaining time in a 30-minute session for familiar practice and the spoken exit. Give private feedback and revision time on day 109. On day 110 use five minutes for directions and return, eight for F and five for closure. Use one screen for practice only.
