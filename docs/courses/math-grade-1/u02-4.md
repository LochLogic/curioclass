## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems” in the taught structures. Day 36 is supported work on missing starts. Unit 6 completes the story types.
- **1.OA.B.4.** “Understand subtraction as an unknown-addend problem.” The same group can be marked in a story-following equation and an addition equation used to solve it.
- **1.OA.D.7.** “Understand” the equal sign and “determine” whether equations are true or false. Day 33 includes the result on either side and expressions on both sides. **1.OA.D.8.** “Determine” the unknown whole number in every position for both operations. Teach the bare removal start on day 34 before N35.
- **Practice.** MP.6 on day 33: build both sides before saying true or false. MP.4 on day 35: trace the same group through two equations.
- **Teacher explanation.** The equal sign compares amounts. A box stands for a story quantity wherever it is placed. In a removal story with the start unknown, restore what left to the group that stayed.

- **Q34a.** 8 = 8. “Is this equation true?” Key: true, 8 and 8. Wrong: false, because there is nothing to add or take away (A).
  Shape: Independently judge an equality with one number per side.
- **Q34b.** 9 = 12 − 3. “Do these sides show the same amount?” Key: true, 9 and 9. Wrong: false because nine comes first (A).
  Shape: Independently judge a result-first removal equation.
- **Q34c.** 6 + 2 = 2 + 6. “Is this equation true?” Key: true, 8 and 8. Wrong: false, because eight is not two, reading only the first number after the sign (B).
  Shape: Independently judge reversed addends.
- **Q34d.** 4 + 3 = 6 + 2. “Do these sides show the same amount?” Key: false, 7 and 8. Wrong: true after checking just 4 + 3 (B).
  Shape: Independently reject the unequal two-sum picture.
- **Q34e.** 11 − 4 = 3 + 4. “Is this equation true?” Key: true, 7 and 7. Wrong: false, because seven is not three, reading only the first number after the sign (B).
  Shape: Independently judge a subtraction and an addition expression.
- **Q34f.** 5 = 8 − 2. “Do these sides show the same amount?” Key: false, 5 and 6.
  Shape: Independently reject a result-first equation with unequal values. For Q, keep the six-judgment sequence and picture quantities out of practice. Practice constructs and traces supplied sides.

**Exit questions**

- **E33 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.B.4, 1.OA.D.7).** Give three independently labeled picture spaces. Lee starts with twenty paper stars and takes seven away. A second mat holds twenty stars, seven red and the rest blue. A third mat has nineteen pins and eighteen tags side by side. Beside each, read all three reusable picture labels for take away, parts and comparing. “Which relationship fits each picture?” Children put the label's mark by its clue: the group that leaves, the known part, or the extras with no partner. Key: take away (seven leave, thirteen left), parts (seven red, thirteen blue) and comparing (one extra pin). Wrong: rings all nineteen pins as the comparing clue (E). Describe unsupported classifications without a letter.
  Shape: Recognize each relationship in a fresh mixed set, with the labels available independently for each. Practice changes one live or pictured scene and evaluates built sides. No mixed sorting deck like this ticket.
- **E34 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.D.8).** Four paper birds sit on a fence and more land. Now there are twenty. Label picture spaces for first, arriving and now. Present 4 + □ = 20. “Which part of the picture does the box name?” Key: mark the arriving birds (sixteen), even if the marks only identify the group. Wrong: marks the whole final flock as the change (C).
  Shape: Locate the symbol's role on a new picture with a finished equation. Practice names roles while moving the pieces before attaching symbols.
- **E35 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.B.4, 1.OA.D.8).** Sixteen paper birds sit on a mat. Some leave and ten stay. Print 16 − □ = 10 above the paper drawing space. “What addition finds the birds that left?” Key: 10 + 6 = 16 or equivalent with six departures marked. 10 + □ = 16 with six marked also works. Wrong: ten departures copied from the ending number (C), or seven from counting ten as a new step (H).
  Shape: Produce a solution relation from a new situation equation and mark the same group. Practice traces that group through two already-completed cards.
- **E36 (1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.D.8).** Give blank picture space and □ + 9 = 20. “What story fits this equation?” Key: one actor with eleven at first, nine added and twenty at the end. For example, Bo puts nine more cushions by eleven. Wrong: twenty-nine at first by adding the known part and whole (D). Read the equation aloud and collect the marked picture. Keep this encounter and retry unscored.
  Shape: Invent a complete story from a bare equation with a missing start. Practice restores a start from a supplied story and fills an adult-started scene.

## 9. Supports and extension

Unknown means the number we need to find. The equal sign tells us both sides have the same amount. True means the two amounts match. False means the amounts differ. Use “The box stands for __.” Read all text and symbols aloud. Provide raised marks, finger-room flaps and a child-directed scribe. Record support. Extend by choosing another matching equation for the same sketch.
