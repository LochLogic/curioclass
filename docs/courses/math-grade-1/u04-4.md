# Section 4.4: Comparing and ordering two-digit numbers

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-grade-1/4/s/4.4), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-grade-1 u04-4.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Read with [the unit plan](s04.md) and [course defaults](DEFAULTS.md). Grade 1 Math, Unit 4, Numbers to 99. **Days 82 to 86.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 2, application 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.NBT.B.3.** "Compare" by the meanings of the tens and ones digits, "recording" comparisons with >, = and <. Day 82 uses words before day 83 ties them to signs. Stay below 100.
- **1.NBT.B.2 and B.2c.** "Understand" the digit values. Whole-ten numerals "refer to" tens with zero loose ones. Revisit 1.NBT.C.5 on day 86: "mentally find" and "explain" a change of ten.
- **MP.6, day 85.** Read each link in an ordered row from left to right and identify the decisive digit. The teacher explains why one more ten outweighs any difference between the lone ones digits. After equal tens, compare ones. When both places agree, the quantities are equal. The wide side of an inequality sign faces the greater quantity.

## 2. Where it sits

Receive E81 in its own slot on day 82. Retrieve kindergarten single-digit numeral comparison before two-digit work. Section 4.5 takes E86 and applies comparison to exchanged forms after children learn the exchange.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read aloud. Everyone marks paper, with shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Use a letter only when the marks or words show the named error. Label invented collections "Invented for practice" and tell children so. Use the worked practice quantities in fields 4 and 6 for the card decks. Keep the exit and packet pairs, their reversals and the exit pictures off practice and repair cards.

**Unit assessment item owned:** field 5 row 3 (P comparisons and Fresh traces).

- **P comparisons.** Show each pair with a blank sign space. Ask each time, “How would you show the comparison between these numbers?” Use the child's drawing and reading of the completed comparison as evidence. Keys: 92 > 57 because of tens; 36 < 39 because of ones; 45 = 45 because both places agree. Wrong: 57 greater on the basis of ones (C); says 36 is less but writes > (D); counts the four rods in one well-drawn 45 as four singles and says that side is worth nine (A).
  Shape: generate two quantities, then fill the sign gap and give the deciding place for each independent pair. Practice orients a sign to a supplied spoken comparison.
- **Fresh comparison traces.** Read one pair at a time. Ask “What comparison does your drawing show?” The child records the two quantities and their sign on the mat. Keys: 72 > 68 by tens; 24 < 29 by ones; 53 = 53 by both. Wrong: 68 greater on the basis of ones (C); says 24 is less but records > (D); treats five rods as five singles on one side and calls that side eight (A).
  Shape: mark blank mats and justify three new standalone comparisons. Avoid this blank two-mat format, its pairs, their reversals and wrong signs in practice.

**E82, 1.NBT.B.3.** Print 47 and 52 with blank proof space. "How can you show which number is greater?" Key: 52, five tens exceed four. Wrong: 47 because seven ones beat two (C); 47 when the child counts its 7 as seven tens (B).
Shape: choose from bare numerals using self-generated place-value marks. Practice physically pairs whole towers first and then reveals the ones.

**E83, 1.NBT.B.3.** Show everyone both cards, separately. “What does this card say?” First, 63 > 58 says sixty-three is greater than fifty-eight. It is true by tens. Next, 38 < 34 says thirty-eight is less than thirty-four. It is false because eight ones exceed four. Children circle the side the printed sign calls greater on each card. The six read one card from left to right. Wrong on the first: reads “sixty-three is less than fifty-eight” despite knowing 63 is greater (D). Wrong on the second: says “I know 38 has more, and the point of this sign tells me 38 has more,” reversing the sign's meaning (D). Correcting the false card gives different evidence from reading its sign. Record reading only when heard.
Shape: read what a supplied sign claims on an unfamiliar card, including one that makes a false claim. Practice turns a sign to match a supplied spoken relationship. The exit never asks for a corrected sign.

**E84, 1.NBT.B.3.** Show pictorial collections already labeled 46, 81, 81, 69 in that order. "What signs make this whole row read correctly?" Key: 46 < 81 = 81 > 69. Wrong: first link > despite saying forty-six is less (D). Middle link < after counting one picture's eight sticks and single as just nine ones (A). Last link < because one is less than nine (C).
Shape: supply the three links on one fixed strip. Practice reads already marked standalone pairs and fixes one flipped sign on a supplied record.

**E85, 1.NBT.B.3.** Show labeled collections 27, 65, 62 in that scrambled order. "How would you lay these out from least to greatest?" Mark the links and the deciding places on the paper arrangement. Key: 27 < 62 < 65, tens for the first link, ones for the second. Wrong: puts 27 above both because its seven is largest (C); orders correctly but copies > between increasing neighbors while saying "less than" (D).
Shape: order a complete new set with two deciding places. Practice inserts one card into a partly ordered row.

**E86, 1.NBT.B.2 and B.3.** Fresh scatters of 33 and 37 tool-tag squares, unlabeled. "What comparison belongs under these collections?" Present the scatters on separate sheets that children can align. Allow loops or tally marks to keep track. Record one comparison statement. Key: 33 < 37, as three tens match and three ones are fewer than seven. Wrong: 33 > 37 despite saying the left has fewer (D); 33 < 73 after putting the seven singles in the tens place (B).
Shape: generate both counts and their comparison from two ungrouped pictures. Practice checks supplied labels and a statement against movable classroom collections.

Predictions are from memory. Show the correct version before the invented wrong one. Kindergarten u01-1 Return and repair returns as written. Unit 1 section 1.1 Puppet wrong example beside the right one is planned only; show how to follow the practice figures without putting a child on the spot.

Return every child's paper. Flag a reason still to hear and offer a private turn in the next repair. Date it separately. E86 goes to day 87.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Review kindergarten u02-5 greater and the grade's one-digit numeral comparison. Unit 2 section 2.3 Check a supplied match is planned only. Demonstrate checking a link on a partial practice record and then let the pair exchange roles. Tie the spoken comparison and model to signs on day 83. Withdraw shared models on day 85; keep individual drawings available.

Ideal: two alignable ten-stick rows, with a flap to reveal loose ones after tens match, and a large sign strip. Paper fits, with no digital spec or key tool. Draw strips in place of rods, dots in place of singles, and cut or pencil-mark the signs and cards. Use a scrap flap in place of a cover. Use drawn pieces or keep loose pieces out of mouths and have an adult count them back in. Use classroom collections.

## 6. Days

Fifteen pairs work simultaneously and swap. Everyone marks paper. Hear one reading, link or deciding place from each named child, 45 seconds each, while others work; reserve 30 seconds for cue and collection. Note names and help. Hand back all thirty papers.

## Day 82

**Understanding.** I can compare tens before ones. Base-ten drawing (light), Model, show why, 10 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Use three minutes for 6 and 9. Ask “Which shows more?” Children mark 9 on a picture of the two matched rows. Use two minutes for familiar within-ten addition and subtraction.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return E81 and group by evidenced unit or place error.
- **5 min. Single-digit repair.** Match two pictured rows, if retrieval showed a miss. Others check a supplied single-digit match.
- **10 min. Model.** Pair off whole tens, then reveal the ones. Name the greater amount as the one with more and the lesser as the one with fewer. Read the C comparison in words both ways.
- **25 min. Practice.** Match the towers of two prepared groups. Compare the loose ones only after the tens tie. Include a practice pair with a whole-ten number with zero loose ones. Give directions orally.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E82. Hear children 13 to 18.

## Day 83

**Understanding.** I can say what a sign tells us about two numbers. Base-ten drawing (light), Model, show why, 10 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix a familiar within-ten fact with a single-digit comparison. If more than a third missed day 82's retrieval, use the full five minutes to match the single-digit pictures.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return E82 and repair the first place compared.
- **10 min. Model.** Review the equal sign from grade 1 section 2.1, planned only. Show identical groups of 22 before naming the sign. Introduce the two inequality signs with D. In 42 < 54 the left quantity is less. In 54 > 42 it is greater. In 22 = 22 both places match. Read each aloud. The wide side faces the larger quantity.
- **30 min. Practice.** Partners place signs to match spoken comparisons, then read the cards aloud. Place two practice cards end to end at their common number. Show how to read each link in a row before day 84 asks for several signs.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E83. Read the cards one at a time. Hear children 19 to 21 on the first and 22 to 24 on the second as everyone marks both.

## Day 85

**Application.** I can order quantities and explain why. Base-ten drawing (light), Order the cards, practice with feedback, 10 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Recall how comparing whole tens can settle a pair.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return E84 and repair a mistaken link.
- **30 min. Order the cards.** Withdraw shared models. Show least (smallest) and greatest (largest) at the ends. Insert 32 between 25 and 44 in a prepared row, because 25 < 32 < 44. Individual models remain available.
- **10 min. Discuss.** Read the links and point to the place that decides each. Finish by teaching how to check a supplied count and comparison for day 86.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E85. Hear children 1 to 6.

## Day 86

**Fluency.** I can practice counting, writing and comparing. Base-ten drawing (light), Partner rounds, practice with feedback, 10 min.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Find ten less than 23 (13), mentally before checking.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return E85. Check the priority place or sign error.
- **35 min. Partner rounds.** Count classroom sets prepared with 22 and 25 pieces. Verify the supplied labels and 22 < 25, equal tens and two ones fewer than five. Swap and retry with another worked record.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Mark the places behind equality on a known pair.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 84 "Which side faces the greater amount?" Day 85 "Which place decides here?" On day 86, draw a link for each matching place in an equal pair. Look for both groups of tens and both groups of ones.

## 8. Practice that returns

Mix familiar within-ten facts with each day's named retrieval. Day 84 rehearses taught sign reading and day 86 rehearses taught count and check rounds. No owned standard sets a fluency target. Each child tries, checks and retries without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

Pair greater than (more), less than (fewer) and equal to (the same amount) with drawings and spoken sentences. Offer "__ is greater because __." Read all prompts aloud. Enlarge signs and allow tactile mats, pointing or neutral scribing. Extend by changing one ones digit in a known pair and deciding whether the sign needs to change.

## 10. Two rooms

At home partners take the two roles in succession, check with marked strips and keep the child's paper. If available, project a practice pair. Children mark their own sheets.

## 11. Not in this section, and still to make
