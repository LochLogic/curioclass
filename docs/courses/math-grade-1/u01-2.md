# Section 1.2: Sorting and showing what we counted

Grade 1 Math. Unit 1, Adding, subtracting and working with data (days 1 to 16): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 7 to 10.** Days by kind: understanding 2, application 1, fluency 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.MD.C.4, this section's share.** "Organize, represent, and interpret data with up to three categories": sort by one rule into at most three groups, show one mark for each object under a label, and begin to interpret (the total, each group, and how many more by matching, days 9 and 10). Asking questions waits for day 13.
- **1.OA.C.5, day 10.** "Relate counting to addition and subtraction", as review: counting on to a total.
- **Limits that bite here.** Three categories, no graph axes. Adding stays within 10; the day 9 total of 15 slips is counted, never added. Single tally marks only; the bundle of five comes on day 11. Exits accept marks, drawings or tallies.
- **Connections.** 1.OA.C.5 on day 10 (the total of two groups by counting on).
- **Strategies.** Sorting and one-to-one marking; no algorithm.
- **Practices.** None named for this section; MP.2 starts on day 12.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A rule puts each object in exactly one group. Labels make a display readable by someone else. "How many more" is the count of unmatched marks, not the larger count.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. D1 and D2 (section 1.4) use a day 15 survey and cloud creatures (kites, puffs, spirals) with counts 6, 2, 3; a sample of 7, 6, 2 is for scoring only. None appears here.

**Exit questions.** Read aloud, one ask at a time; every child marks paper. Scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Timing for about 30 children: 3 minutes to mark, then the rotating six are heard in the Synthesis at 45 seconds each (4.5 minutes), names recorded. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. Each listed wrong answer carries one letter; an answer that matches no letter is described on the record.

- **E7.** Picture: six shapes, 2 striped triangles, 1 plain triangle, 1 plain circle, 1 striped square, 1 plain square. (a) "Draw loops to sort these by one rule." (b) "Write how many in each group." Key: by shape 3, 1, 2; or striped and plain, 3 and 3. Patterns make the choice of rule real and are safe for color-blind children. Wrong: 3, 2, 2 with one shape in two loops (D); 3, 1, 1 with one left out (E). Shape: practice sorts real objects; no printed shape pictures; loops are modeled on a spoon pile.
- **E8.** Picture: 4 triangles, 3 circles, 1 square, unsorted, and a blank sheet. "Make a display another child could read." Key: three picture-labeled groups with 4, 3, 1 marks. Wrong: no labels (G); counts 4, 3, 0 with the square left out (E); 9 marks with one shape marked twice (D). Shape: practice uses real sorted piles and ready-made columns.
- **E9.** The teacher reads pretend answers to "Drum or bell?": "drum, bell, drum." (a) "Make one mark for each answer I read." (b) "Make a labeled display." Key: 3 marks; drums 2, bells 1, labeled. Wrong: 2 and 2 with one answer in both groups (D); only 2 marks (E); no labels (G). Shape: practice slips are visible on mats, never read aloud; no drum or bell.
- **E10.** Display: paper boats 3, paper planes 6. (a) "Mark a count for how many altogether." Key: 9 ("six, seven, eight, nine" from 6 as the kept group, or from 3: four to nine). Wrong: 8, "six" counted as a step (A); one to nine (C). (b) "Mark how many more planes than boats." Key: 3. Wrong: 6, the larger count (F). Shape: practice uses counters on mats, with no printed two-group picture and no 3 and 6.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

Puppet pairs, right before wrong, invented for practice and said so.

| | Wrong example beside a right one; day | If many made it |
|---|---|---|
| D | Day 8: 10 shapes sorted, one square in two columns, 11 marks, beside each shape in one column, 10 marks. | Next Warm-up and repair, 5 min: each object onto exactly one mat (a fresh pile of 7). |
| G | Day 8: the model display with no labels beside the same columns with one picture label each. | 5 min: a picture label for each group, drawn by the child. |
| E | Day 9: five slips, four marks, beside five slips, five marks. | 5 min: tick each slip as it gets its mark. |
| F | Day 10 (own example; the register's is day 13): red 8, blue 5, puppet says "8 more", beside matching pairs and 3 left over, "3 more red". | 5 min: match pairs, count the extras (7 and 4: 3). |

A and C appear in exit 10 only, as review from section 1.1 (the day 3 and day 5 repairs there). Over a third (more than 10 of 30) is "many": a whole-class 5-minute repair takes one letter in the Warm-up and repair; fewer go to a 5-minute teacher group in the next practice. Day 10 misses go to day 11.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The ten-frame leaves the shared display on day 7. Objects are tied to labeled marks on days 7 and 8 (a spoon pile, then a labeled column). The three-category tally display (light) is paper: folded three-column sheets and category mats. Each use has one job: show why in the model, practice with feedback in practice. No key tool.
- **Key moment 1, day 8: each object becomes one labeled mark.** Ideal: real objects moved onto a mat, then a mark drawn for each. Spec: the folded sheet and mat fit; no digital tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 10: how many more is the unmatched count.** Ideal: counters in two matched rows. Spec: paper mats fit.
- **A room with nothing.** Spoons, cloth squares and paper strips are household stand-ins; cut paper squares and circles replace shape cards; a sheet folded in three is the display. Objects are larger than a small-parts tube and carry no food.

## 6. Days

Pairs and tables work at once; the teacher hears the rotating six in the Synthesis; every child may pass in public talk. Sorting is of objects only, never of classmates by family, income, bodies or home life.

## Day 7

**Understanding.** I can sort things by one rule and count each group.

- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Day 6 exit repair, one letter, if many missed; otherwise retrieval from the pool in field 8.
- **5 min. Sorting retrieval.** Kindergarten review, each child at once: sort 2 paper circles and 4 paper squares onto a two-part mat, write each count on the mat, and circle the group with more (circles 2, squares 4, squares circled). The teacher scans the mats and notes each child; those who miss it get 10 minutes in day 8 practice.
- **10 min. Model one rule.** 4 spoons, 3 cloth squares and 2 paper strips: sorted by kind, 4, 3, 2. Sorted by "bends or not": bends 5 (cloth and strips), not 4 (spoons); loops are drawn around each group.
- **20 min. Sort and count.** Tables of 4 or 5 children, piles of 10 to 12 classroom objects. Each table picks a rule, sorts, and writes the count for each group; six tables, the teacher visits each.
- **10 min. Compare rules.** Tables show their rule; "Does every object fit one group?"
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Rotating six.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7.
- **Carried and tool.** The tally display (light): show why, 10 min; practice with feedback, 20 min. Credit: adapted from IM G1 U1 L7, Sort Math Tools.

## Day 8

**Understanding.** I can make a display with labels that another child can read.

- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Day 7 exit repair, one letter.
- **10 min. Model.** Sorted circles 5, squares 2, triangles 3 moved into labeled columns, one mark each. Puppet pairs for D and G.
- **25 min. Fill the display.** Pairs get a ready sheet and a sorted pile of squares, circles and triangles: 5, 1, 4; then 2, 4, 5; then 6, 3, 1. The first round has labels drawn in; the second, the child draws the labels. The teacher takes the day 7 retrieval group for up to 10 minutes.
- **10 min. Read a partner's display.** Partners trade, say what each column shows and the count; "How can a display keep every response?"
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Rotating six.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E8.
- **Carried and tool.** Tally display (light): show why, 10 min; practice with feedback, 25 min. Credit: adapted from IM G1 U1 L8, Sort and Count Shape Cards.

## Day 9

**Application.** I can gather our choices and show them in a labeled display.

- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Day 8 exit repair (D or G, one letter).
- **10 min. Model one response each.** Five pretend slips, five marks; puppet pair for E.
- **15 min. Gather choices.** "Which color for our paper chain: red, blue or yellow?" Two groups of 15. Each child drops a color slip, or a pretend slip, 20 seconds a child, 5 minutes a group, both groups at once. Choosing is optional and anonymous. If fewer than 15 are present, the teacher fills with pretend slips and says they are invented; a color nobody chose stays as an empty labeled group.
- **15 min. Record the slips.** The slips lie on mats in view; each child makes a labeled display with one mark per slip.
- **5 min. Model matching.** Invented: red 8, blue 5; match them in pairs, 3 are left, "3 more red than blue".
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Rotating six.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E9.
- **Carried and tool.** Tally display (light): practice with feedback, 15 min. Credit: adapted from IM G1 U1 L9, What Is Your Favorite?

## Day 10

**Fluency.** I can find how many altogether and how many more, and check with counters.

- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Day 9 exit repair (error E), or retrieval.
- **5 min. Re-model matching.** Red 8, blue 5: match in pairs, 3 left over, "3 more red"; puppet pair for F.
- **30 min. Partner rounds.** Pairs build two counter groups on mats and find the total by counting on and how many more by matching (total, more): 9 and 1 (10, 8); 7 and 3 (10, 4); 8 and 1 (9, 7); 3 and 3 (6, 0, "the same"); 6 and 4 (10, 2); 4 and 2 (6, 2); 8 and 2 (10, 6); 7 and 1 (8, 6). Then partners read a day 9 display. No timer.
- **10 min. Check records and retry.** Pairs recheck marked cards with counters.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Rotating six: "How do the extra marks tell how many more?"
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E10.
- **Carried and tool.** Tally display (light): practice with feedback, 30 min. Credit: adapted from IM G1 U1 L10, Center Day 2.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 7, "Does every object fit one group?" Day 8, "How can a display keep every response?" Day 10, "How do the extra marks tell how many more?"
- Written task, day 8: the child's labeled display.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: one mark for each object, a label on each group, counts that match the marks.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Daily, 5 minutes, no timer. Pool: count on 8 to 12; one more than 13; 3 + 2, 4 + 2, 5 - 2, 6 - 1, 9 + 1. No section 1.1 exit facts and no 11 + 3 or 16 - 12.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Day 10 rehearses counting on with checks and retries, no clock.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sort, rule, group, label, display, more. Frame: "I sorted by __. There are __ here."
- **Access.** Tactile mats, large shapes, an adult scribe; a child may move objects instead of drawing loops.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept pointing; a scribe writes counts.
- **Reading load.** None; the only printed words are group labels, and pictures stand in for them.
- **Extension, same standard.** Sort the same pile a second way and say why both rules work.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A 35-minute path (5, 5, 15, 5, 5); the adult writes pretend slips and the child counts them.
- **One projector.** It may show a mat; children work on paper.
