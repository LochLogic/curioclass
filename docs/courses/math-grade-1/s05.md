## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When do we make a new ten?"
- **Hook, day 96:** Show 24 paper squares grouped in tens. Compare adding three singles with adding three ten-strips. What changes? Ask the compelling question; return to it when ones fill a ten. Source: field 10.
- **Supporting questions:** Which units combine (day 97)? Where does the new ten come from (day 100)? Can we predict an exchange (day 102)? Why do different methods agree (day 106)?
- **Closing claim:** "I can add ones, tens and two-digit numbers, show when a new ten forms, and explain my written method." The Addition record and Fresh transfer establish this on day 110.
- **Teaching:** Model exchanges without changing quantity. Connect each drawing to equalities, then step back on days 102 and 106. Teacher pages explain like units, the value of the new ten and why either order of combining units works. Section 5.1 carries adding ones or tens with no new ten; 5.2 composing a ten; 5.3 two-digit pairs; 5.4 the explained written record and application.


- **Practices:** MP.7, day 97, Like-unit pairs: sort and combine tens and ones, explaining the units. MP.3, day 106, Two methods: compare adding tens first with combining ones first for 38 + 24, and answer a partner's objection using the drawings.
- **Fluency and balance:** The owned standard sets no fluency target. Keep eight understanding, three fluency, three application and one check day. Understanding leads because composition needs explanation; practice repeats taught methods without time pressure. Daily mixed retrieval covers addition and subtraction within 10 and one earlier idea.
- **Focus:** One major-work unit, 100 percent; fourteen of fourteen teaching lessons, 100 percent; 810 of 810 instructional minutes, 100 percent. Exclude day 110 and the thirty observation minutes on days 108 to 109. No supporting cluster is assigned.
- **Limits:** Two addends, sums through 100, shown as ten tens at the endpoint. Use place-value and properties strategies, accepting related subtraction explanations. No standard algorithm, general two-digit subtraction, number line or three-addend extension. These belong later.
- **Carried:** Tie towers and drawings to equations on day 96; teacher records methods on day 97 and children do so on day 98. Withdraw shared towers on day 99. Restore them on day 100, connecting the double ten-frame's filled ones to a new ten-stick and equation. Withdraw shared ten-frames on day 101, retaining drawings. Withdraw shared worked drawings on day 106; individual models remain available. Pass tens-and-ones drawings to unit 6.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask "What unit did you add?" on day 97, "Where did the new ten come from?" on day 100, and "Why do the methods agree?" on day 106. Judge written methods with field 5's look-fors.

## U8. Practice that returns

Use five-minute mixed retrieval of within-10 addition and subtraction with one earlier story or place-value idea; on days 97 to 99 include one within-20 make-ten sum, such as 8 + 5, before day 100 needs it. Days 99, 103 and 108 repeat taught partner routines with feedback and retries. No races or timed sheets; objects support reasoning but do not establish arithmetic fluency.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read every prompt aloud; require no independent reading. Pair tens, ones, exchange and equal with pictures. Offer "These ten ones become __; the amount stays __." Use large tactile mats, shape as well as color, movable pieces and neutral dictation. Pair students by the current error, then regroup from exits. Extend within the same standard by finding two addend pairs with the same sum, one requiring an exchange and one not, and explaining why.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, use five minutes retrieval, twenty modelling and paper work, five discussion and five exit. Keep day 108's eighteen-minute record and hear P5 once, replacing partner time; later feedback and revision still precede scoring. With one projector, display a picture while everyone marks paper; no device is required.
