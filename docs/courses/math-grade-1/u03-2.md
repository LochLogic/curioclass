Grade 1 Math. Unit 3, Adding and subtracting within 20 (days 40 to 68), [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 45 to 49.** Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 1, application 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson. Tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **~1.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems.” Day 45 uses joining with a missing start and a total within ten. Keep attempts supported and unscored for proficiency.
- **1.OA.C.6.** “Add and subtract” and “Use strategies.” Day 46 rehearses facts within ten. Days 47 to 49 use a teen's ten and further ones to find a missing part.
- **1.NBT.B.2a and 1.NBT.B.2b.** Ten “can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones.” Eleven through nineteen “are composed of a ten” and one to nine further ones. Use ten visible singles before representing them as one unit.
- **Practices.** MP.4, day 45: connect the first, joined and final amounts to a joining scene. MP.7, day 47: connect a filled frame to a tower and a numeral.
- **Teacher explanation.** A hidden initial amount belongs to the story even when the first picture is empty of visible marks. One ten is a new unit name for ten ones. Folding or snapping preserves every one.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. T53 is collected in section 3.3. Read each stem aloud separately and have every child mark an individual paper picture. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark supplied help. Say and print “invented for practice” with fictional scenes.

- **E45 (~1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.C.6).** A closed cover stands for Rin's earlier mat. Her final mat shows two paper boats. Read “Rin put two paper boats on her mat. Then she had two. How many boats were there at first?” Mark the start by drawing the boats or writing a zero on the covered panel. Key: zero, because 0 + 2 = 2. Wrong: one, counts the already held two as a new step from two to two (A). Record help descriptively.
  
  Shape: Infer a possibly empty first scene from an arrival and finish. Practice lifts a cover to verify a supplied nonempty start. Keep this empty-start boat scene out of teaching.
- **E46a (1.OA.C.6).** Show a game score card marked 2 + 2, a blank score picture and space for working marks. “How would you show this card's score?” Key: four. Accept a represented double or valid two new counts after two. Wrong: three after saying two, three as the two new counts (A).
  
  Shape: An individual solved score picture with a strategy trace. Practice checks single partner cards with loose pieces and keeps this exact double and the score slip out.
- **E46b (1.OA.C.6).** On a separate score panel read 10 − 5. “How would you show this card's score?” Key: five. For example, five and five make ten, so crossing out either five leaves five. Wrong: six, copies the answer for 10 − 4 as if the removal were unchanged (H).
  
  Shape: An individual subtraction score represented on its own panel. Keep the paired addition and subtraction score slip and both fact families out of the practice decks.
- **E47 (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b).** Show sixteen loose squares and a picture of an empty sleeve with ten spaces. “How can you show the whole set after the sleeve closes?” Children draw a labeled ten-stick and all singles outside it, with the whole labeled. Key: one ten and six further ones, sixteen. Wrong: marks the stick as one one and labels the whole seven (B).
  
  Shape: Make a closed-bundle inventory from an unpartitioned set. Practice exchanges an already filled frame and opens a supplied strip. No loose sixteen sleeve scene or independent redraw of that packed set appears in practice.
- **E48 (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** Show a folded ten-strip and a covered singles pocket, labeled thirteen in all. Read 10 + □ = 13. “How many ones are in the pocket?” Children draw the hidden ones beside the strip. Key: three. Wrong: twelve, treating the strip as one one in thirteen (B), or four, counting ten as the first new step (A).
  
  Shape: Infer hidden ones from a fixed numeral and a folded unit. Practice builds or unfolds a shown teen. Reserve the missing-ones equation with the covered thirteen picture.
- **E49a (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** Provide a seventeen picture with one folded part and seven visible singles. Read □ + 7 = 17. “What amount does the folded part stand for?” Children connect that part to the box mark. Key: ten. Wrong: one, counting one stick as one one (B).
  
  Shape: Identify a missing ten in an addition statement while the rest is pictured. Practice exposes single groups by lifting a cover. Reserve these quantities and the addition panel.
- **E49b (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** On the second panel show the same collection and read 17 − 10 = □. “What amount is left without the ten?” Children mark the part left and its value. Key: seven visible singles. Wrong: eight, counting ten through seventeen as eight new steps (A).
  
  Shape: Connect a subtraction statement to the complementary part of the same collection. Practice exposes one covered part at a time and keeps the paired seven/seventeen recordings out.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Days 45 to 46.** Personal ten-frames and labeled pictures connect actions to equations. A two-panel before and after mat fits the day 45 story. Use a paper flap to show or hide the first group.
- **Day 47.** Review two frames with ten individual ones visible. Children fill the first frame and connect those ten to a tower and then a drawn stick beside the teen numeral. The foldable ten-mark strip fits because it lets the child open and account for every mark. Shared frames withdraw on day 58 and shared towers on day 64. No key tool or digital spec is needed.
- **No-kit path.** Draw one or two frames on paper. Use scrap counters, handwritten cards and a folded ten-mark strip with loose marks in place of cubes and singles. If a tower separates, align its ten pieces with the strip and refold the strip.

## 6. Days

Use fifteen simultaneous pairs with exchanged roles. Each synthesis hears a rotating six on the roster for one 45-second turn each. All others point or mark their own sheets. Record names and help and return all collected exits in the next Practice return.

## Day 45

**Understanding.** I can find a missing starting group. Children hold a personal mat, ten-frame and counters. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Read the invented scene of four paper flags and two added. On one mat guide the class to retell, point to arrivals and read the expression in separate short prompts. Key: 4 + 2, six.
- **10 min. Model.** Use the ten-frame (light) to show why. Rin lifts a cover and reveals five paper seals, then adds two to make seven. Rewind the scene, separating the two arrivals from the seven to verify the hidden five. Model the field 4 A count contrast in a separate puppet scene.
- **25 min. Practice return.** First five for E44, next five for the retrieval misses acting an arrival. Use the last fifteen for partners to lift covers and verify their retelling of a joining scene.
- **10 min. Compare.** Point to the first and last groups in separate supplied pictures and say which the cover hides.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear six on what arrived.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E45, paper, supported.

## Day 47

**Understanding.** I can show a teen as one ten and further ones. Children hold double frames, strips or cubes and paper. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Read separate prompts about seven occupied places needing three more, and twelve with ten ones and two further ones. Mark the parts on paper.
- **10 min. Model.** Double ten-frame (light), show why. Transfer the ten visible singles from a full frame to a tower beside four loose ones. Draw the stick, label it ten and connect it to 14. Count the ten pieces on the opened strip before folding it.
- **25 min. Practice return.** First five for E46 and fresh retry. Open and refold supplied strips and trade a filled frame, tower or strip that preserves the same quantity.
- **10 min. Compare.** Connect the same set across the full frame, stick and numeral.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear six on what the ten means.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E47, paper.

## Day 49

**Understanding.** I can find a missing ten or further ones. Children hold models and paper covers. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Mix taught facts and further ones in a known teen.
- **10 min. Model.** Double ten-frame (light), show why. In fourteen, cover the bundle and lift to verify ten with four visible. On a separate reset, cover and verify the four. Connect each to a single matching addition or subtraction statement.
- **25 min. Practice return.** First five E48. Then pairs trade which part is covered and confirm by lifting once. Keep their label beside that single part.
- **10 min. Explain.** Compare a stick label with a loose-ones label and trace the numeral to the pictured amount.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear six point to the part their cover hides.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E49a and E49b, read separately. Send results to day 50.

- **Fluency.** Aim for accurate recall or an efficient mental strategy within ten. Make independent attempts, check with paper pieces and retry without time pressure.

## 9. Supports and extension

Say “One bundle holds __ ones.” On day 47, name bundle as ten ones held together and ten as the name for that group. Read teen numerals and labels aloud. Use large textured marks and strips, separate panels and child-directed marking. Extend by showing a different teen with a folded and an open strip.
