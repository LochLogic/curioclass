# Section 3.2: A missing part, then a ten

Grade 1 Math. Unit 3, Adding and subtracting within 20 (days 40 to 68), [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 45 to 49.** Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 1, application 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson. Tasks are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **~1.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems.” Day 45 uses joining with a missing start and a total within ten. Keep attempts supported and unscored for proficiency.
- **1.OA.C.6.** “Add and subtract” and “Use strategies.” Day 46 rehearses facts within ten. Days 47 to 49 use a teen's ten and further ones to find a missing part.
- **1.NBT.B.2a and 1.NBT.B.2b.** Ten “can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones.” Eleven through nineteen “are composed of a ten” and one to nine further ones. Use ten visible singles before representing them as one unit.
- **Practices.** MP.4, day 45: connect the first, joined and final amounts to a joining scene. MP.7, day 47: connect a filled frame to a tower and a numeral.
- **Teacher explanation.** A hidden initial amount belongs to the story even when the first picture is empty of visible marks. Show the box as the place for the number we need before using a covered part. One ten is a new unit name for ten ones. Folding or snapping preserves every one.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. T53 is collected in section 3.3. Read each stem aloud separately and have every child mark an individual paper picture. Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Mark supplied help. Say and print “invented for practice” with fictional scenes.

- **E45 (~1.OA.A.1, 1.OA.C.6).** The first mat is hidden. Draw at most ten boats. Show Rin putting the whole tied bunch on the mat. The final picture shows just that same bunch, still tied with its ribbon. “How many boats were on the mat before Rin put the bunch there?” Key: zero. Every boat in the final picture arrived together. Mark zero on the first mat or point from the whole bunch back to its arrival. Wrong: writes five and says Rin started with five in the seal story, carrying that answer over to the changed start (H). Record any help.
  
  Shape: Infer a possibly empty first scene from an arrival and finish. Practice lifts a cover to verify a supplied nonempty start. Keep this empty-start boat scene out of teaching.
- **E46a (1.OA.C.6).** A folded game card shows seven dots. One is a loose bonus dot. The others came from two equal groups. “How many points came from just one of the equal groups?” Key: three. Set aside the bonus and match the six other dots in two rows of three. Wrong: keeps four from the familiar four-and-four double after marking that each group has one fewer (H).  
  Shape: Infer one equal part from a final score and a bonus. Practice starts with the fact showing. Keep this card out of rounds.
- **E46b (1.OA.C.6).** Print six spaces. Leave one empty and show four of the other five with a cross. “What subtraction shows the score that is left?” Key: 5 − 4 = 1. The empty space was never occupied. Four of the five points were crossed out. Wrong: Crosses out one of the five first and then crosses out four more, leaving zero (G).
  Shape: Recover the starting score before subtracting. Practice starts with the number on the card. Keep the six-space strip and both kinds of marks out of rounds.
- **E47 (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** Give a four-square strip, a seven-square strip, six loose squares and a sleeve holding ten. “How could you pack these squares so a friend can count them with the sleeve shut?” Key: Pack the four-square strip and the six loose squares. A closed ten and seven visible squares make 17. Accept any packing preserving all 17. Wrong: Labels the drawing 8 after counting the closed ten as one one (B).
  Shape: Build a ten across groups and replace it with one closed unit. Practice exchanges an already filled frame. Reserve the two short strips and six loose squares.
- **E48 (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** A box holds thirteen beads. Show a folded strip labeled ten, two loose beads and a closed pocket inside the box. “How many more beads are in the pocket?” Key: one. The strip and two loose beads account for twelve, and the one in the pocket completes thirteen. Wrong: labels the pocket ten after counting the strip as one bead alongside the two loose ones (B).  
  Shape: Account for a ten and visible ones to infer only the still-hidden ones. Practice shows all the loose pieces. Reserve this partly visible thirteen.
- **E49a (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** On a mat labeled seventeen, show four loose squares on a pad and three in a little open dish beside a wrapped bundle. Read “a box plus all the loose squares makes seventeen.” “What number goes in the box for the bundle?” Key: ten. Four and three are seven singles outside the wrap. Wrong: writes one because there is one wrapped stick (B).  
  Shape: Identify the missing ten when the visible singles are in two places. Practice lifts a single cover to check a given part.
- **E49b (1.NBT.B.2a, 1.NBT.B.2b, 1.OA.C.6).** On a separate picture read “Seventeen take away one ten.” A child has drawn three of the leftover squares. “How many more squares does this answer picture need?” Key: four more. There are seven loose squares in all, and three are already drawn. Wrong: adds five by counting three itself as a new step to seven (A).  
  Shape: Finish a partly drawn difference. Reserve these two seventeen panels and their missing parts from practice.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Days 45 to 46.** Personal ten-frames and labeled pictures connect actions to equations. A two-panel before and after mat fits the day 45 story. Use a paper flap to show or hide the first group.
- **Day 47.** Review two frames with ten individual ones visible. Children fill the first frame and connect those ten to a tower and then a drawn stick beside the teen numeral. The foldable ten-mark strip fits because it lets the child open and account for every mark. Shared frames withdraw on day 58 and shared towers on day 64. No key tool or digital spec is needed.
- **No-kit path.** Draw one or two frames on paper. Use scrap counters, handwritten cards and a folded ten-mark strip with loose marks in place of cubes and singles. If a tower separates, align its ten pieces with the strip and refold the strip.

## 6. Days

Fifteen pairs work at once and swap roles. Start this section with children 1 to 6. Move to the next six each day, returning to children 1 to 6 after 25 to 30. In Synthesis or Close, six turns of 45 seconds leave 30 seconds to switch. The other 24 mark their own answer to the same question. Record who was heard and any help. Check those marks after class and return them with each child's exit in the next named return. Use the child's next 45-second turn for any explanation still needed. A later answer has its own date. In each teaching day's five-minute retrieval, use a familiar addition and subtraction alongside the named earlier idea. Draw from worked practice only. Keep field 3 cards and their number combinations out of models, repairs and decks.

## Day 45

**Understanding.** I can find a missing starting group. Children hold a personal mat, ten-frame and counters. Digital tool: None.

- **10 min. Model.** Use the ten-frame (light) to show why. Rin lifts a cover and reveals five paper seals, then adds two to make seven. Rewind the scene, separating the two arrivals from the seven to verify the hidden five. Model the field 4 A count contrast in a separate puppet scene.
- **25 min. Practice return.** First five for E44, next five for the retrieval misses acting an arrival. Use the last fifteen for partners to lift covers and verify their retelling of a joining scene.
- **10 min. Compare.** Point to the first and last groups in separate supplied pictures and say which the cover hides.
- **5 min. Synthesis.** Hear six on what arrived.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E45, paper, supported.

Day 45 reviews an arrival, day 46 a part, day 48 a bundle and day 49 further ones, with taught mixed facts each day.
- **Fluency.** Aim for accurate recall or an efficient mental strategy within ten. Make independent attempts, check with paper pieces and retry without time pressure.

## 9. Supports and extension

Say “One bundle holds __ ones.” On day 47, name bundle as ten ones held together and ten as the name for that group. Read teen numerals and labels aloud. Use large textured marks and strips, separate panels and child-directed marking. Extend by showing a different teen with a folded and an open strip.

## 10. Two rooms
