## 1. Standards and practices

- **1.MD.B.3.** "Tell and write time" in hours and half-hours using face and digit clocks. On 141 connect hands to :00. On 142 use the half-turn of a circle and move both hands together. On 143 connect it to :30. MP.6 on 144: use hand length, hour and minute to check a school-event card.
- At a whole hour the short hour hand points to the hour and the long minute hand to 12. At half past, the long hand points to 6 and the short hand is halfway from that hour to the next, including between 12 and 1. The hour hand moves continuously as the minute hand travels. Keep all examples to whole and half hours.
- On 145 use "Add and subtract within 20" (1.OA.C.6) in retrieval and "Compose" (1.G.A.2) in review. On 146 reuse 1.G.A.1 "Distinguish" and "build and draw", 1.G.A.2 "Compose", and 1.G.A.3 "Partition", "describe" and "Understand". Use school events with invented times.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

Show right before wrong. On 141, follow each hand on a whole-hour practice clock, then show the G reversal. On 142 for F, move the short hand from 4 halfway toward 5 with the long hand's half-turn, then show the short hand pinned on 4. On 143 for H, pair the practice half-past-four face with 4:30, then contrast 4:06 as a misplaced numeral. On 145 for D, compare a joined prism and cone with the same pair displaced. G and H are predictions from memory. Use a new practice display for a retry.

Return E140 to E144 at the next opening. At 11 or more misses, give five opening and five practice minutes for one repair and independent retry. On 143 to 145 place the first five practice minutes immediately before R4 when needed. For fewer misses use small-group time and let others rehearse on paper. Return E145 on 146 and file its priorities for 149 or 150. Day 146 has revision from feedback and fresh supplements. Day 149 handles number gaps. Day 150 has four ten-minute stations for A/B/C/J, D, E/I and F/G/H, then fifteen fresh transfer minutes and five to record. Keep fresh 7:30 and 9:30 faces and digits for clock transfer on 150.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Teach time with a movable-hands model, then picture and digits on 141 and 143. On 142 move linked hands coherently or move two paper hands together by hand, with the short hand steadily advancing. Withdraw shared worked clocks after the launch on 144. The ideal instrument distinguishes both hands by length and makes their positions visible together. A plain teaching clock or adult-secured paper pivot fits. In a room with scrap paper, draw three snapshots and mark hands with pencil. Use clean boxes and roll or fold scrap paper for solid practice. Digital tools: None.

## 9. Supports and extension

On 141 teach clock, hour hand, minute hand, digits and o'clock with a large labeled face and a matching record. Show how each written number mark on the display is a digit. The place-value use in Grade 1 section 4.2 is planned only. On 142 teach half past as half of the hour gone. On 143 teach how :30 records that time. Use hand length and spoken labels, high contrast and tactile or movable hands. "The short hand is between ___ and ___." Extend by correcting one misplaced hand on a familiar time.
