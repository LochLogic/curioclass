- **1.OA.C.6:** Add and subtract within 20, demonstrating fluency for addition and subtraction within 10. Use strategies such as counting on; making ten (e.g., 8 + 6 = 8 + 2 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14); decomposing a number leading to a ten (e.g., 13 – 4 = 13 – 3 – 1 = 10 – 1 = 9); using the relationship between addition and subtraction (e.g., knowing that 8 + 4 = 12, one knows 12 – 8 = 4); and creating equivalent but easier or known sums (e.g., adding 6 + 7 by creating the known equivalent 6 + 6 + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13). (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.B.3:** Apply properties of operations as strategies to add and subtract. Examples: If 8 + 3 = 11 is known, then 3 + 8 = 11 is also known. (Commutative property of addition.) To add 2 + 6 + 4, the second two numbers can be added to make a ten, so 2 + 6 + 4 = 2 + 10 = 12. (Associative property of addition.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.C.5:** Relate counting to addition and subtraction (e.g., by counting on 2 to add 2). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **~1.OA.A.1:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems involving situations of adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing, with unknowns in all positions, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.B.2a:** 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones - called a “ten.” Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.B.2b:** The numbers from 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.A.2:** Solve word problems that call for addition of three whole numbers whose sum is less than or equal to 20, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.D.8:** Determine the unknown whole number in an addition or subtraction equation relating three whole numbers. For example, determine the unknown number that makes the equation true in each of the equations 8 + ? = 11, 5 = _ – 3, 6 + 6 = _. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
