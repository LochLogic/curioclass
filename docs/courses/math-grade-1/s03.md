## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What makes a sum easy to find?"
- **Hook, day 40:** Show an unfinished addition table. Children mark a familiar sum and explain what makes it familiar. Ask the compelling question. Adapted from the opening named in field 10.
- **Supporting questions:** Why start with the larger addend (day 41)? How can ten things be one unit (day 47)? Which pair helps first (day 55)? When does counting up help subtraction (day 63)?

- **Teaching:** Model preserved quantities beside equations, then compare children's methods. Step back on days 58 and 64. Teacher pages explain unitizing, conservation when regrouping (from memory), and why subtraction cannot be reversed (from memory).

- **1.OA.C.6:** Add and subtract within 20, demonstrating fluency for addition and subtraction within 10. Use strategies such as counting on; making ten (e.g., 8 + 6 = 8 + 2 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14); decomposing a number leading to a ten (e.g., 13 – 4 = 13 – 3 – 1 = 10 – 1 = 9); using the relationship between addition and subtraction (e.g., knowing that 8 + 4 = 12, one knows 12 – 8 = 4); and creating equivalent but easier or known sums (e.g., adding 6 + 7 by creating the known equivalent 6 + 6 + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13). (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.B.3:** Apply properties of operations as strategies to add and subtract. Examples: If 8 + 3 = 11 is known, then 3 + 8 = 11 is also known. (Commutative property of addition.) To add 2 + 6 + 4, the second two numbers can be added to make a ten, so 2 + 6 + 4 = 2 + 10 = 12. (Associative property of addition.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.C.5:** Relate counting to addition and subtraction (e.g., by counting on 2 to add 2). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **~1.OA.A.1:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) Use addition and subtraction within 20 to solve word problems involving situations of adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing, with unknowns in all positions, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.B.2a:** 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones - called a “ten.” Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.NBT.B.2b:** The numbers from 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.A.2:** Solve word problems that call for addition of three whole numbers whose sum is less than or equal to 20, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **1.OA.D.8:** Determine the unknown whole number in an addition or subtraction equation relating three whole numbers. For example, determine the unknown number that makes the equation true in each of the equations 8 + ? = 11, 5 = _ – 3, 6 + 6 = _. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U7. Talk and writing

Day 47: "How can one stick still mean ten things?" Day 55: "Where did every group go?" Day 63: "What tells you when to stop counting?" Judge each child's Three-group story with field 5's look-fors.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve mixed within-10 addition and subtraction for five minutes daily, adding one earlier counting, story or taught place-value idea. Teach plus zero, plus one and doubles through the initial fact sorting and counting work before their fluency practice. Fluent means accurate recall or an efficient mental strategy, without a timer. Observation groups cover all thirty children once on days 53 to 54 and once on days 66 to 67. Check days reuse those records.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all text aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair ten, ones, remaining and total with pictures. Offer "I moved __; __ stayed." Make groups differ by shape as well as color. Use large tactile mats and neutral dictation; never supply a strategy during assessment. Extend by showing two valid methods and explaining which fits the same numbers better, within twenty.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, use a thirty-five-minute path: five retrieval, twenty modelling and paper work, five discussion and five exit. Replace partner talk with an adult listening. On product days prioritize field 5's work and later revision; one child needs one brief mental observation. Day 68 keeps its two fresh items and retained records. With one projector, display one picture while every child works on paper; no device is required.
