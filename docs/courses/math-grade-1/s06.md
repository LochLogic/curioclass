## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we say how long something is?"
- **Hook, day 111:** Three classroom strips lie with staggered starting points. Which is longest? Children try moving them, then ask the compelling question. Adapt the direct-comparison opening named in field 10.
- **Supporting questions:** Why align endpoints (111)? Can another strip help (112)? What makes a measurement valid (116)? Why do unit sizes change counts (117)? How does a bar show the missing quantity (122)?
- **Closing claim:** "I can compare lengths, measure with equal units, count and record through 120, and show what is missing in a story." The Length, Measurement, Number and Story records establish this on day 128.
- **Teaching:** Model endpoint alignment, then release comparisons on day 113. Connect units to numerals before long counts. Match objects before drawing bars. Teacher pages explain transitivity, iteration, the crossing of 100, and why a situation equation can differ from a solution equation. Section 6.1 maintains earlier arithmetic and completes length, counting and story coverage.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all prompts aloud; require no independent reading. Pair longer, shorter, unit, whole and unknown with pictures. Offer "This is longer because __" and "The unknown is __". Use raised endpoints, large pieces, high contrast and neutral dictation. Accept pointing to a labelled picture. Regroup from current errors. Extend by proving two different correct arrangements measure the same whole length; keep equal units and whole-number spans.
