## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1.NBT.C.4** (days 104, 105, 106, 107): Add within 100, including adding a two-digit number and a one-digit number, and adding a two-digit number and a multiple of 10, using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method and explain the reasoning used. Understand that in adding two-digit numbers, one adds tens and tens, ones and ones; and sometimes it is necessary to compose a ten.
- skill m1-add-within-100 (grade 1)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Supporting questions:** Which units combine (day 97)? Where does the new ten come from (day 100)? Can we predict an exchange (day 102)? Why do different methods agree (day 106)?
>
> - **Practices:** MP.7, day 97, Like-unit pairs: sort and combine tens and ones, explaining the units. MP.3, day 106, Two methods: compare adding tens first with combining ones first for 38 + 24, and answer a partner's objection using the drawings.

Restore them on day 100, connecting the double ten-frame's filled ones to a new ten-stick and equation. Withdraw shared ten-frames on day 101, retaining drawings. Withdraw shared worked drawings on day 106; individual models remain available. Pass tens-and-ones drawings to unit 6.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read every prompt aloud; require no independent reading. Pair tens, ones, exchange and equal with pictures. Offer "These ten ones become __; the amount stays __." Use large tactile mats, shape as well as color, movable pieces and neutral dictation. Pair students by the current error, then regroup from exits. Extend within the same standard by finding two addend pairs with the same sum, one requiring an exchange and one not, and explaining why.
