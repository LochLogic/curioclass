## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1.NBT.B.2** (days 79, 80, 81): Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones. Understand the following as special cases:
- **1.NBT.B.2c** (days 79, 80, 81): The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones).
- **1.NBT.C.5** (days 79, 80, 81): Given a two-digit number, mentally find 10 more or 10 less than the number, without having to count; explain the reasoning used.
- **1.NBT.C.6** (days 80): Subtract multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 from multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 (positive or zero differences), using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method and explain the reasoning used.
- skill m1-two-digit-place-value (grade 1)
- skill m1-ten-more-less (grade 1)
- skill m1-subtract-tens (grade 1)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Supporting questions:** What does one tower count (day 70)? Which digit changes with another ten (day 79)? Which place decides a comparison (day 82)? Can different bundles show equal amounts (day 88)?
>
> - **1.NBT.B.2:** Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones. Understand the following as special cases: Parts, by verb: a (1.NBT.B.2a) 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones - called a “ten.”; b (1.NBT.B.2b) The numbers from 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones.; c (1.NBT.B.2c) The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones).
>
> - **Limits:** Quantities stay below 100; ten-more prompts start below 90. Coverage of 1.NBT.C.5 is partial: starting numbers 90 to 99 still need ten-more teaching, practice and assessment. Subtraction removes whole tens from whole tens, including zero differences. No other two-digit subtraction, three-digit extension, number line or standard algorithm. Unit 5 owns general addition within 100. Day 79 adds one or two whole tens to a two-digit number whose total stays below 100, as repeated ten more, to build 1.NBT.C.5; the method is practice only here and is assessed in unit 5 (1.NBT.C.4).
> - **Carried:** Keep unit 3 towers and ten-sticks. Tie towers to numerals on day 69, drawings to subtraction equations on day 72, and ones to written numerals on day 77. Withdraw blocks for mental work on day 80; drawings follow the mental answer. Remove shared models on day 85; restore them for exchanges on day 87, then withdraw shared help again on day 89. Individual models remain available except during mental evidence. Pass towers and drawings to unit 5. 1.NBT.B.2a is practised on days 69 and 87 (ten ones as one ten, exchanging a ten); 1.NBT.B.2b on days 74 to 76 (a teen as a ten and ones); both were first taught in unit 3, days 47 to 51.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read all prompts and number words aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair ten, ones, digit and equal with drawings. Offer "The tens are __; the ones are __." Use shape as well as color, enlarged tactile mats, movable numeral cards and neutral dictation. Assessment help preserves mental independence. Extend on the same standards by finding several equal tens-and-ones forms below 100 and explaining why each is equal.
