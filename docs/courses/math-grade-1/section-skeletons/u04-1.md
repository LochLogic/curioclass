## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1.NBT.B.2** (days 69, 70, 71, 72, 73): Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones. Understand the following as special cases:
- **1.NBT.B.2c** (days 69, 70, 71, 72, 73): The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones).
- **1.NBT.C.6** (days 71, 72, 73): Subtract multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 from multiples of 10 in the range 10-90 (positive or zero differences), using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method and explain the reasoning used.
- **1.NBT.C.5** (days 71): Given a two-digit number, mentally find 10 more or 10 less than the number, without having to count; explain the reasoning used.
- skill m1-two-digit-place-value (grade 1)
- skill mk-teen-ten-ones (grade K, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill m1-subtract-tens (grade 1)
- skill m1-ten-more-less (grade 1)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 69:** Offer forty loose paper squares. How could someone count them without starting over? Ask the compelling question. Field 10 names the opening source.
> - **Supporting questions:** What does one tower count (day 70)? Which digit changes with another ten (day 79)? Which place decides a comparison (day 82)? Can different bundles show equal amounts (day 88)?
>
> - **1.NBT.B.2:** Understand that the two digits of a two-digit number represent amounts of tens and ones. Understand the following as special cases: Parts, by verb: a (1.NBT.B.2a) 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones - called a “ten.”; b (1.NBT.B.2b) The numbers from 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones.; c (1.NBT.B.2c) The numbers 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine tens (and 0 ones).
>
> - **Practices:** MP.7, day 72: Tens removal links crossed-out towers to equations, including zero differences. MP.6, day 85: Order the cards requires reading each symbol statement aloud and identifying its decisive digit.
> - **Carried:** Keep unit 3 towers and ten-sticks. Tie towers to numerals on day 69, drawings to subtraction equations on day 72, and ones to written numerals on day 77. Withdraw blocks for mental work on day 80; drawings follow the mental answer. Remove shared models on day 85; restore them for exchanges on day 87, then withdraw shared help again on day 89. Individual models remain available except during mental evidence. Pass towers and drawings to unit 5. 1.NBT.B.2a is practised on days 69 and 87 (ten ones as one ten, exchanging a ten); 1.NBT.B.2b on days 74 to 76 (a teen as a ten and ones); both were first taught in unit 3, days 47 to 51.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
