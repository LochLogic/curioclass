## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1.NBT.C.4** (days 96, 97, 98, 99): Add within 100, including adding a two-digit number and a one-digit number, and adding a two-digit number and a multiple of 10, using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method and explain the reasoning used. Understand that in adding two-digit numbers, one adds tens and tens, ones and ones; and sometimes it is necessary to compose a ten.
- skill m1-add-no-new-ten (grade 1)
- skill mk-teen-ten-ones (grade K, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 96:** Show 24 paper squares grouped in tens. Compare adding three singles with adding three ten-strips. What changes? Ask the compelling question; return to it when ones fill a ten. Source: field 10.
> - **Supporting questions:** Which units combine (day 97)? Where does the new ten come from (day 100)? Can we predict an exchange (day 102)? Why do different methods agree (day 106)?
>
> - **Practices:** MP.7, day 97, Like-unit pairs: sort and combine tens and ones, explaining the units. MP.3, day 106, Two methods: compare adding tens first with combining ones first for 38 + 24, and answer a partner's objection using the drawings.
> - **Carried:** Tie towers and drawings to equations on day 96; teacher records methods on day 97 and children do so on day 98. Withdraw shared towers on day 99. Restore them on day 100, connecting the double ten-frame's filled ones to a new ten-stick and equation. Withdraw shared ten-frames on day 101, retaining drawings. Withdraw shared worked drawings on day 106; individual models remain available. Pass tens-and-ones drawings to unit 6.

### U9. Supports and extension

Extend within the same standard by finding two addend pairs with the same sum, one requiring an exchange and one not, and explaining why.
