## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1.OA.A.2** (days 54, 55, 56): Solve word problems that call for addition of three whole numbers whose sum is less than or equal to 20, e.g., by using objects, drawings, and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem.
- **1.OA.B.3** (days 54, 55, 56, 57): Apply properties of operations as strategies to add and subtract. Examples: If 8 + 3 = 11 is known, then 3 + 8 = 11 is also known. (Commutative property of addition.) To add 2 + 6 + 4, the second two numbers can be added to make a ten, so 2 + 6 + 4 = 2 + 10 = 12. (Associative property of addition.)
- **1.OA.C.6** (days 54, 55, 56, 57): Add and subtract within 20, demonstrating fluency for addition and subtraction within 10. Use strategies such as counting on; making ten (e.g., 8 + 6 = 8 + 2 + 4 = 10 + 4 = 14); decomposing a number leading to a ten (e.g., 13 – 4 = 13 – 3 – 1 = 10 – 1 = 9); using the relationship between addition and subtraction (e.g., knowing that 8 + 4 = 12, one knows 12 – 8 = 4); and creating equivalent but easier or known sums (e.g., adding 6 + 7 by creating the known equivalent 6 + 6 + 1 = 12 + 1 = 13).
- **1.NBT.B.2a** (days 57): 10 can be thought of as a bundle of ten ones - called a “ten.”
- **1.NBT.B.2b** (days 57): The numbers from 11 to 19 are composed of a ten and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones.
- skill m1-three-addends (grade 1)
- skill m1-properties-strategies (grade 1)
- skill m1-add-sub-within-20 (grade 1)
