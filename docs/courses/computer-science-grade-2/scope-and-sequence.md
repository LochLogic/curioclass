# Scope and sequence: Computer Science, Grade 2

Course `computer-science-grade-2`, grade 2, draft, 2026-11-26. Written from docs/courses/computer-science-grade-2/frame.md; the days live in data/plans/computer-science-grade-2/days.csv.

**Calendar.** 32 course days, one 30-minute session a week, so course day n falls in week n. `school_day` is filled as
5n (the last school day of week n), so week = (school_day - 1) // 5 + 1 = n for every day. Computer science is a
special in grade 2 and does not count against the classroom teacher's stagger (CALENDAR.md rule 1). Every session from
day 1 opens with a three-minute retrieval task (day 1's is grade 1's part names, as the frame's Seams table sets), and
card and device sessions keep their last four minutes to put cards, desk mats and devices away.

**Readiness and retrieval.** Every earlier-grade row the course assumes is a grade 1 row (the frame's Seams table:
g1-hardware-parts, g1-responsible-use, g1-keep-safe, g1-split-task, g1-change-steps, g1-changing-things,
g1-code-sequence, g1-explain-events, g1-fix-sequence, g1-two-kinds-data, g1-data-questions, g1-patterns-ai,
g1-computing-around, g1-then-and-now, g1-people-or-machine), last used in grade 1 computer science, so each opens as a
retrieval task inside the session that first uses its grade 2 row, and no unit has a readiness check. Later openings
bring back this year's earlier units (the `retrieves` column).

**Device days and the paper path.** The frame's six device sessions are days 2, 14, 15, 16, 17 and 18. Day 2 uses any
school device with a text box and a Curio Class page that plays a tone. Days 14 to 18 are built for the picture-block
editor mode (event block, counted repeat, step-through), which is queued with tool work (later.csv L148) and does not
exist yet: until it does, those days run on their paper path. The paper path is a printed desk mat per pair (a small
grid with a paper token as the robot) for movement programs such as the square; for dance programs the partner acts
the cards out at the desk, seated or standing in place. The taped floor grid is used only by the teacher at the front
(the day 13 anchor, the day 16 worked bug, the day 19 sample act-outs), so no pair waits for a turn on it, and the
four put-away minutes return cards and mats to the pair envelope. On day 14 the program's score is a labeled cup
"score" by the mat whose value card the teacher changes after each run (labeled, not coded). The frame shows that path meets E2-PRO-PD-04, E2-PRO-TR-07 and E2-PRO-RD-06 in full, so
no grade 2 id is part met for lack of a device. Day 25's optional one-screen sorter is the on-device sorter trainer
(also L148); the class itself is the sorter meanwhile, which meets E2-ALG-ML-02 in full (only grade 5's E5-ALG-ML-02
waits on the trainer).

**Sources not yet loaded.** The archived Teach Computing Curriculum (2023) is not in the source store yet (later.csv
L149), so the frame and these days stay provisional where they lean on it: days 1, 2 and 3 (Connecting computers
lessons 1 to 3), 14 and 15 (Sequencing sounds lessons 1 to 4), 16 and 17 (Events and actions lessons 3, 5 and 6) and 24
(Branching databases lessons 1 and 2), all placed from lesson titles. No source map is written yet: the frame's Source
map table holds the placement until the store folder is made (For the lead 3).

## Unit 1. Inputs, outputs and our choices online (days 1 to 6, weeks 1 to 6)

Concept area: systems and security. Arc: the parts of a device by name, sorted input or output, and the system-diagram
(day 1); one real press traced from input to output on a device (day 2); the benefit-risk-chart for one pictured tool
(day 3); what happens to a post on a paper pretend page, placed on the chart (day 4). The diagram comes before the device
so the trace has a picture to land on, and the chart comes before online consequences because it is that row's model_in.
Practices: IC3 (days 2, 3, 4), ESR1 (day 3), ESR2 (day 4, the copied-picture card), IC5 (day 2 pair roles).

Assessment: the end-of-unit assessment on day 5 (sort new part cards and place a new action on the diagram; one benefit,
one harm and a remedy for a new tool; spoken, what happens next to a new post). E2-SYS-HW-11's spoken part, how the parts
work together for an unseen action, is heard during the partner work of days 3 and 4, fifteen children each, about six
minutes, after days 1 and 2 taught it: each child explains one new action card (a press, the part, what changes) to the
teacher, and both days' goals name the task. Response time is day 6. No readiness check (all retrieval).

Candidate settled: kept, the base's "how do devices help us" lesson as the helps column on day 3, zero added days.
Sensitivity: online safety (day 4, health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word) and screen-time
harms (day 3, no home questions).

## Unit 2. Steps that repeat (days 7 to 10, weeks 7 to 10)

Concept area: algorithms and design. Arc: trace a worked step-card dance with a start card and a repeat card (day 7),
modify one card and predict (day 8), make a pair algorithm another pair follows (day 9), then judge a classroom rule for
fairness (day 10). Trace, modify, make in that order on one construct; the fairness day sits with the algorithm it judges
and gives unit 4 its first words for "left out". Days follow load: three sessions for the repeat row, one for the
fairness row. Practices: CT6 (day 7), CT8 and IC3 (day 9), ESR1 (day 10).

Assessment: the mid-year course-level check on day 11 stands for this unit's end-of-unit assessment, and day 12 is the
unit's respond day (the frame's Days). Day 9's pair algorithm has a three-line product look-for, by exception and
unscored, that day 12 uses: it has a start card; the repeat holds the steps that repeat; it was tried by the other pair
and changed when it went wrong. The repeat count stays at 2 to 4, counted pass by pass (math teaches equal groups in weeks
27 to 29).

Candidate settled: kept, a drawing algorithm (a square) beside the dance on day 9, zero added days. Sensitivity: day 10's
fast-runner rule (disability; NCDJ wording from the sensitivity read).

## Unit 3. Programs that repeat (days 13 to 20, weeks 13 to 20)

Concept area: programming. Arc: named boxes (day 13); a worked block program with an event and a repeat, traced, then
with blanks, its score labeled (day 14); modify the count or the event (day 15); debug one bug at a time (day 16); make
the pair's own unit 2 algorithm into a program and test it (day 17); tell the steps taken and whose idea each part was
(day 18). The named box opens the unit so the score on day 14 can be labeled; trace, blanks, modify, debug, make, then
explain the make, since the explain row needs a program the child built. The anchor (one clap too many) is shown on day
13 and solved on day 16. Days follow load: four rows, three with device versions, eight days. Practices: CT7 (day 13),
CT8 (day 17), CT6 and CT9 (day 16), IC5 (days 14 to 17: the navigator reads and lays the cards, the driver, or the
robot on the paper path, runs them; switch at the halfway signal), IC3 and ESR2 (day 18).

Assessment: the end-of-unit assessment on day 19 (label three new pieces of information; lay block cards for a new
algorithm; find and fix one bug; spoken, the steps taken and whose idea), response time day 20. E2-PRO-RD-06 is met
twice: the block look-for on day 17 (after days 14 and 15 taught what each block does) and the spoken steps on day 19.
Day 17's pair program also has a three-line product look-for, by exception and unscored, that day 20 uses: it has a
start (event); the repeat holds the steps that repeat; it was tested and changed when it went wrong. Day 19 stays the
scored code item.
ELA bridge on day 18 (the sentence frame is taught here). Unit 3 spans core check weeks 14 to 16 and 18 to 20 and holds
no course-level check; day 19 is a unit assessment in a special's slot.

Candidate settled: kept, the base's debugging order (test, spot, fix) inside day 16, zero added days.

## Unit 4. Fair questions and a sorting machine (days 21 to 25, weeks 21 to 25)

Concept area: data and analysis, with machine learning. Arc: number and word data in a two-column table beside a
picture-graph (day 21); refine a vague question (day 22); ways of collecting and who they leave out (day 23); a class
sorter learns from a training set (day 24); an uneven training set and its uneven mistakes (day 25, the anchor). The
table comes first, then questions of it, then how asking leaves people out, so the training set arrives as data that can
also leave a kind out. Days follow load: one each for the data-table and the question, one for collection bias, two for
the new model. Practices: CT7 (days 21, 24), CT6 (day 22), ESR1 and HCD10 (days 23, 25), IC3 (day 21). Math leans are
met (math grade 2 unit 1, weeks 1 to 4).

Assessment: the year-end course-level check on day 26 stands for this unit's end-of-unit assessment, and day 27 is the
unit's respond day. E2-DAT-DI-09 is heard from fifteen children on each of days 23 (the last 5 minutes) and 25 (the
last 7 minutes), after day 22 taught it: each child refines one new vague prompt card, said or drawn to the teacher,
and both days' goals name the task. Day 24, the training set's first day, carries no look-for so the new model's day
stays whole. Day 25 carries `observe` as the close of that observation, a real record, and day 26 records it without
collecting again.

Candidate settled: kept, the base's yes or no questions as the sorter's look-for on day 24, zero added days.
Sensitivity: data about things only, no named child, sorter mistakes about birds and leaves.

## Unit 5. People who made computing tools (days 28 to 32, weeks 28 to 32)

Concept area: computing and society. Arc: familiar machines matched to the problems they solve (day 28); case cards of
the abacus, khipu and braille on a timeline, with what problem each solved or what idea it helped people record or
share (day 29); an interest linked to computing in jobs (day 30). One session each,
since the three ids ask for different cases. Each society day gives two views with one dated source each. Practices:
ESR1 (days 28, 29), HCD10 (days 28, 29), IC3 (days 28 to 30).

Assessment: the end-of-unit assessment on day 31 (match two new machines; place a new case card and say what problem the
tool solved or what idea it helped its people share; spoken, an interest and a computing job), response time day 32.
Each case card on the timeline is a span, not a point: from the earliest use its source documents to today where the
source says it is still used. The Computer History Museum's abacus page gives no origin date (used for centuries,
still common today), so the abacus is drawn as the longest span; khipu as the Smithsonian National Museum of the
American Indian page words it (the Inka period and earlier Andean peoples; that page is a "browser check" for the
lead); braille from the 1820s to today (Library of Congress). Braille and khipu carry E2-SOC-HI-14's "express ideas"
part (reading and writing, records), the abacus its problem part. Week 31 is a core check week; this is a unit assessment
in a special's slot.

Candidate settled: dropped, a visit by a school worker who uses computing; picture cards carry the row and a visit needs
the school's own arrangement, zero days. Sensitivity: Native and disability content on day 29 (the frame's starting pack
for the sensitivity read; the NFB braille page is a "browser check" for the lead).

E2-SYS-SE-12 is sampled on the mid-year check only: the year-end check drops it so its paper part fits 10 minutes.

**How each assessment day runs** (days 5, 11, 19, 26 and 31). Not at once: first the paper items, read aloud to the
whole class together, about 10 minutes, collected from every child; then the day's one spoken item heard in five
groups of six, about 3 minutes a group (about 15 minutes), while the rest of the class does a quiet unscored picture
task (draw your own step-card, or color the next unit's anchor card). With the three-minute retrieval that is 28 of 30
minutes, so the retrieval stays only while the paper part keeps to 10 minutes.

Neither check follows a separate end-of-unit assessment: each is its unit's end-of-unit assessment, so its unit items
are unseen and its earlier-unit items are new forms, never the cards of days 5 or 19 (both more than two weeks before).

**Owned codes not sampled on a course-level check**, and why: E2-SYS-IM-13 (unit 1's day 5 record is carried unscored
on day 11; the mid-year check holds one spoken item already); E2-PRO-PD-04 and E2-PRO-RD-06 (unit 3's day 19 record,
each child's laid program and spoken steps, is carried unscored on day 26, where the one spoken item is E2-DAT-IM-10);
E2-SOC-HU-15, E2-SOC-HI-14 and E2-SOC-CE-16 (unit 5 follows the year-end check and is checked by its own day 31). The
frame sets both checks this way; any change is the frame's.

Check weeks 11 and 26 sit off the grade 2 core check weeks (14 to 16, 18 to 20, 28, 29, 31, 32). In the after-core
calendar, week 11 also holds health's mid-year check and week 26 visual arts' and SEL's year-end checks; computer science
is a special, so rule 1 does not count it (For the lead 2).

## Shorter path

**A shorter period (25 minutes).** The three-minute retrieval and the four put-away minutes stay; the cut comes from
practice time. Device days keep about 8 to 15 minutes at the keys (day 2 and days 14 to 18), the worked example and its
trace are never cut, and the five assessment days (5, 11, 19, 26, 31) open with settling and instructions, not a
retrieval task, at every length: 10 minutes of paper items read aloud, then 15 minutes of spoken groups.

**A year about 10 school days short (two weeks, two sessions).** Two fold-in days, both Fluency, nothing assessed and no
owned part on them alone:

| Fold-in day | Folds into | Minutes saved |
|---|---|---|
| 8 (modify the dance) | Day 9's opening: each pair changes one card of the worked dance and predicts before writing its own | 30 |
| 15 (modify the program) | Day 16's opening: change the repeat count of the worked program, run, compare, before the bugs | 30 |

Each fold-in replaces the host day's three-minute retrieval opening and takes about five minutes in all (one card or
one block changed, predicted and run), so the host day keeps its own teaching. That is 2 of the frame's 32 days (30
kept), 60 minutes. E2-ALG-PS-01 stays on days 7, 9 and 11; E2-PRO-PD-04 on days 14, 17 and 19. The shorter-year rule, the same from kindergarten to grade 5: a respond day after an end-of-unit sitting may fold into the next day's opener with a self-check; a respond day after a course-level check never folds; check days keep their weeks, and a lost week before a check moves the teaching days, never the check. The course-level checks keep weeks 11 and 26.
