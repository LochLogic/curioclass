# Course frame: Computer Science, Grade 2

Course `computer-science-grade-2` (catalog id `cs-k12`), grade 2, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written on the shape of the kindergarten pilot (docs/courses/computer-science-kindergarten/frame.md and its fix report); the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, one for each concept area at its grade 2 depth, in the order the ideas build: inputs, outputs and
  our choices online (systems and security, so the input-to-output picture and the device rules sit before the first
  program), steps that repeat (algorithms, the first event and repeat on step-cards, and whether a rule is fair),
  programs that repeat (programming, turning unit 2's step-cards into block programs, then debugging and explaining
  them), asking fair questions and training a sorter (data, with machine learning on the class's own data cards),
  and people who made computing tools (computing and society, the year's closer).
- **Days.** 32 course days: 28 in units, 2 for course-level checks (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). One lesson a week of about 30 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  kindergarten to grade 2 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a specials
  rotation, so course day n falls in week n. The 30 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens
  with a three-minute retrieval task from the second session on (APPROACH.md, Pacing), and card and device
  sessions keep four minutes at the end to put cards, desk mats and devices away, so a session teaches for about 23
  minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing), with
  the pilot's one shape for a unit that a course-level check follows directly: in units 2 and 4 the check stands for
  the unit's own end-of-unit assessment and the check's respond day is the unit's respond day (pilot For the lead 2).
  So units 1 to 5 teach on 4, 4, 6, 5 and 3 days, 22 of the 32. Days follow the load of the rows: unit 3 owns four
  programming rows, three of them with device versions and a worked, blanks, modify and make order, and gets 8 days;
  unit 4 owns three rows and four ids, with a new model (the training set) taught over two sessions, and gets 5;
  unit 5 owns three ids whose boundaries ask for different cases (familiar machines today, tools from other places and
  times, the children's own interests), one session each, and gets 5; unit 2's two rows get 4 teaching sessions, three
  of them for the repeat (trace, modify, make). 32 sessions for 14 rows is the progression read's "about two sessions
  a row", counted with checks (docs/reviews/progression-computer-science-2026-11-19.md). Each course-level check takes
  one check session and one respond session. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are no mid-unit assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The grade 2 core checks take weeks 14 to 16, 18 to 20, 28, 29, 31 and 32 (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md,
  target weeks: ELA 15 to 16 and 32, math 18 to 19 and 29, science 20 and 28, social studies 14 and 31). This course's
  checks sit in week 11 (mid-year, after unit 2) and week 26 (year-end, after unit 4), with their respond days in weeks
  12 and 27 (respond days are not check days). Week 26, not 27, holds the year-end check so that unit 5 has the five
  weeks 28 to 32 its three society ids need, as kindergarten's unit 5 keeps weeks after its year-end check. Unit 3 runs
  across weeks 13 to 20, which include the core check weeks 14 to 16 and 18 to 20, because it holds no course-level
  check; unit 5's own end-of-unit assessment falls in week 31, a unit assessment, not a course-level check. Overlaps
  with other after-core subjects' check weeks are the lead's calendar pass (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** The 16 E2 ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, all taught here (STANDARD.md
  section 2): E2-ALG-PS-01, E2-ALG-ML-02, E2-ALG-IM-03, E2-PRO-PD-04, E2-PRO-VD-05, E2-PRO-RD-06, E2-PRO-TR-07,
  E2-DAT-DC-08, E2-DAT-DI-09, E2-DAT-IM-10, E2-SYS-HW-11, E2-SYS-SE-12, E2-SYS-IM-13, E2-SOC-HI-14, E2-SOC-HU-15,
  E2-SOC-CE-16. Each owner row's boundary was read in the source store's standards PDF for this frame (2026-11-25,
  S:\CurioClass-sources\computer-science\csta-2026-11-21, not copied or quoted), and each is taught at the depth its
  owner row's notes set, in our own words (STANDARD.md "Depth"); "What students learn" says what is out. Plans cite ids
  and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md "Quoting"); the credit line "Standards: 2026 CSTA
  PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any day that leans on a statement. Check set: each unit
  plan checks itself against the computer science bar (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5, items 1 to 9) and the
  lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and reads the partial base unit it adapts for order; the "Check set
  adds" column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. No Specialty
  id, and no X + CS id, is claimed.
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's Practices and concept areas column names the concept area each unit teaches and the CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code
  and our label (STANDARD.md section 3), as bar items 1 and 2 ask. Each unit plan names the practice's day and task.
  Across the year: CT6 define the problem (units 2, 3, 4), CT7 find patterns and make reusable parts (units 3, 4),
  CT8 build and plan (units 2, 3), CT9 test, debug and refine (unit 3), IC3 explain and give feedback (units 1 to 5),
  IC5 be a reliable teammate (units 1, 3, driver and navigator), HCD10 learn from and include different users (units
  4, 5), ESR1 weigh who gains and who is harmed (units 1, 2, 4, 5), ESR2 respect people, credit others' work and
  protect information (units 1, 3). Dispositions are named in the unit plans, not here.
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, archived edition
  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived, key stage 2, resources dated 2023), the same fixed edition the kindergarten
  frame maps (its For the lead 4). Year 3 is the grade 2 age (Year 1 is kindergarten in the pilot). License as the
  pilot states: the Open Government Licence v3.0 "unless expressly stated otherwise" on the publisher's terms page; the
  archived unit pages carry no license line of their own, so each file's license line is read when the store folder is
  made. Adapted unit by unit and rephrased for an American audience (STANDARD.md section 2, Cory 2026-11-16): US
  spelling, school years named as US grades, no money in pounds, no product or company name, our own browser tool or
  paper in place of the named block language. Four of its six Year 3 units feed units 1, 3 and 4 (see Source map); its
  two creating-media units are left out, and the network half of "Connecting computers" and the branching-database
  half of "Branching databases" are offered to grade 3. Units 2 and 5 are our own. The source's name appears only in a
  day's credit line, once a day. Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#teach-computing-curriculum): the source store holds
  no copy of this base, so the Source map below rests on the public unit pages (lesson titles, opened 2026-11-25; the
  lessons' content is read from their titles and unit overviews), and each adapted lesson's check for third-party
  images, text and tool screenshots waits on the store folder (For the lead 2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is the first explicit teaching of anything new, a construct, an idea, a model or a method (a step-card
  dance with a start card and a repeat card, a part-card sort, a given block program traced or predicted and watched
  before it runs, debugging as a method), unplugged or traced. Fluency is a modify
  session: change one card or one block of a working set or program and check it; grade 2 has a little of it (unit 2
  session 2, unit 3 session 3, and the retrieval openings). Application is a make or design session: write a
  step-card algorithm for a dance or drawing and have a partner follow it (unit 2 session 3), or turn an algorithm into
  a working block program, test it and fix it (unit 3 session 5). Check is the end-of-unit assessment or a course-level
  check; Respond acts on it by error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make, and no make day
  before a trace or modify day on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Grade 2 answers by pointing, sorting, ordering, drawing or saying aloud to a partner; the teacher notes
  by exception on a class checklist (APPROACH.md, "The form of daily evidence"); a program is checked on the screen
  or acted out on the paper path during the session. Every end-of-unit assessment is a sitting with unseen items,
  never the unit's own anchor, run in two parts, not at once: first the paper items (sort new part cards, lay block
  cards for a new algorithm, circle the first wrong block, sort new data cards), read aloud to the whole class
  together, about 10 minutes, collected from every child; then one spoken answer a child, heard in five groups of six
  (about three minutes a group: about 20 seconds a child, plus the change-over and the checklist mark; about 15
  minutes), while the rest of the class does a quiet unscored picture task. With the three-minute retrieval that is 28
  of 30 minutes, so the retrieval stays only while the paper part keeps to 10 minutes. A unit's other spoken or individual look-fors are gathered earlier, on
  unseen items, during independent or partner work in the unit's teaching sessions after the row was first taught,
  and the unit or check cell names the sessions, the children heard in each and the minutes; partner talk alone is
  never a child's scored record. Each owned id has its own
  look-for: a "create" id (E2-ALG-PS-01, E2-PRO-PD-04) is met by a child arranging an algorithm or a program, an
  "explain" or "describe" id (E2-SYS-HW-11, E2-ALG-IM-03, E2-PRO-RD-06, E2-SYS-SE-12) by a spoken answer
  (E2-PRO-RD-06 twice: what the event, the repeated steps and the repeat count each do in the child's program, and the
  steps taken to make it), a "use methods" id (E2-DAT-DC-08) by the child sorting data by kind, naming how it was
  collected and what each kind tells, a "develop
  a question" id (E2-DAT-DI-09) by the child's own refined question, never by recognition of a supplied one, and a
  "debug" id (E2-PRO-TR-07) by a child finding and fixing one bug. Units 2 and 4 are assessed in the course-level
  check that follows them (Days). No score comes from a session that first teaches a row. The respond day reteaches by
  error letter and repeats the missed item in a new form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per child: 15 devices for a class of
  30, with the few-device and one-screen versions below for rooms with fewer. Device sessions: 6 of the 32, at least
  grade 1's 5: unit 1 session 2 (one key or button press traced from input to output) and unit 3 sessions 2 to 6 (the
  block program with an event and a repeat: traced, modified, debugged, made, then explained with the pair's own
  program on the screen), with block cards on a printed desk mat per pair as each one's paper path; unit 4 session 5 may also show
  one optional sorting tool on one screen. At about 12 minutes at the device in unit 1 session 2 and 10 to 20 in each
  unit 3 session, that is about 100 minutes of device time, so about 50 minutes per child at the keys over the year
  with pair turns. 26 of the 32 sessions, about four fifths, are away from a device, more than APPROACH.md's "about two
  thirds" for kindergarten to grade 2 (ours, untested): the E2 rows are written unplugged first, the unplugged form
  teaches them as well, and the device days carry the parts that gain from a screen. That sits below APPROACH.md's
  device share; the grade 1 frame's For the lead 6 proposes the line that states it. Every device day is written four ways
  (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at stations with the other pairs on the paper
  version of the same idea, one screen with a child at the device for each step, or no device. No E2 id needs a
  device to be met in full: the boundary of E2-PRO-PD-04 counts coding a robot to dance from an algorithm of arrows,
  so picture-block cards with an event card and a repeat card, run by a partner acting as the robot (a token on the
  pair's desk mat, or the partner acting a dance out at the desk), meet E2-PRO-PD-04 and E2-PRO-TR-07 in full on the paper path; picture cards of parts and a pictured press-to-screen
  chain meet E2-SYS-HW-11; no E2 boundary asks for a computational tool. So the course record never marks a grade 2
  id part met for lack of a device.
- **Paper path.** Every session runs on paper, cards, tape and objects; the block programs of unit 3 have the
  picture-block cards beside them, laid on a printed desk mat per pair (a small grid with a paper token as the robot)
  for movement programs such as the square, while dance programs are acted out by the partner at the desk, seated or
  standing in place. The taped floor grid is used only by the teacher at the front (the anchor, the worked bug, sample
  act-outs on the end-of-unit day), since one grid for 15 pairs leaves most of them waiting. Unit 4's sorter is the class itself
  acting as the machine. No tool asks for a student account, stores a child's name or sends anything off the device
  (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety").
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 2 children read simple words; no code is read alone (APPROACH.md, Reading
  load): blocks and cards carry an icon and a short word, every card is read aloud, and the teacher writes longer
  text. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth, with the everyday words that have a computing meaning
  taught as two meanings (loop, bug, run, label, train). The words input and output are taught with the part cards in
  unit 1 (E2-SYS-HW-11 asks for the right names); repeat and loop in unit 2; the word variable is not used (the E2-PRO-VD-05
  boundary keeps programming words out): a named box is "a box with a label". Sentence frames: "First I ___, then I
  ___" and "I predict it will ___ because ___". A child may answer with arrows, pointing, drawing or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. In this course every unit carries the only ids of its concept area, so units are swapped, not left out:
  unit 5's cases may be swapped for tools the school's own community made or uses (the same three ids, one case a
  session), and unit 4's question prompts for other neutral class prompts, so the course always claims the data ids
  and the society ids (the bar part 5 fails a course with nothing on data or society). Unit 1's pretend page may be
  swapped for another pretend page on the same two consequences. Units 2 and 3 carry the step-card and block-program
  models the rest of the year and grade 3 use and are not swapped. A school that leaves out a unit anyway names in the
  course page and the unit's family note the ids not claimed that year.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. The teacher's floor grid sits in a
  cleared space with tape that lies flat, and a child who walks it in a demonstration walks (no running, no
  blindfolds); pairs work at desk mats, seated or standing in place. Device cords stay behind the table; devices are carried with two hands. Unit 2's dance algorithms use moves done in place (clap, stomp,
  turn, wave), never a spin with eyes closed. The scope and sequence copies these lines into each card, dance and device
  day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $4 (printing part cards,
  system-diagram mats, benefit-risk charts and the paper pretend page), unit 2 $4 (step-card sets with start and repeat
  cards), unit 3 $12 (one roll of masking tape for the teacher's floor grid, 15 printed desk mats with paper tokens, paper cups and labels for the named boxes, printing
  the picture-block cards with event and repeat cards), unit 4 $5 (data cards, data-table sheets, the sorter's picture
  card sets), unit 5 $3 (printing case cards, the timeline strip and interest cards), about $28 in all.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 has online-safety content and screen-time harms: the agent
  checks the online-consequences section carries health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground) and that no card asks what devices or apps a child
  has at home. Unit 2's fairness session touches disability (a rule that needs a fast runner): before it reads, the
  agent studies the National Center on Disability and Journalism's Disability Language Style Guide (ncdj.org/style-guide/)
  and checks that talk stays on the rule, never on a classmate. Unit 4 touches data about people: the agent checks
  every prompt is about class things, not children, homes, food or bodies. Unit 5 has Native and disability content
  (knotted-cord records of the Andes, a reading code for people who are blind): before it reads, the agent studies a
  starting pack: the Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian's khipu material (americanindian.si.edu, the
  page the agent records), one Andean or Quechua community's or scholar's own account of khipu found by the agent, the
  National Federation of the Blind's pages on braille (nfb.org), an organization of blind people, and the NCDJ guide
  above. The agent widens the pack to the brief's five sources, two of them owned by those communities, and records
  them. The course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on in the laboratory sense, so no safety
  agent read is planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity read confirms are on each day.
