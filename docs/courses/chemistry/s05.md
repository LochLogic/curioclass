## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does the energy come from, and where does it go?"
**Hook, day 57:** The teacher activates a closed urea cold pack and a closed sodium acetate snap pack over trays. Students observe cooling and growing crystals without touching contents. Ask the compelling question, collect notices and wonders, and draw tentative energy arrows before naming mechanisms.
**Closing explanation:** Day 62 explains the anchor using observed changes and supplied composition cards: dissolving urea absorbs energy overall, while crystallization releases it. Neither requires new substances. Day 72 adds measured thermal transfer and computational conservation to explain where that energy goes.
Model a boundary when competing explanations need one. After day 60, ask students to defend arrows rather than copy them. Students choose safe investigation conditions and revise expressions; the teacher checks safety and asks what evidence would change a claim.

<!-- skel -->
- **HS-PS1-4:** Model how a reacting system takes in or gives off energy depending on how its total bond energy changes. Emphasis: the reaction treated as a system that sets the energy change; models might be particle drawings, energy level graphs of reactants and products, or diagrams showing energy is conserved. Limit: No computing the overall bond energy change from listed bond energy values.
- **HS-PS3-4:** Plan and carry out an investigation showing that when parts at different temperatures mix in a closed system, energy spreads more evenly among them. Emphasis: analyzing students' own data and describing energy changes in numbers and in concepts; examples: mixing liquids of different temperatures, or placing warm objects in water. Limit: Investigations use only the materials and tools students are given.
- **HS-PS3-1:** Build a computational model that finds the energy change of one part of a system when the other parts' changes and the energy flowing in and out are known. Emphasis: explaining what the math in the model means. Limit: Basic algebra only; two or three parts; only thermal energy, kinetic energy, and energy stored in a gravitational, magnetic or electric field.

**Question board:** Why do two sealed packs change temperature? Days 58 to 60 distinguish thermal transfer from reaction energy. Does warming prove a reaction? Days 61 to 62 compare new substances with changed attractions. Where does released energy go? Days 63 to 67 investigate equilibration. Can we predict an unseen component's change? **Limits:** Keep reaction bonds qualitative; no bond-energy sums, enthalpy algorithms, entropy calculations or rate law. Temperature is not total thermal energy. Equal equilibrium temperatures do not imply equal component energies. A closed system retains matter but can exchange energy; insulation only approximates isolation. No engineering expectation is claimed.
