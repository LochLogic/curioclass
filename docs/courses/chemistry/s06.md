## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do chemists speed up a reaction and get more of what they want from it?"
**Hook, day 75:** Compare two original recordings of identical effervescent tablets in equal water volumes at measured temperatures. Display elapsed time without explaining the difference. Show an original ammonia-production record that levels off while reactants remain. Ask the compelling question before collecting notices, questions and tentative particle drawings.
**Closing explanation, day 85:** Students use repeated timings, supplied progress records and equilibrium trials to explain faster fizzing and the factory plateau. Heating can accelerate reaction without increasing equilibrium product. Nitrogen from air still needs supplied hydrogen; air alone is not fertiliser feedstock.
Model one controlled comparison on day 76 and the forward and reverse distinction on day 81. Then require predictions before evidence, challenge competing explanations and let students choose refinements. Return unresolved questions to the board between lesson sets.

<!-- skel -->
- **HS-PS1-5:** Use science principles and evidence to explain how changing temperature or concentration of reacting particles changes how fast a reaction goes. Emphasis: reasoning about how many collisions happen between molecules and how much energy they carry. Limit: Only simple two-reactant reactions; evidence is temperature, concentration and rate data; rate and temperature linked in words only.
- **HS-PS1-6:** Refine a chemical system's design by naming one change in conditions that would raise the amount of product present at equilibrium. Emphasis: applying Le Chatelier's principle and linking a change made at the visible scale to what molecules do; examples: adding reactant or taking product away. Limit: One variable changed at a time; no calculating equilibrium constants or concentrations.

**Question board:** What changes the fizz time? Days 76 to 78 test temperature. Could crowding particles do the same without heating? Days 79 to 80 compare concentration. Why does product stop increasing while reactants remain? Day 81 models opposing reactions. Can conditions move that balance? Days 82 to 83 compare changes. Which change is worth its costs? Days 84 to 85 test and improve a recommendation. Transfer uses an unfamiliar molecular reaction and a symbolic gas-production system, reserved in field 5.
**Limits:** Section 6.1 teaches HS-PS1-5; sections 6.2 and 6.3 develop and apply HS-PS1-6; day 85 integrates both. Keep rate-temperature relations qualitative. No rate laws, equilibrium constants, equilibrium-concentration calculations or industrial apparatus. Fizz time includes dissolution and gas escape; it is a proxy, not a rate constant. Concentration does not itself make particles faster. Equilibrium requires equal opposing rates, not equal amounts. A catalyst changes approach time, not equilibrium composition.
**Carried:** Keep unit 5's energy-transfer, system-model and computational-model, and unit 4's particle and graph models. Cause-effect begins day 75; day 78 timings and particle records add collision energy, and day 80 concentration evidence separates encounter frequency from speed. Stability-change begins day 75 with the recalled feedback model; day 81 opposing-reaction records revise it into dynamic equilibrium, distinct from homeostasis. Day 82 interventions revise system boundaries. Design-cycle begins day 81 with criteria, gains one-variable tests on days 82 to 83, and changes on day 85 after day 84's recommendation meets new cost evidence. Day 85 establishes consensus across these models; all remain available for transfer.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

**Reserved records, invented for practice:** T1 is a paper-only reaction of invented atom types, R2 + S2 -> 2RS. Its qualitative diagram puts separated atoms highest, reactants intermediate and products lowest. In equal volumes, with S2 fixed, the product amounts formed during the first minute are: 18 degrees Celsius, one relative concentration of R2, 3 units; 33 degrees, one concentration, 7 units; 18 degrees, two concentrations, 6 units. A different reported comparison changes temperature and concentration together. No supplied value is a rate constant.

T2 is an unfamiliar gas system, X2 + Y2 <=> 2XY, at fixed temperature and volume. Initial equilibrium counts are 4, 4 and 4 respectively. Adding 9 X2 groups gives final counts 12, 3 and 6 at cost 3; adding 30 X2 groups gives final counts 32, 2 and 8 at cost 6; a catalyst leaves 4 XY at cost 2 and shortens approach time. All options pass a supplied safety criterion. The budget limit is 4 cost units; rank product amount first among feasible options. Counts represent molecular groups, not measured industrial yields. Keep both records and values out of teaching, tools and exits.

**Evidence sequence:** The individual rate synthesis is made on day 80 from days 76 to 79 records, receives written feedback after class and is revised on day 82. The design is made day 84, receives written feedback and is revised day 85. Neither is first-teaching work. Score only these earlier revised products on day 86. New transfer responses are unscored drafts on day 86; provide written feedback before day 87 revision, then score after class. Twenty-four of 28 points, about 86 percent, require practice with an idea on a new case. No reteaching occurs day 86.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | More concentrated particles move faster. | Frequency is confused with speed. Day 80: contrast denser equal-speed drawings with warmer drawings; revise the speed arrows. | From memory. |
| B | Every collision produces a reaction. | Energy and orientation are omitted. Day 78: compare unsuccessful encounters with an energetic, suitably oriented encounter; add the condition. | From memory. |
| C | Completion time is a rate constant. | An endpoint proxy is confused with a kinetic parameter. Day 77: contrast seconds to a fixed endpoint with product formed per minute; relabel the evidence. | From memory. |
| D | Breaking a bond releases energy. | Separation and formation are reversed. Day 78: redraw separation uphill and formation downhill using unit 5 evidence. | From memory. |
| E | Equilibrium means both reactions stop. | Stable amounts are mistaken for inactivity. Day 81: annotate ongoing equal forward and reverse events. | From memory. |
| F | Equilibrium requires equal reactant and product amounts. | Rate equality is confused with amount equality. Day 81: compare unequal stable inventories alongside equal opposing rates. | From memory. |
| G | A catalyst raises equilibrium product amount. | Approach time is confused with equilibrium position. Day 83: compare two approach curves ending at the same plateau. | From memory. |
| H | A faster reaction must have more product at equilibrium. | Kinetics and final balance are conflated. Day 84: compare a warmer, faster ammonia record with its lower equilibrium product amount. | From memory. |
| I | Removing product must leave more product in the vessel than before removal. | Newly formed product is confused with vessel inventory. Day 82: track removed, newly formed and remaining amounts separately. | From memory. |

Each row gives its matching wrong example, correct contrast and repair.
