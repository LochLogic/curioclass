- **HS-PS1-3:** Plan and carry out an investigation of bulk properties of substances to infer how strong the electric forces between their particles are. Emphasis: how strong the attractions between particles are, not the names of intermolecular force types; examples: melting and boiling points, vapor pressure and surface tension of ionic, molecular or network solids. Limit: No vapor pressure calculations using Raoult's law.
- **HS-PS2-6:** Explain to others why the structure of a designed material at the molecular level matters for how it works. Emphasis: the attractions and repulsions that make the material work; examples: metal conductors, long-chain flexible materials, medicines shaped to fit receptors. Limit: Only molecular structures given to students for particular designed materials.
