## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why do some liquids hold together and others let go, and how do chemists design materials from that?"

**Hook, day 17:** Show original recordings of equal-volume water, rubbing alcohol and cooking-oil drops on cleaned coins. Pause before outcomes: students predict beading, spreading and disappearance separately. Identify composition, surface and temperature; the persistent oil mark is an observation, not proof of a single attraction ranking.
**Closing explanation, day 29:** Students explain the recorded differences using their observations, supplied phase-property evidence and revised electrical models. Surface interactions affect beading; liquid composition and conditions affect drying. They connect controlled bulk patterns to particle attractions without treating evaporation as a pressure measurement.
Elicit competing models, model measurement and evidence limits on day 19, then let groups choose comparisons. Reveal supplied structures after students identify what bulk observations cannot explain. Step back as students defend revisions.

Day before: inspect room controls, stock safety sheet, glove compatibility and recording conditions; test equal drop volumes. Stop for spills or discomfort. Reserve seven minutes for teacher-directed spill and waste collection, handwashing and surface cleaning; follow local disposal rules.

**Safety and set-up, day 21:** Per group: 10 mL water, one dropper, cleaned coin, cup and tray; the same protective gear. Count drops to a defined overflow endpoint across repeated trials; this is a surface-tension proxy affected by drop size and surface cleanliness. Day before: clean coins, check droppers and remove alcohol stock. No tasting; wipe slip hazards immediately. Teacher handles waste; reserve five cleanup minutes. All other days use recordings, paper or screens, with no chemical handling.
