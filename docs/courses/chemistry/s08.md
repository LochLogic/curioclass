## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What happens inside a nucleus that releases so much energy and keeps time so well?"
**Hook, day 103:** Show original drawings of a mammoth bone and a fingertip-sized fuel pellet, beside the sourced bone-dating report and approximate coal comparison in field 10. Nothing radioactive is handled. Ask the compelling question before students propose mechanisms.
**Closing explanation, day 110:** Use decay evidence to explain dating preserved bone carbon, and nuclear-change diagrams to explain the fuel comparison. Population half-life supports a clock; nuclear changes release much more energy per comparable amount than chemical rearrangements. Real dating also needs initial-carbon estimates, contamination controls and calibration.
Model evidence-based revision on day 104 and particle bookkeeping on day 106. Then ask students to defend changes before offering the class account. Return unresolved questions to the board.

Emphasis: simple qualitative pictures or diagrams, and how large nuclear energy release is compared with other changes. Limit: No calculating the amount of energy released; only alpha, beta and gamma decay.
