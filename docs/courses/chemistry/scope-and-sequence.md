# Scope and sequence: Chemistry

Course `chemistry`, grade 10, draft, 2026-11-14.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/chemistry/days.csv): 160 daily meetings of 50 minutes over 32 weeks, with 149 days in the frame's 11 units and 11 course-level check and respond days. Course day and school day coincide, so `school_day` is blank. Unit order, ownership and lengths follow the frame. Section divisions wait for unit planning, so each unit currently uses `<unit>.1`.

Each unit begins with the frame's phenomenon, a question board and an initial explanatory model. New evidence changes that model before students use the consensus model in a transfer task. Daily notebook evidence informs instruction. The end-of-unit assessment uses an unseen case and has a separate respond day inside the unit. Response is counted once. No unit exceeds 25 days, so none requires a mid-unit assessment; investigations, model revisions and design trials provide evidence between the opening and final assessment.

Every listed assessment code requires evidence for all its parts, including the practice, core ideas and crosscutting element, within its boundary. A short linked prompt addresses each part. Practical and computational portfolios supply performances that cannot be demonstrated by a written answer alone. An unfinished practical performance is recorded as owed, never credited from interpreting supplied results. Unit plans will write items, scoring guidance and precise timing, not add days. The coverage commitments below use the progression and the expectation dimensions in `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv`; state-framework references are crosswalks only.

## Unit 1. The table that predicts

16 course days, days 1 to 16, weeks 1 to 4. Recorded lithium, sodium and potassium reactions beside inactive neon make the class need an explanation beneath the table. Atomic charge and structure precede outer-electron patterns, relative properties and bond predictions. Students revise the initial model on day 5, test the table's predictive history on day 9, then construct and revise explanations for the frame's sodium-chlorine, carbon-oxygen and carbon-hydrogen cases. Consensus on day 14 answers the original puzzle. Main-group cases carry the assessment; there is no live reactive-metal or combustion experiment.

The two owned rows receive unequal work: table and atom modeling dominates days 1 to 9 because later explanations need it; days 10 to 13 focus on prediction, evidence and revision. Readiness: none, with Biology-maintained atom and reaction retrieval on day 1. Mid-unit assessment: none required. End-of-unit assessment day 15 covers HS-PS1-1 and HS-PS1-2 in unseen cases, including all named property and reaction examples across linked prompts. Response is day 16, with unit 2 readiness in its last ten minutes.

Candidate cell: both elements kept. Model relationships based on evidence use day 5; scientific knowledge revised by evidence uses the predicted-gap work on day 9. Cost is those two existing teaching days within 16, zero added days.

Metal conductors, long-chain materials and receptor fit receive distinct days before communication in several forms.

Bulk-properties investigation carries days 19 to 24, more than the three structure-function example days, because choosing, conducting and improving the investigation is itself owned. Readiness is day 16, reteaching day 18. Mid-unit assessment: none required. Candidate cell: substructures determining function and communication in multiple formats both kept. Their cost is the existing days 25 to 28, four of the 15 unit days, with no added day or extra report.

## Unit 3. Water shapes the land

10 course days, days 32 to 41, weeks 7 to 9. Cave growth and frost-split rock require a model linking water properties to Earth materials. Water's uneven molecular charge and attraction connect structure to these properties. Water and rock cycles are rebuilt before students plan mechanical and chemical comparisons. Freezing starts on day 34, while stream-table work at two soil moistures and carbonate-versus-non-carbonate comparisons fill the elapsed freezing time. Day 37 returns to expansion evidence before thawing and combines it with chemical evidence. Supplied dissolving evidence contrasts soluble salt with insoluble rock. Wet-versus-dry rock data extend the model to melting at depth; students do not heat rock. Day 39 joins properties, cycles and plate setting in a consensus explanation.

The one owned row still needs multiple practical performances; days 34 to 37 protect them instead of treating all ten days as a reading unit. Readiness day 31, reteaching day 33. Mid-unit assessment: none required. Day 40 assesses HS-ESS2-5 through carried mechanical and chemical planning and collected evidence plus a new landscape transfer, including freezing, dissolution, transport and the supplied melting evidence. Day 41 responds, then checks scientific notation for unit 4 in the last ten minutes.

Candidate cell: both kept. Choosing control variables is within day 33; linking water structure and properties to surface effects is within days 38 to 39. Cost: three existing days within ten, zero added days. Heat storage, light transmission and flow also enter the day 38 evidence so water is not reduced to dissolution alone.

## Unit 5. Hot packs and cold packs

18 course days, days 57 to 74, weeks 12 to 15. Closed packs generate competing initial energy explanations. Thermal prerequisites are rebuilt before later measurement. The iron-warmer and vinegar-baking-soda reactions supply chemical bond-energy evidence; breaking takes energy and forming releases it. That model is revised before applying attraction ideas to the physical pack changes. Students next plan, conduct, repeat and analyse their own water-mixing and warm-spoon investigations. Only after they have thermal data do they create a two-component computational account, extend it to three components and revise it against new measurements. Day 72 joins the models without conflating physical changes and chemical reactions.

This unit has the largest physical-science allocation because its three rows require a bond model, a student-run investigation and a student-created computational representation. Thermal investigation and analysis use days 63 to 67, computational creation and revision days 68 to 71. Readiness is day 56, with reteaching day 58. Mid-unit assessment: none required. Day 73 assesses HS-PS1-4, HS-PS3-4 and HS-PS3-1 using short transfer prompts and the retained investigation and computational portfolios. Bond-energy arithmetic is outside proficiency work; the computational case is thermal with two or three components and explained expressions. Day 74 responds.

Candidate cell: conservation and transfer of energy and computational-model creation both kept in existing days 67 to 71, five of 18 days, zero added days. A printed account preserves reasoning while a device is unavailable, but actual computational creation is still owed.

## Unit 6. Faster, and more

The model is revised on day 80. Forward and reverse processes then make the class distinguish equilibrium from a reaction stopping, and yield from speed. Supplied fertiliser data support one-variable changes, a ranked-criteria design refinement and revision when new evidence arrives. Day 85 joins the collision and equilibrium models.

Temperature and concentration reasoning use days 76 to 80; equilibrium and design use days 81 to 85. That balance gives the design verb time beyond identifying a condition. Earlier-year atom conservation and unit 5 temperature are retrieval on days 75 and 81; no new readiness slot. Mid-unit assessment: none required. Day 86 assesses both temperature and concentration explanations in HS-PS1-5 and evidence-based refinement of one equilibrium condition in HS-PS1-6, including ranked criteria and trade-offs. No equilibrium constants or concentrations are calculated. Day 87 responds.

Candidate cell: stability and change and evidence-based refinement with trade-offs both kept within days 81 and 84 to 85, three existing days of 13, zero added days.

## Shorter path

For a shorter period, protect the hook, student decisions, evidence collection, model revision, assessment and cleanup. Move repeated recording, diagram finishing and an oral explanation into the next day's retrieval rather than require homework. Use small-group parallel presentations. Prepare trays and pre-measured teacher-dispensed materials before the meeting. Split active practical work from its following analysis; never compress the safety or cleanup interval. A freezing run still needs elapsed time, and device tests still need actual test conditions. Assessment accommodations can reduce copying or permit an oral explanation without reducing the scientific demand.

For a year about ten days short, fold these ten unassessed consolidation days into their named neighbours. The retained day includes the named work, not only its title. Units, owned parts, practical performance days, readiness checks and all assessment/respond days remain. This is an adoption option, not an alteration of the canonical 160 numbered rows.
