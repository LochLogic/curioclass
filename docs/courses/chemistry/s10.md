## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does the carbon from burned fuel go, and what does it change on the way?"

**Hook, day 131:** Display the historical emissions and atmospheric-growth records from field 10, without reservoir labels. Students compare the two quantities, sketch where the difference might go and ask the compelling question. The ocean takes up a share of global emissions over time, not an identifiable quarter of each tank immediately.
**Closing explanation, day 141:** Students use their revised carbon ledger, ocean chemistry evidence and published outputs to explain atmospheric accumulation alongside ocean uptake, declining pH and conditional biological effects.
**Teaching:** Model units and one update on days 132 and 133. Then ask students which evidence warrants each arrow, stepping back as they select comparisons.

Field 4 schedules retrieval. Reuse labeled boundaries, units and evidence-versus-output distinctions throughout; accuracy, not speed, governs fluency.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach reservoir, flow, net, uptake and acidity beside diagrams. Frame: "The evidence changes this arrow because ___." Supply patterned large-print tables, tactile arrows, read-aloud prompts and recorded responses. Keep independent reading below 200 words daily; read longer cards together. Extend by testing sensitivity to one flow while preserving conservation.
