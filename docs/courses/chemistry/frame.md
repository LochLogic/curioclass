# Course frame: Chemistry

Course id `chemistry`, grade 10, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 11 units, each driven by one anchor phenomenon or design problem.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 149 in units and 11 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year: 50-minute lessons, five a week, 32 weeks. Every investigation fits one lesson; a school with double periods may join two. Chemistry owns 21 expectations in 20 rows (chem-energy-device-design carries two), about 7 days an expectation; units with a planned investigation (2, 3, 5), a computational model (5, 10) or a design cycle (7) carry more, and the single-expectation Earth units (3, 8) carry less.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards high school performance expectations Chemistry owns: HS-PS1 (all eight), HS-PS2-6, HS-PS3-1, HS-PS3-3, HS-PS3-4, HS-ETS1-2, and the Earth and space expectations folded in under the modified science domains model (HS-ESS2-2, HS-ESS2-4, HS-ESS2-5, HS-ESS2-6, HS-ESS3-1, HS-ESS3-2, HS-ESS3-5, HS-ESS3-6; Desk card q-hs-science-core). Each expectation is taught in full: practice, core idea and crosscutting concept together, every clarification example named in the row, and inside its assessment boundary. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md (high school Gateway 2). In the Units table the **C3** column, a social studies column, holds for science the practices and crosscutting concepts the unit develops. **Check set adds** holds candidate practice, crosscutting concept and nature of science elements (NGSS appendices F, G and H) that the unit plan confirms or drops. "NGSS" never appears in student, teacher or family copy. Values rule (b): facts and backed sources decide claims; climate change is taught as the evidence shows, with the uncertainty the models themselves state (BASELINE rule 5).
- **Base text.** None. High school science is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"); the open high school units listed there are reference only, for sequence and ideas, never copied (no shared run of 8 words). **How phenomena are chosen:** something a class can see, measure or read as data; puzzling at grade 10 and explainable with the unit's core ideas; not an anchor the grades 6 to 8 base text or Biology already uses (the bath bomb, the flameless heater, the one-way mirror, the double-walled cup, the hail storm, the maple tree, the river dam of Biology unit 9 and the others); a local swap is allowed if it needs the same ideas. **Sources:** public-domain data from federal science agencies (the climate, ocean, ice and geological records), open-licence (CC BY) material checked on its own terms, published numbers redrawn as our own tables and graphs, and data the class collects. No paid text, no library trip, no student account.
- **Labs and materials.** Every investigation has three paths: hands-on with classroom-cheap or household chemicals (vinegar, baking soda, table salt, Epsom salt, antacid tablets, rubbing alcohol, hand warmers), a safe substitute named where one exists; a no-kit path from a provided data set or a recorded run; and, where no class could run it safely (reactive metals, radioactive decay, climate), a Curio Class simulation tool (listed per unit, built later through the tool pipeline, DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Every simulation runs with no account and has a paper path. Course rules: eye protection on every hands-on day that uses a liquid, heat or a chemical; copper solutions are collected in a labelled jar, never poured down the drain; steel wool is handled with gloves. No open flame is required anywhere in the course; heat comes from hot tap water, a kettle the teacher handles, or a hot plate where the school has one. A unit's cost to the school is stated in its unit plan; each hands-on day carries a Safety and set-up section (rubric row S5).
- **Storyline and models.** Each unit opens on its anchor, builds a question board, and ends on a transfer task with a phenomenon the class has not seen. Every unit names its initial class model and the points where evidence revises it.
- **Nature of science, taught on purpose.** Unit 1 (a table that predicted elements before anyone found them), unit 8 (an idea about the atom overturned by evidence) and unit 9 (a forecast stated with its uncertainty).
- **Engineering.** Unit 7 runs a full design cycle and owns HS-ETS1-2 (breaking a problem into smaller ones); units 2, 6 and 11 use the criteria, constraints and trade-offs Biology unit 9 taught.
- **Math readiness.** Algebra I (grade 9) is done, so rearranging formulas, linear fits, correlation and exponential decay are retrieval. Geometry runs the same year and owns the quantity codes (N-Q.A.1 to A.3), which Algebra I already used in part; Geometry teaches N-Q.A.2 and N-Q.A.3 in its unit 4 (weeks 14 to 17) and N-Q.A.1 with geo-modeling-objects-density in its unit 5 (weeks 17 to 21). Each lean has an "if later" line under Cross-subject. pH is taught as powers of ten (grade 8 exponents); logarithms are not used, since Algebra II comes in grade 11.
- **Reading level.** Grade 9 to 10 band; every reading is our own or adapted public-domain text, with words taught before they are needed.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent read. Every hands-on day needs a qualified science teacher's safety and materials read (FOUNDATION 7.3), above all units 2, 4, 5 and 6. Unit 8 (nuclear weapons and power, medical radiation) and units 9 to 11 (climate change, disasters, communities losing land to the sea) need a teacher reader experienced in handling worry and contested public topics. Who reads, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Units

The order runs from the atom out: inside structure and periodic patterns, then the forces between particles that set bulk properties, then water acting on Earth materials, then counting atoms by mass. Energy follows (bonds, heat, accounting), then rates and equilibrium, then a design cycle that uses all of it. The nucleus comes after chemical energy so the scale comparison has something to compare with. The last three units carry the energy and carbon models to the whole planet: climate, the carbon cycle in numbers, and how resources and hazards shape where people live.
