## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can something as soft as water carve rock?"
**Hook, day 32:** Show original cross-sections of a limestone cavern and an ice-filled mountain crack, based on field 10's geological accounts. Students draw competing mechanisms before hearing explanations.
**Closing explanation, day 39:** Explain cave enlargement and frost splitting using collected chemical and expansion evidence, distinguishing the analogues from natural processes. Model safe measurement on day 33; groups then choose comparisons and defend revisions.

**Question board:** Can water change rock without striking it? Days 32 to 33 recover cycles and plan tests. What can freezing and flow change? Days 34 to 35 test physical mechanisms. Does rock type matter? Days 36 to 37 compare chemical change and revise. What else can water's properties explain? Day 38 extends to climate, light and deep melting. Day 39 joins the answers; transfer uses an unfamiliar salt-bearing volcanic coast.
**Limits:** Section 3.1 owns the whole expectation, whose verb is "Plan and conduct". Qualitative polarity, hydrogen bonding and Earth processes only; no equilibrium chemistry, melt calculations or engineering claim. Vinegar accelerates an acid-carbonate analogue, not natural cave growth. Bottle expansion does not prove a boulder's history.
The teacher explains that water molecules are neutral with uneven charge; hydrogen bonds join neighboring molecules, while covalent bonds hold each molecule together. Ice has more open spacing, not larger molecules. Energy can change intermolecular arrangements; dissolved salt separates into hydrated ions. Natural cave water commonly gains carbon dioxide and forms weak carbonic acid. Deep melting requires hot rock and appropriate pressure.
**Carried:** Keep particle-model, data-graph, structure-function and evidence reasoning from unit 2. Initial day 32 water-cycle and rock-cycle drawings gain runoff and sediment evidence on day 35, chemical transport on day 37, and energy and deep melting on day 38. Initial plate-model gains water release above a sinking plate on day 38. Particle drawings gain an open ice arrangement on day 37 and dissolved-ion evidence on day 38. Day 39's consensus joins these models for day 40; none is withdrawn.
