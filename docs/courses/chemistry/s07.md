## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we design a device that turns one form of energy into another, and how do we judge it fairly?"
**Hook, day 88:** Show original recordings of the frame's solar box melting cheese and the comparison water barely warming. Ask the compelling question before students draw energy paths. These unequal loads motivate a fair water-to-water test, not an efficiency claim. No food is handled or eaten in class.
**Closing explanation, day 97:** Explain the box using measured water warming, matched comparisons and revisions: absorbed sunlight increases internal energy; covering reduces air exchange, while insulation slows transfer. Energy also reaches the surroundings. A warmer endpoint alone cannot establish better conversion.
Model a boundary on day 88 and decomposition on day 89; model consistent units on day 91. Thereafter students choose changes, predict, test and defend them. Revisit unanswered board questions after each test.

<!-- skel -->
- **HS-PS3-3:** Design, build and improve a device that converts energy from one form to another while staying within set constraints. Emphasis: judging devices both in words and in numbers; examples: chain-reaction machines, wind turbines, solar cells or ovens, generators. Limit: Quantitative judging only compares total output with a given input; devices are built from materials provided.
- **HS-ETS1-2:** Design a way to solve a large real-world problem by splitting it into smaller problems that engineering can handle.

**Question board:** Why does the box warm differently? Days 88 to 89 separate capture, transfer, insulation and measurement. Which sub-solutions fit together? Day 90 compares options. How can we judge fairly? Day 91 establishes output for given input. Does our design work? Days 92 to 93 build and test. Where does energy go? Day 94 diagnoses losses. Can one change help? Days 95 to 96 refine and retest. Who benefits, at what cost? Day 97 recommends. Transfer concerns a wind-powered evening signal, reserved in field 5.
**Limits:** Section 7.1 teaches decomposition, criteria and energy comparisons. Section 7.2 develops both expectations through construction, testing and refinement. Section 7.3 integrates, checks and responds to both. Provided materials only; quantitative evaluation compares total output with supplied input. No radiation measurement, circuit construction, turbine equations or chemical heaters. Teacher background must distinguish temperature from stored energy, reflection from absorption, and thermal transfer from energy creation.
**Carried:** Keep unit 6's design-cycle and unit 5's energy-transfer and system-model, with data-graph. Initial personal and class energy-path models appear day 88. Day 91's equal-input comparisons add units and boundaries; day 94's test evidence adds losses; day 96's retest revises sub-system connections. Day 97 establishes consensus for recommendation and transfer. Retain particle and computational models for later units; equilibrium is not a mechanism for this device.

**Reserved case, invented for practice:** A community garden needs a wind-powered evening signal. Community lighting normally draws electricity through a distribution network; this signal is a backup during supply interruptions. The mechanical input at the rotor shaft is 2000 joules per cycle. The generator must deliver at least 500 joules electrically, cost no more than six tokens, and have guarded moving parts. Safety is mandatory; among feasible devices prefer reliable delivery, then lower cost. Supplied repeated tests of complete transmission-generator assemblies give: A, original, 400 joules, four tokens, guarded; B, larger generator, 600 joules, seven tokens, guarded; C, reduced-friction transmission, 560 joules, six tokens, guarded; D, unguarded version, 580 joules, five tokens. Each output repeats on three matched cycles. These are full-cycle totals with no net energy retained in the assembly; the remainder reaches the surroundings, ultimately as thermal energy. Supplied storage and light delivery need 500 joules at their input per signal, with no extra token charge. Capture, storage and guarding diagrams are provided, but their interfaces still need checking. No electrical apparatus is used. Keep this case and its values out of teaching and tools.

**Evidence sequence:** Days 89 and 92 first-teaching work is practice. The individual decomposition and recommendation draft is made day 94, receives written feedback after class, and is revised day 97. Day 95's built refinement receives written feedback before day 96's retest; give written feedback on retest interpretation before day 97 revision. Retain original practical observations unchanged and score once on day 98, without requiring another performance. Corrections to explanations do not substitute for an uncompleted physical refinement. Day 98 transfer answers are unscored drafts; give written feedback before day 99 revision and score afterward. Twenty of 24 points, about 83 percent, use practice with an idea on the new case. No reteaching on day 98.

**Teacher judgment:** Award only the listed components, tied to evidence rather than vocabulary. Item 1 meets: "C supplies 560 J, above 500, at cost six with guards. Its 0.28 output fraction exceeds A's 0.20. Motion becomes electrical energy, later light and thermal energy; 1440 J goes elsewhere without disappearing. Repeat equal-input trials. D's output cannot excuse exposed moving parts. The community needs the distribution network to light shared spaces after dark; interruptions remove that lighting. Reduced losses support a backup signal but changing transmission costs more." Does not: "D wins because 580 is largest; safety is optional" (E). Item 2 meets: "Separate capture, transmission and conversion, storage and light delivery, and guarding. Test C's 560 J at 2000 J input, then test whether storage delivers one signal from its specified 500 J input. The connection passes the same energy onward, not a second supply. C leaves 60 J above the need but costs two tokens more than A. A working generator alone does not establish delivery after storage or safe operation." Does not: "Each part works separately, so the whole system must work" (D). Earlier-product look-fors are the four components in the rows; require dated feedback and revision.
