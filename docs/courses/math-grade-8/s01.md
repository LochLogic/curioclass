## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we tell that two figures are the same shape and size?"

**Hook, day 1:** In eight minutes, match a paper triangle to a turned copy, a flipped copy and a doubled copy. Which matches without stretching? Ask the compelling question before naming moves.

**Questions:** What information determines a move (day 5)? What stays unchanged (day 8)? What makes a congruence argument complete (day 14)? Why do triangle angles form a straight angle (day 17)?

**Closing claim:** Students justify sameness through reproducible rigid moves and preserved measurements, using day 14's argument, day 18's pattern and day 19's assessment.

**Teaching:** Model reference points on day 3; compare routes on day 5; step back on day 8. Day 10 retains half-turns about line intersections for vertical angles and about midpoints between transversal intersections for parallel lines, preparing day 15. Keep its translation task. Day 17 distinguishes measurement from general argument.



**Practices:** MP5, day 8: choose tracing paper, ruler or protractor to test a line, segment, angle and parallel pair under each motion. MP3, day 14: defend a complete matching sequence and challenge an equal-area claim.

**Fluency:** No owned standard specifies expected fluency. Days 4, 7 and 13 practice already taught moves through repeated construction and partner checking without time pressure. Balance follows the frame: nine understanding, three fluency and six application days.

**Limits:** Experimental verification and informal arguments, without formal congruence proofs. Coordinate effects begin here; unit 2 completes 8.G.A.3 and the angle-angle part of 8.G.A.5. Supporting 6.G.A work measures sides of moved polygons on days 6 and 7.
For extension, find two different sequences for one match and explain why both work.

**Carried:** Tie tracing-paper moves to written instructions on day 5; students handle the overlay through day 12; withdraw the required overlay on day 13. Tie the coordinate plane to ordered pairs on day 6; withdraw supplied arrows on day 7. Retain blank grids and access aids into unit 2.

**Teacher judgment:** Record each component separately. Each item has its own look-fors and samples. Construction samples below include the stated drawings and comparisons.

- **S1:** Look for all three endpoint pairs, straight supporting lines and experimental length comparisons. Meets: "My three separate drawings give (-5,2),(0,2) on y = 2; (7,1),(2,1) on y = 1; and (-1,-7),(-1,-2) on x = -1. A maps to the first point and B to the second each time. Counting grid intervals gives five units for AB and each image; a straightedge confirms each full line." Does not: "Both endpoints move equally, so length stays five," with only a translated segment and no reflected or rotated lines.
- **S2:** Look for both labeled rays and the vertex under every move, with measured or traced angle comparisons. Meets: "I draw ray r along the positive horizontal axis and ray s 47 degrees above it. Translation puts the vertex at (2,1), with rays directed at 0 and 47 degrees. Reflection puts it at (0,0), with rays at 180 and 133 degrees. Rotation puts it at (0,0), with rays at 90 and 137 degrees. My labeled r and s images measure 47 degrees apart in each drawing, matching the original." Does not: "The rotated angle is 137 degrees" (G), with no other constructions.
- **S3:** Look for three constructed image pairs and an experimental parallelism check for each. Meets: "I draw the original horizontal lines y = -4 and y = 3. Translation gives y = -3 and y = 4, reflection gives y = -4 and y = 3, and rotation gives x = 4 and x = -3. On each copy I check directions with a right-angle corner against the grid; both lines have the same direction and stay seven grid units apart." Does not: "One rotated line is horizontal and the other vertical, so they are perpendicular" (H).
- **S4:** Look for a reproducible sequence, every vertex correspondence, the rigid-motion reason and the rectangle counterexample. Meets: "Rotate 90 degrees counterclockwise about (0,0): A, B, C become (-1,1),(-1,4),(-3,1). Translate each five units up to D(-1,6), E(-1,9), F(-3,6). Thus A maps to D, B to E and C to F. Rigid moves preserve lengths and angles and match the whole triangle, establishing congruence. Any other sequence whose image of every vertex is verified to land on D, E and F also meets. Both rectangles have area 12, but a side of six cannot match any side of the three-by-four rectangle." Does not: "Turn and slide them until they match. The rectangles are congruent because their areas agree" (J).
- **P1:** Look for the auxiliary parallel line, the alternate interior equality justified by a half-turn, and general triangle-sum and exterior-angle arguments; the revision adds corresponding and same-side interior sentences. Short Meets: "I draw a line through A parallel to BC. A half-turn about the midpoint of AB makes the alternate interior angles equal, so angle B sits beside angle A on the line. The straight angle at A holds the three angles, so the sum is 180 degrees. Since ACD and ACB make a straight angle, the exterior angle ACD is 180 minus C." Full model, not the threshold: "My drawing has B left of C, A above BC, P,A,Q on the parallel line in that order, T beyond A on ray BA, and D beyond C on ray BC. A half-turn about the midpoint of AB maps B to A and angle ABC to PAB. These alternate interior angles are equal. Vertical angles PAB and TAQ are equal, so corresponding angles TAQ and ABC are equal. PAB and BAQ form a straight angle, so same-side interior angles BAQ and ABC sum to 180 degrees. A half-turn about the midpoint of AC similarly gives QAC = ACB. On straight line PAQ, PAB + BAC + CAQ = 180 degrees; substitution gives ABC + BAC + ACB = 180 degrees. Since ACD + ACB = 180 degrees, exterior ACD equals BAC + ABC. No measurements depend on this triangle's shape." Does not: "My protractor proves every triangle totals 180 degrees" (L), with no auxiliary line or transversal and exterior-angle arguments.
- **P2:** Look for a reproducible pattern, all vertex correspondences and a gap-free repeating unit. Meets: "Label (0,0),(6,0),(0,4) as A,B,C. A half-turn about (3,2) maps them to D(6,4), E(0,4), F(6,0), with A to D, B to E and C to F. The two triangles share the segment from (6,0) to (0,4) and fill the six-by-four rectangle without overlap. Translating six right sends A,B,C to (6,0),(12,0),(6,4); translating four up sends them to (0,4),(6,4),(0,8). Apply these translations to both halves. Repeated horizontal and vertical shifts put rectangles edge to edge in rows and columns, so the congruent triangles cover the plane without gaps." Does not: "Turn copies around and repeat them. They look as though they fit," with no center, correspondences or gap-free unit (F).

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Any line dividing equal areas is a symmetry line. | Equal areas do not ensure folded superposition. Repair: fold a nonsquare rectangle on a diagonal. | From memory. |
| B | The first coordinate gives vertical position. | Coordinate order is reversed. Repair: walk horizontally first on a paper grid. | From memory. |
| C | Reflection across the horizontal axis changes both signs. | The unchanged horizontal location is lost. Repair: trace a perpendicular crossing of that axis. | From memory. |
| D | Crossing-zero distance is the difference of endpoint magnitudes. | Signed subtraction is replaced by subtraction of magnitudes. Repair: count intervals through zero. | From memory. |
| E | Vertices may travel different displacements in a translation. | Translation is treated as reshaping. Repair: draw equal parallel arrows at every vertex. | From memory. |
| F | A rotation description needs no center. | The location of the turn is unspecified. Repair: compare two centers for one turn. | From memory. |
| G | Rotating an angle adds the turn to its measure. | Orientation is confused with angle size. Repair: overlay the angle's two rays before and after. | From memory. |
| H | A parallel pair can become perpendicular under one rigid motion. | Parallelism is not tracked for the whole pair. Repair: move both traced lines together. | From memory. |
| I | A rigid motion can turn a line into a curve. | A motion is treated as bending. Repair: trace collinear points together with a straightedge. | From memory. |
| J | Equal area guarantees congruence. | Area replaces the matching condition. Repair: compare equal-area rectangles with unequal longest sides. | From memory. |
| K | Changing sequence order never changes the image. | Composition is treated as commutative. Repair: compare a translation before and after a turn. | From memory. |
| L | Measuring examples establishes a universal angle claim. | Observations replace reasons for arbitrary figures. Repair: label the equal angles on an auxiliary parallel line. | Progressions, Geometry 2016, PDF page 10. |
| M | Same-side interior angles on parallel lines are always equal. | A supplementary pair is treated as an equal pair. Repair: mark its straight-angle decomposition. | From memory. |
| N | An exterior angle equals its adjacent interior angle. | Adjacent and remote angles are confused. Repair: subtract the adjacent interior angle from a straight angle. | From memory. |
| O | Clockwise and counterclockwise give the same quarter-turn image. | Turn direction is ignored. Repair: follow one marked ray in each direction. | From memory. |
| P | A triangle's interior angles total 360 degrees. | A full turn replaces the straight-angle sum. Repair: rearrange the three corners on a straight line. | From memory. |
| Q | A rigid move changes a segment's length. | Rigidity is not used to compare corresponding segments. Repair: overlay marked endpoints. | From memory. |
| R | Polygon area is the sum of side lengths. | Boundary length replaces covered surface. Repair: tile a rectangle with unit squares. | From memory. |
| S | Reflection across the vertical axis changes the vertical sign. | The coordinate perpendicular to the axis is misidentified. Repair: connect corresponding points horizontally. | From memory. |

Repairs are listed once above. A later section uses the same letter only for that misconception.
