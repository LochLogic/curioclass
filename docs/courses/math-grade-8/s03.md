## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does the steepness of a line tell us?"

**Hook, day 37:** In ten minutes, compare two drawing robots, invented for practice: one travels three quarters of a meter in half a second; the other follows d = 2t, with meters and seconds. Draw their graphs. Ask the compelling question.
**Questions:** Can scale change apparent steepness (day 38)? What does a starting amount change (day 42)? Why does slope stay constant (day 46)? Which predictions make sense (day 52)?
**Closing claim:** Day 54 checks proportional comparisons, slope arguments and equations; day 52's product explains model limits.
**Teaching:** Model axis units on day 38 and separate start from change on day 41. Construct from two values on day 43. Connect triangles to equations on day 46; extend signed change on day 47. Step back on day 52.


**Practices:** MP8, day 46: draw differently sized slope triangles, explain their equal ratios by similarity and generalize both line equations. MP2, day 46: translate a starting-amount description into y = mx + b, then restore units to m and b.
**Fluency:** No owned standard specifies expected fluency. Follow the frame: eight understanding, four fluency and three application days. Days 39, 48, 51 and 53 mix previously taught representations, slope calculations and solution checks with feedback, without time pressure. Day 53 returns unit 2's similar triangles and scale factors.
**Limits:** Teach and sample the linear portions of ~8.F.A.3 and ~8.F.B.4: straight-line equations, construction from descriptions and two values, and rate and initial-value interpretation across tables and graphs. Unit 6, days 94, 100 and 101, adds the function definition and nonlinear counterexample, completing both records. No systems solving here. Day 49 contrasts horizontal and vertical lines without calling a vertical line a function.
**Carried:** Keep unit 2's blank grids and student-drawn slope triangles. Tie the coordinate plane to y = mx on day 38; withdraw supplied points on day 39. Tie tables to starting amount plus repeated change on day 43; withdraw supplied tables on day 46. Tie line graphs to y = mx + b on day 46; students draw through day 51; withdraw model templates on day 52. Blank grids and access aids remain.

**Teacher judgment:** M1 and S1 require labeled axes, origin, correct rate and a comparison in common units.
- **M1.** Meets: "Graph: minutes horizontal, meters vertical; a straight line through (0,0), (1,2.5) and (2,5). The slope is 2.5 meters per minute, the unit rate. B's slope is 8/4 = two meters per minute, so A is faster." Does not: "A is slower because its pictured line looks flatter" (B).
- **S1.** Meets: "Graph: seconds horizontal, meters vertical; a straight line through (0,0), (3,7) and (6,14). The slope is 7/3 meters per second, the unit rate. B's rate is 8/3 and A's is 7/3, so B is faster." Does not: "A's rate is 3/7 meters per second" (A).
- **M2.** Requires a constructed relationship plus rate and start interpreted in words, table and graph. Meets: "The rate is (25 - 13)/(5 - 2) = four liters per minute. The starting amount is 13 - 4(2) = five liters, so y = 4t + 5. The reservoir starts with five liters and gains four each minute. Table: at zero, two and five minutes, volumes are five, 13 and 25 liters. The table's output change divided by input change is four; stepping back to zero gives five. Graph: minutes horizontal, liters vertical; a straight line through (0,5), (2,13) and (5,25). Its intercept is the five-liter start; its slope is the four-liter gain per minute." Does not: "Thirteen liters is the start because it is the first table entry" (G).
- **S2.** Requires drawn slope triangles, a similarity reason, signed changes, both derivations, the numerical slope and equation, the exceptional cases, and a substitution test of two pairs. Meets: "On the line y = 2x + 3, draw triangles with vertices (0,3), (1,3), (1,5) and (0,3), (2,3), (2,7). Their rises and runs are two and one, four and two. For any point pair on a nonhorizontal, nonvertical line, right angles and equal acute angles made with parallel horizontal legs give similar triangles by angle-angle, so corresponding leg ratios agree. Reversing endpoints negates both signed changes, preserving slope; moving right gives negative vertical change on a falling line. Through the origin, y/x = m for nonzero x gives y = mx. Through (0,b), (y - b)/x = m gives y = mx + b. At x = 0 each equation gives its intercept. For (-2,5) and (4,-4), m = (-4 - 5)/(4 - (-2)) = -9/6 = -1.5; b = 5 - (-1.5)(-2) = 2, so y = -1.5x + 2. Horizontal y = -6 has zero rise, slope zero and equation y = 0x - 6. Vertical x = 7 has zero run and undefined slope, so it has no equation y = mx + b." Does not: "The ratios match in my two examples, so they always match" (E).
- **P1.** Requires consistent representations, units and comparison; the capacity limit is unscored practice. Meets: "With t in minutes and y in liters, y = 11 + 1.75t. Table: at zero, four and eight minutes, volumes are 11, 18 and 25 liters. Graph: minutes horizontal, liters vertical; the filling segment passes through (0,11), (4,18) and (8,25). Eleven liters is the starting amount, the table's zero-time output and the graph's intercept. The 1.75 liters per minute is the equation's coefficient of t, the table's seven-liter increase every four minutes and the graph's slope. Added volume v = 1.75t starts at zero. The other feed's slope is 6/4 = 1.5 liters per minute, so the added volume grows faster." Does not: "The total volume is y = 1.75t because all linear graphs pass through the origin" (C).
