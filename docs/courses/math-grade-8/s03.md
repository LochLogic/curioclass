## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does the steepness of a line tell us?"
**Hook, day 37:** In eight minutes, compare two drawing robots, invented for practice: one travels three quarters of a metre in half a second; the other follows d = 2t, with metres and seconds. Draw their graphs. Ask the compelling question.
**Questions:** Can scale change apparent steepness (day 38)? What does a starting amount change (day 42)? Why does slope stay constant (day 46)? Which predictions make sense (day 52)?
**Closing claim:** Day 54 checks proportional comparisons, slope arguments and equations; day 52's product explains model limits.
**Teaching:** Model axis units on day 38 and separate start from change on day 41. Construct from two values on day 43. Connect triangles to equations on day 46; extend signed change on day 47. Step back on day 52.

- **~8.F.B.4:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) Construct a function to model a linear relationship between two quantities. Determine the rate of change and initial value of the function from a description of a relationship or from two (x, y) values, including reading these from a table or from a graph. - **~8.F.A.3:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) Interpret the equation y = mx + b as defining a linear function, whose graph is a straight line; give examples of functions that are not linear. For example, the function A = s^2 giving the area of a square as a function of its side length is not linear because its graph contains the points (1,1), (2,4) and (3,9), which are not on a straight line. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **8.EE.B.6:** Use similar triangles to explain why the slope m is the same between any two distinct points on a non-vertical line in the coordinate plane; derive the equation y = mx for a line through the origin and the equation y = mx + b for a line intercepting the vertical axis at b. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

For any point pair on a nonhorizontal, nonvertical line, right angles and equal acute angles made with parallel horizontal legs give similar triangles by angle-angle, so corresponding leg ratios agree. Reversing endpoints negates both signed changes, preserving slope; moving right gives negative vertical change on a falling line. Through the origin, y/x = m for nonzero x gives y = mx. Through (0,b), (y - b)/x = m gives y = mx + b. At x = 0 each equation gives its intercept. For (-2,5) and (4,-4), m = (-4 - 5)/(4 - (-2)) = -9/6 = -1.5; b = 5 - (-1.5)(-2) = 2, so y = -1.5x + 2. Horizontal y = -6 has zero rise, slope zero and equation y = 0x - 6. Vertical x = 7 has zero run and undefined slope, so it has no equation y = mx + b." Does not: "The ratios match in my two examples, so they always match" (E).
