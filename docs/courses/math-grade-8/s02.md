## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When are two shapes the same, only bigger?"
**Hook, day 21:** In eight minutes, compare paper rectangles two by three, four by six and four by five. Which is an enlargement? Test rigid moves and ask the compelling question.
**Questions:** Why a center (day 23)? What changes in coordinates (day 25)? Why two angles (day 29)? Why is slope unchanged (day 31)?
**Closing claim:** Day 34's measurement product and day 35's arguments establish similarity through reproducible sequences.
**Teaching:** Model center-to-point rays on day 23, connect them to coordinates on day 25, then withdraw worked routes on day 27. On day 29, align and dilate a side before arguing about intersecting angle rays.

<!-- skel -->
- **8.G.A.4:** Understand that a two-dimensional figure is similar to another if the second can be obtained from the first by a sequence of rotations, reflections, translations, and dilations; given two similar two-dimensional figures, describe a sequence that exhibits the similarity between them. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **8.G.A.3:** Describe the effect of dilations, translations, rotations, and reflections on two-dimensional figures using coordinates. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **8.G.A.5:** Use informal arguments to establish facts about the angle sum and exterior angle of triangles, about the angles created when parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and the angle-angle criterion for similarity of triangles. For example, arrange three copies of the same triangle so that the sum of the three angles appears to form a line, and give an argument in terms of transversals why this is so. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **~8.EE.B.6:** (begun here; unit 3 completes it (day 31 note)) Use similar triangles to explain why the slope m is the same between any two distinct points on a non-vertical line in the coordinate plane; derive the equation y = mx for a line through the origin and the equation y = mx + b for a line intercepting the vertical axis at b. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A dilation adds the same amount to corresponding lengths. | Additive change replaces multiplication. Repair: compare center-to-point distances as multiples. | From memory. |
| B | Every dilation uses the origin, regardless of the stated center. | The center is ignored. Repair: draw the ray from the stated center before measuring. | From memory. |
| C | Dilation multiplies angle measures by its scale factor. | Length scaling is applied to angles. Repair: overlay corresponding angle rays. | From memory. |
| D | Similarity can be established by appearance without a complete match. | The definition is not used. Repair: trace every corresponding vertex through a stated sequence. | From memory. |
| E | Translation multiplies coordinates by the displacement numbers. | Multiplication replaces displacement. Repair: count the same horizontal and vertical changes at every vertex. | From memory. |
| F | Axis reflection negates the coordinate along the mirror axis. | The changed coordinate is misidentified. Repair: draw the perpendicular segment joining a point to its image. | From memory. |
| G | A quarter-turn is performed opposite the named direction. | Turn direction is reversed. Repair: follow one marked ray in the named direction. | From memory. |
| H | Equal corresponding triangle angles imply congruence. | Shape determines size. Repair: align a small triangle with a larger equal-angle triangle. | From memory. |
| I | A length ratio uses reversed correspondence on just one side of the equality. | Unlike quantities are paired. Repair: label height over shadow in both triangles before calculating. | From memory. |
| J | One measured example establishes a general angle claim. | Observation replaces an argument. Repair: replace measurements with arbitrary labelled angles and matching rays. | From memory. |
