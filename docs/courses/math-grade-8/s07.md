## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we write and compare very large and very small numbers?"
**Hook, day 118:** For eight minutes, compare 100,000,000 with eight factors of ten. Which representation makes the size easier to explain? Ask the compelling question.

**Questions:** Why is a negative exponent a reciprocal (day 122)? What does the number line reveal (day 130)? When must powers match (day 134)?

**Closing claim:** Equivalent forms preserve value. Scale comparison on day 135 and F1 to F3 on day 136 show how exponent laws support estimates, comparisons and calculations.

**Teaching:** Model factors on days 118 to 120, cancellation on day 121 and reciprocal patterns on day 122. Include rational bases and signed-base examples before day 123 practice. Day 125 teaches combining bases (equal exponents, pairing factors), then chains the laws. Day 128 teaches a digit times a power of ten for estimates and ratios, and scaled lines; days 129 and 130 practise placing quantities on them. Day 131 applies taught product and quotient laws to power-of-ten measurement problems, regrouping a coefficient of ten or more, and teaches choosing units. Day 132 teaches common-power addition and subtraction, names scientific notation and normalizes results. Days 123, 124, 129, 130, 133 and 134 practise what earlier days taught. Step back on days 125 and 135.

<!-- skel -->
- **8.EE.A.1:** Know and apply the properties of integer exponents to generate equivalent numerical expressions. For example, 3^2 × 3^(–5) = 3^(–3) = 1/3^3 = 1/27. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **8.EE.A.3:** Use numbers expressed in the form of a single digit times an integer power of 10 to estimate very large or very small quantities, and to express how many times as much one is than the other. For example, estimate the population of the United States as 3 × 10^8 and the population of the world as 7 × 10^9, and determine that the world population is more than 20 times larger. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **8.EE.A.4:** Perform operations with numbers expressed in scientific notation, including problems where both decimal and scientific notation are used. Use scientific notation and choose units of appropriate size for measurements of very large or very small quantities (e.g., use millimeters per year for seafloor spreading). Interpret scientific notation that has been generated by technology. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## 7. Response allocation

Teacher meets the largest group; others work in pairs from the written move. At home, discuss each miss before an independent changed example. No assessment-day reteaching.
