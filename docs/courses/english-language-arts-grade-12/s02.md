## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does Hamlet know for certain, and how does not knowing shape what he does?"
Open day 16 on Horatio demanding that the apparition speak in 1.1. His witness changes before its identity is established. Return to the compelling question through the daily questions in U3. Model one evidence distinction on day 16; students choose themes on day 17 and lead the synthesis on day 29.

The closing argument asks how far uncertainty explains Hamlet's choices. Students qualify a claim using the whole play and Coleridge, distinguishing evidence available to Hamlet from evidence available to an audience.

## U6. Days

Every period is 50 minutes. The day list fixes all first-reading segments. On days 16 to 29 reserve 18 class minutes for reading and rereading, plus the scope and sequence's 30-minute home or supervised in-school access alternative. Divide the next segment into manageable portions; preview content before assigning it. These are the same reading minutes, not duplicate audio and silent assignments. The complete local play has about 32,608 words in the scope and sequence's count. Fourteen class reading blocks plus fourteen access blocks give 672 minutes; this is a planning allowance, not a measured reading rate. Set the first access block after day 15's private content notice. No student must read the whole play aloud. Continue the unit 1 independent book at the learner's recorded pace, with one passage-and-evidence log each week during independent work.

The six literal stage relationships needed are supplied on day 16: former king, current king, queen, prince, counselor and friend. Distinguish characters' religious claims about ghosts, prayer, burial and providence from established facts or students' beliefs. No belief statement is requested.

## U7. Talk and writing

On days 20, 23 and 29, prepare evidence, agree goals and roles, cite, probe, invite and synthesize. The teacher models and leads before observing student leadership. Each learner's written closing record names a changed claim and remaining uncertainty; feedback cites one actual turn.

## U8. Practice that returns

Word forms, clause order and evidence distinctions return through the dated tasks. Reread hard clauses before revising a judgment. Fluency means accurate expressive meaning, with no rate target.

## U9. Supports and extension

Provide bilingual glosses, clause marking and audio beside print during teaching; fade routine annotations by day 26. Keep documented access supports separate from independent cold-reading evidence. Extension tests a competing interpretation with equal textual rigor.

## U10. Two rooms

One projector displays source passages while students annotate print. At home the parent reads the supplied versions and uses the same criteria for discussion and feedback. Parent conversation alone leaves diverse-partner and group contexts unobserved; do not claim them complete.
