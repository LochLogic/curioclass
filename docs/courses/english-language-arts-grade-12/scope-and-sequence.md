# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 12

Course `english-language-arts-grade-12`, grade 12, draft, revised 2026-11-15 after both sequence reads and the lead calls.

The course has 160 course days: 149 in eight units and 11 in three course-level check windows and their respond days. It meets every school day for about 50 minutes, so course day and school day are identical; `school_day` is blank in the CSV. The frame's unit order, lengths and 30 owned progression rows are unchanged. Each unit currently uses section `<unit>.1`; section boundaries belong to the next planning layer. The day rows, rather than this prose, hold the daily sequence. There is no base curriculum and no source map is required.

## Reading, writing and evidence across the year

The demand rises through increasing independence rather than an increasing numerical measure on every text. Short reflective models establish routines; Hamlet is the early qualitative peak because later plays and the novel use it. Gatsby's unreliable narration and withheld history justify its place despite its lower reported measure. Du Bois and Woolf require independent idea tracking through unfamiliar structures; speeches add original delivery; legal texts add technical meanings and competing reasoning. Research finally transfers those practices to independently selected new sources. Routine background, first-read modeling and annotations fade; check 1 and check 3 have no routine scaffolds. Targeted instruction and access supports continue and are recorded separately from independent proficiency. No text is replaced by a simpler rewrite. Measures and exact cold-text choices remain to be completed in the text records before unit planning.

Hamlet, Earnest and Gatsby are read whole. The provisional load ledger below fixes first-read segments and 20 to 30 minute school-night blocks, with the same minutes in supervised in-school reading access for anyone who cannot read at home. Access is a scheduled alternative to home reading, not simultaneous extra work or an extra ELA course day. Edition-specific word totals and timing still need verification where the text record has no count. Pride and Prejudice is the first independent long-book offer, with the frame's other shelf choices available. Selection starts on day 3, logs run weekly, and conferences/log reviews fall on days 9, 71 and the final portfolio window, with brief ongoing conferences during independent work. Students finish at least one whole long book. No student needs a prior course's book or an account.

Daily exit tickets require the day's text. The weekly one-sitting paragraph and one student-led anchor-sentence study occupy existing reading or drafting time, never a separate added lesson. Paragraph slots are days 5, 10, 12, 17, 22, 27, 32, 39, 44, 46, 54, 59, 64, 67, 72, 77, 87, 92, 97, 103, 108, 113, 117, 125, 130, 135, 140, 145, 149, 154 and 159; the course-level essays supply the remaining check weeks. On a presentation day a paragraph is the speaker's source-linked revision note or the nonspeaker's draft work. There are three cold two-text essays, on days 52, 84 and 122: argument, argument/analysis, then informative after unit 6. Narrative evidence remains the day 13 short narrative and day 14 process piece. By principal purpose, the five process products are three arguments, one explanation and one narrative, 60/20/20 by product count; the senior paper defends a claim and counts once as argument despite explanatory passages. Cold essays and routine paragraphs are separate from that denominator. The five process products close on days 14, 37, 101, 119 and 158, with models, student revision plans and feedback before submission. The year ends with all five and one piece rewritten with a new approach. Short research occurs in unit 6, on days 125 to 126, and again as cold transfer on day 159; the sustained inquiry occupies unit 8.

Every unit has at least two seminars, including one full student-led seminar with self-set goal, deadline and roles. For 30 students, whole-class launches lead to six groups of five; assessment observers record turns and the teacher rotates as lead judge, rather than requiring 30 sequential full-class performances. On day 14 collect only the prepared seminar: the process narrative, portfolio and digital/conference records are submitted products, not additional live performances. Their days are 7/14, 20/29, 41/47, 60/66, 72/74, 90/96, 112/116 and 129/149. Partner conferences and teacher-led passage discussions supply the other settings. The formal unit 5 seminars finish by day 74, more than ten school days before the assessed seminar on day 85; later comparison work uses paired writing rather than another seminar.

End windows distribute full performances across the closing days instead of combining two long products in one period. Each code listed for an assessment is covered in full: lettered parts appear separately in the CSV, and a named product or record supplies parts not recollected live that day. The full-evidence ledger below and each assessment note name the parts and source records; unit plans turn those demands into prompts without expanding the sample. A conference or individual performance is recorded once per window. Later scoring uses that record, particularly days 9/14, 32 to 34/37, 113 to 115/119 and 148/150 to 158. A two-audience talk has one record per distinct audience, not repeated takes for a second assessment. Response time is reserved once per assessment window, as listed below. Daily feedback remains ordinary teaching.

Shared school devices and teacher posting provide W.11-12.6 and SL.11-12.5 without student accounts. Each learner's digital action is recorded, with publication/update in unit 1 and again in unit 8, and strategic digital media in unit 8. Binder publication, transcripts and printed visual handouts preserve the paper path; they do not demonstrate the technology parts, which stay unfinished until demonstrated.

## Unit 1. Telling your own story: reflective writing, 1883 to 1916

15 days, course days 1 to 15, weeks 1 to 3. Twain's changed perception, Addams's choice of work and Frost's ambiguous retrospective voice supply craft before students draft. The original model joins those readings to a manageable 650 to 1,000 word narrative. Seven owned rows carry a heavier opening load than their short texts suggest: reading independence, vocabulary, discussion, routine writing, conventions and process all begin here. The order moves from model and audience through drafting, conference, changed approach and digital publication/update before submission.

There is no readiness check: grade 11 retrieval follows the hook and is distributed over days 1 to 5. There is no mid-unit assessment. The end window is days 12 to 14: independent literary/nonfiction reading, two-source interpretation and vocabulary; a short new narrative and full convention/reference evidence; then the seminar, process narrative and routine-writing portfolio. Day 15 responds to that one window. Personal or invented material is equally acceptable; no required disclosure, public reading or application submission.

Candidate cell: drop the additional reflective model, zero days, because the original model and three anchors already support the full process. Also drop the optional recent-memoir quotation, zero days: the core reading is sufficient and the quotation's selection and rights are unresolved.
