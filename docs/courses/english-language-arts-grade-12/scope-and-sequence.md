# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 12

Course `english-language-arts-grade-12`, grade 12, draft, 2026-11-15.

The course has 160 course days: 149 in eight units and 11 in three course-level check windows and their respond days. It meets every school day for about 50 minutes, so course day and school day are identical; `school_day` is blank in the CSV. The frame's unit order, lengths and 30 owned progression rows are unchanged. Each unit currently uses section `<unit>.1`; section boundaries belong to the next planning layer. The day rows, rather than this prose, hold the daily sequence. There is no base curriculum and no source map is required.

## Reading, writing and evidence across the year

The demand rises through increasing independence rather than an increasing numerical measure on every text. Short reflective models establish routines; Hamlet is the early qualitative peak because later plays and the novel use it. Gatsby's unreliable narration and withheld history justify its place despite its lower reported measure. Du Bois and Woolf require independent idea tracking through unfamiliar structures; speeches add original delivery; legal texts add technical meanings and competing reasoning. Research finally transfers those practices to independently selected new sources. Routine background, first-read modeling and annotations fade; check 1 and check 3 have no routine scaffolds. Targeted instruction and access supports continue and are recorded separately from independent proficiency. No text is replaced by a simpler rewrite. Measures and exact cold-text choices remain to be completed in the text records before unit planning.

Hamlet, Earnest and Gatsby are read whole. Home reading of long texts is distributed in 20 to 30 minute school-night blocks, with an in-school reading alternative; the unit plan must specify the exact pages and access time. Pride and Prejudice is the first independent long-book offer, with the frame's other shelf choices available. Selection starts on day 3, logs run weekly, and conferences/log reviews fall on days 9, 71 and the final portfolio window, with brief ongoing conferences during independent work. Students finish at least one whole long book. No student needs a prior course's book or an account.

Daily exit tickets require the day's text. The weekly one-sitting paragraph and one student-led anchor-sentence study occupy existing reading or drafting time, never a separate added lesson. Paragraph slots are days 5, 10, 12, 17, 22, 27, 32, 39, 44, 46, 54, 59, 64, 67, 72, 77, 87, 92, 97, 103, 108, 113, 117, 125, 130, 135, 140, 145, 149, 154 and 159; the course-level essays supply the remaining check weeks. On a presentation day a paragraph is the speaker's source-linked revision note or the nonspeaker's draft work. There are three cold two-text essays, on days 52, 84 and 122. The five process products close on days 14, 37, 101, 119 and 158, with models, student revision plans and feedback before submission. The year ends with all five and one piece rewritten with a new approach. Short research occurs in unit 6, on days 125 to 126, and again as cold transfer on day 159; the sustained inquiry occupies unit 8.

Every unit has at least two seminars, including one full student-led seminar with self-set goal, deadline and roles. Their days are 7/14, 20/29, 41/47, 60/66, 72/74, 90/96, 112/116 and 129/149. Partner conferences and teacher-led passage discussions supply the other settings. The formal unit 5 seminars finish by day 74, more than ten school days before the assessed seminar on day 85; later comparison work uses paired writing rather than another seminar.

End windows distribute full performances across the closing days instead of combining two long products in one period. Each code listed for an assessment is covered in full: lettered parts appear separately in the CSV, and a named product or record supplies parts not recollected live that day. Unit plans must provide a prompt per remaining part. A conference or individual performance is recorded once per window. Later scoring uses that record, particularly days 9/14, 32 to 34/37, 113 to 115/119 and 148/150 to 158. A two-audience talk has one record per distinct audience, not repeated takes for a second assessment. Response time is reserved once per assessment window, as listed below. Daily feedback remains ordinary teaching.

Shared school devices and teacher posting provide W.11-12.6 and SL.11-12.5 without student accounts. Each learner's digital action is recorded, with publication/update in unit 1 and again in unit 8, and strategic digital media in unit 8. Binder publication, transcripts and printed visual handouts preserve the paper path; they do not demonstrate the technology parts, which stay unfinished until demonstrated.

## Unit 1. Telling your own story: reflective writing, 1883 to 1916

15 days, course days 1 to 15, weeks 1 to 3. Twain's changed perception, Addams's choice of work and Frost's ambiguous retrospective voice supply craft before students draft. The original model joins those readings to a manageable 650 to 1,000 word narrative. Seven owned rows carry a heavier opening load than their short texts suggest: reading independence, vocabulary, discussion, routine writing, conventions and process all begin here. The order moves from model and audience through drafting, conference, changed approach and digital publication/update before submission.

There is no readiness check: grade 11 retrieval follows the hook and is distributed over days 1 to 5. There is no mid-unit assessment. The end window is days 12 to 14: independent literary/nonfiction reading, two-source interpretation and vocabulary; a short new narrative and full convention/reference evidence; then the seminar, process narrative and routine-writing portfolio. Day 15 responds to that one window. Personal or invented material is equally acceptable; no required disclosure, public reading or application submission.

Candidate cell: drop the additional reflective model, zero days, because the original model and three anchors already support the full process. Also drop the optional recent-memoir quotation, zero days: the core reading is sufficient and the quotation's selection and rights are unresolved.

## Unit 2. Hamlet: what can be known, and what can be done

23 days, course days 16 to 38, weeks 4 to 8. The whole play builds evidence, uncertainty and interacting themes before the critic is introduced. Reference work accompanies Early Modern meaning and inverted syntax; tone and performance judgments follow literal comprehension. Three versions of a common scene precede the supplied Trifles retrieval. Coleridge supplies the owned nonfiction uncertainty demand. The model, argument map, draft and conference then support the 1,500 word literary argument. Seven owned rows warrant the long reading allocation; it is not seven equal skill blocks.

Grade 11 retrieval follows the day 16 hook, with language/argument retrieval across days 16 to 19 and interpretations retrieval on day 31. No readiness or mid-unit check. Days 35 to 37 assess cold Shakespeare/nonfiction evidence, themes and words; new interpretations, all four word strategies and syntax for reading/writing; and the complete argument, all W.1a to e. Day 38 responds once. Suicide, grief, murder and the treatment of women have content notes on the relevant reading days; no personal testimony, self-harm advocacy or violent reenactment.

Candidate cell: retain Ophelia's songs on day 27 as part of the already-required whole play, zero added days. Retain a second Shakespeare play's soliloquy with sufficient scene context as the cold literary text on day 35, costing that one existing assessment day inside the 23, not an additional play study. The exact cold selection belongs to the unit plan and must support all listed parts.

## Unit 3. Money, marriage and class: Swift, Austen and Wilde, 1729 to 1895

12 days, course days 39 to 50, weeks 8 to 10. Sustained Swift satire makes the stated/meant gap harder than its grade 11 form; Austen adds narrator irony before Earnest is read whole. The complete comic ending can then be compared with Hamlet's tragedy and the supplied Gatsby beginning/ending. Figures and near-synonyms are studied for their role in those arguments, not isolated identification. Swift's taught one-sitting analysis is on day 41.

Day 39 retrieves the grade 11 rows after the hook; unit 2's ending is same-year retrieval. No readiness or mid-unit check. Days 48 and 49 assess an annotated director commentary and small-group oral explanation on unfamiliar material: irony, beginning/ending choices, whole structure/meaning/aesthetic impact, figures/role and near-synonym nuance. Day 50 responds once. The Irish setting is 1720s hunger and colonial poverty, distinguished from the later Great Famine; harmful satire is explained before reading and is never imitated against a real group.

Candidate cell: drop the further Wilde essay, zero days, because Earnest already supplies epigrams while the 12 days must sustain the whole comedy and the cross-play comparison.

## Unit 4. The 1920s: the American dream and its price

18 days, course days 54 to 71, weeks 11 to 15. Gatsby is read whole, allowing setting, delayed history and character development to be traced together. Hughes, McKay, Cullen, Eliot and Toomer complicate the narrator's account before students select a same-period pair, including a supplied Sugimoto, Mena or Mourning Dove choice. Brief supplied eighteenth- and nineteenth-century works bring the full American literature span into the comparison. These two owned rows have substantial whole-text and independent-selection loads.

Day 54 retrieves grade 11 after the hook. No readiness or mid-unit check. Day 68 assesses the chosen pair and period-span product; day 69 transfers author-choice and period comparison to a cold story/poem pair, reusing the day 68 period-span record. Day 70 responds once; day 71 applies the learning in the independent book and log. Racism, racial violence, violent deaths and antisemitic stereotyping are named where encountered. Chapters 4 and 9 are first assigned after day 56, so Wolfsheim material follows history's Holocaust unit. Period slur omissions are marked and disclosed.

Candidate cell: drop Winter Dreams, zero days. The full novel, five literary pairings and three culturally distinct choice options already exceed what an extra story would add within this unit's load.

## Unit 5. Du Bois and Woolf: who gets to think and write

11 days, course days 72 to 82, weeks 15 to 17. Du Bois chapters 1 and 3 first make ideas, individuals and events visible together. Woolf chapters 1 and 3 then remove the familiar structural signals; students find interacting ideas, summarize objectively and judge a looping structure for different audiences. Home reading distributes the long extracts rather than putting all 15,000 Woolf words into three class periods. The early seminars use bounded passages before later close rereading; day 79 is paired comparative writing rather than another formal seminar.

Day 72 retrieves the three grade 11 rows after the hook. No readiness or mid-unit check. Day 80 assesses ideas, interactions, event development and objective summary in a cold essay map. Day 81 assesses structural effectiveness through a different cold essay and an annotated reordering memo. Day 82 responds once. Racism, lynching references, gender exclusion and Woolf's imagined sister's suicide get content notes and privacy/support controls. Fictional illustration is distinguished from historical evidence.

Candidate cell: drop the additional Du Bois chapter, zero days, to preserve independent reading of the two long essays and full evidence on three demanding informational rows. The progression's mention of chapters 1 to 3 does not silently expand the frame/text-record selection of chapters 1 and 3.

## Unit 6. Public words and a public decision, 1933 to today

16 days, course days 87 to 102, weeks 18 to 21. Roosevelt, Kennedy and Reagan are heard and read whole before the current inaugural comparison. Students distinguish evidence and reasoning from delivery and appeal. A three-source inquiry for a familiar concept precedes explanatory drafting. The housing table, report, minutes, hearing, original news report/op-ed and student-added source build a decision before the student writes a 1,500 word explanation for a general reader. This sequence teaches three owned rows across rhetorical, multi-format and writing demands without making the ELA lesson an untaught economics lesson.

Day 87 retrieves grade 11; day 92 retrieves research before the short inquiry. No readiness or mid-unit check. Day 99 assesses a new recorded speech, including a significant claim, actual support and delivery; day 100 assesses integration, accuracy, credibility and discrepancies in a new housing set; day 101 assesses all W.2a to f in the process product. Day 102 responds once. Housing circumstances are never solicited. Challenger deaths receive a content note, no disaster footage; current politics uses the year's balanced set rather than an endorsed exemplar.

Candidate cell: drop transit as a second policy option, zero days, because choosing an entirely different evidence set would dilute the shared source work. Also drop the optional recent-speech quotation, zero days: the current inaugural already supplies recent government-work rhetoric.

## Unit 7. What does equal mean? The law reads the founding, 1787 to 2021

18 days, course days 103 to 120, weeks 21 to 24. Opinion anatomy and Madison's advocacy prepare refined key terms and constitutional reasoning before Plessy, Harlan's dissent, Brown and Tinker. Foundational-document pairs span the three required centuries and connect shared themes to changed purpose and rhetorical features. Tinker explicitly supplies the owned structure row's second text after Woolf. The mock Mahanoy opinion is drafted completely before the ruling is released on day 116; a model and conference precede legal-style revision. This is three new owned rows plus a second substantial argument, not just reading a list of decisions.

Day 103 retrieves the grade 11 rows after the hook; no readiness or mid-unit check. Days 117 and 118 assess cold legal/public-advocacy reasoning, term refinement and structure in a bench memo, then foundational analysis in document-display labels. Day 119 assesses the 1,200 word opinion and full conventions evidence. Day 120 responds once. Segregation and racist premises are named as such; Harlan's anti-Chinese passage is named too. Walker is situated in 1829; Mahanoy profanity is summarized, not printed. No debate over a group's humanity or demand for a student's own political position.

Candidate cell: drop the further court opinion, zero days. The required majority/dissent comparisons, three-century documents and process opinion already fill the 18 days; adding another contested case would crowd their reasoning and response.

## Unit 8. The senior research project

36 days, course days 125 to 160, weeks 25 to 32. The shared question is chosen on day 124 beside history's day 123 and confirmed with the hook on day 125. A distinct two-day, three-source project retrieves the whole cycle before the sustained self-generated inquiry. Advanced searching and source limitations precede selective integration and drafting. The annotated list closes on day 135 with eight sources, three primary; substantial draft evidence is assessed on day 142; the full 2,500 word draft is ready on day 145. Presentation is taught and rehearsed by day 147, before history's day 148 capstone. The school schedules adult talks on days 150 to 153 and adapted younger-audience talks on days 154 to 156. Final publication/update is day 158, with cold research transfer day 159 and response day 160. Two owned rows carry a sustained essay, two contexts of speech, digital media and the full source cycle, justifying the seven-week allocation.

Day 124 and the day 125 hook retrieve grade 11, so no readiness check. The mid-unit assessment on day 142 covers the short/sustained research evidence bundle, with one respond allocation on day 143. The end window uses each learner's two distinct audience performances, the final paper/portfolio and the day 159 cold dossier; every research and presentation part is evidenced. The day 159 dossier includes a literary fragment and literary nonfiction, with a source-linked synthesis applying both reading strands. Day 160 responds once to the whole end window; work on day 157 is ordinary revision instruction, not another allocated assessment-response day.

Talks last about ten minutes plus two minutes of questions, so one 50-minute panel can hear at most four learners. Required parallel panel counts are `ceil(class size / 16)` for the four adult sessions and `ceil(class size / 12)` for the three younger-audience sessions. Each panel needs a school-arranged audience and an assigned observer who can record all presentation demands. The school must resolve staffing and spaces before unit planning; no outside invitations are sent by this plan. Sensitive questions/sources are screened, audiences come through school, and students choose what personal information to share.

Candidate cell: drop an externally prescribed research-question pattern, zero days. The owned expectation is a self-generated question revised in response to evidence; a second framework should not constrain that choice or add a parallel project.

## Shorter path

For a shorter period, retain direct reading, the source-dependent goal and the exit ticket. Use a 35 to 40 minute path with a brief hook/retrieval, one purposeful reread, one integrated writing/talk task and an exit ticket. Model only the difficult move; fold repeated chart completion and extra replay into the existing task. Continue scheduled reading in the frame's home block or in-school access time. Spread an end performance across its already-reserved window and use small groups, never shorten it by omitting a listed part. A 35-minute talk panel hears at most two complete ten-minute talks with questions; more parallel panels are required, not shorter assessed talks.

For about ten fewer meetings, these ten unassessed days fold into named neighbors. Keep their whole texts, unique skills and reference work; remove duplicate practice or chart copying. Original day labels identify the work even where the school maps it to fewer meetings.

The frame permits a standalone early-release cut to a second talk, but this shorter path keeps both contexts so no owned presentation part is lost.
