## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What question do you need answered, and what do the sources say?"
Open 125 with McKay choosing poetic form beside Hughes defending artistic freedom. Ask what their words establish about artistic choice in the 1920s, introduce the compelling question and confirm the student's question.
Supporting questions: what does McKay claim about form (125)? How does Hughes's concluding image shape that claim (126)? What can neither establish about all artists (131)? Which contrary source changes the senior answer (132)?
Assigned on 127, the main product is a 2,500-word research argument answering a self-generated question from eight authoritative sources, three primary. Plan 138, draft 140 to 145, revise 157, submit 158. Cite contrary evidence and limits. U6 names shorter writing.
Model question revision on 127, search on 128, attribution on 133 and argument on 138. On 139 read the complete model; withdraw prompts during drafting. Teach presentation on 146; students lead rehearsal and adaptation on 147 and 149.
