## The kinds of day, in career education's words

| Shared kind | In career education | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding | Explore day | A field, a job or a part of the self studied from a source: a job profile with dated data, a worker's account, an inventory used as reflection |
| Fluency | Skill day | A career task taught with a model and practised with feedback: a resume section, an interview answer, an email to an employer, reading a pay table |
| Application | Field task day or plan day | A task a field does, decided and defended with data; or the personal plan revised with what the unit found |
| Check | Assessment day | The end-of-unit assessment: a demonstration or plan piece scored on a rubric, plus a few short items on reading data |
| Respond | Respond day | Revision of the piece from rubric feedback |

Every unit has at least one field task day. Order of a unit's last days: field task day (the piece made, and the
plan's changed line drafted), plan day (written feedback on both returned, both revised), assessment day (scores the
revised piece and the plan, with a few short data items), respond day. A piece is scored only after that feedback and
revision (docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md, field 5). In Pathways the task is the same shape at higher
demand: a real data comparison, a defended decision, and a revision of the plan from feedback. Each practice taught in
a unit is judged with two or three look-fors on the piece the student produces, never on a list. Any task with
equipment follows STANDARD.md section 6 ("Hands-on tasks").

## The form of daily evidence

- **Explore days:** a short job or field card filled from the source, with the figure's source and year.
- **Skill days:** the practised piece (one resume line, one answer), marked against two or three look-fors.
- **Field task and plan days:** the decision and one line of data that supports it; the plan's changed line.
- The portfolio holds the unit's best piece; students choose and say why (self-judging, bar C8).

## What career education adds to the plan fields

- Unit field 1: sessions by kind, sessions a week and minutes as the frame states them (see "Pacing").
- Unit field 2: the clusters and sub-clusters studied, the practices taught and, once loaded, the learner-goal ids,
  each with what the student does in our own words (STANDARD.md section 1); the data sources with their year; what
  is carried across the year (the plan and the portfolio) and the day a model is withdrawn.
- Unit field 3 (where it sits): builds on (the earlier unit's plan line), with the handoff marked strong or weak; leads to; comes back (the plan and
  portfolio, every unit); and the one link to this grade's core courses a teacher may use, by row id (STANDARD.md
  section 7).
- Unit field 4 (readiness): one short item on reading a table or a percent, and one on reading a dated figure; the
  ten-minute reteach is a model of the same reading.
- Unit field 5 (end-of-unit assessment): a demonstration, plan piece or portfolio piece scored on a rubric with two
  sample pieces at different levels, plus a few data-reading items. The plan is scored on its reasoning (a choice
  defended with a figure), never on which path the student chose or on the student's own circumstances. The scored
  piece had written feedback and a revision first (see "The kinds of day"). One line on accommodations: read aloud,
  large print, a scribe, an oral answer.
- Unit field 6 (error register): a national figure read as local; highest pay read as best; one training route (a
  four-year degree) read as the default; a job read as for one group of people.
- Unit field 7 (response allocation): the marking time and the response time, named separately, with the response on
  the respond day, counted once. The short data items are marked with the shared marking step (docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md,
  field 7). The rubric piece is judged by the teacher after class against two or three look-fors, never by hands-up
  self-marking. Respond-day groups come from the rubric cells marked "not yet" and the missed data items, at most four.
- Unit field 8 (tools and supplies): see "What a tool may and may not do"; every tool day has a paper path; any task
  with equipment names its safety step (STANDARD.md section 6).
- Unit field 9 (family note): in English and Spanish: what the unit explores, what a student may take home (the job
  card, the plan page), one question about a person's own work, to ask a family member or instead any trusted adult,
  or to answer for an invented worker or from a public interview or profile, so no student is made to reveal a
  family's work status (Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-career-1); a line that sharing is the student's choice; and,
  for a unit the school leaves out, its ids not claimed that year (STANDARD.md section 5).
- Unit field 10 (sources): a sources table (read status, what is from memory), the data sources with their year, and
  a line that no study stands behind the course and that the logic model is on the course page (C16).
- Section field 1: the id of the cluster, sub-cluster, practice or learner goal, the share of it the section carries,
  what the student does in our own words (cluster ids are names, not verbs, and the practice text is never quoted,
  STANDARD.md section 1), and the data source with its year. The teacher's note on the field (what the work is,
  training routes, conditions) goes in the section's before-you-teach text, with any note STANDARD.md section 6 asks
  for on a cluster it names. A section on Behavioral and Mental Health, Emergency Response, Judicial Systems or Public
  Safety, or in the pay-gap unit, carries health's steps on its teacher page (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a
  student tells you something"; Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-career-1).
- Section field 2 (where it sits): what comes just before and just after; the readiness items it uses, and where its
  own results go.
- Section field 3: the numbers of the end-of-unit assessment items this section owns, then each day's evidence in the
  form above.
- Section field 4 (expected errors): each letter as a wrong example beside a right one (a pay figure with no year; a
  plan line with no reason), the day it is shown, and the five-to-ten-minute action if many made it.
- Section field 5: what is carried: the student's plan and portfolio, returned to each unit; the model piece, held by
  the teacher on the skill day and withdrawn on the field task day.
- Section field 6: kind of day; how a field task day opens (the decision to make and the data sheet).
- Section field 7 (talk and writing): the talk routine for a job comparison (claim, figure, source); one written
  task; interview practice with the teacher or a card as the interviewer, a model answer first.
- Section field 8 (practice that returns): one earlier skill (reading a pay table, one resume line) tried again on a
  new field.
- Section field 9 (supports and extension): English learners: six to eight workplace words in depth with a picture,
  a sentence frame for each interview or comparison step, and a key line that separates a language gap from a content
  gap. Access: any spoken task can be done in writing, with a card or with the teacher. Reading load: a job profile is
  at most 150 words at the course's reading level; the rest is read aloud. Check accommodations: read aloud, oral
  answer, scribe, extra time. Extension: the same task on a less familiar field, never the next course's standard.
- Section field 10 (two rooms): a parent teaching one child at the table (a paper job card, a family member as the
  interview practice partner); one projector (one profile shown and compared by volunteers).
- Section field 11 (not in this section): what a school adds (speakers, visits, a counselor, a lab), with the paper
  path when a school cannot.

## What a tool may and may not do

A tool may give a dated job profile, practice reading a pay table or an outlook figure, a private plan template, and a
mock-interview question bank with a model answer and a feedback list. It may not store a student's plan, interests or
answers off the device, score a person, or tell a student what job to do.

## Pacing

Settled 2026-11-16 (Cory): each course is one year at two or three sessions a week. Default time (settled by the expert
reader, ours and untested): the 32-week year of docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, three sessions a week of 45 minutes, so 96
sessions or 72 hours a year; a school at two sessions a week runs the short path, 64 sessions or 48 hours. The scope
and sequence names the 32 sessions the short path drops; every unit keeps a field task day, its plan day, its
assessment day and its respond day. No national body publishes a time figure for these courses that we found (Advance CTE, the Association for
Career and Technical Education and NCDA searched 2026-11-20; the guidelines' stages are not tied to an age), so none is
cited, and the course page says so. Each frame may change these numbers with a reason.
