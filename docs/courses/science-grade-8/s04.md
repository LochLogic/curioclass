| 97 | MS-ESS1-1 | Build and use a model of Earth, sun and moon to explain the repeating patterns of moon phases, eclipses and seasons. | **M1, sky record:** Twenty minutes, 12 points. Invented for practice: an unfamiliar observatory records a half-lit moon, then a nearly full moon a week later. Separately, on an unfamiliar planet with a different axis tilt, a site at 45 degrees north has longer days and a higher midday sun in its warmer season; a site at 45 degrees south has the opposite seasonal pattern. Earth's tilted axis during its orbit changes day length and sunlight concentration in opposite hemispheres. Wrong: phases are Earth's shadow (A); every full moon must eclipse (B); distance causes seasons (C). | section 4.1 |
| 97 | MS-PS4-2 | Build and use a model showing that materials reflect, absorb or let waves pass through them. | **M2, wave record:** Twenty minutes, 12 points. Invented for practice: a remote observation chamber has a reflective rigid wall, dark padding and a clear flexible membrane. Light observations show strong reflection at the wall, absorption at the padding and transmission through the membrane. Quiet sound records show an echo, reduced sound energy with slight warming, and vibration passing through, respectively. Three candidate panels have records: panel X transmits red and absorbs blue, panel Y reflects red and transmits blue, panel Z transmits red and blue. White light through a plastic prism separates into differently bent colors. A brighter red source keeps the same color. A sealed empty gap passes light but no sound. Develop and use a qualitative model explaining the observations and choose the panel that passes a red signal while blocking blue background light, citing its record. Key: label reflected, absorbed and transmitted energy paths for both waves; absorbed energy warms material. Light-wave amplitude represents brightness at fixed color; wavelength represents color. Colors bend differently at material boundaries and travel straight within uniform material. Light crosses empty space, while sound needs vibrating matter. Choose panel X, citing that it transmits red and absorbs blue. Two points each for light paths, sound paths, brightness and color, boundary bending, empty-space contrast and evidence-based panel choice. Wrong: absorption destroys energy (D); brighter red means higher frequency (E); colors bend identically (F); light needs matter (G); sound crosses a vacuum (H); transparent means all light passes (I). | section 4.1 |
