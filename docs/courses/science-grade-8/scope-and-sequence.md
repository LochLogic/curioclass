# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 8

Course `science-grade-8`, grade 8, draft, 2026-11-14.

The six units keep the frame's order, owned rows and 26, 20, 28, 28, 27 and 27 days. There are 156 unit days and four course-level days, 160 in all. Course day equals school day, with five 50-minute meetings each week. The day records are in `data/plans/science-grade-8/days.csv`; no sections are set yet, so every unit uses its `n.1` placeholder.

The local standard middle-school base supplies the lesson order and evidence, not ready-to-copy activities or wording. `source-map.csv` places all 91 base lessons, including the one cut optional lesson, and labels Curio Class additions separately. Its allocations give minutes on each destination day. A base lesson may cross a period boundary, and two neighboring lessons may share a period. These are planned allocations, not measured classroom timings. Each day ends with a notebook record or a model change that the next day uses. Each later section plan will place an exit ticket at its section boundary.

## Unit 1. Contact forces

**Calendar:** days 1 to 26, weeks 1 to 6, 1,300 minutes.

A dropped device that sometimes breaks motivates the first model and question board. Evidence about deformation leads to paired forces, controlled comparisons of force and mass, and separate graphs for kinetic energy against mass and against speed. Those explanations make the packaging problem worth solving: students compare cushions, test structures, combine features and justify a protective design. The three force and energy development rows take the first 17 days; the two engineering completion rows use nine days and the earlier collision evidence, rather than equal shares for five rows.

**Assessment:** day 1's last ten minutes check s4-collisions, s3-balanced-forces, s4-speed-energy and the force-diagram model, after the hook. Day 2 is the one whole-class reteach, targeted from those results; when secure, it deepens the same models. Grade 7 design and system models and grade 6 particle energy enter as retrieval tasks. Day 17 is the mid-unit assessment at the base lesson 10 synthesis: an unseen protective-device solution, a one-variable investigation plan and both energy graphs. It uses 40 minutes plus ten minutes of response on that day. Day 25 is the end-of-unit assessment, 40 minutes after the last design revision. It assesses all five owned expectations with an unseen design brief, the student's design and test record, investigation plan and graphs. Day 26 provides its response once.

**Bridges and care:** days 13 and 14 model axes and equal input increases in words before independent curved-graph reading; math's functions unit is later. There is no function notation or energy-formula derivation. Touch and memory sources on day 11 become evidence for unit 2. Concussion cases on days 11, 12, 17 and 25 use supplied sports examples, never personal disclosure. Drops use low-energy school-owned stand-ins, eye protection and a soft stop. The laser viewer is replaced by displacement records.

**Candidate settled:** no extra check-set content, zero added days. Momentum equations remain outside this grade's boundary. The optional four-day selling-designs lesson is cut because it serves no owned expectation.

## Unit 2. Sound waves

**Calendar:** days 27 to 46, weeks 6 to 10, 1,000 minutes.

The window moved by distant music raises questions about the source, the intervening space and the receiver. Source-motion records lead to amplitude and frequency comparisons before the class tries to explain energy moving through matter. Particle and wave models then explain reflection, absorption and transmission. Ear sources make the receiver part of the same system, and a ten-minute synthesis joins hearing to earlier sight, smell and touch. Most time develops the wave row; the senses row completes earlier work through focused source synthesis.

**Assessment:** no new readiness check or reteach. Days 28 and 32 retrieve the wave and light-ray models last used in grade 6 and the force and energy work just taught. Day 45 uses 40 minutes for the end-of-unit assessment after ten minutes finishing the controlled amplitude-energy comparison: a new regular-wave source with numerical graphs and a new sensory-response case using supplied sources. Both owned codes are assessed in full. Day 46 has 40 minutes of response, then the next unit's readiness check, ten minutes on s3-magnet-static, s4-energy-device and s5-gravity-down.

**Bridges and care:** day 33 provides a worked graph-shape example because math's qualitative functions come later. Days 42 and 43 use a supplied source set with credibility, accuracy and method checks because ELA research is later, days 63 to 88. No personal hearing information is requested. Ear and hearing sources on days 42, 43, 45 and 46 must present Deaf culture and deafness respectfully, using the community-informed sensitivity read at unit planning. Safe quiet sound and a visual or tactile route remain available. No laser or student vacuum apparatus is used.

**Candidate settled:** no extra content, zero added days. The ten minutes for unit 3 readiness are recovered from base lesson 14's repeated whole-class reporting, reducing its 90 minutes to 80 while keeping model revision, unseen assessment and response.

## Unit 3. Forces at a distance

**Calendar:** days 47 to 74, weeks 10 to 15, 1,400 minutes.

A teacher-opened speaker makes the force-at-a-distance question concrete. Magnetic comparisons lead to a field model and evaluation of an investigation's design. Distance and energy comparisons then make the speaker explanation testable. Two added 50-minute blocks after base lesson 8 ask questions about electric force, vary charge and distance, evaluate an electric-field investigation and model electric stored energy. Magnetic-strength comparisons follow in the base order. A further 50 minutes after base lesson 11 apply stored energy to the Earth-book system at different heights. Four rows share this sequence because the same evidence supports questions, investigation design, models and argument.

**Assessment:** readiness is on day 46, and day 48 is the one targeted whole-class reteach. Day 47 stays with the hook. Retrieve grade 7 reaction energy, the earlier particle model and this year's force diagrams. Day 66, at the base lesson 9 synthesis after the electric addition, is the mid-unit assessment: 40 minutes on electric and magnetic field evidence, evaluating both investigation designs, and an energy-transfer argument, followed by ten minutes of response. This placement avoids assessing the complete field expectation before the electric case exists. Day 73 is the 50-minute end-of-unit assessment. New cases require electric and magnetic force questions, both field-investigation evaluations, qualitative stored-energy models for all three interaction types and a before-and-after kinetic-energy argument. Day 74 provides the single 50-minute response.

**Bridges and care:** the claim, evidence and reasoning frame from earlier grades remains in use on days 53, 57, 62 and 63 during the overlap with ELA argument writing, days 31 to 62. The later argument can use that completed routine. Teachers open speakers beforehand and remove or tape sharp edges. Low-voltage coils run briefly and are disconnected at warmth; magnets stay apart and away from medical implants, phones and cards. Static work offers latex-free balloons or tape, and uses no mains electricity.

**Candidate settled:** no extra content, zero added days. The electric and gravitational additions are required completion of owned expectations, not extensions. MS-PS3-5 is applied here and remains owned in grade 6.

## Unit 4. Earth in space

**Calendar:** days 77 to 104, weeks 16 to 21, 1,400 minutes.

Sky alignments and the moon's changing shape drive the initial model. Apparent paths and illumination evidence establish seasons before phases and eclipses. The red moon exposes a limit in a shadow-only explanation and creates the need for a qualitative wave model of light. Scattering, material interactions and color-dependent bending revise that model. Instruments and spacecraft then supply scale evidence; gravity explains orbits, the solar system's formation from a disk, and its place within the Milky Way. Historical model comparisons on days 98 and 99 make evidence-driven changes to scientific knowledge explicit.

**Assessment:** day 76's last ten minutes check s5-star-brightness, and day 78 is the one targeted reteach. Day 77 keeps the hook; days 79 and 82 retrieve sky patterns and light-ray models from grade 6, and days 99 and 100 retrieve gravity and fields from this year. Day 96 is the mid-unit assessment at base lesson 12: 40 minutes on all phase, solar and lunar eclipse, season and light-material model parts, then ten minutes of response. Gravity and scale are not claimed on this assessment. Day 103 is the 50-minute end-of-unit assessment on all five owned codes, using unfamiliar sky, optical-material, mass-and-orbit and spacecraft-data cases. Day 104 has 40 minutes of response plus ten minutes checking unit 5 readiness on s3-inherited-traits and s3-environment-traits.

**Bridges and care:** scatter plots are available from math days 56 to 69. Distances on days 98 and 99 are written out or expressed using grade 5 powers of ten and grade 7 scale ratios; scientific notation is taught later, days 118 to 137 in the current calendar. If math runs late, use paired plots and verbal pattern descriptions without fitting a line. Supplied sky records replace solar viewing: no looking at the sun, no lenses aimed at it, no laser apparatus. Cultural sky knowledge is used only from a community's own permitted published sources after the sensitivity study, never as a generic belief set.

**Candidate settled:** no extra content, zero added days. Base lesson 13 keeps its ground and spacecraft comparisons but uses 80 minutes rather than 90 by combining repeated group reports; that funds the outgoing readiness check.

## Unit 5. Genetics

**Calendar:** days 105 to 131, weeks 21 to 27, 1,350 minutes.

Unusually muscled cattle provoke competing environmental and inherited explanations. Muscle and growth data precede pedigree and chromosome evidence. The added whole-cell model sits after base lesson 5 and before lesson 6: students revise both plant and animal cell systems before placing the gene-protein-trait mechanism within them. Causal models then support offspring predictions, breeding and technology-source synthesis. Plant reproduction and asexual cases widen the model, and contrasting environmental evidence prevents a genes-only explanation of growth. The inheritance and gene-model development rows receive more time than the short whole-cell and technology completions.

**Assessment:** readiness is on day 104, with the one targeted reteach on day 106. Retrieve grade 7 respiration, photosynthesis and cell-part roles on days 108, 112 and 113. Day 116 is the mid-unit assessment at base lesson 7: 40 minutes using a new gene-protein-trait case and both working whole-cell models, five minutes of response and five minutes opening the offspring-variation question. It does not claim sexual-asexual reproduction before that comparison is taught. Day 130 is the 50-minute end-of-unit assessment covering all five owned codes. The technology synthesis and revised whole-cell models are assessed products alongside new inheritance, mutation and growth cases. Day 131 gives 40 minutes of response and ten minutes of unit 6 readiness on s3-fossils, s3-variation-advantage, s3-habitat-survival and s3-animal-groups.

**Bridges and care:** ELA's research routine is available, and the short technology inquiry finishes on day 120, week 24, before ELA's gene-claims research begins on day 129, week 26. All reading stays within a supplied source set. Scatter plots and grade 7 compound-event probabilities are available. Use plants, animals and invented pedigrees only; never ask for family traits, health or genes. Genetic conditions and disability are discussed with dignity. Fertilization stays at chromosome level within school health policy. Days 122 and 123 use supplied extraction observations instead of handling rubbing alcohol; students do not cut living organisms on days 125 and 126. Plant reproduction evidence on day 124 is retained for the unit 6 argument.

**Candidate settled:** no extra content, zero added days. Mechanisms of meiosis, DNA-base changes and protein-synthesis steps stay outside the boundary.

## Unit 6. Natural selection and common ancestry

**Calendar:** days 132 to 158, weeks 27 to 32, 1,350 minutes.

The giant fossil penguin makes relationships across time an explanatory problem. External and internal structures and breeding behaviors lead to comparisons of living and fossil organisms before a consensus ancestry model is possible. Short-term population data then test how inherited variation, environmental conditions and differential reproduction can change trait proportions. Day 145 compares a 15-minute paper-beak model with 35 minutes of real population evidence: feeding success in a model does not show that an organism acquires an inherited trait. Numerical models are applied back across deep time. Matched-stage embryo comparisons provide a further, independent line of evidence, including relationships not evident in adult anatomy. The fossil, natural-selection and quantitative rows carry the longest evidence runs; the anatomy, embryo and reproduction completions are shorter and connect to them.

**Assessment:** readiness is on day 131 and day 133 is the one targeted whole-class reteach. Day 132 keeps the hook. Retrieve grade 6 rock-layer time and the earlier social studies deep-time timeline on day 134, grade 7 populations and disruption on day 145, and unit 5 inheritance and plant evidence on days 136 and 146. Day 143 is the mid-unit assessment at base lesson 6: 40 minutes interpreting unfamiliar fossil sequences and anatomical evidence, then ten minutes of response. Day 157 completes the embryo comparison for 15 minutes, then uses 35 minutes for the end-of-unit assessment. The student's numerical selection analysis, matched-stage embryo analysis and plant-animal reproduction argument are assessed products; the new case requires transfer across fossil, anatomical, population and reproductive evidence. All six owned codes are assessed. Day 158 is the single 50-minute response.

**Care:** evolution is explained as the evidence supports, including extinction, common ancestry and the distinction between a scientific theory and a guess. No personal statement of belief is requested. No exercise ranks human groups or requires human ancestry; gross animal embryos suffice. Natural laws are assumed to have operated consistently across the fossil record. Models distinguish population change over generations from an individual acquiring a needed trait. Simulations use supplied rules and data, never cultures of microbes.

**Candidate settled:** no extra content, zero added days. No species-name or geologic-era recall and no population-genetics equilibrium calculations.

## Shorter path

For a shorter period, preserve the phenomenon, evidence interpretation, notebook record and model revision. Replace repeated whole-class reports with one selected comparison and a short written contribution from everyone. Use pre-set apparatus or the no-kit route to reduce setup, not evidence quality claims. Retrieve earlier work within the opening minutes. Never move required investigation planning, scientific argument, assessed products or safety into unsupervised homework.

A roughly ten-day-short year folds these ten unassessed consolidation days into the named neighbor. Original day labels remain the reference in this draft; an adopting calendar counts 150 meetings after folding. No readiness, mid-unit, end-of-unit, course-level check or respond day is removed, and every owned part and its assessed product remains.
