<!-- skel -->
- **MS-LS4-1:** Use fossil data to find patterns of when life forms existed, varied, died out and changed over Earth's history, assuming natural laws have always worked the same way. Emphasis: patterns in how complex body structures became and the time order in which fossils show up in rock layers. Limit: Leave out names of single species and of geologic eras.
- **MS-LS4-2:** Use science ideas to explain body likenesses and differences among living organisms and fossils, and infer how they are related. Emphasis: relationships judged from the overall look of body structures.
- **MS-LS4-4:** Explain from evidence how differences in inherited traits make some individuals more likely to survive and reproduce in a given environment. Emphasis: explanations that use simple statements of probability and proportional reasoning.
- **MS-LS4-6:** Use math to back up explanations of how natural selection can make traits more or less common in a population over time. Emphasis: mathematical models, probability and proportional reasoning about population trends. Limit: Leave out Hardy-Weinberg calculations.
- **MS-LS1-4:** Argue from evidence and reasoning how typical animal behaviors and special plant parts raise the chance that animals and plants reproduce.
- **MS-LS4-3:** Compare pictures of embryos of several species to find patterns that show relationships hidden in the adult bodies. Emphasis: inferring general relatedness from diagrams or pictures of whole embryos. Limit: Comparisons use only the overall look of body structures during development.
