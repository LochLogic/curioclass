# Course frame: Science, Grade 8

Course id science-grade-8, grade 8, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6 units, one for each OpenSciEd grade 8 unit, in its order (8.1 to 8.6): contact forces, sound waves,
  forces at a distance, Earth in space, genetics, natural selection and common ancestry.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 156 in units, 4 for course-level checks and respond days (two checks of 1 day, each
  followed by 1 respond day). One 50-minute lesson a day, five a week, 32 weeks. The base text plans its six grade
  8 units at 173 days of 45 minutes (7,785 minutes, leaving out 8.1's optional lesson 16); 156 days of 50 minutes
  is 7,800 minutes, so the base time fits with about 15 minutes to spare and each unit gets its base days times 0.9,
  rounded up by a day where the rows add a readiness check or a model reactivation. This is the tightest of the
  three years (see For the lead): the twelve dormant readiness checks are short openers, and their reteaches run as
  small-group work inside unit time, not as added days. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards middle school performance expectations (never named in
  student, teacher or family copy). The course owns 26 codes on 26 progression rows (MS-PS3-5 is applied here
  and owned in grade 6, marked ~ on s8-energy-transfer-argument). Expectations started earlier and completed here
  name the earlier part: MS-LS1-2 (grade 7 unit 4), MS-LS1-5 (grade 7 unit 3), MS-LS1-8 (grade 7 unit 1),
  MS-ETS1-2 and MS-ETS1-3 (grade 7 unit 2). Practices and crosscutting concepts are counted by element in the unit
  plans (S11).
- **Base text.** OpenSciEd Middle School, 2022 standard units, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms;
  docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md). Each unit adapts one base unit whole, in the base's lesson order; the "## Source
  map" section places all 18 middle school units and their lessons, and gives the loss check's result (order kept,
  no loss brought to Cory). The "+CS" variants of 8.1 and 8.2 are CC BY-NC and never a base. One short credit line
  per adapted day. Never carried over: third-party photos and videos, the base's character names, brand names, kit
  links.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor phenomenon (named in the table), builds a question board, an
  initial class model and a consensus model, and revises the model as evidence arrives; each lesson answers a
  question the last one raised. The end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has not seen.
- **Engineering and the nature of science.** The design unit is unit 1 (a protective case and a cushioning
  material, completing MS-ETS1-2 and MS-ETS1-3 started in grade 7). The nature of science is taught on purpose in
  unit 4 (models of the solar system revised by evidence over centuries), unit 6 (a theory built from fossil,
  anatomical, embryo and genetic lines of evidence; what a scientific theory is) and unit 5 (claims about gene
  technologies judged on evidence).
- **Materials.** Household stand-ins wherever safe, then a no-kit data path (a data sheet of real readings, said
  plainly to be weaker than collecting them), then a Curio Class simulation for what no class can run (collisions at
  speed, orbits, selection over generations); simulations are listed per unit now and built later as tools
  (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Each unit plan states its cost. Every electronic tool has a paper path and needs no
  student account.
- **Safety.** The year safety sheet is retaught in unit 1's first day; every hands-on day has a Safety and set-up
  section (drops and collisions with eye protection, magnets kept from devices and medical implants, coils that
  warm, no looking at the sun). A science teacher reads safety and materials before the course is called finished
  (FOUNDATION 7.3; people are Cory's).
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own readings at grade 8 level, written from the base's readings in our words;
  data sets from public agencies (NASA, NOAA, USGS), each with its rights line.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. A reader from the Deaf and hard of
  hearing community reads unit 2 (hearing and the ear); unit 5 (inheritance, disability-adjacent traits, gene
  technologies) and unit 6 (evolution, human ancestry) get the fresh-agent read with the values rule in hand.
