# Course frame: Science, Grade 8

Course `science-grade-8` (catalogue id `science-ms`), grade 8, draft frame (2026-11-13). The current day-level draft is [the scope and sequence](scope-and-sequence.md), with its record in data/plans/science-grade-8/days.csv (fix pass 2026-11-15).

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6 units, one for each OpenSciEd grade 8 unit, in its order (8.1 to 8.6): contact forces, sound waves,
  forces at a distance, Earth in space, genetics, natural selection and common ancestry.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 156 in units and four course-level days, one 50-minute lesson a day, five a week, 32 weeks. Units remain 26, 20, 28, 28, 27 and 27 days, in the base's order. The base has 173 required 45-minute days (7,785 minutes), excluding optional 8.1 lesson 16. The original 13 whole-base-day condensations remain: 8.1 lessons 9 and 13; 8.2 lessons 3 and 10; 8.3 lessons 1, 4, 9 and 11; 8.4 lesson 13; 8.5 lesson 1; 8.6 lessons 1, 7 and 10. Each retains the relevant evidence. The revised map distinguishes actual base instruction from assessments and response, even where the base supplies the assessment idea. Minute budgets (base instruction plus Curio Class allocations) are: unit 1, 1,075 + 225 = 1,300; unit 2, 880 + 120 = 1,000; unit 3, 1,050 + 350 = 1,400; unit 4, 1,200 + 200 = 1,400; unit 5, 1,100 + 250 = 1,350; unit 6, 1,165 + 185 = 1,350. The scope and sequence gives each addition, assessment, response and consolidation; no minutes are booked twice.
  Returning rows use the counted rule: last used in grade g means retrieval in grade g+1 and readiness in grade g+2 or later. A passing mention is not a use. Readiness is before the receiving unit, in the last minutes of the preceding final or respond day, except unit 1 in day 1's final ten minutes after its hook. Readiness days are 1, 26, 46, 76, 104 and 131. Day 131 has 15 readiness minutes and 35 response minutes because five older rows return. Each receiving unit has a whole-class targeted reteach after the hook, except unit 2: by the lead's 2026-11-15 call, two ten-minute responses inside days 28 and 32 replace one separate reteach lesson. No further whole base day is condensed for that exception. Grade 7 and earlier-year rows have retrieval tasks; all assumed rows, last-use grades and delivery slots are listed once in the scope and sequence. Math leans use retrieval by default, with bridges for later same-year work.
  Mid-unit assessments are days 17, 66, 97, 121 and 143. Day 96 builds the light consensus before day 97 assesses it. Day 121 uses the base lesson 10 transfer point; day 116 preserves the lesson 7 consensus. Further minute consolidations are explicit: unit 1 lesson 15 reporting, ten minutes, funds unit 2 readiness; unit 2 lessons 1 and 4 each yield ten repeated-reporting minutes for readiness response, and lesson 14 yields ten for outgoing readiness; unit 3 lesson 9 keeps 40 of its previously planned 90 consensus minutes, with 50 booked as assessment/response; unit 4 lesson 12 keeps 40 of 90, and lesson 13 yields ten reporting minutes for outgoing readiness; unit 5 lesson 10 keeps 25 teaching minutes plus the separately booked transfer task, lesson 11 question selection keeps 15 of 45 minutes, and five additional response minutes move to outgoing readiness; unit 6 lesson 6 keeps 40 of 90 consensus minutes and books the other 50 as assessment/response. These replace repeated synthesis or question selection, retain the evidence and models, and need classroom timing checks at unit planning.
  Every unit includes its end-of-unit assessment and response. End days use named individual products rather than repeating several full performances. Course-level checks sample three named codes each through new cases and forms, reuse recent individual records without a second score or collection, and remain days 75 and 159 with respond days 76 and 160.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards middle school performance expectations (never named in
  student, teacher or family copy). The course owns 26 codes on 26 progression rows (MS-PS3-5 is applied here
  and owned in grade 6, marked ~ on s8-energy-transfer-argument). Expectations started earlier and completed here
  name the earlier part: MS-LS1-2 (grade 7 unit 4), MS-LS1-5 (grade 7 unit 3), MS-LS1-8 (grade 6 units 1 and 6, light and the body's systems; grade 7 unit 1, smell; unit 1 here, touch),
  MS-ETS1-2 and MS-ETS1-3 (grade 7 unit 2). The scope and sequence now reserves band practice and crosscutting elements by day and observable evidence; unit plans turn these reservations into tasks (S11).
- **Base text.** OpenSciEd Middle School, 2022 standard units, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms;
  docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md). Each unit adapts one base unit whole, in the base's lesson order; the "## Source
  map" section places all 18 middle school units and their lessons, and gives the loss check's result (order kept;
  the base's gaps taught as named Curio Class additions and 13 base days condensed, all named there). The "+CS" variants of 8.1 and 8.2 are CC BY-NC and never a base. One short credit line
  per adapted day. Never carried over: third-party photos and videos, the base's character names, brand names, kit
  links.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor phenomenon (named in the table), builds a question board, an
  initial class model and a consensus model, and revises the model as evidence arrives; each lesson answers a
  question the last one raised. The end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has not seen.
- **Engineering and the nature of science.** The design unit is unit 1 (a protective case and a cushioning
  material, completing MS-ETS1-2 and MS-ETS1-3 started in grade 7). The nature of science is taught on purpose in
  unit 4 (models of the solar system revised by evidence over centuries), unit 6 (a theory built from fossil,
  anatomical, embryo and genetic lines of evidence; what a scientific theory is) and unit 5 (claims about gene
  technologies judged on evidence).
- **Practices across the year.** Asking questions and defining problems (unit 3); developing and using models
  (units 2 to 5); planning and carrying out investigations (units 1, 3); analysing and interpreting data (units 1,
  4, 6); using mathematics and computational thinking (unit 2, wave graphs; unit 5, Punnett square probabilities;
  unit 6, trait numbers over generations, with ratios and percent from math 7); constructing explanations and
  designing solutions (units 1, 6); engaging in argument from evidence (units 2, 6); obtaining, evaluating and
  communicating information (units 4, 5). Crosscutting concepts: systems and system models (units 1, 3), structure
  and function (units 1, 5), scale, proportion and quantity (units 2, 4), patterns (units 2, 4, 6), cause and
  effect (units 3, 5, 6). Each unit's list is the base unit's own focal list (its unit page in
  S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms).
- **Literacy standards.** The science and technical reading and writing standards for grades 6 to 8 (RST.6-8,
  WHST.6-8; data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt) are met alongside the science where the work already does them
  (Cory, Desk 2026-11-14: "Load them", no extra days). Each unit's "What students learn" cell ends with the codes
  it meets; they are never owned standards and add no days.
- **Materials.** Household stand-ins wherever safe, then a reduced-materials investigation or learner-controlled simulation where conducting is required, with supplied data identified as incomplete practical evidence, then a Curio Class simulation for what no class can run (collisions at
  speed, orbits, selection over generations); simulations are listed per unit now and built later as tools
  (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). The scope and sequence gives six provisional class-of-30 and one-child estimates; unit plans verify stock, quantities and prices. Every electronic tool has a paper path and needs no
  student account.
- **Safety.** The year safety sheet is retaught in unit 1's first day; every hands-on day has a Safety and set-up
  section (drops and collisions with eye protection; the base's laser-and-mirror viewers replaced by supplied observations
  unless the safety read sets a controlled apparatus; speakers opened by the teacher in advance; magnets kept from
  devices and medical implants; coils that warm; no looking at the sun). Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md), against published school science safety guidance, laser and eclipse
  viewing safety guidance and the safety data sheet of every chemical named (unit 5's rubbing alcohol), before the
  course is called finished; that read is the "safety read" the unit rows name for any laser apparatus. The
  course's checks get the answer-key agent's read.
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own readings at grade 8 level, written from the base's readings in our words;
  data sets from public agencies (NASA, NOAA, USGS), each with its rights line.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 2 (hearing, the ear, deafness): the agent first studies the
  Deaf and hard of hearing community's own published voice (national associations of the Deaf and of people with
  hearing loss, Deaf studies scholarship, a disability language style guide for journalists), so deafness is named
  as a way of being and a culture, not only a loss. Unit 5 (inheritance, genetic conditions, gene technologies): it
  first studies disability-led organisations' published writing on genetics and genetic testing, a genetic
  alliance's family-facing pages, a national genome research institute's educator guidance, and adoption and
  donor-conceived people's own writing on family-trait lessons. Unit 6 (evolution, human ancestry): it studies
  science teachers' associations' guidance on teaching evolution to students of faith, with the values rule in
  hand.
