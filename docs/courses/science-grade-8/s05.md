**P1, 8 points, original pond-animal model, invented for practice:** Three gene variants occur at the same chromosome location. Compared with the usual protein, variant A makes an altered protein that lets animals use a new food, variant B makes an altered protein that prevents normal swimming, and variant C leaves measured protein function, feeding and swimming unchanged. Draw and use a model to explain all three outcomes and why a gene change need not change a visible trait. Key: locate gene on chromosome, connect gene to possible protein change and organism function; A is helpful with that food, B harmful under the stated conditions, C neutral for the measured functions. Do not infer that C must have unchanged DNA. Four two-point components: location and causal chain; helpful use; harmful use; neutral use and limits. Wrong: all mutations harm (B).

**P2, 8 points, pond-cell comparison, invented for practice:** An animal cell and a green pond-plant leaf cell have intact nuclei and mitochondria. A treatment makes their membranes freely permeable; the plant's wall remains intact. Draw both whole-cell systems, predict a consequence and explain why the wall cannot replace membrane control. Key: membrane regulates exchange, wall supports the plant cell, nucleus holds inherited instructions, mitochondria help release usable energy from food, chloroplasts use light to make food in the green plant cell. Plant cells also need mitochondria; animal cells lack chloroplasts and walls. Four two-point components: whole-cell interactions with prediction; membrane and wall contrast; nucleus role; chloroplast and mitochondrion roles. Wrong: walls control all entry (D); plants lack mitochondria (E).

**P3, 8 points, pond-plant nursery decision, invented for practice:** A nursery wants an inherited trait that limits insect damage. Gather information from the supplied crop-modification and gene-therapy pages in field 10. Explain how choosing parents differs from directly changing genetic material, and why treating body cells is not automatically a change inherited by offspring. Compare the sources' authorship, evidence, purpose and limits; then give one possible societal benefit and one unresolved cost, access or ecological question (practice only, unscored). Key: breeding selects inherited variants; genetic engineering can introduce or change genetic material; body-cell treatment need not affect reproductive cells. The medical page cannot establish plant outcomes. Practice only: food production could benefit; evidence is needed about effects and access. Four two-point components: breeding mechanism; modification and body-cell distinction; synthesis with source evaluation of authorship and evidence; purpose, method and limits of the two supplied pages (taught day 119). Wrong: breeding guarantees every offspring's trait (F); body-cell therapy automatically passes to offspring (I); official authorship proves every claim (J).
