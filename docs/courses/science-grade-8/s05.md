<!-- skel -->
- **MS-LS1-5:** Explain from evidence how conditions in the surroundings and genes affect how organisms grow. Limit: Leave out how genes work, how genes are switched on and off, and biochemical processes.
- **MS-LS3-2:** Build and use a model showing why asexual reproduction gives offspring identical genes while sexual reproduction gives offspring variation. Emphasis: Punnett squares, diagrams and simulations showing how genes pass from parents to young and cause variation.
- **MS-LS3-1:** Build and use a model showing why changes to genes on chromosomes can alter proteins and so harm, help or not affect an organism. Emphasis: the idea that changed genetic material can lead to different proteins. Limit: Leave out molecular-level changes, how proteins are made, and named kinds of mutation.
- **MS-LS1-2:** Build and use a model of how a whole cell works and how its parts help it work. Emphasis: the cell as one working system, and the main job of five named parts: nucleus, chloroplasts, mitochondria, the membrane and the wall. Limit: Structure and function links only for the cell wall and membrane; other parts only in relation to the whole cell; no biochemistry of cells or their parts.
- **MS-LS4-5:** Collect and combine information about technologies that changed how people shape the inherited traits of organisms. Emphasis: reliable sources on human-driven selection (breeding, genetic modification, gene therapy), its effects on society, and the technology behind the discoveries.
- **MS-LS1-4:** Argue from evidence and reasoning how typical animal behaviors and special plant parts raise the chance that animals and plants reproduce.
