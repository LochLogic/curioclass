# Section 1.6: From first draft to a revision plan

English Language Arts Grade 6. Unit 1, River town: the flood year, [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 21 to 23.** Days by kind: application 2, understanding 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.6.3, “Write.”** “Engage and orient” and “organize” (a) and “Use narrative techniques” (b) in the carried draft. Teach “Use a variety of transition words, phrases, and clauses” (c) on day 21, and “Use precise words and phrases” (d) and “Provide a conclusion” (e) on day 22 before application. The teacher explains how a transition locates the reader, how a detail makes an event tangible and what earlier decision makes an ending possible.
- **W.6.4, “Produce.”** Check that the sequence and voice serve classmates. **W.6.6, “Use” and “demonstrate.”** Use school devices for drafting where available. The grade 6 single-sitting sample comes later.
- **W.6.5, “develop and strengthen.”** On day 23, use written adult and peer guidance to choose and try a substantive change, including rewriting a passage or trying another approach. **L.6.3, “Use,” “Vary” (a) and “Maintain” (b).** Combine and vary sentences for meaning and sustain the narrator's tone. A deliberate change when a character speaks can serve the scene.
- Content includes relocation, costs and restricted access after the flood. Discuss the writing choices in the fictional scenes. Keep students' own experiences private.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned: P1 and P5** from [unit field 5](s01.md). Day 27 provides revised N1 evidence. Day 30 collects it without new sitting work. Apply the unit's 2, 1, 0 component scale.

- **P1.** Separate the narrator and context, natural sequence, purposeful dialogue, pacing and description, time and setting shifts, precise sensory detail and a following conclusion. Use the unit's model calibration. Missing orientation (G), an unrelated ending (H).

  **Shape: P1.** Judge components of an independently revised whole narrative collected from the folder. Use pieces of the anchor and the unit model for practice. Keep any completed N1 or matching full assessment prompt off cards.

- **P5.** Read the day 27 N1 alongside the dated change note. Look for a purposeful pattern change and consistent voice in separate cells. A longer setup followed by a brief realization can slow the moment. Repeated structure (P), an unexplained switch to report language (Q).

  **Shape: P5.** Use actual narrative versions and the writer's explanation after feedback. Practice compares clause order in released sentences. Keep any completed change note or full scored response out of the models.

Daily exits use met, partly or not yet. Keep first responses on separate slips.

**E21 (day 21).** (1) I wrote the question at home. (2) I showed it at the exhibition the following afternoon. “Join these events in a short scene. Show when and where they happen with different transitions.”

Look-for: time and place shift clearly and events stay in order. “That evening at my kitchen table, I wrote the question. By the following afternoon I was holding it up in the exhibition hall.” Accept equivalent shifts. Wrong: “I wrote it after I showed it” (E).

**Shape: E21.** Join fixed events in a new scene with varied transitions. Practice locates chapter shifts and marks the move in private drafts. Reserve these two events and the supplied-events composition.

**E22 (day 22).** (1) A visitor offered a photograph to help identify an uncertain date. (2) The club checked its stamped envelope. “Write an ending that follows these events. Include one precise detail a reader can sense.”

Look-for: the detail is specific and inquiry produces a plausible outcome. “The envelope rasped under my thumb. We penciled its postmark beside the photograph as a date to investigate.” Accept other grounded endings. Wrong: “Then our club launched a rocket” (H), “The postmark proved the photograph's exact date” (B).

**Shape: E22.** Add sensory detail and resolution to an unseen sequence. Practice annotates the numbered drawer and final drawing and completes private drafts. Hold the photo, envelope, postmark and joint prompt.

**E23 (day 23).** Draft: “I checked it. I read it. I fixed it. Yo, the official report concludes.” Feedback: “The discovery feels rushed. The ending changes voice.” “Revise the passage with that feedback. Explain how your changes help a reader.”

Look-for: substantive revision, purposeful sentence variation and consistent narrative tone. “When I held the page to the lamp, I saw that the corner had torn away. So that was where the last line went.” The opening clause delays the discovery, and the closing sentence stays in the teller's voice. Accept other rewrites that answer both parts of the cue. Wrong: a clean copy (J), repeated identical sentence patterns (P), “The agency hereby certifies completion” closing an otherwise personal account (Q).

**Shape: E23.** Revise a supplied rushed passage from written feedback and explain its effect. Practice compares two approaches in the anchor and selects the stronger of two feedback notes on different material. The writer also tries an unjudged change to N1. Reserve the draft and cue.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

The teacher holds the paired chapter selections and *The Noticeboard*. Students carry a first draft and dated source note through day 22. On day 23 the ideal instrument is an earlier passage facing its dated feedback and revision plan. Paper fits seeing what changed and why. Mark the shifts with the class, then withdraw marks for exit work. With limited supplies, number handwritten versions and clip each note to the relevant passage. Tool each day: None.

## 6. Days

## Day 21

Application. “I can show when and where the story moves.” Students keep N1 and their own transition marks.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E20 and discussion cues, then locate a time cue in a released passage.
- **9 min. Model shifts.** Compare chapter 3's walk with chapter 15 after the signing. Mark when and where each passage places Mara. Teach a time word, a place phrase and an opening clause.
- **15 min. Reading conferences.** Seven 90-second turns including moves leave 4½ minutes for records. Reuse recent records. Classmates read independently and log.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Move an opening clause in a released sentence. Try the pattern in a draft sentence and check order.
- **13 min. Draft transitions.** Writers mark the shifts they need, then draft by hand or on a school device. Date the first version before any partner reads it.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E21 on paper, alone.

## Day 22

Application. “I can use a precise detail and make my ending follow.” Students hold chapter 16, the model and N1.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E21. Use one released event-order or transition cue.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing at the final door drawing. Fifteen pairs read up to 45 seconds each and exchange a cue before retrying.
- **10 min. Study ending.** Mark a tangible detail in *The Noticeboard* and the physical space made in chapter 16. Place the early and late drawings side by side. Explain how the last action follows an earlier choice.
- **25 min. Finish first draft.** Check orientation, technique, transitions, details and outcome. The child's draft remains folder work. Collect dated copies for written teacher comments, one located change to investigate per pupil before day 23.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E22 on paper, alone.

## Day 23

Understanding. “I can use feedback to make a change readers can follow.” Students hold the draft, teacher cue and peer comment.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E22 and distribute the separate written teacher comments. Each pupil receives one priority cue.
- **10 min. Model revision.** Compare how people are moved in chapter 4 and how space is rearranged in chapter 10. Show how combining released clauses changes emphasis. Compare two possible revisions for meaning and voice.
- **10 min. Peer feedback.** Save separate first readings of the drafts. Fifteen pairs talk for up to 45 seconds per writer and 45 seconds per reader in each direction, three minutes total. Each reader leaves a written location and question. A teacher note supplies a missing partner cue.
- **20 min. Try another approach.** Date an individual revision plan and try a changed passage. Keep the earlier wording, both comments, the decision and its reason. Send them to day 24.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E23 on paper, alone.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 21: “Which words tell you Mara has left the office scene?” Day 22: “Which earlier drawing gives the last drawing its force?” Day 23: “What do the changes to the room let people do?” Look for a located cue, a named choice and what it makes possible. The day 23 writing is a private revision trial. The teacher's descriptive look-for is whether a reader can follow the choice through a shift in time or place.

## 9. Supports and extension

A transition tells readers when or where the action moves. A sensory detail shows what someone can see, hear, smell, taste or feel. Feedback is a reader's response that can guide a change. A pattern is an arrangement a writer can vary. Style is the way the writing sounds across a piece. Tone is how a voice sounds about what's happening. Preview, mark and reuse these words on the named days. “I moved ___ so a reader could ___.” Keep selected anchor excerpts to 300 words per work segment, alongside complete print and optional audio. Log independent choice reading separately. Large print or a scribe supports access. Extension: explain what a different clause order makes readers notice first.
