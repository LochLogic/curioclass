# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 6

Course id ela-k12 (grade 6 row), grade 6, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6, each one specific topic and its set of texts: a flood-town novella, the first written laws, how
  forecasters read the sky, how writing systems work, storm poems and a one-act play, and storm warnings from 1900
  to today.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units and 12 at course level (6 for three course-level checks of 2 days, 6
  respond days, 2 after each check). It assumes one ELA lesson of about 50 minutes every school day, five a week,
  for 32 weeks (grade 5's 90-minute block ends here; its skills block's word study moves into the unit lessons),
  about 20 minutes of independent reading on four nights a week, logged, and 15 minutes of in-class independent
  reading twice a week, when the teacher confers with each student about once every two weeks. Choice books come
  from the Curio Class Reading Room shelf, printable, or any book the student already has. The course places 29
  progression rows: a long text read whole, three pieces through the full writing process and six shorter ones, two
  research projects (taught in unit 3, applied again in unit 6), two rounds of talks, a formal text discussion at
  least every two weeks and the oral reading check; that load fills the full year (the units are weighed in the
  table, not given equal shares).
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 6 (RL, RI, W, SL and L; RF ends in grade 5). Every
  grade 6 code is owned by a row placed below, and every lettered part (W.6.1a to e, W.6.2a to f, W.6.3a to e,
  W.6.9a and b, SL.6.1a to d, L.6.1a to e, L.6.2a and b, L.6.3a and b, L.6.4a to d, L.6.5a to c) is named in the
  unit that teaches it. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (grades 6 to 12 have no first gateway) and the approach is
  docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule governs every unit (rule b, facts and backed sources, for the weather and
  hazard texts).
- **Base text.** None: ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Texts are chosen
  by BASELINE rule 29: anchors are original Curio Class texts written to the grade 6 text spec (present-day
  settings, children like the class) and read by a qualified ELA teacher before use, and recent US government
  nonfiction, which is public domain as a work of the federal government (17 U.S.C. 105). An older public-domain
  text (published 1930 or earlier) appears only as a paired or optional read, never the anchor: in units 2 and 6,
  where a primary source matters most (King's 1910 translation of Hammurabi's code, Cline's 1900 report), the
  standard is taught first on the original and federal anchors and then applied to a short paired excerpt read
  with full support. The grade 6 text spec gives each original literary text its own named narrator or speaker,
  voice and setting, and no two anchors in a unit share a voice. Each original is a commission
  to that spec; its text record (the measure where usable, the qualitative demands, reader and task, the
  placement reason) comes with the scope and sequence, and the informational share is counted from those records.
  No text is sent home from a library or a paid copy. Each unit's texts and their rights line are in the
  Units table's C3 column (ELA has no C3 framework; see "For the lead"). Images inside government pages are
  checked one by one, since some carry a third party's copyright.
- **Reading level.** The 2012 supplement's grades 6 to 8 band (925 to 1185 on its first measure) is the year's
  target. Working split, ours: most grade 6 anchors in the lower half of the band, a few at the top with support
  (the forecasting articles and the Champollion profile; the paired Hammurabi translation and Cline's 1900 report
  are read with full support). A poem or play has no usable computed
  measure; its text record rests on the qualitative judgment and the task. Anchors aim at about 55 percent
  informational, counted in the scope and sequence. Grade 6 is the first year in the new band, so support returns
  at its upper end: before, during and after reading, matched to what makes each text hard, never a summary in its
  place.
- **Knowledge threads.** Two topic sets run through grades 6 to 8 (e6-reading-range). Thread 1, laws and records
  of early societies, in units 2 and 4 beside social studies grade 6. Thread 2, Earth's systems and hazards,
  picks up grade 5's water unit (how much of Earth's water is usable and how communities share it) in unit 1's
  flood town, and runs through units 3, 5 and 6 beside science grade 6's weather and hazards units. Each unit's
  word list is drawn from its texts and words come back in later units: law, code, record, society, justice
  (thread 1); system, hazard, forecast, data, evidence (thread 2).
- **Writing calendar.** Nine pieces: argument 3 (unit 2 process piece and a short on-demand piece, unit 6
  on-demand), informative 3 (unit 3 process piece, units 4 and 6 on-demand), narrative 3 (unit 1 process piece,
  units 4 and 5 on-demand), a third each against the 35, 35 and 30 the rubric reports. The shares are counted by
  pieces; the scope and sequence also reports them by days. Every reading day
  ends in the evidence paragraph or written question (W.6.9, W.6.10). Typing climbs: about one typed page in unit
  1, two by unit 2, three in one sitting by unit 6.
- **Paper path and no accounts.** Every anchor has a print copy. Curio Class has no student accounts and hosts no
  student work; collaboration is peer comment in whatever the school already uses. Unit 3's report is published
  through the school's own digital route (a shared classroom device or a lab session in the school day), with
  peer comments that lead to a revision. A room without devices writes by hand and types later where it can, and
  the plan says plainly that the paper path does not meet W.6.6: that part is recorded unfinished for the class.
- **Fluency.** In the first two weeks a one-minute oral reading check (the teacher holds the watch) flags any
  student below about 112 words correct a minute for a closer look; once a week partners reread a short anchor
  passage aloud. No rate target is shown to students; Curio Class is not a reading intervention.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read, and every original text gets the
  qualified ELA teacher's read (rule 29). Units 1 and 6 (floods, a hurricane's deaths) and unit 2 (the code's
  punishments, slavery in Babylon) are flagged for care; unit 4's Korean family story is named for a reader who
  knows the language and culture, and unit 1's retelling of Yu the Great for a reader who knows Chinese language
  and culture (who, and any pay, is Cory's).
- **Heroes and their trials.** Cory's 2026-11-12 decision for the grade 6 pilot (keep heroes and their trials; a
  new Curio Class hero story set today as the anchor, one myth retold in our own words as the paired text) is
  carried by unit 1: the novella is a present-day hero's trials in a flood year, paired with a Curio Class
  retelling of Yu the Great, who tamed China's floods. The frame reconcile settles where the pilot itself runs. No Holocaust literature is picked in grade 6.
