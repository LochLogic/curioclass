## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned: B1, B2 and B3** from [unit field 5](s01.md), day 15. The first two are teacher-judged. Use separate component cells with 2 accurate, located and explained, 1 partial, 0 absent or contradicted. Read the first two numbered paragraphs under “Snowmelt” and the unit plan's nine-cell, patterned map with caption. The exact passage and map are release copies. [Flood Related Hazards](https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-hazards)

- **B1, 12 minutes.** Paragraph 1 describes water stored as snow until temperatures rise. Paragraph 2 explains infiltration and runoff. Flooding is possible if water exceeds absorption or storage. Together the paragraphs explain how a thaw could flood a place on a day without new rain. Wrong: snowfall reaches the soil immediately (A), “It floods because it is warm” without supporting evidence (B).

  **Shape: B1.** Give the stated form of storage and a cited inference about a rainless day. Keep frozen storage, thaw and the specific no-new-rain inference off worked examples, oral practice and cards. Use rain and mapped observations as teaching cases before release.

- **B2/E15, 18 minutes.** Thaw releases the stored water (paragraph 1). If soils or water bodies cannot absorb or hold it, it can run off and cause flooding (paragraph 2). The pattern covers the stream, low field and low road in the invented past event. Cite the caption to limit what the location record can establish. A supported key can explain possible exposure. Claiming a cause for that mapped event requires further evidence. Wrong: “It is cold there in winter” (V), “It floods there at every thaw” (B).

  **Shape: B2/E15.** Synthesize a cold cause chain with mapped exposure, citing the caption. Withhold both Snowmelt paragraphs and the entire map, key and caption. Practice single-source notes, never a parallel combined case.

- **B3, record only.** Reuse dated informational logs and conferences with support conditions intact. Keep day 6's separately dated literary evidence.

  **Shape: B3.** Check existing range and comprehension records. Add no fresh passage, conference or writing prompt.

Daily exits use met, partly or not yet. Preserve first work. E15 is the B2 sitting item, scored once on the assessment record.

**E13 (day 13).** (1) After the town meeting, / Ren checked the labels. (2) When a date was uncertain, / she left a space. “Read the passage aloud using the marked phrases. Summarize Ren's response without judging it.”

Look-for: Ren checks the labels and leaves the uncertain date blank. Wrong: “Ren carelessly leaves gaps” (D), “She supplied every date” (A). For oral reading, note accurate words, grouping each opening clause and any self-correction. Save oral observations in the reading log with listener and date.

**Shape: E13.** Give a first reading of marked clauses and a neutral written summary. Practice rereads an unmarked passage for phrasing and examines different released paragraphs for summary coverage. Reserve Ren's text, marked boundaries and combined task.

**E14 (day 14).** (1) Isha expected praise for her drawing. (2) The builder asked where the doorway went. (3) Isha erased a chair and widened the entrance on paper. “What happens because the builder asks about the doorway? Quote words that show how Isha responds.”

Look-for: the question draws attention to how people would enter, and Isha is willing to change the drawing she expected others to admire. “Erased a chair” or “widened the entrance on paper” (3) supports the inference. Wrong: “She dislikes chairs” (B), “She revised before he asked” (E).

**Shape: E14.** Explain a consequence and support a reading of a changed response. Collect separate quotations on actual staffing and calls in chapter 11. Keep Isha's entrance and chair off models, practice and cards.

**E16 (day 16).** (1) Survey note: This outline records one flood's extent. (2) Story: The council asked for more records before choosing a room. “Revise this paragraph using both notes.” Paragraph: “The outline proves no other land ever floods, so the council already has every answer.”

Look-for: the outline records how far one flood reached (1). The council seeks more records (2) because other events could reach different places. Wrong: “The outline guarantees safety” (B), “They had already chosen the room” (A).

**Shape: E16.** Correct an overclaim in a supplied paragraph with two new sources. Repair actual B responses about thaw, absorption and storage. Withdraw the earlier paragraph frame and reserve the council prompt for closing.

## 6. Days

## Day 13

Fluency. “I can reread smoothly and give a neutral summary.” Pairs hold chapter 10, each student a first-response sheet.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E12. Match a theme to an action or order released events.
- **10 min. Oral rereading.** Model one clause boundary in chapter 10. Fifteen pairs alternate up to 45 seconds each on a first read and then give a cue and retry. Teacher hears a rotating six, six 45-second reads taking 4½ minutes. Keep the listener and date. No rate target.
- **30 min. Summarize the turn.** Read selected paragraphs, under 300 words. Compare the earlier drawing with what Mara sends. Mark an event that belongs and set aside a judgment in supplied notes. Keep each child's first summary response.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E13. The teacher hears both readers from each of three named pairs in six 30-second turns including notes. The other twelve pairs have up to 45 seconds per reader in parallel. Everyone writes an individual summary before hearing feedback. Take all summaries and peer observation slips. Mark individual teacher listening as pending for the others.

## Day 14

Understanding. “I can trace what a choice causes.” Students keep chapter 11 and quotation notes.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E13. Repair a summary or give an individual phrasing cue. Record any needed private listening turn.
- **14 min. Read.** Read all 1,583 words of chapter 11.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Eight 90-second turns including moves leave three minutes for notes. Reuse recent records. Other pupils read their choice books.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Combine anchor clauses with the same speaker and commitment.
- **8 min. Quotation notes.** Mark why Mara offers to make calls and what Bea can actually promise. Sort notes by speaker and record a location.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E14 on paper, alone. Keep replies private until day 16 feedback.

## Day 15

Check. Students hold the fixed text, invented map and response sheet.

- **10 min. Cold reading.** Read the two numbered “Snowmelt” paragraphs and the original grid with caption. Gloss snowpack as accumulated snow.
- **12 min. Explicit and inferred evidence.** B1, individual paper responses.
- **18 min. Synthesis.** B2/E15, individual paper responses.
- **5 min. Check citations.** Check locations independently.
- **5 min. Collect.** Retrieve the B3 logs without a new sitting task. After class allow four minutes per child for provisional marking and written feedback, 120 minutes for 30.

## Day 16

Respond. “I can make my evidence paragraph more accurate.” Students have earlier answers, feedback and chapter 12.

- **5 min. Hand-back.** Return B1 and B2 with written cues. Give each pupil their pending E14 reply privately.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Route one priority letter per child. Use concurrent source-location or cause-order work.
- **5 min. Earlier-answer revision.** Correct the earlier answer independently before partner comparison. Finalize the earlier records after class, two minutes per child.
- **14 min. Read.** Read all 1,627 words of chapter 12.
- **1 min. Model study.** Locate Mara's hours and the sentence showing she can name someone for each part of the promise.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Seven 90-second turns including moves leave 4½ minutes for notes. Others read independently. Reuse recent eligible records.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E16 without the frame. Give a specific cue to day 17.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use consequence as on day 12 and objective for free of personal judgment. Resolution tells how the story problem is worked out. The grade 5 resolution entry is planned only. “After ___, Mara ___.” Revisit each word in a released paragraph and its summary. Complete chapter print sits beside any audio. Give large print, tactile or row-ordered map details with the same labels, key and caption. Directions may be spoken and responses scribed. Passage audio is supported reading and goes in that record. Extension: test a second inference with another chapter detail or name what further evidence would settle a survey claim.

## 10. Two rooms

At home the parent listens to each first reading and gives one phrasing cue after the child saves the summary. Give the cold print set for independent work. At school one screen can model phrasing, and each pupil gets a separate stimulus. Hold back exact passages, caption, map and audio until release.
