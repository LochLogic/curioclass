## The working period and continuing routines

The frame allocates 50 minutes each school day, plus 20 minutes of independent reading on four nights a week. Home reading is choice reading or an announced novella installment, not two separate assignments on the same night. Class print copies, audio beside print and a supported in-school reading route provide the same complete anchor for students who cannot read at home. Homework is never the only route to a required passage.

A usual period holds up to five minutes of necessary background and words, 20 minutes reading or modelling, 15 minutes applying or writing, five minutes discussion, and five minutes written evidence or an exit question. On a conference day, 15 minutes of independent reading replace 15 minutes of that main-work block, leaving 35 minutes. The CSV names two conference slots every week, including weeks with checks. A short teacher conference with roughly three pupils a slot reaches a class of about 30 once every two weeks; the rest read and log. Class size may require shorter conferences, not less anchor reading. A weekly five-minute partner oral reread also sits inside the period, on the CSV's named day. When it shares a conference day, 30 minutes remain for other work. Teacher models phrasing and gives feedback, rather than asking partners to time each other.

Every reading day closes with a short text-based written answer, inside the main-work minutes. Formal text discussions fall on days 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 58, 68, 76, 86, 96, 105, 112, 116, 124, 131, 136, 146, 154 and 160. There is no gap over ten course days. Day 5 teaches the routine; later discussions fade the frames. Daily partner talk is additional. The discussion look-for follows preparation and citation, agreed rules and goals, elaborated questions and answers, and review with paraphrase of others' views, SL.6.1a to d.

The first two weeks include private one-minute oral reading samples on days 7 to 10. The frame's approximately 112 words-correct threshold prompts closer attention, not a diagnosis, student-facing target or lowered anchor. Shared reading and audio support maintain grade-level access. RF has no grade 6 ownership.

Protect days 78 and 149 as full 50-minute three-page typing sittings, with no conference slot. Three pages means an agreed ordinary format, approximately 600 to 750 words, not enlarging type to reach a page count. Production, publishing and online peer comments use the school's own device route; no student account or hosted work is required by Curio Class. Paper supports composition but leaves W.6.6 unfinished. Unit 1 begins the standard, unit 3 completes its three-page and digital-collaboration demands, and unit 6 applies it again.

Sentence practice uses the long-sentence routine, sentence combining and sentence imitation, chosen for the actual difficulty in the anchor. In the long-sentence routine, students take apart one sentence into its propositions and rebuild it without changing its meaning; imitation keeps a useful pattern while changing its content. Reserve two three-minute practices each week, inside the existing reading or revision time, never added to the period. Use the first and last available teaching days, excluding protected typing sittings and days collecting end evidence; when a week has no such day, use its respond day. When only one day is available, it holds both short practices. Start with grade 5 conventions, label that work retrieval, and apply grade 6 language only after its explicit teaching: pronouns and sentence variety after days 23 to 26, nonrestrictive punctuation after days 97 to 98. Use the long-sentence routine only where syntax is the obstacle. The pilot gets an episode map and a who-is-speaking note; old-language worksheets from the former pilot do not transfer to a present-day novella. The methods follow docs/APPROACH-ELA.md; the two short weekly slots are a judgment call, retained from the earlier plan at a smaller time cost.

Each unit plan must collect its English learner supports in one place: the text-derived word list, sentence frames for that unit's writing type and discussion, appropriate sentence unpacking, and audio beside the complete print text. Frames fade after modelled and shared practice. Each support needs a teacher note saying what difficulty it addresses and when to withdraw it. A simpler replacement text does not stand for the anchor. The pilot packet needs this same package independently of day 1.

The three writing-type rubrics are shared across the year. Introduce a model, a rubric and an annotated sample at N1's model study on day 17, A1's on day 42 and I1's on day 72. Use them during feedback and revision, then reuse the same resources for later pieces. The unit plan writes these resources; they add no piece, assessment or day here. This retains the earlier plan's intended scoring resources and applies docs/APPROACH-ELA.md's writing-process rule to all three types.

Tasks and scoring stay within grade 6: one theme or central idea and its conveying details; one narrator's developed point of view; supported versus unsupported claims; no required counterclaim, judgment of reasoning's soundness or evidence sufficiency, medium-specific production techniques, effective search terms or standard citation style. Research requires credibility, refocusing where appropriate and basic bibliographic information. These boundaries follow the grade 6 standards and the grade 7 additions in data/progressions/ela.csv. Later-unit teaching is not silently required in an earlier task.

Assessment plans below reserve evidence, not items or answer keys. Each listed code receives a prompt for every applicable part, or the named portfolio or observed performance. Lettered codes are carried explicitly in the CSV. Parent codes on ordinary teaching days identify continuing practice, not a claim to teach all their parts for the first time that day. W.6.10 and the range codes use the accumulated writing folder, reading log and observed reading as well as the cold task.
