## 3. Checks, written first

**Owned from unit field 5: A3, given on day 6.** Give two short stems on the same sheet. “Where does paragraph 1 say the rain fell?” “What reason for returning the key can you infer from paragraph 5?” Students locate evidence beside each answer. Its literal key is the distant hills (paragraph 1). Returning the key and bringing drawings downstairs (paragraph 5) support the inference that Lina no longer decides alone. Mark the location and inference about Lina’s reason separately. Wrong: rain only at the gate (A), she hates keys (B).

**Shape: A3.** A cold literal location and a cited motive from separate paragraphs. Keep the gate story, key and paired place-and-motive prompt out of models, cards and practice.

The following fresh scenes are Curio Class originals, invented for practice. On all three exit slips print the item code beside its number: E1 RL.6.1 and RL.6.3, E2 RL.6.2 and RL.6.3, E3 RL.6.2 and W.6.9a. Keep first answers individually. Sort by the stated look-for as met, partly or not yet.

**E1 (day 1).** (1) Rafi kept his clay bird under a bowl while the others painted. (2) When a child asked about its beak, Rafi lifted the bowl and turned the bird toward her. “How would you describe Rafi’s change using one quotation?”

Sample: At first he hides his work. When he “turned the bird toward her” (2), he let someone study it. Look for a change supported by exact words. Wrong: “He kept it covered” (A), “He wanted to sell the bird” (B).

**Shape: E1.** Infer a change from a new action. Practice marks information in Mara’s two drawings. Hold the clay bird for closing.

**E2 (day 2).** (1) Eda tried to carry the empty display alone. (2) It tilted, so she put it down and asked Kit to hold one end. “What message belongs under a two-box map of Eda’s actions?” Give each box an event and use the caption for your own theme.

Look-for: solo attempt, tilt, request for help in that order. Recognizing a limit can lead someone to seek help. Accept another general claim grounded in that sequence. Wrong: “Carrying” (C), “Kit helps before the display tilts” (E).

**Shape: E2.** Map two episodes and independently infer a theme. Practice arranges teacher-supplied event labels and tracks decisions on the unfinished trial arc. Save Eda's display and the combined map-and-theme task for the exit.

**E3 (day 3).** (1) Lio's paper model kept falling. (2) He listened to a younger child's suggestion and widened its base. (3) The model stood through the exhibition. “How does one quotation help explain what the outcome suggests about whose ideas matter?”

Look-for: a theme tied to the result. Useful advice can come from someone younger. Lio “widened its base” (2) after hearing the suggestion, and the model stayed upright (3). Allow an equivalent theme with relevant words. Wrong: “It fell at the exhibition” (A), a copied line with no explanation (F).

**Shape: E3.** Produce and explain a cited theme from an untaught ending. Model point, located words and explanation on the job of the name slips. Keep Lio’s model and the question about younger people’s ideas for closing.

## 6. Days

## Day 1

Read literature (understanding). “I can quote a detail and trace a response.” Teacher holds the chapter. Students keep separate quotation notes.

- **5 min. Door drawings.** Mark what could stay or move in the opening drawings, then ask what a river town keeps and changes after a flood.
- **4 min. Quotation retrieval.** Use the fresh opening to locate exact words and contrast Mara's drawing plans with Grandpa's interest in the photograph. Ask what message those different responses might suggest. A river is a large natural stream of flowing water.
- **2 min. Spring records.** Use the incoming log and a familiar word from existing work. Retrieve a literary and an informational log entry by title, type and support when available. Privately identify missing or flagged evidence. Keep this retrieval unscored.
- **15 min. Reading conferences.** Students read available choice books. Seven individual turns of 90 seconds including moves leave 4½ minutes for notes. Reuse recent qualifying notes and work only with pupils due a turn.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Use an opening sentence to combine two details without changing meaning. Try the pattern in a draft sentence.
- **16 min. Mark and discuss.** Read selected chapter 1 paragraphs in order. Mark each drawing's location before comparing marks. Fifteen pairs take two 45-second turns in parallel. Each student hands in a marked line. Show the nonfiction note from the novella packet: “Bellwether, its characters, and its visits, maps, and rebuilding decisions are fictional.” Ask what the note establishes about using Mara’s decision as a record of a real town. Accept that the invented decision cannot document a town’s history.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E1 on paper, alone.

## Day 2

Read literature (understanding). “I can map Mara’s choices and suggest a message they might show.” Teacher holds the arc model. Pairs keep maps.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E1 and a targeted cue. Standalone entrants start with the two drawings.
- **27 min. Shared reading.** Read all 3,179 words of chapters 1 and 2 with complete print.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing from the folded-notice passage. In fifteen pairs, each reads for up to 45 seconds and hears a brief cue before retrying.
- **8 min. Episode map.** Draw one arrow for “then” on a released pair of events. Review topic (what the story is about) and theme (a message its events support). Partners arrange announcement, conversations and blank reverse labels on the story arc. Each draws an arrow and labels what the blank reverse makes possible.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E2 on paper, alone.

## Day 3

Write (understanding). “I can explain a theme with a detail.” Teacher holds the citation model. Students retain the story packet and paragraph frame.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E2. Identify where the unfinished trial stopped.
- **24 min. Shared reading.** Read all 2,860 words of chapters 3 and 4 through Bea's closing exchange. Keep chapters 1 to 4 in the standalone packet.
- **16 min. Cited paragraph.** Put the main idea above its supporting details. Model a complete theme answer on a released name-slip detail, including what the outcome adds. Have students mark a detail about the changed entry or returning customers, then supply the missing explanation in a partial paragraph. Partners compare after each has written. In their source notes, cite the packet’s description of close third-person narration to explain why Mara’s thoughts are available to us. This is a short nonfiction response about the author’s technique.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E3 on paper, alone. Give individual cues to day 4.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 1: “What does Grandpa enlarge in the photograph?” The blue four brings the old drawer system to mind. Day 2: “What can Mara use the back of the notice for?” She can make room for names and needs the first announcement did not include. Day 3: “What can Bea tell Grandpa after he changes his question?” Look for a point about the next event backed by her actual exchange. In the short explanation on day 3, look for exact words, a location and what the words show.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 1 retrieves exact quotation, inference, response and character comparison. Review “quotation: a source word sequence copied exactly inside paired marks,” “infer: work out an unstated idea using text details,” and “response: what a character says or does about it” in their written grade 4 senses. Use the written “reading log: date, title, my purpose, one detail.” Grade 5 unit 1 entries for quotation, infer, respond and the log are planned only. Start from actual work or chapter 1. Days 2 and 3 return to the arc and support for a “theme within one text: a message supported by its events and details.” On day 1 review “sentence-combining: preserve complete thoughts when joining or separating clauses.” Day 2 reviews “partner rereading with a feedback card: phrases and expression, no speed.” No rate target.

Request the dated grade 5 days 157 and 158 handoff, with words read alone and in context, spelling, supports and named gaps. Save each new date, support and missing component for private makeup and the course's days 157 and 158 portfolio handoff.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview trial, episode and flood in context. A trial is a challenge that tests Mara's plan. The grade 4 trial entry is planned only. An episode is one part of a connected story. Floodwater covers land that is usually dry. Return to the words during reading and on the map. Review “evidence: the line or page that shows an idea is true” from grade 4. Use “At first Mara ___. The words ___ suggest ___.” Mark the main action in a long sentence, then reattach its opening phrase. Preview and annotate up to 1,500 chapter words on day 1, besides independent choice reading. Days 2 and 3 keep complete chapter print beside any shared or recorded reading. Large print, accessible text and scribed responses preserve access. Record passage audio separately from independent reading. Extension: test a second inference about Grandpa with a different detail.

## 10. Two rooms

In each section, a public speaker may pass. Collect the individual written claim and give a cue privately; for speech evidence, name the next listening turn. At home, the adult marks a different detail, then trades explanations after the child has saved a first answer. Use one projected paragraph for modeling and paper packets afterward. Reserve exact exit sentences, pictures, captions and any audio before making teaching copies.
