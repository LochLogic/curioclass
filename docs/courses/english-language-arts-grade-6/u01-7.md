**E24 (day 24).** “Jo and ___ made this label. The volunteer handed it to ___. It is ___.” “Fill the blanks with first-person pronouns. Rewrite the first sentence to emphasize your role with an intensive pronoun.”

Key: I, me, mine. “I myself made this label with Jo.” Removing myself leaves a complete subject. Wrong: “to I” (K), “Myself made this label” (L).

**Shape: E24.** Supply three forms and rewrite to add emphasis. Practice sorts roles and removes an intensive from released chapter dialogue. Reserve the label blanks and the combined task.

**E25 (day 25).** “I checked my labels because you wanted them accurate. The workers carried her boxes. When Ana met Jo, she offered a date.” The narrator wants accuracy. The workers own the boxes. Ana offers the date. “Type these lines with clear, consistent pronouns.”

Key: “I checked my labels because I wanted them accurate. The workers carried their boxes. When Ana met Jo, Ana offered a date.” Accept equivalent unambiguous revisions. Wrong: retains “you” (M), “her” (W) or ambiguous “she” (N).

**Shape: E25.** Repair a held set in a typed revision. Practice traces Kit's referents or marks intentions in private N1 drafts. Keep the supplied errors and triple repair off the slides.

**E26 (day 26).** A character says to a friend, “Me and Jo was checking.” Jo writes to the exhibition committee, “Jo and I were checking the labels.” “Why does each version fit its setting? Type a two-sentence note to the committee.”

Look-for: explain the two audiences and use the reference to check the committee note. “Jo and I checked the labels. Could you tell us who should sign them?” Wrong: “That speaker is less capable” (O), “Me checked the labels” in the formal note (K). Save the typed note before a one-comment exchange. The exchange and the day's own spoken example are separate language and digital records.

**Shape: E26.** Explain fit and compose an unseen committee note. Instruction uses the board transcript for a setting sort and reference check. Seal the label exchange, exact transcript or audio and prompt.

**E27 (day 27).** “I checked it. I checked it again. I checked it again.” Peer comment: “Show what made you hesitate.” “Revise this short draft. Explain how the change helps a reader.”

Look-for: substantive cause for hesitation, varied sentences and consistent narrative voice. “Although the knot looked tight, a thin loop slid loose. I pulled again.” The longer setup reveals why the narrator hesitates. The short sentence gives the response. Wrong: a clean copy (J), “The undersigned hereby verifies the knot” as the closing line (Q).

**Shape: E27.** Revise an anonymous repetitive draft from one cue and explain the change. Keep N1 comment response in the private folder and judge a feedback choice on separate material. Reserve the supplied draft and cue.

## 9. Supports and extension

Subjective is the subject form. Objective is the object form. Possessive shows belonging. An intensive emphasizes a subject or other named person or thing. Objective in a summary means free of personal judgment. An antecedent is who or what a pronoun points to. A shift is a change. Person identifies the speaker, listener or someone spoken about. Number distinguishes one from more than one here. A reference gives a form to check. Conventional wording follows the usual form for a formal setting. Register is the way words fit a setting. Coherent writing is easy to follow from one idea to the next. A revision changes a piece to help readers. To publish here is to let the class read the finished version. Preview the words, mark them in actual sentences and reuse them in the reference notes. “My reader is ___, so I chose ___.” Use selections under 240 words with complete print available, and choice reading during conferences. Allow large print and scribing for language evidence. Extend by explaining when a repeated name clarifies a paragraph.
