English Language Arts Grade 6. Unit 1, River town: the flood year, [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 4 to 7.** Days by kind: read literature 2, speak and listen 1, check 1. Course kinds: understanding 1, application 1, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **RL.6.2, “Determine” and “provide.”** Compare details that convey a theme and give a summary free of judgment. **RL.6.3, “Describe.”** Track episode order and the character's response. **RL.6.1, “Cite.”** Connect what the myth states and what its ending implies to located evidence.
- **W.6.9, “Draw,” and W.6.9a and b, “Apply.”** Write from the literary pair here. Keep the Yu source note beside the retelling. **RI.6.1, “Cite,”** applies to that factual note. Day 8 adds scientific evidence. **W.6.10, “Write routinely.”** File short responses by date. **RL.6.10 and RI.6.10, “read and comprehend.”** Keep logs separate by genre and by reading support.
- **SL.6.1, “Engage.”** Teach the grade 6 discussion moves on day 5 during the application day. In a, “Come” prepared with a located detail and “draw” on it. In b, “Follow,” “set” and “define” cover rules, goal, deadline and roles. In c, “Pose and respond” with elaboration. In d, “Review” ideas and “demonstrate understanding” by confirming a paraphrase.
- Explain the hall frame and earlier journey in Yu. The dialogue is imagined for this retelling of a story from a living Chinese tradition. Content includes failed flood walls, families displaced to hills, crop loss and food scarcity. The novella also includes money worries. Keep questions on the texts and allow students to pass.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Owned from unit field 5: A1, A2, A4 and A5.** A3 belongs to section 1.1 and is administered with them on day 6. Use the complete *The Gatekeeper and the River*, an original modern myth, for the fixed first form. Use the unit's component scale, 2 accurate and supported, 1 partial, 0 absent or contradicted. Keep separate cells for each part. Only A3's location is self-checked. Open components receive written feedback and a later revision before the record is finalized.

- **A1, 9 minutes.** Give separate stems. “What theme do two of Lina’s choices convey?” “How would you summarize the myth in three sentences for someone who missed it?” Accept any text-supported theme. For example, a leader can change what it means to hold office. Lina seeks villagers’ knowledge (4) and gives up the key while keeping her post (5). Sample summary: Lina adds a chain, but water rises above the gate. The river describes the former channel, and Lina asks the villagers to help change the drawing. The next spring water follows the cleared channel, and Lina returns the key. Mark theme, both details, summary coverage and objectivity separately. “Water” (C), “Lina was stupid” (D).

  **Shape: A1.** Independently give a theme with two details and a three-sentence objective summary of one myth. Practice sorts selected actions and accounts. Reserve the whole myth and the combined task.

- **A2, 7 minutes.** “How does Lina’s way of making decisions change between the second chain and the next spring?” The water still rises after Lina adds the chain (2). The river’s account leads her to check the drawing (3). Villagers reshape the plan and the meadow receives water the following season. Look for why the steps follow and how Lina changes. “The villagers cleared the channel before Lina heard the river speak” (E). The meadow was already taking water in paragraph 2.

  **Shape: A2.** A causal chain and changing response in the gate story. In Yu practice trace where news and food come from and how the hall frames the journey. Keep Lina’s second chain, the river’s account and the later channel work off retell cards.

- **A3, 7 minutes.** Section 1.1 holds the key and error examples.

  **Shape: A3.** Locate a fact and cite a motive from another paragraph. Keep the myth's rain location and the returned key off practice sheets, questions and displays.

- **A4/E6, 7 minutes.** Returning the key to the meeting room (paragraph 5) shows that Lina shares decisions. Prompt: “How does one action in the final paragraph support your theme?” Wrong: “She returned the key” with no interpretation (F), “She quit the watchtower” (A).

  **Shape: A4/E6.** Explain how a final action supports a theme in a cited paragraph. Use separate evidence moves in practice. Keep Lina's returned key and any modeled paragraph about it for the check.

- **A5, record only.** Retrieve dated short responses from days 3 to 5 and A4 for writing frequency. Extended work comes later in N1.

  **Shape: A5.** Verify existing dates. Schedule no additional response or practice replica.

Read E4 and E7 for RL.6.2 and E5 for RL.6.2 and SL.6.1. Use the following original scenes for individual first evidence. Use met, partly or not yet on daily exit slips. Keep the unit scale on the assessment record.

**E4 (day 4).** Two invented tales. In one, a fox wants to eat the last ripe plum. Instead, she buries the whole fruit on a bare hillside. Years later travelers rest in the tree’s shade. In the other, a boy wants to spend his last coin on a sweet. He buys thread instead and mends a fishing net his cousins use all summer. “What shared theme do the fox’s and the boy’s choices develop?”

Sample: Giving up a small pleasure now can do lasting good. The fox gives up the fruit, and the tree eventually provides shade. The boy buys thread that keeps the net in use. Accept a different message supported by both. Wrong: “Food” (C), “The boy buys the sweet” (A).

**Shape: E4.** Compare what each invented ending adds to a shared theme. Use Yu’s messengers and Mara’s repair slips for practice. Reserve the plum and net for the exit.

**E5 (day 5).** (1) Neri planned a display alone. (2) Bo proposed larger labels, and Neri tried them. (3) Visitors could read them from the doorway. Writing slip: “How would you summarize the display story without giving your opinion?” Partner card: “How would you describe Neri’s response to Bo?” Use the prepared detail and the agreed paraphrase routine.

Look-for: Neri plans the display, tries Bo's larger labels and visitors can read them from the doorway. A student cites the change and confirms the partner's claim. If the partner says Neri tested the idea, “You think she gave it a trial” preserves it. Wrong: “Neri was foolish” (D), “You think she refused” for that partner's view (U).

**Shape: E5.** Write a first summary and discuss a new decision. In practice mark the detail that belongs in a supplied summary and rehearse roles on Mara's and Yu's choices. Keep Neri's passage and the combined summary and talk card for closing.

**E7 (day 7).** (1) Tavi spent all morning sewing bright stars on the outside of a dancer’s costume. (2) A dancer tripped on the long hem in rehearsal. (3) Tavi shortened the hem and turned the costume inside out to check for loose thread. “How does a quotation about Tavi’s changed way of working support a theme?”

Sample: Caring for someone who wears your work can matter more than showing it off. Tavi “turned the costume inside out” (3) to check a part the audience would never see. Wrong: “Costumes” (C), “Tavi lengthened the hem” (A).

**Shape: E7.** Explain a theme using the change in priorities in a new scene. Chapter 5 practice sorts reports by observer. Hold the dancer and hem for the exit.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Pairs carry section 1.1's maps to Yu's hall, journey and return. Start on day 5 with the written grade 4 discussion moves chart and add a goal, deadline and three roles. The teacher models an elaborated reply. Students keep a cited claim, role slip and confirmed paraphrase. The ideal instruments are complete paired texts and a paper discussion sheet. Print fits. Hand-draw a hall and field map and hand-copy the held prompts if copies are scarce. Tool each day: None.

## 6. Days

## Day 4

Read literature (understanding). “I can use what two characters do to explain a theme they share.” Pairs hold both texts and the trial map.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E3. Locate Bea’s closing response. Her hands show how much space she needs. Put the name notes and a gloss for channel (a path water follows) on the first page for the shared read.
- **10 min. Read Yu.** Read all 1,198 words with complete print.
- **15 min. Map episodes.** Trace hall, earlier journey and hall. Label who carries news, helps with food or sows. Date a nonfiction note citing the packet’s statement that dialogue was imagined. It tells us why Yu’s speech here is evidence about this retelling, rather than a transcript of an ancient speaker.
- **15 min. Write comparison.** Students connect a specific responsibility to one detail in each anchor. Fifteen pairs take two 45-second turns after individual notes. Keep the notes.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E4 on paper, alone.

## Day 5

Speak and listen (application). “I can summarize a trial and discuss a choice.” Students hold the trial packet and discussion sheet.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E4. Match evidence to the point or correct the literal reading.
- **15 min. Reading conferences.** Choice reading. Eight 90-second slots including moves, three minutes for records. Reuse recent records.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Combine two released trial details while preserving who did each action.
- **22 min. Discussion.** Use two minutes to retrieve a prepared quotation and the grade 4 discussion moves. Include the Yu source note when a group distinguishes the reteller’s words from a claim about the past. Model a neutral account and an elaborated answer using separate bits of the trial. Give each group a facilitator, evidence-finder and questioner. Agree on a question, goal and deadline. After a private claim, rehearse in pairs with two 45-second turns, then in six concurrent groups of five with one 45-second contribution each. The teacher leads one exchange. Everyone writes a key idea and a confirmed paraphrase. Return named preparation cues before day 9.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5. Allow two minutes for separate paper summaries and claim marks. Then allow 45 seconds per speaker in fifteen pairs and 90 seconds to write and confirm paraphrases. Collect all first work and give each pupil a written cue. Partner notes document what peers heard.

## Day 6

Check. “I can use a new myth to explain how a character changes.” Students hold the cold myth and separate response sheet.

- **8 min. Cold reading.** Read the complete five-paragraph myth independently.
- **30 min. Individual writing.** A1, A2, A3 and A4/E6 take 9, 7, 7 and 7 minutes. A5 uses existing records.
- **5 min. Literal check.** Display only A3's answer about where rain fell. Students mark that component after the first answer is saved. Keep the rest for teacher judgment.
- **7 min. Check and collect.** Check that pages are present and collect them. After class allow four minutes per child for provisional components and written cues, 120 minutes for 30.

## Day 7

Read literature (respond). “I can repair an answer and return to the story.” Students hold A sheets, cues and chapter 5.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back written A feedback. Route one letter per child to a short contrast. Use E5 cues here where the assessment shows no higher-priority need.
- **5 min. Earlier-answer revision.** Students revise their own marked answer before any partner comparison. Finalize earlier component records after class, about two minutes per child.
- **14 min. Chapter five.** Read all 1,618 words. Track who saw each condition at the old shop.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Seven 90-second turns leave 4½ minutes for notes. For missing or flagged records, replace a due turn with a fresh private one-minute reading and one minute for notes. Up to seven such turns fit here. Use choice reading for everyone else.
- **6 min. Compare reports.** First review one dated informational log entry, including what the reader understood and the help used. Classify a chapter 5 observation independently, then compare in pairs. An eighth flagged reading can use two minutes here. Carry further names to day 8.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7 on paper, alone.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 4: “Who can tell Yu where the water went next?” Look for people downstream and the messenger. “What does Bea’s gesture tell Grandpa about next month?” She holds her hands a chair seat apart and moves them sideways to ask for more room. Day 5: “Why does Yu ask where an empty basket came from?” Ask students to distinguish what he sees from what he must ask. Look for a located detail, a specific question or reason and a paraphrase the speaker accepts. The comparison is an evidence paragraph with the relevant difference supported from both readings.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview channel, trial and respond, then revisit them in the episode notes. Review topic from day 2. An objective summary reports important events without adding the writer’s opinion. To paraphrase a partner, keep their point when you say it back. The grade 5 targets for topic and paraphrase are planned only. “Yu changes ___ when ___.” Read the 1,198-word retelling and the 1,618-word chapter 5 with complete print and optional audio beside it. For the cold myth, directions may be spoken. A scribe may record the student's own answer. Passage audio yields a supported reading entry. Keep print, enlarged or tactile copies, and optional audio sealed until first use. Extension: support a different reading of a character's response with two details.

## 10. Two rooms

At home the parent takes a reader or questioner role after the child writes a first claim. With one projector, model one excerpt while everyone has both reading sheets. Give the complete myth privately at the check and keep the key out of view.
