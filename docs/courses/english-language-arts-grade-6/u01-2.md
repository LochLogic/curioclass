English Language Arts Grade 6. Unit 1, River town: the flood year, [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 4 to 7.** Days by kind: understanding 1, application 1, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **RL.6.2, “Determine” and “provide.”** Compare details that convey a theme and give a summary free of judgment. **RL.6.3, “Describe.”** Track episode order and the character's response. **RL.6.1, “Cite.”** Connect what the myth states and what its ending implies to located evidence.
- **W.6.9, “Draw,” and W.6.9a and b, “Apply.”** Write from the literary pair here. Informational application and **RI.6.1, “Cite,”** begin with the source and map on day 8. **W.6.10, “Write routinely.”** File short responses by date. **RL.6.10 and RI.6.10, “read and comprehend.”** Keep logs separate by genre and by reading support.
- **SL.6.1, “Engage.”** Teach the grade 6 discussion moves on day 5 during the application day. In a, “Come” prepared with a located detail and “draw” on it. In b, “Follow,” “set” and “define” cover rules, goal, deadline and roles. In c, “Pose and respond” with elaboration. In d, “Review” ideas and “demonstrate understanding” by confirming a paraphrase.
- Explain the hall frame and earlier journey in Yu. The dialogue is imagined for this retelling of a story from a living Chinese tradition. Content includes failed flood walls, families displaced to hills, crop loss and food scarcity. The novella also includes money worries. Keep questions on the texts and allow students to pass.

- **A1, 9 minutes.** Look for a theme about listening that fits the river consultation and villagers' contributions. The neutral summary includes gate failure, consultation and the changed route. Mark theme, both details, summary coverage and objectivity separately. “Water” (C), “Lina was stupid” (D).

  **Shape: A1.** Independently give a theme with two details and a three-sentence objective summary of one myth. Practice sorts selected actions and accounts. Reserve the whole myth and the combined task.

- **A2, 7 minutes.** Failed gate leads Lina to hear the river and check the drawing. Villagers reshape the plan and the meadow receives water the following season. Look for why the steps follow and how Lina changes. Meadow opened before the failed gate (E).

  **Shape: A2.** A causal chain and changing response in the gate story. In Yu practice trace where news and food come from and how the hall frames the journey. Keep the failed-gate, consultation and diverted-channel sequence off retell cards.

- **A3, 7 minutes.** Section 1.1 holds the key and error examples.

  **Shape: A3.** Locate a fact and cite a motive from another paragraph. Keep the myth's rain location and the returned key off practice sheets, questions and displays.

- **A4/E6, 7 minutes.** Returning the key to the meeting room (paragraph 5) shows that Lina shares decisions. A copied action without its meaning (F), “She quit the watchtower” (A).

  **Shape: A4/E6.** Explain how a final action supports a theme in a cited paragraph. Use separate evidence moves in practice. Keep Lina's returned key and any modeled paragraph about it for the check.

- **A5, record only.** Retrieve dated short responses from days 3 to 5 and A4 for writing frequency. Extended work comes later in N1.

  **Shape: A5.** Verify existing dates. Schedule no additional response or practice replica.

Use the following original scenes for individual first evidence. Use met, partly or not yet on daily exit slips. Keep the unit scale on the assessment record.

**E4 (day 4).** Tale A: A talking heron asks a gardener to leave a gap for water. The gardener listens, and water passes through. Tale B: A child listens to a neighbor and leaves a gap in a model wall. Poured water passes through. “How do both tales show the same theme?”

Look-for: both change a barrier in response to advice and let the water through. Advice can improve a plan when someone acts on it. Accept an equivalent theme supported by each tale. Wrong: “Water” (C), “The child closed the gap” (A).

**Shape: E4.** Compare how two invented tales convey a shared claim. Practice pairs match prompts to differences between Mara's and Yu's responsibilities. Keep the heron, model wall and complete shared-theme response out of practice. These two tales are modern inventions.

**E5 (day 5).** (1) Neri planned a display alone. (2) Bo proposed larger labels, and Neri tried them. (3) Visitors could read them from the doorway. “Give an objective summary. Discuss Neri's response with a partner.” Discussion card: cite one detail and check that your paraphrase keeps your partner's meaning.

Look-for: Neri plans the display, tries Bo's larger labels and visitors can read them from the doorway. A student cites the change and confirms the partner's claim. If the partner says Neri tested the idea, “You think she gave it a trial” preserves it. Wrong: “Neri was foolish” (D), “You think she refused” for that partner's view (U).

**Shape: E5.** Write a first summary and discuss a new decision. In practice mark the detail that belongs in a supplied summary and rehearse roles on Mara's and Yu's choices. Keep Neri's passage and the combined summary and talk card for closing.

**E7 (day 7).** (1) Tavi's first sketch hid the narrow entrance. (2) After neighbors questioned it, Tavi drew the doorway at its actual width. (3) Everyone changed the seating plan. “Which quotation shows how Tavi responds? What theme does the change convey?”

Look-for: “drew the doorway at its actual width” shows Tavi accepts correction. Accurate information helps a group reconsider a plan. Wrong: “Doorways” (C), “Tavi widened the actual entrance” (A).

**Shape: E7.** Give a cited change and theme from a drawing story. Sort chapter 5 observations by observer in practice. Keep Tavi and the seating decision for closing.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Pairs carry section 1.1's maps to Yu's hall, journey and return. Start on day 5 with the written grade 4 discussion moves chart and add a goal, deadline and three roles. The teacher models an elaborated reply. Students keep a cited claim, role slip and confirmed paraphrase. The ideal instruments are complete paired texts and a paper discussion sheet. Print fits. Hand-draw a hall and field map and hand-copy the held prompts if copies are scarce. Tool each day: None.

## 6. Days

## Day 4

Understanding. “I can compare how two characters respond.” Pairs hold both texts and the trial map.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E3. Locate Bea's closing response to Grandpa's question about next month.
- **10 min. Read Yu.** Read all 1,198 words with complete print. Preview channel as a path water follows, and use the text's name notes.
- **15 min. Map episodes.** Trace hall, earlier journey and hall. Label who carries news, helps with food or sows.
- **15 min. Write comparison.** Students connect a specific responsibility to one detail in each anchor. Fifteen pairs take two 45-second turns after individual notes. Keep the notes.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E4 on paper, alone.

## Day 5

Application. “I can summarize a trial and discuss a choice.” Students hold the trial packet and discussion sheet.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E4. Match evidence to the point or correct the literal reading.
- **15 min. Reading conferences.** Choice reading. Eight 90-second slots including moves, three minutes for records. Reuse recent records.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Combine two released trial details while preserving who did each action.
- **22 min. Discussion.** Use two minutes to retrieve a prepared quotation and the grade 4 discussion moves. Model a neutral account and an elaborated answer using separate bits of the trial. Give each group a facilitator, evidence-finder and questioner. Agree on a question, goal and deadline. After a private claim, rehearse in pairs with two 45-second turns, then in six concurrent groups of five with one 45-second contribution each. The teacher leads one exchange. Everyone writes a key idea and a confirmed paraphrase. Return named preparation cues before day 9.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5. Allow two minutes for separate paper summaries and claim marks. Then allow 45 seconds per speaker in fifteen pairs and 90 seconds to write and confirm paraphrases. Collect all first work and give each pupil a written cue. Partner notes document what peers heard.

## Day 6

Check. Students hold the cold myth and separate response sheet.

- **8 min. Cold reading.** Read the complete five-paragraph myth independently.
- **30 min. Individual writing.** A1, A2, A3 and A4/E6 take 9, 7, 7 and 7 minutes. A5 uses existing records.
- **5 min. Literal check.** Display only A3's answer about where rain fell. Students mark that component after the first answer is saved. Keep the rest for teacher judgment.
- **7 min. Check and collect.** Check that pages are present and collect them. After class allow four minutes per child for provisional components and written cues, 120 minutes for 30.

## Day 7

Respond. “I can repair an answer and return to the story.” Students hold A sheets, cues and chapter 5.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back written A feedback. Route one letter per child to a short contrast. Use E5 cues here where the assessment shows no higher-priority need.
- **5 min. Earlier-answer revision.** Students revise their own marked answer before any partner comparison. Finalize earlier component records after class, about two minutes per child.
- **14 min. Chapter five.** Read all 1,618 words. Track who saw each condition at the old shop.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Seven 90-second turns leave 4½ minutes for notes. For missing or flagged records, replace a due turn with a fresh private one-minute reading and one minute for notes. Up to seven such turns fit here. Use choice reading for everyone else.
- **6 min. Compare reports.** Classify a chapter 5 observation independently, then compare in pairs. An eighth flagged reading can use two minutes here. Carry further names to day 8.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7 on paper, alone.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 4: “Who can tell Yu where the water went next?” Look for people downstream and the messenger. “How does Bea change Grandpa's question?” She asks him to find out what she needs. Day 5: “Why does Yu ask where an empty basket came from?” Ask students to distinguish what he sees from what he must ask. Look for a located detail, a specific question or reason and a paraphrase the speaker accepts. The comparison is an evidence paragraph with the relevant difference supported from both readings.

## 9. Supports and extension

Preview channel, trial and respond, then revisit them in the episode notes. A topic names what the story is about. To paraphrase a partner, keep their point when you say it back. The grade 5 targets for topic and paraphrase are planned only. “Yu changes ___ when ___.” Read the 1,198-word retelling and the 1,618-word chapter 5 with complete print and optional audio beside it. For the cold myth, directions may be spoken. A scribe may record the student's own answer. Passage audio yields a supported reading entry. Keep print, enlarged or tactile copies, and optional audio sealed until first use. Extension: support a different reading of a character's response with two details.

## 10. Two rooms

At home the parent takes a reader or questioner role after the child writes a first claim. With one projector, model one excerpt while everyone has both reading sheets. Give the complete myth privately at the check and keep the key out of view.
