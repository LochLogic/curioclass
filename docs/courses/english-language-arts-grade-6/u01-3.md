- **W.6.3, “Write.”** Day 11 retrieves narrative in a short folder sample. “Engage and orient” and “organize” (a), “Use” technique (b), transitions (c) and precise details (d), and “Provide” a following conclusion (e) get their full teaching on days 17 to 22. **W.6.4, “Produce.”** Name the reader, purpose and form. **W.6.6, “Use” and “demonstrate.”** Plan for publishing. Keep student addresses and disaster accounts private.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned: none.** E8 uses RI.6.1, E9 uses RI.6.1 and RL.6.3, E10 uses RL.6.2 and SL.6.1, E11 uses RL.6.3 and W.6.3a, and E12 uses RL.6.2 and RL.6.3. Day 10 contributes earlier observations for P7. Its portfolio judgment uses the unit component record and the written feedback path. Exit slips use met, partly or not yet. Release each original scene only at its exit.

**E8 (day 8).** An invented flood survey has a square grid. North row, orchard and mill. South row, footbridge and boathouse. Key: rings mark places where a crew measured water depth. Only the orchard and boathouse have rings. Caption: “Tuesday’s depths: orchard, knee-deep; boathouse, ankle-deep.” Map slip: “Which marked site had deeper water?” Inference slip: “How well does the survey support calling the orchard knee-deep all Tuesday?”

Sample: The ring and depth entry identify the orchard. The caption dates the observation to Tuesday. It gives neither its hour nor how long that depth lasted. Wrong: “The mill has a ring” (A), “The water stayed at knee height all day” (B).

**Shape: E8.** Read a new key and compare two depth entries before judging what the date permits about duration. Practice sorts Bellwether reports by source. Hold the depth grid and its accessible copy for closing.

**E9 (day 9).** (1) Observer’s note: During the downpour the first puddles formed on the paved yard while the grass still absorbed water. (2) Nila had planned to chalk hopscotch squares on the pavement. After reading the note she put the chalk away and laid ropes on the grass. “How does the observation explain Nila’s change of plan?”

Sample: Puddles on the paving (1) would spoil chalk lines. Nila uses rope to mark the squares on grass instead (2). Wrong: “She laid the ropes before she read the note” (E), “The ropes will keep the grass dry” (B).

**Shape: E9.** Use two pieces of a new account to explain a choice. Practice labels the sources of the shed and road reports separately. Hold the chalk-and-rope scene.

**E10 (day 10).** (1) Tari wanted every photograph displayed. (2) Jo said two labels were guesses. (3) Tari moved those photographs to a table headed Questions. Writing slip: “What happened to the photographs, in your own neutral summary?” Partner card: “What do you make of Tari’s choice?” Use the agreed question, evidence and paraphrase routine.

Look-for: Tari plans to display every photograph, hears that two labels are guesses, and moves those photos to the Questions table. “How could the Questions table help someone supply a date?” builds on sentence 3. Confirm what the partner actually meant. Wrong: “Tari foolishly ruined it” (D), “I agree” with no reason or question (T), a paraphrase that substitutes a different view (U).

**Shape: E10.** Give an individual objective summary and then discuss the fresh choice. Use the chapter's three-column page to weigh a missing cost in oral practice. Save the photographs and the combined summary and discussion for closing.

**E11 (day 11).** (1) The meeting ended before Sana could show her model. (2) She brought it back with an explanation prepared. Event arrow: “Why did Sana prepare an explanation?” Story card: “What opening line would carry that preparation into Sana’s next episode?” Use the card’s narrator and purpose labels for your choices.

Look-for: the missed opportunity motivates preparation. An opening in the chosen voice carries the plan into a story for peers. Sample plan: Sana narrates to build suspense for classmates. She arrives early so she will have time to show her model. “This time I set my model down before anyone called the meeting to order.” Accept other coherent continuations. Wrong: a rainfall report (I), “Somebody did something somewhere” (G).

**Shape: E11.** Link two events already read and plan an opening from them. Practice retrieves an unjudged chapter 8 folder sample and tries a sentence change. Any model comparison uses a different response format. Keep Sana and the linked explanation and story opening for closing.

**E12 (day 12).** (1) Omi dismissed an unsigned account. (2) A dated photograph supported it. (3) Omi pinned both up and asked who could identify the writer. “How does Omi's changed response show a theme?” Use evidence from the passage.

Look-for: the photograph prompts Omi to investigate. Displaying both items and asking a question show that incomplete evidence can deserve a closer look. Accept a supported equivalent theme. Wrong: “Omi's inquiry produced the photograph” (E), “Photographs” (C).

**Shape: E12.** Explain a response and theme across three new episodes. Map Mara's submissions and what they make possible during practice. Keep Omi's evidence board and a three-episode theme explanation for closing.

## 6. Days

## Day 8

Read information (understanding). “I can show what a flood map records.” Teacher holds the model. Students keep annotations.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E7 and repair one letter per child.
- **16 min. Map and excerpt.** Retrieve one located chapter detail before pairing it with a factual source. In two minutes, infer meaning in a released sentence and verify with a dictionary. Distribute the day 8 excerpt with the entire flood-causes sentence removed from print, audio and the screen until day 16. On the copy, mark the heading (the name above a section), the definition of overtopping and the map caption. A map shows places in relation to one another. Its caption gives information about what it shows. Streamflow means water moving along a stream. Teach [D] as an owner report and [?] as unconfirmed. Point to [W] for surveyed water and a blank cell for no observation. Practice sorting the marks by what was actually recorded.
- **16 min. Independent annotations.** Each child labels a released map cell with its evidence source. Teacher can take eight private missing or flagged readings at two minutes each, including recording.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing the overtopping definition. Fifteen pairs trade 45-second turns and a brief cue.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Join two released details about the river and its banks. Carry that pattern into one sentence of your own.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E8 on paper, alone.

## Day 9

Read information (understanding). “I can link a reading to the next choice.” Students hold *Reading Floodwater* and the novella.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E8 and distinguish observed from unknown.
- **15 min. Read together.** Read “Flash Flooding” (278 words) and chapter 6 (1,464 words).
- **16 min. Episode maps.** Mark the dry floor report and the road closure on separate notes. Give the source of each and order the visit notes on the arc. Up to eight due private readings replace teacher coaching at two minutes each. Students keep their own annotations before comparing in pairs.
- **6 min. Chapter seven preview.** Read the first 647 words through pulling out a chair for Kit.
- **3 min. Preparation revision.** Reopen day 5 feedback. Revise an individual claim or question for the chapter 7 discussion.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E9 on paper, alone.

## Day 10

Speak and listen (application). “I can discuss choices and summarize without judging.” Students hold the three-column page and private discussion slips.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E9. Check order or evidence and confirm roles.
- **7 min. Finish chapter seven.** Read the remaining 784 words.
- **15 min. Discussion.** The teacher visits three groups of five for five minutes each. Within each group, use five 20-second two-person exchanges in a circle: each child asks one prepared question and answers a neighbor’s. In a group round each pupil has 18 seconds to extend a point with evidence. In a teacher-led round each has 16 seconds to restate a different view. That leaves 30 seconds per group for moves and ticks. Read the marked details, agreements and written reflections afterward. Other groups work at the same time. Record only turns heard in each context. Give everyone a written cue, including the other fifteen who will revise on day 19. Offer a private turn with a peer and teacher after a pass. Record missing components by name.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Eight 90-second turns leave three minutes for records. If replacements are due, fit up to seven private two-minute samples and use unclaimed time for other conference turns. Sort support needs and carry any outstanding names to day 11.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Combine chapter details, keeping the costs attached to the right option.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E10. Take two minutes to write first summaries and mark details. Use parallel paired turns, 45 seconds each, and 90 seconds to confirm and record paraphrases. The teacher gives each student a cue based on their own sheet and records any speech actually heard.

## Day 11

Write (understanding). “I can follow an episode and plan a short story.” Teacher holds an anchor excerpt. Students date their folder sample.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E10 and a cue on the relevant move.
- **13 min. Read.** Read chapter 8 (1,502 words) and mark where the cart changes the order of work.
- **6 min. Narrative retrieval.** From an unused chapter detail, invent a short episode. Note the reader and purpose. Keep the sample unjudged in the folder.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Seven 90-second turns, or replace them with up to seven private two-minute samples if still due. Use remaining time for records and have classmates read independently.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Model how a chapter sentence combines ideas. Students privately revise a sentence from the sample.
- **3 min. Plot link.** Label the cart or door detail in the map. Take an eighth private sample here if due.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E11 on paper, alone.

## Day 12

Read literature (understanding). “I can explain how events change Mara's response.” Each child keeps an episode map.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E11 and match the planning cue to the writing purpose or narrator.
- **27 min. Read.** Read all 3,236 words of chapters 9 and 10. The in-class chapter sequence covers chapters 1 to 10 in full.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing a selected line from the turning point. Partners alternate 45-second turns and exchange short cues.
- **8 min. Map.** Individually mark what changed between the two drawings and locate a line showing who is invited. Pairs compare these entries. On the map margin, quote a sentence from the day 9 explainer and mark whether the chapter itself establishes that condition. Send the map and E12 slips to day 13.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E12 on paper, alone.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 8 checks a context clue against a dictionary, reuses section 1.1's located evidence and reviews “heading: the main idea of a group of notes, saying what the group is for” on grouped notes. Review grade 4's “context-clues: a supplied phrase matched to its nearby sentence meaning.” The grade 5 heading, quotation and chapter-record targets are planned only. For a text heading, read the title above the passage to predict its subject. A group-of-notes heading names what the writer grouped. Review “detail: something that happened or is there in a book, the proof for a reason” from grade 4. Days 9 to 12 return map evidence, discussion, sentence combining and story order. For day 11 review “narrator: the teller of a story” and “reader, purpose, form” from grade 4. Name conflict (a struggle between wants or needs) and perspective (a way of seeing or thinking) when Mara and Grandpa consider the drawings. In the map, show how a detail can convey, or communicate, a theme. Review support: the line of the text that supports what you say. Request existing narrative, process, grammar and keyboard records. Use the released sample if evidence is missing. In choice-reading conferences retrieve actual nonfiction and literature records, with a detail showing comprehension and the support used. Save dated reading observations, supports, named pending components and private follow-up turns for the days 157 and 158 handoff. No rate target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Teach hazard as a possible danger and overtopping as flowing over the top. Review using a caption, map title and key together, from grade 3 u01-4. Explain runoff as water flowing over the ground. Infiltrate means soak into the soil. Review the words in the excerpt and annotations. “The caption limits ___ because ___.” Use “consequence” for the change an event brings about and keep that sense on days 13 and 14. Grade 5 runoff and consequence are planned only. Separate the main clause from its technical phrase during shared reading. Keep complete chapters beside optional audio. Give the map as clear print, braille or ordered cell descriptions with the original distinction between report and surveyed mark. Scribe a student's own words and record reading support separately. Extension: find a different passage that supports or qualifies an inference about Mara.
