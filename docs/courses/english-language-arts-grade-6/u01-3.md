## 1. Standards and practices

- **RI.6.1 and RL.6.1, “Cite.”** Trace stated observations and supported inferences in the map, excerpts and chapters. **W.6.9, “Draw,” and W.6.9a and b, “Apply.”** Use evidence by genre. Argument tracing comes in unit 2. **W.6.10, “Write routinely.”** Date short responses. **RL.6.10 and RI.6.10, “read and comprehend.”** Keep records of full chapters, excerpts and the support used.
- **RL.6.3, “Describe.”** Explain a causal link between episodes. **RL.6.2, “Determine” and “provide.”** Trace a theme and summarize without opinion. **SL.6.1, “Engage.”** Day 10 returns to all four day 5 moves. “Come” prepared and “draw” on evidence (a). “Follow” rules, “set” goals and deadlines and “define” roles (b). “Pose and respond” with detail (c). “Review” ideas and “demonstrate understanding” through paraphrase (d).
- **W.6.3, “Write.”** Day 11 retrieves narrative in a short folder sample. “Engage and orient” and “organize” (a), “Use” technique (b), transitions (c) and precise details (d), and “Provide” a following conclusion (e) get their full teaching on days 17 to 22. **W.6.4, “Produce.”** Name the reader, purpose and form. **W.6.6, “Use” and “demonstrate.”** Plan for publishing. Review existing typing evidence on days 25 to 26. The grade 6 single-sitting evidence comes later.
- Explain that a street mark is a dated observation and a shed report has a speaker. A blank cell without a survey is unknown. The flood texts describe overtopping, rapid rises and threats to life and property. The chapters depict displacement, money worries and uncertainty about access. Discuss the fictional town. Keep student addresses and disaster accounts private.

**E8 (day 8).** Invented for practice.

**Tuesday’s survey**

North is at the top.

| Row | West | East |
|---|---|---|
| North | Orchard ○ | Mill |
| South | Footbridge | Boathouse ○ |

**Key.** Rings mark places where a crew measured water depth.

**Caption.** Tuesday’s depths: orchard, knee-deep. Boathouse, ankle-deep.

**Map in reading order.** North row: orchard with a ring, then mill. South row: footbridge, then boathouse with a ring.

- **s01-X8a (RI.6.1, Paper).** Which marked site had deeper water?
  Look-for: The orchard. It is marked, and the caption gives knee-deep there and ankle-deep at the boathouse.
  Error A: The mill has a ring and had deeper water. The mill is unmarked and has no depth in the caption.
- **s01-X8b (RI.6.1, Paper).** How well does the survey support calling the orchard knee-deep all Tuesday?
  Look-for: It supports one observation on Tuesday. The caption gives neither an hour nor a duration.
  Error B: The water stayed at knee height all day. Infers duration without evidence.

**Shape: E8.** Read a new key and compare depths, then judge what a date permits about duration. Practice sorts the sources of Bellwether reports. Hold the depth grid and its ordered description for closing.

**E9 (day 9).** Invented for practice.

(1) An observer noted puddles on the paved yard during the downpour. The grass still absorbed water.
(2) Nila planned to chalk hopscotch squares on the pavement. After reading the note, she used ropes to mark squares on the grass.

- **s01-X9 (RL.6.3, Paper).** How does the observation explain Nila’s change of plan?
  Look-for: Puddles would spoil chalk lines on pavement. Reading the observation led Nila to use ropes on grass instead.
  Error E: She laid the ropes before she read the note. Reverses the sequence.
  Error B: The ropes will keep the grass dry. The text gives no evidence for that claim.

**Shape: E9.** Use two parts of a new account to explain a changed plan. Practice labels the sources of the shed and road reports separately. Hold the chalk-and-rope scene.

**E10 (day 10).** Invented for practice.

(1) Tari wanted every photograph displayed.
(2) Jo said two labels were guesses.
(3) Tari moved those photographs to a table headed Questions.

- **s01-X10a (RL.6.2, Paper).** What happened to the photographs, in your own neutral summary?
  Look-for: Tari wanted to display all photos, heard two labels were guesses, and moved the two photos to the Questions table.
  Error D: Tari foolishly ruined it. Judges the choice instead of reporting it.
- **s01-X10b (SL.6.1, Spoken).** What do you make of Tari’s choice?
  Look-for: Use an agreed turn and sentence 2 or 3. Extend the exchange and confirm the paraphrase with the partner.
  Error T: I agree. Gives no reason or question.
  Error U: You said to throw out the photos. Replaces a partner’s suggestion to keep and question them.

**Shape: E10.** Give an individual objective summary, then discuss a fresh choice. Practice uses a three-column chapter page to weigh a missing cost. Hold the photographs and combined task for closing.

**E11 (day 11).** Invented for practice.

(1) The meeting ended before Sana could show her model.
(2) She brought it back with an explanation prepared.

- **s01-X11a (RL.6.3, Paper).** Why did Sana prepare an explanation?
  Look-for: The meeting ended before she could show her model, so she came prepared to explain it at the next one.
  Error E: She prepared the explanation after showing the model. The first meeting ended before she could show it.
- **s01-X11b (W.6.3, Paper).** What opening line would carry that preparation into Sana’s next episode?
  Look-for: Accept an opening for peers in a clear voice that follows from Sana’s preparation. The card labels name narrator and purpose.
  Error G: Somebody did something somewhere. Gives no identifiable narrator or situation.
  Error I: A rainfall report lists the week’s totals. Does not continue the episode as a story.

**Shape: E11.** Explain the link between two events and plan an opening that carries the preparation forward. Practice keeps a chapter 8 sample unjudged and revises one sentence. Hold Sana’s scene and the linked explanation and opening.

**E12 (day 12).** Invented for practice.

(1) Omi dismissed an unsigned account.
(2) A dated photograph supported it.
(3) Omi pinned both up and asked who could identify the writer.

- **s01-X12 (RL.6.2, Paper).** How does Omi’s changed response show a theme? Use evidence from the passage.
  Look-for: The photo prompts Omi to investigate. By displaying both items and asking about the writer, Omi shows that incomplete evidence deserves a closer look.
  Error E: Omi’s inquiry produced the photograph. The photograph comes before the inquiry.
  Error C: Photographs. Gives only a topic.

**Shape: E12.** Explain a response and theme across three new episodes. Practice maps Mara’s submissions and what they make possible. Hold Omi’s evidence board for closing.

## 6. Days

## Day 8

Read information (understanding). “I can show what a flood map records.” Teacher holds the model. Students keep annotations.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Give back E7 and repair one letter per child.
- **16 min. Map and excerpt.** Retrieve one located chapter detail before pairing it with a factual source. In two minutes, infer meaning in a released sentence and verify with a dictionary. Distribute the day 8 excerpt with the entire flood-causes sentence removed from print, audio and the screen until day 16. On the copy, mark the heading (the name above a section), the definition of overtopping and the map caption. A map shows places in relation to one another. Its caption gives information about what it shows. Streamflow means water moving along a stream. Teach [D] as an owner report and [?] as unconfirmed. Point to [W] for surveyed water and a blank cell for no observation. Practice sorting the marks by what was actually recorded.
- **16 min. Independent annotations.** Each child labels a released map cell with its evidence source. Teacher can take eight private missing or flagged readings at two minutes each, including recording.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing the overtopping definition. Fifteen pairs trade 45-second turns and a brief cue.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Join two released details about the river and its banks. Carry that pattern into one sentence of your own.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E8 on paper, alone.

**Shape: E8.** Read a new key and compare depths, then judge what a date permits about duration. Practice sorts the sources of Bellwether reports. Hold the depth grid and its ordered description for closing.

## Day 10

Speak and listen (application). “I can discuss choices and summarize without judging.” Students hold the three-column page and private discussion slips.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E9. Check order or evidence and confirm roles.
- **7 min. Finish chapter seven.** Read the remaining 784 words.
- **15 min. Discussion.** The teacher leads three groups of five for five minutes each. In each group, hear one 45-second contribution per pupil and allow 75 seconds for moves and notes. In waiting groups, partners use parallel 45-second turns for prepared questions, responses and confirmed paraphrases. Before speaking, each pupil prepares a claim with a location. Save separate paired records, teacher-heard turns and written evidence. Give each pupil a private cue. Keep missing speech components pending and schedule their next named turn.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E10. Take two minutes to write first summaries and mark details. Use parallel paired turns, 45 seconds each, and 90 seconds to confirm and record paraphrases. The teacher gives each student a cue based on their own sheet and records any speech actually heard.

## 9. Supports and extension

Teach hazard as a possible danger and overtopping as flowing over the top. Review using a caption, map title and key together, from grade 3 u01-4. Explain runoff as water flowing over the ground. Infiltrate means soak into the soil. Review the words in the excerpt and annotations. “The caption limits ___ because ___.” Use “consequence” for the change an event brings about and keep that sense on days 13 and 14. Grade 5 runoff and consequence are planned only. Separate the main clause from its technical phrase during shared reading. Keep complete chapters beside optional audio. Give the map as clear print, braille or ordered cell descriptions with the original distinction between report and surveyed mark. Scribe a student's own words and record reading support separately. Extension: find a different passage that supports or qualifies an inference about Mara.
