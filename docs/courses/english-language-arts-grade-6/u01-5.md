English Language Arts Grade 6. Unit 1, River town: the flood year, [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 17 to 20.** Days by kind: write 3, speak and listen 1. Course kinds: understanding 2, application 2. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.6.3, “Write.”** On day 17, “Engage and orient” and “organize” in a: establish a narrator and context and start a causal sequence. On day 19, “Use” in b: develop a choice with dialogue, pacing and description. Preview familiar craft moves in plans: “Use” varied transitions and precise sensory detail (c, d), and “Provide” a following conclusion (e). Explicit teaching of c to e comes on days 21 and 22.
- **W.6.4, “Produce,”** for classmates, with a clear purpose and form. **W.6.6, “Use” and “demonstrate.”** Identify intended readers now. Device work begins with drafting where available. Single-sitting grade 6 keyboarding belongs to unit 3.
- **W.6.9, “Draw,” and W.6.9a and b, “Apply.”** Day 19 draws a craft move from literature and a setting constraint from a taught explainer. **W.6.10, “Write routinely.”** Keep the short source note and dated extended draft. **RL.6.3, “Describe,”** the approach to resolution. **RL.6.2, “Determine” and “provide.”** Day 20 traces a theme through the final choice and records the result neutrally. **RL.6.10 and RI.6.10, “read and comprehend.”** Log text kind and support.
- **SL.6.1, “Engage.”** Day 20 retrieves earlier discussion. “Come” prepared and “draw” on evidence (a). “Follow” agreements, “set” a goal and deadline and “define” roles (b). “Pose and respond” with elaboration (c). “Review” and “demonstrate understanding” with a confirmed paraphrase (d).
- Explain how the family's deposit and workroom choices constrain the ending and how the narrator makes those costs visible. The story and model include flood damage, displacement and money worries. Keep accounts of personal hardship private. Allow a pass in public talk.

**Shape: P7.** Evidence comes from the earlier prepared partner, group and teacher-led exchanges about actual readings. Practice rehearses single moves on released details. Keep complete scored responses and full observation prompts off model cards. Day 30 talk is practice.

E17 checks W.6.3a, E18 checks W.6.3a with W.6.4 audience fit, E19 checks W.6.3b, and E20 checks RL.6.2 and RL.6.3. Its partner follow-up practices SL.6.1. The exit scenes are Curio Class originals, invented for practice. Save each first response before partner feedback. Record exits as met, partly or not yet.

**E17 (day 17).** Use the held exit-17.md stimulus and response spaces. “How would you rewrite the opening for classmates at a river-town exhibition?”

Look-for: a reader can locate the teller and setting and see what starts the next action. “I spread my river sketch on the hall table. A gust curled the paper, so I reached for a weight.” Accept other connected openings. Wrong: “Somebody somewhere said something” (G), an announcement of hours (I).

**Shape: E17.** Produce an opening from a bare draft. Practice marks the narrator, place and first choice in *The Noticeboard*. Reserve the exhibition and supplied draft.

**E18 (day 18).** Use the held exit-18.md stimulus and response spaces. “What story about the printer’s choice would you plan for classmates?”

Sample: I promised posters to two clubs. I have enough dry sheets for one set, so I put both announcements on each poster. Both clubs send someone to hang them. Look for a choice that causes the next event. Optional dialogue, time cues and sensory details are practice for later drafting. Wrong: a report on rainfall (I), an ending about winning a race unrelated to the printer’s choice (H).

**Shape: E18.** Plan from a constraint supplied for the first time. N1 practice starts with the writer’s own source and character. Hold the damaged paper and both poster requests.

**E19 (day 19).** Use the held exit-19.md stimulus and response spaces. “How would you slow the moment before the rope falls using dialogue and a precise detail?” “What do the words you added make the reader wait for?”

Sample: “Ready down there?” The rope pressed a red line across my palm. From below: “Clear!” I opened my hand. The line about the palm holds us at the moment before Fen’s reply. Look for a deliberate pause whose effect the writer explains. Wrong: only “a red line across my palm” as the explanation (F).

**Shape: E19.** Expand a spare scene, then account for one chosen phrase. Locate a pause in the printed model during practice. Save the curtain and rope for closing.

**E20 (day 20).** Use the held exit-20.md stimulus and response spaces. “What theme can you support with a detail from Enid’s final choice?” “What last action would follow Enid’s choice?” The partner check follows collection of the independent responses.

Sample: A record made ahead of time helps when memory fails. Enid matches the two stars (3). She lifts the marked bundle carefully and sets the jar upright. Accept a different ending supported by the match. In pairs, use a detail to explain the claim and confirm the peer’s restatement. Wrong: an unrelated spaceship landing (H), “I agree” without elaboration in the exchange (T).

**Shape: E20.** Interpret a decision and supply a following ending in a new plot. Discuss the last drawing in the novella in practice. Hold the notebook and star-marked bundle.

## 6. Days

## Day 17

Write (understanding). “I can open a story so readers can follow it.” The teacher holds the model. Students hold their own notes.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E16 with one cue. Name the intended reader and purpose of today's story.
- **15 min. Read.** Read all 1,697 words of chapter 13 with complete print. Locate what the deposit changes.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing at the deposit conversation. Fifteen pairs trade two 45-second turns, cue and retry.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Combine two released details and keep the original actions and speaker. Try that pattern in a folder sentence.
- **17 min. Model study.** Read *The Noticeboard*, about 160 words. Mark the narrator and first choice beside the shared rubric and annotated sample. Model an opening that places a narrator with the old board. Follow the model’s choice to the wider bench as a reminder of how an ending can grow from an action. Point out one sensory detail (what a person can feel or see) and a transition (words marking when the scene changes) before writers use either in a plan.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E17 on paper, alone.

**Shape: E17.** Practice marks the teller, setting and choice in a complete model. The exit writes an opening from the held exhibition draft.

## Day 18

Write (application). “I can plan a story around a choice.” Students hold their own plans.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E17. Locate the model's choice and match a reader to a form.
- **14 min. Read.** Read chapter 14, 1,615 words. Follow what Mara can promise about hours.
- **26 min. Plan N1.** Plan an invented choice forced by a river's consequence, for classmates. Date the story map, change line and source note. Choose a setting detail from *Reading Floodwater* and a technique from the novella. Keep actual plan work unjudged. Fifteen pairs take two 45-second turns after individual notes and return one descriptive cue each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E18 on paper, alone.

**Shape: E18.** Students plan from their own source and character in private folders. The exit supplies a different constraint and asks for a linked plan.

## Day 19

Write (understanding). “I can use speech and a pause to develop a choice.” Students keep their own draft and preparation card.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E18. Trace the chosen cause or restore the intended form on a released example.
- **14 min. Read.** Read chapter 15 in full, 1,628 words.
- **7 min. Model technique.** Mark the pause before widening the bench in *The Noticeboard*. Compare what the touch and question let a reader infer.
- **6 min. Draft trial.** Each student dates a private N1 scene and records the source of a craft move from the novella and a separate setting fact from the explainer.
- **3 min. Revise preparation.** Day 20's named cohort revises a claim or question using day 10 written feedback. Everyone saves a first note.
- **10 min. Start final chapter.** Read the first 1,101 words of chapter 16, through Grandpa finishing the hinge.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E19 on paper, alone.

**Shape: E19.** Practice locates a pause and describes its effect in the printed model. The exit develops the held curtain scene and accounts for one changed phrase.

## Day 20

Speak and listen (application). “I can explain a choice in the ending and plan my own ending.” Students keep change maps and claim slips. Conferences use dated literature and nonfiction records with a detail from each available genre.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E19. Confirm roles, a goal and deadline from prepared slips.
- **7 min. Finish novella.** Read the final 824 words and locate the last drawing.
- **15 min. Discussion.** The teacher hears the other three groups of five, five minutes each, after their day 19 preparation revisions. Per group, five 20-second two-person exchanges give each pupil a question and reply. Allow 18 seconds each in a group round to elaborate with a detail, then 16 seconds each in a teacher-led round to confirm a paraphrase. Keep 30 seconds per group for entering observations and moving. All six groups use the same agreements and save individual claims and reflections. Return a cue to each child and mark which contexts the teacher heard. Carry any missing observation to the named makeup.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Eight 90-second turns leave three minutes for notes. Use eligible earlier records and record support. Other pupils read independently.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Apply a released anchor pattern to an ending sentence while keeping cause and sequence.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E20. Use 3½ minutes for individual first claims and endings. Collect first papers before two 45-second turns per pair to discuss a detail and paraphrase the claim.

**Shape: E20 and P7.** Practice discusses a final drawing. The exit gives a fresh decision for a theme and ending. P7 records the prepared novella discussion in named contexts.

## 9. Supports and extension

Context gives the reader the situation. Sequence is the order of events. Pacing lets a writer linger on a moment or move past it quickly. Purpose is why you write. For N1, the audience is the classmates who will read it. Preview these words and dialogue before the model, find the moves in it and name them again in the draft note. “When ___ chooses ___, ___ follows.” Read the named chapter spans on the in-class route. Offer complete print beside audio and mark its use separately. Large print and dictated responses preserve composition evidence. Extend by finding another technique that makes a choice matter.
