## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.6.3, “Write.”** On day 17, “Engage and orient” and “organize” in a: establish a narrator and context and start a causal sequence. On day 19, “Use” in b: develop a choice with dialogue, pacing and description. Preview familiar craft moves in plans: “Use” varied transitions and precise sensory detail (c, d), and “Provide” a following conclusion (e). Explicit teaching of c to e comes on days 21 and 22.
- **W.6.4, “Produce,”** for classmates, with a clear purpose and form. **W.6.6, “Use” and “demonstrate.”** Identify intended readers now. Device work begins with drafting where available. Single-sitting grade 6 keyboarding belongs to unit 3.
- **W.6.9, “Draw,” and W.6.9a and b, “Apply.”** Day 19 draws a craft move from literature and a setting constraint from a taught explainer. **W.6.10, “Write routinely.”** Keep the short source note and dated extended draft. **RL.6.3, “Describe,”** the approach to resolution. **RL.6.2, “Determine” and “provide.”** Day 20 traces a theme through the final choice and records the result neutrally. **RL.6.10 and RI.6.10, “read and comprehend.”** Log text kind and support.
- **SL.6.1, “Engage.”** Day 20 retrieves earlier discussion. “Come” prepared and “draw” on evidence (a). “Follow” agreements, “set” a goal and deadline and “define” roles (b). “Pose and respond” with elaboration (c). “Review” and “demonstrate understanding” with a confirmed paraphrase (d).
- Explain how the family's deposit and workroom choices constrain the ending and how the narrator makes those costs visible. The story and model include flood damage, displacement and money worries. Keep accounts of personal hardship private. Allow a pass in public talk.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Shape: P7.** Evidence comes from the earlier prepared partner, group and teacher-led exchanges about actual readings. Practice rehearses single moves on released details. Keep complete scored responses and full observation prompts off model cards. Day 30 talk is practice.

The exit scenes are Curio Class originals, invented for practice. Save each first response before partner feedback. Record exits as met, partly or not yet.

**E17 (day 17).** Draft opening: “Then it happened. Somebody spoke. It ended.” “Write an opening for classmates about a river-town exhibition. Place your narrator in the scene and start the action.”

Look-for: a reader can locate the teller and setting and see what starts the next action. “I spread my river sketch on the hall table. A gust curled the paper, so I reached for a weight.” Accept other connected openings. Wrong: “Somebody somewhere said something” (G), an announcement of hours (I).

**Shape: E17.** Produce an opening from a bare draft. Practice marks the narrator, place and first choice in *The Noticeboard*. Reserve the exhibition and supplied draft.

**E18 (day 18).** “The low room is closed. Adults offer the hill room for an exhibition.” “Map a story for classmates about a choice this change forces.” Map labels: narrator and situation, two linked events, ending. Add familiar craft notes where useful, such as speech, a time cue or a sensory detail.

Look-for: a narrator faces the situation, two events connect and the ending follows. “I move our display uphill. I worry nobody will make the climb. Later a visitor holds the squeaking door for the people behind her.” Craft notes are diagnostic. Wrong: a weather report (I), an unrelated dream ending (H).

**Shape: E18.** Independently map events from a supplied constraint. In folder practice writers choose their own situation and use separate source and character notes. In judged practice choose which setting note would fit a released choice. Reserve the exhibition's relocation and supplied-situation map.

**E19 (day 19).** “I asked about the date. Jo answered. I changed the label.” “Develop the scene with dialogue, a precise detail and a pause. Explain what one of those moves does.”

Look-for: a developed scene and explanation tied to its words. “‘Which winter?’ My chalk stopped at the last digit. Jo unfolded the program. ‘The winter we moved.’ I rubbed the three away.” Stopped chalk makes the reader wait for Jo's answer. Wrong: only “My chalk stopped” as explanation (F).

**Shape: E19.** Expand a bare scene and explain a craft choice. Practice marks a pause in *The Noticeboard* before private N1 drafting. Hold Jo's label, any date clue from the sample and the combined task.

**E20 (day 20).** (1) The opening had begun, but a label still held a guessed date. (2) I erased it and wrote a question. (3) A visitor began searching her bag for an old photograph. “How does the narrator's last choice help resolve the trial and show a theme? Sketch an ending that follows.”

Look-for: erasing the guess (2) invites someone to provide evidence (3), showing how honest questions invite help. The visitor might offer a photograph to examine. Its date is unknown. Save the first claim and sketch before the pair checks the evidence. Wrong: a spaceship lands (H), “I agree” without elaboration in the follow-up (T).

**Shape: E20.** Explain how a new choice starts a resolution and sketch what follows. Practice discusses the final drawing using prepared claims and a different follow-up question. Reserve the visitor's search and the combined written task.

## 6. Days

## Day 17

Understanding. “I can open a story so readers can follow it.” The teacher holds the model. Students hold their own notes.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E16 with one cue. Name the intended reader and purpose of today's story.
- **15 min. Read.** Read all 1,697 words of chapter 13 with complete print. Locate what the deposit changes.
- **5 min. Oral reread.** Model phrasing at the deposit conversation. Fifteen pairs trade two 45-second turns, cue and retry.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Combine two released details and keep the original actions and speaker. Try that pattern in a folder sentence.
- **17 min. Model study.** Read *The Noticeboard*, about 160 words. Mark the narrator and first choice beside the shared rubric and annotated sample. Discuss what the painted border lets the narrator keep.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E17 on paper, alone.

## Day 18

Application. “I can plan a story around a choice.” Students hold their own plans.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E17. Locate the model's choice and match a reader to a form.
- **14 min. Read.** Read chapter 14, 1,615 words. Follow what Mara can promise about hours.
- **26 min. Plan N1.** Plan an invented choice forced by a river's consequence, for classmates. Date the story map, change line and source note. Choose a setting detail from *Reading Floodwater* and a technique from the novella. Keep actual plan work unjudged. Fifteen pairs take two 45-second turns after individual notes and return one descriptive cue each.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E18 on paper, alone.

## Day 19

Understanding. “I can use speech and a pause to develop a choice.” Students keep their own draft and preparation card.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E18. Trace the chosen cause or restore the intended form on a released example.
- **14 min. Read.** Read chapter 15 in full, 1,628 words.
- **7 min. Model technique.** Mark the pause before widening the bench in *The Noticeboard*. Compare what the touch and question let a reader infer.
- **6 min. Draft trial.** Each student dates a private N1 scene and records the source of a craft move or setting fact.
- **3 min. Revise preparation.** Day 20's named cohort revises a claim or question using day 10 written feedback. Everyone saves a first note.
- **10 min. Start final chapter.** Read the first 1,101 words of chapter 16, through Grandpa finishing the hinge.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E19 on paper, alone.

## Day 20

Application. “I can explain a choice in the ending and plan my own ending.” Students keep change maps and claim slips.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E19. Confirm roles, a goal and deadline from prepared slips.
- **7 min. Finish novella.** Read the final 824 words and locate the last drawing.
- **15 min. Discussion.** Six groups of five work concurrently. The teacher visits three named groups for five minutes each. In each, a pair and trio share marked details at up to 20 seconds per pupil in parallel. Use about 15 seconds to move into five 45-second teacher-led turns. Each turn builds on a prepared peer question with a detail and restates a peer's idea. Check the role record. Other groups work through the same moves and then plan endings. Every child keeps a claim, question, confirmed paraphrase and ending note. Return named cues and keep the day 10 and day 20 observation sets.
- **15 min. Conferences.** Eight 90-second turns leave three minutes for notes. Use eligible earlier records and record support. Other pupils read independently.
- **3 min. Sentences.** Apply a released anchor pattern to an ending sentence while keeping cause and sequence.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E20. Use 3½ minutes for individual first claims and ending sketches, then two 45-second turns per pair to check a detail and paraphrase the claim. Collect every first paper.

## 9. Supports and extension

Context gives the reader the situation. Sequence is the order of events. Pacing lets a writer linger on a moment or move past it quickly. Purpose is why you write. For N1, the audience is the classmates who will read it. Preview these words and dialogue before the model, find the moves in it and name them again in the draft note. “When ___ chooses ___, ___ follows.” Read the named chapter spans on the in-class route. Offer complete print beside audio and mark its use separately. Large print and dictated responses preserve composition evidence. Extend by finding another technique that makes a choice matter.
