- **C1, 6 minutes.** Place slip: “Where do the musicians rehearse in paragraph 1?” Motive slip: “What do Dev’s silent beats suggest about his purpose?” Use a location with each answer. Uphill meeting room (story paragraph 1). Dev leaves room for Mei's flute (paragraphs 4 and 5). Wrong: riverside room (A), he dislikes music (B).

  **Shape: C1.** Locate a fact and infer a motive. Practice tags chapter details by speaker or action. Reserve the rehearsal venue, Dev's pauses and paired demand.

- **C2, 6 minutes.** Fact slip: “What measurement can a crew recover after the peak has passed?” Inference slip: “Using the caption, what can this survey establish about earlier floods at the uphill room?” The opening names peak height; the next paragraph explains comparing a visible trace with a known gage elevation. The survey and caption cannot establish all past flooding at the uphill room. Wrong: the hill has never flooded (B).

  **Shape: C2.** Cite an explicit measurement purpose and an inferred limit using a cold excerpt and map. Practice sorts individual taught claims by source. Keep the high-water procedure, grid, caption and combined question held.

- **C3, 8 minutes.** Theme slip: “What theme do two of Dev’s choices convey?” Summary slip: “How would you summarize the rehearsal and concert without judging either player?” Participation can mean leaving space for others. Dev lays the sticks across his knees (paragraph 4) and leaves a pause in the concert (paragraph 5). Also accept that listening can overturn a first judgment: Dev doubts Mei has practiced (2), hears how quiet her opening is (3), then plays differently (4). Sample summary: Dev plays over a missed flute entrance and doubts Mei has practiced. Hearing her during the break helps him understand how she finds her entrance. At the concert he counts the silence and waits for her note. Mark theme, conveying details, coverage and objectivity separately. Wrong: “Music” (C), “Dev was foolish” (D).

  **Shape: C3.** State a theme with details and summarize a cold story. Practice examines separate actions or chooses a neutral supplied description. Reserve the joint task and the rehearsal case.

- **C4, 6 minutes.** “How does hearing Mei alone change what Dev does at the concert?” Dev rushes and fills the silence when Mei misses her entrance. He hears Mei alone, changes his playing, and the flute contributes. Wrong: the concert pause precedes the solo listening (E).

  **Shape: C4.** Trace an unseen sequence and explain the response changes. Practice connects released drawing records and labels decisions separately. Reserve the concert chain and the full explanatory task.

- **C5, 5 minutes.** “How does the silent count in paragraph 5 develop a theme?” Dev could fill the rest and instead waits through the flute's contribution. Explain how this makes space for others. Wrong: copying the pause without its meaning (F).

**E28 (day 28).** (1) Before the curtain rose, / Eri kept the understudy’s costume in its trunk. (2) When the lead actor lost her voice, / Eri laid the costume out and called the understudy. Give one partner Eri’s card and the other this held card: “While the sand was wet, / Val pressed a shell into it. After the tide took the print, / Val made the next one in clay.” Reading card: “Read the passage aloud in the marked phrases.” Map caption: “What theme do the two boxes of your character’s change map show?”

Sample map: Costume held in reserve → costume put to use when the lead cannot speak. Preparing for a change lets the show go on. Val’s map moves from a print washed away to a new one in clay: losing a first attempt can lead to a different method. Wrong on Val’s card: “Shells” (C), “Val used clay before the tide rose” (E). Listen for accurate words grouped at the clause boundaries; record any self-correction separately. Wrong: “Costumes” (C), “Eri called the understudy before the actor lost her voice” (E).

**Shape: E28.** Read fresh marked clauses and caption a change map. Reread Mara’s drawing passages for practice. Hold the understudy’s scene and slash-marked copy.

**E29 (day 29).** A, story: Imani saved a chipped tile from the old reading room. In the rebuilt room, she set it into the wall at eye level where old readers could find it. B, test note: The chipped tile cracked when the mason pressed weight on it. C, plan caption: Wall recess for a display tile; a separate beam carries the weight above it. “What claim about rebuilding can you defend with Imani’s choice and the builders’ evidence?”

Sample: A town can keep a memory while changing what an object does. Imani puts the old tile where readers can see it (A). It cracked under weight (B), so the plan uses a beam for support (C). The test concerns this tile. Wrong: “Every tile in the town is too weak to bear weight” (B), “The mason lives near a river” (V).

**Shape: E29.** Use a literary detail for meaning and two technical details for the limit on a rebuilding choice. Practice sorts the contributions of Mara, Yu and the flood explainers. Hold the chipped tile, test and caption.

**E30 (day 30).** A fresh draft reads, “Tess rolled the blanket up. **Shape: E30.** Apply feedback to a new two-line draft, then mark a possible change in an earlier portfolio. The anchor reference and annotation are practice. Reserve Tess’s reversal and the feedback card.

**E32 (day 32).** (1) Kemi stopped clipping all the spent flowers when she found finches eating the seeds from one. (2) Garden note: Monday’s count lists only birds seen at the feeder by the gate. ## 9. Supports and extension

Meet the words in the anchor and return to them in the evidence note. “My evidence supports ___ within ___.” For day 28 use up to 300 words per selected reread. For day 29 keep the four excerpts together under 400 words. Complete independent day 31 reading uses the cold story, excerpt and map. Read directions aloud if needed. Preserve identical wording and labels in large print, braille or an ordered map description. Record passage audio as supported reading and an oral or scribed response in its access condition. Extend by explaining a second reading of the ending with different evidence.
