## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook:** Present a fictional town with an unsafe crossing. Residents petition, a neighborhood group gathers evidence, and the council funds a crossing. Who took part, who decided, and why have government?

**Supporting questions:** Write these together, accepting revisions: What needs public authority (day 1)? Who rules, and how do beliefs develop (day 2)? Does participation mean equal influence (day 3)? Why associate, and who benefits (day 4)? On day 5, students answer the title question using Census data, Tocqueville and modern analysts, with a counterclaim and a limit.

**Teaching:** Model author, date, purpose and claim on day 1. Teach forms before comparing them on day 2. On day 2 teach conservatism, liberalism, libertarianism and socialism, each from its own published self-description, with one shared policy question; no student places themselves or their family. Model a claim with evidence and reasoning on day 3; students test another theory independently. On day 4, step back during comparisons. Teach citizenship, politics, authority, republic, ideology, socialization, pluralism, elitism and civil society through examples and nonexamples.
