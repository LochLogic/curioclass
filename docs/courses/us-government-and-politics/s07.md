## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When should the United States act abroad, and through whom?"
**Hook:** Day 56 opens with Pakistan's October 2022 appeal and a regional locator map. Why ask other countries and international organizations for help? Connect possible responses to the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Compose together: Why cooperate or clash (56)? Who may commit the United States (57)? Which tools and pressures matter (58)? What can international bodies do (59)? What response does the evidence support (60 and 61)?

- **E, hearing digest, April 11:** A shipping association seeks access; dockworkers request safety guarantees; a peace coalition opposes force; a probability poll finds divided opinion; a diaspora association supports medical aid; an interfaith council requests civilian protection. - **F, organization and vote record, April 12:** The United Nations coordinates relief; member governments conduct diplomacy and supply funds. Its General Assembly recommends action; the Security Council can take Charter-authorized decisions. The North Atlantic Treaty Organization is a state alliance for collective defense; Article 5 leaves each ally to determine necessary action. The Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement includes independent humanitarian bodies.
