# Scope and sequence: US Government and Politics

Course `us-government-and-politics`, grade 12 (taken in grade 11 or 12), fall half (80 days), draft (2026-11-14), revised after the expert and Codex reads of 2026-11-14. The days live in data/plans/us-government-and-politics/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day of the fall half (50 minutes; a timer only where it has a grounded purpose), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1, so the course runs weeks 1 to 16. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Days with Native, hard-history, disability or community content carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. The partial base, American Government 3e, is credited by chapter in the notes; there is no full base text, so there is no source-map.csv. Own-state examples follow the method: Massachusetts as one worked example beside the student's own state, with a link to that state's standards.

## End-of-unit assessments

Each end-of-unit assessment is a composite: an unseen task for the codes a single sitting can test, plus the unit's products scored on the course rubric for the codes a sitting cannot (a presentation, a seminar or hearing record, research, a written position). Each end-of-unit day's note lists every part of every code under "Parts asked", with a short prompt for each part the main task misses, so no code is scored from one part (settled rule 4). No day is added.

## Unit 1. Why government, and what kind? (days 1 to 6, weeks 1 to 2)

Arc: what government and citizenship are and why governments exist, then the forms of government with political ideologies and socialization, then the theories of democracy tested against participation data, and why citizens join voluntary groups. Then the end-of-unit assessment and its respond day. The forms sit beside ideologies (both describe what a government or a person stands for); the theories come next because they need the participation data to judge.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook (see the table). The reteach is a station on day 2; day 4 uses the civic-groups items.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 5: unseen participation data and two short readings, covering T1.1 to T1.5, D2.Civ.2 and D2.Civ.6, with day 4's written comparison scored for T1.5 and D2.Civ.6. Respond day 6.
- Candidate: political ideologies and political socialization, kept, 0 added days of its own: it shares day 2 with T1.3 and stays counted as 1 of the 7 additions. No student is asked about family beliefs.

## Unit 2. Founding ideals and the gap (days 7 to 15, weeks 2 to 3)

Arc: the Declaration read for its ideas, then the Constitution beside a state constitution, Federalist 10 and 51 against Brutus No. 1, a two-day research presentation on how one principle changed through later documents, efforts to close the gap with a bounded excerpt set from the Letter from Birmingham Jail and a seminar, and a mock hearing with a written position. The documents come first, then their later history, then the two deliberation settings.

- Readiness and retrieval: day 6's last ten minutes and day 7 (see the table). The documents were read last year, so they open as a retrieval task with the passages printed; civil rights laws, untouched since grade 8, get the readiness check, and the reteach station sits on day 12 where the unit needs them.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 14: an unseen founding-era provision and a later document, covering T1.6 to T1.10 and D2.Civ.9, with day 11's presentation (T1.8), day 13's written position (T1.10) and the day 12 and 13 look-for records (D2.Civ.9) scored. Respond day 15.
- Candidate: the Letter from Birmingham Jail, kept inside T1.9 on day 12 as excerpts in context, the whole Letter as further reading, 0 added days. The amendment process and the Bill of Rights read whole sit in unit 5 (T3.1); Federalist 70 and 78 sit in unit 4 (T2.7, T2.6). All 0 added days.
- ELA 11 runs its founding unit in weeks 7 to 11; this unit sits in weeks 2 to 3, so the two never share weeks.

## Unit 3. Federalism and sovereignty (days 16 to 22, weeks 4 to 5)

Arc: unitary, confederal and federal systems and the grants and limits of power, then enumerated and implied powers with McCulloch, the modern Commerce Clause cases, the student's own state and its towns with fiscal federalism, and tribal nations as a third sovereign. Tribal sovereignty closes the unit so it is studied as a full sovereign beside the others.

- Readiness: day 15's last ten minutes (see the table). The reteach is a station on day 17.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 21: an unseen dispute over who decides (a state law, a town rule or a tribal court's reach), covering T2.1 to T2.5 and D2.Civ.1 (national, state, local and tribal part). Respond day 22.
- Candidate: fiscal federalism, kept inside T2.4 on day 19, 0 added days. The modern Commerce Clause cases, kept, 1 day (day 18).
- Day 20 adapts Since Time Immemorial: content unchanged, our structure and tone, one credit line. Native flag for the sensitivity read.

## Unit 4. The branches and their balance (days 23 to 35, weeks 5 to 7)

Arc: two days on Congress, then the President, executive agencies, the courts and judicial review, a two-day Youngstown case with a moot, two days on checks and balances under strain (1937 and 1973, then reconciliation and war powers), the movement to limit government, and a mock hearing on whether the design still works, where four short source cards compare a decision procedure at a town council, a state legislature, Congress and the UN Security Council. The procedure comparison sits in the hearing (D2.Civ.11 with T2.12) so day 31 keeps its two lettered parts; the Security Council card is a one-paragraph brief and unit 7 teaches it in full.

- Readiness: day 22's last ten minutes on ss8-three-branches, reteach station day 24; day 27 retrieves courts and judicial review.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 34: an unseen recent executive action and a court excerpt, covering T2.6 to T2.12 (all lettered parts of T2.8 and T2.10), D2.Civ.4, D2.Civ.11 and Congress, with day 26's agency research (T2.8), the day 29 moot record (T2.9, D2.Civ.4), day 32's argument (T2.11) and day 33's written judgment (T2.12, D2.Civ.11) scored. Respond day 35.
- Candidate: Congress in full, kept, 2 days (days 23 and 24). Federalist 70 kept inside T2.7 (day 25) and Federalist 78 inside T2.6 (day 27), 0 added days.

## Course-level check 1 (days 36 to 37, week 8)

Day 36 is the midpoint check on units 1 to 4: an unseen document set and an argument on how the branches balance, plus a short knowledge section. It falls right after unit 4's assessment (day 34), so it does not repeat it (settled rule 3): its items on unit 4's skills draw content from units 1 to 3 (a check applied to a unit 2 founding provision or a unit 3 federalism dispute), never unit 4's own cases. Day 37 is its respond day.

## Unit 5. Rights and a free press (days 38 to 46, weeks 8 to 10)

Arc: the Bill of Rights read whole with the amendment process, then the 14th Amendment and the Universal Declaration compared, three days on a rights pair (by default Betts v. Brady and Gideon v. Wainwright), then two days on press freedom and government control of information.

- Readiness: day 37's last ten minutes (see the table). The reteach is a station on day 39; day 43 retrieves press freedom from US History I.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 45: an unseen rights case summary and a press-freedom source, covering T3.1, T3.2 and NML.T1.1 to T1.4, with day 42's presentation scored for T3.2. Respond day 46.
- Candidate: incorporation and the rights of the accused, kept inside T3.2 through the default pair (days 40 to 42), and the religion clauses through Minersville and Barnette where a class takes that pair, 0 added days. Where ELA 12 runs beside the course the pair is never Plessy and Brown; the default meets this.

## Unit 6. Parties, interest groups and media (days 47 to 55, weeks 10 to 11)

Arc: parties and how candidates are promoted, then platforms and campaign finance, turnout data, the Electoral College and the student's own state's election rules, interest groups, digital news and social media, and consolidation, media effects and polls.

- Readiness: day 46's last ten minutes on ss8-elections-parties, reteach station day 48; civic groups (checked on day 1) open as a retrieval task on day 47.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 54: an unseen election package (platform excerpt, ad, turnout table, news post), covering T4.1 to T4.5 and NML.T3.1, T3.3 to T3.5. Respond day 55.
- Candidate: the Electoral College and the student's own state's election rules, kept, 1 day (day 50). Public opinion and polls, kept inside T4.5 on day 53, 0 added days.
- Bridge for math:S-IC.B.6 on days 49 and 53: Algebra II teaches it in weeks 28 to 32, later than this unit, so reports are judged by sample, source and margin, with no significance test.

## Unit 7. The United States in the world (days 56 to 63, weeks 12 to 13)

Arc: how nations deal with each other and why they clash, then the foreign affairs powers with treaty practice, the tools and forces of foreign policy, international organizations and law, and a two-day research and argument on a world issue.

- Readiness: day 55's last ten minutes on wh2-middle-east-since-1980 (last used in grade 10), reteach station in day 56's first ten minutes; day 59 retrieves the postwar order and the Security Council procedure met on day 33.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 62: an unseen foreign policy case, covering T5.1 to T5.8 and D2.Civ.1 (international part), with day 61's argument scored for T5.8. Respond day 63.
- Candidate: treaty ratification and executive agreements, kept inside T5.3 on day 57, 0 added days.

## Unit 8. Policy in the news (days 64 to 71, weeks 13 to 15)

Arc: one past and one current policy debate, then the two compared by intended and unintended results, a current local issue chosen by each team with the body that decides it and the date of its next public meeting, the coverage taken apart, information gathered and synthesized (searches, an interview, a peer survey or an information request to the named office sent through an adult), and a factual report and an editorial, each with a captioned visual. The team's issue carries into unit 9; the information request sent on day 68 and the meeting found on day 66 come back on days 77 and 80.

- Readiness: none. Day 64 retrieves judging election information (kept in use through grade 11), day 65 civic groups and day 67 news reading from US History I.
- Digital and paper routes: the digital route runs a student-directed search with an organized source record and synthesis on day 68 and a digital visual on a shared device on day 69. The paper route uses printed clippings and a drawn visual; on it NML.T5.1 and T5.3 are met in part, said plainly to the teacher, in the same way as T5.4 and T5.5 in unit 9.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 70: an unseen past policy and its record for T4.6 (all lettered parts as options), T4.7, NML.T4.4 and D2.Civ.13, with day 65's comparison, the day 68 source record and synthesis (NML.T5.1), the day 69 report and editorial (NML.T5.2) and the visuals (NML.T5.3) scored on the course rubric, not retaken. Respond day 71.
- Candidate: the policy process stages, kept inside T4.7 on day 66, 0 added days.
- ELA 12 teaches argument in its unit 2 (weeks 4 to 8), before this unit; informative writing is known from ELA 11.

## Course-level check 2 (days 72 to 73, week 15)

Day 72 is the final check on units 1 to 8, weighted to units 5 to 8: an unseen news package on a policy issue to source, sort report from opinion and connect to rights, branches and groups. It falls right after unit 8's assessment (day 70), so it does not repeat it (settled rule 3): its items on unit 8's skills draw content from earlier units, and the news package's issue is a rights, federalism or election question from units 3 to 6, never a unit 8 debate or a team's issue. Day 73 is its respond day and confirms each team's readiness for unit 9 (issue, outside expert if the teacher has arranged one, plan).

## Unit 9. Local action (days 74 to 80, weeks 15 to 16)

Arc: weigh views on the local issue and learn how to contact officials and comment at a public meeting, then draft position papers and plan the portfolio, build it with contributors, take the action, and present it. Then the end-of-unit assessment and a respond day that holds the look back on the day 68 request and the day 77 action. This is the year's informed action.

Both routes run in the same seven days. The digital route makes a portfolio, site or podcast on a shared classroom device and an adult's account. The paper route makes a printed portfolio and is complete on its own. The outside expert is optional: the teacher arranges one through school staff or a teaching parent, and the team exchanges drafts with them through an adult. Without one, the team writes to a named office by letter through an adult and works with classmates, and NML.T5.5 is met in part, said plainly to the teacher. On the paper route NML.T5.4 is also met in part, said the same way. Where the deciding body's meeting falls outside weeks 15 and 16, the day 77 action is a written public comment where the body accepts one, or a letter.

- Readiness: none; day 73 confirms each team's readiness.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 79: an unseen local policy case to judge and propose a compromise on, with the team portfolio scored on the same rubric, covering T4.8, NML.T5.4, NML.T5.5, D2.Civ.5, D2.Civ.7 and D4.8. Respond day 80, with the look back.
- Candidate: contacting local officials and public meetings, kept inside T4.8 on day 74, 0 added days.

Check set additions with no home code: 5 of the 7 allowed days used (ideologies 1, Commerce Clause 1, Congress 2, Electoral College 1).

## Shorter path

For a class that loses days (no day is cut from the plan; this is the teacher's order of what to fold):

1. Fold the Commerce Clause day (18) into day 17's McCulloch lesson as one case.
2. Fold Congress day 2 (24) into day 23, keeping the bill's path as a diagram.
3. Fold the Electoral College day (50) into day 49 as a short state-rules reading.
4. Run one rights case on day 41 instead of the three-day pair, keeping Gideon.
5. Keep every end-of-unit, check and respond day, the unit 8 products and all of unit 9: they carry the scored codes.
