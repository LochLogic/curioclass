# Scope and sequence: US Government and Politics

Course `us-government-and-politics`, grade 12 (taken in grade 11 or 12), fall half (80 days), draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/us-government-and-politics/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day of the fall half (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1, so the course runs weeks 1 to 16. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment built on an unseen task, and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Days with Native, hard-history, disability or community content carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. The partial base, American Government 3e, is credited by chapter in the notes; there is no full base text, so there is no source-map.csv. Own-state examples follow the method: Massachusetts as one worked example beside the student's own state, with a link to that state's standards.

## Unit 1. Why government, and what kind? (days 1 to 6, weeks 1 to 2)

Arc: what government and citizenship are and why governments exist, then forms of government and theories of democracy tested against participation data, political ideologies and socialization, and why citizens join voluntary groups. Then the end-of-unit assessment and its respond day. The purposes and forms come before the theories that judge them; civic groups close the unit because they lead into the founding's ideas of a free people.

- Readiness: day 1, one combined check on grade 8's citizenship and Greek and Roman roots (ss8-citizenship, ss8-greek-roman-roots, both dormant). The reteach is a station on day 2.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 5: unseen participation data and two short readings, covering T1.1 to T1.5, D2.Civ.2 and D2.Civ.6. Respond day 6.
- Candidate: political ideologies and political socialization, kept, 1 day (day 3). No student is asked about family beliefs.

## Unit 2. Founding ideals and the gap (days 7 to 15, weeks 2 to 3)

Arc: the Declaration read for its ideas, then the Constitution beside a state constitution, Federalist 10 and 51 against Brutus No. 1, a two-day research presentation on how one principle changed through later documents, efforts to close the gap with the Letter from Birmingham Jail and a seminar, and a mock hearing with a written position. The documents come first, then their later history, then the two deliberation settings that ask how close practice has come.

- Readiness: day 7, short items on grade 8's Declaration, Convention and ratification debate (ss8-declaration, ss8-articles-convention, ss8-ratification-debate, idle two or more grades), with no reteach lesson, because the unit rereads the documents in full (For the lead 1).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 14: an unseen founding-era provision and a later document, covering T1.6 to T1.10 and D2.Civ.9. Respond day 15.
- Candidate: the Letter from Birmingham Jail, kept inside T1.9 on day 12, 0 added days. The amendment process and the Bill of Rights read whole sit in unit 5 (T3.1); Federalist 70 and 78 sit in unit 4 (T2.7, T2.6). All 0 added days.
- ELA 11 runs its founding unit in weeks 7 to 11; this unit sits in weeks 2 to 3, so the two never share weeks.

## Unit 3. Federalism and sovereignty (days 16 to 22, weeks 4 to 5)

Arc: unitary, confederal and federal systems and the grants and limits of power, then enumerated and implied powers with McCulloch, the modern Commerce Clause cases, the student's own state and its towns with fiscal federalism, and tribal nations as a third sovereign. The national and state frame comes first; tribal sovereignty closes the unit so it is studied as a full sovereign beside the others, not as a footnote.

- Readiness: day 16, one combined check on grade 8's federal and state powers and state and local government (ss8-federal-state-powers, ss8-state-local-government, both dormant). The reteach is a station on day 17.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 21: an unseen dispute over who decides (a state law, a town rule or a tribal court's reach), covering T2.1 to T2.5 and D2.Civ.1 (national, state, local and tribal part). Respond day 22.
- Candidate: fiscal federalism, kept inside T2.4 on day 19, 0 added days. The modern Commerce Clause cases, kept, 1 day (day 18).
- Day 20 adapts Since Time Immemorial: content unchanged, our structure and tone, one credit line. Native flag for the sensitivity read.

## Unit 4. The branches and their balance (days 23 to 35, weeks 5 to 7)

Arc: two days on Congress (structure, representation, redistricting, committees, how a bill becomes law), then the President, executive agencies, the courts and judicial review, a two-day Youngstown case with a moot, two days on checks and balances under strain (1937, 1973, reconciliation, war powers, decision procedures compared), the movement to limit government, and a mock hearing on whether the design still works. Congress opens because the anchor's checks and balances need the first branch in full; the courts follow the executive so the separation-of-powers case can use both.

- Readiness: day 23, one combined check on grade 8's branches and courts (ss8-three-branches, ss8-courts-review, both dormant). The reteach is a station on day 24.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 34: an unseen recent executive action and a court excerpt, covering T2.6 to T2.12 (all lettered parts of T2.8 and T2.10), D2.Civ.4, D2.Civ.11 and Congress. Respond day 35.
- Candidate: Congress in full, kept, 2 days (days 23 and 24). Federalist 70 kept inside T2.7 (day 25) and Federalist 78 inside T2.6 (day 27), 0 added days.

## Course-level check 1 (days 36 to 37, week 8)

Day 36 is the midpoint check on units 1 to 4: an unseen document set (a founding text beside a recent case or executive action) and an argument on how the branches balance, plus a short knowledge section. Day 37 is its respond day.

## Unit 5. Rights and a free press (days 38 to 46, weeks 8 to 10)

Arc: the Bill of Rights read whole with the amendment process, then the 14th Amendment and the Universal Declaration compared, three days on a rights pair (by default Betts v. Brady and Gideon v. Wainwright), then two days on press freedom and government control of information. Rights documents come before the cases that apply them; the free press closes the unit and leads into unit 6's media.

- Readiness: day 38, on grade 8's state declarations of rights (ss8-state-rights-documents, dormant). The reteach is a station on day 39.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 45: an unseen rights case summary and a press-freedom source, covering T3.1, T3.2 and NML.T1.1 to T1.4. Respond day 46.
- Candidate: incorporation and the rights of the accused, kept inside T3.2 through the default pair (days 40 to 42), and the religion clauses through Minersville and Barnette where a class takes that pair, 0 added days. Where ELA 12 runs beside the course the pair is never Plessy and Brown; the default meets this.

## Unit 6. Parties, interest groups and media (days 47 to 55, weeks 10 to 11)

Arc: parties and how candidates are promoted, then platforms and campaign finance, turnout data, the Electoral College and the student's own state's election rules, interest groups, digital news and social media, and consolidation, media effects and polls. Parties and elections first, then the groups and media that press on them.

- Readiness: day 47, one combined check on grade 8's parties and elections and civic groups (ss8-elections-parties, ss8-interest-groups-policy, both dormant). The reteach is a station on day 48.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 54: an unseen election package (platform excerpt, ad, turnout table, news post), covering T4.1 to T4.5 and NML.T3.1, T3.3 to T3.5. Respond day 55.
- Candidate: the Electoral College and the student's own state's election rules, kept, 1 day (day 50). Public opinion and polls, kept inside T4.5 on day 53, 0 added days.
- Bridge for math:S-IC.B.6 on days 49 and 53: Algebra II teaches it in weeks 28 to 32, later than this unit, so reports are judged by sample, source and margin, with no significance test.

## Unit 7. The United States in the world (days 56 to 63, weeks 12 to 13)

Arc: how nations deal with each other and why they clash, then the foreign affairs powers with treaty practice, the tools and forces of foreign policy, international organizations and law, and a two-day research and argument on a world issue. Powers come before tools, and organizations before the issue the student argues.

- Readiness: none. Day 56 opens with a retrieval task on a region's conflicts and US involvement (grade 10); day 59 retrieves the postwar order.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 62: an unseen foreign policy case, covering T5.1 to T5.8 and D2.Civ.1 (international part). Respond day 63.
- Candidate: treaty ratification and executive agreements, kept inside T5.3 on day 57, 0 added days.

## Unit 8. Policy in the news (days 64 to 71, weeks 13 to 15)

Arc: one past and one current policy debate, then the two compared by intended and unintended results, a current local issue chosen by each team with the policy process stages, the coverage taken apart, information gathered, and a factual report and an editorial written. The team's issue chosen on day 66 carries into unit 9.

- Readiness: day 64, on grade 8's news literacy (ss8-news-literacy, dormant). The reteach is a station on day 65.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 70: an unseen past policy and its record, covering T4.6 (all lettered parts as options), T4.7, NML.T4.4, T5.1 to T5.3 and D2.Civ.13. Respond day 71.
- Candidate: the policy process stages, kept inside T4.7 on day 66, 0 added days.
- ELA 12 teaches argument in its unit 2 (weeks 4 to 8), before this unit; informative writing is known from ELA 11.

## Course-level check 2 (days 72 to 73, week 15)

Day 72 is the final check on units 1 to 8, weighted to units 5 to 8: an unseen news package on a policy issue to source, sort report from opinion and connect to rights, branches and groups. Day 73 is its respond day and confirms each team's readiness for unit 9 (issue, outside expert if the teacher has arranged one, plan).

## Unit 9. Local action (days 74 to 80, weeks 15 to 16)

Arc: weigh views on the local issue and learn how to contact officials and comment at a public meeting, then draft position papers and plan the portfolio, build it with contributors, take the action, and present it. Then the end-of-unit assessment and a respond day that holds the look back on the action. This is the year's informed action.

Both routes run in the same seven days, as the frame's For the lead 1 settles. The digital route makes a portfolio, site or podcast on a shared classroom device and an adult's account. The paper route makes a printed portfolio and is complete on its own. The outside expert is optional: the teacher arranges one through school staff or a teaching parent, and the team works with them through an adult. Without one, the team writes to a named office by letter through an adult and works with classmates, and NML.T5.5 is met in part, said plainly to the teacher. On the paper route NML.T5.4 is also met in part, said the same way.

- Readiness: none; day 73 confirms each team's readiness.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 79: an unseen local policy case to judge and propose a compromise on, with the team portfolio scored on the same rubric, covering T4.8, NML.T5.4, NML.T5.5, D2.Civ.5, D2.Civ.7 and D4.8. Respond day 80, with the look back on the action.
- Candidate: contacting local officials and public meetings, kept inside T4.8 on day 74, 0 added days.

Check set additions with no home code: 5 of the 7 allowed days used (ideologies 1, Commerce Clause 1, Congress 2, Electoral College 1).
