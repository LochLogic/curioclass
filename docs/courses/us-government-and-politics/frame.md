# Course frame: US Government and Politics

Course id `government`, grades 11 to 12 (half year; rows sit at grade 12 in the data), draft frame (2026-11-13). The
scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9, from why government exists to the student's own local action.
- **Days.** 80 teaching days (a half year): 76 in units and 4 for two course-level checks, each a check day and a
  respond day. Each unit's days include its readiness check and, where a grade 8 row is dormant knowledge, the
  one-lesson reteach for students who miss it (units 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8), its end-of-unit assessment and its respond day.
  Where a unit has two dormant grade 8 rows (units 1 and 3), one combined readiness check opens day 1 and one reteach
  lesson covers both.
- **Check-set additions.** Additions ride inside an owned code's lesson where one fits; at most 7 days in the course are
  for additions with no home code. Riding inside: Federalist 70 (T2.7) and 78 (T2.6), the Letter from Birmingham Jail
  (T1.9), the Bill of Rights read whole (T3.1), fiscal federalism (T2.4), polls (T4.5), the policy process stages
  (T4.7), treaty practice (T5.3), contacting officials (T4.8); incorporation, the religion clauses and the rights of the
  accused enter unit 5 as case pairs students may choose for T3.2. Taking days (5 of the 7): Congress (2, unit 4), the
  Electoral College and the student's own state's election rules (1, unit 6), political ideologies and socialization
  (1, unit 1), the Commerce Clause cases (1, unit 3). The comparison uses the college-level US government framework in
  its fall 2026 edition, and each unit plan records a decision on the founding documents that edition added. The exact
  check-set lines cited below are from the writer's knowledge and are read against the documents at the unit plan's
  comparison table.
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 GOV.T1 to GOV.T5 is the content anchor, with News/Media Literacy NML.T1.1 to T1.4,
  T3.1, T3.3 to T3.5, T4.4 and T5.1 to T5.5 placed here (STANDARD.md: NML is placed in the high school courses; grade 11
  US History I holds NML.T1.5, T2, T3.2 and T4.1 to T4.3; US History II and Economics hold none). Every numbered code
  is owned by one row and every row sits in one unit, so the whole topic range has a unit. Skills cite C3: this course
  owns D2.Civ.1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11 and 13 and D4.8 at 9-12, and practises the indicators grades 9 and 10 own.
  Each unit plan compares the unit with California, Tennessee and New York and with the college-level US government
  course framework, and adds inside its days what a strong set treats as essential ("Check set adds"); check-set names
  never appear in student, teacher or family copy. The values rule governs: court rulings as the higher court decided
  them; claims from backed sources; every group present where the record includes it; parties compared on their own
  published platforms; the student's position is the student's own.
- **Open to grade 11 or 12** (Cory, Desk card q-ss-progression-1). No row assumes US History I or US History II; the
  examples a row once took from US History I are retaught in brief from the source inside the row's unit. Skills come
  from grade 8 civics (ss8-*), grade 9 and 10 world history and argument writing.
- **Own-state method.** Where the anchor names Massachusetts (its 1780 constitution, town meetings, state and local
  relations, state executive offices), the course teaches the student's own state as the case, with Massachusetts as
  one worked example beside at least one other state, and links each state's own standards (bar item 13).
- **Base text.** None for the course (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, P2). Partial base: the retired CC BY American
  Government 3e (store folder `openstax-retired-ccby\`), adaptable with a one-line credit. Where it can serve: unit 1
  (chapter 1), unit 2 (chapter 2; appendices A to C hold the Declaration, the Constitution and Federalist 10 and 51),
  unit 3 (chapters 3 and 14), unit 4 (chapters 11, 12, 13 and 15), unit 5 (chapters 4 and 5), unit 6 (chapters 6 to
  10), unit 7 (chapter 17), unit 8 (chapters 8 and 16), unit 9 (chapters 1 and 14). Since Time Immemorial's high school
  materials (CC BY 4.0, `since-time-immemorial\`) for tribal sovereignty in unit 3, after the sensitivity read. The
  frame does not map either lesson by lesson: the unit plan names what it adapts.
- **Inquiry arc and informed action.** Every unit runs on its compelling question with sources sourced before use and
  an end-of-unit task from unseen sources. The year's informed action is unit 9: a student-chosen local issue, a
  completed action through a safe route (a public comment given, a letter sent, a proposal presented), then a look back.
  Adult contact goes through school staff, or the parent where a family teaches the course; no public posting or
  student account is needed. Teams choose their local issue in unit 8 and arrange their outside collaborator before
  unit 9 begins.
- **Deliberation.** Units 2, 4 and 9 run structured deliberation (seminar, mock public hearing, structured online
  exchange). A mock hearing casts students as citizens and officials stating positions, never as victims or
  perpetrators of a harm.
- **The grade 12 timetable.** The table is in docs/courses/us-history-ii/frame.md: US History II all year in one period,
  Government and Economics one half each in a second; suggested Government first, so this course's local action does
  not fall in the weeks of US History II's capstone. A grade 11 student takes it beside US History I.
