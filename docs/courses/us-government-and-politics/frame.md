# Course frame: US Government and Politics

Course `us-government-and-politics` (catalogue id `government`), grades 11 to 12 (half year; rows sit at grade 12 in the data), draft frame (2026-11-13). The
days are set in docs/courses/us-government-and-politics/scope-and-sequence.md and data/plans/us-government-and-politics/days.csv.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9, from why government exists to the student's own local action.
- **Days.** 80 course days (a half year): 76 in units and 4 for two course-level checks, each an assessment day and a
  respond day. One lesson of about 50 minutes a day, five a week, across 16 weeks. Each unit's days include its readiness check or retrieval task, its end-of-unit assessment and one
  respond day (a unit over 25 days would also hold a mid-unit assessment; none is that long). A row used the year
  before (US History I) or earlier this course opens as a retrieval task inside a day; a row last used two or more
  grades back gets a readiness check before its unit opens, and a one-lesson reteach station follows for
  students who miss it (units 1 to 7; units 8 and 9 open with retrieval tasks only). Where a unit has more than one such row, one combined check
  covers them. The table is in the scope and sequence.
- **Check-set additions.** Additions ride inside an owned code's lesson where one fits; at most 7 days in the course are
  for additions with no home code. Riding inside: Federalist 70 (T2.7) and 78 (T2.6), the Letter from Birmingham Jail
  (T1.9), the Bill of Rights read whole (T3.1), fiscal federalism (T2.4), polls (T4.5), the policy process stages
  (T4.7), treaty practice (T5.3), contacting officials (T4.8); incorporation, the religion clauses and the rights of the
  accused enter unit 5 as case pairs students may choose for T3.2. Taking days (5 of the 7): Congress (2, unit 4), the
  Electoral College and the student's own state's election rules (1, unit 6), political ideologies and socialization
  (1, unit 1), the Commerce Clause cases (1, unit 3). The comparison uses the college-level US government framework in
  its fall 2026 edition, and each unit plan records a decision on the founding documents that edition added. The exact
  check-set lines cited below are from the writer's knowledge and are read against the documents at the unit plan's
  comparison table.
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 GOV.T1 to GOV.T5 is the content anchor, with News/Media Literacy NML.T1.1 to T1.4,
  T3.1, T3.3 to T3.5, T4.4 and T5.1 to T5.5 placed here (STANDARD.md: NML is placed in the high school courses; grade 11
  US History I holds NML.T1.5, T2, T3.2 and T4.1 to T4.3; US History II and Economics hold none). Every numbered code
  is owned by one row and every row sits in one unit, so the whole topic range has a unit. Skills cite C3: this course
  owns D2.Civ.1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11 and 13 and D4.8 at 9-12, and practices the indicators grades 9 and 10 own.
  Each unit plan compares the unit with California, Tennessee and New York and with the college-level US government
  course framework, and adds inside its days what a strong set treats as essential ("Check set adds"); check-set names
  never appear in student, teacher or family copy. The values rule governs: court rulings as the higher court decided
  them; claims from backed sources; every group present where the record includes it; parties compared on their own
  published platforms; the student's position is the student's own.
- **Open to grade 11 or 12** (Cory, Desk card q-ss-progression-1). No row assumes US History I or US History II; the
  examples a row once took from US History I are retaught in brief from the source inside the row's unit. Skills come
  from grade 8 civics (ss8-*), grade 9 and 10 world history and argument writing.
- **Own-state method.** Where the anchor names Massachusetts (its 1780 constitution, town meetings, state and local
  relations, state executive offices), the course teaches the student's own state as the case, with Massachusetts as
  one worked example beside at least one other state, and links each state's own standards (bar item 13).
- **Base text.** None for the course (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, P2). Partial base: the retired CC BY American
  Government 3e (store folder `openstax-retired-ccby\`), adaptable with a one-line credit. Where it can serve: unit 1
  (chapter 1), unit 2 (chapter 2; appendices A to C hold the Declaration, the Constitution and Federalist 10 and 51),
  unit 3 (chapters 3 and 14), unit 4 (chapters 11, 12, 13 and 15), unit 5 (chapters 4 and 5), unit 6 (chapters 6 to
  10), unit 7 (chapter 17), unit 8 (chapters 8 and 16), unit 9 (chapters 1 and 14). Since Time Immemorial's high school
  materials (CC BY 4.0, `since-time-immemorial\`) for tribal sovereignty in unit 3, after the sensitivity read (see Since Time Immemorial below). The
  frame does not map either lesson by lesson: the unit plan names what it adapts.
- **Since Time Immemorial.** Where a unit adapts Since Time Immemorial (Washington OSPI's tribally developed curriculum, CC BY 4.0), the content is kept as written (the facts, the tribes' own words and framing); the lesson shape and voice follow Curio Class; each adapted day carries one credit line saying it is adapted from Since Time Immemorial and that the content is the tribes' (Cory, 2026-11-14). Here: unit 3 (tribal sovereignty, the third sovereign).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md)
  before it ships; no outside human reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). The agent studies first, for: unit 2, Black
  historians of the founding and slavery and the Letter from Birmingham Jail in its setting; unit 3, the Cherokee Nation's
  and the Medicine Creek Treaty nations' (the Nisqually, Puyallup and Squaxin Island tribes') own pages, a national
  tribal governments' association's materials on sovereignty, Since Time Immemorial and a Native journalists'
  association guide; unit 5, the communities behind the case pair taught (for the default pair, public defenders' and
  legal historians' accounts of Gideon; for Barnette, Jehovah's Witnesses' own account of the flag salute cases); unit 8,
  where the class takes the disability rights debate, disability rights organizations' own histories and a disability
  language guide; unit 9, the community the team's local issue concerns, from its own published voice. Units 1, 4, 6 and
  7 get the read for fairness across parties, groups and countries.
- **Paper path.** Every source, data set and task has a printed version; no day needs a student account. NML.T5.4 and
  T5.5 ask for a digital product and digital collaboration: a shared classroom device and an adult's account carry them,
  and a printed portfolio with the outside contact made by letter through an adult is the paper path (then NML.T5.4
  and T5.5 are met in part, said plainly to the teacher).
- **Literacy standards (also met).** The unit work also meets these Common Core literacy standards for history and
  social studies (data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt; the rows' notes name them), with no added days: unit 2,
  RH.11-12.5, RH.11-12.6, RH.11-12.8; unit 5, RH.11-12.1; unit 6, RH.11-12.8; unit 8, RH.11-12.7, WHST.11-12.1,
  WHST.11-12.2. Across the half year: RH.11-12.2, RH.11-12.4 and RH.11-12.10 (every unit reads and summarizes sources
  at the band's complexity) and WHST.11-12.10 (routine short and long writing). WHST.11-12.6 is not claimed: the paper
  path keeps every product off a device.
- **Review roles.** Review roles follow FOUNDATION section 7.3, revised 2026-11-14: the sensitivity, safety and answer-key agents do the content reads, each cold and after studying its sources; the cases and sources named here stay binding. The course-level checks get the answer-key agent's read before the course is called finished.
- **Inquiry arc and informed action.** Every unit runs on its compelling question with sources sourced before use and
  an end-of-unit task from unseen sources. The year's informed action is unit 9: a student-chosen local issue, a
  completed action through a safe route (a public comment given, a letter sent, a proposal presented), then a look back.
  Adult contact goes through school staff, or the parent where a family teaches the course; no public posting or
  student account is needed. Teams choose their local issue in unit 8, with the body that decides it and its next
  meeting date; an outside expert is optional (For the lead 1).
- **Deliberation.** Units 2, 4 and 9 run structured deliberation (seminar, mock public hearing, structured online
  exchange). A mock hearing casts students as citizens and officials stating positions, never as victims or
  perpetrators of a harm.
- **The grade 12 timetable** (Cory, 2026-11-14: yes, as picked). The table is in docs/courses/us-history-ii/frame.md: US History II all year in one period,
  Government and Economics one half each in a second; suggested Government first, so this course's local action does
  not fall in the weeks of US History II's capstone. A grade 11 student takes it beside US History I.
