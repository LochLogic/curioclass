## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who shapes what voters see and choose?"

**Hook:** Day 47 opens with the two parties' own 2024 housing proposals. Both promise affordability. What differs in their means, audiences and evidence? Connect these choices to the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Compose together: Why form parties (47)? What do platforms, money and participation data reveal (48 and 49)? Who administers elections (50)? How do groups gain influence (51)? What changes when news moves online (52 and 53)?
**Closing claim:** Day 53 answers the compelling question with the platform research record, participation table, contrasting group statements and King, Schneer and White study. Include a counterclaim and a limit on inference.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing and an evidence-based comparison on 47; guide research on 48 and data interpretation on 49; fade completed examples on 51 and all guides on 54. Before using each source, identify author, date, audience, purpose and context. Teach platform, coalition, turnout, lobbying, political action committee, independent expenditure, algorithm, consolidation and corroboration through contrasts.


**Parts by verb:** T4.1: **Trace**, **analyze**, **evaluate** history, organization, functions and promotion. T4.2: **Research**, **analyze**, **draw conclusions** across all four evidence types. T4.3: **Trace**, **analyze**, **evaluate** growth, strategies, effects and views. T4.4 and NML.T3.1: **Evaluate** benefits and costs. NML.T3.3 to T3.5: **Explain** reach, industry changes and discerning consumption. T4.5: **Analyze** research and explain judgment. Day 47 includes smaller parties, winner-take-all incentives and ballot-access barriers. Day 53 practices D3.1.9-12 and RH.11-12.8. Citizens United protects independent corporate and union spending while preserving disclosure and the corporate contribution ban. Independent expenditure-only committees cannot contribute or coordinate with candidates.

**Limits:** No endorsement, campaign work, personal voting disclosure or significance test. Teach historical context directly; no US History I prerequisite. Policy evaluation and informed action belong to units 8 and 9.
**Carried:** Retain unit 5's blank claim-evidence-reasoning guide, continuity-and-change chart and separate branch and authority diagrams. Revise the chart with party and group growth on 47 and 51. Extend familiar sourcing and corroboration to tracing digital claims on 52; these are new applications, not first instruction. Return to both routines on 53 and preserve blank versions for unit 8.
