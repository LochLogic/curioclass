## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who shapes what voters see and choose?"
**Hook:** Day 47 opens with the two parties' own 2024 housing proposals. Both promise affordability. What differs in their means, audiences and evidence? Connect these choices to the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Compose together: Why form parties (47)? What do platforms, money and participation data reveal (48 and 49)? Who administers elections (50)? How do groups gain influence (51)? What changes when news moves online (52 and 53)?
**Closing claim:** Day 53 answers the compelling question with the platform research record, participation table, contrasting group statements and King, Schneer and White study. Include a counterclaim and a limit on inference.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing and an evidence-based comparison on 47; guide research on 48 and data interpretation on 49; fade completed examples on 51 and all guides on 54. Before using each source, identify author, date, audience, purpose and context. Teach platform, coalition, turnout, lobbying, political action committee, independent expenditure, algorithm, consolidation and corroboration through contrasts.

**Tools:** None. Printed comparison cards and source records fit; no key tool or tool request. Supply sources, blank charts, separate assessment packets, pencils and class record sheets. Every source, data display and task has a paper version; a projector is optional.

## 9. Family note

Students investigate how parties, groups and news shape public choices, then test claims against evidence. Ask what a source can establish; no family voting preference needs to be shared.
