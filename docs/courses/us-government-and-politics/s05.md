## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How have Americans' rights, and the freedom to publish, grown or narrowed?"
**Hook:** Day 38 opens with the original 1789 proposal beside the ten ratified amendments. Why were only ten proposed articles ratified in 1791? Connect amendment choices to the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Compose together: Who is protected, against whom (38 and 39)? How can a person challenge a denial of rights (40 and 41)? What changed, and what remained (42)? Why protect competing accounts (43)? When does government control information (44)?
**Closing claim:** Day 44 answers the compelling question using the rights documents, Betts, Gideon, Near and the Pentagon Papers opinions. Explain a change, a continuity, a counterclaim and the evidence's limits. Use the selected alternate opinions where appropriate.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing, context and corroboration on day 38, comparison on 39 and an evidence-based case explanation on 40. Withdraw completed examples on 41; students research and present independently on 42. Source every record before using it. Teach civil liberty, civil right, human right, due process, equal protection, incorporation, precedent, censorship and prior restraint through contrasts.

- **Mapp summary:** In 1961, the Supreme Court reviewed Dollree Mapp's Ohio conviction. Officers had obtained evidence through an unconstitutional search. The Court held that evidence obtained through searches violating the federal Constitution could not be admitted in a state criminal trial. It applied the exclusionary rule to states through the 14th Amendment. Source: Mapp opinion, pages 655 to 660. Omit details of the seized material.
- **Lovell summary:** In 1938, Alma Lovell challenged her conviction for distributing religious literature without the city manager's permission in Griffin, Georgia. The ordinance required permission to distribute literature of any kind. The Supreme Court invalidated that sweeping restriction. Press freedom extends to pamphlets and leaflets, and the 14th Amendment protects it against state and municipal interference. Source: Lovell opinion, pages 450 to 452. Her faith is not an invitation to infer any student's belief.
- **Bridge report A, fictional, by reporter Elena Ruiz:** "The council approved money to repair the bridge. Its chair says the vote shows safety is the priority. The budget authorizes work but does not record completed repairs."
- **Bridge report B, fictional, by reporter Malik Chen:** "The inspection report says the bridge remains unsafe. Residents question the council's priorities. The report gives no completion date or evidence that the approved money was spent."
