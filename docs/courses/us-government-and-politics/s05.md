## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How have Americans' rights, and the freedom to publish, grown or narrowed?"

- **Day 40, Johnson v. Zerbst (1938):** Johnson could not afford counsel for his federal counterfeiting trial. On January 23, 1935, he learned of his indictment, pleaded not guilty, stood trial and received sentence without counsel. He later sought release through habeas corpus. The Court required an informed waiver of counsel and returned the case for findings on waiver. Butler disagreed, believing the existing record showed waiver. Opinion pages 459 to 460, 464 to 465 and 469.
- **Day 41, Argersinger v. Hamlin (1972):** Florida denied appointed counsel for petty offenses punishable by at most six months. Argersinger could not afford counsel, was tried without one for carrying a concealed weapon and received ninety days in jail. He challenged his detention. The Court applied the Sixth Amendment through the Fourteenth: without a valid waiver, no criminal defendant may be imprisoned after a trial without counsel, including for a misdemeanor. Opinion pages 26 to 27 and 37.

- **Day 40, Hamilton v. Regents (1934):** In 1933, Hamilton and Reynolds sought exemption from required military training at California's state university because of religious objections to war. Suspended after refusing training, they sought a court order allowing attendance without it. Their petition said other colleges cost more than they could afford. The Court upheld the requirement, reasoning that the state had not compelled university attendance. Opinion pages 253 to 254 and 261 to 263.
- **Day 41, Wooley v. Maynard (1977):** New Hampshire required its motto on vehicle plates and punished covering it. The Maynards objected to displaying the state's message; after repeated prosecutions, they sought protection against future enforcement. The Court upheld an injunction. It applied First Amendment protection against state-compelled expression, extending Barnette's reasoning to displaying a message on private property. Vehicle identification and state pride did not justify that compulsion. Opinion pages 707 to 709 and 713 to 717.
