## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who should decide: the nation, the state, the town or the tribal nation?"

**Hook:** Day 16 opens with the Nisqually constitution beside Massachusetts home rule: why can both governments make rules, yet their authority has different origins?
**Supporting questions:** Compose together: What makes a system federal (day 16)? When is an unwritten power lawful (day 17)? Where does commerce authority stop (day 18)? Who authorizes, funds and delivers local services (day 19)? How do tribal nations govern today (day 20)?
**Closing claim:** Day 21 answers the compelling question through the postal dispute, constitutional provisions, McCulloch and the Nisqually constitution, with a counterclaim and a jurisdictional limit.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing and constitutional inference on days 16 and 17. Pairs compare holdings on day 18; students independently trace local authority on day 19. Day 20 contrasts present governance with removal and changing jurisdiction. Teach vocabulary through provisions, then withdraw completed examples.


- **D2.Civ.1.9-12:** Tell institutions apart by their powers and duties. Part by verb: **Distinguish** local, state, tribal and national institutions here; international institutions in unit 7. The local file lists identifiers only; wording checked against the C3 framework, page 32.

**Limits:** Branch procedures belong to unit 4, rights cases to unit 5 and international institutions to unit 7. Do not generalize one tribal jurisdiction ruling to every nation or dispute.
