## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Does the Constitution's design still work?"
**Hook:** Day 23 opens with Article I beside an original representation diagram. Can equal Senate representation and population-based House representation both provide fair voice? Return to the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Compose together: Who is represented (day 23)? Where can a bill stop (24)? What authorizes executive action (25 and 26)? What makes review independent (27)? Who decides disputed limits (28 and 29)? Which checks survive pressure (30 and 31)? How much government (32)? Which procedures serve civic purposes (33)?
**Closing claim:** Day 33 answers the compelling question using constitutional provisions, Youngstown, dated executive orders from two parties and four procedure cards. Day 34 tests transfer to an unseen action.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing, context and corroboration on days 23 to 27, distinguishing primary records from secondary explanations. Model a legal claim on day 28; withdraw the completed example for the moot and hearing. Teach apportionment, redistricting, veto, agency, jurisdiction, precedent, reconciliation, deregulation and privatization through examples. Students answer counterclaims without disclosing politics.

## 7. Response allocation

- **Respond days (days.csv):** day 35.
- **Day length (data/plans/frames.csv):** 50 minutes.
Day 34: read eight minutes, short responses seventeen, source argument twenty, mark five. Students mark the short items against the key on the screen, pen down, five minutes. Hands up by item sets the groups. Teacher-judged items are read after class and answered in the next day's first five minutes. Paper keys and private signals work equally.

Response occurs only on day 35: feedback five minutes, reteach twenty, fresh reassessment fifteen, extension ten. A traces legislation; B to F trace authority and checks; G compares procedures; H and I revise evidence and counterclaims. Secure students test a changed fact. For one child, discuss misses together, then reassess. Unit 5 readiness belongs to day 37 after the midpoint check, not day 35.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

None. No key tool or tool request. Print every source, task, diagram, research folder, opinion card, procedure card, glossary, key and record sheet; gather pencils. Projection is optional; no student timers. At home, the adult supplies the counterargument and hears both positions. Students track one corrected claim and supporting evidence on their self-check sheet.

## 9. Family note

Students investigate how public institutions exercise and limit power, then defend a position with evidence. They may disagree about policy without sharing family beliefs, finances, disability, benefits or military experience.
