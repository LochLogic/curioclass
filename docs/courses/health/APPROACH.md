## What a health unit carries (unit field 2)

- **The standards**: each NHES id with its action in our own words (STANDARD.md sections 1 and 2; the indicator's
  wording is never quoted).
- **The behaviour outcomes** it is for (HECAT 2021 numbers; our wording is in data/standards/hecat-topics.txt).
  Every day ties to one (bar criterion 1).
- **Three objectives**: one knowing, one valuing (what the student thinks matters, in a character's case or as a
  belief about what most peers do) and one doing (criterion 8).
- **The skill** it teaches or practises: one of NHES standards 2 to 8 (influences, resources, communication,
  decisions, goals, practising behaviours, advocacy), with its steps written out.
- **The facts that matter for the decision**: short, accurate and dated (criterion 2). A unit lists its figures
  with their source and year, so they can be checked when they go stale.
- **The situations**: three to five realistic scenarios at the students' age, used for practice and the check.

## Kinds of day (principle 2)

- **Understanding.** The facts a decision needs, what peers really do (a unit uses a current national survey figure
  for its age group, with its source and year; below grade 6, where no national use survey of the age exists, the
  unit states the norm in plain words and may give the nearest figure, named for its age group (that national youth use
  surveys begin at grade 6 is from memory, checked at the STANDARD.md section 6 read); that students tend to
  overestimate risk behaviour is from memory and not yet read), and the influences on a choice (criterion 4). Ends
  in a decision or a belief, not a list of facts.
- **Fluency.** A skill taught in the five steps the bar names: why it matters, the steps, a model, practice on
  real situations, feedback (criterion 3). Short scripted rehearsals in pairs: a refusal, an "I" statement,
  asking an adult for help.
- **Application.** The skill used on a new scenario, or on the student's own life: a goal plan, a decision
  worked through, an advocacy piece for a real audience, a talk with a family member (criterion 6).
- **Check.** A performance task: the student uses the skill on a scenario not practised in class, or shows a
  finished plan or advocacy piece, scored with a guide on the skill steps. Never a fact-recall test as the
  end-of-unit assessment.
- **Respond.** Acts on the check: re-rehearse the step marked "not yet" with a new scenario.

## The end-of-unit assessment (unit field 5)

The unit check is teacher-judged on the skill steps, with one short facts-in-use item per fact the unit lists.
**Valuing** is checked with a scenario that asks what a good choice would be for this person and why, scored on
whether the reason uses a unit fact, never on which choice.

## Marking and response (unit field 7)

The unit check is marked by the teacher on the skill steps, with no hands-up self-marking. The respond-day groups
come from the class record sheet.

## Tools and supplies (unit field 8; instruments in section field 5)

A tool may give practice with feedback on facts that support a decision, on reading a label or a source, and on
sorting scenarios; it may give a private self-check. It may not collect any student's health information, ask
about a student's own behaviour, stand in for the rehearsal with people, or give medical advice.

## Family note (unit field 9)

Every unit gives a home task (a talk with a trusted adult the student chooses, or a short list to read together); a
student with no adult to talk with can use a school adult. Family notes are in English and Spanish, written when the
unit is finished; a unit with a leave-out note gets the longer form (what it covers, the dates, how to opt out: in a
school through the school, at home the parent decides).

## Sources (unit field 10)

A unit's field 10 names the source and year of every figure it lists, and points to its dated read record
(STANDARD.md section 6).

## Standards and the teacher page (section field 1)

Each section names the NHES standard and indicator ids it works on, and the behaviour outcome. A section that
gives facts names the decision the facts are for. What the teacher page must explain:

- **Which lines of "What a health day never does"** (below) matter most in the unit.
- **The teacher's own views.** Every unit's teacher page has a short note on the assumptions the topic may touch
  (family structure, food, bodies, faith) and one way to keep them out of the room.
- **If a student tells you something.** Every sensitive unit's teacher page says: listen, do not promise to keep
  it secret, follow the school's duty to report, and tell the student who the next adult is. For a parent teaching
  at home it names the national child abuse hotline and the 988 crisis line by purpose, and says a child's
  disclosure is not handled alone.

## What a health day never does

- No scare tactics, shock images or worst cases offered as the usual outcome.
- No blame of a person, a body or a family type for an illness.
- No weighing, no calorie counting by students, no "good" or "bad" foods: foods that help, and foods to have less
  often.
- No method detail on suicide or self-harm, and no lesson on them without a help line and an adult plan.
- No medical advice, and no claim that a product or practice treats anything.
- No task asks a student about their own drug use, sexual activity, diet, weight, mental health, abuse or family.

## The daily evidence (section field 3)

- **A fluency or application day** ends with the skill used once more, alone, on a short scenario: a written
  line of what the student would say or do, using the steps. K to 2 students say or act it to the teacher or a
  partner, and the teacher marks a short list of named students.
- **An understanding day** ends with one question that asks for a choice and a reason that uses a fact from the
  day.
- **Twice a unit**, a self-check: the student rates their own use of the skill steps and names one next step
  (criterion 8).

The teacher sorts into met, partly and not yet, with no mark. No evidence asks a student to disclose their own
behaviour, health or family. Scenarios are about characters, and personal plans stay with the student unless
they choose to share.

## What is carried (section field 5)

What is carried in a health unit is the skill's step card: the steps of the unit's one skill, in short words. The
teacher holds the model on the first fluency day (says each step aloud on a scenario). On the second fluency day
pairs hold the card and the teacher only prompts. The card is withdrawn on the first application day, and the
section plan names that day. The unit check is always taken without the card. A skill returns in a later unit with
the card for one day, then without it. K to 2 use the same steps as three or four pictured words said aloud.

## How a day opens (section field 6)

A day opens with a short scenario or a question about what most students that age do, five minutes at most. In K
to 5, anything new is taught before it is tried; the fluency day always teaches the steps before pairs try them.

## Rehearsal in sensitive units (section field 7)

In sensitive units rehearsal uses written scripts of the refusal line only. The teacher or a card plays the
pressure; no student plays a person who pressures, abuses or threatens.

## Practice that returns (section field 8)

Each skill returns in later units on other topics: refusal taught in tobacco returns in alcohol and in safety.
Each topic returns in the next year at more depth. Getting help from a trusted adult returns every year.

## Supports (section field 9)

- Scenarios offer names, families and settings that vary, and the teacher can swap them for local ones
  (criterion 9).
- Every unit with personal content gives a way to take part without sharing.
- Every unit names where students and families can find help, in plain words.
- Sensitive units follow STANDARD.md section 5: their own units, a leave-out note, a family note before them.
- **Personal goals** come from a short, safe list (sleep, water, handwashing, time outside, screen breaks, a kind
  act, asking for help), never about weight, body shape, food restriction or calories; a character's goal is always
  an option.
- **English learners** (C11): each unit teaches six to ten health words in depth, with correct body part names
  from the start, and gives a sentence frame for each skill step (for example "I won't do that. I'm going to ...").
  A key line says: the right idea in another language or by pointing is a language gap; a wrong idea is a content
  gap.
- **Access and check accommodations**: any rehearsal, exit or check item can be done in writing, by pointing at a
  card, by a tool or with an adult; no student must speak aloud. **Reading load**: students read at most a short
  scenario alone; the rest is read aloud or pictured. **Extension**: the same skill on a harder or less familiar
  scenario, or a skill returned in another topic, never the next grade's standard (C10).

## Pacing

Settled 2026-11-16 (Cory, Desk card q-later-arts-health-pe): high school health is one semester. Default time,
ours and untested: K to 5 about 30 sessions a year of 30 minutes; 6 to 8 about 45 sessions a year of 45 minutes;
9 to 12 about 80 days of 45 minutes. Each band frame may change these with a reason, and each band's scope and
sequence names its short path (bar C2). How a band splits into grades is in STANDARD.md section 4. The national
figure: the National Health Education Standards (second edition, 2007, page 76, Joint Committee on National Health
Education Standards) recommend at least 40 hours a year in pre-K to grade 2 and 80 hours a year in grades 3 to 12.
Our defaults come to about 15 hours a year in K to 5, 34 in 6 to 8 and 60 in the high school semester, so they are
below it. Cory kept ours (2026-11-21, Desk q-prework-open-2): health shares the day with every other subject, so the
course page names the national figure and the gap so a school can add time.
