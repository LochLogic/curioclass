# Section 4.4: Point of view and links in water texts

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


This section's share: RL.3.6 and RI.3.6 "distinguish their own point of view from that of the narrator or those of the characters" and "from that of the author of a text" are taught on 133 (three labeled views: character, narrator, writer, plus the reader) and on 134 and 135 for an informational author; RI.3.3 "describe the relationship between a series of ... steps in technical procedures ... using language that pertains to time, sequence, and cause/effect" and RI.3.8 "describe the logical connection between particular sentences and paragraphs" on 134; RI.3.2 on 134; RI.3.1 and RI.3.4 on 135; L.3.5a "distinguish the literal and nonliteral meanings of words and phrases in context" on 135 (one phrase); W.3.1a to W.3.1d on 134 (O03) and 136; L.3.1b "form and use regular and irregular plural nouns" and L.3.1c "use abstract nouns" on 136, explicitly taught here because they were not completed in unit 2. RF.3.4a to RF.3.4c are met on 134 in a partner reading of the town text.

Limits that bite: three views at once is the hardest part of 133, so each view gets a color label and one column; no more than three answers are compared in a minute. Plurals are limited to child, woman, mouse, man and regular -s and -es (the written move with -es is in 145). Abstract nouns are limited to care, kindness, hope.

Connections: point of view and narrator (grade 3 u03-1, u03-2; grade 2 u04-2), the opinion model (u03-5, u03-6), title capitals (u03-6), the word check of dictation plus six oral samples (u01-7). The teacher page must explain that "the reader's view" is not the same as "the author's view", and why a character can disagree with a narrator.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


- **J** Wrong: "The writer says we should explain the pond first." Right: "I say pond first. The writer says both jobs." Shown 136. If many: 7 minutes, label each sentence "writer" or "me" with two colours.
- **K** Wrong: "Staff put an eye on the gauge." Right: "Staff watch the gauge carefully." Shown 136. If many: 6 minutes, test both readings against the next sentence.
- **AM** Wrong: "The narrator says there is plenty of water." Right: "Mutheu says it; the narrator tells what she did." Shown 136. If many: 6 minutes, write the speaker's name beside each judgment before comparing.
- **AU** Wrong: "Mr. Kioko says there is plenty of water in the sand." Right: "Mutheu asks it, on page 6." Shown 136. If many: 5 minutes, reread the stating sentence and correct the claim.

## 6. Days

## Day 133


- **10 min. Long words.** Prompts on development, important, community; irregular words; the ending-rule line for -ing.
- **20 min. Skills block, log day.** Four conferences (16) and two log lines read aloud (4).
- **5 min. Opening.** Name the speaker and the writer in two one-line cards (two minutes), then say the three labels aloud.
- **20 min. Read and label.** The teacher reads "New Well, Shared Work" whole aloud (about 8 minutes); the class labels three views in three columns (character, narrator, article writer) using the Kitui account for the writer (12 minutes).
- **20 min. Write.** Four sentences: "The character thinks ...", "The narrator thinks ...", "The writer thinks ..." and "I think ... because ..."; the teacher models the first (3 minutes), children write for 14 and check labels for 3.
- **5 min. Partner talk.** Say your own view to a partner (2 minutes each, 1 to change over).
- **5 min. Word in context.** "viewpoint": its sentence, then the glossary entry.
- **5 min. Exit.** The bucket card (exit 133).

Carried: the label sheet. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: exit 133. Hand-backs in: none. Out: label sheet. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book; tell someone one thing you think about sharing; No score.

## Day 134


- **10 min. Long words.** Irregular words and a prompt on -ous and -ly words from week 27.
- **20 min. Skills block, Wednesday.** Grammar 5 minutes (a pronoun and its noun: "The plant cleans water. It ..."); partner rereading 15 minutes with a feedback card.
- **5 min. Opening.** Retrieve the three labels; one line each.
- **20 min. Read and annotate.** The teacher reads steps 1 to 4 of "How a Town Cleans Its Water" aloud (4 minutes); pairs reread in turn (2 minutes each way, 1 to change over, 5 minutes); the class marks time words (first, next, then) and a cause word (because, so) on a print (11 minutes).
- **15 min. Paragraph links.** A two-column table: "sentence", "joined to ... by ..."; the teacher models one between two sentences (3 minutes); pairs fill two more (12 minutes); main idea in one line.
- **5 min. Partner talk.** Which link word helped most (2 minutes each, 1 to change over).
- **5 min. Word in context.** "therefore" in its sentence; the same job as "so".
- **10 min. Exit (O03).** The pond card: a response to the writer.

Carried: the table. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: exit 134 (O03). Hand-backs in: none. Out: O03 in the folder. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes; No score.

## Day 136


- **10 min. Long words and word repair.** The patterns missed in the 135 word check, taught again, then the week 28 prompts: therefore, since, example.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: "Whose view is this sentence, the character's, the narrator's or the writer's?" (133); "How does this link word join these two sentences?" (134); "What does the author think, and what do you think?" (135, 136). Written task: O03 on 134 (response to the author, opinion, one sitting). Look-fors: the two views are labeled; the reasons use the text; a link word; a closing sentence; no reader view put in the writer's mouth.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: three color cards "character, narrator, writer"; a sentence frame "The narrator thinks ... I think ..."; pictures for "gauge" and "pond". Access: read the story aloud; allow drawing and labelling for the three views. Check accommodations: extra time on P135 (to 50 minutes of items), a scribe. Reading load: one story read whole aloud; the town text read in short parts. Extension: compare the story narrator's view with the account's writer's view in two sentences.

## 10. Two rooms

A parent teaching one child: read the story aloud, then ask "Who says that?" for three lines and "What do you think?" for one; for plurals, say the singular and ask for the plural. One projector: show the three-column sheet; children label on paper; the plural pairs go on the board one at a time.
