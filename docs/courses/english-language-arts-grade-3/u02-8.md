**71A (35 minutes, silent, one sheet).** Printed on the sheet, Panel D (g3-u2-pD, an invented practice panel; its items have needs_text no), Curio Class wording, facts from the museum account named in the book's sources: "The western water-holding frog sheds layers of skin in its burrow. These layers form a cocoon, a covering around the frog. Inside it the frog can remain inactive for months until rain comes.

After summer rains it breeds in temporary water. Females lay large groups of eggs there."
- **Part 1 (W.3.2a to W.3.2d, 20 minutes).** "Write a paragraph about the western water-holding frog. Tell the reader what it is about. Keep the facts about its skin and shelter together and the facts about the rains together. Say what a cocoon is. End with a sentence that fits. You may draw and label the frog in its cocoon." Key: a topic sentence naming the two groups; skin-and-shelter facts apart from the rain-and-eggs facts; a definition (a covering around the frog) in the child's words; a link word such as and, until or after; a labeled sketch if drawn; a closing sentence that returns to the dry-time shelter. Wrong: egg facts mixed into every skin sentence (AA); a claim the panel does not give, such as the covering is made from leaves (A); because joined to an unrelated lunch reason (AB); an ending about an unrelated bird (Q).
- **Part 2 (RI.3.3, RI.3.8, 8 minutes).** (a) "Tell what the frog does with its skin, what it can do inside, and what happens after the summer rains." Key: it sheds layers that form a cocoon; inside it can stay inactive for months until rain; after the rains it breeds in pools. Wrong: breeding causes the summer rain (Y). (b) "How do the first two sentences connect? How does the second paragraph differ from the first?" Key: the layers produce the covering; the first paragraph is about shelter in the dry time and the second about breeding in the wet time. Wrong: both paragraphs described as parts of the body without checking (Z).
- **Part 3 (RI.3.4, L.3.6, 7 minutes).** (a) "What does burrow mean in Panel D?" and "What does inactive mean? Use the sentence to explain." Key: burrow is the place where the frog stays, its shelter, a hole in the ground; inactive means not moving or not active for months until rain. Wrong: inactive means very busy, ignoring the sentence clue (D). (b) "Write a sentence about the frog with cocoon, the word explain or detail, and a time or place phrase." Key: for example "The details explain how the cocoon covers the frog in its burrow until the rains return." Wrong: cocoon used to mean rain (D).

- **E69.** Card shown only in your one-minute turn at the device. Strip: "Leaf frogs have large eyes. Their skin is smooth and green." (b) Give one detail that shows your answer. Answer: The excerpt tells about glass frogs, not about every frog. Wrong: accepts a claim the source does not make (A); a bare answer with no detail (AO).
- **E71 (s02-X71, s02-X71bv2).** Strip: "While the forest frog rests among fallen leaves, brown patches on its back make its skin mottled. Its colors help it blend in." ## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview related, introduce, evidence, cocoon and conclusion with the book's drawings; frames "This page explains how ___ and ___." and "You said ___; which words show it?"; say the sentence aloud before typing or writing; a partner who shares a language.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of each drawing; a scribe for the plan and conclusion, not for the typing turn; oral rehearsal before the discussion; a printed copy of Panel D.

- **Extension, same standard.** Add a fourth paragraph that compares what the book and the excerpt each give and link it to the conclusion.
