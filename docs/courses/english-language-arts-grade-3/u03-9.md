# Section 3.9: Presenting the scene and ending the unit

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.5, RL.3.6, RL.3.9, RI.3.6** ("refer to parts ... chapter, scene, and stanza"; "distinguish their own point of view" from the narrator, characters or author; "compare and contrast the themes, settings, and plots" of stories by one author): not taught here; sampled on 114 (items run here, with RI.3.1 judged inside the RI.3.6 task).
- **W.3.1a, b, c, d** (opinion, reasons, links, closing): the 106 review record is reused on 115 with no new item and no new score. **L.3.2a** (capitals in titles), **L.3.1a** and **L.3.1f** (function of the five word classes; both agreements): new evidence on 115 from the distinct G112 record (items run here).
- **SL.3.4, SL.3.6, L.3.3b** (report with facts, detail and pace; speak in complete sentences when asked for detail; spoken and written conventions differ): rehearsed on 116 (N10 retelling), reported and scored on 119 (items run here).
- **RF.3.4a, b, c and RF.3.3c** ("read ... with purpose and understanding"; "accuracy, appropriate rate, and expression on successive readings"; "use context to confirm or self-correct"; "decode multisyllable words"): oral rotation on 117 and 118 (items run here), each child sampled once.
- **W.3.3a, b, c, d, W.3.10, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f:** the 116 retelling and the skills block; practised, no item.

Limits that bite: the day budgets are tight. On 114 the four items take 35 minutes, the exit 8 and the cards 17. On 115 the feedback takes 5, the G112 audit 15, the cards 32 and the exit 8. On 117 and 118 fifteen children each get exactly four minutes at the teacher's table (60 minutes), and the rest of the class works alone. On 119 six groups of five get seven minutes each (42), so each child has about 84 seconds: a 60-second report, one clarification and one convention. Nothing on these days is taught as new. Reading load: 114 asks for about 200 words of new reading across four panels; 117 and 118 read an 80-word passage twice. The poem records (Pond Voices) were taken between 107 and 112, five children a day.

The teacher page must explain: the 2/1/0 scale; that the 106 review record is reused and not rescored; that G112 is a distinct record from G110; that each child is heard once on 117 or 118; that same-day feedback and the reflection on 119 are never scored; and that the oral keys are accepted with dialect variants.

## 2. Where it sits


- Before: section 3.8, ending day 113 (the scene folder and audit card). After: the end of unit 3; day 120 opens the water book and the clean freshwater question, with no hand-off needed from this section.
- No readiness check. Results go to the scene folder, the oral records for 117 and 118, the report record for 119 and the cards; the unit's records are filed for the end-of-unit read.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Letters this section meets; if many made it, 5 to 10 minutes of repair (taken in the 119 response or the next unit).
- **A, an invented answer.** Wrong: "It makes every book sell out." Right: "The author says it lets readers notice smaller changes." Shown 114. Repair: underline a supporting phrase before answering; 5 minutes.
- **B, reversed order.** Wrong: the later chapter told first. Right: number the earlier part first. Shown 114. Repair: number the earlier part; 5 minutes.
- **E, one view for another.** Wrong: the narrator given Ben's view. Right: label whose words show each judgment. Shown 114 and 115. Repair: label each view; 5 minutes.
- **F, similarities only.** Wrong: both are school stories. Right: both are school stories, but one is indoors and one outdoors. Shown 114. Repair: add a contrast in each of theme, setting and plot; 7 minutes.
- **R and AM, title capitals.** Wrong: a place for everyone; A Place For Everyone. Right: A Place for Everyone. Shown 115. Repair: compare with the title model; 5 minutes.
- **X, Y and Z, functions and agreements.** Wrong: "quietly is an adverb"; partners chooses; partners choose his seats. Right: quietly tells how; partners choose; partners choose their seats. Shown 115. Repair: say what the word does, then match the subject and referent; 7 minutes.
- **AA, AB, AC, AD, oral reading.** Wrong: guessing invitation from the first letter; rushing past a pause; not explaining the event; accepting a word that clashes with the sentence. Right: chunks blended, a pause at the boundary, the event explained, the sentence reread. Shown in written feedback after each turn (117, 118). Repair: in the 119 independent apply time; 5 minutes.
- **AE, a direction spoken.** Wrong: reading "Ada pauses" aloud as a line. Right: a pause and no words. Shown 116, 117, 118 and 119. Repair: bracket the direction before the line is read; 5 minutes.
- **AF, a fragment.** Wrong: "Because page." Right: "Ada unfolds the page so Ben can check it." Shown 116 and 119. Repair: rehearse with a subject and a verb; 5 minutes.
- **AN, rushing.** Wrong: the report in one breath. Right: a pause between facts. Shown 116 and 119. Repair: mark the pause points, say it once for the listener; 5 minutes.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Children carry the scene folder and the cards (structure on 114, key points on 115), the G112 sheet with its 114 note and 115 revision, and the 116 retelling (N10) with the teacher's written note. The oral records for 117 and 118, the Pond Voices record (107 to 112) and the 119 report record stay with the teacher. The 117 or 118 revised reading is kept with the written decoding feedback.
- **Key moment 1, days 117 and 118: the oral turn.** Ideal instrument: a shared local recorder so a first and second reading can be compared. Spec: partly fits; a live teacher record on paper meets every goal, and the recorder is optional.
- **Key moment 2, day 119: the report window.** Ideal instrument: the same recorder, in a group. Spec: partly fits; the paper record of facts, detail, pace, complete sentences and the applied difference is enough.
- **A room with nothing.** Pencil, paper, printed script and books, the printed F117 and F118 forms, the cards and a clock.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: day 114, how does the later part build on the earlier part, in a chapter, a scene and a stanza? Day 116, what does a listener need from your report? Day 119, what does the listener need clarified? Written task: the N10 retelling on 116 (one sitting), and the report on 119 (spoken). Judged by the teacher with look-fors: facts that are accurate, a descriptive detail, a pace a listener can follow, complete sentences when asked, and a pause where the script marks one without reading the direction aloud.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview report, pace, pause and clarify with a gesture and a picture; frames "First I changed ___", "This matters because ___" and "I pause here because the script says ___". Access: audio beside print for the script and Pond Voices; large print; the cards may be drawn and spoken; a child who stammers or needs more time may give the report to the teacher alone and record the pace as a note. Check accommodations: items read aloud (not the oral passages' decoding), extra time inside the 90 minutes, answers by mouth, a scribe; support is recorded. Reading load: the printed passages are 80 words (F117, F118) and about 200 words across the 114 panels, with the books and maps open. Extension, same standard: report a second revised action and compare its effect with the first.

## 10. Two rooms

Teaching one child: the parent is the listener on 116 and 119. On 116 the child rehearses the report and the parent says one fact heard. On 117 and 118 the parent hears the child read an 80-word passage twice and writes one sentence about how the second reading differed. On 119, the last day of the unit, the parent hears the report from the cards, asks one clarification question and asks the child to say what a stage direction does when a script is read aloud; day 120 begins a new book. One projector: show the card layout, the pace and pause marks, and the printed F117 and F118 forms while the child reads aloud; the items, the exits and the cards stay on paper.
