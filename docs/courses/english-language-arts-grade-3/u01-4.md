# Section 1.4: Book carriers: information, main idea, photographs and maps, and a first informative paragraph

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.3.1 (days 14, 15, 17, 18, 19):** "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers." The first informational use: the answer is found under a heading or in a caption and pointed to.
- **RI.3.2 (days 15, 17, 18, 19):** "Determine the main idea of a text; recount the key details and explain how they support the main idea." The main-idea chart starts on day 15 and runs through the unit.
- **RI.3.7 (days 15, 18, 19):** "Use information gained from illustrations (e.g., maps, photographs) and the words in a text to demonstrate understanding of the text (e.g., where, when, why, and how key events occur)." Title, key and caption only; no map skill beyond that.
- **W.3.2a to d, W.3.4 (day 16):** "Introduce a topic and group related information together"; "Develop the topic with facts, definitions, and details"; "Use linking words and phrases"; "Provide a concluding statement or section." A first paragraph is written without a frame, to show what needs modeling on day 20.
- **SL.3.1a to d, SL.3.3, SL.3.2 (day 17):** the discussion moves again, and SL.3.2 for the first time: "Determine the main ideas and supporting details of a text read aloud or information presented in diverse media and formats." Expert groups of three; each listener states the main idea and two details.
- **L.3.4a to d (days 18, 19):** sentence clue, known affix, known root and glossary or dictionary, now on informational words. Choosing among them comes on day 27.
- **RF.3.3a, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 14 to 18):** affix meanings and the week 4 patterns and words in the sequence; day 15 holds the word check and the grade 2 retest.
- **RF.3.4a to c (days 14, 17), W.3.10:** partner rereading with a feedback card, no speed target; routine writing.
- **Limits that bite here.** No map skill beyond title and key; bar graphs with a labeled scale of one are retrieved from grade 2 mathematics on day 17 and not taught. No child is asked about their own library use. The packhorse text names the hard time plainly in one sentence, without war or violence. Real places are named; the photograph is credited to the archive.
- **Connections.** Main-idea charts run on from day 15 to day 38; SL.3.1 returns on days 25, 36 and 39; RI.3.7 returns on day 38.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How an article differs from a story; how a caption, map and photograph each give only what they say (never more); how to run expert groups of three in a ten-minute window; how the first paragraph is read for needs, not scored.

**Exit questions (E14 to E19).** One ask each. E14, E15 and E18 sit on sections of the two articles the day does not model; E16, E17 and E19 on cases printed "invented for practice". Unscored. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's opening repairs that letter.
- **E14 (day 14, s01-X14v2).** Books on the Move (g3-u1-05), the section By Bus and by Mail in New Mexico (the model uses By Road in England). Ask: Explain how you know this section is information, not a story. Answer: it gives facts, three bookmobiles and books by mail in canvas bags, and no character faces a problem. Wrong: "It is information because the books come in boxes" (A); "It is about mail" (B).
- **E15 (day 15, s01-X15v2).** Books on Horseback (g3-u1-06), the section The Library Grew and the Kentucky map (the model uses How It Worked). Ask: Write the main idea, using the words and the map. Answer: the pack horse libraries grew to 30 and reached country readers in Kentucky, the state the map shows. Wrong: "It is about horses" (B); "The map shows where all 30 libraries were" (V).
- **E16 (day 16).** Strip: "Reed Lake book boat: It ties up each Saturday. Readers return finished books at the dock. The dock was painted green last year. The boat then lends the returned books to new readers." Ask: "Write a topic sentence for a paragraph about how the boat shares books and copy the two facts that belong with it." Answer: a topic such as the boat lets finished books go to new readers; facts: readers return books at the dock, the boat lends them again. Wrong: includes the paint (H); a topic that ends on a new topic such as paint (J).
- **E17 (day 17).** Spoken version, read aloud once and also printed: "The Reed Lake book boat has two jobs. It lends books at the dock, and it takes finished books back. A sign by the dock points to the return box." Ask: "Tell your partner the main idea with one detail you marked and ask one question about your partner's detail; then write one sentence saying what your partner's answer showed you." Answer: main idea is the two jobs, lending and taking back; a detail such as the sign or the return box; the sentence names what the partner added. Wrong: no detail (Q); "I still think the same" (T); an answer of "a thing" (U).
- **E18 (day 18, s01-X18v2).** Books on the Move, By Road in England, with the world map (the model uses By Boat in Bangladesh). Ask: Write the main idea of the section and its map. Answer: a mobile library brings books to villages in Lincolnshire on planned stops, and the map shows where Lincolnshire is. Wrong: "The map proves the bus comes every day" (V); "It is about a bus" (B).
- **E19 (day 19, replaces review five minutes).** Sidebar, invented for practice: "A caption is a short explanation below a picture. A useful caption names the place. Copying means making a copy." Ask: "State the sidebar's main idea with two details, then explain useful using its -ful ending." Answer: captions explain pictures; a good one names the place; useful means full of use. Wrong: "It is about copying" (B); useful means without use (AC). The sidebar and useful are reserved from teaching.

**Day 19 items (35 minutes).** The s01.md items: RI.3.1 (where the book wagon traveled; wrong: a train ride across the country, A), RI.3.2 (main idea and two details; wrong: "It is about horses", B), RI.3.7 (picture, map and panel; wrong: the picture was made today, V), L.3.4a (isolated from its clue; wrong: crowded together, F), L.3.4b (un- in unpacked; wrong: packed again, AC), L.3.4c (dust for dusty; wrong: a clean place, AC) and L.3.4d (janitor from the glossary; wrong: a person who drives horses, guessed, G).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The teacher holds the main-idea chart on day 15; partners extend it on day 15 and hold it on day 18; children read the reserved Maryland book wagon panel independently on day 19. Prepared notes are the children's on day 17. The reading log continues. All return in section 1.5 (paragraph planning) and on day 38.
- **Key moment 1, day 15: photograph, caption and map with the text.** Ideal instrument: the three displays on one printed page beside the article. Spec: fits; print is enough. Gaps and asks: none (no tool; see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 17: a bar graph with labels.** Ideal instrument: a printed graph with a labeled scale of one. Spec: fits; print.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper, one wall chart and a pencil; the map can be hand drawn on a board.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview carrier, route, stop, remote, borrow, return, caption and map before reading; frame "The words ___ explain ___"; an oral or drawn first answer; a partner who shares a language where possible.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of the photograph, caption and map for a child who cannot see them, never the answer; a scribe for explanations, not for spelling evidence.
- **Check accommodations.** Items on day 19 may be read aloud except where reading or decoding is the evidence; record any help. A child may speak or point on day 17, and the support is recorded.
- **Reading load.** Alone: one printed strip under 40 words for each exit and the panel on day 19. Read aloud: both articles.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different main idea of the article with two details.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the expert partner: read a section aloud, say its main idea and two details, ask one question, and say what became clearer. Record by hand.
- **One projector.** Show the articles, photograph and map; keep the exit strips, the panel and the graph on paper until needed.
