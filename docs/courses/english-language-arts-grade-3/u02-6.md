# Section 2.6: Research notes, the glass frog and the first research check

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.3.5, W.3.7, W.3.8 (days 57, 60, 61):** "Use text features and search tools ... to locate information relevant to a given topic efficiently"; "conduct short research projects"; "take brief notes on sources and sort evidence into provided categories". Taught on day 57 with the research question, a note of under eight words, a category (body, habitat or survival) and a source label with the page; checked on day 60; repaired on day 61.
- **RI.3.4, L.3.6 (days 57 to 61):** meanings of relevant, category, transparent, dorsal, speckling and habitat from their own sentences, and place and time phrases (beneath, beside, by day). Taught on 57 and 58, applied on 59, checked on 60.
- **RI.3.1, RI.3.3, RI.3.8 (days 58 to 60):** a question the page answers, with the words pointed to; the cause and result between sentences; the job of each paragraph (body, then habitat). Taught on 58, applied on 59, checked on 60.
- **W.3.2a to W.3.2d, L.3.1d, L.3.1e, L.3.1g (day 59):** a topic, two facts with a definition, linking words and a closing sentence (Product I06, one sitting). Verb and comparison forms are practiced only inside it.
- **L.3.1b, L.3.1c (days 57, 58, 59, 61):** regular plurals, plurals that change (leaf, leaves), and an abstract noun as an idea you cannot touch. First instruction on 57; the day 59 noun sheet is the portfolio piece for 75B (feedback 61, revision 62, score 75).
- **RF.3.4a to RF.3.4c (days 57, 59), RF.3.3b, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 57, 58, 59, 61), W.3.10:** partner rereading on 57 and 59 with the feedback card; week 12 contrasts -ment (act or result) with -ous (full of) on related bases; irregular words eyes, many and some are mapped from the book. Day 60 is the word check.
- **Limits that bite here.** The notes are brief and never copied: under eight words. The unit's reserved words for later checks stay untaught: prey, temporary, cocoon, permanent, vulnerable and undisturbed. The excerpt's Habitat paragraph is read aloud on day 61 without defining those words. The portfolio words mice, bravery, reed and teeth are not used as examples. No sentence on feeding is read aloud beyond the book's plain lines.
- **Connections.** The research cycle and note slip come from grade 2 and the category sort from grade 1. Habitat comes from section 2.4, time and cause links and the page-lookup card from 2.5. Day 61 hands to 2.7: it opens on day 62 with the frog book as the second source and a first look at what each source adds.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to keep a note under eight words and still in the child's own words; how a category is decided by its definition, not by the word it contains; how the one device turn on day 60 is taken inside the check.

**Items owned.** The eight day 60 rows above: 60A (RI.3.1 30 minutes, then RI.3.5, W.3.7 and W.3.8 inside it) and 60B (RI.3.3 20 minutes, then RI.3.8, RI.3.4 and L.3.6 inside it). **60A (30 minutes, silent, one sheet).** Texts: Panel B, the printed excerpt (g3-u2-07) with its headings Description and Habitat, and the local keyword page with links to just those pages. Directions printed: "Our research question: How do a glass frog's body and home go together? Choose only relevant facts: facts that help answer the question."
- **Part 1 (RI.3.1, W.3.7).** "Write one question about the glass frog that Panel B or the excerpt can answer. Write the answer. Copy the 3 to 6 words that show where you found it." Key: any question the sources answer, for example "Where do glass frogs live?" with "forests with running streams" or "forests that contain running water such as streams and waterfalls". Wrong: an answer the sources do not give, such as deserts (A); a list of unrelated frog facts with no question (W).
- **Part 2 (W.3.8).** "Make three brief notes, one under each heading: body, habitat, survival. Under eight words each. Write the source and what you used to find it." Key: body, transparent skin on the belly (Panel B or Description); habitat, forests with running streams (Panel B) or streams and waterfalls (excerpt Habitat); survival, blends into leaves by day (Panel B). Any true source-labeled fact in the right heading counts. Wrong: forest location under body (X); a copied sentence; no source label (W).
- **Part 3 (RI.3.5, at the one device, 1 minute each).** Children go to the station in ten groups of three, 3 minutes a group, 30 minutes in all while the rest write; this uses the whole 30. At the station the child (1) points to the Habitat heading in the printed excerpt, then (2) types glass frog in the keyword box and follows the Habitat link, and says what they found. Key: both routes, print then keyword and link. Wrong: opens pages at random or types a whole question (V).

**60B (20 minutes, silent, one sheet).** Frog book pages 15 and 16 (Frogs and Toads) for s02-A43v2 to A45v2, page 12 for A46v2, the excerpt's Habitat paragraph for A47v2. Panel B stays for the W and L items.
- **Part 1 (RI.3.3).** "Say what the tadpole eats first and what it eats later, and what eating the algae does." Key: algae first, small animals later; eating algae gives food so it can grow (cause, then result). Wrong: the algae grow because the tadpole eats them (Y).
- **Part 2 (RI.3.8).** "What does the word This point back to in the third sentence? Then say how the second paragraph of Panel B differs from the first." Key: This points back to the tadpole eating the algae; the first paragraph is about the body, the second moves to where the frog lives. Wrong: both are in time order with no check (Z).
- **Part 3 (RI.3.4, L.3.6).** "In the directions and Panel B, what do relevant and habitat mean? Write a sentence about a glass frog that uses habitat and beside, beneath or by day." Key: relevant means helps answer the question; habitat means the place where it lives; for example "The frog's habitat is a forest, and it rests beneath the leaves beside a stream." Wrong: habitat means skin color or a kind of frog (D).

**Exit questions (E57 to E61).** One ask each, on a new case. Unscored; shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Strips are invented for practice; their frog facts are generic and the source is to check against the national health frog page before print.
- **E57 (s02-X57v2, s02-X57b).** (a) Frog book page 8, the Eyes paragraph, read aloud twice: say which words belong under habitat. Answer: lives mostly in water. (b) On the invented Tullo frog strip ("It lives in tall grass at a pond's edge"): write that fact as a brief note. End it with the label Tullo frog book, p. 21. Answer: tall grass at a pond's edge; Tullo frog book, p. 21. Wrong: a body fact named as the home (X); a survival fact named as the home (X); a note with no source label (A); a body fact written as the home (X).
- **E58 (s02-X58v2, s02-X58bv2).** Frog book page 11. Asks, one each: (a) "Its green color blends into the leaves around it. This makes it hard to see." Say what blends into means. Answer: Looks so much like the leaves that it is hard to see. (b) The bones paragraph: why do scientists look at the bones? Answer: To tell one kind of glass frog from another. Wrong: the meaning does not use the sentence clue (D); the meaning contradicts the clue (D).
- **E59 (s02-X59v2).** Frog book page 12, paragraph 1, reread. Ask: Write how the leaf helps the eggs. Use because. Answer: The leaf helps because it holds the eggs above the water and its tip keeps water running over them. Wrong: a fact the page does not give (A); cause and result reversed (Y).
- **E60.** Strip, "river frog book, page 9, heading Home": "River frogs lay their eggs in slow, shallow water at the river's edge. The tadpoles hide among the stones." Asks, one each: (a) Write one question about river frogs that this strip answers. Answer: Where do river frogs lay their eggs?. (b) Write a brief note that answers it. Give its category and the label river frog book, p. 9. Answer: slow, shallow water at the river's edge; habitat; river frog book, p. 9. Wrong: no focused question (W); the strip does not answer it (W); a home fact under body (X); a detail the strip does not give (A).
- **E61 (s02-X61v2, s02-X61b).** (a) Request: how toad eggs look. Show the frog book's contents; say which section you open first. Answer: Frogs and Toads, page 15. (b) On the invented wildlife page (headings Description, Habitat, Food): write one brief note on where a chorus frog lays its eggs. Give its category and the label wildlife page, Habitat. Answer: cool, shallow pools in the woods; habitat; wildlife page, Habitat. Wrong: the search ignores what the heading tells (V); a place the strip does not give (A); a home fact under body (X).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview habitat, transparent, category, brief and note with the book's drawings; frames "The words ___ explain ___." and "This note goes under ___ because ___."; say the note aloud before writing; a partner who shares a language.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of each drawing; a scribe for the notes and answers, not for the spelling and noun work; extra time on 60 by the same rule for all.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 60 sheets may be read aloud except where decoding is the evidence (record help); the device turn can be taken with a partner reading the keyword aloud; record help.
- **Reading load.** Alone: one page or 150 to 250 words. The glass-frog section is read aloud first, then reread in chunks.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second linked explanation that joins the trait to a second need of the frog and say how it differs from the first.
