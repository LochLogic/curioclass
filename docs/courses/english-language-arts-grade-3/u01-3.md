# Section 1.3: The grandmother story and a credited tale: linked events, figurative language and the message

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.1 (days 10 to 13):** "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers." Each answer points to a sentence. Finishes here for the literary stories; RI.3.1 starts on day 14.
- **RL.3.2 (days 10, 12, 13):** "Recount stories, including fables, folktales, and myths from diverse cultures; determine the central message, lesson, or moral and explain how it is conveyed through key details in the text." The recount comes from linked events; the message is argued from two details.
- **RL.3.3 (days 10, 13):** "Describe characters in a story (e.g., their traits, motivations, or feelings) and explain how their actions contribute to the sequence of events." Day 13 asks for one motivation and one trait.
- **RL.3.4, L.3.5a (days 11, 12, 13):** "distinguishing literal from nonliteral language." Day 11 is taught on the grandmother story; day 12 meets it again inside a credited tale; day 13 samples it.
- **L.3.4a to d, RF.3.3a (day 11):** sentence clue, a known affix (un-, re-, dis- from grade 2, then -less and -ful), a known root (help, helpful, helpless) and a dictionary look-up. Day 11 is the second application of the care and help families; choosing among strategies comes on day 27.
- **RL.3.7 (days 11, 13):** "Explain how specific aspects of a text's illustrations contribute to what is conveyed by the words in a story." Lamplight and the cloud are read on day 11; day 13 samples it on a new picture.
- **RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 10 to 12), RF.3.4a to c (day 12), W.3.4 and W.3.10 (day 12):** the week 3 pattern and words, partner rereading with a feedback card, and one supported written explanation.
- **Limits that bite here.** The tales are credited to the named storyteller and edition, with no accents, costumes or generic "folk" voice. The grandmother learns to read as an adult, and she is shown with dignity. No child is asked about family reading.
- **Connections.** Story maps carry on from 1.2 and return on day 38; the context and figurative chart runs on to day 38.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to recount with linked events (event, arrow, result); how a fable's message is argued from two details, not recited; how the day 13 items are given without help; why the Crow tale is kept back from all earlier work.

**Exit questions (E10 to E13).** One ask each, on Reading Beside Grandmother (g3-u1-03) or The Lion and the Mouse, never the page the day models. Unscored. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's opening repairs that letter.
- **E10 (day 10, s01-X10v2).** Page 7 of the grandmother story (the model is pages 1 to 3). Ask: Tell what happens on page 7, from first to last. Answer: the wind still rattles the window; Ama carries the lamp and Grandmother the book; tomorrow they will read another page. Wrong: "They climb another page, then they carry the lamp" (C); "Ama likes the lamp" (B).
- **E11 (day 11, s01-X11v2).** Page 4 of the grandmother story, words and picture (the model is page 3). Ask: What does the picture add to the words She reads the lines again, slowly? Answer: Ama's scrunched eyebrows and her pencil on the lines show she is working hard to check the words. Wrong: "Ama is cross with Grandmother" (V); "Ama is in the picture with a pencil" (D).
- **E12 (day 12, s01-X12v2).** The Mouse's last speech in The Lion and the Mouse ("You laughed when I said I would repay you"), held back until the exit. Ask: Explain the message of the tale, using what happened and the Mouse's last words. Answer: the Lion let the Mouse go and later she freed him; a small friend can repay a kindness. Wrong: "It is about a lion and a mouse" (B); "The Lion was free, so the Mouse gnawed the rope" (C).
- **E13 (day 13, replaces review five minutes, s01-X13v2).** Page 5 of the grandmother story, reread on day 13. Ask: What does Grandmother's answer about the mountain show? Answer: hard work gets done one step at a time when people help each other. Wrong: "It is about a mountain" (B); "They will climb the real mountain tomorrow" (E).

**Day 13 items (31 minutes).** Not a set of scored rows here: the rows are the s01.md items for RL.3.1 (why the crow could not drink; wrong: "it was full of sand", A), RL.3.2 (recount and message; wrong: "It is about birds", B), RL.3.3 (motivation and trait; wrong: reversed cause, C), RL.3.4 (help us out; wrong: "walk out of the jug", E), L.3.5a (in a pinch; wrong: "someone squeezes the crow", E) and RL.3.7 (size contrast; wrong: "there is a crow", D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Partners carry the story map on day 10; children extend the context and figurative chart on day 11 and hold their own maps on day 12, transferring the map to the tale. The reading log continues. All return in 1.4 and on day 38.
- **Key moment 1, day 11: a picture beside its words.** Ideal instrument: the picture and the line on one printed page. Spec: fits; print is enough. Gaps and asks: none (no tool; see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 13: the Crow tale with its illustration side by side.** Ideal instrument: print. Spec: fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper, a wall chart and a printed illustration.

## 6. Days

## Day 10


- **10 min. Opening.** Five minutes repair the commonest letters of E8 and E9, and return the day 8 response with its feedback; five minutes recalling event order: put four cards from the bus story in order and say the arrow words (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Four minutes: week 3 words mapped; sixteen minutes: four reading conferences of 4 minutes (the four-minute reading conference routine) while the rest read from their logs. The four children heard go on the heard-six column.
- **20 min. Model.** Read the grandmother story whole (426 words, about 4 minutes) with picture pauses. Teacher builds the recount for pages 1 to 3 with events and arrows (16 minutes): the wind, supper reading, the knotted word, cutting it into pieces. Content note: Grandmother is learning to read as an adult; she is shown with dignity.
- **25 min. Hand work.** Partners finish the recount for pages 4 to 6 on their own map (15 minutes): the skipped line, the tired eyes, the bookmark; then write one sentence joining two events with "so" (10 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes recounting the events in order with a detail (4 minutes); ask for detail, 3 minutes; three volunteers share, 1 minute each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E10.

Carried: partners hold the story map. Tool: None. By hand: recount map, "so" sentence, exit. Evidence: E10. Hand-backs in: E8, E9 and the day 8 response. Out: E10 to day 11's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read pages 1 to 3 to an adult and say the events in order; then read any book together. No questions about your own home or reading.

## Day 11


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E10's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieval of affix and base: un-, re-, dis- on four base words, on slates (unscored; RF.3.3a).
- **20 min. Skills.** Four minutes: week 3 words; sixteen minutes: four reading conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs. The four children heard go on the heard-six column.
- **20 min. Model.** Reread pages 1, 3 and 5 aloud. Teacher models the chart on the mountain wears a white tablecloth and a knot of letters (8 minutes); then the help family: help, helpful, helpless (5 minutes, second known-root application); then the lamplight and the cloud as picture choices (4 minutes) and one dictionary entry (3 minutes).
- **25 min. Hand work.** Pairs add three phrases to the chart: her laugh is as wide as the sky, a mountain is climbed one step at a time and like a little flag, each with the intended meaning and a clue (12 minutes); they use the printed dictionary page for one word (4 minutes); then compare the page 3 picture with its words (9 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on one phrase and what the picture adds (4 minutes); ask for detail, 3 minutes; three volunteers share, 1 minute each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E11.

Carried: children hold the context and figurative chart. Tool: None. By hand: chart additions, dictionary look-up, exit. Evidence: E11. Hand-backs in: E10. Out: E11 to day 12's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread two pages aloud and find one phrase that is not meant exactly. No score.

## Day 12


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E11's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieving grade 2 fables and folktales: name two tale features and say where stories can come from, in two places (unscored; e2-fables-folktales, ss2-culture-migration).
- **20 min. Skills.** Ten minutes: partner rereading of a short passage with the feedback card; the teacher listens to two pairs, 3 minutes each, and records who was heard. Ten minutes: grammar retrieval, one-owner possessives on four phrases.
- **40 min. Hand work.** The teacher reads The Lion and the Mouse aloud, all but the Mouse's last speech, held for E12 (about 2 minutes), crediting Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece, and the 1919 edition. Pairs recount the beginning, middle and end on their own map (13 minutes); each child then writes three or four sentences on the message with two details (25 minutes), using the frame "The words ___ show ___".
- **15 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 3 minutes on the message with a detail (6 minutes); ask for detail and link, 4 minutes; five volunteers share, 1 minute each (5 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** The teacher reads the Mouse's last speech aloud (1 minute); E12 is written in 4 minutes.

Carried: children hold their maps. Tool: None. By hand: map, message paragraph, exit. Evidence: E12. Hand-backs in: E11. Out: E12 and the message paragraph, returned with feedback, to day 14's opening (day 13 is a Check day with no repair slot). At home (30 to 40 minutes): tell an adult a short tale you know and what it teaches. No score.

## Day 13


- **30 min. Skills, silent.** Ten minutes retrieval on slates, silent: the week 3 words and pattern; ten minutes of partner rereading of a favorite page; ten minutes of reading log work. No teaching.
- **12 min. Reading.** Each child reads The Crow and the Pitcher twice with its new illustration (the tale is about 165 words), silently, and may mark one phrase.
- **31 min. Items.** Six items, alone, in writing: RL.3.1, 6 minutes; RL.3.2, 8; RL.3.3, 5; RL.3.4, 4; L.3.5a, 4; RL.3.7, 4. The teacher records support given and teaches nothing.
- **7 min. Silent review.** Children check their answers silently against the page.
- **5 min. Collection and transition.**
- **5 min. Exit.** E13.

Carried: the check sheet is the teacher's. Tool: None. By hand: six items and exit. Evidence: the six rows of field 3 and E13. Hand-backs in: none (a Check day has no repair slot). Out: E13 and the item results go to day 14's opening and are repaired in the next teaching lessons (never during the check). At home (30 to 40 minutes): read for 20 minutes and tell an adult the title of your book.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 10: "What happened first, next and last, and what linked them?" Day 11: "What does the writer mean, and what does the picture add?" Day 12: "What do the events show about the Mouse and the Lion?"
- **Written task, day 12.** Three or four sentences on the message of the tale, with two details as proof.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors:** a message, two events as proof, a link word, a closing line. Recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 10:** event order. **Day 11:** un-, re- and dis- with a base. **Day 12:** grade 2 fables and folktales, and how stories travel between places; possessives. **Day 13:** the week 3 words and pattern, silent retrieval only, no teaching. The care and help families return on days 11 and 27.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on successive readings, no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview Cape Town, mountain, lamp, net, rope and puddle; frame "The words ___ show ___"; an oral or drawn first answer; a partner who shares a language where possible.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of each picture for a child who cannot see it, not an explanation of the answer; a scribe for explanations, not for spelling evidence.
- **Check accommodations.** Items on day 13 may be read aloud except where reading or decoding is the evidence; record any help. The tale is read silently, aloud, or by a peer, and the support is recorded.
- **Reading load.** Alone: one strip of under 40 words for each exit and the Crow tale on day 13. Read aloud: the grandmother story and the Lion tale.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different detail from the same tale for the same message.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the partner role: read a page aloud, ask one question, listen, say what became clearer. Record by hand. The day 13 items go on paper with no hints.
- **One projector.** Show the story pages and the tale; keep exit strips and the Crow tale on paper until the check.
