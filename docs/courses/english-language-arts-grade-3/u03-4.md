## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.9** ("compare and contrast the themes, settings, and plots of stories written by the same author about the same or similar characters"): taught on 94 to 97 across the two books. Theme, setting and plot each get a similarity and a difference. Assessed on 99 and 114.
- **RL.3.5** ("refer to parts of stories ... describe how each successive part builds on earlier sections"): practiced on 94, 96 and 97 with book 2's chapters.
- **RL.3.2** ("recount stories ... determine the central message, lesson, or moral and explain how it is conveyed through key details"): taught 95; the retained 97 recount and message is revised on 99 (owned item row, sampled there).
- **W.3.3a, b, c, d** ("establish a situation ... narrator and characters"; "dialogue and descriptions of actions, thoughts, and feelings"; "temporal words"; "a sense of closure"): taught on 96 through N08; N04 on 95 is practice.
- **RF.3.3c, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f, W.3.10** (decode multisyllable words; irregular words; suffix spelling; patterns; routine writing): weeks 19 and 20.
- **RF.3.4a, b, c** (purpose and understanding; accuracy and expression on successive readings; context to self-correct): the 94 and 97 partner rereadings.

Limits that bite: three evenings of short written notes (N04 on 95, N08 on 96, the recounts on 97) are real teacher time; one note each, no more. The pairs' rereads on 97 and 91 stay off the passages the reserved panels copy. Day 96's narrative runs 15 minutes: 10 from the writing block and 5 from the exit slot, with no extra exit. The teacher page must explain the two-book map (three rows by two books) and the difference between a message and an event.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Letters this section meets; if many made it, 5 to 10 minutes of repair.
- **F, similarities only.** Wrong: Ben checks the plan in both books. Right: in both he checks it, but in book 1 he finds the door problem and in book 2 no mistake. Shown 94 and 97. Repair: a two-column alike and different chart; 7 minutes.
- **G, event as theme.** Wrong: the message is that Lena adds a verse. Right: asking people what they want helps. Shown 95 and 97. Repair: sort statements into what happened and what it teaches; 7 minutes.
- **A, invented evidence.** Wrong: Lena won a prize. Right: she added a quiet verse. Shown 94 and 95. Repair: finger on the page; 5 minutes.
- **S, no situation.** Wrong: an episode that opens with the dialogue. Right: Zoe arrived at the courtyard with no card. Shown 96. Repair: add one opening sentence naming who, where and the problem; 5 minutes.
- **U, reversed time word.** Wrong: Finally, Ada asked Ben. First, she checked the list. Right: First, she checked the list. Finally, she asked Ben. Shown 96. Repair: number the events, then choose the words; 5 minutes.
- **V, unrelated problem at the end.** Wrong: the book swap is saved, then a storm comes. Right: the closing settles the swap problem. Shown 96. Repair: reread the first paragraph and end on it; 7 minutes.
- **AI, wrong syllable division.** Wrong: be-ginn-ing. Right: be-gin-ning. Shown 97. Repair: split aloud, check each chunk, blend; 5 minutes.

## 6. Days

## Day 94


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper: read and spell isolated words (3 minutes), read and spell a sentence from today's chapter (3), decode one word in its paragraph and say what it means (4). Three-syllable words, split, check each chunk, then blend: dif-fer-ence, in-ter-est, fa-vor-ite. Irregular: move, great. One line of ending-rule retrieval (drop e, double the consonant, change y).
- **20 min. Rereading and grammar.** Partner-rereading day. Five minutes of grammar retrieval on slates: name the subject and the verb in a sentence from book 2 chapter 1, and check that they agree. Then 15 minutes of partner rereading with the feedback card; the teacher hears three pairs at 3 minutes each (6 children, 9 minutes) and floats for the rest.
- **5 min. Opening repair and retrieve.** Repair from E93 (2 minutes: the alike-only answer beside one with a difference). Retrieve setting from grade 2 and the story map with message box from unit 1: pairs name one setting detail from book 1 (3 minutes).
- **20 min. Reading and reread.** Teacher reads book 2 chapter 2 aloud (about 600 words, 6 minutes): two readers want one seat in the courtyard. Pairs reread the page where Priya and Mateo argue (8 minutes) with book 1's door page beside it. Share: three pairs say the new obstacle in one sentence (3 minutes) and the 3 remaining minutes go to the teacher's two-row model below.
- **20 min. Writing.** Teacher models one similarity and one difference for setting and one for plot, as sentences with a located detail from each book (5 minutes). Children fill the setting row and the plot row of the first two-book map (book 1 beside book 2, columns for chapters read so far) (10 minutes), then write one sentence: how the new problem is like and unlike the door problem (5 minutes).
- **5 min. Word in context.** Word: difference. Children say what it means in a comparison and use it in a sentence about the two problems.
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes each child: one way the settings are alike, one way they differ.
- **5 min. Exit.** E94, alone, on paper.

- **Shared shape.** The model sentences for setting and plot, and the first two-book map, share their shape with s03-X94v2 (RL.3.9). The exit reads book passages the models and the map do not use.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview alike, different, message with a pair of drawings; offer the frames "Both books ___, but in book 1 ___ and in book 2 ___" and "The message is ___ because ___." Access: audio beside print; large print for the maps; oral rehearsal before N08. Check accommodations: extra time inside the 90 minutes; the recount told to the teacher. Reading load: a chapter read aloud; rereads 150 to 250 words. Extension: add a third column for the optional classic, if the child has read it, as a compare column only (never assessed).
