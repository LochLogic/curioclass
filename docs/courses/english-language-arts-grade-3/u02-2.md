## 3. Checks, written first


**Items owned (days 52 and 76 rows).** The W.3.3a and W.3.4 rows of days 52 and 76 are given by sections 2.4 and 2.9; the draft sittings here (the day 43 to 45 beginning, middle and end) are practice and carry no score. Plan point: the days 46 and 51 rows name a silver crown and "cautious" in Under the Leaf, but that poem has neither; day 46B uses a short invented poem that holds both, printed "invented for practice".

**Day 46 items (45 minutes).** Silent. Read aloud only where decoding is not the evidence, and record any help. One marking scale per component: 0, 1 or 2 on the s02 keys.
- **46A (18 minutes), RL.3.1 and RL.3.2.** Latona at the Pool, read cold (about 360 words, 3 minutes). Ask 1: "Why is Latona angry? Show the sentence." Meets: the workers refuse her water and muddy it. Not yet: "The pool has no water" (A). Ask 2: "Tell the myth in four sentences, then say what it teaches through two events." Meets: refusing water and muddying it lead to the change into frogs; cruelty has consequences. Not yet: "It is about frogs" (B).
- **46B (12 minutes), RL.3.4 and L.3.5a, b, c.** Poem, invented for practice: "The moon laid a silver crown upon the pond. / A cautious frog sat still beneath the reeds. / I heard a splash out in the dark / And felt almost sure a frog was there." Ask 1: "What is a crown exactly, and what does silver crown mean here?" Meets: headwear; moonlight shining on the water. Not yet: the frog wears a metal crown (C). Ask 2: "Tell one real thing a person does that is cautious." Meets: looks before crossing a road. Not yet: "Cautious means loud" (D). Ask 3: "Choose wondered, suspected or knew for the speaker and say why." Meets: suspected, heard but did not see. Not yet: all three mean the same (E).
- **46C (15 minutes), L.3.4a, b, c, d.** Note, invented for practice: "Latona was parched and pleaded for a drink. The workers gave no help; they were unhelpful. She acted to protect her children; they needed protection." Glossary: pool, a small body of still water; a game played on a table. Protect: keep safe. Ask 1: parched from its sentence. Meets: very thirsty. Not yet: angry (D). Ask 2: unhelpful. Meets: not helpful. Not yet: very helpful (F). Ask 3: protection from protect. Meets: the safety the children need. Not yet: -tion means do it twice (F). Ask 4: use the glossary to say what pool means in the myth. Meets: a small body of water. Not yet: the table game, no glossary used (G).

**Exit questions (E43 to E47).** One ask each, on a new case printed "invented for practice". Unscored; shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- **E43.** Card: "I waited at the edge of a dry pool. My friend needed a damp hiding place." Ask: Write two sentences that tell what the narrator did next. Use one regular past verb and one irregular past verb. Answer: I crept along the bank. I looked under the reeds. (crept is irregular, looked is regular). Wrong: an irregular verb given a regular ending (L); words with no sentence or action (S).
- **E44.** Card: "Frog reached the bank. Her friend was still behind her. She wanted him to hear her warning." Ask: Write what happens next. Put what Frog says in speech marks, and add one thing she does or feels. Answer: "Hurry," called Frog. She crouched on the bank, afraid. Wrong: an event with no spoken words, action or feeling (P); speech with no speech marks or comma (U).
- **E45.** Card: "Frog found a hollow log. His friend climbed inside. Rain began outside." Ask: Write one closing sentence that follows these events. Answer: So the two friends stayed dry inside the log. Wrong: a closing about something unrelated (Q); words with no closing sentence (S).
- **E46.** Card: "The path was empty. I guessed my friend had gone home. The porch light was off." Ask: Write the word that shows the speaker is not sure. Answer: guessed. Wrong: a claim the card does not make (A); a word about the path, not about being sure (x).
- **E47.** Card: "Little Frog wanted company. She called from a dark hollow. Her friend followed the sound." Ask: Rewrite the first two sentences so a reader sees where Little Frog is and why she calls. Answer: Little Frog sat alone in a dark hollow. She felt lonely, so she called. Wrong: no reason for the call (S); the friend and the call put in the wrong order (Y).

## 6. Days

## Day 43


- **10 min. Long words.** Three minutes repair E42's commonest letter. Week 9: read and spell -tion and -sion on invention, tension and confusion; map the irregular word because from the tale (7 minutes).
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Four conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs (16 minutes); four minutes for a log line. Add the four to the heard-six column.
- **15 min. Reading and reread.** Hard-part reread, in pairs: how does the opening make Kikker's goal clear? Pairs reread the first two paragraphs and point to the words (6 minutes); the class names them (3 minutes). The teacher shows the start of the Ranna tale beside it: narrator, situation, goal (6 minutes).
- **10 min. Word and sentence work.** Verb retrieval: write the past forms of hop and go (3 minutes); the teacher models hopped and went against the miss goed (2 minutes); pairs sort eight cards of verbs with their past forms into regular and irregular (5 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes a child: tell the beginning and the partner says back the narrator and the situation (4 minutes). Six volunteers, 1 minute each (6 minutes).
- **20 min. Writing.** Draft the narrator, situation and beginning of the tale from the plan card (W.3.4), with at least one regular and one irregular past verb (20 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E43.

Carried: the draft and plan card stay in the folder. Tool: None. By hand: slate words, verb cards, the draft beginning, exit. Evidence: E43. Hand-backs in: E42. Out: E43 to day 44's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the beginning aloud to an adult and ask them to say who is telling it and where. No score.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview dry, hollow, log, warning and taunt with pictures; frames "First ___. Then ___." and "I said ___."; say the draft aloud before writing; a partner who shares a language.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of any picture; a scribe for ideas on drafts and for spelling not being checked; for the oral sample on day 45, a shorter list is not allowed because decoding is the evidence, but time may be extended.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 46 items may be read aloud except where decoding is the evidence; record every help. Extra time on days 45 and 46, recorded.
- **Reading load.** Alone: a poem or 150 to 250 words. Latona is about 360 words; a child who cannot read it alone has it read aloud and the support is recorded.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second spoken exchange with a feeling to the middle, or write the same event from the friend's view.
