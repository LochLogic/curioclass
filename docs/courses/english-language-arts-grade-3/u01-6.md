# Section 1.6: Washington's account and the retelling: context, voice, an explanation, and a reading goal


## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.3.1 and RI.3.2 (days 26 to 28):** "Ask and answer questions ... referring explicitly to the text as the basis"; "Determine the main idea ... recount the key details". Day 26 builds a main-idea chart for the retelling and separates it from the context text. Day 27 rereads for voice. Day 28 scores two items on a fresh panel.
- **L.3.4a to d, RL.3.4, L.3.5a (day 27):** sentence clues, word parts (-ful, -less, with care and help), the root free, a beginning dictionary entry, and the nonliteral "devour this book" and "did not give up", each compared with its literal reading.
- **RF.3.3a, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 26, 27, 29):** week 6 teaches -ful (full of) and -less (without) on days 26 and 27 and retrieves them on 29; irregular words from the texts (enslaved, determined, alphabet) are mapped, then read and spelled. **RF.3.4a to c (days 27, 29):** partner rereading with a feedback card; no speed target.
- **W.3.2a to d (day 28):** one unscored explanation, written in one sitting from a fresh panel. **W.3.5 (day 29):** the child sets a goal from evidence. **W.3.10 (days 26, 27, 29):** the log, the chart and the goal card, each dated.
- **Limits that bite here.** The texts name slavery and freedom plainly, in the words "enslaved people" and "emancipation". No child is asked what slavery was like, for family history or to imagine it. The three reserved sentences of Washington's chapter II are not read in shared work. Facts come only from the two texts and the panel; an added fact is a letter A.
- **Word record (RF.3.3a, RF.3.3d).** Owned by section 1.7 (its day 30 completes it); four six-minute rotations run here, days 26 to 29, six children a day. The list is enough, laugh, through, untwist, resend, hopeful, dislike, misplace; ask the affix and meaning of untwist and hopeful. Those words and their bases (lock, fill, hope, like, place) are kept out of every example, retrieval and exit.
- **Connections.** The grouping and link moves of section 1.5 return on day 28; the repair routine is the one of day 25. Section 1.7 uses the goal card on day 30.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to preview the history in eight minutes without asking children for stories; how to tell Washington's own words (first person, in quotation marks) from the retelling; why -ful has one l; why a goal must name a move and a place to use it; and that day 28's explanation is practice, with feedback on 29.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned.** Word record: the four rotations above are scored here and filed on the check sheet for section 1.7's day 30 (meets: reads both words and explains not and full of; miss: un- means again, AC; guessing laugh, P). Day 28, mid-unit: RI.3.1 and RI.3.2 (6 minutes each) on the cap panel, as keyed in the table above. The four writing rows W.3.2a to d of day 28 (H, A, I, J) are read in the 25-minute explanation as unscored practice and keyed in s01.md; feedback on day 29. Day 38: the ten rows above are run from the unit packet by the section that holds that day. What they sample is taught here and checked on days 26 and 27: the voice-and-gist reading (RI.3.1, RI.3.2), a nonliteral phrase against its literal reading (RL.3.4, L.3.5a: devour, did not give up), a sentence clue (L.3.4a: determined), -ful and -less (L.3.4b), a root (L.3.4c: free, freedom, teach, teacher) and a dictionary entry (L.3.4d: alphabet). The cap panel is kept out of all teaching.

**Exit questions (E26 to E29).** One ask each. Unscored. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The strips of E26 to E28 are Washington's reserved sentences, kept out of shared work; E29 is invented for practice.
- **E26 (day 26).** Context: "Booker was born enslaved. Emancipation set him free before the learning in this account." Sentence: "I recall that I had an intense longing to learn to read." (longing: a strong wish). Ask: "Using the context and the sentence, explain why Washington could begin to learn to read." Answer: freedom had come, so learning was allowed, and he wanted it very strongly. Wrong: an added fact such as a teacher or school that neither gives (A); only "he wanted to" with no use of the context (W).
- **E27 (day 27).** Two lines. One: "After a while I succeeded in making arrangements with the teacher to give me some lessons at night, after the day's work was done." Two: "A teacher gave Washington lessons after work." Ask: "Which line is Washington's own account, and how can you tell?" Answer: the first, because it says "I" and is his own words; the second tells about him. Wrong: the second, or both, with no reason (A).
- **E28 (day 28; replaces five minutes of the explanation).** Sentence: "But my boyish heart was still set upon going to the day-school, and I let no opportunity slip to push my case." (set upon: determined about). Ask: "Ask one question about what Washington wanted, and answer it with words from the sentence." Answer: for example, "What did he want?" - to go to the day-school; he took every chance to ask. Wrong: a topic word only, "school" (B); a fact the sentence does not give (A).
- **E29 (day 29).** Strip, "invented for practice": "Mina read the page fast and ran past the full stops. Her partner said, 'I did not follow the story.' Mina read the page again." Ask: "Write one reading goal for Mina and the move she will use." Answer: pause at the end marks and read in phrases so the meaning is clear (a move, and where to use it). Wrong: "read even faster" (X); "skip words she cannot read" (P).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The teacher holds the context text and the main-idea chart on day 26; children hold their own annotated copies from day 27 and their own goal card from day 29. The context-and-story chart returns in section 1.7. The goal card goes to the day 30 opening.
- **Key moment 1, day 27: first-person words against retelling.** Ideal instrument: a printed text where children mark voice (W for Washington, R for retelling). Spec: fits; print.
- **Key moment 2, day 29: a goal grounded in two records.** Ideal instrument: a card beside the child's own feedback and the teacher's note. Spec: fits; print.
- **One word, one sense.** "Account" means his own telling of events here; it is not a bank or a story told by someone else. Say so once on day 27.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper, pencils, scissors, a dictionary and wall space for the chart.

## 6. Days

## Day 26


- **10 min. Opening.** Eight minutes: the context bridge. The teacher previews and reads g3-u1-08 aloud (about 1.5 minutes at 120 words a minute), then three words on cards: enslaved, emancipation, freedom (about 6 minutes, teacher voice and picture timeline). Nobody is asked about their own family or what slavery was like. Two minutes: retrieval of the main-idea chart from the Kentucky article, one sentence.
- **20 min. Skills.** Three minutes: repair the commonest E25 letter. Ten minutes: week 6 sequence. In the first four, -ful (full of) and -less (without) are taught on care and help, with enslaved and determined mapped as irregular words; in the last six, the teacher hears six children at one minute each for the unit word record (rotation 1 of 4) while the rest write the pattern words. Seven minutes: two reading conferences of 3.5 minutes while the rest read from their logs; heard-six column.
- **20 min. Text and model.** The teacher reads g3-u1-07 aloud while children follow (336 words, about 3 minutes) and previews devour, determined and account in context (3 minutes). The teacher then models a two-column chart: "context" (g3-u1-08 and the first paragraph of g3-u1-07) and "what Booker did", with the main idea in one sentence and three details (14 minutes).
- **25 min. Hand work.** Five minutes: each child rereads g3-u1-07 silently (about 3.5 minutes) and underlines the sentence for each detail. Twelve minutes: pairs complete the chart with a main idea and three details, each with its sentence marked. Eight minutes: each child writes two sentences on how the context helps explain why learning was new for Booker.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on the details that support the main idea, using "The words ___ explain ___." (4 minutes); then six volunteers share, 1 minute each (6 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E26.

Carried: the teacher holds the context and the chart; children hold their annotated texts. Tool: None. By hand: the chart, two sentences, exit. Evidence: E26. Hand-backs in: the day 25 repair list. Out: E26 to day 27's opening; the annotated texts to day 27. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread the first two paragraphs aloud to an adult and tell one detail; then read any book together. No questions about family history.

## Day 27


- **10 min. Opening.** Retrieval of the context strategy: from memory, each child writes the main idea and one context fact on a slate and checks it against the day 26 chart (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Eight minutes: week 6 sequence, two minutes on -ful and -less with play, thank and use, then six children heard at one minute each for the word record (rotation 2) while the rest spell and use those words in sentences. Five minutes: grammar retrieval, joining two facts with and or but. Seven minutes: partner rereading of a 90-word part of g3-u1-07 twice each with the feedback card (phrasing, expression, no speed); the teacher listens to two pairs, 3.5 minutes each.
- **20 min. Text and model.** The teacher models on the first quoted line: quotation marks and "I" mark Washington's own words, the sentence around them is the retelling. Then three word strategies on short examples from the text: sentence clue (determined), nonliteral against literal (devour this book, did not give up), and a beginning dictionary entry found by guide words (alphabet). Root: free, freedom. (20 minutes, about 5 minutes each)
- **25 min. Hand work.** Pairs mark W (Washington) and R (retelling) beside each part of g3-u1-07 (8 minutes). Each child writes the nonliteral phrase, its literal meaning and its meaning in the text (7 minutes), looks up alphabet (4 minutes), and writes two sentences, one in Washington's voice and one in the retelling's, on when he learned (6 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on which words are Washington's and how they know (4 minutes); class chart "How can a reader tell?" (6 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E27.

Carried: children hold their annotated text and the W/R marks. Tool: None. By hand: marks, word notes, exit. Evidence: E27. Hand-backs in: E26. Out: E27 to day 29's Return and repair (day 28 holds no opening). At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the annotated text aloud to an adult and show how you can tell Washington's words; then read any book together.

## Day 28


- **30 min. Skills, silent.** Ten minutes: dictation of ten words (-ful, -less, pre-, mis- and un- words) on slates, retrieval only. Twenty minutes: independent reading from the logs, within which six children are heard at one minute each for the word record (rotation 3). No teaching.
- **8 min. Panel reading.** Alone, silently, the cap panel (about 65 words, under 1 minute to read, read twice); the teacher teaches nothing.
- **12 min. Items.** RI.3.1 (6 minutes) and RI.3.2 (6 minutes) in writing, alone. The teacher records support given.
- **25 min. Explanation.** Silent. Twenty minutes: the unscored single-sitting explanation of the mother's response (topic, grouped facts, one definition, one link, a closing sentence). Five minutes: E28 replaces the last five.
- **10 min. Reading groups.** Six groups of five, unscored. Each child reads two sentences of g3-u1-07 (about 30 words, 15 seconds) twice and gets one comment on phrasing from the group (about 80 seconds each, 7 minutes for five children); 3 minutes to move into groups and start. The teacher sits with one group and records phrasing. Day 35 supplies the unit fluency record.
- **5 min. Collection.** Items and explanations are collected. After class the teacher marks the two items (about 4 minutes a child) and reads the explanations for feedback.

Carried: nothing; this is a check day. Tool: None. By hand: items, explanation, exit, group reading. Evidence: the two reading rows and the four unscored writing rows of field 3, E28. Hand-backs in: none (E27 goes to day 29). Out: results and notes returned on day 29. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read for 20 minutes and tell an adult one thing your book explains.

## Day 29


- **10 min. Opening.** Recall of the written next step: each child names the target from the day 25 paragraph feedback and shows where they used it in the day 28 explanation.
- **20 min. Skills.** Eight minutes: week 6 sequence, two minutes of -ful and -less retrieval, then six children heard at one minute each for the word record (rotation 4) while the rest write words. Five minutes: grammar retrieval. Seven minutes: partner rereading of a 90-word part of g3-u1-01 twice each with the feedback card; the teacher listens to two pairs.
- **30 min. Return and repair.** Five minutes: the day 28 item results and explanation notes are returned; each child marks one letter. Twenty minutes: four groups by letter: reading (A, B), grouping (H), links and closing (I, J), and a group for those who met every look-for. The teacher leads the largest group for 15 minutes; the rest work in pairs from written repair cards; each child repairs one sentence of their own. Five minutes: a fresh one-sentence example to check the repair.
- **20 min. Strategy and goal.** Eight minutes: the teacher models a five-strategy menu, each with a boat detail and a bus detail from g3-u1-01 and g3-u1-02: get ready before; take small steps; read slowly and reread; ask and listen; mark where to begin. She models choosing one by rereading a hard sentence of g3-u1-01. Twelve minutes: each child reads two records (the explanation notes and the teacher's reading note) and fills a goal card: "My strategy is ___. I will use it when ___. My evidence is ___."
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes to tell the goal and the evidence; 1 minute to move.
- **5 min. Exit.** E29.

Carried: children hold the goal card and the feedback. Tool: None. By hand: repair sentence, goal card, exit. Evidence: E29; the repaired sentence. Hand-backs in: the day 28 results, E27 and E28 (Return and repair). Out: the goal card to day 30's opening; the repaired explanation stays in the folder. At home (30 to 40 minutes): tell an adult your goal and one place you will use it; then read any book together.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 26: "How does the context text help you understand what Booker wanted?" Day 27: "How can a reader tell Washington's own words from the retelling?" Day 29: "What is your strategy, and what in your work shows you need it?"
- **Written task, day 28.** The panel explanation (practice): how his mother responded to the cap problem. **Day 29:** the goal card.
- **Look-fors, six.** A topic that names the problem; grouped facts; an accurate definition; a link inside one group; a closing sentence that fits; a goal that names a move and a place to use it. Recorded on the shown, shown with help or not yet seen scale; day 28's explanation is unscored.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 26:** the main-idea chart; the repair of E25. **Day 27:** context strategy; -ful and -less; and and but. **Day 28:** all word parts silently. **Day 29:** -ful and -less; the paragraph feedback; the repair routine of day 25. Phrase reading and the feedback card carry from section 1.5.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on rereading; no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview enslaved, freedom, emancipation, account, devour and goal with a picture before reading; frames "The words ___ explain ___." and "My strategy is ___."; oral first answers; a partner who shares a language.
- **Access.** Large print; the text read aloud; the photograph and timeline described in words, never the answer; a scribe for content, never for spelling evidence.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 28's items and E26 to E29 may be read aloud except where reading is the evidence; record any help. A child may speak an exit and the teacher writes it.
- **Reading load.** Alone: one 90-word part of g3-u1-07, the 65-word panel and the exit strips. Read aloud: the rest of g3-u1-07 and g3-u1-08.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different detail from g3-u1-07 as the one that best supports the main idea.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult previews the history gently, reads aloud, and asks only the questions on the cards. Mark W and R together. Day 28 is a quiet half hour; the adult records support and returns notes next day.
- **One projector.** Show the context text and chart; keep the exit strips and the panel on paper until needed.
