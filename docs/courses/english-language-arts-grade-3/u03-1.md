# Section 3.1: A first chapter-book series: chapters 1 to 6

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.1** ("ask and answer questions ... referring explicitly to the text"): taught 81, 83, 84; practised on 85 (owned items). Questions are written about a printed page and answered with a located sentence.
- **RL.3.3** ("describe characters ... explain how their actions contribute to the sequence of events"): taught 81 and 83; assessed on 85.
- **RL.3.4** and **L.3.5a** ("determine the meaning of words and phrases ... distinguishing literal from nonliteral"): taught on 83 with hit a wall; assessed on 85 with the context and figurative chart from unit 1.
- **RL.3.5** ("refer to parts of stories ... using terms such as chapter ... describe how each successive part builds on earlier sections"): taught 81 to 84, practised 86. Only chapters are used here; scene and stanza come on 107.
- **RL.3.6** ("distinguish their own point of view from that of the narrator or ... the characters"): taught 84, assessed 85.
- **RI.3.1** and **RI.3.6** ("ask and answer ... referring explicitly"; "distinguish their own point of view from that of the author"): taught 82 on the article; practised on 114 (not owned here).
- **RF.3.3c, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f, W.3.10** ("decode multisyllable words"; "irregularly spelled words"; "conventional spelling ... suffixes"; "spelling patterns"; "write routinely"): the 30-minute skills block, week 17 (three-syllable division) and the retained response each day.
- **RF.3.4a, RF.3.4b, RF.3.4c** (purpose and understanding; accuracy, rate, expression on successive readings; context to self-correct): the 82 and 84 partner rereadings. Judged by expression and sense, never by speed.
- **L.3.1a** and **L.3.1f** ("explain the function of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs"; "subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent agreement"): review on 86 only. The functions are grade 1 and 2 words; agreement is taught on 88.

Limits that bite: reading is about 120 words a minute, so a chapter of 790 to 930 words fills 8 minutes of read-aloud; pairs reread 150 to 250 words, never a whole chapter. Chapter 5 is read unaided on day 85. The teacher page must explain the three-syllable method (split, check each chunk, blend) and that the reserved packet P85 is not previewed. Connections: story-map, narrator and point of view come from grades 1 and 2 (see field 8); "series" is new on 82.

## 2. Where it sits


- Before: the start of unit 3. Retrieval only; no readiness check. Retrieval prompts sit in each day's opening (field 8), and their results go to the dated folder and the conference record.
- After: section 3.2, opening day 87 on chapter 7 (day 86 reads chapter 6, so each chapter n falls on day 80 plus n).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Day 85 items (30 minutes, silent, reserved packet P85, owned by this section).** Marking scale: 0, 1 or 2 on the unit keys, scored once after feedback on day 86; the revision is not rescored.
- **RL.3.1 (8 minutes).** Write one question P85's words can answer, then answer it with the sentence that proves it. Key: a question like Why does Ada hide the plan? with the answer She feared its arrow was wrong, and the proving sentence. Wrong: a question the text cannot answer (O), or She hates Ben (A).
- **RL.3.3 (8 minutes).** Explain Ada's feeling, motive and the effect of her action. Key: her worry makes her hide the page, so Ben waits. Wrong: She is kind, with no action or effect (C).
- **RL.3.4 (6 minutes).** Explain arrow and my heart sank. Key: a direction mark; feeling disappointed, not an organ falling. Wrong: Her heart fell to the floor (D).
- **L.3.5a (2 minutes).** Contrast the literal and intended readings of fallen flat. Key: a plan lying flat, against a plan that failed. Wrong: a plan actually lying flat (D).
- **RL.3.6 (6 minutes).** Separate narrator, Ada and reader views of Ben. Key: the narrator thinks he wastes time; Ada accepts his help; I think his question is useful, with a reason. Wrong: Everyone thinks Ben wastes time (E).
Rows for day 114 (RI.3.1, RI.3.6) belong to section 3.9.

**Exit questions (E81 to E86).** One ask each, on a new case: the unit plan's reserved exit panels are not used, because an exit must be a case not seen anywhere in the unit (plan point). Printed "invented for practice", at most 15 words a sentence. Unscored.
- **E81.** Book 1, chapter 1, the paragraph that begins "Ben walked across the courtyard" (before the door page the day works). Ask: Copy the words that tell why Ada followed Ben. Answer: because she could not stand to wait. Wrong: Ada followed him (B); She wanted to see the rug (A). Data: s03-X81v2.
- **E82.** The series article, paragraph 5 (the model works paragraphs 1 and 3). Ask: What does the writer think about keeping old drafts? Answer: The writer thinks keeping old drafts is very useful. Wrong: drafts should be thrown away (E); drafts should be typed (A). Data: s03-X82v2.
- **P82-1 (student page, second judged item).** The article, paragraph 7, which asks the reader. Ask: What do you think a series needs most? Give a reason from the article. Answer: any view with an article reason, for example the same characters, because readers can compare the books. Wrong: Making a series takes patience (E); a series needs pictures, because the article says so (A). Data: s03-P82-1v2.
- **E83.** Book 1, the door problem at the end of chapter 1 and the first sentence of chapter 2 (the day works chapters 2 and 3). Ask: How does that chapter grow from the door problem? Answer: The plan was wrong about the door, so Ada begins to crumple it. Wrong: Ada crumples the plan, so the door hits the rug (B); Ada wanted to tear up Ben's plan (A). Data: s03-X83v2.
- **E84.** Book 1, chapter 2, from "A wrong plan is no use" to "did the narrator" (the day works the chapter 4 nuisance passage). Ask: Copy the sentence that gives the narrator's view of Ben. Answer: She thought he was being silly, and so, if we are honest, did the narrator. Wrong: Ada's line about a wrong plan (E); Ben's "Then may I have it?" (E). Data: s03-X84v2.
- **P84-1 (student page, second judged item).** Book 1, chapter 2, from "Ben smoothed the page flat" to "we can look". Ask: Write your own view of keeping the old drawing, with a reason from the chapter. Answer: any view with a chapter reason, for example it is smart, because they can look if they forget why they moved the corner. Wrong: Ben is silly (E); he keeps it to draw on the back (A). Data: s03-P84-1v2.
- **E85.** Book 1, chapter 5, the paragraph that ends "as heavy as a rock" (outside the P85 lines). Ask: What does "as heavy as a rock" mean here? Use a clue. Answer: the hidden plan weighed on Ada; she hid it because she feared its arrow was wrong. Wrong: The plan was made of stone (D); Ada carried a box of books (A). Data: s03-X85v2.
- **E86.** Card: "The brown cat sleeps. It dreams." Ask: What job does It do in the second sentence? Answer: It stands in for the brown cat, so the words brown cat are not said again. Wrong: It is a pronoun (X); It tells how the cat dreams (X). Printed "invented for practice". Data: s03-X86.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Letters this section meets, each shown on the day named; if many made it, spend 5 to 10 minutes on the repair.
- **A, invented evidence.** Wrong: She wanted to see the rug (E81). Right: because she could not stand to wait. Shown 81 and 83. Repair: put a finger on the page for every claim; 5 minutes.
- **B, reversed order.** Wrong: Ada crumples the plan, so the door hits the rug (E83). Right: the door problem comes first, then the crumpling. Shown 81 and 83. Repair: number the events on the map; 5 minutes.
- **C, trait without action.** Wrong: Ada is kind. Right: Ada hides the page because she worries, so Ben waits. Shown 85 and 86. Repair: trait plus action plus effect on three cards; 7 minutes.
- **D, literal for nonliteral.** Wrong: her heart fell to the floor. Right: she felt disappointed. Shown 83 and 86. Repair: context and figurative chart, two phrases; 7 minutes.
- **E, misattributed view.** Wrong: Ada's line given as the narrator's view of Ben. Right: the narrator's own sentence. Shown 82, 84 and 86. Repair: three-column view table, one passage; 8 minutes.
- **X, class without function.** Wrong: It is a pronoun. Right: It stands in for the brown cat. Shown 86. Repair: finish the sentence "A ___ does this job: ___"; 5 minutes.
- **Z, pronoun disagreement.** Wrong: The readers check it, It reads slowly. Right: They read slowly. Seen in 86 revisions; taught on 88.
- **AI and AJ, long-word errors.** Wrong: dif-fe-rent, or spelling people as peeple. Right: dif-fer-ent; people kept as an irregular word. Shown in the daily word prompt. Repair: split the word aloud, check each chunk, then blend; 5 minutes.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Children hold the chapter maps, the view tables, the log and the dated folder. The teacher holds the conference record and the heard-six column. The day 85 packet is collected and returned on day 86.
- **Key moment 1, day 85: the packet.** Ideal instrument: printed pages with the chapter open beside them. Spec: fits; paper is enough. No tool.
- **Key moment 2, days 82 and 84: partner rereading.** Ideal instrument: a recorder, so successive readings can be heard again. Spec: optional; a teacher note per pair is the paper path.
- **A room with nothing.** Pencil, paper, the chapters printed, the shared board.

## 6. Days

## Day 81


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper: read and spell isolated words (3 minutes), read and spell a sentence from today's chapter (3), decode one word in its paragraph and say what it means (4). Three-syllable words, split, check each chunk, then blend: dif-fer-ent, fur-ni-ture, wa-ter-ing. Irregular: people, answer. One line of ending-rule retrieval (drop e, double the consonant, change y).
- **20 min. Independent reading and conferences.** Log day. Children read their own book and fill the log line. Four 4-minute conferences (16 minutes, four children, one record each) and two children read a log line aloud at 2 minutes each (4 minutes); the heard-six column is filled. No new teaching.
- **5 min. Opening repair and retrieve.** Hook: two drawings of one reading corner, printed, invented for practice. Ask the series question once: would you recommend this series to a friend, and why? Retrieve the plan card (situation, events in order, ending) from unit 2 on a tale the class knows: pairs, 30 seconds each.
- **20 min. Reading and reread.** Teacher reads chapter 1 aloud (about 790 words, 8 minutes, two stops to point at the two plans). Pairs reread the page where the door swings across the rug (8 minutes, one reads, one listens, then swap). Share: three pairs say the problem in one sentence (4 minutes).
- **20 min. Writing.** Teacher models the start of the chapter map (5 minutes): title, the two plans, the problem. Children copy the frame and mark the two plans and the opening problem (7 minutes). Causal response, one or two sentences (8 minutes): what did Ben's pointing at the door change?
- **5 min. Word in context.** Word: plan. Children say what it means in chapter 1 (a drawing and an idea) and one other use of the word. Pairs, 1 minute each.
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes each child: which of the two plans would you try first, and what in the chapter makes you say so?
- **5 min. Exit.** E81 (field 3), alone, on paper.

Carried: the chapter map and the log stay with the child; the teacher holds the conference record. Tool: None. By hand: the map, the causal response, the exit. Evidence: the map and the causal response; E81. Hand-backs in: none. Out: E81 to day 82's opening repair. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then tell someone at home the two plans from chapter 1 in three sentences. No score.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: day 81, what did Ben's pointing change in the plan? Day 83, which choice in chapter 2 caused the trouble in chapter 3? Day 84, do you agree with the narrator, and what in the chapter makes you say so? Written task: the chapter map with a link sentence each day, and the day 85 packet. Look-fors: a located sentence for each claim; events in order; each view given to the right speaker; a reason that uses the page.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 81: plan card from unit 2 and the story map with message box (unit 1). Day 82: author purpose and two accounts (grade 2); partner rereading with the feedback card. Day 83: context and figurative chart and literal against nonliteral (unit 1). Day 84: speaker, teller and point of view (grades 1 and 2); the discussion moves chart. Day 86: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective and adverb functions (grades 1 and 2). Every day: ending rules and irregular spellings.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview chapter, series, narrator, nuisance with a picture or gesture, and offer the frame "The narrator thinks ___; I think ___ because ___." Access: large print, audio beside print, oral rehearsal before writing; chapters are read aloud to everyone. Check accommodations: read the packet items aloud on day 85 (not the chapter), extra time inside the 90 minutes, answers by mouth to the teacher. Reading load: a chapter is read aloud; independent rereading is 150 to 250 words. Extension: defend a different supported recommendation, or write which chapter you would cut and why.

## 10. Two rooms

One child at home: the parent is the partner for the 2-minute talks and reads chapter pages aloud while the child follows; the parent asks "Where does it say so?" before the child writes. One projector: show the chapter map and the view table; the packet and the exits stay on paper.
