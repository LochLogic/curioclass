**65A (40 minutes, silent, one sheet; then 15 minutes of silent verification).** Printed on the sheet, Panel C (g3-u2-pC, an invented practice panel; its items have needs_text no), Curio Class wording, facts from the national zoo account named in the book's sources: "Cranwell's horned frog waits without moving in plants. When an insect comes near, the frog strikes. Waiting lets prey come within reach. Prey means an animal hunted for food.

During cold, dry months, temporary ponds disappear. The frog stays buried underground until rains return."
- **Part 1 (RI.3.1, 6 minutes).** "Write one question that Panel C answers. Write the answer. Copy the words that show it." Key: for example "Why does the frog wait?" with "Waiting lets prey come within reach". Wrong: an answer the panel does not give, such as chasing prey for miles (A).
- **Part 2 (W.3.2a to W.3.2d, 24 minutes).** "Write a paragraph about Cranwell's horned frog. Tell the reader what it is about. Keep the feeding facts together and the dry-season facts together. Say what prey means. End with a sentence that fits. You may draw and label a waiting frog." Key: a topic sentence that names two groups; the feeding group (waits, strikes, prey is hunted food) kept apart from the dry-season group (ponds disappear, buried underground, rains return); a link word such as when, also or so inside a group; a labelled sketch if drawn; a closing sentence about waiting and shelter. Wrong: dry-season facts scattered through the feeding group (AA); also joining feeding to an unrelated plan (AB); a claim that it eats only plants (A); an ending about an unrelated bird (Q).
- **Part 3 (RI.3.3, RI.3.8, RI.3.4, L.3.6, 10 minutes).** (a) "Say what happens when an insect comes near, what happens to the ponds in cold, dry months, and what the frog does then." Key: it strikes; after the ponds disappear it stays underground. Wrong: the frog's burial makes the ponds dry (Y). (b) "How does the sentence Waiting lets prey come within reach connect to the one before it? How does the second paragraph differ from the first?" Key: waiting leads to feeding; the second paragraph changes from feeding to dry-season behavior. Wrong: calls the whole panel an ordered life cycle without checking (Z). (c) "What do prey and temporary mean in Panel C? Use the clues." Key: prey is an animal hunted for food; temporary ponds do not last, since they disappear in the dry months. Wrong: temporary means permanent despite the ponds disappearing, or prey is a place (D). (d) "Write one sentence about why the frog waits. Use prey, the word explain or detail, and a time or place phrase." Key: for example "Before striking, the frog waits among plants, and the detail explains why prey comes close." Wrong: prey used as a place (D).

**Exit questions (E62 to E66).** One ask each, on a new case. Unscored; shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Strips are invented for practice; their frog facts are generic and the source is to check against the national health frog page before print.
- **E62 (s02-X62v2, s02-X62cv2, s02-X62b).** (a) Which source tells what the males' call sounds like, the frog book or the glass frog page? Answer: The frog book, page 11: a harsh, creaking sound. Wrong: a claim the book strip does not make (A); a book fact under the wildlife page label (X); a place neither source gives (A).
- **E63.** Four notes: long back legs; can jump far; lives in tall grass; the grass hides it. Ask: Pick two notes that belong together. Join them in one sentence with and or because. Answer: It can jump far because it has long back legs. (or: It lives in tall grass, and the grass hides it.). Wrong: a joining word links unrelated ideas (AB); notes from two groups joined (AA).
- **E64.** Strip: "Yesterday the frog sitted on a log. Then it runned to the pond. It is the more small frog by the pond. My cousin has a blue bike." Asks, one each: (a) Say the right word for sitted in sentence 1. Answer: sat. (b) Say the right word for runned in sentence 2. Answer: ran. (c) Say the right words for more small in sentence 3. Say the frog is small next to one other frog. Answer: smaller. (d) Say which sentence does not fit the paragraph. Answer: Sentence 4, about the cousin and the blue bike. Wrong: a regular ending on an irregular verb (L); a comparison made two ways at once (N); the form for three or more used for two (N); keeps the unrelated sentence (Q).
- **E65.** Strip: "Green treefrogs live near lakes and streams. Many green plants grow there. The frogs are hard to see among them." Asks, one each: (a) Write one question that this strip answers. Answer: Why are green treefrogs hard to see?. (b) Answer your question in one sentence. Use because. Answer: Green treefrogs are hard to see because they are green like the plants near the water. Wrong: the strip does not answer it (A); a statement, not a question (W); cause and result reversed (Y); a reason the strip does not give (A).

Asks, one each: (a) Say a focused research question from this feedback. Answer: How big is a horned frog?. (b) Open the index on page 19: say the index word and page for the size. ## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview unusual, source, group, because and conclusion with the book's drawings; frames "The book adds ___. The page adds ___." and "These facts belong together because ___."; say the sentence aloud before writing; a partner who shares a language.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of each drawing; a scribe for the paragraph, not for the edit of verbs, tenses and comparisons; a printed copy of Panel C.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 65 may be read aloud except where decoding is the evidence (record help); extra time by the same rule for all; the sketch may replace one written sentence.
- **Reading load.** Alone: one page or 150 to 250 words. The excerpt is read aloud first, then reread in chunks.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second group paragraph that compares what the book and the excerpt each add, with one linking word per comparison.
