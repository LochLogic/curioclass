# Section 4.2: Two texts on water access

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


This section's share: RI.3.9 "compare and contrast the most important points and key details presented in two texts on the same topic" is taught on 126 (shared and single points of the access account and the town-treatment text) and 127 (the same chart reread and checked); RI.3.1 "ask and answer questions ... referring explicitly to the text" returns on 126 as one question per text; W.3.7 and W.3.8 "short research ... take brief notes on sources and sort evidence into provided categories" are met on 126 as a two-column note chart with a source tag on each line; RI.3.2 "determine the main idea ... recount the key details" on 127 for each text; RI.3.4 and L.3.6 on 127 as two domain words ("borehole" and "filter") met in sentences; RF.3.4a to RF.3.4c on 127 as pair reading of a 150-word stretch with rereading to confirm.

Limits that bite: the two texts are about 493 and 406 words; the account was read on 125 and is only re-skimmed on 126. Children compare two texts, never three. Printed stems are at most 15 words. The town text is our own writing, not a health-agency page.

Connections: contents and index (grade 3 u02-5), note card with source tag, research question, relevant (u02-6), main-idea chart (u01-4), time and cause links (u02-5). The teacher page must explain that a "shared point" is an important idea both texts make, not a shared word, and that "different" is only half of comparing.

**Exit 126 (two one-ask slips, s04-X126v2 and s04-X126bv2).** A: Water on Earth, section 6, paragraph 3 ("Supply can shrink, too. When little rain falls, rivers and wells may hold less water."). B: Water Paths in Kitui County, rain in seasons and rivers drying after poor rains. Neither is the town text section the day works. Ask: "Write one important point both texts share." Answer: rivers hold less water when little rain falls. X126bv2, Ask: "Write one detail only B has." Answer: rain comes in seasons, or ponds dried up. Wrong: a detail from one text as the shared point (C); an unstated claim such as "the wells are broken" (A); only differences (F).

**Exit 127 (s04-X127v2).** Water on Earth, section 8, paragraph 1, printed on the slip (conserve water: turn off the tap, fix a leaky pipe); not the two whole texts the day works. Ask: "Write the main idea of this part in one sentence." Answer: people can protect water by using less and wasting less. Wrong: "Fixing a leaky pipe helps" as the whole idea (B); a list of the actions with no joining idea (C).

## 6. Days

## Day 126


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts (important, detail, supply); irregular words "answer" and "people"; the ending-rule line for -ed.
- **20 min. Skills block, log day.** Four conferences of 4 minutes (16) and two log lines read aloud at 2 minutes (4).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest letter from 125's exit, 3 minutes; 2 minutes to reread the 125 table.
- **20 min. Read.** The teacher reads "How a Town Cleans Its Water" aloud (about 4 minutes); children reread silently (5 minutes); then pairs skim the Kitui account (4 minutes); the class starts a two-column chart (7 minutes).
- **20 min. Write.** The chart: one important point per text with a source tag, and one detail each; then "Both texts say ..." and "Only ... says ...". The teacher models the first line (3 minutes); 14 minutes of writing; 3 minutes to check against the cards.
- **5 min. Partner talk.** Read the shared point to a partner (2 minutes each, 1 to change over).
- **5 min. Word in context.** "filter" in its sentence, then the glossary entry.
- **5 min. Exit.** The two-text slip on shrinking supply (exit 126).

Carried: the table and chart. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: exit 126. Hand-backs in: none. Out: chart in folder. At home (40 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book; No score.

## Day 127


- **10 min. Long words.** Prompts on treatment, community, protect; irregular words; the -ly suffix rule on "easily" and "clearly".
- **20 min. Skills block, Tuesday.** Grammar 5 minutes (pronouns for the two texts' nouns: "it", "they"); partner rereading 15 minutes with a feedback card (5 minutes each way, 5 for card marks).
- **5 min. Opening.** Retrieval of the chart: name one shared point aloud.
- **10 min. Pair reading.** Each pair reads a 150-word stretch of one text aloud in turn and rereads one sentence to confirm (RF.3.4c).
- **15 min. Main idea.** The class builds a main-idea chart for each text: the idea, then two details joined by "because" (RI.3.2); the teacher models one (4 minutes) and pairs do the other (11).
- **15 min. Write.** One sentence per text beginning "The main idea of this text is ..." and one sentence joining them with "Both".
- **5 min. Partner talk.** Which detail in each text helps its idea most (2 minutes each, 1 to change over).
- **5 min. Word in context.** "borehole" in the account's sentence and the glossary entry.
- **5 min. Exit.** The town pump card (exit 127).

Carried: the chart. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: exit 127. Hand-backs in: none. Out: both main-idea charts. At home (40 minutes): read for 20 minutes; say one difference between two books you know; No score.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: "What important point do both texts make about clean water?" (126, partner talk); "Which detail in this text helps its main idea most?" (127). Written task: the chart and the two joined sentences on 127. Look-fors: a shared point that is about the idea, not a shared word; each detail tagged to its text; two main ideas that cover the text; no unstated fact.

## 8. Practice that returns

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: a two-column chart with the headers "both" and "only" and pictures of a tap and a filter; sentence frame "Both texts say ...". Access: print a larger chart; read the town text aloud twice. Reading load: the account is already known. Extension: add a third column for a detail that neither text gives and mark it "not in the texts". Check accommodations: none, nothing is scored here.

## 10. Two rooms

A parent teaching one child: read both texts aloud together, then fill the two-column chart with one point each; ask "Do both say this?" for each line. One projector: show the chart on screen; children copy no more than two lines and write their own sentences on paper.
