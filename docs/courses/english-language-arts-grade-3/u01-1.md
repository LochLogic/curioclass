# Section 1.1: Launching the year: the boat story, talk and first screens

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-3/1/s/1.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-3 u01-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.1, this section's share (day 1):** "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers." Who, where and why questions on the boat story, each answer with the sentence pointed to. Days 5 to 7 finish the standard.
- **RL.3.7 (day 1):** "Explain how specific aspects of a text's illustrations contribute to what is conveyed by the words in a story." One picture, one contribution (mood or setting). Character emphasis comes on days 7 and 11.
- **SL.3.1a to d and SL.3.3 (day 1):** the five discussion moves taught once, on the boat story: "come to discussions prepared" (mark one detail), "follow agreed-upon rules" (a listening cue), "ask questions to check understanding", "explain their own ideas and understanding in light of the discussion", and "ask and answer questions about information from a speaker". Days 2 to 8 practice in pairs; day 9 is the first scored window.
- **RF.3.3d, L.3.2e and L.3.2f (days 1 to 4):** the week 1 irregular words come from the story and are mapped, not guessed: friend, heart, neighbors, once, says, does, water. Weeks 1 and 2 retrieve the six syllable types and vowel teams.
- **L.3.2d (day 2):** "Form and use possessives": one owner only (reader's, girl's). More than one owner waits for weeks 7 and 8.
- **RF.3.4a to c (days 2 to 4), W.3.2a to d and W.3.4 (day 3), RL.3.10 and RI.3.10 (days 3 and 4), W.3.10:** partner rereading, one unscored writing sample, and a first reading log. Screens place children; they are never grades, and no independent-comprehension code is claimed from them.
- **Limits that bite here.** Placement work is read for pattern, not scored. No child is asked about reading at home or about family reading. No map skill beyond a title and a key (social studies unit 1 is not assumed).
- **Connections.** SL.3.1 on days 1 to 9 (talk), RF.3.4 on days 2 to 4 (reading aloud).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to hear a child read without correcting mid-sentence; what a placement screen shows and does not show; why the Manaus floods are named plainly and without fear; the one marking scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen); why exits are unscored.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: the start of the year. There is no readiness check (retrieval only): the grade 2 rows e2-wh-questions, e2-images-and-text, e2-discussion, e2-vowel-teams, e2-two-syllable-words, e2-prefixes-suffixes, e2-tricky-spellings, e2-spelling, e2-sentences, e2-fluency and e2-tell-and-record are retrieved inside days 1 to 3. If more than a third of the class miss a retrieval, the next day's opening repairs it; otherwise the existing conference group does. After: section 1.2. Day 3's oral samples finish in day 5's conference slot, and the screens set the conference groups from day 5. Results go on the teacher's own record sheet.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none given in this section. Rows owned and given later: SL.3.1a to d and SL.3.3 (day 9, section 1.2 holds the window) and RL.3.10 and RI.3.10 (day 38). Not given here, so nothing below uses them.

**Exit questions (E1 to E4).** One ask each, on the boat story (g3-u1-01) except the E2 dictation and the E3 card. Unscored. One marking scale per record: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's opening repairs that letter.
- **E1 (day 1, s01-X1v2).** Page 3 of the boat story, its words and picture (the model is page 2). Ask: What stands between Lia and the landing? One sentence, using the words or the picture. Answer: water stands between Lia and the landing; the words say water covers the path, and the picture shows brown water under the boards. Wrong: "The boat is late, so Lia cannot reach it" (A); "The picture has people and boards" (D).
- **E2 (day 2, diagnostic).** Say once, slowly, and let the children write: "The baker's cat was sitting on the step." Answer: The baker's cat was sitting on the step. Wrong: "The bakers cat was sitting on the step" (N); "The baker's cat was siting on the step" (O).
- **E3 (day 3).** This card is invented for practice. Read: "Lia opened the pond book. The first page named a frog she had seen. She read its name twice, then showed the page to her sister." Ask: Explain to a younger child what Lia did with the frog page. Write three or four sentences. Answer: Lia used the frog page. She read the frog's name twice. Then she showed the page to her sister. Wrong: "Lia read the name twice and showed the page to her mother" (A); "frog, page, sister" (K).
- **E4 (day 4, s01-X4v2).** Pages 7 and 8 of the boat story, reread in partner rereading. Ask: You want to find out how Lia gets home. Write a log line: the page, your question, and the words that told you. Answer: page 7; how does Lia get home?; she follows her neighbors home along the boards. Wrong: "Page 8, because its picture is nice" (AD); "It says Lia rides the boat home" (A).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

|---|---|---|
| A | Wrong: "She showed her mother the page." Right: "She showed her sister the page" (the card says sister). Days 1 and 3. | 5 minutes in the next opening: read the card, put a finger on the sentence, redo the answer aloud on a new card. |
| D | Wrong: "The picture has people and boards." Right: "The brown water under the boards shows Lia cannot walk to the landing." Day 1. | 5 minutes: two columns, the words say and the picture shows, one new picture. |
| N | Wrong: bakers cat. Right: baker's cat. Day 2. | 5 minutes: ask who owns it, say it, put the apostrophe after that owner. |
| O | Wrong: siting. Right: sitting. Day 2. | 5 minutes: mark the short vowel, double the last letter, then add -ing; three new words. |

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The five-move discussion chart is taught on day 1, held by the teacher through day 4, then by partners. The sound and spelling wall holds the week's irregular words, marked by the class. The reading log begins on day 4 and each child holds it. All are read and returned to in later sections.
- **Key moment 1, day 1: a picture adds to the words.** Ideal instrument: the page and its picture side by side, printed or on one screen. Spec: fits; print is enough. Gaps and asks: none (no tool; see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **A room with nothing.** Everything here is print and a wall chart; nothing needs a device.

## 6. Days

## Day 1


- **8 min. Hook.** Show the page 1 picture of the boat story alone; read page 1 aloud. Children match three word cards (river, boats, buses) to picture details (retrieval, e2-images-and-text). Put the compelling question on the board. Teach the listening cue. Content note: floods are named plainly; nothing is shown as frightening.
- **8 min. Map bridge.** A world map: read its title and key, find Brazil, then Manaus and the river. Pairs point to the title and the key. Taught here because the social studies map lesson may not have happened.
- **14 min. Read and ask.** Teacher reads the whole story aloud with picture pauses (502 words, about 7 minutes). Teacher models asking and answering one question with the sentence pointed to (4 minutes). Pairs answer one who and one where question and point to the words (3 minutes; retrieval, e2-wh-questions).
- **30 min. Skills.** Ten minutes of syllable retrieval on story words (closed, open, silent e); ten minutes mapping the week 1 irregular words; ten minutes of reading from the picture-book tub, no logs yet.
- **20 min. Hand work.** Children mark three pages in two colors, words that tell and picture details (10 minutes), then fill a table "The words say / The picture shows", two rows (10 minutes). They mark one detail to talk about.
- **5 min. Prepared talk.** The teacher models the five moves from a written script (prepare, wait, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer). Pairs do one turn each, about 45 seconds a child (retrieval, e2-discussion). The question starter is "Which part do you mean?" Anyone may pass.
- **5 min. Exit.** E1, alone, on paper.

Carried: the discussion chart is the teacher's. Tool: None. By hand: marked pages, the two-column table, exit. Evidence: E1. Hand-backs in: none. Out: E1 to day 2's opening (3 minutes, the commonest letter). At home (30 to 40 minutes): read pages 1 and 2 aloud to an adult and say one thing the picture shows that the words do not; then read any book together. No questions about your own home or reading.

## Day 2


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E1's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieval on slates: boat and rain (vowel teams), sunset and table (syllables), reread and playful (parts), said and does (tricky words), a sentence with reading. Unscored.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Possessive retrieval: the girl's hat, the dog's bone (one owner; grade 2 apostrophe).
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Pairs reread story pages 5 and 6 (about 155 words, 1.3 minutes a pass) twice each, using a feedback card (accurate, in phrases, expressive; no speed talk). The teacher listens to three pairs, 3 minutes each, and records who was heard.
- **55 min. Screens.** First 15 minutes, whole class: the written spelling screen, twelve words and one sentence (rain, seed, float, napkin, tiger, cradle, baking, running, rerun, unfair, said, again). Then 40 minutes: the teacher hears the long-word reading card one child at a time, 2 minutes each, 18 children (36 minutes, 4 for changes) in list order. Everyone else works from a menu: read from the tub, sort word cards by syllable type, draw and label a river library.
- **5 min. Exit.** E2 (dictation).

Carried: the sound and spelling wall. Tool: None. By hand: spelling screen, menu work, exit. Evidence: E2; the screens are placement records. Hand-backs in: E1. Out: E2 to day 3's opening (3 minutes). At home (30 to 40 minutes): read a page of any book aloud and spell three words from it with an adult. No score is kept.

## Day 3


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E2's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieving sentence combining: join "Lia chose a book. Nina listened." with and, and retell two story sentences to a partner for a listener (unscored).
- **20 min. Oral samples, first slot.** The teacher hears 13 children read an unseen 60-word practice passage once and tell what happened, 1.5 minutes each (19.5 minutes). The teacher notes the time on a watch only to record pace; it is never told to the child. The rest read from the tub.
- **55 min. Rotations.** First 15 minutes, whole class: the supported writing sample on the boat story, with a frame card (topic, fact, link, ending); unscored. Then 40 minutes: the teacher hears the 12 children not yet screened on the long-word card (24 minutes) and 10 more oral samples (15 minutes); others read, then work the menu.
- **5 min. Exit.** E3.

Carried: the oral-reading record; each child is heard once for each piece of evidence. Tool: None. By hand: writing sample, menu work, exit. Evidence: E3. Hand-backs in: E2. Out: E3 to day 4's opening (3 minutes). The last 7 oral samples (10.5 minutes) go to day 5's conference slot. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread a favorite page twice for an adult, the second time like talking. No score.

## Day 4


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E3's commonest letter; seven minutes recalling reading purpose: each child says one reason a person reads something (not their own reading at home).
- **5 min. Grammar.** One-owner possessives from the story: Lia's book, Nina's hand.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Pairs reread pages 7 and 8 (about 105 words) with the feedback card. The teacher listens to three pairs, 3 minutes each, and records who was heard.
- **20 min. Model.** The teacher thinks aloud choosing a book by purpose from three class-shelf books: title, cover, words on the back, first page. The wrong way (the cover alone) is shown once, about a book, not a child. The log columns are shown: date, title, my purpose, one detail.
- **25 min. Hand work.** Children browse the class shelf (8 minutes), read a first page (5 minutes) and write their first log line (6 minutes). Meanwhile the teacher holds four reading conferences of 4 minutes each (16 minutes of the 25, four-minute reading conference routine), others reading silently.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs tell why they chose their book (prepared talk, 2 minutes a child, 2 minutes to link and clarify); then three volunteers share (4 minutes). Passing is allowed.
- **5 min. Exit.** E4.

Carried: each child now holds a reading log. Tool: None. By hand: log line, exit. Evidence: E4. Hand-backs in: E3. Out: E4 to day 5's opening (3 minutes). The six children heard today are the four conferences plus two pairs; the heard-six column names them. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read your chosen book with an adult for 20 minutes and say why you picked it.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 1: "What does the picture show that the words do not?" Day 4: "What do you want this book to tell you?" Day 3: "Who is the listener, and what do they need to know?"
- **Written task, day 3.** A short supported explanation of how Lia got her books, on a frame card (unscored diagnostic).
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors:** a topic sentence, facts from the story only, a link word, a closing sentence. Recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 1: syllable types on story words. Day 2: grade 2 vowel teams, syllables, parts and spelling. Day 3: sentence combining and a retell for a listener. Day 4: reading purpose and one-owner possessives.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing, no speed target. Partners give feedback on phrases and expression.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview the words river, boat, library, borrow, return before reading. Frame: "The words ___ show ___." Pair with a partner who shares a language where possible; accept a drawn answer first.
- **Access.** Large print and a factual description of each picture for a child who cannot see it; a scribe for explanations, not for spelling evidence. Allow a pointer or a typed answer for writing small.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits may be read aloud, except where reading or decoding is the evidence (E2 and the screens); record any help.
- **Reading load.** Alone: pages 5 to 8 of the story and one printed card a day, under 150 words. Read aloud: the rest.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different detail from the story that answers the same question.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the partner role: read a page aloud, find a detail, ask one question and listen. No timing. Save notes on paper.
- **One projector.** Show only the story pages and the world map; keep exit cards on paper and out of sight until the exit.
