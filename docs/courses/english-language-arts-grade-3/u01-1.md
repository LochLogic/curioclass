- **8 min. Hook.** Show the page 1 picture of the boat story alone; read page 1 aloud. Children match three word cards (river, boats, buses) to picture details (retrieval, e2-images-and-text). Put the compelling question on the board. Teach the listening cue. Content note: floods are named plainly; nothing is shown as frightening.
- **8 min. Map bridge.** A world map: read its title and key, find Brazil, then Manaus and the river. Pairs point to the title and the key. Taught here because the social studies map lesson may not have happened.
- **14 min. Read and ask.** Teacher reads the whole story aloud with picture pauses (502 words, about 7 minutes). Teacher models asking and answering one question with the sentence pointed to (4 minutes). Pairs answer one who and one where question and point to the words (3 minutes; retrieval, e2-wh-questions).
- **30 min. Skills.** Ten minutes of syllable retrieval on story words (closed, open, silent e); ten minutes mapping the week 1 irregular words; ten minutes of reading from the picture-book tub, no logs yet.
- **20 min. Hand work.** Children mark three pages in two colours, words that tell and picture details (10 minutes), then fill a table "The words say / The picture shows", two rows (10 minutes). They mark one detail to talk about.
- **5 min. Prepared talk.** The teacher models the five moves from a written script (prepare, wait, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer). Pairs do one turn each, about 45 seconds a child (retrieval, e2-discussion). The question starter is "Which part do you mean?" Anyone may pass.
- **5 min. Exit.** E1, alone, on paper.

Carried: the discussion chart is the teacher's. Tool: None. By hand: marked pages, the two-column table, exit. Evidence: E1. Hand-backs in: none. Out: E1 to day 2's opening (3 minutes, the commonest letter). At home (30 to 40 minutes): read pages 1 and 2 aloud to an adult and say one thing the picture shows that the words do not; then read any book together. No questions about your own home or reading.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 1: "What does the picture show that the words do not?" Day 4: "What do you want this book to tell you?" Day 3: "Who is the listener, and what do they need to know?"
- **Written task, day 3.** A short supported explanation of how Lia got her books, on a frame card (unscored diagnostic).
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors:** a topic sentence, facts from the story only, a link word, a closing sentence. Recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 1: syllable types on story words. Day 2: grade 2 vowel teams, syllables, parts and spelling. Day 3: sentence combining and a retell for a listener. Day 4: reading purpose and one-owner possessives.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing, no speed target. Partners give feedback on phrases and expression.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview the words river, boat, library, borrow, return before reading. Frame: "The words ___ show ___." Pair with a partner who shares a language where possible; accept a drawn answer first.
- **Access.** Large print and a factual description of each picture for a child who cannot see it; a scribe for explanations, not for spelling evidence. Allow a pointer or a typed answer for writing small.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits may be read aloud, except where reading or decoding is the evidence (E2 and the screens); record any help.
- **Reading load.** Alone: pages 5 to 8 of the story and one printed card a day, under 150 words. Read aloud: the rest.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different detail from the story that answers the same question.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the partner role: read a page aloud, find a detail, ask one question and listen. No timing. Save notes on paper.
- **One projector.** Show only the story pages and the world map; keep exit cards on paper and out of sight until the exit.
