# Section 1.2: The boat and bus stories: message, effort, word meaning and a first discussion

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.1 (days 5 to 8):** "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers." Every answer names the sentence or picture it came from. Finished on days 10 to 13.
- **RL.3.2 (days 5, 6, 8):** "Recount stories ... determine the central message, lesson, or moral and explain how it is conveyed through key details in the text." Recount first, then message, in a story map with a message box. Folktales and fables begin on day 12.
- **RL.3.3 (days 5, 6, 8):** "Describe characters in a story (e.g., their traits, motivations, or feelings) and explain how their actions contribute to the sequence of events." One motivation and one trait per character, joined to one event.
- **RL.3.4, L.3.5a (day 7):** "distinguishing literal from nonliteral language." Four phrases from the bus story are sorted; the intended meaning is said in the child's own words.
- **L.3.4a to d, L.3.2g (day 7):** sentence clue, a known affix, a known root (care, careful, careless) and one look-up on a printed dictionary page. Choosing among the four is practiced on day 27, not here.
- **RL.3.7 (day 7):** "Explain how specific aspects of a text's illustrations contribute to what is conveyed by the words in a story." One picture, mood or effort.
- **RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 5 to 8):** the week 2 words and pattern in the scope and sequence, decoded and spelled, never guessed.
- **RF.3.4a to c (day 7):** partner rereading with a feedback card; no speed target.
- **W.3.4, W.3.10 (days 5 to 8; W.3.4 on day 8):** one supported written response on day 8; short routine writing on the other days.
- **SL.3.1a to d, SL.3.3 (day 9):** the first scored discussion window. Day 1 taught the moves; days 5 to 8 rehearse them in pairs.
- **Limits that bite here.** The discussion window is one assessed part: reading logs guide teaching and no independent-comprehension code is claimed from talk. No child is asked about their own reading at home or their own library. Floods and distance are named plainly and without fear; real places are only Manaus, Brazil, and Swaledale, England.
- **Connections.** RL.3.1 and RL.3.7 resume in section 1.3 (days 10 to 13); SL.3.1 returns on days 17, 25, 36 and 39.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to build a story map with a message box; why message comes from actions and results, not from the topic; how to run six groups of five through a seven-minute window with a record sheet; why the day 9 evidence is recorded child by child and never given as a grade.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned (mid-unit assessment, day 9):** the five rows of s01.md field 5 for day 9: SL.3.1a, SL.3.1b, SL.3.1c, SL.3.1d and SL.3.3, all sampled inside one seven-minute group window per group of five (see day 9). Reading choices and logs guide teaching and are not scored here. Day 13 and day 19 rows belong to sections 1.3 and 1.4.

**Exit questions (E5 to E9).** One ask each: E5 on the boat story, E6 to E8 on the bus story (g3-u1-02), each on a page the day does not model; E9 on a strip printed "invented for practice". Unscored. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's opening repairs that letter.
- **E5 (day 5, s01-X5v2).** Page 2 of the boat story (the model is page 3). Ask: Write one sentence: what does Lia getting ready the night before show? Answer: getting ready early means you do not miss your chance; the boat does not stay long, so Lia sets her book by the door. Wrong: "Page 2 is about a book" (B); "Lia sets the book by the door, so the boat does not stay long" (C).
- **E6 (day 6, s01-X6v2).** Page 8 of the bus story (the model is page 4). Ask: Explain how reading the new book changes Sam's long wait for Thursday. Answer: he reads slowly and crosses off a day, so the week feels shorter. Wrong: "The week is shorter, so Sam reads the book" (C); "He likes books" (B).
- **E7 (day 7, s01-X7v2).** Page 1 of the bus story, words and picture (the model is page 3). Ask: What does stone walls climb the hills tell you? Use the words and the picture. Answer: the walls do not really climb; they run up the hillsides, and the picture shows gray walls going up the slopes. Wrong: "The walls walk up the hill" (E); "The picture has sheep and a barn" (D).
- **E8 (day 8, s01-X8v2).** Page 7 of the bus story (practice uses pages 3 to 6). Ask: Write three sentences a little child could follow: what do the events on page 7 show? Use them as proof. Answer: a hard trip feels shorter once you have what you worked for; Sam has book two, the hill feels shorter, and he smiles. Wrong: "The story is about a hill" (B); a list of steps with no events as proof (K).
- **E9 (day 9, after the windows).** This strip is invented for practice. Read: "Mina could not carry her books to the stop because her arms were full of bags. Her neighbor Joao set the books in a wheelbarrow and pushed it up the lane." Ask: Tell your partner what Joao's action did for Mina, using a detail from the strip. Then write one sentence saying what your partner's answer showed you. Answer: A detail from the strip, such as the wheelbarrow or the lane: Joao carried the books for her. Then a sentence that names what the partner added. Wrong: "I like Joao" (Q); "I still think the same" (T); "It was good" (U).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The story map with a message box: the teacher holds it on day 5, partners on day 6, each child on day 8, and it returns in 1.3 for the grandmother story. The context and figurative chart begins on day 7 and the teacher holds it; children add to it in later sections. Each child holds a reading log. The discussion record sheet is the teacher's and is filled on day 9.
- **Key moment 1, day 5: a story map beside the picture page.** Ideal instrument: a printed map with boxes for events and a message, beside the page it maps. Spec: fits; print is enough. Gaps and asks: none (no tool; see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 7: words beside the picture.** Ideal instrument: the picture and its words on one page. Spec: fits, print. No ask.
- **A room with nothing.** Everything is paper and a wall chart; the map can be chalked on a board.

## 6. Days

## Day 5


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E4's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieval of grade 2 character response: say what a character wants and does, on two practice strips (unscored; e2-character-response).
- **20 min. Skills.** Ten minutes: the teacher finishes day 3's last 7 oral samples, 1.5 minutes each (10.5 minutes) while the rest read from their logs; ten minutes: week 2 words and pattern, mapped from the story, never guessed (P is repaired in the conference slot).
- **20 min. Model.** Reread the boat story whole (502 words, about 4 minutes) with picture pauses. The teacher builds a story map for pages 3 to 7 with a message box (16 minutes): who, want, loose board, neighbors cross, result, message. Connect Lia's action to the way across.
- **25 min. Hand work.** Pairs fill their own copy of the map for pages 3 and 5 (15 minutes), then add one sentence of message with "so" (10 minutes). Each child points to the sentence used.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on the message, with a prepared detail (4 minutes); then ask for detail and link, 3 minutes; three volunteers share, 1 minute each (3 minutes). Passing is allowed.
- **5 min. Exit.** E5, alone, on paper.

Carried: the teacher holds the story map. Tool: None. By hand: partner map, message sentence, exit. Evidence: E5. Hand-backs in: E4 (day 4's commonest letter, 3 minutes). Out: E5 to day 6's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read pages 3 to 4 aloud to an adult and say what Lia did; then read any book together. No questions about your own home or reading.

## Day 6


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E5's commonest letter; seven minutes recalling cause and result: put two sentence cards in order and say "so" or "because" (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Four minutes: week 2 words mapped; sixteen minutes: four reading conferences of 4 minutes (the four-minute reading conference routine) while the rest read from their logs. The four children heard go on the heard-six column.
- **20 min. Model.** Read the bus story whole (543 words, about 5 minutes) with picture pauses. Teacher starts the story map on the board (15 minutes): Sam's wish, the plan, the hill, the counting-walls game, the result, the message. Content note: weather and distance are named plainly; nothing is shown as frightening.
- **25 min. Hand work.** Partners complete the map for pages 3 to 6 (15 minutes), then write two lines: Sam's motivation and how his effort changed what happened (10 minutes), pointing to the words.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on Sam's effort with a prepared detail (4 minutes); ask for detail and link, 3 minutes; three volunteers share, 1 minute each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E6.

Carried: partners hold the map. Tool: None. By hand: map, two lines, exit. Evidence: E6. Hand-backs in: E5. Out: E6 to day 7's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread pages 3 and 4 aloud, the second time like talking, and say what Sam wants. No score.

## Day 7


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E6's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieval on slates: read a sentence with a comma aloud, name two words that mean about the same, find a clue in a sentence for a new word (unscored; e2-rhythm-and-meaning, e2-word-relationships, e2-word-strategies).
- **20 min. Skills.** Ten minutes: partner rereading of pages 3 and 4 (about 150 words, about 1.2 minutes a pass) twice each with the feedback card; the teacher listens to two pairs, 3 minutes each, and records who was heard. Ten minutes: grammar retrieval, fix a fragment and a run-on on two sentence cards (e2-sentences).
- **20 min. Model.** Teacher models the context and figurative chart on four bus-story phrases: legs like jelly, clouds like gray wool hats, a library that has grown wheels, stones like a puzzle (10 minutes). Then the care family: care, careful (page 3 of the boat story), careless, with -less taking care away (5 minutes). One printed dictionary page, one entry read aloud (3 minutes). Where an entry has two senses, the teacher reads the one that fits. A picture is read beside its words (2 minutes).
- **25 min. Hand work.** Pairs sort the four phrases, say each intended meaning and mark the clue (12 minutes), each pair looks up one word on its own printed dictionary page at the same time (5 minutes), then compares the page 3 picture with its words (8 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on one phrase and what the picture adds (4 minutes); ask for detail, 3 minutes; three volunteers share, 1 minute each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E7.

Carried: the teacher holds the context and figurative chart. Tool: None. By hand: phrase sort, look-up, picture note, exit. Evidence: E7. Hand-backs in: E6. Out: E7 to day 8's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read a page of any book aloud and find one phrase that is not meant exactly. No score.

## Day 8


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E7's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieving a spelling pattern: spell four words with the week 2 pattern and add -ing or -ed to two base words, on slates (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Partner rereading of page 6 (about 70 words) twice each (7 minutes); the teacher listens to two pairs, 3 minutes each, and records who was heard (the heard-six column). Then 13 minutes of conference groups for the grade 2 cycle on the diagnosed gap, the rest reading from their logs.
- **40 min. Hand work.** Twenty-five minutes: each child writes a response for a younger reader on how Sam's actions carry the story's message, with a topic sentence, two events as proof, a link word and a closing line, from their own map (supported frame "The words ___ show ___"). Then fifteen minutes: they mark one detail on each of the two day 9 preparation pages (printed, invented for practice), one boat and one bus, and rehearse a claim aloud in a quiet voice.
- **15 min. Talk.** Pairs rehearse the day 9 exchange: each child 3 minutes with a prepared detail (6 minutes). The teacher listens to three pairs, 3 minutes each, six children, and records them (9 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E8.

Carried: children hold their maps and discussion notes. Tool: None. By hand: response, marked pages, exit. Evidence: E8. The response is read and marked with feedback after class and returned next lesson. Hand-backs in: E7. Out: E8 to day 10's opening (the day 9 Check day has no repair slot). At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread your marked passages and say your detail to an adult.

## Day 9


- **30 min. Skills, silent.** Ten minutes retrieval on slates, silent: the week 2 words, no teaching. Ten minutes: partner rereading of a favorite page. Ten minutes: reading log line.
- **13 min. Independent preparation.** Each child rereads the two preparation pages and rehearses one prepared detail and one question for a peer, silent, writing three words on a note card.
- **42 min. Group windows.** Six groups of five, 7 minutes each (42 minutes), seated at one table. The topic: how Lia and Sam get ready to borrow again. In each window every child gets about 80 seconds: a prepared claim (25 seconds), one linked question to a peer (15 seconds), one answer with elaboration (25 seconds) and one sentence on what the discussion clarified (15 seconds). Five children times 80 seconds is 6 minutes 40 seconds, and 20 seconds to change groups. The teacher records each of the five children on the record sheet (SL.3.1a, b, c, d, SL.3.3) and teaches nothing. The 25 children not at the table read silently from their logs.
- **5 min. Exit.** E9, whole class, in pairs for 2 minutes then one written sentence.

Carried: the record sheet is the teacher's. Tool: None. By hand: note cards, record sheet, exit. Evidence: the five rows of field 3 and E9. Hand-backs in: none (a Check day has no repair slot). Out: the record sheet; E8, E9 and the day 8 response go to day 10's opening. Repairs go to the next teaching lessons. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read with an adult for 20 minutes and say one thing the book made clear.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 5: "What did Lia do, and what happened because of it?" Day 6: "What does Sam want, and what does he do to get it?" Day 9: "Which detail did you prepare, and what did your partner help you see?"
- **Written task, day 8.** A short response for a younger reader on how Sam's actions carry the story's message.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors:** a claim about the message, two events as proof, a link word, a closing line. Recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 5:** grade 2 character response. **Day 6:** cause and result. **Day 7:** rhythm and meaning, word relationships, word strategies; grammar of fragment and run-on. **Day 8:** a spelling pattern with -ing and -ed. **Day 9:** the week 2 words and the discussion moves from day 1, by silent retrieval only.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on successive readings, no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview river, boat, library, borrow, bus, hill before reading; a sentence frame, "The words ___ show ___"; an oral or drawn first answer; a partner who shares a language where possible.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of each picture for a child who cannot see it, not an explanation of the answer; a scribe for explanations, not for spelling evidence.
- **Check accommodations.** Read exits aloud except where reading or decoding is the evidence (the week 2 words, rereading); record any help. A child may speak or point on day 9, and the support is recorded.
- **Reading load.** Alone: one printed strip of under 40 words for each exit. Read aloud: both stories.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different detail from the same text for the same message.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the partner role: read a page aloud, ask one question, listen, and say what became clearer. Record by hand on paper. Day 9 is one prepared talk with the adult, with no score.
- **One projector.** Show only the story pages and the map; keep the exit strips on paper until the exit.
