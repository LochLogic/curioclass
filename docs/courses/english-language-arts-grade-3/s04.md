## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why does clean freshwater matter, and how can people protect it?"
Day 120 opens with Water on Earth contrasting ocean water and needed freshwater, then the compelling question.
Day 122: what do diagram labels add to the explanation? Day 127: which important points and details do the access and treatment accounts share? Day 129: how does the second paragraph explain the first? Day 134: how does sequence support the author's explanation?

Model word-and-diagram evidence on 122, paired comparison on 123 and 126, categorized notes on 128, paragraph links on 134 and revision on 136. Students choose evidence independently by 137.

**P154A, reserved access article, Distance Is One Part of Access.** Curio Class, ours; original prose from the July 2025 Kitui County report's executive summary and water section and The Voice of Wasya, printed page 10. In July 2025, a report described water collection in different parts of Kitui County, Kenya. The report did not give one distance for every person. In the Marginal Mixed Farming zone, the reported average round-trip distances to water sources were five to seven kilometres. Some places had longer journeys. In the Mixed Farming zone, the reported round-trip distances ranged from two to four kilometres. These ranges describe particular areas at the time of the report. They do not tell us the length of one child's journey today. Distance is only part of access. A person may reach a water point and still wait to collect water. Broken equipment can also affect the supply. Another Kenyan account described the Kyeni Kya Wasya community group's contributions to sand dams and wells. Such local work belongs in an account of water access alongside the difficulties. Having water nearby and knowing whether it is safe to drink are different questions. A distance report alone cannot answer both.
