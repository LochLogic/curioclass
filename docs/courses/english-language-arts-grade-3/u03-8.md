# Section 3.8: Revising a scene from the play

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.3.3a, b, c, d** (situation and characters; dialogue, action, thought and feeling; time words; closure): taught in unit 3 on 96 and 108 here (the planning), drafted on 109 and 110, revised on 111 and 112, audited on 113 (items run here, W.3.3a to d).
- **L.3.3a** ("choose words and phrases for effect"): the effect of one word taught on 108, practised on 109 to 112, an item on 113.
- **SL.3.4, SL.3.6, L.3.3b** (report in complete sentences at a pace a listener can follow; spoken and written conventions differ): taught on 108 through the spoken model of scene 1 and a partner report; practised from 116 to 119 (3.9). No item here.
- **W.3.5** (planning, revising, editing): taught on 108, practised 111 to 112, an item on 113.
- **W.3.4** (writing that suits the task and the listener): 109 only, through the opening written for a listener's request.
- **L.3.2c** (commas and quotation marks in dialogue): not taught here; retrieved from unit 2 (days 44 to 52) in the minute before dialogue on 110, practised on 111, judged inside the 113 audit.
- **SL.3.1b, c** (follow the rules; ask questions to check understanding and link comments): practised on 111 in the critique exchange.
- **L.3.1a, f** (the five word classes and their functions; subject-verb and pronoun agreement): taught in unit 3 on 88; retained here as two distinct grammar records, G110 (made on 110, feedback 111, revised 112, audited 113) and G112 (made on 112, for the audit on 115 in 3.9).
- **RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f, RF.3.3c, W.3.10:** the skills block and the dated short writing; week 23 transfer of syllable patterns into scripts and scenes. **RF.3.4a, b, c:** partner rereading on 109 and 112.

Limits that bite: the scene is one product (N05) across 108 to 113 and is counted once; no day adds a second narrative product. Day 108 reads the spoken model aloud (about 330 words of scene 1) and every child reports a plan to a partner, which the teacher can listen to in part only; spoken evidence is formative until 116. The revised scene is scene 1 of the play rewritten as prose with one action changed; the unit text names it the "original second scene", and the unit key matches scene 1, so this plan uses scene 1 and the teacher page should say so. Day 111 needs a written critique from a partner and a written note from the teacher on every draft and every G110 sheet (about 30 reads in the evening). Day 113 is silent with no reteaching.

The teacher page must explain: the plan card for the scene (situation, events in order, response, closure); that a stage direction is for the performer and is not spoken; that the audit on 113 scores the child's own earlier drafts and notes, not a new scene; that G110 and G112 are separate records; and the 2/1/0 scale.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Items run on 113 (60 minutes of lesson, silent, audit card at the desk).** Marking scale: 2 meets, 1 partly, 0 not yet, scored once from the card after the folder is collected. The child audits the 110 draft, the 111 critique and teacher note, and the 112 revision, not a new scene. Forty minutes of audit on the card, in the order of the skeleton rows: W.3.3a 8, W.3.3b 8, W.3.3c 3, W.3.3d 3, L.3.3a 4, W.3.5 4, L.3.1a 5, L.3.1f 5 (the L.3.1a and f rows print fresh lines on the card, not the G110 sheet). L.3.2c takes 0 additional minutes: the teacher judges the dialogue in the 110 draft, the 111 note and the 112 revision, each separately on the same scale. Of the 60 minutes: 40 audit, 12 independent preparation, 8 exit.

**Exit questions (E108 to E113).** One ask each, on a new case printed "invented for practice", sentences of at most 15 words. A stem that needed two answers is split into an a item and a b item. Unscored. The exact strips are in items.csv and these lines match it. If many miss one, the next day's opening repair uses that letter's wrong example.
- **E108 (s03-X108).** Strip: A script says, [Wren pauses.] Wren: 'Did you take the mittens?' The teacher reads it aloud with a pause and does not say the bracket. Ask: Write what the reader's voice does at the bracket. Answer: The voice pauses and the words in the bracket are not said. Wrong: Wren pauses (AE); A pause (AF).
- **E108b (s03-X108b).** Strip: Sana asks Rafe to hold the tape measure. Rafe says yes. Ask: Write one opening sentence for a scene about this. Tell who is where. Answer: In the workshop, Sana asked Rafe to hold the tape measure. Wrong: Sana asked Rafe to hold the tape measure (S); In the workshop (AF).
- **E109 (s03-X109).** Strip: A listener asks where Orin is. The opening says: Orin stood by the shed door. 'Er, could you hold this?' he asked. He looked at his shoes. Then he grinned. Ask: Write the words that tell the listener where Orin is. Answer: by the shed door. Wrong: Orin (S); Then he grinned (S).
- **E109b (s03-X109b).** Strip: The opening says: Orin stood by the shed door. 'Er, could you hold this?' he asked. He looked at his shoes. Then he grinned. Ask: Write one thing Orin says or does that shows he hesitates. Answer: He looked at his shoes (or the word Er). Wrong: Then he grinned (W); He asked if someone could hold this (W); He stood by the shed door (S).
- **E110 (s03-X110).** Strip: Rafe tapped the shelf. Was the space too small? Then he said let us measure it. Ask: Rewrite the last sentence with speech marks and a comma. Answer: Then he said, "Let us measure it.". Wrong: Then he said let us measure it (AL); "Then he said let us measure it." (AL).
- **E110b (s03-X110b).** Strip: Rafe tapped the shelf. Was the space too small? Then he said let us measure it. Ask: Write one action or thought that shows Rafe's response. Answer: Rafe tapped the shelf (an action) or Was the space too small (a thought). Wrong: Rafe felt unsure (T); He said let us measure it (T).
- **E111 (s03-X111).** Strip: A peer note says, "I cannot hear or see that Orin is worried." Ask: Write one action or spoken line that shows Orin is worried. Answer: Orin rubbed his sleeve. "Is it too late?" he asked. Wrong: Orin feels very worried (T); Orin feels worried (L).
- **E112 (s03-X112).** Strip: A draft says, "Before the class folded the tables, Sana swept the floor." The writer meant that the class folded the tables first. Ask: Rewrite the sentence so the time word shows that order. Answer: After the class folded the tables, Sana swept the floor. Wrong: Before the class folded the tables, Sana swept the floor (U); While the class folded the tables, Sana swept the floor (U).
- **E113 (s03-X113).** Strip: The twins packed lunches. Then she walked to the bus. Ask: Change one word so the pronoun matches the twins. Answer: they: Then they walked to the bus. Wrong: Then she walked to the bus (Z); Then it walked to the bus (Z).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Letters this section meets; if many made it, 5 to 10 minutes of repair.
- **S, no situation.** Wrong: "Ada said she was done." Right: "At lunch, in the garden, Ada said she was done." Shown 108 and 109. Repair: name who is where when the problem opens; 5 minutes.
- **T, a feeling without a response.** Wrong: "Ben felt nervous." Right: "Ben rubbed his folder. Was he too late? 'May I look?' he asked." Shown 110 and 111. Repair: add an action, a thought or a spoken line; 6 minutes.
- **U, a reversed time link.** Wrong: "After she erased it, she checked." (the check came first) Right: "Before she erased it, she checked." Shown 112. Repair: place two events on an arrow, then choose the word; 5 minutes.
- **V, a new problem at the end.** Wrong: the scene ends with a new question about lunch. Right: the ending closes the consequence of the scene's action. Shown 110 and 113. Repair: reread the last action and close it; 5 minutes.
- **W, a word whose effect clashes.** Wrong: "stomped" for a gentle walk. Right: "tiptoed". Shown 108 and 112. Repair: say two verbs aloud against the feeling; 5 minutes.
- **X, a class named, no function.** Wrong: "careful is an adjective." Right: "careful describes the readers." Shown 112. Repair: say what the word does here and in general; 5 minutes.
- **Y and Z, agreement.** Wrong: The readers shares his plan. Right: The readers share their plan. Shown 110 and 112. Repair: mark the subject and the referent, then choose the form; 6 minutes.
- **AE, a direction spoken as dialogue.** Wrong: reading "pause before Ben answers" aloud. Right: the voice pauses and the words stay unsaid. Shown 108. Repair: bracket the directions before the line is read; 5 minutes.
- **AF, a fragment for a clarification.** Wrong: "Her worry." Right: "I want you to see her worry in an action." Shown 108 and 111. Repair: rehearse with a subject and a verb; 5 minutes.
- **AL, speech without marks.** Wrong: Ben said Let us try. Right: Ben said, "Let us try." Shown 110 and 111 (retrieval from unit 2). Repair: mark the spoken words, then place the marks and the comma; 6 minutes.
- **L, recopying as revision.** Wrong: the same lines in neater writing. Right: one added gesture after the note, original beside. Shown 111, 112 and 113. Repair: model one margin change; 5 minutes.
- **O, an unrelated question.** Wrong: What did you have for lunch? Right: Which line shows how Ada feels? Shown 111. Repair: build the question from the writer's own line; 5 minutes.
- **AN, a report too fast.** Wrong: all four sentences in one breath. Right: a pause between each sentence for the listener. Shown 108. Repair: mark pause points and say it once for the listener; 5 minutes.
- **AH and AJ, word check.** Wrong: runing; becuase. Right: running; because. Shown in the skills block. Repair: in the next skills session; 5 minutes.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Children carry the scene folder (N05 plan, draft, critique, teacher note, revision), the G110 sheet, the printed script and the dated short writing. The G112 sheet made on 112 is collected and carried into 3.9. The spoken model is the teacher's own reading; nothing is kept from it except the notes children make.
- **Key moment 1, day 108: the spoken model against the script.** Ideal instrument: a recording the teacher can replay for the pause. Spec: partly fits; the teacher's own live reading meets the goal and a shared local recorder is optional, never required.
- **Key moment 2, day 113: the audit.** Ideal instrument: the printed folder laid beside the audit card. Spec: fits; paper is enough.
- **A room with nothing.** Pencil, paper, printed script and books, the scene folder, the plan card and the audit card.

## 6. Days

## Day 108


- **10 min. Long words.** Week 23, transfer to scripts. Three prompts on paper: whis-pered, hes-i-tate, di-rec-tion; two irregular words (again, their); one line of ending-rule retrieval (running, hoping).
- **20 min. Independent reading and conferences.** Log day: four 4-minute conferences (16) and two log lines read aloud at 2 minutes each (4); the heard-six column is filled. No new teaching.
- **5 min. Retrieve.** E107 returned with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). Pairs say what the exchange at the end of 107 showed about how scene 2 answers scene 1 (1 minute each, 1 to change over).
- **12 min. The spoken model and the script.** The teacher reads scene 1 aloud (3 minutes), the class following the brackets. The teacher then rereads Ben's lines, pausing where the stage direction says and leaving the direction unspoken (2). Teaching, 7 minutes: a stage direction is for the performer and the voice does what it asks; the pause in speech becomes a bracket in print; paragraphs and a speaker's name show who talks. Each child annotates one place in scene 1 where the voice does what the print shows with a mark.
- **8 min. Plan a revised scene.** The teacher models the plan card on the scene with one action changed (Priya, not Ben, asks to see the arrow): who is where when it opens, events in order with time words, a spoken line, an action and a thought, an ending that closes the action. The teacher models a stronger word (said becomes murmured) and a report in four complete sentences with a pause between each.
- **20 min. Plan.** Each child fills the plan card for a scene 1 rewritten as prose with one changed action and gives a reason for the change.
- **5 min. Report.** Pairs, 2 minutes each, 1 to change over: report the plan in complete sentences at a pace the partner can follow.
- **5 min. Word in context.** Evidence: one sentence about the script and what the sentence shows about the word.
- **5 min. Exit.** E108.

- **Shared shape.** The spoken reading of Ben's lines (pause, bracket unsaid) shares its shape with s03-X108 (L.3.3b), and the plan card shares its shape with s03-X108b (W.3.3a). The exit cases (a mitten question, a tape measure) are not in the script or the worked plan.

Carried: the plan card and the annotation go in the scene folder (N05, counted once across 108 to 113). Tool: None. By hand: the plan card, the annotation, the report, the exit. Evidence: N05 plan (formative), report to a partner (unscored), E108. Hand-backs in: E107 and the repaired note on the exchange. Out: E108 to the teacher; its commonest letter is repaired in the 109 opening. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then tell someone at home your plan in four sentences. No score.

## Day 109

- **15 min. Partner rereading with a feedback card.** Pairs reread 100 to 150 words of scene 1, a part each, the directions unspoken (12 minutes, 3 to change over). Expression on the second reading; no speed.
- **5 min. Grammar retrieval.** Pairs say the job of two underlined words in a narrator line from the script.
- **5 min. Recall situation.** E108 returned with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). Pairs say who is where when scene 1 opens (the situation), 1 minute each, 1 to change over.
- **20 min. Reading and rereading.** The class rereads scene 1 and the book 1 chapter where Ada's plan goes missing (a marked passage of 150 to 250 words, read in 3 minutes). Children mark one detail of place and one word that shows Ada's feeling, then explain in a margin note how each fits the opening they will write (hard-part reread annotation).
- **20 min. Draft the opening.** Each child drafts the opening and the first events of the revised scene, to meet a listener's request for where and why, and writes one sentence on how the opening relates to scene 1 and one on the effect of a chosen word.

- **5 min. Word in context.** One sentence on a word from the opening and what it adds.
- **5 min. Exit.** E109.

- **Shared shape.** The opening draft and its listener question share their shape with s03-X109 and s03-X109b (W.3.3a, L.3.3a): where is the speaker, and which word shows hesitation. The exit case (a shed door) is not in the script or the book.

Hand-backs in: E108. Out: E109 to the teacher. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then say your opening aloud to someone at home and ask where they think the scene is. No score.

## Day 110

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: day 108, what does the voice do that the print shows with a mark? Day 111, which response do you mean? Day 113, which earlier revisions answer a new reader? Written task, days 108 to 113: the scene (N05), planned on 108, drafted on 109 and 110, revised on 111 and 112, audited on 113. ## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview situation, closure, effect and stage direction with a picture of a stage and a script; frames "Ada is in ___ at ___", "First ___, then ___" and "I chose ___ because it sounds ___". Access: audio beside print for the script; large print; oral planning before writing; a child who cannot write much may draw the plan card and say each box. Check accommodations: read the audit card aloud (not the folder), extra time inside the 90 minutes, answers by mouth. Reading load: the script is read aloud by the teacher and volunteers; children read alone only a marked 150 to 250 words. Extension, same standard: change a second action and say how the ending changes.

## 10. Two rooms

Teaching one child: on 108 the parent reads scene 1 aloud, once with the stage directions spoken and once as the performer would, and asks which was better and why; on 111 the parent writes one question on the child's draft with the frame. One projector: show the plan card, the spoken model on 108 and the audit card on 113; the audit, the drafting and the exits stay on paper.
