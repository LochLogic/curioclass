## The reading and skills thread

Every unit day includes ten minutes of explicit long-word reading and spelling. In a full week the other 100 minutes are independent reading, logs, conferences and small groups for 20 minutes on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, and a five-minute grammar retrieval plus 15 minutes of partner rereading on Tuesday and Thursday. The teacher listens for accuracy, understanding, natural rate and expression. Context confirms a decoded word or clarifies its meaning; it never replaces decoding. The two screen days use the main block for rotations; remaining fall oral samples finish during conferences by day 10. On assessment days the code cell names the assessed evidence, while ordinary skills practice continues without adding more assessed codes.

The pattern order below is explicit instruction, followed by reading and spelling words, then sentences and a short passage carrying that pattern. The grade 2 vowel and syllable work is retrieval before applying new affixes. Common irregular words from the current text are mapped and retrieved every week. Word checks on days 15, 30, 45, 60, 90, 105, 120 and 135 take the ten-minute word slot plus, when needed, five minutes of the skills block. Check results group the next day's instruction: reteach the mistaken spelling-sound or affix relationship on fewer examples, then return to the passage; secure readers apply it on longer bases. Days 75 and 150 are regrouping practice. Course-level windows collect cumulative individual evidence once. A clean day 76 poem recording supplies that child's winter fluency record; day 79 does not hear that child again for the same evidence.

## Unit 1. Reading against the odds

39 course days, 1 to 39, weeks 1 to 8. The question is how people get past what keeps them from books and school. Days 1 to 13 establish reading, talk and evidence routines through the three whole picture-book stories and credited tales; the two additional screens are days 2 and 3 after the hook. Days 14 to 25 move to the book-carrier and packhorse articles, main ideas, photographs, maps and a complete informative paragraph. Days 26 to 39 use the Washington selection, model and revise a reading contract, record fluent reading and publish a strategy bookmark. Short stories precede articles and a longer process task because the latter depend on the new routines. This unit bears 15 owned rows; its extra launch time and continuing skills instruction reflect that load.

There is no older-row readiness check. Retrieval days are listed above. Source assessment slots remain: mid-unit assessments on days 9 and 13 for prepared discussion and literary reading; 19 and 24 for informational reading and an informative paragraph; and 28 for an explanatory paragraph and oral samples. Day 16 is an unmarked first writing sample used to plan instruction, not a readiness check. Respond lessons and immediate feedback follow these source assessments, with the next draft or reading using the result.

The end-of-unit assessment is spread across day 35, individual fluent story audio and a clarifying illustration; day 37, the revised contract, writing process and conventions; and day 36, listening and prepared-discussion evidence in six groups of five; and day 38, brief literary/informational/image/word-meaning transfers with the bookmark. Each code listed on these days has its own short prompt or individual product/performance evidence, including each lettered part. Day 38 uses short stations and existing process evidence, not another essay. The day 36 listening task uses words, images and a labelled bar graph in an original classroom library presentation. Day 30 supplies the unit word record. Day 35 closes the story-reading collection across days 31 to 35, 30 children at 3 minutes each, 18 teacher minutes daily. Day 28 oral groups practise. Day 38 reuses these records without new oral collection. Response time is counted once on day 39: returned evidence, one applied repair and regrouping. The unit's band reading samples show current performance; independent reading at the band's top remains a year-end requirement.

Same-year leans: social studies map skills are in its unit 1, school days 1 to 24, with no day-level completion assumed before the ELA hook. Day 1 teaches the frame's title-and-key map bridge in eight minutes within reading. Washington's historical context is supplied in the first eight minutes of day 26 because social studies unit 5 is school days 90 to 121. The teacher previews the short selection, names slavery and emancipation plainly, and asks about the selection. No child is asked to disclose family reading struggles. Children and places are named concretely and shown through effort, with no graphic danger or reenactment.

Candidate cells: keep 1g to 1n through the ten-minute daily sequence and recurring word checks; keep 1t and 1u through twice-weekly feedback and day 35's recording; keep 2i through day 1's taught routine and the four named discussions; keep 2e through the three different story settings and paired accounts. ## Unit 3. A chapter-book series

39 course days, 81 to 119, weeks 17 to 24. The question is whether to recommend this series and why. Days 81 to 92 read book 1 whole in twelve connected chapters, following how each chapter changes the narrative arc, with a short series-making article on day 82. Days 93 to 97 read five connected chapters of book 2. Days 98 to 106 compare theme, setting and plot, then write and revise a review and discuss recommendations. Days 107 to 119 read a two-scene script and a returning two-stanza poem, revise a scene and prepare a spoken report. The same-author comparison follows a whole-book reading; the opinion follows comparison; the report follows explicit instruction on script and delivery. Five newly owned rows require sustained reading and several performances, not five equal small blocks.

There is no readiness check on day 80 or 81: grade 2 rows are retrieved inside the unit. Mid-unit assessments on days 85 and 92 check the literary reading; day 99 checks the two books; day 106 checks the revised review and discussion; day 113 checks scene revision. Days 86, 93, 100 and 107 act on the corresponding evidence; day 114 planning uses the scene feedback. The end-of-unit assessment is split across day 114, individual chapter/scene/stanza, view and same-author transfers; day 115, review, title capitals, parts-of-speech functions and agreement evidence; days 117 and 118, unfamiliar multisyllable decoding followed by connected-text fluency, 15 children per day at 4 minutes each inside the 60-minute main block; and day 119, small-group scene reports with facts, descriptive details, clear pace and complete sentences. Day 115 reuses the review record from day 106 without rescoring it. On day 119 a learner names and applies a spoken/written difference as part of the report. Six groups of five at 7 minutes use 42 minutes, preparation 3, response 15, skills 30; reuse days 117 to 118 oral/decoding records. Response time is counted once in day 119's final 15 minutes with feedback, an applied change and reflection.

Social studies claim-and-evidence work was taught in its unit 1 by school day 24, before this unit. Day 98 still teaches ELA's opinion/reason structure directly. The series must have one author and recurring characters; the five-chapter book 2 selection must support all three comparison dimensions. The optional independent classic is optional, never evidence that all students read book 1. The contemporary conflict is handled gently and previewed by the teacher.

Candidate cells: keep 2a through book 1 read whole on days 81 to 92, commissioned near the lower end of the frame's length range, about 10,000 to 12,000 words; keep 2j through the explicit speaking model on day 108, rehearsals and the split performances on days 117 to 119. ## Grade 3 word-part inventory

Meaning/application instruction is spread across common affixes: retrieve the grade 2 reading/meaning of un-, re- and dis-, then extend them to longer bases; teach pre-, mis-, -ful, -less, -ness and -ly, plus -ment and the meanings/longer-base applications of the Latin suffixes. Retrieve grade 2 -able, -tion, -sion and -ous before new-base application; do not allocate a reteach week to a secure pattern. Weeks 3 and 4 retrieve un-/re-/dis- briefly before longer-base and meaning applications; week 5 and 6 teach pre-/mis-/-ful/-less; week 7 and 8 retrieve -able while adding -ness/-ly; week 9 and 10 apply retrieved -tion/-sion to longer bases; week 11 and 12 add -ment and extend -ous.
