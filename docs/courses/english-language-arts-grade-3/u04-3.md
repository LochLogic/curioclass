# Section 4.3: Research, discussion and the first check

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


This section's share: W.3.7 and W.3.8 "short research projects ... take brief notes on sources and sort evidence into provided categories" are taught on 128 (four named categories: access, demand, pollution, solutions) and used on 129 and 130; RI.3.2 on 128, 129 and 131 as "recount the key details and explain how they support the main idea"; RI.3.9 "compare and contrast the most important points and key details" on 129 (two texts, shared and single points) and scored on 131; SL.3.1a to SL.3.1d and SL.3.3 on 130 and 132 as the formal discussion with five boxes (prepared passage, norms, link to a peer, ask for detail, explain after listening); RI.3.1 on 131; L.3.1a "explain the function of nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs" and L.3.1f "ensure subject-verb and pronoun-antecedent agreement" on 131 and 132; RI.3.4 and L.3.6 on 132 with the word "issue"; W.3.1a to W.3.1d on 132 as O09.

Limits that bite: no more than twelve minutes of any discussion; each child speaks once in each round (2 minutes a turn, six groups of five). N06 and N07 are imagined episodes labeled as invented, not research notes. The Population Growth excerpt is read aloud by the teacher with its clauses unpacked; children do not decode its quantity words alone.

Connections: the formal discussion with five boxes (grade 3 u02), the note card with source tag, research question, relevant (u02-6), noun, pronoun, verb, adjective and adverb jobs and agreement (u03-1, u03-2), the opinion model (u03-5, u03-6). The teacher page must explain that "imagined" is shown by a label on the page and never goes in the notes.

## 3. Checks, written first


Items owned here: RI.3.9 15 minutes, RI.3.2 8 minutes and L.3.1a and L.3.1f 10 minutes on day 131 (P131A "The Repair List" and P131B "The Check List"; the unit plan owns their wording), with the rows listed above (RI.3.9 on 154 is scored with section 4.5's card). RI.3.1 on 131 (7 minutes) is written and owned in section 4.2's table. Marking is 2, 1, 0 on each item, unscored on 131.

Meets and does not, for 131's grammar item (a strip on P131B: "The careful workers inspect the pump slowly"): Meets: careful says what kind of workers, so it describes workers (an adjective); slowly says how they inspect, so it describes inspect (an adverb). Does not: "careful is an adjective" with no job (G); chooses "inspects" with "workers" (H); gives "it" for "workers" (I).

**Exit 128.** Unseen note: "Paint from a boat yard drips into a creek. The water turns cloudy." Ask: "Which category does this note go under: access, demand, pollution or solutions? Write it." Answer: pollution. Second card, X128b, Ask: "Write the word from the note that shows its category." Answer: "paint" (or "cloudy"). Wrong: access because of "creek" (AQ); a fact the note does not state, such as "fish died" (A).

**Exit 129 (s04-X129v2).** Water Paths in Kitui County, "More than distance", paragraph 1, sentences 2 and 3, printed on the slip (broken boreholes, so more people used working sources); not the population excerpt the day works. Ask: "Write one sentence that links the boreholes to the sources with so or because." Answer: some boreholes had broken down, so more people used the sources that still worked. Wrong: only restates "boreholes are deep wells" (C); reversed, so the crowds broke the boreholes (E).

**Exit 130.** A partner says: "Litter can hurt a stream." Ask: "Write one question to ask your partner for more detail." Answer: any question that asks for a detail, such as "What kind of harm do you mean?". Wrong: "Yes" or a yes-or-no question only (AJ); changes the subject to "I like swimming" (AH).

**Exit 131 (5 minutes).** Unseen strip: "Shade blocks sunlight. It steadily keeps the path cool." Ask: "Which word does 'It' stand for? Write it." Answer: Shade. Wrong: sunlight (I).

**Exit 132 (O09, replaces the usual exit, 10 minutes).** Unseen cards. A: "A tap in a village stays dry in the afternoons." B: "A pond near a farm has green slime on top." Ask: "Which issue should a class explain first? Write your opinion, two reasons from the cards, a link word and a closing sentence." Answer: either, supported by the card's words (A: people cannot reach water; B: the water looks unclean). Wrong: a shared point only (F); "I like B" (M); no closing (O); a claim not on the cards (A).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


- **F** Wrong: "A says water, B says water." Right: "Both say water is hard to use well; A says it is out of reach, B says it is dirty." Shown 132. If many: 7 minutes, rebuild one two-row table, shared row first.
- **G** Wrong: "'slowly' is an adverb." Right: "'slowly' tells how the workers inspect." Shown 132. If many: 6 minutes, each child says the job aloud before the name of the word class.
- **H** Wrong: "The workers inspects." Right: "The workers inspect; the worker inspects." Shown 132. If many: 6 minutes, find the subject, say singular or plural, match the verb.
- **I** Wrong: "It" for plural nouns. Right: "They" for plural nouns, "it" for one thing. Shown 132. If many: 6 minutes, draw an arrow from the pronoun back to its noun.
- **AP** Wrong: a note "Mina's well sang to her" filed under research. Right: the note is labeled "invented" and kept out of the notes. Shown 132 by the teacher. If many: 8 minutes, children label four lines "fact" or "invented", then repair one note.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Carried: note card with source tag (u02-6), formal discussion with five boxes (u02), heard-six column, partner rereading with a feedback card, Return and repair (commonest letter, at most four groups), the check day routine (u03-9). Instruments: a printed 6-box note sheet, a discussion record with five boxes per child, and a clean copy of each panel. Spec: 30 note sheets, 6 discussion records. A room with nothing uses a ruled paper note sheet and the teacher's own record held on a clipboard; no recording device is used here.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: "Which category does this note belong under, and why?" (128); "Which text gives more about pollution, and where does it say so?" (130); "Which issue should we explain first?" (132). Written tasks: N06 (128) and N07 (130), each a short imagined episode, labeled, one sitting. Look-fors: the label "invented" is on the page; the episode is tied to a book fact; one event; no research claim made from it. Discussion look-fors: the child brought a passage, linked to a peer by name, asked for a detail, explained after listening.

## 8. Practice that returns

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: a word strip of "access, demand, pollution, solutions" with pictures; sentence frames "I agree with ... because ..." and "Can you say more about ...?". Access: print each passage; allow a drawn episode for N06 and N07 with labels. Check accommodations: extra time on P131 (to 55 minutes of items), a scribe for answer lines. Reading load: the excerpt is read aloud with its clauses unpacked. Extension: write a third reason in O09 or link to two peers in discussion.

## 10. Two rooms

A parent teaching one child: read a passage aloud, sort three notes under the four categories, then talk for two minutes while the parent asks "Can you say more?" One projector: show the four-category sheet and the discussion norms; children work from shared print; groups talk in six places in the room.
