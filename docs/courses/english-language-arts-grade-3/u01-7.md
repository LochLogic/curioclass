# Section 1.7: The reading contract: a model, a plan, four drafted parts, and recording practice


## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.3.2a to d and W.3.4 (days 30 to 34):** "Introduce a topic and group related information together"; "Develop the topic with facts, definitions, and details"; "Use linking words and phrases"; "Provide a concluding statement or section"; writing in which "development and organization are appropriate to task and purpose". One contract (product I04): an introduction on 31, a first proof paragraph on 32 (a boat detail), a second on 33 (a bus detail), a conclusion on 34. It explains a reading strategy to a younger reader with two story details.
- **W.3.5 (days 30, 31):** "planning, revising, and editing". Plan on 30, draft on 31 to 34 with a peer look-for each day; written feedback and revision belong to days 36 and 37, in section 1.8. **W.3.10 (days 30 to 34):** the dated folder.
- **RF.3.3a, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 30 to 34):** day 30 is a word check on un-, re-, dis-, pre-, mis-, -ful and -less; week 7 teaches -ness and -ly (days 31, 32), then -able with the spelling rules for -ed and -ing (days 33, 34): drop a silent e (smiled, smiling) and double the last letter after a short vowel (sitting).
- **RF.3.4a to c (days 32, 34), SL.3.5 (day 34):** partner rereading with a feedback card; recording practice: say your purpose, read, reread one sentence more expressively, show a picture that clarifies one detail.
- **Limits that bite here.** Story details come only from g3-u1-01, 02 and 03; an added detail is a letter A. The contract is explanatory, not personal: no child writes about their own home, family or reading. Recording is classroom audio saved locally, with one shared device and no sharing outside the room.
- **Connections.** Grouping, links and closings return from section 1.5; the repair routine from 1.5 and 1.6; the goal card of day 29 supplies each child's strategy. The word record (RF.3.3a, RF.3.3d) is completed here on day 30.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How a contract differs from a report (it explains one strategy so another reader can use it); the five strategies and the boat and bus details that fit each; how to run six one-minute word samples and six three-minute recordings without a second adult; how to write a recording note that names a move; and the rules for -ed and -ing.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: section 1.6, ending day 29. The goal card (a strategy and a reason) and the day 28 feedback are the starting evidence. After: section 1.8, which opens on day 35 with recordings in small groups. The word check, the recordings and written notes go to the check sheet; the draft folder goes to section 1.8 for written feedback.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned.** Day 35, the four reading and recording rows RF.3.4a, RF.3.4b, RF.3.4c and SL.3.5 (three minutes in all), run by section 1.8 from the key above. What they sample is taught here: a stated purpose, a reread with better phrasing, a word confirmed by letters and context, and a picture that clarifies one detail (days 30, 32, 34). Day 37, the thirteen writing and spelling rows, run by section 1.8: the plan (day 30), the grouped drafts (31 to 34), the strategy and its definition, two story details, links, a conclusion and the dated folder are built here; the -ed and -ing rules are taught on day 33, with silent-e and doubled-letter words other than the dictation words. The dictation words, the word record list (enough, laugh, through, untwist, resend, hopeful, dislike, misplace) and their bases are kept out of all examples, retrieval and exits. The word record is completed here: six children at one minute each on day 30 (meets: reads both words and explains not and full of; miss: un- means again, AC; guessing laugh, P).

**Exit questions (E30 to E34).** One ask each, on a new case, unscored. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Strips print "invented for practice".
- **E30 (day 30).** Strip: "Before she passes the ball, Noor looks to see which teammate is open. She sees Ines by the goal. Noor passes to Ines." Ask: "Write one sentence that names this strategy for a younger reader, and one question a peer could ask to make it clearer." Answer: for example, "Looking before you pass is a strategy, a plan for a game" and "What does strategy mean?". Wrong: only "Noor looks. Noor passes." with no name or explanation (K); a fact the strip does not give, such as a coach (A).
- **E31 (day 31).** Strip: "Kofi sets his football boots by the door. He puts his water bottle inside one boot. In the morning he picks up both and goes." Ask: "Write one sentence that gives a reason for getting ready the night before, using both details." Answer: any reason both details support, such as "so he can pick up his boots and his bottle at once". Wrong: the two details listed with no reason (H); a reason the strip does not support, such as the coach letting him play (A).
- **E32 (day 32).** Strip: "Before the trip, Rosa checks the packing list on the fridge. She takes the red torch from the shelf and puts it in her bag." Ask: "Write two sentences for a younger reader: what a packing list is, and how checking it helps Rosa." Answer: a list of things to bring; checking it helps her pack the right thing. Wrong: "She checks. She packs." (K); adding that the list tells her the day (A).
- **E33 (day 33).** Strip: "Tomas checks the time on the wall card. He puts his towel in his bag. Then he sets off for the pool." Ask: "Write two sentences that explain how these facts help Tomas swim on time. Use also or so." Answer: for example, "Tomas checks the wall card, so he knows when to leave. Also, he packs his towel, so he is ready when he gets to the pool." Wrong: "Also" joining a fact the strip lacks (I); an added detail such as running (A).
- **E34 (day 34).** Strip: "Pia ties a ribbon round her recorder and puts it in its case. Her brother hangs the case on the hook by the door." Ask: "Write a closing sentence for a contract about getting ready to practise again, using this detail." Answer: the ribbon and the hook make the recorder easy to find, so she is ready next time. Wrong: a closing about the weather (J); only restating the strip (K).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The model contract is the teacher's on day 30. From day 30 each child holds their own plan (strategy, definition, two details, link, closing, a feedback question) and a dated folder, with their chosen passage and display from day 33. The recording device is shared and returns to the teacher at the end of each day. The goal card of day 29 starts the plan. Drafts and notes go on in section 1.8.
- **Key moment 1, day 30: a model contract with its parts marked.** Ideal instrument: a printed model beside a marking key. Spec: fits; print.
- **Key moment 2, days 31 to 34: audio recording with a purpose, a reread and a picture.** Ideal instrument: a recorder children can play back. Spec: the recording device (light) fits; one shared device, local recordings, no sharing outside the room. A room with nothing records by reading aloud to the teacher with a written note; the check on day 35 then uses the same note.
- **One word, one sense.** "Strategy" means one plan for reading; "stop" is a place where a carrier or bus halts. Say so aloud if a child mixes them.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper, pencils, printed pictures and strips, a dictionary.

## 6. Days

## Day 30


- **10 min. Opening.** Retrieval of affix meaning: cards for un-, re-, dis-, pre-, mis-, -ful and -less; each child says each meaning and one word on a slate, checked as a class (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Ten minutes: the day 30 word check, taking the whole word slot. Dictation of eight taught-pattern words and two sentences, and two meaning questions, written by everyone (7 minutes); during it the teacher hears six children at one minute each for the word record (the last rotation). Ten minutes: two reading conferences of 5 minutes while the rest read from their logs. Results regroup the pattern teaching on day 31.
- **20 min. Text and model.** Four minutes: the teacher reads g3-u1-01 aloud (502 story words, about 4 minutes). Ten minutes: the teacher reads and marks a model contract: "Taking Small Steps. A strategy is a plan that helps you do something. Taking small steps is a strategy for a hard task. It means doing one small piece at a time. Lia walks the long boards one slow step at a time, and she reaches the boat. Also, Sam counts the stone walls one by one, and he reaches the book bus. Both children got there because they did not try to do everything at once. When a book looks hard, I will read one page, then the next. Could a younger reader follow my explanation?" Parts marked: topic, definition, detail, link, detail, conclusion, question. Six minutes: the teacher shows the recording routine: states a purpose, reads two sentences of page 7, rereads one with more feeling, and shows the picture that clarifies a detail; children say what they heard. Nobody records today.
- **25 min. Hand work.** Five minutes: reread pages 2 to 7 and the day 29 goal card. Ten minutes: each child fills a plan on the frame: strategy, definition, one boat detail with its page, one bus detail with its page from their memory of the stories and the class chart, link word, closing idea, one feedback question. Ten minutes: partners check each other's plan against the model's parts.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on their plan and 2 minutes on one feedback question (8 minutes); then the class lists good feedback questions (2 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E30.

Carried: the teacher holds the model and the recording routine; children hold their plans. Tool: the recording device (light), for the teacher's demonstration only, 2 minutes. By hand: the word check, plan, exit. Evidence: E30; the word check; six word samples. Hand-backs in: E29 (opening); the day 28 results are already returned. Out: E30 and the word check to day 31's opening; the plan to day 31. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the model aloud to an adult and say which part is the definition; then read any book together.

## Day 31


- **10 min. Opening.** Retrieval of the spelling pattern missed most often on the day 30 word check: a three-minute reteach on fewer words, then a five-word dictation and a sentence (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Eight minutes: week 7 sequence, -ness (state of being) on kind and sad, read and spell. Twelve minutes: independent reading from the logs, with two conferences of 4 minutes.
- **40 min. Hand work.** Five minutes: reread the plan and the page of the boat detail. Twenty-five minutes: draft the introduction (topic sentence naming the strategy, a definition, and the reasons grouped into two groups). Five minutes: reread aloud to oneself. Five minutes: date and file the draft. During the 40 minutes the shared device is with the teacher for 24: six children are recorded for 3 minutes each (purpose, read 60 to 80 words of a story passage of their choice, reread one sentence, show a picture) and six children, recorded on a later day, take a one-minute turn each to practise a rerecording sentence. Everyone else writes. No writing conferences today.
- **15 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child reads the introduction aloud, 2 minutes, and the partner answers the look-for card (is the strategy named? are the reasons grouped?), 2 minutes (8 minutes); the class lists "what a younger reader needs" (7 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E31.

Carried: children hold their plans and drafts. Tool: the recording device (light), 24 minutes inside hand work, teacher run, local files. By hand: the introduction and exit. Evidence: E31; six recordings (written note after class, about 2 minutes each). Hand-backs in: E30 and the word check. Out: E31 to day 32's opening; the six written recording notes to day 32. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread your introduction to an adult and ask if the strategy is clear; then read together.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 30: "Which part of the model tells a younger reader what the strategy is, and which tells how it works?" Day 32: "How does your detail show the strategy at work?" Day 34: "What does your conclusion add that the details did not say?"
- **Written task, days 31 to 34.** The reading contract: a strategy explained to a younger reader with two story details, in four dated parts.
- **Look-fors, six.** Topic names the strategy; reasons or facts grouped; a definition where a younger reader needs one; a link word joining facts of one group; a conclusion that says what the details show; a dated folder. Recorded on the shown, shown with help or not yet seen scale; the first feedback is written, on day 36.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 30:** affix meaning retrieval. **Day 31:** the word check's missed pattern; the grouping and links of section 1.5. **Day 32:** phrase reading. **Day 33:** the possessive. **Day 34:** phrase boundaries. The word-part sequence continues daily.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on rereading; no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview strategy, definition, conclusion, purpose, practice and improve with a picture; frames "My strategy is ___. It means ___. For example, ___." and "The words ___ show ___."; an oral first draft; a recording in a shared language where it exists, and an English one beside it.
- **Access.** Large print; strips read aloud; a scribe for the content of the draft and not for spelling evidence; a child who cannot be recorded reads aloud to the teacher and gets a written note.
- **Check accommodations.** E30 to E34 and the word check may be read aloud except where reading is the evidence; record the help. A child may speak an exit.
- **Reading load.** Alone: strips under 45 words, a 90-word part of a story and their own drafts. Read aloud: the stories and the model.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second strategy with its own details and show how the two work together.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult is the partner and the listener; use the phone or a tape recorder, or just listen. Write the contract in four sittings, one part a day. Record the word check by hand.
- **One projector.** Show the model contract, the stories and the pictures; keep the exit strips on paper until needed.
