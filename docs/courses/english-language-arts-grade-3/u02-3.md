## 3. Checks, written first


**Day 51 items (35 minutes).** Silent; read aloud only where decoding is not the evidence, with support recorded. One scale per component, 0, 1 or 2, on the s02 keys. Plan point: the unit plan's row 51B names cautious in Under the Leaf, but that poem has none; 51B uses a short note of ours, printed "invented for practice".
- **51A (5 minutes), RF.3.4a, b, c and SL.3.5.** Each child writes a reading note beside the saved clip: a purpose sentence and a scene sentence about Under the Leaf, then one decoded word, the clue that checked it and the reread. The display is the drawn labeled leaf. Meets: "I help a listener picture a hidden frog waiting under leaves"; phrases kept whole in the clip; a labeled leaf. Not yet: cannot explain the scene (H); races through the ending (I); guesses a word from the picture (J); an unrelated mountain display (K).
- **51B (10 minutes), L.3.5b and L.3.5c.** Note, invented for practice: "The frog under the leaf is cautious. It waits and watches before it hops out. I heard a plop beyond the leaves and could not see what made it, but I thought it was a pebble." Ask 1: tell a real thing a person does that is cautious and quote the line that matches. Meets: looks before crossing; "waits and watches". Not yet: "Cautious means loud" (D). Ask 2: choose wondered, suspected or knew for the speaker and quote the line. Meets: suspected; the speaker did not see it. Not yet: all three mean sure (E).
- **51C (20 minutes), L.3.1d, L.3.1e and L.3.1g.** The language card, taking only its verb, tense and comparison parts, on the reed and the pond. Write sentences that match their source. Ask 1: put swim and leap into past actions. Meets: swam, leaped. Not yet: swimmed (L). Ask 2: put croak into the past, present and future. Meets: croaked, croaks, will croak. Not yet: "It croak yesterday" (M). Ask 3: compare a taller and the tallest reed, and two ways of croaking with loudly. Meets: a taller reed, the tallest reed; more loudly, most loudly. Not yet: "more loudlier" (N).

**Days 52 and 76 rows.** The W.3.3b, W.3.3c, W.3.3d, L.3.3a, W.3.10 and L.3.2c rows are run by sections 2.4 and 2.9. Here they are taught on other cases. Plan points: day 50's exit sample answer (whispered) and the day 52 and 76 key (whispered, shouted) share a pair, so the 2.4 writer should vary its pair.

**Exit questions (E48 to E51).** One ask each, on a new case printed "invented for practice". Unscored; shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- **E48.** Card: "Three frogs heard a crack. One spoke. The other two moved." Ask: Write one sentence that compares how the other two moved. Use more or most. Answer: The second frog moved more quietly than the first. Wrong: the form for three or more used for two (N); an invalid comparison form (N).
- **E49.** Card: "The frog found a shaded pool. The bank was dry. Its friend needed shade." Ask: Write the resolution in two or three sentences. Start with a time word, use and in one sentence, and use because or when in another. Answer: First the frog found a shaded pool, and its friend swam in. Then they rested because the bank was dry. Wrong: because joins unrelated ideas (AB); a dependent clause used as a sentence (AG); the events out of order (O).
- **E50.** Card: "Frog shouted softly to avoid waking her friend." Peer note: Make the action fit the effect. Asks, one each: (a) Rewrite the sentence with a verb that fits the peer note. Answer: Frog murmured to avoid waking her friend. (any quiet verb). (b) Say why your new verb fits. Answer: Murmured is quiet, so it fits not waking her friend. Wrong: the sentence copied without the change (AC); a loud verb kept for a quiet effect (R); a word said to give the opposite effect (R); no reason from the effect (x).
- **E51.** Card: "Show how the leaf helps me understand where the frog waits." Asks, one each: (a) Point to one label on your drawn leaf that shows where the frog waits, and read it. Answer: A label on the leaf, such as "frog waits here, under the leaf". (b) Say one phrase from your recording that shows where the frog waits. Answer: A phrase heard in the clip, such as "beneath it waits a small brown frog". Wrong: a label that does not show the hiding place (K); a phrase that does not say where the frog waits (x).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Children carry the dated folder (plan, draft, peer note, teacher note, revision) from day 48 to day 52, and the display and reading note on day 51. The teacher holds the recording list, the saved clips, the record sheet and the check papers.
- **Key moment 1, days 49 and 50: five children record, 3 minutes each.** Ideal instrument: a recording device (the light tool from unit 1). Spec: fits; local only, saved without accounts, one child at a time while the rest read or reread. The fifteen minutes are the whole slot, so the teacher hears no pairs on those days.
- **Key moment 2, day 51: a saved clip beside a reading note and a labeled leaf.** Ideal instrument: the clip, playable by the teacher alone later. Spec: fits; no oral queue and no replay in class. Gaps and asks: none (see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **A room with nothing.** The child reads the poem to an adult, who notes pauses and writes a one-line fluency note; the child's reading note and drawn display still go in the folder.

## 6. Days

## Day 48


- **10 min. Long words.** Three minutes repair E47's commonest letter. Week 10: read and spell -tion and -sion on expression, permission and imagination; map the irregular word other from the strip (7 minutes).
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Four conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs (16 minutes); four minutes for a log line. Add the four to the heard-six column.
- **15 min. Reading and reread.** Three minutes retrieving a descriptive word: write one word for how Kikker looks. Hard-part reread, in pairs: how does tiny change across the three frogs? Pairs reread the Ranna tale's lines about Drab, Ash and Grey and point to the words (7 minutes); the class names them (5 minutes).
- **10 min. Word and sentence work.** The teacher models comparing with -er and -est on tiny and bright, and with more and most on quickly (6 minutes); children fill a three-row slate table of adjective, comparative, superlative (4 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes a child: read the problem in your tale and the partner says what the response shows (4 minutes). Three volunteers, 2 minutes each (6 minutes).
- **20 min. Writing.** Revise the problem and the response in the draft: one spoken line with speech marks, one description with a comparison word (20 minutes).
- **Shared shape.** The model, worked example and practice here share the shape of E48 (rewrite the strip) and of its check item; the exit and the check item use new cases, so none of them is shown or practiced on the same case.
- **5 min. Exit.** E48.

Carried: the draft stays in the folder with a dated note. Tool: None. By hand: slate table, the revision, exit. Evidence: E48. Hand-backs in: E47. Out: E48 to day 49's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the revised problem aloud and ask an adult what the response shows. No score.

- **Data note.** E48 uses more with an adverb; the day 48 model (tiny, bright, quickly) differs in case.

## Day 50


- **10 min. Long words.** Three minutes repair E49's commonest letter. Week 10: read and spell -tion and -sion on confession, version and conclusion; map through (7 minutes).
- **20 min. Independent reading and recording.** The teacher records five children at 3 minutes each (15 minutes, the sixth window, so all 30 have been recorded once); the rest read from their logs; the last 5 minutes everyone writes a log line. The four conferences are not held today.
- **15 min. Reading and reread.** Three minutes retrieving verbs: change jump and catch to past forms. Hard-part reread, in pairs: which precise verb changes the reader's picture? Pairs reread tiptoeing in Under the Leaf and snapped in Kikker's tale and say what each shows (7 minutes); class share (5 minutes).
- **10 min. Word and sentence work.** The teacher models choosing between stomped and tiptoed for a frog who sneaks, and reads the same sentence both ways (5 minutes); children choose a verb for a stated effect on four cards (5 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Groups of three, 3 minutes a child: read one paragraph of the draft; the next speaker links a question to what was said, then the reader notes one change (9 minutes). The teacher circulates; the heard-six column is not used. One minute to hand back the notes.
- **20 min. Writing.** Apply the peer note and the teacher's written note: change one verb for the effect and keep yesterday's time words clear; write one sentence on why the new verb fits (20 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E50.

Carried: the folder with the peer note, the teacher's note and the revision; the clips stay with the teacher. Tool: recording device (light), five children, 3 minutes each. By hand: verb cards, the revised verb, exit. Evidence: E50; the discussion moves in groups of three (SL.3.1b, c), practice only. Hand-backs in: E49 and the teacher's note on each draft. Out: E50 to day 51's opening; the folder to day 52. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the revised sentence aloud with the old verb and the new verb and say which fits. No score.

- **Data note.** E50 uses shouted softly, then murmured as the answer; the model uses stomped and tiptoed, and the effect-verb cards use four other verbs.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 48: "How does tiny change across the three frogs?" Day 49: "What makes the escape sequence coherent?" Day 50: "Which precise verb changes the reader's picture?"
- **Written task, day 50.** One revised sentence with a verb that fits the effect, and one sentence on why it fits.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors:** a verb that fits the effect, the effect named, time words still clear, a change that matches the note. Recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 48:** descriptive words; -tion and -sion. **Day 49:** sentence building; time words. **Day 50:** past forms of jump and catch; peer discussion moves. **Day 51:** nothing new; silent retrieval and the language card. The week 10 words return daily; day 51 opens week 11.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on successive readings; saved clips are judged by phrasing, no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview crack, escape, tiny, tinier, tiniest, shade and listener with pictures; frame "The word ___ shows ___"; a partner who shares a language; an oral first draft of the display note.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of each picture; a scribe for ideas on notes and displays, not for spelling or decoding evidence; a child who will not record may read to the teacher and the support is noted.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 51 items read aloud except where decoding is the evidence; record every help. Extra time on day 51, recorded.
- **Reading load.** Alone: a poem or 150 to 250 words, the 51B note and the 51C card. Longer tales are heard first.
- **Extension, same standard.** Choose a verb for the opposite effect and say what changes.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the partner role and the peer's: listen, ask one linked question, say what became clearer. A phone or computer may record locally; paper alone leaves SL.3.5 unfinished. Day 51 goes on paper, silent.
- **One projector.** Show the tale and the comparison table; keep the exit strips and day 51 items on paper.
