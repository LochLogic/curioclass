## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.3.3a, W.3.3b, W.3.3c, W.3.3d, W.3.4 (day 52):** the fresh tale, in one sitting: "Establish a situation and introduce a narrator and/or characters; organize an event sequence that unfolds naturally"; dialogue and actions, thoughts and feelings; temporal words; closure; organization for the task and purpose. Nothing is taught that day.
- **L.3.3a, L.3.2c (day 52):** one word chosen for a stated effect and explained; commas and quotation marks in dialogue.
- **W.3.10 (days 52, 53):** the dated folder beside the fresh sitting (52B), then routine writing on day 53.
- **RI.3.1, RI.3.4, L.3.6 (day 53):** "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers"; the meaning of a domain word (habitat) from the book's own definition; the first informational reading of the unit. The gist is stated from the text's words.
- **RF.3.4a, RF.3.4b, RF.3.4c (day 53), RF.3.3b, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 52, 53):** day 52 is retrieval of -tion and -sion only; day 53 teaches -ment with its meaning (the first teaching of week 11); irregular words come from the day's text.
- **Limits that bite here.** The day 52 tale is invented and printed "invented for practice"; each child chooses a frog feature that is not the one in the day 52 exit. No graphic feeding scene. The frog book's one plain feeding line (page 4) is not read aloud on day 53. The opening section is read, not the whole book.
- **Connections.** The folder from day 51 comes here; the tale and its note go on to day 56 for revision. The book section carries to 2.5 (headings and index on day 54).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to give the tale without help and keep the folder untouched; how to write one narrative note per tale (about 45 minutes outside class, the evening of day 52); how to tell a topic from a gist.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Day 52 items.** The tale (40 minutes, silent), then the folder (5 minutes), 10 minutes of silent verification and the exit. Marking scale: 0, 1 or 2 on the s02 keys, scored once after feedback on day 53 and revision on day 56; the same score is carried to day 76 without rescoring.
- **52A (40 minutes), W.3.3a, b, c, d, L.3.3a, W.3.4, L.3.2c.** Reread both model tales (8 minutes), plan (4 minutes), then write (28 minutes): invent a different explanation for a frog feature, with a narrator, a situation, connected events, dialogue, action, thought, feeling, time links and a closing; then name one word you chose for an effect and explain it. Printed "invented for practice". Meets: a narrator and situation, then ordered events (W.3.3a); "Help," I cried, and an action and a feeling (W.3.3b); First, after that, finally in order (W.3.3c); a closing that names the feature (W.3.3d); a word for a stated effect with the reason (L.3.3a); a situation, a change and an outcome (W.3.4); "Wait," said Frog (L.3.2c). Not yet: arrives before setting out (O); only a list of actions (P); ends with an unrelated adventure (Q); claims a word contradicts its effect (R); an unexplained list (S); Wait said Frog (U).
- **52B (5 minutes), W.3.10.** The child sets the fresh tale beside the dated folder. Meets: dated responses, the independent tale and the revised earlier tale show different lengths and purposes. Not yet: only a copied final is kept (T). Day 42's first plan is unscored.

**Exit questions (E52, E53).** One ask each, on a new case. Unscored.
- **E52.** Card: "A frog's feet left round marks in wet clay." Asks, one each: (a) Write one sentence that gives the situation for a tale explaining these marks. Answer: A frog crossed a soft wet bank at dusk. (b) Write one sentence that closes a tale explaining these marks. Answer: That is why her feet leave round marks. Wrong: words with no situation (S); an ending on an unrelated topic (Q).
- **E53 (s02-X53v2).** Frog book page 1, the paragraph that starts "Frogs live almost everywhere," read aloud twice. Ask: Say in one sentence what this paragraph is mostly about. Answer: Frogs live almost everywhere on Earth, in many kinds of places. Wrong: a topic given as the main idea (B); a fact the paragraph does not give (A).

## 6. Days

## Day 52


- **30 min. Skills (retrieval only).** Ten minutes: retrieve -tion and -sion words, no new pattern. Five minutes: grammar retrieval, comparisons on slates. Fifteen minutes: partner rereading of the Kikker tale, no recording and no new teaching.
- **40 min. Tale.** Silent. Reread both model tales (8 minutes), plan (4 minutes), write (28 minutes). Printed "invented for practice".
- **5 min. Folder.** 52B: the child sets the fresh tale beside the dated folder and marks which pages are dated.
- **10 min. Silent verification.** Children reread their tale; the teacher answers nothing about content and gives no hint.
- **5 min. Exit.** E52.

Carried: the tale and the folder go to the teacher; E52 stays in the folder. Tool: None. By hand: the tale, the folder, verification, exit. Evidence: 52A and 52B (field 3); E52. Hand-backs in: E51. Out: the tale to the teacher for a note on the evening of day 52; E52 to day 53. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book. No score.

- **Data note.** E52 asks two things in the plan; the data splits it into X52 (situation) and X52b (closing). The case is wet clay marks, different from the tale prompt on the item sheet (a bumpy back).

## Day 53


- **10 min. Long words.** Three minutes repair E52's commonest letter. Week 11 opens: teach -ment (the act or result of) on movement, treatment and statement; read and spell on slates; map the irregular word Earth from the book (7 minutes).
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Four conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs (16 minutes); four minutes for a log line. Add the four to the heard-six column.
- **10 min. Feedback.** The teacher hands back the tale with one written note (3 minutes); children mark the one change the note asks for (3 minutes); the commonest letter is repaired on the board (4 minutes).
- **20 min. Written repair.** Each child makes the change the note names in a margin version of one part of the tale; copying without the change does not count (AC) (20 minutes). The whole-tale revision is on day 56.
- **10 min. Reread.** The teacher reads the opening section of Frogs: Bodies and Homes aloud, Frogs Live in Many Places (about 450 words, 4 minutes), without the figure notes. Hard-part reread, in pairs: how do bodies and homes belong together? Pairs reread the two paragraphs on a stream frog and a tree frog and point to the words (4 minutes); class share (2 minutes).
- **10 min. Word and sentence work.** Habitat: read the definition sentence in the book; each child writes a sentence about a frog with habitat in it and says it to a partner (6 minutes); the teacher models changing a topic into a complete idea, Frogs to A frog's body and home go together (4 minutes).
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs, 1 minute a child: say the gist of the section in one sentence; the partner asks which words show it (5 minutes).
- **Shared shape.** The model, worked example and practice here share the shape of E53 (say in one sentence what the passage is mostly about) and of its check item; the exit and the check item use new cases, so none of them is shown or practiced on the same case.
- **5 min. Exit.** E53.

Carried: the tale, the note and the repair page stay in the folder; the book section is kept for day 54. Tool: None. By hand: slate words, the margin repair, habitat sentence, exit. Evidence: E53; the repair page. Hand-backs in: E52 and the tale with its note. Out: the folder and the tale to day 56; the book to day 54. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the opening section aloud to an adult and say in one sentence what it is mostly about. No score.

- **Data note.** E53 is on a bat passage whose facts are cut to what the public sources support (many bats rest in caves by day, most are active at night).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 52: "What changes first, and what does the ending explain?" Day 53: "How do bodies and homes belong together?"
- **Written task, day 52.** The fresh tale, in one sitting, with one word chosen for an effect and explained.
- **Judged by the teacher with look-fors:** a situation and narrator, ordered events with time words, dialogue with marks, closure naming the feature, a word and its effect. Recorded by component, 0, 1 or 2.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 52:** -tion and -sion; comparisons; the story map in the planning step. **Day 53:** the Kikker note, verb forms in the repair, the habitat idea from the unit's poems and tales. The word and sentence work returns the week 11 words daily.
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on successive readings, no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview habitat, stream, tree and conditions with pictures; frames "The words ___ explain ___" and "This section is mostly about ___ and ___"; say the gist aloud before writing; a partner who shares a language.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of any figure; a scribe for the tale, with spelling and punctuation left as the child writes them where those are the evidence; extra time on day 52, recorded.
- **Check accommodations.** The tale may be dictated except where conventions are the evidence; record any help. Models may be read aloud.
- **Reading load.** Alone: a poem or 150 to 250 words. The opening section is about 450 words, read aloud first, then reread in two chunks.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write the gist of the section in two ways, a short one and a longer one, and say which keeps more of the text.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult takes the partner role: read the note aloud, ask which one change the note asks for, say what became clearer. Day 52 goes on paper, silent, with the models on a printed page.
- **One projector.** Show the model tales and the book section; keep the day 52 tale and the exits on paper.
