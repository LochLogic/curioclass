Unscored. The exact strips are in items.csv and these lines match it. If many miss one, the next day's opening repair uses that letter's wrong example.
- **E105 (s03-X105).** Strip: A reader says, "The Boat Club and Sled Club books are the same. Both have a club." Boat Club, page 6: Orin mends a torn sail alone. Sled Club, page 9: Wren asks three friends to hold the ropes. Ask: Write one sentence you could say to this reader. Use a page. Answer: On page 6 Orin mends the sail alone, but on page 9 Wren asks friends to help, so the books differ. Wrong: They are different (M); They are the same because both have a club (P).
- **E106 (s03-X106).** Strip: A review says, "I recommend the Moon Club because it is a good series." In book 1, Pia shares her map when a friend gets lost. Ask: Write one new reason for the review. Use what happens in the book. Answer: I recommend the Moon Club because Pia shares her map when a friend gets lost. Wrong: I recommend it because it is a good series (I); Pia is in the Moon Club (H).
- **E107 (s03-X107v2).** The script: scene 1, Ada: "by myself"; scene 2, her last line, "we checked it together" (the day works Ben's question). Ask: How does the second scene answer the first? Answer: in scene 1 Ada wants to fix the plan alone, but in scene 2 it works because they checked it together. Wrong: the plan works, then Ada says she will start again (B); They talked (T).
- **E107b (s03-X107bv2).** Leaf Boats, both stanzas, on the reserved P107 panel (the day works Pond Voices). Ask: How does the second stanza develop the first? Answer: in stanza 1 the leaf rests alone by the stone, and in stanza 2 the rain lifts it so it sails. Wrong: the leaf sails, then it rests (B); It is about a leaf (T).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Letters this section meets; if many made it, 5 to 10 minutes of repair.
- **M, no preparation.** Wrong: "I did not mark a page." Right: "On page 12 Ada says she is sorry, so I recommend it." Shown 105. Repair: mark one passage before taking a turn; 5 minutes.
- **N, interrupting.** Wrong: speaking before the speaker ends. Right: wait for the end, then use the turn cue. Shown 105. Repair: rehearse the cue in the group, one pass; 5 minutes.
- **O, an unrelated question.** Wrong: What is your favorite food? Right: Which page shows that? Shown 105. Repair: build the question from the speaker's last word; 5 minutes.
- **P, repeating the first idea.** Wrong: "I still think the same." Right: "Your page helped me see why she changes." Shown 105 and 106. Repair: name one peer detail that strengthened the idea; 5 minutes.
- **Q, a vague answer.** Wrong: Stuff happens. Right: She unfolds the plan, so Ben can check it. Shown 105 and 107. Repair: replace stuff with the action and why it matters; 5 minutes.
- **L, recopying as revision.** Wrong: the same sentences in neater writing. Right: one added explanation from the note, original beside. Shown 105 and 107. Repair: model one margin change; 5 minutes.
- **I and J, reasons and links.** Wrong: I recommend it because it is good; Therefore the beans are green. Right: I recommend it because Ada asks the readers; so the plan improves. Shown 106 (audit). Repair: read both ideas and choose the link; 7 minutes.
- **AJ, AG and AH, word check.** Wrong: friend read as fried; agen for again; runing for running. Right: the letters mapped to the sound; double the n after a short vowel. Shown 105 (word check) and 106 (audit). Repair: in the next skills session, on fewer words; 5 minutes.
- **B, reversed parts.** Wrong: "Scene 2 comes first, then scene 1." Right: "Scene 1 asks the question; scene 2 answers it." Shown 107. Repair: number the earlier part before the later; 5 minutes.
- **S and T, no situation and a thin response.** Wrong: "They talked." Right: "Beside the torn notice, Ada said she would find the other half and ran to look." Shown 107. Repair: name who is where, then add a line or an action; 6 minutes.

## 6. Days

## Day 105


- **10 min. Word check.** Thirty children at twenty seconds each, called one at a time to the teacher's table (10 minutes); the class meanwhile writes six dictated words (said, because, sitting, smiled, cries, happiness), writes stopped, hoping and happiness from stop, hope and happy and names each base change, reads a short passage silently, and answers two affix-meaning questions. At the table each child reads four irregular words (said, does, were, their) and one longer word; the teacher records each reading.
- **20 min. Independent reading and conferences.** Log day: four 4-minute conferences (16) and two log lines read aloud at 2 minutes each (4); the heard-six column is filled. No new teaching.
- **5 min. Read the note.** E104 comes back with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). The draft comes back with the written note from 104; each child marks the one change to make (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Roles and preparation.** The discussion moves chart is read (mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer). Teacher models one linked question (2); children choose a question frame (3).
- **10 min. Prepare.** Children mark one passage in each book (150 to 250 words each, reread in 3 minutes), then write a prepared claim that answers "Would you recommend this series to a friend, and why?" with the detail and its page, and one linked question for a neighbor (7).
- **5 min. Hard-part reread.** Children reread one marked passage and note what a speaker could point to.
- **25 min. Revise and rehearse (writing 20, talk 5).** Everyone revises the review from the note, one change at a time, original beside. Six groups of five come to the teacher's table for four minutes each (24 minutes). Each child gets about 48 seconds: claim and detail (15 seconds), a neighbor's linked question (10), the answer (15), what it clarified (8). The teacher records five boxes. A late group is not extended.
- **5 min. Word in context.** Evidence: one sentence about a book and what the sentence shows about the word.
- **5 min. Exit.** E105.

- **Shared shape.** The prepared claim card shares its shape with s03-X105 (SL.3.1a): a claim that points to a page. The exit case (two club series) is not in either book or the rehearsal.

Carried: the revised review with the original beside, the prepared card and the rehearsal record. Tool: None. By hand: the revision, the prepared card, the word sheet, the exit. Evidence: revised review, individual rehearsal record (five boxes, unscored), word-check record, E105. Hand-backs in: the 104 draft with its note; E104. Out: the word sheet and rehearsal record to the teacher for a written note that evening; E105. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then tell someone at home whether you would give a friend this series, and one reason. No score.

## Day 106


- **30 min. Skills, retrieval only.** The 105 word record comes back with its note (6 minutes): pairs read the four irregular words to each other from the corrected card (2), rewrite the six dictated words (2) and write stopped, hoping and happiness with the base change named (2). Four minutes of ending-rule retrieval on slates. E105 returns with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). Eighteen minutes of independent reading from the log book, no conferences, no teaching.
- **25 min. Audit and discussion window.** Silent, with the folder. Everyone completes the audit card (21 minutes, directions read silently in the first 5): the introduction, the opinion, the reasons, the links, the closing, the title, and the plan, draft, note and revision laid side by side. Six groups of five come to the teacher's table for four minutes each (24 minutes): the revised exchange from 105, five boxes recorded. A child's own time is 21 at the desk and 4 at the table.
- **15 min. Edit and publish.** Children copy the revised review in a clear hand, checking capitals, the title, spelling and end marks against the card. The 104 draft and the 105 revision stay beside it.
- **10 min. Silent verification.** Each child rereads the published copy against the audit card and the title rule card and fixes up to two misses in the margin.
- **5 min. Collection.** Plan, draft, question, note, revision, published review and audit card go in the folder and are collected (2 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E106 (3 minutes of writing, 2 to hand in).

- **Shared shape.** Audit row 2 (the reason that supports the opinion) shares its shape with s03-X106 (W.3.1b): a reason that names an event. The exit case (the Moon Club) is not on the card or in the folder.

Carried: the dated folder goes to the teacher with the audit card; the printed script and poem are not out yet. Tool: None. By hand: the audit card, the published review, the exit. Evidence: the audit card, the discussion record (five boxes), the word record, O02 published (counted once across 101 to 106), E106. Hand-backs in: the 105 word sheet and rehearsal note; E105. Out: all to the teacher; scores and a written note are returned on 107. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book; no writing needed. No score.

## Day 107


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper. Week 22: per-form-ance, care-ful-ly, di-rec-tion. Irregular: break, great. One line of ending-rule retrieval.
- **15 min. Partner rereading with a feedback card.** Pairs reread 100 to 150 words of scene 2 of the script aloud, a part each, saying the lines and leaving the bracketed directions unspoken (12 minutes, 3 to change over). No speed.
- **5 min. Grammar retrieval.** Pairs say the job of the underlined words in two narrator sentences from the script.
- **5 min. Returned feedback.** The audit card and the written note come back; each child marks one explanation to revise.
- **10 min. Return and repair after the check.** Whole class, 2 minutes: one wrong example from the 106 audit (the commonest letter) beside its correction. At most four teacher groups by letter, 1.5 minutes each (6 minutes); everyone else applies the same move in pairs. Close in pairs, 2 minutes.
- **25 min. Reading.** The script, both scenes, 12 minutes: the teacher and four volunteers read scene 1 and then scene 2 aloud, any child may pass, and the class follows the brackets silently to hear where the directions fall. Pond Voices, 6 minutes: the teacher reads it aloud, pairs reread a stanza each. Link teaching and annotation, 7 minutes: scene and stanza are parts; scene 2 acts on the question asked in scene 1; stanza 2 of Pond Voices follows from stanza 1. Children annotate one link in each form in the margin.
- **10 min. Revise one explanation.** One note, one change, the original beside, on the explanation marked above.
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes each, 1 to change: say how scene 2 builds on scene 1.
- **5 min. Exit.** E107, the new exchange, which replaces the usual response and adds no task.

- **Shared shape.** The scene and stanza link teaching shares its shape with s03-X107v2 and s03-X107bv2 (RL.3.5): the later part answers or develops the earlier one. The exits read script lines and the P107 poem, not the lines or the poem the teaching works.

Carried: the annotated script and poem go in the folder with the revised explanation. Tool: None. By hand: the revised explanation, the annotations, the exchange. Evidence: three-form annotation (chapter from before, scene, stanza), N09 exchange at E107 (formative). Hand-backs in: the 106 audit card and note; E106. Out: E107 to the teacher; its commonest letter is repaired in the 108 opening. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then tell someone at home what happened in one scene or poem you read this week, in order. No score.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: day 105, would you recommend this series to a friend, and why? (prepared claim with a page). Day 106, the linked question in the revised exchange. Day 107, how does scene 2 build on scene 1? Written task, day 106: the published review (O02) and the audit card. Judged by the teacher with four look-fors on the review: opinion naming the series; reasons that are not the opinion again; links that fit; a closing that names who might like the series. In the discussion: a marked detail, a linked question, an answer that explains, and what became clearer.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview audit, publish, scene and stanza with a picture of a play and a poem; frames "I marked page ___, which shows ___", "Which part shows that?" and "Scene 2 answers ___." Access: audio beside print for the script and poem; large print; oral rehearsal before the discussion; a child who cannot write small may mark the audit card with ticks and say each answer to the teacher. Check accommodations: read the audit card aloud (not the review), extra time inside the 90 minutes, answers by mouth. Reading load: marked passages of 150 to 250 words on 105; the script and poem are read aloud by the teacher and volunteers on 107, with the class following; only 100 to 150 words are read alone. Extension, same standard: link the question in the discussion to a speaker in a different group.

## 10. Two rooms

Teaching one child: on 105 the parent is the discussion partner: ask one linked question about a marked page and write one note on the review before the child changes one thing. On 106 the parent reads the audit card with the folder and the child says which part answers each line. One projector: show the discussion moves chart, the audit card layout and the printed script; the check, the audit and the exits stay on paper.
