# Section 3.6: Drafting the book review

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-3/3/s/3.6), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-3 u03-6.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.3.1a** ("introduce the topic or text they are writing about, state an opinion, and create an organizational structure that lists reasons"): day 101, the introduction and the reasons listed in order.
- **W.3.1b** ("provide reasons that support the opinion"): days 102 and 103, one reason paragraph a day, each with a detail from a book and a sentence on what it tells a reader.
- **W.3.1c** ("use linking words and phrases (e.g., because, therefore, since, for example) to connect opinion and reasons"): day 103.
- **W.3.1d** ("provide a concluding statement or section"): day 104.
- **L.3.2a** ("capitalize appropriate words in titles"): taught on day 102, applied in the review heading, audited on 106 and 115.
- **W.3.4** ("development and organization are appropriate to task and purpose"): days 102 and 103; each paragraph stays on one reason.
- **W.3.5** ("planning, revising, and editing"): day 104, one peer look-for and a written teacher note; the revision is day 105.
- **SL.3.1b and SL.3.1c** (follow the rules; "ask questions to check understanding ... stay on topic"): practiced in the day 104 partner exchange.
- **RF.3.4a, b, c:** partner rereading on 102 and 104, no speed target. **RF.3.3c, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f, W.3.10:** the skills block, weeks 21 and 22.

Limits that bite: opinion writing was taught in grade 2 and practiced on 98 and 100, so only the title rule is new (day 102). Children reread marked passages of at most 250 words, never a whole chapter. The review is one product, O02, counted once across 101 to 106. Connections: RL.3.9 (the two-book maps from section 3.4 supply reasons, day 101); L.3.1a (word jobs retrieved on 102 and 104).

The teacher page must explain: the title rule and its small-word list (a, an, the, and, but, or, for, of, in, on, at, to, by stay lowercase unless first; the word after a colon starts with a capital); a reason says why, a detail says what happened; because gives a cause, therefore and so give a result, for example gives an instance; and that the draft, the note and the revision are judged together on 106.

## 2. Where it sits


- Before: section 3.5, ending day 100 (the printed review model and O01 returned with a note). After: section 3.7, opening day 105 (read the written note, revise, rehearse the discussion).
- No readiness check. Results go to the dated folder: plan, draft, partner question, teacher note, revision. They feed the 106 audit and the 115 title row.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Items run on these days.** None by number. The 106 rows for W.3.5, L.3.2a and W.3.4, and the 115 row for L.3.2a, test what 101 to 104 teach. They are run and scored in sections 3.7 and 3.9. This section supplies the records they read: the 104 draft with its look-for question, the teacher's written note (returned 105), the title as written on 102 and checked on 104.

**Exit questions (E101 to E104).** One ask each, on a new case printed "invented for practice", sentences of at most 15 words. A stem that needed two answers is split into an a item and a b item. Unscored. The exact strips are in items.csv and these lines match it. If many miss one, the next day's opening repair uses that letter's wrong example.
- **E101 (s03-X101).** Strip: "The Rain Boot Gang, book 1. Four friends mend a leaky bus shelter. They learn that careful work takes patience." Ask: Write one opening sentence for a review of this series. Name the series and give your opinion. Answer: I think The Rain Boot Gang is worth reading because the friends work with patience. Wrong: Four friends mend a leaky bus shelter (H); It is good because it is good (I).
- **E102 (s03-X102).** Strip: "I recommend the Tidepool Twins because they are loyal friends. Details from book 1: (a) Wren waits on the rocks for Kip. (b) The twins live in a blue house. (c) Kip's shoe is red." Ask: Write one sentence: the detail that proves loyalty and what it shows. Answer: Wren waits on the rocks for Kip, which shows she is loyal. Wrong: Wren waits on the rocks for Kip (H); They are loyal because they are friends (I).
- **E103 (s03-X103).** Strip: "The Pocket Moon books. Nell packs a flashlight before every trip. ___ she is never left in the dark." Ask: Write the linking word that fits the blank. The second sentence is a result. Answer: Therefore (or So): the second sentence is the result of the first. Wrong: Because (B); For example (J); But (J).
- **E104 (s03-X104).** Strip: A partner's note on a review of the Hill Street Gang books. The last line says, "Anyway, my lunch was good." The note says, "Your last line is about lunch. What do you want me to do about the books?" Ask: Write a new last line for the review that answers the note. Answer: Try the Hill Street Gang books if you like neighbors who share. Wrong: Anyway, my lunch was good (K); Anyway, my lunch was good, so read on (L).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Letters this section meets; if many made it, 5 to 10 minutes of repair in the next opening or a small group.
- **H, retells without an opinion.** Wrong: In book 1 Ada hides a plan and Ben waits. Right: I recommend book 1 because Ada learns to share her plan. Shown 101. Repair: write the recommendation first, then add the story as proof; 5 minutes.
- **I, opinion repeated as a reason.** Wrong: I recommend it because it is a good series. Right: I recommend it because Ada asks the readers where they want to sit. Shown 101 and 102. Repair: ask what happened in the book that proves it; 7 minutes.
- **J, a link between unrelated ideas.** Wrong: Ben asks questions. Therefore the beans grow tall. Right: Ben asks questions, so Ada's plan improves. Shown 103. Repair: say both ideas, then choose cause, result or example; 7 minutes.
- **K, an unrelated ending.** Wrong: Now I will go and eat lunch. Right: Try these books if you like friends who solve problems together. Shown 104. Repair: reread the reasons and name who they suit; 5 minutes.
- **L, a neat recopy as revision.** Wrong: the same last line in better writing. Right: one change made from the question, original kept beside. Shown 104. Repair: model one margin change; 5 minutes.
- **O, an unrelated peer question.** Wrong: What is your favorite color? Right: Which detail shows she learned to ask? Shown 104. Repair: build the question from one word in the draft; 5 minutes.
- **R and AM, title capitals.** Wrong: a hat for the winter fair; A Hat For The Winter Fair. Right: A Hat for the Winter Fair. Shown 102. Repair: compare with the title rule card and lowercase the small words; 5 minutes.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Children carry the printed review model with its four colours (day 100), the printed title model, their review plan page, the two books with marked passages and the dated folder. The model is read and returned to on 101 (opening), 102 (a reason), 103 (links) and 104 (closing). The title rule card is read on 102 and returned to on 104. Nothing is withdrawn here; the plan page loses its supplied frame on 101 and is the only guide after that.
- **Key moment 1, day 104: the peer exchange and the written note.** Ideal instrument: the look-for card in the child's hands and the teacher's written note on the draft. Spec: no tool is used; paper fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Pencil, paper, the printed books, the printed model and title model, the look-for card.

## 6. Days

## Day 101


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper: read and spell isolated words (3 minutes), read and spell a sentence from the day's reading (3), decode one word in its paragraph and say what it means (4). Week 21, syllable types with affixes: dis-a-gree-ment, re-mem-ber-ing, care-ful-ly. Irregular: laugh, answer. One line of ending-rule retrieval (drop e, double the consonant, change y).
- **20 min. Independent reading and conferences.** Log day. Children read their own book and fill the log line. Four 4-minute conferences (16 minutes, four children, one record each) and two children read a log line aloud at 2 minutes each (4 minutes); the heard-six column is filled. No new teaching.
- **5 min. Opening repair and recall.** E100 returned with its commonest letter (K) beside the correction (2 minutes). Pairs say the parts of the printed model from day 100 and which part a review needs first (3 minutes).
- **20 min. Choose and plan.** Children reread marked passages from the two books, at most 250 words in all (3 minutes), and look again at their two-book maps from section 3.4. Each chooses an opinion and two reasons and notes the page of a detail that proves each (7). On a blank plan page with no frame they write the series and book titles, the opinion, reason 1 and reason 2 in order with the strongest last, a link for each, and a closing idea (10).
- **20 min. Draft the introduction.** Teacher reads the model's opening aloud (2 minutes, nothing new taught). Children write the introduction: it names the series and both books, states an opinion, and says that two reasons follow (13). They then list the two reasons beneath it as headings for 102 and 103 (5).
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes a child, 1 to change: read your opening; your partner says the opinion and the first reason promised.
- **5 min. Word in context.** Recommend: each child writes one sentence about a book and says what the sentence shows about the word (3 minutes); three children read theirs (2).
- **5 min. Exit.** E101.

- **Shared shape.** The printed review model's opening line (day 100) shares its shape with s03-X101 (W.3.1a): an opening that names the series and says what I think. The exit case (the Rain Boot Gang) is not in the model, the books or the plan page.

Carried: the plan page, the introduction and the reasons list go in the dated folder; the model stays on the desk. Tool: None. By hand: the plan page, the introduction, the word sentence, the exit. Evidence: plan page and introduction (O02 draft, part 1), E101. Hand-backs in: E100 (K) and O01 with its note. Out: E101 to the teacher. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then tell someone at home which book you would give a friend first, and one reason. No score.

## Day 102


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper (3, 3 and 4 minutes). Week 21: ques-tion-ing, pre-par-ing, use-ful-ness. Irregular: thought, enough. One line of ending-rule retrieval.
- **15 min. Partner rereading with a feedback card.** Pairs, from 100 to 150 words of book 2, chapter 4. Each child reads (3 minutes), the partner marks one phrase and one place for expression on the card (1), the child rereads (2); then swap (12 minutes in all, 3 to change over). No speed.
- **5 min. Grammar retrieval.** Pairs say the job of the underlined noun, pronoun, verb, adjective and adverb in three sentences from the passage (review of section 3.1).
- **5 min. Opening repair and recall.** E101 returned with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). Retrieve: a reason says why; a detail says what happened (3).
- **10 min. Model a reason paragraph.** Teacher models one reason that the places are easy to picture, with a detail (beans curled around a string fence, book 2, chapter 1) and a sentence on what it tells a reader (7). Pairs mark the reason, the detail and what it tells (3). Not a reason the children are likely to choose.
- **10 min. Title capitals, taught.** Teacher shows the rule (first word and important words start with a capital; a, an, the, and, but, or, for, of, in, on, at, to, by stay lowercase unless first; the word after a colon starts with a capital) on "The Courtyard Club: The Missing Plan" (3 minutes). On slates children fix three invented titles, a hat for the winter fair, the dog in the tall grass and ten steps up the hill, and partners check (5). Children write the heading of their review with both book titles and check it against the rule card (2).
- **20 min. Draft reason 1.** Children reread the strongest detail they marked (3), then write the first reason paragraph: the reason, the detail with its page, and a sentence on what the detail tells a reader (17).
- **5 min. Talk.** Pairs, 2 minutes a child, 1 to change: read your reason; your partner names the detail and says what it shows.
- **5 min. Word in context.** Detail: one sentence about a book that uses the word, and what the sentence shows (5).
- **5 min. Exit.** E102.

- **Shared shape.** The modeled reason paragraph (a reason, a detail, a sentence on what it shows) shares its shape with s03-X102 (W.3.1b). The exit case (the Tidepool Twins) is not in the model or in either book.

Carried: reason 1, the heading and the title rule card go in the dated folder. Tool: None. By hand: the slate titles, the heading, reason 1, the exit. Evidence: reason 1, the title as written (kept for 104 and 106), E102. Hand-backs in: E101. Out: E102 to the teacher. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then find one title at home and say its capital letters aloud. No score.

## Day 103


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper. Week 22: un-wor-ried, for-get-ful, ex-plain-ing. Irregular: people, would. One line of ending-rule retrieval.
- **20 min. Independent reading and conferences.** Log day: four 4-minute conferences (16) and two log lines read aloud at 2 minutes each (4); the heard-six column is filled. No new teaching.
- **5 min. Opening repair and recall.** E102 returned with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). Retrieve links: because (cause), therefore and so (result), since (cause first), for example (an instance) (3).
- **10 min. Choose the link.** Teacher shows one reason joined two ways, a link that fits and a link between unrelated ideas (J), and says why one fits (4). Pairs choose the link for three printed pairs of sentences taken from book 2, chapters 3 and 4, with the link left blank (6).
- **5 min. Hard-part reread.** Children reread about 150 words of book 2, chapter 3, the lines where a name is not enough, and write in the margin what the lines tell a reader deciding about the book.
- **20 min. Draft reason 2.** Children write the second reason paragraph with a link joining the opinion to the reason and another joining the reason to its detail (15), then reread it for one detail that is not yet explained (5).
- **10 min. Link check in pairs.** Partners name the relation of each link in the other's paragraph: cause, result or example (3 minutes each, 6); each writer revises one link (4).
- **5 min. Word in context.** Therefore: one sentence about a book and what the word does in it (5).
- **5 min. Exit.** E103.

- **Shared shape.** The link pairs from book 2 (link left blank) share their shape with s03-X103 (W.3.1c): choose the word that fits a result. The exit case (a flashlight) is not in the pairs or the book.

Carried: reason 2 and the link notes go in the dated folder. Tool: None. By hand: the hard-part margin note, reason 2, the revised link, the exit. Evidence: reason 2 with its links, E103. Hand-backs in: E102. Out: E103 to the teacher. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then say two sentences about your day joined by because, and two joined by so. No score.

## Day 104


- **10 min. Long words.** Three prompts on paper. Week 22: dis-cuss-ing, im-prove-ment, con-clu-sion. Irregular: through, whose. One line of ending-rule retrieval.
- **15 min. Partner rereading with a feedback card.** Pairs, from 100 to 150 words of book 2, chapter 5; each child reads, hears one phrase note, rereads (12 minutes, 3 to change over). No speed.
- **5 min. Grammar retrieval.** Pairs read two sentences from their own drafts and check each subject and verb for agreement (review of section 3.2).
- **5 min. Opening repair and recall.** E103 returned with its commonest letter beside the correction (2 minutes). Recall the model's closing line: it tells who might like the series (3).
- **20 min. Draft the conclusion.** Children reread their two reasons (3). They write a closing section of three or four sentences: the opinion in new words, who might like the series, and an invitation to try it (12). They check the heading and every title against the rule card (5).
- **10 min. Peer exchange.** Pairs, with the look-for card (names both books and an opinion; two reasons; a detail for each; links; a closing). The writer reads the whole draft (2 minutes), the partner names one look-for met and asks one question linked to the draft (1), the writer answers (1); then swap. The question goes in the margin (8 minutes, 2 to change over).
- **15 min. Revise after the question.** Each child changes the conclusion and one reason using the partner's question, with the original kept beside it. The teacher collects the draft at the end.
- **5 min. Word in context.** Conclusion: one sentence about a book and what the word shows (5).
- **5 min. Exit.** E104.

- **Shared shape.** The model's closing line shares its shape with s03-X104 (W.3.1d): a closing that tells who might like the books. The exit case (the Hill Street Gang) is not in the model or the look-for card.

Carried: the complete draft with its margin question and the title as checked go to the teacher; the plan page and the rule card stay in the folder. Tool: None. By hand: the closing section, the margin question, the revised reason, the exit. Evidence: the complete draft with the peer question (O02, part 2), E104. Hand-backs in: E103. Out: the draft and E104 to the teacher; one written note on every component, including the title, is written that evening and returned on 105. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book, then read your draft aloud to someone at home and ask them one question about it. No score.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: day 101, which reason goes first, and which page proves it? Day 103, how do these two ideas connect: because, therefore or for example? Day 104, the partner's question, linked to one word of the draft. Written task: the O02 draft across 101 to 104 (introduction, two reason paragraphs, conclusion). Judged by the teacher with six look-fors: the opening names the series and both books and states an opinion; two reasons are named in order; each reason has a detail from a book; a sentence says what the detail tells a reader; each link fits the relation of its ideas; the closing names who might like the series. Titles follow the rule.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 101: opinion with because (grade 2) and the maps from section 3.4. Day 102: word jobs (section 3.1) and the printed title model. Day 103: agreement in the child's own sentences (section 3.2). Day 104: the model's closing line and the discussion rules chart (unit 1). Every day: ending rules and irregular spellings.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: preview recommend, reason, detail and conclusion with a gesture and a picture of the books; offer the frames "I recommend ___ because ___", "For example, ___. This shows ___" and "Try it if you like ___." Access: audio beside print for marked passages; large print; a child who cannot write small may speak each paragraph to a scribe and then copy the heading. Check accommodations: exits read aloud, extra time inside the 90 minutes, answers by mouth to the teacher. Reading load: marked passages of 150 to 250 words, reread alone; the books were read whole earlier and are not reread. Extension, same standard: write a reason that answers a reader who prefers stories without a problem to solve.

## 10. Two rooms

Teaching one child: the parent is the partner on 104. Read the draft aloud, say which look-for you heard, ask one question about a word, and write it in the margin before the child changes one thing. One projector: show the printed model, the title model and the rule card; drafts, the look-for card and the exits stay on paper.
