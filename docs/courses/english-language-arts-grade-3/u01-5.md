# Section 1.5: A grouped informative paragraph about book carriers: plan, draft, revise, check, respond


## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.3.2a to d and W.3.4, this section's share (days 20 to 24, 25 for repair):** "Introduce a topic and group related information together"; "Develop the topic with facts, definitions, and details"; "Use linking words and phrases"; "Provide a concluding statement or section"; and writing in which "development and organization are appropriate to task and purpose". One paragraph, the access paragraph (product I02), is planned on day 20, drafted on 21, revised on 22 and 23, scored from the folder on 24 and repaired on 25.
- **W.3.5 (days 20, 22, 23):** "planning, revising, and editing". Plan with a supplied frame (20), revise after one peer look-for and one written teacher note (22), revise again and edit (23).
- **W.3.10 (days 20 to 25):** a dated folder: day 16 first paragraph, day 20 plan, day 21 draft, day 22 note and change log, day 23 revision. Short and extended writing are told apart by their dates.
- **RF.3.3a, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 20 to 23, 25):** week 4 retrieves un-, re- and dis- on longer bases; week 5 teaches pre- (before) on day 21 and mis- (wrongly) on day 22. Irregular words come from the two articles (photograph, another, region).
- **L.3.2d (day 20):** "Form and use possessives": count the owners, then place the apostrophe (carrier's, carriers'). Regular plurals only; the irregular plural children's is met in reading with no rule.
- **L.3.2g (day 20):** "Consult reference materials, including beginning dictionaries, as needed to check and correct spellings": try two likely spellings, use the guide words, compare letter by letter.
- **SL.3.1b, SL.3.1c, SL.3.3 (days 22, 25), SL.3.1a, SL.3.1d, SL.3.2 (day 25):** the discussion moves on a peer's draft (22) and a formal group talk on a library presentation with words, a picture and a labeled bar graph (25). RF.3.4a to c (day 22): partner rereading with the feedback card.
- **RI.3.1, RI.3.2 (day 24):** two scored reading rows on a fresh source card. Nothing is taught from them here.
- **Limits that bite here.** The paragraph explains and does not argue. Facts come only from g3-u1-05 and g3-u1-06; a child who adds an outside fact is sent back to the sentence (A). An illustration (a labeled route sketch) is allowed and not scored. The hard time of the 1930s is one plain sentence; no war or violence. No child is asked about their own library, books or home. Plural possessives are taught on regular plurals only.
- **Connections.** RI.3.2 main-idea charts (section 1.4, day 15) choose each group's heading; sentence combining returns on day 20; SL.3.1 returns on days 36 and 39; W.3.2 returns unscored on day 28 and scored in the contract on day 37.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How grouping differs from listing (a group has a heading that says what its facts are for); why a definition sits by the fact that needs it; how to write a note that asks for a change of meaning and never a spelling fix; what counts as a revision (a moved, added or explained fact) and what does not (recopying); and how day 24 is scored from the folder.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned (day 24, mid-unit assessment):** the six writing rows W.3.2a, W.3.2b, W.3.2c, W.3.2d, W.3.4 and W.3.10 in the table above, scored from the folder with no added minutes; and, because the day is here, the two reading rows RI.3.1 (6 minutes) and RI.3.2 (6 minutes) on the new source card, which section 1.4's table lists. W.3.5 is not scored on day 24; its record is the change log and revision of days 22 and 23, read on day 25. **Exit questions (E20 to E25).** One ask each. E24 sits on Books on the Move; the others on cases printed "invented for practice". Unscored. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's opening repairs that letter.
- **E20 (day 20).** Five note strips, "invented for practice": "water the beans", "pick ripe peas", "pull the weeds", "carry the peas to the kitchen", "turn the soil". Ask: "Put these five notes into two groups and write a heading for each group." Answer: the three plant-care notes together and the two pea notes together, each heading saying what its group is for (for example "Caring for the plants" and "Gathering the peas"). Wrong: all five in one list, or in the order given (H); a split that parts the two pea notes (H).
- **E21 (day 21).** Strip: "A den is a sheltered home for a fox. This den has two doors, one on each side of the hill." Ask: "Write two sentences for a younger reader: what a den is, and how this den's doors help the fox." Answer: "A den is a sheltered home for a fox. The two doors, one on each side of the hill, give the fox two ways in and out." Wrong: a fact the strip does not name, such as a stream or pups (A); a second sentence joined by "Also" to an unrelated fact (I).
- **E22 (day 22).** Unworked model: "Visitors read the park sign. Ducks are protected. The sign names the rules. Protected birds are left alone." Notes: partner, "put the sign facts together"; teacher, "say what protected means". Ask: "Rewrite the four sentences using both notes." Answer: the two sign sentences side by side, and "protected" explained (for example "protected, which means kept safe and left alone"). Wrong: the four sentences copied unchanged (M); the sign sentences still apart (H).
- **E23 (day 23).** Unworked model for a younger reader: "Rinse the pots. Also, dry the brushes. Shelf." Ask: "Rewrite this so a younger reader can use it, and end with one closing sentence." Answer: says what rinse means, keeps "Also" between the two jobs, and closes by saying what the jobs make possible (the paints ready for the next class). Wrong: still short reminders with no explanation (K); a closing about another topic, such as the class pet (J).
- **E24 (day 24; replaces five minutes of independent reading; s01-X24v2).** Books on the Move, the last section, The Same Job (the day 24 source card stays with the check items). Ask: What two things does each book carrier do? Answer: it brings books to readers who live far away, and it takes finished books back so others can borrow them. Wrong: "It sells books to readers" (A); "It is about a road and a river" (B).
- **E25 (day 25).** Strip: "The art club made three clay bowls and five clay cups today." Two labeled bars show 3 and 5, and a drawing shows both kinds. Ask: "Which kind did the club make more of, and how many more?" Answer: cups, two more. Wrong: bowls, from swapped bars (Z); "five more", reading the bar height as the difference (Z).

The strips of E20 to E25 are not used in any worked example.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The supplied paragraph frame (topic, group one, group two, link, closing): the teacher holds it on day 20, children hold it on days 21 and 22, and it is withdrawn on day 23; partners reread before writing and each child works from their own plan. The dated folder (the writing-process record) is the child's from day 20 and is handed in on day 24. The main-idea chart of section 1.4 helps choose a heading. The discussion moves chart (mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer) is carried from section 1.1 into days 22 and 25. All return in section 1.6 (the explanation on day 28) and in the contract (days 30 to 37).
- **Key moment 1, day 20: related facts moved into groups.** Ideal instrument: note strips that can be moved on a table or board. Spec: fits; print strips cut with scissors. Gaps and asks: none (no tool; see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 25: a bar graph with a labeled scale of one.** Ideal instrument: a printed graph. Spec: fits; print.
- **One word, one sense.** "Stop" is a noun here, a place where a carrier meets readers. Do not use it as a verb here.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper strips, a pencil, scissors and a wall space to sort on.

## 6. Days

## Day 20


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair the commonest day 19 letter, from the check sheet (whole class if more than a third missed it, otherwise a conference group); seven minutes retrieval of grade 2 revise and edit: a four-sentence practice paragraph with one sentence that does not belong and one missing capital; children fix both and say why (unscored; e2-revise-edit).
- **20 min. Skills.** Eight minutes: week 4 words and the pattern sequence (un-, re- and dis- on longer bases: unwrap, rebuild, disconnect), including a three-minute beginning-dictionary spelling check of photograph (try "foto" and "photo", use the guide words, compare letters) for L.3.2g. Twelve minutes: three reading conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs; the three children heard go on the heard-six column.
- **20 min. Model.** The teacher reads the Bangladesh boat section of g3-u1-05 (about 95 words, under 1 minute), then models a grouped paragraph on library boats, our own and not about the carriers: ten note strips sorted into two groups, a heading for each, a topic sentence, a definition of one word, link words and a closing sentence (12 minutes). Five minutes teach possessives on the model: "the boat's roof" (one owner) and "the boats' roofs" (many owners): count the owners, then place the apostrophe. Three minutes sentence combining with also and but.
- **25 min. Hand work.** Five minutes: each child rereads their day 16 paragraph beside the teacher's list of needs (topic, grouped facts, link, closing) and underlines what is missing. Twelve minutes: pairs sort ten note strips from g3-u1-06 and g3-u1-05 into two groups and name each group on the supplied frame. Eight minutes: each child writes the topic sentence, the link words and the closing idea in the frame; nothing is drafted yet.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child 2 minutes on the two groups and why each fact belongs (4 minutes); ask for detail, 3 minutes; three volunteers share, 1 minute each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E20.

Carried: the teacher holds the frame and the model; children hold their plans. Tool: None. By hand: note strips, frame, exit. Evidence: E20. Hand-backs in: the day 19 item results (opening). Out: E20 to day 21's opening; the plan to day 21. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread the Kentucky article aloud to an adult and say which facts go together; then read any book together. No questions about your own home or reading.

## Day 21


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E20's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieval of studied spelling: six week 4 words on slates, un-, re- and dis- words spelled and used in a sentence (unscored).

Hand work.** Each child drafts the access paragraph (product I02) from their plan and the supplied frame, on how book carriers brought books to readers who lived far away, using g3-u1-05 and g3-u1-06: five minutes to reread the plan and the two sections, thirty minutes to write, five minutes to read it back and mark the definition and the link. The teacher circulates for writing conferences. After class she writes one line on each draft (about 2 minutes a draft, 60 minutes outside the day), a question that asks for a change in meaning.
- **15 min. Talk.** Pairs: each child reads the paragraph aloud, about 1.5 minutes, and the partner says one thing they learned, about 2.5 minutes (8 minutes); then the class lists on the board what a reader needs that a first draft often lacks (7 minutes).

Carried: children hold the frame and their draft; the teacher holds the board list. Tool: None. By hand: draft and exit. Evidence: E21; the draft is read for needs and kept, scored on day 24 only after revision. Hand-backs in: E20. Out: E21 and the written notes to day 22's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read your paragraph aloud to an adult and say what you would add. No score.

## Day 22


- **10 min. Opening.** Three minutes repair E21's commonest letter; seven minutes retrieval of phrase reading: partners read a four-sentence strip with phrase marks, then swap (unscored).
- **20 min. Skills.** Eight minutes: week 5 sequence, mis- (wrongly): misread, misspell, mislead. Five minutes: grammar, join two facts with also or but. Seven minutes: partner rereading of one article section (about 90 words) twice each with the feedback card (phrasing, expression, no speed); the teacher listens to two pairs, 3 minutes each, and records who was heard.
- **40 min. Hand work.** Five minutes: each child reads the teacher's one-line note on their draft. Ten minutes: partners swap drafts; each looks for the one thing the note names (for example, "are the travel facts together?") and writes a sentence saying yes or not yet (the peer look-for). Twenty minutes: revise the draft for that note, moving, adding or explaining a fact. Five minutes: write in the change log what changed and where.
- **15 min. Talk.** Pairs, all at once: each child takes a 6-minute turn as writer (say the note in your own words, answer your partner's one question about it with a detail, hear what they would change), then the partner takes theirs (12 minutes); then two volunteers share a revision, 1.5 minutes each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E22.

Carried: partners hold the draft and the note; the frame is still allowed. Tool: None. By hand: revised draft, change log, exit. Evidence: E22; the change log. Hand-backs in: E21 and the day 21 notes. Out: E22 to day 23's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read your revised paragraph aloud to an adult and ask what a younger reader might not know.

## Day 23

## Day 25

- **20 min. Skills.** Eight minutes: week 5 words and the pattern sequence. Twelve minutes: three reading conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs; heard-six column.
- **10 min. Feedback.** The day 24 component scores are returned. Each child reads theirs, marks the one letter to repair and writes a target in the folder (5 minutes); the teacher's list of the commonest letters goes on the board (5 minutes).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 20: "Which notes belong together, and what are they for?" Day 22: "What does your partner's note ask you to change in the meaning?" Day 25: "What do the words, the picture and the bars each tell, and what do all three tell together?"
- **Written task, day 21 (revised 22 and 23).** The access paragraph (product I02): how book carriers brought books to readers who lived far away.
- **Judged by the teacher with six look-fors:** a topic that says what the carriers did for readers; related facts grouped, with each group's purpose clear; a definition where a younger reader needs one; a link word between facts of one group; a closing sentence that says what the facts show; a revision that changes meaning after a note. Recorded by component on day 24 (0 or 1); the earlier steps are unscored, in the scale shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview carrier, route, stop, group, topic, link and conclusion with a picture before the model; frames "First ___. Also ___. So ___." and "The words ___ show ___"; an oral or labeled-drawing first plan; a partner who shares a language where possible.
- **Access.** Large print; strips read aloud and a factual description of the photograph and map for a child who cannot see them, never the answer; strips that can be touched and sorted; a scribe for the paragraph's content, never for spelling evidence.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 24's two items and E20 to E25 may be read aloud except where reading is the evidence (E24 is read by the child); record any help. A child may speak the exit and the teacher writes it; the support is recorded.
- **Reading load.** Alone: strips under 40 words, the source card (about 45 words) and one article section (about 100 words). Read aloud: the model and the presentation.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second group of facts for the same paragraph, with its own heading and link, and defend where it belongs.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult is the partner. Cut the facts into strips, sort them into two groups, name each group, and write a one-line note back that asks for one change. Record by hand, shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Day 24 is a quiet half hour: the child does the two items alone and the adult files the dated pages.
- **One projector.** Show the model paragraph, the articles and the graph; keep the exit strips and the source card on paper until needed.
