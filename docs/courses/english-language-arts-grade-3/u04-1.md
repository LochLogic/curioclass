# Section 4.1: Water around the world

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


This section's share: RI.3.1 "ask and answer questions ... referring explicitly to the text" is taught on 120 to 122 with the question frame "The text says ... so ..."; RI.3.2 "determine the main idea ... recount the key details" on 120, 121 and 123 with the main-idea chart (ledger: main-idea chart, grade 3 u01-4); RI.3.7 "use information gained from illustrations ... and the words" on 121 and 122; RI.3.9 "compare and contrast the most important points and key details" is new and has one lesson here (123, two short model cards); L.3.4a to L.3.4d are taught on 122 as four word moves on four words, one move each. W.3.1a to W.3.1d "state an opinion ... reasons ... linking words ... concluding statement" is taught once on 120 with a model, then used for O07 (120) and O08 (125). RF.3.3c, RF.3.3d, L.3.2e and L.3.2f run through the 10-minute Long words slot on the week 24 and 25 words.

Limits that bite: the water book is about 819 words in eight short sections, so no day reads more than two sections. Printed stems are at most 15 words. O07 and O08 are opinions, not essays: one paragraph, with a sentence frame on 120 only.

Connections: the opinion model comes from grade 3 u03-5 and u03-6; the main-idea chart from u01-4 and u02-5; the context and figurative chart and known-root move from u01-2; un-, re-, dis- from u01-3.

The teacher page must explain: that "freshwater" is not "safe water" (error AL), that the book's numbers are read as the book gives them, and that nobody is asked about water at home.

## 3. Checks, written first


Items owned here: RI.3.1 and RI.3.2 are scored on day 124 (P124, "mid", unscored this time); the five end-of-unit rows for RI.3.7 and L.3.4a to L.3.4d are taught on 121 and 122 and scored on 154 with section 4.5's card. Each exit is one ask on a case not in the book or in any teaching card; marking uses 2, 1, 0.

**Exit 120 (O07, replaces the usual exit, 8 minutes).** Unseen card: "The harbor sign says: Salt water fills the bay. A creek brings fresh water to the shore. Visitors may not know if the creek water is safe to drink." Ask: "Would you tell a visitor to read this sign first? Write your opinion, two reasons from the sign, a link word and a closing sentence." Answer: yes, with reasons that name salt water and fresh water and the safety question, linked by because or for example, and a closing; the opposite opinion is right if it is supported by the sign. Wrong: fresh means safe to drink (AL); liking the sign alone (M); no opinion stated (L); reasons that do not use the sign (N); no closing (O). Scored 2 if all four parts, 1 if three, 0 otherwise.

**Exit 121 (s04-X121v2).** Water on Earth, section 7, paragraph 1, sentences 2 to 4, printed on the slip (runoff carries litter, oil and fertilizer into streams); not section 2, the worked section. Ask: "Write one sentence that says what this part is mainly about." Answer: rain that runs over the ground carries harmful things into water. Wrong: "It can carry litter" (B); "The lake water is safe to drink" (AL).

**Exit 122 (s04-X122v2).** How a Town Cleans Its Water, the diagram "The steps in order" with the Sedimentation line, printed on the slip; not Diagram 2, the worked diagram. Ask: "Which label comes right after Sedimentation? Write it, then write one thing the diagram shows that the line does not." Answer: Filtration; the diagram shows the water then goes by pipe to homes. Wrong: Flocculation (E); "the diagram only repeats the line" (AN).

**Exit 123 (s04-X123v2 and s04-X123bv2).** Text A, Water on Earth, section 5, paragraph 4 (communities dig wells, build tanks, lay pipes, repair pumps); text B, Water Paths in Kitui County, "Water that stays", paragraph 1 (sand dams), printed on the slip; not the school and farm cards the day works. Ask: "Write one important point both texts share." Then: "Write one detail only text B has." Answer: communities build things so people can reach water; only B says the water stays in the sand behind a dam. Wrong: a detail from one text as the shared point (C); an unstated fact such as "every village has a well" (A).

**Exit 124.** Unseen card: "The creek's current was strong near the bridge. Hikers held a rope to cross." Ask: "What does current mean here? Write a clue from the card." Answer: moving water in the creek; clues are strong, creek, held a rope to cross. Wrong: current as electricity or the present time (D). Scored 2, 1, 0.

Days 124 and 125: 124 is the check (P124 in field 6); the exit on 125 is O08 (in the day).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


Each letter is shown once, a wrong line beside a right line, in the opening repair (3 minutes on the commonest exit letter) and in Return and repair on 125.

- **A** Wrong: "Both say every village has a well." Right: "Text A says they dig wells, build tanks, lay pipes and repair pumps." Shown 125. If many: 7 minutes, children point to the sentence in a card and say "The text says ... so ..." for three questions, one of which the text does not answer.
- **B** Wrong: "It can carry litter." Right: "Rain that runs over the ground carries harmful things into water." Shown 125. If many: 6 minutes, test "does this fit every line?" on three candidate lines.
- **C** Wrong: "Many communities build sand dams." Right: "Both say communities build things so people can reach water." Shown 125. If many: 8 minutes, add "because" to each detail on a slip and pair slips to the main idea.
- **D** Wrong: "current" as electricity. Right: "moving water in the creek." Shown 125. If many: 6 minutes, read the whole sentence with each meaning and keep the one that fits.
- **E** Wrong: "The gutter comes after the barrel." Right: "The arrow goes gutter, barrel, tap." Shown 125. If many: 5 minutes, finger-trace the arrows on a fresh diagram and say first, next, last.
- **AK** Wrong: reads "evaporates" as "every" from its first letter. Right: eh-VAP-uh-rates, blended by syllable and confirmed by the sentence. Shown 121. If many: 8 minutes, scoop each syllable on three long words, then read each in a sentence.
- **AL** Wrong: "Fresh water is safe to drink." Right: "Fresh means not salty; the sign says it may not be safe." Shown 121. If many: 7 minutes, sort six sentences into "salt content" and "safe to drink".
- **AN** Wrong: "The picture shows a tap." Right: "The picture shows the water path in order; the line only names the tap." Shown 125. If many: 8 minutes, label the picture, then add one word from the sentence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Carried and kept: the heard-six column and four-minute reading conference (grade 3 u01), partner rereading with a feedback card (u02), the word check of dictation plus six oral samples (u01-7), Return and repair (the commonest letter, at most four groups), the opening repair and the dated folder. The log day (Monday, Wednesday, Friday) runs four conferences of four minutes plus two log lines read aloud; Tuesday and Thursday run five minutes of grammar and fifteen of partner rereading with the feedback card.

Instruments: the ideal is a printed text per child plus a card of labels; the spec is a paper set of 30 book sections and 15 pairs of diagram cards. A room with one projector reads the section aloud once, then children reread in pairs from a shared print; nothing here needs a device.

## 6. Days

## Day 120


- **10 min. Word check.** Dictation of four words (4 minutes: "reflect", "explain", "water", "source", each said twice in a sentence), then six oral samples at one minute each (the child reads a word card aloud) while the other 24 children match the same words to meanings on a strip. Irregular words are "water" and "source".
- **20 min. Skills block, log day.** Four 4-minute conferences (16) and two log lines read aloud at 2 minutes (4); heard-six column filled.
- **5 min. Hook.** A glass of water and two cards, "salt" and "fresh": which can we drink? Two children answer, then the class says the word "freshwater".
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Grade 2 science: where is water found? Children name three places on a strip (s2-water-where). The teacher does not ask where their own water comes from.
- **17 min. Read and annotate.** The teacher reads section 1, "Why Water Matters", aloud (2 minutes); children reread silently (3 minutes); pairs say one key detail each (2 minutes each, 1 to change over, 5 minutes); each child writes a one-line gist (7 minutes).
- **15 min. Model and frame.** The teacher models one opinion, "I think the book's first section is a good opening because ...", with the frame opinion, reason 1, reason 2, link word, closing; children underline each part in the model.
- **5 min. Partner talk.** Each child tells a partner an opinion about the opening in one sentence (2 minutes each, 1 to change over).
- **5 min. Word in context.** "freshwater": read in its sentence, test "fresh" against "water", state the meaning in a short line.
- **8 min. Exit (O07).** The unseen harbor sign card; one paragraph.

Carried: the grade 2 water-where list. Tool: None. By hand: all written work. Evidence: exit 120 (field 3), word record. Hand-backs in: none. Out: O07 for the dated folder. At home (35 minutes): read for 20 minutes from the log book; talk about one fact from the day; no questions about water at home. No score.

## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: "What does the text say about where water is found, and how do you know?" (121, partner talk); "Which detail supports the main idea best?" (121 and 123); "Do the book and the account agree about access?" (125). Written task: O08 on 125, an opinion paragraph. Look-fors: the opinion is clear; two reasons use the text's words; a link word (because, for example); a closing sentence; no unsupported fact.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: a word strip with "freshwater, salt water, source, supply, demand", a picture each; sentence frame "I think ... because ...". Access: read the sections aloud for children who need it; larger print for low vision; finger-traced diagrams. Check accommodations: extra time on P124 (to 45 minutes of items) and a scribe for answer lines. Reading load: sections are short; the account is read aloud in parts. Extension: write a third reason, or compare with the grade 2 water-where list.

## 10. Two rooms

A parent teaching one child: read the section aloud together, ask the child to say the main idea in one sentence and point to the line that shows it; for the opinion, say "I think ... because ...". One projector: show the section and the diagram one at a time; children use shared print and pair for rereading; all writing is on paper.
