# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 3

Course `english-language-arts-grade-3` (catalogue id `ela-k12`), grade 3, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text's grade 3 year, in the base text's order.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 153 in units and 7 at course level (4 for two course-level checks, 3 respond days).
  One day is one literacy block of about 90 minutes, five days a week: the unit lesson (60 minutes: the base
  text's module lesson kept whole, reading, talk and writing on the unit's texts) and the skills block (30
  minutes). The skills block's week, 150 minutes: the long-word and spelling sequence 10 minutes every day (50); on
  Monday, Wednesday and Friday, independent reading for 20 minutes with the teacher's conferences and small-group
  help inside it (60); on Tuesday and Thursday, a 5-minute grammar warm-up on a sentence from the anchor (10) and 15
  minutes of partner rereading for fluency, the teacher listening to one group and giving feedback (30). Grammar is
  taught in the module lessons where the base text teaches it; the warm-ups retrieve it inside the 30, so the
  60-minute lesson is not stretched. This split is provisional until a representative day is timed in the probe.
  The base text plans 153 module lessons plus a second 60-minute block with five parts (independent reading;
  additional work with complex text; fluency with grammar, usage and mechanics; writing practice; word study and
  vocabulary). Curio Class keeps the module lesson whole, so 153 lessons become 153 unit days (unit 1 adds 2 for
  the start-of-year screens, as the first year of the long-word sequence and of the 3 to 5 block, unit 4 drops 2 by recording its announcement as audio, not video); it folds the second
  block's word study, fluency, grammar practice and independent reading into the 30 minutes and leaves out its
  additional complex-text work and writing practice, which the module lessons' close reading and daily writing carry
  (For the lead 1). Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and its mid-unit assessments: the base text's
  assessments for the module's first two base units are the unit's mid-unit assessments, and its third base unit's
  assessment with the performance task is the end-of-unit assessment (source map). A unit that assumes an earlier grade's row opens with a readiness check inside its days where that row has been idle two or more grades, and with a retrieval task inside a lesson where it was used the year before (frame-write lesson 16).
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 3 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt), all
  28 progression rows of grade 3 (data/progressions/ela.csv), every lettered part taught in the unit named below.
  The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (the grades 3 to 5 criteria, all three gateways, Gateway 1 in full: Cory's yes,
  2026-11-12) and the approach is docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) governs every
  unit.
- **Base text.** The 2017 K to 5 language arts curriculum recorded in docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md (CC BY 4.0; copy
  in S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017), grade 3, modules 1 to 4. Its lesson design, sequence, protocols
  and assessments are adapted; its trade books are replaced by Curio Class texts (BASELINE rule 29: newer texts,
  mostly original Curio Class texts written to the grade 3 text spec, each read by the text agent before use,
  docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); its own CC BY texts may be adapted; its third-party excerpts are replaced; US government texts it uses
  (public domain) may stay. Its name is never used on a Curio Class page; one credit line per adapted day. The
  2025 edition and the 2024 skills block are never used. The 2017 curriculum is CC BY 4.0 (the store's terms
  excerpt, licence/eleducation-terms-of-use-excerpt-2026-11-12.txt); only the 2024 Skills Block is CC BY-NC. The
  source map closes this frame.
- **Which row sits where.** A row sits in the unit that first teaches its grade 3 step (the part new this year),
  which is where the kept base lessons first teach and assess it. Where a row's lettered parts come in later units,
  the owning unit says which part waits and the later unit names it as continued. Before a row's unit, a unit uses
  the grade 2 form of the skill, and the first unit that leans on a grade 2 skill opens with a readiness check on
  it (unit 1 for most). Later units practise every placed row again on new texts; the table names those returns as
  review.
- **Reading level.** Anchors sit in the grades 2 to 3 band (a quantitative reading measure of about 420 to 820),
  climbing across the year from short picture-book stories and articles (300 to 800 words) in unit 1 to a chapter
  book read whole in unit 3 and the 2 to 3 band's top read alone by year end (e3-reading-range). About half the
  anchors are literary and half informational. The measures named in the units are targets the commissioned texts must
  meet; each anchor's text record (measure, what makes it hard under the four headings, the reader and
  task, why here, rights, whole or excerpt) is written in data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-3/texts.csv with
  the scope and sequence, before its unit is planned, and the balance is counted from those records.
- **Word lists.** About 8 to 10 words a week from the unit's texts, met before, during and after reading and met again in later units; the scope and sequence sets each list.
- **Long text.** One chapter book read whole (unit 3), beside a second book of the same series read in part.
- **Words, read and never guessed.** The long-word sequence (e3-long-words) runs every day from day 1 with a
  check every few weeks and next steps; spelling follows the same pattern sequence. No unit teaches guessing a
  word from a picture or the sentence.
- **Writing.** Every reading day ends in a written exit ticket that needs the day's text. Each unit takes at
  least one piece through the whole process (plan, draft, peer critique against one look-for, teacher feedback,
  revise, edit, publish). The year's writing keeps the grades 3 to 5 shares: about 30 percent opinion, 35
  informative, 35 narrative. Short research runs twice (units 2 and 4).
- **Talk.** Partner talk daily; a formal text-based discussion about every two weeks (at least three a unit), on
  the taught routine with roles and fading sentence frames.
- **Paper path and devices.** Every anchor has a print copy and every task a paper route. Keyboarding, publishing
  and the audio recordings happen in the school day on a shared classroom device rotation or a lab session, with
  no student account; home practice is optional and never the route. If no device route exists by year end, W.3.6
  is recorded unfinished for that class (e3-writing-process).
- **Record sheet, family note and sub-ready day.** One per base unit (three per Curio Class unit, 12 a year), since a unit here is a whole base module of eight weeks; the unit stays the base module, which carries the compelling question and the performance task.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 (children kept from school; a short excerpt from Booker T.
  Washington's own account of learning to read after slavery): before it reads, the agent studies Black-led sources
  on teaching slavery to young children (a Black historians' association's teaching guidance, a national African
  American history museum's teaching pages), scholarship by Black historians on Washington and on literacy under
  slavery, and the style guidance on "enslaved people", plus the named countries' own education pages for the
  children kept from school. Unit 4 (water scarcity and disease): it studies the named region's own government and
  community water pages, a public health body's guidance on writing about waterborne disease for children, and the
  federal water-agency pages the unit reads.
