# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 3

Course id ela-k12 (grade 3 row), grade 3, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text's grade 3 year, in the base text's order.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 153 in units and 7 at course level (4 for two course-level checks, 3 respond days).
  One day is one literacy block of about 90 minutes, five days a week: the unit lesson (about 60 minutes: reading,
  talk and writing on the unit's texts) and the skills block (about 30 minutes: the long-word and spelling sequence
  every day, then partner rereading for fluency or independent reading conferences on alternate days). The base
  text plans one 60-minute module lesson a day (153 lessons in grade 3) plus a second 60-minute block; Curio Class
  keeps the module lesson whole, so 153 lessons become 153 unit days (unit 1 adds 2 for the start-of-year screens,
  unit 4 drops 2 by recording its announcement as audio, not video), and folds the second block's word study,
  fluency and independent reading into 30 minutes (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 3 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt), all
  28 progression rows of grade 3 (data/progressions/ela.csv), every lettered part taught in the unit named below.
  The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (the grades 3 to 5 criteria, all three gateways, Gateway 1 in full: Cory's yes,
  2026-11-12) and the approach is docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) governs every
  unit.
- **Base text.** The 2017 K to 5 language arts curriculum recorded in docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md (CC BY 4.0; copy
  in S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017), grade 3, modules 1 to 4. Its lesson design, sequence, protocols
  and assessments are adapted; its trade books are replaced by Curio Class texts (BASELINE rule 29: newer texts,
  mostly original Curio Class texts written to the grade 3 text spec, each read by a qualified ELA teacher before
  use); its own CC BY texts may be adapted; its third-party excerpts are replaced; US government texts it uses
  (public domain) may stay. Its name is never used on a Curio Class page; one credit line per adapted day. The
  2025 edition and the 2024 skills block are never used. The source map closes this frame.
- **Which row sits where.** A row sits in the unit that first teaches its grade 3 step (the part new this year).
  Before then a unit uses the grade 2 form of the skill, and the first unit that leans on a grade 2 skill opens
  with a readiness check on it (unit 1 for most). Later units practise every placed row again on new texts; the
  table names those returns as review.
- **Reading level.** Anchors sit in the grades 2 to 3 band (measure about 420 to 820), climbing across the year
  from short picture-book stories and articles (300 to 800 words) in unit 1 to a chapter book read whole in unit 3
  and the 2 to 3 band's top read alone by year end (e3-reading-range). About half the anchors are literary and
  half informational. Every anchor gets a text record (measure, what makes it hard under the four headings,
  the task, why here) in data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-3/texts.csv when its unit is planned.
- **Long text.** One chapter book read whole (unit 3), beside a second book of the same series read in part.
- **Words, read and never guessed.** The long-word sequence (e3-long-words) runs every day from day 1 with a
  check every few weeks and next steps; spelling follows the same pattern sequence. No unit teaches guessing a
  word from a picture or the sentence.
- **Writing.** Every reading day ends in a written exit answer that needs the day's text. Each unit takes at
  least one piece through the whole process (plan, draft, peer critique against one look-for, teacher feedback,
  revise, edit, publish). The year's writing keeps the grades 3 to 5 shares: about 30 percent opinion, 35
  informative, 35 narrative. Short research runs twice (units 2 and 4).
- **Talk.** Partner talk daily; a formal text-based discussion about every two weeks (at least three a unit), on
  the taught routine with roles and fading sentence frames.
- **Paper path and devices.** Every anchor has a print copy and every task a paper route. Keyboarding, publishing
  and the audio recordings happen in the school day on a shared classroom device rotation or a lab session, with
  no student account; home practice is optional and never the route. If no device route exists by year end, W.3.6
  is recorded unfinished for that class (e3-writing-process).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 1 (children kept from school, a
  short excerpt from a formerly enslaved man's own account of learning to read) and unit 4 (water scarcity and
  disease) also need a qualified person's read when one is found (FOUNDATION section 7).
