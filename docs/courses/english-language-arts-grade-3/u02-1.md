# Section 2.1: Frog poems and two origin tales: close reading, discussion and a first plan

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.3.1 (days 40 to 42):** "Ask and answer questions to demonstrate understanding of a text, referring explicitly to the text as the basis for the answers." Each answer points to words first. The day 46 rows sample it on a cold tale.
- **RL.3.4, L.3.5a, L.3.5b, L.3.5c (day 40):** literal against nonliteral (unit 1's term, reused), real-life links and shades of certainty. Taught on drummed, lean close and wakes the water, and on a made-up sentence for wondered, suspected and knew. Not taught here: the day 46 and 51 poem phrases.
- **SL.3.1a, SL.3.1b, SL.3.1c, SL.3.1d, SL.3.3 (day 40):** the discussion moves chart from unit 1, applied to poems. One group of five is the demonstration; the record is for those five only.
- **RF.3.3a (day 40), RF.3.3b (days 41, 42), RF.3.3d, L.3.2e, L.3.2f (days 40 to 42):** day 40 closes week 8 (retrieve -ness, -ly and -able); days 41 and 42 open week 9 (retrieve -tion and -sion, then apply them to longer bases). Irregular words are mapped from the day's text. No spelling test.
- **RL.3.2 (days 41, 42), RL.3.3 (day 41):** recount a tale and name its lesson through two events; say what Kikker's action leads to.
- **W.3.3a, W.3.3b, W.3.3c, W.3.3d, L.3.3a (days 41, 42), W.3.5 (day 42), W.3.10 (days 40 to 42):** situation and narrator, dialogue, time words, closure, a word chosen for effect; the first plan for the tale that runs to day 50 (Product N01). Dialogue punctuation itself is taught on day 44.
- **RF.3.4a, RF.3.4b, RF.3.4c (day 42):** reread a poem stanza with a partner for sense, phrasing and expression, with a feedback card; no speed target. The poem recording window (five different children, 3 minutes each, on days 40, 42, 44, 47, 49 and 50, all 30 once) opens on day 40 with Under the Leaf, the recording poem.
- **Limits that bite here.** The Belloc poems are credited to Hilaire Belloc (1896, public domain) and read aloud first. "Polly-wog" is an old word for tadpole. Day 42's tale has a fire in a cave: say before reading that nobody is hurt. No graphic feeding scene.
- **Connections.** Unit 1's discussion moves, heard-six column and literal and nonliteral chart carry on. The story map is made here for the first time this unit and returns on days 43 to 50.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to read a poem twice, once for sound and once for the hard part; how to use one group of five as a model while the rest watch with the chart.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Items owned.** The days 46, 51 and 76 rows above are run later, on cold texts (a new myth and a new poem), never on a text taught here. This section does not teach any phrase those rows use. Plan point: the day 46 and 51 rows cite a silver crown and "cautious" in Under the Leaf, but that poem has neither; the item writer should use the reserved cold poem panel for these.

**Exit questions (E40 to E42).** One ask each, on a new case printed "invented for practice". Unscored. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one letter, the next opening repairs it.
- **E40.** Card: "Raindrops drummed upon a leaf. Frog leaped below to find relief." Ask: Tell a partner why the frog leaped below the leaf. Use the word drummed. Answer: The rain drummed on the leaf, so the frog went under it for shelter. Drummed compares the sound of rain to a drum; no real drum plays. Wrong: nonliteral drummed read as a physical event (C); a fact the card does not give (A).
- **E41 (s02-X41v2).** The last paragraph of Why the Frog Has a Long Tongue, reread. Ask: Write one sentence with because: why does every frog now catch flies with its tongue? Answer: Every frog catches flies with its tongue because Kikker showed them the exercises that made his tongue long and strong. Wrong: cause and result reversed (Y); a topic fact with no events (B).
- **E42.** Card: "A grey frog hid beneath a painted leaf. Rain carried the colors onto its back. At sunrise it wore the leaf's bright marks." Ask: Plan a different tale about why a frog has marks. Write who tells it, the starting situation, two events in order and the ending. Answer: Any coherent plan, for example: I am a plain frog. A beetle dusts my back with gold. I wash in a pool. The gold spots stay, and that is why I have spots. Wrong: the ending placed before its cause (O); a list; no situation or explanation (S).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Children hold their poem copies, the story map and the written plan in their folders; the teacher holds the record sheet (heard-six and the day 40 group) and the discussion moves chart. All return on days 43 to 50.
- **Key moment 1, day 40: one group of five heard.** Ideal instrument: a record sheet and the moves chart. Spec: fits; paper is enough. Gaps and asks: none (no tool; see the course's TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, days 40 and 42: a recording to listen back to.** Ideal instrument: a recording device (the light tool from unit 1). Spec: fits; local only, saved without accounts, five children at 3 minutes each on each recording day. Plan point: Under the Leaf is the recording poem from day 40, so the first five children read it after one hearing; later windows rehearse more.
- **A room with nothing.** A parent or teacher listens to the reread and writes where each pause fell; the child checks it against the printed lines.

## 6. Days

## Day 40


- **10 min. Long words.** Folders and the day 39 regroup list handed back (2 minutes). Then week 8: read and spell -ness, -ly and -able on stillness, softly and breakable on slates; map the irregular words answer and word from the poem (8 minutes).
- **20 min. Independent reading and first recordings.** The teacher records five children at 3 minutes each (15 minutes), the first recording window: each says a purpose, reads Under the Leaf, hears the replay and rereads one sentence (the routine from unit 1, section 1.7). The rest read from their logs; the last 5 minutes everyone writes one log line. The four conferences move to day 41, so the heard-six column loses today's six.
- **15 min. Reading and reread.** The hook: Pond Voices read aloud twice, the second time with eyes closed; the compelling question goes on the chart: How does where a frog lives shape how it looks and acts? (4 minutes). The teacher reads Under the Leaf once, the poem every child will record (a copy goes in each folder, 2 minutes), then The Learned Fish and The Frog by Hilaire Belloc (1896, public domain; polly-wog is an old word for tadpole, 2 minutes). Hard-part reread, in pairs: what words make the quiet pond a chorus? Pairs mark hushed, peep, answers and sing along on their copies (4 minutes); the class lists them (3 minutes).
- **10 min. Word and sentence work.** The teacher models the leaning reeds and wakes the water from its sleep as said exactly against meant another way (4 minutes), then a made-up sentence, Mira heard a peep in the dark and ___ it was a frog, with wondered, suspected and knew on a certainty line (4 minutes). Each child explains one choice on a slate to a partner (2 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs, 4 minutes: each child 2 minutes on the reread question, naming a line, then asking the partner for a detail. One demonstration group of five, 6 minutes: five children at 70 seconds each is 5 minutes 50 seconds. The teacher records those five children (SL.3.1a to d, SL.3.3); the 25 others watch with the moves chart. This slot loses nothing; the other five groups are not heard today.
- **20 min. Writing.** Three sentences in the folder: choose one image from either poem; say what it is exactly, what the poet means and what it tells about the frog's home, using The words ___ explain ___ (4 minutes talk with a partner, 14 minutes writing, 2 minutes swap and read).
- **5 min. Exit.** E40.

Carried: poem copies, the moves chart and the day's sentences stay in the folder; the teacher holds the recording list. Tool: recording device (light), five children, 3 minutes each, saved locally with no account. By hand: slate words, marking, three sentences, exit. Evidence: E40; the day 40 record for the demonstration group; five saved clips. Hand-backs in: the regroup list and folders from section 1.8. Out: E40 to day 41's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): reread Pond Voices aloud to an adult twice and find one line not meant exactly; write one sentence saying what it really means. No score.

## Day 41


- **10 min. Long words.** Three minutes repair E40's commonest letter. Week 9 opens: retrieve -tion and -sion on vacation, station and decision, read and spell on slates; map does from the tale for its odd spelling (7 minutes).
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Four conferences of 4 minutes while the rest read from their logs (16 minutes); four minutes for a log line. Add the four to the heard-six column.
- **15 min. Reading and reread.** Two minutes retrieving unit 1's The Lion and the Mouse (Aesop, 1919 edition): who, where and the first event, on a slate (unscored). The teacher reads Why the Frog Has a Long Tongue aloud (adapted from EL G3 M2 U1 L2, about 6 minutes), glossing startled, flexible and smirk with a picture or gesture as they come. Hard-part reread, in pairs: which words show what changes Kikker's approach to the contest? Pairs reread from All day long to the idea and point to the words (7 minutes).
- **10 min. Word and sentence work.** The teacher models the tale on a chart: situation, an outside narrator, three events with arrows, and an ending (6 minutes). Children mark one time word (Long, long ago; Just then; Finally) and one word for effect (smirk) on their copy (4 minutes).
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs, 90 seconds a child: what changes Kikker's approach? Then each child says how the partner's answer changed their account (4 minutes in all). Six volunteers at 1 minute each tell the class (6 minutes).
- **20 min. Writing.** Each child makes their own map of Kikker: situation, events, ending (6 minutes). Then three or four sentences: how Kikker's actions lead to his long tongue and what the tale says about practice, with two events and so (14 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E41.

Carried: the map and sentences stay in the folder. Tool: None. By hand: slate words, the map, time-word marks, sentences, exit. Evidence: E41. Hand-backs in: E40. Out: E41 to day 42's opening. At home (30 to 40 minutes): tell the tale to an adult in order, with the word then, and write the one thing Kikker did that made the difference. No score.

- **Data note.** E41's because sentence shares a shape with the day 41 writing (two events and so); the exit case (hot sand) differs from the Kikker tale. Grade 2 story parts are written in the ledger (grade 2 unit 2.1 day 33).

## Day 42


- **10 min. Long words.** Three minutes repair E41's commonest letter. Week 9: read and spell -tion and -sion on direction, collection and extension; map the irregular word would (7 minutes).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 40: "Which words make the pond a chorus?" Day 41: "What changes Kikker's approach to the contest?" Day 42: "Which event turns danger into escape?"
- **Written task, day 41.** Three or four sentences on how Kikker's actions give him a long tongue, with two events and so.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors:** events in order, a cause linked to the result, a time word, a closing line. Recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 40:** unit 1's discussion moves; -ness, -ly, -able (week 8 ends). **Days 41 and 42:** -tion and -sion retrieved (week 9). **Day 41:** grade 2 story parts; The Lion and the Mouse; the story map. **Day 42:** one-owner possessives; the story map; phrase boundaries in a poem. 
- **Fluency target.** Natural phrasing and expression on successive readings, no speed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview pond, reeds, leaf, tongue, cave and crack with pictures; frame "The words ___ explain ___"; say the first answer to a partner or draw it; a partner who shares a language where possible.
- **Access.** Large print; a factual description of any picture; a scribe for ideas, not for spelling evidence.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits may be read aloud except where decoding is the evidence; record help.
- **Reading load.** Alone: a poem, or 150 to 250 words. Longer tales are first heard, then reread in chunks.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a second supported connection between a frog's home and its behavior.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult is the partner: read the poem aloud, ask for one line, say what became clearer. Record or write where the child paused. The exits go on paper.
- **One projector.** Show the poems and the tales; keep the exit strips on paper until the exit.
