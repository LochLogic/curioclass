# Scope and sequence: Statistics

Course `statistics`, grade 12, draft, 2026-11-24.

Ten units keep the frame's order, owned rows and lengths: 17, 13, 13, 9, 19, 11, 24, 19, 13 and 13 days, 151 in all, plus 9 course-level check and respond days, 160 course days. The course meets every school day for 32 weeks, so course day n is school day n and `school_day` is blank. The days live in data/plans/statistics/days.csv and the base-text placements in data/plans/statistics/source-map.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them. Units 1 to 9 end with one sitting of unseen items on the second-last day and a respond day on the last; unit 5 sits twice; no unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Unit 10, the investigation, is spread as the frame sets it: days 47 and 48 after Check 1, days 70 and 71 inside unit 5, and days 152 to 160. Every day opens with five minutes of spaced, mixed practice; the named fluencies (CUR-ST.2 and CUR-ST.3 from day 15, CUR-ST.8 from day 55, CUR-ST.12 and CUR-ST.13 from days 98 and 103, CUR-ST.16 and CUR-ST.18 from days 122 and 133) stay in that opening for the rest of the year, untimed. Every unit works on the real, public data the frame names, each with its source and "as of" year in the day's note; values made up for a task are labelled invented for practice.

## Unit 1. Describing one variable

Course days 1 to 17, weeks 1 to 4. The unit opens where students are surest. Categorical data first (days 1 to 4): the commuting table hooks the question and gives frequency tables on day 1, with the readiness check on two-way tables in its last ten minutes; bar charts and levels of measurement on day 2; two-way tables, read as joint, marginal and conditional distributions, on day 3, grouped by the readiness results; association argued on a new table on day 4. Quantitative distributions next (days 5 to 9): displays, then summaries, then the box plot by hand with the 1.5 times IQR rule and the choice of resistant summaries, practice, and a comparison in context. Position and the normal model last (days 10 to 15), because z-scores need the standard deviation: percentiles and z-scores, linear change (Fahrenheit to Celsius), density curves and the normal model, the table, and checking the model on lap times and on Old Faithful, where it fails. The normal model sits here because units 5 to 8 lean on it.

Assessment: day 16, one sitting on CUR-ST.1 to CUR-ST.3 with a prompt for each part; response on day 17. Review: about 5.5 days, all raised to college vocabulary (days 2 and 3 categorical, days 5, 6 and half of 8 displays and summaries, day 12 Algebra II's normal areas). Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (real data, conceptual understanding) and the Progressions check against S-ID, applied as read checks on this scope and the unit plan, no day added. Extensions, no day added: a second skewed set summarized two ways (day 8), a normal probability plot by hand (day 14). Sensitivity: commuting tables by state, never by group, and no student shares a commute or home (days 1, 3, 4, 9).

## Unit 2. Two quantitative variables

Course days 18 to 30, weeks 4 to 6. Scatter plots and correlation first (days 18 and 19, the unit's two review days, with r built from unit 1's z-scores), then the least-squares line and its formulas, r squared and prediction (days 20 and 21), practice on published textbook prices (day 22) and the full fuel economy analysis (day 23). The second half asks what to do when the line fails: residual plots and influence (day 24), a logarithm straightening the census counts 1790 to 1900 (day 25), the square root or reciprocal for stopping distances (day 26), a transformed fit turned back into a curve (day 27), and the Mauna Loa series, where the residual plot shows the curve (day 28). Unit 9 tests the slope fitted here.

Assessment: day 29 on CUR-ST.4 and CUR-ST.5; response on day 30. Candidate kept: the Progressions check against the Modeling progression and S-ID, and the GAISE lens, as read checks, no day added. Extension, no day added: a power model on log-log axes (day 27). Sensitivity: fuel economy data by vehicle class with makes and models removed (days 18 to 23); the carbon dioxide series read as measured data (day 28).

## Unit 3. Producing data: samples and experiments

Course days 31 to 43, weeks 7 to 9. Sampling first (days 31 to 35): population and parameter (the review day), simple random and stratified samples drawn from the printed county list (day 32), cluster and systematic samples with the class's pooled estimates compared (day 33), the kinds of bias (day 34), and a sampling plan of the students' own (day 35). Experiments next (days 36 to 41): the principles and confounding, the polio field trial's two designs side by side, blocking and matched pairs (the design unit 8 analyzes), the scope of inference grid, Biology's feedback investigation redesigned on paper, and news reports judged. Inference in units 6 to 9 means something only once students know where data came from, and the investigation's plan (days 47 and 48) needs this unit.

Assessment: day 42 on CUR-ST.6 and CUR-ST.7; response on day 43. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (active learning: the class draws its own samples on days 32 and 33) and the Progressions check against S-IC, as read checks, no day added. Extension, no day added: a blocked paper-helicopter experiment (day 38). Sensitivity: the polio trial told with its history and the people in it as people (day 37); Biology's investigation on paper only, with no heart rate or health data (day 40).

## Unit 4. Probability and counting

Course days 49 to 57, weeks 10 to 12, after Check 1 and the investigation's two held days. Day 1 hooks with the shared-birthday puzzle and opens with simulation and the law of large numbers, so the rules that follow have a long-run meaning. The three review days of Geometry's rules come next (days 50 to 52: complement and addition, conditional probability and independence from tables, trees and genetics crosses), grouped by day 46's readiness results. Counting comes last (days 53 and 54: permutations, then combinations), and day 55 solves the birthday problem and new compound problems, with a short independent practice set on the rules.

Assessment: day 56 on CUR-ST.8 and S-CP.B.9; response on day 57. Readiness: day 46, Check 1's respond day, on geo-conditional-independence and geo-addition-rule. Candidate kept: the Progressions check against S-CP and the GAISE lens, as read checks, no day added. Extension, no day added: a counting problem solved two ways (day 54). Genetics crosses use plants and fruit flies, never a student's family (day 52).

## Unit 5. Random variables and decisions

Course days 58 to 78, weeks 12 to 16: 19 unit days, with the investigation's days 70 and 71 held inside. Random variables first (days 58 to 63): a distribution graphed, expected value, standard deviation, linear change, sums and differences of independent random variables (the spread unit 7's two-proportion methods use), and a card lab. Binomial and geometric next (days 64 to 68), opening on empirical households-by-vehicles data (S-MD.A.4), then the quiz-guessing binomial with expected scores under grading schemes (S-MD.A.3), the geometric wait, and the normal approximation. Day 69 is the random-variable sitting. The investigation's data are gathered and cleaned on days 70 and 71, while the teacher marks the sitting. Decisions close the unit (days 72 to 76): the lottery and a dice game, insurance deductibles, fair decisions by lot, a screening test and product testing, and pulling the goalie.

Assessment: two sittings, as the frame sets them. Day 69 on S-MD.A.1 to S-MD.A.4, CUR-ST.9 and CUR-ST.10, with its response in the first 15 minutes of day 72 so expected value is repaired before the decision days (counted once). Day 77 on S-MD.B.5 with S-MD.B.5a and S-MD.B.5b, S-MD.B.6 and S-MD.B.7; response on day 78. Candidate kept: the Progressions check against S-MD (every (+) code taught in full, each lettered part on its own day: 5a day 72, 5b day 73) and the GAISE lens, as read checks, no day added. Extension, no day added: a game show's final choice (day 76). Sensitivity: households by vehicles with no task on a student's own household (day 64); the lottery analyzed, never played (day 72); insurance with invented rates, never a family's own (day 73); the screening test with invented rates, never a student's health (day 75).

## Unit 6. How samples vary

Course days 79 to 89, weeks 16 to 18. The hinge of the course. Proportions first (days 79 to 81): many samples drawn by hand, the sampling distribution of a sample proportion with its spread formula, and bias told from variability. Means next (days 82 to 87): a flat parent from dice and random digits, the distribution of the sample mean, the central limit theorem on Old Faithful's two-humped intervals, the county populations as the counter-case where 30 is not large enough, the theorem used on the teacher's jar of coins, and practice. Every idea is seen in simulation before it is used with a formula, and pooled class results are the paper path.

Assessment: day 88 on CUR-ST.11; response on day 89. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (conceptual understanding through simulation) and the Progressions check against S-IC.A, as read checks, no day added. Extension, no day added: the sampling distribution of a median (day 87).

## Unit 7. Estimating and testing proportions

Course days 93 to 116, weeks 19 to 24. The logic of interval and test is built once, on proportions. Intervals (days 93 to 98): many class intervals checked against the published state percent, the one-proportion interval with its conditions, the meaning of the level, sample size, the seat-belt survey's published margin beside the simple formula, and the four-move write-up. Tests (days 99 to 104): a simulated null first, then hypotheses, the p-value in words, the decision and conclusion, the write-up, and test and interval set side by side. Errors and power (days 105 to 108) come once a test exists to make errors: the two errors, power by simulation, what changes it, and a study planned. Two proportions last (days 109 to 114), using unit 5's spread of a difference: the interval for two independent random samples, the test, what each design allows, and the polio trial's randomized half analyzed in full.

Assessment: day 115 on CUR-ST.12 to CUR-ST.15, with one extended write-up at most and short items for the other codes; response on day 116. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (assessment that improves learning: each write-up day returns feedback by move) and the Progressions check against S-IC.B, as read checks, no day added. Extensions, no day added: a news claim tested in full (day 104), power by simulation for one more test (day 108). Sensitivity: place of birth read only from supplied microdata, no student asked, groups never ranked (days 93 to 96, 104, 110, 111); seat-belt use rates only (day 97); the polio trial (day 113).

## Unit 8. Estimating and testing means

Course days 117 to 135, weeks 24 to 27. One mean first (days 117 to 123): the known-spread interval as a one-day bridge into t, the t distribution and its table, the t interval and test on coins weighed in class (the post-1982 test is a case whose answer is known by design), the effect of skew and outliers, practice, and the home costs lab. Paired data next (days 124 to 127), on station temperatures in two climate-normal periods, because pairing reuses the one-sample t just taught and unit 3's matched pairs. Two means last (days 128 to 133): coins before and after 1982, the two-sample interval and test, a randomized experiment beside its re-randomization, two regions' stations (an answer not known in advance), and choosing between paired and two-sample designs.

Assessment: day 134 on CUR-ST.16 to CUR-ST.18, one extended item at most; response on day 135. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (technology to explore: the conditions day 121 and the re-randomization day 130) and the Progressions check against S-IC.B, as read checks, no day added. Extensions, no day added: rank-based tests (day 121), a two-sample test run as a re-randomization and compared (day 130). Sensitivity: home prices from supplied published data only (day 123); climate normals read as measured data (days 124 to 126, 131).

## Unit 9. Counts in categories and regression slopes

Course days 136 to 148, weeks 28 to 30. Chi-square first (days 136 to 141): a die rolled in class, Mendel's pea counts for goodness of fit, commuting mode by region for homogeneity (one sample per region), commuting mode by a second variable for independence, choosing the test from how the data were gathered, and a full analysis. Slope inference last (days 142 to 146), because it needs unit 8's t: the slope as a statistic, conditions from residuals and the Old Faithful data's limit, reading regression output with the interval and test, the reaction-rate conditions case logged as in unit 2, and practice from printouts. The year closes its own loop on unit 1's two-way tables and unit 2's lines.

Assessment: day 147 on CUR-ST.19 and CUR-ST.20; response on day 148. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens and the Progressions check against S-ID and S-IC, as read checks, no day added. Extensions, no day added: the correlation test matched to the slope test (day 144); why a goodness-of-fit test cannot flag Mendel's fit as too good (day 137). Sensitivity: commuting microdata by region, never by group, with income and household fields dropped (days 138 and 139).

## Unit 10. The investigation

Thirteen days, spread: days 47 and 48 (week 10), 70 and 71 (weeks 14 and 15), and 152 to 160 (weeks 31 and 32). The question and plan are drafted on day 47, given written feedback, and revised and approved on day 48, using unit 3's designs. Data are gathered on day 70 and cleaned on day 71. After Check 3's respond day has repaired any procedure, the data are described (day 152), the inference is chosen and carried out (day 153), the report is drafted and handed in (day 154), the presentation visual is made while the teacher writes feedback (day 155), the report is revised and handed in for its score (day 156), a third of the class presents each day (days 157 to 159, about 3 minutes and one question each), and day 160 responds. All days but the last are Application.

Assessment: the revised report (day 156) and the presentation (days 157 to 159) are the end-of-unit evidence; response on day 160. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (real data, active learning) as a read check, and our own scoring rubrics for the report and the presentation, written with the unit plan and used on days 156 to 159, no day added. Extension, no day added: a second procedure on the same data (day 153). Sensitivity: questions avoid sensitive personal data; class data anonymous, voluntary and harmless; no student measures or shares their body; the teacher approves each plan before any data is gathered (days 47, 48, 70, 71).

## Shorter path

**A shorter period (45 minutes).** The opening drops from five minutes to three on teaching days, and each teaching day's practice set loses its last few items (the exit ticket stays). Hands-on days keep their five minutes to count kits back in; readiness checks keep their ten minutes. Sittings and check days keep their items, with the extended task in the full period; presentation days hold ten students each at about 3 minutes and one question.
