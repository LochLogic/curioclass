# Scope and sequence: Statistics

Course `statistics`, grade 12, draft, 2026-11-24.

Ten units keep the frame's order, owned rows and lengths: 17, 13, 13, 9, 19, 11, 24, 19, 13 and 13 days, 151 in all, plus 9 course-level check and respond days, 160 course days. The course meets every school day for 32 weeks, so course day n is school day n and `school_day` is blank. The days live in data/plans/statistics/days.csv and the base-text placements in data/plans/statistics/source-map.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them. Units 1 to 9 end with one sitting of unseen items on the second-last day and a respond day on the last; unit 5 sits twice; no unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Unit 10, the investigation, is spread as the frame sets it: days 47 and 48 after Check 1, days 70 and 71 inside unit 5, and days 152 to 160. Every day opens with five minutes of spaced, mixed practice; the named fluencies (CUR-ST.2 and CUR-ST.3 from day 15, CUR-ST.8 from day 55, CUR-ST.12 and CUR-ST.13 from days 98 and 103, CUR-ST.16 and CUR-ST.18 from days 122 and 133) stay in that opening for the rest of the year, untimed. Every unit works on the real, public data the frame names, each with its source and "as of" year in the day's note; values made up for a task are labeled invented for practice. Every inference day runs on a random sample or a randomized experiment, and its conclusion says which kind of randomness it rests on; a full-population table or a listing is read for its own published margin, as a known value, or as a conditions case, never fed into a sampling formula.

Each unit names its investigative cycle ("Cycle:", where students pose a question, collect or consider data, analyze and interpret, or why the unit works on supplied data) and the days on which students do each practice the frame's C3 column places there ("Practices:").

## Unit 1. Describing one variable

Course days 1 to 17, weeks 1 to 4. The unit opens where students are surest. Categorical data first (days 1 to 4): the commuting table hooks the question and gives frequency tables on day 1, with the readiness check on two-way tables in its last ten minutes; bar charts and levels of measurement on day 2; two-way tables, read as joint, marginal and conditional distributions, on day 3, grouped by the readiness results; association argued on a new table on day 4. Quantitative distributions next (days 5 to 9): displays, then summaries, then the box plot by hand with the 1.5 times IQR rule and the choice of resistant summaries, practice, and a comparison in context. Position and the normal model last (days 10 to 15), because z-scores need the standard deviation: percentiles and z-scores, linear change (Fahrenheit to Celsius), density curves and the normal model, the table, and checking the model on lap times and on Old Faithful, where it fails. The normal model sits here because units 5 to 8 lean on it.

Assessment: day 16, one sitting on CUR-ST.1 to CUR-ST.3 with a prompt for each part; response on day 17. Review: about 5.5 days, all raised to college vocabulary (days 2 and 3 categorical, days 5, 6 and half of 8 displays and summaries, day 12 Algebra II's normal areas). Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (real data, conceptual understanding) and the Progressions check against S-ID, applied as read checks on this scope and the unit plan, no day added. Extensions, no day added: a second skewed set summarized two ways (day 8), a normal probability plot by hand (day 14). Sensitivity: commuting tables by state, never by group, and no student shares a commute or home (days 1, 3, 4, 9).

Cycle: pose (day 1, the commuting question), consider published tables (days 1 and 5), analyze (days 2 to 15), interpret (days 4, 9 and 14). The data are supplied published tables because students never collect their own commute, home or money data. Practices: MP6 day 3 (precise joint, marginal and conditional language), MP2 day 9 (comparing distributions in context).

## Unit 6. How samples vary

Assessment: day 88 on CUR-ST.11; response on day 89. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (conceptual understanding through simulation) and the Progressions check against S-IC.A, as read checks, no day added. Extension, no day added: the sampling distribution of a median (day 87).

Practices: MP8 day 81 (spread shrinking as samples grow, seen across repeated samples), MP5 day 84 (the simulation applet beside the paper path).

## Unit 7. Estimating and testing proportions

Course days 93 to 116, weeks 19 to 24. The logic of interval and test is built once, on proportions. Intervals (days 93 to 98): many class intervals checked against the published state percent, the one-proportion interval with its conditions, the meaning of the level, sample size, the seat-belt survey's published margin beside the simple formula, and the four-move write-up. Tests (days 99 to 104): a simulated null first, then hypotheses, the p-value in words with what it does not tell (day 101), the decision and conclusion with statistical told from practical significance (day 102), the write-up, and test and interval set side by side. Errors and power (days 105 to 108) come once a test exists to make errors: the two errors, power by simulation, what changes it, and a study planned. Two proportions last (days 109 to 114), using unit 5's spread of a difference: the interval for two independent random samples, the test, what each design allows, and the polio trial's randomized half analyzed in full.

Assessment: day 115 on CUR-ST.12 to CUR-ST.15, with one extended write-up at most and short items for the other codes, its CUR-ST.13 prompts including what a p-value does not tell and statistical against practical significance; response on day 116. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (assessment that improves learning: each write-up day returns feedback by move) and the Progressions check against S-IC.B, as read checks, no day added. Extensions, no day added: a news claim tested in full (day 104), power by simulation for one more test (day 108). Sensitivity: place of birth read only from supplied microdata, no student asked, groups never ranked (days 93 to 96, 104, 110, 111); seat-belt use rates only (day 97); the polio trial (day 113).

Cycle: pose (day 108, a study planned for a new question), consider data (days 93 and 97, supplied random samples beside published values), analyze (days 94 to 113), interpret (days 95 and 112, what a level and a design allow). Every interval and test rests on a supplied random sample or the polio trial's random assignment, and its conclusion says which. Practices: MP3 day 104 (arguing a claimed percent from a test and an interval), MP6 day 113 (a precise scope of inference for the polio trial).

## Unit 8. Estimating and testing means

Course days 117 to 135, weeks 24 to 27. One mean first (days 117 to 123): the known-spread interval as a one-day bridge into t, the t distribution and its table, the t interval and test on coins weighed in class (day 120 tests against the mint's specified mass, where wear may move the mean, and revisits statistical against practical significance), the effect of skew and outliers, practice, and a single mean and a single proportion on supplied random samples (day 123), with the home costs lab kept only as a conditions case: a listing is not a random sample, so students say why its interval does not reach all homes. Two means last (days 128 to 133): coins before and after 1982 (the case whose answer is known by design), the two-sample interval and test with test and interval agreement (day 129), a randomized experiment beside its re-randomization, two regions' stations (an answer not known in advance), and choosing between paired and two-sample designs, where one paired set is analyzed wrongly as two samples and rightly as pairs (day 132).

Assessment: day 134 on CUR-ST.16 to CUR-ST.18, one extended item at most, its CUR-ST.18 prompts including test and interval agreement; response on day 135. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (technology to explore: the conditions day 121 and the re-randomization day 130) and the Progressions check against S-IC.B, as read checks, no day added. Extensions, no day added: rank-based tests (day 121), a two-sample test run as a re-randomization and compared (day 130). Sensitivity: home prices from supplied published data only, with no inference run on them (day 123); climate normals read as measured data (days 123 to 126, 131).

Cycle: pose (day 131, a two-region question whose answer is not known in advance), collect (days 117 and 119, coins weighed in class), consider (day 124, stations drawn at random from the national list), analyze (days 118 to 133), interpret (days 126 and 131). Practices: MP5 day 121 (simulation to judge when t procedures are safe) and day 131 (a spreadsheet beside the printed t table), MP2 day 126 (what the differences mean against the raw values).

## Unit 10. The investigation

Thirteen days, spread: days 47 and 48 (week 10), 70 and 71 (weeks 14 and 15), and 152 to 160 (weeks 31 and 32). Projects are in groups of three (about 10 projects for a class of 30), so the teacher writes feedback on about 10 plans and 10 reports; each student writes, and is scored on, one section of the group's report and the part of the talk they give. The question and plan are drafted on day 47, given written feedback, and revised and approved on day 48, using unit 3's designs. Data are gathered on day 70, class experiments run as a station rotation with classmates as volunteer subjects, and cleaned on day 71; the unit plan sets the rotation. After Check 3's respond day has repaired any procedure, the data are described (day 152), the inference is chosen and carried out (day 153), the report is drafted and handed in (day 154), the presentation visual is made while the teacher writes feedback (day 155), the report is revised and handed in for its score (day 156), a third of the class presents each day (days 157 to 159, about 3 minutes of the group's talk and one question for each student), and day 160 responds. All days but the last are Application.

Assessment: the revised report (day 156) and the presentation (days 157 to 159) are the end-of-unit evidence; response on day 160. Candidate kept: the GAISE lens (real data, active learning) as a read check, and our own scoring rubrics for the report and the presentation, written with the unit plan and used on days 156 to 159, no day added. Extension, no day added: a second procedure on the same data (day 153). Sensitivity: questions avoid sensitive personal data; class data anonymous, voluntary and harmless; no student measures or shares their body; the teacher approves each plan before any data is gathered (days 47, 48, 70, 71).

Cycle: the whole cycle: pose and plan (days 47 and 48), collect (day 70), clean (day 71), analyze (days 152 and 153), interpret and report (days 154 to 159), with the report's scope of inference. Practices: MP1 day 47 (posing a question and a plan), MP5 day 152 (choosing displays and tools), MP4 day 153 (the inference model chosen for the design), MP3 day 157 (presenting and answering a question).
