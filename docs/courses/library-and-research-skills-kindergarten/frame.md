# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, kindergarten

Course `library-and-research-skills-kindergarten` (catalog id `library-k`), kindergarten. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, the pilot for the library subject; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one year of library and research skills, each unit one full inquiry cycle (ask, find, share,
  reflect) on a small question, in the order a kindergarten class needs the moves: wondering and the cycle itself
  first, then finding a book, then finding the part of a book that answers, then sharing in a group and saying who
  made the book.
- **Days.** 30: 24 in units, 3 course-level check days (1 after unit 2, 2 after unit 4) and 3 respond days (1 after
  the mid-year check, 2 after the year-end check). One session of about 25 minutes, one session a week, for 30
  weeks (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: the K to 2 default of 30 sessions of 25 minutes, about 12.5 hours a year, at
  the one-a-week end of the settled "one or two sessions a week"). The 25 minutes hold getting to the carpet or the
  book corner, the session, putting books back and collecting drawings; a lesson plan budgets about 3 minutes at each
  end for that, so about 19 working minutes, and every assessment form below is costed against those 19 minutes
  for a class of 30. One adult runs the room (the classroom teacher or a librarian); while the adult checks a small
  group, the rest of the class works on a book-corner task it has already practiced. Seven kindergarten rows, all
  short spoken and pointing skills, fit 24 unit days: units 1, 2 and 4 each carry two rows and get 6, 6 and 7 days
  (unit 4 also holds the year's last product); unit 3 carries one row and gets 5. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and its respond day (no unit is over 25 days, so no mid-unit
  assessment). These are course days: with 30 days over 30 weeks, course day n falls in week n of
  docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, and the scope and sequence places them on school days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day):
  Understanding is the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through the move on an invented or read-aloud case,
  holding the pictured skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, repeated tries of the same move on new
  cases, with feedback: say an "I wonder" about a new picture, fetch a book from a named bin, raise a hand at the
  answering sentence); Application is the inquiry day (the class runs part of the cycle on its host question and
  makes its drawing). Fluency is small in kindergarten: one practice day a unit, plus the five-minute opening
  retrieval on later days. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the compelling question.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words
  only, data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone).
  Ownership rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone.
  Kindergarten owns one id, AASL.V.C.1 (k-wonder, unit 1), and carries nine as "with help" contributions, each named
  in its unit's What students learn with its later owner. Skills: the seven progression rows, one unit each. Check
  set: the Empire State Information Fluency Continuum, PK to 2 band file, K column and its bold priority skills
  (read cell by cell for this frame, later.csv L103; result in the Check set read bullet); the "Check set adds" cells name
  what each unit plan should weigh from it. Values: K to 5 case sets stay neutral (no politics, elections, religion,
  identity groups or conflict; Cory, 2026-11-21).
- **Base text.** None. Every day is Curio Class's own. Curio Class writes: four short read-aloud fact books (one
  a unit), picture sets for the wonder and group tasks, the pictured four-step cycle card, one pictured skill card
  a unit, the bin label cards and the story-or-fact sort set, and a printable mini-book set for a book corner that
  has none. Unit 2 also uses whatever picture books the room already has. No day needs a trade book, a paid source,
  a library visit or a database (no library access is assumed; Cory, 2026-11-11).
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from another kindergarten course, named by its row id
  for the teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question
  with a closed source set of Curio Class read-alouds and pictures) so the unit runs when no core task is in step or
  at home (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence records both.
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question taken from the class wonder board (k-wonder,
  unit 1) and closes with the share step and the self-check: the child points at the pictured steps (ask, find,
  share, reflect), says which step they did well and one thing to do differently next time (APPROACH.md, the form
  of daily evidence; the "did well" half answers the continuum's K reflect skill). The lens's six steps (ask, find,
  judge, use, share, reflect) are met at K depth inside the four pictures: judge, with help, is choosing the fact
  book over the story book for the question at the find step (unit 2's sort); use is drawing the found fact at the
  share step. Grades 1 and 2 say when judge becomes its own picture.
- **Evidence.** Daily: the teacher's observation of the one named skill per child on a class checklist kept by the
  teacher and thrown away at the end of the unit, plus the child's drawing or spoken answer (APPROACH.md, K to 2).
  End-of-unit assessment: a talk-and-point task on a case not seen before, in one of three forms whose time a unit
  plan shows: a circle round (each child takes a turn while the others listen, about 30 seconds a child, about 15
  minutes for 30); a whisper walk (the teacher reads once to the circle, then walks round and each child whispers
  the answer, about 15 to 25 seconds a child, so no child repeats a neighbor); or a station of three to five
  children (about 2 to 2.5 minutes a group, about 15 minutes for 30) while the others do the book-corner task. A
  pointing item every child can do at once (point at a picture on a strip, or at a name on a cover) is scanned across
  the circle in about 2 minutes. Each unit names its forms and total below; no unit assessment or course-level check
  spills into its respond day. The teacher marks the class checklist. It is scored on a day after the move was
  practiced, never on a show-me day. The respond day's groups come from the checklist by the step missed.
- **Reading level.** Kindergarten children are pre-readers or early readers. Every source is read aloud or is a
  picture; any student page is a picture with a word or a short label; the only print a child handles alone is a bin
  label, a book cover and their own drawing.
- **Paper path.** The whole course is the paper path: printed read-alouds, picture sets, bin labels and sort cards,
  the class book corner and crayons. No day needs a device, an internet connection or a student account.
- **Devices.** None per student, and no owned or carried code needs technology. A projector or one shared screen may
  show a read-aloud page bigger; the printed page does the same job in full. The stop rule (close, tell, no blame) is
  taught in unit 2 as three pictured words on pretend picture cards, so it is in place for any device day a school
  adds and for home.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond books, paper and crayons, no field work and no trip, so no
  laboratory or field guide applies. The screen-safety side is the stop rule, checked by the sensitivity read
  (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, item 2).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned
  (Cory, 2026-11-14). No unit carries cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content: the case sets
  are seeds, a rolling ball, weather, rabbits and other animals, and trees. What the agent studies first: APPROACH.md
  "Sensitive ground" items 1 (neutral K case sets), 2 (the stop rule), 3 (privacy), 4 (honest use without shaming,
  unit 4), 5 (whose voices a source set shows, C12) and 8 (unit 1's visiting staff member); and, for unit 2's stop
  rule, the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children's free K to 2 online-safety presentation "Be Safer
  Online with NetSmartz: Grades K-2", listed at https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/resources (listing read 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26; the listing names the presentation but does not show its rule, so whether its K to 2 rule matches the stop rule rests on the presentation itself, a browser check at grade close, since the kit is a download not yet opened). Unit 2's borrowing follows its own rule (class book corner, no question about books at
  home). No organization's name goes on a student, teacher or family page.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30: unit 1 about $4,
  unit 2 about $8 (with color dot stickers for the bin labels, about $3, reused all year, and a stop-picture strip
  per child), unit 3 about $3, unit 4 about $6 (with a printed cover card per pair for the maker item); about $21 a
  year. A talking object is anything the room already has.
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The PK to 2 band file's K column and priority boxes, every indicator row
  (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-pk-2\esifc-priority-pk-2.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, nothing copied). Met by the rows: developing questions with help (k-wonder); library labels to find
  picture books, picture books as true or not true, and sorting things and information (k-picture-book-labels);
  identifying facts about a topic (k-listen-and-retell); drawing to express facts (k-class-inquiry); listening and
  responding to others' ideas (k-talk-in-group); crediting author and illustrator (k-who-made-book); library rules
  (k-library-visit). Differences, each now in the row's note: ABC order of picture books (grade 2, g2-catalog-lookup);
  private information online (grade 1, g1-device-stop-rule); the fiction and nonfiction book sort (the continuum's
  grade 1 cell, done here with help on a small pile); own ideas against others' ideas (taught in k-who-made-book).
  Met without a row change: one's own strengths (the self-check's "one step I did well"); connecting to one's own
  interests and choosing materials (k-wonder's "a topic you enjoy" and unit 2's borrowing-day choice; reading choice
  is ELA's, STANDARD.md section 4). Left out: group research with technology (no device is assumed in K); retelling a
  story in sequence, multicultural texts and design thinking (ELA and science, STANDARD.md section 4; C12's voices
  record covers who appears in the source sets). Beyond the band file: the whole cycle run each unit (the band's
  modeled inquiry process sits in its grade 2 cell), the stop rule, and checking V.C.1 alone in K (the band's K cell
  is "with help"; the row checks only that each child can say a question). No row is two or more grades from its
  cell.

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten has no course before it in Curio Class. The rows' assumes are empty: the course assumes no library
skill, only children who can listen to a short read-aloud and take a turn in a circle with help, which unit 1 and
unit 4 teach anyway. No unit opens with a readiness check.
