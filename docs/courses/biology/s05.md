- **HS-LS2-1:** Use math or computer models to back explanations of what limits how many organisms an ecosystem can support, at different scales. Emphasis: comparing in numbers how linked factors such as boundaries, resources, climate and competition relate; with graphs, charts and population data from simulations or records. Limit: No deriving equations to make the comparisons.
- **HS-LS2-6:** Judge the claims, evidence and reasoning that ecosystem interactions keep life fairly steady under stable conditions, while changed conditions can produce a new ecosystem.
- **RST.9-10.7:** Translate quantitative or technical information expressed in words in a text into visual form (e.g., a table or chart) and translate information expressed visually or mathematically (e.g., in an equation) into words. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **HS-LS2-2:** Use math to back and revise evidence-based explanations about what affects biodiversity and population sizes in small and large ecosystems. Limit: Data are provided to students.
- **HS-LS2-8:** Weigh evidence for how living in groups affects an individual's and a species' chances to survive and reproduce. Emphasis: telling group behavior from individual behavior, finding evidence of what group behavior achieves, and arguing from that evidence; examples: flocking, schooling, herding, cooperative hunting, migration.
- **RST.9-10.8:** Assess the extent to which the reasoning and evidence in a text support the author’s claim or a recommendation for solving a scientific or technical problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **WHST.9-10.1:** Write arguments focused on discipline-specific content. Parts, by verb: a (WHST.9-10.1a) Introduce precise claim(s), distinguish the claim(s) from alternate or opposing claims, and create an organization that establishes clear relationships among the claim(s), counterclaims, reasons, and evidence.; b (WHST.9-10.1b) Develop claim(s) and counterclaims fairly, supplying data and evidence for each while pointing out the strengths and limitations of both claim(s) and counterclaims in a discipline-appropriate form and in a manner that anticipates the audience’s knowledge level and concerns.; c (WHST.9-10.1c) Use words, phrases, and clauses to link the major sections of the text, create cohesion, and clarify the relationships between claim(s) and reasons, between reasons and evidence, and between claim(s) and counterclaims.; d (WHST.9-10.1d) Establish and maintain a formal style and objective tone while attending to the norms and conventions of the discipline in which they are writing.; e (WHST.9-10.1e) Provide a concluding statement or section that follows from or supports the argument presented.
