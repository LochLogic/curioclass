# Course frame: Biology

Course `biology` (catalogue id `biology`), grade 9, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10 units, each driven by one anchor phenomenon or design problem.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 149 in units and 11 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year: 50-minute lessons, five a week, 32 weeks. Each investigation's active classroom sessions fit 50-minute lessons; longitudinal investigations (unit 3's seedlings) name the advance preparation, the elapsed observation time, the repeated measurements and any teacher processing between sessions (drying), and unit 3 reserves those sessions inside its existing days. A school with double periods may join two. The 24 rows are not equal: Biology owns 30 expectations, and five rows combine two or three performances (each with a Load line in its notes), so days follow performances, not rows. About 5 days a performance on average; units with a planned investigation (3), a new model (5) or a design cycle (9, 10) carry more, and the two-performance units 1, 2 and 4 carry 11 to 13. Unit 9 holds four expectations and the year's first full design cycle (17 days, so the refine step is not lost); unit 7 holds four, one a written and graphic communication task (18 days). Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments). A row last used in grade g gets retrieval in grade g+1 and a readiness check in grade g+2 or later; use earlier this year gets retrieval. Readiness takes the last ten minutes before the receiving unit opens, on the preceding unit's last day or an intervening respond day. Unit 1 alone checks after its day 1 hook. Responses run inside the first lesson that uses each row, with no added day. The scope and sequence lists each last use and treatment.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards high school performance expectations Biology owns (all of HS-LS, HS-ETS1-1, HS-ETS1-3, HS-ETS1-4, and HS-ESS2-7, HS-ESS3-3, HS-ESS3-4 under the modified science domains model, Desk card q-hs-science-core). Each expectation is taught in full: its practice, core idea and crosscutting concept together, every clarification example named in the row, and inside its assessment boundary. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md (high school Gateway 2 reads the expectations this course claims). In the Units table the **C3** column, a social studies column, holds for science the practices and crosscutting concepts the unit develops. **Check set adds** holds candidate practice, crosscutting concept and nature of science elements (NGSS appendices F, G and H) settled in the scope and sequence, with later unit plans tracing the kept elements. "NGSS" never appears in student, teacher or family copy. Values rule (b): facts and backed sources decide claims; evolution and the age of the Earth are taught as the evidence shows (BASELINE rule 5).
- **Base text.** None. High school science is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"); the open high school units listed there are reference only, for sequence and ideas, never copied (no shared run of 8 words). **How phenomena are chosen:** something a class can see, measure or read as data; puzzling at grade 9 and explainable with the unit's core ideas; not an anchor, design problem or check case any other science course uses, kindergarten to Physics, unless the unit says it returns on purpose and what is new (the grades 6 to 8 base text's bath bomb, maple tree, palm oil rainforest, penguin fossil and cattle with extra muscle among them); a local swap is allowed if it needs the same ideas. **Sources:** public-domain data and images from federal science agencies, open-license (CC BY) material each checked on its own terms, published research numbers redrawn as our own tables and graphs (numbers are facts; figures are not copied), and data the class collects. No paid text, no library trip, no student account.
- **Labs and materials.** Seeds are dry beans from the grocery store, never treated garden seed (it is often coated with fungicide); hands are washed after handling. Every investigation has three paths, said plainly: hands-on with classroom-cheap or household materials (a safe substitute named where one exists); a no-kit path from a provided data set the class reads for patterns; and, where no class could run it, a Curio Class simulation tool (listed per unit, built later through the tool pipeline, DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Every simulation runs with no account and has a paper path (a printed table stepped by hand or a printed run of the model). The scope and sequence states provisional costs for 30 students and one child, with reusable equipment counted once; unit planning refines quantities and costs. Each hands-on day carries a Safety and set-up section (rubric row S5); hazards for the year are named in the last column below.
- **Storyline and models.** Each unit opens on its anchor, builds a question board, and ends on a transfer task with a phenomenon the class has not seen. Every unit names its initial class model and the points where evidence revises it.
- **Nature of science, taught on purpose.** Unit 5 (an explanation changed by a second data set), unit 7 (independent lines of evidence that agree) and unit 8 (a claim judged by whether the evidence covers it).
- **Engineering.** Units 9 and 10 run full design cycles; HS-ETS1-1, HS-ETS1-3 and HS-ETS1-4 are owned here and used again in Chemistry and Physics.
- **Math readiness.** No unit uses math the math progression has not taught by then. Algebra I runs the same year and its frame gives the weeks: each same-year lean has an "if later" line under Cross-subject. Unit 5 steps a population forward before Algebra I's exponential unit (weeks 17 to 22), so it teaches its own step rule from grade 7 percent, with no exponential notation.
- **Reading level.** Grade 9 to 10 band; every reading is our own or adapted public-domain text, with words taught before they are needed (the rows' words lists).
- **Literacy standards.** The science and technical reading and writing standards for grades 9 and 10 (RST.9-10, WHST.9-10; data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt) are met alongside the science where the work already does them (Cory, Desk 2026-11-14: "Load them", no extra days). Each unit's "What students learn" cell ends with the codes it meets; they are never owned standards and add no days.
- **Safety read.** Units 1, 2, 3 and 6 are hands-on (and unit 9 if a stream visit is offered), so each gets the safety agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) before the course is called finished, against published school science safety guidance and the safety data sheet of every chemical named (unit 1's clear nail polish, unit 2's rubbing alcohol and salt, unit 3's yeast and sugar); the course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). Unit 6 (genetic conditions, human variation): the agent first studies disability-led organizations' published writing on genetics and genetic testing, a genetic alliance's family-facing pages, a national genome research institute's educator guidance on human variation (no sorting of human groups), and adoption and donor-conceived people's own writing on family-trait lessons. Unit 7 (evolution with students of faith): it first studies science teachers' associations' guidance on teaching evolution to students of faith and a national science academy's guide to science and religion, with the values rule in hand. Unit 9 (the Elwha River): before this unit ships, the sensitivity agent studies the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe's own published account (the Tribe's own pages and publications on the river and the dams) and checks the description of the Tribe and its role against it, with a journalism style guide on reporting Native nations. Unit 10 (fishing towns after the cod collapse): it studies Newfoundland fishing communities' own published accounts.

## Units

The order runs from the body inward to the cell, out to ecosystems, then to inheritance and evolution, and ends on human impact and design. Each step uses a model the one before built: cells before the molecules in them, photosynthesis and respiration before the energy and carbon they move through ecosystems, DNA before inheritance, variation before selection, and ecosystem and population models before the design work that protects them. The algebra-heavy units (5, 9, 10) sit after Algebra I has had time to teach what they lean on.
