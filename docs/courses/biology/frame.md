# Course frame: Biology

Course id `biology`, grade 9, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10 units, each driven by one anchor phenomenon or design problem.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 149 in units and 11 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year: 50-minute lessons, five a week, 32 weeks. Every investigation fits one lesson; a school with double periods may join two. The 24 rows are not equal: Biology owns 30 expectations, and five rows combine two or three performances (each with a Load line in its notes), so days follow performances, not rows. About 5 days a performance on average; units with a planned investigation (1, 3), a new model (5) or a design cycle (9, 10) carry more.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards high school performance expectations Biology owns (all of HS-LS, HS-ETS1-1, HS-ETS1-3, HS-ETS1-4, and HS-ESS2-7, HS-ESS3-3, HS-ESS3-4 under the modified science domains model, Desk card q-hs-science-core). Each expectation is taught in full: its practice, core idea and crosscutting concept together, every clarification example named in the row, and inside its assessment boundary. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md (high school Gateway 2 reads the expectations this course claims). In the Units table the **C3** column, a social studies column, holds for science the practices and crosscutting concepts the unit develops. **Check set adds** holds candidate practice, crosscutting concept and nature of science elements (NGSS appendices F, G and H) that the unit plan confirms or drops. "NGSS" never appears in student, teacher or family copy. Values rule (b): facts and backed sources decide claims; evolution and the age of the Earth are taught as the evidence shows (BASELINE rule 5).
- **Base text.** None. High school science is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"); the open high school units listed there are reference only, for sequence and ideas, never copied (no shared run of 8 words). **How phenomena are chosen:** something a class can see, measure or read as data; puzzling at grade 9 and explainable with the unit's core ideas; not an anchor the grades 6 to 8 base text already uses (the bath bomb, the maple tree, the palm oil rainforest, the penguin fossil, the cattle with extra muscle and the others named in the grade 6 to 8 rows); a local swap is allowed if it needs the same ideas. **Sources:** public-domain data and images from federal science agencies, open-licence (CC BY) material each checked on its own terms, published research numbers redrawn as our own tables and graphs (numbers are facts; figures are not copied), and data the class collects. No paid text, no library trip, no student account.
- **Labs and materials.** Every investigation has three paths, said plainly: hands-on with classroom-cheap or household materials (a safe substitute named where one exists); a no-kit path from a provided data set the class reads for patterns; and, where no class could run it, a Curio Class simulation tool (listed per unit, built later through the tool pipeline, DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Every simulation runs with no account and has a paper path (a printed table stepped by hand or a printed run of the model). A unit's cost to the school is stated in its unit plan. Each hands-on day carries a Safety and set-up section (rubric row S5); hazards for the year are named in the last column below.
- **Storyline and models.** Each unit opens on its anchor, builds a question board, and ends on a transfer task with a phenomenon the class has not seen. Every unit names its initial class model and the points where evidence revises it.
- **Nature of science, taught on purpose.** Unit 5 (an explanation changed by a second data set), unit 7 (independent lines of evidence that agree) and unit 8 (a claim judged by whether the evidence covers it).
- **Engineering.** Units 9 and 10 run full design cycles; HS-ETS1-1, HS-ETS1-3 and HS-ETS1-4 are owned here and used again in Chemistry and Physics.
- **Math readiness.** No unit uses math the math progression has not taught by then. Algebra I runs the same year: each same-year lean has an "if later" line under Cross-subject.
- **Reading level.** Grade 9 to 10 band; every reading is our own or adapted public-domain text, with words taught before they are needed (the rows' words lists).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent read. Units 1, 3 and every hands-on day need a qualified science teacher's safety and materials read (FOUNDATION 7.3). Unit 6 (genetic conditions, human variation) and unit 7 (evolution with students of faith) need a teacher reader experienced in those conversations. Unit 9 needs a reader from, or the published materials of, the Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe for how the Tribe and its role are described. Who reads, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Units

The order runs from the body inward to the cell, out to ecosystems, then to inheritance and evolution, and ends on human impact and design. Each step uses a model the one before built: cells before the molecules in them, photosynthesis and respiration before the energy and carbon they move through ecosystems, DNA before inheritance, variation before selection, and ecosystem and population models before the design work that protects them. The algebra-heavy units (5, 9, 10) sit after Algebra I has had time to teach what they lean on.
