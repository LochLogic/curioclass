# Unit 2: One cell, many kinds

Course `biology` (Biology). **Days 14 to 25** (12 course days; school days 14 to 25, weeks 3 to 5). Sections: 2.1 (days 14 to 17); 2.2 (days 18 to 21); 2.3 (days 22 to 25). Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 2, application 4, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- **2.1:** How DNA carries instructions.
- **2.2:** How cells multiply and specialise.
- **2.3:** How the parts rebuild an organism.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can cells with the same DNA rebuild a leg made of many different kinds of cells?"

**Hook, day 14:** Show non-graphic schematics of an axolotl's missing limb, outgrowth and restored limb, grounded in the regeneration study below. Reveal no cellular explanation. Students notice returning structures.
**Closing explanation:** On day 22, students explain regrowth using sequence evidence, division images and specialised-cell records: copied information supports more cells, while different proteins support different jobs. These mechanisms do not fully explain spatial patterning.
**Teaching:** Separate observations from inferences on day 15. On day 16, model sequence-to-function links in earlier and present records, assuming unchanged natural processes. Day 18 models copying before division; day 19 practises it.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


**Question board:** Group day 14 notices: information, cell number and jobs. Days 15 to 17 ask, "What information controls cellular work?" Then, "How does tissue gain cells?" drives days 18 and 19; "Why are cells different?" drives days 20 and 21. Days 22 and 23 connect answers to regrowth. Revisit each section; retain patterning questions. Day 24 transfers to unseen seedling repair.
**Limits:** Teach sequences and shape qualitatively. Do not assess named proteins, cell types, biochemical synthesis, gene-control mechanisms or division phases. No engineering expectation is claimed. The middle-school codes support day 25 readiness only, not new unit teaching.
**Carried:** Keep unit 1's systems, structure-function, graph, cause-effect and stability models. Build an initial molecular-to-organism model on day 14. Revise particle-model and structure-function on day 17 using sequence and protein evidence; revise systems on day 19 using complete DNA copies and on day 21 using differentiated-cell evidence. Day 22 establishes consensus; day 23 reconciles it, without new content. Apply it independently on day 24.

**Packet:** Keep this seedling case unseen until the individual drafts on day 21. All numerical records and schematic sequences are Curio Class original, **invented for practice**, not research findings. Supply stem-repair records: cell counts 12, 24 and 48 across three observation times. Within each plant, two newly formed cell groups share the same full DNA record but have different protein profiles and jobs, transporting water and protecting the surface. Supply a copied-DNA record, a developmental diagram beginning with a fertilised cell, and matched DNA, amino-acid-chain, shape and function representations. Compare two otherwise matched plants that differ at one DNA segment: the changed sequence gives a changed chain and a shape that no longer fits its interaction partner; the second plant transports less water under matched conditions. These are stipulated observations within the invented case. Do not ask students to derive real proteins from short DNA strings or identify named cell types.

**Scoring timeline:** Day 21 drafts are unscored practice using mechanisms taught on days 16 to 20. Return written component feedback on day 22; day 23 revises the drafts as unscored practice. Award transfer points only for reasoning tied to the supplied case, never isolated facts. The shorter path omits day 23 practice; day 22 keeps its existing consensus and closing explanation, with no added revision time.

**Sitting:** Day 24 uses five minutes reading, 20 minutes per scored item and five minutes checking: 50 minutes, without reteaching. Withhold a second seedling packet until then: leaf repair, counts 9, 18 and 36, shared DNA but different transport and protection protein profiles. Give the same two prompts above on these new records. Keep scored responses separate from the practice drafts.

**Item 1, teacher judged:** Award one point for each of ten look-fors: DNA base order carries information; amino-acid order follows that information; chain composition and arrangement affect shape; shape affects an interaction; proteins do cellular work; specialised cells cooperate in the repaired system; observations and models are distinguished; converging evidence supports more than a single representation; the explanation assumes consistent natural processes across past and present cases; extraction alone cannot reveal coding. Partial explanations earn only the components shown.

Meets: "The changed base order is linked to a changed amino-acid chain. Its different arrangement changes the protein's shape and interaction, consistent with the lower transport record. Proteins let specialised cells do different jobs; transport and protection together maintain the seedling. Sequence records, function observations and the shape model support successive links, although the model alone cannot prove them. I assume the same natural processes connected DNA and proteins during earlier growth as during repair. Seeing an extraction precipitate would not establish its base order or function." Does not: "The extract's colour reveals its DNA base sequence" (A).

**Item 2, teacher judged:** Award one point each for copying before division; complete identical DNA in each daughter cell; repeated division from a fertilised cell; differentiation during development; replacement of lost cells; different jobs in repair and maintenance; labelled information arrows; connected cell, tissue and organism levels; use of supplied counts and cell-profile evidence; and a supported prediction that blocked division reduces replacement even if surviving cells enlarge. Draw DNA copies before mitosis, then separate them into daughter cells. Assume accurate copying for this model.

Meets: "My model starts with a fertilised cell, copies its DNA before mitosis and passes a complete identical set into each daughter. Repeated divisions increase cell number; differentiation produces cells with different protein profiles and jobs. The 9, 18 and 36 counts support increasing cell number, while the profiles show different jobs despite shared DNA. The linked cells rebuild transport and protection in tissues, maintaining the whole seedling. Labelled arrows follow information from DNA through proteins to cellular work. If division stops, enlargement alone cannot replace the lost cells." Does not: "Each daughter receives half the original genes" (C).

**Section exit tickets:** Reserve the final five minutes on days 17 and 21. Both cases are **invented for practice**, withheld until their exit. Worked examples use axolotl records with different sequences, chain labels and counts; neither exit uses the seedling assessment case.

- **Day 17 record:** Matched pond-cell cultures clear waste. Sequencing and chain measurements show DNA segment GAA with chain segment P-Q-R, or GCA with P-S-R; letters P, Q, R and S represent amino-acid kinds, not DNA bases. Measured waste removal is respectively 18 or 6 units per minute. Earlier and present cultures give the same respective records. These are stipulated observations. A shape model predicts that the changed chain bends differently and fits its waste-binding partner poorly; binding was not measured. Ask: "Explain the changed waste removal from these records; what assumption and evidence limit your explanation?" Key: the changed base order corresponds to changed amino-acid order; altered arrangement could affect shape, binding and waste removal. Assume the same natural processes link DNA and proteins in earlier and present cultures. Sequence, chain and removal records are observations; shape and binding remain model predictions needing an independent binding test. Wrong: DNA physically becomes protein (B); the shape drawing proves waste removal (F).
- **Day 21 record:** An intact animal tissue has 60 cells at both observations. Tracking shows 15 old cells lost and 15 new cells formed locally, with no incoming cells. Division images show complete DNA copying before division and one identical set in each daughter. Newly formed cells share the full DNA record but have different protein profiles: one group contracts; another forms a protective barrier. Counts, tracking and profiles are stipulated observations. Ask: "Using both records, revise a model to explain how division and differentiation maintain this tissue despite its unchanged cell count." Key: division replaces lost cells, so a constant total does not mean no division; differentiation gives different protein profiles and jobs despite shared DNA. Model arrows connect replacement and specialised work to tissue and organism maintenance. Wrong: division alone makes different jobs (E); different jobs require different inherited genes (D).
