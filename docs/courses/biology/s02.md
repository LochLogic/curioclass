- **HS-LS1-1:** Explain with evidence how DNA's structure sets the structure of proteins, which do life's key jobs through systems of specialized cells. Limit: No naming particular cells, tissues or body systems, no particular proteins and their jobs, and no chemistry of protein making.
- **HS-LS1-4:** Use a model to show how cell division by mitosis and cell differentiation build and maintain a complex organism. Limit: No particular gene control mechanisms and no memorizing the stages of mitosis.
- **RST.9-10.4:** Determine the meaning of symbols, key terms, and other domain-specific words and phrases as they are used in a specific scientific or technical context relevant to grades 9–10 texts and topics. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **RST.9-10.7:** Translate quantitative or technical information expressed in words in a text into visual form (e.g., a table or chart) and translate information expressed visually or mathematically (e.g., in an equation) into words. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **WHST.9-10.2:** Write informative/explanatory texts, including the narration of historical events, scientific procedures/ experiments, or technical processes. Parts, by verb: a (WHST.9-10.2a) Introduce a topic and organize ideas, concepts, and information to make important connections and distinctions; include formatting (e.g., headings), graphics (e.g., figures, tables), and multimedia when useful to aiding comprehension.; b (WHST.9-10.2b) Develop the topic with well-chosen, relevant, and sufficient facts, extended definitions, concrete details, quotations, or other information and examples appropriate to the audience’s knowledge of the topic.; c (WHST.9-10.2c) Use varied transitions and sentence structures to link the major sections of the text, create cohesion, and clarify the relationships among ideas and concepts.; d (WHST.9-10.2d) Use precise language and domain-specific vocabulary to manage the complexity of the topic and convey a style appropriate to the discipline and context as well as to the expertise of likely readers.; e (WHST.9-10.2e) Establish and maintain a formal style and objective tone while attending to the norms and conventions of the discipline in which they are writing.; f (WHST.9-10.2f) Provide a concluding statement or section that follows from and supports the information or explanation presented (e.g., articulating implications or the significance of the topic).
- **MS-PS1-5:** Build and use a model showing that atoms are neither gained nor lost in a chemical reaction, so total mass stays the same. Emphasis: conservation of matter shown with physical or drawn models of atoms, including digital ones. Limit: Leave out atomic masses, balancing chemical equations with symbols, and forces between molecules.
- **MS-PS1-2:** Compare property data for substances from before and after they interact to decide whether a chemical reaction took place. Limit: Only these properties are analyzed: density, melting and boiling points, how well it dissolves, whether it burns, and smell.
- **MS-PS1-1:** Build models showing which atoms make up simple molecules and large repeating structures. Emphasis: models of molecules of differing complexity (ammonia, methanol) and repeating structures (table salt, diamond); models may be drawings, ball and stick kits or computer images. Limit: Leave out valence electrons, bond energy, the ionic character of repeating units, and drawing every single atom of a large molecule or structure.
