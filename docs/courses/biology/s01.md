## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How does a living thing keep conditions inside it nearly steady while conditions outside keep changing?"

**Hook, day 1:** Compare an original runner recovery graph, labelled "invented for practice", with the published leaf drying and rehydration evidence in field 10. Students circle changes, sketch hidden connections and ask why responses change while internal conditions remain bounded.
**Closing explanation, day 11:** Explain the runner and leaf responses using investigation records, tissue evidence and supplied internal-condition records. Distinguish observations from inferred mechanisms.
**Teaching moves:** Model one evidence-to-arrow revision on day 2. Require individual plans before negotiation on day 4. Thereafter ask which observation supports each arrow; leave competing explanations open until evidence distinguishes them.

(days 6 to 8), and "What is kept steady?" (day 9). The last question raises "Can feedback amplify change?" (day 10), then "Where does each response stop?" (day 11). Revisit the board daily, marking answers and unresolved questions. Section 1.1 uses hierarchy models to explain interacting subsystems; section 1.2 investigates regulation within limits; section 1.3 revises models to distinguish stabilising from amplifying feedback. Transfer: an unseen cold-response organism record, field 5.
**Limits:** No molecular reactions, signal transmission mechanisms or cellular feedback processes. Pulse is a response, not the regulated internal condition; recovery alone does not establish feedback. Root growth is excluded.

Day 11 produces the connected consensus model, including uncertainty; students use it on day 12.

**Sitting:** Five minutes read the unfamiliar packet, 20 minutes per item 1 and 2, five minutes check work: 50 minutes. No teaching or reteaching. Item 3 is scored outside the sitting. Items 1 and 2 require practices with ideas on new cases: 16 of 20 points, 80 percent.

**Teacher judgement:** Award each named point only when the response connects the relevant evidence to the claimed relationship; accept equivalent accurate models and words.

- Item 1 look-fors are the four two-point components above. Meets: "Flat cells form lining tissue within a lung in the respiratory system. Its thinness helps oxygen reach blood supplying shivering muscles; thickening could limit this supply and sustained shivering. The transport record connects digestion through blood to the calf: stopping calf flow reduces nutrient and water delivery. Nerve signals initiate contractions; interrupting them reduces shivering." The drawing nests these levels and traces material and information flows. Does not: "The stomach sends food directly to every muscle" (B).
- Item 2 look-fors are causal loops, evidence, stopping conditions, prediction and a usable observational design. Meets: "Temperature rises back toward its earlier range while the air remains cool. Shivering generates heat and reduced skin flow limits heat loss. Less deviation reduces the response; failed shivering could delay recovery. Birth amplification: contractions increase stretch at the uterine opening; increased stretch strengthens contractions. Delivery removes the initiating pressure. Thus this loop amplifies change until an endpoint, while the temperature loop counteracts deviation. Evidence comparison: compare each participant's warm-air and cool-air sessions with clothing, food and resting activity matched. Compare baseline and 5-, 10- and 15-minute readings using the 0.1-degree thermometer resolution; that resolution does not establish accuracy. Repeated observations show a trend, but observational records cannot isolate muscle heat production from every other influence. In conclusion, the two loops have different directions and stopping conditions." Does not: "All readings must be exactly 37.0 or regulation failed" (C).
- Item 3 look-fors are the four one-point components above. Meets: "I proposed equal counting windows to make our group's comparisons fair; we adopted them. My revised plan uses a seated baseline and two recovery trials at fixed intervals. Voluntary light activity with seated roles limits risk; existing timers cost nothing, and two trials with rest fit our practical time. I measured and labelled counts. Timer resolution and counting error limit precision. I retained the supplied 58, flagged its unexpected rise and proposed checking count timing and repeating rather than deleting it; it might be a real response. I revised the protocol to record each counting window's start." The teacher's dated record must support competent revised execution. Does not: "I removed the count that spoiled the pattern" (G).

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Routes:** Choose pulse work by default. Days 4 and 5 use paper planning and graphs. On each of days 6, 7 and 8, each trio needs a timing device, paper and pencils. Use 30-second wrist counts before voluntary light movement and beginning at zero, one, two and three minutes of recovery; repeat after rest. Students keep pulse values private; share only anonymised patterns. Clear space, stop for discomfort, offer seated timing and recording without explanation. No eye protection is needed for pulse work. The day before each session, check the space, access roles and protocol; reserve three minutes for clearing and handwashing.

For pores, use matched garden peas (Pisum sativum) from untreated seed. Contrast watering mildly; fix light and temperature. Teacher preparation starts day 3. Each trio needs two comparable leaves, tape, teacher-prepared slides, splash goggles and shared microscope access. Never substitute pore density for opening. The day before each practical, the teacher prepares tiny polish impressions with ventilation, no ignition, capped bottles and product-specific protective gear. Teacher alone handles solvent and glass; students observe prepared slides. No tasting; wash hands. Reserve five minutes for teacher-controlled cleanup. Pair observations with original pea water-status and water-loss records, labelled "invented for practice". Never substitute fern records or infer identical responses across species.

**Data labels:** Mark internal-condition, water-status, water-loss and no-kit heart-rate records "Curio Class original, invented for practice", with variables, units and conditions. Keep constructed and measured records separate.

**No-kit:** Printed time series and micrographs are weaker: planning and interpretation remain, execution does not. A seated observer can time and record a volunteer's real trial. Otherwise record missing execution.
