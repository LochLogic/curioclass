- **HS-LS4-5:** Weigh evidence that changing environments can make some species grow in number, give rise to new species over time, and drive other species extinct. Emphasis: cause and effect between changes such as deforestation, fishing, fertilizer, drought or flooding, and their pace, and how traits spread or vanish in species.
- **HS-ESS2-7:** Argue from evidence that Earth's systems and life on Earth have changed each other together over time. Emphasis: feedbacks where Earth conditions steer evolution while life reshapes the surface; examples: oxygen from early photosynthesis, soil from microbes, coral reefs. Limit: No full account of every way living things interact with Earth's other systems.
- **RST.9-10.9:** Compare and contrast findings presented in a text to those from other sources (including their own experiments), noting when the findings support or contradict previous explanations or accounts. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **RST.9-10.8:** Assess the extent to which the reasoning and evidence in a text support the author’s claim or a recommendation for solving a scientific or technical problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **WHST.9-10.1:** Write arguments focused on discipline-specific content. Parts, by verb: a (WHST.9-10.1a) Introduce precise claim(s), distinguish the claim(s) from alternate or opposing claims, and create an organization that establishes clear relationships among the claim(s), counterclaims, reasons, and evidence.; b (WHST.9-10.1b) Develop claim(s) and counterclaims fairly, supplying data and evidence for each while pointing out the strengths and limitations of both claim(s) and counterclaims in a discipline-appropriate form and in a manner that anticipates the audience’s knowledge level and concerns.; c (WHST.9-10.1c) Use words, phrases, and clauses to link the major sections of the text, create cohesion, and clarify the relationships between claim(s) and reasons, between reasons and evidence, and between claim(s) and counterclaims.; d (WHST.9-10.1d) Establish and maintain a formal style and objective tone while attending to the norms and conventions of the discipline in which they are writing.; e (WHST.9-10.1e) Provide a concluding statement or section that follows from or supports the argument presented.
- **MS-LS2-5:** Judge competing designs for protecting biodiversity and the services ecosystems give people.
- **MS-ESS3-3:** Use science ideas to design a way to track and reduce one human impact on the environment.
