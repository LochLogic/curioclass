# Scope and sequence: Biology

Course `biology`, grade 9, draft, revised 2026-11-15.

The calendar is 160 course days, five 50-minute meetings a week: 149 days in the frame's ten units and 11 course-level check and respond days. Course day and school day coincide, so `school_day` is blank. The authoritative day order, section assignments, goals, skills, codes and materials are in `data/plans/biology/days.csv`. Sections divide changes in the question being pursued, not equal shares of the owned rows. No unit exceeds 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment is required or reserved.

Each unit starts with its phenomenon or design problem, a question board and an initial model. Evidence prompts the revisions named below and in the day notes. The notebook and model give daily evidence; section plans will place their closing exit tickets. This sequence reserves performances and assessment purposes, not items or keys. End-of-unit assessments combine independent work on an unseen case with earlier observed performances where carrying out an investigation, communicating or iterating a design cannot fit into one assessment period. Those earlier performances are assessed through the portfolio on the named assessment day, not counted as extra assessment days. Response to each unit assessment occurs once, on its final Respond day.

For every code on an end-of-unit assessment, the later items or named performance must address every part of its statement, practice, core idea, crosscutting element and relevant connections in `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv`, including secondary core ideas. A short prompt per part or recorded performance supplies evidence; a code label alone does not. Boundaries in that file and the frame bind the task. Science expectations are the base; the Massachusetts framework is a crosswalk only. Literacy codes in the frame accompany science work without becoming owned science rows or adding days. Course-level checks are samples of the parts named in their blueprint below; they do not claim a complete expectation or re-score unit portfolios. Each listed code receives its named short prompts after teaching. Individual and small-group evidence is collected once per window; an end day within ten days reuses its record, without another performance. Earlier portfolio evidence is first scored on the unit's named end day.

## Unit 1. Steady inside

**Days 1 to 13, weeks 1 to 3, 13 days.** The runner and leaf-pore records raise the question of how interacting systems keep internal conditions within a range. Days 1 to 3 develop a hierarchy model before the class needs it to interpret feedback. Days 4 to 8 plan, conduct, repeat and improve an investigation, day 9 revises the model from its results, and day 10 contrasts counteracting feedback with the required amplifying blood-clotting case and its stopping conditions. Day 11 reconciles the accounts before transfer.

**Readiness on day 1, last ten minutes after the hook:** `s7-body-systems-food`, last used in grade 7. Day 2 responds with a worked levels-and-pathway model before the artery evidence. Day 1 retrieves `s8-senses-brain` and recurring systems models. Day 5 teaches worked dot plots and histograms because Algebra I's displays unit is underway on days 1 to 17. The investigation chooses heart-rate recovery or leaf pores linked to water regulation, never root growth toward water. Pore observations are paired with water-loss/recovery records so a static pore image alone is not called feedback evidence. For the pore option the teacher applies small amounts of clear nail polish in a ventilated room away from ignition and prepares fragile slides; eye protection and a shared microscope are listed on days 4 to 8. Supplied micrographs are the no-kit option. Exercise is voluntary and light, with seated recording or class data, no required health disclosure.

**Assessment day 12, response day 13.** Both owned codes are checked through a new organism model and feedback evidence, together with the individual and collaborative investigation record from days 4 to 8: variables, amount and accuracy of data, repetition, constraints and improvement. Positive and negative feedback both appear; cellular mechanisms do not. For 30 students, ten groups of three rotate roles across days 6 to 8; the teacher observes four, three and three groups for three minutes each, with a contribution recorded for every student. Other groups collect repeats or graph. Day 12 first scores that record and uses an independent model/feedback task, without collecting execution again.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Investigation planning and data accuracy occupy day 4 and the protocol-improvement work on day 7. Stabilising and destabilising feedback occupies day 10. These three existing sessions carry the candidates with no days added to the 13; they also deepen required performances.

## Unit 2. One cell, many kinds

**Days 14 to 25, weeks 3 to 5, 12 days.** The axolotl's regrowth makes the shared-DNA/different-tissue puzzle visible. Days 14 to 17 develop the information-to-protein explanation; extraction supplies an observation to question, not proof of the coding mechanism. Days 18 to 21 move from counts in supplied root-tip images to a model of copying, division and differentiation. Days 22 and 23 reconnect these mechanisms to the organism model. This order makes protein function available before differentiated cells must be explained.

**No readiness check.** Retrieve `s8-genes-proteins-traits`, `s8-cell-parts-function` and `s8-growth-factors` on day 14. Day 15 has the extraction and recorded no-kit alternative. Rubbing alcohol is flammable, used in small amounts, poured by the teacher with no flame in the room; eye protection is required. Stem cell questions receive brief factual treatment without a request for beliefs.

**Assessment day 24, response day 25.** An unseen repair phenomenon requires an evidence-based DNA-to-protein explanation and a division-and-differentiation model showing shared information, growth and maintenance. No biochemical protein-making steps, gene-control mechanisms or mitosis phase recall are assessed.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Structure explained through substructures occupies day 16; explanation using representations occupies day 17, with a later model revision on day 21. These are existing teaching sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 3. Where the mass goes

**Days 26 to 43, weeks 6 to 9, 18 days.** Dark and light seedlings raise a matter question before the energy account is settled. Days 26 to 29 establish matched cohorts and photosynthesis; days 30 to 33 distinguish respiration, qualitative bond-energy accounting and revised explanations with and without oxygen. Days 34 to 36 explain growth materials using models and carbon-tracer evidence, including incoming nitrogen and sulfur. Days 37 to 40 bring gas records, dry mass and tracer evidence together. The load is four distinct performances, including two evidence-driven revisions, rather than three equal skill blocks.

**Readiness on day 25, last ten minutes of unit 2's respond day:** `s7-reactions-atoms`, `s7-food-molecules` and matter-cycle, last used grade 7. Day 26 responds to atom conservation and matter-cycle with a worked atom/reservoir representation before new modeling; day 34 responds to food subunits before growth-molecule work. Day 26 retrieves `s7-photosynthesis`, `s7-cell-respiration-growth`, `s7-reaction-energy` and energy-transfer, explicitly used in grade 8; day 29 retrieves molecule ratios with a worked atom-count representation. Teacher preparation includes sprouts 3 to 5 days before day 26 and matched dry-mass baselines. Short light-grown cohorts may lose less dry mass rather than gain it; longer-grown supplied records carry the net-gain comparison. Unit planning pilots species, light and elapsed timing and retains the actual class result. Cohorts begin day 27; brief observations on days 28, 30, 32 and 34 are inside those sessions; harvest is day 36. The teacher dries samples in a low oven between days 36 and 38, with repeated drying to stable mass, and supplies cooled samples on day 38. Provided replicate data replace unavailable equipment or failed cohorts. Day 32's warm-water yeast observation is compared with supplied oxygen-controlled data: an open or covered classroom cup alone cannot establish absence of oxygen. No tasting or flames; drying and hot sample handling are teacher-only.

**Assessment days 41 and 42, response day 43 once for both.** Day 41 checks HS-LS1-5 and HS-LS1-7 through two linked models on an unseen organism: photosynthesis inputs/outputs and stored energy, then respiration bond costs, new products and net energy including warmth, with atoms and energy separate. Day 42 checks HS-LS2-3 and HS-LS1-6 through initial and revised explanations with new oxygen-controlled and tracer evidence; include incoming nitrogen/sulfur and evidence limits. Each day allows 20 minutes per performance and ten to read/check. Dry mass alone cannot establish amino-acid synthesis. Former day 41 consolidation moves into day 40.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Flow accounting occupies days 28 to 31, four existing sessions; explanation revision occupies days 33 and 36, two existing sessions. Zero additional days; these candidate elements are integral to the four owned performances.

## Unit 4. Energy and carbon through an ecosystem

**Days 47 to 57, weeks 10 to 12, 11 days.** The river's large plant production and small fish biomass make proportional energy accounting necessary. Days 47 to 50 separate new biomass, respiratory heat and detrital chemical energy while conserving atoms. The atmospheric seasonal record then asks where carbon moves: days 51 to 53 develop and revise the four-reservoir model, distinguishing biological and nonbiological arrows and long storage. Day 54 uses the linked models; day 55 consolidates. Unit 3's processes must precede both the energy budget and the carbon arrows.

**Readiness on day 46, final ten minutes after 40 minutes of course-check response:** `s7-ecosystem-matter-energy` and food-web, last used grade 7. Day 47 responds with a worked producer/consumer/decomposer budget, then retrieves matter-cycle and unit 3's processes; day 48 retrieves grade 7 percent. Transfer efficiency varies; a tenth is illustrative. This is a no-kit data unit, with offline or printed model runs and no physical investigation hazard.

**Assessment day 56, response day 57.** An unseen food web requires a proportional energy argument, conserved carbon/oxygen/hydrogen/nitrogen atoms and a carbon model linking biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere and geosphere through photosynthesis and respiration. Energy is not recycled, and energy not passed to a consumer is not all immediately heat.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Conservation of energy and mathematical support for claims occupy days 49 and 50, two existing sessions; the carbon comparison applies them again on day 54. Zero additional days.

Days 66 to 68 evaluate stability and disturbance arguments; days 69 to 71 evaluate group behavior inside the island case. Day 72 connects the evidence and absorbs the former day 73 consolidation; days 73 and 74 separate the four assessment performances. The short group-behavior row has about three sessions; it does not receive the same allocation as the new computational model.

**No readiness check.** Retrieve `s7-resources-populations`, `s7-ecosystem-disruption` and carried models on day 58, and `s8-reproduction-behaviors` on day 69. Algebra I center/spread ends day 17 and equations ends day 46, before they are used here. ELA argument evaluation comes days 100 to 116, so day 66 includes the frame's worked example checking whether evidence covers a claim's scale; it serves both stability and group-behavior arguments. Hunting and predator control stay evidence questions without a request for personal positions.

**Assessment days 73 and 74, response day 75 once for both.** Day 73 checks HS-LS2-1 and HS-LS2-2: new multi-scale carrying-capacity records, prediction before a supplied-rule run, and a mathematical biodiversity explanation revised on a second survey. Day 74 checks HS-LS2-6 and HS-LS2-8: stable/modest/extreme change claims and causal group-behavior evidence for individuals' and species' survival and reproduction. Allow 20 minutes per performance plus ten to read/check on each day. No diversity index is required.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Explanation revision as a feature of science occupies day 65; stability and change through evidence judgment occupies days 67 and 68. Three existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 6. Inheritance and variation

**Days 79 to 94, weeks 16 to 19, 16 days.** The Labrador litter prompts questions about inherited instructions before probability is used to explain its outcomes. Days 79 to 81 practice asking model-derived, answerable questions about DNA and chromosomes. Days 82 to 84 compare recombination, viable copying errors and environmental mutation; days 85 to 89 distinguish predicted cross probabilities, measured continuous variation and environmental expression. Days 90 and 91 revise and apply the combined account; day 91 absorbs former day 92 consolidation before the two-part assessment. Questions, defended claims and mathematical analysis are three different performances.

**No readiness check.** Day 79 retrieves `s8-inheritance`, `s8-human-trait-technology`, `s8-genes-proteins-traits` and unit 2's DNA model. Day 85 retrieves compound-event probability; Algebra I displays and linear-model instruction have finished by days 17 and 59. Day 84 supplies the grade 8 claim-evidence-reasoning frame because ELA argument writing is later, days 100 to 116; a short model compares a supplied alternative interpretation, then students weigh evidence and defend the stronger claim against it. Days 87 and 88 plan and measure a pooled 100-bean sample, with a provided-data alternative. Grocery beans only, never treated seed; wash hands. Use animal, plant and invented cases, never students' family traits, ancestry or health. If illness arises, keep it general and provide a quiet alternative.

**Assessment days 92 and 93, response day 94 once for both.** Day 92 checks HS-LS3-1 through model-derived questions about chromosomes/DNA/genes, coding and noncoding roles, shared DNA and different gene use, and evidence to distinguish cause from correlation. Day 93 checks HS-LS3-2 and HS-LS3-3: defend all three heritable sources against a supplied alternative (20 minutes), then use probability/statistics and an algebraic relationship to explain genes/environment and distributions (20 minutes), with ten to read/check. Day 89's individual written analysis is first scored on day 93 for reciprocal technology/science and society/research connections; no second collection. The specified two-gene cross has a 9:3:4 expectation, not a promise for a small litter. No other gene interactions, meiosis phases or Hardy-Weinberg calculations are required.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Model-derived question refinement occupies days 80 and 81; cause versus correlation occupies the evidence comparison on day 89. Three existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 7. The evidence for evolution

**Days 95 to 112, weeks 19 to 23, 18 days.** Stickleback armour and molecular evidence connect the ancestry question to a mechanism of selection. Days 95 to 98 establish independent sequence, anatomy, embryo and fossil evidence. Days 99 to 101 explain four interacting selection factors; days 102 to 105 analyze trait-distribution shifts and explain adaptation from supplied biotic and abiotic contrasts. Days 106 to 109 develop, share and revise written, graphic, spoken and quantitative explanations; day 110 consolidates. This protects both performances in the combined selection row and the substantial communication performance.

**No readiness check.** Day 95 retrieves `s8-fossil-record`, `s8-anatomical-relationships`, `s8-embryo-patterns`, `s8-natural-selection`, `s8-selection-math` and units 5 and 6. Day 102 retrieves Algebra I displays and center/spread, complete by day 17. ELA informative writing is days 117 to 130, later: day 106 gives a model explanation with headings and a figure and a planning frame. The communication product is complete on day 109, week 22, before ELA's weeks 24 and 26 process pieces and available before ELA's island-bird unit. Evolution is taught as the evidence shows; respect students' beliefs and never ask them to state or give up a belief. This note also accompanies ELA's later evolution reading.

**Assessment day 111, response day 112.** A new lineage supplies evidence for an independent explanation using ancestry, all four selection factors, distribution shifts and adaptation. The earlier communication portfolio supplies the observed multi-form performance. Day 109 records six groups of five, each student giving a one-minute evidence account, 30 minutes total while others revise figures. Day 111 first scores that record for HS-LS4-1, the day 103 statistical comparison for HS-LS4-3 and the day 105 adaptation revision for HS-LS4-4, without another talk or full performance. Independent work is one four-factor explanation (25 minutes), short transfer prompts for other listed parts/connections (20), and checking (5). Gene-frequency data are provided for adaptation; students do not calculate allele frequencies. Drift, gene flow and coevolution mechanisms are not required.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Scientific theory and independent evidence occupy day 98; patterns across scales occupy days 96 and 97 and the quantitative comparison on day 102. Four existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 8. Life and Earth change each other

**Days 113 to 123, weeks 23 to 25, 11 days.** The mineral and red-rock contrast starts a reciprocal Earth-life model. Days 113 to 115 add oxygen, weathering, microbial soil, land plants, reefs and erosion. Days 116 to 118 ask which evidence supports increased numbers, speciation or extinction under different rates of change. Days 119 and 120 judge past-environment claims and construct spoken and written feedback arguments; day 121 reconciles them. The order makes organism-level selection and photosynthesis available before planetary feedback and species-scale outcomes.

**Readiness on day 112, final ten minutes after 40 minutes of unit response:** `s6-plate-evidence` and plate-model. Grade 7 day 143 explicitly retrieves the row; no grade 8 day uses it. Day 113 responds with worked past-position evidence before the Earth-life model, and day 117 uses the result again before barrier/speciation evidence. Day 113 retrieves `s8-natural-selection` and `s8-fossil-record`. ELA argument writing overlaps the opening, ending day 116; day 113 supplies the earlier claim-evidence-reasoning frame, used again on day 120. Deep time and Earth's age follow the evidence. Extinction is handled without fatalism, ending with evidence of what changes its rate.

**Assessment day 122, response day 123.** A new environmental record requires evaluation of increase/speciation/extinction claims, rates and evidence coverage, and a reciprocal Earth-life argument. It checks feedback rather than a one-way list of impacts, without requiring every Earth-system interaction. Day 120 collects spoken arguments once: six groups of five, three minutes each (18 total), 22 minutes independent writing and ten revision, with a contribution recorded per student. Day 122 reuses that spoken record and reserves 20 minutes per code for new written evidence plus ten read/check; no second oral collection.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Quantified change rates occupy day 118; consistency of natural laws and the limits of past-environment inference occupy day 119. Two existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 9. Designing for a river

Days 124 to 127 define needs, measurable criteria, constraints and candidate solutions. Days 128 to 130 evaluate impact records and feedback, including local reduction/reuse/recycling and one supplied large-scale geoengineering proposal. Days 131 to 135 design, test, compare and refine, including a second challenge to the revision. Day 136 refines multi-term queries in an account-free searchable offline collection, compares authoritative sources for usefulness and records selections/citations in its first 20 minutes, then plans synthesis for 30 minutes. Days 137 and 138 draft/edit the report, integrating selected evidence and tests with accurate citations and no patchwriting. Four owned performances and the first full high school design cycle receive time without sacrificing refinement.

**Readiness on day 123, final ten minutes after 40 minutes of unit response:** `s7-biodiversity-solutions` and `s7-human-impact-design`, last used grade 7. Day 126 retrieves unit conversion. Linear-model precision was taught by Algebra I day 59, before impact-data judgment on day 128. ELA source work ends day 84 and informative writing ends day 130, before report planning on day 136. **Report due day 139, week 28**, apart from ELA's week 26 explanation and World History I's week 31 argument. **Assessment day 139, response day 140.** The design report and observed test/refine portfolio cover the complete cycle; an unseen river constraint tests independent transfer. ## Unit 10. Testing rules before the ocean does

**Days 141 to 155, weeks 29 to 31, 15 days.** Cod-collapse and whale-mortality records require a model that makes assumptions inspectable before a rule is tried. Days 141 to 145 construct, check and revise a resource-management simulation that tracks both human sustainability and biodiversity. Days 146 to 148 revise a species-protection simulation, not merely its settings. Days 149 to 152 predict, test and refine rule packages across interacting systems; day 153 reconciles the claims and limits. The single owned row contains three performances and receives separate time for each.

**Readiness on day 140, final ten minutes after 40 minutes of unit response:** `s7-population-consumption`, last used grade 7. Day 141 responds with a worked population-times-per-person-use comparison before defining the model. Retrieve `s7-human-impact-design` after unit 9, `s8-design-test-combine` and units 5 and 7. Days 142 and 144 retrieve expressions, percent and scatter plots. Algebra I exponentials ended day 107; the supplied update rule is still taught explicitly, with cell and formula instruction on day 143 (Understanding). Learners construct a simple resource spreadsheet and change a rule or relationship in the protection model. Printed steps preserve hand checking and reasoning; minimum access is one offline device per group of three, ten devices for 30 students, with each learner making a recorded formula/rule edit. Paper work is weaker evidence and cannot alone show computational construction or revision. Fishing communities and lost livelihoods are treated with respect, without assigning blame or requiring personal views.

**Assessment day 154, response day 155.** Construction and revision portfolios plus an unseen-data, changed-constraint run cover all three codes: human-harm reduction for a threatened species, resource management linked to human sustainability and biodiversity, and prediction of within-system and between-system effects under multiple criteria and constraints. First score the constructed resource model (HS-ESS3-3), revised protection rule (HS-LS4-6) and cross-system tests (HS-ETS1-4) here; do not rebuild all three. Day 150 teaches and retains evidence on technological reliance, unintended effects and creative human model choices, including human-population and urban-demand scenarios. One new changed-constraint run takes 25 minutes, remaining-part/connections prompts 20, checking five. This is evidence about assumptions and model consequences, not proof that a rule will work in the ocean.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Model boundaries and initial conditions occupy day 142; predicting solution impacts with simulations occupies day 149, applied again on days 150 and 151. Four existing sessions, zero additional days.
