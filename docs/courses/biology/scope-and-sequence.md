# Scope and sequence: Biology

Course `biology`, grade 9, draft, 2026-11-14.

The calendar is 160 course days, five 50-minute meetings a week: 149 days in the frame's ten units and 11 course-level check and respond days. Course day and school day coincide, so `school_day` is blank. The authoritative day order, section assignments, goals, skills, codes and materials are in `data/plans/biology/days.csv`. Sections divide changes in the question being pursued, not equal shares of the owned rows. No unit exceeds 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment is required or reserved.

Each unit starts with its phenomenon or design problem, a question board and an initial model. Evidence prompts the revisions named below and in the day notes. The notebook and model give daily evidence; section plans will place their closing exit tickets. This sequence reserves performances and assessment purposes, not items or keys. End-of-unit assessments combine independent work on an unseen case with earlier observed performances where carrying out an investigation, communicating or iterating a design cannot fit into one assessment period. Those earlier performances are assessed through the portfolio on the named assessment day, not counted as extra assessment days. Response to each unit assessment occurs once, on its final Respond day.

For every code on an assessment or course-level check, the later items or named performance must address every part of its statement, practice, core idea, crosscutting element and relevant connections in `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv`, including secondary core ideas. A short prompt per part or recorded performance supplies evidence; a code label alone does not. Boundaries in that file and the frame bind the task. Science expectations are the base; the Massachusetts framework is a crosswalk only. Literacy codes in the frame accompany science work without becoming owned science rows or adding days.

## Unit 1. Steady inside

**Days 1 to 13, weeks 1 to 3, 13 days.** The runner and leaf-pore records raise the question of how interacting systems keep internal conditions within a range. Days 1 to 3 develop a hierarchy model before the class needs it to interpret feedback. Days 4 to 8 plan, conduct, repeat and improve an investigation, day 9 revises the model from its results, and day 10 contrasts counteracting feedback with the required amplifying blood-clotting case and its stopping conditions. Day 11 reconciles the accounts before transfer.

There is **no readiness check**: retrieve `s7-body-systems-food`, `s8-senses-brain` and the carried models within day 1. Day 5 teaches worked dot plots and histograms because Algebra I's displays unit is still underway on days 1 to 17. The class chooses heart-rate recovery, root growth toward water or leaf-pore response. For roots, teacher preparation starts 3 to 5 days before day 4, bags are set on day 4 and observed on days 5 to 8; the days reserve those observations. Exercise is voluntary and light, with seated recording or class data, no required health disclosure. Bags remain sealed and are discarded sealed.

**Assessment day 12, response day 13.** Both owned codes are checked through a new organism model and feedback evidence, together with the individual and collaborative investigation record from days 4 to 8: variables, amount and accuracy of data, repetition, constraints and improvement. Positive and negative feedback both appear; cellular mechanisms do not.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Investigation planning and data accuracy occupy day 4 and the protocol-improvement work on day 7. Stabilising and destabilising feedback occupies day 10. These three existing sessions carry the candidates with no days added to the 13; they also deepen required performances.

## Unit 2. One cell, many kinds

**Days 14 to 25, weeks 3 to 5, 12 days.** The axolotl's regrowth makes the shared-DNA/different-tissue puzzle visible. Days 14 to 17 develop the information-to-protein explanation; extraction supplies an observation to question, not proof of the coding mechanism. Days 18 to 21 move from counts in supplied root-tip images to a model of copying, division and differentiation. Days 22 and 23 reconnect these mechanisms to the organism model. This order makes protein function available before differentiated cells must be explained.

**No readiness check.** Retrieve `s8-genes-proteins-traits`, `s8-cell-parts-function` and `s8-growth-factors` on day 14. Day 15 has the extraction and recorded no-kit alternative. Rubbing alcohol is flammable, used in small amounts, poured by the teacher with no flame in the room; eye protection is required. Stem cell questions receive brief factual treatment without a request for beliefs.

**Assessment day 24, response day 25.** An unseen repair phenomenon requires an evidence-based DNA-to-protein explanation and a division-and-differentiation model showing shared information, growth and maintenance. No biochemical protein-making steps, gene-control mechanisms or mitosis phase recall are assessed.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Structure explained through substructures occupies day 16; explanation using representations occupies day 17, with a later model revision on day 21. These are existing teaching sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 3. Where the mass goes

**Days 26 to 43, weeks 6 to 9, 18 days.** Dark and light seedlings raise a matter question before the energy account is settled. Days 26 to 29 establish matched cohorts and photosynthesis; days 30 to 33 distinguish respiration, qualitative bond-energy accounting and revised explanations with and without oxygen. Days 34 to 36 explain growth materials using models and carbon-tracer evidence, including incoming nitrogen and sulfur. Days 37 to 40 bring gas records, dry mass and tracer evidence together. The load is four distinct performances, including two evidence-driven revisions, rather than three equal skill blocks.

**No readiness check.** Day 26 retrieves `s7-photosynthesis`, `s7-reactions-atoms`, `s7-cell-respiration-growth`, `s7-reaction-energy`, `s7-food-molecules`, matter-cycle and energy-transfer; day 29 retrieves molecule ratios with a worked atom-count representation. Teacher preparation includes sprouts 3 to 5 days before day 26 and matched dry-mass baselines. Cohorts begin day 27; brief observations on days 28, 30, 32 and 34 are inside those sessions; harvest is day 36. The teacher dries samples in a low oven between days 36 and 38, with repeated drying to stable mass, and supplies cooled samples on day 38. Provided replicate data replace unavailable equipment or failed cohorts. Day 32's warm-water yeast observation is compared with supplied oxygen-controlled data: an open or covered classroom cup alone cannot establish absence of oxygen. No tasting or flames; drying and hot sample handling are teacher-only.

**Assessment day 42, response day 43.** A new growing-organism case requires photosynthesis and respiration models with atoms and energy tracked separately, a revised oxygen/no-oxygen explanation, and a revised explanation of sugar atoms becoming larger molecules with other incoming elements. The assessment supplies new oxygen and tracer evidence. Dry mass alone cannot establish amino-acid synthesis. Day 41 consolidates without adding a new performance.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Flow accounting occupies days 28 to 31, four existing sessions; explanation revision occupies days 33 and 36, two existing sessions. Zero additional days; these candidate elements are integral to the four owned performances.

## Unit 4. Energy and carbon through an ecosystem

**Days 47 to 57, weeks 10 to 12, 11 days.** The river's large plant production and small fish biomass make proportional energy accounting necessary. Days 47 to 50 separate new biomass, respiratory heat and detrital chemical energy while conserving atoms. The atmospheric seasonal record then asks where carbon moves: days 51 to 53 develop and revise the four-reservoir model, distinguishing biological and nonbiological arrows and long storage. Day 54 uses the linked models; day 55 consolidates. Unit 3's processes must precede both the energy budget and the carbon arrows.

**No readiness check.** Day 47 retrieves `s7-ecosystem-matter-energy`, food-web and carried matter/energy models; day 48 retrieves grade 7 percent. Transfer efficiency varies; a tenth is illustrative. This is a no-kit data unit, with offline or printed model runs and no physical investigation hazard.

**Assessment day 56, response day 57.** An unseen food web requires a proportional energy argument, conserved carbon/oxygen/hydrogen/nitrogen atoms and a carbon model linking biosphere, atmosphere, hydrosphere and geosphere through photosynthesis and respiration. Energy is not recycled, and energy not passed to a consumer is not all immediately heat.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Conservation of energy and mathematical support for claims occupy days 49 and 50, two existing sessions; the carbon comparison applies them again on day 54. Zero additional days.

## Unit 5. Populations rise, fall and hold

**Days 58 to 75, weeks 12 to 15, 18 days.** Island moose and wolf records first need population limits, then a rule that steps populations forward. Days 58 to 62 introduce computational-model, with a prediction before each changed-input run. Days 63 to 65 compare provided biodiversity data at local and watershed scales and revise the explanation when a second set limits the first. Days 66 to 68 evaluate stability and disturbance arguments; days 69 to 71 evaluate group behaviour inside the island case. Days 72 and 73 connect the evidence. The short group-behaviour row has about three sessions; it does not receive the same allocation as the new computational model.

**No readiness check.** Retrieve `s7-resources-populations`, `s7-ecosystem-disruption` and carried models on day 58, and `s8-reproduction-behaviors` on day 69. Algebra I centre/spread ends day 17 and equations ends day 46, before they are used here. Day 60 nevertheless teaches the population step rule explicitly from grade 7 percent, because exponentials come later, on days 84 to 107. No exponential notation or derived equations are needed. ELA argument evaluation comes days 100 to 116, so day 66 includes the frame's worked example checking whether evidence covers a claim's scale; it serves both stability and group-behaviour arguments. Hunting and predator control stay evidence questions without a request for personal positions.

**Assessment day 74, response day 75.** A new ecosystem's provided data require multi-scale carrying-capacity modelling, prediction before running, biodiversity explanation revised on a second set, and judgments of stable/modest/extreme change and causal group-behaviour evidence. No diversity index is required.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Explanation revision as a feature of science occupies day 65; stability and change through evidence judgment occupies days 67 and 68. Three existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 6. Inheritance and variation

**Days 79 to 94, weeks 16 to 19, 16 days.** The Labrador litter prompts questions about inherited instructions before probability is used to explain its outcomes. Days 79 to 81 practise asking model-derived, answerable questions about DNA and chromosomes. Days 82 to 84 compare recombination, viable copying errors and environmental mutation; days 85 to 89 distinguish predicted cross probabilities, measured continuous variation and environmental expression. Days 90 to 92 revise and apply the combined account. Questions, defended claims and mathematical analysis are three different performances.

**No readiness check.** Day 79 retrieves `s8-inheritance`, `s8-human-trait-technology`, `s8-genes-proteins-traits` and unit 2's DNA model. Day 85 retrieves compound-event probability; Algebra I displays and linear-model instruction have finished by days 17 and 59. Day 84 supplies the grade 8 claim-evidence-reasoning frame because ELA argument writing is later, days 100 to 116; counterclaims are not required here. Days 87 and 88 plan and measure a pooled 100-bean sample, with a provided-data alternative. Grocery beans only, never treated seed; wash hands. Use animal, plant and invented cases, never students' family traits, ancestry or health. If illness arises, keep it general and provide a quiet alternative.

**Assessment day 93, response day 94.** An unseen nonhuman case requires clarifying questions about coding and noncoding DNA, a defended claim about all three heritable sources and mathematical explanation of trait distributions shaped by genes and environment. The specified two-gene cross has a 9:3:4 expectation, not a promise for a small litter. No other gene interactions, meiosis phases or Hardy-Weinberg calculations are required.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Model-derived question refinement occupies days 80 and 81; cause versus correlation occupies the evidence comparison on day 89. Three existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 7. The evidence for evolution

**Days 95 to 112, weeks 19 to 23, 18 days.** Stickleback armour and molecular evidence connect the ancestry question to a mechanism of selection. Days 95 to 98 establish independent sequence, anatomy, embryo and fossil evidence. Days 99 to 101 explain four interacting selection factors; days 102 to 105 analyse trait-distribution shifts and explain adaptation from supplied biotic and abiotic contrasts. Days 106 to 109 develop, share and revise written, graphic, spoken and quantitative explanations; day 110 consolidates. This protects both performances in the combined selection row and the substantial communication performance.

**No readiness check.** Day 95 retrieves `s8-fossil-record`, `s8-anatomical-relationships`, `s8-embryo-patterns`, `s8-natural-selection`, `s8-selection-math` and units 5 and 6. Day 102 retrieves Algebra I displays and centre/spread, complete by day 17. ELA informative writing is days 117 to 130, later: day 106 gives a model explanation with headings and a figure and a planning frame. The communication product is complete on day 109, week 22, before ELA's weeks 24 and 26 process pieces and available before ELA's island-bird unit. Evolution is taught as the evidence shows; respect students' beliefs and never ask them to state or give up a belief. This note also accompanies ELA's later evolution reading.

**Assessment day 111, response day 112.** A new lineage supplies evidence for an independent explanation using ancestry, all four selection factors, distribution shifts and adaptation. The earlier communication portfolio supplies the observed multi-form performance. Gene-frequency data are provided for adaptation; students do not calculate allele frequencies. Drift, gene flow and coevolution mechanisms are not required.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Scientific theory and independent evidence occupy day 98; patterns across scales occupy days 96 and 97 and the quantitative comparison on day 102. Four existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 8. Life and Earth change each other

**Days 113 to 123, weeks 23 to 25, 11 days.** The mineral and red-rock contrast starts a reciprocal Earth-life model. Days 113 to 115 add oxygen, weathering, microbial soil, land plants, reefs and erosion. Days 116 to 118 ask which evidence supports increased numbers, speciation or extinction under different rates of change. Days 119 and 120 judge past-environment claims and construct spoken and written feedback arguments; day 121 reconciles them. The order makes organism-level selection and photosynthesis available before planetary feedback and species-scale outcomes.

**No readiness check.** Day 113 retrieves `s8-natural-selection`, `s8-fossil-record` and plate-model. Although its named row is `s6-plate-evidence`, grade 7's `s7-resource-distribution` uses the model, so it was idle only in grade 8. It needs retrieval, not a readiness check. ELA argument writing overlaps the opening, ending day 116; day 113 supplies the earlier claim-evidence-reasoning frame, used again on day 120. Deep time and Earth's age follow the evidence. Extinction is handled without fatalism, ending with evidence of what changes its rate.

**Assessment day 122, response day 123.** A new environmental record requires evaluation of increase/speciation/extinction claims, rates and evidence coverage, and a reciprocal Earth-life argument. It checks feedback rather than a one-way list of impacts, without requiring every Earth-system interaction.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Quantified change rates occupy day 118; consistency of natural laws and the limits of past-environment inference occupy day 119. Two existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 9. Designing for a river

**Days 124 to 140, weeks 25 to 28, 17 days.** Elwha salmon and sediment evidence motivates a global river-fragmentation challenge with a particular river as the design case. Days 124 to 127 define needs, measurable criteria, constraints and candidate solutions. Days 128 to 130 evaluate impact records and feedback, including local reduction/reuse/recycling and one supplied large-scale geoengineering proposal. Days 131 to 135 design, test, compare and refine, including a second challenge to the revision. Days 136 to 138 prepare the report. Four owned performances and the first full high school design cycle receive time without sacrificing refinement.

**No readiness check.** Retrieve `s8-design-evaluate`, `s8-design-test-combine`, `s7-biodiversity-solutions`, `s7-human-impact-design` and design-cycle on day 124. Day 126 retrieves unit conversion. Linear-model precision was taught by Algebra I day 59, before impact-data judgment on day 128. ELA source work ends day 84 and informative writing ends day 130, before report planning on day 136. **Report due day 139, week 28**, apart from ELA's week 26 explanation and World History I's week 31 argument. No stream visit is required or scheduled. The Lower Elwha Klallam Tribe is represented as a present-day nation with its own government and as a leader of the removal effort. The unit plan's sensitivity read studies the Tribe's own account; no role play represents any group.

**Assessment day 139, response day 140.** The design report and observed test/refine portfolio cover the complete cycle; an unseen river constraint tests independent transfer. All four codes are checked: global-to-local problem definition, qualitative and quantitative criteria, reduction of biodiversity harm, technology judged with impact and feedback evidence, and prioritised trade-offs across cost, safety, reliability, appearance and social, cultural and environmental effects.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Analysing a global challenge into criteria occupies day 125; unintended technological effects and costs/benefits occupy day 129 and the revised-design challenge on day 135. Three existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Unit 10. Testing rules before the ocean does

**Days 141 to 155, weeks 29 to 31, 15 days.** Cod-collapse and whale-mortality records require a model that makes assumptions inspectable before a rule is tried. Days 141 to 145 construct, check and revise a resource-management simulation that tracks both human sustainability and biodiversity. Days 146 to 148 revise a species-protection simulation, not merely its settings. Days 149 to 152 predict, test and refine rule packages across interacting systems; day 153 reconciles the claims and limits. The single owned row contains three performances and receives separate time for each.

**No readiness check.** Day 141 retrieves `s7-human-impact-design`, `s7-population-consumption`, `s8-design-test-combine` and units 5, 7 and 9. Days 142 and 144 retrieve expressions, percent and scatter plots. Algebra I exponentials ended day 107; the supplied update rule is still taught explicitly, with cell/formula instruction on day 143. Learners construct a simple resource spreadsheet and change a rule or relationship in the protection model. Printed steps preserve hand checking and reasoning; access to a shared offline device is needed to demonstrate the actual computational construction. Fishing communities and lost livelihoods are treated with respect, without assigning blame or requiring personal views.

**Assessment day 154, response day 155.** Construction and revision portfolios plus an unseen-data, changed-constraint run cover all three codes: human-harm reduction for a threatened species, resource management linked to human sustainability and biodiversity, and prediction of within-system and between-system effects under multiple criteria and constraints. This is evidence about assumptions and model consequences, not proof that a rule will work in the ocean.

**Candidates settled: keep both.** Model boundaries and initial conditions occupy day 142; predicting solution impacts with simulations occupies day 149, applied again on days 150 and 151. Four existing sessions, zero additional days.

## Materials, tools and sources

The tools named consistently in the CSV are the frame's proposed body-feedback tool, DNA-to-protein code tool, sealed-jar gas tool, energy pyramid and carbon reservoir tool, population model tool, cross and meiosis tool, natural selection tool, river and dam trade-off tool, and fishery and species-protection tool. They are planning names for later tool work, not claims of existing assets. Each needs an offline, account-free path and printed tables or model runs. The sealed-jar name describes a simulation; physical yeast containers stay vented.

Hands-on days in units 1, 2, 3 and 6 list student-handled supplies and hazard controls in the CSV. Shared balances can use a household kitchen scale only if its resolution is adequate for the samples; otherwise use the same supplied replicate data. Unit plans must itemise quantities, actual costs, safe set-up, clean-up and the frame's safety reads, rather than assume a laboratory kit. Units 4, 5 and 7 to 10 use provided data and drawn or computational models. The optional stream visit is omitted because the river design can be done from records without changing its performances.

No base text is used, so no `source-map.csv` is required or written. This is original planning against the frame and science progression. Later data packs need cleared public-domain or individually checked open material and original redrawn tables, with source lines. No reference-only wording, problem or image is adapted here. Literacy practice is embedded in the procedures, data-to-graph work, source comparison, explanations, arguments and research named in the day goals and notes.

## Shorter path

For a shorter period, keep the phenomenon, student reasoning and notebook/model evidence. Pre-position materials and supply graphs or source excerpts before class; use the paper model run when device start-up would consume the session. Brief seedling observations already share teaching sessions. Reduce repeated whole-class reporting by using paired comparisons and one class revision. Preserve the evidence-dependent revisions, independent assessment work and response time. If an extraction cannot be safely completed with clean-up, use its recorded observation; it is supporting evidence, not the required investigation performance of unit 1. Unit plans must time the hands-on procedures before use in a shorter period.

## For the lead

1. The frame fits without a unit, owned row, day count or check move. All 20 candidate elements are kept inside existing sessions; none adds days. No scope change is requested.
2. Unit 1's data-only alternative supports planning and analysis but cannot itself demonstrate carrying out an organism investigation. The unit plan must identify how a learner can complete the safe practical performance, or record the missing execution evidence explicitly. Similarly, paper simulation work prepares the computational reasoning but does not alone demonstrate constructing or revising a computer simulation in unit 10. The supplied-data and paper routes must not be described as equivalent evidence of those actions.
3. The proposed tools, cleared data packs, actual materials costs and course safety page remain later work. All units require the frame's sensitivity reads; units 1, 2, 3 and 6 require safety reads, and course checks require the later answer-key read. The Tribe's own account and disability-led, family and fishing-community sources still need the study steps the frame names. This sequence does not claim those reads have occurred.
4. Uncertainty is practical timing, especially seedling drying to stable mass, classroom-scale resolution and multi-parameter simulation work in 50 minutes or less. Unit plans must preserve the supplied-data fallback and explicit formula teaching when timing or equipment fails. The gates check structure, not those classroom conditions.
