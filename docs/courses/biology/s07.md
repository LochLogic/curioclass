- **HS-LS4-1:** Share scientific information showing that several independent kinds of evidence support common ancestry and evolution. Emphasis: what each kind of evidence adds; examples: shared DNA sequences, body structures, and the order structures appear as embryos develop.
- **HS-LS4-2:** Explain with evidence that evolution comes mainly from four factors: growth potential, inherited variation, competition for scarce resources, and the spread of those best able to survive and reproduce. Emphasis: how each factor affects numbers, behavior, body form or body function through competition, survival and adaptation; evidence might include simple graphs of how traits are spread and proportional reasoning. Limit: No other ways evolution happens, such as genetic drift, migration moving genes, or coevolution.
- **RST.9-10.9:** Compare and contrast findings presented in a text to those from other sources (including their own experiments), noting when the findings support or contradict previous explanations or accounts. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **HS-LS4-3:** Use statistics and probability to back explanations that organisms with a helpful inherited trait come to make up a larger share of a population. Emphasis: reading shifts in how traits are distributed as evidence. Limit: Basic statistics and graph analysis only; no allele frequency calculations.
- **RST.9-10.7:** Translate quantitative or technical information expressed in words in a text into visual form (e.g., a table or chart) and translate information expressed visually or mathematically (e.g., in an equation) into words. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **HS-LS4-4:** Explain with evidence how natural selection leads populations to adapt. Emphasis: data showing how living and nonliving differences between ecosystems, such as temperature range, acidity, light or barriers, shift gene frequencies over time.
- **WHST.9-10.2:** Write informative/explanatory texts, including the narration of historical events, scientific procedures/ experiments, or technical processes. Parts, by verb: a (WHST.9-10.2a) Introduce a topic and organize ideas, concepts, and information to make important connections and distinctions; include formatting (e.g., headings), graphics (e.g., figures, tables), and multimedia when useful to aiding comprehension.; b (WHST.9-10.2b) Develop the topic with well-chosen, relevant, and sufficient facts, extended definitions, concrete details, quotations, or other information and examples appropriate to the audience’s knowledge of the topic.; c (WHST.9-10.2c) Use varied transitions and sentence structures to link the major sections of the text, create cohesion, and clarify the relationships among ideas and concepts.; d (WHST.9-10.2d) Use precise language and domain-specific vocabulary to manage the complexity of the topic and convey a style appropriate to the discipline and context as well as to the expertise of likely readers.; e (WHST.9-10.2e) Establish and maintain a formal style and objective tone while attending to the norms and conventions of the discipline in which they are writing.; f (WHST.9-10.2f) Provide a concluding statement or section that follows from and supports the information or explanation presented (e.g., articulating implications or the significance of the topic).
- **MS-ESS2-3:** Use data on rocks, fossils, continent shapes and sea floor features as evidence that plates moved in the past. Limit: Magnetic striping (paleomagnetic patterns) in the crust is left out.
