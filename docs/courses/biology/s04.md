## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where do the energy and the carbon that a river's plants take in end up?"
**Hook, day 47:** Show an original river sketch beside historical Silver Springs production and standing-biomass records, with units visible. Students notice abundant producers and little top-consumer biomass, then ask the compelling question. Field 10 names the study.
**Closing explanation, day 54:** Use the river budget and atmospheric record to explain limited feeding transfers, detrital routes and carbon exchanges. Distinguish a local budget from a hemispheric seasonal signal.
**Teaching:** Demonstrate a bounded budget on day 48 and distinguish stock from production. Then ask students to justify destinations, challenge arrows and revise from evidence before offering the class account.

**Transfer packet, first seen on day 56:** All wetland records are Curio Class original, **invented for practice**. Each energy value is kilojoules per square metre over one year. Producer net production stores 2,400; of this, 360 becomes new herbivore biomass, 840 becomes heat through herbivore respiration and 1,200 is in uneaten material and waste. Of the herbivores' 360, 54 becomes new predator biomass, 126 becomes heat through predator respiration and 180 enters detritus. These are separate downstream budgets, not additional losses to subtract twice. Producer respiration occurred before the stated net production. Decomposer growth is observed on uneaten material and waste. Standing dry biomass is 400, 60 and 9 grams per square metre for producers, herbivores and predators respectively.

A marked matter parcel enters the consumer system in food, water, oxygen and mineral nutrients. Its atom inventory is carbon 24, oxygen 60, hydrogen 80 and nitrogen 8. Remaining biomass holds 6, 12, 20 and 2 respectively. An exit card claims all other atoms vanished. Students must supply the combined outgoing inventory, not infer individual compounds from these totals.

A second record shows falling dissolved carbon dioxide around illuminated wetland plants and rising dissolved carbon dioxide in darkness. Supply observation cards: dissolved carbon dioxide rises in water exposed to carbon dioxide-rich air and falls in water exposed to carbon dioxide-poor air; old sedimentary rock contains plant remains; volcanic gases contain carbon dioxide. Infer and justify exchange, storage and return pathways rather than copying labelled arrows. The model boundary includes living organisms, air, water and rock, with different timescales explicitly marked.

**Item 1 key and scoring, 12 points:** Award two points for each justified transfer percentage: 360 divided by 2,400 and 54 divided by 360 are each 15 percent. Award two for both complete labelled budgets, each summing to its input, two for a decomposer pathway retaining chemical energy before respiration, two for the combined outgoing atom inventory (18 carbon, 48 oxygen, 60 hydrogen, 6 nitrogen), and two for connecting lower consumer energy supply with the observed biomass pattern while distinguishing annual production from standing stock. Each pair earns one point for a sound representation and one for reasoning from it. The case supports an energy limitation, not an exact prediction of standing biomass or animal numbers; turnover and individual size also matter.

Meets: "Both transfers are 15 percent. The first budget is 360 + 840 + 1,200 = 2,400. The second is 54 + 126 + 180 = 360. Detritus still holds chemical energy for decomposers. The outgoing inventory is 18 carbon, 48 oxygen, 60 hydrogen and 6 nitrogen atoms. Less annual energy reaches predators, consistent with lower standing biomass, but production is not a stock measurement." Does not: "Each level must pass ten percent (I); all remaining energy becomes heat immediately (B); 2,400 is the standing biomass (H)."

**Item 2 key and scoring, eight points:** Award two points for a connected four-reservoir model, two for correctly directed photosynthesis and respiration arrows including plant and decomposer respiration, one for physical air-water exchange, one for geologic storage and a slower return route, one for external sunlight stored as chemical energy and dissipated heat leaving, with no energy-return loop, and one for using light and dark evidence to explain the plant-water link while acknowledging that it does not establish rock transfers. Every point requires using a model on this wetland, not naming terms alone.

Meets: "Plant photosynthesis moves dissolved carbon into living material and stores solar energy chemically; heat leaves without cycling back. Plant and decomposer respiration return carbon to water or air, matching the dark record. Air exchanges carbon with water; burial stores some carbon in rock over longer times, with geological return shown separately. The light and dark observations are consistent with biological carbon transfers; they do not establish the physical or geological routes." Does not: "Only animals respire (E), photosynthesis sends carbon into air (F), and heat cycles back as sunlight (G)."

All 20 points require mathematics or modelling with an ecosystem idea in a new case. No earlier notebook work is scored. Day 56 creates the independent responses; teacher scoring occurs before class on day 57, on the following day. No teaching or revision occurs during the assessment. Accommodations: read prompts aloud, enlarge diagrams, offer a scribe or accept an oral model explanation with the same evidence requirements.
