## What the arts carry (unit field 2)

- **The standards and their parts.** The unit lists each performance standard by id with its parts, one part per
  action, in one line of our own words (STANDARD.md section 2), and the limit (for example, "with a given pattern,
  not an invented one"). Every part has an end-of-unit item. The three things below sit under this.

An arts unit also carries three things, named in the unit plan:

- **The technique**: one skill of the discipline taught in steps that get harder (a brush stroke, a rhythm
  pattern, a vocal warm-up, a stage cross, a dance phrase, a camera shot).
- **The works studied**: two to four works by other artists (see "Works and their settings" and "Rights").
- **The showing**: what the class makes and shows or performs at the end, and to whom (the class, another class,
  families).

**Works and their settings.** Each year's works include living makers and more than one region and period; a unit
plan lists each work with its maker, place, date, purpose and rights line. A setting is when, where, by whom and
for what. No single tradition is the default. Where the open copy of a work is thin, the unit uses a description
and a link to the maker's or museum's own page, never a copy, and does not drop the work. Where a work has a sacred
or ceremonial use, the unit says what it is, studies it by looking and listening only, and does not have students
perform or imitate it. Students learn a tradition's form from the community's own published teaching or a named
practitioner's words, never by costume or invented imitation, and no student plays a character by an accent or a
stereotype. The unit plan records the works' makers and places in one line each. Every unit gets the fresh-agent
sensitivity read, which covers the works with the rest of the unit (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md).
Museum open-access images and public domain recordings lean to older, European and American works, so the year's
list is checked for range, not taken as the open sources give it (docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md).

**Rights.** Every work a unit uses has a rights line in the unit plan, checked on the item's own page. A work that
is not public domain or openly licensed is described in our own words and linked to the maker's or museum's page,
never copied or embedded. Scripts, songs and dances students perform are public domain, openly licensed, original
to Curio Class or written by the students. A showing for families uses only those. Media arts remix uses only
public domain or openly licensed sources and names them. A student's work is the student's: it is shown outside the
class only with the family's yes, and carries the student's choice of name.

## Kinds of day (principle 2)

The five kinds of day are the same as in every subject. In the arts:

- **Understanding.** A technique is shown and named in steps, or a work is studied with the discipline's words
  (look, listen, describe, interpret). The artistic process "responding to a work" lives here: it is a teaching
  day, not a respond day. Connecting (Cn10, Cn11) is taught as each id's words line asks: an id that asks for a tie
  or a study (the student's own life, or the work's time, place or purpose) lives in the understanding day that
  studies a work, in the day's evidence sentence; an id that asks the student to make, research or perform (for
  example `VA:Cn10.1.Ka`, `DA:Cn10.1.6b`) lives in an application day and is scored as making. The unit plan's
  parts list says which.
- **Fluency.** Short, repeated practice of a technique with feedback: rhythms, scales, sketchbook drills, warm-ups,
  a phrase run several times, a scene beat repeated.
- **Application.** Students make, rehearse or produce their own work using the technique, with some choice. The
  second application day holds the unit's one critique (see "Critique"); there is no extra day.
- **Check.** A showing or performance scored with a guide (see "Scoring creative work"), or a response to a work
  not studied in the unit. The end-of-unit assessment is always a check day.
- **Respond.** Acts on a check: a second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second
  showing.

A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application (with the critique), check,
respond. Every unit has at least one showing (bar criterion 4) and one response to another artist's work
(criterion 5). The 32-session path, which keeps four things of each unit, is in STANDARD.md 3.4.

## Readiness and the baseline look (unit field 4)

A student new to the band, or to the year, takes the year's first unit as written; each unit's opening retrieval or
readiness task names the earlier-year technique it assumes and the ten-minute reteach (UNIT-PLAN field 4, whose
timing rules hold). The baseline look under "Growth" is an observation during the unit's first practice days, not a
readiness check, so day 1 stays clean.

## Scoring creative work (unit field 5)

- **The guide** has three to five look-fors, drawn from the unit's technique and words, each scored met, partly or
  not yet. It never marks talent, speed, neatness, effort, the maker's identity, or how the work compares with
  another student's. A student's choice of subject is never marked down. Students score their own work with the
  same guide first.
- **Samples.** A sample is a written description of a work or performance at each level, or a work the team made as
  an adult example. Samples at two levels sit in the unit plan beside the look-fors.
- **The check day.** Performances are shown in small groups on the check day, and any group the teacher cannot
  watch live is recorded that day (under "Kept work stays on the device or on paper"). The teacher scores from the
  live showing and the recordings before the respond day, which starts from the marks.
- **Unseen work.** In visual arts, music and media arts, the unseen work is a public domain or openly licensed work
  or a Curio Class original (see "Rights"). In dance and theater, it is a short written description, a live
  demonstration by the teacher or a Curio Class original, never an embed.
- **Growth.** Each unit opens with a baseline look at the unit's main look-for (rotating groups, recorded met,
  partly or not yet) and ends with the same look at the check, so each student has two marks and the growth is the
  change. Kept work becomes a portfolio: one piece a unit, with its one-line reflection, kept in a folder (paper, a
  file or a photo).

## Errors (unit field 6 and section field 4)

As UNIT-PLAN.md. An arts letter is one technique or reading error (for example, a steady beat taken for the
rhythm), marked "from memory" unless a source is named.

## Marking and response (unit field 7)

Items are teacher-judged; groups for the respond day come from the record sheet, at most four, and the others work
in pairs from the written move (the social studies form of unit field 7).

## Tools, supplies and safe use (unit field 8; instruments and a room with nothing in section field 5)

**What a tool may and may not do.** "Tool" means Curio Class software only; paint, instruments and the like are
materials. A tool may show a technique step by step, give practice with feedback on things that can be checked
(rhythm reading, note names, color mixing, shot types, stage directions), and keep work on the student's own
device. It may not judge a finished work or a performance, stand in for the performing in the room, or generate the
student's work. A tool for color names every color in words and in numbers and works with no color judgment alone;
a tool for sound shows the beat on screen and works with the sound off; every tool plays by keyboard.

**Supplies.** Materials and instruments that cost money are never required; a free or found-material option is
always given. The room, class size and cost answer is in STANDARD.md 3.4.

**Safe use.** The unit lists the clear space, the surface, the equipment and the hazard (criterion 9), and every
unit with hands-on work gets the fresh-agent safety read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). Rules for every
course, all from memory until that read checks them. Dance: a floor with room to move arms out, soft-soled or bare
feet on a clean floor, no lifts, no jumping from heights, partner contact only by agreement and never required.
Theater: no real weapons, no contact stage fights, props checked, falls not rehearsed. Visual arts: blades only by
an adult, no heat sources for children below grade 6, powdered clay and dyes with ventilation, wash hands,
materials labelled non-toxic, no sharing of face-contact items. Music: volume limits for headphones and drums, no
loud sound in a student's ear, hearing and voice rest, no forced singing alone. Media arts: no filming a student
without the family's yes, no flashing lights, screen breaks. A safety statement without a source is marked "from
memory".

## Family note (unit field 9)

Each finished unit carries a family note, in English and Spanish. It is where the family's yes is asked for a
showing outside class (see "Rights").

## Sources (unit field 10)

Field 10 names where each work's rights line was checked (see "Rights"). Nothing here has been tried with children,
and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result. Each course names a one-page logic model in
its frame.

## Practices (section field 1)

Each section names the artistic process or processes it works on (create, perform/present/produce, respond,
connect) and the anchor standards by code. Over a year every course touches all four processes (criterion 1). The
teacher page says what to look and listen for, in plain words (STANDARD.md 3.4).

## The daily evidence (principle 8; section field 3)

The form of evidence depends on the day:

- **A making day** ends with the work in its current state, kept (a photo of the page, a short recording, a saved
  file), plus one line by the student: what technique I used and what I will change. A K to 2 student says it to
  the teacher or a partner instead of writing it.
- **A study day** ends with one written or spoken sentence about the work that must use one of the day's words
  and point to something in the work.
- **A performing day** ends with a short self-check against one look-for, and the teacher's mark of met, partly or
  not yet for a few named students (a rotating list, so every student is seen within the unit).

The teacher sorts the evidence into met, partly and not yet, with no mark, and the plan says what the next
session's first minutes do for "not yet". Over the year the kept work becomes a portfolio (criterion 8).

**Kept work stays on the device or on paper.** A recording, photo or saved file stays on the student's, family's or
school's device and goes nowhere else. A recording may be of hands, the work or the sound alone; a face or voice is
never required. A student who does not want to be recorded is observed live and the teacher's mark stands as the
evidence.

## What is carried (section field 5)

The technique is shown by the teacher in named steps (the teacher holds it), tried together with a step card (the
class holds it with the card), then made alone with the card in hand, then made with the card withdrawn. Each
section plan names the day the step card goes and the day a student uses the steps from memory. A model work or
demonstration is copied as a step, never as the end, and the card goes before the check. A technique is never first
met in independent work. In the section plan the verb is: shown, carried with a step card, withdrawn. A room with
nothing uses the printed step card (STANDARD.md 3.4).

## How a day opens (section field 6)

Every day opens in the first five minutes with one of three things, named in the day row: a warm-up from earlier
technique (a rhythm, a warm-up, a sketch drill), a look or listen at a work with no prior knowledge asked (taught
on the same day), or the set-up routine. A making day opens with the demonstration of the step, never with a free
choice. Teaching first is the default for anything new in every grade (UNIT-PLAN rule 13); no opening task asks for
a technique not yet taught. The day row's minutes include set-up and clean-up (STANDARD.md 3.4). A timer may be used
for a set-up and clean-up cue (classroom management, rule 14).

## Critique (section field 7)

A critique follows one routine, always in this order: describe what you see or hear, ask a question about a choice,
suggest one change tied to a look-for of the unit. Feedback names a look-for; praise alone is not feedback. Students
revise and say in one line what they changed. Each unit has one critique before the check, inside the second
application day, and the check scores work revised after it. The respond day acts on the check as before.

## Practice that returns (section field 8)

Techniques return in the next unit's fluency openers and in later years at a harder step. The discipline's words
come back in every response. Each year revisits the year before's grade ids as practice, never as a claim.

## Supports (section field 9)

- Every making task has more than one way in: a choice of material, size, instrument part or role.
- Every performing task has a way to take part without performing alone (ensemble, small group, recorded take).
- **English learners.** Each unit teaches three to five discipline words in depth, with a picture, a movement or a
  sound and a frame ("I see ___ because ___." "The rhythm goes ___." "My character wants ___."). A student may show
  or perform the word instead of saying it. The unit's check marks what the student made, showed or performed
  separately from what they said, so a language gap is not marked as a skill gap.
- **Access.** Each section names what changes for a student who cannot see (describe the work aloud, touch models,
  large print step cards), cannot hear (a visual beat, a floor vibration, a written cue), cannot move freely (seated
  or one-limb versions, a role such as director or sound lead), or is sensitive to noise, touch, light or crowds
  (hearing protection, gloves, brush grips, a quiet corner, no forced contact, no strobe).
- **Reading load.** Step cards are picture-led with the words read aloud on the card for grades K to 2. A student
  reads at most one short card alone in grades 3 to 5.
- **Extension, same standard.** A harder pattern, a second voice or layer, a tighter constraint, or teaching the
  step to a partner. Never the next grade's standard.

## Two rooms (section field 10)

One child at home is in STANDARD.md 3.4 (the parent is the audience and the critique partner). One projector or none:
every day runs from printed step cards, and a screen only shows a work, a step or a playback.

## High school levels and music

The levels, entry, placement and the music strand's rules are in STANDARD.md 3.2 and 3.3. What the plans fill:
Choice grows by level (Level 1 sets the problems, Level 2 lets the student pick among them and research, Level 3
has the student set the problem). Level 4 is one sustained project with checkpoints as check days and critique as
respond days. High school music is composing and theory, practised by voice, body percussion and a free keyboard or
Curio Class tool; a day may offer "use your own instrument" as one way in, and listening and score study carry the
responding standards.
