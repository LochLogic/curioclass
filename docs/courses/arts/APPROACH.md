# Arts: approach (DRAFT, for Cory's yes)

**DRAFT, 2026-11-16. Not in force until Cory says yes.** This is the layer 3 approach page for the five arts
(visual arts, music, theater, dance, media arts). It fills the section plan's subject slots (docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md)
under the shared page docs/APPROACH.md. The standard and the band-year rule are in docs/courses/arts/STANDARD.md;
the bar is the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md; the lens is docs/agents/lenses/arts.md.

## What the arts carry (unit field 2)

An arts unit carries three things, named in the unit plan:

- **The technique**: one skill of the discipline taught in steps that get harder (a brush stroke, a rhythm
  pattern, a vocal warm-up, a stage cross, a dance phrase, a camera shot).
- **The works studied**: two to four works by other artists, from more than one time and culture across the
  year, each with its setting taught. Images and recordings come from open or public domain sources where they
  exist (docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md).
- **The showing**: what the class makes and shows or performs at the end, and to whom (the class, another class,
  families).

## Kinds of day (principle 2)

The five kinds of day are the same as in every subject. In the arts:

- **Understanding.** A technique is shown and named in steps, or a work is studied with the discipline's words
  (look, listen, describe, interpret). The artistic process "responding" lives here: responding to a work is a
  teaching day, not a respond day.
- **Fluency.** Short, repeated practice of a technique with feedback: rhythms, scales, sketchbook drills, warm-ups,
  a phrase run several times, a scene beat repeated.
- **Application.** Students make, rehearse or produce their own work using the technique, with some choice.
- **Check.** A showing or performance scored with a guide that names the technique and the words, or a written or
  spoken response to a work not studied in the unit. The end-of-unit assessment is always a check day.
- **Respond.** Acts on a check: a second try at the part marked "not yet", revision after critique, or a
  re-rehearsal before a second showing.

A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application, check, respond. Every unit has at
least one showing (bar criterion 4) and one response to another artist's work (criterion 5).

## Practices (section field 1)

Each section names the artistic process or processes it works on (create, perform/present/produce, respond,
connect) and the anchor standards by code. Over a year every course touches all four processes (criterion 1).

## The daily evidence (principle 8)

The form of evidence depends on the day:

- **A making day** ends with the work in its current state, kept (a photo of the page, a short recording, a saved
  file), plus one line by the student: what technique I used and what I will change. A K to 2 student says it to
  the teacher or a partner instead of writing it.
- **A study day** ends with one written or spoken sentence about the work that must use one of the day's words
  and point to something in the work.
- **A performing day** ends with a short self-check against one look-for, and the teacher's mark of met, partly or
  not yet for a few named students (a rotating list, so every student is seen within the unit).

The teacher sorts the evidence into met, partly and not yet, with no mark, and the plan says what the next
session's first minutes do for "not yet". Over the year the kept work becomes a portfolio (criterion 8).

## Practice that returns (field 8)

Techniques return in the next unit's fluency openers and in later years at a harder step. The discipline's words
come back in every response. Each year revisits the year before's grade ids as practice, never as a claim.

## Supports (field 9)

- Every making task has more than one way in: a choice of material, size, instrument part or role.
- Every performing task has a way to take part without performing alone (ensemble, small group, recorded take).
- Equipment and safe use of space are listed in each section (criterion 9). Tools that cost money are never
  required; a free or found-material option is always given.
- Students who join mid-band start from the year's first unit openers; nothing assumes last year was taken.

## High school levels (Cory 2026-11-16, Desk card q-later-arts-health-pe)

The 9 to 12 course in each discipline runs as Level 1 (Proficient), Level 2 (Accomplished), Level 3 (Advanced)
and an optional Level 4 (STANDARD.md 3.2). In the plans:

- Each level is written for students placed by what they have done, so classes mix grades. Level 1's first unit
  teaches from the start; later levels open with a short review unit that doubles as a check of placement.
- Choice grows by level: Level 1 sets the problems, Level 2 lets the student pick among them and research, Level
  3 has the student set the problem. This follows the national level descriptors.
- Level 4 is one sustained project the student leads (a portfolio of related works, a production, a composition
  set), with checkpoints as check days and critique as respond days.
- Each level's end-of-course assessment also serves as the placement showing for the level above.

## High school music (Cory 2026-11-16, Desk card q-later-arts-health-pe)

Music 9 to 12 is composition and theory (STANDARD.md 3.3). In the plans:

- The technique is composing and the theory it needs (notation, rhythm, scales, chords, form), practised with the
  voice, body percussion and a free keyboard or Curio Class tool. No day requires an instrument; a day may offer
  "use your own instrument" as one way in.
- Performing days perform the students' own and others' works by voice, body percussion or keyboard, live or
  recorded, or present a composition through the tool's playback when the standard is about presenting it.
- Listening and score study carry the responding standards, so the course also covers what a music appreciation
  course would.
- An ensemble companion page (no days, no ids) serves students who also play or sing in an ensemble.

## What a tool may and may not do

A tool may show a technique step by step, give practice with feedback on things that can be checked (rhythm
reading, note names, color mixing, shot types, stage directions), and keep work on the student's own device. It may not judge a finished
work or a performance, stand in for the performing in the room, or generate the student's work.
