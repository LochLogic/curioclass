# Course frame: Physical Education, grades K to 2

Course `physical-education-grades-k-to-2` (catalogue id `pe-k-2`), grades K, 1 and 2, one band course in three
school years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time. Each
year's part of this file (its units and its two course-level checks) is that year's frame, as the subject page's
"one frame per grade year" asks.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 22: 7 in kindergarten (units 1 to 7), 7 in grade 1 (units 8 to 14), 8 in grade 2 (units 15 to 22).
  Each year is whole on its own: its own units, end-of-unit assessments and mid-year and year-end checks.
- **Days.** 240: 80 a year for three years. Each year: 74 days in units and 6 for course-level checks and respond
  days (two check days and one respond day at mid-year, the same at year-end); kindergarten 74 + 6, grade 1 74 + 6,
  grade 2 74 + 6. One session of about 30 minutes, two or three sessions a week (2.5 on average), over the 32 weeks of
  the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md): the PE dose APPROACH.md "Pacing" sets for K to 2 (80 sessions of
  30 minutes, Cory 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-pe-1; elementary PE 2 to 3 sessions a week, Cory 2026-11-16). This frame
  keeps that default. The 30 minutes hold the instant activity, warm-up, focus, cool-down and closure, and the
  set-up, clean-up and collecting of equipment (about 3 minutes, done by students in their station groups). Each
  unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment. No unit is over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. The
  course says openly that 2 to 3 sessions a week is below the 150 minutes a week SHAPE America's guidelines set for
  elementary school (shapeamerica.org/standards/guidelines); the course page, teacher pages and family notes offer
  short active breaks and home activity. On the even spread the calendar uses, course day d of a year falls on about
  school day 2d of that year.
- **Standard.** SHAPE America National Physical Education Standards, 2024 (data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt): all
  49 span-2 learning indicators (SHAPE.S1.2.1 to S1.2.17, S2.2.1 to S2.2.12, S3.2.1 to S3.2.10, S4.2.1 to S4.2.10)
  are owned in this band, each by the one progression row that owns it (data/progressions/physical-education.csv,
  grades K, 1 and 2, 48 rows). A row's `~` codes are begun in that unit and named in What students learn with
  their owner. Ids and our own words only; no indicator sentence is quoted (STANDARD.md 2). Check set: CDC's PECAT
  2019 K to 2 grade tables, keyed to the 2013 five standards and mapped through docs/standards-notes/pe-2014-to-2024.md,
  read for placement only (outcome stated, content teaches it, assessment checks it). Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS
  2026-11-12) and the appropriate practice points (STANDARD.md 4) hold in every unit.
- **Grade outcomes.** Bar criterion 1 asks for an outcome for every standard in every grade (STANDARD.md 3 rule
  2). In our words; the scope and sequence copies them and adds the indicators each year works on.
  - Kindergarten. S1: travels, jumps and lands, balances, moves in place, and rolls, catches, kicks, taps and strikes
    from a fixed spot, with two or three cues each (units 1 to 5). S2: knows a personal space, names activities that
    build fitness and notices body changes when moving (units 1, 6). S3: shares space and equipment with words, stops
    on the signal and handles equipment safely (units 1, 3). S4: names activities to express feelings, and picks and
    keeps at a choice in class (unit 7). Checked by skill tasks, picture-card knowledge tasks and conduct marks.
  - Grade 1. S1: travels in changing pathways, directions and speeds, shifts weight, bounces, hits with the hand and
    dances to a beat (units 8, 9, 11, 13). S2: names movement ideas (level, speed, pathway, direction), shows dance
    ideas and says the heart is a muscle (units 8, 12, 13). S3: cheers and acts kindly, names and plays games from
    other places (units 10, 14). S4: uses calm steps, names a star and a wish, picks favorites alone or with others
    (units 11, 14).
  - Grade 2. S1: throws overhand, strikes with a long tool, jumps a self-turned rope, shows dry-land water safety
    skills, and links skills in sequences and stations (units 15 to 19, 21, 22). S2: names chase and flee tactics,
    shows movement ideas while moving, says why we stretch, picks energy foods and drinks, and shows water safety
    knowledge (units 15, 18, 20, 21, 22). S3: explains rules and fair choices and talks over problems (units 17,
    19). S4: sets and checks a short goal, names a hard moment and what helped, says why PE mattered (units 16, 22).
- **Base text.** None. Physical education is Curio Class's own. Cue words, task cards and knowledge cards are ours;
  the appropriate practice guidelines (2009) and PECAT are read into our words, never quoted. Each cultural game or
  dance is named only after its own source (the community's own organization or a public folklife archive) says it
  is open to be taught (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day": Understanding (a skill
  shown and named in two or three cues, then tried at once, first try watched by the teacher); Fluency (many tries
  with feedback on one cue: stations, partner tasks, task cards, no lines); Application (the skill in a changing
  setting: a sequence, a small-sided game, a dance, a circuit); Check; Respond. Fluency is the largest share in K to
  2 (about half of each unit's teaching days): a skill is learned by many tries. Application in K stays in fixed or
  barely changing settings, as the span-2 indicators ask; small-sided play and tactics in play start in grade 3.
- **The C3 column** holds, for PE, the unit's cues and the settings it moves through (alone, partner, station,
  sequence or game), as science's holds its practices.
- **Assessment.** Each unit's first skill day has a baseline watch of the unit's main cue in rotating groups (met,
  partly, not yet); its end-of-unit assessment repeats that cue as the skill task, adds a knowledge task (shown by
  moving, a picture card or a one-sentence answer said to the teacher; no writing in K to 2) and marks conduct once,
  against the S3 behaviors the unit names. Growth is the change against the child's own baseline, never a rank.
  Units of 10 days or fewer end with one check day; longer units with two, so the teacher sees every child (six
  groups of five, two to three minutes a group while the others work at stations). No score comes from a day a skill
  is first taught. A respond day follows each unit check only where the unit's days list it (see each unit);
  otherwise the next unit's instant activity takes the "not yet" cue.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every row this course assumes was last taught the year before inside this course
  (grade 1 rows assume kindergarten rows, grade 2 rows assume grade 1 rows; Seams gives the unit), so each assumed
  row opens as a retrieval task inside a day, in the instant activity of the first skill day that needs it. No
  row assumes one two or more grades back, so this course has no readiness checks. Day 1 of every unit stays clean
  for the hook and the question. The count starts from kindergarten; a student who joins in grade 1 or 2 meets the
  same retrieval tasks, and the teacher re-teaches the cue on the spot where a child has never met it.
- **Space, weather and equipment.** Every unit runs in a gym, on a field or in a cleared room, with a low-equipment
  version (rolled socks, paper-plate paddles, tape lines, string ropes) and a wet-weather version named in the unit
  plan (bar criterion 5). Class of thirty: stations in groups of four or five, one item a pair or better.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30, counting reusable items once, if the school
  has no PE equipment at all: shown in each unit's last column. Across the band about $370, plus mats (unit 9) from
  the school's stock. A school with an ordinary PE closet spends nothing; the low-equipment path costs nothing.
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared teacher device is optional: it shows cue pictures and plays the music
  track for dance and rhythm days (units 2, 7, 13, 21). No owned code needs technology: a teacher who has no device
  claps or sings the beat and holds up the printed cue card, and every child meets every part in full.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs from printed cue cards, picture task cards and the teacher's record sheet; no tool
  is needed, no student account exists, and no tool judges movement, ranks students or collects fitness data
  (APPROACH.md, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Reading load.** Task and cue cards are picture-led and read aloud by the teacher; no child reads alone in PE in
  K to 2. Each unit names its movement words (the rows' `words`), taught with a picture or demonstration and a frame
  ("I land on my ___."). A child slow to say a cue may show it; the check marks movement and understanding apart.
- **Supports.** Every lesson, closure and check task has an adaptation for disabilities and a range of skill
  (equipment size, distance, speed, rules, roles). A child with a short-term injury or a medical condition follows
  the nurse's and family's guidance, with a moving role for the parts that are fine or a seated helping role (cue
  watcher, keeper of their own count). Groups are made by the teacher, quickly and privately. No exercise as
  punishment, no elimination, no human targets, no public team picks, no activity split by gender.
- **Water safety.** Dry land only, for every school: unit 22 teaches the rules, fastening a life jacket and a reach
  and throw rescue on the floor. In-water tasks (float and kick) belong to a school's own aquatics program with a
  qualified lifeguard on site and the school's swim policy; no child is made to enter water (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Swaps and leave-outs.** A school or family may swap a named cultural game or dance (units 13, 14, 21) for one
  whose source says it is open to be taught, or an outdoor activity that does not fit its place (STANDARD.md 5). A
  unit left out takes its owned codes off the course page's claim; no other unit claims them.
- **Shorter path.** The 64-session path each year (APPROACH.md, "Pacing") drops 10 Fluency days spread over the
  year's units (one or two a unit, a Fluency day only, never a check, respond, baseline or first-teaching day) and
  keeps both course-level checks; the scope and sequence names the days.
- **Sensitive ground and teacher notes.** Every PE teacher page carries health's disclosure steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"), and changing clothes follows the school's
  policy with no one changing in front of others (Cory, 2026-11-21). No weighing, no body talk, no calorie or diet
  talk; fitness words are habits, never scores; heart counts are never compared.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside human reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14). Units with cultural content (13, 14, 21) name the
  communities whose games and dances they use and, before the read, the agent studies that community's own
  published sources on the activity and on whether it is open to be taught. Units with emotion, body or food
  content (6, 7, 11, 12, 18, 22) and disability adaptations in every unit name their sources in the last column.
- **Safety read.** The safety agent reads, before they go live, every unit with striking tools or mats (units 5, 9,
  12, 15, 17, 19) and the water unit (22), against these free guides: the Nova Scotia Department of Education's
  "Physical Education Safety Guidelines" (2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf)
  and Alberta's "Safety Guidelines for Physical Activity in Alberta Schools" (2014,
  https://education.alberta.ca/media/160206/sg_pa_final_2014.pdf), and for water CDC's drowning prevention page
  (https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html, from memory: the agent opens it first). Both guides found
  by search on 2026-11-25 and not yet opened by this writer. No student sign-off form. Answer keys get the
  answer-key agent's read.
