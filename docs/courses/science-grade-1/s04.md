## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why does the rubber band go blurry when our box guitar plays, and why does the sound stop when we touch it?"
- **Hook, day 28.** Pluck the low-held box guitar, then gently still its band. Children notice the blur and sound changing before hearing the compelling question.
- **Closing, day 32.** Dictate the anchor explanation using the band's stopping test and the drum comparison: the band vibrates when sounding; stilling it quiets the sound. Add the reverse direction from paper and rice observations.
- **Teaching moves.** Model recording an action beside an observation on day 28. Thereafter ask partners what comparison would test their idea, then step back while they choose, observe and explain.

- **Storyline and question board.** Group notices under making sound, stopping sound and moving something else. Day 28 asks what the blur means. Day 29 compares band and throat, raising whether another material agrees. Day 30 tests the drum, raising whether motion is necessary. Day 31 revises the stopping arrow; day 29's reverse question waits for day 32. Day 32 tests sound moving paper and rice. Day 33 investigates an unfamiliar source beside a drum and competing causes of strip motion; day 34 closes remaining questions.
- **Sections and dimensions.** Both sections ask children to "Plan and conduct investigations". Section 4.1 tests vibrating materials producing sound; section 4.2 tests sound producing vibration. Peer plans, observations and causal comparisons join the three elements above. No engineering expectation is claimed.
- **Limits.** No wave drawings, wavelength, speed, pitch rules, ear anatomy or hearing tests. Back-and-forth motion need not carry an object away. A motionless-looking source may move too little or too quickly to see.
- **Carried.** Retain unit 3's patterns and evidence-account charts and unit 2's design and shape models for later use. Day 28 starts an action-motion-sound drawing. Day 29 adds felt throat vibration; day 30 adds drum evidence; day 31 revises the stopping arrow. Day 32 adds sound-to-material-motion evidence to the consensus model. Children use this two-direction model on day 33 and retain it.

| Day | Code | Part (own words) | The item, answer and wrong answers (error letter) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 33 | 1-PS4-1 | Plan and carry out tests showing that vibrating things produce sound and that sound sets things vibrating. | **Item 1, 12 minutes:** "A plastic ruler is flicked beside this paper drum and we hear a sound. What will you compare to find which part is sounding? Plan together, test and record." Key: select actual trials separating ruler and drum actions, then compare equal ruler flicks with its free end free and gently stilled. Observe both sources; a separate gentle drum tap supplies a comparison. Attribute sound only from the observations. Saying the stilled source keeps producing sound is A. **Item 2, 12 minutes:** "This thin paper ribbon hangs from a stand beside a playing speaker on the same table. What could explain that? Choose comparisons, test and use both investigations to revise your account." Key: separate supports to remove table contact (the day 32 question "could the table be moving it?"), then compare sound off and on with ribbon position fixed; record actual motion without touching. B denies possible sound-driven motion; C accepts touching as sound-only evidence. Failure to control table contact stays unclassified. | sections 4.1 (item 1) and 4.2 (item 2) |

**Collection, day 33.** Use 30 minutes: launch 3, six group turns of 3, independent records 6, cleanup 3. Each item's 12 minutes is half of the shared 18-minute observation block plus half of the 6-minute record block. The 6-minute record block has two minutes before turns for individual picture plans and four afterward for accounts. Each group of five agrees a plan; every child contributes a marked choice, taps the drum once, follows the agreed observation procedure for both directions and records their own observations. The teacher operates the speaker on children's requests. During each turn, use about 30 seconds for planning, 45 for shared comparisons and five 15-second individual reasons. Each group runs one item's comparisons live in its turn (items 1 and 2 alternate by group) and takes the other item's comparisons from held-back records at the child's request, marked indirect, so a live turn holds two comparison sets; the second item is evidenced on the record sheet. Waiting children match familiar evidence cards independently. No reteaching or score that day.

**Transfer materials.** Reserve actual unlabelled ruler-and-drum records, including isolated actions and free versus stilled ruler trials, for this assessment only. The ruler is an unfamiliar source, not another shaped drum. Children request comparisons under their plan, observe recordings and tap the unfamiliar drum gently for a direct comparison. Mark recorded evidence as indirect. For the ribbon, a light material not used or shown in any exit on days 28 to 34 (not tissue strip, foil, card or fringe), offer shared-table and separate-support records plus fixed-position sound-on and sound-off trials. Children must distinguish sound from table contact, not follow a named cause. Teacher checks every trial before use; no hands, breath or fan moves the strip during sound-only trials. If classroom-volume motion is not visible, use actual recordings with equivalent controls. Never invent success; without valid records, leave that evidence incomplete.

**Record and points.** Give each child a two-panel sheet showing apparatus, blank comparison boxes and their earlier model. Award one point for each of six components in each case: identify the causal question; identify materials and intended action; choose a comparison with a stated observation; contribute to the peer plan and follow it; record their own paired observations; connect those observations to a supported causal arrow. Item 2's last component must preserve or revise both arrows using both results. All 12 points need investigation practice with the sound idea and causal evidence on new cases, above the 80 percent needed. A child meets the code with at least 10 of 12 points and at least 4 in each case; below that, response groups follow the missing component. Do not award points for naming vibration alone. The teacher hears one linked reason per child and checks paper for the remaining detail. Missing evidence stays incomplete unless the answer expresses a registered error.

**Samples, invented for practice.** Item 1 meets: "We asked for ruler-only and drum-only trials. Then we compared equal ruler flicks, free and gently held. It shook and sounded when free; holding the end quieted it. Our vibration-to-sound arrow also fits the ruler." Does not meet: "The ruler keeps making sound after it is held still" (A). Item 2 meets: "The table might move the ribbon. We chose separate supports and sound off, then on, without touching. It moved during sound, so I added that arrow and kept our ruler arrow." Does not meet: "Sound cannot move paper" (B). These are possible evidence patterns, not supplied results. Score against actual trials. Honest null results earn observation credit but need further safe evidence before a positive causal claim.

**Feedback and scoring.** Allow 30 minutes after day 33 to write one picture-linked comment per child. Day 34 provides ten minutes to hear that written feedback and revise the earlier account before scoring. Keep the original observation checklist for actions already completed. New day 34 work is unscored; work first made on day 32 is never a scored product. Allow 15 minutes after day 34 to finalize scores. Day 36 reuses day 33's peer-plan, procedure and own-data record without another conference or score on that work; gather only its new comparison reasoning.

**Access.** Read all prompts aloud. Every child marks pictures on paper; accept pointing, large prints, dictation or adapted marking. Observe visually or through an accessible neutral description of the actual motion, without giving its cause. Do not score hearing acuity or require humming. Teach vibrate as moving back and forth, with gesture and pictures; use "We changed __ and watched __."

**Daily evidence and exits.** The written days and items.csv (ids s04-X) now hold the exits as taught; where a case here differs (day 28: a spring doorstop, not a spoon), the written day governs and this text records the plan-stage intent. Keep notebook action-and-observation drawings daily. For each exit on days 29 to 32, children choose comparisons from actual held-back trials, follow their observation plan and mark their own findings. The cards organize choices; they do not supply results. Reserve three minutes on days 28 to 32 and within day 34's fresh-task block for one held-back exit question. All children mark pictures while the teacher reads; hear a rotating group of about six reasons. Each exit's stimulus is a real, dated recording to be made (see "Still to make"); the printed card beside it is a schematic that says "invented for practice", shows apparatus labels only and supplies no outcome. Cards appear only at the exit. Accept other testable initial ideas on day 28. Unmatched responses stay unclassified.
