# Section 5.2: What do materials do to light?

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-PS4-3, this section's share.** All of "Investigate what happens when objects of different materials are put in a beam of light: clear, partly clear, blocking or mirror-like." Day 43 plans and carries out the test with clear plastic, wax paper and cardboard; day 44 adds a mirror and foil; day 45 sorts, counts and plans for unfamiliar pieces; day 46 checks; day 47 responds.
- **1-PS4-2, this section's share.** "Observe and then explain, with evidence, that we see an object only when light shines on it or it makes its own light." Taught in section 5.1; here it is checked on day 46 (item 1) and again on day 47 on fresh cases. Nothing new is taught about seeing.
- **Limits that bite here.** Lamps, mirrors and shadows only: no speed of light, no wave model, no reflection mechanism, no deliberate shadow lengthening (the Candidate was dropped). The three labels (lets light through, lets some through, blocks light and makes a shadow) describe what we see, not measured percentages; clear plastic passes most light, not all. The mirror goes in the blocks group, and "does it send light a new way?" is a separate yes-or-no question, never a fourth group. Plastic or edge-taped mirrors, no lasers, no glass.
- **Connections.** Math 1.MD.C.4, day 45: sorting into three groups and counting each, as review taught in weeks 1 to 4. Kindergarten picture graphs, review on day 45. Kindergarten push-and-pull planning and shade design, retrieval on day 43. W.1.2 (ELA): dictated and framed accounts on days 46 and 47.
- **Practices.** Planning and carrying out investigations: day 43 (choose samples, keep the light still), day 44, day 45 (peer plan), day 46. Cause and effect: the sample, not the light, is what changes the screen.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A beam on a paper screen makes a bright patch. Clear plastic lets most light on to the screen, so the patch is nearly as bright. Wax paper lets some through and spreads it, so the patch is dim and has no clear edge. Cardboard stops the light, so there is a shadow where the patch was. A smooth shiny mirror sends the beam off in a new direction; a patch on a second screen at the side is light that was turned, not light that went through. Crumpled foil turns light in many directions and gives no clear patch, which is why day 44 uses smoothed foil. The test is fair only if the light and the screen stay still: if the flashlight moves, the patch changes for that reason (error E). A bright room washes out the patch, so dim it gently, never dark.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** items 1 and 2 (day 46, unit plan field 5). Item 1: the cupboard label and the enclosed battery light. Item 2: new clear plastic, cardboard, tissue paper and a differently shaped plastic mirror. Their cases are held back from days 43 to 45 and 47, and from every exit below; day 43's exit pieces differ from item 2's.

**Evidence form.** Spoken, drawn, pointed to or sorted. Each exit is one ask, read aloud, answered by touching a picture card, after the teacher says "this is invented for practice". A rotating six is heard, 20 seconds each, with a "heard" column. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Day 46 is scored by the unit plan's components on the same scale.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example (field 4).
- **E43a (day 43).** Three trial cards of new pieces in the same beam, each showing the piece and its screen only: a small wooden board (no patch), tracing paper (a dim patch), a clear page protector (a bright patch); results are not said aloud. "Touch the piece that blocked the light." Answer: the felt square. Wrong: the tracing paper (unclassified: dim read as none).
- **E43b (day 43).** "Pip tried three new pieces. Each time Pip moved the flashlight closer, so the patch was easy to see. Is that a fair test?" Every child shows thumb up for yes or thumb down for no at once and is marked; the six heard say why. Answer: no; keep the light still and change only the piece. Wrong: yes (E).
- **E44 (day 44).** Cards: a new shiny tray tried in the beam, a bright patch on the side screen and no patch behind it. "Pip says the light went through the tray. Is Pip right?" Every child shows thumb up for yes or thumb down for no at once and is marked; the six heard say why. Answer: no; the tray sent the light a new way. Wrong: yes (C).
- **E45a (day 45).** A fresh six-card record, invented: five cards laid in the three hoops, the sixth held up (a shiny steel bowl on its side in the beam, no patch behind, a bright side patch). "Touch the hoop this card goes in." Answer: blocks light, with a turns light tag. Wrong: a fourth hoop for it (D); lets light through (C). Counting and comparing the hoops is watched in the Sort and count part, not asked again in the exit.
- **E45b (day 45).** Two plan pictures for testing a new piece, the flashlight taped in both: the screen taped in place, or the screen moved for each piece. "Touch the plan that is fair." Answer: the taped screen. Wrong: the moved screen (E).
- **E47a (day 47).** Cards: a firefly glowing on a dark evening and a leaf on a far branch, too far for the glow to reach (source 9L). "Touch the one we can see with no other light." Answer: the firefly. Wrong: the leaf "after a wait" (A); "neither, the firefly needs a lamp" (B).
- **E47b (day 47).** Trial cards: a kitchen-roll sheet with a dim patch behind it and a tin tray with a patch at the side. "Touch the piece where light went through." Answer: the kitchen-roll sheet. Wrong: the tin tray (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The condition-to-seeing drawing and the class chart (seeing, sources, barriers) from section 5.1. Day 43 adds a "materials" row to the chart: lets light through, lets some through, blocks light and makes a shadow. Day 44 adds the mirror and foil and a separate "turns the light?" column. Day 45 revises the kindergarten picture graph into three hoops with a count chart, redirection recorded apart. Day 46 uses the consensus account; day 47 revises and keeps it. The stand and peer plan made on day 45 are kept for day 46.
- **Key moment 1, days 43 and 44: the patch on the paper screen.** Ideal instrument: a light meter at the screen. Spec: none in this unit; the eye reads bright patch, dim patch, no patch with a shadow, and a patch at the side. Fits, because three groups and a yes-or-no are all the standard asks. If the room is too bright, dim it gently and set the screen close to the piece. If a reading fails (the patch is not clear, a battery is low), repeat once and keep both, then use the adult-made dated photographs of the same trials, said aloud to be recordings and marked indirect. No invented result is passed off as a measurement.
- **Key moment 2, day 44: the side screen beside the mirror.** Ideal: a second beam path drawn on the table. Spec: none; the second screen is the instrument, below faces.
- **Equipment, counted.** Day 43: one flashlight, screen and three samples per group of five; five placements at 40 seconds (3 minutes 20 seconds) inside an 11-minute part. Day 44: one plastic mirror and one smoothed foil for each group; with only two mirrors three groups share one, 3 minutes 20 seconds each, 10 minutes inside a 12-minute part; with one mirror it becomes a demonstration with readings shared. Day 46: one stand set at the observation table for six turns of 3 minutes (18 minutes, the whole part); all other groups use paper.
- **Clean-up.** Days 43 and 44, 2 minutes: flashlights off into a tray, samples counted back by the teacher, foil smoothed and bagged by the teacher. Day 45, 2 minutes: stands put on a low shelf, tape off the table. Day 46, 3 minutes: flashlights off and counted, samples and mirror trimmed pieces counted, screens collected.
- **A room with nothing.** A flashlight, white paper, kitchen wax paper, a cardboard box piece, smoothed foil and an edge-taped plastic mirror run days 43 to 46. With none of these, the adult-made dated photographs of each trial carry days 43 to 46 as recordings. No key tool.

## 6. Days

## Day 43


- **6 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs in: day 42 exit, errors A (3 min) and B (3 min). A: a toy in a shut toy chest, before and after the lid lifts. B: a flashlight on under a blanket, its glow seen at the edge (both invented for practice). Others do the same pair on a printed card.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** "In our ball test, what did we change and watch?" (kindergarten rolled one ball gently, then harder). "Which picture shows the cover blocking sunlight?" (kindergarten's cardboard cover over a toy animal). A miss: roll one ball gently and hold a card over a toy.
- **2 min. Rug.** The board's new heading, barriers: "What lets light through?" The class guesses aloud.
- **5 min. Plan.** Partners choose from clear plastic, wax paper and cardboard and say what to keep still (the flashlight spot on a taped mark and the screen), what to change (the piece), what to watch (the screen). The empty opening is looked at first.
- **11 min. Test.** Groups of five, a gently dimmed room, the beam on the screen only, flashlights below faces. Each child places one piece in the beam; the group marks the screen: bright patch, dim patch, or shadow. Sample edges are trimmed the day before.
- **3 min. Record.** Each child marks the paper test chart: all, some or no light through, and the cardboard's shadow.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E43a, E43b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice places pieces in the beam and ticks the chart; the exit reads three new trial cards to find the blocker and judges Pip's test, where the flashlight moved each time, a fairness question the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (board, samples); groups (flashlight, screen); children (chart). Tool: None. By hand: the test chart (about 60 words printed). Evidence: E43a, E43b. Hand-backs in: A and B. Out: E to day 44.

## Day 44


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 43 exit, error E (4 min, plus 1 min to settle). Tape the light spot on a picture; change only the piece.
- **3 min. Rug.** "Does a mirror let light through? Does it do something else?" Two questions, asked apart.
- **6 min. Plan.** Add a second screen at the side, below faces. Keep the light spot taped and the first screen still. Each piece gets two marks: does light go through (behind), does light go a new way (side).
- **12 min. Test.** Plastic mirror (edge-taped) and smoothed foil, and the three earlier pieces for comparison, beside the empty opening. Each child places one piece; the group marks both screens. The mirror is aimed so its beam falls on the side screen and never at a face.
- **4 min. Record.** The test chart gains the mirror in the "no light through" group, with "turns the light: yes" marked apart.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E44.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice tests mirror and foil and marks the screens behind and beside; the exit gives a new tray's two screens and Pip's claim that light went through, so the child judges a claim from screens already marked.

Held by: teacher (board, mirrors, foil); groups (flashlight, screens); children (chart). Tool: None. By hand: the chart (about 70 words printed). Evidence: E44. Hand-backs in: E. Out: C to day 45.

## Day 46


- **2 min. Launch.** Groups of five take places; this is a check; recordings are called recordings; children may pass; the room is gently dimmed, door seats are open to anyone, the exit is lit. Hand-backs in: none; no reteaching.
- **4 min. Picture plans.** Each child numbers the four printed piece pictures on the response sheet for the order and rings one prediction under each (bright, dim, shadow); what stays still is on the day 45 peer plan.
- **18 min. Six group turns, 3 minutes each.** One observation table with the day 45 stand, a flashlight, four new samples (a clear cup wall piece, a thick, tightly woven dark cloth square that shows a shadow in rehearsal, tissue paper, a differently shaped plastic mirror) and two screens. Per turn: 60 seconds of shared trials (each child places one piece, one repeated so all five act; all four effects are seen), five 20-second accounts, each child the same place-your-piece account (item 2: say what the screen behind and the side screen showed; all four effects scored after class from the what I saw row); item 1 is asked of every child in Records, 20 seconds to change over. Waiting children independently match familiar evidence cards and draw a reason (unscored).
- **4 min. Records.** Each group opens its four cupboard records; every child touches the lit and unlit looks and draws the thing seen and why; the rotating six dictate; the teacher marks item 1 for every child from the drawings after class.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**

Held by: teacher (table, record of who was heard); groups (their turn); children (sheet). Tool: None. By hand: the response sheet (about 80 words printed). Evidence: unit assessment items 1 and 2. Hand-backs out: gaps to day 47, by letter.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: through, block, shadow, mirror, bright, dim, patch, screen, fair. Frames: "The light went through." "It made a shadow." "I kept ___ still and changed ___." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** Observe by sight or by a neutral adult description of the patch, without its cause; do not score eyesight. A child who dislikes the dimmed room sits by the door and looks at the screen from there.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all prompts aloud; every child marks pictures; accept large print, pointing, dictation or an adapted marking.
- **Reading load.** None. Each student page stays under 100 words (labels, boxes, a few words); all else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child plans a test of one more household piece from the teacher's tray with the same plan sheet and predicts its group.
