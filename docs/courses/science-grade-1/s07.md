## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why does a baby bird cheep with its mouth open when its parent lands on the nest?"
- **Hook, day 56.** Show the feeding photograph in field 10 without its explanatory caption. Children notice open mouths and an adult carrying food. Read the Curio Class original account of cheeping, whose facts are checked against the university robin account, then ask the compelling question. A photograph cannot supply sound evidence.
- **Closing, day 59.** Dictate why cheeping and an adult carrying food to open mouths help a young robin receive food. Cite the text for cheeping and the photograph for the open mouths and the food; compare the deer evidence for another survival pattern.
- **Teaching moves.** Model one page-based question, then ask children to locate evidence before explaining. Invite a different pattern, rather than forcing every animal into the robin sequence.

**Held-back sources, all original practice cases.** Print "invented for practice" on each invented account and illustration. Their facts follow field 10's sources; they are not records of an observed event. No duck case appears in teaching or exits before day 60.

- Animal text: "These ducklings hatched from eggs laid by this adult duck. The adult calls to keep the ducklings together, and they answer. When the adult gives a danger call, the ducklings usually stop moving. Staying still can help keep them safe from an animal hunting them."
- Animal media: two original three-frame illustrated sequences, presented without explanatory conclusions. In tall plants, adult and young have distinct call symbols, followed by the reunited group. In a separate danger episode, the adult calls, then the young freeze beside vegetation while a distant hunting animal passes. The adult is labeled parent, the small birds young. Explain the call-symbol convention without interpreting either sequence. These are two representations of sourced behavior, not independent observations proving it happens.
- Plant text: "This grown sunflower made seeds. One seed grew into this young sunflower." An original diagram shows the adult, seed and seedling with growth arrows. It does not show feeding, carrying or guarding. Use a different plant from unit 3's maple diagram.

**Thirty-minute sitting.** Launch takes two minutes; item 1 takes 21 minutes, including three for reading and showing the sources and six three-minute groups of five; item 2 takes five minutes; collection takes two. Each child has about 30 seconds to point to a marked source and give one survival reason, leaving 30 seconds per group for transitions. Paper responses supply the other evidence. Waiting children complete their own responses, then independently match familiar robin and deer cards. No reteaching or extra exit occurs. Rehearse the schedule before use.

**Ten points.** Item 1 earns eight: identify young behavior; identify adult behavior; locate relevant text evidence; locate relevant media evidence; compare one agreement or difference in source coverage; describe the repeated protective relationship across the two episodes; explain how the adult's behavior helps survival; explain how the young's behavior helps survival. Item 2 earns two: use the caption to connect an adult plant with young; use the diagram to explain the seed-to-young relationship without animal-style feeding. The animal text also establishes adult animals producing young. Every point requires obtaining or comparing information with a core idea on a new case, exceeding 80 percent. Do not score the earlier model or first-teaching work.

**Samples, invented for practice.** Meets: "The words say they answer. These marks show both calling. The pictures show them together, but the words tell why calls help when they cannot see. The adult warns them; the little ones stop moving, which helps keep them safe. This seed grew into the young plant; the adult did not bring it food." Does not meet: "Only the big duck does anything useful" (A); "The grown sunflower brings food to its seedling" (D). Judge each component separately; incomplete evidence is unclassified unless it expresses a listed error.

**Feedback and scoring.** After day 60, allow 25 teacher minutes to annotate paper evidence and observation records with picture-linked written feedback. Children revise explanations on day 61 before final scores are entered in 15 teacher minutes afterward. For any point recorded orally, day 61 revision is spoken again to the teacher, written feedback is still given on the record, and the original is kept. Preserve the original observation record; a new response performance is unscored. Reuse that record on day 67 without re-scoring it.

**Access.** Read every prompt aloud. Every child marks pictures on paper; accept pointing, enlarged illustrations, neutral descriptions and dictation. Descriptions report visible actions without supplying survival conclusions.
