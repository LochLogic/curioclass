# Section 5.1: What lets us see?

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-PS4-2, this section's share.** All of "Observe and then explain, with evidence, that we see an object only when light shines on it or it makes its own light." Both halves are taught and checked here: an ordinary object is seen when light shines on it (days 38 to 40, 42), and a source is seen because it makes its own light (days 39 to 42). Section 5.2 asks for them again only on day 46 and day 47.
- **Limits that bite here.** No eye-entry model (nothing about light going into the eye), no reflection mechanism, no speed of light, no waves. Children say "light shines on it" and "makes its own light". The adult words "illuminated" and "self-luminous" stay on this page. The moon is not used as a case. Never look at the sun; the sun appears only in pictures and in review.
- **Connections.** W.1.2 (ELA), day 40: a supported sentence by frame, dictation and copying key words. Kindergarten sunlight, sun and shade, unit 1's shadow, unit 3's evidence arrow and unit 4's cause and effect are review.
- **Practices.** Constructing explanations: days 38, 40 and 41, the drawing and the sentence. Cause and effect: light reaching the object causes us to see it, tested by changing only the light (day 38).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** We see a thing when light from it reaches our eyes. An ordinary object has no light of its own; light from a source shines on it, and some of that light goes on to our eyes. Children are told only the observation: no light, no seeing; light on the object, seeing; a source, seeing without another lamp. The toy never disappears, so day 38 says "the toy stayed; our seeing changed". A dim room is not no light: the eyes slowly adjust, so waiting in a box that leaks a little light really can bring the toy into view. That is where error A comes from, so the box must be fully dark (lined and taped, rehearsed the day before) and the repair is a closed bag, not an argument. A dull glow is not the lamp shining on things: day 39 separates seeing the lamp from seeing the wall beside it.

## 2. Where it sits


- **Before:** the start of unit 5; nothing is handed forward into day 38. Day 38 uses retrieval of kindergarten sunlight and the before-and-after evidence account (unit plan field 4, 3 minutes, not a readiness check).
- **After:** day 42's exit hand-backs (A and B) go to day 43, which holds a named repair part for them. The drawings, sentence sheets and card sorts go to the unit record folder for days 43, 46 and 47.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Items 1 and 2 of the unit assessment are on day 46 and belong to section 5.2. Their cases (the unlabelled cupboard label and the enclosed battery light) are held back from every day below: this section's lamps, boxes and cupboards are different objects.

**Evidence form.** Spoken, drawn, pointed to or sorted. Each exit is one ask, read aloud, answered by touching a picture card. The teacher says "this is invented for practice" first. About six rotating children give a reason in 20 seconds each, and the record sheet has a "heard" column. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Day 40's sentence is judged on the same scale.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example (field 4).
- **E38 (day 38).** Three held-back pairs of cards: a sock in a shut lunch box and then with the lid open; the same sock in a shut lunch box, then a long time later by the clock, still shut; a sock in a shut box and then a different toy in an open box. "Touch the pair that checks whether light lets us see." Answer: lid shut, then lid open. Wrong: the long wait (A); the pair with a different toy (unclassified: two things changed).
- **E39 (day 39).** Card: a string of little battery lights switched on along a fence in a dark yard. "Does the string need another lamp shining on it, for us to see it?" Every child shows thumb up for yes or thumb down for no at once and is marked; the six heard say why. Answer: no, it makes its own light. Wrong: yes (B).
- **E40 (day 40).** Card pair: a key hanging in a shut shed, then the door open and the key seen. Three finishing cards for "We can see the key when ___": light reaches it; we wait a long time; the door is blue. "Touch the card that finishes our sentence." Answer: light reaches it. Wrong: the long wait (A); the door is blue (unclassified).
- **E41 (day 41).** Cards: the switched-on battery lantern in a dark room, still seen; the same lantern with the room light on; a glossy toy car with the room light on. "Touch the picture that shows the lantern makes its own light." Answer: lantern, room light off. Wrong: lantern, room light on (B); the car (unclassified).
- **E42 (day 42).** Cards: a button in a shut box and a switched-on night light. "Touch the one we can see." Answer: the night light. Wrong: "neither, the night light needs another lamp" (B); the button "after a wait" (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class chart under three headings (seeing, sources, barriers) and each child's condition-to-seeing drawing: the object, the viewing place, an arrow for light, and "seen" or "not seen". Day 38 starts it (unit 3's evidence arrow and unit 4's cause and effect are the parents). Day 40 revises it from room and cave observations and adds the source exception. Day 41 strengthens it on a new lamp. Day 42 adds blocked light. Kindergarten's sun and shade picture stays on the wall as review. The drawings go on to day 43.
- **Key moment 1, days 38 and 42: the toy seen or not seen in the box.** Ideal instrument: a light meter inside the box. Spec: none in this unit, and none is needed; the eye reads seen, hard to see, not seen (no numbers). Fits, because the question is only whether light is there. A leaky box is the real failure: a child sees the toy with the flap shut and no flashlight. Fix it the day before with black paper and tape; if it still leaks, use the adult-made dated picture sequence of the box and say it is a recording and indirect.
- **Key moment 2, day 39: the lamp and the wall beside it in the gently dimmed room.** Ideal: a light meter at the wall. Spec: none; the eye compares a bright wall near the lamp with a dim wall far from it. Fits for a comparison, not for any number. If the room cannot be dimmed, the cave picture sequence carries the day and is said to be indirect.
- **Equipment, counted.** Days 38 and 42: one box, one secured toy and one flashlight per group of five (six groups). Each child takes three 15-second looks, 45 seconds; five children take 3 minutes 45 seconds, inside an 11-minute part. With only three boxes, two groups share a box: 10 children at 45 seconds is 7 minutes 30 seconds, and the part still fits. With one box, the day becomes a demonstration with a rotating six looking and the adult-made sequence for the rest. Day 39's lamp and flashlight are teacher-only and everyone watches together. Day 41 is one teacher demonstration or picture sequences.
- **Clean-up.** Days 38 and 42, 2 minutes: the flashlight holder switches off and lays the flashlight in a tray, the looker shuts the lid, the teacher counts boxes and flashlights. No water or spills. Day 39: the teacher raises the lights (2 minutes). Days 40 and 41: papers only.
- **A room with nothing.** A shoebox with a peephole and a light hole, a cloth and a household flashlight run days 38 and 42. A paper bag runs the A repair. Pictures carry days 39 to 41: the cave prints and the lamp sequence, each labeled as a recording. No key tool; the section runs on objects and print.

## 6. Days

## Day 38


- **5 min. Hook.** The teacher holds one box with a toy inside. A rotating six children look through the peephole with the flap shut ("I see..."), then with the flap open. The teacher asks "Did the toy go away?" and gathers notices without explaining.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Two questions, one each, on the old shade picture: "Point to what makes the light here." "Which before-and-after drawing showed us our idea was true?" A miss: point back to the old chart.
- **5 min. Notices to question.** The question board has three headings: seeing, sources, barriers. The class gives the teacher a notice for "seeing"; the compelling question is read aloud.
- **11 min. Boxes in groups.** Roles: looker, flashlight holder, recorder; or sit by the door (no one needs a reason). Plan aloud: keep the toy and the viewing place, change only the light, watch the toy. Three looks each: flap shut; flap open; flap shut with the tape tab peeled back from the light hole (about 2 cm, cut in the box top away from the peephole the day before), the flashlight shining down through it at the toy and never at a face or the peephole. Each child draws seen or not seen for each look.
- **6 min. Our first drawing.** The teacher models one look beside its changed condition (flap shut: not seen; flap open: seen) and draws an arrow for light. Each child draws their own.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E38.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice looks through the peephole shut, open and lit; the exit gives three new sock-in-a-box pairs, one with a wait and one changing two things, and asks which pair tests whether light lets us see.

Held by: teacher (box, chart, question board); groups (boxes, flashlights); children (drawing). Tool: None. By hand: the drawing sheet (about 40 words printed). Evidence: E38. Hand-backs in: none. Out: A to day 39.

## Day 39


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 38 exit, error A. A closed paper bag with a sticker inside, before and after it is opened (invented for practice). Children point to the picture where light got in.
- **3 min. Rug.** The question board: "What if the thing makes light?" The class names today's three places: the box, the room, the cave pictures.
- **9 min. The gently dimmed room.** Blinds and lights down by half, never dark; seats by the door are open to anyone; the exit is lit. The teacher alone switches on a cool enclosed lamp and a flashlight, each aimed at a wall, away from faces. Children look briefly at the lamp from the side and at the wall near it. Questions: "What do we see? Why can we see the wall? Why can we see the lamp?" A rotating six answers.
- **9 min. Cave pictures.** The teacher reads the captions aloud. Each group of five has a printed sequence of flashlight photographs from a cave. Children point to what the light shines on, and to what makes the light.
- **5 min. Evidence chart.** Each child marks three picture cards on the paper chart: "light shines on it" or "makes its own light".
- **2 min. Lights up.** The teacher raises the room; the lamp and flashlight go back in the tray.
- **2 min. Exit.** E39.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice points to what is lit and what makes light in the room and cave cards; the exit asks whether a new string of lights in a dark yard needs another lamp to be seen, a yes or no the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (lamp, flashlight); groups (cave prints); children (chart). Tool: None. By hand: the evidence chart (about 50 words printed). Evidence: E39. Hand-backs in: A. Out: B to day 40.

## Day 40


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 39 exit, error B. A book light off, then on, inside a closed cupboard (invented for practice): children point to the picture where nothing else is lit.
- **3 min. Rug.** The question board: "What lets us see? What about things that glow?"
- **8 min. Revise the drawing.** Paper light-observation records from the box, the room and the cave. Each child adds a second kind of arrow, "makes its own light", for the lamp and flashlight, and keeps the first arrow for the wall.
- **12 min. A supported sentence.** The teacher models "We can see the ___ when ___ because ___" (3 min). Partners say their own aloud to each other, 30 seconds each (3 min). Each child dictates a sentence to the teacher, who writes it; the child copies or traces the key words and draws the proof (6 min). The teacher hears a rotating six while the rest work.
- **2 min. Scientists find out.** "We tested, we looked, we drew." Add one line to the board.
- **3 min. Exit.** E40.
- **2 min. Put away.** Sheets collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice revises the seeing drawing and says a supported sentence about the class box; the exit gives a new key in a shed and three finishing cards, one a long wait, so the child picks the reason instead of saying one.

Held by: teacher (board, scribing); children (sheet). Tool: None. By hand: drawing and sentence sheet (about 40 words printed). Evidence: E40, the sentence. Hand-backs in: B. Out: A to day 41.

## Day 41


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 40 exit, error A. A closed pencil case with a sticker inside, before and after the lid is opened (invented for practice).
- **3 min. Rug.** The question: "Does a new lamp need light from elsewhere? Does the toy next to it?"
- **10 min. Lamp off and on beside an unlit toy.** The teacher runs a new small cool enclosed lamp beside an unlit toy inside the shut day 38 peephole box (lamp off: toy not seen; lamp on: both seen), or children use dated, unlabelled picture sequences of it, one set per group. Each child points: the lamp, "makes its own light"; the toy, "needs light from elsewhere". A rotating six says why.
- **8 min. Say it again.** Partners say the supported sentence aloud for two new picture cards (not drafted, not written); 30 seconds each; the teacher hears a rotating six.
- **4 min. Strengthen the drawing.** Each child adds the lamp and the toy to their drawing with the right arrow.
- **3 min. Exit.** E41.
- **2 min. Put away.** Lamp switched off by the teacher; papers collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice asks of a lamp and duck whether each needs another light; the exit shows a new lantern on three cards, one in a lit room and one a shiny toy, and asks which picture shows it makes its own light.

Held by: teacher (lamp); groups (picture sequences); children (drawing). Tool: None. By hand: the drawing. Evidence: E41. Hand-backs in: A. Out: B to day 42.

Shorter path (plan point): fold the lamp case into day 40 as a 5-minute demonstration after the drawing, and drop day 41.

## Day 42


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 41 exit, error B. A bike light in a closed drawer, seen through a crack (invented for practice).
- **3 min. Rug.** Return to the box: "What if the light cannot get in, or cannot get out?" The class guesses aloud.
- **8 min. Sort the cards.** Ten picture cards (five that make their own light, five that do not): children place two each under "makes its own light" or "needs light from elsewhere" and say why. The sun may be on a card and is never viewed.
- **10 min. Box and blocked light.** Groups predict, then try. Looks: flap shut with the flashlight shining down through the light hole; the same with a cardboard held over the light hole; flap open with the flashlight off. Each child draws seen or not seen. The flashlight stays aimed down.
- **4 min. Add to the account.** "Block" joins the drawing. The question board gains "what blocks light?" for day 43.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E42.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts ten cards by light source and looks with the light hole blocked; the exit puts a new button in a shut box beside a lit night light and asks which can be seen, joining blocking and source in one choice.

Held by: teacher (board); groups (boxes); children (drawing). Tool: None. By hand: the drawing sheet. Evidence: E42. Hand-backs in: B. Out: A and B to day 43.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 38, "Did the toy go away? How do you know?"; day 39, "What lets us see the wall? What lets us see the lamp?"; day 42, "What changes if we block the light?"
- Written task, day 40: one supported sentence, by frame and dictation, with a drawn proof.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names the thing seen; says light reaches it or that it makes its own light; the "because" points to something the child saw (shown, shown with help, not yet seen).

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 38 pulls kindergarten sun and shade and unit 3's evidence account. Day 39 pulls the day 38 drawing. Day 40 pulls days 38 and 39. Day 41 pulls the sentence frame. Day 42 pulls the box test of day 38 and the source sort of day 39.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: light, shine, see, dark, lamp, box, flap, block. Frames: "I can see the ___ when ___." "It makes its own light." Pointing and home-language words are accepted.
- **Access.** Observe by sight or by a neutral adult description of what is seen; do not score eyesight. A child who dislikes a dim room may sit by the door and still look at the cards. The teacher holds the flashlight for any child who prefers it.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all prompts aloud; accept pointing, dictation or an adapted marking.
- **Reading load.** None. Each student page stays under 100 words (labels, boxes, a sentence frame); everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child finds a thing at home that makes its own light and a thing that does not, and tells the class how they know.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Days 38 and 42: a shoebox with a peephole, a light hole, a toy, a flap and a flashlight; look through the peephole with the flap shut and open, and with the flashlight shining through the light hole. Day 39: a bike light off and on in a dim room; name what makes light and what is lit. Day 40: the adult writes the child's sentence and does not correct. Day 41: a lamp beside a toy. Never shine a light at a face.
- **One projector.** Show one cave print at a time, large; each child still marks their own chart. Box looks run in turn at one table.
