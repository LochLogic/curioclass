# Section 5.1: What lets us see?

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 1. Unit 5, Light. *Why can't we see the toy inside the box until we open the flap, and why can we see a hand through wax paper but not through cardboard?* Anchor: a shoebox with a peephole and a toy inside, flap shut then open (days 38 to 47): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 38 to 42.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (38), fluency 1 (41), application 3 (39, 40, 42). ## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1-PS4-2** (days 38, 39, 40, 41, 42): Observe and then explain, with evidence, that we see an object only when light shines on it or it makes its own light.
- skill s1-see-with-light (grade 1)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

Day 46 transfers to an unfamiliar cupboard display and new material samples; day 47 resolves remaining questions.
> - **Carried.** Keep earlier design, shape and resemblance charts. Day 38 starts a condition-to-seeing drawing using unit 4's causal comparisons and unit 3's evidence account. Day 40 revises it from room and cave observations and adds the source exception; day 41 strengthens it. Days 43 and 44 add transmission, shadow and redirection evidence. Day 45 revises kindergarten picture graphs into three transmission groups, with redirection recorded separately. Use the consensus account on day 46; revise and retain it on day 47.
<!-- /skel -->

- **1-PS4-2, this section's share.** All of "Observe and then explain, with evidence, that we see an object only when light shines on it or it makes its own light." Both halves are taught and checked here: an ordinary object is seen when light shines on it (days 38 to 40, 42), and a source is seen because it makes its own light (days 39 to 42). Section 5.2 asks for them again only on day 46 and day 47.
- **Limits that bite here.** No eye-entry model (nothing about light going into the eye), no reflection mechanism, no speed of light, no waves. Children say "light shines on it" and "makes its own light". The adult words "illuminated" and "self-luminous" stay on this page. The moon is not used as a case. Never look at the sun; the sun appears only in pictures and in review.
- **Connections.** W.1.2 (ELA), day 40: a supported sentence by frame, dictation and copying key words. Kindergarten sunlight, sun and shade, unit 1's shadow, unit 3's evidence arrow and unit 4's cause and effect are review.
- **Practices.** Constructing explanations: days 38, 40 and 41, the drawing and the sentence. Cause and effect: light reaching the object causes us to see it, tested by changing only the light (day 38).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** We see a thing when light from it reaches our eyes. An ordinary object has no light of its own; light from a source shines on it, and some of that light goes on to our eyes. Children are told only the observation: no light, no seeing; light on the object, seeing; a source, seeing without another lamp. The toy never disappears, so day 38 says "the toy stayed; our seeing changed". A dim room is not no light: the eyes slowly adjust, so waiting in a box that leaks a little light really can bring the toy into view. That is where error A comes from, so the box must be fully dark (lined and taped, rehearsed the day before) and the repair is a closed bag, not an argument. A dull glow is not the lamp shining on things: day 39 separates seeing the lamp from seeing the wall beside it.

## Day 38

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Open the closed-flap box puzzle and compare seeing the toy with and without light reaching it.
- note: Retrieve sk-sun-warms naming the sun as light source and sk-change-environment evidence accounts. Draw initial causal model. Darkness choice: hold flashlight or sit by door.

- **5 min. Hook.** The teacher holds one box with a toy inside. A rotating six children look through the peephole with the flap shut ("I see..."), then with the flap open. The teacher asks "Did the toy go away?" and gathers notices without explaining.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Two questions, one each, on the old shade picture: "Point to what makes the light here." "Which before-and-after drawing showed us our idea was true?" A miss: point back to the old chart.
- **5 min. Notices to question.** The question board has three headings: seeing, sources, barriers. The class gives the teacher a notice for "seeing"; the compelling question is read aloud.
- **11 min. Boxes in groups.** Roles: looker, flashlight holder, recorder; or sit by the door (no one needs a reason). Plan aloud: keep the toy and the viewing place, change only the light, watch the toy. Three looks each: flap shut; flap open; flap shut with the flashlight held at the gap, aimed down at the toy and never at a face or the peephole. Each child draws seen or not seen for each look.
- **6 min. Our first drawing.** The teacher models one look beside its changed condition (flap shut: not seen; flap open: seen) and draws an arrow for light. Each child draws their own.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E38.

Held by: teacher (box, chart, question board); groups (boxes, flashlights); children (drawing). Tool: None. By hand: the drawing sheet (about 40 words printed). Evidence: E38. Hand-backs in: none. Out: A to day 39.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: light, shine, see, dark, lamp, box, flap, block. Frames: "I can see the ___ when ___." "It makes its own light." Pointing and home-language words are accepted.
- **Access.** Observe by sight or by a neutral adult description of what is seen; do not score eyesight. A child who dislikes a dim room may sit by the door and still look at the cards. The teacher holds the flashlight for any child who prefers it.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all prompts aloud; accept pointing, dictation or an adapted marking.
- **Reading load.** None. Each student page stays under 100 words (labels, boxes, a sentence frame); everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child finds a thing at home that makes its own light and a thing that does not, and tells the class how they know.
