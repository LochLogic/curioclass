## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why can't we see the toy inside the box until we open the flap, and why does a beam show a bright patch behind wax paper but a shadow behind cardboard?"
- **Hook, day 38.** Children look through the box peephole before and after the teacher opens its separate light flap. The toy stays put. Gather notices before asking the compelling question.
- **Closing, day 47.** Dictate why opening the flap makes the toy visible and why wax paper and cardboard differ, using the box comparisons and the beam and screen records. Light must illuminate the toy; wax paper passes some light, cardboard blocks it and casts a shadow.
- **Teaching moves.** Model one observation beside its changed condition on day 38. Ask which comparison would answer each new question; gradually hand choices and recording to partners.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


- **Question board.** Group notices under seeing, sources and barriers. Day 38 asks whether the toy disappeared. Day 39 compares box, room and cave records and first meets sources that make light. Day 40 writes the source exception into the account; day 41 practices it on a fresh lamp and unlit toy; day 42 predicts interrupted illumination. This raises the barrier question: day 43 tests transmission, day 44 adds redirection, and day 45 organizes results and prepares a peer plan. Day 46 transfers to an unfamiliar cupboard display and new material samples; day 47 resolves remaining questions.
- **Dimensions together.** Section 5.1 uses constructing explanations. Section 5.2 uses planning and carrying out investigations. Both use the elements above.
- **Limits.** No eye-entry model, reflection mechanism, light speed, wave model or deliberate shadow-length variation. No engineering expectation is claimed. The three transmission labels describe observations, not measured percentages; clear plastic passes most light.
- **Carried.** Keep earlier design, shape and resemblance charts. Day 38 starts a condition-to-seeing drawing using unit 4's causal comparisons and unit 3's evidence account. Day 40 revises it from room and cave observations and adds the source exception; day 41 strengthens it. Days 43 and 44 add transmission, shadow and redirection evidence. Day 45 revises kindergarten picture graphs into three transmission groups, with redirection recorded separately. Use the consensus account on day 46; revise and retain it on day 47.

**Collection, day 46.** Use 30 minutes: launch 3; six group turns of 3; individual records 6; cleanup 3. Item 1 takes six minutes across the group turns and two record minutes; item 2 takes twelve turn minutes and four record minutes. Split records before and after turns. Each group turn allows one minute for shared material trials, five linked 20-second accounts covering both codes, and 20 seconds for changeover. Every child performs one placement, with one repeated sample so all five act, observes all four effects and records each. Waiting children independently match familiar evidence cards, without rehearsing the transfer cases. No reteaching.

**Transfer evidence.** Reserve actual unlabelled cupboard-label and enclosed battery-light records, showing the label with outside illumination off and on while the little source stays off, then the source alone switched off and on. In the source records the label is out of frame or shielded from the little light, and the label records are taken with the little light off. Record both objects' appearances, rather than giving conclusions. The ordinary label needs light from elsewhere; the switched-on source makes light. The material problem uses a clear cup wall piece, a thick dark cloth square that shows a shadow in rehearsal and tissue paper (materials not met on days 43 to 45) plus a differently shaped plastic mirror, as day 46 is written; day 43's exit uses different pieces from item 2. Use day 45's stand and peer plan; children choose order and predict effects, with light position and screen distance fixed. Move the second screen into the redirected beam while keeping it below faces. Compare each sample with the empty opening. Paper supplies the previous model, not answers about the new case.

**Points and look-fors.** Item 1 has four one-point components: mark the ordinary object's paired appearances; mark the source's paired appearances; explain illumination using the first comparison; explain the self-luminous exception using the second. Item 2 has eight one-point components: state the question about these materials; on day 46, choose the order of the new samples, predict each effect and state the fixed light and screen positions; follow the plan; record clear transmission; record partial transmission; record blocking with shadow; record mirror redirection; use those results to support or revise the causal model. Each point requires observation or investigation practice with the light idea on a fresh case, so all twelve exceed the 80 percent requirement. Naming a material without evidence earns no point. Blank or unmatched answers remain unclassified.

**Samples, invented for practice.** Item 1 meets: "The label appeared when outside light reached it. The little light appeared when switched on even with the outside light off. It makes light." Does not: "Wait long enough and the unlit label appears" (A). Item 2 meets: "We chose these pieces and kept the light still. The clear piece lit the back screen strongly; tissue paper lit it less; the dark cloth made a shadow. The mirror lit the side screen, so I added a new direction to our model." Does not: "The side patch proves light passed through the mirror" (C). These are possible patterns; score against actual observations.

**Feedback and score.** After day 46, allow 30 teacher minutes for a component checklist and one written picture-linked comment per child. Day 47 begins with ten minutes to hear that feedback and revise the earlier account and the item 2 records (add or correct a picture or label); the spoken accounts are scored only as written on the record. Finalize scores after day 47, allowing 15 minutes. Only day 46's earlier work, revised after feedback, is scored; day 45's preparation, first-teaching products and new day 47 work are unscored. Preserve observations already collected without repeating conferences.

**Access.** Read prompts aloud; every child marks pictures on paper. Accept large print, pointing, dictation or adapted marking. Give neutral descriptions of actual visible changes without explaining causes; do not score visual acuity. No child must enter darkness. Use spoken, pictured words: light, source, beam, shadow, through and turn.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

- **Tiers.** Household boxes, flashlights, packaging and foil serve both investigations. Actual unlabeled observation sequences with child-selected comparisons are the weaker no-kit path; children collect their own records. No simulation is necessary or requested; no tool mark, verdict or TOOL-ASKS.md entry applies. Use at most one shared device; printed frames carry every task.
- **Cost.** Plan at most $8 for paper and tape within unit 5's allocation of the approximately $40 annual class allowance. Use clean packaging offcuts for samples; reuse foil, lights, boxes and mirrors. Inventory first; prices are unverified.
- **Safety and set-up.** The day before each hands-on day, adults cut openings, trim plastic, smooth foil, secure battery compartments and rehearse trials. Use cool enclosed lights on AA or AAA cells in taped compartments, never button cells; the day 39 and 41 lamp is battery LED, no cord. Battery and dimming rules hold on every flashlight day (38, 39, 42 to 44, 46). No lasers, glass, heat or chemical reagents. Fix lights below faces, directing mirror beams onto screens. Only one group handles lights at a time; others use paper. Never view the sun. Children stay seated in gentle dimming with a lit exit and a full-light alternative. No eye protection is needed with intact, blunt samples and no flying or splashing material; stop for damaged equipment or discomfort.
- **Per group of five:** days 38 and 42 need one box, secured toy and flashlight; day 39 uses a teacher-only flashlight and enclosed lamp demonstration and five records; day 41 uses paper records or that teacher-only demonstration. Days 43 and 44 need one flashlight, screen, clear plastic, wax paper, cardboard and five records; day 44 adds a plastic mirror and smoothed foil. Day 45 needs three paper hoops, five cards, count chart, blunt cardboard stand pieces and tape. Day 46 reuses the stand with four new samples, two screens and five sheets. Reserve two cleanup minutes on each named day, three on day 46; collect lights and samples. Other days use paper.
