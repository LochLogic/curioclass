# Section 3.1: Like their parents

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-LS3-1, the whole standard, this section's share.** Children "observe" pictured and pressed leaves and animal families, and "explain, with evidence", that young plants and young animals look a lot like their parents but are "not exact copies". Plants come first and stay on the chart every day (days 22, 25, 26, 27); animals are dogs, cats and, for the check, rabbits. Both halves are taught: alike (days 22 to 24) and not exactly (days 23 to 25), and the check asks for both on a new plant pair and a new animal pair.
- **Limits that bite here.** No genes, no way traits are passed on, no predicting what a young one will look like, no hybrids, no animals that change form as they grow, no children or relatives compared with anyone. No cause of a difference is given from looking. A leaf is evidence about its plant, not a young plant. No live animals; all families are original drawings, said aloud to be invented for practice.
- **Connections.** Math 1.MD.A.1 (order three objects by length), day 22 and again days 25 and 26: leaf bases on one tape line, tips show which is longer, no units. Practice: constructing an explanation from observations. Crosscutting: patterns (alike but not exactly).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Looking alike does not prove parentage: a seedling under a maple may or may not have come from it, so the schoolyard question stays open and no one is told "the parent". In the drawn families the caption supplies the parent and the seed path, so the class never infers it from looks or place. "Alike but not exactly" has two parts that need each other: a shared feature is the reason two organisms are one kind, and a difference does not break the kind. Young plants have parents too; a seed path on the picture shows it. An adult's own spots or leaf size can differ from the other adult of its kind, so the class also sees variation among adults.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** the six oak items (26a to 26f) and the six rabbit items (26g to 26l) of unit plan field 5, each one ask, all on day 26, scored from the revised earlier work on day 27 as that field says. Their cases (the oak family OP, OY1, OY2, OA and the fern OF; the rabbit family RP, RY1, RY2, RA and the fish RF) are held back from every day. The exit cases (M22E, D23E, C24E, V25E, P27E, A27E) are held back too and are not taught; each is a new individual of its kind, never a family from a teaching day.

**Evidence form.** Spoken, drawn, pointed to or laid out. Every exit is asked aloud, one ask each, on picture cards the teacher says were invented for practice; every child marks or lays out a card on paper, a rotating six is heard, and the record sheet has a column naming who. One scale on every record: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The unit plan's six components for each case (12 in all) are each read on that scale: shown carries the point; shown with help and not yet seen carry none. Components 1 to 5 come from the paper marks, component 6 from the oral turn.

**Exit questions.** If many (more than a third) miss one, the next teaching day repairs that error's wrong example in its Return and repair part (field 4).
- **E22a (day 22).** Invented: M22E, an oval-leaf plant and its two young, seed path drawn. "Point to something that is the same on the parent plant and its young." Answer: the oval, smooth-edged blade; the stem or roots alone is shown with help (every plant has them). Pointing to a difference only is shown with help, no letter.
- **E22b (day 22).** Same card, three loose blades: a small other adult EA, the young EY2 and the parent EP. "Lay the three blades bases together, shortest to longest." Answer: EA, EY2, EP, so the order comes from the tips, not from "young are small". Blades with the bases apart are shown with help and a length prompt, no letter.
- **E23a (day 23).** Invented: D23E, a brown horse with two young, a brown foal with a white star on its face the parent lacks, and a calf. "Touch the young one that is this horse's foal." Answer: the foal, by its long face, mane, legs and hooves. Wrong: the calf (no letter); "the star means it is not hers" (B).
- **E23b (day 23).** Same card. "Pip says the foal is an exact copy of its parent. Tell Pip one way the foal is like its parent and one way it is not." Answer: like in the mane, long face, legs or hooves; not like in the white star. Wrong: "they are the same" (A); "the star means it is not hers" (B).
- **E24a (day 24).** Invented: C24E, a white sheep with a black face and black legs, two lambs (one with a black patch on its back) and a duckling. "Touch every young one that could be this sheep's lamb." Answer: both lambs. Wrong: only the plain lamb (B); the duckling (no letter).
- **E24b (day 24).** Same card and three small cards: shared face, legs and wool; the black patch; it stands on grass. "Touch the card that shows the lamb with the patch is still a sheep." Answer: the shared-features card. Wrong: the patch card (B); grass (no letter).
- **E25a (day 25).** Invented: a pig family and a willow family, with two printed groupings to choose between, one by size and one by kind. "Touch the grouping that puts the same kind together." Answer: the second grouping, willows together and pigs together. Wrong: the first grouping, "the little one is a different thing" (B).
- **E25b (day 25).** Same cards. "Tell me why those go together even though they do not all look the same." Answer: the willows share long narrow pointed blades and the pigs share a snout, four legs and a curly tail, though each differs a little. Wrong: "The small willow does not go with the big one because plants do not have babies" (C); "The pig with the black ear is not like the other" (B).
- **Day 26.** No exit; rows 26a to 26l hold the task.
- **E27a to E27d (day 27, unscored).** Invented: P27E, a dandelion parent and its young, and A27E, a gray goose and a gosling. "Point to something that is the same on the dandelion parent and the young one." "Point to something that is not the same." Then the same two asks for the goose and the gosling. Answer: jagged leaf edges shared, the young leaf shorter; flat beak and feathers shared, the goose's white neck band against the gosling's white wing tip. Wrong: "That is not its young, plants have no babies" (C); "The young one looks different, so it is another kind" (B); "They are copies" (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The comparison model, a movable paper chart of pictured organisms, shared-feature links and difference labels. Day 22 starts it with the maple family and the pine contrast; day 23 revises it with dogs, keeping plants beside; day 24 adds cats as cases against exact copying; day 25 adds variation among young and among adults and ends in one dictated account that returns to the schoolyard question and claims no parent; day 26 children use it, on paper, to sort the new families; day 27 they revise their own day 26 record against it. Retained for later: the final account (unit 5 day 38, unit 7 day 56, day 36 critique). Unit 2's parts chart supports the words for leaf, stem, ears and fur; the design cycle and the shape-and-job model are not used here and are kept.
- **Key moment, days 22, 25 and 26: leaf ordering.** Ideal instrument: real leaves or life-size blades laid beside each other with the bases on one line. Spec: fits; the paper blades are life-size, the gap between any two blades in an ordered set is at least 15 mm, so tips differ by eye, and no unit is read. Where two real pressed leaves are nearly equal at the hook, call it "about the same" (kindergarten) and do not force an order. Blades are cut by an adult beforehand; children do not use scissors.
- **Equipment, counted.** Teaching sets are one packet for each group of five (six packets) on days 22 and 25; pairs on days 23 and 24 are 15, so 15 printed copies; every child has their own paper record. Exit and check blades are one set for each child (30 sets of three) because each child orders their own. The conference table on day 26 serves six groups in 3 minutes each (30 seconds a child, 30 seconds to change).
- **A room with nothing.** The packets, blades and record sheets print. With no pressed leaves or seedling photograph, day 22's hook opens on the M22 cards alone and the question still goes on the board. Children with a plant-dust allergy see only the photograph.

## 6. Days

## Day 22


- **3 min. Hook: unit 2's pressed leaves and seedling photograph.** The work handed forward from unit 2 day 12. The teacher alone holds the dated pressed leaves from the big tree and the other trees of its kind beside the dated seedling photograph; children look, no one touches. Ask the compelling question before anyone offers a claim, hear three guesses, and say aloud: "Under the tree is not the same as from the tree." The question goes on the board; the schoolyard stays open.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Kindergarten's old nest picture, invented for practice: "What changed, and what did you see that shows it?" (claim and evidence, review). Then point to leaf and stem on the parts chart. A miss: the teacher models one "I saw ___, so ___" and the child tries again in the investigation.
- **3 min. Teacher model.** M22, invented for practice: the maple parent MP, young MY1 and MY2, another adult MA and the pine. The teacher lays two blades with bases on a tape line, points from one feature to a claim ("both have five pointed lobes, so they are alike") and reads the caption that shows the seed path.
- **7 min. Groups: line up and order.** Six groups of five, one packet each. Bases on the tape, tips show shorter and longer; each child places one blade in turn. The teacher hears a rotating six, one child from each group, about a minute each.
- **3 min. Records.** Each child circles one shared feature and marks one difference on their record; labels are dictated or copied from the chart.
- **3 min. A different kind.** The pine's needles against the maple's lobes: what feature tells the kind apart? Class answers aloud.
- **5 min. Start the comparison model.** The class dictates "alike in ___; different in ___" for MP, MY1 and MY2, and the pine goes beside as a different kind.
- **5 min. Exit.** E22a and E22b, each child with three loose blades; a rotating six heard.
- **3 min. Hand washing and clean-up.** Plant parts were near; two sinks, 15 children each at about 12 seconds, with fragrance-free wipes at tables if there is one sink.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice lays blades in length order and links lobes on a maple family; the exit names a feature on a new oval-leaf family and orders three blades where a small adult is shortest, so length alone cannot pick the parent.

Held by: teacher (pressed leaves, model); groups (packets); children (own record). Tool: None. By hand: blades, tape, record. Evidence: E22a, E22b. Hand-backs out: none.

## Day 23


- **4 min. Open.** Read the board question aloud: does another family show the pattern? Retrieve the parts chart (ears, fur). The schoolyard trees stay unanswered.
- **4 min. Teacher model.** D23, invented for practice: dog parent, two puppies, another adult and a fish. One link and one difference spoken as "alike in ___; different in ___".
- **7 min. Pairs: compare.** Fifteen pairs, one printed D23 copy each, link lines for shared features and a ring for each difference.
- **4 min. Share.** The teacher hears six pairs, about 40 seconds each, and asks which observation supports the claim; partners may challenge.
- **5 min. Revise the model.** Dogs go beside the maples, plant evidence kept in view. Does the dog family agree with the maples?
- **3 min. Exit.** E23a and E23b, rotating six heard.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Packets into envelopes, pencils in cups, tables wiped from the table tub.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice links and rings the features a dog parent shares with its puppy; the exit asks the child to use shared features to pick a horse's foal from two young, then judge Pip's claim that the foal is an exact copy.

Held by: pairs (copy); teacher (chart). Tool: None. By hand: link lines, rings. Evidence: E23a, E23b. Hand-backs out: A to day 24.

## Day 24


- **4 min. Open.** Read a partner's earlier dog claim aloud and the board question: can all of one kind look the same? The schoolyard stays open.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: A from day 23. Lay the wrong and right sentences beside the dog packet; each child marks one same and one not-the-same.
- **10 min. Pairs: the same routine on cats.** C24, invented for practice: cat parent, two kittens, another adult and the dog as contrast. Nothing new is taught; children replace vague labels ("looks the same") with a named feature. The teacher hears six pairs, about a minute each. No speed target.
- **5 min. Revise the model.** Cats go on the chart as a second case against exact copying; "does that difference erase the similarities?" put as "is it still a cat?".
- **3 min. Exit.** E24a and E24b, rotating six heard.
- **3 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice repeats the link-and-ring routine on a cat family; the exit asks which of three young could be a sheep's lamb, one with a patch the parent lacks, and which card is the evidence that it is still a sheep.

Held by: pairs; teacher (chart). Tool: None. By hand: link lines, rings, labels. Evidence: E24a, E24b. Hand-backs in: A. Out: B to day 25.

## Day 25


- **3 min. Open.** "Are all young the same?" The teacher lines up two blades on tape for the class (leaf alignment, review) and the class says which tip is longer.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: B from day 24. Two cats with different patches side by side; children point to what stays the same.
- **7 min. Groups: one kind each.** Six groups, two for maple, two for dog, two for cat, each with a V25 packet that now shows both young, the other adults and a contrasting kind. Compare young with young and adults with adults; maple groups line up the blades on tape.
- **5 min. Report.** Each group tells one same and one different, about 40 seconds each. "Which evidence changed our model?"
- **5 min. Revise: one shared account.** The class dictates; the teacher scribes. It joins plants and animals, names features alike and features different, and returns to the schoolyard question without claiming a parent for the seedlings.
- **3 min. Exit.** E25a and E25b, rotating six heard.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice compares one kind in groups and reports one same and one different; the exit gives two groupings of pig and willow families and asks which puts the same kind together, and why they belong though they look different.

Held by: groups (packets); teacher (chart, account). Tool: None. By hand: link lines, rings, leaf blades on tape. Evidence: E25a and E25b; the dictated account. Hand-backs in: B. Out: B and C to day 27.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 22, "What did you observe?"; day 24, "Is it still a cat if one thing is different?"; day 25, "Which evidence changed our model?"
- Written task, day 25: the class dictates one shared account of plants and animals, the teacher scribes (W.1.8 bridge).
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: it names one feature that is alike and one that is different for a plant and an animal; it links them to the claim "alike but not exactly"; it returns to the schoolyard seedlings without saying where they came from.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: parent, young, alike, different, kind, feature, shorter, longer, ears, fur, paw, leaf. Frames: "Alike in ___; different in ___." "They are alike because ___, but ___ is different." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Large high-contrast prints, raised blade outlines, a neutral adult who describes visual features without giving the comparison; pointing, dictation, and a picture instead of a word are accepted.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; children mark pictures on paper; a child may answer by pointing or by dictation.
- **Reading load.** None. The student page is boxes, pictures and short labels, under 100 words; the teacher reads all prompts.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second visible feature, such as tail length, and test whether it supports the same claim.
- **Sensitivity.** Only plants and animals are compared. No child is asked about their own family or how they look like anyone; if a child shares, thank them and return to the picture. No child is the example of a mistake: wrong examples are read as invented sentences.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 22: line up three paper leaves on a tape and say what is alike and what is not. Day 23: do the same with the dog packet. Day 24: repeat with the cat packet. Day 25: choose which grouping is better and say why. Day 26: order the new leaves and explain the oak and the rabbit pictures aloud while the adult writes the words down. Day 27, the last day of the unit: hear the picture comment, revise, make a fresh account; the adult writes the child's words and does not correct.
- **One projector.** Show one picture at a time, large; each child still marks their own paper. Day 26's turns run in order at one table.
