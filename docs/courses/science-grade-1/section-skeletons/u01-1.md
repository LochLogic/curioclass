## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **1-ESS1-1** (days 1, 2, 3, 4): Watch the sun, moon and stars and describe the sky patterns they show that can be predicted.
- skill s1-sky-patterns (grade 1)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Closing explanation, day 6.** Children dictate that changing apparent sun position accompanies changing shadow direction, using repeated fixed-stick marks. Days 2 and 3 teach that sunlight comes from the side opposite the shadow. Moon sequences show another apparent path; clear-sky records distinguish daytime and nighttime star visibility.
> - **Teaching moves.** Model dating one mark on day 1. Children choose landmarks and record day 2 times; adults read clocks. Its three two-minute looks and 24-minute lesson total 30 minutes. Later, ask which observation supports each prediction rather than supply it.
>
> - **Question board.** Group notices as shadow changes, sky paths and visibility. Where will the next shadow fall (day 2)? Does it repeat (day 3)? Does the moon follow a path too (day 4)? What else shows at night (day 5)? Will these patterns hold elsewhere (raised day 5, answered day 6)? Day 7 transfers to an unfamiliar hillside observation station.
> - **Carried: patterns.** Day 1 draws the stick and first shadow. Day 2 adds ordered marks from the fixed stick; day 3 adds repeated evidence. Day 4 adds the moon's apparent path. Day 5 revises visibility with daytime moon evidence. Day 6 reaches consensus from the other-site records: repeated observations support predictions. Keep this chart available on day 7.

> - **Tiers and cost.** Household pencil and clay serve shadows, days 1 and 2; naked-eye moon viewing serves day 4. Dated paper observations replace either and supply stars, days 5 and 6; this no-kit route is weaker because children cannot collect the original evidence. No simulation is required. About $2 a class, within the $40 yearly allowance.
> - **Days 1 and 2, per five children:** one pencil, clay base, paper, chalk and five drawing pencils. The adult secures the blunt pencil upright and marks the base location. Never look at the sun; keep eyes on ground shadows. No optics. Keep clay and chalk from mouths; avoid chalk dust and check ingredient allergies. Wash hands. No goggles are needed for stationary observation; omit dusty chalk and use paper if dust rises.
> - **Day 4, per five:** one timed moon strip, five logs and pencils. The adult selects a view with the sun screened by a building; otherwise use records. No binoculars or telescopes. Stay within the supervised accessible route. No protective eyewear is needed for this screened view.
> - **The day before each outdoor day:** check weather, route, allergies and sightlines; prepare indoor records. Stop for unsafe weather, glare or an obstructed route. Reserve three minutes for collection and handwashing on days 1 and 2. Days 3 and 5 to 8 use paper only.
