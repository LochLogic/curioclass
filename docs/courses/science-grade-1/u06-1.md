# Section 6.1: Can our message reach a friend?

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-PS4-4, this section's share.** "Build a device of your own design that sends a message far away by light or by sound." This section carries "of your own design" (a sketch with a shape-to-job arrow, day 49), "build" (each child makes one device, day 49) and "sends a message far away" (a marked near and farther distance, measured on day 50 and tried on day 51). Section 6.2 carries the repeat evidence, the improved design and the observed test.
- **Limits that bite here.** Farther means a safe marked classroom distance, five strips in teaching (the exit path is a different count). No formal codes: two agreed practice messages, each with one pattern and a picture. No phone or radio workings. Lights are aimed at low cards, never at eyes; patterns never copy an emergency signal. No ranking of designs.
- **Connections.** 1.MD.A.2, day 50: identical strips laid end to end, no gaps or overlaps, the count is the length (taught here as a bridge; math reaches it later).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A signal is not a message: the signal reaches the receiver, but only an agreed meaning lets the receiver read it. In a cup-and-string pair the shake travels along a gently tight string (review: vibration makes sound; no wave account). Strips must touch end to end or the count does not tell the length. A fair distance test changes only the distance: the same message, signal and string tightness at both marks.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none (item 1 is owned by section 6.2, day 54). Every case below is invented for practice, said aloud, and held back from the teaching on days 48 to 51.

**Evidence form.** Spoken, drawn, pointed to or sorted. Each exit is asked aloud, one thing at a time, answered by touching a picture card. A rotating six is heard, 20 seconds each, with a "heard" column. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Nothing in this section is scored. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's repair (field 4).

**Exit questions.**
- **E48a (day 48).** Three new pictures: a girl at a garden gate with a friend across the lawn taps a flowerpot softly with a spoon; she rings a small hand bell held low at her side; she shines a flashlight at the friend's face. "Touch a safe way to send a signal that is not shouting." Answer: the flowerpot tap or the hand bell. Wrong: the flashlight at the face (no letter; a safety gap, said aloud, and the light-never-at-eyes rule is repeated).
- **E48b (day 48).** The friend at the gate marks the "wait" picture; the sender's hidden card said "line up". "Did the friend understand the message?" Every child shows thumb up for yes or thumb down for no at once and is marked. Answer: no. Wrong: yes, the friend got the signal (A).
- **E48c (day 48).** The same garden gate card. "The girl wants her friend to line up. Say the problem she needs to solve." The rotating six are heard, 20 seconds each. Answer: send a message across the lawn without shouting, so the friend understands it. Wrong: shout louder, or a device named first (no letter).
- **E49a (day 49).** A paper plan of a drum beside a drum made from a small box. "Touch the one that can send a message now." Answer: the made drum. Wrong: the plan (B).
- **E49b (day 49).** Two pictured pairs of yoghurt pots joined by string: one with the string lying in loops, one with the string straight and gentle. "Touch the pair where the string is ready to carry a message." Answer: the straight string. Wrong: the loops (no letter; slack string, said aloud).
- **E50a (day 50).** Three pictured rows of identical wooden blocks along a new path: one with a gap, one with a block partly on top of its neighbor, one with the blocks touching in a line. "Touch the row that tells how long the path is." Answer: the touching row. Wrong: the gap row (E); the row with a block on top (no letter).
- **E50b (day 50).** A different path of five identical crayons, touching end to end. "Count the crayons out loud. How many crayons long is the path?" Every child counts in a whisper and shows the number on fingers. Answer: 5 crayons. Wrong: a crayon missed or counted twice (no letter).
- **E50c (day 50).** Sam will send wait with his cup pair at the near mark, then line up at the farther mark. "Two things change: the distance and the message. Which one should Sam keep the same?" The rotating six are heard. Answer: the message, so only the distance changes. Wrong: keep the distance (no letter).
- **E51a (day 51).** Two pictured trial pairs: in one the sender uses the same card and the same signal at the near mark and at the farther mark; in the other the signal changes between marks. "Touch the pair that shows whether distance changes what the friend gets." Answer: the same-signal pair. Wrong: the changed-signal pair (D).
- **E51b (day 51).** Pip's device worked at the near mark. Three pictures: try the same device and signal at the farther mark; say it works anywhere and stop; change the signal and try it far away. "Touch what Pip should do to find out about the farther mark." Answer: the first. Wrong: stop and say anywhere (C); change the signal (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


**Carried.** The system model (unit 2, review): sender, device and receiver drawn as parts that need each other on day 48; shapes and jobs added to it on day 49 (cup shape, string, flap, drum top); causal arrows ("I did ___, so the friend ___") revised on day 51 from the receiver's marks. The design-cycle chart (unit 2: problem, sketch, build, test) is retrieved on day 48, used for sketch and build on day 49, and for the test on day 51. Unit 4's sound account and unit 5's light account are review.

**Key moments and instruments.**
- Distance (days 50 and 51). Ideal: a tape measure. Spec judged against it: identical paper strips fit grade 1; the step is one whole strip (no half strips), and the strips are used because this grade counts units, not tape marks. No digital tool is requested.
- The receiver's reading (days 48 and 51). Ideal: the receiver's own mark on a picture card, made before the sender's card is shown. It fits, with no sensor needed.
- Tautness (day 51). Ideal: a tension gauge. Fits partly: the child feels "gentle and straight" and the chart records tight or not; a string that is not straight is recorded, not hidden.

**A room with nothing.** Paper cups and string are the minimum; a box drum is the no-kit sound route; where lights are scarce the group shares one. If a reading fails (a quiet drum is not heard at the farther mark), the receiver marks "could not tell" and the result stays on the chart; one repeat is made and both are kept. If no live lane is possible (weather, noise), the teacher gives invented trial cards, says they are invented, and the day practices the chart only.

## 6. Days

## Day 48


- **4 min. Retrieval.** From the old ramp and tower pictures: "Mark the problem and the test that showed whether the design worked. Point to a sketch feature and tell its job." Then mark the sound-making part on the old drum picture and the light source on the old box record. A miss: replay the earlier picture comparison and model one problem, sketch, test link. Gaps are recorded, not scored.
- **6 min. Hook.** The recess bell is recalled. From a near marker the teacher taps four slow taps on a box drum; the class sits at a second marker. "Who heard it? Who knows what it said?" Some noticed the sound and did not know its meaning. Pip asks the compelling question aloud. Drum at classroom level, never near ears.
- **6 min. Sender, signal, receiver.** Teaching, with a picture of three boxes: the sender, the device, the receiver. Words: message (what the sender wants the friend to know), signal (the sound or light that goes across), send, receive. The class agrees two practice messages, "line up" and "wait", each with a picture and one pattern (two quick taps or blinks; one slow tap or long blink). No emergency patterns.
- **7 min. Send and mark.** The teacher sends the agreed sound ("line up") from the near marker; every child marks a picture on their own page before the sender's card is shown. The same with the agreed light, aimed down at a low card. Then the teacher sends one pattern nobody agreed: children mark a guess, and the card shows many differ. A rotating six say what they marked.
- **4 min. The problem.** On a paper with a sender marker and a distant marker the class says the problem and the success test: the friend marks the same picture as the sender's card at the marked distance. The question board opens with three headings: meaning, reach, dependable.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** The teacher collects drum, flashlight and markers.
- **3 min. Exit.** E48a, E48b, E48c.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice taps and blinks agreed messages while the class marks them; the exit asks which new garden-gate signal is safe, whether a friend whose mark differs understood, and what problem the girl must solve, none of which the sends ask.

Held by: teacher (drum, flashlight, board, cards); class (own marking page); Pip the puppet (one line at a time). Tool: None. By hand: the marking page (about 30 printed words). Evidence: E48a, E48b, E48c. Hand-backs in: none. Out: A to day 49.

## Day 49


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 48 exit, error A. The sender's card beside the friend's mark in a pictured case (invented for practice); each child touches "same" or "not the same".
- **6 min. Three kinds.** The teacher and an adult show, at a short distance, a cup-and-string pair (string gently straight, then lying slack), a flashlight blinked behind a cardboard flap, and a box drum tapped. For each: the part and its job (cup catches the shake, string carries it, flap makes a short light, drum top shakes), as review of shape and job. Light low, drum at classroom level.
- **4 min. Sketch.** On the paper sketch each child draws the device they will make, the message it sends, and one arrow from a shape to its job.
- **13 min. Make.** Groups of five. Each child makes one device from their sketch: a cup pair with the string ends taped inside (an adult helps with knots), a flap for the shared flashlight, or a drum top on a small box. Two blunt scissors for the group, used seated and counted; only flap makers cut, from pre-cut cardboard. A child who has only a plan keeps building. Cups are pre-punched by an adult.
- **3 min. Share.** A rotating six hold up their device and tell one part and its job; the record sheet names who.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Scissors counted; adults collect loose string; each device is named and stored.
- **3 min. Exit.** E49a, E49b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sketches and makes one device and names its parts; the exit shows three new devices, one unfinished and one with a flap taped flat, and asks which could send now and why, a check the making never asks.

Held by: teacher (kinds shown, hand-backs, record sheet); groups (supplies); children (own sketch and device). Tool: None. By hand: the sketch page (about 25 printed words). Evidence: E49a, E49b. Hand-backs in: A. Out: B to day 50.

## Day 50


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 49 exit and the build, error B. A plan beside a made device; each child with only a plan finishes the missing part with help.
- **4 min. Strips.** The teacher lays three identical paper strips along a taped line, once with a gap and once touching end to end. "Which tells how long the path is?" Taught: strips touch end to end, no gaps or overlaps, the count is the length. No tape measure.
- **10 min. Measure.** Groups of five, six strips each. The group lays its strips along a taped line to a farther mark of five strips (each child lays one), counts them aloud, then two children lay the near mark at two strips. Cup devices have their string cut by an adult to reach the farther mark with room to spare.
- **8 min. Plan the trial.** Partners fill a plan card: the device, the message picture (one of the two), keep (message, signal, string tight), change (distance only), watch (the friend's mark).
- **2 min. Share.** A rotating six say "I will keep ___, change ___, watch ___".
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Strips collected, markers taped down.
- **4 min. Exit.** E50a, E50b, E50c.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice lays strips touching and fills the class plan card; the exit reads new block rows with a gap and an overlap, counts a crayon path, and fixes Sam's plan that changes two things, a flaw the practice never shows.

Held by: teacher (the demonstration, hand-backs); groups (strips); children (plan card). Tool: None. By hand: the plan card (about 40 printed words). Evidence: E50a, E50b, E50c. Hand-backs in: B. Out: E to day 51.

## Day 51


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 50 exit, error E. Children lay six strips along a taped line, touching, and recount.
- **3 min. Set-up.** The one closed lane is read aloud: sender line, near mark (two strips), farther mark (five strips), receivers at the marks, a walking route around the string. Each child draws a hidden message card.
- **18 min. Six group turns, 3 minutes each.** One group at a time in the lane under the teacher's eye; each child sends the same card once at the near mark and once at the farther mark, and the receiver marks a picture before the card is shown. Five children, ten sends, about 15 seconds each, plus changeover. Cup senders wind spare string on a card so it stays gently straight; the chart notes tight or not. Waiting groups at their desks sort invented trial cards into "understood" and "not understood" and draw their guess for each mark.
- **4 min. Chart.** Each child writes the receiver's marks on the paper results chart and draws two arrows: "I did ___, so the friend ___" for near and for farther.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Devices stored; string collected.
- **3 min. Exit.** E51a, E51b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sends hidden messages at two marks and charts them; the exit gives two new pictured trial pairs and Pip's stop at the near mark, so the child picks the fair pair and the next step instead of running a trial.

Held by: teacher (lane, hand-backs, record of who sent); groups (their turn); children (chart). Tool: None. By hand: the results chart (about 45 printed words). Evidence: E51a, E51b and the chart, kept for day 52. Hand-backs in: E. Out: C and D to day 52.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 48: "What did the friend need besides hearing a sound?" Day 49: "Which part of your device does which job?" Day 51: "What did you keep the same and what did you change?"
- **Written task, day 51.** The results chart and two arrows, each with a sentence the teacher scribes: "I sent ___ at ___ strips; the friend marked ___."
- **Look-fors.** The message and signal are the same at both marks; the friend's mark is written as marked, not as the sender's card; each arrow names what the child did and what the friend got.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: message, signal, send, receive, near, farther, strip. Frames: "I sent ___." "The friend marked ___." "The same / not the same." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** Offer either medium. A child who is deaf or hard of hearing uses light or the string, where the shake can be felt; a child sensitive to light or noise sits farther back or marks as receiver. No one is asked to disclose why.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; accept pointing, large pictures, dictation and adapted grips.
- **Reading load.** None. Each student page stays under 100 words (labels and boxes); all else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child sketches a second device for the same message by the other medium and says which part does which job.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 48: tap a spoon on a box from across the room and ask what it said; agree two messages and their patterns, then send and mark. Day 49: make a cup pair with string, or a cardboard flap for a flashlight. Day 50: lay equal paper strips end to end and count; plan a near and a farther try. Day 51: the child sends and the adult marks a picture; the adult writes the child's words.
- **One projector.** Show the pictured cases large, one at a time; every child marks their own page. Day 51's turns run in order in one lane.
