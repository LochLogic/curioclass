# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 1

Course `science-grade-1`, grade 1. Draft, 2026-11-14.

The frame's eight units retain all 62 unit days and their owned rows. Four course-level assessment days and two course-level respond days bring the total to 68. The chronological days, section groupings, goals, standards, materials and safety controls live in [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-1/days.csv). Section groupings here reserve manageable runs of days, not section plans. No unit exceeds 25 days, so none needs a mid-unit assessment.

The school-day placements follow the common 29-week calendar, with one change inside its envelope: day 9, owned by unit 8, falls immediately after unit 1 on school day 19. Later unit days shift one course-day number until the spring block, where the fall meeting replaces one spring meeting. The check weeks remain 15 and 29. The frame's reference to 34 science weeks is superseded for placement by CALENDAR.md and frames.csv, which say 29. This leaves a meeting-frequency conflict for the lead below.

All assumed science rows were used in kindergarten or earlier this year. They receive short retrieval tasks after each hook, not readiness checks. There are no assumed rows last used two or more grades back, so there are no readiness slots. Unit 8's spring return retrieves its fall work. The observation windows below reserve an individual talk, drawing, account or built performance on an unfamiliar case. Every task addresses every part of each code it lists, with a short prompt or observed performance for each part. Unit plans will write the tasks and scoring guidance. Response time is counted once at the location named for each unit.

Science words are spoken, pictured and read aloud. Children may point, draw or dictate; sentence frames begin in unit 5. No assessment depends on independent decoding, family participation or access to a device at home. Each unit starts a question board and initial chart or model; later evidence changes it before students use the revised account in their individual task. Models stay descriptive and causal at this grade, without adding mechanisms reserved for later grades.

## Unit 1. Sun, moon and stars

Eight days, course days 1 to 8, school days 3 to 18, weeks 1 to 4. Sections 1.1, days 1 to 4, and 1.2, days 5 to 8. The shadow stick opens a question about a repeatable change. Children first record and compare shadows from a fixed position, then use timed moon records to look for another apparent path, and finally compare day and night star records. Rehearsing predictions across all three records lets the class revise its first shadow account using evidence. No rotation or orbit explanation is introduced.

Day 1 retrieves sk-weather-watch. W.1.8 is taught in ELA unit 2, school days 34 to 75, later than this unit. Days 1 and 2 therefore use the frame's bridge: children answer from shadow marks aloud and the teacher charts their words. Three shadow looks across the school day do not become three science meetings. ELA's sky work in weeks 7 to 15 can use this completed foundation.

Day 7 is the observation task on unfamiliar sun and moon sequences and star records. It covers both apparent paths, predictions from those paths, and stars other than the sun seen at night rather than day, using the pattern chart as evidence. Day 8 holds the response and a fresh prediction. There is no readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate kept: daytime moon evidence uses about 10 minutes of day 4, within the existing moon-record day, with no added day. A class photograph replaces an unavailable safe daytime sighting. Home logs remain optional and ungraded. The next meeting, day 9, is unit 8's opener, described below and counted only there.

## Unit 2. Parts that help

Twelve days, course days 10 to 21, school days 20 to 43, weeks 4 to 9. Section 2.1, days 10 to 15, develops parts, needs and responses; section 2.2, days 16 to 21, uses that evidence in design. The tree beside the tipping tower raises the need to understand what parts do. Plant observations include roots, stems, leaves, flowers and fruits, and dated evidence of a shoot responding to light. Animal observations include sensing and responding as well as movement, taking in food and water, breathing and protection. Parts work together as a system. These observations support the introduced structure-function model rather than serving as an unrelated identification exercise.

Only then do children consider all four kinds of copied solution: protection, staying upright, keeping out and detecting. A root-like tower support becomes a planned sketch, a build, a controlled gentle-airflow test and a revision. Groups then choose a second design job. The larger day allocation carries both the life-science idea and the engineering performance, rather than splitting time equally between two vocabulary lists. Revisions occur in the part-and-job chart and again in the sketch after evidence and partner explanation.

Day 10 retrieves sk-living-needs, sk-needs-places and sk-define-problem. RI.1.2 comes in ELA unit 3, school days 79 to 119, so days 10 and 13 use single-part, single-job read-aloud pages with the teacher naming the topic and children finding the detail. The design sketch begins on day 16, school day 32, just before ELA unit 2 begins teaching SL.1.5 on school day 34; day 16 therefore carries the drawing-and-dictated-label bridge. Day 12 gathers and presses known leaves for unit 3, with photographs of a parent plant and its seed-grown young if parentage cannot be established.

Days 20 and 21 are one individual whole-task observation window. Each child addresses both codes in full: plant and animal parts and responses, including the plant response record; a new human problem solved by copying an outside part; and a sketch and small model that explain how shape helps the solution work. Children complete the whole task over the window, rather than being assigned one code per day. The final 10 minutes of day 21 hold response and revision, counted once. There is no readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate dropped: a live pill bug or snail adds care, permission and handling demands without necessary new evidence. The existing picture-based second animal response on day 14 keeps the scientific comparison at no extra day cost. General human problems replace personal disability contexts; no wild-animal handling or plant tasting.

## Unit 3. Like their parents

Six days, course days 22 to 27, school days 45 to 56, weeks 9 to 12. Section 3.1. The known parent tree and seedlings use the parts vocabulary just built. Children compare leaf shapes and directly order lengths, then compare an animal parent and young, check another animal case and revise a claim about resemblance with variation. Observations of other individuals of the same kind keep the conclusion from becoming a claim that all young are identical. This is the first sustained evidence account of the year, before unit 5 writes one.

Day 22 retrieves sk-change-environment's claim-and-evidence habit and unit 2's part names. Math's length unit is school days 111 to 128, weeks 23 to 26, much later. Day 22 uses the frame's direct-ordering bridge, leaves side by side on a tape strip, with no measurement units. The individual task retains that access path.

Day 26 observes an evidence account from both a fresh parent-plant pair and a fresh parent-animal pair. Shared features, non-identical features and variation within a kind are all required. Day 27 holds response and a fresh account. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed.

Candidate dropped: growing a second bean parent-young record creates a delayed growing requirement that duplicates the required verified tree or seed-grown-young photograph comparison. No day is added. All days use plants and animals only, never children's bodies, baby photographs or family resemblance. No inheritance mechanism, hybrids or metamorphosis.

## Unit 4. Sound

Seven days, course days 28 to 34, school days 58 to 71, weeks 12 to 15. Section 4.1, days 28 to 31, develops vibrating materials making sound; section 4.2, days 32 to 34, develops sound making materials vibrate and checks both together. The blurry shoebox-guitar band starts the causal question. Students plan what to do and watch for, compare another vibrating source, and stop vibration to test their account. That raises the reverse question: can sound make something move? Paper and sealed-rice tests supply two cases of the less obvious direction. The model changes when the reverse evidence arrives.

Day 28 retrieves sk-push-pull. ELA unit 2 is concurrently developing W.1.8, so a drawing and dictated sentence support the investigation record. Day 33 observes each child's simple peer investigation plan and causal evidence for both directions on unfamiliar cases. Day 34 holds response and a fresh case. No readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate kept in full: the box drum is the second sound source on day 30, costing that existing repeated-evidence day; the stopping test occupies the investigation on day 31. Both costs are inside the seven days, not additions. A phone is unnecessary. Keep sounds at classroom level and away from ears, bands away from faces with eye protection for flying risk, and use an actual motion record if available sound equipment cannot move samples safely. No wave model or speed of sound.

## Unit 5. Light

Ten days, course days 38 to 47, school days 79 to 99, weeks 16 to 20, after the mid-year check and response. Section 5.1, days 38 to 42, develops seeing; section 5.2, days 43 to 47, develops material effects and checks both expectations. The closed-flap toy box gives a reason to compare the box, a gently darkened room and cave media. Children distinguish an object receiving light from one making its own light and revise a supported evidence account. The next question asks what changes when a material interrupts the beam. Peers choose samples, keep the light position steady and record the four material kinds.

Days 38 and 43 retrieve sk-sun-warms, sk-change-environment, sk-push-pull and sk-shade-design as their ideas become useful. W.1.2 was taught in ELA unit 1, school days 1 to 33; day 40 still supplies the frame's shared sentence support for the science account. Math unit 1, school days 1 to 16, has already taught three-category data. Day 45 reuses it to compare transmission groups, all, some and none. Mirror redirection is a separate yes-or-no question, not a fourth transmission group. The class causal chart is revised after both the seeing and material evidence.

Day 46 observes an unfamiliar seeing account and a planned material test covering both codes in full: illuminated and self-luminous objects, observational evidence, clear and partial transmission, blocking with shadow, and redirection by a mirror. Day 47 holds response. No readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate dropped: deliberately varying shadow length is a later-grade bridge with no necessary role in these ten days. Blocking light and producing a shadow remain required on days 43 and 46. No added day. Offer control of the flashlight or a seat by the lit exit for children who fear darkness; plastic or edge-taped mirrors only, no lasers, no sun or eye viewing. No eye-entry or reflection mechanism and no speed of light.

## Unit 6. Sending a message

Eight days, course days 48 to 55, school days 101 to 116, weeks 21 to 24. Section 6.1, days 48 to 51, defines, sketches, makes and first tests; section 6.2, days 52 to 55, repeats, improves and observes. The familiar recess signal creates the design question. Children meet cup-and-string, blink and beat examples, choose a device, measure the route and test whether a receiver gets the intended message nearby and farther away. Repeated trials precede improvement so revisions follow evidence. The sketch explains a working system, reusing unit 2 rather than teaching it anew.

Day 48 retrieves sk-define-problem, sk-ramp-design and the year's sketch, light and sound work. Measurement on day 50 is school day 105, week 21, before math unit 6 begins on school day 111. The no-gaps bridge is therefore required, not optional: identical paper strips end to end, counted together, no gaps or overlaps. Keep the number of units within secure counting range.

Day 54 observes an individual product for a fresh message problem: design, make, send and receive across a tested distance. The product and trial, not a picture alone, stand for 1-PS4-4. Day 55 holds response and a fresh trial, and preserves the product evidence. No readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate dropped: comparing two groups' devices at the same distance starts grade 2's owned comparison without helping each child's required design. Use day 52 for repeated trials of the same device instead. No added day. Strings stay low with clear walking routes, cup holes are teacher-made, light never enters eyes, sounds stay at classroom level, and practice patterns cannot be confused with real emergency signals. No internal workings of commercial communication devices.

## Unit 7. Parents and young

Six days, course days 56 to 61, school days 118 to 128, weeks 24 to 26. Section 7.1. The robin's cheeping and the adult's arrival start a question about survival. Read-aloud captioned pages establish possible patterns; media of robin and deer provide a second source for each studied case. Children compare the sources, then revise a signal-response-survival model. Both young and adult behaviours must do explanatory work, rather than becoming a list of animal names. Feeding, comforting, carrying and protecting are examples across the sources; many animals, not all, care for young.

Day 56 retrieves sk-forecast-severe's information-seeking habit and unit 2's sensing and response. RI.1.1 was taught in ELA unit 1, so the page-based modelled question is access support rather than a timing bridge. Day 60 observes an unfamiliar animal case using text and media, evidence of both behaviours, comparison between sources and an account of how the repeated pattern helps survival. Day 61 holds response and a fresh case. No readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate dropped: a local nest cannot be guaranteed, and the shared dated media already supplies comparable evidence without disturbance. No day is added. Animal cases only, never children's family arrangements. Unit planning must carry the frame's sensitivity-read preparation from adoptive, foster and separated families' own published voices. No family-tree or baby-photo assignment.

## Unit 8. Daylight and the seasons

Five days: day 9 on school day 19, week 4, then days 62 to 65 on school days 133 to 139, weeks 27 to 28. Section 8.1 holds the split record-to-explanation arc. Day 9 is adjacent to unit 1 in the teaching order and is the first day of this unit only. It opens the fixed-time light-or-dark question and starts the chart beside a locally prepared light-and-dark day strip. Monthly two-minute routines outside the 68 meetings keep dated records through fall, winter and spring. Optional family reports at about 5:30 pm are never graded; a teacher-prepared local public sunrise-and-sunset day strip supplies evidence when reports are absent. Adults select dates and clock times. Children compare the shaded lengths without reading times or counting daylight hours.

The spring return retrieves the chart habit and day 9's record, organizes seasons, and compares relative light-strip lengths. A fixed-time report alone cannot show the length of the whole day; the strips are essential evidence. Math's three-category work was taught in weeks 1 to 4. Children revise the claim using winter against fall or spring, applying local season words without changing the unit order. Social studies unit 2 begins school day 18, week 4: its calendar can receive this record from school day 19 and revisit it all year. This course requires no social studies prerequisite.

Day 64 observes seasonal records not rehearsed on the class chart: observations across times of year, relative daylight related to season, and winter compared with fall or spring. Day 65 holds response and a fresh comparison. No readiness or mid-unit assessment.

Candidate kept: a same-clock-time window photograph is a second record during the existing monthly routine, about one minute of that routine, with no extra course day. A teacher or adult takes a safe frame excluding the sun; dated paper copies support comparison. If the school is closed at that clock time, omit the photograph that month and use the prepared strip. It supports, but never replaces, the full-day strip. No child supplies photographs or describes an evening routine. Across the eight Candidate cells, three are kept, including both additions in unit 4's cell, and five are dropped.

No separate course-level review days are specified by the frame, so none are added. In the grade 1 calendar, science weeks 15 and 29 are distinct from social studies weeks 14 and 31, math weeks 19 and 30, and ELA weeks 16 and 32. ## Materials, access and source decisions

There is no base text, so no source-map.csv is required or written. All planned text and tasks are Curio Class's own. Unit plans must select credited open or public-domain media for the specified cases; a picture-sequence printout can carry the same recorded evidence as a video. No paid book, account or family purchase is required. No digital or drawn interactive tool is assumed, so the tools column is empty; paper records are supplies. Safety cells name controls whenever supplies are named, with `none` only for weighed low-risk paper work.

The frame's approved annual allowance is about $40 per class beyond existing classroom materials. Planning allocations within that cap are $2 for unit 1, $10 for unit 2, $1 for unit 3, $8 for unit 4, $8 for unit 5, $8 for unit 6, $1 for unit 7 and $2 for unit 8. These are allocation limits, not researched prices; shared flashlights, eye protection and a teacher-operated speaker are inventoried before purchase, and unit plans give their actual costs. Household stand-ins include a pencil in clay, paper and cardboard structures, a box drum, paper cups, foil and identical paper measuring strips. The teacher pre-punches holes and gathers identified leaves. No specialised kit is assumed.

When direct observation is unavailable, days 1 to 8 use dated sky records; unit 2 uses part-and-response sequences and tower-test records; unit 3 uses verified parent and seed-grown-young photographs; unit 4 uses actual motion and sound test records; unit 5 uses actual lit/unlit and four-material test records; unit 6 uses receiver-trial records; unit 7 already uses read-aloud and media sources; unit 8 uses local day strips. The teacher identifies these as indirect evidence and therefore a weaker opportunity than first-hand investigation. Students still propose a test, read evidence and revise an account. A no-kit record alone cannot demonstrate the owned making performances in units 2 and 6: paper, cardboard, tape and a box drum or paper-cup device remain the minimum household construction route. All children make a model and a working message device, even if the distance evidence also uses recorded trials.

Safety and sensitivity reviews remain at unit planning as the frame requires. Optional home sky records, animal-only family examples, no personal disability context, darkness choices, non-emergency signals, and safe sun observation are carried into the relevant day notes. No course-finished or external-review claim is made here.

## Shorter path

For a 25-minute period, prepare and distribute records or materials beforehand. Keep about 3 minutes for the question and retrieval, 12 for one evidence cycle, 7 for the account or model revision and 3 for recording and cleanup. Stage the original three shadow marks as brief looks outside the period, and keep monthly daylight looks to two minutes. Replace a second live demonstration with its actual recorded results when needed, while students still plan and interpret the test. Do not shorten individual assessment coverage or cut safety and cleanup; on days 20 and 21 use the existing two-meeting observation window, and on course-level checks rotate short stations across the existing two meetings. Use dictated responses where writing would consume the evidence time. A school needing longer individual conferences must use the calendar's free weeks, without changing the check weeks or adding owned content.

For a year about ten course meetings short, fold these ten unassessed repeated-evidence days into their neighbours. This is a 58-meeting delivery path, retaining the 68-day reference numbering for the map. Fold-ins retain the named evidence and all owned parts; keep every observation, course-level check and respond slot. Use prepared records and one combined record sheet to make the combined meeting fit, rather than doubling two full activities.
