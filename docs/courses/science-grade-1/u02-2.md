# Section 2.2: Can a copied shape solve our problem?

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-LS1-1, this section's share (days 16 to 21).** The back half of "Solve a people problem with a design that copies how plants or animals use their outside parts to survive, grow and get what they need." Children copy a spreading root-like support for the tower (days 16 to 18), choose another problem and copy a part (day 19), and solve an unfamiliar one (days 20 and 21).
- **K-2-ETS1-2, this section's whole share.** "Make a simple sketch, drawing or model showing how an object's shape helps it do the job a problem needs." The sketch is the new design-cycle step (day 16); the model is the made paper shape (days 18 to 21). Both parts, the sketch and the model, are taught and checked.
- **Limits that bite here.** Paper, cardboard and tape only. The test is stood or tipped after three gentle strokes, with no measured force. Cardboard feet only model spreading support; roots anchor trees in soil, and some trees fall. Scissors are used seated, pieces are soft and blunt, no sharp spikes, nothing is worn on a head or body, and paper protective shapes are never protective equipment. Only the teacher fans. Human problems stay general, never a child's own body or need.
- **Connections.** ELA SL.1.5 (add drawings to a description), days 16, 17 and 21: the drawing carries the explanation and labels may be dictated.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A sketch is a plan drawing: the thing, the part we add, where it touches, and one arrow from the shape to the job. It need not look real. A fair test keeps the same marks and the same three strokes; a support that stood once did not show it always stands ("sometimes, every time", kindergarten). Copying means taking the shape that does the job, not a picture of the animal; a picture stuck on does nothing (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The design-cycle chart from kindergarten (problem, try an idea, test it, review) grows the sketch step on day 16: problem, sketch, build, test. Day 17 revises the sketch after a partner explains it. Day 18's results revise the supports and the sketch. Day 19 the class agrees one chart joining needs, parts and tested shapes; it stays on the wall, without a model answer, for days 20 and 21. The part-and-job chart, the sensing-to-action arrows and the shape-and-job links from section 2.1 are review and are not taught again.
- **Key moment 1, day 18: the gentle-airflow test.** Ideal instrument: a steady airflow of the same strength every time. The teacher's folded paper fan, three strokes from one taped mark with the tower on a taped mark, partly fits: it gives stood or tipped after whole strokes, nothing finer. If the plain tower stands three strokes on day 16, move the fan mark one tape-length nearer before day 18 so the plain tower tips. Record only what happened. If no fan test can run, the teacher reads an invented test record aloud, said to be invented.
- **Key moment 2, day 20: the marker test.** Ideal instrument: the same slow slide every time. A broad cardboard marker slid slowly along the table to the cover from one taped line, stopped on contact, with an adult near; it gives touched or not touched. It fits.
- **Key moment 3, day 21: each child's own account.** Ideal instrument: one-to-one watching. The group turn is the closest fit, three minutes for five children. Fits partly.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 16: one tower demonstration, 5 minutes; six sets of base pieces, no sharing; sketching 12 minutes with a rotating six heard, 2 minutes each. Day 17: 15 pairs; the teacher hears three pairs, 4 minutes each. Day 18: six towers, one for each group; five tests of about 12 seconds make a 1-minute turn at the test table, 6 minutes for six groups, 4 minutes for the changed ones; the other groups improve their sketches. A second adult watches the scissors. Glasses for cutting and fan tests go back to the bag with a table wipe in the 3 clean-up minutes. Day 20: one marker for each group of five, about 15 seconds a child. Day 21: six 3-minute turns, with the unit plan's schedule; a helper adult moves round the waiting children.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper, cardboard and tape serve every day; a hand-held folded paper moved three times stands in for the fan; the teacher says so.

## 6. Days

## Day 16


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: D (E15a, E15b). The seed card beside the paper ball with its glued picture: "its shape does the job".
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Read the design-cycle chart (problem, try an idea, test it; review) and say the tower problem aloud.
- **5 min. The test we will use.** The teacher fans the plain tower from a taped mark, three gentle strokes away from faces; it tips. The class agrees: same marks, three strokes, stood or tipped. Only the teacher fans; stop if anything lifts.
- **4 min. Model a sketch.** The chart grows a step: problem, sketch, build, test. The teacher sketches a tree with spreading roots, an arrow from the roots to "holds it up", and a dictated label.
- **12 min. Sketch.** Each child sketches the tower with a root-like support. Cardboard base pieces are held up to choose a shape; nothing is cut. Groups share ideas; hear a rotating six, 2 minutes each.
- **3 min. Exit.** E16a, E16b, E16c.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Sketches kept for day 17.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sketches a wide base for the class tower; the exit gives two new lighthouse plans and asks which feet can hold it up, to draw a working foot, and why the plan as drawn tips, finding a flaw the practice never shows.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own sketch). Tool: None. By hand: sketch, base pieces. Evidence: E16a, E16b, E16c. Hand-backs out: D to day 17.

## Day 18


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: D (E17a, E17b). The colored pattern beside the outward supports.
- **7 min. Build.** Each child builds the sketched support from a cardboard base and paper strips taped on; scissors seated, thicker card pre-cut by an adult. The teacher checks each build before it is tested.
- **6 min. Test.** The agreed test: tower on its mark, fan on its mark, three gentle strokes. Each group's five supports are tested in turn, about a minute a group; the group record notes stood or tipped for each. Other groups improve their sketches.
- **4 min. Revise.** Change one thing on the support and on the sketch.
- **4 min. Retest.** Only changed supports, same marks. "Sometimes, every time" is said: one stand is not always.
- **3 min. Closing explanation.** The class dictates how spreading supports helped, using the root observations and the tests. Cardboard feet only model spreading support; some trees fall.
- **3 min. Exit.** E18a, E18b.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Scissors and tape counted; glasses wiped and bagged.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds, fans and changes one support; the exit reads new flag sketches and a two-frame tip, picking the working strips and saying the fix from pictures, with no build and no test of its own.

Held by: teacher (chart, test); children (own support and record). Tool: None. By hand: build, test, redraw. Evidence: E18a, E18b. Hand-backs out: D to day 19.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sketch, shape, job, support, wide, touch, stand, tip, test, copy. Frames: "My shape ___, so ___." "It stood because ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged or tactile outlines, adapted marking, dictated labels. An adult may cut to the child's indicated shape; the child chooses, assembles and explains. No handwriting speed, independent reading or hearing test is scored.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; children mark pictures on paper; picture cues for feedback; unfinished revision stays unscored until done.
- **Reading load.** None. The student page is boxes to draw in and labels under 100 words; the teacher reads every prompt.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict whether a cover with a wider arch still leaves the viewing side open, and sketch it.
- **Sensitivity.** No child is the example of a tipped tower; the group record shows stood or tipped for supports, not names, when it is read aloud.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 16: sketch a support for a paper tower. Day 17: explain the sketch aloud and redraw. Day 18: build it and blow it over three times with a folded paper. Day 19: pick one of three problems and make a shape. Day 20: do the unfamiliar task. Day 21, the last day of the unit: the child explains the cover while the adult writes it down; do not correct.
- **One projector.** Show one picture at a time, large; each child still draws and makes their own. Day 21's turns run at the same table in the unit plan's order.
