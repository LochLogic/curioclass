# Section 2.2: Can a copied shape solve our problem?

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 1. Unit 2, Parts that help. *How does a tree stay standing in a storm, and can we copy it to keep our tower from tipping?* Anchor: a photograph of a tall tree standing after a windstorm beside a tall paper tower that keeps falling over (days 10 to 21): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 16 to 21.** Days by kind: fluency 1 (17), application 3 (16, 18, 19), check 2 (20, 21). - **1-LS1-1, this section's share (days 16 to 21).** The back half of "Solve a people problem with a design that copies how plants or animals use their outside parts to survive, grow and get what they need." Children copy a spreading root-like support for the tower (days 16 to 18), choose another problem and copy a part (day 19), and solve an unfamiliar one (days 20 and 21).
- **K-2-ETS1-2, this section's whole share.** "Make a simple sketch, drawing or model showing how an object's shape helps it do the job a problem needs." The sketch is the new design-cycle step (day 16); the model is the made paper shape (days 18 to 21). Both parts, the sketch and the model, are taught and checked.
- **Limits that bite here.** Paper, cardboard and tape only. The test is stood or tipped after three gentle strokes, with no measured force. Cardboard feet only model spreading support; roots anchor trees in soil, and some trees fall. Scissors are used seated, pieces are soft and blunt, no sharp spikes, nothing is worn on a head or body, and paper protective shapes are never protective equipment. Only the teacher fans. Human problems stay general, never a child's own body or need.
- **Connections.** ELA SL.1.5 (add drawings to a description), days 16, 17 and 21: the drawing carries the explanation and labels may be dictated.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A sketch is a plan drawing: the thing, the part we add, where it touches, and one arrow from the shape to the job. It need not look real. A fair test keeps the same marks and the same three strokes; a support that stood once did not show it always stands ("sometimes, every time", kindergarten). Copying means taking the shape that does the job, not a picture of the animal; a picture stuck on does nothing (D).

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 16, "How could a wide shape help it stand?"; day 17, "How does your shape do its job?"; day 18, "Did it stand, and what will you change?"; day 19, "Which part did you copy, and why?"

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sketch, shape, job, support, wide, touch, stand, tip, test, copy. Frames: "My shape ___, so ___." "It stood because ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged or tactile outlines, adapted marking, dictated labels. An adult may cut to the child's indicated shape; the child chooses, assembles and explains. No handwriting speed, independent reading or hearing test is scored.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; children mark pictures on paper; picture cues for feedback; unfinished revision stays unscored until done.
- **Reading load.** None. The student page is boxes to draw in and labels under 100 words; the teacher reads every prompt.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict whether a cover with a wider arch still leaves the viewing side open, and sketch it.
- **Sensitivity.** No child is the example of a tipped tower; the group record shows stood or tipped for supports, not names, when it is read aloud.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 16: sketch a support for a paper tower. Day 17: explain the sketch aloud and redraw. Day 18: build it and blow it over three times with a folded paper. Day 19: pick one of three problems and make a shape. Day 20: do the unfamiliar task. Day 21, the last day of the unit: the child explains the cover while the adult writes it down; do not correct.
- **One projector.** Show one picture at a time, large; each child still draws and makes their own. Day 21's turns run at the same table in the unit plan's order.
