# Section 2.1: What jobs do outside parts do?

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-LS1-1, this section's share (days 10 to 15).** The front half of "Solve a people problem with a design that copies how plants or animals use their outside parts to survive, grow and get what they need." Here children learn what outside parts do (a plant's roots, stem, leaves, flowers and fruits; an animal's parts that sense, move, take in and protect) and which of those jobs could help people. Building a design that copies a part is section 2.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No internal organs, photosynthesis, nerves, hormones, inheritance or measured forces. Say "roots take in water and hold"; never "roots eat soil", and never "plants need no food" (kindergarten's words: a plant is not fed a meal; we saw it take in water and use light). Flowers help flowering plants make seeds; fruits keep seeds safe; not every plant has flowers. A plant's response is shown by dated pictures, with no cause named. Animals sense, then move: no brain or nerve talk. No tasting, no sap, no digging, no live or wild animal in the room. Paper shapes are never protective equipment. Human problems stay general, never a child's own body or need.
- **Connections.** ELA RI.1.2 (main topic and key details), days 10 and 13: after each read-aloud page the teacher names the topic and children say the detail. SL.1.5 begins on day 16 (section 2.2).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: roots anchor a plant and take in water; the plant makes its own food in its leaves from light, air and water, but children are told only "takes in water, uses light". A shoot leans toward a light because it grows more on one side; children say only "it bent toward the lamp". Animals have sensing parts (eyes, ears) and moving parts (legs, fins); the arrow chain "what happened, the part that senses, the part that moves" is a picture, not a mechanism. A part's shape fits its job; we copy the shape that does the job, never the picture of the animal.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. The day 20 and 21 rows are owned by section 2.2. This section's evidence is the exits below.

**Evidence form.** Every exit is asked aloud, one ask each, with picture cards that are invented for practice (the teacher says so). Each child touches a card or draws; the teacher hears a rotating six and the record sheet names who. Exit cases are held back until the exit and are not the day's taught cases. Kindergarten's weed, squirrel, spider plants, goat, cow, cat and chipmunk cases are not used. If many (more than a third) miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example (field 4).

**Exit questions.**
- **E10a (day 10).** Invented: an upright bean plant beside a straight paper tube. "Touch the part of the plant that is like the tube." Cards: leaves, stem, flower. Answer: the stem. A wrong touch is shown with help, no letter (a first idea).
- **E10b (day 10).** Invented: a girl at a pot of beans says the roots eat the soil. "Do roots eat the soil? Touch yes or no." Answer: no. Wrong: yes (A).

- **E11b (day 11).** Same picture. "Touch the part that faces the light." Answer: leaves. A wrong touch is shown with help.
- **E11c (day 11).** Invented: a pea shoot picture with a lamp on the left; the next picture, lamp now on the right. "Touch the picture of the shoot afterwards." Cards: still straight, leaning to the right. Answer: leaning to the right. Wrong: still straight, "plants do not move" (B).
- **E12a (day 12).** Invented: two leaf cards on a vine, one joined to its stem and tilted toward a window, one fallen on the sill. "Touch the leaf that the plant can use to face the light." Answer: the joined leaf. A wrong touch is shown with help.
- **E12b (day 12).** Invented: a bean sprout in a closed box with one hole on the left, a lamp outside the hole; Thursday's picture covered. "Touch where the sprout points on Thursday." Cards: toward the hole, straight up, away from the hole. Answer: toward the hole. Wrong: straight up (B); away from the hole (no letter).
- **E13a (day 13).** Invented: a cat card; the caption read aloud: "A cat hears a toy squeak behind the sofa and walks over to it." "Touch the chain that tells the story." Chains: squeak, ears, legs; squeak, legs, ears; squeak, nose, mouth. Answer: squeak, ears, legs. Wrong: squeak, legs, ears (C); squeak, nose, mouth (shown with help).

- **E14a (day 14).** Invented: the duck chain card (the fox, the eyes, the feet) beside a fish card: a heron's shadow falls on the water and the fish swims under a lily pad. - **E15a (day 15).** Invented: a part card, a seed with a wide, flat, thin wing on one side; three job cards, fall slowly through the air, dig into the soil, make a garden look pretty. - **E15b (day 15).** Same seed card and a paper ball with the seed picture glued on. "Pip wants a paper helper that falls slowly. Will the picture make the ball fall slowly?" Thumb up or down. Answer: no, copy the flat wing shape. Wrong: thumb up (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class part-and-job chart, a paper chart with one row for each part and a picture. Day 10 starts it with the plant parts and one guessed job each. Day 11 confirms jobs with evidence arrows and adds light and water. Day 12 adds leaves. Day 13 starts the animal side (sensing parts and moving parts). Day 14 joins both sides in the paper class model (parts that work together; kindergarten's system model, review). Day 15 adds a shape-and-job link to each row. Unit 1's class pattern chart and kindergarten's needs (water, light, food) are review.
- **Key moment 1, day 11 and day 12: dated shoot pictures.** Ideal instrument: the class's own shoot beside a window, photographed on dated days with the lamp or window side changed. Partly fits: it needs a real sequence collected before day 11. Fallback, said aloud to be invented: a Curio Class original sequence headed "invented for practice"; the teacher says it shows what leaning looks like, not what this plant did. If the real pictures show no lean, say so plainly and use the invented set.
- **Key moment 2, day 14: a second animal.** Ideal instrument: the animal itself watched at a distance. Fits as pictures only: dated printable frames from the adult's own video of a pet dog turning to a routine voice (not on file). Fallback: captioned federal photographs. No animal is in the room.
- **Key moment 3, days 10 and 16: the fan on the paper tower.** Ideal instrument: the same gentle push every time. The folded paper fan, three strokes from one marked spot, partly fits: it gives stood or tipped, not a measured force. Day 10 is a demonstration only.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 10: one tower, one fan, 2 minutes, teacher only. Day 11: six trays (one for each group of five), no sharing, 8 minutes; the teacher alone lifts one potted plant over a tray for 1 minute. Handwashing: fragrance-free wipes at the tables in the 3-minute clean-up; a sink wash (at least 20 seconds of scrubbing) follows at the next transition, groups in turn, since 30 real washes do not fit in 3 minutes. Day 12: six leaf trays; the press stays with the teacher. Days 13 and 14: six card sets; one shown picture or video for the class. Day 15: six sets of eight part cards and four job cards; one paper ear cone held by the teacher.
- **A room with nothing.** The photographs and picture cards carry every day; the teacher says aloud that no plant or animal is in the room.

## 6. Days

## Day 10


- **3 min. Warm-up.** Read the unit 1 class pattern chart aloud: a pattern is the same thing again and again (review). Say one pattern from the sky chart.
- **7 min. Hook.** The storm photograph of standing trees beside a paper tower on a low clear table. The teacher fans the tower with the folded paper fan, three gentle strokes from the marked spot, away from faces; it tips. Ask the compelling question aloud before any roots are shown. Children say what they notice; the teacher scribes. The notices are sorted on the question board as holding, sensing and copying. Say that some trees do fall in storms.
- **3 min. Retrieval (unscored).** Show an invented picture of a potted plant by a window and a pond edge. "Touch the picture where a plant can get water and light." "Say what a land animal needs there." Then the fallen tower: "Say the problem people want to solve." (Water, light, food, keep it standing.) A miss: match each need to its picture together.
- **8 min. Read-aloud: "Which parts could help it stand?"** A Curio Class picture read-aloud, five pages, one part and one job on each: roots (hold, take in water), stem (holds leaves up), leaves (face the light), flower (helps make seeds), fruit (keeps seeds safe). After each page the teacher names the topic and children say the detail.
- **7 min. Parts drawing.** Each child draws a plant, places five part cards and circles the part they think helps it stand; labels are dictated or copied from the cards. The teacher hears a rotating six, about a minute each. The teacher starts the class part-and-job chart from their words.
- **3 min. Exit.** E10a, E10b.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Tower, fan and cards put away; paper edges handled carefully.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice names part jobs from the read-aloud and labels a drawn plant; the exit compares a plant with a paper tube to pick the part that holds it up, and answers a girl who says roots eat soil, which no practice asks.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own parts drawing). Tool: None. By hand: cards, drawing. Evidence: E10a, E10b. Hand-backs out: A to day 11.

## Day 11


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: A (E10b). The root picture with the water arrow, traced by finger; "roots take in water and hold".
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Read the part-and-job chart aloud (review).
- **8 min. Look at the parts.** Six trays, one for each group of five, each with a stem and leaves of an identified non-toxic plant (adult checked for allergies; photographs for any child who needs them) and root close-up cards. Groups find the stem, a leaf and a root on the cards; no tasting, no sap, no digging. The teacher lifts one potted class plant over a tray for 1 minute so roots in soil are seen. Hear a rotating six.
- **8 min. Dated shoot pictures and the evidence arrow.** The teacher models one evidence arrow: from what we saw (the shoot leaning toward the lamp) to what it shows (a plant responds to light). Children draw their own arrow on the shoot strip. The pictures are dated; no cause is named.
- **4 min. Add to the chart.** The class dictates roots: hold and take in water; stem: holds leaves up; leaves: face the light; flower and fruit: seeds. Each row gets an evidence arrow.
- **3 min. Exit.** E11a, E11b, E11c.
- **3 min. Hand washing and clean-up.** Tidy and wipes at the tables; the sink wash follows at the next transition.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice finds parts on a tray and draws an evidence arrow on a leaning strip; the exit names parts on a new pea shoot and predicts a covered Thursday picture after the lamp moves, a prediction the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (chart, modeled arrow); children (own arrow). Tool: None. By hand: trays, cards, strip, arrow. Evidence: E11a to E11c. Hand-backs out: B to day 12.

## Day 13


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: B (E12b), the same strip walk.

- **6 min. Read-aloud: "Parts that sense and act."** A Curio Class picture read-aloud, six pages, one animal in each scene: a hare hears a sound and runs (ears, legs); its eyes see; its nostrils take in air; its mouth takes in food and water; a fish's scales cover its body. - **2 min. Pack.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts part cards and lays the modeled hare chain; the exit gives a new cat story with three ready chains, one with sense and move swapped, so the child judges chains and a claim instead of building one.

Held by: teacher (chart, chain); children (own sort). Tool: None. By hand: cards, arrows. Evidence: E13a, E13b. Hand-backs out: C to day 14.

## Day 15


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: C (E14b), the chain card again.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Revisit the tower: what job do roots do that the tower lacks?
- **9 min. Four examples.** The teacher shows four paired examples, each a part's shape and a general human job: a nut's hard shell and keeping something safe; spreading roots and staying upright (the tower); overlapping feathers and keeping rain out; a hare's wide ear and detecting. The paper ear cone is shown as a model of the ear, held by the teacher and not put to an ear or mouth; its sound test comes later. On the design chart each row gets "shape" and "job" cards.
- **12 min. Match.** Six groups each get four new job cards and eight part cards (four fit by shape, four are pretty but do not). Groups match each job to a part and draw one shape-to-job arrow. Hear a rotating six.
- **2 min. Question board.** Which of these jobs could our tower use? Raise: how can someone else understand our idea?
- **3 min. Exit.** E15a, E15b.
- **2 min. Pack.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice matches job cards to part shapes and draws shape-to-job arrows; the exit runs the other way, from a new winged seed to the job its shape could do, and asks whether a glued-on picture would do that job.

Held by: teacher (design chart); children (own arrows). Tool: None. By hand: cards, arrows. Evidence: E15a, E15b. Hand-backs out: D to day 16 (section 2.2 holds the slot).

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 10, "What do you notice?" and the compelling question; day 11, "What did you see, and what does it show?"; day 13, "What does it sense, and what does it do next?"; day 15, "Which part's shape could help, and how?"
- Written task, day 11: each child's evidence arrow on the dated shoot strip, with a label the teacher scribes.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the arrow starts at something seen; it ends at what that shows (a job or a response); the part named is the one that does the job.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: root, stem, leaf, flower, fruit, eyes, ears, legs, hold, sense, job, shape. Frames: "The ___ helps by ___." "I saw ___, so ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Safe real leaves and raised-line part cards to feel, a spoken description of every picture, a scribe, point-and-say answers. No child is asked about their own body, hearing or sight. No handwriting or independent reading is required.
- **Check accommodations.** Every prompt read aloud; pictures to touch; a scribe or an oral answer.
- **Reading load.** None. The student page is boxes to draw in, five part words and the words "job" and "shape", under 100 words; the teacher reads the rest.
- **Extension, same standard.** Pick another plant part, say its job, and draw an arrow from its shape to the job.
- **Sensitivity.** Check plant allergies first and offer photographs. Storm pictures stay calm; no child reports on a storm at home.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the printed cards at a table. Day 10: look at the tower photograph and draw a plant. Day 11: find the parts and draw one arrow. Day 13: sort the part cards. Day 15: match the jobs. The adult asks the exit aloud and writes down what the child says.
- **One projector.** Show one picture at a time, large; each child still draws or touches a paper card. Group trays become one tray passed round, 2 minutes a group, or the projector shows the card.
