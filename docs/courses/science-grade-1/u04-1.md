# Section 4.1: Vibrating things make sound

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#science/science-grade-1/4/s/4.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py science-grade-1 u04-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-PS4-1, this section's share.** "Plan and carry out tests showing that vibrating things produce sound": here, children plan with a partner and carry out tests of a plucked band (days 28, 29, 31), their own throat (29) and a drum (30, 31), saying what they did, what they saw or felt and what they heard, and whether stilling the motion stills the sound (31). The other half of the code, sound setting things vibrating, is section 4.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No waves, wavelength, speed, pitch rules, ear anatomy or hearing tests. A band moves too fast and too little to count, so it is "blurry"; no one counts shakes. A source that looks motionless may move too little to see, so the drum is felt with a fingertip on the box rim. Sounds stay at classroom level, never near ears; no loudness is raised to force a result.
- **Connections.** W.1.8 (ELA, concurrent unit), days 28 and 29: a drawing with one dictated sentence from the investigation. No new arithmetic.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Plucking moves the band back and forth, called vibrating; the blur is the band moving too fast to see apart. The sound comes from the band, not from the hand: the hand only starts it. Stilling is a test, not a rule that things go silent; a short fading tail after the hold is normal, so children compare "much quieter" and the teacher repeats an unclear pair once and keeps both records. Children's first ideas ("the hand makes it", "the box makes it") are written down in their words and tested, not corrected. Review words (push, touch, keep, change, watch) are used as kindergarten and unit 2 taught them. Everyone near a band wears eye protection (unit plan field 8).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none; items 1 and 2 (day 33) belong to section 4.2.

**Evidence form.** Every exit is asked aloud, one ask each, on picture cards after the teacher says "this is invented for practice". The stimulus beside the card is a real, dated recording to be made (field 11); the card is a schematic with apparatus labels only and supplies no result. Each child marks a card on paper; the teacher hears a rotating six, 20 seconds each, while the others draw; the record sheet has a "heard" column. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Wrong answers the register does not name stay unclassified.

**Exit questions.** If many (more than a third) miss one, the next teaching day repairs it in its warm-up (field 4).
- **E28 (day 28).** Picture card, invented for practice: a spring doorstop at the bottom of a wall, flicked at its tip by a finger that then lets go, with motion lines on the spring. X28: "Pip says the finger is making the sound. Touch the part that is moving fast back and forth while we hear it." Cards: the spring; the finger; the wall. Answer: the spring. X28b: "Say: I think ___ makes the sound, because I saw ___." Answer: the spring, because it moved back and forth while we heard it. Wrong: the finger or the wall (unclassified). The case is not flicked at a table edge, so day 33's ruler stays new.
- **E29 (day 29).** Recording: a plastic comb swept slowly through the air, and its teeth plucked. "Pip says anything that moves makes a sound. Touch the two comb pictures you would compare to test Pip." Cards: comb swept slowly; teeth plucked; comb lying still. Answer: swept and plucked. Wrong: still comb with either (unclassified).
- **E30 (day 30).** Recording: a tray tapped once. "Pip tapped this tray once and says that is enough to know how it works. Touch the picture of what to do to be surer." Cards: the same gentle tap several times; one much harder tap; a different tray. Answer: the same gentle tap several times. Wrong: the other two (unclassified); a child who says it keeps ringing when held is A, met first on day 31.
- **E31 (day 31).** Recording: a bell rung, with its body free and then gently held, handle position the same. "Pip says the bell rings because its handle moves. Touch the picture that is a fair check." Cards: ring with the body free, then held, handle alike; ring harder; watch the handle only. Answer: free then held. Then the rotating six say what they expect to hear when the body is held. Wrong: "it would still ring loudly when held" (A); the other cards (unclassified).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class sound model, a paper chart held by the teacher; each child holds an "I did, I saw, I heard" strip. Day 28 starts the chart with the children's drawings and guesses; day 29 adds "my throat shakes when I hum"; day 30 adds the drum; day 31 revises the stopping arrow ("it stopped shaking, so the sound stopped"). Kindergarten's picture chart, three-box strip and cause-and-effect model are the parent forms; unit 2's evidence arrow ("I saw ___, so ___") is reused. Unit 3's evidence account is kept. The model is kept for 4.2.
- **Key moment, day 28 and 31: seeing the band.** Ideal instrument: slow-motion video of the band. Spec: none in this unit; eyes see the blur. If a gentle pluck shows no blur, pluck slightly more, keeping the guitar low and away from faces, or show the dated rehearsal photograph and say so aloud.
- **Key moment, day 29: feeling the throat.** Ideal: a fingertip on one's own throat. Fits. Humming is optional; a child may whisper "mmm", or watch a partner's record. Nothing is marked on hearing.
- **Key moment, day 30: the drum.** Ideal: a seen motion. Spec: the paper top moves too little to see; the instrument's step is "felt or not felt" at the box rim. A quiet tap that is not felt is recorded as "not felt" and repeated once.
- **Equipment, counted.** Six guitars (one per five), six drums on days 30 and 31, 30 eye protection pairs on days 28, 29 and 31. A turn on the band is one pluck, about 45 seconds with the group watching, so five children take under 4 minutes; the others draw.
- **Clean-up (2 minutes).** Each child wipes and bags their own glasses (1 minute); the teacher counts and collects bands and checks the guitars (1 minute). Day 29 adds one hand wipe each from a table tub before the throat touch, counted in the part.
- **A room with nothing.** A shoebox, household non-latex bands and a paper-covered tub do everything. With no eye protection, run no band trials by children: the teacher plucks from a distance, children feel their throats, and day 30's drum needs none.

## 6. Days

## Day 28


- **4 min. Hook.** Everyone near the guitar wears eye protection. Held low, away from faces, the teacher plucks the band, then gently stills it with one finger. Children notice the blur and the changing sound; then ask the compelling question aloud. No cause is given.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** An old toy-cart picture: "Mark the push that could start it moving." Then "What did we watch in our tower test?" A hand pushes the cart; the tower stays up under the fan. A miss: the teacher pushes a classroom box gently and the child marks its new place. Recorded as shown, with help or not yet seen; no score.
- **5 min. Teaching first.** The worked example: the teacher plucks once and fills three boxes aloud, "I did ___, I saw ___, I heard ___", a kindergarten three-box strip with new labels. Pip says the hand makes the sound; the teacher writes the guess.
- **12 min. Pluck and draw, in groups of five.** Each child plucks once and stills once (45 seconds) while the group watches, then says their three boxes to the group; the others draw their own strip. A rotating six is heard.
- **4 min. Rug.** Notices and wonders to the question board. "I think ___, because I saw ___." The class sound chart begins with the strips. Teach "vibrate": moving fast back and forth, shown with hands.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E28.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice plucks the class band and says the I did, I saw, I heard strip; the exit gives a new spring doorstop and Pip's claim that the finger makes the sound, so the child picks the moving part on a card.

Held by: teacher (chart, board, Pip); groups (guitar); children (strip). Tool: None. By hand: three-box strip, one dictated sentence (W.1.8). Evidence: E28. Hand-backs out: none.

## Day 29


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval of the day 28 chart: "What did the band do when it sounded?" Hand-backs in: none.
- **4 min. Plan.** Partners say the test: "We change ___, we keep ___, we watch ___" (change: band or throat; keep: a gentle start). Each pair marks its plan on the paper observation chart.
- **10 min. Band test.** Per five: each child plucks once (45 seconds), watching and listening, then records their strip. Eye protection on.
- **7 min. Throat test.** One wipe each (1 minute). All together and quietly, each child hums "mmm" with fingertips on their own throat (1 minute); a child may whisper or pass. Records: draw what you felt (3 minutes); partners compare band and throat (2 minutes).
- **3 min. Rug.** "What is alike about the band and your throat?" Added to the chart. The question "can sound make something move too?" goes on the board, parked to day 32.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E29.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice plucks a band and hums with a hand on the throat; the exit asks which two comb pictures would test Pip's claim that anything moving makes a sound, choosing a comparison the practice never asks for.

Held by: teacher (chart, board); pairs (plan); children (record). Tool: None. By hand: observation chart, strip. Evidence: E29. Hand-backs out: none.

## Day 30


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval of the chart rows for the band and throat. "Will a drum agree?" Guesses are written in children's words. Hand-backs in: none.
- **4 min. Plan.** The same plan frame: change the thing we sound, keep a gentle tap, watch and feel. A fingertip rests on the box rim, never on the paper top.
- **13 min. Drum test.** Per five: each child taps three times gently (about 1 minute), feels the rim, listens, and marks three trial boxes on the chart. Five children take 5 minutes; the rest of the part is recording and a repeat of any unfelt trial.
- **6 min. Compare.** Lay the band, throat and drum strips side by side; children point to what is alike. Revise the chart. "Does it have to shake to sound?" goes on the board for day 31.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E30.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice taps a box drum three times and compares rows; the exit gives a new tray tapped once and Pip saying one tap is enough, so the child picks what would make the test surer instead of doing it.

Held by: teacher (chart); groups (drum); children (chart rows). Tool: None. By hand: observation chart. Evidence: E30. Hand-backs out: none. This fluency day teaches nothing new; the drum is a second case of the same test.

## Day 31


- **5 min. Warm-up.** The chart: "What did the drum, the band and your throat do?" Hand-backs in: none from day 30.
- **4 min. Plan.** Partners plan the stopping test, saying what changes (free or gently held), what stays the same (same band, same gentle start) and what to watch (blur, and the sound). Eye protection on.
- **14 min. Stopping test.** Per five: each child plucks the band and listens, then plucks and gently holds it still (about 1 minute); then taps the drum and, after a moment, rests a fingertip lightly on the paper (about 40 seconds). About 8 minutes of turns, 5 of drawing, 1 to repeat an unclear pair.
- **5 min. Rug.** Children say "it stopped shaking, so the sound stopped" with evidence arrows. Revise the stopping arrow on the class chart. Day 29's reverse question waits for day 32.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** The teacher counts the bands.
- **3 min. Exit.** E31. Section 4.1 closes.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice plucks the band free and held and says what changed; the exit gives a new bell and three test pictures, two of them unfair, so the child picks the fair test and predicts the held sound before hearing it.

Held by: teacher (chart); groups (guitar and drum); children (record). Tool: None. By hand: free-and-held strip. Evidence: E31. Hand-backs out: A to day 32.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 28, "What did you do, and what did you see and hear?"; day 29, "What is alike about the band and your throat?"; day 31, "What did you change, and what stayed the same?"
- Written task, day 28: a three-box strip with one dictated sentence, "I think ___ because I saw ___" (W.1.8 bridge). Day 31 revises it.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the action is in box one; box two shows the blur or a felt mark; box three shows a sound mark.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 29 retrieves day 28's chart. Day 30 repeats the day 29 test on a new source. Day 31 uses both sources and the plan frame (keep, change, watch). Each warm-up is also the day's repair slot, at most 5 minutes.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sound, shake, vibrate, blur, band, pluck, drum, still, quiet. Frames: "I did ___. I saw ___. I heard ___." "It stopped shaking, so ___." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** Evidence may be seen or felt; a child who does not hear well uses the blur, the felt throat and the rim, and nothing scores hearing. Large labels and a closer seat for low vision. A child may direct a partner who plucks.
- **Check accommodations.** Spoken stems; answer by pointing or placing a counter.
- **Reading load.** None. The student page is boxes and labels (I did, I saw, I heard, band, drum, throat), well under 100 words; the teacher reads all prompts.
- **Extension, same standard.** Test a second approved object from the teacher's tray (a plastic cup, a cardboard tube) with the same strip.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 28: an adult plucks a band on a shoebox held low, everyone in eye protection; fill three boxes together. Day 29: pluck, then hum with a hand on the throat. Day 30: tap a paper-covered tub, rest a finger on the rim. Day 31: pluck free, pluck held; ask what changed.
- **One projector.** Show one picture of the day's strip; the trials stay at the table.
