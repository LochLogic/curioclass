# Section 7.1: Parents and young


## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-LS1-2, this section's share.** The whole standard: "Use books and media to find patterns in how parents and their young behave that help the young stay alive." "Use books and media": captioned pages read aloud (days 57, 58, 60, 61) and photographs and original picture sequences (days 56, 58, 60, 61). "Find patterns": the same three links, a young action, an adult action and staying alive, seen in the robin and the deer (days 56 to 59), then on new animals (days 60, 61). "How parents and their young behave": both generations act (days 57, 59). That grown plants make young is part of the core idea; one plant caption carries it (days 56, 57, 60).
- **Limits that bite here.** Animals and plants only; no child is asked about their own family, and no human family is compared. No talk of how animals reproduce. No claim that every cheep gets food. Hiding quietly counts as a young action. Say what an animal does, not what it feels. Stills never prove a sound. No live nest, no nest visit, no wildlife handling. Plants do not feed their young.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 unit 2, sensing and responding (days 56, 57); unit 3, parent and young and the seed path on the maple family (days 56, 57); unit 6, a signal as a sound that goes across (days 56, 59); kindergarten's forecast question (day 56); ELA RI.1.1, asking about key details, already taught (day 56).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The pattern is a repeated relationship, not a rule for every animal: a young action, an adult action and staying alive, in different forms for different animals. In the robin, the young cheep and open their mouths and the adult brings food. In the deer, the fawn lies still and quiet and the adult comes back to feed it milk; a lone fawn is normal and is never touched (from the cited fawn account, not re-read here). "Signal" keeps unit 6's sense, a sound or sight that goes across, and a cheep is not an agreed message. A picture shows what can be seen. Words can report what a picture cannot show, such as a sound or a reason. The word "source" is for teachers only. Children say "the words" and "the picture", because unit 4 and unit 5 used "source" for light and sound.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** items 1 (animal case, ducks) and 2 (plant case, sunflower), both on day 60 (on the unit plan, field 5).

**Evidence form.** Spoken, drawn, pointed to or sorted. The teacher says aloud that every exit case is invented for practice. Each exit is asked aloud, one thing at a time; every child touches a picture or words card on paper, and a rotating six is heard, 30 seconds each, with a "heard" column. One scale on every record: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Day 60 marks each of the unit plan's ten components on that scale. Only "shown" counts toward the ten; "shown with help" means after the one neutral re-reading the day allows. No exit is scored. Printed words on a child's page stay under 100.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example (field 4). Any slip with no letter is recorded in plain words.
- **E56 (day 56).** Case, read aloud: "Young alligators call from their nest. Their mother uncovers it so they can get out." Two frames: an adult resting beside the still-covered nest; an adult taking nest material away.
  - **E56a.** "Touch the words that say what the young do." Answer: "They call." Wrong: "They do nothing, the adult does it all" (A).
  - **E56b.** "Touch the picture that shows what the adult does." Answer: the uncovering frame.
  - **E56c** (six heard). "Say one thing you want to find out about how this helps them." Answer: a question that ties an action to getting out or staying alive, such as "Does the calling help?" Wrong: "Why does the mother do that? The babies don't do anything" (A).
- **E57 (day 57).** Case: "The mother alligator carries her young to water in her mouth. She gently shakes her own snout to help them swim out." No frames; children draw from the words. Plant words: "A pea plant made pods full of seeds. One seed was planted, and a young pea plant came up." Three loose pictures out of order, no arrows and no words: a young pea plant, a grown pea plant with pods, one pea seed.
  - **E57a.** "In the adult box, draw what the adult does." Answer: carries the young to water.
  - **E57b.** "In the young box, draw what the young do." Answer: swim out. Wrong: an empty young box, "she does it all" (A).
  - **E57c** (six heard say how they know). "Touch the picture the young pea plant came up from." Answer: the pea seed. Wrong: the grown plant, "it fed the young one" (D); "Neither, a plant has no young" (E).
- **E58 (day 58).** Case: "A young sea otter floats. Its mother wraps it in kelp, long seaweed, while she looks for food. The kelp keeps the pup from drifting away." One picture only: the pup floating in kelp, no mother. "Say one thing the words tell that our picture does not show." Answer: any one of the wrapping, the mother looking for food, or the kelp keeping the pup from drifting. Wrong: "nothing, the picture shows her wrapping it" (C); the pup floating (no letter).
- **E59 (day 59).** Case: "A small emperor penguin chick stays under its parent's skin fold. The parent covers it to keep it warm." Picture: a chick under a fold. "Say how staying under the fold helps the chick stay alive." Answer: it keeps the chick warm, because it cannot keep itself warm yet; the chick stays under and the parent covers it. Wrong: "it is hiding from its parent" (no letter). Wrong: "The chick does nothing, the parent does it all" (A).
  - **Closing (six heard).** Each of six children dictates, in 30 seconds, "The young ___, the adult ___, this helps because ___" about the robin, and the teacher scribes it. The unit plan's closing asks for the words about the cheeping and the photograph for the open mouths and food, then the deer as another pattern.
- **Day 60.** Items 1 and 2, no exit. Item 1: duck text and two three-frame sequences; mark what the young and the adult do, point to the words and the picture, give one survival reason. Item 2: the sunflower caption and seed diagram. Ten components, as in the unit plan.
- **E61 (day 61).** Case: "Many young bats hang together in a big group in a cave. A mother bat comes back. Her pup calls, and she finds her own pup by its call and its smell." One drawing: the pups hanging together, one with its mouth open. "Pip says this picture shows the pup calling, so we do not need the words. Is Pip right?" Thumb up or down. Answer: no, a picture makes no sound. Wrong: thumb up (C). Source 12X.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** One class picture model with three links: the young does, the adult does, so the young stays alive. Day 56 starts it with the robin and leaves the last link as a question. Day 57 adds a deer row. Day 58 puts a words mark or a picture mark on each link. Day 59 reaches the consensus: both generations act, their actions can help the young stay alive, and the actions differ across animals. Day 60 uses it on ducks. Day 61 keeps the revisions. The paper question board runs beside it, and each child's day 60 record and revised account go to day 67. Unit 6's evidence habit is kept; its devices are stored for day 67.
- **Key moment 1, days 57 to 59: telling what a source can show.** Ideal instrument: a recording of the robin nest with sound beside the photograph. Spec: does not exist in verified form (unit plan field 10), so the plan uses the photograph, the words and a drawn call mark, and says aloud that a drawn mark is not a sound. No tool is used.
- **Key moment 2, day 60: each child's own reasoning.** Ideal instrument: a per-child record sheet, on paper. It fits. There are no measured readings in this section; every practice case is invented, printed so, and said aloud.
- **A room with nothing.** No printer: draw the frames large by hand and read each page aloud. No display: hold up one large print. No photograph: use the Curio Class drawing. The deer stills are Curio Class drawings unless the fawn photograph's rights notice is confirmed. If a card is lost, a child draws it.

## 6. Days

## Day 56


- **4 min. Hook.** The robin feeding photograph on the display, caption covered. Children say what they notice (open mouths, an adult with food); hear three. Read the Curio Class original account, "Cheep at the nest", then ask the compelling question, hear three guesses, and post it on the paper question board. The teacher says once that a photograph cannot make a sound and the words tell us about the cheeping.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** 2 min: "Which old forecast picture helped answer a question about preparing for a storm? Mark it. Then point to the animal that sensed something and responded on our parts chart." 2 min: unit 3's maple family M22, invented for practice: "Mark the grown plant and the young plant. What does the seed arrow mean?" A miss: model asking a question of the picture, or trace the seed arrow together and repeat on day 57.
- **3 min. Teacher models a question.** Frame 2 of the robin nest sequence. The teacher points and asks, "What is the young bird doing?", answers from the page, and shows the same move on the caption.
- **8 min. Groups look and ask.** Six groups of five, one sequence packet each (four frames). Each child points to one thing and says "I see ___" (5 min, one minute a child). The group agrees one question and posts it under young, adult or staying alive (3 min).
- **5 min. Start the model.** The teacher draws the first two links on the class model. Each child draws on their page: the open mouth, the adult with food, and an empty third box with a question mark.
- **4 min. Exit.** E56a and E56b on paper (1 min); E56c heard from a rotating six (3 min).
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Packets back in envelopes.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice says what is seen in the robin packet and agrees a group question; the exit gives a new alligator case and asks the child alone to touch the words for the young and the picture for the adult, then say a question.

Held by: teacher (hook, question board, class model); groups (packets); children (own page). Tool: None. By hand: the three-box page (about 15 printed words). Evidence: E56a to E56c, the page, the board. Hand-backs in: none. Out: A to day 57.

## Day 57


- **4 min. Open and repair.** Hand-back in: day 56 exit, error A. If many missed, up to 3 minutes: the robin frames, tracing the open mouth and cheep to the food. If few, those children work at the rug with the teacher while the rest sort robin cards. Then 1 minute: "Do deer act like robins?"
- **5 min. Robin page.** The teacher reads the Curio Class robin page aloud (two short captions). Children mark what the young does and what the adult does on their chart box.
- **5 min. Deer page.** The Curio Class fawn page, read aloud: the fawn lies still and quiet in the grass; the adult goes off to eat and comes back to feed it. Children mark the same two boxes. The teacher names hiding quietly as something the young does.
- **3 min. Plant caption.** Last, so the exit can check it. A bean plant page, invented for practice: "This grown bean plant made seeds. One seed grew into this young bean plant." Children point to the grown plant, the seed and the young plant. The teacher says no food arrow is drawn, because the plant did not feed it like a robin.
- **2 min. Chart.** The class signal-response chart gets a robin row and a deer row, scribed from children's marks.
- **5 min. Revise the model.** Add the deer row to the model: the fawn lies still, the adult comes back to feed it, so the fawn stays alive. The teacher posts the next question: do the pictures agree with the words?
- **4 min. Exit.** E57a and E57b drawn on paper (2 min with the reading); E57c touched by all and heard from a rotating six (2 min, 20 seconds each).
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice marks robin and deer rows as captions are read; the exit asks the child to draw the adult and young actions from new alligator words, then pick the seed a new pea plant came from with no arrows drawn.

Held by: teacher (reading, chart, model); children (marks). Tool: None. By hand: chart boxes and the drawn deer row (about 20 printed words). Evidence: E57a to E57c, the chart. Hand-backs in: A. Out: A, D, E to day 58.

## Day 58


- **4 min. Open.** The board question: do the pictures agree with the words? Pip (the puppet, one line at a time) says, "I can hear the baby birds in the photograph!" Children say what the photograph shows and what it cannot.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 57 exit, errors D and E in one slot. The seed sequence redrawn large, arrows labeled "made" and "grew", beside a food arrow marked "not for plants". Children point and say it. If A is also many, it moves to day 59's open.
- **4 min. Teacher model.** One robin page set beside one robin frame: the words say the adult brings food, the frame shows food in the beak. A words mark and a picture mark go on the link.
- **8 min. Groups compare the deer.** Six groups, one deer page and one three-frame sequence each. Each child points to one link and says which shows it, the words or the picture (5 min). The group notes one thing the words tell that the frames do not (3 min).
- **2 min. Share.** Four groups say that one thing, 30 seconds each; the other two point to theirs on the class model during the revise.
- **4 min. Revise the model.** Each child puts a words mark or a picture mark on each of their links; the class model gets the same marks.
- **6 min. Exit.** The words read aloud and the picture shown, then E58 whispered to a partner by all (1 min); the answer heard from a rotating six (3 min, 30 seconds each), with two minutes for reading and changeover.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Six packets collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice marks which links of the fawn page the words or the frames show; the exit keeps a new sea otter case, alone and spoken, and asks what the words tell that the one picture does not show.

Held by: teacher (model, marks); groups (packets); children (own marks). Tool: None. By hand: link marks (about 10 printed words). Evidence: E58, the marked pages. Hand-backs in: D, E. Out: C to day 59.

## Day 59


- **4 min. Open.** Pip says, "Only the grown-up helps the babies stay alive." Children point on the robin and deer rows to a young action. Hand-back A from day 57 is repaired here.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back in: day 58 exit, error C. Six large cards from the robin and deer packets are sorted into "we can see it" and "the words tell it".
- **5 min. Robin cards.** Groups of five with a set of large cards: young does, adult does, stays alive. They lay the robin chain.
- **5 min. Deer cards.** The same for the deer, laid beside the robin chain.
- **5 min. Same and different.** Pip says, "All young animals call for food." Children point to what is the same (the young act, the adult comes, the young stays alive) and to one thing that is different.
- **5 min. Revise the model.** Six rotating children dictate the robin closing (3 min, 30 seconds each); then the class dictates the consensus and the teacher scribes it: the young and the adult both act, their actions can help the young stay alive, and different animals do different things. The empty third box is filled.
- **4 min. Exit.** The words read aloud and the picture shown; E59 whispered to a partner by all (1 min); the answer heard from a rotating six (2 min, 20 seconds each), with a minute for reading and changeover.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Card sets counted back.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice lays the robin and deer chains and names same and different; the exit gives a new penguin chick, alone and spoken, and asks how its action and its parent's help it stay alive, saying the reason instead of laying or comparing a chain.

Held by: teacher (Pip, scribe); groups (cards); children (marks). Tool: None. By hand: the large card sets and the model (about 25 printed words). Evidence: E59, the dictated closing. Hand-backs in: A, C. Out: A to day 61; B only if heard in the same-and-different part.

## Day 60


- **1 min. Launch.** This is a check and is called one. Children may pass. The ducklings are invented for practice, said aloud. No help is given beyond one neutral re-reading.
- **2 min. Read and show.** The duck text read aloud once; the two three-frame sequences shown, with the call-symbol convention explained and no meaning given. Printed pages handed out.
- **4 min. Mark.** Each child marks the young and the adult in the words and in the pictures. The teacher circulates and does not coach. Then pencils down on the sheets.
- **18 min. Six group turns, 3 minutes each.** At the teacher's table, five 30-second child turns: point to a marked place and give one survival reason (E60a only); 30 seconds to change over. Waiting groups match familiar robin and deer cards on their own and draw a reason on scrap paper, unscored; the sheets stay down.
- **3 min. Every animal.** E60c asked aloud to the whole class: every child shows thumbs up for yes or down for no (30 seconds); a rotating six say their answer, 20 seconds each.
- **4 min. Plant case.** The sunflower caption read aloud with its seed diagram. Each child marks the young sunflower and draws how it got there.
- **2 min. Collect.** Sheets collected; records kept for day 67.

Held by: teacher (reading, record sheet with a "heard" column); groups (their turn); children (pages). Tool: None. By hand: the response sheet (about 40 printed words). Evidence: unit assessment items 1 and 2. Hand-backs in: none. Out: gaps by letter to day 61.

## Day 61


- **5 min. Read feedback and revise.** The teacher says the three most common picture comments aloud (1 min). Each child fixes one picture or label on the day 60 sheet from their own comment (4 min). New work is unscored; the original is kept.
- **12 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs in: day 60 gaps and day 59's A and B, by letter. Tables: A (both actions), B (compare), C (seen or heard), D and E (plant diagram, separate prompts). The teacher visits the three largest tables, 4 minutes each; the fourth works a picture move card alone. One child follows the same moves with an adult.
- **8 min. Fresh case and closing.** Groups mark a fresh robin page with different feeding pictures: the young, the adult, and the words or the picture for each (5 min). Meanwhile the teacher takes the dictated closing from six children not heard on day 59, 30 seconds each (3 min), then circulates.
- **3 min. Exit.** E61 shown by all (1 min); the why heard from a rotating six (2 min, 20 seconds each).
- **2 min. Collect and close.** The class dictates one answer to the compelling question while pages are collected. The board is closed; revised accounts are kept for day 67.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice marks a fresh robin page for young, adult, words or picture; the exit gives a new bat case and Pip's claim that a drawing shows a pup calling, so the child judges whether a picture can stand in for the words.

Held by: teacher (tables, scribe); groups (fresh page); children (revisions). Tool: None. By hand: the move cards and the fresh page (about 30 printed words). Evidence: E61, the revision, the fresh page. Hand-backs in: gaps by letter. Out: none.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 57: "What does the young do?" Day 58: "Which shows it, the words or the picture?" Day 59: "What changed our model?"
- **Written task, day 59.** The dictated closing, "The young ___, the adult ___, this helps because ___", scribed by the teacher. Children write only labels.
- **Look-fors.** Both a young action and an adult action named. The words or the picture named for each. A link to staying alive that says "helps", not "always".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words, each with a picture: young, adult, parent, cheep, hide, feed, stay alive, words, picture. Frame: "The young ___, the adult ___, this helps because ___." Pointing and home-language words are accepted.
- **Access.** Every card is large and touchable. A child who cannot see the frames has them described neutrally, with actions and no conclusions. A child who cannot point answers by eye gaze, a cue card or dictation.
- **Check accommodations.** All prompts are read aloud. Accept pointing, dictation and enlarged pictures. A re-reading is marked "shown with help".
- **Reading load.** None. Each student page stays under 100 words; everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child says why two sources that agree might both come from one account.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 56: look at the photograph, hear the account, point to what the young and the adult do. Day 57: read the robin, deer and plant pages and mark the boxes. Day 58: say whether the words or the picture shows each link. Day 59: lay the cards for both animals and say what is the same. Day 60: the duck pages and the sunflower page; the adult marks the child's pointing. Day 61, the last day of the unit: fix one picture, try the fresh page, and dictate an answer to the question. The adult writes the child's words and does not correct.
- **One projector.** Show pages and frames large; every child marks their own paper. On day 60 the turns run in order at one table.
