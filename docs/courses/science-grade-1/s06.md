## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we tell a friend across the playground it is time to come in, without shouting?"
- **Hook, day 48.** Recall the familiar recess bell, then observe a teacher-only box-drum signal from two hallway markers. Some receivers notice sound without knowing its meaning. Ask the compelling question before agreeing on a safe practice message.
- **Closing, day 55.** Dictate how the device carries an agreed message to a receiver, using trial records to explain success and repairs. A signal must reach someone who can interpret it.
- **Teaching moves.** Model recording what the receiver reports, without supplying the answer. Hand sketching and test choices to children; ask what evidence would settle disagreements.

**Transfer and preparation.** Teaching on days 48 to 52 develops the recess problem; no earlier task uses the display-helper case. Introduce the display-helper problem on day 53, printed "invented for practice". Its limits are provided materials, classroom-level sound, safe light direction and a correctly received message at the farther mark. Children choose sound or light, make a dated sketch and cite earlier trial evidence for a feature. Each child adapts the device built on day 49 by adding or changing one designed part (a screen, a stand, a shutter or a drum surface); a new device is built only if the old one cannot be adapted. Identify each partner's making contribution. A borrowed flashlight alone is not construction: add a child-designed cardboard shutter or stand. A paper box drum is the minimum household route. A drawing alone never replaces making.

Day 53 takes 30 minutes: introduce the problem 3 (one question, why do people send messages far away, answered by pointing to the recess bell and the display table; no phone or radio workings); prepare 22; reserve a fresh exit 3; clean up 2. During preparation the teacher gives each child a spoken check; the written picture-linked comment is written in the teacher's 30 minutes after the day, and children revise from it on day 54. These are applications of ideas taught on days 48 to 52, not scored first-teaching work. Day 54 contains no construction or reteaching. Neither the practice trials nor the new exit product earns points.

**Observation logistics.** Day 54 takes 30 minutes: launch 3; six groups of five have 3-minute turns, 18 total; paper records 6; cleanup 3. Item 1 uses the 18 turn minutes and 6 record minutes, with construction already complete. Each group turn provides five 30-second child turns plus 30 seconds for changeover. Pre-set both marks and ready the devices. The two sends are the child's turn, with receivers already stationed and the card hidden. Receiving is recorded from the receiver's own mark during a partner's turn. The spoken why is taken in the 6 record minutes from rotating pairs reading their paper records to the teacher; the paper record, not the turn, supplies those points. Station two receivers at the marks before each sender begins; alternate roles across the five turns so every child receives. Use an adult-rehearsed cup route where moving between marks is safe. Partners mark their actual interpretation before seeing the sender's intended-message card. Keep message, signal pattern and device steady as distance changes. For cup devices, record string tautness too; near slack and farther taut trials cannot isolate distance as the cause. Do not require that nearer always works better.

Waiting children independently match familiar device-evidence cards to their model and draw a reason. They do not rehearse the held-back positions. Each child marks a paper trial record; there is no extra assessment-day exit or repeated conference.

**Ten points and look-fors.** Four points use day 53's revised work: one for a sketch defining this message and distance problem, one for a feature justified by prior light or sound evidence, one for the child's identifiable construction using provided materials, and one for a revision explaining how a changed part should help the message reach its receiver, responding to written feedback. Six points use day 54: one for sending at each distance, one for accurately recording receipt at each distance, one for explaining how the parts send or receive light or sound using the model, and one for judging the solution's usefulness to the display helper from results. Credit an honest failed trial as evidence, but mark successful communication separately and require a successful farther send before recording the full expectation as demonstrated. All ten points use design or testing practice with the communication idea in this new problem, exceeding 80 percent. During teaching, use whole-strip distances other than the four-strip exit case; never rehearse held-back signal patterns or receiver positions. Unmatched or missing evidence is unclassified, not automatically a misconception.

**Samples, invented for practice.** Meets: "I added a stand so the light stays on the picture card. My helper marked ready at both places. The light carried our signal, so the helper knew when to collect the picture." The record also shows the child's construction, evidence-based sketch and written-feedback revision. Does not meet: "My helper saw the blink, so the message worked," when the helper marked a different meaning (A). Score each component from actual work, not fluent speech.

**Feedback and score.** Allow 30 teacher minutes after day 53 for preparation records and any remaining written comments. Day 54's first record minutes let children revise earlier explanations from those comments without rebuilding. After day 54 allow 25 teacher minutes for written feedback on trial evidence. Day 55 provides revision before scores are finalized in 15 teacher minutes afterward. Score only earlier work after written feedback and a later chance to revise; preserve observed performances. A day 55 fresh trial after a repair is the revision for the day 54 send and receipt points: it replaces the day 54 value for those points, marked revised, and earns no other points. Other new response trials are unscored. The day 55 closing dictation is taken in the fresh-trial minutes from three or four rotating children.

**Access.** Read all prompts aloud. Every child marks pictures on paper; accept pointing, enlarged pictures, dictation and adapted grips. Offer either signal medium without requiring personal disclosures. An adult may steady materials without choosing the design or sending for the child. Say and picture: message, send, receive, signal and distance. Extension: explain a failed trial using evidence while retaining the same standard.

Notebooks retain sketches and receiver records. Each exit is one question with at most two parts, read aloud; every child marks a paper picture and about six rotating children give reasons. Hold back the exit case until then. Print every invented case "invented for practice". Reserve three minutes each day. No exit work is scored.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Noticing a signal means its message was understood. | Receipt and interpretation are confused. Repair: compare the sender's intended picture with the receiver's independently selected picture. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | A sketch alone is a built communication device. | A plan is substituted for construction. Repair: have the child use the sketch to make a working paper box drum. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | A device that works nearby must work at any distance. | Reach is assumed without evidence. Repair: test the same signal at two marked distances and limit the claim to those observations. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Distance alone caused the changed result when the signal also changed. | Two changed conditions prevent that causal claim. Repair: repeat with one signal pattern while changing distance. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | Gaps between strips count as measured length units. | The unit does not cover the whole distance. Repair: lay identical strips end to end without gaps and recount. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | One successful trial guarantees the next trial succeeds. | A single result is treated as proof of dependability. Repair: repeat the trial and compare the receiver's records. | Anticipated, from memory. |

These are anticipated ideas, not observed findings. Use only the letter matching the child's claim; record other gaps in plain words.
