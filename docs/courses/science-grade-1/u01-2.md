## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-ESS1-1, this section's share (days 5 to 8).** "Watch the sun, moon and stars and describe the sky patterns they show that can be predicted." Day 5 adds the part not yet met: the stars. Day 6 tests all three patterns at another place. Day 7 checks every part (sun path, moon path, stars at night rather than day). Day 8 responds.
- **Limits that bite here.** For stars, only that they show at night and not in the daytime: no constellations, changes in stars, rotation, orbits, moon phases, compass words or measured shadow lengths. Night visibility needs a clear, sufficiently dark sky, so a cloudy night card shows no stars. Never assume the moon is up at the same clock time each day. Adults read clocks. No sun viewing.
- **Connections.** ELA W.1.8 (recall observations to answer a question), days 5 and 6: answers spoken from the cards, teacher scribing.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: the sun is a star, and the stars other than the sun are in the sky by day too, but the daytime sky is too bright to see them. So the children's claim is only "we see them on clear dark nights, not in the day"; "stars are gone by day" is the error to repair. The daytime moon is the same lesson: not seen is not absent. Cards are schematic and not to scale; clock times differ between cards on purpose.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Patterns, on the class sky picture chart. Day 5 revises it for the daytime moon and adds the night stars (D is repaired once, aloud). Day 6 tests the chart on another place's records and reaches the closing explanation: where the sun seems to be changes with the side its shadow falls on, and repeated marks support predictions. The chart stays on the wall on day 7; no earlier notebook work is scored.
- **Review, in the ledger's words.** Pattern; "I think ___, because I saw ___"; the class sky picture chart; the weather chart row each school day; the dated sky-watch archive; return and repair; rotating six heard; exit with picture cards, invented for practice. None is taught as new, and 1.1's sun and moon cases are not reused.
- **Key moment 1, day 6: records from another place.** Ideal instrument: the other school's own dated pictures with fixed-view notes. The packet is still to collect; it fits if collected. Fallback: Curio Class original records headed "invented for practice", said aloud to be invented; then the teacher says plainly that "elsewhere" has not been shown by real records, and the closing explanation names only what the cards show.
- **Key moment 2, day 7: each child's own reading.** Ideal instrument: one-to-one watching; the group turn is its closest fit, three minutes for five children. Fits partly.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 5: six sets of eight cards for groups of five, no sharing. Day 6: three stations, two copies of each card set, two groups at a station, 4 minutes a station, 12 minutes. Day 7: the unit plan's schedule: 3 to launch, 6 paper, 18 in six three-minute group turns, 3 to collect; waiting children use familiar unscored cards. Day 8: at most four groups; others work from picture directions.
- **A room with nothing.** The cards are paper, so nothing else is needed.

## 6. Days

## Day 5


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs: day 4 exit, B and C, 2 minutes each. B: lay yarn from picture to picture on the moon strip. C: look at the daytime moon picture and say what it shows. Return the logs.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Read the class chart aloud: the shadow dots, the moon's path (review).
- **9 min. Sort the sky cards.** Six sets of eight day and night cards, from the archive: sunny day, day with a pale moon, clear night with stars, cloudy night, and the like. Groups sort DAY and NIGHT, then put a finger on the cards where stars show. Hear a rotating six; the teacher moves round all six groups.
- **6 min. The class pattern chart.** Day heading and night heading. Sun in the day; the moon under both headings; stars under night, "clear and dark". The teacher says once: the stars are up there in the day too, but the daytime sky is too bright to see them.
- **4 min. Revise the chart.** The moon can be in the day sky too. Children dictate; the teacher scribes (W.1.8 bridge).
- **2 min. Question board.** Raise: will these patterns hold at another place?
- **3 min. Exit.** E5a, E5b, E5c.
- **2 min. Set-up: sky-watch archive.** The teacher alone adds today's picture and date to the folder.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts sky cards into day and night and marks where stars show; the exit asks the child to say why an afternoon card has no stars, a reason the sort never asks, then picks the star and day-moon cards.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own sort). Tool: None. By hand: cards, sort. Evidence: E5a to E5c. Hand-backs out: C and D to day 6.

## Day 6


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs: day 5 exit, C and D, 2 minutes each: the daytime moon picture; "not seen, but still there" with the bright-sky card.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** The class chart's predictions, read aloud: the sun's shadow dots, the moon's path, stars on clear dark nights.
- **12 min. Test elsewhere.** Three stations, each with a record from the other school: sun and shadow dots, a timed moon strip and day and night star records. Two groups to a station, 3 minutes 30 a station, 30 seconds to move and reset flaps. Each group predicts the next picture, uncovers it and says "I think ___, because I saw ___". Hear a rotating six.
- **6 min. Did our patterns hold?** Compare with the chart. Answer the day 5 question aloud. The class dictates the closing explanation: where the sun seems to be changes, and the shadow falls on the other side. The teacher scribes it.
- **4 min. Revise the shadow account.** Each child redraws the day 1 account (sun, stick, shadow, arrow) with the sun on the side opposite the shadow, using what the three records showed.
- **3 min. Exit.** E6a, E6b, E6c.
- **1 min. Pack.** Cards to the tubs.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice stations predict the next sun dot, moon place and night from another school's cards; the exit covers the middle look instead, so the child finds where the dot and moon were between two known places, and reads a covered afternoon.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own redrawn account). Tool: None. By hand: cards, redrawn account. Evidence: E6a to E6c. Hand-backs out: A and B to day 8 (day 7 reteaches nothing).

## Day 7


- **3 min. Launch.** The teacher reads the three prompts aloud; the chart is on the wall. Children use pictures only.
- **6 min. Paper responses.** Each child marks paper for S, M and T, two minutes each, as prompts are read aloud: each ask read on its own: draws the path, marks the later position, circles the sheet or cards that show it. Nothing is scored or fed back.
- **18 min. Group turns.** Six groups of five, three minutes each, with the teacher. Each child tells one item's reason aloud (S, M and T rotate across the group); the teacher writes the spoken reason on the child's sheet in checklist words or a short scribe note. Waiting children use familiar unscored cards independently; a helper adult moves round.
- **3 min. Collect.** Sheets in; no feedback.

Held by: child (own sheet); teacher (record). Tool: None. By hand: marks, drawing, one spoken reason. Evidence: the unit plan's rows S, M, T. Hand-backs out: none. No repair: day 7 reteaches nothing.

## Day 8


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs: day 6 exit, A and B, and the written feedback on day 7 sheets. Each child reads or hears their feedback; at most four groups after the sheet shows who needs what.
- **10 min. Compare records and revise.** A and B: trace the sun's or moon's dated pictures in order with a finger. C: compare the daytime moon pictures. D: say "not seen, but still there" with the day and night cards. Children revise their day 7 reasons by pointing, retelling or redrawing; the teacher notes the revised reason for two children a table, 2 minutes a table for the repair ask only, the rest from the sheet after class.
- **10 min. Fresh predictions (unscored).** Three practice cards (a sun strip over a pond, shed and barn; a morning moon over a playground; a desert school's star records), not the exit cards; each child marks the prediction strip as prompts are read aloud (sun 2 minutes, moon 2, stars said aloud 1); hear a rotating six, 15 seconds each. Then the exit, 3 minutes, on held-back cards: E8a, E8b, E8c.
- **5 min. Collect.** Sheets in; the question board is read once and closed.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice cards predict the next sun and moon place; the exit asks where the sun was at a covered middle look, answers a girl who says the moon is only out at night, and reads a covered night instead of a day.

Held by: teacher (chart, feedback); children (own sheet). Tool: None. By hand: marks, redrawing. Evidence: E8a to E8c. Scoring follows after class, using the revised day 7 work (unit plan field 5).

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 5, "What can we see in the night sky that we cannot see in the day?"; day 6, "Did our patterns hold at another place?" and "How do you know?"; day 7, each child's one reason aloud.
- Written task, day 6: each child's redrawn account (sun, stick, shadow, arrow) with a label the teacher scribes.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the path is drawn in order across a fixed view; the next place is predicted from it; the reason points to records, and says "not seen" rather than "not there".

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 5 pulls the shadow dots, the moon strip and the claim sentence. Day 6 pulls the chart, keep, change, watch and the claim. Day 7 pulls the chart, which stays on the wall. Day 8 pulls the day 7 reasons. The weather chart row continues each school day, outside lesson time.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: star, night, day, clear, cloud, bright, path, again. Frames: "We can see ___ at night." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Raised-line or enlarged records, verbal description of each position, adapted marking or a scribe, communication symbols for the same reasoning. No handwriting, independent reading or home observation is required.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; each child marks pictures on paper; offer enlarged records or a scribe.
- **Reading load.** None; the teacher reads every prompt.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict the next picture for a record with a cloud on one night, and say what the cloud hides.
- **Sensitivity.** Some children fear the dark; keep night pictures calm, require no night outing, and give no home observation task.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the printed cards at a table. Day 5: sort day and night. Day 6: predict the next card, then check it. Day 7, the last day of the unit: ask the three questions, write down what the child says, and do not correct. Day 8: look again at any that were missed.
- **One projector.** Show one record at a time, large; each child still marks their own paper. Stations on day 6 become three turns on the screen.
