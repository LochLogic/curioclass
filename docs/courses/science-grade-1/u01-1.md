# Section 1.1: How can we predict a sky path?

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **1-ESS1-1, this section's share (days 1 to 4).** "Watch the sun, moon and stars and describe the sky patterns they show that can be predicted." Here children watch the sun through its shadow, never directly, and the moon through timed pictures and one safe look. They describe each path in order and predict the next place. Stars are section 1.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Stars only that they show at night and not in the daytime, so none here. No rotation, orbits, moon phases, compass words, measured shadow lengths or children reading clocks (adults read them). Do not assume the moon is up at the same clock time each day. Never look at the sun; its place is found by shadows, and the moon look uses a view the teacher has screened from the sun.
- **Connections.** ELA W.1.8 (recall observations to answer a question), days 1 and 2: spoken observations, teacher scribing. Kindergarten sunlight, shade and weather chart, day 1 (review).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a shadow is where an object blocks sunlight, and it lies on the side away from the sun. As the sun seems to cross the sky, the shadow swings the other way, so marks at three dated times make an ordered record of the sun's path without looking at it. The cause is the Earth turning; it is not taught here. The moon also seems to cross a view in order and can be up in daytime for part of each month (from memory); it is not up at the same clock time each day, so children predict from a picture sequence, never from a clock. The class stick's marks may run left to right or the other way, by how the yard faces: the teacher checks the day before.

## 2. Where it sits


- Before: the start of the unit. Kindergarten's weather chart habit and sunlight-to-surface picture are review. Retrieval only, day 1 (3 minutes); a miss goes on the record sheet on the one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- After: section 1.2. Day 5 opens with day 4's hand-back, so day 5 holds the slot.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Rows S, M and T of the unit plan belong to section 1.2. Every exit is asked aloud with picture cards that are invented for practice (the teacher says so); each child touches the card while the teacher hears a rotating six. Rule 4: none of these cards is a taught case. The invented cards run left to right; the class stick may run the other way, and the teacher says "ours is the other way round" if so.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E1a (day 1).** Invented: a lamp-post at a bus stop on two cards, "morning" and "after lunch"; the post is the same, its shadow lies left on the first and right on the second. "Touch what is different on the two cards." Cards: the post, the shadow, the bus stop. Answer: the shadow. Wrong: "the sun stays put, so nothing changed" (A); touches the post, "it moved" (no letter, shown with help).
- **E1b.** Invented, no cards. "Draw a flagpole on a school lawn, the sun, an arrow for its light, and the shadow." Answer: the pole, the sun, an arrow and a shadow; where the sun goes is the child's own idea. Wrong: no shadow, or no arrow (no letter).
- **E1c.** Invented: a swing set in a sunny yard. "Touch the question we can find out by watching the swing's shadow again and again." Cards: will the swing's shadow move again tomorrow; is the swing fun; what color is the swing. Answer: will it move again tomorrow. Wrong: fun or color (no letter).
- **E2a (day 2).** Invented: a school bench with its shadow on the ground to its right. Cards: sun on the left of the bench, sun on the right, sun on top. "Touch where the sun is." Answer: the left. Wrong: the right, "where the shadow is" (no letter, not yet seen).
- **E2b.** Invented, run right to left: a fence post with a dot for the morning shadow on the right and a dot for the late-morning shadow in the middle; the third is blank. "Touch where the third dot goes." Cards: left, middle, right. Answer: left. Wrong: the middle, "it stays" (A); right, "it goes back" (no letter).
- **E3a (day 3).** Invented: a sandbox pole. Strip 1, kept from last week: left, middle, right. Strip 2, from yesterday: left, middle, third covered. "Touch where the covered dot is." Answer: right. Wrong: the middle, "it stays where it is" (A).
- **E3b.** Same strips. "Say: I think ___, because I saw ___." Answer: I think it is on the right, because last week it went left, middle, right. Wrong: "because the sun stays there" (A); "because I want it" (no letter, shown with help).
- **E4a (day 4).** Held-back moon strip, invented: gate on the left, windmill in the middle, silo on the right. Look 1: low above the gate. Look 2: high above the windmill. Look 3 is blank. "Touch where the moon will be at look 3." Cards: low above the gate, high above the windmill, lower above the silo. Answer: lower above the silo. Wrong: high above the windmill, "it stays" (B); low above the gate, "it goes back" (no letter).
- **E4b.** Held-back shadow record, invented, run right to left: a birdhouse post with a dot on the right and a dot in the middle. "Touch where the next dot goes." Answer: the left. Wrong: the middle, "it stays" (A); the right (no letter).
- **E4c.** Invented card of a pale moon over a rooftop at lunchtime, with the sun on the other side of the sky. "Touch: can the moon be in the day sky?" Cards: yes, no. Answer: yes. Wrong: no, "the moon comes out only at night" (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Patterns, on the class sky picture chart. Day 1 draws the stick and its first shadow with kindergarten's sun-to-surface picture. Day 2 adds ordered marks and the side opposite. Day 3 adds repeated evidence from another day. Day 4 adds the moon's path beside the sun's shadow marks. The chart stays on the wall into 1.2. Kindergarten's blocked sunlight (a roof between the sun and a spot stops it) is the root: the stick is the roof, the shadow is its shade.
- **Review, in the ledger's words.** Pattern; "I think ___, because I saw ___"; keep, change, watch; the sun-to-surface arrow picture; the weather chart row each school day; return and repair; rotating six heard; exit with picture cards, invented for practice. None is taught as new.
- **Key moment 1, days 1 to 3: seeing the shadow tip move.** Ideal instrument: a fixed stick, a taped paper and the real sun. A crayon dot is enough: no measuring. Spec: fits (no tool). Fallback with no sun at a look: write "cloud" for that look and say aloud that the marks are not complete; use the teacher's saved sunny-day marks, said aloud to be from another day.
- **Key moment 2, day 4: the moon's path.** Ideal instrument: watching the moon over a whole day, which a class cannot do. The timed picture strip plus one safe live look fits partly; the strip carries the path, the look shows the moon is really there. Fallback if the moon is not up in school hours: the class's archive photograph of a daytime moon, with date and clock picture, said aloud to be a saved picture.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 1: one stick, thirty children in a ring of two rows, 8 minutes. Day 2: six sticks, one for each group of five; one child marks a look (20 seconds), so each look is 2 minutes for all six groups and three children mark in a day; all five draw their own record. Day 3: paper only; 15 pairs. Day 4: one live look, 30 children draw one dot each in about 3 minutes. Clean-up: thirty children at two sinks takes about 6 minutes, so tables use wipes (three packs of wipes a table) in 2 to 3 minutes and the sink wash joins the lunch wash.
- **A room with nothing.** No yard or sun: the saved marks and moon photographs from the archive, said aloud to be from other days. No window: the same.

## 6. Days

## Day 1


- **8 min. Hook.** The teacher made a tracing at arrival: the stick's shadow on a taped paper, base outlined in chalk, dated with a clock picture. After lunch the class goes out (2 minutes), rings the stick in two rows, eyes on the ground only, and lays the live shadow beside the tracing (4 minutes). The teacher asks the compelling question: "Why is our shadow stick's shadow on one side in the morning and the other side after lunch?" No explanation is offered. Two minutes back.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** The weather-watch row from kindergarten. The teacher holds up an invented strip, said aloud to be invented: sunny, cloudy, sunny. "Mark what happened twice." Key: sunny. Hear a rotating six. A miss: match the two sunny pictures together, then record today's weather once; note it on the record sheet and ask again on day 2.
- **4 min. What we noticed.** Children say what they saw; the teacher scribes on the chart (W.1.8 bridge). Shadow is named as the shade the stick makes where it blocks sunlight (review of shade).
- **6 min. Draw the stick and its shadow.** Each child draws the stick, its shadow and the sun-to-surface arrow with the sun wherever they think it is: the first account, kept to revise. Then one sentence to a partner: "I think the shadow is here because ___." Hear a rotating six.
- **4 min. Dating a mark.** The teacher models one mark: a dot at the shadow tip, the date and a clock picture. A helper from the rotating six makes the chalk dot at the base. Children copy the dated dot onto their drawing.
- **3 min. Question board.** Notices become questions on the board: where will the next shadow be, will it do this again.
- **5 min. Exit.** E1a, E1b, E1c.
- After class, the teacher alone starts the sky-watch archive: a dated folder with today's fixed-view picture and date (the 2 minutes went to the exit).
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Chalk and clay to the tub; hand wipes at tables; the sink wash joins the next hand wash.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice notices the live shadow, draws the class stick and dates a dot; the exit spots what changed on two lamp-post cards, draws a new flagpole alone, and picks a question that watching can answer, which no practice part asks.

Held by: teacher (chart, record sheet); children (own drawing). Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dated dot. Evidence: E1a, E1b, E1c. Hand-backs out: A to day 2.

## Day 2


- **2 min. Look 1.** At arrival, before the lesson. Each group's stick is already fixed in clay on a taped paper with the base outlined. One child from the group makes the first dot at the shadow tip; an adult reads the clock and the group draws the clock picture. Eyes on the ground.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 1 exit, A. Lay the day 1 tracing and today's look 1 mark in order; walk a finger from the first to the second. Return each child's first account.
- **4 min. Plan the three looks.** Review keep, change, watch: keep the stick, the spot and the paper; change only the time; watch the tip. Each group picks a landmark (a door, a tree) and draws it at the top of the paper so the spot is known. The looks are now, just before lunch and after lunch. Hear a rotating six.
- **2 min. Look 2.** Late morning. A different child marks the dot; the adult reads the clock.
- **8 min. Which side is the sun?** The sun-to-surface picture, extended: three cards in a row, sun, stick, shadow. The class finds that the shadow lies on the side away from the sun, because the stick blocks the sunlight. Each group lays the cards for its two dots. Then each child draws where the third dot will go in a new color and tells a partner: "I think ___, because I saw ___." Hear three; the other three of the six at Check and revise.
- **2 min. Look 3.** After lunch. A third child marks the dot.
- **5 min. Check and revise.** Compare each prediction with the third dot. Revise the class chart from the three dated dots, with a dictated sentence the teacher scribes. A cloud at a look: write "cloud" and use the saved sunny-day marks for that dot, said aloud.
- **3 min. Exit.** E2a, E2b.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Sticks, clay and chalk to the tub; wipes; the sink wash joins the lunch hand wash.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice chalks and predicts dots on the class stick, which runs one way; the exit finds the sun from a bench shadow, says when to look at a new post, and predicts a fence post running the other way.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own record of three dots and a prediction). Tool: None. By hand: dots, drawn prediction. Evidence: E2a, E2b. Hand-backs out: A to day 3. Set-ups: the day before the teacher fixes six sticks and checks that the sun reaches the spot at all three looks.

## Day 3


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 2 exit, A. Show day 2's three dots on the chart; children trace the order with a finger and say left, middle, right as they fit the dots.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Read the day 2 record aloud as a class: three dots, in order, with the clock pictures.
- **8 min. Another day's marks.** The dated shadow-mark cards, from the archive: the same stick on another day, three dots. Pairs (15 pairs) lay them beside day 2's record and say whether the dots went the same way. The teacher names it: the same thing happens again and again, a pattern (review).
- **8 min. Rehearse a prediction.** Each pair has four paper strips, two dots shown and the third covered; pairs predict, say "I think ___, because I saw ___" and uncover. The strips are cases from the archive and invented ones, and the teacher says which are invented. Hear a rotating six.
- **4 min. Where is the sun?** Four cards with a shadow on one side: pairs touch the side the sun is on, and say "opposite the shadow".
- **3 min. Add to the chart.** The chart now says: it did the same on two days.
- **3 min. Exit.** E3a, E3b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice orders another day's cards and predicts covered dots from two dots on one strip; the exit predicts the covered dot from last week's full strip and says the reason from that other day, joining the two practice tasks.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own strips). Tool: None. By hand: strips, cards. Evidence: E3a, E3b. Hand-backs out: A to day 4.

## Day 4


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 3 exit, A. Show the chart's three dated dots; a child runs a finger along them; the class says what the next dot would do.
- **8 min. Hook: the moon in time order.** Six sets of the timed moon pictures, in a mixed row (the school's gym roof on the left, the flagpole in the middle, the far trees on the right); each picture has a clock picture and date. Adults read the clocks. Groups put them in time order and say where the moon went from first to last. One picture is a pale moon in the daytime sky.
- **8 min. A look at the sky.** If the moon is up in school hours, the class goes out (2 minutes) with their backs to the sun and the sun screened by the building, stays with the teacher, and draws the moon in the log beside the landmarks (about 3 minutes). Two minutes back. Never look at the sun. If not, the archive photograph of a daytime moon is the look.
- **6 min. Draw the path.** Each child draws a line through the logged moon's places, in order, on the landmark view. The teacher asks: "Does the moon follow a path too?" Children compare it with the shadow dots: another path, drawn the same way.
- **4 min. Add to the chart.** The moon's path is added; "the moon can be up in the day" is written beside it. Question board: what else shows at night?
- **3 min. Exit.** E4a, E4b, E4c.
- **1 min. Clean-up.** Logs and pencils collected; wipes (no clay or chalk today).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice orders moon pictures, says where the moon went and draws its path; the exit predicts a covered third moon place on a new strip, a new shadow dot, and whether the moon can be up in the day.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own log). Tool: None. By hand: log, ordered pictures. Evidence: E4a to E4c. Hand-backs out: B and C to day 5, which has the slot (1.2).

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 1, "Why is our shadow stick's shadow on one side in the morning and the other side after lunch?"; day 2, "Where will the next dot be, and what makes you think so?"; day 4, "Does the moon follow a path too?"
- Written task, day 2: each child's record of three dated dots and a drawn prediction, with a one-line reason the teacher scribes.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the dots are in time order on one fixed stick; the predicted dot continues the order; the reason points to the dots, not a hunch.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 1 pulls the weather chart and the sun-to-surface picture. Day 2 pulls keep, change, watch and day 1's tracing. Day 3 pulls day 2's dots and the side opposite. Day 4 pulls the chart's dots, then the claim sentence. Every day pulls the claim "I think ___, because I saw ___".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sun, shadow, stick, dot, morning, after lunch, moon, path, again. Frames: "The shadow is on the ___ side." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Raised or taped dots for a child who cannot see the marks; verbal description of each position; a seat at the ring's front; pointing for speech; a child who cannot go out marks from the window or the saved pictures.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each prompt aloud; accept pointing; record help as "shown with help".
- **Reading load.** None.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a post or tree at school and say which side its shadow will be on after lunch, then check.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A pencil in clay on a paper at a sunny sill or step. Mark the shadow tip three times in the day with the time written by you; the child predicts the third dot. Moon day: use a picture strip you have drawn; looking up is optional. No report on the home.
- **One projector.** Show the dated cards and moon strip large; each child still marks their own paper.
