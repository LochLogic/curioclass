## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Is it light or dark at 5:30 in the evening, and will spring be like fall or like winter?"
- **Hook, day 9.** Place the first dated evening report beside the local day strip. If no report arrives, leave its square blank. Ask the compelling question: will this strip's light part stay this long? Children draw an initial prediction before the answer is known.
- **Closing, day 63.** Children dictate that winter has less daylight than the sampled fall and spring days, citing the year's strips. Evening reports describe one moment; the strips support whole-day comparisons.
- **Teaching moves.** Model recording one date, then let children choose records that could test their prediction. Ask what evidence changes an idea. Keep unanswered questions visible between fall and spring.

<!-- skel -->
- **1-ESS1-2:** Observe at different times of year and connect how much daylight there is to the season. Emphasis: comparing winter daylight with spring or fall daylight. Limit: Only more or less daylight is compared; hours of daylight are not counted or measured.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 64 (End-of-unit assessment (observation)): Observe each child using unfamiliar seasonal observations and day strips to compare winter daylight with fall or spring and support a pattern claim.
<!-- skel -->
- **Time:** 30 to 35 minutes a day (data/plans/frames.csv); 1 row below to sample.
<!-- /skel -->

| Day | Code | Part (own words) | The item, answer and wrong answers (error letter) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 | 1-ESS1-2; observing to collect comparison data, ESS1.B, patterns | Observe seasonal records, compare relative daylight and support the seasonal relationship. | **24 minutes:** six for individual paper recording and eighteen across six group turns. Provide unfamiliar Boston strips for October 20, 2025, January 20, 2026, and April 20, 2026, with season-labeled sunrise and sunset pictograms. Children observe and mark each light segment before comparing. Beside the strips, supply an October 20 evening-only report saying "light at 5:30", labeled "invented for practice". Read: "What pattern do these records show about winter daylight compared with fall and spring? Point to the record or records that show it." Leave the evening report on the table as one of the records, with no hint about its use. Children mark both comparisons and point to their evidence, then give one brief reason during their group turn. Key: winter's light segment is shorter than fall's and spring's. Wrong: all have equal daylight (A); winter has more (B); pointing only to the evening report as whole-day evidence (C); the dark segment is daylight (D). A claim that winter has more, with the dark part given as the reason, is D; with no reason about shading, B. Missing evidence alone is unclassified. | section 8.1 |

**Schedule for 30 children.** Launch three minutes, paper six, six three-minute groups eighteen, collection and cleanup three: 30 minutes. Each child has about 36 seconds for pointing and a reason. Waiting children independently match familiar cards and draw reasons, without helping the observed group. No reteaching occurs on day 64.

**Look-fors, four points.** Award one point each for recording the observed light segments from all three dated records, comparing winter with fall using those segments, comparing winter with spring using them, and supporting the seasonal pattern by pointing to the full-day strips and not to the evening report. All four require observation with the daylight idea on a new case, so 100 percent integrates practice and idea. Use the consensus model to organize reasoning, without displaying solved assessment comparisons. Meets: "This winter light part is shorter than both these parts. Fall and spring have more daylight in these records." Does not: "The dark part is daylight, so winter, with the longest dark part, has the most daylight" (D). Do not also label that same reasoning B.

**Feedback and scoring.** After day 64, allow 25 teacher minutes to annotate each child's paper and record with picture-linked written feedback. Day 65 gives a chance to revise before final scores are entered in 15 teacher minutes afterward. Preserve the original observations alongside revisions. Day 65's fresh performance remains unscored. Day 67 reuses the record without another score on old work.

**Access.** Read every prompt aloud; every child marks pictures on paper. Accept pointing, dictation, enlarged strips and tactile boundaries. Neutral descriptions identify the shading, dates and labels without giving comparisons.
