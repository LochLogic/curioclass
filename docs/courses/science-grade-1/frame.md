# Course frame: Science, Grade 1

Course id science-k5, grade 1. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8, each opening on an anchor phenomenon or design problem: the sky first (it starts the year-long
  daylight record), then plant and animal parts and the year's sketch-and-build design, young like their parents,
  sound, light, a message device that uses both, parents and young, and the daylight record read in spring.
- **Days.** 68: 62 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 4, 2 after unit 8) and 2 respond days (one
  after each check). Science shares the grade 1 day with reading, math and social studies, so the course assumes
  two lessons a week of 30 to 35 minutes (about 65 minutes a week) across about 34 weeks (a school year of about 36
  weeks, of which about 34 carry science lessons here), not the full-year
  yardstick of 160. Unit days follow the rows' load: unit 2 (a core idea and a design, two rows, one of them
  engineering) and unit 5 (two light investigations) carry the most; the reading row (unit 7) and the daylight
  comparison (unit 8, mostly recorded across the year in two-minute looks) carry the least.
- **Split unit.** Unit 8 owns s1-daylight-seasons, which needs a record across the year (the row: "opens in fall
  and closes in spring"). Its first day is taught at the end of unit 1 (about week 4), where the class starts the
  daylight chart; the monthly look (light or dark at about 5:30 in the evening, from two or three optional family
  reports, beside a monthly light-and-dark day strip drawn from the town's public sunrise and sunset table) is a
  two-minute routine outside the lesson count; its other
  4 days come in spring. The day is counted once, in unit 8.
- **Standard.** The 9 grade 1 performance expectations of the Next Generation Science Standards (1-PS4-1 to
  1-PS4-4, 1-LS1-1, 1-LS1-2, 1-LS3-1, 1-ESS1-1, 1-ESS1-2) and the band's second engineering expectation,
  K-2-ETS1-2, done in full here. Each is taught with every part its clarification names and inside its boundary;
  1-LS1-1's two moves (the core idea of outside parts, then the design that copies them) are both taught in unit
  2. The C3 column of the Units table holds, for science, the practices and crosscutting concepts each unit
  develops. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the evidence statements of its expectations and the
  K-2 practice and crosscutting concept elements (Appendices F and G), and reads OpenSciEd elementary (reference
  only, never copied) for order and pacing; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add.
  Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds; BASELINE rule 5: facts as the evidence shows.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Phenomena are
  chosen so a grade 1 class meets them first hand at school (a shadow stick's shadow moving, a rubber-band box
  guitar, a dark box with a peephole, leaves from one tree); where it cannot (the moon at night, a turtle pulling
  into its shell, a bird feeding its chicks), the stand-in is an open or public-domain photograph or video
  (NASA, National Park Service, US Fish and Wildlife Service, NOAA), adapted with a credit line. The texts for
  unit 7 are Curio Class's own short read-alouds and captioned pages. No trade book, paid video or library visit.
- **Materials: substitutes, then no-kit.** Every investigation has a household stand-in where safe and a no-kit
  data path otherwise (a class video or photo set with its data on a chart, said plainly to be weaker). Expected
  kit, all classroom-cheap: a shadow stick (a pencil in clay), flashlights, a shoebox, rubber bands, paper cups,
  string, rice, wax paper, clear plastic, cardboard, plastic or edge-taped mirrors, craft materials for the
  designs, leaves gathered from one tree. Our own online tools may later serve as simulations (a sky-at-night
  view for the stars, for example); each unit plan lists any it wants (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13), with a paper
  path beside it and no student account.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 1 students are early readers. Science words are taught as spoken and
  visual language (said aloud, shown with objects, labelled on pictures); no student is asked to decode them
  alone. Students write a sentence with a frame from unit 5 on (s1-see-with-light); before that the teacher
  writes or children label drawings.
- **The storyline in every unit.** The unit opens on its anchor; the class says what it notices and wonders and
  builds a question board; each lesson answers a question the last one raised; the unit closes by explaining the
  anchor (an evidence-based account, units 3 and 5) or with a design that works (units 2 and 6). The class model
  or chart is revised when new evidence comes in. Each unit also ends with a short individual task (talk, draw and
  point, a sentence with a frame from unit 5 on) on a phenomenon or problem the class has not seen, using a practice
  with the unit's idea and crosscutting concept, inside the unit's days.
- **Practices across the year.** Asking questions (units 1, 7); developing and using models (unit 2, the sketch);
  planning and carrying out investigations (units 4, 5, with students now saying what they will watch for and
  keeping one thing the same); analysing data (units 3, 8); using measurement (unit 3 by ordering, unit 6 in
  length units); constructing explanations and designing solutions (units 2, 5, 6); obtaining and communicating
  information (unit 7, more than one source). Crosscutting concepts: patterns (units 1, 3, 7, 8), cause and
  effect (units 4, 5, 6), structure and function (introduced unit 2), systems (unit 2). Nature of science: unit 1
  (science assumes natural events happen in patterns that can be predicted), unit 3 (science knowledge rests on
  evidence), unit 5 (scientists use tools and investigations to find out).
- **Safety.** A one-page course safety sheet for the year (never look at the sun, never shine a light in eyes,
  sounds kept at classroom level, rubber bands held away from faces, no handling wild animals, plant parts never
  tasted), and every hands-on day names its hazards and controls.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Units 3 and 7 (young like their
  parents; parents and young) need a reader who knows adoptive, foster and separated families; every hands-on
  unit needs a science teacher's safety read before the course is called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3). Who, and any
  pay, is Cory's.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 4 (2 days, mid-year).** A short talk, draw and point check on cases not seen before: where the sun
  will be on a picture of a shadow stick later in the day; a drawn design copying a new animal part (a porcupine's
  quills, a duck's feet) with a spoken sentence on how its shape helps, which the teacher writes down; an "alike but
  not exactly" claim from two new photograph pairs, a parent plant with its young plants and a parent animal with
  its young; what is making the sound in a picture of a plucked guitar string
  and how to stop it. The teacher records what each child can do.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Re-teach what the check shows (often the shape-helps-function
  sentence and the cause of sound), in small groups, ending with a fresh short task.
- **After unit 8 (2 days, end of year).** Check on new cases: which of four new materials will make a shadow and
  why; why a cat in a dark room cannot be seen until a lamp is on (a written sentence with a frame); whether a
  shown message device works, from a test result; the signal and response in a new animal source; a daylight
  chart from another place read for its season pattern.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teach the gaps, in small groups, before the record goes on to
  the grade 2 teacher with what the respond day changed.

honours it. Nothing in grade 1 science leans on social studies.

Weeks are counted at two lessons a week, with the mid-year check and respond day included; the scope and sequence
sets the exact dates.

## For the lead

1. **Time a week.** The frame assumes two science lessons a week of 30 to 35 minutes (68 days). Pick: keep it. If
   schools give more time, unit 2 (parts and the design) and unit 5 (light) take the extra days first.
2. **The split unit.** Unit 8's first day is taught at the end of unit 1 to start the daylight chart, as the row
   asks; the chart records light or dark at about 5:30 in the evening (family reports) beside a monthly day strip,
   because going-home time is light all year at most schools (settled in the fix pass of 2026-11-14, with the row's
   notes). Pick: keep it, with the scope and sequence showing that day in both places and counting it once in unit 8.
3. **Seasonal order.** Units 1 and 3 lean on fall (shadows, leaves on the ground) and unit 7 on spring nests; a
   school in the southern hemisphere or a warm climate keeps the order but uses its own season words in unit 8.
   Pick: keep the order.
4. **Materials cost.** Pick: a stated ceiling of about $40 a class for the year beyond what a grade 1 room already
   has, each unit plan giving its own cost. A money question, so Cory's to confirm.
