## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why is our shadow stick's shadow on one side in the morning and the other side after lunch?"
- **Hook, day 1.** Compare the pencil's live shadow with the teacher's dated morning tracing, after lunch. Ask the compelling question before offering an explanation. Use the schoolyard; windows may block later sunlight.
- **Closing explanation, day 6.** Children dictate that changing apparent sun position accompanies changing shadow direction, using repeated fixed-stick marks. Days 2 and 3 teach that sunlight comes from the side opposite the shadow. Moon sequences show another apparent path; clear-sky records distinguish daytime and nighttime star visibility.
- **Teaching moves.** Model dating one mark on day 1. Children choose landmarks and record day 2 times; adults read clocks. Its three two-minute looks and 24-minute lesson total 30 minutes. Later, ask which observation supports each prediction rather than supply it.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


- **Question board.** Group notices as shadow changes, sky paths and visibility. Where will the next shadow fall (day 2)? Does it repeat (day 3)? Does the moon follow a path too (day 4)? What else shows at night (day 5)? Will these patterns hold elsewhere (raised day 5, answered day 6)? Day 7 transfers to an unfamiliar hillside observation station.
- **Limits.** No rotation, orbits, moon phases, constellation changes, compass bearings or measured shadow lengths. Predict from supplied observations, never assume the moon appears at the same clock time daily. Other stars remain present when unseen; night visibility needs a clear, sufficiently dark sky. No engineering expectation is claimed.
- **Carried: patterns.** Day 1 draws the stick and first shadow. Day 2 adds ordered marks from the fixed stick; day 3 adds repeated evidence. Day 4 adds the moon's apparent path. Day 5 revises visibility with daytime moon evidence. Day 6 reaches consensus from the other-site records: repeated observations support predictions. Keep this chart available on day 7.

**Day 7 schedule, 30 children:** three minutes to launch, six minutes for paper responses, six three-minute groups of five for individual pointing and talk, three minutes to collect. Each item uses two paper minutes and six minutes distributed across the group turns, totaling 24 minutes. No product from teaching days contributes points. Waiting children use familiar unscored cards independently. Teacher records each child's marks, path drawing and evidence, not a group answer. Each child tells one item's reason aloud, rotating S, M and T across the group; the other two items' evidence comes from the marked paper. The teacher writes that spoken reason on the child's response sheet in checklist words or a short scribe note. Day 7 collects work without scores, feedback or reteaching. Written feedback, on the sheet holding the spoken reasons too, follows after class; revision and scoring follow on day 8.

**Daily evidence and section exits.** The written days and items.csv (ids s01-X) now hold the exits as taught; where a case here differs (day 4: a moon strip and a next dot, not one paths question), the written day governs and this text records the plan-stage intent. Days 1 to 6 leave an individual picture mark and notebook drawing tied to that day's goal; hear a rotating group of about six reasons daily. Day 1 records the puzzling change and a question; day 2 records three marks and a prediction; day 3 compares dates; day 4 traces a moon path; day 5 compares visibility; day 6 tests each predicted pattern. These are formative records, not scores. Day 4's three-minute exit uses a held-back moon sequence and shadow record: "What repeating paths do these records show, and where would each next mark go?" Key: each continues its observed path. It tests section 1.1, with B and A for stationary paths. Day 8's three-minute exit, inside its ten-minute fresh-prediction block, shows unused sun, daytime moon and star records: "Which pattern pictures fit these records, and what pictures should come next?" Key: both apparent paths continue; other stars appear in the clear dark-night record. Use A to D only where the response matches. Every child marks paper as prompts are read aloud; hear six rotating reasons. No exit reuses that day's worked case. Section plans must supply the held-back masters before use.

**Scoring after day 8 revision.** On day 7 children first place time-labeled pictures in the guided chart. Use day and night headings for visibility; include sun absent at night and moon present on daytime and nighttime records. Each item earns three points: describes the pattern, predicts from it, and identifies evidence. For T, the description point rests on the core pattern alone: stars seen on clear dark nights, not in the day. The idea that stars are still present by day, hidden by the bright sky, is weighted lightly: the teacher notes it on the sheet and a disappearance claim (D) gets day 8 feedback, but it never costs a point the core pattern earned. All nine points require analyzing observations or schematic records with ESS1.A and patterns on new cases, exceeding 80 percent. No earlier notebook work is scored. Look for movement across a fixed view rather than a recited direction word. Judge one linked reason per child, heard on day 7 and written on the sheet, and the other items from the marked paper; on day 8 the child re-tells, points or redraws before the score, and the teacher notes the revised reason. Item S meets: "It went from the tree to the roof to the hill; sheets 1 and 2 show it. Next it is lower, over the hill." Does not: "It stays there" (A). Item M meets: "It goes from the pole past the barn toward the ridge. Sheet 4 shows that, so the covered picture should be lower above the ridge." Does not: "It stays there" (B). Item T meets: "Night records show stars, so the next clear night should too. The blank day record means we cannot see them, not that they stopped existing." Does not: "Stars are in the day pictures too" or "There will be no stars next night." "Stars stop existing each morning" with correct pictures still meets; note D for feedback. Accept pointing to evidence, drawing or communication symbols with the same reasoning demand.

**Accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud. Each child marks pictures on paper; offer enlarged or raised-line records, verbal descriptions of positions, adapted marking or a scribe. Do not require handwriting, independent reading or home observations.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

- **Tools:** None, days 1 to 8. Fixed observations and paper fit the job; no key tool, simulation or tool request is needed. All investigations are feasible without digital equipment. One shared display is optional.
- **Tiers and cost.** Household pencil and clay serve shadows, days 1 and 2; naked-eye moon viewing serves day 4. Dated paper observations replace either and supply stars, days 5 and 6; this no-kit route is weaker because children cannot collect the original evidence. No simulation is required. About $2 a class, within the $40 yearly allowance.
- **Days 1 and 2, per five children:** one pencil, clay base, paper, chalk and five drawing pencils. The adult secures the blunt pencil upright and marks the base location. Never look at the sun; keep eyes on ground shadows. No optics. Keep clay and chalk from mouths; avoid chalk dust and check ingredient allergies. Wash hands. No goggles are needed for stationary observation; omit dusty chalk and use paper if dust rises.
- **Day 4, per five:** one timed moon strip, five logs and pencils. The adult selects a view with the sun screened by a building; otherwise use records. No binoculars or telescopes. Stay within the supervised accessible route. No protective eyewear is needed for this screened view.
- **The day before each outdoor day:** check weather, route, allergies and sightlines; prepare indoor records. Stop for unsafe weather, glare or an obstructed route. Reserve three minutes for collection and handwashing on days 1 and 2. Days 3 and 5 to 8 use paper only.
