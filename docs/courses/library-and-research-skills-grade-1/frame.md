# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, grade 1

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-1` (catalog id `library-1`), grade 1. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the kindergarten pilot's shape and fixes; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one year of library and research skills, each unit one full inquiry cycle (ask, find, share,
  reflect) on a small question, in the order a grade 1 class needs the moves: saying what we know and choosing the
  right kind of book first, then finding the answering page in a book or on a page the teacher opened, then finding
  facts as a team and saying which book they came from, then the year's product, a class information book with a
  star and a wish on each page.
- **Days.** 30: 25 in units, 2 course-level check days (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 3 respond days (1 after
  the mid-year check, 2 after the year-end check). One session of about 25 minutes, one session a week, for 30
  weeks (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: the K to 2 default of 30 sessions of 25 minutes, about 12.5 hours a year, at
  the one-a-week end of the settled "one or two sessions a week"). The 25 minutes hold getting to the carpet or the
  book corner, the session, putting books back and collecting sheets; a lesson plan budgets about 3 minutes at each
  end for that, so about 19 working minutes, and every assessment form below is costed against those 19 minutes for
  a class of 30. One adult runs the room (the classroom teacher or a librarian); while the adult checks a small
  group, the rest of the class works on a book-corner task it has already practiced. Eight grade 1 rows, two to a
  unit, fit 25 unit days: units 1 to 3 get 6 days each, and unit 4 gets 7 because it holds the year's product (a
  drafted page, a star and a wish, and a revision before its share). Each unit's days include its end-of-unit
  assessment and its respond day (no unit is over 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment). These are course days: with 30
  days over 30 weeks, course day n falls in week n of docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, and the scope and sequence places
  them on school days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day):
  Understanding is the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through the move on an invented or read-aloud case,
  holding the pictured skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, repeated tries of the same move on new
  cases, with feedback: fill a know and wonder sheet on a new picture, find the answering page by its heading, copy
  a title and author onto a sticky note); Application is the inquiry day (the class runs part of the cycle on its
  host question and makes its drawing or page). Fluency is small in grade 1: one practice day in each of units 1 to 3
  (course days 2, 8 and 17), plus the five-minute opening retrieval on later days; unit 4's star-and-wish pair work
  is an inquiry day, because each maker revises a page (GLOSSARY, teaching day). Every unit's day 1 is a
  show-me day with the hook and the compelling question, and nothing else.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words
  only, data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone).
  Ownership rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone.
  Grade 1 owns one id, AASL.I.A.2 (g1-what-i-know, unit 1), and carries ten as "with help" contributions, each named
  in its unit's What students learn with its owner (AASL.V.C.1, owned in kindergarten, comes back as review). Skills:
  the eight progression rows, two a unit. Check set: the Empire State Information Fluency Continuum, PK to 2 band
  file, grade 1 column and its bold priority skills (read cell by cell for this frame, later.csv L103; result in the
  Check set read bullet); the "Check set adds" cells name what each unit plan should weigh from it. Values: K to 5
  case sets stay neutral (no politics, elections, religion, identity groups or conflict; Cory, 2026-11-21).
- **Base text.** None. Every day is Curio Class's own. Curio Class writes: four short read-aloud information books
  (one a unit) and a short story book for unit 1's sort, the know and wonder sheet, story-or-information cover
  strips, three short contents-and-headings mini-books for unit 2, pretend screen cards for the stop rule, the three
  pictured group-job cards, cover cards for copying, the class information book page template and the star-and-wish
  frame, one pictured skill card a unit, and the invented "pretend class" pages used in checks. Any information books
  the room already has may be added. No day needs a trade book, a paid source, a library visit or a database (no
  library access is assumed; Cory, 2026-11-11).
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from another grade 1 course, named by its row id for the
  teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question with a
  closed source set of Curio Class read-alouds and pictures) so the unit runs when no core task is in step or at home
  (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence records both.
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question, a wonder from the class board, and closes with
  the share step and the self-check: the child points at the pictured steps (ask, find, share, reflect), says which
  step they did well and one thing to do better next time, and adds one new "I wonder" to the board (the continuum's
  grade 1 reflect cells: one's own strengths and a goal, and "what do I wonder about now?"). Grade 1 keeps the
  kindergarten cycle card's four pictures and step words. The lens's judge step sits inside find: choosing the
  information book over the story book (unit 1) and checking a "know" against the book (unit 1's know and wonder
  sheet); use is the found fact drawn and labeled at share. Judge becomes its own picture, "check", in grade 2
  (g2-two-books-differ).
- **Evidence.** Daily: the teacher's observation of the one named skill per child on a class checklist kept by the
  teacher and thrown away at the end of the unit, plus the child's drawing, sheet or spoken answer (APPROACH.md,
  K to 2). End-of-unit assessment: a task on a case not seen before, in the kindergarten forms whose time a unit plan
  shows: a circle round (each child takes a turn, about 30 seconds a child, about 15 minutes for 30); a whisper walk
  (the teacher walks round and each child whispers the answer, about 10 to 20 seconds a child, so no child repeats a
  neighbor); a station of three to five children (about 1.5 to 2.5 minutes a group) while the others do a practiced
  book-corner task; a scanned pointing item (about 2 minutes); and, new in grade 1, a sheet item the whole class
  answers at once on paper, the teacher reading every word aloud, by circling, drawing, labeling or copying (about 4
  to 10 minutes), marked on the checklist from the page afterwards. Each unit names its forms and total below; no
  unit assessment or course-level check spills into its respond day. It is scored on a day after the move was
  practiced, never on a show-me day. Sheets go home after marking, so no per-child record outlives its unit. The
  respond day's groups come from the checklist by the step missed.
- **Reading level.** Grade 1 children are early readers. Every source and every check item is read aloud; any
  student page is a picture with a label or one short sentence; the print a child handles alone is a bin label, a
  cover, a heading, a page number, a title and an author to copy, and their own labeled drawing and sentence (written
  or dictated).
- **Paper path.** The whole course is the paper path: printed read-alouds, mini-books, sheets, cover cards, pretend
  screen cards, the class book corner, sticky notes and crayons. No day needs a device, an internet connection or a
  student account.
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared class device (the teacher's) is used in unit 2 for g1-device-stop-rule:
  the teacher opens one page and children use only that page, then practice the stop rule on pretend cases. No owned
  code needs technology; the carried AASL.VI.A.1 is met in full on the paper path, where the pretend screen cards
  hold the same cases and the teacher opens a printed "page" in a card frame. A projector may show a read-aloud page
  bigger; the printed page does the same job in full.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond books, paper, crayons and sticky notes, no field work and no trip, so
  no laboratory or field guide applies. The screen-safety side is the stop rule and never typing a name or school,
  checked by the sensitivity read (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, items 2 and 3).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned
  (Cory, 2026-11-14). No unit carries cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content: the case sets
  are sunflowers and snails, tree roots, bird nests and feet, and how animal parents feed and keep their young warm.
  What the agent studies first: APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground" items 1 (neutral K to 5 case sets), 2 (the stop rule,
  unit 2), 3 (privacy: never typing a name or school, unit 2), 4 (honest use without shaming, units 3 and 4), 5
  (whose voices a source set shows, C12) and, for unit 4's star and wish, kind feedback on a classmate's work; and,
  for unit 2's device day, a free K to 2 online-safety presentation from a national child-safety nonprofit, listed at https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/resources
  (listing opened 2026-11-26: it names the presentation as a free download in English and Spanish but does not
  state its rule, and the presentation file was not read, so the stop rule rests on nothing in it). No organization's name goes on a
  student, teacher or family page.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30: unit 1 about $6
  (know and wonder sheets, cover strips, the two read-alouds), unit 2 about $6 (mini-books, contents sheets, pretend
  screen cards), unit 3 about $5 (job cards, cover cards) plus sticky notes, about $3, reused all year, unit 4 about $7
  (page templates on heavier paper, the star-and-wish frames, a binding of yarn or staples); about $27 a year. The
  shared device is one the room already has; no day buys one.
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The PK to 2 band file's grade 1 column and priority boxes, every indicator
  row (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-pk-2\esifc-priority-pk-2.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, ids and meanings only, nothing copied). Met by the rows: sharing what is known about a topic and
  checking new ideas against it, and "I wonder" questions with the class (g1-what-i-know); books as fiction or
  nonfiction, the fiction and nonfiction sort, and choosing sources with guidance (g1-story-or-fact-book); finding
  facts with help to answer a basic question and reading information from pictures and labels
  (g1-find-in-a-book); crediting the author and title of a source used, with help (g1-author-and-title); expressing
  new learning by drawing and emergent writing, and a group product for classmates and families (g1-class-book);
  compliments and kind advice as modeled by the adult (g1-star-and-wish); contributing in group talk
  (g1-group-helpers); staying safe online and using only the pages the teacher provides (g1-device-stop-rule). Met
  without a row change: one's own strengths and a goal, and "what do I wonder about now?" (the self-check, The
  inquiry arc); simple inquiry to answer questions (each child finds the fact for their own page, unit 4, at
  with-help depth). Differences, each recorded in its row note in data/progressions/library.csv: the bold skill on how
  the library orders fiction and picture books (met by bin labels here; shelf order with g2-catalog-lookup in grade
  2); the bold skill on a username that protects private information (Curio Class sets up no student account, so the
  move is never typing a name or school, g1-device-stop-rule). Left out: fact and opinion, opinion and claim, main
  idea and supporting details, story points of view, predictions and multicultural texts (ELA grade 1: e1-opinion,
  e1-author-reasons, e1-main-topic, e1-characters; STANDARD.md section 4; C12's voices record covers who appears in
  the source sets); print against digital books and the catalog's purpose (the catalog comes in grade 2,
  g2-catalog-lookup, one grade on); technology tools for products (no device is assumed); design thinking and the
  social and emotional cells (science, computer science and SEL). No row is two or more grades from its cell.
