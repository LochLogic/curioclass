# Scope and sequence: Theater, grades 9 to 12

theater-grades-9-to-12, grades 9 to 12, Levels 1 to 4, draft 2026-11-26, fix pass 2026-11-26 (docs/reviews/sequence-theater-grades-9-to-12-fixes-2026-11-26.md)

**Mixed-level classes.** A school that runs two levels in one room keeps each student on their own level's ids and products: the warm-ups, shared rehearsal and critique days are shared; the problems, the guides and the scored items are the level's own. A student new to theater starts at Level 1 in any grade; Levels 2 and 3 open with a review unit that is also the placement.

## How the course runs

- **Days.** 640 course days, 160 a level: Level 1 is band days 1 to 160, Level 2 161 to 320, Level 3 321 to 480, Level 4 481 to 640. One 45-minute lesson a day, five a week, 32 weeks a level. Each level holds five units (156 days), two course-level check days and two respond days.
- **school_day** is left blank and counts inside each level, since the levels are taken in separate years (ARTS-FIX lead call 5): the course meets every school day, so band day n is school day ((n - 1) mod 160) + 1 of its level, and its week is (school_day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit and check sits in the frame's weeks by that formula (checked 2026-11-26); calendar.csv is regenerated by the lead and is not re-counted here (lead call 8). frame.py spreads the band over four school years, one level a year; this file names weeks as level weeks.
- **Standards by level** (STANDARD.md 3.2). Level 1 owns the 24 `.Ia` to `.Ic` theater ids, Level 2 the 24 `.IIa` to `.IIc` ids, Level 3 the 24 `.IIIa` to `.IIIc` ids: 72 in all, each taught and assessed in its own level. Level 4 owns no id; it assesses the Level 3 ids again on new, self-directed work, named in each Level 4 unit with its owning unit. No grade 8 id is claimed, by the bridge or anywhere.
- **Kinds.** Understanding days are hooks, demonstrations with a step card and mini-lessons; Fluency days are timed practice runs of a technique already taught, with a partner's look-for, and never draft or revise (a Level 4 Fluency day is a skill run, then a second round on a new prompt of the same skill); Application days are rehearsals (the production run-throughs among them, as rehearsal with choice), design work, critiques and revisions; Check days hold the mid-unit, end-of-unit and course-level assessments; Respond days are the second try at what was marked not yet.
- **Notes.** Every working day's note names its space and equipment (the clear floor, the floor rules, the free path) and any sensitivity flag the frame sets; no supplies, tools or safety columns are used (arts).

## Logic model

- **Inputs.** A 45-minute lesson five days a week; step cards; scene and problem cards; public domain and student-written scripts; found materials; the free path named in every note.
- **Activities.** Technique taught in steps; works studied with their time and place; rehearsal with a critique and a revision; a showing each unit; a production each level (units 5, 10, 15 and 20).
- **Outputs.** A portfolio of five pieces a level, each with a one-line reflection; a rehearsal log; two marks a unit per student on the main look-for (the baseline look and the end-of-unit).
- **Outcome claimed.** Met marks on the level's 24 ids (Level 4: the Level 3 ids met again on new work) and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result.

## Level 1 (band days 1 to 160): Proficient, the .I ids

### Unit 1. Bridge: back to the stage

*What does a company need to know before it can rehearse a play together?*

- **Days.** Band days 1 to 12 (12 days), level days 1 to 12, weeks 1 to 3.
- **Arc.** Twelve bridge days reteach the grade 8 techniques each Level 1 unit leans on: the warm-up routine, stage areas and blocking, script markup, objective, obstacle and tactic, the story map, the critique protocol, group agreements and the rehearsal log, a prop sketch and a three-cue sheet, the audience journal and the two-ties card. The class rehearses a Curio Class original two-person scene and shows it in pairs on day 12.
- **Order.** Day 1 opens with the hook and holds the readiness check in its last ten minutes, so the results choose which bridge days each student needs most. Each technique is taught before the scene uses it; the critique (day 10) and revision (day 11) come before the showing.
- **Assessment.** Readiness check on day 1 (grade 8 rows, named in the note). Day 12 holds the `end` slot for the bridge's readiness record only: the frame gives the bridge no end-of-unit assessment and no id, and the record sets the supports in units 2 to 5, so no respond day is held.
- **Candidate settled.** Kept, no added day: the readiness check on day 1 is written in the words of sequence-write.md rule 2 (rows by id in the note, last ten minutes after the hook, its results setting day-by-day support).
- **Short path (80-day).** Keeps 6 days: 1, 5, 8, 10 to 12; the other 6 merge into a kept day, as each day's note names.

### Unit 2. Where a play comes from

*How do relationships between characters drive a play, and how do a play's roots shape how a company could stage it?*

- **Days.** Band days 13 to 46 (34 days), level days 13 to 46, weeks 3 to 10.
- **Arc.** The unit joins two threads. The first half works TH:Pr4.1.Ia, TH:Cn11.2.Ia and TH:Cn11.2.Ib on the excerpt of Spreading the News, toward the staged excerpt with its relationship map, with a critique, a revision and a final observation round before the mid-unit assessment. The second half opens TH:Cr1.1.Ia and TH:Cr2.1.Ia with its own hook and builds the concept page, again through a critique and a revision, and the unit closes with two end days and a respond day.
- **Order.** Technique and the look at the studied works come first in each half, so the making that needs them follows. The first-half thread is assessed at mid-unit while it is fresh, which leaves the end days free for the second-half thread and the parts of the first-half product revised since. Day 2 carries the retrieval so day 1 stays clean for the hook.
- **Assessment.** Mid-unit on day 29 (the research note on the play's roots and the staged excerpt with its relationship map (th-g9-story)); end of unit on days 44 to 45: day 44 the staged excerpt for the class, the look-for the relationship that turns the scene made visible in the blocking; day 45 a sitting item on an unseen description of a devised scene (how its makers told the story, compared with a scripted one, TH:Cn11.2.Ia); the kept research note (TH:Cn11.2.Ib, the mid-unit mark updated only where the respond day reworked it) and the concept page (TH:Cr1.1.Ia a stated look tied to research; TH:Cr2.1.Ia one way history or culture changed the first idea, with the reason) collected and scored after the session. Response time: the respond day 46, a second try at the parts marked not yet.
- **Candidate settled.** Kept, no added day (day 16): a third storytelling tradition from Asia or Africa, described and linked from the community's own published page, picked in the unit plan with its rights line.
- **Sensitivity (in the notes).** Spreading the News is a comedy of gossip; no student is cast to type. - **Short path (80-day).** Keeps 17 days: 13 to 14, 16, 18, 25 to 26, 28 to 31, 33, 40 to 41, 43 to 46; the other 17 merge into a kept day, as each day's note names.

## Level 2 (band days 161 to 320): Accomplished, the .II ids

### Unit 6. Review and placement

*What does a Level 1 company already know how to do?*

- **Days.** Band days 161 to 166 (6 days), level days 1 to 6, weeks 1 to 2.
- **Arc.** Six review days retrieve every thread of the level before and hold the placement. A student who took the previous level here sat its year-end check as the written component; a student new to the level (from another school or after a gap) sits a parallel form of that check in the first 35 minutes of day 162 while the class stages the scene (day 161 on the short path); these days add the four-part practical component (a performed scene with a character explanation, a design solution in a cued run, a rehearsal record of feedback and revision, a source note), scored on the previous level's guides, and record readiness by thread.

## Shorter path

**The 80-day path** (a semester or a rotation; frame "The short path", STANDARD.md 3.2). Every unit and every id stay. Fluency days fold into the warm-ups. Days kept per unit: Level 1, 6, 17, 17, 17 and 19; Levels 2 and 3, 3, 17, 17, 17 and 22; Level 4, 19, 19, 12, 11 and 15; each level's two checks keep their check day and respond day: 80 a level.
