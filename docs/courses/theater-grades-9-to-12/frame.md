# Course frame: Theater, grades 9 to 12

Course `theater-grades-9-to-12` (catalogue id `theater-9-12`), a band course for high school run as four levels one after another (Level 1 Proficient, Level 2 Accomplished, Level 3 Advanced at honors depth, Level 4 optional, a production year the student leads). A level is not a grade: a student new to theater starts at Level 1 in any grade (STANDARD.md 3.2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one level at a time, and each level's part of this file is that level's frame.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 20: five in each level. Levels 1 to 3 share one shape, so a technique and a thread come back one step
  harder a level later in the same slot: an opening unit (the Level 1 bridge, or the Level 2 and 3 review that
  doubles as placement), a story and concept unit, a character and audience unit, a design and critique unit, and a
  world and production unit that ends the level with a production for an audience the class chooses. Each of the
  four main units joins two progression threads, one of creating or performing and one of responding or connecting,
  so every unit makes, studies other artists' work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Level 4 has five units that are
  the phases of one project the student leads (research, concept, role, criticism, production), with checkpoints as
  check days and critique on respond days (APPROACH "High school levels and music"). Choice grows by level: Level 1
  sets the problems, Level 2 lets the student pick among them and research, Level 3 has the student set the problem,
  Level 4 is the student's own production. Level 4 company model (fix pass 2026-11-26): the class works as companies
  of about five (one company in a small class); units 16 to 19 stay each student's own work, with the company as
  cast and readers; in unit 20 each company stages one short piece (about 5 minutes) built from its members' plays,
  each student leading one creative role and its research and running the element they design in the cued run;
  each student's full design folio and its defense are for their own play, on paper; the company chooses the
  audience.
- **Days.** 640: 160 a level for four levels. Each level: 156 days in its five units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One lesson of about 45 minutes a day, five a
  week, 32 weeks (STANDARD.md 3.4: a high school level is 160 course days of 45 minutes, about the 120 hours of the
  national Proficient descriptor). The 45 minutes hold set-up (chairs and desks to the walls, about 3 minutes), a
  warm-up (5), the teaching and rehearsal, and strike and clean-up with collecting the rehearsal logs (about 4
  minutes; 6 on a set, prop or light-test day); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days
  include its end-of-unit assessment and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessment. Unit lengths follow the
  rows' load: the world and production unit is longest in Levels 1 to 3 (six ids and a production with its
  rehearsal weeks); the Level 1 bridge is 12 days, the most STANDARD.md 3.2 allows (7.5 percent of the year, inside
  the bar's 10 percent limit on days outside the standards); the review units are 6. National time figure
  (STANDARD.md 3.4): the theater association gives no weekly figure, so none is stated and no gap is named. These are
  course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places each level's 160 on one school year, course day
  n of a level in that level's week n / 5, rounded up.
- **The short path.** A school that runs theater as a semester or a rotation keeps every unit and every id in 80 days
  by merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.2, 60 hours). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look at a work that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one rehearsal day with the critique; a revision day; the showing or sitting (the check); one respond
  day. Fluency days fold into the warm-ups. Days on that path: Level 1, units of 6, 17, 17, 17 and 19; Levels 2 and
  3, units of 3, 17, 17, 17 and 22; Level 4, units of 19, 19, 12, 11 and 15; each level's two checks keep 1 check
  day and 1 respond day. The bridge's 6 short-path days are 7.5 percent of 80. A fold-in never takes a critique, a
  feedback, a revision or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, theater performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The levels rule binds
  (STANDARD.md 3.2): Level 1 claims only the 24 `.Ia` to `.Ic` ids, Level 2 only the 24 `.IIa` to `.IIc` ids, Level
  3 only the 24 `.IIIa` to `.IIIc` ids: 72 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its
  own level. Level 4 owns no id: it assesses the Level 3 ids again on new, self-directed work, named in each Level 4
  unit with its owner. No grade 8 id is claimed, by the bridge or anywhere. Every level touches all four artistic
  processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at least two. In
  the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor
  standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Each id's action is checked against its national standard
  text, not only the words file (the pilot's VA:Re7.1.Ka lesson). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's
  error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule
  (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21);
  each level's scope and sequence still states its id count (24) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Entry and placement.** A student new to theater starts at Level 1, whatever their grade. Each level requires the
  one before or a placement: the Level 2 and Level 3 review units are the placement, scored against the previous
  level's year-end check. Advanced is claimed by assessed work, not by finishing Level 3; a student who ends Level 3
  at "partly" on an id has Level 4 to reach it. One level is a complete course and is enough for Proficient; the bar
  does not ask a school to offer all four (STANDARD.md 3.2).
- **Mixed-level classes.** A school that runs two levels in one room (common in high school theater) keeps each
  student on their own level's ids and products; the shared rehearsal and warm-up days are shared, the problems and
  the scored items are the level's own. The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Theater is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Scripts students
  perform are public domain (a pre-1930 play or translation), openly licensed, Curio Class originals or written by
  the students (APPROACH "Rights"); recent and living makers' plays and productions are studied from a description in
  our own words with a link to the maker's, company's or theater's page, never a copy or an embed. Each work named
  below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work
  must keep the unit's ids met and the level's range. Each level's works include living makers and more than one
  region and period, and the band reaches Africa, the Americas including Native American theater makers, Asia and
  Europe (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). Curio Class writes every step card, scene card, problem card, record
  sheet, family note, Curio Class original scene and the Curio Class translation of the Fuenteovejuna scenes (unit 15).
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Acting: given circumstances and objectives from the script (Level
  1), a character file joining script clues, research and the director's vision (Level 2), several researched
  techniques chosen and defended for one role (Level 3), a self-chosen role with a technique log (Level 4). Staging:
  standard conventions (stage areas, blocking, cheating out, levels) (Level 1), a team interpretation tested in
  rehearsal (Level 2), a style, genre and form the ensemble invents (Level 3), a production the student directs or
  designs (Level 4). Design: a technology's effect on design researched and one light, sound or set element tested
  (Level 1), a design that carries a concept (Level 2), a full design across every element, defended (Level 3).
  Writing about theater: criteria drafted from a response (Level 1), criteria read more than one way and backed with
  proof (Level 2), a critique organized by history and culture (Level 3), a researched critique of a play of the
  student's choosing (Level 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown in
  named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day; a connecting id that asks for a study lives here, one that asks students to
  make or perform lives in an application day and is scored as making). Fluency: short repeated practice with
  feedback: a warm-up sequence, a scene beat run several times with one change, a cue sheet called in order, a
  ten-minute concept sketch; in 9 to 12 it is the five-minute warm-up and one or two fluency days a unit. Application:
  rehearsing, devising, designing or drafting a written piece with choice; the second application day holds the
  unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the revision after it is application too. Check: a showing marked
  live, or a sitting with unseen items. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a
  second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique,
  revision, check, respond, twice over in a unit with a mid-unit assessment.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an item or a look-for, named id by id in the unit's "End-of-unit evidence"
  line; a row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one item for each id, so it is never the whole
  line. Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet),
  marked live as each group performs; a kept written or drawn product (a concept page, a dramaturgy page, a design
  folio, a rehearsal log, a written critique), drafted, given written feedback on the record sheet and revised on
  earlier days counted in the unit, then scored after the session; and sitting items on an unseen scene (a short
  written description of a staging, a three-minute teacher demonstration or a Curio Class original scene read
  aloud), answered in writing. Each paired unit has a mid-unit assessment, which scores its first row's product, and
  an end-of-unit assessment of two check days, so the teacher never marks a performance and a sitting at once. The
  showing day's 45 minutes: set-up 3; warm-up 5; six groups of five perform for about 5 minutes each (30) while the
  others are the audience; each student's self-check on the guide 3; strike 4. The sitting day: set-up 2; the unseen
  scene (3 to 5); written items (33); collect 3. The teacher keeps a student-by-id observation roster for each unit:
  every student's owned acting, design or production evidence is ticked against their name, not given as a group
  mark. A unit whose showing is in pairs (unit 3) or that needs an individual scripted contribution (unit 5) plays
  shorter excerpts on the showing day (about 2 minutes a pair or a speech), and any pair, role or student not yet
  seen is observed in a final live observation round on the last rehearsal days, before the respond day; these
  rounds are counted in the unit on both the full and short paths. Recording is optional. A student who declines is
  observed live during a scheduled final observation round; the teacher's mark is the evidence (APPROACH "Kept
  work"). An end day holds at most one full performance. No score comes from the day a
  technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days
  (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into
  the portfolio (paper, a file or a photo) with a one-line reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Level 1 assumes no earlier theater learning (STANDARD.md 3.2), and Theater, grades 6
  to 8 is not a core course, so no unit assumes it was taken (lesson 32). The Level 1 bridge (unit 1) teaches the
  grade 8 techniques and words Level 1 needs, as readiness: its readiness check takes the last ten minutes of day 1,
  after the hook, on the grade 8 threads it reteaches (warm-up routine, stage areas and blocking, script markup, the
  critique protocol), and its results set which bridge days each student needs most. A grade 9 student who took
  grade 8 theater the year before meets the same check as retrieval (one grade back). Units 2 to 5 then retrieve the
  bridge's techniques (used earlier the same level) inside day 2's warm-up, so day 1 stays clean for the hook and
  the question. Levels 2, 3 and 4: every row assumes the level before's row in its thread, one level back, so the
  review unit (Levels 2 and 3) or day 2 of each unit (Level 4) opens with a retrieval task; a student placed in from
  outside takes the review unit's placement, which doubles as the readiness check, and a student who comes back
  after a gap of two or more years takes each unit's retrieval as a readiness check with its ten-minute reteach
  (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline look"). The count starts from the level order (Level 1, then 2, 3, 4), never
  from a grade. The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** 9 to 12 has one calendar rule: week 32 is a finals week (CALENDAR.md rule 4). Every level:
  the mid-year check in week 17 (Level 1, course days 81 and 82) or week 16 (Levels 2 to 4, days 77 and 78), and
  the year-end check in week 32 (days 159 and 160), the finals week. Neither is the end of the unit before it: each
  unit ends in its own end-of-unit assessment, and the checks sample earlier units.
- **Grade 12 calendar (lesson 31).** Any level may hold seniors; Level 4 mostly does. The last weeks hold teaching:
  the world and production unit (or Level 4's production unit) runs to week 32, day 158, and the year-end check sits
  in week 32. A school whose seniors leave two weeks early (finishing on school day 150) runs, for those students, a
  ten-day consolidation path in the level's final unit: Level 1's unit 5 in 32 days instead of 42, Level 2's unit 10
  and Level 3's unit 15 in 35 instead of 45, Level 4's unit 20 in 23 instead of 33; the final unit ends on day 148,
  the year-end check falls on day 149 and the respond session on day 150. The scope and sequence names the ten
  consolidation days by the short-path rules (fluency run-throughs folded first); instruction, feedback, critique,
  revision, the checks and the respond day are never folded. This allocation is a planning judgment, not a tested
  delivery claim. A student 18 or over gets the family note themselves and
  gives their own yes to be shown or recorded (AGENTS.md); the school's own rules still apply.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units a course page names: unit 7 (it studies Noh, a tradition with Buddhist roots, by looking
  only), unit 12 (it studies a production that borrowed from Kathakali and Kabuki, and
  the question of borrowing) and unit 14 (Sovereignty: Cherokee removal and a family's violence, hard history). A
  student who does not want to act may take a director, designer, dramaturg or stage manager role in any showing
  that allows one, which keeps the unit's production ids; a role does not by itself show an owned acting id, so the
  acting ids of the character units (3, 8, 13) and unit 5's scripted contribution are observed in a seated, supported
  or small-group live take in a final observation round (a recording only by the student's choice), never a forced
  solo. Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course
  page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that level.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 with a clear floor (desks to the walls, a hall, or a stage when there is one),
  chairs, printed scripts, masking tape for spike marks, found props and fabric, LED flashlights and colored
  cellophane for light tests, the voice and found objects for sound; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a
  free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience and the
  critique partner, scene partners are doubled (a parent reads the other part, or the student plays a monologue
  version), and the showing is to the family or a recorded take. The teacher needs no theater training: each unit's
  teacher page says what to look and listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (printing of scripts and cards, tape, cellophane;
  flashlights and fabric counted once in the level they are first bought), all optional beside the free path:
  Level 1 $5, $20, $20, $35, $40 ($120); Level 2 $5, $20, $20, $25, $40 ($110); Level 3 $5, $20, $20, $30, $40
  ($115); Level 4 $10, $15, $15, $10, $40 ($90). Planning figures, not quotes; the ceiling question is under For
  the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a maker's or theater's page, a still or a step; every
  day runs from the printed step card and scene card without it. The design ids that name technology (TH:Cr1.1.Ib,
  TH:Pr5.1.Ib, TH:Cr1.1.IIb, TH:Pr5.1.IIb, TH:Cr1.1.IIIa, TH:Cr1.1.IIIb, TH:Cr3.1.IIIc) are met with stage technology
  that needs no computer: light from LED flashlights, gels of cellophane and a paper cue sheet called by a student
  operator, sound made live or from the school's one playback device, a set as a scale model of card. A paper-path
  student meets every id in full. Recordings stay on the school's, student's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept
  work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on the floor, the voice, paper scripts, printed cards and found objects; any Curio
  Class tool a unit plan adds (a cue-sheet practice tool, a stage-directions drill, a script-markup page) has a
  printed version that does the same job and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Scripts are read aloud in rehearsal before they are studied alone; a scene for study is at most
  six pages, a research reading at most two pages of Curio Class's own text a day, at the level of the student's ELA
  course. English learners: each unit teaches its six words in depth, with a gesture, a picture or a sound and a
  frame ("My character wants ___ and is stopped by ___." "The light tells the audience ___."), a student may perform
  or show a word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student performed or designed apart from what
  they wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on (movement on a shared floor; prop, set and light work), so every unit gets
  the fresh safety agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against free guides, named here and opened
  by the agent: the Drama and Theater Safety Handbook of Montgomery County Public Schools, Maryland
  (https://www.montgomeryschoolsmd.org/siteassets/district/departments/facilities/safety/drama-and-theater-safety-handbook.pdf),
  a school district's own guide; the "Play It Safe" school performing arts safety manual of British Columbia's
  performing arts safety association with the Vancouver School Board
  (https://actsafe.ca/department/live-events/play-safe-safety-school-theatre-studios-manual/); and the federal
  consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide"
  (https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Guides/hobbies/art-and-craft-safety-guide) for prop and set making.
  The second-model read of 2026-11-25 located the handbook and read its introductory supervision, training and
  housekeeping provisions, and checked the manual's landing page and scope only; neither is the specialist safety
  read, which still opens all three. Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" and marked "from memory" until that read checks
  them: a clear floor, chairs and bags to the walls; soft-soled shoes or bare feet on a clean floor; no lifts, no
  rehearsed falls, no contact stage fights and no real or toy weapons (a weapon in a script is mimed or replaced by a
  word); partner contact only by agreement and never required; voice rest and no forced shouting; props checked by
  the teacher before use (no sharp edges, no glass, no cords long enough to wrap a neck); no open flame, no
  theatrical haze or smoke, no flashing or strobe light and no light shone into eyes; LED flashlights only, no work
  at height, no climbing on set pieces, no hanging or focusing of stage lights by students, and no school stage
  lighting or rigging touched except by the trained adult the school names; blades used by an adult only; paint and
  glue labeled non-toxic; hands washed after glue and found objects. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and
  controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural, Native or hard-history work name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 2 (the
  American Indian Theatre Ensemble, Kiowa and Delaware founder), unit 3 (Twilight: Los Angeles, 1992), unit 4 (The
  Rez Sisters, Cree playwright, and its Cree and Ojibwe trickster), unit 5 (Nigeria's Yoruba setting in The Lion and
  the Jewel; Bengal in Ganashatru), unit 7 (Noh, Japan), unit 8 (Sweat: a factory town's job losses and an assault),
  unit 9 (Where We Belong, Mohegan playwright), unit 10 (Ghana in The Dilemma of a Ghost; Philippine community
  theater), unit 12 (Japan in Ninagawa Macbeth; Kathakali and Kabuki in Les Atrides), unit 14 (Sovereignty, Cherokee
  Nation), unit 15 (a global issue the class picks), unit 16 (Native Earth Performing Arts) and every Level 4 unit's
  self-chosen work.
  No student plays a character by an accent, a skin color or a stereotype; a tradition's form is learned from the
  community's own published teaching or a named practitioner's words, never by costume or invented imitation
  (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). Every task that ties theater to a student's own beliefs or life (units 3, 5,
  8, 10, 13, 15 and Level 4) offers an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Each
  section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its
  teacher page.
- **Content notes to families.** A studied work gets a content note to families (to the student at 18 or over) when
  its subject includes violence against a person, a killing, sexual violence or drug use, even when only described;
  other hard history is named plainly with no note (group read of the theater scopes, 2026-11-26, finding 6; the lead
  states the same line once in docs/courses/arts/APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground"). Twilight: Los Angeles, 1992 (unit 3,
  day 68), Sweat (unit 8, day 224) and Sovereignty (unit 14, days 417 to 432) carry the note.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: a 45-minute lesson five days a week, step cards,
  scene and problem cards, public domain and student-written scripts, found materials. Activities: technique taught
  in steps, works studied with their setting, rehearsal with a critique and a revision, a showing each unit, a
  production each level. Outputs: a portfolio of five pieces a level with reflections, a rehearsal log, two marks a
  unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the level's 24 ids (Level 4: the Level 3 ids
  met again on new work) and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class
  makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

### Where the threads end

Level 4 is the last course of the subject: its five rows (th-g12-concept, th-g12-role, th-g12-production,
th-g12-criticism, th-g12-research) hand on nothing (their `hands_on` cells are empty). The theater threads end in the
student's own production, role, critique and researched play. A student who takes no Level 4 ends at Level 3's
production (unit 15) and its year-end check.
