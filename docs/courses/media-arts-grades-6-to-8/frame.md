# Course frame: Media Arts, grades 6 to 8

Course `media-arts-grades-6-to-8` (catalogue id `media-arts-6-8`), a band course for grades 6, 7 and 8, run as three years one after another (year 6, year 7, year 8). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a reading-and-society unit, an ideas-and-production unit, a
  making-and-judging unit, and a screening unit that ends the year with the class showing. Each unit joins two
  progression threads, as the K to 2 media arts frame does, and every unit makes, studies another maker's media
  work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Media arts here means pictures, sound and moving pictures framed, put in
  order, captured, edited and shown: photo sequences, stop-motion and drawn animation, audio pieces, short videos,
  posters and screen layouts. In grades 6 to 8 a shared school device is the usual way in where a school has one,
  and every unit has a full paper path (storyboards, flip books and drawn animatics, photo-essay layouts, sound
  scores performed live, a paper screen), so no id waits on a device.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 5 minutes: devices, trays and the day's step card
  out), the teaching and making, and clean-up, hand-back of devices and collecting work (about 5 minutes, 7 in a
  cutting and gluing or stop-motion session); a retrieval task takes ten minutes inside an opener. Each unit's days
  include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over
  25 days) and one respond day after it. Unit days follow the rows' load: the ideas-and-production unit of each year
  is longest (18 days) because it owns five ids, the planning portfolio and the production roles, and its team piece
  needs plan, prototype, production and a critique before the check; the reading-and-society unit (14) holds a
  written analysis and a short made investigation with its draft, critique and revision; the making-and-judging unit
  (14) makes from the plans and tool skills already built; the screening unit (14) owns five ids, but its making is
  joining content already gathered, and its screening is one of its two check days. These are course days: the
  school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls on that year's
  school day 2.5 x n, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the visual and media arts association's middle school
  checklist and its general PreK to 12 program standards were read at source on 2026-11-25 (the second-model read);
  neither gives a numerical weekly minimum for media arts. This course gives 80 minutes a week, 40 on the once-a-week
  path; no numerical gap is claimed (For the lead item 2). The course is written so a teacher with no media
  arts training can teach it; a school with a media arts teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look or listen at a work that carries the unit's response and its
  connecting sentence; one making session with the critique; one revision session; the check; one respond session
  after every mark is in. Fluency days fold
  into the openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path, every year: units of
  7, 9, 6 and 6 sessions; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond session. The mid-year check
  session falls in week 17, as on the full path, and its respond session in week 18; the year-end check session
  falls in week 31 and its respond session in week 32 (week 31 holds no core check in grades 6 to 8). Each
  six-session screening unit (4, 8, 12) runs: 1, study the works and demonstrate the technique (with the source
  work); 2, the research, the draft made, the critique and a trial showing; 3, the showing formats, revision, the
  records finished and the showing improvement made; 4, the scored screening; 5, the written items and the self-check; 6,
  respond from the finished marks. No assessment is ever done in a respond session. A merged day never drops a
  critique, a revision or a respond session; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, media arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 6 claims only the 18 grade 6 ids, year 7 only the 18 grade 7 ids, year 8 only the 18
  grade 8 ids: 54 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its own year. A year
  revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches all four artistic
  processes (creating, producing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at least two. In
  the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor
  standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  words-file lines of its ids, and each id's action against the national standard's own text where a words line may
  have drifted (For the lead item 1), the nine criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the
  lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add.
  Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done
  2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence still states its id count (18) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met.
  The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Media arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain films and photographs from library, archive and agency collections (the Library of Congress, the
  federal space agency's image and video library), and, for living or recent makers, a description in our own words
  with a link to the maker's, museum's or film board's own page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). A
  work is shown in class only as the holding page offers it (a public domain copy, or the film board's own free
  viewing page opened by the teacher), and a long film is met through excerpts the unit plan names. Each work named
  below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work
  must keep the unit's ids met and the year's range. Each year's works include living makers and more than one
  region and period (APPROACH "Works and their settings"); across the band they come from Africa (Egypt, Senegal,
  Kenya), the Americas (Inuk, Ho-Chunk and Cree makers, Canada, the United States), Asia (India, Japan, China) and
  Europe (France, Ireland, Russia, the United Kingdom, Ukraine). No work repeats an earlier media arts band, and each
  is studied once in the media arts line, K to 12; a maker who returns with a different work is named as a revisit
  with a harder question (the Lumière brothers in unit 1, met in grade 1). Ties to other subjects' frames are links,
  not repeats, each named in its unit: Hine's child-labor photographs (unit 4), Chaplin and Ray (unit 7). Open film and photograph collections lean to old
  European and American work, so the living and recent picks carry the range. Media remix uses only public domain
  or openly licensed sources, named on the piece. Curio Class writes every step card, work card, case card,
  invented feed, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Framing and point of view: shot sizes and angles named and used
  for a point of view (6), a shot list that carries a story's structure (7), framing and style conventions blended
  for unity (8). Order and motion: a storyboard and a mockup tested against a purpose, with a 12-drawing flip book
  or stop-motion sequence (6), a prototype (a drawn or photographed animatic) judged against resources (7), a whole
  workflow planned, prototyped and critiqued (8). Sound: a sound layer (voice, found sound, silence) placed to back a
  message (6), sound stressed for an audience and setting (7), sound shaped to a style and polished for level and
  timing (8). Presenting: one screening with a showing plan comparing formats and a written review (6), a format
  chosen by evaluation with a growth review (7), one theme shown in several formats with an improvement put in place
  (8).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills of a technique with feedback (a shot-size and
  angle call, a thumbnail storyboard sprint, a cut-on-the-beat timing drill, a sound-level match, a credit-line
  drill); it is mostly the five-minute opener and one or two fluency days a unit. Application: making and producing
  with some choice; the second making day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next
  day's revision is application too. Check: the self-check and the written and table items of the unit's evidence
  line; a kept piece is scored after the session, and a screening is scored where it happens. Respond: a second try
  at the part marked "not yet". A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with
  the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept piece after the session; a kept card or sheet made on the
  unit's last making days (a planning portfolio page, a role record, a showing plan, a source log, a media-ethics
  check), scored with the piece; written items on an unseen public domain, openly licensed or Curio Class original
  piece, at most two written items a student on a check day; and, in units 4, 8 and 12, look-fors marked at a
  screening. A written check day's 40 minutes: set-up 3; each student's self-check on the guide 5; the written items
  25, in silence, with the teacher reading the room; collecting and clean-up 5. That is 38 minutes, and nothing is
  scored live. A screening check day (units 4, 8 and 12, the first of their two check days): set-up 5; 15 pair
  pieces of about a minute screen in turn at the one screen, wall or paper stage, with turnover (25), while the rest
  are the audience and the teacher marks each student's presenting task and Pr4 look-for on their own half; collecting and devices back 5;
  each student's self-check is written in the first five minutes of the next session, the second check day. No written items are given that
  day; they come on the unit's second check day. The piece's draft, its critique with the teacher's look-for marks
  and written feedback on the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score
  comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each production id (MA:Pr5.1.6b, 6c, 7b, 7c, 8b and 8c) is scored from what each student did, never from a
  description alone: the teacher marks a look-for on each student's kept trials over the unit's last three
  production sessions, ten students a session for about a minute and a half each, beside a production-log line in
  the student's own hand; a team piece never stands in for one student's evidence. Each unit opens with a baseline
  look at its main look-for during
  the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change.
  One piece a unit goes into the portfolio (paper, or a file on the school's device) with a one-line written
  reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every year 6 unit assumes the grade 5 rows of its threads (Media Arts, grades 3 to 5,
  year 5), last used one grade back, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never a readiness
  check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. Media Arts, grades 3 to 5 is not a core course, so this
  is a "by course order" seam (lesson 32): each year 6 retrieval task carries its ten-minute reteach of the grade 5
  technique as written, for a student new to media arts (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline look"). Every year 7
  and year 8 unit assumes the year before's rows in the same threads, one grade back, and opens the same way. The
  count starts from the band's order (6, then 7, then 8). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a student may
  meet year 8 before year 7: each retrieval task then carries its reteach. The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 6 to 8 after-core checks stay off the school days where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md
  shows a core check for the grade (stagger rule 3, by school day), and week 32 is a finals week that may hold every
  subject's year-end check. Mid-year: course days 33 and 34 of each year, school days 83 and 85 (week 17); no core
  subject holds a check day on school day 83 in grade 6, 7 or 8 (the nearest are grade 6 math on 80 and grade 6
  science on 90). Year-end: course days 63 and 64 of each year, school days 158 and 160 (week 32, the finals week).
  Each year's screening unit ends in its own screening and written check before the year-end check, which then
  draws its items from the year's first three units.
- **Recording and kept work.** No student is recorded, photographed or filmed without the family's yes; a face or a
  voice is never required (hands, the work, a shadow, an object or the sound alone will do); a student who does not
  want to be recorded is observed live and the teacher's mark stands. Media in the room points at things, places,
  hands and work, never at a student who has not said yes, and never at another class or a passer-by. Filming is in
  the school's own spaces with the teacher's leave; any home filming is optional, with the family. Kept work stays
  on paper or on the school's or family's device and goes nowhere else (APPROACH "Kept work"); no student posts or
  shares media of others online, and no task asks a student to post anything (the rows' teacher notes).
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 4 (it studies child labor photographs and a memorial to
  children killed in a church bombing; the second work may be swapped for another public domain historical
  photograph set and keep every id met), unit 5 (it studies social platforms through an invented feed; a school
  that keeps social media out of class swaps the feed for an invented school notice board, and the ids stay met)
  and unit 12 (its photographs show India's independence, which came with the partition of the country; the
  photographs may be swapped for another public domain set of a local event seen worldwide and keep every id met). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course
  page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, card from cereal boxes, found objects for sound,
  string and clips for a paper screen; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4,
  APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, every team task
  has a solo or doubled version (parent and student; the student holds every production role in turn), and the
  showing is to the family. The teacher needs no media arts training: each unit's teacher page says what to look
  and listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as six clip
  lamps or flashlights for stop-motion counted once, printing of step cards, work cards, case cards and storyboard
  sheets), all optional beside the free path: year 6 $10, $15, $5, $10 ($40); year 7 $10, $15, $5, $10 ($40); year 8
  $10, $15, $5, $15 ($45). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. Where a school has them, one shared school device (a camera, a sound recorder, or a
  free editing or drawing program on a school computer) per team of five or six is one way in; one teacher screen
  may show a work, a step or a playback. Every day runs from the printed step card, work card and paper media tools
  without them, and no student account is used, for any Curio Class tool or any outside service. The owned codes
  that name tools, technical quality or tool spaces (MA:Pr5.1.6c, MA:Pr5.1.7c, MA:Pr5.1.8c, MA:Cr3.1.8b,
  MA:Cn11.1.6b, MA:Cn11.1.7b, MA:Cn11.1.8b) are met in full on paper: in this course a media tool is anything that
  frames, captures, orders, plays or shows pictures and sound, so a paper-path student uses the viewfinder, the flip
  book, the drawn animatic and the sound score in ordinary and experimental ways, polishes technical quality as
  framing, timing, legibility and sound level in a live or paper showing, and analyzes tool spaces, social platforms
  and virtual worlds through Curio Class case cards and an invented paper feed (For the lead item 4). Shared
  devices are school-approved; each is wiped and handed back by the device card's steps.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, card, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  frame-sequence viewer that plays drawn frames in order, a shot-list sheet, a work viewer) has a printed version
  that does the same job (the flip book, the paper film strip, the printed shot list) and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards are picture-led with short numbered steps; work cards and case cards are at about the
  grade's reading level, one page, read aloud on day 1 for any student who needs it. English learners: each unit
  teaches its six words in depth, with a picture, a gesture or a sound and a frame ("The shot is ___ so the viewer
  ___." "I changed ___ because ___."), a student may show the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the
  student made apart from what they wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against three free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov), the World Health Organization's
  "Make Listening Safe" pages on safe headphone volume (who.int), and the web accessibility guidelines' rule on
  flashing, success criterion 2.3.1 "Three Flashes or Below Threshold" (w3.org). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH
  "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): no filming, photographing or recording a student without
  the family's yes; no flashing light in any piece, screen or shadow play, and no strobe, and any work shown is
  checked against the flash rule first; screen breaks, with no student at a screen for more than about fifteen
  minutes at a stretch; headphones, where used, at low volume, and no loud sound in a student's ear; lamps and
  flashlights are low-power, never shone in eyes and kept off paper, and no laser pointers; device and lamp cords
  taped down or none; card cut by an adult or by a student with safety scissors, blades only by an adult; supplies
  labeled non-toxic; hands washed after glue and found objects. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and
  controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural, community or hard-history setting name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1
  (Inuit of Igloolik, Nunavut, with an Inuk filmmaker; a Hindu epic story in an Indian film), unit 3
  (a Soviet-era film made in Ukraine), unit 4 (child workers in the United States, 1908 to 1916; the 1963 bombing of
  the 16th Street Baptist Church, Birmingham, Alabama), unit 5 (Dakar, Senegal, a city cart driver; social
  platforms), unit 6 (a Ho-Chunk filmmaker and the Ho-Chunk language), unit 7 (India, two boys of different means),
  unit 9 (a Black government worker in segregated Washington, 1942; a Cree filmmaker on how films show Native
  people), unit 10 (Kenya, a future of water scarcity), unit 11 (a Chinese classic novel and Peking opera; Irish
  folklore) and unit 12 (India's independence in 1947 and the partition that came with it; the 1969 Moon landing broadcast). Every task that ties
  media to a student's own life, identity or community (units 1, 4, 8, 9 and 12) offers an invented, public or
  class-shared option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). No task asks what a family watches, owns or
  posts, which platforms a student uses, or anything about a student's own online life, and home media is never
  judged. Civic and environmental units (9) study how media serves values; no student is asked to take or argue a
  side on a current political question, and the values rule holds. Each section carries health's steps for "If a
  student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, work
  cards, case cards, paper media tools, found objects, an optional shared device per team. Activities: technique
  taught in steps, works studied with their setting, planning and producing in roles with a critique and a revision,
  a showing or screening each unit. Outputs: a portfolio of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings,
  two marks a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 18 ids and growth on
  the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").
