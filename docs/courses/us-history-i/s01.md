## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was the American Revolution radical?"

**Hook, day 1:** Compare two self-government records with the Declaration's consent principle: the Massachusetts Body of Liberties (1641), provision 74, on choosing town officials, and Pennsylvania's Charter of Privileges (1701), article II, on an assembly "yearly chosen, by the Freemen". Who already chose officials, and what would independence change? Teach assemblies and town meetings after students answer.

**Supporting questions:** Co-write on day 3, then revise: What counts as radical change? Why did resistance become independence (day 10)? Whose rights did independence secure (day 12)? What made victory possible (day 16)?

**Closing claim, day 17:** Defend a qualified answer using the Declaration, Wheatley's Dartmouth poem, Cornplanter's 1790 address and one war map. Answer a counterclaim and distinguish political change from equality in practice.

**Teaching:** Model causal weighing on day 10 and an argument on day 11. Teach representation, sovereignty, natural rights and alliance in context, the boycott with the Stamp Act (day 7), and Samuel Adams with the Sons of Liberty (day 9).


C3 parts, paraphrased from the [framework](https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.pdf), printed pages 23 to 25, 47 and 53:

- **D1.1.9-12:** Explain the lasting disciplinary issue behind a question.
- **D1.2.9-12:** Explain expert agreements and disagreements about the compelling question's concepts and uses.
- **D1.3.9-12:** Explain those agreements and disagreements for supporting questions too.
- **D1.4.9-12:** Explain supporting questions' contribution and how evidence generates new questions of both kinds.
- **D1.5.9-12:** Determine useful source kinds, considering viewpoints, availability and possible uses.
- **D3.1.9-12:** Gather varied, relevant evidence; select through origin, authority, structure, context and corroboration.
- **D3.2.9-12:** Evaluate credibility through experts' judgments.
- **D2.His.11.9-12:** Critique usefulness for an inquiry through maker, date, place, audience and purpose.
