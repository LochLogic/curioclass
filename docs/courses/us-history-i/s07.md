## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who built industrial America, and who gained from it?"

**Hook, day 110:** Put Carnegie's account of expanding production beside a Hine photograph of tenement work. Who appears in each account of industrial progress? Begin the compelling question before retrieval.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write these: What enabled growth (110 and 111)? Who controlled its rewards and costs (112 to 115)? Why did people migrate, and what work did they find (116 and 117)? How did law and violence shape opportunity (118)? What can sources establish (119)?
**Closing claim, day 120:** Answer with Carnegie, immigrant testimony and the exclusion law. Weigh cheaper goods against unequal power, wages and environmental costs; name a source limit. Partners question evidence before independent revision. This is practice; score only day 122's revision after written feedback.
**Teaching:** Model causal chains on 112, supply shifts on 111 and source comparison on 116. On 116, read each source for reason and work. On 117, teach with a map who built the Central Pacific and Union Pacific lines. Teach entrepreneur, trust, monopoly, union, strike, nativism and exclusion in context. On 119, model how a photograph's framing and an interview's later recording affect inference; students compare independently. Model answering a counterclaim before 120. Practice C3 D2.His.4.9-12, D2.His.14.9-12, D3.1.9-12 and D4.1.9-12. Daily evidence is specified below.


**Limits:** Keep political machines inside 113 and the Triangle fire inside 114, anticipating reform in unit 8. Compare the four strikes through a short chronology and one paired account; no strike reenactments. Earlier migration supplies context, not evidence that the 1840s famine caused every later departure. Later quotas belong to grade 12.
**Carried:** Retain unit 6's timeline, map-scale, region, cause-effect-chain, source-sourcing, perspective, corroboration, claim-evidence-reasoning and continuity-change. Retrieve supply-demand on 111; tie increased output to lower prices with demand held constant, then distinguish trusts' attempts to restrict competition on 112. Keep market power and changing demand visible as limits. Withdraw completed graphs on 115 and sentence prompts on 120; keep blank organizers and source credits.
