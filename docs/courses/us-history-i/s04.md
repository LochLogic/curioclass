**Look-fors and sample answers:** These illustrate reasoning, not scripts to memorize.

- **1:** Explain all four transport forms, trade and city growth, with a mechanism. Meets: "Steamboats linked rivers, canals connected waterways, roads reached inland producers and railroads extended routes beyond waterways. More reliable movement connected distant buyers and sellers, while transfer points attracted warehouses, processing and people." Does not: "Trains made everyone rich" (A).
- **2, synthesis:** Connect all three industries and identify unequal power. Meets: "Mills bought machinery and cotton; machine shops supplied equipment; ships carried cotton and cloth. Owners and merchants could reinvest profits, while workers depended on wages." Does not: "Each industry grew independently" (Z); "Everyone who worked a machine owned a share of the profits" (M).
- **2a (inside prompt i):** One relevant invention with its effect. Meets: "Power looms increased weaving capacity, so mills bought more machinery and more shipped cotton. The loom did not supply its own capital or raw materials." Does not: "Machines made the economy grow by themselves" (AC).
- **2b:** Explain processing, expansion, field coercion and the textile link. Meets: "Faster cleaning made more short-staple cotton worth planting. Owners expanded cultivation and demanded more enslaved field labour; Northern mills obtained more cotton. The machine did not pick the crop or abolish slavery." Does not: "The gin freed enslaved field workers" (L); "The North did not depend on cotton" (D).
- **2c:** One origin, one attraction and one consequence. Meets: "Famine in Ireland pushed people to migrate. American jobs attracted them; newcomers expanded industrial workforces and cities, encountering nativism as well as opportunities." Does not: "All Europeans were Irish famine refugees" (E).
- **2d:** Explain accumulation and ownership. Meets: "Merchants financed and sold goods; manufacturers controlled production. Profits could finance further investment and influence, unlike a worker's wage." Does not: "Working a machine gave every employee ownership" (M).
- **2e:** Identify women, a demand and collective action. Meets: "Young women predominated in early textile mills. They used strikes against wage reductions and petitions for shorter hours, showing agency despite employers' power." Does not: "Women accepted every condition and had no collective demands" (W).
- **3:** Distinguish every region, explain Northern links, and name bank and insurer mechanisms. Meets: "Southern cotton expansion relied on enslaved labour, coerced domestic sales and natural population increase after overseas imports were banned. Cuba's sugar expansion drew on continued overseas trafficking; Brazil's coffee expansion also used enslaved labour and overseas imports until 1850. Haiti's revolutionary emancipation and independence broke slavery rather than extending it. British Caribbean emancipation also makes a single regional growth story false. Northern mills processed cotton; merchants shipped it. Banks earned interest on plantation credit secured partly by enslaved people, and insurers collected premiums on policies treating people and cargo as property, bearing claims risk." Does not: "The import ban freed everyone" (I); "All Caribbean islands followed one path" (C); "Northern banks earned nothing from slavery" (D). Do not describe people as property outside explaining the legal system that did so.
- **4:** Explain both revivals and their timing, educational ideology and exclusion. Meets: "The First Great Awakening challenged established religious authority in the eighteenth century; the Second spread evangelical revival and helped energize nineteenth-century reforms. Republican Motherhood encouraged women's education for raising republican citizens while restricting civic roles. Anti-Catholic nativism cast Catholic immigrants as threats and supported exclusion; those accusations are evidence of prejudice." Does not: "One awakening made every American Protestant" (O); "Republican Motherhood made mothers equal voters" (Q).

- **5b:** One link to the convention. Meets: "Women who had organized against slavery brought that experience to Seneca Falls in 1848, where they demanded the vote without yet winning it." Does not: "The convention immediately enfranchised all women" (Q).
- **5c:** Free schooling distinguished from compulsory attendance, with one link to literacy or print. Meets: "Free schooling meant taxpayers paid for school, while compulsory attendance meant the law required children to go; more schooling widened literacy and the readership of newspapers and magazines." Does not: "Mann invented education" (AA); "Free schools ended every barrier" (Q).
- **5d:** One gain and one limit. Meets: "Schools for deaf students taught through signed communication, which let students learn, but they were boarding schools that kept children away from home." **Teacher background for the section plans (dated lines checked 2026-11-14):**

- **Made-for-practice records:** Every teacher-made record in this unit (the day 52 machinery order and shipping entry, the day 59 loan and insurance entries, and any financial record without a named archive) carries the label "invented for practice, based on [cited source]", so no student takes it for a real document. Policy entries are based on the California Department of Insurance Slavery Era Insurance Registry (public report); loan entries on a published, cited bank or court record. No business names are printed.
- **Period terms:** Some period sources and institution names use words such as "feeble-minded" or "lunatic". Teacher note: these are period terms and are now offensive. Read each once, inside the quoted source, name it as a period term, and do not reuse it; the plan's own words follow the disability language guide above.
- **Modern historians:** Modern historians are paraphrased with attribution, never quoted.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools:** None; no tool asks. Print all maps, source packets, regional tables, research choices, tasks and keys. Supply paper, pencils and a record sheet. One projector shows identical pages. ## 9. Family note

Students will investigate how mills, cotton and slavery connected, and how people sought changes in work, education and rights. We name enslavement, family separation, religious prejudice and confinement plainly; students use sources without reenactments or requests for personal disclosure.
