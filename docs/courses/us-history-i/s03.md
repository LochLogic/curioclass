## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who paid for the growth of the United States?"

**Hook, day 35:** Read the Cherokee Nation's account of rebuilding its government beside an expansion map. Who appears in the map's boundaries, and whose government does the account reveal? Introduce the compelling question before teaching Jackson's policies.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write and revise these: Whose power did Jackson defend (day 36)? What choices did removal's opponents pursue (day 38)? What survived removal (day 39)? What drove expansion (day 42)? How did borders change people's lives (day 43)?
**Closing claim, day 45:** Weigh who gained and who bore costs using Ross's 1836 protest, the Choctaw removal history, the 1848 treaty and the teaching map sequence. Answer a counterclaim about national growth; distinguish public spending from dispossession, coerced labour and deaths.
**Teaching:** Model causal weighing on day 36, perspective without endorsement on day 37, geographic inference on day 40 and a qualified claim on day 42. Step back on days 44 and 45. Teach patronage, veto, nullification, sovereignty, annexation, cession and Manifest Destiny in context.

**Teacher background for the section plans (dated lines checked 2026-11-14):**

- **McGirt and after, day 39.** As of November 2026, the Muscogee (Creek) reservation recognized in McGirt v. Oklahoma (2020) still stands. Two later rulings changed who may prosecute there. In Oklahoma v. Castro-Huerta (2022) the Supreme Court held that a state may also prosecute non-Indians for crimes against Indians in Indian country. In City of Tulsa v. O'Brien (December 5, 2024) Oklahoma's criminal appeals court held that a city may also ticket and prosecute tribal citizens for city offenses, a ruling in tension with the federal appeals court's Hooper decision (2023). In June 2025 the City of Tulsa agreed with the Muscogee (Creek) Nation to send city cases involving Nation citizens to the Nation's prosecutors; that agreement has since been challenged in Oklahoma's courts, so check its status before the day is taught. Official sources to check before the day: the Supreme Court's own opinion pages for McGirt and Castro-Huerta, the Oklahoma Court of Criminal Appeals page for O'Brien, the Tenth Circuit page for Hooper, and the City of Tulsa's or the Nation's own announcement of the agreement. In class this is one teacher sentence, stated as law and not argued by party.
