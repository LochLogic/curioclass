## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the Constitution make a stronger union at too high a price?"

**Hook, day 20:** Compare the Articles' state sovereignty provision with the Constitution's taxing power. What problem might each solve, and whose power might each protect?

**Supporting questions:** Co-write and revise these: Why replace the Articles (days 20 and 21)? Who gained power through compromise (days 22 and 23)? What do treaty rights show about constitutional promises (day 25)? Who could shape public debate (day 28)? How did presidents test federal power (days 29 to 31)?

**Closing claim, day 32:** Answer the compelling question using a constitutional clause, a ratification document, a press source and a presidential policy. Weigh a counterclaim and distinguish stronger government from equal freedom.

**Teaching:** Model a causal explanation on day 21, clause analysis on day 22, corroboration on day 24 and a press comparison on day 27. Step back on days 28 and 32. Teach confederation, federalism, ratification, sovereignty, partisan press and judicial review in context. Day 30 includes the Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions as opposition to the Alien and Sedition Acts.

**Limits:** Recall the Bill of Rights, Federalist 51 and Brutus No. 1 with brief printed reminders. Government's present-day operation belongs to its own course. The later treaty and media cases are explicitly dated comparisons; expansion and the War of 1812 belong to unit 3. Informed action belongs to unit 8.

**Carried:** Introduce branches-of-government on day 20; tie each branch to a constitutional provision on day 22 and revise its powers with Marbury on day 31. Keep unit 1's cause-effect-chain for day 21, corroboration for day 24, timeline for day 26, source-sourcing for day 28 and claim-evidence-reasoning for day 32. Withdraw completed examples by day 32 and all organizers for day 33. Map-scale and perspective pass forward without a separate exercise here.

**Teacher background for the section plans (dated lines checked 2026-11-14):**

- **Alien Enemies Act, day 30.** As of November 2026: of the 1798 acts, the Alien Enemies Act is still law. It was used in both world wars and invoked by proclamation on March 14, 2025. In 2025 the Supreme Court required that people held under it get notice and a real chance to go to court. On August 13, 2026 the full Fifth Circuit dismissed the main appeal (W.M.M. v. Trump) as moot, without deciding whether the Act applies in peacetime. Taught as fact, not argued by party; no student is asked about immigration status.
- **Day 25, Since Time Immemorial.** The guide allows the cartoon as homework and sharing in the next class; here both happen on day 25, because day 26 belongs to the information-history survey. Day 25's fifty minutes: five for constitutional retrieval, seven for salmon and justice, twelve for the reading, eight for discussion, twelve for the cartoon, three for sharing and three for the exit ticket. Adult preparation sits outside those minutes. In production, replace the guide's relative-age phrase "42 year-old decision" with "the 1974 decision".
- **Northwest Ordinance, day 21.** The Northwest Territory was the homeland of nations including the Shawnee, Miami, Delaware (Lenape) and Potawatomi. Article 3 of the Ordinance promised that "the utmost good faith shall always be observed towards the Indians". That promise was broken within a decade, at Fallen Timbers (1794) and in the Treaty of Greenville (1795). Name these nations in the present tense: each has its own government today.
- **News today, day 26.** The same checks apply to any news: who made it, who stands behind it, and whether an independent source confirms it.
