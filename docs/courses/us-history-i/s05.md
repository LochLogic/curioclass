## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why did the Civil War come, and did Reconstruction fail or was it overthrown?"

**Hook, day 72:** Compare an 1820 free-state and slave-state map with a cotton route from unit 4. Why could admitting one state threaten a national settlement?
**Supporting questions:** Co-write these: How did expansion change power (72)? Why did compromises unravel (75)? How did emancipation change the war (78)? Who served (80)? What did freedpeople build (85)? Why did protection retreat (87)?
**Closing claims:** Essay 1 answers why war came on 84. Essay 2 answers the Reconstruction question on 92, using convention proceedings, Du Bois and contrasting historians. Both answer counterclaims.
**Teaching:** Model long-term cause against trigger on 72, causal explanation on 76, historical argument on 77, sourcing on 80 and interpretation critique on 87. Release independent writing on 84 and 92. Teach sectionalism, popular sovereignty, secession, blockade, emancipation, equal protection and suffrage in context.

- **R2:** An original teaching chronology, based on the community report, Colfax account and Hayes library reference below: 1870, the 15th Amendment prohibited racial exclusion from voting; 1873, white supremacists killed at least 60 Black men at Colfax, Louisiana, and perhaps many more (historians' estimates range from about 60 to about 150; a 2026 study by David Ballantyne and Charles Lane counts 62 to 80); 1876, the presidential election was disputed amid racial intimidation; April 1877, Hayes ended federal troop protection of the remaining Republican state governments in South Carolina and Louisiana. Federal troops remained elsewhere in the South, and the Reconstruction amendments remained law. This modern synthesis samples two states, not the entire region. R2 is context: one short federal or archive record of the Colfax events or the 1877 withdrawal (to choose and check before day 89) stands beside it, and the key accepts either. - **1:** Look for both chambers and the mechanism linking admission to sectional power. Meets: "Each admitted state added two senators, so free-state admissions could alter the balance over slavery. Population shifts also changed House representation. Expansion intensified bargaining over laws Congress could pass." Does not: "The Midwest grew, so everyone gained the same votes" (P).
- **2:** Look for all four policies or decisions, their causal links and the distinction between slavery and an election trigger. Meets: "The Missouri settlement limited slavery's territorial spread while balancing admissions. The 1850 settlement admitted California free but strengthened fugitive rendition, provoking resistance. Kansas-Nebraska displaced the Missouri restriction with popular sovereignty and intensified violent competition. Dred Scott denied Congress power to exclude slavery from territories and denied Black citizenship, deepening the crisis. Lincoln's election then triggered secession by leaders defending slavery; it did not originate the conflict." Does not: "Four compromises failed because Lincoln first invented the slavery dispute in 1860" (A).
- **3:** Look for strategy, policy limits, change over time, obstacles and Black agency. Meets: "The blockade restricted Confederate supplies and export earnings. Enslaved people's flight and Black organizing helped press emancipation onto the Union agenda. Lincoln linked preserving the Union to weakening slavery and recruiting Black troops. His Proclamation excluded loyal slave states and named exceptions, so armies and further law still mattered. Opposition to emancipation and uncertain military success constrained him." Does not: "The Proclamation freed every enslaved person in the country on January 1, 1863" (C). "Lincoln freed people because the army won battles" (L).
- **4:** Look for three distinct roles, one decision with its effect and, for the two chosen battles, an immediate and a lasting consequence each. Meets: "Davis directed the Confederate government and signed the 1862 draft law to keep its armies full; Lee commanded major Confederate armies; Grant led Union campaigns and later overall command. Antietam stopped Lee's 1862 invasion at once, and it gave Lincoln the opening for preliminary emancipation, which changed the war's aims for good. Grant's capture of Vicksburg, with Port Hudson's surrender on July 9, gave the Union the Mississippi in 1863 and kept Confederate territory split for the rest of the war." Does not: "Davis and Lee both commanded armies" (D). "The Union victory at Gettysburg ended the war" (M).
- **5:** Look for an inference from each passage, provenance, distinct purposes and a bounded conclusion about equality. Meets: "Gooding argues soldiers who did a soldier's duty should get a soldier's pay; the petition documents Black military service and discrimination.
