- **W1:** Corporal James Henry Gooding, letter to President Lincoln, September 28, 1863, protesting unequal pay: "We have done a Soldiers Duty. Why cant we have a Soldiers pay?" Original spelling is retained.
- **W2:** Susie King Taylor, *Reminiscences of My Life in Camp*, 1902, remembering service with a Black Union regiment in 1864: "My work now began. I gave my assistance to try to alleviate their sufferings." Context: she describes helping wounded soldiers. This is remembered experience, not a wartime diary.
- **R1:** Rutherford B. Hayes, inaugural address, March 5, 1877: "only a local government which recognizes and maintains inviolate the rights of all is a true self-government." Gloss *inviolate* as protected from violation. This states an incoming president's public position, not evidence that every government fulfilled it.
- **R2:** An original teaching chronology, based on the community report, Colfax account and Hayes library reference below: 1870, the 15th Amendment prohibited racial exclusion from voting; 1873, white supremacists killed at least 60 Black men at Colfax, Louisiana, and perhaps many more (historians' estimates range from about 60 to about 150; a 2026 study by David Ballantyne and Charles Lane counts 62 to 80); 1876, the presidential election was disputed amid racial intimidation; April 1877, Hayes ended federal troop protection of the remaining Republican state governments in South Carolina and Louisiana. Federal troops remained elsewhere in the South, and the Reconstruction amendments remained law. This modern synthesis samples two states, not the entire region.
- **R3, separate follow-up card:** 15th Amendment, section 2, ratified 1870: "The Congress shall have power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation." Keep this card closed until item 8. It supplies additional primary evidence about legal authority rather than proof of effective enforcement.

**Teacher-judged look-fors and scoring anchors:** Score each named part separately. A factual list does not meet an explanation or analysis criterion. Give the samples to students only after assessment.

- **1:** Look for both chambers and the mechanism linking admission to sectional power. Meets: "Each admitted state added two senators, so free-state admissions could alter the balance over slavery. Population shifts also changed House representation. Expansion intensified bargaining over laws Congress could pass." Does not: "The Midwest grew, so everyone gained the same votes" (A, B).
- **2:** Look for all four policies or decisions, their causal links and the distinction between slavery and an election trigger. Meets: "The Missouri settlement limited slavery's territorial spread while balancing admissions. The 1850 settlement admitted California free but strengthened fugitive rendition, provoking resistance. Kansas-Nebraska displaced the Missouri restriction with popular sovereignty and intensified violent competition. Dred Scott denied Congress power to exclude slavery from territories and denied Black citizenship, deepening the crisis. Lincoln's election then triggered secession by leaders defending slavery; it did not originate the conflict." Does not: "Four compromises failed because Lincoln first invented the slavery dispute in 1860" (A).
- **3:** Look for strategy, policy limits, change over time, obstacles and Black agency. Meets: "The blockade restricted Confederate supplies and export earnings. Enslaved people's flight and Black organizing helped press emancipation onto the Union agenda. Lincoln linked preserving the Union to weakening slavery and recruiting Black troops. His Proclamation excluded loyal slave states and named exceptions, so armies and further law still mattered. Opposition to emancipation and uncertain military success constrained him." Does not: "Lincoln abolished all slavery at once with no opposition and no action by enslaved people" (C, L).
- **4:** Look for three distinct roles, one decision with its effect and, for the two chosen battles, an immediate and a lasting consequence each. Meets: "Davis directed the Confederate government and signed the 1862 draft law to keep its armies full; Lee commanded major Confederate armies; Grant led Union campaigns and later overall command. Antietam stopped Lee's 1862 invasion at once, and it gave Lincoln the opening for preliminary emancipation, which changed the war's aims for good. Grant's capture of Vicksburg, with Port Hudson's surrender on July 9, gave the Union the Mississippi in 1863 and kept Confederate territory split for the rest of the war." Does not: "President Lee won at Gettysburg and ended the war" (D, M).
- **5:** Look for an inference from each passage, provenance, distinct purposes and a bounded conclusion about equality. Meets: "Gooding claims soldiers' duties to demand equal pay from Lincoln; the petition documents Black military service and discrimination. Taylor remembers caring for wounded comrades, showing another form of service. Her later memoir may select memories; his demand cannot establish every soldier's treatment. Service coexisted with unequal pay and recognition." Does not: "Both authors were generals and their service proves racial equality" (E, H).
- **6:** Look for all four boxes, with the three amendments kept distinct. Meets: "War destroyed Southern railways, farms and homes while demand helped expand Northern manufacturing. Federal authority grew through war and Reconstruction legislation. The 13th ended slavery except as punishment for crime; the 14th established national citizenship, due process and equal protection; the 15th barred racial denial of voting rights, without universal suffrage." Does not: "The amendments all gave everyone a safe vote, so Reconstruction had no unfinished work" (F, G).
- **7:** Look for multiple connected causes, both sources, dated numerical evidence and evaluation for the explanation's purpose. Meets: "R2 records at least 60 Black men killed at Colfax in 1873, showing coercion before the 1876 dispute. Hayes promised equal rights in R1, but ending statehouse protection in 1877 helped opponents take control despite those promises. Violence and federal retreat reinforced each other. This explains interaction better than a single-deal story; a presidential statement and a two-state chronology cannot show every local transition or all motives." Does not: "The amendments were repealed when every soldier left in 1877, so no earlier violence mattered" (G, H).
- **8:** Look for a question raised by both initial sources, actual investigation of R3 and a warranted answer with a limit. Meets: "If Hayes promised rights while protection retreated, did Congress still have legal power to protect voting? R3 explicitly gives Congress enforcement power. That distinguishes authority from willingness; these sources do not establish why Congress failed to use it effectively in each locality." Does not: "I would search for something else; the card proves all Black citizens voted safely" (F, H).
