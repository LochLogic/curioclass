- **MA.USI.T5.3, this section's share.** "Analyze" Lincoln's presidency: the blockade, the change from saving the
  Union to ending slavery as a war measure, the Proclamation's reach and limits, and the obstacles he faced. Day 82
  returns to it through memory: what later accounts did to the war's cause.
- **MA.USI.T5.4.** "Analyze" and "evaluate": Davis, Lee and Grant by office and decision; Antietam, Gettysburg and
  Vicksburg by immediate and lasting effect. The 1862 Homestead and Pacific Railway Acts sit here as home-front
  federal power (frame check set).
- **MA.USI.T5.5.** "Use" diaries, newspapers, photographs and cartoons to document the men and women who served,
  the United States Colored Troops and Harriet Tubman among them.
- **D3.4.9-12.** "Refine" claims and counterclaims: day 81 is essay 1, draft 2.
- **D2.His.12.9-12.** "Generate" questions from several sources and pursue one with a further source: day 82.
- **Limits that bite here.** No battle tactics, no casualty tallies beyond one facts card, no battle role play
  or reenactment. The Lost Cause is a historical account tested against the record, never one of two views.
  Confederate symbols appear only inside a source. Slavery and violence are named plainly.
- **Connections.** D2.His.16.9-12 and D3.3.9-12 on day 81 (the revision deals with an inconsistency). D4.1.9-12
  on day 81 (a counterclaim answered in each section).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the Proclamation freed no one at once in Kentucky or Maryland and
  still changed the war. Why a memoir written in 1881 or 1885 is weaker evidence for 1861 motives than a speech
  given in 1861. That enslaved people's flight to Union lines pushed policy (Fortress Monroe, May 1861). That the
  Pacific Railway Act promised to take land that named Native nations still held, and that those nations
  negotiated, resisted and govern themselves today.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: blockade, emancipation, proclamation, contraband, United States Colored Troops,
  Union, Confederacy, counterclaim. Frame: "This source shows __, but it cannot show __ because __ (who made it, when, or for whom)." Words
  for people: "United States Colored Troops" is the army's name; "contraband" is the army's word, used only inside
  a source; we say people who escaped slavery.
- **Access.** Source sheets come in large print; images are described in words under each image; a student who
  cannot write long dictates the exit.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits may be answered aloud and scribed; the look-fors stay the same.
- **Reading load.** 100 to 200 words a source, glossed, read aloud before students work alone; exit sources under
  120 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Day 80: a second Douglass' Monthly item on the 54th Massachusetts
  (Susie King Taylor's memoir stays reserved for day 89); day 82: compare Lee's order with Grant's Memoirs on the surrender.

## 10. Two rooms

- **One projector.** Show the source sheet; students keep their own printed copy for marking.
