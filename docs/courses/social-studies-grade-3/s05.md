## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?"
- **Hook, day 90:** open on an image of the St. Augustine shore and the Florida Museum's account of the Timucua town of Seloy, where Spanish colonists settled in 1565. Ask how settlement could change life for people already there. Plymouth comes in its turn.
- **Co-write on 90:** Why sail west (93)? Whose account and map (95 to 96)? What made cooperation possible (103)? What undermined it (106)? Who worked and who benefited (109 to 116)?
- **Closing, day 118:** answer the compelling question from two colonies, using Winslow's treaty account (Plymouth) and the Pamunkey Indian Tribe's history (Jamestown). Explain a possibility and a limit; evidence cannot prove what would have happened.
- **Teaching:** model chronology on 91, sourcing on 95 and two-source reasoning on 117. Step back on 118; hear another supported claim before revision. Teach century, colony, alliance, conversion, enslaved and harbor in context. Read an original Ousamequin biography on 107 from the leadership account. Colony cases follow founding order; each follows its own later consequences before returning to the next founding.

## 7. Response allocation

- **Respond days (days.csv):** day 121.
- **Day length (data/plans/frames.csv):** 40 minutes.
Marking takes five minutes on each assessment day, separate from response. Students mark the short items against the key on the screen, pen down, five minutes. Hands up by item sets the groups. Teacher-judged items are read after class and answered in the next day's first five minutes.

Day 106 opens with five minutes of feedback and five of reteaching. Day 121 uses five for feedback, twenty for error groups, ten for a fresh task and five for reflection. Groups A to Y use their own field 6 repairs within the twenty minutes, largest gap first. If many miss, reteach together; otherwise group. One child discusses misses.
