# Section 1.2: Where we are on Earth

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/1/s/1.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u01-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain; the teacher reads every card and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a placed dot or a filled table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Every place named is real and marked "source to check" until its source is logged; every made-up card prints "invented for practice".

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.Geo.10.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain why environmental features differ among world regions." Days 3 to 5 teach where a place is (globe and flat map, continent and hemisphere, a letter-and-number grid). Day 6 teaches why two places differ: how direct the sunlight is, and how much rain. This section carries the whole part "explain variation" for the unit.
- **Limits that bite here.** Latitude and longitude are named as lines only: no degrees, no numbers on the lines (numbers wait for grade 4). Direct sunlight is not the seasons or the tilt of Earth. Rain has two reasons only: wet air from a sea, and a mountain in the way (grade 2's ridge model). The three climate cards in the unit's withheld packet are never used for teaching. Our town's facts come from a free source the teacher fills in (source to check).
- **Connections.** Supporting question 1, "Where are we on Earth?", opens day 2 and is answered day 6 (D1.3, D1.4 return day 20). Science 3-ESS2-2, day 6: this day teaches warmth and rainfall itself; science deepens it later. Grid and compass directions return in section 1.3 (days 7 to 10).
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Globe (a model of the round Earth), continents and oceans, land, water, cardinal directions, climate and weather, the ridge-and-rain model, "Both ___, but ___" (kindergarten, for a globe and a map), the flat map that must stretch or cut (grade 2). Equator was met, not tested, in grade 2: it is taught and tested here.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A continent, an ocean and a hemisphere are three kinds of label; a place has one of each. The prime meridian is a line people agreed on, and the eastern and western halves are drawn from it (source to check). A flat map is a picture of one view and must stretch or cut something. Near the equator the sun's light is more direct and warms more; near the poles the same light spreads out. Two places at about the same latitude can differ in rain.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next opening uses that error's wrong example.
- **E3.** "Use the Globe View Card and the flat world card (new, printed). Find Asia and the Indian Ocean on each. Then write one way the flat card changes something." Answer: both found on both; the flat card stretches sizes, bends shapes or cuts the edges. Wrong: names the Indian Ocean as a continent, or Asia as an ocean (A). The data asks it as E3, E3b, E3c, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E4.** "A new World Outline Card shows the equator and the prime meridian. A dot marks Dakar, Senegal. Give its continent and its two hemispheres." Answer: Africa; northern and western. Wrong: "Atlantic" or "northern" given as the continent (A). The data asks it as E4, E4b, E4c, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E5.** "A new Exit Grid has columns A to D, west to east, and rows 1 to 4, south to north. A star marks one cell. Name the cell. Which kind of line runs east to west, latitude or longitude?" Answer: the star's cell (C3 on the card); latitude. Wrong: "3C" or the column and row swapped (L). The data asks it as E5, E5b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E6 (Lowlands Card, invented for practice).** "Lowland M is near the North Pole. Lowland N is near the equator. M gets steady rain. N gets almost none. Someone says, 'N must be wetter, it is hot.' Why is M colder than N? Is the someone right?" Answer: M is colder, because near the pole it gets less direct sunlight; no, how much rain a place gets is a different fact (N is dry). Wrong: "Yes, the equator is always wet" (E). The data asks it as E6, E6b, one ask each; items.csv governs.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The globe and flat map as model and picture (day 3, used days 4 to 6); the equator and prime meridian lines (day 4), named with latitude and longitude as lines (day 5); the letter-and-number grid (day 5, used for our town on day 6). The question board's first question is answered day 6. Nothing is withdrawn; the lines and grid return day 22.
- **Key moment 1, day 4: the lines on the globe.** Ideal instrument: a globe with two string loops, one at the equator and one over the prime meridian. Spec: partly fits; one globe is a teacher demonstration (two volunteers trace, the rest look and draw on printed outlines). Fallback: a ball and string.
- **Key moment 2, day 6: sunlight.** Ideal instrument: a lamp or torch and a ball. Spec: partly fits; it shows direct against spread-out light, not the seasons. Fallback: the printed ray picture.
- **A room with nothing.** A ball, string and a wall map; a window or lamp; outlines drawn on paper; the grid drawn on the board.

## 6. Days

## Day 3


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 2's most-missed letter (O or J): its wrong example read aloud on a card, then the right one (the card's words, not a child's). The other letter goes to a small group of up to 6 during the last pairs part.
- **10 min. Retrieval, then repair.** Pairs hold a Globe View Card and a flat world card. Find South America and the Pacific Ocean on each. Does Greenland look the same size on both? (a reminder of grade 2's case, not an exit.) 4 minutes. Then the repair, 6 minutes: lay matching outlines side by side. Whole class if more than a third missed; otherwise a small group while the others label the continents on a blank outline.
- **7 min. A model and a picture.** The teacher turns the globe; two volunteers trace Africa. A globe keeps shapes and sizes; a flat map is a picture of one view and must stretch or cut. Post supporting question 1 on the board.
- **10 min. Seven continents, five oceans.** Pairs label a blank outline from a word bank (the Southern Ocean is the water round Antarctica; source to check). The teacher circulates and gives one cue to each pair; practice, no score.
- **4 min. Both ___, but ___.** Each pair writes one sentence about the globe and the flat map (review of kindergarten).
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E3.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-3-6.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The paired globe and flat cards of the worked example share the shape of E3 (circle a place on both cards, say one change); E3 uses new cards.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the globe; pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: tracing, labelling, writing the sentence, the exit. Evidence: the sentence, E3.

## Day 4


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Label two continents and one ocean on a blank outline. If many made error A on E3, this is the repair: sort continent, ocean and hemisphere labels on a fresh globe view, in place of the labelling.
- **10 min. Two lines.** On the globe the teacher holds a string loop at the equator and another over the prime meridian (people agreed on it; source to check). Children draw both lines on a printed World Outline Card and name the four halves: northern, southern, eastern, western hemisphere.
- **14 min. Where in the world?** The class works a chart: place, continent, north or south, east or west. The teacher models Lima, Peru. Pairs do Oslo, Norway; Sydney, Australia; Vancouver, Canada; and our town (the teacher fills its dot). The projector shows the overlay (coordinates and continents source to check).
- **6 min. A location sentence.** Each child picks one of three cards (Tokyo, Japan; Cape Town, South Africa; Mexico City, Mexico) and writes "___ is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres." Say it to a partner first.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E4.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-3-6.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The location chart and the worked cities share the shape of E4 (continent and two hemispheres); E4 is a new place on a new card.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the globe and the string; pairs hold their outlines. Tool: None. By hand: drawing lines, filling the chart, one sentence, the exit. Evidence: the chart, the sentence, E4.

## Day 5


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Give the continent and two hemispheres of one new place on a fresh outline (2 minutes). If many made error A on E4, the other 3 minutes are the repair: sort labels again.
- **10 min. A letter and a number.** A grid on a map of our classroom (columns A to D, west to east; rows 1 to 4, south to north). The teacher finds the door: the letter first, then the number. Pairs name the cell of the sink and the cell of the clock.
- **10 min. Latitude and longitude.** On the globe and on a printed outline, the lines that run east and west are latitude; the lines that run from pole to pole are longitude. The equator is a line of latitude; the prime meridian is a line of longitude. No numbers.
- **10 min. The world grid.** On a World Grid Card (letters across, numbers up) pairs find the cell of four places the teacher names, then name a cell and the place in it. Where do cells and lines agree? Mark the cell that holds the equator.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-3-6.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The classroom grid and the World Grid Card share the shape of E5 (name the cell, letter first); the exit uses a different grid and cell.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the globe; pairs hold their grid. Tool: None. By hand: grid reading, naming cells, the exit. Evidence: the grid page, E5.

## Day 6


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Place a star on a fresh grid and name its cell. If many made error L on E5, this is the repair: rebuild the labeled columns and rows, then place a new cell.
- **8 min. Sunlight on a ball.** A torch shines on a ball: straight on at the middle, slanted near the top. The spot is small and bright at the middle, spread out near the top. Near the equator the sun's light is more direct and warms more; near the poles it is spread out and warms less. Two volunteers hold the ball; the class watches and draws.
- **10 min. Two places, one latitude.** Death Valley National Park in California and Great Smoky Mountains National Park in Tennessee lie at about the same latitude (source to check: the park service pages). One is very dry, one is very wet (source to check). Why? Mountains stand between Death Valley and the sea (source to check); the ridge-and-rain model from grade 2 is the review. Both are warm in summer; rain is a different fact.
- **12 min. Where are we on Earth?** Each pair fills a Where We Are card for our town: continent, two hemispheres, cell on the class world grid, and how sunny and how rainy (the teacher supplies a free weather summary; source to check). Then answer supporting question 1 on the board in one sentence.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E6.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-3-6.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The park cards share the shape of E6b (warm and dry beside warm and wet); E6 is a new invented pair.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the ball and the board; pairs hold the card. Tool: None. By hand: drawing the light, filling the card, the sentence, the exit. Evidence: the Where We Are card, E6.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 3, "Why can a flat map not copy a globe exactly?"; day 4, "How do you know it is southern and western?"; day 6, "Why can two places at the same latitude have different rain?"
- Written task, day 6: one sentence for supporting question 1 on the frame "Our town is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres, and it is ___ and ___."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) the continent and both hemispheres right; (2) a warm or cold reason from sunlight; (3) a rain fact kept apart from latitude, from the named source.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 3 retrieves ss2-continents-features and ss2-projections. Day 4 labels continents and oceans again. Day 5 gives the location of one new place. Day 6 places a cell on the grid. All of these return on day 22. No fluency target: the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: globe, flat, continent, ocean, equator, hemisphere, latitude, longitude, grid, warm, cold, dry, wet. Frames: "___ is in ___, in the ___ and ___ hemispheres." and "Both ___, but ___." Accept a pointed card or a labeled drawing; repeat in English.
- **Access.** Raised-line outlines, a tactile globe, string for the lines, movable letter and number cards for the grid. A pointed answer is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits read aloud once; a scribe or a spoken answer is fine; spelling is not judged.
- **Reading load.** Cards are at most 350 words with no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every caption aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Give the continent, hemispheres and grid cell of a second place, and say whether its sunlight is more or less direct than ours.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: a ball, a string and a lamp. Draw the equator and a prime meridian on a printed outline, then place one new city. For the grid, make a letter-and-number grid of the table.
- **One projector.** Show the outline and the overlay. The teacher turns one globe; children draw and write on printed cards.
