Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a labelled drawing, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen on 158 and 160; the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to day 159 only, are given by the teacher after class and are never self-marked. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names and are living nations today. The stop card and the right to pass hold.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
- Day 159: assessment "end" (Check day).
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** none of the rows (sections 7.1 to 7.4 own them); this section runs day 159 whole and keys it in the unit plan's field 5. Day 159 is the check: five unaided prompts, then P0a to P4 scored from revised work. The check is not an exit.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, day 159 does not repair; the day 160 groups do.
- **E158 (the revision exit).** "Which rubric criterion did you change in your work, and what did you change?" Key: a named criterion and the change, such as "I added the reason that joins his action to the change." Wrong: a biography with an action but no change link left as it was (E); a claim still naming two sources with no details (I).
- **E160 (the fresh response task, Bus Card, invented for practice).** "A bus route band runs 1940 to 1960. A library band runs 1930 to 1990. The library has a stop on the route." Ask 1: "In which years did the bus route and the library both run?" Ask 2: "Say how the stop may have helped people reach the library." Ask 3: "What evidence is needed before calling the route a cause?" Key: 1940 to 1960; the stop may have helped riders get to the library; route and library records. Wrong: "The library replaced the bus route in 1940" (K); "The library proves everyone rode the bus" (D); "The stop proves the route caused it" (M).

**Day 159 flow (unit plan field 5 holds the keys):** Q1 nine minutes (B and C read aloud, 3; Q1, 6). Q2 six (A read aloud, 2; Q2, 4). Q3 three (B and C again). Q4 four. Q5 eight (E read aloud, 2; Q5, 6). Total 30, then 2 minutes of directions before and 8 of reflection after. P0a, P1, P2, P3, P4 are scored from revised work, not repeated.

## Day 158

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Fluency. Practises what earlier days taught until it comes easily; no new content is taught and no writing is drafted, revised or edited.
- goal: Review the project and the biography before the assessment
- note: written feedback 5, independent portfolio revisions 15, fresh observed deliberation and contribution retry 10, review 8, exit 2 minutes; revised external sharing completed by this day

- **5 min. Written feedback.** Each child reads the teacher's one-line notes, one on each component, and circles the two that flag a change.
- **15 min. Revise.** Each child revises the two flagged components alone on a clean copy; a new line, not a copy of the note. The teacher takes a small group of up to 6 for five minutes on the most-missed letter (E or I). A school adult hears up to six children who have no outside listener on their record sheet, about two minutes each, and initials the record; a second adult takes any more.
- **10 min. Observed retry.** Groups of six decide a fresh made-up question (a name for a class book, three names offered, invented for practice) by the unit 2 steps: hear and restate another plan, consider a compromise, vote privately, then add one correcting line to the group's existing contribution record. The teacher sits with each of five groups for two minutes and ticks the look-fors for the six children in turn, about 20 seconds each; a child not seen is heard first on day 160.
- **8 min. Review.** The question board's five answers and the evidence list are read aloud. Each child tells a partner in 30 seconds the one source that best shows who made the community, and why. No new content.
- **2 min. Exit.** E158.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the look-for sheet and the notes; each child the revised folder. Tool: None. By hand: revisions. Evidence: E158 and the observed retry (P4). Hand-off: tomorrow, the check.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 158: the teacher's notes. Day 160: the day 159 note.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 158).** A written note, a revision by pencil and a ten-second look-for tick in the retry; no student timer and no speed. The teacher's "switch" in the retry is a pace cue, not a clock the child sees.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: criterion, revise, overlap, contribution, evidence, limit, claim, source. Frames: "The source shows ___; this matters because ___." "I changed ___ because ___." "They overlapped; we need ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print packets and maps, described photographs, a scribe, extra time on 158, the strip on a clipboard.
- **Check accommodations.** Read sources and prompts aloud; enlarge maps; describe photographs; accept speech or pointing with an explanation through a scribe; allow processing time. Sources stay available; worked answers do not. Judge history apart from spelling and English fluency.
- **Reading load.** A child reads only labels and own work; the teacher reads the packets and prompts aloud.
- **Content notes.** The transfer packet and the check name enslavement and unequal work plainly and without graphic detail. No slur is read. The stop card and the right to pass hold.
- **Extension, same standard.** Test another source against a claim.
