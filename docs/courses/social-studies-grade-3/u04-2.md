# Section 4.2: The nations of our region and where they settled

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/4/s/4.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u04-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, map key and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labeled map, a filled strip or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Day 75 names the three nations of the Northeast edition and tells a homeland from the lands a nation holds today. Day 76 explains, with a feature and a practice together, why people lived where they did. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Teaching maps and cards are Curio Class originals drawn from each nation's own pages (unit plan field 10, checked 14 to 17 November 2026; wording "source to check" until the cards are made). The made-up river village is said aloud to be invented. Each nation is one example, never its whole region.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T2.3a, this section's share (day 75).** "Name at least three Native groups of the region, such as the Massachusett, Narragansett, Nipmuc, and Wampanoag." Part: name three nations by their own names. This edition: the Penobscot Nation, the Narragansett Indian Tribe and the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah). The standard's other examples are named aloud only where a source is on file (the Mashpee Wampanoag Tribe, sources.csv U4X).
- **MA.3.T2.3b (day 75).** "Locate the tribal territories within Massachusetts." Part: locate a nation's homeland and the lands it holds today as two different marks. The class's own region is the anchor; the Northeast edition is New England.
- **MA.3.T2.3c (day 76).** "Explain how physical features influenced where traditional settlements were located." Part: a feature and a practice together, with the page's words.
- **D2.Geo.6.3-5 (day 76).** "Describe how environment and cultural practices influence where people live in particular places." Parts: environmental influence (river, coast); cultural influence (a practice the nation's own page names).
- **Limits that bite here.** Homeland areas are approximate and may overlap; the nation's own account, never a state border, is the authority (B). The lands a nation holds today are not the whole homeland and not where every citizen lives (T). State names are not nation names (A). The river alone did not decide a settlement (C). One nation's practice is not every nation's (J). A listing of resources explains nothing (L). Seasonal movement is said in the past only where the page says so; the nations live and govern today. No origin theories, sacred teachings or reenactment.
- **Connections.** Map-key (day 75, from unit 1 day 7 to 9); region and New England (u01-4); physical features, river, bay, shoreline (u01-3, u01-4); the culture and land frame (u01-5); who made it, when and why (4.1).
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Legend, symbol, physical feature, scale bar (u01-3); state, border, political and physical map, region, New England, river, bay, shoreline, settle (u01-4); culture shapes how a group changes the land or adapts, "This group ___. So it ___." (u01-5); nation (u02-2); homeland, primary and secondary (4.1); claim, evidence, because (u02-6).
- **New here.** Present lands (the lands a nation holds today), seasonal camp (a place people stay in one season), the feature-and-practice chain.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The three-mark legend (homeland shading, present-land marker, state border) and why none is the others; why the three-nation edition is a choice of the region's pages, not a full list; why Penobscot is the model and the other two are located from their cards; that "traditional" names a way a page describes, not a finished past.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q2 (MA.3.T2.3a: name three nations, A and S) and Q3 (MA.3.T2.3b: homeland and lands today, B and T), both on the unit plan. The Q2 worked-case error S is first met on day 82.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's first five minutes open with that error's wrong example. Exit maps are new Curio Class drawings, not used in modeling.
- **E75a (New Regional Exit Map, part 1).** A locator of New England marks three areas 1, 2 and 3 by a river, a bay and an island; no names printed. "Write the names of the three nations marked." Answer: Penobscot Nation, Narragansett Indian Tribe, Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah), in any order; spelling not scored. Wrong: three state names (A).
- **E75b (part 2).** A second panel shows the bay area with a shaded homeland, a square and a triangle; the key reads: shading, where the tribe's own page says its people have lived a very long time (approximate); square, lands the tribe holds today; triangle, a city today. One ask: "Put an X on the lands the people hold today." Answer: the X goes on the square, not on the shading. Wrong: an X on all the shaded area (T).
- **E76 (New River Settlement Case, invented for practice).** "Some people in a made-up village, Alder Bend, use canoes to carry food between their homes and riverside gardens. How does the river help connect where they live and work?" Answer: canoes on the river carry people and food between homes and gardens. Wrong: the river alone decided where they live (C); every nation uses canoes the same way (J); there is a river there (L).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The question board's "Whose homelands are here?" is answered on day 75 and "Why settle here?" is begun on day 76 (finished on 82). The three-mark legend (homeland shading, present-land marker, state border) is taught on day 75 and kept to day 87. The feature-and-practice chain (feature, practice, place; "People ___, so they lived near ___") is new on day 76 and kept to day 82. Map-key: the legend trace is returned on day 75 (review).
- **Key moments.** Tools: None. Day 75, locating: ideal instrument, a printed outline map with a key and paper markers. Spec: print fits. No key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** The teacher draws the outline, the river, the bay and the island by hand on the board; children use paper markers on copies; cards are read aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 75


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The legend trace on a fresh nation-free map (review of u01-3): "The map shows ___; I can find it because the legend says ___." The check 1 map-key repair, for the whole class if many missed (more than a third), otherwise a small group while the rest repeat the trace.
- **8 min. Three regional nations.** Teacher reads three cards in our words from each nation's own page: the Penobscot Nation (river), the Narragansett Indian Tribe (bay) and the Wampanoag Tribe of Gay Head (Aquinnah) (island of Noepe; long-presence paragraphs). Each is named by its own name and lives and governs today. The class names the three aloud twice; state names are set apart. Question board: "Whose homelands are here?" gets its answer.
- **10 min. Homeland and present-land maps.** Teacher models the three-mark legend on the Penobscot map: homeland shading along the river, the present-land marker at the place the Nation's page names (sources.csv U4D; the square shows no town name), a state border drawn for contrast. Pairs trace the legend and say: "The shading shows ___; the square shows ___." Teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **15 min. Locate and name.** Each child places three numbered dots on a blank New England outline from the cards' feature words and writes each nation's name, then marks the Penobscot homeland and present-land marker. Teacher circulates; a scribe or pointing is allowed.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E75a and E75b.

Who holds what is carried: each child the legend card and the outline. Tool: None. By hand: the map, the exit. Evidence: the located map; E75a, E75b.

## Day 76


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of A and T from E75, five-minute form, whole class if many; the other letter at a side table during the River case.
- **8 min. Settlement explanation.** Teacher models a chain on the Narragansett Indian Tribe's Early History seasonal-homes paragraph (our short summary, sources.csv U4F; says only what the page says): People ___ (practice), so they lived near ___ (feature). A seasonal camp is a place people stay in one season. The culture and land frame is returned: "This group ___. So it ___."
- **10 min. River case.** The Penobscot Nation's own River description (curriculum index; our summary in grade 2 to 3 words; sources.csv U4A). Pairs fill the three-box chain, feature, practice, place, and write "the page does not say" where it is silent. Teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **15 min. Explain connections.** Each child writes two chains, one for the river and one for the bay, then one sentence: how the land or water and a practice together explain where people lived. Coast and river are the two physical features; no listing of resources.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E76.

Who holds what is carried: each child the chain strip; the teacher the two page summaries. Tool: None. By hand: the chains, the sentence, the exit. Evidence: the chains; E76.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 75, "How do we know which mark is the homeland?"; day 76, "What did the river and what the people did explain together?"
- Written task: day 76, two chains and one sentence.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors on the sentence: (1) a feature named from the page; (2) a practice named from the page; (3) no claim that the feature alone decided. Marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 75: the legend trace (u01-3), state and border (u01-4). Day 76: E75's letters; the three-mark legend; who made it, when and why asked of each card.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: homeland, nation, river, bay, island, seasonal, camp. Frames: "The shading shows ___; the square shows ___." "People ___, so they lived near ___." Accept home language with a pointed map and then the English word.
- **Access.** Enlarged high-contrast maps with patterns instead of color only; tactile markers for the square and the shading; read aloud; speech or dictation. Pointing counts for locating.
- **Check accommodations.** Read the stems and the key aloud once; a scribe; pointing plus a spoken name for E75a.
- **Reading load.** Cards are 40 to 60 words, read aloud; independent reading is captions and a key at grade 2 to 3.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second chain for the island and say what the page does not tell.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the cards, draws the three marks on one map with the child and asks "Which mark is the homeland?" The chain strip is filled by the child with the adult as partner. No question about the family's own place or nation.
- **One projector.** Show the legend card and one map at a time; children hold paper copies.
