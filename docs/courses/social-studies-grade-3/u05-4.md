# Section 5.4: Plymouth, the Compact and the Wampanoag, with the mid-unit check


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a filled card, an arrow or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to days 105 and 120, and day 105 is scored by the teacher after class, never self-marked. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names and live today; the 1621 harvest is told from the record and from Wampanoag pages, with no reenactment.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T4.1 (days 100, 105).** "Explain who the Pilgrims were, why they left Europe, how they traveled and governed Plymouth, and analyze their relationships with the Wampanoag and Abenaki/Wabanaki." Taught on 100 (who, why, how), with the parts below; relations on 103. Scored from the day 100 exit and the day 103 record.
- **MA.3.T4.1a (day 101).** "Explain the purpose of the Mayflower Compact and the idea of self-government." Taught: who it was made by, what it was for, whom it reached.
- **MA.3.T4.1b (day 102).** "Describe challenges Pilgrim men, women, and children faced." Taught on a Hardship Table by role.
- **MA.3.T4.1c (day 103).** "Describe contacts with Samoset and Massasoit, the events leading to a harvest thanksgiving, and later relations." Ousamequin is the name the nation uses; English records say Massasoit. Samoset was an Abenaki sachem.
- **MA.3.T5.1 (day 103).** "Compare the roles and decisions of leaders ... such as John Winthrop and William Bradford, with those of Native leaders such as Massasoit and Metacom." Here only Bradford and Ousamequin (role and one decision; interest waits for day 107).
- **D2.His.1.3-5 (days 104, 105).** "Make and use a time-ordered list of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time." Practised on 104; the overlap idea is first met on day 100 (which colonies existed in 1620).
- **MA.3.T3.1 to T3.3 (day 105).** The mid-unit check asks M2 and M3 (see field 3).
- **Limits that bite here.** Native nations are named as Wampanoag, Abenaki and Wabanaki; the harvest is called a harvest thanksgiving; the Compact's words are unchecked, so day 101 uses a labelled summary; the 1675 Mashpee entry is held for E103 and day 106. No child reports on family or religion; "church" is described only as the colonists' own history.
- **Connections.** Timeline (equal intervals, day 91; overlap, day 100 and 104); written cue then revision (day 104); sourcing (day 99); map arrows (days 94 to 96); grade 3 unit 2 rules.
- **New here.** separatist, self-government, alliance, sachem.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Who the Pilgrims and others were; why the Compact did not govern Native nations; the harvest from the record and from Wampanoag pages; how to give the written cue; how to score day 105.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** M1 (D2.His.1.3-5, day 105); M2 and M3 (MA.3.T3.1, MA.3.T3.2) are asked on day 105 but belong to section 5.2; MA.3.T4.1 to T4.1c are judged on day 105 from the day 100 to 103 records and revised on day 104. Day 105 is scored by the teacher after class (the unit plan's "five for marking" is a completeness check, not marking).

**M1 (day 105, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** On an evenly spaced strip 1500 to 1700, place Colony P 1585, Colony Q 1610 and Colony R 1625 (Colony Q lasted to 1650). Key: P in the 1500s (sixteenth century); Q and R in the 1600s (seventeenth century); Q still existed when R began. Wrong: gaps equal for unequal dates (A); the 1500s as the fifteenth century (AA).
**M2 and M3 (day 105, from the unit plan).** M2: on a fresh printed map, circle North America, Europe and the Atlantic, then write one reason Europeans sailed west from the voyage card. M3: draw arrows on the supplied map for Champlain's 1608 route, from France across the Atlantic to the St. Lawrence River and Quebec. Wrong: the land was empty (Z); every route ends at Plymouth (AE); reversed arrows (B).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. The teacher reads each stem aloud once.
- **E100 (Passenger Comparison, invented for practice).** "Why did Ann cross? Why was Bo not a Pilgrim? (invented card: Ann's church was not allowed to pray in its own way; Bo was hired)" Key: Ann, to worship in her church's own way; Bo was hired for work, not of the church group. Wrong: all had one reason (AF); everyone aboard was a Pilgrim (M). The teaching cases are sourced groups and different invented names.
- **E101 (Compact Exit Card; an unused signer list, settled in sources.csv U5R4).** "Name one group of people who did not sign the Compact. (the card says forty-one men signed)" Key: Native nations, women or children, or any group not on the signer list. Wrong: it governed Native nations (E).
- **E102 (Winter Care Card, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "Sickness stops the adults from gathering wood. A child carries water while a caregiver tends the sick. How does sickness make a family's work harder?" Key: fewer people to provide shelter, water and care. Wrong: only men's work mattered (V).
- **E103 (Later Relations Exit Card, from the unit plan; the Mashpee timeline's 1675 entry, settled in sources.csv U5R6, with the sale into slavery left out).** "What does the 1675 war show about one harvest gathering?" Key: it did not make lasting peace; later relations need other evidence. Wrong: permanent peace (D). The 1675 entry is not used in teaching or in day 106's cases.
- **E104 (Colony Overlap Card, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "Was R there when S began? Say how you know. (R ran 1630 to 1660; S began 1650)" Key: yes; the years 1650 to 1660 are on both bands. Wrong: S replaced R at once (K). Day 104 fixes the dates and all teaching bands use other dates.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The wall strip: 1620 and 1621 added; St. Augustine and Jamestown bands drawn as lines from the start year (days 100 and 104). (2) The record folder with a written cue on each record: one short line from the teacher after class, such as "add one real detail from the source" (day 104 revisions; the folder is kept for 105 to 120). (3) Leader cards for Bradford and Ousamequin (role, one decision). (4) The passenger sort. (5) The Hardship Table. (6) The question board: day 103 answers "What made cooperation possible?". (7) Return and repair, first part. (8) Withdrawn on day 119: strip, cause chart and claim frame, leaving the blank frames.
- **Key moment 1, day 100: the strip with bands.** Ideal instrument: the wall strip with movable bands (a line of paper for each colony from start to end). Spec: print and paper; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 104: the written cue.** Ideal instrument: a half-page record with a one-line cue at the foot, and a coloured pencil for the revision, so the first answer stays visible. Spec: print; the pencil is a supply.
- **Key moment 3, day 105: the mid-unit check.** Ideal instrument: a printed map and strip and two short source cards, each child's paper, one pencil. Spec: print.
- **A room with nothing.** A strip on rolled paper with bands of string; cues in a notebook margin; the day 105 maps drawn by the teacher.

## 6. Days

## Day 100


- **10 min. Return and repair (C from E99).** A fresh pair of papers on one beach rule, labelled by maker and purpose; pairs say what each says and leaves out. If few missed it, the missing children use the side table.
- **4 min. Timeline and overlap.** Place 1620 on the wall strip. Three bands are drawn: St. Augustine from 1565 and Jamestown from 1607, both still going. Ask: which colonies were there in 1620? (All three at once.) This is the first meeting of "at the same time".
- **9 min. Who came and why.** Passenger cards from a source about the Mayflower's passengers (Pilgrim Hall or a Plimoth source, to check); the class sorts them into separatist church members (left England to worship in their own way), hired workers, and families, and names each group's reason. If the sourcing is not checked, the cards are invented ones, said aloud. M and AF are shown from the sort.
- **7 min. The crossing.** The class traces the Mayflower's route on a fresh map, tail at the start (route and port to check; not Champlain), and says the direction and the ocean. Link to the day 94 arrows.
- **8 min. Record card.** Each child fills the record card: who came, one reason, how. The teacher gives a written cue after class. The teacher hears a rotating six; the record names them.
- **2 min. Exit.** E100.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the strip and bands; each child a record card. Tool: None. By hand: the sort, the arrow, the card. Evidence: E100; the record card is revised on day 104.

## Day 101


- **10 min. Return and repair (AF 5 and M 5, from E100).** Two fresh invented passengers: each pair states each reason (AF). Then five invented cards, Pilgrim or not Pilgrim (M).
- **4 min. Timeline.** Place the Compact's year on the wall strip (1620, the same year, shown as one more card on the same mark; to check).
- **8 min. Self-government.** A swim club (invented): the members make the rules for the club, vote and pick a leader; a table of "who makes the rule" and "who it reaches" (the members, not the town pool next door). Define self-government as the people of a group making the rules for the group.
- **8 min. The Compact.** A labelled Curio Class summary (the manuscript page is shown only for the look; its words are unchecked, source to check): men of the colony agreed to make laws for their own group. Fill the same table for the Compact: who made it, who it reached. The class says what it did not say.
- **8 min. Pairs: whom does it reach.** Each pair sorts a fresh set of six authority claims into "reaches the club or colony" and "does not reach"; E is repaired on the spot. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **2 min. Exit.** E101.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the swim-club table; each pair a claim set. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the sort, the exit. Evidence: E101.

## Day 102


- **5 min. Return and repair (E from E101).** A fresh set of authority claims; each child sorts which reach the colony. If few missed it, the missing children use the side table.
- **10 min. Hardships.** The Hardship Table (to check each row's source): building a house in winter, a garden that failed, sickness, keeping the community together, for each of men, women and children. The class reads rows aloud and adds a role to each.
- **8 min. Women and children.** A source (a museum page, to check) on women's and children's work: cooking, water, care and shelter. The class adds rows; the table says no row belongs to men alone.
- **12 min. Pairs.** Each pair fills a Hardship Table from three invented family records (a failing garden, a cold house, a long walk for water); each says what each person did. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **3 min. Record sentence.** Each child writes one sentence: one hardship and who faced it. The teacher gives a written cue after class.
- **2 min. Exit.** E102.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the big table; each pair a table. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the sentence, the exit. Evidence: E102; the sentence is revised on day 104.

## Day 103


- **5 min. Return and repair (V from E102).** Fresh care and shelter records; pairs name the work of each role.
- **4 min. Timeline.** Place 1621 on the wall strip; Plymouth's first year is one mark from 1620.
- **8 min. Samoset and Ousamequin.** Samoset, an Abenaki sagamore from Maine who greeted Plymouth in English in March 1621 (U5K); Ousamequin, the Wampanoag sachem (called Massasoit in English records), made an agreement with the colonists; both facts are source to check. Teach alliance with the invented swim club: two clubs agreeing to help one another. Bradford and Ousamequin: fill two leader cards, role and one decision each. The 1621 agreement's mutual-help terms are not read (they belong to dossier A and day 117), and interest waits for day 107.
- **8 min. The 1621 harvest.** From the record (Winslow's account as cited on a secondary page, sources.csv U5Y; Mourt's Relation was refused and is cut) and from Wampanoag pages (the descendants' view, U5Y): a harvest gathering in 1621, held by the colonists, with the Wampanoag invited and arriving. No reenactment; no costume; the harvest is not called "the first Thanksgiving". Wampanoag people live today and tell their own history.
- **8 min. Later relations.** The Sipayik Wabanaki history (to check): refugees, continued conflict and alliance over time; the Abenaki and Wabanaki nations are named; the Mashpee 1675 entry is not used.
- **5 min. Ordering record.** Each child puts three dated cards in order (Samoset's visit, the alliance, the harvest), and writes one line on why later relations need other evidence. The teacher gives a written cue after class.
- **2 min. Exit.** E103.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the two leader cards; each child an ordering record. Tool: None. By hand: the cards, the exit. Evidence: E103; the ordering record is revised on day 104. This day carries the sensitivity read.

## Day 104


- **5 min. Return and repair (written feedback).** Each child gets the folder of records from days 93, 95, 96 and 100 to 103, each with a one-line written cue from the teacher; read the cues silently or with a partner. D (from E103) is repaired in the revision part with a new dated sequence.
- **25 min. Independent revisions.** Each child revises the records whose cue says "not yet", at least four, writing in a coloured pencil below the first answer; about three minutes each. The ordering record's revision is rebuilt from a fresh sequence of dated cards for D. No new teaching. The teacher hears a rotating six, by name.
- **8 min. Timeline review.** On the wall strip: St. Augustine, Jamestown and Plymouth with their first meetings; children draw the bands, find the overlap years and say which came first and how many tens apart.
- **2 min. Exit.** E104.

Who holds what is carried: each child the record folder; the class the strip. Tool: None. By hand: the revisions, the bands, the exit. Evidence: E104; the revised records.

## Day 105


- **3 min. Directions.** The teacher reads the directions aloud: three tasks, pencil only, no talk; no self-marking; an adult scores later.
- **10 min. M1.** The evenly spaced strip with three cards (P, Q, R): place and say each century. The teacher hears no one; the sheet is the record.
- **10 min. M2.** On a printed map, circle North America, Europe and the Atlantic; write or say one reason Europeans sailed west from the voyage card. At most six children answer aloud at the side table (the record names them); the rest write.
- **10 min. M3.** Trace Champlain's route on the supplied map with arrows (source to check; not taught).
- **5 min. Check and collect.** Each child checks that every task has an answer (a completeness check, not a mark) and hands in the sheet.
- **2 min. Collect records.** The folder is collected for scoring after class: each part of each listed code once, from the day 104 revisions.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the folders; each child a sheet and map. Tool: None. By hand: the strip, the circles, the arrows. Evidence: M1, M2, M3 (field 3); scored 2, 1 or 0 after class. The response is in the first part of day 106 (field 2).

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 100, "Why did each group cross?"; day 101, "Who made this rule, and whom does it reach?"; day 102, "Who faced this hardship?"; day 103, "What made the alliance possible?"
- Written task, day 100 to 103: the four records (card, table, sentence, ordering), each with a written cue and revised on day 104.
- Judged by the teacher with look-fors: (1) a source detail on each record; (2) a reason or hardship named; (3) who is included and not; (4) dates in order. Present, with help or not yet seen on days 100 to 103; 2, 1, 0 only on days 105 and 120.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 100: sourcing from day 99. Day 101: the passenger sort from 100. Day 102: the Compact table from 101. Day 103: leader cards from 98 and the strip.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 104).** Revision by pencil, feedback read silently, the timeline drawn on the wall, no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: Pilgrim, passenger, voyage, hardship, self-government, alliance, sachem. Frames: "___ came because ___." "The rule reached ___ but not ___." "___ and ___ agreed to ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print cards, a colour for each band on the strip, speech, pointing or a scribe for all answers, extra time on 104 and 105 with the sheets read aloud on request.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem and each M task aloud; accept a point, a circle or a spoken answer; the six side-table answers on day 105 are open to any child who needs to say it.
- **Reading load.** Source lines are one to three short sentences, read aloud; a record is a card or a sentence; the Hardship Table is words and pictures.
- **Extension, same standard.** A fifth band on the strip for another colony or a fresh pair of leaders (invented).

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads each source, helps place each card and gives a written cue; on 104 the child revises in pencil, on 105 the adult reads each task and writes the child's answers. Nothing in this section is the last day of the unit.
- **One projector.** Project the strip, one table and one source at a time; children use paper copies.
