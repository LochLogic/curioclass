# Section 1.3: Reading and drawing our town map

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 1, Where we live (days 1 to 24): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 7 to 10.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (7, 9), fluency 1 (8), application 1 (10). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Why is our town where it is?"
- Section 3 of 6 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain; the teacher reads every caption and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a drawn map, a count or a filled table. Each day runs 40 minutes. The taught town map is a current official map of the class's own town; every made-up map and card prints "invented for practice".

- **MA.3.T1.1, this section's share.** "Use a current Massachusetts map with directions, scale, legend, and title to locate and describe the student's own town, its geographic features, and historic landmarks." Day 7 reads the four parts and locates the town; day 8 counts distances and finds physical features; day 9 finds historic landmarks and says what one tells about the past. The whole standard is carried here, in general form for the class's own town and state (Massachusetts is a worked example, not the case every class answers).
- **D2.Geo.1.3-5, this section's share.** "Make maps and other graphic pictures of both familiar and unfamiliar places." Day 10 makes a map of the school grounds (familiar) and a short map of Fern Hollow Trail, an invented place (unfamiliar). Both are practice; the grounds map is scored only after written feedback and revision on day 22.
- **Limits that bite here.** The taught map is a current official map of the class's own town; the map in the withheld packet (L) is a different map. Scale is whole bar lengths only: no fractions, ratios or rulers, and a count is a whole-number estimate, "about three bars". Public places only; no home maps, no family addresses. Each taught map and card that is made up prints "invented for practice".
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1 lean (a ruler's lengths, grade 2), day 7, for the bar length. Day 9's "shows, suggests, cannot tell" returns on day 15 (D2.Geo.2) and on day 19 (D4.1). The legend and north arrow return on day 18.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Title, key, symbol, compass rose, cardinal directions, the four-part map card, a paper strip laid end to end for scale (grade 2), "The map shows ___. I can find it because the key says ___.", the classroom map counted in paces and a written cue then a revision (grade 2), "the record does not say". New here: legend (the key's second name, the standard's word), bar length and whole bars, physical feature, historic landmark, shows, suggests, cannot tell.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Each map part has one job. North is where the arrow points, not the top of the page. A scale bar stands for a ground distance and is counted in bar lengths, never in steps. A map chooses what to show. Seeing a landmark on a map does not tell why it was built; a dated caption tells only what it says. How to choose the map: a current official town map from the town's own page or a federal map office, credit naming the agency, each image credit checked; print at full size and check the printed bar against a ruler.

- **E7 (Clover Court Map, invented for practice).** The north arrow points toward the right edge. The legend shows a fountain, a bench and a gate. "Use the legend to find the fountain. Which way is the bench from the fountain? About how many bars apart are they?" Answer: the fountain by its symbol; west (the bench is on the page's upper side and north is to the right); the count on the card, two bars. Wrong: picks the fountain by colour alone (B); says "north" because the bench is at the top (C); says "two steps" (D). The data asks it as E7, E7b, E7c, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E8.** A new printed bar scale and map with a pond and a gate marked. "About how many bars apart are the pond and the gate? Name one physical feature on the card and how you found it." Answer: the count on the card (a different pair and route from day 22's); a feature found by its symbol in the legend. Wrong: "six steps" (D); finds a feature by colour alone (B). The data asks it as E8, E8b, E8c, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E9 (Landmark Exit Caption).** A dated caption from a public archive for a different public landmark, withheld from teaching; the fallback is an invented caption printed "invented for practice" (for example "Mill Road Depot, 1912: freight trains stopped here"). "What past use does the caption say this place had?" Answer: the use the caption states. Wrong: names a builder, an age or a reason the caption does not give (G).
- **E10 (Stone Yard Plan, invented for practice).** "The gate is west. The well is east. A path runs between. One grid step is 5 metres. ## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: none.** A globe, printed maps and evidence cards do every job; no TOOL-ASKS.md entry. Supplies: a globe, world, physical and political maps, the local map pair (a sourced worked pair stands in on days 18 and 21 if no historical map exists), a satellite view, outline maps, grids, photographs, the Landmark Exit Caption, source cards, answer overlays, tactile copies, string, rulers, pencils. Everything is printed; one projector shows enlarged sheets and the answer key. The day 10 walk follows school supervision policy; no home maps.
>
> At home, the adult takes the partner's turn and writes down the child's spoken reasons; the public library grounds can replace the school map. Teach the key words with labelled pictures. Frame: "The source shows __, which supports __ because __."
<!-- /skel -->

- **Carried.** The four-part map card (title, legend, north arrow, scale bar), modelled on day 7 and kept on the wall; the paper strip for one bar length (handed out day 7, used day 8, withdrawn day 10 when children draw their own bar); the idea that a landmark's caption shows, suggests and cannot tell (day 9, returns days 15 and 19). Direction is tied to the arrow on day 7 and reused on day 18. Scale is tied to whole bars on day 8 and reused on day 22.
- **Key moment 1, day 7: the scale bar.** Ideal instrument: a town map with a printed bar, and a paper strip cut to the bar's length. Spec: fits; print at full size and check the bar against a ruler; fallback: draw the strip from the printed bar on that sheet.
- **Key moment 2, day 10: the school-grounds count.** Ideal instrument: the teacher paces the long wall or a path while the class counts aloud, and the class picks a bar of whole paces (as in grade 2). A pace is not exact; a count of about ten is good to a pace or two, so the count is an estimate. Fallback: a measured length from the school's site plan or an aerial photograph with its scale. Spec: fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper strips, a pencil-drawn map of the room with a bar, the town map printed from the town's page, a photograph of the school.

- note: retrieval task on grade 2 ss2-map-parts; earlier-grade lean math 2.MD.A.1 (ruler) for the bar length
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)

- **10 min. Return and repair.** 3 minutes: on the Maple Park Map (invented for practice) point to the title, the legend, the direction arrow and the scale, and say what each is for (retrieval of ss2-map-parts). 5 minutes: a whole-class repair of the more-missed of two things: error E from day 6 (compare two rain cards at the same latitude) or matching each map part to what it helps you do. The other goes to a small group of up to 6 in part 4. 2 minutes: hand back day 6's exit tickets with the written cue.
- **8 min. Title, legend, arrow.** Our town's current official map (maker, date and place read aloud). The title says which town or part; the legend ties each symbol to a place: "The map shows ___. I can find it because the legend says ___." Turn the paper: north stays where the arrow points. Pairs find the school's symbol and the nearest water, and say which way it is.
- **11 min. Scale in whole bars.** The scale bar stands for a ground distance. Each pair has a paper strip cut to one bar length. The teacher lays the strip end to end between two public places and counts whole bars: "about three bars". Pairs do two routes on a card. Bars are not steps.
- **6 min. What the map shows.** Pairs say one sentence using all four parts: "On this map the ___ is ___ of the ___, about ___ bars away." The small group of up to 6 takes its repair at the side table, 5 minutes.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-7-10.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Maple Park Map shares the shape of E7 (symbol from the legend, direction from the arrow, bars apart); the Clover Court Map is a new case.

Who holds what is carried: the wall card holds the four parts; pairs hold the strip. Tool: None. By hand: laying the strip, counting, the sentence, the exit. Evidence: the sentence, E7.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E7 (B, C or D): its wrong example read from a card, then the right one, then one fresh item together. The other letter goes to a small group in part 2.
- **12 min. Distances in whole bars.** Pairs take six route cards between public places on the town map (school, library, park, fire station and the like). - **10 min. Physical features.** Pairs find the town's physical features by their legend symbols (water, hills, shoreline where the town has them). A physical feature is a land or water place that nature made. Sort five legend symbols into "made by nature" and "made by people". Say where one feature is from the school, and about how many bars.
- **8 min. Mixed cards.** Five quick cards, each asking one thing (a direction, a distance or a feature). A partner checks each against the overlay and writes the answer.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E8.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 7, "Which way is the school from the nearest water, and how do you know?"; day 9, "What does the caption show, and what can it not tell?"
- Written task, day 10: the school-grounds map (four features, four parts) on the frame, a sentence "The ___ is ___ of the ___, about ___ bars away."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) each feature in the right place (direction and about how far); (2) all four parts present and used (a title that names the place, a legend that explains each symbol, a north arrow, a bar with its length); (3) the sentence uses direction and bars, not steps. Written cues are returned before day 22.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 7 retrieves ss2-map-parts (grade 2) and repairs day 6's rain-card idea. Day 8 counts bars again on a new set of routes. Day 9 opens with whole-bar counting on a fresh route and uses direction from the arrow. Day 10 retrieves ss2-make-map (a sketch of a reading corner's door and shelf). Fluency target: none; the standard sets none. **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 8):** short repeated sets of routes with an answer overlay after each set, a new route each time, accuracy before ease, no clocks or races; ease shows when a pair counts without the strip.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: title, legend, north arrow, scale bar, about, landmark, caption, shows, suggests, cannot tell. Frames: "On the map the ___ is ___ of the ___, about ___ bars away." and "The caption shows ___. It cannot tell ___." Accept pointing, a labelled sketch and dictation.
- **Access.** Raised-line or tactile copies of the map, a tactile bar and a strip that clicks to the bar's length, large-print legends and movable symbol tiles.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits read aloud once; spoken answers and a scribe are fine; spelling and handwriting are not judged.
- **Reading load.** Printed cards at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads the caption aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second route with a different bar count, or a second landmark with its shows and cannot-tell lines.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: the town map, a paper strip and a pencil. Find the title, the legend and the arrow, then count bars between two public places. Day 10: draw a map of the library grounds or the school's front yard (public), never a home.
- **One projector.** Show the town map, the overlay and the caption. Children use printed copies and strips; the screen never takes the place of a child's own count.
