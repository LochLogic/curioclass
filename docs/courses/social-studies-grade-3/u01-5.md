# Section 1.5: How people changed the land

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain; the teacher reads every card, passage and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a filled card or a chart. Each day runs 40 minutes. Every made-up card prints "invented for practice". Records use one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. This section has sensitive ground (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md): no child reports on home, family, religion or origins, and no child is the example of a nation.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.Geo.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain how culture shapes the way people change and adapt to their environments." Day 16 teaches the "change" half: what people did to the land, what they wanted and what else happened. Day 17 teaches "culture shapes" for both halves, change and adapt. Both parts of the unit plan's list (explain modification; explain adaptation) are taught.
- **D2.Geo.5.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain how the cultural and environmental character of places changes over time." Day 17 sets before, after and what stayed for a custom and a yard; day 18 does the same for land from two dated maps. Both parts (cultural change; environmental change) are taught.
- **Limits that bite here.** Native nations are named by their own names, in the present tense where the nation's own page does so; one nation never stands for all (N), and a nation is never said to do burning, farming or trails unless its page says so. Passages come from the local nation's own pages first (source to check; the sensitivity read comes first), are read aloud in short pieces, and any quotation is one sentence, word for word and credited. No sacred or ceremonial material, no dress-up, no role play, no "empty land" wording. Removal and loss are units 4 and 5. A land change is shown with a purpose and an effect, never as only good or only bad. The Elwha River, the Flood Garden Card and the Boat Parade Card are withheld for day 23 (Q7, Q8).
- **Connections.** Legend, north arrow and date on a map (days 7 and 10) are read again on day 18. Shows, suggests, cannot tell (day 9) returns on day 18 as "the map does not say". Science K-ESS3-3 lean on day 16 (people's choices change land); science 2-ESS2-2 lean on day 18 (a map as a model of land and water). Supporting question 4, "How have people changed this place?", is answered on day 18.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Change the land and adapt (grade 2); culture (what people make, do and share; people who share a culture still differ); tradeoff (one good thing and one hard thing); physical change and cultural change; changed, stayed the same, date; Native nation, named by its own name and living today; who made it, when and why, "the record does not say"; a bridge's benefit and harm (grade 1). New: purpose and effect, wetland, early map and modern map, "culture shapes" as an idea.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Culture is what people do and share, and it changes; it is not a fixed trait. A nation's own page says what the nation says, no more. A blank place on a map was chosen by the mapmaker; it is not proof that no one was there (the early map's maker and purpose; source to check). To choose a passage: read the whole page, choose three to five sentences about land care or ties to land, copy them word for word into the production file with the page's address and check date, and read them aloud with the maker named. If no passage passes the read, days 16 and 17 are not built (field 11).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q7 (D2.Geo.4) and Q8 (D2.Geo.5), on the withheld Elwha cards and maps. Teaching never uses them.

**Evidence by day.** Day 16: the card sort, the Nation Card, E16. Day 17: the frame card, the Before, After, Stayed chart, E17. Day 18: the Changed and Stayed chart, one sentence for supporting question 4, E18.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. Each is a new card, read aloud once.
- **E16 (Park Change Card, invented for practice).** "A town replaced a paved strip with a planted rain garden to soak up rain." "What did people change, and what did they want?" Answer: paving became planted ground; to soak up rain water. Wrong: "The rain made it; people did not change anything" (Q); "pavement, plants, rain" (P). The data asks it as E16, E16b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E17 (Garden Group Card, invented for practice).** "Families on one street pass on seed-saving skills. They replace a paved yard with garden beds and share seeds each spring." "What do the families share, and how does it help explain the beds?" Answer: they share seed-saving skills; the skills help them grow plants in the new beds. Wrong: "The families changed nothing" (Q); "seeds, yard, beds" (P). The data asks it as E17, E17b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E18 (Millpond Map Pair, invented for practice, dated 1900 and 2000).** A footpath appears only on the 2000 map; the pond is on both. "Name one change and one thing that stayed." Answer: the path was added; the pond stayed. Wrong: "In 1900 nothing was there" (I); "Everything is new, the pond is gone" (R). The data asks it as E18, E18b, one ask each; items.csv governs.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The culture and land frame, "This group ___. So it ___." (day 17, used on day 18, kept for section 1.6). The purpose and effect card (day 16, used day 17). The four-part map card and "who made it, when and why" (from section 1.3, day 18). The shows, suggests, cannot tell card (from day 9) as "the map does not say" (day 18). The local nation's name stays on the wall from day 16, in the present tense. Nothing is withdrawn here.
- **Key moment 1, day 16: the nation's own words.** Ideal instrument: the nation's own page, read aloud in short pieces with its maker and date said. Spec: fits if a page passes the sensitivity read; if none does, the day is not built (field 11).
- **Key moment 2, day 18: two maps side by side.** Ideal instrument: the early map and the modern map on tracing paper at one size, lined up on one fixed place. Spec: partly fits; scales and edges differ, so pairs line up one landmark and say "about". Fallback: a sourced worked pair from a federal or state archive.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed pages, tracing paper or two sheets laid edge to edge, a card set, the board.

## 6. Days

## Day 16


- **10 min. Return and repair.** 3 minutes: retrieval of ss2-people-environment: "People build a footbridge over a stream. What changed, and how did people change the way they travel?" Key: a bridge was added; people now cross above the water. 7 minutes: the repair for day 15's more-missed letter, G (sort three claim cards into shows, suggests and cannot tell) or P (link a fresh map feature to a photograph, 5 minutes). The other letter, or a retrieval miss, goes to one small group of up to 6 in part 4: the most-missed of those only.
- **7 min. Five ways people change land.** Land Change Cards (invented for practice): a field, a dam, a road, a filled wetland (land soaked with water much of the year; source to check) and a park. Each card has a picture, what people wanted and one other effect: a good thing and a hard thing. Pairs match each card to its purpose.
- **11 min. One nation's land care.** The teacher reads a short passage (three to five sentences, in two pieces) from the pages of the local nation, named by its own name and living today (source to check; credit and date read aloud). Pairs fill the Nation Card: the nation; what its page says it does with the land; what for. Where the page is silent the card says "the page does not say". Burning, farming and trails are named only if the page names them.
- **7 min. Made by people or by nature.** Pairs sort six new cards (invented for practice: a ditch dug, a gully cut by rain, a path worn by walking, a fence, a pond made by a dam, a river's new bend after a flood) and say "People ___ the land to ___." The small group takes its repair for the first 5 minutes, then sorts two cards.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E16.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-16-18.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Land Change Cards share the shape of E16 (what people changed, what they wanted); the Park Change Card is a new case.

Who holds what is carried: the wall holds the nation's name and the purpose and effect card; pairs hold the Nation Card. Tool: None. By hand: matching, filling the card, sorting, the exit. Evidence: the card sort, the Nation Card, E16.

## Day 17


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 16's more-missed letter, Q or P, as in field 4. The other goes to a small group of up to 6 during part 4.
- **8 min. Culture and land.** Review: culture is what people make, do and share; change the land and adapt (grade 2). The frame card: "This group ___. So it ___." The teacher models with the Dry Hollow Card (invented for practice): neighbours help each other fit gutters and rain barrels each spring, so every house keeps water for the dry summer; that is adapting.
- **12 min. A nation's own words.** A second short passage from the same local nation's pages (or a second nation, named by its own name, if its page fits better), read aloud with the maker and date (source to check; credit read aloud). Pairs fill the frame: "The ___ ___. So it ___." Where the page does not give the "so", the card says "the page does not say why". The teacher does not explain the answer.
- **10 min. Before, after, stayed.** The Orchard Lane Card (invented for practice): neighbours replace a gravel lot with fruit trees and still share the harvest with the street each autumn. Pairs fill a chart: what changed on the land, what changed in what people do, what stayed. The small group takes its 5-minute repair first.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E17.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-16-18.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Dry Hollow card and the Elm Lane card share the shape of E17 (a group practice tied to a place); the Garden Group Card is a new case.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the frame; pairs hold the chart. Tool: None. By hand: filling the frame, the chart, the exit. Evidence: the frame card, the chart, E17.

## Day 18


- **7 min. Return and repair.** Day 17's more-missed letter, Q or P (five minutes, the rest of the part spent on one fresh item). If M or N was heard in talk on the record sheet and is more common, that letter takes the 7 minutes instead.
- **5 min. Maker, date and why.** The town's early map (an archive map of the area taught on days 7 to 9, not the one in the withheld packet; credit read aloud; source to check), or a sourced worked pair if none exists. The teacher models "who made it, when and why", and reads its legend, arrow and date.
- **14 min. Early beside modern.** Pairs set the early map beside the town map from days 7 to 9, line up one landmark, and fill the Changed and Stayed chart: three changes and two that stayed, each with the date of its map. They mark one blank place on the early map and write "the map does not say what was here."
- **4 min. Swap and check.** Pairs swap charts. Does each change name its map's date? Is one thing that stayed named?
- **5 min. Supporting question 4.** Each child writes one sentence: "Between ___ and ___, people ___ the ___, but the ___ stayed."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E18.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-16-18.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Changed and Stayed chart shares the shape of E18 (one change and one thing that stayed, with a map date); the Millpond pair is a new case.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the maker routine; pairs hold the chart. Tool: None. By hand: lining up maps, filling the chart, one sentence, the exit. Evidence: the chart, the sentence, E18.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 16, "What did people want when they changed this land, and what else happened?"; day 17, "What does the page say the nation does, and what does it say why?"; day 18, "What changed, what stayed, and how do you know?"
- Written task, day 18: one sentence for supporting question 4 on the frame "Between ___ and ___, people ___ the ___, but the ___ stayed."
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: (1) names a change both maps support, with the date of each map; (2) names something that stayed; (3) does not treat a blank place as empty land; (4) names a group or nation as making a change only where the source says so. Mark: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 16 retrieves ss2-people-environment. Day 17 reviews culture, change the land and adapt (grade 2) and uses day 16's purpose and effect. Day 18 reads legend, arrow and date from section 1.3 and the maker routine from grade 2.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: purpose, effect, wetland, culture, adapt, nation, early, modern, stayed, "the page does not say". Frames: "People ___ the land to ___. Another effect was ___." "The ___ ___. So it ___." "Between ___ and ___, ___ changed. ___ stayed." Accept pointing, drawing and dictation.
- **Access.** Passages are read aloud, not printed. Tactile or raised maps, large-print cards, maps cut into strips, tracing paper for children who cannot line up two sheets.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits read aloud once; spoken answers, pointing and a scribe are fine; spelling is not judged.
- **Reading load.** Cards at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words; the nation's passages are read aloud, three to five sentences each.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second Land Change Card with its tradeoff, or a second change that the map pair shows.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the nation's passage aloud and fills the three-box card with the child, writing the child's spoken words. Day 18: the library's or the town's public map of the area beside the modern map; no home maps.
- **One projector.** Show the passage page with its maker, the early and modern maps and the overlay. Children keep printed cards; the screen never fills a child's chart.
