# Section 6.4: Closing claim, timeline, check and response

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/6/s/6.4), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u06-4.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to the teacher's scoring of day 135 only, from the record sheet, never self-marked. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names and govern themselves today. The stop card and the right to pass hold. No role play and no required patriotic act.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D3.4.3-5, days 133 and 135.** "Develop an answer to the compelling question using evidence." Day 133 builds the claim from details of the unit's sources with reasoning; Q5 on day 135 asks it unaided.
- **D4.1.3-5, days 133 and 135.** "Construct an argument supported by several sources." The claim uses one detail from the Declaration or Constitution and a check against the petition; Q5's second strand uses two named sources, A and D.
- **D2.His.1.3-5, day 134.** "Build and use a time sequence; compare developments occurring together." The fresh strip, its written check and its revision; the revised strip is scored.
- **Day 135 and 136.** The check asks every part of MA.3.T6.3, MA.3.T6.4, D2.Civ.8 and D2.Civ.5 once; day 136 responds to it. Neither day teaches new content.
- **Limits that bite here.** Grade 5 owns the war, battles and the debates. The Black freedom petition (January 1777) is used by description, never by modern simplified wording; its speakers are named as Prince Hall and seven other Black men, and the manuscript copy's limits are said (source to check). Slavery is stated as cruel and resisted. No claim is made for every member of a Native nation. A claim defends what consent requires and explains a gap between promise and practice.
- **Connections.** Unit 5's claim frame and two-source model (133), unit 2's norms card for hearing claims (133 pairs), unit 6's sections 6.1 to 6.3 (everything).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The petition's opening argument in plain words (that all people have a natural right to freedom; wording unchecked), and why it is checked beside the Declaration. How a claim differs from a list of titles (G). How to read a spacing error on a strip (H) and an overlap error (M). How to score a strand 2, 1 or 0 and record the missing part with its letter, not a total. How to write short criterion feedback on 90 records in one line each.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q5 (D3.4.3-5 and D4.1.3-5, two strands) and the revised strip of day 134 (D2.His.1.3-5). The whole of day 135 (Q1 to Q5) is run in this section and keyed in the unit plan's field 5. Packets A to D, the own-state card and C's persons are used on no teaching day, exit or record.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's repair slot opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E133 (Detail Card Exit, invented for practice; two asks, E133 and E133b).** Card: question "Do the people of Willow Park have a say about the park?"; Detail X "A sign at the gate lists the park rules"; Detail Y "Each year neighbors vote on new park rules at the meeting". E133: "Which detail answers the question, X or Y?" Key: Y. Wrong: X, because it is a fact (G). E133b: "Write a claim that answers the question, using that detail." Key: a claim that they have a say because neighbors voting on the park rules is a say; X only lists the rules. Wrong: "The park should be kind to people" (Q).
- **E134 (Overlap Card, invented for practice; added so the day has an exit, with the revised strip still the main evidence).** Card: "A mill ran from 1785 to 1835. A canal opened in 1810 and still runs." E134: "When did the mill and the canal run together?" Key: from 1810 to 1835; both ran then. Wrong: "The canal replaced the mill" (M). The strip itself (1787 and 1791 on a ten-year scale inside the New Jersey band, revised after the written check) is scored on day 135 as R4 and R4b; H is read there.
- **E136 (the response task; two asks, E136 and E136b).** Two new labeled glosses from the class's state constitution (Gloss Cards, Massachusetts Declaration of Rights Articles V and IX, sources.csv U6R). E136: "Do these two statements prove everyone could vote?" Key: no; officials answer to the people, but the plan still limited who could vote. Wrong: "Yes, power from the people means every person voted" (R). E136b: "Use both glosses to explain your answer." Key: the first says where power comes from, the second says the plan set who may elect. Wrong: only the first gloss used, so everyone voted (R).

**Day 135 is the check, not an exit:** seven unaided prompts (unit plan field 5); the sheet is scored after class.

**Records (not exits).** Day 133: the closing claim, details underlined and explained, checked against the petition; revised once on 133 after hearing a partner. Day 134: the three earlier records (125, 128, 131) revised, plus the strip.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

(2) The blank claim frame (unit 5): a worked example on invented material early in 133, then withdrawn; blank frames kept for the check. (3) The strip with bands: filled examples withdrawn on 134. (4) The record folder: the 125, 128 and 131 records are revised on 134. (5) The 0 to 2 record sheet for day 135 and the response groups on day 136.
- **Key moment 1, day 133: the claim and its reasoning.** What must be seen: a detail, a named source and a because. Ideal instrument: the blank frame on paper. Spec: print fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 134: the strip.** What must be seen: the same gap for the same years and the overlap marked. Ideal instrument: a ruled strip, one centimetre a year, 1770 to 1810. Spec: print fits.
- **Key moment 3, day 135: the sheet.** What must be seen: each prompt answered unaided. Ideal instrument: a printed sheet with a box for each prompt. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** The frame and strip copied on plain paper; packets read aloud from the teacher's copy.

## 6. Days

## Day 133


- **5 min. Return and repair (F or L from E132, whichever more missed).** One repair of five minutes: a fresh symbol caption beside a dated rights record (F), or two ways to study a symbol with the choice to decline kept (L). The other letter goes to the small-group table.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** The question board's six answers read aloud; the blank claim frame from unit 5 on the wall.
- **7 min. Gather.** For three minutes the teacher links two fresh details to a made-up claim about a club rule (invented for practice) in the frame; the frame is then taken down. Each child lists two details from the unit and the source each came from, on the evidence list (the Declaration's consent line, an article, the speech clause, a New Jersey rule, a symbol caption). The teacher hears a rotating six, one detail each.
- **6 min. Check against the petition.** The teacher describes the petition of January 1777 in words, not quotes: Prince Hall and seven other Black men asked for freedom, arguing that all people have a natural right to it (source to check; manuscript limits said). The class says what the promise was and what the practice was. The petition's comparison with the colonists' complaints is not used (reserved for the check).
- **9 min. Write the claim.** Each child writes a blank-frame claim: "I think consent means ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." Words, drawing or dictation are fine. A last line names a gap between promise and practice.
- **6 min. Hear another.** Pairs read their claims; each partner says which detail is strongest and why; each child makes one change. The norms card from unit 2 is used.
- **4 min. Exit.** E133.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the board; each child the claim. Tool: None. By hand: the claim. Evidence: E133; the closing claim, retained. Hand-off: tomorrow, the timeline and revising earlier records.

## Day 134


- **3 min. Retrieval, with the H and M model.** The unit's six questions said back. A fresh invented line (a bridge, 1790 to 1810, and a flood in 1835; invented for practice) shows year gaps drawn to scale. Avoid two overlapping bands here: that is E134's shape. If many missed G on E133, the small-group table also takes five minutes of G in the revision part.
- **5 min. Written feedback.** Each child reads the teacher's one-line note on the 125, 128 and 131 records (written across days 126 to 133, one line each).
- **15 min. Revise the records.** Five minutes each for importance to others (125), who drafted and approved each plan (128) and the three-question sort (131). Words only; a new line, not a copy of the note. The teacher hears nothing aloud; the notes do the work. The small-group table takes the G repair for those who need it.
- **5 min. Fresh strip.** Each child draws a strip with 1787, 1791 and the New Jersey band, 1776 to 1807.
- **5 min. Teacher written check.** The teacher walks the room and writes a cue on each strip (order, spacing, overlap): about ten seconds a child for 30 children. Children wait in silent reading; no one is held up.
- **5 min. Revision.** Each child revises the strip from the cue on a clean copy. No student timer; no self-marking.
- **2 min. Exit.** E134 (Overlap Card, half slip, one paper item).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the notes; each child the folder. Tool: None. By hand: the records and the strip. Evidence: the revised records and strip, scored with day 135. Hand-off: tomorrow, the check; the sheet is unaided.

## Day 136


- **5 min. Feedback.** Each child gets the teacher's note on the day 135 sheet: one missed idea, circled, with one sentence.
- **20 min. Groups.** Four groups of the 0 and 1 cells, largest gaps first, meet the teacher in turn for five minutes (the letters from the sheet: C, T, E, D, J, S, K, P, G, I, Q, N, H and M; each group's move is its letter's "if many" action in the unit plan, on fresh cards, never packet text); the rest work in pairs from the written move. Who holds what is carried: each child the note and the fresh task. Tool: None. By hand: the fix. Evidence: E136. Hand-off: tomorrow is course check 3, whose civics task asks what consent of the governed means for our town's or state's government.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 133, "What does consent require, and what was the gap?"; day 134, "What was going on at the same time?"; day 136, "What did you fix, and why is it right?"
- Written task, day 133: one claim with a named source, a detail, a because and a gap.
- Judged by the teacher with look-fors: (1) a claim that answers the big question; (2) a detail from a named source; (3) a because; (4) the petition or a second source used; (5) a gap between promise and practice named. Shown, shown with help or not yet seen on days 133 and 134; 2, 1, 0 on day 135.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 133: the repair slot and the question board. Day 134: retrieval and the H and M model. Day 136: the day 135 note.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 134).** A written cue, a revision by pencil and the strip drawn on paper; no student timer, no speed. The cue takes the teacher about ten seconds a child, not the child's time.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: consent, claim, detail, because, gap, promise, practice, overlap. Frames: "I think consent means ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." "The promise was ___; the practice was ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print cards, a color for each source, speech or a scribe for claims, extra time on 133 and 134, the strip drawn by stamp or tape.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all directions, packets and prompts aloud; accept a spoken, pointed or dictated answer; the six heard may include any child who needs to say it. Sources stay available; worked answers do not. Content is judged apart from spelling and English fluency.
- **Reading load.** Children read only their own claim and labels on the cards; the packets are read aloud by the teacher.
- **Content notes.** Days 133 and 135 name enslaved people, unequal voting rules and denied rights, plainly and without graphic detail. No slur is read. The stop card and the right to pass hold.
- **Extension, same standard.** A claim whose second detail strengthens the first, or a second gap between promise and practice.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads each card and the petition description aloud, lets the child say the claim and writes it, and draws the strip with a ruler. On day 135 the adult reads each prompt and writes the child's words without giving reasoning. On day 136, the last day of the unit, the adult reads the note, helps with one fresh card and asks the child to say what changed.
- **One projector.** Project the frame, the strip and the packets; each child holds a paper copy of the sheet.
