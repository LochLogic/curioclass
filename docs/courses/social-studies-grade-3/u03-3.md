# Section 3.3: Choices, rewards, banks and interest

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/3/s/3.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u03-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Every amount, bank, credit union and insurer is made up and said aloud to be made up (a baker's oven shelf, a recess box, Riverbend Credit Union, Shore Insurance); no real offer is recommended and no child says what they or their family have, save or owe. Exit-only cases are the gardener, the reading corner, the library, Maple Credit Union with Tern Insurance, and the Oak and Fir banks. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.Eco.1.3-5, this section's share.** "Compare benefits and costs of individual choices." Parts: compare benefits; compare costs (days 56, 57). A cost has two parts: the money it takes and the next-best thing given up. Day 57 names the opportunity cost, the next-best choice given up (review).
- **D2.Eco.2.3-5, this section's share.** "Identify rewards and penalties influencing choices." Parts: identify positive incentives; identify negative incentives (day 58). An incentive pushes; it does not force.
- **D2.Eco.9.3-5, this section's share.** "Describe financial institutions beyond banks." Parts: describe credit unions; describe insurers (day 59, with the bank reviewed).
- **D2.Eco.10.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain interest rates." Part: explain payment relative to amount and time (day 60): the same $100 for the same year, compared by dollars. The savings record on day 66 is the scored evidence; E60 is practice.
- **Limits that bite here.** No percent, compound interest, account recommendations or family finances. The class model leaves out fees and uses one fixed year. Made-up banks only, no company names. Scarcity happens in rich and poor places alike; being poor is not proof of poor choices. Never ask a child about accounts, income, debts, purchases or insurance.
- **Connections.** D2.Eco.5 and the budget model: the savings ledger returns on day 57 (second $20, Kept $40). D2.Eco.7: a sale price is a reward to buyers (day 58, using day 54's profit). Unit 2's tax and fee (review of social-studies-grade-3 u02-2). Math grade 3 unit 3 has taught sums within 1,000 by day 60; if not, the teacher shows the adding.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Opportunity cost (review of social-studies-grade-2 u04-2); benefit and cost card, the choose-and-give-up mat, given up (grade 1 u08-2); bank, loan, interest "a little extra a bank pays for keeping savings there" and save (grade 1 u08-3); profit, budget and the ledger (review of social-studies-grade-3 u03-2); fee and tax (u02-2); the question board (u02-1).
- **New here.** Scarcity (not enough time, money or space to do everything we want); money cost; incentive, reward, penalty; credit union; insurance; interest rate (dollars a year on each $100, no percent).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Scarcity is a limit, not laziness (C) and not an empty world (X). The word fee has two senses: unit 2's fee is money a person pays to use a service; a late fee is a penalty. Interest, from both sides: a borrower pays for using money, and a bank pays savers some of what it earns by lending (source to check: unit plan field 10; to read before day 59: banks may pay interest on savings and charge it on loans, not every account pays and terms differ). Credit union: owned by its members, who save and borrow there (the federal credit-union guidance in unit plan field 10; deposit, loan and member-ownership sections read; source to check). Insurance pays losses its agreement covers, not every expense; the insurance source is still to read.

- **E56 (Gardener Card, invented for practice).** "Gardener Card, invented. Name one benefit of weeding." "Gardener Card. What does she give up if she weeds?" Answer: The beds look neat. Re-potting the herbs, the other job, not the $6. Wrong: "It costs $6" (D); "Nothing, she can do both" (Y).
- **E57 (Reading Corner Card, invented for practice).** "Reading Corner Card, invented. Cushions are chosen. What is the opportunity cost?" "Reading Corner Card. How do you know?" Answer: The shelf: it was ranked second, next best after the cushions. It was ranked second, the next best after the cushions. Wrong: "The shelf, the plant and the lamp" (Z); "The shelf costs the most" (D).
- **E58 (Canoe Card, invented for practice).** "Canoe Card, invented. Which one is a penalty?" "Canoe Card. What might the penalty make a renter do?" Answer: The $3 charge for a late canoe. Bring the canoe back on time. Some may still pay it. Wrong: "The free hour" (Q); "It makes every renter come back" (E).
- **E59 (Sedge Credit Union and Tern Insurance cards, invented for practice).** "Sedge Credit Union Card, invented. Who owns Sedge Credit Union?" "Tern Insurance Card. What does Tern do with the money people pay?" Answer: Its members, the people who joined. It pays for the losses its agreement lists. Wrong: "The bank" (W); "It keeps your savings for you" (AA).
- **E60 (Oak and Fir cards, invented for practice).** "Bank Oak pays $1 a year on $100. Bank Fir pays $3. Which pays more?" "Same $100, same year. How much more does the better bank pay?" Answer: Bank Fir. $2 more. Wrong: "Bank Oak" (x); "$103" (H). Exit box extra (practice): "What is interest?" Answer: Payment for using money.

**Day 60 sentence (practice).** "Interest is ___. At Bank ___ the same $100 earns ___ more in a year."

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The benefit and cost card and the choose-and-give-up mat (grade 1, grade 2) are used on days 56 and 57, with ranking added on 57. The savings ledger returns on day 57 (In $20, Out $0, Kept $40). The question board gets its answers for days 58 and 60. The profit model from day 54 backs the sale price on 58.
- **Key moment 1, day 57: the ranked mat.** Ideal instrument: three ranked cards, one chosen, one given up. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 60: deposit and interest counters.** Ideal instrument: a $100 card and loose $1 counters. Spec: a pair needs 8 counters at most (offers $2 and $6) plus the card, inside the ten each pair has; nothing is shared across 15 pairs. The teacher hears a rotating six.
- **A room with nothing.** Bottle caps, index cards for the $100 card and chart paper for the mat.

## 6. Days

## Day 56


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of V and A (from E55), five-minute form, whole class; the other goes to the side table during Benefit and cost cards.
- **8 min. Not enough room.** A baker (invented) has one oven shelf with room for six trays. She wants four trays of bread, three of rolls and two of cookies. Counters on a shelf strip show nine trays cannot fit six places. Scarcity: not enough time, money or space to do everything we want. Pairs put six counters on their own strip and choose.
- **7 min. It is not about effort.** A second baker works twice as hard and has the same shelf (C). There is flour in the store; there is only so much shelf (X). The class sorts four sentences into "a limit" and "not a limit". No one is the example.
- **12 min. Benefit and cost cards.** Pairs fill two cards for made-up choices from eight counters: a set of markers or a bird feeder, five each. Each card has what I get, what it costs in money and what I give up. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of V or A for 5 minutes.
- **5 min. Say it.** "I choose ___. I get ___. It costs ___. I give up ___." Partners say it, one minute each way; the teacher hears a rotating six.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E56.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the shelf strip; pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: the strip, the cards, exit. Evidence: E56.

## Day 57


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of D and Y (from E56), five-minute form, whole class; the other goes to the side table during Rank and name.
- **9 min. Rank, choose, name the next-best.** The mat (three ranked cards, one chosen, one given up) from grade 1 comes back. The teacher models a class trip fund (tickets, a book, a game): the chosen is tickets, the opportunity cost is the book, not the game (Z).
- **12 min. Rank and name.** Pairs rank a fresh set of three cards (a jump rope, a ball and chalk; three sets), choose one and name what they gave up and why not the third. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of D or Y for 5 minutes.
- **7 min. Savings check-in.** The second $20 goes in the made-up ledger: In $20, Out $0, Kept $40; the goal bar gets a second box. Saving gives up spending now; the $40 is not lost (AD). Each child marks confident, needs practice or revised beside their ledger.
- **4 min. Say it.** "I choose ___. The opportunity cost is ___ because ___ was next best." Partners, one minute each way; the teacher hears a rotating six.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E57.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the mat and the goal bar; children hold their ledgers. Tool: None. By hand: the mat, the ledger line, exit. Evidence: E57; the ledger marks are not scored.

## Day 58


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of Z and D (from E57), five-minute form, whole class.
- **8 min. Pushes on a choice.** The teacher reads six cards (invented): a cafe's free cookie after ten coffees, a rink's $2 for each extra half hour, a shop's lower price on notebooks in August, a $20 fine for leaving trash in a park, a sticker for a tidy desk, a $1 fee for a lost book. Reward: a good thing that may make people more likely to act. Penalty: a cost that may make them less likely. A push is not a force: some will still do the other thing (E).
- **12 min. Sort and predict.** Pairs sort eight cards into reward and penalty and write one "might" for each (about 15 pairs). The teacher visits a rotating six. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of Z or D for 5 minutes.
- **8 min. A sale price and a late fee.** Side by side: a sale price is a reward to buyers; a seller hopes for more sales. A late fee is a penalty; it is not the fee for using a service from unit 2. Who might not change what they do?
- **4 min. Say it.** "This reward or penalty might make people ___." Partners, then written.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E58.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the six cards; pairs hold the sort. Tool: None. By hand: the sort, the sentence, exit. Evidence: E58. The board answer for day 58 is added.

## Day 59


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of Q and E (from E58), five-minute form, whole class.
- **6 min. The bank, again.** The bank record card from grade 1 returns: a bank keeps savers' money safe and lends money that is repaid. Eastside Bank (invented) is the example.
- **8 min. A credit union.** Riverbend Credit Union (invented): its members own it and save and borrow there; members elect its board (mycreditunion.gov, checked). The class says how it differs from a bank.
- **8 min. Insurance.** Shore Insurance (invented): many people pay a little into a shared fund, and it pays for losses its agreement lists. A coverage card lists a covered storm-damaged window and an uncovered ordinary repair (N). Insurance is not a savings jar (AA).
- **10 min. Match the needs.** Pairs match six need cards (two for each place) to a bank, a credit union or insurance and say what it does. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of Q or E for 5 minutes (G if many).
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E59.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the three place cards; pairs hold the need cards. Tool: None. By hand: the match, exit. Evidence: E59. The question board's institutions answer is added on day 60.

## Day 60


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of W and AA (from E59), five-minute form, whole class; the other, with N, goes to the side table during Compare two offers.
- **8 min. Why interest?** A baker (invented) borrows $100 for a year and pays back the $100 and $6 for using it. That payment is interest: the price of using money. The bank pays savers some of what borrowers pay (source to check). Review: interest as "a little extra a bank pays for keeping savings there".
- **10 min. Deposit and interest counters.** A $100 card and loose counters keep the deposit apart from the interest (H). The class says who pays and who receives (AB). Pairs trace one fresh saving card.
- **10 min. Compare two offers.** Offers of $2 and $6 a year on each $100 (invented banks). A table shows: same $100, same year, so compare by dollars: $4 more. Over different years, the offers cannot yet be compared (O). Pairs use 8 counters and the card. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs; the side table meets for 5 minutes.
- **4 min. Say it.** "Interest is ___. At Bank ___ the same $100 earns ___ more in a year." Aloud to a partner, then written.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E60.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the table; pairs hold counters and cards. Tool: None. By hand: the counters, the sentence, exit. Evidence: E60 (practice, a cue before day 66). Day 61 opens with Return and repair and a retrieval of grade 2 trade; day 61 does not take new interest content.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: scarcity, choice, benefit, cost, reward, penalty, bank, credit union, insurance, interest. Frames: "I choose ___. I give up ___." "A ___ might make people ___." "Interest is ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation or a pointed card.
- **Access.** Read aloud, enlarge type, counters and strips, speech or dictation, extra processing time inside an existing block. Score reasoning, not handwriting or adding speed.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe; counters allowed for E60.
- **Reading load.** The teacher reads every card; printed pages at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a different ranking for the same choice and name its opportunity cost; or say which of two incentives would work on a different person.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult and child use counters for the shelf, the mat and the $100 card, and read the made-up cards. Do not use the home's own savings, loans, insurance or purchases. For the mat, rank made-up items, not the child's own wishes about money.
- **One projector.** Show the shelf strip, the mat, the place cards and the table enlarged; children use paper copies.
