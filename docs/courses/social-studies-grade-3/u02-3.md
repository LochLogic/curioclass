# Section 2.3: Why rules began, rights and duties, and taking part


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every source and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled drawing, a filled table, or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. People in cases are a documented person from a credited local page, or invented and said aloud to be made up (made-up places here: Brindle and Tumbleford for teaching; Rilla and Jun for exits only). Real-fact lines rest on the unit plan's pages and the town's own pages and are marked "source to check" until sources.csv rows exist.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.Civ.3.3-5, this section's share.** "Examine where rules, laws and key parts of the U.S. Constitution came from and what they are for." Day 33 only: one school rule and one town law traced to a problem, a maker and what each protects. Key parts of the U.S. Constitution: 2.5, day 41.
- **D2.Civ.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain how groups make rules that set responsibilities and protect freedoms." Day 34: a group agrees a rule; each person's duty keeps a freedom for others.
- **D2.Civ.2.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain why a democracy depends on people taking part responsibly, and what that means for how each person acts." Day 35: five ways to take part and what a child can do.
- **D2.Civ.6.3-5, this section's share.** "Describe how people gain from working together, and what makes it hard, in government, workplaces, volunteer groups and families." Day 36: one benefit and one difficulty in each of five settings.
- **D2.Civ.11.3-5, this section's share.** "Compare how decisions are made in different settings, such as class, school, government and society." Day 37: class vote, council or town meeting, and election.
- **Limits that bite here.** A right is not a prize for obeying; a rule must respect a right. A broken rule gets a small, fair fix, never the loss of a right. Families are many kinds: the family in day 36's cards is invented, and no child describes their own home. No child says who in their home votes. The biography is a documented person from the town's past; no living officeholder, no campaign page. A child may pass in talk. Not here: how a class discusses (2.4), constitutions (2.5).
- **Connections.** MA.3.T1.3, how people take part (day 35, from the day 32 record). D2.Civ.9 and D2.Civ.7 begin in 2.4, which uses day 37's class vote. ss3-biography (day 35).
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Right (something every person here may do or have); responsibility; law; vote; election (voting to choose a leader; a vote on a plan is not one); ballot; citizen; resident; cooperate; freedom, power and duty, why groups make rules (review of social-studies-grade-3 u02-1); council, town meeting, elected and hired (review of social-studies-grade-3 u02-2); biography and the before, action, after boxes (review of social-studies-grade-3 u01-1); "who made it, when and why".
- **New here.** Problem, maker, rule and what it protects (the four boxes); duty for everyone, not only officials; taking part (vote, go to a meeting, serve on a board, volunteer, stay informed); public comment (a time at a public meeting when people may speak); benefit and difficulty of working together; the three ways of deciding.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A rule has a maker and a reason; saying "it was always there" removes both. A duty and a freedom come as a pair in a group rule: my duty to listen is how your freedom to speak stays. Speaking at a meeting is not voting. Cooperation has costs in every setting, and the costs differ. An election chooses a person; a vote decides a matter. How to choose the biography: read the whole page first; the person is no longer living, the page names a deed and what it changed, and the page is credited.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q4 (a rule traced to its problem), Q5 (why a democracy needs people to take part), the day 34 record (D2.Civ.4), the day 36 card sort (D2.Civ.6) and the day 37 comparison chart (D2.Civ.11), asked or judged on day 47 from revised work or fresh cases (unit plan field 5). Day 33's national-provision part is on day 41, not here. The day 34, 36 and 37 records are practice; each gets written feedback before its day 46 revision.

**Exit questions.** Each is a quarter page read aloud once, marked on one scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen). If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E33 (Rule Record Card, invented for practice).** "Rule Record Card, invented. What problem might have led to Rule 12?" Answer: Loud music at night kept neighbors from sleeping or resting. "Rule Record Card. What does Rule 12 protect?" Answer: Neighbors' sleep and quiet at night. Wrong: "Nobody made it; rules were always there." (S); "The teacher made it because people were bad." (S); "It protects the music." (x).
- **E34 (Reading Group Card, invented for practice).** "Reading Group Card, invented. What duty does each person get?" Answer: To listen while another person talks. "Reading Group Card. Whose freedom does that duty protect?" Answer: The other people's freedom to speak. "Reading Group Card. Jun talked over someone. Should the group take away his turns?" Answer: No. Jun keeps the right to speak. The group reminds Jun of the duty. Wrong: "Each person may talk any time." (x); "Only the leader's." (N); "Yes, take his turns away for good." (M).
- **E35 (Service Card, invented for practice).** "Service Card, invented. Why did the board need the residents' letters?" Answer: The letters told the board about a need, so it could answer it. Wrong: "The leaders already know everything." (Y); "Only voters letters count." (D).
- **E36 (Pool Card, invented for practice).** "Pool Card, invented. Name one benefit of the town working together on the swim program." Answer: A benefit such as the town can pay together for lifeguards and a pool no one family could, or many staff share the work. "Pool Card. Name one difficulty of planning that swim program together." Answer: A difficulty tied to this setting, such as people want different days or times, or choosing which hours come first. Wrong: "It is always easy." (E); "People just do not get along." (Z); "No difficulty, governments are easy." (E).
- **E37 (Rilla Card, invented for practice).** "Rilla Card, invented. Which vote is an election?" Answer: Saturday's vote. Voters choose a person. "Rilla Card. On Saturday, what do the voters decide?" Answer: Who will fill the school committee seat. They choose a person. Wrong: "Tuesday's." (P); "Whether to change the bell schedule." (P).

**Day 34 record (practice).** Choose one group rule card. "The group agreed ___. Each person's duty is ___. This protects ___'s freedom to ___." Day 36 sort: five rows (family, school, workplace, volunteer group, government), each a benefit and a difficulty. Day 37 chart: three rows (class vote, council or town meeting, election): who discusses, who decides, what is decided.

## 6. Days

## Day 33


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E32 (B or K), wrong example then right, whole class.
- **8 min. A rule's story.** The teacher models the four-box strip with the school's own rule for walking in the halls: the problem, who made it (read from the handbook, or "the class and teacher" for a classroom rule), the rule, what it protects. Review the three reasons for rules from day 26.
- **12 min. A town law.** Read one law from our town's own code page, with maker, date and link, chosen and checked by the teacher before the day (source to check; fallback: Tumbleford's leash law, invented, about dogs scaring small children in the park). Pairs fill the four boxes, the teacher asking "How do you know the problem?" (the page says, or "the page does not say").
- **5 min. A small, fair fix.** If someone breaks a rule, what fixes the problem? Teacher models a fix that matches (move the thing that blocks, repair what was spoiled). No one loses a right. Pairs say one fair fix for the school rule.
- **7 min. Match the rule.** Pairs match 6 made-up rule cards to 6 problem cards and name one thing each protects.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E33.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-33-37.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The rule and problem cards share the shape of E33 (the problem and what a rule protects); E33 is a new record, Rule 12.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the strip; pairs hold the boxes. Tool: None. By hand: filling boxes, matching, exit. Evidence: the boxes, the match, E33.

## Day 34


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E33, wrong example then right, whole class. Day 26 retrieval misses (D, up to 5 children) meet the teacher for five minutes during the next part, at the side table, 1 minute each.
- **8 min. Rights and duties.** Right (review: something every person here may do or have) and freedom (day 26). The teacher models with Brindle's sandbox (invented): the right is that everyone may use the sandbox; the group rule is to put toys away; each person's duty keeps the freedom for the next child. Duty now said of everyone, not only officials.
- **10 min. Two group rules.** A garden club card and a recess ball card (both invented). Pairs fill, for each: the rule, each person's duty, whose freedom it protects (the four-box strip reused, maker = the group).
- **4 min. A right is not a prize.** A card: a child forgets a duty once. Does the right stop? The class says what the group does (remind, help, ask to try again). The teacher shows the wrong idea from a "someone might say" card.
- **10 min. The record.** Each child chooses a rule card and fills "The group agreed ___. Each person's duty is ___. This protects ___'s freedom to ___." Seven minutes writing; up to 5 children speak it at the side table, 1 minute each. Practice, kept for day 46.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E34.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-33-37.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Brindle sandbox and the group rule cards share the shape of E34 (a duty, a freedom, a right kept); E34 is a new case, the Reading Group Card (E34c asks whether the group takes Jun's turns away).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the strip; pairs hold the rule cards. Tool: None. By hand: boxes, the record, exit. Evidence: the day 34 record (practice), E34, the small-group notes.

## Day 35


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E34 (M first if many), wrong example then right, whole class.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Recall the unit 1 day 1 biography: what did the person do, and what changed? Slips. A miss: a small group of up to 5 at the side table during the next part, before, action and after cards, 1 minute each.
- **10 min. A person who took part.** Read the short biography aloud (about 200 words; one documented person from the town's past who served on a board or volunteered, chosen from the town's historical page by the teacher, who checks the source, the date and the rights line; fallback: a Curio Class card about an invented person, said aloud to be made up). Say who made the page and when. Pairs fill before, action and after (review).
- **9 min. Five ways to take part.** Five cards: vote, go to a meeting, serve on a board, volunteer, stay informed. Define public comment. Pairs match the biography's action to a card, then say one thing a child can do (learn, ask a question, speak at public comment with an adult, volunteer with an adult).
- **7 min. Why leaders need us.** Two cards (invented) about a service decision: one before residents spoke, one after. Pairs say what changed and why the board needed to hear.
- **3 min. Our town.** From the day 32 record, box C: one way people take part in our town. Each child says it to a partner.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E35.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-33-37.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The biography page and the two recycling cards share the shape of E35 (why leaders need people to say what is needed); E35 is a new case, the Service Card.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the five cards; pairs hold the boxes. Tool: None. By hand: boxes, matching, exit. Evidence: the boxes, retrieval slip, E35.

## Day 36


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E35, wrong example then right, whole class.
- **7 min. A benefit and a difficulty.** The teacher models with a school group project (invented): one benefit, one difficulty, and how the group handles it. Say that every group has both.
- **10 min. Five settings.** Pairs sort 10 made-up cards (a benefit and a difficulty each for a family, a school, a workplace, a volunteer group and a government) onto five mats. The family is invented; no child tells about their own home.
- **12 min. The record.** Each child fills five rows: "In a ___, working together helps because ___. It is hard when ___." Eight minutes writing; up to 5 children speak it at the side table, 1 minute each. Practice, kept for day 46.
- **3 min. Handling it.** One difficulty and one way a group handles it, said to a partner.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E36.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-33-37.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The five-setting cards share the shape of E36 (a benefit and a difficulty in a setting); E36 is a new case, the Pool Card (a town summer swim program).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the mats; pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, the record, exit. Evidence: the day 36 record (practice), E36.

## Day 37


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E36, wrong example then right, whole class.
- **9 min. Three ways of deciding.** The teacher reviews: a class vote decides a matter and every member votes; a council hears people, then its elected members decide (day 29); a town meeting is voters who gather and decide; an election chooses a person (review of social-studies-grade-1 u04-1). A reminder of the grade 1 kite club may be said, not asked.
- **18 min. The comparison chart.** Pairs fill three rows from three invented Brindle cards: Room 3 picks a name for the class plant by vote; the council hears speakers and votes on a stop sign; voters choose a clerk. Who discusses, who decides, what is decided. The teacher visits 8 pairs for about 1 minute each. Practice, kept for day 46.
- **5 min. Close the board.** Add the answer to "How can people participate?" The teacher says the next day's work: how a class talks a choice through, and hears each view.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E37.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-33-37.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The three deciding cards share the shape of E37 (an election beside a vote on a matter); E37 is a new case, the Rilla Card (a Saturday seat on the school committee beside a Tuesday bell schedule).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board; pairs hold the chart. Tool: None. By hand: the chart, exit. Evidence: the day 37 chart (practice), E37.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: problem, rule, law, right, duty, freedom, benefit, difficulty, vote, meeting, volunteer. Frames: "The problem was ___. The rule protects ___." "My duty is ___, so ___ is free to ___." "A benefit is ___. A difficulty is ___." "In ___, the ___ decide ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation or a pointed card.
- **Access.** Large print and raised mats and strips; pointing or a communication aid is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe writes the child's words without adding; spelling and handwriting are not judged.
- **Reading load.** The teacher reads the town law, the biography and every card. Printed pages are at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second rule traced to its problem, or a benefit and a difficulty for a sixth setting.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the strip and cards at the table; the adult reads the town law and the biography and plays the second person in each group rule. Nobody describes their own home or family.
- **One projector.** Show the strip, the five cards, the mats and the chart enlarged; children write on paper copies.
