# Section 7.3: Questions, notes and a short biography

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/7/s/7.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u07-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the 2, 1, 0 scores belong to day 159 only. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". People of many backgrounds are on the choice list, women and men, and groups as well as persons. A child's own family member is never required. The stop card and the right to pass hold.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.His.3.3-5, days 149 and 150.** "Generate questions about individuals and groups shaping historical change and continuity." Day 149 teaches the two kinds of question and the choice of a person; day 150 asks one question about the person, one about a group and one about change or continuity, from the notes.
- **D4.2.3-5, days 150 and 151.** "Construct explanations with reasoning, ordered events, examples, details and relevant information and data." Day 150 gathers dated details and links from two sources; day 151 writes them in time order with a reason that joins an action to a change.
- **D4.3.3-5, day 152.** "Present summaries of arguments and explanations beyond the classroom through print and speech, with digital forms where appropriate." Day 152 makes a printed card and an oral summary for a listener outside the class. No digital form, upload or photograph is used.
- **Limits that bite here.** The list excludes the four reserved transfer cards (the charter's founding context, the Faneuil trade, John Peters, Lewis Hayden); no choice is a card of map A, B, C or E. Every choice has two sources with different makers; two details from one book are one source. Enslaved people are named as enslaved, with resistance and lives built. No student identifier is printed or posted. A person who is a child's family member is never required.
- **Connections.** ELA grade 3 W.3.7, W.3.2 and SL.3.4 are met in their units: the gathered-notes frame and the time-order template are practice here. D4.1.3-5: two named sources (150). D2.His.1.3-5: the person's dates go on the wall strip (150). Unit 1 before, action, after boxes (149). The day 148 interim answer (152).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A question about an action or a continuing practice differs from one about a name, a date or looks (P). A name and a date do not explain; an action joined to a change does (E). How to hold two sources: different makers, each with a detail. Why partner talk is rehearsal and the outside listener is the evidence (F). How to write a one-line cue on 30 notes sheets and 30 paragraphs.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q3 (D2.His.3.3-5; a question about a person, from cards B and C, with the group and continuity questions judged from the day 150 notes), P1 (D4.2.3-5; the biography, scored revised on 159) and P2 (D4.3.3-5; the revised sharing record). B and C are used on no teaching day, exit or record.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E149 (Reading Room Card, invented for practice).** "A town library nearly closed and neighbors kept it open. A. What color did the neighbors paint the hall? B. What was the first name of the neighbor who led the group? C. How did the neighbors keep it open?" Ask: "Which question asks about the neighbors' action: A, B or C?" Key: C. Wrong: A (P); B (P).
- **E150 (Source Pair Card, invented for practice; the notes record is retained evidence).** "Source 1: a museum page about a baker. Source 2: another page about the same baker, by the same museum." Ask: "Do these two sources have different makers?" Key: no; the same maker. Wrong: "Yes, they are two pages" (I). The notes record is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.
- **E151 (Mara Card, invented for practice; the biography is retained evidence).** "A. In 1890 the school grew. B. In 1880 Mara opened a school. C. In 1900 the school closed." Ask: "Write the letters in time order." Key: B, A, C. Wrong: A, B, C (E). The biography is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.
- **E152 (Sharing Card, invented for practice; the sharing record is retained evidence).** "A. Two partners read to each other. B. A poster hangs in the room. C. A librarian hears a summary at the library desk." Ask: "Which one shares with someone outside the class?" Key: C. Wrong: A (F); B (F). The sharing record is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.

**Records (not exits).** Day 149: three questions. Day 150: the notes. Day 151: the paragraph. Day 152: the card and the record sheet.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The question board, with the answers for 149 to 152. (2) The before, action, after boxes (unit 1), used on 149 and 150. (3) Source-sourcing: two named sources with different makers. (4) The class wall strip: the chosen person's dates added on 150. (5) The gathered-notes frame and time-order template (ELA): the filled example is shown on 150 and withdrawn on 151; blank frames stay. (6) The interim answer of day 148, which goes on the card on 152.
- **Key moment 1, day 150: two sources, two details, a link.** What must be seen: each detail beside its source and one arrow from action to change. Ideal instrument: a printed notes frame. Spec: print fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 152: the outside listener.** What must be seen: who heard it, and the question asked. Ideal instrument: a printed record sheet. Spec: print fits; no digital form.
- **A room with nothing.** The frame copied on plain paper; the sources read aloud from the teacher's copy.

## 6. Days

## Day 149


- **5 min. Return and repair.** K or D (from E148 and the day 148 cue), whichever more missed: new dated bands with an overlap; the other to a small group of up to 6.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** The before, action, after boxes for an earlier biography (grade 3 unit 1, or the unit 4 biography of Carol A. Dana, as a reminder only); each child says one contribution on a slip. The teacher hears a rotating six; the record names them.
- **8 min. Two kinds of question.** The teacher shows a made-up person, Tomas Reyes, a baker who lit the ovens before dawn (invented for practice), and a made-up garden club (invented for practice). Sort six questions: those about looks, names and dates; those about an action, a change or something that went on. Model one question about the person, one about the club, one about change or continuity.
- **9 min. Choose a person.** The choice cards (8 to 10, women and men, persons and groups; each has a one-line note and two named sources) are read aloud by the teacher; each child reads their own card. Each picks a person or group; several may pick the same. A second choice is noted. Local packet; Boston edition excludes the four reserved cards.
- **10 min. Three questions.** Each child writes three questions about the chosen person or group: one about the person, one about a group they belonged to or led, one about change or continuity. Pairs sort each other's questions into the two kinds.
- **3 min. Exit.** E149.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the board; each child the questions. Tool: None. By hand: the questions. Evidence: E149 and the three questions. Hand-off: tomorrow, notes from two sources.

## Day 150


- **5 min. Return and repair.** P (from E149): a fresh appearance question replaced by one about an action; the rest of the class starts the retrieval slip.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Source-sourcing: two sources need different makers; two details from one book are one source.
- **6 min. Model once.** The teacher fills the gathered-notes frame for Tomas Reyes from two made-up sources (invented for practice): source, maker, date, a detail; then one arrow from his action to its change, with a sentence that says why. This is the single filled example; it is withdrawn on day 151.
- **16 min. Notes.** Each child fills the frame from their two sources: source, maker, date and a dated detail for each, then the dates added to the wall strip. Each card is at most 120 words; the teacher reads aloud to a small group at the side table, up to 8. While children write the teacher walks and writes a one-line cue on each frame: about ten seconds a child for 30 children.
- **6 min. Links and questions.** Each child draws one arrow from an action to a change and writes one question about change and one about continuity.
- **3 min. Partner check.** Partners find each other's two sources and say which detail came from which.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the cue; each child the frame. Tool: None. By hand: the frame. Evidence: E150, the notes record (practice). Hand-off: tomorrow, writing it in time order.

## Day 151


- **5 min. Return and repair.** E (from E150): fresh dated notes joined to an action and a change, then a retry alone; the rest begin the time words.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** First, then, later, so, in the order of a day; used on a sentence aloud.
- **4 min. The template.** The blank time-order paragraph template is shown. The filled example from day 150 is taken down. Nothing is modeled on the child's person.
- **20 min. Write.** Each child writes five to seven sentences in time order from the notes: the person, at least one dated detail from each source named, and one sentence that says what changed because of the action. Words, a labeled drawing or a scribe. The teacher walks and hears no one aloud.
- **5 min. Partner read.** Each child reads the paragraph to a partner; the partner points to the place where the action leads to the change; the writer adds one "so" if it is missing.
- **3 min. Revise one line.** Each child changes one line on a clean copy. Practice.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the blank frames; each child the paragraph. Tool: None. By hand: the paragraph. Evidence: E151, the biography (practice, revised on day 158). Hand-off: tomorrow, sharing it.

## Day 152


- **5 min. Return and repair.** E (from the day 151 paragraphs), or F if many: a fresh paragraph with dated facts and no link; each child fixes one line.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** The claim frame (unit 2) said back; the day 148 interim answer is read to a partner.
- **10 min. The card.** Each child writes a printed summary of at most 60 words: the person, the action, the change, and the interim answer to "Who has made our community what it is?" A first name only; no photograph, school or class name is printed.
- **12 min. Rehearse.** Pairs say the summary in one minute, then the listener asks one question and writes it, 4 minutes each way and a switch. The teacher hears six children one at a time at the side table; the record names who was heard. Partner talk is rehearsal only.
- **6 min. Plan the listener.** Each child names, on the record sheet, a family adult or neighbor who will hear it by day 158, or says they will use a school adult on day 158. The sheet has a place for the kind of listener, the day and the question. Nothing is asked about home.
- **3 min. Check the sheets.** Each child shows the listener line; the teacher initials it.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the written feedback to come; each child the card and sheet. Tool: None. By hand: the card and sheet. Evidence: E152, the sharing record (practice; revised and shared again by day 158 after written feedback). Hand-off: tomorrow, how people took on a bigger problem.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 149, "What does this question ask for, a look or an action?"; day 150, "Which source gave this detail?"; day 151, "What changed because of this action?"
- Written task, day 151: a time-order paragraph with dated details from two named sources and an action joined to a change.
- Judged by the teacher with five look-fors: time order; two named sources; a dated detail from each; a reason that joins an action to a change; a question about change or continuity. Shown, shown with help or not yet seen on 150 to 152.

- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: question, biography, source, detail, action, change, continue, summary. Frames: "I want to know how ___ changed ___." "Source 1 says ___; Source 2 says ___." "First ___. Then ___. So ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large print, a color for each source, speech or a scribe for notes and paragraph, the frame on a clipboard.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each prompt aloud; accept speech, drawing or pointing with a reason through a scribe; sources stay in view; content is judged apart from spelling.
- **Reading load.** Two cards of at most 120 words each (the source read aloud to those who need it) and the child's own notes.
- **Content notes.** Choice cards that name enslavement, loss or injustice do so plainly and without graphic detail; no slur is read; the stop card and right to pass hold.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third source tested against the first two on one detail, or a question about a group's continuing work.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the two sources aloud, lets the child say each detail and the action and change, and writes the words. On day 152 the adult listens to the summary, asks one question and writes it on the sheet with the day.
- **One projector.** Project the cards and the frame; each child holds a paper copy of the frame.
