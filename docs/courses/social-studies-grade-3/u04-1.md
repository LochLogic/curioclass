# Section 4.1: Whose land, and how we know

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/4/s/4.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u04-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Day 72 opens the unit's big question and asks who else needs it answered. Day 73 teaches primary and secondary as a relationship to a question. Day 74 sorts four sources linked to Native nations. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Every invented case is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Every real fact rests on a page named in unit plan field 10 (checked 14 to 17 November 2026) or is marked "source to check". No sacred or restricted knowledge is used, no child speaks for a nation, and no child reports on home.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T2.3, this section's share (day 72).** "Explain the diversity of Native Peoples, past and present, in Massachusetts and New England." Part: only the opening idea that the nation near the school lives and governs today. The hook sets it; diversity itself is taught from day 75.
- **D1.1.3-5 (day 72).** "Explain why the compelling question matters to other people." Part: explain importance. Day 72 is practice: the exit is saved, given written feedback and revised on day 87; only that revision is scored on day 88.
- **D2.His.12.3-5 (days 73, 74).** "Generate questions about multiple historical sources and their relationship to events or developments." Parts: generate questions (a source question, days 73 and 74); connect sources to events (primary or secondary for one question, days 73 and 74). Days 85 and 88 finish both parts.
- **Limits that bite here.** The nation near the school is named by its own name, in the present tense, from its own pages; one nation never stands for all. Primary and secondary are said of a source for one question, never of age, looks or format, so one record can be both. Primary does not mean true. An oral history is primary for what the teller says and knows, not proof the teller saw older events. No sacred knowledge, origin teaching or ceremony. No child authenticates a source or speaks for a nation. A made-up case never stands for a real nation.
- **Connections.** Grade 2: maker, date, photograph, account and retelling, two tellers who agree (day 73 retrieval). Unit 1: who made it, when and why; shows, suggests, cannot tell. Units 1 and 2: big question, supporting question, matters to others, two readings, the question board and the supporting-question record. Day 75 takes map-key; day 85 takes corroboration.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Big question, supporting question, matters to others, two readings (u01-1); source, maker, date, photograph (grade 2 u01-2); account and retelling (grade 2 u05-4); who made it, when and why (u01-5); shows, suggests, cannot tell (u01-3); the nation nearest the school, named by its own name and governing today (u02-2); climate (grade 2 u02-2); the legend trace (u01-3); the supporting-question record (u02-1); written cue then revision (u03-5).
- **New here.** Homeland (named on 72, located on 75), primary source, secondary source, source question, oral history, the relationship strip.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why one photograph can be primary for who was there and secondary for a question it was not made to answer; why an oral history, an object, a museum label and a textbook page sort differently; the difference between a climate region and a nation's homeland; why the local nation's cards are made per school (the Northeast regional set starts on day 75); why the day 72 exit is a practice and is revised on 87.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** D1.1 (the saved day 72 exit, revised on 87 and judged on 88) and half of Q7 (D2.His.12, shared with 4.4: this section teaches the source question and primary or secondary on 73 and 74; 4.4 teaches asking across sources on 85). **Retrieval tasks (before-teaching evidence, not exits; keys from unit plan field 4).**
- **R72 (invented map, invented for practice).** A map with two wet coastal places and two dry inland places. "Which two places share a climate characteristic?" Key: either matching pair, with its moisture clue. Wrong: matches by a name or a shape on the map. Repair: if many, compare rain symbols together, then a new pair, five minutes in the day 72 questions part.
- **R73 (Seed Pot Records, invented for practice).** A dated classroom photograph of three seed pots; the teacher's same-day note says pupils planted three pots. "Who made each, when, and what do they agree on?" Key: photographer and teacher, the stated date, three pots. Neither says whose seeds sprouted. Repair: if many, point to the captions, match the shared detail and retry with two fresh records, five minutes in the day 73 compare part.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's first five minutes open with that error's wrong example.
- **E72 (New Community Question Case, invented for practice).** "A library display tells about a nation near us only in the past. Families come to learn about their region. Who besides our class needs our big question answered? Say why." Answer: families (or visitors, neighbors, library staff), because they need to know the nation lives and works today. Wrong: only our teacher needs to know (R); nobody needs it because the nation is gone (K). Saved; revised day 87.
- **E73 (Meeting Records Card, invented for practice).** Record 1, a drawing made at a town meeting by a person sitting in it that evening. Record 2, a history written years later by a historian who was not there. Two asks: "For 'Who came?', which record is primary?" and "Why is that record primary?" Answer: Record 1, the drawing, made at the meeting by a person who was there. Wrong: the older paper is primary for every question (D); it is primary so it must be right (M).
- **E74 (Snow Records Card, invented for practice).** Record 1, a neighbor's note written the morning the snow fell. Record 2, a town weather history in an old, worn book. Two asks: "Which record is firsthand for that snowfall?" and "Why is it firsthand?" Answer: the neighbor's note, written that morning by a person who saw the snow. Wrong: the weather book, because it looks oldest (D); a firsthand note is always right (M).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The question board: the big question and five supporting questions with their days (75, 82, 83, 84, 85), posted on day 72 and kept to day 86 (review of u02-1). The supporting-question record (question, who else needs the answer, a fact it needs, two readings, link) is filled on day 72. The relationship strip (maker, date, question, primary or secondary) is new on day 73, used on 74 and 85, and kept. The legend trace is used in the day 72 hook and returns on day 75. The day 72 exit is saved and returns on day 87.
- **Key moments.** Tools: None. Days 73 and 74: the sort. Ideal instrument: printed cards laid in two baskets. Spec: print fits. No key tool; nothing needs a spec or TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **A room with nothing.** The teacher draws the cards by hand and reads the oral history excerpt aloud from the printed card. The four day 74 sources are printed with their contexts, so no screen or recording is needed.

## 6. Days

## Day 72


- **5 min. Hook.** A town physical map beside a short card from the local nation's own page about its homeland and how it governs today (the 50-word nearest-nation card, review of u02-2; named by its own name, present tense; credit read aloud). Children say what each shows with the legend trace. Teacher asks: what does each tell us about who shapes this place?
- **5 min. Retrieval.** R72 on a card; pairs answer, teacher scans. A miss gets the repair in the questions part.
- **8 min. Local homeland.** Teacher models: the nation's own words for its homeland, said back as "homeland: the land a nation's people have known as home for a very long time" (our words; sources.csv U4D). A climate region is a weather word; a homeland is a nation's own account. The map shows ___; the page says ___.
- **10 min. Questions.** The class co-writes the five supporting questions with their days on the board. Pairs fill one supporting-question record, choosing "shaped" read two ways (it changed the land; it shared knowledge of the land). Teacher hears a rotating six pairs, 1.5 minutes each; a record column names who was heard. The R72 repair, if many missed, takes the first five minutes of this part and the record shrinks to the question and the link.
- **10 min. Importance record.** Each child writes a sentence, draws and labels, or says to the scribe: "Who besides our class needs our big question answered, and why?" Practice, kept in the folder. No one is asked about home.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E72.

Who holds what is carried: the class board; each child a record and a folder. Tool: None. By hand: the record, the importance sentence, the exit. Evidence: R72; E72 (saved).

## Day 73


- **5 min. Retrieval and repair.** R73 (two minutes, children say it to a partner), then the more-missed of R and K from E72 (three minutes, whole class if many). A retrieval miss waits for the compare part.
- **8 min. Photograph and map sourcing.** Teacher models the relationship strip on two invented cards, a dated Pond Day photograph and a Pond Map the class made later (invented for practice; makers and dates printed). The question is "Who took part in Pond Day?" Rule said once: primary for a question is made by someone who was there, or who tells what they know themselves; secondary is made by someone who was not there and looks back. The same map is primary for "Where is the pond today?" and secondary for "Who took part?" Limit said aloud: primary does not mean true; ask what it cannot tell.
- **10 min. Compare records.** Pairs add an invented object (a jug made in 1970 by a potter) and an invented oral history (a boat builder's recorded telling, 2019) to the strip, with a source question each. Teacher hears a rotating six pairs; the R73 retry, if needed, is a small group here.
- **15 min. Sort.** Pairs sort six invented cards, primary or secondary for one stated question, and each child fills the table with a because. One card is primary for a different question and the pair says so.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E73.

Who holds what is carried: each child the strip and a folder of six cards. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the exit. Evidence: R73; the table; E73.

## Day 74


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of D and M from E73, five-minute form, whole class if many, otherwise at a side table while the rest re-read the strip.
- **8 min. The relationship test, one new source.** The Makah cedar-bark canoe basket (Norma Hottowe Pendleton, 1984 to 1985, National Museum of the American Indian; photograph with maker, material and date attached). Question: "How do these sources show Native people's use of plants?" Primary for that maker's plant-material craft in that period, not for ancient practice. Printed photograph, credit and museum notices kept with it.
- **10 min. Four-source sort.** Pairs sort four printed cards for the same question: David Grignon's oral history (Menominee History interview, public broadcast, 25 August 2014; the short seasonal-use excerpt fixed in unit plan field 5, read aloud with his name), the basket, the museum label (maker, date, cedar bark, twill plaiting; secondary) and the Curio Class textbook page (15 November 2026; secondary). Each card gives its maker, date and relationship. Teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **15 min. Explain classifications.** Each child writes "For the question ___, this is primary/secondary because ___" for each card, and one question of their own that a card could answer. Words only; no replica making, no impersonating a speaker.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E74.

Who holds what is carried: each child the strip and four cards. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the question, the exit. Evidence: the sort table; E74. Compressible: the sort may drop to eight minutes if the repair takes its full five.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 72, "Who else needs our big question answered, and why?"; day 73, "Who made it, when, and was that person there?"; day 74, "Does a source stay primary if the question changes?"
- Written task: day 72, the importance record (practice, kept with E72); day 74, the four-source table with a because each.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors. Importance: (1) a group beyond the class; (2) a reason about what that group needs; (3) nothing about the child's home. Sort table: (1) maker and date named; (2) the question named; (3) primary or secondary with a reason about relationship, not age. Marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 72: grade 2 climate regions (R72), unit 1 big question, supporting question, matters to others and two readings, the legend trace. Day 73: grade 2 maker and date, account and retelling (R73); who made it, when and why; shows, suggests, cannot tell. Day 74: day 73's relationship test and the E73 letters.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: nation, homeland, source, maker, primary, secondary, question. Frame: "For the question ___, this is primary/secondary because ___." Accept home language with a drawn or pointed answer, then the English word.
- **Access.** Read aloud; enlarged, high-contrast cards; a raised outline of the basket; speech, dictation or a scribe; extra processing time inside an existing block. Reasoning is scored, not handwriting.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits read aloud once; pointing plus a spoken because is enough; no timer.
- **Reading load.** Children read card titles and captions of grade 2 to 3 level; the teacher reads every source and prompt aloud once. Printed pages at most 350 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second question that the same source answers as the other kind, primary or secondary.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads each card aloud; the child lays cards in two baskets and says the because. The adult is the partner for the pair parts. Do not ask about the family's own past, home or nation.
- **One projector.** Show one card at a time and the legend; children use paper copies. A room with no screen uses the printed cards.
