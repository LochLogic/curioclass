# Section 4.3: Five worked cases and a comparison

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 4, The first peoples of our land (days 72 to 89): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 77 to 82.** Days by kind: understanding 5 (77, 78, 79, 80, 81), application 1 (82). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "How have the Native nations of our region shaped this place, then and now?"
- Section 3 of 5 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, passage and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled map, a filled chain strip or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes: five to repair or retrieve, eight to model, ten supported, fifteen on their own, two for the exit. Days 77 to 81 are five worked cases, one nation a day, each taken from the nation's own pages (unit plan field 10, checked 14 to 17 November 2026; card wording "source to check" until made). Each nation is one example, never its whole region. Day 82 compares the cases and returns to the three regional nations. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Invented cases are said aloud to be made up.

- **E77 (Mattamiscontis Stream Card; sources.csv U4C, no date shown).** A short card: over about ten years the Nation has rebuilt the outlets of three lakes and finished more than twenty stream projects; alewife and blueback herring live in these lakes and streams; as numbers grow the Nation expects more harvest. Two asks: "Why does the Nation work along this stream?" and "Is this work in the past or now?" Answer: the fish live in these lakes and streams, and the Nation expects more harvest for its people; now, the work goes on. Wrong: there is a stream with fish (L); the stream alone decided (C); the Nation fished here long ago, and that is all (K, also for "long ago").
- **E78 (Choctaw Card, from the Choctaw Nation and Mississippi Band pages).** In the 1830s the United States sought to remove the Choctaw from Mississippi and many were forced away, many to what is now Oklahoma; about 4,000 stayed. Two asks: "Why are the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma lands in Oklahoma?" and "Did every Choctaw family leave Mississippi?" Answer: many Choctaw people were forced away, so the Nation's lands today are in Oklahoma; no, about 4,000 stayed. Wrong: everyone chose to leave (I); every family left (P).
- **E79 (Shawano Lake Card).** A college in Wisconsin writes that wild rice camps were common on Shawano Lake until the early 1900s and people harvested wild rice there (sources.csv U4Q); no map. Two asks: "Why did people camp by the lake?" and "Does the card show where all Menominee people lived?" Answer: people harvested wild rice there; no, it names camps at one lake. Wrong: every Midwest nation lives the same way (J); the lake decided where all Menominee people lived (C); yes, all Menominee people lived there (J).
- **E80 (Navajo Exit Locator).** The Navajo History page's sentence, read aloud: the Nation extends into Utah, Arizona and New Mexico (sources.csv U4S); a schematic with a dot marked "presence only, no territorial boundaries". Two asks: "Write the states the passage names." and "Do those state edges mark the homeland edge?" Answer: Utah, Arizona and New Mexico; no, the dots show presence only, not a border. Wrong: yes, the state edges are the homeland edges (B).
- **E81 (Shore Case, invented for practice).** People of a made-up village live beside a calm bay, keep their boats on the sand and fish beyond the bay each morning. Two asks: "Why do people live near that bay?" and "Name one thing the people do that helps explain it." Answer: they keep their boats on the sand there and fish beyond the bay; keeping boats there or fishing offshore. Wrong: the bay alone decided (C); every group lives by a bay (J); there is a bay (L).
- **E82 (Potter Card and Herder Card, invented for practice).** A clay bank beside a creek where potters make bowls and jars; herders who place summer camps beside upland grass for their sheep. Two asks, one for each card: how the clay bank and pottery work explain the place, and how the upland grass and herding explain the camps. Answer: clay at the bank supports the pottery work, so potters settle near it; summer grass feeds the sheep, so herders place summer camps beside it. Wrong: there is clay or grass (L); the land alone decided everything (C).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: homeland, present lands, removal, village, camp, river, irrigation, feature, practice. Frame: "The ___ and the ___ together explain ___." Accept home language with a pointed map, then the English word.
- **Access.** Enlarged high-contrast maps with patterns; tactile markers; read aloud; speech or dictation; a child may look away or pass on day 78. Pointing counts for locating.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems and passages aloud once; a scribe; pointing plus a spoken because.
- **Reading load.** Cards are 40 to 60 words, read aloud; independent reading is captions and keys at grade 2 to 3. Printed pages at most 350 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a feature and a practice the card does not name and say what the page does not tell.
