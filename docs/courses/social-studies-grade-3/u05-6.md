# Section 5.6 skeleton: First meetings and early colonies

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 5, First meetings and early colonies (days 90 to 121): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 111 to 112.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (111, 112). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?"
- Section 6 of 8 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record). Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Enslaved people are named as enslaved people, with the words from grade 2 (never "slaves" or "owners"). No role play and no simulation; no child is asked about their family, their body or their religion, and no child is asked to speak for a group they may belong to.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.3.T5.4** (days 111, 112): Explain that slavery was legal in all the European colonies of the 1600s and 1700s, including Massachusetts, which had both free and enslaved Africans.
- skill ss3-slavery-colonies (grade 3)
<!-- /skel -->

- **MA.3.T5.4, days 111 and 112.** "Explain that slavery was legal in all the European colonies of the 1600s and 1700s, including Massachusetts, which had both free and enslaved Africans." Day 111 teaches legal in every colony, who was enslaved and that some Africans were free; day 112 teaches enslaved people's own words and their resistance.
- **Limits that bite here.** The law is taught as legal and cruel, not as just. No colony is said to have existed through both centuries. Do not say every Black person was enslaved. Enslaved people are never shown only as victims: they petitioned, kept families and built lives. No graphic detail, no slurs, no reenactment. The petition's freedom request is taught; its family lines stay unused.
- **Connections.** Grade 2 words for enslaved people and forced moves (reminders only); Venture Smith's own words from grade 2 as a reminder, never an exit; the 5.5 work terms table (day 109); source-sourcing (day 95); retrieval of grade 3 unit 1's Booker T. Washington selection (day 111).
- **New here.** legal (the law allowed it), free (not enslaved), petition (a written request to officials), resist.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The plain statement that slavery was legal in every colony and cruel; why "legal" is not "right"; how free and enslaved Africans lived in Massachusetts (Boston records, to check); what a petition is and who could send one; how to keep the room calm (a stop card, the right to pass, no one asked to be an example).

- **5 min. Return and repair (O and AB from E110).** One repair: a fresh dated caption beside a current classroom record; each child names a change and a thing that stayed. If few missed it, those children use the side table.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** What the class read in grade 3 unit 1 about Booker T. Washington (what the selection says to be checked with the English plan; ask only what the selection states). Then the grade 2 words enslaved people and enslavers, and forced. The teacher states plainly that slavery was legal and cruel.
- **8 min. Legal in every colony.** A colony table made by us for Virginia and Massachusetts only (the pages name no others; sources.csv U5R, U5L), each with the year it began on the wall strip and one line: slavery was legal here. No colony is said to last through both centuries. Ask: "Which colonies were in the North? Which in the South?" Slavery was in all of them.
- **8 min. Who was enslaved.** From the Boston slavery exhibit (source to check): Africans, some brought across the Atlantic and some born in the colony, worked in homes, shops and on wharves; they were enslaved for life. A card names one person as an enslaved person (to choose; no graphic detail).
- **8 min. Some Africans were free.** A card on a free Black resident of Boston, an invented free person labelled made up; Atkins is held for day 120: who they were and one thing they did. Beside it, the enslavement card. The class says which was free and which was enslaved, and that Africans in Boston were both (R).
- **5 min. Record.** Each child fills the Boston lines: one free, one enslaved, with the frame "___ was free." "___ was enslaved." The teacher hears a rotating six; the record names them.
- **2 min. Exit.** E111.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the table; each child a record. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the two lines. Evidence: E111; the record. This day carries the sensitivity read. Hand-off: tomorrow, the words of enslaved people themselves.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: legal, free, enslaved, petition, resist, North, South. Frames: "___ was legal in ___." "They asked for ___ because ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print cards, a colour for free and for enslaved lines, speech or a scribe for any answer, the table filled by pointing.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a spoken or pointed answer; the six heard may include any child who needs to say it.
- **Reading load.** Cards are one to three short sentences read aloud; primary words are one short sentence.
- **Content notes.** Both days name slavery (held for life, denied freedom, sold) and the denial of rights. No graphic detail, no slurs, no role play. Children may use the stop card or pass at any point; no child is asked for the view of any group.
- **Extension, same standard.** A fresh colony row for the table, or a second petition clause to name the action in.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the cards aloud and slowly, lets the child stop at any time and writes the child's words if asked. Nothing is acted out. Nothing in this section is the last day of the unit.
- **One projector.** Project the table or the clause; each child holds a paper copy.
