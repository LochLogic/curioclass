# Section 2.2: What governments do, and how ours is organized

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/2/s/2.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u02-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every source and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table, or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. People in cases are offices and titles from a credited page, or invented and said aloud to be made up (made-up places here: Bayview, a country, for day 28; Cedar Hill, a district, for day 30; Quill Town, exit only). The real-fact lines rest on the unit plan's pages, read 14 November 2026, and stay marked "source to check" until sources.csv rows exist.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T1.3, this section's share.** "Explain why schools, towns, and cities have governments, what they do, how Massachusetts local governments are organized, and how people take part in their communities." Here: what they do and how they are organized (days 28 to 30, 32) and one way people take part, from our own town's record (day 32). The fuller account of taking part is 2.3, day 35. Why: 2.1.
- **D2.Civ.1.3-5, this section's share.** "Tell apart what different government officials, levels and branches may do and must do, across times and places." Day 28 adds the three jobs of a state and national government (make laws, carry them out, decide cases) to 2.1's levels; days 29 and 30 tell apart who does what in a town and in a school district.
- **D2.Eco.12.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain how the government pays for the goods and services it provides." Day 31: taxes and fees, and that a government collects, then spends. No arithmetic is scored.
- **Limits that bite here.** The three jobs are named for the state and nation only; our town and district are studied as they are, and no class is told every town copies one form. A tribal nation is named by its own name, in the present tense, from its own page, never as a past or generic people and never fitted to a template; no costume, role play or sacred material. Cases name offices and titles, no officeholder's name or party. No child says whether their home is owned or rented, or what anyone pays. Property tax is one source of school money, not the only one. Not here: the Menominee comparison (43), constitutions (2.5).
- **Connections.** D2.Civ.2: day 32's notice names a way to take part, taught day 35. Unit 1 map (legend, symbol): day 32 retrieval.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Government; leader; mayor, governor, President; law; tax (money many people pay together for things everyone uses); nation (a people with their own government); level of government, power and duty (review of social-studies-grade-3 u02-1); question board; "who made it, when and why"; legend and symbol (review of social-studies-grade-3 u01-3). Grade 1's Navajo Nation line may be said again as a reminder, never as the day 28 check.
- **New here.** Branch and the three jobs; legislature; council; town manager; town meeting; school committee; superintendent; elected and hired; fee; property tax (money collected from owners of buildings and land); collect and spend.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The three jobs are a pattern for states and the nation, not for every government; a town may split them another way, and a tribal nation sets up its own. Check each real line against the page before the day: the three town forms (mayor and council; council and manager; Select Board and Town Meeting), our town's record, and the district page (committees may be elected or appointed, a superintendent is hired). School governance (policy for all schools) is not classroom management. A fee is paid by the person who uses one service; a tax is paid by many for what many use. Money used for one service may come from several levels.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 (what a government does and how ours is organized; day 27 feeds the "why"), Q2 (powers at different levels and branches) and Q3 (how services are paid for), asked on day 47 from fresh cases (unit plan field 5). Nothing from days 28 to 32 is scored the day it is taught; the Local Government Record, class edition, is practice.

**Exit questions.** Each is a quarter page read aloud once, marked on one scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen). If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E28 (Job Card, invented for practice).** "Job Card, invented. Who makes the state's laws: Brynn, Cole or Tali?" Answer: Brynn, the state lawmaker, makes the state's laws. "Job Card. Who carries out the state's laws?" Answer: Cole, the governor, carries out the state's laws. "Job Card. Who decides cases about the state's laws?" Answer: Tali, the judge in a state court, decides cases about the laws. Wrong: "Cole, the governor, makes all the laws." (A); "All three make the laws." (A); "Brynn does." (A); "Tali does." (A); "Cole decides them." (A).
- **E29 (Town Form Card, invented for practice).** "Town Form Card, invented. Which person is the town manager?" Answer: Marit. She was hired to run the town's daily work. "Town Form Card. Write one way Quill Town's form is different from Boston's." Answer: Quill Town hires a manager to run the daily work; in Boston the elected mayor leads the city. Wrong: "Marit is the mayor." (B); "Ezra is the manager." (A); "She votes on town laws." (A); "Both of them make the laws." (A); "She is called the manager." (X).
- **E30 (School Card, invented for practice).** "School Card, invented. Who is on the school committee?" Answer: Tess, and the six others who vote on the school budget. "School Card. Who leads the daily running of all the schools?" Answer: Ormond, the superintendent the committee hired. Wrong: "Rex is on it." (A); "Rex is the superintendent." (A); "The committee runs the buses each day." (A).
- **E31 (Town Hall Card, invented for practice).** "Town Hall Card, invented. Which government spent the tax money on the heating?" Answer: The town spent it. The state collected the tax and gave some to the town. "Town Hall Card. A child says the repair was free. What paid for it?" Answer: Tax money that many people paid together. It was not free. Wrong: "The state spent it." (x); "Nothing, the government has its own money." (C); "People gave it as a gift." (L).
- **E32 (Town Exit Notices).** "Notice A. Name the body in this notice that votes on rules." Answer: The body Notice A names. Local: the teacher's overlay for our town's new notice governs (default card: the Alder Town Council). "Notices A and B. Write one way the two towns differ in who votes on rules." Answer: the body each notice names, and how they differ. Local: the teacher's overlay for the comparison town's new notice governs (default card: the voters at Fenn Town Meeting). Wrong: "The visitors vote." (K); "The council, like the other town." (B); "Everyone who comes votes on every rule." (K).

**Local Government Record, class edition (day 32, practice).** Box A: who makes our town's rules. Box B: who runs its daily work. Box C: one way people take part. Box D: one way the comparison town matches or differs. Titles from the page; no names.

## 6. Days

## Day 28


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E27, wrong example then right, whole class. Small group names from day 27 (up to 5, 1 minute each) are heard during the sort below.
- **14 min. Leaders and the three jobs.** 3 min review: place mayor, governor, President cards on town, state, country (review of social-studies-grade-3 u02-1). 11 min: the national government is the worked example. The teacher shows three cards, one person each, in plain words: a legislature (Congress) makes laws; the President carries them out; courts decide cases about laws (source to check). Name the three jobs as branches. Say what each may do and may not do ("lawmakers may make laws, not carry them out").
- **8 min. A made-up country.** Bayview (a made-up country, invented for practice) has a lawmaking council, a chief and a court, shown as picture cards. Pairs sort 8 job cards into three baskets. A small group (up to 5, ss2 misses from day 27) meets the teacher here, 1 minute each.
- **10 min. A nation that governs itself.** The teacher reads a 50-word card about the tribal nation nearest the school, by its own name, in the present tense, from its own page (maker, date and link read aloud; sensitivity read before use; fallback: the Navajo Nation line from grade 1, said as a reminder, source to check). Say: this nation has its own leaders and laws; it sets up its government itself. Pairs fill "The ___ Nation: its page says ___." Where the page does not say, write "the page does not say." No child is asked to speak for a nation; heard from a rotating six, named on the record sheet.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E28 (the tribal nation is not in it).

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-28-32.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The three-jobs cards and the Bayview cards share the shape of E28 (who makes, carries out or decides); E28 is a new set of people on the Job Card.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the three baskets and the board; pairs hold the sort and the nation card. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, the nation card, the exit. Evidence: the sort, the nation card (shown, shown with help, not yet seen), E28.

## Day 29


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E28 (the nation letters I and T go first if many), wrong example then right, whole class.
- **7 min. Same jobs in a town.** Show that a town also makes rules and runs daily work, with two words: elected (chosen by voters) and hired (chosen to do a job). The teacher shows one picture of two people at two jobs.
- **15 min. Three real forms.** Three cards, one for each form, written by the teacher from the pages read: Boston (mayor and council), Boulder (council and manager) and Lexington (Select Board and Town Meeting), each with maker, date and link. The teacher reads one card every 2 minutes (6 min); pairs add each town's row to the chart "Our towns" (who makes the rules, who runs daily work; 9 min). If a class lives in one of these towns, the comparison town on day 32 is another.
- **10 min. Which form?** Six invented role cards ("hired to run the daily work", "voters gather and vote on rules" and others) are sorted by pairs onto the three forms. Then: "Does every town do it the same way?" answered from the chart.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E29.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-28-32.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Boston, Boulder and Lexington cards share the shape of E29 (who is the manager); E29 is a new town, Quill Town.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the chart; pairs hold the rows. Tool: None. By hand: chart rows, sort, exit. Evidence: the chart row, the sort, E29.

## Day 31


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter from E30, wrong example then right, whole class.

- **10 min. Tax or fee?** The teacher explains: a tax is paid by many for what many use (property tax goes to schools, roads and more; source to check); a fee is paid by the person who uses one service (a ticket to the town pool, invented). - **8 min. Collect, then spend.** The three-box strip: payers, the collector (the government), the spender. ## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: branch, council, manager, elected, hired, committee, superintendent, tax, fee, collect, spend. Frames: "The ___ makes the laws." "___ is hired by ___." "The town collects ___, then spends it on ___." "Both ___, but ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation or a pointed card.
- **Access.** Large print and raised baskets or strips; pointing or a communication aid is an answer; the jar can be shown with large counters.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe writes the child's words without adding; spelling and handwriting are not judged.
- **Reading load.** The teacher reads every source card and packet page. Printed pages are at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** A fourth form, or a second service traced from payer to spender.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Print the town's page and the other town's page. Read each aloud and fill the record. Use a jar and counters for the strip. The adult takes the second person in each card.
- **One projector.** Show the three baskets, the strip, the chart and each page enlarged; children mark paper copies.
