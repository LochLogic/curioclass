# Section 7.4: Informed action and a closing claim


## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the 2, 1, 0 scores belong to day 159 only. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". The class chooses its problem from the project's evidence; the course never picks the cause. The stop card and the right to pass hold, in public talk and in every vote.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D4.7.3-5, days 153 and 154.** "Explain individual and collective approaches to local, regional and global problems; predict results." Day 153 teaches two given cases and how to predict; day 154 weighs two or three ways to help, alone and together, with a prediction and a limit for each.
- **D4.8.3-5, days 155 and 156.** "Use varied deliberative and democratic procedures to decide and act on classroom and school civic problems." Day 155 deliberates and decides; day 156 acts.
- **D3.4.3-5 and D4.1.3-5, day 157.** "Use evidence to develop an answer to the compelling question." "Construct an argument supported by several sources." Day 157 looks back at the action and writes one claim with a detail from each of two named sources, checked against a third.
- **Limits that bite here.** The contribution must finish in day 156's 40 minutes with existing materials. No travel, fundraising, hazardous cleanup or required public contact (ours, untested). Roles are accessible to every child. A prediction names a reason and a condition and is not a claim of impact. Only what is seen on day 157 is reported; longer effects are recorded as unknown. The two given cases (William Cooper Nell's writing, the New England Freedom Association's aid; source to check) name slavery plainly and show freedom seekers acting for themselves. Grade 2's storm-readiness work returns only if the class chooses it.
- **Connections.** Unit 2 norms card, ballot rules card and Class Decision Record (155). Grade 2 Action Record look-back (157). Unit 6 claim frame and unit 5 two-source model (157). The question board and the days 142, 145 and 146 sources (157).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** One person's action and a group's action differ in reach and in limit (G). A prediction joins an action to a result by a reason and a condition (L). Hearing everyone before a vote is part of the procedure (H). A finished product is not a result; a result is seen in use, and the rest is unknown (M). A claim needs details from two sources and a reason (I). How to screen nominations in six minutes, how to hear four of five groups, how to record who was heard.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q4 (D4.7.3-5, a new case, "Our school history display leaves out workers"), P3 (the strategy comparison from day 154, revised on 158, scored 159), P4 (D4.8.3-5, the decision and action record from 155 to 157, with the observed retry on 158) and Q5 (D3.4.3-5 and D4.1.3-5, two strands, from source E and one project source). Source E is used on no teaching day.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E153 (Shade Card, invented for practice).** "At a made-up school, children wait for the bus in the hot sun. No shade is near the stop." Ask 1: "Name one thing you could do alone." Ask 2: "Name one thing a group could do." Exit box: "Say a likely result of the group's action, and why." Key: an action alone (for example, ask to play in a shady spot) and a group action (for example, many children sign one note asking the adults for shade), with a reason for the result. Wrong: "A group poster will fix the whole problem" (G); "The group will win because we want it" (L).
- **E154 (Sign Card, invented for practice; the strategy comparison is retained evidence).** "A child draws a sign for the school door asking people to walk, not run, on the ramp." Ask: "Name one limit of the child's sign." Key: it reaches only people who pass that door. Wrong: "No limit; a sign fixes every problem" (G). The comparison is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.
- **E155 (Vote Card, invented for practice; the decision record is retained evidence).** "Eight children like red, three like blue, three have not been heard." Ask: "Should the class vote now? Say why." Key: no; hear every plan first. Wrong: "Yes, red has the most" (H). The record is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.
- **E156 (Display Card, invented for practice; the action record is retained evidence).** "A class finished a display on Friday. Nobody has visited yet." Ask: "Does finishing the display show visitors learned from it?" Key: no; we must see what visitors do. Wrong: "Yes, it is finished, so it worked" (M). The record is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.
- **E157 (Claim Card, invented for practice; the closing claim is retained evidence).** "I think workers made our community because they are important." Ask: "What is missing from this claim?" Key: a detail from a source. Wrong: "Nothing, it is a good claim" (I). The claim is kept in the folder, not judged as an exit.

**Records (not exits).** Day 154: the strategy comparison. Day 155: the decision record. Day 156: the action record. Day 157: the claim and the look-back.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The question board: the five answers become the evidence list on 157. (2) Unit 2's norms card (listen, say it back, give a reason), ballot rules card and Class Decision Record, used on 155. (3) The grade 2 Action Record: what we did, what happened, what we would change, with no claim of impact (157). (4) The claim frame (units 2 and 6) and the two-source model (unit 5): the filled example is shown briefly on 157 and withdrawn; blank frames stay. (5) The action-result chain and the four-box prediction card (153), withdrawn on 156. (6) The research and biography folders.
- **Key moment 1, day 153: the action-result chain.** What must be seen: an action, a reason, a result and a limit, for one person and for a group. Ideal instrument: a printed four-box strip. Spec: print fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 155: the decision.** What must be seen: every view heard, a restating, a compromise tried, a private vote. Ideal instrument: a covered box and paper ballots with two counters. Spec: print and a box fit.
- **Key moment 3, day 157: prediction beside observation.** What must be seen: what was predicted, what was seen and what is unknown. Ideal instrument: a printed three-column sheet. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** The strips copied on plain paper; a shoebox for the ballot; cases read aloud from the teacher's copy.

## 6. Days

## Day 153


- **5 min. Return and repair.** F (from the day 152 sheets): each child without a listener line picks one; written feedback on the 150 to 152 records begins to return, one line each.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Grade 2 people-environment problem and its Action Record, and unit 2's fair-decision process, on slips; the teacher hears a rotating six and the record names them.
- **8 min. One person alone.** The teacher gives a short Curio Class paragraph on William Cooper Nell's writing (source to check). Pairs fill a four-box strip: the action, the reason, the result, a limit.
- **7 min. A group together.** The teacher gives a short paragraph on the New England Freedom Association's aid (source to check). Pairs fill a second strip. Freedom seekers had their own agency, said aloud. Stop card.
- **5 min. Model a prediction.** On a separate made-up problem (the school bike rack is full by eight, invented for practice) the teacher fills one strip: an individual option and a group option, a result joined by a reason, and a limit.
- **8 min. Independent practice.** Pairs fill two strips for a made-up caption: plastic litter in waterways worldwide (invented for practice). Practice only, not scored; written feedback returns before day 154.
- **3 min. Exit.** E153.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the strips; each child the practice sheet. Tool: None. By hand: the strips. Evidence: E153 and the strategy record (practice). Hand-off: tomorrow, the class names a problem from its own project.

## Day 154


- **5 min. Return and repair.** G (from E153): a fresh action-result chain with what is still needed; the rest read the question board.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** The board's answers and the evidence list are read aloud; the teacher hears a rotating six.
- **7 min. Nominate.** Each child writes a nomination card: a problem found in the project's evidence (a line from the nation's page, the interview, a photograph, the labor card or a biography), and who it touches. Nothing about home. Grade 2's storm-readiness work is one possible nomination only.
- **6 min. Pairs practice.** While the teacher sorts the cards against a screen card (finishes in day 156's 40 minutes; existing materials; in school; no travel, fundraising, hazardous cleanup or required public contact; duplicates joined), pairs predict on a fresh made-up problem (a hallway with no hooks, invented for practice).
- **5 min. Choose the problem.** The teacher reads the two or three screened problems. The class hears one reason for each, then each child places one dot; a tie means more talk. The class chooses; the course does not.
- **14 min. Strategy comparison.** For the chosen problem each child writes one individual option and one group option, a result for each joined by a reason, and a limit of each. Practice; revised after written feedback.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the chosen problem; each child the comparison. Tool: None. By hand: the cards and comparison. Evidence: E154, the comparison (practice; revised on 158). Hand-off: tomorrow, the class decides which way to help.

## Day 155


- **5 min. Return and repair.** L (from the day 154 records) in a small group of up to 6; the rest read the norms card.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Unit 2: the norms card (listen, say it back, give a reason), the ballot rules card (one each, the same rules for all, private, a tie means talk then a runoff) and the Class Decision Record, on slips.
- **10 min. Hear each plan.** Groups of six; each child tells a plan and a reason in one minute (six minutes), then each restates the plan before theirs (four minutes). The teacher sits with two groups for five minutes each; the record names who was heard.
- **8 min. A compromise.** Groups look for a plan that uses useful parts of others and write one reason for each view. The teacher joins two other groups, so four of five groups are heard by the end of the part.
- **8 min. Decide.** A reporter from each group tells its plan. The class votes by private ballot in a covered box; two counters read the marks; a tie means talk, then a runoff.
- **5 min. Decision record.** The Class Decision Record is filled for the class; each child writes another view restated, their own evidence, a compromise considered and how the class decided. Rehearsal; revised on 158.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the board and ballots; each child the record. Tool: None. By hand: the ballot and record. Evidence: E155, the decision record (practice). Hand-off: tomorrow, the class makes the contribution it chose.

## Day 156


- **4 min. Return and repair.** H (from the day 155 records): equal turns and a compromise on a fresh decision, a small group; the others check the plan on the wall.
- **3 min. The plan and the roles.** The teacher reads the chosen plan. Roles go to every child by a card: maker, labeler, checker, reader, carrier, placer. No role needs lifting, small print or speaking in public.
- **26 min. Make.** Groups make the contribution with scissors, tape, markers, paper and the materials named on day 154. The teacher guides. Fallback: if a group runs late, it finishes only the label; nothing is carried past this day.
- **4 min. Action record.** Each child writes what we made, who will use it, what we expect and one limit.
- **3 min. Clean up.** Scissors, tape and markers are counted back into trays; paper scraps go to the reuse bin; the contribution is set where its audience will see it. No water or sinks are needed.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the placed contribution; each child the action record. Tool: None. By hand: the contribution and record. Evidence: E156, the action record (practice). Hand-off: tomorrow, we look at what came of it and write the claim.

## Day 157


- **5 min. Return and repair.** M (from the 156 records): compare recorded observations with the predictions and list what is unknown.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** The question board's five answers and the evidence list are read: the Local Nation Account (142), the Labor History Card (145), the Local Interview (146) and the action record.
- **8 min. Look back.** The class sees or hears what came of the contribution that day (for example, who read or used it). Each child fills a three-column sheet: what we predicted, what we saw, what we do not know. Grade 2's Action Record is the model: what we did, what happened, what we would change.
- **15 min. Write the claim.** The claim frame, blank: "I think ___ helped make our community because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." Each child uses the nation's page and the interview, checks against the labor card and the action record, and names a reason. The unit 6 filled example is shown for two minutes at the start and taken down.
- **6 min. Hear another and revise.** Pairs read their claims; each partner restates the other's, says which contribution mattered and why, and the writer changes one line (norms card).
- **2 min. Collect.** The claim and the three-column sheet go to the teacher, who writes a criterion cue on each before day 158.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the cues; each child the claim and sheet. Tool: None. By hand: the claim and sheet. Evidence: E157, the closing claim (practice, revised on 158). Hand-off: tomorrow, feedback comes back and the portfolio is revised.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 153, "What did one person do alone, what did a group do together, and what came of each?"; day 155, "What did you hear that you had not thought of?"; day 157, "Who has made our community what it is?"
- Written task, day 157: the closing claim with a detail from each of two named sources and a reason, and a line of what the action showed and what is unknown.
- Judged by the teacher with five look-fors: a claim that answers the question; a detail from each of two named sources; a because that joins them; people beyond founders named; the action's result separated from its prediction. Shown, shown with help or not yet seen on 153 to 157.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: alone, together, predict, result, limit, vote, compromise, claim. Frames: "Alone I could ___; likely ___ because ___." "A group could ___; a limit is ___." "I heard ___; I would add ___." "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large print, labeled roles that need no lifting or speaking aloud, a scribe, a ballot a child can mark by touch or speech, the strips on a clipboard.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each prompt aloud; accept speech or a scribe; sources stay in view; judge content apart from spelling and English fluency.
- **Reading load.** Two short paragraphs (153) and the child's own cards; everything else is read aloud.
- **Content notes.** Day 153 names slavery plainly and shows people acting for freedom without graphic detail; no slur is read; the stop card and right to pass hold.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third option weighed against the first two, or a second limit for each option.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the two cases aloud, fills the four boxes with the child, and on day 155 reads a made-up decision card in which each person gives a view, the other restates it, and both vote privately. On day 157, the last day of this section, the adult reads the claim frame, helps the child find a detail in each source and writes the child's words. Nothing is asked about the family's decisions.
- **One projector.** Project the strips, cards and ballot rules; each child holds a paper copy; the box is passed hand to hand.
