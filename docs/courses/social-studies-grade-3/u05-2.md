# Section 5.2: Why Europeans sailed west, where the routes ran, and whose account

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 5, First meetings and early colonies (days 90 to 121): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 93 to 96.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (93, 94), application 2 (95, 96). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?"
- Section 2 of 8 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. Answers may be an arrow on a map, a labelled strip, a sentence or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to days 105 and 120. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names and live today.

- **MA.3.T3.1 (day 93).** "Locate North America, the Atlantic and Europe, and explain how Native Peoples first met Europeans and why Europeans sailed west." Parts: locate; explain first meetings; explain why sail west (trade, riches, land, religion), as causes and effects. All three are taught here. M2 on day 105 scores the locating and one reason; the effect of a first meeting is judged from the day 93 exit.
- **MA.3.T3.2 (day 94).** "Trace on a map the voyages of European explorers of the Northeast coast." Whole standard. Taught on two explorers, Verrazano (worked) and John Smith (pairs); the dates and routes are source to check. Cabot (1497) is the day 94 exit and Champlain (1608) is M3 on day 105, so neither is taught.
- **MA.3.T3.3 (days 95, 96).** "Explain how one explorer described Native Peoples and the new lands, and compare an early 1600s map of New England with a current one." Parts: explain one explorer's description (day 95, Smith beside the Massachusett account); compare two maps (day 96). Smith's description is read as one person's account with his own aims, never as the whole truth.
- **Limits that bite here.** One compass rose and one key per map; arrows show direction; routes only for the explorers named. "New France" and "New Netherland" are map labels only (no history taught). Native peoples are named as the Massachusett and the nations on the coast, in the present tense where they live on; the Massachusett Tribe's own pages are the nation's voice (unit field 10). Day 95 and 96 passages are short, read aloud, and chosen by the sensitivity read; every fact is source to check until the page is inspected.
- **Connections.** Map-key and compass (return, day 94); cause-effect chain (tied, day 93); source-sourcing (taught day 95, used 99); the day 91 and 92 strip for dates; grade 2 route and chain strips (review).
- **New here.** explorer, voyage and route, first meeting, promotion (a writer's picture of a place meant to attract settlers; day 95).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why "discovered" is not used (people lived here); how to read a route arrow; why an explorer's report has a purpose; that the Native nations kept their own records and speak for themselves; why a 1600s map has different labels but the same coast.

## 6. Days

## Day 93

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Explain how Native peoples first met Europeans and why Europeans sailed west (trade, riches, land, religion) as causes and effects
- note: exit uses the new Encounter Card, invented for practice; revise after written feedback on day 104 before scoring
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Return and repair (AA from E92).** A fresh row of year cards (1790, 1805, 1990, 2024, 1850); each child says the century aloud for each. If few missed AA, those children do this at the side table.
- **5 min. Locate.** A printed world map: find Europe, the Atlantic and North America with a finger and say each aloud. Seloy, the Timucua town from day 90, is on the North American coast. Teach with a compass rose and key (a short review).
- **8 min. Why sail west.** Four reason cards (trade, riches, land, religion; invented for practice and read aloud): each says in a line what some Europeans hoped for. The teacher links each card to the cause-effect strip: hope, sail west, arrive. Say plainly that people already lived on every coast. Pairs choose one card and say what the hope was.
- **10 min. First meetings.** Salt Cove (invented): a ship arrives where fishers live. The class strips the meeting: what each group saw, what each learned (a few words, news of the other's land), what each wanted. The chain: a reason, a voyage, a meeting, an effect. Discuss "discovered": people were already there. No part of the story is tools or corn.
- **10 min. Pairs.** Each pair gets one fresh invented meeting card (Fern Point: a ship arrives where a nation has a town) and fills the chain strip: reason, voyage, meeting, one effect. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs; the record names them.
- **2 min. Exit.** E93.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the world map; each pair a strip. Tool: None. By hand: the chain strip, the exit. Evidence: E93.

## Day 94

- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Trace explorers' routes along more than one coast
- note: Candidate kept (Curio Class context addition; no Tennessee grade 3 requirement claimed; New France and New Netherland on the map): this day, no added day

- **10 min. Return and repair (Z from E93).** Two accounts of one coast, one a newcomer's (invented) and one from the nation who lives there (invented, said aloud); the class finds a farm or town in each. If few missed it, the missing children do this at the side table while others start the map-key return.
- **5 min. Map-key and compass, returned.** A short review with the key, the compass rose and "from" and "to". The teacher models one arrow with its tail at the start dot.
- **8 min. Worked trace: Verrazano.** On the projected map, trace his voyage from the French side across the Atlantic to the coast (1524, from France along the coast from Florida to New Brunswick; sources.csv U5L). Children draw it on paper over tracing paper. Say the start, direction and end aloud. Cabot is not used.
- **10 min. Pairs: Henry Hudson.** Pairs trace Hudson's 1609 voyage from Europe to New York Bay (sources.csv U5T) on a fresh map, tail at the start, and say north, south, west. The teacher checks a rotating six pairs; AE (a route that ends at Plymouth) is checked on the spot: show a route to another coast, five minutes if needed.
- **5 min. French and Dutch labels.** The map labels New France and New Netherland; pairs find each and say which direction it lies from Plymouth and the Smith coast. Labels only; no history is taught.
- **2 min. Exit.** E94.

Who holds what is carried: each child a map and tracing paper. Tool: None. By hand: the arrows, the exit. Evidence: E94.

- **5 min. Return and repair (B from E94).** A fresh made-up map (Pell Harbor voyage); each child draws the arrow with the tail at the start dot and says start and end. If few missed B, those children use the side table.
- **5 min. Sourcing.** Teach the three questions: who made it, when, why. A made-up tourist flyer and a town's own beach page show that the same beach can be told in two voices. Introduce promotion in a line.
- **10 min. Smith.** The teacher reads one short Smith passage about the coast aloud (the "exceeding fat & fertill" line, sources.csv U5N; not the exit passage). Children underline two details. Ask the three questions: who made it, when and why. He wanted readers to come and settle.

Who holds what is carried: each pair the two-column strip; the teacher the two passages. Tool: None. By hand: the strip, the exit. Evidence: E95. This day carries the sensitivity read (unit note).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: explorer, voyage, route, meeting, trade, promotion. Frames: "The arrow starts at ___ and ends at ___." "___ made this to ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print maps with thick coast lines; raised arrows or a yarn route to follow with a finger; speech, pointing or a scribe for answers; the reasoning stays the child's.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a point, an arrow or a spoken answer; a sentence is never required for E94 or E96.
- **Reading load.** Passages are three to five lines, read aloud once; children underline, not copy; labels are one to three words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Trace a third explorer's route (source to check) and say which coast it reached.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult prints one map and traces the route with the child, reads each passage aloud and asks the three questions; the child draws the arrow and says the answer. Nothing in this section is the last day of the unit.
- **One projector.** Project the map and one passage at a time; children trace on paper copies.
