# Section 1.6: Claims, questions and the unit check

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 1, Where we live (days 1 to 24): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 19 to 24.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (19), fluency 1 (22), application 2 (20, 21), check 1 (23), respond 1 (24). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Why is our town where it is?"
- Section 6 of 6 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain; the teacher reads every card, prompt and exit aloud once. A claim is said first, then written as one short sentence or two. Each day runs 40 minutes. Every made-up card prints "invented for practice". Records use one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Days 19 to 21 build the claim and the question work; day 22 revises; day 23 is the unit's assessment; day 24 responds.

- **D3.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question." Day 19 models a claim from two sources; day 20 gathers evidence for supporting answers; day 21 writes the closing claim for "Why is our town where it is?" with one or two pieces of evidence. Both parts (use evidence; develop a claim) are taught.
- **D4.1.3-5, this section's share.** "Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources." Day 19 and day 21: a claim that names two sources and says what each adds. Both parts (construct a claim; support it from more than one source) are taught.
- **D1.3.3-5 and D1.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Name the subject concepts tied to a supporting question that people can interpret in more than one way"; "Explain how answering supporting questions helps answer the big question of an inquiry." Day 20 teaches both on the unit's five supporting questions.
- **Asked on day 23, not taught here.** D1.1, D1.2, D2.Geo.1, D2.Geo.2, D2.Geo.4, D2.Geo.5, D2.Geo.10, MA.3.T1.1, MA.3.T2.1 and MA.3.T2.2. Day 22 retrieves the map codes; D1.1 and D1.2 are judged from the day 2 records.
- **Limits that bite here.** Evidence is a detail you can point to in a named source. A claim says what the sources show and what they suggest, and one thing they cannot tell ("maps alone do not prove why people chose a place"). One written claim is a sentence or two; no paragraph, no counter-claim, no citation form. Sources are named by what they are: the current map, the early map, the photograph. Students choose their own evidence on days 20 and 21; the teacher's frame is shown on day 19, offered as a card on day 20 and withdrawn on day 21. Practice records are not scored; only day 22's revisions are.
- **Connections.** D2.Geo.2 (day 19, the pair of sources). D2.Geo.5 (day 21, the early map). Shows, suggests, cannot tell (day 9, day 15). ELA W.3.1 bridge, days 19 and 21: a claim spoken first, then one written sentence on "I think ... because the source shows ..." (ELA grade 3 unit 3 is later; its full paragraphs are not assumed).
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Claim, evidence, because, a named source and its maker (grade 2); who made it, when and why; big question and supporting question; two readings; matters to others; fact and priority sort; closing claim (grade 2); the question board; the written cue then revision routine (day 10). New: reasoning (how a detail supports the claim).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A claim is what you say is true; evidence is a detail in a named source; reasoning says why the detail supports the claim. Two titles alone are not evidence. A supporting question has words people read two ways ("best", "help", "good"); a fact can be checked, a priority is a choice. Answering supporting questions gives facts and ideas the big claim uses. Day 22's revisions are scored on day 23; the first tries are practice. Day 23 is read aloud without explaining the answers.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: claim, evidence, reasoning, source, because, shows, supports, question, matters. Frames: "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." "The maps cannot tell ___." "Knowing ___ helps because ___." Accept pointing, drawing and dictation; a spoken claim first.
- **Access.** Raised or tactile maps, large print, movable sentence strips, a scribe for written claims and revisions.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 23: prompts and labels read aloud; spoken answers, pointing and a scribe are fine; the geographic and evidence choices stay the child's; spelling and handwriting are not scored.
- **Reading load.** Cards at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words; day 21's claim is written by the child, two or three short sentences; day 23's sources are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second claim from the other pair of sources, or a question for the claim: "What else would help show it?"
