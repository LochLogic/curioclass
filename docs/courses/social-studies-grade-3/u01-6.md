# Section 1.6: Claims, questions and the unit check

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain; the teacher reads every card, prompt and exit aloud once. A claim is said first, then written as one short sentence or two. Each day runs 40 minutes. Every made-up card prints "invented for practice". Records use one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Days 19 to 21 build the claim and the question work; day 22 revises; day 23 is the unit's assessment; day 24 responds.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D3.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question." Day 19 models a claim from two sources; day 20 gathers evidence for supporting answers; day 21 writes the closing claim for "Why is our town where it is?" with one or two pieces of evidence. Both parts (use evidence; develop a claim) are taught.
- **D4.1.3-5, this section's share.** "Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources." Day 19 and day 21: a claim that names two sources and says what each adds. Both parts (construct a claim; support it from more than one source) are taught.
- **D1.3.3-5 and D1.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Name the subject concepts tied to a supporting question that people can interpret in more than one way"; "Explain how answering supporting questions helps answer the big question of an inquiry." Day 20 teaches both on the unit's five supporting questions.
- **Asked on day 23, not taught here.** D1.1, D1.2, D2.Geo.1, D2.Geo.2, D2.Geo.4, D2.Geo.5, D2.Geo.10, MA.3.T1.1, MA.3.T2.1 and MA.3.T2.2. Day 22 retrieves the map codes; D1.1 and D1.2 are judged from the day 2 records.
- **Limits that bite here.** Evidence is a detail you can point to in a named source. A claim says what the sources show and what they suggest, and one thing they cannot tell ("maps alone do not prove why people chose a place"). One written claim is a sentence or two; no paragraph, no counter-claim, no citation form. Sources are named by what they are: the current map, the early map, the photograph. Students choose their own evidence on days 20 and 21; the teacher's frame is shown on day 19, offered as a card on day 20 and withdrawn on day 21. Practice records are not scored; only day 22's revisions are.
- **Connections.** D2.Geo.2 (day 19, the pair of sources). D2.Geo.5 (day 21, the early map). Shows, suggests, cannot tell (day 9, day 15). ELA W.3.1 bridge, days 19 and 21: a claim spoken first, then one written sentence on "I think ... because the source shows ..." (ELA grade 3 unit 3 is later; its full paragraphs are not assumed).
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Claim, evidence, because, a named source and its maker (grade 2); who made it, when and why; big question and supporting question; two readings; matters to others; fact and priority sort; closing claim (grade 2); the question board; the written cue then revision routine (day 10). New: reasoning (how a detail supports the claim).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A claim is what you say is true; evidence is a detail in a named source; reasoning says why the detail supports the claim. Two titles alone are not evidence. A supporting question has words people read two ways ("best", "help", "good"); a fact can be checked, a priority is a choice. Answering supporting questions gives facts and ideas the big claim uses. Day 22's revisions are scored on day 23; the first tries are practice. Day 23 is read aloud without explaining the answers.

## 2. Where it sits


Follows section 1.5 (ends day 18). Day 19's first part holds the repair for day 18's exit (R, or I) and the retrieval of ss2-claim-sources. Each day to day 22 opens with the repair for the day before. The unit ends here: day 25 opens unit 2 clean. The readiness items for unit 2 sit in day 24's last ten minutes (s02.md field 4). Day 23's results go on the record sheet by component, then to day 24's four groups.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q9 (D3.4 and D4.1), Q12 (D1.3) and Q13 (D1.4). Q10 and Q11 belong to section 1.1 and are judged from the day 2 records. Day 23 asks Q1 to Q9 on the withheld packet and judges Q10 to Q13 from records. Teaching never uses P1, P2 or L.

**Evidence by day.** Day 19: the written claim, E19. Day 20: the question record, E20. Day 21: the closing claim, E21. Day 22: the revised map and question records, E22. Day 23: the packet, Q1 to Q9, with Q10 to Q13 from records. Day 24: the parallel redo and the readiness answers (reasoning, not reading speed).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E19 (Court Map and Photo Pair, invented for practice).** The map places a ramp on the court's east side. The photograph shows the ramp reaching the entrance. "Say one claim about how people get into the court. Name both sources." Answer: "I think people can walk or roll up the east ramp to the entrance, because the map shows a ramp on the east side and the photograph shows it reaching the entrance." Wrong: "The map and the photograph say it" (K); "The ramp was built for wheelchairs and everyone uses it" (G). The data asks it as E19, E19b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E20 (the board's questions).** The card lists the five supporting questions. "Pick one you did not choose today. Write one fact you still need to answer it, and one word in it people could read two ways." Answer: a fact that would help; a word with two readings, such as "best", "help" or "changed". Wrong: "my favourite colour" (O); "north" as the two-reading word (J). The data asks it as E20, E20b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E21 (Town Exit Pair, invented for practice).** Map A places a town at a road crossing. Record B says a market opened beside that crossing. "Why might the town be there? Use both." Answer: "I think the town might be there because people could meet and trade at the crossing, because Map A shows the crossing and Record B says a market opened beside it." Wrong: "A and B prove why" with no detail from either (K); "The map proves the town began for the market" (G). The data asks it as E21, E21b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E22 (a fresh map with a printed bar).** "How many whole bars apart are the two marked places?" Answer: the count on the card (a pair and route not used on day 8, E8 or in the retrieval). Wrong: "six steps" (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The claim frame, "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___" (modeled day 19, offered as a card on day 20, withdrawn on day 21; a child who still needs it is marked "shown with help"). The shows, suggests, cannot tell card (from day 9; days 19 and 21). The question board (from day 2; day 20, closed on day 21). The written cue then revision routine (the day 10 map and the day 2 and 20 records; day 22). Models from the unit are retrieved on day 22 and withdrawn at the assessment.
- **Key moment 1, day 19: two sources side by side.** Ideal instrument: a map and a photograph of one place on one sheet. Spec: fits; print the pair, project it, keep the labels readable.
- **Key moment 2, day 23: the unaided packet.** Ideal instrument: the printed packet, read aloud from a script, with pointing items marked on the sheet. Spec: fits; no tool. Raised copies and a scribe on request.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed sheets, a board, sentence strips, a pencil.

## 6. Days

## Day 19


- **12 min. Return and repair.** 3 minutes: retrieval of ss2-claim-sources: "A photograph shows bicycles on a ferry. What claim does it support, and what is the source?" Key: this ferry carries bicycles; the photograph. Up to 8 minutes: the repair for day 18's more-missed letter, R (5 minutes, with a fresh dated pair) or I (8 minutes, one account). A retrieval miss goes to a small group of up to 6 in part 4. 1 minute: hand back day 18's exit tickets.
- **7 min. Who made it, and what it shows.** The Fountain Square Map and Photo Pair (invented for practice): the teacher models "who made it, when and why" for each source, then what each shows, using the card of shows, suggests and cannot tell.
- **8 min. A spoken claim.** The teacher models "I think ___ because the map shows ___ and the photograph shows ___", then the partner's question "Why do you think that?" Pairs say a claim to each other, 1 minute each way, about the Market Square pair. The class adds reasoning: how the detail supports the claim.
- **8 min. One written claim.** Each child writes one sentence naming both sources and one detail from each, on the board's frame. A partner checks: both sources named? One detail from each? The small group takes its retrieval repair first (5 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E19.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-19-22.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The Fountain Square pair shares the shape of E19 (a claim from a map and a photograph); the Court Pair is a new case.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the frame and the card; pairs hold the pair of sources. Tool: None. By hand: speaking, writing a sentence, the exit. Evidence: the written claim (practice), E19.

## Day 20


- **10 min. Return and repair.** Day 19's more-missed letter, K or G, eight to ten minutes: K, link a detail from each of two sources to a new claim; G, sort three claim cards. The other letter goes to a small group of up to 6 in part 3.
- **7 min. Two readings of a supporting question.** The board's five questions. The teacher models two: "How did land and water help place towns?" ("help": a way to travel, or fresh water) and "Whose needs does a good location serve?" ("needs": water, or a safe road). A fact can be found; a priority is a choice. "Two readings" is from day 2.
- **10 min. My question record.** Each child picks a supporting question the teacher did not model and fills the record: the question; two facts it needs; one idea people could read two ways. A partner reads it back. The teacher gives one cue to about 8 children, noted for written feedback.
- **8 min. Two sources.** The Source Table lists the unit's four sources (the current town map, the early map, the landmark photograph and caption, the nation's passage). Each child picks two, names both and says what each adds, then writes one sentence: "Knowing ___ helps explain why the town is here because ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E20.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-19-22.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The question record shares the shape of E20 (a fact needed, a word read two ways); E20 uses a question the child did not pick.

Who holds what is carried: the board holds the five questions; each child holds the record. Tool: None. By hand: the record, the Source Table, the exit. Evidence: the question record (practice; the teacher keeps it for written feedback on relevance, interpretations and links before day 22), E20.

## Day 21

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 19, "What does each source show, and what do you think because of it?"; day 20, "What word could mean two things here, and which part is a fact?"; day 21, "Which detail from which map supports your claim?"
- Written task, day 21: a short claim answering "Why is our town where it is?" (two or three sentences), practice for day 23's Q9.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: (1) a claim that answers the question, not an opinion; (2) a physical feature and a human choice; (3) a detail from each named map; (4) one thing the maps cannot tell. The question records on day 20 are judged by the unit's look-fors (relevance, interpretations, links).

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 19 retrieves ss2-claim-sources and day 18's early-and-modern maps. Day 20 uses days 2 and 15's two readings and "shows, suggests, cannot tell". Day 21 uses the early map (day 18) and the town map (days 7 to 9). Day 22 retrieves the unit's map, grid, region and sunlight work. Day 24 reteaches by letter. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none. **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 22):** fresh cards, a partner checking against the overlay, accuracy before ease, no clocks or races.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: claim, evidence, reasoning, source, because, shows, supports, question, matters. Frames: "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." "The maps cannot tell ___." "Knowing ___ helps because ___." Accept pointing, drawing and dictation; a spoken claim first.
- **Access.** Raised or tactile maps, large print, movable sentence strips, a scribe for written claims and revisions.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 23: prompts and labels read aloud; spoken answers, pointing and a scribe are fine; the geographic and evidence choices stay the child's; spelling and handwriting are not scored.
- **Reading load.** Cards at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words; day 21's claim is written by the child, two or three short sentences; day 23's sources are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second claim from the other pair of sources, or a question for the claim: "What else would help show it?"

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: the adult takes the partner's turn and writes down the child's spoken reasons; a claim is said first, then written. Day 23: the adult reads each prompt once and writes the spoken answers. Day 24 (the last day of the unit): go over the missed items together and redo them, then read the three readiness cases aloud and listen to the child's answers.
- **One projector.** Show the source pair and the frame on day 19 and the five questions on day 20. Day 23: show nothing. Day 24: show the answer key after the packets are handed in.
