## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How does our community get what it needs?"
- **Hook, day 49:** inspect School Needs Record: paper, water and cleaning. Trace who supplies each. Why does the answer matter to workers and pupils?
- **Co-write on 50:** Who produces what (52)? Why use money (53)? What shapes what sellers make (54)? What rewards and penalties push choices (58)? What do financial institutions do (60)? How do places depend on trade (64)?
- **Closing, day 65:** defend an answer using Local Production Record and Techiman Traders Record, with a distinct detail from each. Local production and distant exchange may receive different supported emphases.
- **Teaching:** model maker, date and purpose on 49, resource classification on 51, choice comparison on 56 and two-source reasoning on 64. Students select evidence and revise after another claim on 65.

- **D2.Eco.9.3-5:** Describe financial institutions beyond banks. Parts: describe credit unions; describe insurers.
- **D2.Eco.10.3-5:** Explain interest rates. Parts: explain payment relative to amount and time.

The local C3 file contains identifiers only. Paraphrases follow the publisher PDF, pages 23, 35 to 38, 54 and 59.
- **Limits:** no percentages, compound interest, account recommendations or family finances. Ancient Ghana belongs to grade 6. Biography writing and informed action belong to unit 7.
- **Models:** retain claim-evidence-reasoning and perspective from unit 2. Withdraw the teacher example on 65. Return to map-key on 61 and 63. Tie opportunity-cost to the next-best alternative on 57. Revive budget as money in, out and remaining on 53; revisit 57, 61 and 66. Withdraw filled examples on 66; blank organizers remain.

**Teacher notes:** human capital means learned knowledge and skills, not a person's worth. A person can produce and consume. Physical capital is equipment used in production, not money. Wheat is a natural resource; flour is a processed material. Keep the day 51 bakery example, but trace flour to wheat rather than calling flour an unprocessed natural resource. Specialization between producers differs from division of tasks within one workplace. Practising skills at school can develop knowledge without determining anyone's future worth or earnings.

Profit is sales income left after costs, not a moral measure of a person. Profit expectations can shape products and prices but do not guarantee sales. Scarcity applies to time and resources in wealthy and poor places. Poverty is not proof of poor choices; access, pay, costs and events can limit options. Use fictional amounts only. Never ask pupils to disclose accounts, income, debts, purchases or insurance, or to act as representatives of their families.

Source to read before day 59 (field 10): banks may pay interest on savings and charge it on loans; not every account pays interest and terms differ. Charging borrowers more than the bank pays savers can contribute to earnings, before other costs. A credit union belongs to members. Insurance pays losses covered by its agreement, not every expense. The class model omits fees and uses one fixed year; no real offer is recommended. Grade 3 compares dollars on each $100 without percentage notation. Teach the arithmetic here if its parallel math instruction is not yet available. On 54, compare a price customers will pay with an unsold higher-priced offering. The origin-label cards for 61 are made by Curio Class: a country of origin, no brand, invented for practice.
