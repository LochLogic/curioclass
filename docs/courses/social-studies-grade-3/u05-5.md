# Section 5.5 skeleton: First meetings and early colonies

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/5/s/5.5), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u05-5.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a filled table, a labeled drawing, an arrow or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to the teacher's scoring on day 120 only. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names and live today. No child reports on home, family, money, religion or the body; nobody plays a role.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T5.2, day 106.** "Explain why many Puritans migrated to Massachusetts, how they spread west, and what this meant for Native Peoples." Taught: one reason Puritans came, how they spread inland, and four effects (lost land, disease, conversion, war) each with its own cause. King Philip's War is named once (grade 5 owns it).
- **MA.3.T5.1, day 107.** "Compare the roles and decisions of leaders." Here Winthrop beside Metacom, after Bradford beside Ousamequin on day 103; each leader's interest and whom he answers to.
- **MA.3.T5.3, days 108 and 109.** "Use paintings, artifacts, buildings, or texts to analyze daily life, housing, schooling, and work." Day 108 teaches housing and schooling from a reconstruction and a short text; day 109 teaches the lives of servants and enslaved people.
- **D2.His.2.3-5, days 108 and 110.** "Compare life in particular periods of the past with life today." Day 108 notices one past detail; day 110 compares.
- **Limits that bite here.** Pilgrim separation is kept apart from Puritan reform; belief is described, never judged. Conversion is told plainly as the nations tell it. No battle detail, no sacred teachings, no reenactment, no role play or simulation of servitude or slavery. The teacher says that legal slavery was cruel and that Native nations govern themselves today.
- **Connections.** Timeline bands (days 100 and 104); cause-effect chain (day 93); leader card and perspective (days 98 and 103); source-sourcing (days 95 and 99); written cue then revision (day 104); biography boxes (grade 3 unit 1); grade 2 words for enslaved people and forced moves; harbor and barter come in 5.7.
- **New here.** Puritan, praying town, conversion, choice and pressure and seizure, reconstruction, artifact, servant, service term.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why Pilgrims and Puritans differ; how to tell a choice from a pressure and a seizure without judging any belief; why a reconstruction is later evidence; how a service term differs from enslavement, in plain words; how to give the written cue; how to keep each hard topic calm (a stop card, the right to pass).

## 2. Where it sits

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 (MA.3.T5.1), Q2 (MA.3.T5.2), Q3 (MA.3.T5.3) and Q4 (D2.His.2.3-5), all on the unit plan and asked on day 120. Q3 and Q4 draw on dossier A's timber-house drawing and work record, so no teaching day uses that house or that work record. Governor T and Captain U (Q1) are not used in teaching; teaching uses Winthrop and Metacom.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. The teacher reads each stem aloud once; one ask in each.
- **E106 (New Cause Card, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "Farms spread over a river meadow where families grazed sheep (a made-up town). Later, a fever spread after a ship came. Name one effect on the people there, and say its cause." Key: lost grazing meadow because farms spread over it; or illness because of the ship's fever; each effect with its own cause. Wrong: farms caused both losses (N); the people chose to give up the meadow (F).
- **E107 (New Leader Pair, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** V built a wall round his town to keep it safe; W made a two-cow grazing rule to keep his town's shared pasture. "Write one way the two leaders' choices were alike or different." Key: alike, each chose to keep something safe; different, a wall and a grazing rule. Wrong: V told W what to do, or one leader ruled both (AC).
- **E108 (New Object Caption, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "A caption says: a prayer book kept on a shelf; a child in this family learned letters from it. What does it show about learning at home?" Key: some children learned letters at home; it does not tell about every home. Wrong: every family lived the same way (G).
- **E109 (New Work Terms Card, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "Who is free to leave after five years, Tom or Hester?" Key: Tom; Hester cannot, because enslavement denied her that choice. Wrong: yes, both leave after a term, enslavement was a term of years (Q).
- **E110 (New Learning Exit Pair, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "Write one thing that changed and one that stayed the same. (then: a neighbor's kitchen table; now: a classroom with a teacher)" Key: changed, the place and teacher; stayed, children learned to read then and now. Wrong: schools were the same then and now (O).

**Retained records (practice, not exits).** Day 106: the cause card, one reason Puritans came, one way they spread, two effects with causes apart. Day 107: Winthrop beside Metacom. Day 108: a home and a way of learning, with what the caption supports. Day 110: one change and one thing that stayed from a dated schooling caption beside a current classroom.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The wall strip: a 1630 band added on day 106. (2) The cause chart, cause card to effect card (day 93's chain), used on 106, withdrawn on day 119 leaving the blank frame. (3) The leader card (name, nation, role, decision, interest, whom he answers to) and biography boxes (before, action, after). (4) The record folder with a written cue on each record. (5) The Work Terms Table. (6) The continuity-change chart (day 110), then and now with a row for what stayed. (7) The question board: "What undermined it?" is answered on day 106; "Who worked and who benefited?" begins on 109.
- **Key moment 1, day 106: the cause chart.** Ideal instrument: a printed chart of cause cards and effect cards with room for one arrow each, so causes stay apart. Spec: print and pencil; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 109: the Work Terms Table.** Ideal instrument: a three-column table (how long, who decides, free after) filled with one line each. Spec: print.
- **Key moment 3, day 110: the chart.** Ideal instrument: a folded sheet, then and now, with a row for what stayed. Spec: print.
- **A room with nothing.** Charts folded from plain paper; maps and cards drawn by the teacher; cues in a notebook margin.

## 6. Days

## Day 106


- **10 min. Return and repair (K from E104 5, mid-unit response 5).** First, K (5 min): two fresh invented colony bands (dates not E104's or M1's) drawn on the wall strip; pairs say which colony was there when the second began. If few missed it, those children use the side table while the rest start the response. Second, the day 105 response (5 min): each child reads the teacher's note on the sheet and fixes the one cell it names in colored pencil; children whose note names a 0 meet the teacher with a fresh map or strip. None of this takes minutes from the worked example.
- **6 min. Why Puritans came.** Add a 1630 band for Massachusetts Bay to the strip (1630, U5Z). A labeled Curio Class summary: Puritans were colonists who wanted their practices free from the king and the Anglican Church (sources.csv U5Z); many came in the 1630s (source to check); they are a different group from the Pilgrims, who had left the church (separatists, day 100). Belief is described, not judged. Post "What undermined it?" from day 90 on the question board.
- **7 min. How they spread.** A dated map of Massachusetts towns and the homelands of the nations there (map and dates to make; names and places source to check): the coast first, then inland. The class traces one arrow, tail at the start dot, and says its direction (map key from day 94).
- **10 min. Four effects, four causes.** The cause chart filled for land loss, disease, conversion and war, each with one fact from the Aquinnah History contact and land paragraphs or a labeled summary of the standard's list (source to check; the Mashpee 1675 entry is not used). Teacher reads each card. Sort every card as a choice, a pressure or a seizure (F); one cause to one effect (N). Conversion told plainly: some Native people were pressed to take English church ways and live in praying towns (to check); no religion is judged. King Philip's War is named once, with no battle detail; grade 5 teaches it. Pairs fill the chart; the teacher hears a rotating six, named on the record.
- **5 min. Record.** Each child fills a cause card with frames: "Puritans came because ___." "They spread to ___." "One effect was ___ because ___." (twice). The teacher gives a written cue after class; practice, revised on day 119.
- **2 min. Exit.** E106.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the strip and the chart; each child a cause card. Tool: None. By hand: the arrow, the sort, the card. Evidence: E106; the cause card. Hand-off: tomorrow, two leaders, one from each side. This day carries the sensitivity read.

## Day 107


- **10 min. Return and repair (N and F from E106).** The more-missed letter gets all 10 minutes with fresh invented cause cards; if both are many, 5 minutes each; if one is few, those children use the side table.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** The leader card with Bradford and Ousamequin (day 103) and perspective (day 98): two people see one event from two sides. One question: "What did each want to keep?"
- **8 min. Ousamequin's life.** Read aloud an original short biography of Ousamequin from the leadership account (source to check; Ousamequin only). The class fills before, action and after boxes with the teacher. He is Wampanoag, the nation's name; the Wampanoag live today. This is a short biography, read as a second source beside the colonial record.
- **10 min. Pairs: Winthrop and Metacom.** Each pair gets two fresh leader cards, written by us from sources to check: John Winthrop, governor of Massachusetts Bay in the 1630s and 1640s, and Metacom, a Wampanoag sachem in the 1670s (sachem 1662 to 1676, U5AC). Each pair fills a card for each: role, one decision, the interest behind it, whom he answers to (AC). The pair finds that the two were leaders in different years (to check) and so were not talking with each other. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs, named on the record.
- **6 min. Record.** Each child writes: "Winthrop ___ because ___. Metacom ___ because ___." Practice; written cue after class; revised on day 119.
- **2 min. Exit.** E107.

Who holds what is carried: each pair two leader cards. Tool: None. By hand: the cards, the sentence. Evidence: E107; the Winthrop and Metacom record. Hand-off: tomorrow, homes and school in Massachusetts Bay.

## Day 108


- **5 min. Return and repair (AC from E107).** Fresh invented leader cards: each child sorts whom each leader answers to. If few missed it, those children use the side table.
- **5 min. Who made it?** The three questions (who made it, when, why) from day 95: a reconstruction is made now, from evidence. A painting or drawing is a later picture. An artifact is an object made and used long ago.
- **10 min. A house.** A reconstructed English home of the 1600s at Plymouth (Plimoth Patuxet description, source to check; Plymouth only) with its caption. The class annotates what the caption supports (what it is built of, what it holds) and what it cannot show (other families' homes; G).
- **10 min. School and learning.** A labeled Curio Class summary of the Paul Revere House article on schooling in 1635 to 1645 (source to check): which children learned letters, where, with what. The class fills three columns (who, where, with what) and adds one line on what the summary does not tell.
- **8 min. Record.** Each child draws and labels one thing in a colonial home and writes one sentence on how children learned. Practice; written cue after class; revised on day 119. The teacher hears a rotating six.
- **2 min. Exit.** E108.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the house annotations; each child a record. Tool: None. By hand: the drawing, the columns. Evidence: E108; the record. No child describes their own home. Hand-off: tomorrow, people who worked in these homes, servants and enslaved people.

## Day 109


- **5 min. Return and repair (G from E108).** Fresh invented claim cards ("Every family had one room") tested against a reconstruction picture; each child says whether the picture supports it.
- **5 min. Words.** From grade 2: enslaved people and enslavers (the words, never "slaves" or "owners") and forced. New: servant (a worker bound to serve for a set time) and service term. The teacher says plainly that slavery was legal and was cruel. No role play, no simulation; no child speaks as either person.
- **10 min. A servant.** A card for Ned, a servant (invented for practice): a four-year term, daily work, free to leave when it ends (source to check for the kinds of work). Fill the Work Terms Table: how long, who decides, free after.
- **10 min. Enslaved people.** The teacher states: enslaved people were held for life, denied freedom and could be sold; in Boston they worked in homes, shops and on wharves (Boston slavery exhibit, labor; source to check). No real person's record is read: it is cut, and the date of the 1641 law is held for day 120's law card. Fill the same table for the record. The class compares Ned's line with it.
- **8 min. Pairs: sort.** Each pair sorts six invented work cards into "service term, then free" and "enslaved, denied freedom"; Q is repaired on the spot. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs, named on the record.
- **2 min. Exit.** E109.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the table; each pair a card set. Tool: None. By hand: the table, the sort. Evidence: E109. This day carries the sensitivity read: a stop card on each desk and the right to pass. Hand-off: tomorrow, what changed and what stayed since then.

## Day 110


- **10 min. Return and repair (Q from E109).** If many missed Q: the Work Terms Table filled again from two fresh invented cards, ten minutes, never acted out. If few missed it: 5 minutes at the side table, while the rest spend 5 minutes on a retrieval of the day 108 home and learning record.
- **8 min. The chart.** The continuity-change chart (then and now, with a row for what stayed; from kindergarten and grade 3 unit 1) is filled for one worked case: light to read by, a candle or lamp then and electric light now (from memory). Changed: the source of light. Stayed: people need light to see and read.
- **10 min. Pairs: two pairs of cards.** Pairs fill the chart for washing clothes by hand at a river beside a washing machine (invented for practice), and for the day 108 home picture beside a present-day house card (invented for practice, not a child's home; AB). The teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **10 min. Record.** The day 108 schooling caption beside a current classroom record (the room the class is in: desks, books, light, heat; made by the teacher). Each child writes one change and one thing that stayed (O). Practice; written cue after class; revised on day 119.
- **2 min. Exit.** E110.

Who holds what is carried: each pair a chart; each child a record. Tool: None. By hand: the chart, the record. Evidence: E110; the schooling record. Hand-off: tomorrow, slavery in every colony.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 106, "What undermined sharing the land?"; day 107, "What did each leader want to keep?"; day 108, "What can this caption tell us, and what can it not?"; day 109, "Who decided how long the work lasted?"; day 110, "What changed, and what stayed?"

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: Puritan, spread, disease, pressure, servant, enslaved, change, stayed. Frames: "___ came because ___." "One effect was ___ because ___." "___ changed; ___ stayed." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print cards, speech, pointing or a scribe for any answer; color for the strip bands; the chart can be filled by arrows and pictures.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every stem aloud; accept a point, a drawing or a spoken answer; the six heard each day may include any child who needs to say it.
- **Reading load.** Cards are one to three short sentences, read aloud; a record is a table or a sentence frame.
- **Content notes.** Days 106 to 109 name disease, loss of land, pressure to change religion, war and slavery (held for life, sold away). No graphic detail, no slurs, no role play.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third leader card for another colony, or a fresh cause card the child writes.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads each card aloud, helps fill the chart and writes the cue; day 109 is read calmly with a pause the child may use, and nothing is acted out. The child tells only about the cards, never about their own home or family.
- **One projector.** Project one chart or card at a time; each child has a paper copy and the stop card.
