## 1. Standards and practices

- **D1.1.3-5** (days 125): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.3.T6.3** (days 126, 127): Analyze how colonists' sense of injustice led them to declare independence, and what the Declaration of Independence shows about its writers' beliefs.
- **D2.His.1.3-5** (days 126): Make and use a time-ordered list of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time.
- **D2.Civ.8.3-5** (days 127): Identify the main civic virtues and democratic principles that guide government, society and communities.
- skill ss3-founding-ideas (grade 3)
- skill ss3-timeline (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 125:** show the Declaration's title and read its consent phrase beside an original explanation. Ask whose agreement a government needs.
> - **Co-write on 125:** Why independence (126)? What did the writers believe (127)? What makes a government plan (128)? What rights were listed (129)? Whose voices counted (130 to 131)? What do symbols promise (132)?
> - **Teaching:** model maker, date, audience and purpose on 126; connect evidence to a claim on 127. Compare constitutions on 128; step back on 133. Teach independence, consent, constitution, right, citizenship, symbol, civic virtue and democratic principle in context. Day 132 gives twenty minutes to five brief symbol cards, with discussion and a retained five-row record afterward.
>
> - **Models:** return to timeline on 126, add overlapping developments on 130, withdraw filled examples on 134. Return to branches-of-government on 128 beside the state's plan. Keep unit 5's sourcing, perspective, cause and effect, corroboration and blank claim frame; withdraw teacher answers on 133. Map and continuity models return in unit 7.
