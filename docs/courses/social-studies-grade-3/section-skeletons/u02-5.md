## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D2.Civ.5.3-5** (days 41, 43): Explain where different systems of government came from, what they do and how they are built, including those in the U.S. and state constitutions.
- **MA.3.T6.5** (days 41, 42, 43): Explain that states have governments too, and that the 1780 Massachusetts Constitution, mainly written by John Adams, is the oldest working constitution in the world and grants basic rights.
- skill ss3-state-constitution (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write on day 26:** Why have governments (27)? Who decides locally (32)? What do rules protect (34)? How can people participate (37)? How can we decide fairly (40)? What limits government (43)?
>
> - **Limits:** own-place government, with Massachusetts and Colorado worked cases. Branch functions stay introductory; detailed national powers return later. Day 41 teaches the First Amendment speech safeguard (Congress proposed it, the states ratified it) at introductory depth; unit 6 returns to it. No party preferences, legal advice, personal finances or identity disclosures. Unit 7 owns informed action.
> - **Models:** keep unit 1's claim-evidence-reasoning, rehearse orally on 44, withdraw the teacher example on 45. Day 38 teaches norms and restating; perspective (view, experience, value) opens day 39, where students supply their own. Tie branches-of-government to job labels on 28, revise against Menominee government on 43 after the state and US parts. Reference charts remain available.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Printed role charts, maps, source cards and ballots fit the jobs; no spec or TOOL-ASKS.md needed. Gather pencils, counters, paper, a ballot box and ballot slips. Print every source and task; one projector only enlarges them.
>
