## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 25): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **D2.Civ.3.3-5** (days 26): Examine where rules, laws and key parts of the U.S. Constitution came from and what they are for.
- **D1.2.3-5** (days 26): Name the subject concepts tied to a big question that people can interpret in more than one way.
- **D2.Civ.1.3-5** (days 27): Tell apart what different government officials, levels and branches may do and must do, across times and places.
- **MA.3.T1.3** (days 27): Explain why schools, towns, and cities have governments, what they do, how Massachusetts local governments are organized, and how people take part in their communities.
- skill ss3-levels-of-government (grade 3)
- skill ss3-rules-rights (grade 3)
- skill ss3-taking-part (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 25:** compare a classroom turn-taking rule with the local government record. Who can change each, and whose needs should count?
> - **Co-write on day 26:** Why have governments (27)? Who decides locally (32)? What do rules protect (34)? How can people participate (37)? How can we decide fairly (40)? What limits government (43)?
> - **Teaching:** model source maker, date and purpose on 27; compare records on 32; model restating and reasoning on 38. Students select evidence on 44 and revise after hearing another claim on 45.

> **Tools: None.** Printed role charts, maps, source cards and ballots fit the jobs; no spec or TOOL-ASKS.md needed. Gather pencils, counters, paper, a ballot box and ballot slips. Print every source and task; one projector only enlarges them.
