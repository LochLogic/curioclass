## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.3.T1.1** (days 7, 8, 9): Use a current Massachusetts map with directions, scale, legend, and title to locate and describe the student's own town, its geographic features, and historic landmarks.
- **D2.Geo.1.3-5** (days 7, 10): Make maps and other graphic pictures of both familiar and unfamiliar places.
- skill ss3-map-skills (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-written supporting questions, day 2:** Where are we on Earth (answered day 6)? What does our town map show (day 10)? How did land and water help place towns (day 15)? How have people changed this place (day 18)? Whose needs does a good location serve (day 20)?
>
> - **Limits:** "our place" means the class's own town, state and region. Massachusetts and New England are worked examples, not the case every student answers.  Not in this unit: degrees of latitude, fractional scale bars, a full biography, a list of source types or an action project. Day 10 adds a short second map of a given invented place, so both parts of D2.Geo.1 are taught before Q2.
> - **Models carried:** map key and directions are tied to symbols on day 7 and reused on day 18. Scale is tied to whole bars on day 8 and reused on day 22. Latitude and longitude are named as lines on days 4 and 5. "Region" moves from climate areas to groups of states on day 11. Claim, evidence and reasoning is modelled on day 19; the teacher's frame is withdrawn on day 21.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: none.** A globe, printed maps and evidence cards do every job; no TOOL-ASKS.md entry. Supplies: a globe, world, physical and political maps, the local map pair (a sourced worked pair stands in on days 18 and 21 if no historical map exists), a satellite view, outline maps, grids, photographs, the Landmark Exit Caption, source cards, answer overlays, tactile copies, string, rulers, pencils. Everything is printed; one projector shows enlarged sheets and the answer key. The day 10 walk follows school supervision policy; no home maps.
>
> At home, the adult takes the partner's turn and writes down the child's spoken reasons; the public library grounds can replace the school map. Teach the key words with labelled pictures. Frame: "The source shows __, which supports __ because __."
