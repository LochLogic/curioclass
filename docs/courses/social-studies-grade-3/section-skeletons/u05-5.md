## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.3.T5.2** (days 106): Explain why many Puritans migrated to Massachusetts, how they spread west, and what this meant for Native Peoples, including lost land, disease, conversion, and conflicts such as King Philip's War.
- **MA.3.T5.1** (days 107): Compare the roles and decisions of leaders of the Puritans and the Pilgrims, such as John Winthrop and William Bradford, with those of Native leaders such as Massasoit and Metacom.
- **MA.3.T5.3** (days 108, 109): Use paintings, artifacts, buildings, or texts to analyze daily life, housing, schooling, and work of Puritans in Massachusetts Bay, including servants and enslaved people.
- **D2.His.2.3-5** (days 108, 110): Compare life in particular periods of the past with life today.
- skill ss3-early-colonies (grade 3)
- skill ss3-biography (grade 3)
- skill ss3-colonial-daily-life (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write on 90:** Why sail west (93)? Whose account and map (95 to 96)? What made cooperation possible (103)? What undermined it (106)? Who worked and who benefited (109 to 116)?
> - **Teaching:** model chronology on 91, sourcing on 95 and two-source reasoning on 117. Step back on 118; hear another supported claim before revision. Teach century, colony, alliance, conversion, enslaved and harbor in context. Read an original Ousamequin biography on 107 from the leadership account. Colony cases follow founding order; each follows its own later consequences before returning to the next founding. Day 106 opens on why Puritans came and how they spread; day 107 adds Winthrop and Metacom; day 108 uses Massachusetts Bay.
>
> - **Limits:** preserve the frame's national worked cases and ports in several colonies. No battle detail, sacred teachings, reenactments or simulations. Name King Philip's War briefly on 106; grade 5 owns its depth. Distinguish Pilgrim separation from Puritan reform; describe belief without judging it. The teacher explains that legal slavery was cruel and that Native nations continue today.
> - **Models:** return to map-key and compass-directions on 94 and 116; perspective on 107; source-sourcing on 95 and 108. Tie timeline to equal intervals on 91 and simultaneous developments on 104; cause-effect-chain on 93; continuity-change on 110. Retain unit 4's corroboration and claim-evidence-reasoning on 117. Withdraw the strip, cause chart and claim frame on 119, retaining blank frames.
