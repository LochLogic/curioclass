## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.3.T4.1** (days 100, 105): Explain who the Pilgrims were, why they left Europe, how they traveled and governed Plymouth Colony, and analyze their relationships with the Wampanoag and Abenaki/Wabanaki.
- **MA.3.T4.1a** (days 101, 105): Explain the purpose of the Mayflower Compact and the idea of self-government.
- **MA.3.T4.1b** (days 102, 105): Describe challenges Pilgrim men, women, and children faced, such as building homes, farming, adjusting to a new place, and keeping their community together.
- **MA.3.T4.1c** (days 103, 105): Describe contacts with Samoset and Massasoit, the events leading to a harvest thanksgiving, and later relations between Europeans and Native Peoples in southeastern Massachusetts.
- **MA.3.T5.1** (days 103): Compare the roles and decisions of leaders of the Puritans and the Pilgrims, such as John Winthrop and William Bradford, with those of Native leaders such as Massasoit and Metacom.
- **D2.His.1.3-5** (days 104, 105): Make and use a time-ordered list of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time.
- **MA.3.T3.1** (days 105): Locate North America, the Atlantic, and Europe, and explain how Native Peoples first met Europeans and why Europeans sailed west in the 1500s and 1600s.
- **MA.3.T3.2** (days 105): Trace on a map the voyages of European explorers of the Northeast coast, such as John Cabot, Verrazano, John Smith, and Champlain.
- **MA.3.T3.3** (days 105): Explain how one explorer described Native Peoples and the new lands, and compare an early 1600s map of New England with a current one.
