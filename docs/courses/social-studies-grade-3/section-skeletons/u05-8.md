## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 117, 118, 120): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D1.4.3-5** (days 117): Explain how answering supporting questions helps answer the big question of an inquiry.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 118, 120): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **D2.His.1.3-5** (days 119, 120): Make and use a time-ordered list of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time.
- **MA.3.T3.1** (days 120): Locate North America, the Atlantic, and Europe, and explain how Native Peoples first met Europeans and why Europeans sailed west in the 1500s and 1600s.
- **MA.3.T3.2** (days 120): Trace on a map the voyages of European explorers of the Northeast coast, such as John Cabot, Verrazano, John Smith, and Champlain.
- **MA.3.T3.3** (days 120): Explain how one explorer described Native Peoples and the new lands, and compare an early 1600s map of New England with a current one.
- **MA.3.T4.1** (days 120): Explain who the Pilgrims were, why they left Europe, how they traveled and governed Plymouth Colony, and analyze their relationships with the Wampanoag and Abenaki/Wabanaki.
- **MA.3.T4.1a** (days 120): Explain the purpose of the Mayflower Compact and the idea of self-government.
- **MA.3.T4.1b** (days 120): Describe challenges Pilgrim men, women, and children faced, such as building homes, farming, adjusting to a new place, and keeping their community together.
- **MA.3.T4.1c** (days 120): Describe contacts with Samoset and Massasoit, the events leading to a harvest thanksgiving, and later relations between Europeans and Native Peoples in southeastern Massachusetts.
- **MA.3.T5.1** (days 120): Compare the roles and decisions of leaders of the Puritans and the Pilgrims, such as John Winthrop and William Bradford, with those of Native leaders such as Massasoit and Metacom.
- **MA.3.T5.2** (days 120): Explain why many Puritans migrated to Massachusetts, how they spread west, and what this meant for Native Peoples, including lost land, disease, conversion, and conflicts such as King Philip's War.
- **MA.3.T5.3** (days 120): Use paintings, artifacts, buildings, or texts to analyze daily life, housing, schooling, and work of Puritans in Massachusetts Bay, including servants and enslaved people.
- **D2.His.2.3-5** (days 120): Compare life in particular periods of the past with life today.
- **MA.3.T5.4** (days 120): Explain that slavery was legal in all the European colonies of the 1600s and 1700s, including Massachusetts, which had both free and enslaved Africans.
- **MA.3.T5.5** (days 120): Explain how sea trade and bartering helped build colonial Massachusetts's economy, using historical society and museum materials.
- **MA.3.T5.5a** (days 120): Describe the fishing and shipbuilding industries.
- **MA.3.T5.5b** (days 120): Describe Atlantic and Caribbean trade, especially the Triangular Trade that sold enslaved Africans and moved goods made by slave labor.
- **MA.3.T5.5c** (days 120): Describe how the seaport cities of New Bedford, Newburyport, Gloucester, Salem, and Boston developed.
- **D2.Geo.7.3-5** (days 120): Explain how cultural and environmental features affect where people, goods and ideas spread and travel.
