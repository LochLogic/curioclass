Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, 141:** compare an old image of our own town with a current public-place photograph; the Long Wharf engraving is the fallback. Ask whose work shaped this place and whose story the images cannot tell.
> - **Co-write on 141:** Who lived here first (142)? Why did towns grow (143 to 145)? What do memories and photographs reveal (146 to 148)? Who shaped change (149 to 152)? How can we contribute (153 to 157)? List needed evidence; discuss two meanings of contribution.
> - **Closing, 157:** answer the compelling question from Local Nation Account (142) and Local Interview (146), checked against Labor History Card (145) and the action record. Defend which contributions mattered; hear another supported answer, then revise.
> - **Teaching:** model sourcing and conflicting accounts on 142, map limits on 143, interviews on 146, overlapping timelines on 148 and explanatory writing on 150. Step back on 151 and 157. Teach founder, immigrant, population, continuity, biography, evidence, contribution and prediction in context.

> - **Carried:** return to source-sourcing on 142, timeline on 148, continuity-change on 147 to 148 and claim-evidence-reasoning on 150 and 157. Retain perspective (units 2 and 5), corroboration (unit 4) and cause-effect (unit 5) models; retrieve unit 1's title and scale reading on 143. ## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Paper maps, source cards, timeline strips, interview sheets, notes frames and action records fit; no tool asks. Supply pencils, rulers, scissors, tape, markers, index cards and reused paper. The supplied interview is a transcript. Print every source and task. One projector enlarges them; at home an adult reads and joins deliberation. No student timers. Read adult originals aloud beside original grade 2 to 3 explanations. Use large print, image descriptions, a scribe and translated vocabulary. Extend by testing another source against a claim.
>
> **Contribution:** on 154 students nominate problems from project evidence. The teacher screens options for completion in day 156's forty minutes using existing materials, then students weigh two or three feasible approaches. On 155 hear minority reasons before compromise and a vote. On 156 assign accessible roles and make the chosen contribution. No travel, fundraising, hazardous cleanup or required public contact (ours, untested). Observe immediate use on 157; record longer effects as unknown. At home, deliberate with the adult and share with another family adult.
