## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 44, 45, 47): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D1.4.3-5** (days 44): Explain how answering supporting questions helps answer the big question of an inquiry.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 45, 47): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **MA.3.T1.3** (days 47): Explain why schools, towns, and cities have governments, what they do, how Massachusetts local governments are organized, and how people take part in their communities.
- **D2.Civ.1.3-5** (days 47): Tell apart what different government officials, levels and branches may do and must do, across times and places.
- **D2.Eco.12.3-5** (days 47): Explain how the government pays for the goods and services it provides.
- **D2.Civ.3.3-5** (days 47): Examine where rules, laws and key parts of the U.S. Constitution came from and what they are for.
- **D2.Civ.4.3-5** (days 47): Explain how groups make rules that set responsibilities and protect freedoms.
- **D2.Civ.2.3-5** (days 47): Explain why a democracy depends on people taking part responsibly, and what that means for how each person acts.
- **D2.Civ.6.3-5** (days 47): Describe how people gain from working together, and what makes it hard, in government, workplaces, volunteer groups and families.
- **D2.Civ.11.3-5** (days 47): Compare how decisions are made in different settings, such as class, school, government and society.
- **D2.Civ.7.3-5** (days 47): Practice civic habits and democratic principles in school.
- **D2.Civ.9.3-5** (days 47): Use group deliberation to make decisions and reach judgments.
- **D2.Civ.10.3-5** (days 47): Identify the beliefs, experiences, views and values behind your own and others' positions on civic issues.
- **MA.3.T6.5** (days 47): Explain that states have governments too, and that the 1780 Massachusetts Constitution, mainly written by John Adams, is the oldest working constitution in the world and grants basic rights.
- **D2.Civ.5.3-5** (days 47): Explain where different systems of government came from, what they do and how they are built, including those in the U.S. and state constitutions.
