# Scope and sequence: Grade 3: Communities Then and Now

Course `social-studies-grade-3`, grade 3, draft, written 2026-11-14 from the frame (docs/courses/social-studies-grade-3/frame.md). The days are in data/plans/social-studies-grade-3/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

## The year at a glance

- 160 course days, one lesson of about 40 minutes every school day (course day n is school day n): 150 in seven units, 10 at course level. Units, owned rows and day counts are the frame's, unchanged.
- Standard: C3 for skills (3 to 5 band); Curio Class's own grade 3 year in place of the anchor's Massachusetts year (bar item 13). Each unit's anchor codes are taught in general form: the student's own town, state, region and Native nations, with worked examples rotated across regions (the own-place method; every unit has a local thread, named in the day notes).
- Every unit runs the C3 arc: the compelling question opens day 1 of the unit, supporting questions are written together, sources answer them, and the unit ends in a short claim with one or two pieces of evidence from named sources, then the end-of-unit assessment (a task from sources not seen before) and one respond day.
- Informed action: once, in unit 7 (days 153 to 157).
- Compressible days: 20 days are marked "compressible (shorter block)" in the note column (days 8, 10, 13, 22, 32, 46, 55, 66, 74, 87, 92, 96, 103, 105, 116, 119, 134, 147, 148, 158), as the frame asks: review days, a second practice day, the school-grounds map, the third colony. A school on a shorter block folds each into the day before or after it; no owned skill or code lives only on a compressible day except the school-grounds map (D2.Geo.1, also on days 7 and 23) and St. Augustine (MA.3.T5.1, also on days 100, 101 and 107).
- Sensitivity: every day with Native, hard-history, immigration or "who was left out" content says "sensitivity read" in its note; the fresh sensitivity agent reads with the unit plan (no outside human readers, Cory 2026-11-14).
- Literacy leans on ELA grade 3: the claim stays a spoken "I think, because" plus one written sentence on a frame until ELA's unit 3 teaches opinion writing (W.3.1, ELA days 81 to 119); from unit 5 the claim may grow past the frame.

## Unit 1. Where we live. "Why is our town where it is?"

Days 1 to 24, weeks 1 to 5. Anchor and C3 codes: MA.3.T1.1, MA.3.T2.1, MA.3.T2.2; D2.Geo.1, 2, 4, 5, 10; D1.1 to D1.4; D3.4; D4.1.

The arc opens on a person (day 1: a short biography tied to the town's founding or a landmark), so the question has a human face, then widens to the globe (days 3 to 6), narrows to the town map (days 7 to 10), widens again to state and region (days 11 to 15), and comes back to how people, Native nations among them, changed this land (days 16 to 18). The claim is modelled (day 19), the evidence gathered with the supporting questions (day 20, D1.3 and D1.4) and the claim written (day 21). Globe before town map because the grid and hemispheres are the frame for "where"; the town map before the region because scale in whole bar lengths is easiest on a familiar place; land change last because it needs both the map and the early map beside it.

- Readiness: day 1, at the first biography read, on grade 1's ss1-leaders (dormant across grade 2); the one-lesson reteach sits in day 2 in a small group.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 23, a map and grid task and a short claim from a source not seen before (map skills, grid and region, land change). Respond: day 24.
- Candidate (New York grade 3, location by continent and hemisphere practised on world communities): kept, inside day 4, no added day.
- Leans: ELA W.3.1 later, bridged on days 19 and 21; science 3-ESS2-2 later (science unit 6), day 6 teaches warmth and rainfall itself.

## Unit 2. Who decides here. "Who should make the rules in our town?"

Days 25 to 48, weeks 5 to 10. Codes: MA.3.T1.3, MA.3.T6.5; D2.Civ.1 to 7, 9 to 11; D2.Eco.12.

Readiness first, because four grade 1 civics rows have slept through grade 2. Then the levels and what each does (days 27 to 32: why governments, leaders and branches at each level, town forms, school governance, taxes, our own town), rules and rights (33 to 34), taking part (35 to 37), deliberation ending in a real class vote with the minority heard first (38 to 40), and last the constitution (41 to 43), taught as a concept before the state and the comparison. The constitution closes the unit because the levels just taught are what a state constitution sets up, and it previews the national plan that unit 6 completes.

- Readiness: day 25 on ss1-elected-leaders, ss1-citizens, ss1-voting and ss1-group-roles; reteach in day 26.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 47, levels of government, a rule traced to its problem, a short claim from an unseen source. Respond: day 48.
- Candidates: California grade 3 (branches at each level, local tribal governments): kept, inside day 28. Tennessee grade 3 civics: read at the unit plan, no day cost here.
- Leans: ELA SL.3.1 runs in ELA unit 1 (days 1 to 39); day 38 teaches the unit's own discussion norms anyway.

## Unit 3. Making a living here. "How does our community get what it needs?"

Days 49 to 68, weeks 10 to 14. Codes: D2.Eco.1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, 14.

Readiness on grade 1's choices and saving, then goods, services and resources (days 50 to 52), money against barter with the class savings plan started (day 53), profit (54 to 55), scarcity, opportunity cost and incentives (56 to 58), banks, credit unions, insurance and interest (59 to 60), then trade outward to the world and one present-day market town in Ghana (61 to 64). The savings plan runs in check-ins across the unit and closes on day 66. Producers before choices because the choices are a business's and a family's; trade last because it needs unit 1's world map and the local economy first.

- Readiness: day 49 on ss1-choices and ss1-saving; reteach in day 50.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 67, resources, a cost, benefit and opportunity cost, a claim from an unseen source. Respond: day 68.
- Candidate (California grade 3, specialization and division of labor; how the local economy changed): kept, specialization in day 52; the change over time goes to unit 7 (day 145).
- Leans: math 3.NBT.A.2 is taught in math unit 3 (days 39 to 59); savings sums stay within 100 until day 60. Personal finance is a light touch here (savings plan, interest in dollars per $100), no added day; all amounts made up.

## Unit 4. The first peoples of our land. "How have the Native nations of our region shaped this place, then and now?"

Days 72 to 89, weeks 15 to 18. Codes: MA.3.T2.3, MA.3.T2.3a to d; D2.Geo.6; D2.His.12.

Day 72 finds whose land the school is on from the nations' own pages. The primary and secondary sort is taught next (days 73 to 74), because every later day reads the nations' own materials as sources. Then the local nation or nations (75 to 76), the five worked cases one day each (77 to 81: Penobscot, Cherokee, Menominee, Navajo, Makah), a comparison on the map (82), contributions (83) and sovereignty (84), and questions of two or three sources on one topic (85) before the claim (86).

- Readiness: none; grade 2's climate regions and source-maker rows were used the year before and open as retrieval tasks (days 72 and 73).
- End-of-unit assessment: day 88, a nation's homeland and present on a map, a primary or secondary sort, a claim from unseen sources. Respond: day 89.
- Candidate (California grade 3, tribal, federal and state governments today): kept, inside day 84.
- Leans: ELA RI.3.7 is taught in ELA unit 1 (days 1 to 39), before this unit.

## Unit 5. First meetings and early colonies. "Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?"

Days 90 to 121, weeks 18 to 25. Codes: MA.3.T3.1 to 3, MA.3.T4.1, MA.3.T4.1a to c, MA.3.T5.1 to 5, MA.3.T5.5a to c; D2.His.1, 2; D2.Geo.7.

Time order. The timeline is taught first (days 91 to 92) so every event after it lands on the line. First meetings (93 to 96, with one explorer's account beside the nation's own), Plymouth and the Wampanoag (97 to 100), Jamestown and the Powhatan with the 1622 war from both sides (101 to 102), the mid-unit assessment, then St. Augustine and the Timucua (105), land loss, disease, conversion and war as colonies spread (106), leaders compared one pair per colony (107), daily life with servants and enslaved people (108 to 110), slavery in every colony with enslaved people's own words (111 to 112), and sea trade ending on the Triangular Trade and port towns (113 to 116). Slavery comes after daily life so students meet enslaved people as people in the colony before the law and the trade; sea trade comes last because the Triangular Trade needs both.

- Mid-unit assessment: day 104 (the unit is 32 days), the timeline, why Europeans sailed west, Plymouth and Jamestown with their Native neighbors; its response opens day 105.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 120, a timeline task, daily life from an image, a claim from two unseen sources. Respond: day 121.
- Candidate (Tennessee 2024 grade 3, New France and New Netherland on the map): kept, inside day 94.
- King Philip's War: not placed (the frame allows it only if days allow; grade 5 unit 1 teaches it with English and Native sources).
- Leans: ELA grade 3 unit 1's Booker T. Washington selection is named as a retrieval point on day 111; the unit teaches the history itself.

## Unit 6. Founding ideas. "What does it mean that government needs the consent of the governed?"

Days 125 to 136, weeks 25 to 28. Codes: MA.3.T6.3, MA.3.T6.4; D2.Civ.8; D2.Civ.5 completed.

Readiness on grade 1's national symbols, then injustice and independence in brief (126), the Declaration (127), the national plan beside unit 2's state constitution (128, D2.Civ.5 completed), the Bill of Rights (129), who was left out (130), citizenship, voting and equal rights sorted (131), and symbols last (132), so symbols are read against the promises and who they reached. The Revolution's events stay in grade 5 (MA.3.T6.2 excluded on purpose).

- Readiness: day 125 on ss1-national-symbols; reteach in day 126.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 135. Respond: day 136.
- Candidate (California grade 3, landmarks and essential documents): kept, inside day 132.

## Unit 7. How our community changed. "Who has made our community what it is?"

Days 141 to 160, weeks 29 to 32. Codes: MA.3.T1.2, MA.3.T6.1; D2.His.3; D4.2, D4.3, D4.7, D4.8.

The project runs in order: who has lived here (142, 145), the state's reach and why its towns grew (143 to 144), interviews and photographs (146 to 147), change and continuity on the timeline (148); the biography (149 to 151); sharing with someone outside the class (152); then the year's informed action (153 to 157): a given case of one person and one group, a local problem from the project with ways to help weighed and predicted, a fair decision, one contribution, and a look back with the closing claim.

- Readiness: none (grade 2's interviews and the year's own rows are retrieved).
- End-of-unit assessment: day 159, a historical map task, what changed and what stayed, a claim from unseen sources; it checks unit 7 only. Respond: day 160.
- Candidate (California grade 3, community history and the economy over time): kept, matches the unit; the economy-over-time part sits in day 145.
- Leans: ELA W.3.2, W.3.7 (ELA units 1 and 2) and SL.3.4 (ELA unit 3) are all taught before; the notes frame stays as practice.
