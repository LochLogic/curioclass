# Scope and sequence: Grade 3: Communities Then and Now

Course `social-studies-grade-3`, grade 3, draft, written 2026-11-14 from the frame, revised the same day from the expert and Codex sequence reads (docs/reviews/sequence-3-5-fixes-2026-11-14.md) (docs/courses/social-studies-grade-3/frame.md). The days are in data/plans/social-studies-grade-3/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

## The year at a glance

- 160 course days, one lesson of about 40 minutes every school day (course day n is school day n): 150 in seven units, 10 at course level. Units, owned rows and day counts are the frame's, unchanged.
- Standard: C3 for skills (3 to 5 band); Curio Class's own grade 3 year in place of the anchor's Massachusetts year (bar item 13). Each unit's anchor codes are taught in general form: the student's own town, state, region and Native nations, with worked examples rotated across regions (the own-place method; every unit has a local thread, named in the day notes).
- Every unit runs the C3 arc: the compelling question opens day 1 of the unit, supporting questions are written together, sources answer them, and the unit ends in a short claim with one or two pieces of evidence from named sources, then the end-of-unit assessment (a task from sources not seen before) and one respond day.
- Informed action: once, in unit 7 (days 153 to 157).
- Biography thread: a short biography read on day 1, then on one existing day in each of units 2 to 6 (days 35, 54, 83, 107, 130), a few minutes inside the day, before the written biography in unit 7 (days 149 to 151).
- Every assessment day (end-of-unit, mid-unit, course-level) lists every code it assesses, and its goal asks about each part of each code, a short prompt per part; codes judged from a product (the D1 question work, the discussion and vote, unit 7's biography, sharing and action) say so in the note and are scored with the unit rubric.
- Compressible days: 20 days are marked "compressible (shorter block)" in the note column (days 8, 10, 13, 22, 32, 46, 55, 66, 74, 87, 92, 96, 97, 104, 116, 119, 134, 147, 148, 158), as the frame asks: review days, a second practice day, the school-grounds map, the oldest colony. A school on a shorter block folds each into the day before or after it, and the work moves with it, not only the code: if day 10 folds, the school-grounds drawing moves into day 9 as a short sketch (day 7 only reads a map, so it does not carry the drawing), and day 23 asks for a small drawn map; if day 97 folds, St. Augustine opens day 98 and is placed on the timeline there (MA.3.T5.1 is also on days 98, 103 and 107). Day 105's mid-unit response opens day 106, which is not compressible.
- Sensitivity: every day with Native, hard-history, immigration or "who was left out" content says "sensitivity read" in its note; the fresh sensitivity agent reads with the unit plan (no outside human readers, Cory 2026-11-14).
- Literacy leans on ELA grade 3: the claim stays a spoken "I think, because" plus one written sentence on a frame until ELA's unit 3 teaches opinion writing (W.3.1, ELA days 81 to 119); from unit 5 the claim may grow past the frame.

## Unit 1. Where we live. "Why is our town where it is?"

Days 1 to 24, weeks 1 to 5. Anchor and C3 codes: MA.3.T1.1, MA.3.T2.1, MA.3.T2.2; D2.Geo.1, 2, 4, 5, 10; D1.1 to D1.4; D3.4; D4.1.

The arc opens on a person (day 1: a short biography tied to the town's founding or a landmark), so the question has a human face, then widens to the globe (days 3 to 6), narrows to the town map (days 7 to 10), widens again to state and region (days 11 to 15), and comes back to how people, Native nations among them, changed this land (days 16 to 18). The claim is modelled from two very short sources, a map and a photograph (day 19), so naming two sources and saying how each supports one claim is met at the start; the evidence is gathered with the supporting questions, each with the facts it needs and one idea people read two ways (day 20, D1.3 and D1.4), and the claim written (day 21). Unit 5 revisits the two-source model on day 117 before its two-source claim. Globe before town map because the grid and hemispheres are the frame for "where"; the town map before the region because scale in whole bar lengths is easiest on a familiar place; land change last because it needs both the map and the early map beside it.

- Readiness: day 1, at the first biography read, on grade 1's ss1-leaders (dormant across grade 2); the one-lesson reteach sits in day 2 in a small group.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 23, every owned code: the town map read and a small map drawn, grid, hemisphere and climate, the region's states and features, a land change and the culture behind it, the D1 question work, and a short claim from a source not seen before. Respond: day 24.
- Candidate (New York grade 3, location by continent and hemisphere practised on world communities): kept, inside day 4, no added day.
- Leans: ELA W.3.1 later, bridged on days 19 and 21; science 3-ESS2-2 later (science unit 6), day 6 teaches warmth and rainfall itself.

## Unit 2. Who decides here. "Who should make the rules in our town?"

Days 25 to 48, weeks 5 to 10. Codes: MA.3.T1.3, MA.3.T6.5; D2.Civ.1 to 7, 9 to 11; D2.Eco.12.

Readiness first, because four grade 1 civics rows have slept through grade 2. Then the levels and what each does (days 27 to 32: why governments, the leaders at each level and the branches of the state and national governments, the town's actual form, school governance, taxes, our own town), rules and rights (33 to 34), taking part (35 to 37), deliberation ending in a real class vote with the minority heard first (38 to 40), and last the constitution (41 to 43), taught as a concept before the state and the comparison. The constitution closes the unit because the levels just taught are what a state constitution sets up, and it previews the national plan that unit 6 completes.

- Readiness: day 25 on ss1-elected-leaders, ss1-citizens, ss1-voting and ss1-group-roles; reteach in day 26.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 47, every owned code: levels, the town's form and taking part, taxes, a rule and a right, working together, deciding, the views behind a disagreement, the constitutions, and a short claim from an unseen source; the discussion and vote (days 39 to 40) also judged with the unit rubric. Respond: day 48.
- Biography read: day 35, a local person who serves or volunteers.
- Candidates: California grade 3 (branches, local tribal governments): kept, inside day 28, as the branches of the state and national governments and the leaders at each level; local governments are not taught as a three-branch copy (days 29 to 32 study the town's actual form), and day 43 describes the tribal nation's government from its own constitution without assuming three branches. Tennessee grade 3 civics: read at the unit plan, no day cost here.
- Leans: ELA SL.3.1 runs in ELA unit 1 (days 1 to 39); day 38 teaches the unit's own discussion norms anyway.

## Unit 3. Making a living here. "How does our community get what it needs?"

Days 49 to 68, weeks 10 to 14. Codes: D2.Eco.1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 10, 14.

Readiness on grade 1's choices and saving, then goods, services and resources (days 50 to 52), money against barter with the class savings plan started (day 53), profit (54 to 55), scarcity, opportunity cost and incentives (56 to 58), banks, credit unions, insurance and interest (59 to 60), then trade outward to the world and one present-day market town in Ghana (61 to 64). The savings plan runs in check-ins across the unit and closes on day 66. Producers before choices because the choices are a business's and a family's; trade last because it needs unit 1's world map and the local economy first.

- Readiness: day 49 on ss1-choices and ss1-saving; reteach in day 50.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 67, every owned code: resources, money against barter, profit, an incentive, cost, benefit and opportunity cost, banks, interest as dollars on each $100, trade and interdependence, and a claim from an unseen source. Respond: day 68.
- Biography read: day 54, a local business owner.
- Candidate (California grade 3, specialization and division of labor; how the local economy changed): kept, specialization in day 52; the change over time goes to unit 7 (day 145).
- Leans: math 3.NBT.A.2 is taught in math unit 3 (days 39 to 59); savings sums stay within 100 until day 60. Personal finance is a light touch here (savings plan, interest in dollars per $100), no added day; all amounts made up.

## Unit 4. The first peoples of our land. "How have the Native nations of our region shaped this place, then and now?"

Days 72 to 89, weeks 15 to 18. Codes: MA.3.T2.3, MA.3.T2.3a to d; D2.Geo.6; D2.His.12.

Day 72 finds whose land the school is on from the nations' own pages. The primary and secondary sort is taught next (days 73 to 74), because every later day reads the nations' own materials as sources. Then the local nation or nations (75 to 76), the five worked cases one day each (77 to 81: Penobscot, Cherokee with the Eastern Band named as governing in its North Carolina homeland today, Menominee, Navajo, Makah), a comparison on the map (82), contributions starting from a nation near the school (83, with a short biography read) and sovereignty (84), and questions of two or three sources on one topic (85) before the claim (86).

- Readiness: none; grade 2's climate regions and source-maker rows were used the year before and open as retrieval tasks (days 72 and 73).
- End-of-unit assessment: day 88, every part of MA.3.T2.3 and its lettered parts (three nations, a homeland and lands today on a map, how land placed towns, a local contribution, a government today), two questions asked of a source set with each source sorted, and a claim from unseen sources. Respond: day 89.
- Candidate (California grade 3, tribal, federal and state governments today): kept, inside day 84.
- Leans: ELA RI.3.7 is taught in ELA unit 1 (days 1 to 39), before this unit.

## Unit 5. First meetings and early colonies. "Could the colonists and the Native nations have shared the land?"

Days 90 to 121, weeks 18 to 25. Codes: MA.3.T3.1 to 3, MA.3.T4.1, MA.3.T4.1a to c, MA.3.T5.1 to 5, MA.3.T5.5a to c; D2.His.1, 2; D2.Geo.7.

Time order. The timeline is taught first (days 91 to 92) so every event after it lands on the line. First meetings (93 to 96, with one explorer's account beside the nation's own), then the colonies in the order they were founded, each placed on the timeline as it opens: St. Augustine and the Timucua (97, 1565), Jamestown and the Powhatan with the 1622 war from both sides (98 to 99, 1607), Plymouth and the Wampanoag, with Samoset and the Abenaki/Wabanaki (100 to 103, 1620), the three on the timeline (104), the mid-unit assessment (105), then land loss, disease, conversion and war as colonies spread (106, opening with the mid-unit response), leaders compared one pair per colony (107, with a short biography read), daily life with servants and enslaved people (108 to 110), slavery in every colony with enslaved people's own words (111 to 112), and sea trade, with colonial barter retrieved from unit 3, ending on the Triangular Trade and port towns (113 to 116). Slavery comes after daily life so students meet enslaved people as people in the colony before the law and the trade; sea trade comes last because the Triangular Trade needs both.

- Mid-unit assessment: day 105 (the unit is 32 days), every part of the first-half codes: the three colonies on the timeline, why Europeans sailed west, an explorer's route, an explorer's account and an early map, and every part of MA.3.T4.1 and its lettered parts; its response opens day 106.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 120, every owned code: the second-half codes in full (leaders compared, the spread and what it meant, daily life and change since, slavery, sea trade, barter and port towns, a route), the first-half codes as one short prompt each, and a claim from two unseen sources. Respond: day 121.
- Candidate (Tennessee 2024 grade 3, New France and New Netherland on the map): kept, inside day 94.
- King Philip's War: not placed (the frame allows it only if days allow; grade 5 unit 1 teaches it with English and Native sources).
- Leans: ELA grade 3 unit 1's Booker T. Washington selection is named as a retrieval point on day 111; the unit teaches the history itself.

## Unit 6. Founding ideas. "What does it mean that government needs the consent of the governed?"

Days 125 to 136, weeks 25 to 28. Codes: MA.3.T6.3, MA.3.T6.4; D2.Civ.8; D2.Civ.5 completed.

Readiness on grade 1's national symbols, then injustice and independence in brief (126), the Declaration (127), the national plan beside unit 2's state constitution (128, D2.Civ.5 completed), the Bill of Rights (129), who was left out (130), citizenship, voting and equal rights sorted (131), and symbols last (132), so symbols are read against the promises and who they reached. The Revolution's events stay in grade 5 (MA.3.T6.2 excluded on purpose).

- Readiness: day 125 on ss1-national-symbols; reteach in day 126.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 135, every owned code: the injustice and the Declaration, the Constitution beside the state's, the Bill of Rights, who could vote, one civic virtue or principle, and a claim from an unseen source. Respond: day 136.
- Biography read: day 130, a person who pressed for rights then.
- Candidate (California grade 3, landmarks and essential documents): kept, inside day 132.

## Unit 7. How our community changed. "Who has made our community what it is?"

Days 141 to 160, weeks 29 to 32. Codes: MA.3.T1.2, MA.3.T6.1; D2.His.3; D4.2, D4.3, D4.7, D4.8.

The project runs in order: who has lived here (142, 145), the state's reach and why its towns grew (143 to 144), interviews and photographs (146 to 147), change and continuity on the timeline (148); the biography (149 to 151); sharing with someone outside the class (152); then the year's informed action (153 to 157): a given case of one person and one group, a local problem from the project with ways to help weighed and predicted, a fair decision, one contribution, and a look back with the closing claim.

- Readiness: none (grade 2's interviews and the year's own rows are retrieved).
- End-of-unit assessment: day 159, a historical map task, who has lived here, what changed and what stayed, questions about a person, ways to help with predictions, and a claim from unseen sources; scored beside it with the unit rubric, each student's biography (D2.His.3, D4.2), share-out (D4.3) and action record (D4.7, D4.8). It checks unit 7 only. Respond: day 160.
- Candidate (California grade 3, community history and the economy over time): kept, matches the unit; the economy-over-time part sits in day 145.
- Leans: ELA W.3.2, W.3.7 (ELA units 1 and 2) and SL.3.4 (ELA unit 3) are all taught before; the notes frame stays as practice.
