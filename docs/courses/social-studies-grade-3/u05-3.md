# Section 5.3: St. Augustine and Jamestown, and the Native nations who lived there

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/5/s/5.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u05-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a filled card or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to days 105 and 120. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names, are in the present tense where they live on, and are credited. Nothing here is role play, and the war is named plainly without graphic detail.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T5.1 (days 97, 98).** "Compare the roles and decisions of leaders of the Puritans and the Pilgrims, such as John Winthrop and William Bradford, with those of Native leaders such as Massasoit and Metacom." Parts: compare roles; compare decisions; the two sides' leaders. This section teaches the first pair of leaders (a Spanish officer with a Timucua leader on 97; Wahunsenaca with John Smith on 98); the Puritan and Pilgrim leaders come in 5.4 and 5.5 (Bradford and Ousamequin on 103; Winthrop and Metacom on 107).
- **MA.3.T5.2 (days 98, 99).** "Explain why many Puritans migrated to Massachusetts, how they spread west, and what this meant for Native Peoples, including lost land, disease, conversion, and conflicts such as King Philip's War." The part taught here is why people came to Virginia (day 98) and one conflict, the 1622 war, from both sides (day 99). The Puritan parts and King Philip's War are taught in 5.5.
- **Limits that bite here.** A leader card holds a name, a nation, a role and one decision; the interest (why) is added on day 98. Native leaders are named as the record names them (spelling and name source to check); the Timucua and the Pamunkey and other Powhatan nations are named by their own names and live today (to check on each nation's own page). The 1622 war is told from two papers, each labeled by maker and purpose. No leader card says who is right.
- **Connections.** Timeline (place 1565, 1607 and 1622 on the wall strip); source-sourcing (taught 95, used 99); perspective (used 98); grade 2 "why people move" and grade 3 unit 2 "who makes the rules" (review).
- **New here.** role, decision, interest, war, report written to defend its maker's side (a source that argues).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** What the Timucua and Powhatan nations were and are; that a leader answers to a group, and Native leaders and colonial officers answered to different people; why a colony's report is a side, not the whole story; how to hold the war without role play.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. The teacher reads each stem aloud once.
- **E97 (Seloy Exit Card, from the unit plan; the food detail is held back from teaching, settled in sources.csv U5R2).** A short factual line from the Florida Museum account of Seloy: the colonists got food from the Timucua. "What could the Timucua decide about that food? Say one thing." Key: any decision that was theirs, such as to give, trade or hold it back. Wrong: the colonists' leader decided for everyone, or the Timucua had no say (AC). Flag: dossier B's St. Augustine card holds the same fact (see field 11).
- **E98 (Pamunkey Exit Card, from the unit plan; the detail is held back from teaching and from dossier B, source to check).** "Why did Wahunsenaca order a siege of the fort? (the card, read aloud, carries the facts)" Key: colonists pressing the Powhatan towns for food gave him a reason to act, so he ordered a siege. Wrong: only an explorer's view tells the whole story (C).
- **E99 (Pier Rule Pair, invented for practice; it replaces the unit plan's Waterhouse and Pamunkey labels, which repeat the worked passages).** "Paper 1, March, made by the pier owner, to keep the rule. Paper 2, April, made by the skiff club, to change it. Who made each, and why might each explain it that way? Say it for one paper." Key: the owner's paper defends the rule; the club's paper pushes for change; each position shapes what it says. Wrong: one paper tells the whole truth, or neither has a reason (C).

## 6. Days

## Day 97


- **10 min. Return and repair (Z from E96, routed from section 5.2).** Two accounts of one coast: pairs find a farm or town in each; the blank or new label does not mean empty. If few missed it, the missing children do this at the side table.
- **7 min. Timeline and hook.** Place 1565 on the wall strip. The teacher reads the Seloy fact again from day 90; St. Augustine is the oldest colony in this unit; a Timucua town was already there (name and place source to check). Say that the Timucua people and their descendants are part of communities today (source to check).
- **12 min. Worked leader cards.** Two cards, filled aloud: Menendez de Aviles (led the settlers and founded St. Augustine, U5B) and a Timucua card whose leader box stays blank (the page names no leader for Seloy, U5A). The class fills name, nation, role, decision, and who each answered to, and discusses how the two roles differ. The food detail is not used.
- **9 min. Pairs: who answered to whom.** An invented picture of two towns, Brook School and Hill School (Brook has a captain who answers to a distant owner; Hill has a council that answers to its families). Each pair sorts which leader answered to whom and writes two lines. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs; AC is caught on the spot.
- **2 min. Exit.** E97.

Who holds what is carried: each pair a leader card; the teacher the Seloy passage. Tool: None. By hand: the card, the exit. Evidence: E97. This day carries the sensitivity read and is compressible (if folded, St. Augustine opens day 98 on the timeline).

## Day 98


- **5 min. Return and repair (AC from E97).** The Brook School and Hill School leaders: each child sorts who each leader answered to. If few missed it, the missing children do this at the side table.
- **5 min. Timeline.** Place 1607 on the wall strip. Jamestown, in Virginia, is later than St. Augustine; say how many tens apart (reading the strip).
- **8 min. Why people came.** The four reason cards from day 93 are returned: for Virginia, a company paid for settlers hoping for profit and land (the Virginia Company's aims are source to check). The cause-effect chain from day 93 gets a new link.
- **12 min. Wahunsenaca and the Virginia Company.** The class reads one short line from the Pamunkey's own history page about Wahunsenaca (he led about thirty groups, U5C; not the exit line) and one on the Virginia Company, which founded James Fort in 1607 (U5L); Smith's role is cut as unsourced and blank boxes stay blank. Fill the two leader cards: name, nation, role, decision, interest, who each answered to. Perspective: the two men saw one event, a meeting, from two sides.
- **8 min. Pairs.** Each pair fills a third leader card from an invented two-line case (Captain Ruiz and Elder Pita of a fishing town): role, one decision, and the interest that explains it. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs; the record names them.
- **2 min. Exit.** E98.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the wall strip; each pair a leader card. Tool: None. By hand: the cards, the exit. Evidence: E98.

## Day 99


- **10 min. Return and repair (C from E98).** A fresh pair of papers about one beach rule, labeled by maker and purpose; pairs say what each says and what each leaves out. If few missed it, the missing children use the side table.
- **5 min. Timeline.** Place 1622 on the wall strip. Say that it was fifteen years after 1607 (one and a half tens).
- **12 min. The 1622 war, two papers.** The war is named plainly: Powhatan people and the English at Jamestown fought in 1622. Cause and context are laid on the board in dated order from the Pamunkey chronology (source to check): this is the repair for X, with an invented dock quarrel to show a dated cause and effect. One paper is a Curio Class summary of a company report (Waterhouse, 1622, label: a report written to defend the colony); the other is the Pamunkey's own history (to check). Read aloud once each; no hostile or graphic wording; children underline one claim in each. The maker and purpose label comes first.
- **8 min. Pairs.** A new invented pair (a library's notice, a reading club's letter, about a quiet room): each pair says who made each and why each might say it that way. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **3 min. Board evidence.** Add the 1622 evidence to the question board: "What undermined it?" is answered later (106); today the board holds "two sides, two papers".
- **2 min. Exit.** E99.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the two source cards; each pair the quiet-room pair. Tool: None. By hand: the underline, the exit. Evidence: E99. No role play.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 97, "Who did this leader answer to?"; day 98, "What did he want, and what did he decide?"; day 99, "Who made this paper, and what is it for?"
- Written task, day 98: one leader card for each of two leaders, filled from two lines of source, kept in the record folder.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: (1) a name and a nation; (2) a role in a few words; (3) one decision; (4) an interest that explains it. Present, with help or not yet seen. Spelling of names is not scored; the record's spelling is the model.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 97: the strip and century from 91 and 92, and the earlier Z repair. Day 98: the leader card and the reason cards from 93. Day 99: the three sourcing questions from 95 and the strip dates.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: leader, role, decision, interest, answer to, war, source. Frames: "___ was the ___ of ___." "He decided ___ because he wanted ___." "This paper was made to ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Cards in large print with one idea per box; a name card with a picture of each leader; speech, pointing or a scribe; the reasoning stays the child's.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a spoken answer or a pointed card; the sentence of E99 may be a point at the maker label with a spoken reason.
- **Reading load.** Source lines are one to three short sentences; the teacher reads them; children underline, not copy.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third leader card (an invented case) with one interest and one decision.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads each source aloud and fills one card with the child; the child says role, decision and interest. For the war day the adult reads both papers and asks only who made each and why. Nothing in this section is the last day of the unit.
- **One projector.** Project one source or card at a time; children fill paper cards.
