# Section 7.1: Who lived here and why the town grew


## Header

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 7, How our community changed (days 141 to 160): see [s07.md](s07.md). **Days 141 to 145.** Days by kind: understanding 5 (141, 142, 143, 144, 145). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Who has made our community what it is?"
- Section 1 of 5 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a labelled drawing, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to day 159 only and are never self-marked. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Native nations are named by their own names and are living nations today. The stop card and the right to pass hold. Boston and Denver are worked cases; the class's own town is the thread, from its local packet.

- **D1.1.3-5, day 141.** "Explain why the compelling question matters to others." Day 141 models one reason for one audience, then each child gives a reason for a new audience. The two readings of contribution are named here and kept all unit.
- **MA.3.T1.2, days 142 and 145.** "Research the origins and people of the town or city, including Native Peoples, colonial founders, and enslaved and free workers, and describe its population and immigrant groups now and in the past through interviews." Day 142 carries the Native nation (from its own page) and the founders (from the town's record). Day 145 carries enslaved and free workers, population and newcomers. The interviews are section 7.2.
- **MA.3.T6.1, days 143 and 144.** "Use a historical map to explain how far the Province of Massachusetts extended, and explain why its towns and cities grew in the 1700s." Taught in the settled own-state form: a map at one date (143), then a growth reason from place and work (144).
- **Limits that bite here.** A town's founding date is not when people first lived there. Native nations are named by their own names, spoken of in the present tense, and no child is asked about ancestry or asked to speak for a nation. Slavery is named as forced labor, with resistance and lives built; forced transportation is kept apart from immigration. No child is asked about family immigration, status, money or religion. A western state's 1700s may be a Native or Spanish story. Denver's growth is a settler town's growth, not first habitation. Reserved and used on no teaching day: map A, the transfer cards B and C, local source E.
- **Connections.** D2.His.1.3-5: the founding date and the map date go on the class wall strip (142, 143). D4.1.3-5: source-sourcing on every card (142). Unit 1 title and scale reading (143). Unit 5 harbor chain (144). Unit 5 and grade 2 forced and chosen moves (145).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between "made a town" and "first lived here" (A). That a map's title says what a sheet leaves out (C) and that borders change over time (Q). Why place alone does not explain growth: place plus work (O). The difference between chosen and forced moves, said plainly (B, J). How to read a population figure as a dated comparison, not today's exact count.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E141 (D1.1.3-5).** "Why might a new neighbor care who shaped this town?" Key: a reason that belongs to someone else, such as "A new neighbor would want to know whose work they live beside." Shown with help: a reason with no one else in it. Wrong: "Only I care because I like it" (I); "Everyone in town thinks the same about who built it" (D).
- **E142 (Founding Card, invented for practice; the town record and the farm book are made up).** Town record: "Made a town in 1722." Old Hill Farm book: "The Hartley family farmed here before the town was made, and we farm here today." Ask 1: "Must one of the two records be wrong?" Ask 2: "Which record tells who lived there before 1722?" Key: no; the town record says when the town was made, the farm book says who lived there before and still does; the farm book. Wrong: "Yes, nobody lived there before 1722" (A); "Yes, the farm family left when the town was made" (A); "The town record" (A).
- **E143 (Map Title Card, invented for practice).** "A sheet drawn in 1790 is titled 'Orchard Province, without the northern district.'" Ask 1: "Does this sheet show how far the whole province reached?" Ask 2: "Circle the words in the title that tell you." Key: no; the words "without the northern district". Wrong: "Yes, the edge of the sheet is the edge of the province" (C); "Yes, and the province has the same outline today" (Q).
- **E144 (Cask Lake Note, invented for practice).** "Cask is a made-up town. A note says it grew after a railroad stop opened in 1885 and a brick works hired many workers. A child says, 'It grew only because of the lake.'" Ask 1: "Does the lake alone explain why the town grew?" Ask 2: "Name one other thing that helped the town grow." Key: no; the railroad stop or the brick works jobs. Wrong: "Yes, towns grow where there is water" (O); "The lake" (O).
- **E145 (Lena and Joel Cards, invented for practice; two made-up cards).** Lena's card: "Lena was taken from her home by force. She was made to work and was not paid." Joel's card: "Joel's family took a train to the city because a factory there was hiring." Ask 1: "Was Lena's move chosen or forced?" Ask 2: "How do you know?" Key: forced; she was taken by force and was not paid. Wrong: "Chosen, because she came to work" (B); "She was paid like the other workers" (J).
