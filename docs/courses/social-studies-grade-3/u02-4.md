# Section 2.4: Talking a choice through, then deciding fairly

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 2, Who decides here (days 25 to 48): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 38 to 40.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (38), application 2 (39, 40). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Who should make the rules in our town?"
- Section 4 of 6 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled drawing, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. People in cases are invented and said aloud to be made up (teaching: Tumbleford, Room 7; exits only: Ana and Ben, Zoe, Room 9 and Hugo). The one real case is the class's own choice on days 38 to 40.

- **D2.Civ.7.3-5, this section's share.** "Practice civic habits and democratic principles in school." Parts: apply virtues (listen, restate fairly, give a reason, no mocking); apply principles (one equal, private ballot). Day 38 teaches the habits; day 40 is the scored retry after written feedback on day 39.
- **D2.Civ.9.3-5, this section's share.** "Use group deliberation to make decisions and reach judgments." Child words: talk a choice through. Day 39 practice, day 40 retry: hear reasons from each side before the vote.
- **D2.Civ.10.3-5, this section's share.** "Identify the beliefs, experiences, views and values behind your own and others' positions on civic issues." Day 39: a view, an experience in our class or school and a value, for oneself and for another child.
- **Limits that bite here.** The choice is real, small and safe to carry out (a Friday game, a reading time, a plant's name). The class chooses it; the course never picks a public cause. Ideas that touch anyone's rights, identity, access or punishment are set aside kindly by the teacher. An "experience" is something that happened in our class or school; no child tells about home or family. A child may pass. Signing, pointing to a reason card and a communication device count as taking part; being quiet is not failing. No child is the example of a mistake. No public contact.
- **Connections.** MA.3.T1.3, how people take part (day 35, from 2.3). The claim frame "I think ___ because ___" is used again on days 44 and 45. Bridge for ELA grade 3 SL.3.1 (its unit 1, running now): this section teaches its own norms before the vote and does not assume the ELA unit's.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Listen to the speaker, wait for a turn (review of social-studies-kindergarten u01-3); fair (everyone gets a turn); vote; equality (each vote counts the same); ballot and secret ballot (review of social-studies-grade-1 u04-1); a tie: runoff, then try both in turn (grade 1 u07-2); perspective (a view with its reason; grade 1 u07-2); reason; the way "listen, ask why, look for a change both accept, vote only if still apart" (review of social-studies-kindergarten u02-2 and social-studies-grade-1 u04-1); election and vote, and the question board, Return and repair, exit ticket (review of social-studies-grade-3 u02-3 and u02-1).
- **New here.** Restate (say another person's view back fairly, without agreeing or arguing); experience (something that happened to you); value (something that matters to you); talk a choice through (hear a reason from each side before deciding); less-supported view (the choice with fewer marks so far); Class Decision Record.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Restating shows you listened; it does not mean you agree. A different view comes from a different experience or value, not a worse person. Hearing the less-supported view first does not reveal anyone's ballot. An equal ballot is one each, counted once, whoever the child is. A tie is a reason to talk more, not to give anyone two ballots. How to screen the class's ideas, and how to keep the two scored retries honest when one adult cannot hear thirty children at once (field 5).

## Day 39

- goal: Hold a structured discussion on a real class question and name the experiences and values behind each view
- note: the question is a real class choice, never a contested public issue picked by the course; practice performance gets individual written feedback before day 40; the values record gets written feedback before day 46 revision

- **5 min. Return and repair.** The most-missed letter of E38 (G, Q or AC), wrong example then right, whole class; a ten-minute G continues in the discussion.
- **4 min. Choose the question.** Four screened ideas, on a card. Each child drops one counter, one each, in a cup for the idea to decide. The teacher reads the result. The top idea becomes the question with two options; the rest go on a later list.
- **4 min. Norms and ballot rules.** The class reads the norms card and agrees the ballot rules: one each, the same rules for all, private, a tie means talk more then a runoff.
- **5 min. First leaning, private.** Each child marks a slip "A, B or not sure" and writes one reason on a card with no name. The teacher sorts reason cards into A and B piles after class and counts the marks; no one sees another child's slip.
- **12 min. Talk it through.** Five groups of six, norms card on each desk. Each child gives a view and a reason; the next child says it back before giving their own (about 1 minute each, 7 minutes). Then 4 minutes of open talk; one minute to stop. The teacher sits with a rotating six.
- **7 min. The values record.** Each child fills: "My view is ___ because ___. Something that happened in our class: ___. I care about ___. ___'s reason was ___." Five minutes writing; two minutes a partner marks "my reason was said right: yes / not yet". Practice, kept for day 46.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E39.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-38-40.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The three-column card and Mo's column share the shape of E39 (an experience and a value); E39 is a new case, the Book Box Card.

Who holds what is carried: groups hold the norms card; the teacher holds the ballot rules card. Tool: None. By hand: marks, reason cards, values record, exit. Evidence: the values record (practice), the slips, E39, the listening notes. After class: the teacher writes one cue for each child on listening, saying it back, reasons and one ballot (written feedback, returned on day 40).

## Day 40

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Hear the minority view first, then decide by class vote
- note: score the retry after day 39 written feedback: feedback 5, hear views 10, deliberate 10, vote 5, reflect 8, exit 2 minutes
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Read feedback and repair.** Two minutes: each child reads the written cue and chooses one thing to do better. Three minutes: the most-missed of Q (from E39) or AC and AA (heard in day 39's groups), wrong example then right. If the repair is a ten-minute one, its other half is the next part.
- **10 min. Hear the less-supported view first.** The teacher says which option had fewer marks, with no names, and reads that option's reason cards (about 2 minutes). In groups of six each child says one of those reasons back before giving their own (about 45 seconds each, 5 minutes); the teacher listens to a rotating six. Three minutes: one reason I had not thought of, said to the class.
- **10 min. Talk it through.** Groups of six, reason cards on the desks; speak in turn, point to a card or sign. Eight minutes of talk; two minutes of private marks: "My view held / changed."
- **5 min. The private ballot.** One slip each, marked in private, dropped in the covered box in two lines (about 6 seconds a child, 3 minutes); two counters read the marks aloud and the teacher records, 2 minutes. A tie takes 3 minutes from the record's whole-class part: one minute of talk, a runoff ballot.
- **8 min. Reflect and record.** Three minutes: the class writes the Class Decision Record (date, question, options, result, one reason each side, a continuing concern, what we will do); post the answer to "How can we decide fairly?" on the question board. Five minutes: each child fills the day 40 slip.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E40.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-38-40.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The ballot rules card and the class vote share the shape of E40 (one ballot each, counted once); E40 is a new case, the Room 9 Card.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the ballot box; two counters rotate and are not the only voters. Tool: None. By hand: marks, ballot, record, slip, exit. Evidence: the day 40 slip (the scored retry) with the partner's mark, the listening notes, E40. The safe class choice is carried out afterwards; no one outside the room is contacted.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: listen, say it back, reason, view, experience, value, ballot, vote. Frames: "I hear you say ___ because ___." "My view is ___ because ___. I care about ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation or a pointed card.
- **Access.** Signing, pointing to a reason card and a communication device are taking part. Large-print cards; a child may pass.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe writes the child's words; spelling and handwriting are not judged. A partner mark may be a tick.
- **Reading load.** Every card is read aloud once; printed pages at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Compare two defensible ways to decide the same choice (a vote or talking until all agree) and say what each protects.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult gives a real view, the child gives theirs; each says the other's reason back; both name an experience and a value. Vote with one slip each. Two people may agree or tie: talk more and make a plan that gives each person part of what they wanted. Do not invent a majority.
- **One projector.** Show the norms card, the three-column card, the ballot rules and the Class Decision Record enlarged; groups need paper copies of the norms card and the reason cards.
