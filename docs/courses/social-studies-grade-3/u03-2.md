# Section 3.2: Money, profit and the businesses near us

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 3, Making a living here (days 49 to 68): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 53 to 55.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (53, 54), application 1 (55). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "How does our community get what it needs?"
- Section 2 of 5 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled drawing, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Teaching cases are invented and said aloud to be made up (a baker, a cobbler and a carpenter; a candle stall; a made-up class savings plan that collects nothing); exit-only cases are the farmer and tailor, soup and fruit, and the shoe repair shop. Day 54's account is an original short text, and day 55's Local Production Record summarizes two public local pages. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

- **D2.Eco.5.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain how money simplifies exchange." Part: explain easier exchange (day 53): everyone accepts money, so a trade does not need two people who each want what the other has.
- **D2.Eco.7.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain how expected profit affects sellers." Part: explain influence on selling decisions (days 54, 55): a seller who hopes to have money left after costs chooses what to make and what price to ask; hope does not guarantee sales.
- **D2.Eco.3.3-5, applied.** "Identify natural resources, workers' knowledge and skills, and equipment used for goods and services." Day 55 uses the three baskets from 3.1 on two local businesses; nothing is taught as new.
- **Limits that bite here.** Profit is sales income left after costs, not a measure of a person. No percent. Barter is a comparison; the plan never says every people once bartered. Amounts are made up, no child shares family money facts and nothing is collected. Day 54 names unfair barriers plainly (Black residents of Richmond faced them), with no reenactment, no assigned identities and no religious performance. Her disability is not part of this short account. It does not say profit was her only purpose and invents no prices.
- **Connections.** Math grade 3 3.NBT.A.2 (its unit 3, running now): the savings sums stay within 100 until day 60; if the parallel lesson has not come, the teacher shows the adding. D3.4.3-5: day 54 uses the before, action, after boxes; day 55 builds the Local Production Record that days 64 and 65 use as a source.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Price and income (review of social-studies-grade-1 u08-1); save (grade 1 u08-3); cost (review of social-studies-grade-2 u03-3); trade (kindergarten u06-2 and grade 2 u04-2); the three baskets and specialize (review of social-studies-grade-3 u03-1); before, action, after, and biography (u01-1); who made it, when and why (u01-5); the question board (u02-1); shows, suggests, cannot tell (u01-3).
- **New here.** Barter; budget (money in, money out, money kept); sales (money buyers pay); profit (money left after costs); expected profit (what a seller hopes will be left).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the three-person trade shows what barter needs (a match) and why money does not mean nobody gives anything up: paying gives up money (B). Profit is not a moral measure; a hoped-for profit shapes choices but sales may not follow, so a higher price can go unsold (M). Maggie Lena Walker: the account rests on the National Park Service pages in unit plan field 10; every name, date and business is source to check and nothing here is from memory. The bank is only the place she kept and lent money; what a bank does returns on day 59.

## 6. Days

## Day 53

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Explain why money makes trading easier than barter, and start a made-up class savings plan toward a goal
- note: bridge for math grade 3 3.NBT.A.2 (its unit 3, days 39 to 59, running now): sums stay small until day 60; all amounts made up, no family money facts; personal finance light touch, no added day
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Return and repair.** S from E52, five-minute form, whole class: contrast jobs inside one workshop with two specialist businesses.
- **8 min. Trading without money.** The teacher holds three "wants cards" (invented): the baker holds bread and wants shoes, the cobbler holds shoes and wants a table, the carpenter holds a table and wants a hat. Nobody wants bread, so no pair can swap. Barter is a trade with no money. The class says why no one got what they wanted.
- **8 min. Add money.** The teacher gives each of the three a few counters as money: each sells for money and buys what they want. Pairs trace a fresh three-person trade from a card (pairs, three card sets); the teacher visits a rotating six. Paying gives up money, so someone gives up something (B).
- **12 min. Our class savings plan.** The teacher shows the made-up plan: $80 toward display supplies, a goal bar of four $20 boxes. Budget: money in, money out, money kept. Model 3 minutes; each child fills a ledger line (In $20, Out $0, Kept $20) in 5; partners read it to each other, 1 minute each; the class shades one box. Nothing is collected and no child says what they have of their own.
- **4 min. Say why money helps.** Partners finish "Money helps because ___ can ___," one minute each way; the teacher hears a rotating six.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E53.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the goal bar; each child holds a ledger. Tool: None. By hand: the trade trace, the ledger line, exit. Evidence: E53.

## Day 54

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Explain how hoping for profit leads sellers to choose what to sell and at what price
- note: short biography read: a local business owner and how they chose what to sell
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of B and AD (from E53 and the ledger), five-minute form, whole class; the other goes to a small group during Profit with counters.
- **12 min. Profit with counters.** The teacher models a candle stall (invented): a candle costs $3 to make and sells for $8, so sales of $8 less the cost leaves $5, the profit. Pairs use ten counters and two cost-and-price cards to find the profit on each (price counters in, cost counters out, the rest is profit). The teacher visits a rotating six; a side group of up to 6 takes AD for 5 minutes.
- **6 min. Hope shapes choices.** A fancier candle costs $14 and the stall asks $20; none sells. A seller hopes for profit, so she may change what she makes or what she asks. Expected profit may not come true (M).
- **10 min. A seller's choices.** The teacher reads the short account of Maggie Lena Walker aloud in about 4 minutes (original, from the sources in unit plan field 10, source to check). Pairs fill before, action, after boxes (6 minutes): what people lacked, what she chose to start, what changed. The teacher says only what the National Park Service pages support: Richmond then was a Jim-Crow city, she led for civil rights, and she urged members to pool their money and lend it among themselves. No other barrier is named.
- **4 min. Say it.** Each child finishes "A seller hopes to ___, so she chooses ___," aloud to a partner, then writes it. Spoken or dictated counts.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E54.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the account; pairs hold the boxes. Tool: None. By hand: the counters, the boxes, the sentence, exit. Evidence: the day 54 sentence, E54.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of F and M (from E54), five-minute form, whole class; the other goes to the Survey side table.
- **5 min. The survey table, modeled.** The teacher fills one row for the day 51 bakery: what it makes (a good), the three kinds of resource, one thing it might choose because it hopes to earn.
- **16 min. Survey two businesses.** Pairs read two business cards from the Local Production Record (the teacher reads each aloud first; maker and date are on each card) and fill two rows. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs, about 1.5 minutes each. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of F or M for 5 minutes. Fast pairs may do businesses three and four; this extension is the compressible part.
- **6 min. Share rows.** Groups of four (two pairs) each read one row; the listener names one resource, about 40 seconds each.
- **5 min. What might a seller choose?** For each business the class names one thing it might choose because it hopes to earn, said with "might"; two go on the board.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E55.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the baskets; pairs hold the survey rows. Tool: None. By hand: the rows, exit. Evidence: the survey rows (not scored), E55. Day 56 opens with: every business has to choose how to use what it has.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: barter, money, ledger, budget, profit, cost, price, sell. Frames: "Money helps because ___." "A seller hopes to ___, so ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation or a pointed card.
- **Access.** Read aloud, enlarge type, counters for hands-on work, speech or dictation, extra processing time inside an existing block. Score reasoning, not handwriting or adding speed.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe; counters allowed for the profit exit.
- **Reading load.** The teacher reads every card and the account; printed pages at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Explain why a seller might not make the highest-priced item, using the unsold candle.
