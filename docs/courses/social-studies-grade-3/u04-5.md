# Section 4.5: Our answer, a closing record and the assessment


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled map, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Day 86 writes one closing claim on the frame "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___" and revises it. Day 87 gives written feedback, revises the day 72 exit and reviews. Day 88 is the unit assessment, read aloud, from sources not seen before. Day 89 returns feedback and reteaches in groups. Exits on 86 and 87 are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen; day 88 records use the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 for each part (one scale per record). The day 86 exit pair is a made-up nation in a made-up place, said aloud to be invented.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D3.4.3-5 (days 86, 88).** "Use evidence to develop a claim answering the compelling question." Parts: use evidence; develop claim. A claim on 86 with a named detail from each of two records; the same on 88 (Q8) from A and B.
- **D4.1.3-5 (days 86, 88).** "Construct an argument using a claim and evidence from multiple sources." Parts: construct claim; support from multiple sources. A detail from each of two different sources, not two from one.
- **MA.3.T2.3, MA.3.T2.3a to d, D2.Geo.6.3-5, D2.His.12.3-5 (day 88).** Asked once each on the assessment; they are taught in 4.1 to 4.4 and owned there. This section runs the packet, scores it with the 2, 1, 0 look-fors and reteaches the letters.
- **Limits that bite here.** The packet's cases are not taught or reviewed; its records are read aloud once. Day 86 uses only the local nation's records. A claim may stress knowledge, settlement or governing, each with reasons. A and B prove my answer (H) is not evidence; two details from one source are one source (V). The day 87 exit and the day 88 packet are different items.
- **Connections.** The claim frame (u01-6, u02-6, u03-5); perspective, used on day 86 and then withdrawn (u02-4); grouped reteach and the packet routine (u02-6, u03-5); ELA grade 3 W.3.1, whose unit 3 runs now, uses the same one-sentence frame; unit 5 keeps it.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Claim, evidence, because, closing claim and reasoning (u01-6, u02-6, u03-5); big question, supporting question, matters to others, two readings, the supporting-question record (u01-1, u02-1); restate and perspective (u02-4); who made it, when and why (u01-5); the written cue then revision routine (u03-5); the assessment packet read aloud once and the grouped reteach (u02-6, u03-5).
- **New here.** Supported emphasis (a claim that stresses one supported thing, such as knowledge, settlement or governing, with reasons).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The 2, 1, 0 look-fors for each of Q1 to Q8 and for D1.1; that Q2 and Q3 are marked by pointing and naming; that spelling and handwriting are not scored; how to read each record once without explaining answers; how the day 89 groups form from the component letters.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q8 (D3.4.3-5 and D4.1.3-5: one claim from A and B, a different detail from each and how each supports it; H, V), both on the unit plan. Q1 to Q7 and D1.1 are asked on day 88 and owned in 4.1 to 4.4. Day 88 asks all eight prompts and the D1.1 revision once each.

**Supporting-question check (day 86 opening, practice, kept).** One line, read aloud: "Pick one of our five supporting questions. Name a word in it that can be read two ways, say the two ways, and say how its answer helps the big question." Look-fors: an idea named; two readings; a link. A missing idea or link gets written feedback and an unscored retry in the day 87 review.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's first five minutes open with that error's wrong example.
- **E86 (Reed River cards, invented for practice, a made-up town).** Card A: a map made by the Reed River Town Council (a made-up town, no Native nation invented) that marks the town's land along a river. Card B: an account by a museum, made by people outside the town, saying the town teaches river travel to its neighbours. In the data the exit is the claim (E86: "Write a claim about how the town uses its river.") and one detail from card A (E86b); the card B detail is a practice item (P86i). Answer: a claim such as the town shares its knowledge of the river with its neighbours; a detail from A, such as the town's land lies along the river. Wrong: the map and the account prove my answer (H); rivers are nice (x); card A is a map, a title only (H).
- **E87 (Aquinnah Review Locator, withheld from teaching).** A map built from the settlement paragraph of the Aquinnah History page alone: homeland shading across Noepe (approximate), and common-land markers at Gay Head Cliffs, Herring Creek and Lobsterville, with a label reading "the whole homeland" on the three markers (sources.csv U4I, U4J). "The label is wrong. Fix it and point to the homeland." Answer: the markers are named places the Tribe holds (common lands), not the whole homeland; point to the shading across the island. Wrong: the markers are the whole homeland (T).

**Redo bank (day 89, one fresh item per component; invented where marked, all fresh; real nations only by their own names).**
- Q1: a new card pair for two real nations not in the packet; name one way their homelands differ and say who governs each today (J, K, F).
- Q2: a new outline with three dots (a river, a bay, an island); write the three nations' names (A, S).
- Q3: a new map with a shaded lake area and a square; point to the homeland and to lands today (B, T).
- Q4 and Q6: Sandbar Camp (invented): each summer people net fish at a river mouth, then move inland; name the season and the activity, and say how the water and the practice explain the camp (C, L).
- Q5: a new card of a nation's own account of something it teaches or makes; name one contribution and the source (G, U).
- Q7: Harbor Race Records (invented): a rower's same-day drawing and a club history; ask two questions, one about each, and say primary or secondary for each (Q, D, M).
- Q8: two new cards from two different nations' pages; one claim with a different detail from each (H, V).
- D1.1: Park Display (invented): a display tells whose land a park is on; who else needs the answer, and why (R).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The question board is closed on day 86: its five answers are the evidence list (review of u02-6). The claim frame is used on 86 and the blank frame stays until unit 5; the teacher's example is not used. Perspective (restating another's view) is used on 86 and then withdrawn. Filled organizers are taken down on day 87; blank strips remain. The day 72 exit returns on 87 and the day 88 packet is read aloud once.
- **Key moments.** Tools: None. Day 88: the packet. Ideal instrument: one printed prompt per page with enlarged, patterned maps; spec: print fits. No key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** The teacher reads each prompt aloud; children answer on paper or at the side table; maps are hand-drawn, with patterns, in black and white.

## 6. Days

## Day 86


- **5 min. Retrieval and supporting-question check.** Children write the one-line check on a card (see field 3); the teacher scans. Missed E85 letters (Q, E) wait for day 87 or 89.
- **8 min. Select evidence.** The teacher models choosing one detail from each of the Local Homeland Record (the day 72 local nation card and, in the Northeast, the Penobscot River description) and the Local Contribution Record (the day 83 card). Only the choosing is shown, not a finished sentence.
- **10 min. Share supported emphases.** Pairs say a claim to one another out loud. Each partner restates the other's emphasis, knowledge, settlement or governing, without arguing (perspective, then withdrawn). The teacher hears a rotating six pairs; a record column names who was heard.
- **15 min. Closing claim and revision.** Each child writes the frame "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___" with a detail from each record, then revises one thing after hearing another emphasis. Scribe or dictation allowed.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E86.

Who holds what is carried: each child the blank frame card and two records; the teacher holds the board. Tool: None. By hand: the claim, the revision, the exit. Evidence: the supporting-question check; the claim; E86.

## Day 87


- **5 min. Feedback and revision.** The written feedback on the day 72 exit (does it name a group and a reason) is returned and read. Missed supporting-question checks get their written note and an unscored retry inside the first review round.
- **5 min. Importance revision.** Each child revises the day 72 exit in a different colour. This revised card is what D1.1 scores on day 88.
- **28 min. Review.** One minute to start, then four rounds of six minutes with a minute to move: the nations (names and the three-mark legend on a fresh map), the sort (primary or secondary for a question on fresh cards), the map (the legend trace and the chain strip) and what we gave and how we govern (contribution and government cards). Tables hold at most eight children; pairs use self-checking cards, answer face down. The teacher sits at one table each round and hears the six children whose exits show most gaps; a record column names them. Filled organizers are taken down; blank strips and the frame card stay.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E87.

Who holds what is carried: each child the day 72 card and the blank strips. Tool: None. By hand: the revision, the exit. Evidence: the revised day 72 card; E87. This is a fluency day, so no new text is drafted; the revision of the saved card is the unit plan's set task.

## Day 88


- **5 min. Directions and the records.** The teacher reads the directions once and reads each source once, about 60 words each: the regional set A and B, then C and D (a made-up regional map, the Narragansett records, a made-up Maple County sugar camp and the Menominee treaty and tribal history). Read aloud without explaining answers.
- **30 min. Eight unaided prompts.** Q1 to Q6 three minutes each, Q7 six, Q8 six. Each prompt is read aloud inside its item time. No student timer. Offer enlarged high-contrast maps with patterns, tactile markers, speech, dictation or a scribe.
- **5 min. Collect and check.** Papers are collected and the teacher checks that every response is recorded.

D1.1 is judged from the revised day 72 exit. A recorded gap in the day 72 record means the replacement was written in two minutes of day 86 and revised on 87. Tool: None. By hand: the packet. Evidence: the packet; the revised day 72 card. Keep the packet, its cases and its questions out of teaching and review.

## Day 89


- **5 min. Feedback.** Each child gets a short written note, with the group (A to V letter) if there is one, and the D1.1 feedback if it is still owed.
- **25 min. Grouped reteach.** At most four groups of at most eight, by the largest gaps, six minutes each with one minute to move; one diagnosed letter each; the groups not with the adult do written pair repairs. One child works with the adult beside the group if needed.
- **10 min. Fresh redo.** Each child does one new item from the redo bank on the component they missed (Q8 for those with H or V).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the groups, the letters and the bank. Tool: None. By hand: the redo. Evidence: the redo. Unit 5 has retrieval only.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 86, "What does each record show, and how do you know?"; day 87, "Which part do we still say two ways?"; day 89, "What would you do differently?"
- Written task: day 86, one claim sentence with a detail from each of two records, revised once; day 88, the packet.
- Judged by the teacher with look-fors per part: Q8 (1) a claim that answers the unit question; (2) a detail from A and a different one from B; (3) a link between each detail and the claim. Present, partly or missing as 2, 1, 0. Spelling and handwriting are not scored. The day 86 claim is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 86: the question board's five answers, the claim frame. Day 87: the day 72 exit and the sort, the nations, the map. Day 88: the whole unit, unaided. Day 89: the packet feedback.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 87).** Four partner rounds on self-checking cards, each pair explaining aloud once; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: claim, because, evidence, source, homeland, nation. Frame: "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." Tell a language gap from a content gap by allowing pointing plus a spoken explanation. Accept home language, then the English words.
- **Access.** Enlarged high-contrast maps with patterns; tactile markers; speech, dictation or a scribe; the reasoning stays the child's.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt and source once; a scribe; pointing and naming aloud are enough for Q2 and Q3.
- **Reading load.** Each record is about 60 words, read aloud; independent reading is captions at grade 2 to 3.
- **Extension, same standard.** Test a different supported claim: choose another emphasis and give a detail from each source.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the packet aloud and the child answers on paper; no group is needed and the redo is the child's own. Do not ask about the family's own nation, home or work. On day 88 the child may point and tell. **The last day of the unit (day 89):** the adult reads the note, the child redoes one item, and the unit is closed; nothing new is started.
- **One projector.** Show one prompt or one record at a time; children use paper copies.
