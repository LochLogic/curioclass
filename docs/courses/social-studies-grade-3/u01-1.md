# Section 1.1: Why our town matters to others

<!-- skel -->
Grade 3: Communities Then and Now. Unit 1, Where we live (days 1 to 24): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 2.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (1, 2). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Compelling question (frame): "Why is our town where it is?"
- Section 1 of 6 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain; the teacher reads every source and exit aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled drawing or a filled table, or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Every person in a case is a public person from a credited town page, or invented and said aloud to be made up.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 1, 2): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **D1.2.3-5** (days 2): Name the subject concepts tied to a big question that people can interpret in more than one way.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 1:** the teacher shows a photograph of a public landmark beside its symbol on the town map, then reads a short biography of a local person tied to that place. The person is no longer living, chosen from a free town page the teacher links and credits. If none is ready, use the fallback card, a Curio Class original, on Frederick Law Olmsted and the Back Bay Fens in Boston, each fact tied to a park service page.
> - **Co-written supporting questions, day 2:** Where are we on Earth (answered day 6)? What does our town map show (day 10)? How did land and water help place towns (day 15)? How have people changed this place (day 18)? Whose needs does a good location serve (day 20)?
<!-- /skel -->

- **D1.1.3-5, this section's share.** "Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults." Day 1 models it through a real person's action and its change. On day 2 each child names one other person or group and a use. Both days are practice. The scored version is the day 2 record, revised after written feedback on day 22 and asked as Q10.
- **D1.2.3-5, this section's share.** "Name the subject concepts tied to a big question that people can interpret in more than one way." On day 2 each child names a word in "a good place for a town" that people can mean in two ways, and keeps it apart from a map fact that has one reading. The same revised record is scored as Q11.
- **Limits that bite here.** "Our place" is the class's own town. No child reports on home, family, money, religion or how they came to live here. People in cases are a public person from a town page (no longer living) or invented and said aloud to be made up. A child may pass in talk. Wrong ideas are read from "someone might say" cards, never from a named child. Not here: a full biography, a list of source types, the other supporting questions' content.
- **Connections.** ss3-biography, day 1: the first biography read (the written biography is unit 7). ss1-leaders readiness, day 1, reteach day 2. ss2-country-report retrieval (a question and a source that could answer it), day 1, repair day 2. D1.3 and D1.4 return on day 20.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Leader, big question, supporting question, question board, "I wonder", the three questions (who made it, when, where made), Return and repair, the exit ticket. Grade 2's exit "mark, then say" becomes a quarter page the child writes, draws or says.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A compelling question has no one answer; supporting questions are smaller and each needs facts. "Matters to others" needs a person and a use, not "I like it". Two readings are both checkable against facts, while a fact such as a direction has one reading. How to choose the biography: read the whole free town page first; the person is no longer living, the page names a deed and what it changed, and the page is linked and credited. Day 1's check is an access observation, not a test.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q10 and Q11 (D1.1, D1.2), judged from each child's day 2 record after written feedback and revision on day 22 (unit plan field 5). Nothing from days 1 or 2 is scored.

**Evidence by day.** Day 1: the Jo page, the wonder card (one question and one source), E1, all on the one scale. Every exit is a quarter page read aloud once; a child may write a sentence, label a drawing or say it at the side table. "Invented for practice" is printed on every made-up card.

**Readiness items R1 to R3, day 1 (Jo Card, invented for practice; one ask each).** Read aloud: "Before, a public garden had no signs on its paths. Jo asked the gardeners where signs would help. Jo organized sign-making and put the signs up with adults. Afterwards, visitors found their way." Ask: What did Jo do as a leader? What changed? What action shows a helpful quality? Key: Jo listened and organized; the signs helped visitors find the paths; listening shows care or organizing shows responsibility. Wrong: "Jo was famous" or "Jo is nice" (H).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next opening uses that error's wrong example.
- **E1 (Sign Card, invented for practice).** "Rae lived by the old bridge. Rae asked neighbours for its stories. Rae put them on a sign at the bridge. What did Rae do? What changed at the bridge?" Answer: Rae gathered the stories and posted them; visitors can now learn the bridge's past. Wrong: "Rae lived by the bridge" or "Rae is kind" (H); "I like old bridges" (O). The data asks it as E1, E1b, one ask each; items.csv governs.
- **E2 (Bike Rack Card, invented for practice).** "Our school will choose the best spot for a new bike rack. Who besides the students might care where it goes? Why? Which word in 'the best spot' could two people read two ways?" Answer: another group with a use, such as the people who keep the doorway clear, or neighbours who walk past; "best" (nearest the door, or out of the walkers' way); both readings can be checked against a plan. Wrong: "I like bikes" with no other person (O); naming "north" or another direction as the word two people read two ways (J). The data asks it as E2, E2b, E2c, one ask each; items.csv governs.

## 6. Days

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding; assessment: readiness. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Open the compelling question "Why is our town where it is?" with a short biography of a person tied to the town's founding or a landmark
- note: unit 1's readiness check on grade 1 ss1-leaders (how a person made a change) is given in the last ten minutes of this day, after the hook; students who miss it get the one-lesson reteach in day 2; exit-only Sign Card, invented for practice, replaces the worked biography on the exit
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **6 min. Hook: a place and its symbol.** Show a photograph of a public landmark of our town beside its symbol on the town map. Read the maker and date aloud. Ask "What do you notice? What do you wonder?" Post the compelling question on the question board.
- **15 min. A person tied to the place.** Read the short biography aloud once (about 250 words; a free town page, linked, credited, a person no longer living; fallback: the Curio Class original card on Frederick Law Olmsted and the Back Bay Fens, each fact tied to the National Park Service page, source to check). Say who made the page, and when, before reading. Pairs read it again with the teacher's help and fill three boxes: who, where, who cares today. Two pairs share. Name the person as a leader if the page shows leading.
- **5 min. Wonder cards.** Each child writes one question about the person or place and names one thing that could answer it (the page, a map, someone to ask). This is the retrieval of ss2-country-report; mark it on the one scale. No child reads aloud unless they choose to.
- **10 min. Start-of-year check.** Items R1 to R3 (the Jo Card). One page, read aloud once. About 25 children write a sentence or label a drawing for about 7 minutes. Up to 5 children who prefer to speak answer at the side table, 1 minute each. The teacher records each child's explanation on the scale, not reading speed.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E1.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-1-2.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The biography boxes (who, where, who cares today) do not share E1's shape (what a person did, what changed). The Jo Card does, as the readiness check on grade 1; E1 is a new case, the Sign Card, not seen in the unit.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board; pairs hold the three boxes (who, where, who cares today). Tool: None. By hand: reading with help, writing, pointing, the wonder card, the exit. Evidence: the Jo page, the wonder card, E1 (all on the record sheet).

## Day 2

- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Say why the question matters to others, hear two readings of "a good place for a town", and write the supporting questions together
- note: one-lesson reteach of ss1-leaders for students day 1's readiness check named, in a small group; question records are practice; return written criterion feedback before independent revision on day 22

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Gap H. The teacher reads the library volunteer card (invented for practice): before, action and after on three strips. Children put the strips in order, name the action and its result, then explain a fresh card aloud to a partner. Whole class if more than a third missed Jo; otherwise a small group of up to 8 at the side table, hearing each pair for 1 minute. The children not in the group sort 8 draft question cards into "helps answer our big question" and "does not", in pairs. For the small group only, add ss2-country-report: match a question to a source and pull out one fact on leveled cards (5 of the 10 minutes).
- **9 min. Who else cares?** Model with a different question, "Where should the class garden go?" (invented): the teacher names two people who care and a use for each ("This matters to ___ because ___"). Pairs do the same for our big question. Each child writes box A of the day 2 record (one person or group and a use). Three minutes of the nine are writing.
- **8 min. Two readings.** On the garden question, the teacher shows two priority cards ("best means sunniest", "best means nearest the tap"; each can be checked on a plan) and one fact card ("The tap is east of the shed"; one reading, found by the arrow). Pairs sort four cards into fact or priority. Each child writes box B: one word in "a good place for a town" and two things it could mean. Three minutes are writing.
- **9 min. Co-write the supporting questions.** Where the sort was done in the first part, read the sorted cards aloud (2 minutes). Otherwise sort now (4 minutes). Then the class words the five supporting questions with the teacher: Where are we on Earth? What does our town map show? How did land and water help place towns? How have people changed this place? Whose needs does a good location serve? Each goes on the board with the day it is answered (6, 10, 15, 18, 20).
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E2.

- **Before class.** The teacher key for the exit cards is the separate piece key-exits-1-2.md; keep it out of the room until the exit is collected.
- **Shared shape.** The garden model (the word "best" read two ways, a tap and a shed) shares the shape of E2c; E2 is a new case, the Bike Rack Card, not seen in the unit.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board and the cards; pairs hold the strips and the sort. Tool: None. By hand: ordering strips, sorting cards, writing boxes A and B, the exit. Evidence: the day 2 record (practice, kept for written feedback before day 22), E2, the small-group notes.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 1, "What did the person do, and what changed?" and "What could answer your question?"; day 2, "Who else might care, and why?" and "What could 'good' mean to two people?"
- Written task, day 2: record boxes A and B, each one or two sentences or a labelled drawing, on the frame "This matters to ___ because ___." and "'___' could mean ___ or ___."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) a person or group other than the child, with a use; (2) one word read two ways, with both readings checkable; (3) the child's own words, not class wording copied. Mark each present, partial or missing, on the written cue returned before day 22.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 1 uses the retrieval of ss2-country-report (a question and a source). Day 2 repairs ss1-leaders and the readiness page. Day 2 reopens the question board and the day 1 biography page. No fluency work.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: question, matters, person, action, change, reading, mean. Frames: "This matters to ___ because ___." and "'___' could mean ___ or ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation, or a pointed card; repeat the idea in English.
- **Access.** Large print and tactile or raised strips; a communication aid or pointing is an answer. The three boxes can be filled by pointing to picture cards.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe writes the child's words without adding ideas; spelling and handwriting are not judged.
- **Reading load.** The teacher reads the biography aloud. The printed page a child reads is at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second person who might care, with a different use, or a second word with two readings.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: read the biography page, then fill before, action and after together. Ask "Who else might care why our town is here?" and write the answer. On day 2, talk through the garden cards, then ask what "good" could mean.
- **One projector.** Show the town map beside the photograph, the biography page and the question board. Children write on printed boxes; the screen never takes the place of a child's own mark.
