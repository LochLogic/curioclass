**Teacher notes for the sensitivity read:** use each nation's own names and public factual materials. The local nation's account establishes the local thread, not a generic land map or a child's identity. Present tribal lands do not contain all citizens, and ancestral homelands can overlap. If sources differ, name whose map and which period each describes; leave unsupported boundaries unresolved.

Each worked nation is one example, never its whole region. On 78 name forced removal and deaths plainly without graphic detail or reenactment. Distinguish the Cherokee Nation, the Eastern Band and other Cherokee nations; do not imply that only two exist. On 79 connect Menominee land and river or forest use without making origin teachings an archaeological claim. On 80 do not reproduce sacred mountains, stories, designs or ceremonies as student activities. On 81 discuss maritime livelihood without costumes, sacred-object replicas or a staged hunt.

On 83 contributions are continuing knowledge and work, not gifts owed to settlers. The biography credits Carol Dana's documented language work, without presenting an old directory as proof of her present role. A 2026 university account confirms that she teaches learners of different ages; describe those documented actions, not an imagined lesson or sacred knowledge. On 84 teach sovereignty as a nation's right to govern itself; nations existed before the United States, and government relationships have legal limits. Compare actual institutions without imposing a universal three-branch pattern. Specific jurisdiction disputes are outside this unit.

Oral histories can be primary for the telling and knowledge transmitted; whether a speaker witnessed an older event is a separate question. A contemporary pot cannot prove how an ancestral pot was used. Public availability does not make cultural knowledge unrestricted. Select only a permissible non-sacred excerpt, preserve its attribution and context, and do not have students impersonate its speaker. Do not ask a Native child to authenticate sources or speak for a nation.
