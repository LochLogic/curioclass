# Section 5.7 skeleton: First meetings and early colonies

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/5/s/5.7), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u05-7.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, map caption and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, an arrow, a filled chain or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record). Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". The trade in enslaved people is named plainly as part of the economy; no child is asked to act it out.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T5.5, days 113 and 116.** "Explain how sea trade and bartering helped build colonial Massachusetts's economy." Day 113 starts with barter and day 116 closes with the route record.
- **MA.3.T5.5a, day 113.** "Describe the fishing and shipbuilding industries." Taught: fish as food and cargo; yards, timber and workers; how a builder and a fisher depend on each other.
- **MA.3.T5.5b, day 114.** "Describe Atlantic and Caribbean trade, especially the Triangular Trade that sold enslaved Africans and moved goods made by slave labor." Taught on a simplified diagram, with what moved on each leg and who made the sugar.
- **MA.3.T5.5c, day 115.** "Describe how the seaport cities of New Bedford, Newburyport, Gloucester, Salem, and Boston developed." Taught with Salem and a second colony's harbor (New Amsterdam), as the unit plan decides; the other named ports return in unit 7.
- **D2.Geo.7.3-5, days 114 and 116.** "Explain how cultural and environmental features affect where people, goods and ideas spread and travel." Sheltered water and sea routes are environmental; trading skills and shared knowledge are cultural.
- **Limits that bite here.** The triangle is a simplified picture, not every voyage. Enslaved Africans are people carried and sold by force, never "cargo" or "goods"; the sugar was made by forced labor. No graphic detail.
- **Connections.** Barter and money (grade 3 unit 3 and day 53); harbor (grade 3 unit 1); map key and compass directions (days 94 and 116); slavery in every colony (5.6).
- **New here.** shipbuilding, fishing industry, cargo, port, Triangular Trade (a simplified picture of sea routes).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How a port grew from harbor, workers and trade together; why coins were scarce (to check) so goods paid for work; what the triangle shows and what it leaves out; how to say the trade in people plainly and calmly.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q6 (MA.3.T5.5, MA.3.T5.5c), Q7 (MA.3.T5.5b), and the judged rows for MA.3.T5.5a (the day 113 exit) and D2.Geo.7.3-5 (the day 116 record). Q6 uses A's captions for Port X and Port Y, and Q7 uses A's trade diagram. Teaching and exits do not use those names, so E116 below says Marlow Quay, not Port X, and day 114 draws its own triangle.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. One ask in each.
- **E113 (Port Work Card, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "How do Rafe and Wyn need each other? Wyn has no coins. How can Wyn pay Rafe?" Key: Rafe's boats let Wyn fish; Wyn pays with fish, barter; each needs the other. Wrong: each worked alone and nothing links them (AD); they cannot trade without coins (I).
- **E114 (route card; invented for practice).** "Who grew the indigo on Isle Corra? Where did the ship carry the indigo, from where to where?" Key: enslaved people, held there by force; from Isle Corra to Bristow. Wrong: paid workers who chose the job (T).
- **E115 (Two-Harbor Exit Map, from the unit plan; invented for practice).** "What does the water at each harbor give? What do the people at each harbor do? (Cask Bay has a fish market and fishers' boats; Reed Harbor has a boat yard and timber)" Key: sheltered water where ships can load; at Cask Bay people catch and sell fish, at Reed Harbor they build boats. Wrong: water alone built the town (W).
- **E116 (Route Card, from the unit plan; invented for practice; Marlow Quay, not Port X).** A sugar arrow runs north from Fennick Island to Marlow Quay and a cloth arrow runs back. "Write where the sugar sailed from and where it went." Key: from Fennick Island north to Marlow Quay. Wrong: reads the cloth arrow, or the arrow backward.

**Retained records (practice, not exits).** Day 113: a builder, a fisher and a seller linked on a fresh card. Day 116: a fresh documented route traced, with why goods and workers gathered at ports. Both revised on day 119.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The map key and compass directions (return on days 94 and 116): arrows with the tail at the start dot. (2) The harbor chain (harbor, work, trade), made on 113 and 115 and used on 116. (3) The simplified diagram on 114, labeled as a simplified network. (4) The barter table from day 113. (5) The question board: "Who worked and who benefited?" gets answers from 109 to 116. (6) The record folder with a written cue on each record; the 113 and 116 records are revised on day 119.
- **Key moment 1, day 114: the diagram beside a documented route.** Ideal instrument: a printed map with both drawn, so the picture and the real routes can be compared. Spec: print.
- **Key moment 2, day 116: the route.** Ideal instrument: tracing paper over a printed map, two marks (goods, people) in a legend. Spec: print and tracing paper.
- **A room with nothing.** Routes drawn on a teacher's map; tracing paper replaced by arrows in two colors.

## 6. Days

## Day 113


- **10 min. Return and repair (S from E112 5, day 53 retrieval 5).** First, S (5 min): name the action and purpose in a fresh invented petition detail. Second, retrieval (5 min) from the unit plan: "A fisher wants bread, but the baker does not want fish. How could money help?" Answer: money is accepted by everyone, so the wants need not match. If a few miss it, they revisit day 53's chart with the teacher in the pairs part, with the goods changed.
- **8 min. Fishing.** A labeled Curio Class summary of fishing in colonial New England (a museum or historical society page; source to check): fish caught, salted or dried, eaten and sent as cargo. Harbors on a map (harbor from grade 1 and 3).
- **8 min. Shipbuilding.** A shipyard builds boats from timber (sources.csv U5Z; crafts beyond that are unsourced and cut). Boats supply fishers and carriers; fishers and carriers supply work for the yard.
- **6 min. Barter.** Barter (a trade of goods or services with no money; from grade 3 unit 3). That coins were scarce is not on a page read; the made-up case gives a person with no coins. A made-up trace: Clare the sailmaker takes corn for a sail (invented for practice). Pairs trace one more exchange, no coins (I).
- **6 min. Link card.** Pairs link a builder, a fisher and a seller on a fresh invented card (AD). The teacher hears a rotating six pairs, named on the record. Practice; written cue after class; revised on day 119.
- **2 min. Exit.** E113.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the harbor map; each pair a link card. Tool: None. By hand: the trace, the link. Evidence: E113; the link card. Hand-off: tomorrow, the sea routes of the Atlantic and Caribbean.

## Day 114


- **10 min. Return and repair (AD 5 and I 5, from E113).** AD: a fresh invented card, a builder, a fisher and a seller. I: trace one fresh exchange of goods for goods. If few missed one, those children use the side table.
- **8 min. The triangle.** A simplified diagram of the Triangular Trade, drawn by us on a map, labeled "a simplified network" (not dossier A's diagram): New England, the Caribbean, Africa. Trace each leg with an arrow, tail at the start dot; say its compass direction. Ask what the picture leaves out.
- **8 min. Goods and people.** A sort: what moved on each leg. Fish and timber, sugar and cloth (to check) are goods. Enslaved Africans were people carried across the sea by force and sold (T). Said plainly, with no graphic detail and no one acting it out.
- **8 min. Who made the sugar.** Sugar was grown and made on Caribbean plantations by enslaved people, by forced labor (Salem maritime history, National Park Service; source to check). Fish and other goods from New England port towns also fed the trade (to check).
- **4 min. One triangle?** The simplified diagram beside a documented return route (the historians' caution that no trader is known to have sailed a full triangle, sources.csv U5AB): ships took many routes (U).
- **2 min. Exit.** E114.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the diagram; each child an arrow map. Tool: None. By hand: the arrows, the sort. Evidence: E114. This day carries the sensitivity read. Hand-off: tomorrow, the harbors where this work happened.

## Day 115


- **10 min. Return and repair (T from E114 10, or U 5).** If many missed T: ten minutes, goods and people sorted on a fresh invented route card. If many missed U: five minutes, the triangle beside a fresh documented route, then a five-minute retrieval of the harbor chain words. If few missed either, those children use the side table.
- **9 min. Salem.** A labeled summary of Salem's harbor, wharves and fish shipments (National Park Service; source to check). Fill the chain: what the water gives (a sheltered harbor), what the people do (build wharves, load, trade), what moves (fish, goods).
- **9 min. New Amsterdam.** The 1624 trading post in a harbor (National Park Service; source to check), in the Dutch colony on the day 94 map. The same chain, filled again.
- **10 min. Pairs.** A fresh invented port record, Tern Landing (not a harbor from the exits). Each pair fills the chain: harbor, work, trade, and says why workers were needed (W). The teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **2 min. Exit.** E115.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the chain; each pair a chain. Tool: None. By hand: the chain. Evidence: E115. Hand-off: tomorrow, tracing routes on a map.

## Day 116


- **10 min. Return and repair (W from E115).** If many missed W: ten minutes, harbor, labor and trade connected on a fresh invented port record. If few: five minutes at the side table while the rest start the map.
- **4 min. Map return.** Map key and compass directions (day 94): tail at the start dot; a legend with two marks, one for goods and one for people carried by force.
- **12 min. Trace a route.** A fresh documented route (fish shipments from a New England port to the Caribbean, sugar back; National Park Service Salem page, to check) traced on tracing paper over a printed map. Mark goods and people, name each leg's direction.
- **8 min. Record.** Each child writes why goods and workers gathered at ports: sea access (environment) and trading skills (culture), and that people were also moved by force. Practice; written cue after class; revised on day 119.
- **4 min. Share.** A rotating six read their lines aloud; the class adds one line to the question board: who worked and who benefited. If the day is shortened, drop the share and map return, trace for 8 minutes, record for 8, exit 2.
- **2 min. Exit.** E116.

Who holds what is carried: each child a traced map and a record. Tool: None. By hand: the route, the legend. Evidence: E116; the route record. Hand-off: tomorrow, using sources to answer the unit question.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 113, "How does one trade help another?"; day 114, "Who made the sugar, and how were they made to work?"; day 115, "What did the water give and what did the people do?"; day 116, "Why did goods and workers gather there?"
- Written task, days 113 and 116: the link card and the route record, each revised on day 119.
- Judged by the teacher with look-fors: (1) each kind of work linked to another; (2) barter named without coins; (3) forced movement named; (4) both sea access and trading skills named. Shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 113: money against barter (day 53). Day 114: the link card. Day 115: the diagram. Day 116: the harbor chain and the map key.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: harbor, barter, cargo, port, route, forced, sugar. Frames: "The builder needs ___ and the fisher needs ___." "___ grew because ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Large-print maps, color for the two marks, speech, pointing or a scribe, the chain filled by arrows.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a point, an arrow or a spoken answer; the six heard may include any child who needs to say it.
- **Reading load.** Cards are one to three short sentences, read aloud; the map and the chain carry most of the load.
- **Content notes.** Day 114 names the trade in people, forced labor and slavery. No graphic detail, no slurs, no role play. A stop card and the right to pass hold.
- **Extension, same standard.** A fresh port record the child writes, or a second route traced.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads each card aloud, traces the route with the child and writes the cue; day 114 is read calmly and nothing is acted out. Nothing in this section is the last day of the unit.
- **One projector.** Project the map and one card at a time; each child has a paper copy and tracing paper.
