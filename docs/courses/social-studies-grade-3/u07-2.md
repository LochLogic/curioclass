# Section 7.2: Interviews, photographs and the community timeline

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-3/7/s/7.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-3 u07-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and stem aloud once. Answers may be a sentence, a labeled drawing, a filled table or words said at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits and records are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the 2, 1, 0 scores belong to day 159 only. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice". Families choose the interview questions and any child may decline any question; no question asks about immigration status, money, religion or family make-up. The stop card and the right to pass hold.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T1.2, days 146 and 147.** "Research the origins and people of the town or city, including Native Peoples, colonial founders, and enslaved and free workers, and describe its population and immigrant groups now and in the past through interviews." Day 146 carries "describe ... through interviews": a speaker, the interview date and the time remembered, and one change and one thing that stayed. Day 147 carries the research of a photograph and one public tradition read through its community's own account.
- **D2.His.1.3-5, day 148.** "Create and use a sequence of related events to compare overlapping developments." Day 148 creates a sequence from dated cards, uses it to say what changed and what stayed, and compares two developments that ran at the same time.
- **Limits that bite here.** An interview is one person's memory, not everyone's. The supplied interview is the class source; no home access, extra adult or personal story is needed. No question about family make-up, status or money. A tradition is read through its own community's public account, with no sacred practice, no re-enactment and no required part. Holiday and symbol facts are not introduced. Unresolved local gaps are written down, never filled by invention.
- **Connections.** D4.1.3-5: source-sourcing on the interview and the photograph (146, 147). Unit 1 "shows, suggests, cannot tell" (147). Unit 5 timeline bands (148). D4.3.3-5: the interim answer written on 148 feeds the day 152 summary. Grade 2 interviews and the four topics of a move (146).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How a recording date differs from the remembered time (N). Why one memory or one image does not speak for everyone (D). How an overlap differs from a replacement (K). How to run the school interview round and the supplied transcript when a child has no interview at home. How to write a ten-second cue on 30 strips.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 (MA.3.T1.2), asked once on day 159 from cards B and C, which are used on no teaching day, exit or record. P0a (the day 146 and 147 records, revised) and the day 148 timeline (D2.His.1.3-5, revised) are scored beside it. Nothing here is self-marked.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E146 (Interview Dates Card, invented for practice).** "In 2015 a woman is recorded. She says: 'When I was a girl, we skated on the pond behind the school.'" Ask 1: "Was the skating in 2015 or earlier?" Ask 2: "Write one open question to ask her next about the pond." Key: earlier, because she says she was a girl; an open question about her memory. Wrong: "In 2015, because that is the date" (N); "Did you skate?", a yes or no question.
- **E147 (Procession Card, invented for practice).** "A photograph shows people walking down a street with banners. The label says only the year and the street." Ask 1: "What does the photograph need the community's own account to explain?" Ask 2: "Does it show that everyone in town joined?" Key: what the procession meant to the people in it; no, only the people in the picture. Wrong: "It shows everyone in town believes the same thing" (D); "Yes, everyone was there" (D).
- **E148 (Strip Card, invented for practice).** "A horse tram band runs 1905 to 1935. An electric tram band runs 1920 to 1950." Ask 1: "In which years did both trams run?" Ask 2: "Write one thing that changed and one that stayed." Key: 1920 to 1935; horse trams gave way to electric trams, and trams ran the whole time. Wrong: "1905 to 1950" (K); "Yes, the electric tram took its place" (K).

**Records (not exits).** Day 146: the interview record (speaker, interview date, time remembered, a change, what stayed). Day 147: the change-and-continuity record. Day 148: the observed strip (ferry, school, bridge) and the interim answer.

## 6. Days

## Day 146


- **5 min. Return and repair.** B and J (from E145): sort four fresh cards, chosen or forced, with a reason each; whole class if many, otherwise a small group of up to 6 while the rest begin the retrieval slip.
- **5 min. Retrieval task.** (From the unit plan's readiness list.) Each child writes: ask an open question about a public place long ago; what must come with the answer? Key: the speaker, the interview date and the time remembered. The teacher hears a rotating six; the record names who was heard. A miss: the teacher demonstrates a question and labels a sample answer before the interview work, and the gap is recorded.
- **12 min. The supplied interview.** The teacher models an open question and a follow-up (grade 2 interviews), then reads the supplied Local Interview transcript aloud (about 300 words; the transcript is checked against the recording first, source to check). Pairs fill the interview record: speaker, interview date, time remembered, one change, one thing that stayed important to people.
- **8 min. Practice.** Pairs take turns, 4 minutes each way. One reads a made-up recall card (invented for practice); the other asks one open question and one follow-up and writes the answer with the speaker and both dates. The teacher hears three pairs, six children; the record names them.
- **7 min. Choose questions.** The three offered questions go on a take-home sheet: What public place do you remember? What changed there? What stayed important to people? A child may replace or decline any. A child with no interview at home uses the supplied one; nothing is asked about home. The sheet says the adult writes the speaker and both dates.
- **3 min. Exit.** E146.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the question board; each child the record. Tool: None. By hand: the record and the take-home sheet. Evidence: E146 and the interview record (practice). Hand-off: tomorrow, photographs and a tradition; an interview done at home is brought, a supplied one is used.

## Day 147


- **4 min. Return and repair.** N (from E146), with D if many missed it: label both dates on a fresh recollection; the other letter to a small group.
- **5 min. Overlap model.** The unit plan's five-minute model on made-up bands, with new dates: a ferry, 1850 to 1880, and a school, opened 1860 (invented for practice). The teacher draws both on a ten-year strip; the class says what was going on at the same time. The model strip is withdrawn after day 148.
- **3 min. Interview contribution.** Each child writes one sentence from their own interview or the supplied one, with the speaker named. A child with neither uses a card. The records are collected.
- **8 min. Old photographs.** The teacher puts a local old photograph beside its label (maker, date, place); the class asks the three questions and sorts three statements into shows, suggests and cannot tell (unit 1). Pairs repeat on a second photograph.
- **9 min. A tradition and its community.** The teacher reads the public account that one community gives of one public tradition it keeps (Boston edition: a community page chosen at plan data; source to check), beside a dated photograph of it. Pairs fill two boxes: what the photograph shows; what only the account tells. No holiday or symbol facts are added. Nobody is asked to take part or to say what their family does.
- **8 min. Change-and-continuity record.** Each child writes, from the interview and one photograph: one thing that changed, one that stayed, and the source of each. Practice.
- **3 min. Exit.** E147.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the strip; each child the record. Tool: None. By hand: the sentence and record. Evidence: E147 and the change-and-continuity record. Hand-off: tomorrow, the class timeline.

## Day 148


- **4 min. Return and repair.** D (from E147): two fresh records, sort supported and unsupported claims; K joins if many missed it.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Unit 5 and unit 6 strips: equal gaps for equal years; ten-year marks; "overlap" said back.
- **8 min. The class timeline.** The wall strip from day 142 gets the cards of days 142 to 147 (the nation, the town, the sheet's date, the interview's remembered time, the photograph). The class says what changed and what stayed between two cards.
- **8 min. Observed strip.** Each child draws made-up bands on a printed strip, 1870 to 1920: a ferry, 1880 to 1910; a school, opened 1890; a bridge, opened 1900 (invented for practice). The strip shows what overlapped. Observed record, practice; written cue next.
- **8 min. What changed, what stayed.** Each child fills the continuity-change chart for the community from the wall strip. While they write, the teacher walks and writes a cue on each strip: about ten seconds a child for 30 children, about five minutes.
- **5 min. Interim answer.** Each child writes: "I think ___ helped make our community because the ___ shows ___." Words or a drawing, from the nation's account and the interview. Kept for day 152.
- **3 min. Exit.** E148.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the strip and the cue; each child the chart and answer. Tool: None. By hand: the strip, chart and answer. Evidence: E148, the strip and the interim answer. Hand-off: tomorrow, choosing a person who shaped the community.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 146, "What must come with an answer from a speaker?"; day 147, "What does the photograph show, and what does it need the community to tell?"; day 148, "What was going on at the same time?"

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: interview, speaker, recording, remembered, photograph, overlap, changed, stayed. Frames: "The speaker is ___. It was recorded on ___. She remembers ___." "The photograph shows ___. The account tells ___." "___ and ___ were both there in ___." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Enlarged photographs with a spoken description, a scribe for the record, the strip drawn with tape, extra time on 148.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each exit aloud; accept speech, pointing or a scribe; sources stay in view.
- **Reading load.** A child reads only labels, own notes and a 15-word stem; the teacher reads the transcript and accounts aloud.
- **Content notes.** The supplied interview and the tradition account are chosen to avoid sacred practice, graphic loss and slurs. The stop card and the right to pass hold.
- **Extension, same standard.** Test a second photograph against the interview on one point.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the three offered questions, lets the child choose, asks one and writes the speaker, both dates and the answer. If the family prefers, the supplied transcript is read instead. Nothing about home, status or money is asked. The adult helps draw the strip with a ruler on day 148.
- **One projector.** Project the photographs, transcript and strip; each child holds a paper copy of the record.
