# Section 5.1: Our big question and a timeline in years, decades and centuries


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled strip, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (one scale for each record); the unit plan's 2, 1, 0 scores belong to days 105 and 120. Every made-up card is said aloud to be made up and printed "invented for practice".

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D1.1.3-5 (day 90).** "Explain why the compelling question matters to others." Whole indicator: explain importance. The child says who else needs the answer, and why. The day 90 exit is saved, given written feedback and revised on day 119 before day 120 scores it.
- **D2.His.1.3-5 (days 91, 92).** "Create and use an ordered sequence to compare developments happening together." Parts: create; use; compare simultaneous developments. This section carries create and use, on a strip in years, decades and centuries. Comparing developments at the same time is first met on day 100 and practised on day 104 (section 5.4).
- **Limits that bite here.** One strip, 1500 to 1700, one mark for ten years; years only, no months. Equal space for equal years, never equal space for each event. Centuries are taught on the 1800s, 1900s and 2000s ("count the hundreds, then one more"; the numbering convention is source to check); the 1500s are met first in the day 92 exit, as a transfer. Day 90's hook uses one Seloy fact only (colonists settled at a Timucua town in 1565); every other Seloy fact waits for day 97 and the day 120 packet (rule 4). No child reports on home or family; retrieval uses made-up families.
- **Connections.** Grade 2 number line (2.MD.B.6), day 91; before and after from unit 4; C3 D1.4, supporting questions, day 90 (linked again on day 117).
- **New here.** colony and colonist (day 90); decade and century (day 91).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why equal years get equal space; counting tens; century numbers and why; that "colony" means a settlement far from the home country, said without judging anyone; that Native nations live today; that "share" can mean two things (use together, or each keep a part).

## 2. Where it sits


Before: unit 4 ends on day 89, a respond day; nothing is handed forward. After: section 5.2 opens day 93 with where the Atlantic and Europe are and why ships sailed west. Readiness: none; unit 5 has retrieval only, grade 2 ideas on day 90 (why people move) and day 91 (the class timeline), each in the first part of its day. Results: the day 90 record and exit go in the record folder for day 119; exits 91 and 92 go on the record sheet.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** D1.1.3-5, judged on day 120 from the revised day 90 exit (unit plan field 5). M1 (D2.His.1, day 105) and the day 119 timeline record belong to section 5.4, where the timeline is taught last.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. The teacher reads each stem aloud once.
- **E90 (Mill Creek Dock Card, invented for practice; it replaces the unit plan's Seloy exit, which would repeat the hook).** "Who besides the town clerk needs the answer? (the card says the clerk must decide whether to build the dock)" Key: a group the dock could touch, such as people who fish at the creek, with a reason in the child's words. Wrong: only our teacher needs it, or no one does (Y). A group with no reason is marked shown with help.
- **E91 (Hollis Cove Strip Card, invented for practice).** "Put both on your strip, one mark for ten years." Key: the library four marks right of the inn, forty years later. Wrong: the library placed in the next slot beside the inn, as if one step apart (A).
- **E92 (Voyage Dates Card, from the unit plan).** "A record dates two voyages to 1530 and 1550. Which century are both in?" Key: the sixteenth. Wrong: the fifteenth (AA).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The class wall strip, 1500 to 1700, ten-year marks: modelled by the teacher on day 91, used by pairs on their own paper strips on 91 and 92, then kept on the wall for the colonies from day 97; each child keeps a paper copy in the record folder. It is withdrawn on day 119, the blank frame staying. (2) The question board with five supporting questions, each with its answer day; answers are added from day 93. (3) The supporting-question record (review of units 1 and 2): question, a fact it needs, one idea read two ways, the link; the day 90 record is kept. (4) Return and repair, the first part of each day after an exit (review).
- **Key moment 1, day 91: equal spaces for equal years.** Ideal instrument: a long wall strip with movable dated cards and a paper strip for each pair, with ten-year marks. Spec: print fits; no tool. Gaps and asks: none (unit plan field 8 holds the supplies).
- **A room with nothing.** A strip drawn on rolled shelf paper, or a string with pegs, one mark each hand-span; children's strips folded from paper and marked with a ruler; cards cut from index paper.

## 6. Days

## Day 90


- **5 min. Retrieval (grade 2, why people move).** Ask the unit plan's prompt: "Why might a family move, and how is being made to move different?" Answer: people may seek work or safety; being forced to move removes choice. If many miss it, contrast two made-up families on cards, one choosing a new job and one ordered to leave, then ask again (1 minute to ask, 3 to contrast, 1 to retry). No child gives their own family's history.
- **7 min. Hook and the word colony.** Show the St. Augustine shore (image and credit to select) and read one fact aloud: in 1565 Spanish colonists settled at Seloy, a Timucua town (Florida Museum, unit field 10). Pairs talk for 2 minutes: how could settlement change life for people already there? Any child may pass. Teach colony in this context: a settlement far from the home country, which claims it. Say that Plymouth and the other places come in their turn. Write the big question on the board.
- **8 min. Co-write the board.** The class turns the unit plan's five prompts into supporting questions in the children's words: why sail west; whose account and map; what made cooperation possible; what undermined it; who worked and who benefited. Each gets its answer day (93; 95 to 96; 103; 106; 109 to 116). The teacher writes; the five stay on the wall.
- **4 min. Worked record (made up).** Big question: "Could skaters and walkers share the new path?" The teacher fills one supporting-question record aloud: a question, a fact it needs, one idea read two ways ("share": use the path together, or each keep a part), and the link. The same four boxes as units 1 and 2.
- **10 min. Each child's record.** Each child picks one board question and fills the four boxes. For the big question, "share" is read two ways: use the land together, or each keep a part. The teacher conferences with a rotating six, about a minute each; the record names who was heard.
- **4 min. Matters to others.** Show the wrong and right example of Y (field 4). Pairs say who else needs the answer to the big question, and why.
- **2 min. Exit.** E90 on a quarter page.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the board and the strip; each child the record folder. Tool: None. By hand: the record, the exit. Evidence: E90 (field 3); the record is kept for day 119.

## Day 91


- **10 min. Return and repair, with retrieval.** 5 minutes of retrieval (grade 2, the class timeline): "The record says beans were planted in May and picked in September. What happened first? Who could tell us about it?" Answer: planting; someone who was there. If many miss it, put two picture cards in order and ask again. 5 minutes for Y from E90: whole class if many; otherwise a small group at the side table while the rest do the retrieval card. If both are many, the retrieval repair takes all 10 and Y moves to the pairs part of day 91 at the side table.
- **4 min. Hook: two strips.** Five Alder Bay cards (invented for practice: market field 1520, first bridge 1570, mill 1590, school 1670). One strip spaces them evenly by event, one by years. Which strip shows how long between? Wrong example of A shown.
- **9 min. The strip.** The wall strip, 1500 to 1700, one mark for ten years. The teacher places the four cards, counting tens aloud; 1570 to 1590 is two tens, twenty years. Children copy the placement of two cards on their own strips. Link to the grade 2 number line.
- **7 min. Decades and centuries.** A decade is one mark, ten years; a century is ten decades, a hundred years. Count the hundreds, then one more: 1815 is in the nineteenth century, 1999 the twentieth, 2026 the twenty-first. Children say the century aloud for three year cards. Wrong example of AA shown.
- **8 min. Pairs.** Each pair places three Pell Harbor cards (invented: dock 1515, school 1545, bakery 1695) on its strip and says how many tens apart each pair of cards is. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs; the record names them.
- **2 min. Exit.** E91.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher the wall strip; each pair a paper strip. Tool: None. By hand: the strips and the exit. Evidence: E91.

## Day 92


- **10 min. Return and repair.** E91's wrong answers (A). Everyone builds a fresh scaled line from three Cobble Ford cards (invented: well 1525, mill 1555, gate 1675) and checks it against an answer strip. If few missed A, those children work at the side table and the rest begin round 1.
- **12 min. Round 1: place and measure.** Pairs deal six dated cards (invented, 1500 to 1700, none from the exit sets), place them on their strips, and say which came first and how many tens apart for two chosen pairs of cards. The partner checks against a face-down answer card. The teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **8 min. Round 2: centuries.** Pairs sort year cards from the 1700s, 1800s, 1900s and 2000s onto a century mat and say the century aloud. No 1500s card is used.
- **8 min. Round 3: a card for a partner.** Each child writes a year card and its century on the back; the partner places it. This round is the compressible block: if the day is shortened, drop it and the day runs 32 minutes.
- **2 min. Exit.** E92.

No new content is taught and nothing is drafted. Who holds what is carried: each pair its strip and deck; the wall strip stays up. Tool: None. By hand: the strips and the exit. Evidence: E92.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 90, "Who else needs this answer, and why?"; day 91, "How many tens are between these two cards?"; day 92, "How do you know which century?"
- Written task, day 90: one supporting-question record, kept for day 119.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: (1) a question taken from the board; (2) a fact it needs; (3) one idea named with two readings; (4) a link to the big question. Present, with help or not yet seen. Spelling and handwriting are not scored.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 90: grade 2 reasons to move and forced moves. Day 91: grade 2 order of events and the number line. Day 92: the strips from day 91 and the century mat.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 92).** Three partner rounds on self-checking cards, answers face down, each pair explaining once aloud; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: colony, timeline, decade, century, before, after. Frames: "___ is ___ tens after ___." "___ is in the ___ century." A home-language word beside each is welcome.
- **Access.** Strips enlarged with raised tick marks for the ten-year marks; a colour band for each hundred years; speech, pointing, dictation or a scribe; the reasoning stays the child's.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a point on the strip for E91; a sentence, a spoken answer or a drawn picture for E90.
- **Reading load.** Cards hold a year and four to six words; the teacher reads the fact on day 90. Nothing is read alone beyond cards.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third town's cards on a second strip: say which events came in the same decade.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult rolls out a paper strip on the table and uses the same cards; the child says each answer aloud. On day 90 the adult asks the hook question and does not ask about the family's own moves. Nothing in this section is the last day of the unit.
- **One projector.** Show the wall strip and one card at a time; children place cards on their own paper strips.
