# Section 4.4: What the nations give and how they govern


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, passage and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a filled table, a labelled strip or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes: five to repair or retrieve, eight to model, ten supported, fifteen on their own, two for the exit. Day 83 teaches contributions from the nation near the school and one short biography. Day 84 teaches sovereignty and how tribal governments work with state and federal governments. Day 85 applies sourcing and corroboration to a new pair. Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. The Northeast edition uses the Penobscot Nation's pages as the local set; elsewhere the local nation's pages replace them. Invented cases are said aloud to be made up.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.3.T2.3d (day 83).** "Describe contributions of a tribal group near the school, such as foods, crafts, words, place names, and useful knowledge." Part: describe one contribution of the nation near the school, in its own account, with the source named; then compare across nations.
- **MA.3.T2.3 (day 84).** "Explain the diversity of Native Peoples, past and present, in Massachusetts and New England." Part: diversity of governing, today; a tribal government compared with other governments, never a town department.
- **D2.His.12.3-5 (day 85).** "Generate questions about multiple historical sources and their relationship to events or developments." Parts: generate questions; connect sources to events, by agreement and difference, and as before and after.
- **Limits that bite here.** Contributions are continuing knowledge and work, named as the nation's own, in the present tense where it continues (O); they are not gifts owed to settlers and not interchangeable across nations (G, U). Sovereignty is a nation's right to govern itself; nations existed before the United States; relations with other governments have legal limits that stay with the teacher; no specific jurisdiction dispute. No three-branch pattern is imposed on any nation. Biography: only what the 2026 university news page states about Carol A. Dana, no imagined lesson, no sacred knowledge. Two copies of one account are one source (N). A question about looks is not a historical question (Q); asking is not corroborating (E). No timeline strip: before and after only.
- **Connections.** Sources and who made them, primary and secondary (4.1); chain strip and the legend (4.2); Cherokee and Navajo cases (4.3); level of government, power and duty, branch, constitution (u02-1, u02-2, u02-5); biography and the before, action, after boxes (u01-1, u02-3). Unit 5 days 91 and 92 take the timeline; days 95, 103 and 108 take source sorting.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Biography, the before, action, after boxes (u01-1); level of government, power, duty, government, nation (u02-1, u02-2); legislature, constitution, judiciary and the Menominee constitution (u02-5, as a reminder only); primary and secondary, source question, relationship strip (4.1); account and retelling, and the grade 2 corroboration in a child's words: two tellers who each saw it agree, and an account and its copy are one teller (grade 2 u05-4).
- **New here.** Contribution, sovereignty, tribal government, federal government, independent account, the before-and-after cards.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the unit plan's "introduced here" corroboration is review of grade 2 (the ledger's wording is kept); why a contribution needs a nation and a source; how to say sovereignty and its legal limits at grade 3 depth, naming only what the page says; why the biography states only the page's facts; why two sources may agree without proving a cause.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q5 (MA.3.T2.3d: one contribution from B and the source named; G, U) and, with 4.1, Q7 (D2.His.12: two questions about D's two passages and primary or secondary for each; Q, D, M). The unit plan's note that day 85 "introduces" corroboration is review in this ladder; Q7 asks questions and sourcing, not corroboration.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's first five minutes open with that error's wrong example.
- **E83 (Contribution Card).** A short summary of the Penobscot Nation curriculum page's Homeland unit: students study maps with place names in Penobscot, explore what the names mean, and use them to find their way along the river (undated; sources.csv U4B). Two asks: "What contribution does the Nation's page document?" and "Name the source that shows it." Answer: sharing Penobscot place names and meanings in its school lessons so students learn to find their way along the river; the Penobscot Nation's own curriculum page. Wrong: all nations gave the same things, such as corn (G); Native peoples shared nothing, so no source (U); they shared names long ago, and that is all (O); a book as the source (G).
- **E84 (Eastern Band Exit Notice).** A fresh summary of paragraphs 1 and 3 of the 16 April 2025 report: leaders of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians visit state officials in Raleigh to speak for the tribe's priorities (sources.csv U4M). "What shows the Eastern Band is acting as a government?" Answer: its leaders speak for its people to state officials, government to government. Does not prove a law changed. Wrong: it is a department of the town or state (F).
- **E85 (Boat Photo and Harbor Note, invented for practice).** A photograph taken at a dock by a visitor, dated 2 May, shows two boats. A harbor note by the harbor office, dated 4 May, counts five boats. Two asks: "Ask one question about both sources." and "Which came first, the photograph or the note?" Answer: a question about the boats, such as why more boats were at the dock later; the photograph, dated 2 May, shows two boats before the note, dated 4 May, counts five, and neither proves why. Wrong: which picture is prettier (Q); I asked why, so I know why (E); the note came first (x).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The question board: "What contributions continue?" is answered on day 83, "Who governs today?" on day 84 and "How can sources help us know?" on day 85; each answer has a one-line link to the big question. The relationship strip (4.1) is used on day 85 and kept. The before, action, after boxes (u01-1) are used for the biography on day 83. The before-and-after cards are new on day 85 and kept to day 87; they prepare the timeline of unit 5 and are not a timeline strip.
- **Key moments.** Tools: None. Day 85 sort: ideal instrument, printed event cards and a two-column mat; spec: print fits. No key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** The teacher writes the before and after boxes on the board; the cards are cut from paper; passages are read aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 83


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of C and L from E82 (carried from 4.3), five-minute form.
- **8 min. Local contribution and biography.** The teacher reads a card in our words from the Penobscot Nation's River description page on what the Nation shares, named as the Nation's own, present tense (sources.csv U4A; the Homeland place-names page is withheld for the exit). Then a short original biography of Carol A. Dana, Penobscot, from the 2026 university news page, stating only what it states about her teaching today. The before, action, after boxes are filled on the board.
- **10 min. Compare contributions.** Pairs sort four cards into a table of kind (food, craft, words, place names, knowledge) with nation and source: the River description, the Dana page, the Makah cedar-bark basket and the Menominee wild rice, the last two reread from day 74. The linked answer: "What contributions continue?" and one line on how that helps the big question. Teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **15 min. Describe with sources.** Each child writes two cards: "The ___ Nation ___. The source is ___." and one sentence about a contribution that continues today. Scribe allowed.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E83.

Who holds what is carried: each child the table and two cards. Tool: None. By hand: the cards, the sentence, the exit. Evidence: the cards; E83.

## Day 84


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of G and O from E83, five-minute form.
- **8 min. Sovereignty.** The teacher says it once: "sovereignty is a nation's right to govern itself" and that nations existed before the United States and still govern. A card from the Eastern Band's Government page says who leads and decides today (sources.csv U4L). Relations with state and federal governments are named at one level of detail: governments talk to one another; legal limits exist and the teacher holds them.
- **10 min. Compare governments.** A table of the Eastern Band (the page), the Navajo Nation (day 80 card), our town (u02-2) and our state (u02-5): who leads, who decides, who it speaks for. No pattern is imposed. Linked answer: "Who governs today?" and its line to the big question. Teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **15 min. Explain authority.** Each child fills a strip: tribal government, state government, federal government, each with who it speaks for, and writes: "A tribal government is a government, not a town department, because ___."
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E84.

Who holds what is carried: each child the table and the strip. Tool: None. By hand: the strip, the sentence, the exit. Evidence: the strip; E84.

## Day 85


- **5 min. Return and repair.** F from E84, five-minute form, and the E83 leftovers at a side table.
- **8 min. Corroboration.** The teacher models with Bridge Day, an invented dated photograph and a later club history (invented for practice): ask what the history is secondary for and what the photograph is primary for; two tellers who each saw it agree; a copy is the same teller. A question connects each source to the event; asking does not check.
- **10 min. Compare sources.** Pairs read two accounts of the removal from the day 78 cards, the Cherokee Nation's and the Eastern Band's own pages. They ask one question of the pair, mark what agrees and what differs, and say whether the makers are separate. Teacher hears a rotating six pairs.
- **15 min. Before-and-after cards.** Pairs lay six event cards from days 78 and 84 sources before or after one another (no dates drawn, no strip) and each child writes "___ came before ___" and one question the cards raise. Linked answer: "How can sources help us know?"
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E85.

Who holds what is carried: each child the relationship strip and six cards. Tool: None. By hand: the sentence, the question, the exit. Evidence: the sentence; E85. The unit plan sets written feedback on E85, to be returned before day 86 where possible.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 83, "Whose contribution is it, and how do we know?"; day 84, "Why is it a government and not a town department?"; day 85, "What do these two sources agree on, and what could only one tell?"
- Written task: day 83, two contribution cards and one sentence; day 85, one before-and-after sentence and one question.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors on the cards: (1) a nation named; (2) a source named; (3) present tense where the nation's account says it continues. On day 85: (1) a question about the event; (2) a before and after said in words; (3) no claim that one source proves a cause. Marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: contribution, sovereignty, tribal, government, federal, agree, differ, before, after. Frames: "The ___ Nation ___. The source is ___." "The source shows ___, so ___." Accept home language with a pointed card, then the English word.
- **Access.** Enlarged cards; tactile markers on the two-column mat; read aloud; speech, dictation or a scribe; extra processing time inside an existing block.
- **Check accommodations.** Read exits aloud once; a scribe; pointing plus a spoken because.
- **Reading load.** Cards are 40 to 60 words, read aloud; independent reading is captions at grade 2 to 3.
- **Extension, same standard.** Name a contribution on a card that the card does not list and say what source would show it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the cards; the child fills the table and says "The Nation ___; the source is ___." No question about the family's own nation, language or work. For day 85 the adult lays two cards and asks "What do they agree on?"
- **One projector.** One card or table at a time; children hold paper copies.
