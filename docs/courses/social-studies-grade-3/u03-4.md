# Section 3.4: Trade: how places depend on each other


Grade 3 children are early readers and writers. Printed stems are short and plain, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads every card, source and prompt aloud once. An answer may be a sentence, a labelled drawing, a filled table or spoken at the side table. Each day runs 40 minutes. Teaching cases are invented and said aloud to be made up (origin-label cards, a lime grower and a snack maker, a hardware shop and a fish stall); exit-only cases are a tea card, a coffee grower and roaster, a locator map with a trader card, and two market cards. Two sources are real: the Local Production Record (our summary of two public local pages) and the Techiman Traders Record (our summary of a newspaper report, 24 June 2023). Exits are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.Eco.14.3-5, this section's share.** "Explain growing dependence between nations through trade." Parts: explain trade; connect increasing interdependence (days 61, 62, 63, 64). Trade moves goods and money; places come to rely on each other more as orders grow, and each side gets and gives something.
- **D3.4.3-5, this section's share.** "Use evidence to develop an answer to the inquiry." Part: use evidence (day 64): compare what two records show about how our town and a market town get what they need; the claim is written on day 65.
- **Limits that bite here.** No ranking of countries as advanced or backward; no claim that every people bartered. Techiman is a present-day market city, not a timeless village; no population figure is used (if one is added it is a count of residents from the Ghana Statistical Service 2021 census, source to check). Present-day Ghana is not the medieval empire called Ghana, which lay farther northwest and belongs to grade 6; the plan names the difference and teaches nothing about the empire. A dated report supports only its period and observations. One market is not all of Ghana (J). Traders, producers, transport workers and buyers act for themselves; nobody is assigned an occupation by who they are; no Ghanaian child is asked to check the account. The Record shows both traders as the article reports them and does not decide that all traders agree. No percent and no trade figures. No Since Time Immemorial content.
- **Connections.** D2.Eco.5 and D2.Eco.1: trade uses money (day 53) and choice (days 56, 57). Map-key: the legend trace from unit 1 is used again on days 61 and 63. The claim frame from units 1 and 2 supports day 64's model; day 65 uses it. Savings check-in on day 61.
- **Review, not new (the ledger's words).** Trade, import and export, and the trade arrow from where it is made to where it is sold (review of social-studies-grade-2 u04-2); country report: land and water, resources and work, people today (review of social-studies-grade-2 u07-1); the legend trace and shows, suggests, cannot tell (review of social-studies-grade-3 u01-3); claim, evidence, because, and the claim frame (u01-6 and u02-6); who made it, when and why (u01-5); the question board and Return and repair (u02-1, u03-1); the class savings ledger (u03-2); goods and services (u03-1).
- **New here.** Origin label (what the card says about the country the good comes from); depend on each other (each side needs what the other gives); interdependence (said once, with the plain phrase first); market town.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Dependence runs both ways and grows with orders, not with an arrow alone (P). Imports do not end local work: a bought input becomes local work (I). The Record's source: a Curio Class summary of one newspaper article of 24 June 2023, labelled "reported in the Daily Graphic, 24 June 2023"; Kusi Appiah's concerns (waste, food on bare ground, broken streetlights, security) are reported, not quoted; Jennifer Owusu-Mensah's account of customers is used only as the article gives it; at most one sentence is quoted, in quotation marks with the link. The Techiman Assembly pages are not used on days 63 to 65 (seen as indexed text only). How to answer "Why does Ghana share a name with an old empire?": it does, and they are different places and times.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q7 (D2.Eco.14: from packet C, how Lee's workshop and a Canadian supplier depend more on each other as orders grow). It is on the unit plan; packet C stays out of teaching.

**Exit questions.** Each is read aloud once and marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E61 (Origin Label Card, invented for practice).** "Origin Label Card, invented. Trace the tea from its country to our town." "Origin Label Card. How does the shop depend on the grower?" Answer: An arrow from Sri Lanka to our town, drawn with the legend. The shop needs the tea to serve its customers. Wrong: "The arrow goes the other way" (x); "Now nobody here works" (I).
- **E62 (Cocoa Card, invented for practice, with a made-up bean dryer).** "Cocoa Card, invented. What does the grower need from the chocolate maker?" "Cocoa Card. What does the chocolate maker need from the grower?" Answer: The bean dryer, or a buyer who pays for the beans. The cocoa beans, or money for the dryer. Wrong: "The beans" (P); "The grower stops growing" (I).
- **E63 (Ghana Exit Locator, invented for practice).** "Ghana Exit Locator. Which shape is the country Ghana today?" "Ghana Exit Locator. How do you know?" Answer: Shape A, the one on the coast. It touches the sea on the coast, as Ghana does on the locator map. Wrong: "The old empire shape" (R); "It has the same name" (R). Exit box extra (practice): "Trader Card, invented. Name one way she earns or spends." Answer: She earns when she sells to cooks; she spends when she buys from the farmer.
- **E64 (Marsh End and Dunmore cards, invented for practice).** "Marsh End and Dunmore cards. Write one likeness, with a detail from each." Answer: Both set aside space for trading: Marsh End has covered stalls and Dunmore has shared bays. Wrong: "Both are markets" (K); "All markets have covered stalls" (J).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Map-key (legend and arrows) is used again on days 61 and 63, then rests. The savings ledger returns on day 61 (In $20, Out $0, Kept $60). The question board gets its answer for day 64. The claim frame is shown on day 64 (a teacher example on an invented pair) and withdrawn on day 65. The Local Production Record (from day 55) and the Techiman Traders Record (day 63) are the two sources of days 64 and 65. Shows, suggests, cannot tell hears the Record.
- **Key moment 1, day 61: the world map and origin cards.** Ideal instrument: a printed world map for each pair with a legend and arrows, and cards to place. Spec: print fits; 15 pairs each have a map, so no map is shared.
- **Key moment 2, day 63: the Techiman Traders Record.** Ideal instrument: the printed Record, read aloud in two chunks, beside a West Africa locator map. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** A wall map, string for arrows and sticky notes for cards; the teacher reads the Record aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 61


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of H and AB (from E60), five-minute form, whole class; O goes to the side table during Map work.
- **5 min. Retrieval: trade.** Grade 2 retrieval, said to a partner at the map: "A fictional town buys rice grown in another country. Trace the route. Why might people trade?" Goods move from grower to buyer to meet needs. A miss: trace one model arrow and a fresh route, five minutes, in the Map work side table.
- **8 min. Read an origin label.** Three origin-label cards (invented for practice; a good and a country, no brand) are read: what it is, where it was made, who made the card. The teacher traces one on the map, using the legend trace: "the map shows ___, I can find it because the legend says ___."
- **10 min. Map work.** Pairs place three cards on their world map and draw an arrow from each country to our town, using the legend. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs. A side table of up to 6 meets the teacher for the retrieval miss or O, five minutes.
- **5 min. Made here or from far away.** The class sorts items from the Local Production Record (made here) beside the origin cards (from far away).
- **4 min. Savings check-in.** The third $20 goes in the made-up ledger: In $20, Out $0, Kept $60; the goal bar has three boxes.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E61.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the legend trace; pairs hold maps and cards; each child holds a ledger. Tool: None. By hand: the map, the sort, the ledger line, exit. Evidence: E61.

## Day 62


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of P and I (from E61), five-minute form, whole class; the other goes to the side table during Two-way arrows.
- **8 min. A dated order history.** The teacher shows a made-up history for a lime grower in Mexico and a snack maker in our state (invented), dated: Year 1, a few orders; Year 2, many. The class says who relies on whom, and whether more or less than in Year 1.
- **10 min. Two-way arrows.** Pairs complete a diagram: goods go one way, money the other, and each side needs the other (pairs, one card set each). They say what changes if a delivery is late (P). The teacher visits a rotating six. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of P or I for 5 minutes.
- **8 min. Local work stays.** The imported limes become a local snack the maker sells; imported things do not end local work (I). Pairs trace one more input.
- **6 min. Prices.** When there are fewer limes or more orders, a seller may choose a higher price; buyers may buy less, so a seller cannot charge just anything. No graphs.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E62.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the history; pairs hold the diagram. Tool: None. By hand: the diagram, exit. Evidence: E62. Day 63 gives a present-day market town.

## Day 63


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of I and P (from E62), five-minute form, whole class.
- **4 min. Retrieval: a country report.** Grade 2: "Name one thing a country report tells you about a place": where it is, who lives there, how people work. Partners, one minute each way; the teacher hears a rotating six. A miss takes the three-part frame (place, people, work) at the side table.
- **5 min. Which Ghana?** On the West Africa locator map, the class finds present-day Ghana on the coast, using the legend. One sentence: a different empire with the same name lay farther northwest long ago; grade 6 studies it. The class does not study it today (R).
- **14 min. The Techiman Traders Record.** Who made it, when and why: a Curio Class summary of a newspaper report of 24 June 2023, labelled "reported in the Daily Graphic, 24 June 2023" (3 minutes). Chunk 1, read aloud (2): Techiman as a present-day market city and a trader's account of customers. A table of who is named, what they do, earn or spend (4). Chunk 2, read aloud (2): a trader's concerns about trading conditions. A table of what is reported (3). Not every trader is said to agree.
- **5 min. Earn and spend.** Pairs name one way a person in the Record earns or spends, with "The Record shows ___, so ___." The teacher visits a rotating six. A side table of up to 6 takes one of: the other letter from E62, or the country-report miss (5 minutes).
- **4 min. Say it.** "The Record shows ___, so ___. It does not say ___."
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E63.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the Record and the map; pairs hold the tables. Tool: None. By hand: the tables, the sentence, exit. Evidence: E63. The sensitivity read (unit plan field 10) happens before this day.

## Day 64


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The more-missed of R and J (from E63), five-minute form, whole class; the other goes to the side table during Compare the records.
- **7 min. A two-source model.** The teacher models on an invented pair: Card 1, a hardware shop orders nails from a factory in the next state; Card 2, a fish stall sells what boats bring in the morning. "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___ and the ___ shows ___." Both show people getting what they need; one relies on far places and one on near ones.
- **12 min. Compare the records.** Pairs fill a table on the Local Production Record and the Techiman Traders Record: how each gets what it needs; same; different; cannot tell. The teacher visits a rotating six pairs. A side table of up to 6 takes the other of R or J for 5 minutes.
- **6 min. Share.** Groups of four (two pairs): each pair reads one same and one different, about 40 seconds each; the listener says the detail back.
- **7 min. Limit the claim.** The class says what each source cannot tell: a dated report supports only its time; one market is not all of Ghana; one record of two workshops is not all of our town (J). The board answer for day 64 is added. Day 65 gives the sentence.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E64.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the frame; pairs hold the tables. Tool: None. By hand: the table, exit. Evidence: the tables (practice), E64.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 61, "What does the map show, and how do you know?"; day 62, "Who relies on whom, and more or less than in Year 1?"; day 63, "What does the Record show, and what does it not say?"; day 64, "What is the same and different about these two places?"
- Written task: day 64's comparison table; one spoken claim with two details. The written sentence is day 65.
- Look-fors on the table: (1) a detail from each source; (2) one same and one different; (3) a "cannot tell" where the source is silent. Present, partly or missing.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: trade, depend on each other, origin, label, market, trader, earn, spend. Frames: "The Record shows ___, so ___." "___ needs ___ from ___." Accept home language, drawing with dictation or a pointed card.
- **Access.** Read aloud, enlarge maps, string for arrows, speech or dictation, extra processing time inside an existing block. Score reasoning, not handwriting.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud once; a scribe; pointing on the map counts.
- **Reading load.** The teacher reads every card and the Record aloud; printed pages at most 350 words, no sentence over 15 words, so the Record is about 220 words (ours, untested).
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict what a delay changes for each side of a made-up trade, and say what the source cannot tell.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The adult reads the Record aloud and the child fills the table; use a printed world map. Do not ask about the family's own shopping, origins or work.
- **One projector.** Show the world map, the locator map and the Record enlarged; children use paper copies.
