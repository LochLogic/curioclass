## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why is our town where it is?"
- **Hook, day 1:** the teacher shows a photograph of a public landmark beside its symbol on the town map, then reads a short biography of a local person tied to that place. The person is no longer living, chosen from a free town page the teacher links and credits. If none is ready, use the fallback card, a Curio Class original, on Frederick Law Olmsted and the Back Bay Fens in Boston, each fact tied to a park service page.
- **Co-written supporting questions, day 2:** Where are we on Earth (answered day 6)? What does our town map show (day 10)? How did land and water help place towns (day 15)? How have people changed this place (day 18)? Whose needs does a good location serve (day 20)?
- **Closing claim, day 21:** students answer the compelling question from the Local Current Map and the Local Historical Map, naming a physical feature, a human choice and evidence from both. Maps alone do not prove why people chose a place.
- **Teaching moves:** the teacher models who made a source, when and why (day 19), where the class also compares a map with a photograph, and says claims aloud before writing. Students choose their own evidence on days 20 and 21.


The local C3 file holds identifiers only. The paraphrases above come from the publisher PDF, pages 23 to 24, 40 to 43, 54 and 59.
- **Limits:** "our place" means the class's own town, state and region. Massachusetts and New England are worked examples, not the case every student answers.  Not in this unit: degrees of latitude, fractional scale bars, a full biography, a list of source types or an action project. Day 10 adds a short second map of a given invented place, so both parts of D2.Geo.1 are taught before Q2.
- **Models carried:** map key and directions are tied to symbols on day 7 and reused on day 18. Scale is tied to whole bars on day 8 and reused on day 22. Latitude and longitude are named as lines on days 4 and 5. "Region" moves from climate areas to groups of states on day 11. Claim, evidence and reasoning is modeled on day 19; the teacher's frame is withdrawn on day 21.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Continent, ocean and hemisphere labels are interchangeable. Wrong: southern is a continent. | Location categories are confused. If many, sort continent, ocean and hemisphere labels on a fresh globe, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| B | Color alone gives map meaning. Wrong: every blue mark is a river. Right: check the key. | Symbols need a key. If many, match new symbols to a key, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| C | The top of the page is always north. Wrong: ignore the arrow. Right: follow it even on turned paper. | Direction is assumed. If many, turn paper and compass together, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| D | Scale bars mean footsteps. Wrong: three bars means three steps. Right: count map bar lengths. | Map distance is confused with walking. If many, repeat a paper strip along a fresh route, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| E | Latitude alone decides climate. Wrong: every equatorial place is wet. Right: rainfall also differs. | One cause replaces several. If many, compare rain cards at the same latitude, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| F | Region, state and landform names are interchangeable. Wrong: a state is a mountain range. | Geographic categories are mixed up. If many, overlay political and physical maps and sort labels, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| G | A visible feature proves an unsupported conclusion. Wrong: water proves why every resident came. | Observation becomes speculation. If many, sort claims into shows, suggests and unknown, ten minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| H | A name or title explains a contribution. Wrong: Jo was famous. Right: name the action and change. | Contribution lacks an action. If many, use field 4 before, action and after cards, ten minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| I | Unmarked land on a map was empty. | Map omissions erase people. If many, compare a dated map with a nation-authored account of the same place, ten minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| J | Every disagreement is just opinion. Wrong: north is optional. Right: evidence limits interpretations. | Facts and priorities are confused. If many, sort one mapped fact and two supported priorities, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| K | A claim needs no relevant source details. Wrong: two titles alone prove it. | Citations replace evidence. If many, link a distinct detail from each of two sources to a claim, ten minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| L | A grid cell is read in the wrong order. Wrong: B4 for column D, row 2. Right: D2. | Grid conventions are unsecured. If many, rebuild labeled columns and rows and locate a new cell, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| M | Native nations exist only in the past. | Continuing presence is erased. If many, match past and present accounts from the same nation, ten minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| N | All Native nations have identical practices. | Distinct nations are generalized. If many, compare two nations' own public land-care accounts, ten minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| O | An unrelated response fully answers an inquiry question. | Importance and question links are missing. If many, link an affected group or needed fact to a supporting answer, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| P | Listing features explains a place relationship. | Description replaces connection. If many, connect a fresh map feature to a photograph and explain their spatial relationship, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| Q | People never modify land. | Human choices are omitted. If many, compare a dated before-and-after land-care pair and name the documented action, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| R | A new development ends everything that came before. | Change erases continuity. If many, mark a change and a continuing feature on a fresh dated pair, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |
| S | Naming some states completes a whole-region map. | Regional membership is incomplete. If many, use the region key to check each state and place missing labels, five minutes. | From memory; anticipated error, not measured. |

Many means more than a third. The teacher notes must explain that maps select and distort, that bar counts are whole-number estimates, that direct sunlight is not the same as the seasons, that culture means changing practices and not fixed traits, and that seeing a feature is not knowing why people chose a place. The five-region convention does not mean everyone in a region is alike. The earlier science leans K-ESS3-3 and 2-ESS2-2 show up in map work. The later 3-ESS2-2 and W.3.1 work is bridged here with rain cards and spoken-then-written claims, not assumed.
