# Course frame: Social and Emotional Learning, Grades 3 to 5

Course `social-and-emotional-learning-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `sel-3-5`), grades 3, 4 and 5, one band course taught over three school years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the pilot's shape (docs/courses/social-and-emotional-learning-grades-k-to-2/frame.md and its fix report); the scope and sequence sets the ordered days and school-day positions later, one year at a time.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 18, six a year (STANDARD section 3): units 1 to 6 in grade 3, 7 to 12 in grade 4, 13 to 18 in grade 5, numbered through the band so a seam or a check can name one unit without a year beside it. The Year column of the Units table names the year. Each year teaches all 40 sub-skills of the five competencies (self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making), grade 3 on school-day cases, grade 4 adding a second person's view, grade 5 (the owner year) adding a cost or a pressure, often the pressure of change before middle school (STANDARD section 3, depth ladder). Each year's units follow the order and the unit notes of the progression rows (data/progressions/sel.csv).
- **Days.** 192 sessions: 64 a year for three years, two sessions a week over 32 weeks, 30 minutes a session in grades 3 to 5 (STANDARD section 2), so 60 minutes a week. Each year is 60 sessions in six units and 4 for its mid-year and year-end course-level checks, each check with its respond session. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (the unit's Check session) and its Respond session; no unit runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. Sizes follow Desk q-fix-sel-1 (STANDARD section 2, "How the sessions add up"): a three-group unit is 11 (3 groups of Understanding, Fluency, Application, then Check and Respond) and a two-group unit is 10 (2 groups of Understanding, two Fluency, Application, then Check and Respond). The course-level check after a unit is that unit's Check session and its respond is that unit's Respond session (frame-write, "After-core courses"): the 30-minute session holds the unit's end-of-unit page and at most two sampled written items from earlier units, which the 20-minute K to 2 session could not, so no unit loses a session to the checks. In the Units table the unit before a check shows its teaching sessions only (an 11 shows 9, a 10 shows 8) and the check rows hold the other 2. Grade 3 keeps q-fix-sel-1 exactly: four units of 11 (1, 3, 5, 6) and two of 10 (2, 4). Grades 4 and 5 use the move STANDARD section 2 allows (a session moved between units, the year still 64): their mid-year check must follow unit 3 at sessions 29 and 30 (week 15), because weeks 9, 10, 12 and 16 hold core checks in grade 4 and weeks 10, 11, 16 and 17 in grade 5, so units 1 to 3 of those years are all two-group units of 10, units 4 to 6 are three-group units of 11, and one of them takes the moved session as a twelfth (grade 4 unit 12, grade 5 unit 17), a second Application session for its project. So grade 4 runs 10, 10, 10, 11, 11, 12 and grade 5 10, 10, 10, 11, 12, 11. Course-level checks fall after units 2 and 5 (grade 3: sessions 20 and 21, weeks 10 and 11; 52 and 53, weeks 26 and 27), 9 and 11 (grade 4: sessions 29 and 30, week 15; 51 and 52, week 26) and 15 and 17 (grade 5: sessions 29 and 30, week 15; 52 and 53, weeks 26 and 27), in weeks with no core course-level check for that grade (Course-level checks). Short path, one session a week (32 a year, STANDARD C2): every unit and group kept, fewer minutes a unit, never fewer units. A three-group unit is 5 (3 sessions where each group's Understanding and Fluency share one, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond together); a two-group unit is 6 (2 shared, a second rehearsal per group, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond). A two-group unit may also run as 5 (2 shared, one second rehearsal, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond), and a three-group unit as 6 (its 5 plus one Fluency session). Grade 3: 5, 6, 5, 6, 5, 5, so the checks fall in weeks 11 and 27. Grade 4: 5, 5, 5, 6, 5, 6 (units 7, 8 and 9 the five-session two-group form; units 10 and 12 six), so the mid-year check falls in week 15 and the year-end check in week 26. Grade 5: 5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 5 (units 13, 14 and 15 the five-session two-group form; units 16 and 17 six), so the checks fall in weeks 15 and 27. Every short-path check week is clear of that grade's core check weeks (Course-level checks); clashes with other after-core courses are the lead's calendar pass. On the short path the shared Check and Respond session of the unit before a check is the course-level check and its respond (32 a year in every grade). Readiness: every row assumes the same sub-skill one year before. Grade 3's rows assume the grade 2 owner rows, taught in SEL K to 2 (units 13 to 18) the year before; grades 4 and 5 assume this course's year before. So each unit opens with retrieval inside the session opening, never a readiness check. A "Given:" readiness item is used only where a school left out the earlier unit (Leave-out, below; STANDARD section 5.6). A class that did not have SEL K to 2 the year before (a new school, a school that starts the course in grade 3) uses the Leave-out bridge in each grade 3 unit: the first Understanding session teaches the grade 2 form of each card in its first ten minutes before the grade 3 step-up.
- **Inside the 30 minutes.** Every session has one opening of about 5 minutes (the calm-down routine and one retrieval item or returning skill with its card, together, never two openings), about 20 minutes of teaching and rehearsal, and about 5 minutes to mark the class sheet, collect cards and put away materials. An Understanding session names and models at most three new sub-skills, each a named strategy with three or four steps on one card and a reason (STANDARD section 3), and every student tries each once; the Fluency sessions after it are partner rehearsal. Units with a build, a timed practice or a project (6, 7, 8, 13, 17, 18) take set-up and clean-up from the 20 minutes, and their unit plans say how. A tracker that runs longer than a session (a week of a calm-down plan, a two-week log, a three-week practice record, a four-week goal) is kept between sessions as the unit's carry-out cue, starts on the unit's first session and ends before its Check session (the progression's pace note).
- **Standard.** CASEL 5 competencies, as written in data/standards/casel-5.txt (40 sub-skills, the five competency names and the codes CASEL.Competency.N). Grade 5 owns every code for the band; grade 3 and grade 4 rows begin each one (~ in the rows), so their Owned standards cells are empty and What students learn names each begun code with its owner year. The seventh column holds, for SEL, the unit's one link to a core course of the same year (bar part 9 item 9): a lean the teacher may use, not a strand. Library: AASL.V.D.1 is taught with SM3 (owner unit 14), AASL.V.D.2 with SA8 (unit 14) and AASL.III.D.2 with RS4 (unit 17) in every SEL band (library STANDARD section 4, Desk q-fix-library-1; data/progressions/library-excluded.csv names them placed in SEL). The three owner rows name the codes in their standard cells and these units' Owned standards name them (scripts/progression.py accepts the three for SEL, owned once in each band's last year, 2026-11-26). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against APPROACH's kinds of day, the lens (docs/agents/lenses/sel.md) and the bar's part 9; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add.
- **Base text.** None. Every story, scene card, step card, data card and page is Curio Class's own, with invented children and families. The ways of working from other places (units 3, 9 and 17, RS3) are invented partners whose one custom the unit plan takes from a cited cultural or education source, in our own words, shown as that one child's way, never a whole culture's; the kinds are named in those units so the three years do not repeat each other, the K to 2 picks (Ampe, acchi muite hoi) or PE grades 3 to 5's picks (jianzi and Pilolo, PE grade 4 unit 16). The unit plan names the place and its cited source, and the sensitivity read checks it before the plan is written. The one historical case, school segregation struck down by the Supreme Court in 1954 (unit 15), is taken from the National Archives' page on Brown v. Board of Education (https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/brown-v-board-of-education, the decision and its date, a settled fact; opened by the Codex read 2026-11-25) and the Brown Foundation's page (https://www.brownvboard.org/brown-foundation-story, for the families who brought the case). The page tells the 1954 ruling apart from the slower work of carrying it out; the sensitivity agent reads both before the unit plan.
- **Shared steps with health and PE.** The talk turn card, calm-down steps, I-statement frame, goal steps card and refusal steps use the one set of words in STANDARD section 7 ("Shared steps"), each grown by a step for grades 3 to 5: the talk turn card to four steps (listen, say back, ask a question, add your idea), the calm-down steps to four (stop, breathe, name it, choose), the I-statement to four parts ("I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___."), the goal steps card to four (goal, steps, block and plan, check), stop-think-choose to four (stop, list two or three choices, think what each would cause, choose and check) and the problem size meter to four levels (tiny, small, medium, big). Where health 3 to 5 teaches a step first in a year, the SEL unit opens with it as retrieval where the school runs health, and teaches it itself where it does not (Cross-subject, Health). Health 3 to 5 now uses the same cards: the I-statement ending "I need ___", the talk turn card's first three steps as its listening steps, SEL's refusal card with "a reason is optional" added, and the goal steps card's rungs (2026-11-25 fix pass).
- **Leave-out.** A school may leave out a unit (STANDARD section 5.6). Where a later unit uses a card the left-out unit grew, that unit's first Understanding session teaches the card's grades 3 to 5 form itself in its first ten minutes, in place of the retrieval opening and part of that session's rehearsal, and claims none of the left-out unit's sub-skills: the feelings faces strength scale, the four calm-down steps and four-step stop-think-choose (unit 1; first needed by units 2, 3 and 5, then 7, 8, 10 and 13); the four-step talk turn card, four-part I-statement and four-level problem size meter (unit 4; units 5 and 6, then 7 and 11); the four-step goal steps card (unit 6; units 8 and 14); the three care steps (unit 3; units 10 and 16); the refusal card and the upstander plan (unit 5; units 11, 16 and 17); the three-step focus plan with its try-again step (unit 6; units 8 and 14); the grade 4 team working agreement (unit 7; unit 8's first Understanding session sets a minimal agreement, how we choose together and how we share jobs, in its first ten minutes, and later grade 4 team tasks use it). In grades 4 and 5 the unit plan checks whether the class was taught each card it uses (the year before, or earlier this year); where it was, a left-out unit needs only its retrieval item moved to the nearest earlier year taught, and where it was not, the receiving unit uses the bridge above (STANDARD section 5.6). A bridge never restores the left-out unit's standards claim.
- **Day kinds.** APPROACH "Kinds of day", with GLOSSARY's teaching-day meanings: Understanding (the strategy shown on an invented case, its steps named on a card with the reason, every student tries it once), Fluency (the steps rehearsed in pairs or small groups until quick, the card held by pairs; this is most of each group's second and third session, and in grades 3 to 5 it is spoken and acted, not drills on paper), Application (the steps used on a new scene card or in a planned class moment, the card withdrawn; drafting a plan or a proposal is Application), Check (the end-of-unit assessment: an unseen scene page and a watched rehearsal from an unseen card, against look-fors), Respond (a short reteach for the step not yet seen, and the rehearsal again). Every session rehearses; no session is posters and discussion alone. Each year is taught a new way, not the same lessons again: grade 3 names the strategies and uses them on school-day cases, grade 4 adds a teammate's or a second person's view, grade 5 adds a cost, a crowd's pull or a coming change.
- **Evidence.** A class sheet per unit, the step marked "seen" or "not yet" on the day it was seen. It records a step, not a rating of the child; no behavior rating of any child is collected (STANDARD C). It is the teacher's working paper and is thrown away at the end of its unit (APPROACH, Daily evidence); a course-level check keeps its own sheet only until its respond session. Feelings in a check are a character's, never the student's own. Private pages (SA7 plans, SA9 interest logs, a student's own identity web) stay with the student, checked only that the steps were followed. End-of-unit evidence in every unit: an unseen scene page (three to five items on invented cases, read aloud on request, answered in a sentence or by circling) taken by the whole class in the Check session; and a watched rehearsal from an unseen card, marked across the unit's last Application session and the Check session, pairs not yet seen going first in the Check (no score from a session where a skill is first taught). Plans and records a unit builds (a calm-down plan card, a goal plan, a proposal) are seen in Application, with written feedback in that session and a revision before the Check, never scored on the day they are first made. On the short path the page sits in the shared Check and Respond session and the rehearsal is marked in the Application session. Any student may do a rehearsal with the teacher alone, in writing or with a card, not in front of the class (APPROACH, supports).
- **Evidence base.** No study has tried this course; it is never called evidence-based or rated. The course page carries the logic model (C16) and this line (bar part 9 item 8; APPROACH, "Where the evidence is thin").
- **Carry it out and families.** Each unit names one cue the teacher uses outside the session ("Carry it out" in What students learn) and one family activity, sent in the unit's family note. Family notes are the only translated pages, in English and Spanish (STANDARD section 2); student pages are English only for now. A school may leave out a unit; its family note and the course page then name the left-out sub-skill ids the course does not claim that year (STANDARD section 5.6).
- **Fidelity and before you teach.** A paper fidelity checklist per session (did it run as planned, which part was cut) and a "before you teach" note opening each unit's teacher page: the skill as an adult uses it and one thing to model. Not training, and not claimed as such (bar part 9 items 11 and 13).
- **Equity.** Families of every kind appear in model pages and cases, LGBTQ families included; no student is asked about a family or culture by name, or asked to teach or explain their own culture. SA6 (snap judgments) is checking one's own guess on invented cases, the wrong guess resting on a neutral trait, never blame of a group. Student voice in class and school life (a routine change in unit 6, a class improvement in unit 11, a proposal to the principal or student council in unit 17), with the analysis of causes left to social studies (bar part 9 item 12).
- **Devices and paper path.** No device for students, and no owned code needs one. Every session runs on the paper path: step cards, scene cards, data cards, planners, practice records and class charts, printed or drawn by hand. Any online tool a later unit plan adds has a paper path beside it and needs no student account.
- **Tools and costs.** Kept all band: the feelings faces chart with its 1 to 5 strength scale, the calm-down plan card, stop-think-choose (four steps), the talk turn card (four steps), the I-statement frame (four parts), the problem size meter (four levels) and the goal steps card (four steps), each grown in grade 3 and reused in grades 4 and 5. Each unit names its tools, what Curio Class writes and a provisional cost for a class of 30: printing about $9 to $10 a unit, about $55 to $60 a year, plus small supplies named in units 6, 7, 13, 17 and 18 (about $3 to $10 each) and $6 once for six sand timers where the room has none. No supply ceiling is set for SEL (For the lead 8); these are planning figures, not quotes.
- **Safety.** A paper tower kept on desks, books stacked low (unit 6); child-safe scissors and tape in a group build (unit 7); body tools (stretches, slow breaths) done standing beside desks with room to reach (units 1 and 13); a class improvement inside the school with no ladders, no cleaning chemicals and gloves for any pick-up (unit 17); a community problem acted on inside the school grounds on ground the teacher has checked, with gloves, no glass, sharp or medical items, or off site only on a school-run trip under the school's field-trip policy (unit 18). Each such unit names its hazards and controls. Guides the safety read uses: for grounds and outdoor tasks, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission's Public Playground Safety Handbook (https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/325.pdf, title page read 2026-11-25 by the K to 2 frame read); for the pick-up and any trip, the school's own grounds and field-trip policy, plus one state education department's field-trip guidance the safety agent finds and links.
- **Disclosure.** Asking for help, calm-down, friendship, teasing, refusal and change units can bring a disclosure. Every unit follows health's steps (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"): the school's reporting route and the counselor. Health owns body safety, substances, puberty, relationship health and mental health topics; SEL never teaches them.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Before units 3, 9 and 17 (an invented partner's custom) the agent studies the cited source the unit plan takes the custom from and checks the partner is shown as one child's way; before units 2, 9 and 18 (identity webs, strengths from language and culture) it studies an education association's published guidance on families and cultures in the classroom; before unit 15 (school segregation, the history of Black Americans' fight for equal schools) it studies the National Archives' Brown v. Board page and the social studies grade 5 frame's unit 9 sources, and checks the case is taught with dignity and as settled fact; in units 13, 16 and 18 (moving, a friend moving away, a new school) it checks no student is asked about their own move or loss. Units 1, 6, 7, 13, 17 and 18 also get the safety agent's read. The Spanish family notes get a translation agent's read (For the lead 7). The course-level checks get the answer-key agent's read.

## Course-level checks

Each year has a mid-year check and a year-end check. Each is the Check session of the unit it follows, widened: the unit's own end-of-unit page and watched rehearsal (so the unit is checked there, not twice), plus at most two sampled items from the year's earlier units on new cases, written only, in one 30-minute session (the page holds five items at most; a sampled step is never a second rehearsal, and rehearsal sampling stays in later retrieval openings and Application sessions; pairs not yet seen in the unit's last Application session are watched first, and the scope budgets the rest); its Respond session is the unit's Respond session, reteaching the steps the sheet shows "not yet" across the unit and the sampled items. They sit after units 2 and 5 (grade 3), 9 and 11 (grade 4), 15 and 17 (grade 5), so none falls in a week with a core course-level check for that grade (grade 3: 9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28, 30 to 32; grade 4: 8 to 10, 12, 16, 19, 21, 23, 27, 30 to 32; grade 5: 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29 to 32; docs/courses/CALENDAR.md). Unit sheets are thrown away at the end of their unit (Evidence); a check's sheet is kept only until its respond session, then thrown away.
