## What a good ELD lesson does

- **States its language goal.** One key language use (narrate, inform, explain, argue), the forms that carry it (a
  sentence pattern, a set of words, a way of linking ideas) and the content it is used on.
- **Uses the grade's own content.** The text, task or investigation comes from the core unit the class is in that
  month. Help is added (pictures, a frame, a reread, talk first); the content is not swapped for an easier one.
- **Teaches a form directly.** The teacher shows the form in the text, names it, and the class tries it, then uses
  it in talk, then in writing.
- **Talks before it writes.** Every lesson has structured talk with a partner or small group, with frames, before
  students write.
- **Sets the work at levels.** The same task at three entry points (levels 1 and 2, 3 and 4, 5 and 6), with notes for
  newcomers, long-term English learners and English learners with disabilities. Help is withdrawn on a named day as students grow.
- **Uses the home language as a help.** Cognates, a comparison of how the home language does the same thing, talk in
  the home language first where it helps.

## Kinds of day in ELD (the GLOSSARY kinds)

| Kind | What it is in ELD |
|---|---|
| Understanding | Meet the content and the language goal: the text or task, the form shown in it, named and explained. |
| Fluency | Practise the form and the words until they come easily: oral rehearsal, sentence building, word work. |
| Application | Use the form in talk and then in writing on the content, at the student's level. |
| Check | The end-of-unit assessment: a listening or reading part, a talk task and a short writing task on new content of the same kind, scored against look-fors for the language goal. |
| Respond | Reteach the forms the checks showed were not yet secure; rehearse again. |

## Daily evidence

The day's evidence is **a short spoken or written sample of the day's form on the day's content**: one or two
sentences said to the teacher or a partner, or written on an exit ticket, seen against two or three look-fors (did
the student use the form; was the meaning clear). The teacher's paper sheet marks "seen / not yet" for each student
to plan the Respond day. A student self-check of the language goal (California map 3.5) is kept by the student.
No scores are stored (promise 2).

## What ELD adds to the unit and section plan fields

Proposed, to be tried on the first ELD unit plans (after the gate):

- Header: days by kind; minutes; the host unit (course and unit) the content comes from.
- Unit field 2 (standards, limits, and what is carried): the ELD standard ids the unit plans to (our own words for
  each, both modes named, starting from the planning-only data/standards/wida-2020-words.txt); the key language use; the forms and words taught; the limits (what this unit leaves to a
  later unit or cluster); what is carried (frames, word cards) and the day each is withdrawn.
- Unit field 3 (where it sits): builds on (the same standard and key language use in an earlier unit this year or in
  the year before, never skipping a grade, handoff strong or weak; where a key language use first appears in a
  content standard, at ELD-SS.1.Argue, ELD-MA.2-3.Explain, ELD-MA.2-3.Argue, ELD-SC.2-3.Argue, ELD-SS.2-3.Explain and
  ELD-LA.4-5.Argue, it builds on the year before's SI row for that key language use and that year's row in the same
  content standard; a kindergarten unit with no earlier unit on its ids builds on nothing earlier and says so); leads
  to; comes back in; the host core unit and, for LA, the ELA rows it pairs with (STANDARD section 5); and the
  two-line note the ELD teacher can share with the host teacher: the content used and the language taught (bar item
  14).
- Unit field 4 (readiness): a short talk or writing task on the forms the unit builds on; the reteach is the form
  modelled again.
- Unit field 5 (end-of-unit assessment): an interpretive part (listening to or reading a new grade-level text of
  the same kind), then a talk task and a writing task at each entry point, with look-fors and two sample answers per
  level, and the accommodations line (UNIT-PLAN field 5). Says how a student moves to the next entry point. Prompts
  follow "Sensitive ground" below.
- Unit field 6 (error register): common errors in the forms taught, with why they happen (often transfer from the
  home language) and a remedy (California map 5.27).
- Unit field 7 (response allocation): the talk task and the writing task are judged by the teacher against the
  look-fors, so nothing is marked by hands up. Respond-day groups come from the sheet's "not yet" cells by form and by
  entry point, at most four groups; the others rehearse in pairs from the frame. Marking time is stated in minutes.
- Unit field 8 (tools and supplies): word cards, frames and the model text by title and record; no product or
  program names; the setting (a mixed group in one room, or a pull-out group).
- Unit field 9 (family note): in English and Spanish: the language goal in plain words, the frame the class uses, and
  one thing to try at home in any language the family chooses. For a unit whose host text is about migration, war or
  loss, the note previews the topic and says how to ask for the other text; a left-out unit's note names the ids not
  claimed that year (the swap and leave-out rule, STANDARD.md section 3; Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-eld-1).
- Unit field 10 (sources): what was read and how much, what is from memory, no claim of effect, and a line pointing
  to the course logic model (C16).
- Section field 1 (standards and practices): the section's share of the unit's ELD standard ids (our own words) and
  the limits that bite here; before you teach: the form explained for the teacher, and a note on how Spanish does the
  same thing (other home languages: see "Home languages" below); a line on telling a language need apart from a
  learning disability; any sensitive ground the section touches, with the steps for when a student tells you
  something ("Sensitive ground" below).
- Section field 2 (where it sits): what comes just before and just after in the unit; the readiness items it uses;
  where its own results go (the sheet's "not yet" cells feed the Respond day).
- Section field 3 (checks): the numbers of the unit assessment items this section owns; the day's evidence as above,
  with look-fors per level.
- Section field 4 (expected errors): each as a wrong sentence beside a right one, the day it is shown, and the five
  to ten minute response if many made it. Errors are never marked as faults of the home language; transfer is named
  as a reason.
- Section field 5 (what is carried, and instruments): the model sentence or passage, who holds it, and the day the
  frame is withdrawn; each key moment with its ideal instrument and the spec judged against it; what a room with
  nothing does (the frame on paper, the model read aloud).
- Section field 6 (days): one row a day, led by the day's language goal in student words; kind of day; how it opens;
  the content read or done; the rest of the row as UNIT-PLAN section field 6.
- Section field 7: the talk routine (named, the same each week), the frames per level, the writing task. Prompts
  follow "Sensitive ground" below.
- Section field 8 (practice that returns): one earlier form, named, used again for three minutes on new content.
- Section field 9 (supports and extension): the three entry points; newcomer notes; long-term English learner notes;
  notes for English learners with disabilities (access, as UNIT-PLAN section field 9); home language helps; check
  accommodations; reading load (how much a student reads alone, and what is read aloud or heard again); extension on
  the same standard (a harder grade-level text or a further change of audience on the same ids, never the next
  cluster's).
- Section field 10 (two rooms): a parent teaching one child at the table (the model sentence read together, the
  child says it back, the adult writes it, talk in the home language first); one projector (the model text on screen,
  the frame on the board, the talk routine in pairs).
- Section field 11 (not in this section): what is left to the host course (the content test), to ELA, or to the
  school's placement.
- Across the unit (bar part 10, items 9 to 14, from docs/RUBRIC-ELD.md): the key language use taught openly from
  a model text first; at least one change of audience or purpose; both modes in every unit, interpretive (listening, reading, viewing) and expressive (talk and writing) (item
  11); a moment
  where students talk about how the language works; each key language use the cluster names returns in a later unit
  with a harder text or task (item 13); the host-teacher note in unit field 3 (item 14).

## Home languages

Teacher notes on how a home language does the same thing are written for Spanish first and read as a Spanish read
(docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md, kind `spanish`). A note for another language (the California map names
Mandarin, Cantonese, Vietnamese, Tagalog and Arabic) waits until a reader for that language exists, and the course
page says so (Cory, 2026-11-21).

## Sensitive ground

A task never asks a student about immigration status, how the family came, what happened on the way, a parent's
work, or whether the family speaks English at home. A student is never asked to translate for the class, a family or
the teacher. Prompts about family, home and "where you are from" are offered as one of several, and a student may
write about an invented character or a place they choose. A home language is a resource, never a ranking; an accent
or a dialect (of English or of the home language) is not corrected as an error of meaning. The teacher page says: a
gap in English is not a learning disability, and slow progress is never read as one; refer to the school's team when
a student is far below in the home language too. If a student tells you something, the teacher page
carries health's steps (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"), for a parent at home
too.

**Reads.** A fresh-agent sensitivity read (brief kind `sensitivity`; community: multilingual learners and immigrant
families) of every host text about migration, war, loss or the student's own group, the newcomer strand (a 20-day
on-ramp beside each grade frame, STANDARD.md section 2), and the family notes, before a unit uses them; the Spanish notes also get a `spanish` read (Cory, 2026-11-21).

## What a tool may and may not do

A tool may give picture-backed word or sentence-frame practice, read a text aloud, let a student hear and replay a
model sentence, and give a private self-check. It may not record or store a student's voice or writing, score a
child's accent or pronunciation, place a student at a proficiency level, translate for a family, or stand in for
talk with a partner. The talk routine is people.

## Pacing

Settled 2026-11-16 (Cory, Desk card q-later-sel-eld): a protected period of 30 minutes in K to 5 and 45 minutes in 6
to 12, 160 days, 80 and 120 hours a year. No national body sets a figure for ELD time (federal guidance gives none,
from memory). California sets no minimum (California Department of Education, instructional services questions, read
2026-11-20). States that publish one set it higher. Arizona: its structured English immersion models must give at
least 120 minutes a day in K to 5 and 100 in 6 to 12 (statute A.R.S. 15-756.01, subsection A, Arizona Legislature
web page, read 2026-11-21). Rhode Island: a period is at least 45 consecutive minutes a day; entering and emerging
students get at least three periods a day and developing students two, at least one of them dedicated English
language development, and expanding students one, dedicated or integrated, in full from school year 2030-31
(200-RICR-20-30-3, sections 3.2 and 3.8, Secretary of State regulations web page, read 2026-11-21). No Montana figure is given: two reads disagreed
(minutes by level, or minutes left to districts) and its page could not be opened (2026-11-21). So the course page says
Curio Class's period is the protected core of a student's English language development and not a replacement for the
school's own program or for more time where a state or district asks for it. Each band frame may change the minutes
with a reason. Cory kept these minutes (2026-11-21).

## Where the evidence is thin

Research supports teaching language through content, structured talk, direct teaching of forms and protected ELD
time (National Academies, 2017, from memory: no page cited yet). No study has tried this course, and no scored
national rubric exists. Curio Class says so on the course page.
