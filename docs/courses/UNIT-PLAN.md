# The unit plan

A unit plan is written before any tool for the unit is specced or built. It says what the unit teaches, how
it will be checked, what each day is for and what job any tool has. It is a plan, not a lesson script.

The fields come from the twelve in [APPROACH.md](../APPROACH.md) and the math additions in
[APPROACH-MATH.md](../APPROACH-MATH.md). They were tried on three grade 4 math units and changed where the
trial showed a problem: [3.1](math-4/u03-1.md) (a concept unit), [9.1](math-4/u09-1.md) (a procedure unit)
and [11.1](math-4/u11-1.md) (the pilot, with a key tool). The changes are listed at the end. The fields have
been tried on math only. No plan has been used with children.

## The rules

1. **Two pages at most.** About 1,800 words. The trial plans came to 1,750 to 1,850, so the cap is tight:
   see the last section. If a plan runs over, cut prose, not check items.
2. **Checks are written before the days.** Fields 3, 4 and 5 are filled in before field 7 is started.
3. **Every check item is real.** It has its numbers, its standard code, its answer, its likely wrong answers
   and what each wrong answer shows. "A problem about area" is not an item.
4. **No tool without a named job.** The job is one of: show why, practise with feedback, check unaided. A
   day with no tool says "None".
5. **Every tool is marked key or practice.** A key tool is one the unit cannot be taught well without. A
   unit that runs on print has no key tool, and says so.
6. **The ideal instrument is named first, then the spec is judged against it.** Fit, partly fit or does not
   fit, with the reason. A tool that is wanted and does not exist is named as "does not exist".
7. **Every day has a kind:** understanding, fluency or use. The counts go in the header line.
8. **The model has a tie day and a withdrawal day.** The plan names the day the object is tied to the
   written form and the day the object is taken away.
9. **No countdown timers in tools.** A fluency unit says how fluency is built without one.
10. **The standard's verb is quoted exactly,** and the unit says which part of the standard it carries.
11. **Nothing claims a result.** Anything written from memory is marked as such. Sources are listed.
12. **House rules hold:** plain words, short sentences, "Curio Class" in full, no company names in the plan
    body.

## The fields, in order

**Header line.** Grade and course; the stretch and its days; this unit's days; days by kind; the words "A
plan, not a lesson". Not here: the length of a day (the spine holds it).

**1. Standards and practices.** The code and the standard's own words; this unit's share of it; the limits
(number sizes, denominators, shapes); strategies or the named algorithm, where the standard sets one; one or
two practices, each with the day and task that uses it. For supporting work, the major work it practises.
Not here: a list of all eight practices, or standards the unit only touches.

**2. Where it sits.** What it builds on, with the progression page's handoff; what it leads to; where it
comes back later in the year. Not here: a summary of the earlier grade.

**3. Checks, written first.** The end-check items this unit owns, in a table: number, code and practice, the
item, the answer, the wrong answers with their error letters. A unit owns items by its share of the
stretch's days (the stretch's end check is thirty minutes). Then each day's exit question, written out with
its answer and wrong answers. Then one line on what is not owned here. Not here: what to do about an error
(field 4), or items for another unit's share.

**4. Expected errors.** A lettered table: the error; what it shows; a wrong worked example beside a right
one, and the day it is shown; what to do if many made it. Mark which errors come from the progression page
and which from memory. Not here: general errors that belong to an earlier unit, or an error with no example.

**5. Readiness.** Only the first unit of a stretch gives the readiness check; it writes the items out, with
the code each tests. For each gap: the ten-minute reteach and the earlier tool, if one exists. Items that
serve a later unit are named, and their results are passed on. A later unit writes one line: where the
check was given and which results it uses. Not here: a reteach longer than ten minutes.

**6. Model and instruments.** What model is carried, the day it is tied to the written form and the day it
is withdrawn. Then each key moment of the unit: the ideal instrument, and whether an existing spec fits,
partly fits or does not fit. Then supplies, including what a room with nothing does. Not here: how the tool
works inside (that is its spec).

**7. Days.** One row a day: day; goal in student words; kind; how it opens and for how long; who holds the
model; tool with its mark, job and minutes, or "None"; work by hand; exit question, by its number in field
3. Not here: a script, the text of the exit question, or the warm-up.

**8. Talk and writing.** Two or three planned questions with their days; one written task; the look-fors the
teacher judges it by. A procedure unit may have one short task and say so. Not here: a full rubric for a
short unit.

**9. Practice that returns.** What the warm-up carries on each day; the fluency target, or "none; the
standard sets none"; for a fluency unit, how fluency is built with no timer. Not here: where this unit's
content comes back (field 2).

**10. Supports and extension.** English learners: the words and a sentence frame. Access: what changes for a
student who cannot see, hear, fold or write small. Extension on the same standard, not the next one. Not
here: a different goal for some students.

**11. Two rooms.** A parent teaching one child: what to do at the table, in a few lines. One projector: how
the day runs with one screen. A family note of one or two sentences. Not here: supplies (field 6).

**12. Not in this unit.** What a reader might expect and will not find, and where it lives. What is still to
make. Whether any tool named is only a spec. Not here: apologies.

**Sources.** The standards file; the progression page; the specs read; any open course read to learn, named
with its licence and what was read; what was not seen.

## What the trial changed

Each line is a change to the twelve proposed fields, with the reason.

1. **A header line was added, with days by kind.** The spine counts understanding, fluency and use days for
   each stretch. The plan is where those counts are settled, so it must state them.
2. **Field 1 gained "this unit's share".** Units 3.1 and 9.1 each carry part of a standard (the visual half;
   adding only). Without the line, a plan seems to promise the whole standard.
3. **"Strategies or algorithm" and the practices moved from the day rows into field 1.** They were the same
   on every day, so the row repeated them.
4. **"Where it comes back" moved from field 9 to field 2.** It was being written in both.
5. **"End check" became "Checks".** The end check belongs to the stretch. In 3.1 and 9.1 it is given in
   another unit, so the plan lists only the items it owns. Exit questions joined the field because they
   need the same parts as an item and do not fit in a table cell.
6. **Expected errors moved up to field 4 and were lettered.** Check items and exit questions now point to a
   letter. Before this, each error was described three times. "If many missed this, do that" moved onto the
   error for the same reason. This saved about 150 words a plan.
7. **Readiness is given only by the first unit of a stretch.** The spine puts it on day 1 of the stretch.
   Some items serve later units (two of four in stretch 3), so the field now says where those results go.
8. **"What is carried" became "Model and instruments".** The proposed fields had no place to name the ideal
   instrument and then judge a spec against it, which is the question each plan most needs to answer.
   Supplies moved here from Two rooms, since they are instruments too.
9. **The day row's "who holds the model" was cut to a few words.** The fade plan lives in field 6.
10. **"Supports and stretch" was renamed "Supports and extension".** "Stretch" already means a run of
    units.
11. **Talk and writing uses look-fors, not a rubric.** A three-day unit has one short written task. A
    rubric for it would be longer than the task.
12. **"Sources" was added.** The plans draw on memory in places, and a reader must be able to tell where.
13. **The length rule was kept at two pages, but "about one page for a five-day unit" was dropped.** Real
    check items, errors and readiness take about 800 words by themselves. The three-day plans came to two
    pages. The five-day pilot fit only after its end check was cut from six items to four.

Still awkward, and not fixed here:

- The last day of a stretch is half check and half response. It has no natural kind. The pilot marks it
  "Use (check and respond)".
- The mark "practice tool" sounds like the job "practise with feedback". A tool can be a practice tool whose
  job is to show why.
- A lesson tool does more than one job. The plans name the day's main job and list the tool's unaided items
  as a second.
- A stretch's end check and readiness check have no home where they sit whole. Each unit plan holds its
  share. A one-page stretch sheet may be needed.
