# Scope and sequence: World Geography and Ancient Civilizations I

Course `world-geography-and-ancient-civilizations-i` (catalogue id `world-geo-6`), grade 6, draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/world-geography-and-ancient-civilizations-i/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from an unseen source, and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so no unit holds a mid-unit assessment. Every unit's days carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. Every end-of-unit assessment and course-level check asks about each part of each code it lists (a short prompt per part, no day added); where a product (a map, a report, a research record) stands for a code, the day's goal says so. A unit opens with a readiness check only on an earlier grade's row last used two or more grades back; a row used the year before, or earlier this year, opens as a retrieval task inside a day. Fixed after the expert and Codex reads of 2026-11-14 (docs/reviews/sequence-6-8-fixes-2026-11-14.md).

One change from the frame's own day ranges, inside its rules: unit 3 holds one of its 14 days for the informed action look-back, which the frame places at the start of unit 5. Unit 3 therefore runs days 26 to 38, unit 4 days 39 to 54, the look-back is day 55, and unit 5 keeps days 56 to 70. Every unit keeps its day count and every course-level check keeps its days.

## Unit 1. How we study the past (days 1 to 10, weeks 1 to 2)

**Topics taught:** the five social sciences and their questions; how specialists work together on one site (Ur); the deep-time timeline (era, century, millennium, circa, BCE and CE, no year zero, drawn to scale); Ice Ages, coastlines and the green and dry Sahara as the climate that let people spread.

Arc: three days on the fields (meet them, sort and explain questions about Ur, one worked investigation), three on the timeline (retrieval of grade 4's to-scale timeline, BCE and CE counting, drawing the long and zoomed strips), two on climate and the spread of people, then the end-of-unit assessment and its respond day. The fields come first because every later unit names the fields its evidence comes from; the timeline comes before climate because the climate days place their dates on it. The last teaching day ends on the out-of-Africa map, the question unit 2 opens. The frame's "about 3, 4 and 3 days" become 3, 3 and 2 teaching days because the end-of-unit assessment and respond day sit inside the 10.

- Readiness: none. Grade 4's to-scale timeline (ss4-exploration) opens day 4 as a retrieval task (grade 5 used timelines, so the row is not idle two grades).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 9: questions sorted by field with what each asks and needs, the fields combining evidence on an unseen site plan, events placed across BCE and CE with the timeline terms, one climate change explained, and a short written answer to the unit's question. Respond day 10.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Leans: math 6 opens negative numbers (its unit 7, days 120 to 141) after this unit; science 6 geologic time (its days 92 to 115) may reuse the class timeline. Both met.

## Unit 2. Early humans and the first farmers (days 11 to 25, weeks 3 to 5)

**Topics taught:** where modern humans arose and how they spread, as a case of changing interpretation (Jebel Irhoud); the kinds of evidence behind an account; Paleolithic life; the Neolithic turn to farming in several places, with Catalhoyuk; three ways of farming life; the seven complex-society traits as a debated definition; how societies spread and meet.

Arc: four days on interpretation (the map, kinds of evidence, a newer view against an older one and where experts agree, the spread and where they still disagree, and a first short claim), four on Paleolithic and Neolithic life (farming, herding and the first metalworking on day 16, ending on a continuity and change chart), four on complex societies (traits in two halves, the checklist tested on Catalhoyuk, how societies spread and meet), one claim day on the compelling question, then assessment and respond. Interpretation leads because the deep past shows most plainly how a view is revised, and it sets the sourcing of secondary accounts used all year. The traits come last because they need the Neolithic town to test on, and units 4 to 11 use them. Each lettered part of MA.6.T2.6 (6a to 6g) has its own place on days 19 and 20.

- Readiness: none. Grade 4's archaeology as evidence (ss4-first-peoples-evidence) opens day 11 as a retrieval task.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 24 (all ten owned codes: the changed interpretation and where experts agree, the sources behind an account, the spread, Paleolithic and Neolithic life, the three farming ways of life, the traits on an unseen site, how societies spread, a short claim from an unseen source). Respond day 25.
- Candidate settled: Catalhoyuk as the worked Neolithic town, kept at no added day inside MA.6.T2.4 (day 16); Jericho named there.
- Leans: ELA 6's comparing accounts comes at its days 137 to 143; this unit cannot wait and teaches comparing accounts from its own pack (day 13), as the frame picks.

## Unit 3. Western Asia, the Middle East and North Africa today (days 26 to 38, and day 55; weeks 6 to 8, and 11)

**Topics taught:** the region's physical and political maps; absolute location in degrees east and west of the prime meridian; relative location by direction and scale; why people live by rivers, coasts and oases (the regional frame); climate graphs; oil and water; environmental change from satellite images; the year's informed action on a local water or energy question.

Arc: four map days, four days on settlement (the regional frame, climate graphs, resources, change over time, the last ending on a short written claim answering the unit's question), three action days (pick a question and gather facts, decide a step, share it), then assessment and respond. The look-back is day 55, about three and a half weeks after the share day, held as the frame sets. Maps come first because the regional frame reads them; the action comes after the change-over-time day so its facts are read with the same tools.

- Readiness: none. Grade 5's scale, latitude and longitude (ss5-republic-growth) open day 26 as a retrieval task.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 37: the physical features and an unseen political map, location in degrees and by scale used to add to a text, a climate graph, the frame on an unseen city, and two dated images of one place read for environmental change and settlement. Respond day 38.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Leans: science 6's climate unit (days 52 to 89) and math 6's four quadrants (days 120 to 141) both come after day 28. Met. The climate-graph day (31) assumes no grade 6 math graphing.

## Unit 4. Mesopotamia (days 39 to 54, weeks 8 to 11)

**Topics taught:** floods, irrigation and the Fertile Crescent; Sumer, Babylon and Assyria on a dated map and the timeline; productivity, surplus and specialization, and who gained less; Mesopotamian society against the traits (MA.6.T3b.4a classes, 4b religion, 4c monuments and art); cuneiform (4d); Gilgamesh and the Code of Hammurabi (4e); judging a law against a public problem.

Arc: two days of place and time, two of economics (productivity, then the surplus chain to how well people lived, and its costs), two of society (4a, then 4b and 4c), six of writing and law (cuneiform, Gilgamesh, the code sourced, laws as answers to problems, a proposed revision, the written claim), one day on the compelling question, one review day placing Mesopotamia on the timeline and the traits, then assessment and respond. The economy comes before society because the surplus explains the classes; the code comes last because it needs cuneiform, the classes and the sourcing questions together.

- Readiness: none. Grade 5's opportunity cost and productivity (ss5-new-england-industry) open day 41 as a retrieval task.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 53: map and timeline, a productivity chain to living standards, each feature of 4a to 4d, Gilgamesh and the code (4e), an unseen law sourced for perspective, and that law and a proposed revision judged against its public problem. Respond day 54.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Leans: ELA 6 unit 2 (days 33 to 58) reads the code beside this unit for purpose and word choice; the claim whether the code was just stays here (day 50). Day 47 carries the bridge: if a school runs ELA's point-of-view work later, the day models purpose on the prologue.

## Unit 5. Egypt and Kush (days 56 to 70, weeks 12 to 14)

**Topics taught:** the Nile flowing north and what its floods gave; the pharaoh, dynasties and one ruler studied closely; Egypt's social order and the Giza workers' town; religion, the afterlife and expected conduct; the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms and their achievements; Kush from Egyptian records, its own monuments and script; kandake queens and the 25th dynasty; Nubian peoples today.

Arc: two days on the Nile, four on society and religion, two on achievements, four on Kush (the peoples today first, then a record written by outsiders, corroboration against Kush's own monuments, the queens and the 25th dynasty), a claim day, then assessment and respond. Egypt comes before Kush because Kush is first met in Egypt's records, which students then test. Day 55 (unit 3's look-back) opens the unit's weeks.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 69: Upper and Lower Egypt and Nubia, the Nile, the pharaoh, dynasties and one ruler from an unseen royal image, the social order, religion with expected conduct, achievements, and an unseen record of Kush sourced with Kush and Egypt compared. Respond day 70.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Course-level check 1 (days 71 to 74, week 15)

## Unit 6. Phoenicia and the alphabet (days 75 to 84, weeks 15 to 17)

**Topics taught:** the ancient Mediterranean on a map; why Phoenicia became a sea-trading power, and its colonies; buyers, sellers and merchants who pool money and share risk; barter, weighed silver and coins, and money as a store of value (the personal finance touch, no added day); who did the work: sailors and craftworkers of differing legal statuses, with documented slavery and dependent labor, and rowers' status uncertain in the sources used; three ways of writing and how the alphabet spread.

Arc: map, place and trade first (four days), innovation, the venture's risk and who did the work (one), then writing (three days, ending on what changed and stayed and the compelling question), then assessment and respond. Trade comes before the alphabet because the alphabet spread by the same ships.

- Readiness: none. What money is for and saving (ss3-money-saving, grade 3) was used in grade 5 (ss5-independence-war), so day 78 opens with a retrieval task on it, where money as a store of value is taught; day 75 is the map day alone.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 83: the map, location and trade, buyers, sellers and merchants, money and the cost of trade, an innovation or venture and its risk, the three scripts, and an unseen source on how ships spread ideas. Respond day 84.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Leans: ELA 6's writing-systems texts (its unit 4, days 87 to 108) follow this unit. Met.

## Unit 7. Judaism, Christianity and Islam at their origins (days 85 to 99, weeks 17 to 20)

**Topics taught:** how faiths are taught as history; the region on a historical map; the Hebrew Bible's account of Abraham, Moses, the kingdom and the temples, beside archaeology; Judaism's ideas (MA.6.T3e.3a); the kingdom to 70 CE (3b); Judaism after 70 CE; Christianity's rise and spread (3c) and its central beliefs (3d); Mecca, Medina, Muhammad's life, the hijra and the Qur'an; Muhammad's teachings; how the three faiths relate; each community today.

Arc: one framing day, four days on ancient Israel and Judaism, three on Christianity, four on Islam, one comparison day on the compelling question, then assessment and respond. The faiths come in the order they began, which is also the order in which each builds on the one before.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 98: the historical map, the route and the roles of Abraham and Moses, the kingdom (David's capital at Jerusalem) to 70 CE, each part of MA.6.T3e.3 (3a to 3d), Islam's places, Muhammad and the Qur'an, how the three relate, and an unseen passage sourced as its followers' account. Respond day 99.
- Candidate settled: how Judaism continued after 70 CE (the synagogue and rabbinic learning), kept at about half of day 89, inside the unit's 15 days. It closes the Israel days honestly (the unit otherwise ends Jewish history at a destruction) and costs no day.

## Unit 8. Connections and contributions (days 100 to 113, weeks 20 to 23)

**Topics taught:** the Silk Roads and sea routes; Persian roads and Alexander as connectors; one event placed in its wider picture and traced through causes and effects; the year's first research project on a contribution to the modern world, with supporting questions, differing sources, the sourcing questions, a revised question and a short report with a source list.

Arc: five connection days (the frame's "about 5"), seven research days (question, sources, testing, revising, drafting, peer reading, handing in), then assessment and respond. The research is nine days counting the assessment and respond days, the frame's "about 9". Connections come first so students choose a contribution with the whole region in view.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 112: a short task on the Silk Roads' exchange and one unseen event in its wider picture, causes and effects (MA.6.T3g.1, D2.His.1). The report (day 111) and its research record (questions, where experts agree, source choices and checks, the revised question) stand for MA.6.T3g.2, D1.3 to D1.5, D3.1 and D2.His.3, so the day does not test the research twice in the week of ELA's and math's checks. Respond day 113.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Leans: ELA 6 teaches the general research steps in its unit 3 (days 63 to 86), before this unit. Met.

## Unit 9. Africa: land and early kingdoms (days 118 to 131, weeks 24 to 27)

**Topics taught:** Africa's regions and features; countries and cities by location; map projections and Africa's true size; one student-made map (with its paper path); the regional frame with less support; drought, desertification and the Sahel; Axum; the Swahili coast and monsoon routes; ancient Ghana, gold and salt, the caravan; Islam with merchants; evidence from Arabic writers and griots.

Arc: six geography days (day 121's keyed map and small graph made on paper, and on an electronic tool where a device is in the room), two on Axum and the Swahili coast (Christianity at Axum, Islam on the coast, so both halves of MA.6.T4b.1 are taught), three on West Africa, a claim day, then assessment and respond. Geography leads as in unit 3, and the trade units read the routes it maps. East Africa comes before West Africa in time (Axum from the first centuries CE, Ghana later).

- End-of-unit assessment, day 130: regions, features and an unseen political map, a projection explained, the keyed map and graph counted, the frame with drought and desertification and its conflict or cooperation, Axum, the Swahili coast, Ghana and Timbuktu, and an unseen trade source. Respond day 131.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 10. Central America and the Caribbean (days 132 to 142, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** the Taino, Kalinago, Lenca and Miskito as living peoples; the region's physical and political maps; location, volcanoes, hurricanes and resources; a keyed map built from data; ways of life tied to islands, coasts and highlands; older European accounts sourced.

Arc: the peoples today open the unit (the frame's rule), then four geography days ending on the student-made keyed map, three days on the peoples (ways of life, sourcing outsiders' accounts, a comparison of highland and coast), a claim day, then assessment and respond.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 141: the region's maps, settlement in one place, the student's keyed map (days 135 to 136) counted with its pattern explained, one people's way of life and identity, and an unseen account sourced for its perspective. Respond day 142.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 11. South America and the Maya and Andes (days 143 to 156, weeks 29 to 32)

**Topics taught:** South America's features, countries and cities; altitude and settlement; how farming, ranching and mining change the Amazon; the Maya and early Andean societies; a longer research report naming what the evidence cannot show; Maya, Quechua and Aymara peoples today.

Arc: four geography days, two content days (day 147 the Maya and Maya peoples today; day 148 the Moche and Nazca, evidence where no writing survives, and Quechua and Aymara peoples today), then six report days from question to hand-in, ending on a short gallery walk that answers the compelling question (day 154), then assessment and respond. Students need what each society left before they plan sources, so the shared content has two days, not one. The report is the year's longest writing and builds on unit 8's.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 155: the maps, settlement, Amazon change from data, and two unseen sources on one society corroborated with a limit and a further question; the report counts for MA.6.T5c.1 and D4.2. Respond day 156.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame (the Inca comparison stays an extension only).
- Leans: ELA 6's comparing-accounts days (137 to 143) come before this unit. Met.

## Shorter path

The year is 160 days of 50 minutes. Two shorter routes keep every owned code, skill and assessment; the unit plans check them against real minutes, so neither is yet shown to fit.

- **45-minute periods, same 160 days.** Each teaching day keeps its source work, its product and its exit ticket, and trims the second worked example or the whole-class share; assessment and respond days stay whole. Units 8 and 11 trim peer reading to one partner, not two.
- **About 150 days.** These days fold into the day before or after, because each code or skill they carry is taught on another day too: 18 (continuity and change chart, into 23), 52 (Mesopotamia review, into 51), 59 (one ruler, into 58), 63 (achievements, second half, into 62), 92 (Christianity's spread, into 91), 96 (how the faiths relate, into 97), 104 (connections on the map, into 103), 139 (Lenca and Miskito compared, into 140), and the second respond day after checks 2 and 3 (117, 160). Course-level check 1's two respond days stay, as it sits before the longest stretch of new content.
