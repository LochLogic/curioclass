## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did Africa's deserts and seas divide its peoples or connect them?"

**Hook, day 118.** Compare Africa's relief map with dated caravan and Indian Ocean routes. Where do barriers become connections? Sources are in field 10.
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** What can maps distort (118 to 121)? How do environments and choices shape settlement and cooperation (122 to 123)? Who connected inland towns with distant shores (124 to 125)? What did caravans carry, at whose cost (126 to 128)?
**Closing claim, day 129.** Answer the compelling question using the Adulis account, Kilwa account and Ghana trade account. Explain two connections, a barrier, human costs and a counterclaim; distinguish periods.
**Teaching.** Model projections on 120, graph axes on 121, environmental mechanisms on 122, source limits on 124 and a qualified claim on 127. Students lead comparison on 128. Teach projection, drought, desertification, monsoon, caravan, griot and enslavement.


**Skill parts, in our words.** The local file lists codes only; the publisher supplies the indicators.

- **D2.Geo.9.6-8:** Evaluate how long-term environmental changes caused by people influence where conflict and cooperation occur.

**Limits.** No continent-wide cultural generalizations or complete medieval chronology. Ancient Ghana differs from modern Ghana; Timbuktu flourished later. Swahili societies are African. Trade circulated Islam through interconnected communities, not one African origin route. Drought alone does not prove human-caused degradation. Mercator enlarges high latitudes relative to Africa; equal-area maps preserve area, not every shape. Informed action remains in unit 3.
