## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What can surviving evidence tell us about Maya or early Andean life, and what remains uncertain?"

**Hook, day 143.** Place the throne vessel beside a regional map. Students distinguish visible evidence from a guess about its maker. Locate Mesoamerica separately from South America.
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** Where are these places (143 and 144)? How do environments and choices shape lives (145 and 146)? What survives, and whose voice survives (147 and 148)? What can two sources support, and what should we seek next (149 to 154)?
**Closing claim, day 154.** Each report answers the compelling question through its chosen society, using a named object, a secondary account and a community source for present-day continuity.
**Teaching and ownership.** Section 11.1: map tools, altitude, population density and deforestation; 11.2: society content, glyphs and codices; 11.3: source questions, corroboration, limits and explanation; 11.4: assessment and response. Distinguish material evidence from writing and modern peoples from ancient societies. Compare accounts on 150 (RH.6-8.9); organize explanations on 152 (WHST.6-8.2).

- **Regional scope for every class:** All classes study South American geography and Maya or early Andean societies, regardless of their home state.

**Skill parts, in our words.** The local file lists codes; the publisher supplies wording.
- **D2.Geo.4.6-8:** Explain how cultural patterns and economic choices change environments and daily life nearby and far away.
- **D2.His.12.6-8:** Use questions about several historical sources to identify further inquiry and additional sources.
- **D3.3.6-8:** Select evidence across sources to support claims and identify its limits.
- **D4.2.6-8:** Construct explanations with reasoning, correct sequence, examples, details and data; acknowledge their strengths and weaknesses.

**Limits.** No empire survey or devotional activity. Inca comparison is optional extension; detailed Inca and Mexica history belongs in grade 9. Name Spanish book burning plainly, without reenactments, hostile quotations or restricted knowledge. Do not portray the sparsely populated Amazon as empty.
**Carried.** Keep unit 10's maps, timeline and source labels. Return unit 5's corroboration record on 149; add independence and limits on 151. Withdraw completed examples on 153. Retain blank records and reference maps; archive reports and corrected evidence on 156.
