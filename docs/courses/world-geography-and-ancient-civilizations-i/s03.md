## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why do people live where they do in a dry land?"
**Hook, day 26.** Show the 2021 Nile delta image beside its regional locator. Why are towns and fields concentrated here? Open the title's compelling question before retrieval.
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** How do maps locate people and water (days 26 to 29)? Why do settlements cluster (days 30 to 32)? How can people change those patterns (day 33)? What should we do about water where we live (days 34 to 36 and 55)?
**Closing claim, day 33.** Answer from the Cairo and Baghdad climate graphs, resource map, marsh images and community accounts. Explain two influences and consider the claim that rainfall alone determines settlement.
**Teaching.** Model keys on day 26, coordinates on 28, scale on 29 and the regional frame on 30. On 31 read rainfall bars and temperature points against separate labelled axes before comparison; differing observation periods prohibit precise city rankings. On 32 explain oil, aquifers, irrigation and desalination without assuming later science. Model evidence and reasoning, then withdraw the example before day 33 writing. Source every account before use; compare community purposes with satellite evidence. Days 34 to 36 and 55 keep a dated action record: question, public facts, alternatives, chosen step, audience, result and revision. Students choose; no promised savings or household disclosures.

- **Maps.** Make original physical, political, relief, resource and population maps with dated sources, north arrows, readable keys and scale bars. The political inset shows Tunisia as an area, Tunis as a capital star and Sfax as a city dot. Plot accurate positions; read coordinates to the nearest whole degree: Tunis about 37 degrees north, 10 degrees east; Rabat about 34 degrees north, 7 degrees west. Print the rounding convention. Tunis to Sfax measures approximately four 60-kilometre scale-bar lengths, about 240 kilometres. Scale changes with enlargement, so enlarge map and bar together. Resource symbols distinguish freshwater and oil, not generic wealth.
- **Muscat pack.** Print the national weather service's twelve monthly values for Muscat (Seeb), 1986 to 2009, with rainfall bars and mean daily maximum temperature points. Label both units and the period; these are climate averages, not today's weather. Under the graph print: "One storm can raise a month's average." (June's 10.9 millimetres falls on about 0.1 rain days a year, so a single storm, such as Cyclone Gonu in June 2007, can carry it.) Supply a dated coastal locator, relief and settlement map, and this original secondary account with source notes: "Muscat is Oman's capital on the Gulf of Oman. Mountains rise behind coastal neighbourhoods. Its harbour supports fishing, trade and visitor services. Water supply combines managed groundwater and desalination, which removes salt from seawater. A 2021 local study examined increasing salinity in a Muscat wellfield; freshwater availability cannot be inferred from proximity to the sea." Sources: Northern Oman image caption, official tourism directory's Muscat entry, and the Omani research ministry's Wadi Adai study summary. Treat the study as evidence about that wellfield, not every water source. Include an observed settlement-density key; do not invent population totals.
- **Dubai pack.** Use the November 11, 2000 and April 25, 2011 images from World of Change: Urbanization of Dubai, at matching extent and scale. Print the dates, locator, author and purpose, and translate the false-colour key into patterns and labelled descriptions. Add an original 65-word secondary account explaining sand dredging, rock barriers and development roads, drawn from the series caption. No company or development names are needed. Distinguish land creation and visible construction from occupancy and demographic growth.
- **Wellfield Case, invented, not Muscat.** "A coastal district's wells become too salty for household use. Its council closes the wells and stops maintaining the outer supply pipes. Some families leave outlying houses and move to the central town, where treated water remains available." A two-panel schematic locates the closed wells, abandoned outer sites, central supply and movement arrows. Ask for analysis of contraction, not a claim that this happened at the real study site.
