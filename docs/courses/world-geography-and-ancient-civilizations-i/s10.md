## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do islands, coasts and highlands shape who a people are?"

**Hook, day 132.** Meet four living peoples through their own published introductions beside a relief map. What does a map reveal, and what must people explain themselves?
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** Where are the region's places (133)? Why do settlements grow there (134 to 136)? How do land, water and community choices connect with identity (137 and 139)? What do outsiders' accounts leave out (138)?
**Closing claim, day 140.** Answer the compelling question using the Kalinago introduction, COPINH's Lenca cultural statement and the Miskitu protocol. Explain two connections and answer the counterclaim that environment determines identity.
**Teaching.** Model map tools on 133, environmental mechanisms on 134, data symbols on 135, and source perspective and a qualified claim on 138. Students lead comparison on 139. Teach isthmus, archipelago, hurricane, resource, territory, descendant and perspective. Ownership: 10.1, map reading and settlement; 10.2, map construction; 10.3, developed cultural description and place-identity reasoning after 132's introduction.

- **Taught and assessed in general form:** Every class studies Central America and the Caribbean; no task assumes students live in Massachusetts or Boston.

**Skill parts, in our words.** The local file lists codes only; the publisher supplies the indicators.
- **D2.Geo.1.6-8:** Construct maps that represent cultural and environmental patterns, and explain their spatial arrangement.
- **D2.Geo.6.6-8:** Explain connections between places' physical and human characteristics and people's identities and cultures.

**Limits.** Four peoples are cases, not interchangeable island cultures. Use Kalinago and Miskitu in new prose; explain older names. Separate islands, countries and territories. Conquest brought killing, enslavement, dispossession and disease; peoples survived and adapted. No sacred knowledge, reenactments or ancestry disclosures. Detailed colonization belongs in grade 9; informed action stays in unit 3.
**Carried.** Keep unit 9's maps, timeline and source labels. Return map keys on 133; students choose symbols on 135 and explain them on 136. Return unit 5's perspective model on 138, adding whose voice is missing. Withdraw completed examples on 139; retain blank records and reference maps for 140 and 141.

**Fresh source portions.** On 138 teach the letter's possession and captivity paragraphs and the protocol's history and livelihood paragraphs. Withhold both excerpts below until 141; use only these excerpts, not adjacent spiritual particulars. Supply the metadata with them.

- **Columbus, letter to Luis de Santángel, 1493.** Audience: a royal official who helped finance the voyage, then print readers. Context: reporting and promoting the voyage to potential backers. From the canoe paragraph in the checked English translation: "And with these they sail around all those islands, which are countless, and trade in their merchandise." Here, "these" means canoes. A separate phrase from the Navidad paragraph anticipates "great trade and profit". Students examine the emphasis on trade as evidence of purpose, not proof of neutrality.
- **MASTA, October 2012, protocol, section 2.2, printed page 19.** Audience: people proposing activities in Honduran Muskitia and the communities negotiating with them. Context: explaining community values and a consultation process concerning territory and resources. Spanish excerpt: "lo colectivo enmarcado en el respeto mutuo". Curio Class translation: "collective life framed by mutual respect". In this resource-use discussion, the phrase describes the organization's cultural ideal, not every individual's behavior.

The sources concern different peoples and centuries. Compare perspectives on relationships with place, not two eyewitness accounts of one event. Use the museum account of island exchange as contextual corroboration; modern voices do not establish uninterrupted sameness since 1493.
