## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.6.T3b.4e** (days 47, 48, 50): The first epic, the Epic of Gilgamesh, and the Code of Hammurabi, an early surviving written law collection (the framework calls it the first written law code), including its "eye for an eye" principle.
- **D2.His.6.6-8** (days 47): Analyze how the makers' views affected what information their historical sources contain.
- **D2.His.16.6-8** (days 48, 50): Arrange fitting evidence into a clear argument about the past.
- **D2.Civ.12.6-8** (days 49): Assess real and proposed rules and laws as ways to address public problems.
- skill ss6-hammurabi-code (grade 6)

> **Supporting questions, drafted together.** Why cities here (39)? Which powers rose where and when (40)? What did higher output cost and enable (41 and 42)? Whose work and beliefs shaped cities (43 to 46)? Whose justice did laws serve (47 to 50)?

| 53 | D2.His.6.6-8 | Analyze how perspective shapes preserved information. | **4b, five minutes.** Source L and K: "Who commissioned them, when, for whom and why? How does the ruler's position influence what each tells us? Whose evidence is missing?" Answer: Hammurabi and royal scribes, eighteenth century BCE, Babylonian subjects and officials and later rulers; establish authority and present royal justice. L emphasizes an owner's compensation and proof of neglect; K promises help to a claimant. Neither preserves a tenant's testimony or proves enforcement. Wrong: the king witnessed each case (AB), or royal purpose makes every statement unusable (Q). | section 4.3 |
| 53 | D2.His.16.6-8 | Organize evidence into a historical argument. | **4c, eight minutes shared with the next row.** "Did this rule address the public problem fairly? Write three to five connected sentences using L and K. Propose one revision; judge both against the same problem. Answer one competing claim and name an evidence limit." Key: relevant details from both cards support a qualified argument. See samples below. Wrong: unsupported verdict or no source detail (AC), or legal promise treated as measured outcome (G). | section 4.3 |
| 53 | D2.Civ.12.6-8 | Assess actual and proposed rules against a public problem. | **4c, same task and time.** Assess the actual rule and the student's revision separately against protecting food production and resolving losses from neglected rented fields. A defensible revision retains proof of neglect and adjusts compensation or payment timing. Judge its benefit and a possible drawback, such as weaker deterrence. No single revision is required. Wrong: praising a revision without evaluating the original, or switching to an unrelated problem (I). | section 4.3 |
