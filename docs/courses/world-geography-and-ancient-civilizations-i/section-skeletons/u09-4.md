## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.6.T4b.4** (days 128, 129, 130): Explain these societies' key role in trans-Saharan trade, the spread of Islam and trade in gold, ivory and enslaved people, and their contributions to the modern world.
- **D2.Geo.9.6-8** (days 129, 130): Evaluate how long-term, human-caused environmental change affects patterns of conflict and cooperation.
- **MA.6.T4a.1** (days 130): Locate Africa, its surrounding oceans and the Mediterranean on a world map, then Africa's regions and features like the Sahara, Kilimanjaro, the Cape of Good Hope, the Great Rift Valley and Lake Victoria.
- **MA.6.T4a.2** (days 130): Read a political map to tell countries, capitals and cities apart and describe their absolute location (coordinates) and relative location, using maps to add to text.
- **MA.6.T4a.3** (days 130): Explain how location, landforms, climate (including drought and desertification) and natural resources shaped settlement, population size and economies in the region.
- **D2.Geo.3.6-8** (days 130): Use paper and digital mapping and graphing to show and analyze spatial patterns of environmental and cultural features.
- **MA.6.T4b.1** (days 130): Identify where the Kingdom of Axum was, what made it wealthy and important, and its role in Ethiopia's adoption of Christianity, and explain how East African societies helped spread Islam across the Indian Ocean to South Asia.
- **MA.6.T4b.2** (days 130): Explain how Swahili coastal societies linked inland Africa to Indian Ocean trade routes reaching South Asia and China, and name key goods traded.
- **MA.6.T4b.3** (days 130): Identify the locations, sources of wealth and importance of West African cities and empires, including Timbuktu and ancient Ghana.
- skill ss6-west-africa-trade (grade 6)
- skill ss6-africa-geography (grade 6)
- skill ss6-axum-swahili (grade 6)
