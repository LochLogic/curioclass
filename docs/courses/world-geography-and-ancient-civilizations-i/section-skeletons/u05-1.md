| 69 | MA.6.T3c.1 | Locate Upper Egypt, Lower Egypt and Nubia, and explain what "Upper" and "Lower" mean in this setting. | **1, three minutes.** On a fresh physical map with a north arrow, delta, Aswan and cataracts, label Upper Egypt, Lower Egypt and Nubia. Draw downstream and explain the names. Key: Upper Egypt is the southern valley; Lower Egypt is the northern delta; Nubia extends south from Aswan through the cataract region. Upper and Lower refer to upstream and downstream, not the page's top and bottom. Wrong: "Upper Egypt is north because north is up" (A). | section 5.1 |
| 69 | MA.6.T3c.4 | Analyze the pharaoh's role as god and king, how pharaohs appeared in painting and sculpture, what dynasties were, and what at least one pharaoh or queen did. | **3, five minutes.** Read reserved Statue S and Painting H. How do royal regalia, divine titles, figure sizes and tribute express authority? Explain a dynasty and one deed of Hatshepsut. Key: rulers governed and embodied divinely sanctioned kingship; formal images emphasized legitimacy and power. A dynasty is a succession of rulers, usually related. Hatshepsut commissioned her Deir el-Bahari temple and a Punt expedition; either deed suffices. Ramses II's Abu Simbel temples are checked by the day 59 exit. Images do not establish universal consent. Wrong: larger figures prove greater body height (C); a dynasty is a building (J); a portrait proves every subject's experience (K). | section 5.1 |
