## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D2.Geo.8.6-8** (days 34, 35, 36, 37): Analyze how the human relationship with environments widens or narrows patterns of settlement and movement.
- **MA.6.T3a.1** (days 37): Use map tools to locate the region's key seas, rivers, straits and peninsulas, such as the Nile, Tigris, Euphrates, Red Sea and Suez Canal, and read other map types.
- **MA.6.T3a.2** (days 37): Read a political map to tell countries, capitals and cities apart and describe their absolute location (coordinates) and relative location, using maps to add to text.
- **MA.6.T3a.3** (days 37): Explain how location, landforms, climate and natural resources shaped where people settled, how large populations grew and what economies developed in the region.
- **D2.Geo.2.6-8** (days 37): Use maps, satellite images and photographs to explain how the location of places and regions relates to changes in their environmental features.
- skill ss6-mena-settlement (grade 6)
- skill ss6-mena-maps (grade 6)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions, drafted together.** How do maps locate people and water (days 26 to 29)? Why do settlements cluster (days 30 to 32)? How can people change those patterns (day 33)? What should we do about water where we live (days 34 to 36 and 55)?
> **Teaching.** Model keys on 26, coordinates on 28, scale on 29 and the regional frame on 30. On 31 separate climate axes; different observation periods prohibit precise city rankings. On 32 explain oil, aquifers, irrigation and desalination. Model evidence and reasoning; withdraw the example on 33. Compare community accounts with satellite evidence. Days 34 to 36 and 55 keep dated action records: question, public facts, alternatives, chosen step, audience, result and revision. Students choose; no promised savings or household disclosures.
