| 112 | MA.6.T3g.1 | Describe the effects of trade, cultural exchange and conquest among the region's societies, especially along the Silk Roads, and the goods, languages and cultural motifs exchanged. | **1, fifteen minutes.** Use P, R and the fresh dated network map. Identify an import and an export; explain how sea and overland Silk Roads linked regions. What do Greek-letter coins circulating in western India and Egyptian motifs on a Cypriot bowl suggest about exchange, and what can neither establish? Use the map's conquest layer to explain a changed connection and a human cost. Answer: metals enter Barygaza and cotton leaves; ports join inland networks. Coins suggest Greek writing circulated beyond Greek-speaking homelands, not that everyone spoke Greek. R suggests adapted designs, not uniform beliefs. Macedonian conquest brought new rulers and Greek speakers east through force; people lost power, lives or homes. Distinguish earlier Mediterranean and Persian networks from Han and Roman exchange. Wrong: only goods moved (A); exchange was always peaceful (B); everyone adopted identical language and beliefs (L); every route simultaneous (C); just one road (K). | section 8.1 |

Answer: changed control obstructed Greek shipping; diversion under guard to Barygaza connects political authority to routes and competing ports. The report does not establish a particular battle. Wrong: isolated ruler list (D), or a later event causes an earlier one (P). | section 8.1 |

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

<!-- skel -->
Error register rows (unit plan field 6) that name one of this section's days:

| | The error or first idea | What it shows; wrong beside right, day and repair | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Only goods moved. | Excludes nonmaterial exchange. On 100 contrast "only cloth" with goods and motifs moving; trace languages and designs on a new network. | Silk Roads overview; anticipated. |
| C | Persian roads, Alexander and Han exchange were simultaneous. | Flattens chronology. On 101 contrast one simultaneous network with earlier Persian and Macedonian links and later Han exchange; order dated layers. | Dated historical accounts; anticipated. |
| P | A later event caused an earlier one. | Reverses causation. On 103 contrast a later closure causing earlier shortages with a closure followed by shortages; order cause before effect. | Anticipated from memory; not observed. |
| Q | A smaller BCE year comes earlier. | Reverses date order. On 104 contrast 240 BCE before 500 BCE with 500 BCE before 240 BCE; order a fresh dated strip. | Anticipated from memory; not observed. |
