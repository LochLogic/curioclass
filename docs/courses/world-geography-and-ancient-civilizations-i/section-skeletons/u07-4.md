| 98 | MA.6.T3e.3 | Describe the history of ancient Israel and the beginnings of Christianity. | **3, three minutes.** Source P by author, audience, setting and purpose. Use a detail to describe early Christian life. What can this source not establish? Answer: Paul addresses Roman Christians in the first century, advising amid differences; the contrasting treatment of days shows variation, not identical practice. It does not prove a divine claim or describe every Christian. This parent item checks historical source use; items 4 to 7 cover its content parts. Wrong: scripture proves every event (L) or is useless evidence (U). | section 7.1 |
| 98 | MA.6.T3e.3a | a: Monotheistic religion, including belief in one God, the Ten Commandments, individual worth and responsibility, and the idea that rulers and ruled share the same moral duties. | **4, three minutes.** Explain Judaism's belief in one God. Choose one of the Ten Commandments and link it to one duty that binds rulers and ruled alike, saying what it shows about each person's worth and responsibility. Answer: monotheism; for example, "do not steal" binds king and people alike, and each person answers for keeping it. Ideals are not proof of universal compliance. Wrong: king is divine (W) or exempt from shared moral duties (D). | section 7.1 |
| 98 | MA.6.T3e.3b | b: The Hebrew Bible's account of early Israel, from the unified kingdom under Saul, David and Solomon and the first temple, to its destruction and the later Roman-era events. | **5, five minutes.** Order fresh event cards: Persian permission to return, Roman destruction of the second temple, Saul then David then Solomon, Babylonian destruction of the first temple, divided kingdoms and Assyrian conquest of northern Israel. Explain Jerusalem's role, exile and rebuilding. Answer: kings, division and Assyrian conquest, Babylon and exile, Persian return and rebuilding, later Roman rule and 70 CE. David makes Jerusalem capital; Solomon builds the first temple in the biblical account. Distinguish the biblical early-kingdom narrative from debated archaeological detail. Wrong: one temple or one destruction (C), or all Jews left the region after 70 CE (N). | section 7.1 |
| 98 | MA.6.T3e.3c | c: The rise of Christianity as a separate religion rooted in Judaism, its growing diversity across the Roman Empire and its relations with Roman officials. | **6, four minutes.** Explain Christianity's Jewish origins, gradual emergence as a separate religion, spread and growing variety across the empire. Use P for variety, and explain relations with Roman officials before Constantine. Answer: Jesus and early followers were Jewish; Paul and others reached Jews and non-Jews through imperial networks. As non-Jewish membership grew and interpretations and community practices diverged, distinct Christian identities emerged unevenly over generations. Communities differed. Some officials demanded civic worship and punished refusal, including execution; persecution varied rather than being constant everywhere. Constantine later supported Christianity. Wrong: no Jewish roots (F), immediate separation everywhere (AA), uniform practice (H), or every emperor continuously persecuted it (Q). | section 7.1 |
| 98 | MA.6.T3e.3d | d: The central features of Christianity, such as belief in a messiah, salvation, the Old and New Testaments, and the life and teachings of Jesus. | **7, five minutes.** In four short sentences describe Jesus's life and a teaching, Christians' beliefs about messiah and salvation, and the place of the Old and New Testaments. Answer: Jewish teacher in Roman-ruled Judaea and Galilee, teaching love of God and neighbor, crucified under Roman authority; Christians believe he is messiah and salvation comes through him, with resurrection central. Their scriptures include the Old Testament, sharing texts with Judaism, and New Testament Gospels and letters. These are attributed beliefs, not demands for agreement. Wrong: Jesus wrote all scripture (P), Christians reject the Hebrew Bible (G), or Jews collectively killed Jesus (O). | section 7.1 |
| 98 | MA.6.T3f.2 | Explain how Islam is historically related to Judaism and Christianity as monotheistic religions. | **9, six minutes.** Read three cards students have not met: Deuteronomy 6:4, from the Hebrew Bible; John 1:1 and 1:14, from the Christian New Testament, where Christians identify the "Word" with Jesus; and Qur'an 112:1 to 4, from Muslims' scripture. Use them with what you learned to explain two historical connections and two differences among these monotheistic traditions. Answer: all three cards teach one God; Christianity arose within Judaism, and Islam later engaged both traditions. John's Word becoming flesh expresses the Christian belief that Jesus is divine; Qur'an 112 says God neither begets nor is begotten, so Muslims honor Jesus as a prophet, not divine; Judaism does not accept the Christian claim about Jesus. Support with attributed card details; shared monotheism does not make beliefs identical. Wrong: identical texts (K), or a ranking that treats one community as replacing the others (T). | section 7.1 |
