## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.6.T5a.4** (days 132): Describe the culture and way of life of the region's indigenous peoples, such as the Carib, Taino, Lenca and Miskito.
- **MA.6.T5a.1** (days 133): Use map tools to locate Central America and the Caribbean Sea, and identify features like the Gulf of Mexico, the Yucatan Peninsula and the Panama Canal.
- **MA.6.T5a.2** (days 133): Locate the present-day countries and major cities of Central America and the Caribbean on a political map, using maps to add to text.
- **MA.6.T5a.3** (days 134): Explain how location, climate, landforms and natural resources shaped settlement, population size and economies in Central America and the Caribbean.
- skill ss6-caribbean-peoples (grade 6)
- skill ss6-caribbean-central-geo (grade 6)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions, drafted together.** Where are the region's places (133)? Why do settlements grow there (134 to 136)? How do land, water and community choices connect with identity (137 and 139)? What do outsiders' accounts leave out (138)?
> **Teaching.** Model map tools on 133, environmental mechanisms on 134, data symbols on 135, and source perspective and a qualified claim on 138. Students lead comparison on 139. Teach isthmus, archipelago, hurricane, resource, territory, descendant and perspective. Ownership: 10.1, map reading and settlement; 10.2, map construction; 10.3, developed cultural description and place-identity reasoning after 132's introduction.
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 9's maps, timeline and source labels. Return map keys on 133; students choose symbols on 135 and explain them on 136. Return unit 5's perspective model on 138, adding whose voice is missing. Withdraw completed examples on 139; retain blank records and reference maps for 140 and 141.
