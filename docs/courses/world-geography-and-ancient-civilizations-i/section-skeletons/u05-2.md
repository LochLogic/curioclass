| 69 | MA.6.T3c.5 | Describe how Egypt's social classes related to one another, from the pharaoh and priests to scribes, artisans, farmers and enslaved people. | **4, four minutes.** In new Account W, draw and explain three resource or power links that include every named group. Distinguish occupation, compulsory service and enslavement. Key: farmers supplied food and taxes; scribes recorded dues; rulers and priests controlled institutions; artisans supplied crafted goods. Enslaved people were owned and compelled to work. Free people could owe labor and receive provisions. Giza settlement evidence does not show every worker was enslaved, or prove slavery absent elsewhere. Wrong: all manual workers were enslaved (D), or provisioning means freely chosen work (L). | section 5.1 |
| 69 | MA.6.T3c.6 | Describe Egypt's polytheistic religion, including beliefs about death, proper behavior, the afterlife, mummification and the roles of the gods. | **5, five minutes.** Read new Account J. Explain polytheism, two gods' roles, the link between conduct and judgment after death, and why preserving a body mattered. Key: Egyptians worshipped many gods; Osiris judged the dead, Anubis was associated with embalming and care for the dead, and Ma'at with truth and order. Proper conduct mattered in judgment; mummification preserved a body for continued existence after death. These describe beliefs, not proof that everyone acted properly or could afford the same burial. Wrong: worship of one god (N), mummification as resurrection proved (E), or wealth alone ensured moral conduct (U). | section 5.1 |
| 69 | MA.6.T3c.7 | Summarize key achievements of the Old, Middle and New Kingdoms, such as farming systems, mathematics, astronomy, the calendar, papyrus, hieroglyphs and the Giza pyramids. | **6, eight minutes.** On a fresh timeline with period bands, place cards showing Giza construction around 2500 BCE, Middle Kingdom water management around 1850 BCE and a New Kingdom temple around 1300 BCE. Give one achievement for each kingdom. In short labels, explain farming systems, mathematics and surveying, astronomy and the 365-day calendar, papyrus and hieroglyphs. Key: Old, Middle and New Kingdom order; organized pyramid building, managed farmland, and major temple construction respectively. Managed water supported crops; measurement supported building and boundary surveys; star observations helped reckon time; the calendar organized seasons; papyrus was a surface and hieroglyphs a writing system. Practices continued across kingdoms. The day 62 and 63 exits remain formative. Wrong: all were invented in one kingdom (F); papyrus is a language (O); hieroglyphs are the sheet (T). | section 5.1 |
