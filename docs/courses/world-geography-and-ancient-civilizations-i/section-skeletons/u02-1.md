## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.6.T2.2** (days 11, 14): Identify sites showing the origins of modern humans, explain theories of how groups spread from Africa to other continents, and show how new research changes dating of migrations.
- **MA.6.T1.2** (days 12, 13): Give examples of how a current historical interpretation can build on, extend, or reject an earlier one.
- **D2.His.9.6-8** (days 12, 13): Sort the kinds of sources a later historian used in an interpretation.
- **D1.2.6-8** (days 13, 14): Explain where experts agree about how the concepts behind a big question are read and applied.
- skill ss6-changing-interpretations (grade 6)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions, drafted together.** How do interpretations change (days 11 to 14)? What changed with farming, and for whom (days 15 to 18)? What makes societies complex (days 19 to 21)? How do societies meet (day 22)?
> **Teaching.** Source accounts before comparison. Model classification on day 12, comparison on day 13 and claim, evidence and reasoning before writing on day 14. Compare Irhoud's 2007 estimate around 160,000 years with 2017's roughly 315,000 years. Explain dating without calculations. Teach tools, fire, hunting, gathering, painting and sculpture on day 15. Day 16 locates independent farming origins in Southwest Asia, China, New Guinea, Africa and the Americas; names Jericho; distinguishes domestication from taming; and places later copper working before bronze and iron. Days 19 and 20 teach every trait. Day 21 tests supported, uncertain and absent evidence. On day 23 identify agreement between the settlement and health accounts before judging better; withdraw worked answers. Section 2.1 owns all parts.
>
> - **MA.6.T2.3:** Explain that the Paleolithic Era is the earliest period of human history, about 2.6 million to 11,700 years ago, marked by stone tools, fire, hunting and gathering, and later cave painting and sculpture. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 1's timeline and unprompted sourcing. Add dates on days 11, 15 and 16. Tie claim, evidence and reasoning to writing on day 14; remove the frame on day 23. Reintroduce continuity and change on day 18; remove sorted examples on day 24. Retain the trait checklist for later units.

Error register rows (unit plan field 6) that name one of this section's days:

| B | "A research summary is an ancient eyewitness." | Confuses source layers. On day 12 contrast a tool with its caption. If many miss, sort objects, reports and summaries for ten minutes. | From memory. |
