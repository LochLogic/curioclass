## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.6.T2.1** (days 7, 8, 9): Describe the large climate and environmental changes that shaped the Earth and eventually allowed human life to grow.
- **MA.6.T2.8** (days 7, 9): Build and read a timeline of key periods in Paleolithic and Neolithic human development, correctly using terms such as era, century, millennium, CE, BCE, and circa, and knowing 3000 BCE is earlier than 2000 BCE.
- **MA.6.T1.1** (days 9): Explain how different social science fields use different approaches to study societies of the past and present.
- **MA.6.T1.3** (days 9): Give examples of how archaeologists, historians, geographers, economists, and political scientists work together to analyze evidence and interpret ancient and classical civilizations.
- **D1.1.6-8** (days 9): Show how a question gets at key ideas of a subject area.
- skill ss6-timeline-deep-time (grade 6)
- skill ss6-social-science-lenses (grade 6)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions, drafted together.** What does each field ask and need (days 1 and 2)? Why combine evidence (day 3)? How can drawings show deep time honestly (days 4 to 6)? How did environments change possibilities (days 7 and 8)?
> **Teaching.** Model observation, inference and claim on day 1. Day 3 is a constructed investigation of Ur's kingdom; its layer, river diagram and authority card are labelled "constructed". The tablet's label reads "an account from Drehem, a livestock centre in Ur's kingdom"; it does not verify them. Distribution does not prove trade. Day 7 teaches ice and coastlines. Day 8 teaches Sahara change before the claim and a brief out-of-Africa map handoff. On day 8 remove the worked example claim from display before students write.
