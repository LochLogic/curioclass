## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.6.T2.7** (days 22, 24): Explain how complex societies interact and spread to new regions through trade, cultural exchange, migration, religious conversion, conquest, or colonization.
- **D2.His.2.6-8** (days 23, 24): Sort events and developments into examples of change, continuity or both.
- **D1.2.6-8** (days 23, 24): Explain where experts agree about how the concepts behind a big question are read and applied.
- **MA.6.T1.2** (days 24): Give examples of how a current historical interpretation can build on, extend, or reject an earlier one.
- **MA.6.T2.2** (days 24): Identify sites showing the origins of modern humans, explain theories of how groups spread from Africa to other continents, and show how new research changes dating of migrations.
- **D2.His.9.6-8** (days 24): Sort the kinds of sources a later historian used in an interpretation.
- **MA.6.T2.3** (days 24): Explain that the Paleolithic Era is the earliest period of human history, about 2.6 million to 11,700 years ago, marked by stone tools, fire, hunting and gathering, and later cave painting and sculpture.
- **MA.6.T2.4** (days 24): Explain that the Neolithic Era began about 10,000 years ago and saw the invention of farming, animal domestication, and metalworking, and the first complex societies.
- **MA.6.T2.5** (days 24): Explain how farming societies differ, with some settling permanently, some nomadic with livestock, and some farming land temporarily before moving on.
- **MA.6.T2.6** (days 24): Explain that scholars have long tried to define what makes a complex society ("civilization"), and that, although debated, many point to these characteristics.
- skill ss6-complex-societies (grade 6)
- skill ss6-changing-interpretations (grade 6)
- skill ss6-paleolithic-neolithic (grade 6)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions, drafted together.** How do interpretations change (days 11 to 14)? What changed with farming, and for whom (days 15 to 18)? What makes societies complex (days 19 to 21)? How do societies meet (day 22)?
> **Teaching.** Source accounts before comparison. Model classification on day 12, comparison on day 13 and claim, evidence and reasoning before writing on day 14. Compare Irhoud's 2007 estimate around 160,000 years with 2017's roughly 315,000 years. Explain dating without calculations. Teach tools, fire, hunting, gathering, painting and sculpture on day 15. Day 16 locates independent farming origins in Southwest Asia, China, New Guinea, Africa and the Americas; names Jericho; distinguishes domestication from taming; and places later copper working before bronze and iron. Days 19 and 20 teach every trait. Day 21 tests supported, uncertain and absent evidence. On day 23 identify agreement between the settlement and health accounts before judging better; withdraw worked answers. Section 2.1 owns all parts.
