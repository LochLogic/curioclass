## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How much did these three faiths share at their beginnings, and where did they differ?"
**Hook, day 85.** Meet three attributed belief cards beside a regional map: the Hebrew Bible's covenant with Abraham, Paul's account of Abraham and the Qur'an's account of Ibrahim, the Arabic name for Abraham. What looks shared, and what must we investigate? Sources are in field 10.
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** What can scripture and archaeology each show (85 to 87)? How did Jewish life continue (88 and 89)? How did Christianity emerge and vary (90 to 92)? What do Muslim sources teach about origins and duties (93 to 95)? How far does shared ancestry explain similarities (96 and 97)?
**Closing claim, day 97.** Use Genesis, Mark, Galatians and the Qur'an comparison cards to answer both halves of the compelling question. Support a judgment about the extent of sharing, address another interpretation and name a source limit.
**Teaching.** Model attribution on 85, chronology on 87 and a sourced explanation on 90. Co-build the comparison chart; remove completed entries on 97. Teach covenant, monotheism, messiah, salvation, scripture, synagogue and hijra. Reading and writing support RH.6-8.2, RH.6-8.4 and WHST.6-8.10.

**Sensitive-content note.** Teach beliefs with attribution and describe living, internally diverse communities. Never ask students to disclose or defend their faith, recite a profession of faith, pray, fast, copy sacred declarations as a devotional act or portray a religious figure. No images of Muhammad. Roman authorities crucified Jesus; reject collective blame against Jews then or now. Name persecution, execution, destruction and displacement plainly without graphic detail or victim role play. Judaism continued after 70 CE; diaspora communities also existed before it, and not all Jews left the region. Do not present Judaism as an obsolete stage of Christianity, Islam as an imitation, or a Christian formulation as every Christian's. Stop stereotypes and return to attributed evidence. Offer a private written response. Period labels such as Canaan, Israel, Judah, Judaea and Palestine require dates; ancient and modern political boundaries differ. An uncertain archaeological identification stays uncertain. No holiday, civic-symbol or current legal claim is taught here.
