## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the Nile make Egypt, and what can we know of Kush from records others wrote?"
**Hook, day 56.** Turn a Nile map sideways beside an original Giza settlement schematic labelled 'reconstruction, not an excavation photograph', grounded in the excavation account. Could a river build this? Open the compelling question before locating places.
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** What did water enable and people organize (56 and 57)? Whose Egypt does royal art preserve (58 to 61)? What changed across kingdoms (62 and 63)? How can we investigate Kush beyond Egyptian accounts (64 to 67)?
**Closing claim, day 68.** Use the Nile map, Giza excavation account, Rekhmire painting and Kushite monuments to answer both halves. Explain human choices, weigh a competing claim and name a limit. 
**Teaching.** Model river direction on 56, perspective on 58 and claim, evidence and reasoning on 59. Teach each source's setting before reading. On 65 retrieve sourcing without prompts; on 66 model corroboration across kinds, then withdraw the completed chart. Students choose evidence on 68. Summarizing, vocabulary and sustained writing support RH.6-8.2, RH.6-8.4, RH.6-8.10 and WHST.6-8.10.

Otherwise teach a group inside supported work while others retrieve earlier work. Follow with an independent fresh retry. No day is added.

**Teacher notes for sensitive content.** Name slavery as ownership and forced labor; captives had lives and agency beyond their captivity. Giza's organized workforce challenges the all-enslaved story without proving every worker freely chose service. Teach religion as history, with no declarations recited by students, devotional practice, personal faith disclosures, mummy making or victim and conqueror role play. Egyptian and Kushite communities were African and internally diverse; painted skin is not a modern racial census. Nubian peoples live today, and their languages are not interchangeable with Meroitic. Attribute modern claims to their particular community, without asking a child to represent it. If a stereotype or faith disagreement arises, stop the generalization, return to the dated evidence and allow a private written response. Discuss human remains with dignity through object descriptions rather than graphic images.
