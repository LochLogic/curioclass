# Course frame: World Geography and Ancient Civilizations I

Course id world-geo-6, grade 6, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 11 units, in the anchor's topic order (6.T1 to 6.T5c): how we study the past, early humans, then
  Western Asia, the Middle East and North Africa, then Africa, then Central America, the Caribbean and South America.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units, 12 for course-level checks and respond days (three checks of 2 days,
  each followed by 2 respond days). A full year, one period a day, five days a week; the scope and sequence states
  the minutes (the pick in "For the lead" is 45).
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 grade 6 (MA.6.T1 to MA.6.T5c) is the content anchor: every one of the 67 codes
  is owned by one of the 29 grade 6 progression rows, so every code sits in one unit below. A parent code carries
  its lettered parts where the rows cite only the parent (MA.6.T2.6 carries 6a to 6g, the seven traits, all taught
  in unit 2). C3 6-8 indicators carry the skills (the C3 column). Each unit plan compares the unit with California,
  Tennessee and New York and records any addition with its source; the "Check set adds" column names the candidates
  this frame found, from the writer's reading, each to be confirmed in that unit's comparison table. Check-set
  names never appear in student, teacher or family copy. The values rule (a to d) governs every contested claim;
  religions are taught as history, each scripture named as its followers' account.
- **Base text.** None (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md: every day is Curio Class's own). Partial bases that may serve a
  unit, taken unit by unit and checked item by item for third-party material: Oak National Academy history and
  geography units (Open Government Licence v3.0, credit line only, written for England and for younger pupils at
  key stage 2, so reworded and reframed): Stone Age (unit 2), Sumer (unit 4), Ancient Egypt (unit 5), Maya (unit
  11), key stage 3 geography Middle East (unit 3) and Nigeria (unit 9). Public domain and CC0 items (National
  Archives, Library of Congress, museum open-access images) are checked one by one. Massachusetts's Investigating
  History units and the New York toolkit are reference only, never copied. The frame maps no base lesson.
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit has a compelling question (the table) with supporting questions set by the unit
  plan, runs the C3 arc, and ends with an end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from a source the student has
  not seen. Content is taught directly and in time order inside the arc; the one break in order (Islam's origins
  in unit 7, beside Judaism and Christianity) follows the anchor's grouping of the region's faiths.
- **The timeline spine.** The deep-time timeline built in unit 1 (ss6-timeline-deep-time) is the year's spine:
  every unit adds its dates to the class timeline, with BCE and CE and no year zero.
- **The regional frame.** Unit 3 sets the frame used for every region (location, landforms, climate and resources
  lead to settlement, population and economy); units 9, 10 and 11 apply it with less support.
- **Informed action.** Once this year, in unit 3 (ss6-mena-settlement): the class picks a local water or energy
  question, gathers public facts, decides one small step, shares it through the school and looks back. The look-back
  is one of unit 3's days, held and taught about four weeks later, at the start of unit 5. Action is the students' choice informed by
  evidence, never a cause the course picks.
- **Writing from sources.** Grade 5's sourcing routine (who, when, for whom, why) is used unprompted from unit 1, and
  unit 2 sources its secondary accounts before comparing them; short written claims from unit 2 on; the first full
  sourcing step on an ancient source, at grade 5's depth, in unit 4 (Hammurabi's code and prologue); the first
  research report in unit 8; a longer report in unit 11. Writing grows from short to long across the year.
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own texts at grade 6 level. Ancient sources are short translated excerpts (a few
  lines of a law code, an inscription, a scripture passage) with a glossary and a stated difficulty; images and
  objects are read as sources. Each source has a rights line (bar item 2).
- **Sensitive content.** Slavery in every ancient society that held it, named plainly with no role play; religions
  taught as history with no devotional practice asked; human origins from the scientific record with no student
  asked to state a belief; Indigenous peoples of the Caribbean and the Americas in the present tense, from their own
  materials, with sacred or restricted knowledge left out. Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read (bar
  item 12). A reader from the community concerned reads units 4, 5, 6 and 9 (slavery in ancient and African
  societies, and how African peoples are shown) and units 10 and 11 (Native peoples of the Caribbean and the
  Americas) (FOUNDATION section 7).
