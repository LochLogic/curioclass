## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we know anything about people who lived thousands of years ago?"
**Hook, day 1.** Show the Dugga tablet and an original Ur and Drehem map. What could these marks reveal? Locate Drehem in Ur's kingdom; the tablet was not found at Ur.
**Supporting questions, drafted together.** What does each field ask and need (days 1 and 2)? Why combine evidence (day 3)? How can drawings show deep time honestly (days 4 to 6)? How did environments change possibilities (days 7 and 8)?
**Closing claim, day 8.** Answer from the Dugga tablet and Beringia reconstruction, explaining an inference and limit. Claims may emphasize records or environments. Address the competing claim that one source tells everything.
**Teaching.** Model observation, inference and claim on day 1; students justify sorts on day 2. Day 3 combines a dated layer, tablet, river course, transaction record and royal dating to test river access and distribution at Ur. Preserve findspots; distribution does not prove trade. Withdraw the worked claim on day 8. Teach field names and timeline terms.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 9 (End-of-unit assessment): End-of-unit assessment: questions sorted by field with what each asks and what evidence it needs, one example of fields combining their evidence on an unseen site plan, events placed on the scale timeline across BCE and CE with era, century, millennium and circa, one climate change explained for what it let people do, and a short written answer to the unit's question from the site plan

**Assessment source pack, unseen until day 9.** Print an original diagram titled "Canal Site: constructed practice case, not an actual excavation." A canal runs along its west edge; a storehouse stands beside it; two houses stand east of the storehouse. Report cards state that excavators dated the rooms to the same period, a record keeper wrote that wool was received from herders, and the storehouse door carried a royal seal. These invented cards test combining evidence; never attribute them to Ur. Beside them, print the real animal-receipt image and basic catalogue facts: Drehem, circa 2041 BCE, a receipt for a bull and sheep. Ask students to identify this as a real ancient source, distinguish its modern caption, and explain why it cannot verify the invented site's location. Correct: the caption describes the object, but the object comes from Drehem and has no established connection to Canal Site. Treat a claimed connection as G. Reserve the diagram, record, object, climate cards and dates for assessment; instruction and exits use different cases. Allow about eight minutes each for items 1, 2 and 4, four for item 3 and eight for item 5. The source distinction is included with item 2.

**Scoring and samples.** Each numbered part receives 2 when its listed look-fors are present, 1 for accurate but incomplete reasoning, and 0 for the corresponding error. Record parts separately, not only a total. These are planning anchors, ours, untested.

- **Item 1 look-fors:** all five fields, appropriate evidence, distinct approaches, and a past-to-present comparison. Meets: "An archaeologist studies layers and objects; a historian tests written accounts and their makers; a geographer maps location and routes; an economist studies production and distribution; a political scientist studies authority and rules. To study a town today, they could use excavated objects, council records, route maps, accounts and laws. Some sources overlap, but the questions differ." Does not meet: "They all dig for old things; present-day towns do not need these fields" (A).
- **Item 2 look-fors:** contributions from all five fields, an explicit combination, a bounded conclusion, and evidence used in the closing claim. Meets: "The archaeologist dates the rooms; the historian reads the wool record; the geographer maps canal access; the economist connects wool receipts to distribution; the political scientist examines the royal seal. Together the plan and record support movement of goods under official oversight, but do not prove every household benefited. We can reconstruct some activities by combining evidence; we cannot recover every life." Does not meet: "The king's seal proves everyone was rich, so nothing else is needed" (B, G).
- **Item 3 look-fors:** identify authority as the key idea and explain why a question belongs to political science. Meets: "Who authorized the wool collection? asks about authority and control, so it is a political-science question; the official seal could help answer it." Does not meet: "It is history because it happened long ago" (A).
- **Item 4 look-fors:** proportional distances, correct order and labels, all six terms, and the counted cross-boundary span. Meets: "The Paleolithic card is 1 centimeter from the start and the Neolithic card is 6 centimeters from it. Circa means approximately. They represent different eras; 1450 BCE is in the fifteenth century BCE and second millennium BCE. 250 CE is in the third century CE and first millennium CE. Four BCE precedes three BCE, two BCE, one BCE, one CE, two CE and three CE: six year intervals." Does not meet: "Space every card equally, put 6000 BCE first, and count year zero for seven years" (C, D, E).
- **Item 5 look-fors:** place all supplied changes in order; describe ice, sea level, coastlines and Sahara moisture; connect a changed resource or route to a possible human response. Meets: "The colder stage came first, followed by warming and rising water, then the drier record. Water stored as ice lowered seas and exposed land; melting later submerged coastlines. More rain supported lakes, plants and animals in parts of the Sahara; drying reduced those resources. The supplied dry-lake record supports people moving toward remaining water, but cannot identify everyone's route." Does not meet: "Warmer weather always exposes more land, and climate forced everyone to move by the same route" (F).

Read sources aloud, enlarge print and maps, or accept a scribed or oral response; students still supply the explanations and placements unaided.
