# Section 3.2: Parents and young, alike and different

## Header


Parents and young, alike and different

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS1-1, this section's share (day 42).** "Make models showing that living things have many different life cycles, yet all are born, grow, reproduce and die." Day 42 uses the day 41 models, not a new one: a seed-packet card is read beside the class record, the next stage is predicted, and harvest is told apart from reproduction.
- **3-LS3-1, this section's share (days 43 to 46).** "Use data as evidence that plants and animals get traits from their parents and that members of a similar group differ in those traits." Day 43 asks the question and teaches the words; day 44 analyzes plant data; day 45 animal data; day 46 practises graphing the tallies. Section 3.4 owns the assessed item.
- **Limits that bite here.** No genetic mechanism, no predicting a future trait, no inference of parentage from looks. Non-human examples only; no child is asked about family resemblance or adoption. A packet picture names a kind, not the actual parents of the class plants. Plant heights in whole centimetres (ruler step 1 cm). Graphs count whole numbers, one square for one; scaled graphs are not needed.
- **Connections.** m2-measure-with-tools (day 44, the class pairs); the grade 2 bar graph, reused with "how many" on the side (days 44 to 46); math 3.MD.B.3 is not required.
- **Practices.** Analyzing and interpreting data (days 44 to 46): tally, graph, then a claim with the observation that supports it.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a trait is a feature we can look at and record; inherited traits are passed from parents to young; members of one kind differ in the same trait. The data show a pattern in a group and cannot say what any one young will be. Looking alike does not show whose young it is; a family record does. Why siblings differ is beyond this grade and is not predicted. Some traits also depend on surroundings (section 3.3).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Item 3 (day 57) belongs to section 3.4. Rule 4: the radish, cat, spinach, fox, pansy and gerbil cards are new to the unit; the goat, nasturtium, puppy and rabbit families and the class seedlings are taught and never used as exits.

**Exit questions.** Cards, "invented for practice", read aloud. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E42 (radish).** Card: sown day 0, sprouts day 4, roots ready to pull day 28; our radishes are on day 15. "What stage comes next? Say why." Answer: roots ready to pull on day 28, 13 days away, after sprouts. Wrong: "The radish makes new radish plants next" (B). Spoken: Max says picking spinach leaves means new plants were made; "What would you need to see?" Answer: seeds made by the plant and a seed beginning to grow.
- **E43 (cat family record; fox picture spoken).** Card: a family record names Mom cat and Dad cat; three kittens have pointed ears like their parents, one has white paws. "Why are the kittens alike yet different? Use trait and inherited." Answer: pointed ears is an inherited trait; white paws is a variation; young need not match in every trait. Wrong: "The kitten with white paws is not their kitten" (F). Spoken: two red foxes beside a fox cub; "What would show they are the parents?" Answer: a family record that names them. Wrong: "That they look alike" (G).
- **E44 (pansy).** Record: pansy parent plants have ruffled petals. Their six offspring all have ruffled petals. Petal widths in whole centimetres: 4, 4, 5, 5, 5, 6. "What is alike? What is different? How do you know?" Answer: ruffled petals are alike (a likeness from the parents); widths differ, 4 to 6 (variation). Wrong: "All the offspring must be 5 centimetres wide" (F).
- **E45 (gerbil).** Record: gerbil parents have long tails. Their four pups all have long tails; two have sandy coats and two gray. A gray gerbil in the next cage looks like the gray pups. (a) "All four pups have long tails like their parents." (b) "The gray gerbil in the next cage is the pups' father." "Which does the record support? Which goes beyond it?" Answer: (a) is supported; (b) goes beyond the record. Wrong: "(b) is supported because they look alike" (G).
- **E46 (section exit, 10 minutes).** Given on the unit plan, field 5. Squash parents and all six offspring have broad leaves; offspring leaf-size tallies are small 2, medium 3, large 1. Duck parents and all six ducklings have broad bills; offspring bill-size tallies are short 1, medium 2, long 3. "Make a labelled bar graph for each family's size tallies. What inherited likeness and within-kind variation does each family record support, and what evidence shows each?" Expected: named size categories, a labelled count axis, bar heights 2, 3, 1 and 1, 2, 3; broad leaves and bills show likeness, the tallies show variation. Wrong: a family must match in every size (F). Graph mistakes get descriptive feedback.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Grade 1's pattern, in the ledger's words: young share features with their parents but are not exact copies, and the frame "alike in ___; different in ___". It is revised on days 43 and 46 with the words trait, inherited, likeness and variation, and with graphs. The day 41 life-cycle models stay on the wall and are used on day 42. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, day 44: real measuring, supplied family.** Ideal instrument: plants grown from one documented parent, measured by the class. Spec: the class seedlings and a ruler (step 1 cm) fit for variation; no documented parent exists in the class, so the parent-young record is supplied (invented for practice) and weaker. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 45: animal families.** Ideal instrument: documented litters. Supplied cards fit partly, said aloud to be invented.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 44: 12 plants in six groups, one ruler a group; the 8-minute measure part fits (each pair reads one plant, about 1 minute a plant). Weather 5 minutes on days 42, 45 and 46. Rotating six heard at each exit: 6 children at 30 seconds is 3 minutes inside the exit.
- **Fallback.** Plants fail or the heights are too close to graph: the day uses the supplied class record, said aloud to be invented, and the day 44 graph is made from it.
- **A room with nothing.** Cards and tally paper only; both families are supplied.

## 6. Days

## Day 42


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Day 41's letters, 2 minutes each.
- **4 min. Readiness item 2 again.** Two sunflower seedlings differ in height but share the parents' leaf shape; each child writes why a height difference does not rule out the same kind (replacing duplicate reporting).
- **15 min. Seed-packet timing.** Candidate kept. A packet-style card (invented for practice, not packet text): "Sown day 0; sprouts day 6; leaves ready to harvest day 24." Pairs set its days beside the dated class record, predict the next stage after sprouting (more leaf growth is supported), and say what the card does not show. Invented timings are not treated as observations.
- **8 min. Where does the next generation come from?** Pairs sort evidence cards: leaves picked, a pod with seeds, a seed beginning to grow, a flower. Only seeds that begin to grow show a new plant. The class pods are looked at if ripe; only seen stages are marked solid.

- **3 min. Exit.** E42.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sets a packet-style card beside the class record and sorts evidence cards; the exit predicts the next stage from a radish card and says what to see before picked leaves count as new plants.

Held by: teacher (board); children (cards). Tool: paper life-cycle model (light): show why, 6 min (the sort, with day 41 models). By hand: card, sort. Evidence: E42; the readiness item 2 sheet. Hand-backs out: B to day 43.

## Day 43


- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 42, B.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Review of grade 1, in the ledger's words: a goat family card (invented for practice, parents named): pairs say one thing alike and one different, "alike in ___; different in ___".
- **7 min. Hook: same kind, not the same.** The class seedlings' recorded heights in a row beside the packet picture of the kind. "What is alike? What is different? Why might that be?" Ideas are kept on the board, not corrected.
- **12 min. Teach the words.** On the goat card: trait (a feature we can look at and record), inherited (passed from parents to young), likeness (a trait the young share with their parents), variation (a trait that differs among young of one kind). A family record names the parents; a picture of a kind does not. We do not predict which young gets which.
- **8 min. Sort the traits.** Pairs sort the goat family's trait cards into likeness and variation, then do the same for the class seedlings (leaf shape and height).
- **5 min. Revise the resemblance chart.** Second colour: the grade 1 pattern chart gains the four words.
- **3 min. Question board.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E43.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts goat family and seedling trait cards with the new words in pairs; the exit explains a cat family's likeness and difference alone using trait and inherited, and says what would show parents.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (cards). Tool: None. By hand: cards, chart. Evidence: E43. Hand-backs out: G and F to day 44.

## Day 44

- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 43, G and F, 2 minutes each.
- **5 min. Graph words.** Review of grade 2: a bar graph on squared paper. Here one square is one plant and the height of a bar says how many; the teacher models a tally chart (one mark a plant) on a practice set.
- **8 min. Measure the class pairs.** Each group measures its plant pair to the whole centimetre without uprooting; pairs read one plant each, about a minute a plant.
- **8 min. Graph the class heights.** The 12 readings are tallied into three bands the teacher sets from the data and drawn as a bar graph. The packet picture gives only the kind, so this shows variation.
- **10 min. A family with a record.** Nasturtium parents have round leaves; their six offspring all have round leaves; leaf widths in whole centimetres: 7, 7, 7, 7, 8, 9 (invented for practice, parent named; parent-plant photograph). Pairs tally, graph and write: alike in ___; different in ___.

- **5 min. Exit.** E44.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice measures class plants, graphs heights and reads a nasturtium record with the teacher; the exit has each child alone say what is alike and what differs in a pansy record.

Held by: teacher (graph words); children (own graph). Tool: None. By hand: ruler, tally, graph. Evidence: E44; the class graph. Hand-backs out: F to day 45.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: trait, inherited, alike, different, record, tally. Frames: "Alike in ___; different in ___." "The record shows ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Large-print tally paper with raised lines; a partner reads the ruler aloud; dictation for the claim.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card aloud; accept a spoken or pointed answer and a partner-drawn graph; a scribe.
- **Reading load.** Cards under 50 words, no sentence over 15; the teacher reads each aloud first.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a second graph for another trait of the same family and say whether it shows likeness or variation.
