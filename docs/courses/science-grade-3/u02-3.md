# Section 2.3: Putting pushes and pulls across a gap to work


## Header


Putting pushes and pulls across a gap to work

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-PS2-3, this section's share (days 29, 31, 33).** "Ask questions to find what causes and changes electric or magnetic pushes and pulls between two objects that are not touching." Day 29 joins the evidence into one class model; day 31 asks questions about unfamiliar static and magnet records; day 33 revises them.
- **3-PS2-4, this section's share (days 29, 30, 32, 33).** "Name a simple design problem that ideas about magnets could help solve, such as a door latch." Day 30 critiques a new statement; day 32 defines a car-guide problem from supplied records.
- **3-5-ETS1-1, this section's share (days 29, 30, 32, 33).** "Name a simple design problem that meets a need or want, with set measures of success and limits on materials, time or cost." Day 32 asks for the need, two or more measurable criteria and every limit.
- **Limits that bite here.** Static electricity is comb and cloth only; no student circuits. The cars case is an invented record, with no promise that a magnet protects at every speed or gap. No comparing many solutions (grade 4) and no fair test of a prototype (grade 5; day 34 starts that). Users are hypothetical; no child reports on home. Children say "limit".
- **Connections.** Arrows and cause and effect (section 2.1, days 17 to 22); the cupboard latch (section 2.2, days 25 to 28). Day 34 opens a fair test on a tray object; the day 31 records go forward to day 35 unchanged.
- **Practices.** Asking questions and defining problems (all days); constructing explanations from evidence (day 29).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: two magnets attract or repel by which ends face, while a magnet pulls steel whichever end faces it; a rubbed plastic comb pulls light paper, weaker with distance; gravity and the thread's pull are two more pushes and pulls on the clip. A design problem has a need, checkable criteria and limits (materials, time, cost); a changed user changes the criteria. Questions are testable when they name one change and one thing to observe.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** items 1, 2 and 3 (table above), in full as written there. Rule 4: the comb-and-tissue arrangement, the car guide, and the four-magnet and token case are new to the unit; the cupboard, pencil holder and pickup tool are never used.
- **E29 (day 29).** **E29a.** Card: a rubbed pen lifts salt grains from 1 cm and a magnet lifts a steel pin from 1 cm. "What is the same about the two? What is different?" Answer: same, both pull across a gap with no touching; different, the pen works only after rubbing and the magnet pulls steel. Wrong: "Neither pulls unless they touch" (A). **E29b.** Card: a brass key and a steel key beside a magnet; Pat says both are pulled because both are metal. "Is Pat right?" Answer: no; only the steel one is pulled. Wrong: "Yes, all metals" (B).
- **E30 (day 30).** Card: "The art room needs a rack to hold six brushes upright all morning." **E30a.** Statement: "Use two magnets and tape." "Can we judge a proposal with this? Why?" Answer: no; it lists things, not who needs what, and nothing to check. Wrong: "Yes, it says what to use" (E). **E30b.** Statement lists a materials limit only. "Name the two other kinds of limit." Answer: time and cost. Wrong: "Nothing else is needed" (F). **E30c.** Proposal holds six brushes but takes ten making minutes; the limit is six. "Does it meet the problem?" Answer: no; it is over the time limit. Wrong: "Yes, it holds six" (F).

A blank or off-topic answer is filed as an incomplete component, not a letter. Item 1 (day 31) wrong claims: contact needed (A), turning makes steel repel (C), changing gap and turning together (D). Item 2 (day 32): every metal repels (B), steel reverses when turned (C). Item 3 (day 32): naming equipment alone (E), ignoring the token allowance (F).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Arrows and cause and effect from 2.1; the cupboard statement from 2.2. Day 29 joins them into one class consensus model: objects interact across gaps; magnets attract or repel by which ends face, while rubbed plastic pulls light paper and steel is pulled by either end. Gravity and the thread's pull are added to the clip drawing. The model is used independently on days 31 and 32 and revised on day 33. Review, in the ledger's words: force arrow, cause and effect, fair test, limit, tradeoff, return and repair. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, day 31: new records.** Ideal instrument: a charge sensor and a force meter, beyond a classroom. Supplied records (comb-and-tissue photographs, magnet evidence cards) fit partly; they are weaker than handling and are said to be. The Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, spec only) does not exist and partly fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 32: the car guide.** Ideal instrument: a track with a speed gate. The supplied records fit partly: three gentle approaches only. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md#non-contact-interactions).
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 29: class model on the board, 10 minutes of pollination by the rotating six. Day 31: six groups of five, each heard 4 minutes (about 48 seconds a child), cards and goggles at hand, 5 minutes clean-up (magnets and clips counted, combs and tissue bits collected). Day 32: paper only. Weather record 5 minutes on days 29, 30, 33.
- **Fallback.** No physical magnets or combs: day 31 runs wholly on records, and says so aloud. No flowering plants: the day 29 pollination slot becomes a swab practice on a drawn flower, stated weaker.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper records and cards only; every assessment prompt is read aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 29


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 28, E and F, 2 minutes each.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **10 min. Pollination (set-up for unit 3), with the consensus chart.** The rotating six work in pairs at the flowering pots, one swab per plant, allergies checked, hands washed after (2 minutes inside the 10). The rest, in pairs, sort the unit's records into a chart: across a gap, magnet to magnet, magnet to steel, rubbed plastic to paper, and write one sentence for each column.
- **10 min. Interaction record.** Each child draws the hung tack and the magnet, with force arrows, gravity and the thread's pull; writes four testable questions (static distance, magnet distance, turning, steel swap); and reads a 3 cm gap with a whole-centimetre ruler (2 minutes inside the 10).
- **10 min. Lid record.** Each child writes the need, two criteria, all three limits, the materials, and the gap and facing ends for a lid latch, with an arrow across the gap.
- **3 min. Exit.** E29a, E29b.
- **1 min. Question board.** Group into distance, materials, turning and uses; mark answered ones.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the hung tack with three arrows and writes a lid record; the exit compares a rubbed pen with a magnet, and says why a brass key is not pulled, which the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (chart, board); children (own model). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): show why, 12 min (consensus model). By hand: chart, arrows. Evidence: E29a, E29b; the revised day 29 record. Hand-backs out: E and F to day 30.

## Day 31

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: gap, pull, push, criterion, limit, token. Frames: "I ask ___ because ___." "The limit is ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Dictation allowed; large-print cards; a partner moves materials.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card aloud; accept pointing or drawing; a scribe for statements.
- **Reading load.** Cards under 60 words; no sentence over 15.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second criterion and say how it pulls against the first.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 29: a magnet, a steel spoon and a rubbed comb; say what is the same. Day 30: rewrite a statement with the need, criteria and limits. Days 31 and 32: ask your own question about new cards; write a statement from the cards. Last day of the unit: fix one question and one statement.
- **One projector.** Show the model, cards and statement; children write their own.
