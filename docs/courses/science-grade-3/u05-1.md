# Section 5.1: Reading a place's past

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 3. Unit 5, Environments change. "How do we know places have changed, and what happens to living things when they do?" Anchor: fossils of tropical plants, ferns and palms, found in rock in the Arctic on Svalbard, Norway, where no such plant grows now (a teacher may swap in a fossil found in the class's own state); the sea fossil on a mountain is grade 6 unit 4's anchor, so it is not used here (days 82 to 97): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 82 to 86.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (82, 83, 85), application 2 (84, 86). - **3-LS4-1, this section's share: all of it (days 82 to 86).** "Use fossil data as evidence of what lived long ago and what their environments were like." The practice is "Analyze and interpret data": children read a find's kind, size and place, say what is seen and what is inferred, and revise one model. Section 5.3 revises it again and checks it.
- **3-LS4-4, day 83 only.** "Judge, with a claim, how well a solution works for a problem caused when an environment changes and so may the plants and animals living there." Day 83 holds only the kindergarten reduce-impact reteach from the day 81 readiness item. The standard is taught in section 5.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No fossil or species names: only major kinds (plant print, shell, track, bone, trunk). Older and younger only, never dates. Extinct means gone everywhere. Deeper is older only in undisturbed layers. Size alone cannot give a climate. No plate motion, no climate change, no greenhouse effect. One change at a time.
- **Connections.** ELA RI.3.7 (pictures, maps and charts), days 82 and 86, with the shared arrows-and-labels routine. Grade 2 fast and slow land change, day 82 retrieval and day 84. Unit 4's needs meet conditions, day 83. Unit 3's "shows, does not show, cannot tell yet", days 83 and 86. Scale and quantity, stability and change: day 85.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The Svalbard trunks grew when that land was warm and near the equator; the land has since moved. Do not imply it was always Arctic, and do not teach the mechanism. (2) A guide card works because living things' needs have stayed steady; that is the "steady patterns" idea, stated, not proved. (3) A fossil piece gives at least the size of the whole. (4) Extinct is a claim about everywhere, so it needs a statement from a guide. (5) Deep time is stated plainly, with no numbers. (6) If a child asks about family belief, answer with what the evidence shows; no one is asked for a belief.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 5.3 owns the items on days 95 and 96. This section writes the day 86 product F (12 minutes); it is scored outside class only after its day 93 revision.

**Product F, day 86 (unit plan field 5 holds the points).** Card, "invented for practice": an unfamiliar grassland site with undisturbed layers. Lower layer: complete freshwater-fish fossils 18 centimetres long and water-plant leaf prints 6 centimetres long, at two mapped places. A guide says this fish form is extinct worldwide, though other fish exist, and that these finds go with lake water. Upper layer: rooted trunk pieces 80 centimetres long, which the guide links to trees growing on land. Children annotate their past-environment model and explain what the kinds, sizes and places support, what changed, which layer is older, and why a week's weather cannot explain this. Written feedback follows day 86; revision is day 93.

**Exit questions.** Each is on a fresh card, "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" opens with that letter's wrong example.
- **E82 (3 minutes).** "A fish print and a shell print are in a rock on a dry hill. Draw the hill long ago. Label what each print shows." Answer: water with a fish and a shell, each labeled with its print; water was there long ago, though the hill is dry now. Wrong: "It was always a dry hill" (A).
- **E83 (3 minutes).** "A print of a dragonfly wing, 3 centimetres long, is in rock at the foot of a hill. A guide card says dragonflies like this live near ponds and slow streams. Write one thing you see and one thing you infer." Answer: sees a wing print 3 centimetres long in rock at the foot of a hill; infers a pond or slow stream was near. Wrong: "There is no pond here now, so there never was one" (A); "A 3 centimetre wing shows it was hot" (F).
- **E84 (6 minutes).** "A desert quarry layer holds prints of broad leaves 20 centimetres long and bark. A guide card says trees with leaves like these grow in rainy forests. What do the prints and card support about this place long ago, and what do they not show?" Answer: a wet forest once grew there; they do not show how hot it was, or the whole tree's size. Wrong: "It is a desert, so a forest could never grow here" (A); "Leaves that big prove it was boiling hot" (F).
- **E85 (3 minutes, two short items).** (a) "A landslide turned a rock block upside down. Now a seed-pod layer is on the bottom and a bone layer is on top. Is the seed-pod layer the older one?" Answer: we cannot say; the layers are no longer in the order they were laid. Wrong: "Yes, the bottom layer is always older" (C). (b) "A card says a certain shell kind lives in other lakes, but none are found in Lake Pine. Is that kind extinct?" Answer: No; extinct means gone everywhere, and it is only missing here. Wrong: "Yes, none live here, so it is extinct" (B).
- **E86 (5 minutes, unit plan field 5).** A map marks freshwater-snail fossils 2 centimetres long at a western site and land-animal tracks 9 centimetres long at an eastern site. A guide states these snails lived in freshwater and these tracks were made on land. "What do the kinds, sizes and places support about past organisms and environments, and what remains uncertain?" Answer: freshwater snails of that length at the west and land animals making those tracks at the east; track length is not body length; the map cannot show whether both places existed together, the water's full extent, an exact date or a climate from size. Wrong: present dryness proves permanent dryness (A).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: fossil, kind, size, place, older, younger, extinct, layer. Frames: "The fossil shows ___. I infer ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged drawings; tactile layer cards; dictation; partners may read cards aloud. Weather helper roles can be taken seated.

- **Reading load.** Cards 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, read aloud by the teacher.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one thing a find cannot show, and the find needed.
