## Header


Reading a place's past

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS4-1, this section's share: all of it (days 82 to 86).** "Use fossil data as evidence of what lived long ago and what their environments were like." The practice is "Analyze and interpret data": children read a find's kind, size and place, say what is seen and what is inferred, and revise one model. Section 5.3 revises it again and checks it.
- **3-LS4-4, day 83 only.** "Judge, with a claim, how well a solution works for a problem caused when an environment changes and so may the plants and animals living there." Day 83 holds only the kindergarten reduce-impact reteach from the day 81 readiness item. The standard is taught in section 5.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No fossil or species names: only major kinds (plant print, shell, track, bone, trunk). Older and younger only, never dates. Extinct means gone everywhere. Deeper is older only in undisturbed layers. Size alone cannot give a climate. No plate motion, no climate change, no greenhouse effect. One change at a time.
- **Connections.** ELA RI.3.7 (pictures, maps and charts), days 82 and 86, with the shared arrows-and-labels routine. Grade 2 fast and slow land change, day 82 retrieval and day 84. Unit 4's needs meet conditions, day 83. Unit 3's "shows, does not show, cannot tell yet", days 83 and 86. Scale and quantity, stability and change: day 85.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The Svalbard trunks grew when that land was warm and near the equator; the land has since moved. Do not imply it was always Arctic, and do not teach the mechanism. (2) A guide card works because living things' needs have stayed steady; that is the "steady patterns" idea, stated, not proved. (3) A fossil piece gives at least the size of the whole. (4) Extinct is a claim about everywhere, so it needs a statement from a guide. (5) Deep time is stated plainly, with no numbers. (6) Teach the evidence plainly. If a child says their family sees it differently, the teacher says: "People bring different ideas. In science class we look at what the rocks and fossils show." No one is asked for a belief.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 5.3 owns the items on days 95 and 96. This section writes the day 86 product F (12 minutes); it is scored outside class only after its day 93 revision.

**Product F, day 86 (unit plan field 5 holds the points).** Card, "invented for practice": an unfamiliar grassland site with undisturbed layers. Lower layer: complete freshwater-fish fossils 18 centimetres long and water-plant leaf prints 6 centimetres long, at two mapped places. A guide says this fish form is extinct worldwide, though other fish exist, and that these finds go with lake water. Upper layer: rooted trunk pieces 80 centimetres long, which the guide links to trees growing on land. Children annotate their past-environment model and explain what the kinds, sizes and places support, what changed, which layer is older, and why a week's weather cannot explain this. After day 86, a six-tick look-for checklist and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; revision is day 93, one change for each look-for marked not yet. The revised product earns a repair mark beside the day 95 score, not the score.

**Exit questions.** Each is on a fresh card, "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" opens with that letter's wrong example.
- **E82 (3 minutes).** "A fish print and a shell print are in a rock on a dry hill. Draw the hill long ago. Label what each print shows." Answer: water with a fish and a shell, each labeled with its print; water was there long ago, though the hill is dry now. Wrong: "It was always a dry hill" (A).
- **E83 (3 minutes).** "A print of a dragonfly wing, 3 centimetres long, is in rock at the foot of a hill. A guide card says dragonflies like this live near ponds and slow streams. Write one thing you see and one thing you infer." Answer: sees a wing print 3 centimetres long in rock at the foot of a hill; infers a pond or slow stream was near. Wrong: "There is no pond here now, so there never was one" (A); "A 3 centimetre wing shows it was hot" (F).
- **E84 (6 minutes).** "A desert quarry layer holds prints of broad leaves 20 centimetres long and bark. A guide card says trees with leaves like these grow in rainy forests. What do the prints and card support about this place long ago, and what do they not show?" Answer: a wet forest once grew there; they do not show how hot it was, or the whole tree's size. Wrong: "It is a desert, so a forest could never grow here" (A); "Leaves that big prove it was boiling hot" (F).
- **E85 (3 minutes, two short items).** (a) "A landslide turned a rock block upside down. Now a seed-pod layer is on the bottom and a bone layer is on top. Is the seed-pod layer the older one?" Answer: we cannot say; the layers are no longer in the order they were laid. Wrong: "Yes, the bottom layer is always older" (C). (b) "A card says a certain shell kind lives in other lakes, but none are found in Lake Pine. Is that kind extinct?" Answer: No; extinct means gone everywhere, and it is only missing here. Wrong: "Yes, none live here, so it is extinct" (B).
- **E86 (5 minutes, unit plan field 5).** A map marks freshwater-snail fossils 2 centimetres long at a western site and land-animal tracks 9 centimetres long at an eastern site. A guide states these snails lived in freshwater and these tracks were made on land. "What do the kinds, sizes and places support about past organisms and environments, and what remains uncertain?" Answer: freshwater snails of that length at the west and land animals making those tracks at the east; track length is not body length; the map cannot show whether both places existed together, the water's full extent, an exact date or a climate from size. Wrong: present dryness proves permanent dryness (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** (1) The class weather log, five minutes on days 82, 83, 85 and 86. (2) The paper past-environment model: drawn in pencil on day 82 from the table of finds (kind, size, place); marked with solid lines for what a find shows and dotted lines for what is inferred on day 83 (review of unit 3's seen and pictured boxes); redone for a dry place on day 84; given layers, older and younger, and extinct forms on day 85; read against a regional record on day 86. It is revised in a second color and keeps the first drawing. (3) The wall table of finds, one row a find. (4) The question board, sorted into past evidence, changed needs and useful responses; the third group is answered in 5.2.
- **Key moment 1, day 85: layers in order.** Ideal instrument: a viewer that shows a new find landing in a layer and the picture changing. The Curio Class past-environment viewer (light, show why, 3 minutes) is spec only and partly fits: it shows one change at a time but cannot show deep time. Paper prediction cards fit. Gaps and asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Equipment and minutes.** Weather: two helpers from the rotating six read the non-glass thermometer (step as marked on the scale, usually 2 degrees, read to the nearest mark; below zero said in words) and the rain jar (water or none; a depth only if it reaches 1 centimetre) for 30 seconds each, while the class fills the dated row; the adult checks conditions, empties the jar and handles any ice; helpers wash at two sinks in the clean-up minute. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud and say it was supplied. Cards: 15 pair sets of statement cards (day 83) and of five layer cards (day 85); paper only, collected in the clean-up.
- **A room with nothing.** The teacher draws on the board; cards are printed; weather comes from the dated adult record read aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 82


- **8 min. Hook.** Reveal three original drawings, each marked "schematic, not to scale": standing trunks with roots in rock; a map with one far-north mark; a treeless snowy landscape. Captions hidden. Say only: "This is one place." Pairs talk for 1 minute: how could both belong to one place? The class shares; any child may pass; no one reports on home. Notices become questions on the board. If a child asks how old, state plainly what the evidence shows, then the respectful line in field 1 (6) if a family sees it differently.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** 2 minutes: pairs sort six older-view and later-view picture pairs (review of grade 2) into quick and very slow changes. 2 minutes: six children from the rotating six each recall one grade 2 fossil-topic note and its source (20 seconds each). If notes are missing, read an original caption together; familiarity is not interpretation. A miss goes to day 83 "Support groups".
- **12 min. Fossil evidence.** 3 minutes: the teacher fills row 1 of the table of finds from the trunk drawing and says "fossil" and "kind, size, place". 5 minutes: 15 pairs fill rows 2 and 3 (a leaf print and a bark print, as on find cards 2 and 3; sizes on cards printed "invented for practice"). 4 minutes: six children each give one row aloud (40 seconds each).
- **10 min. First past-environment model.** Each child draws, in pencil, the place when the plants lived and labels each part with the row that made them draw it. Day 81 results seat pairs; this does not delay the hook.
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine (field 5).
- **3 min. Exit E82.**
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Jar emptied by the adult; helpers' hands washed; drawings filed.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice fills a table of finds and draws a first Arctic model; the exit has each child alone draw a dry hill long ago from a fish print and a shell print, and label what each shows.

Held by: teacher (wall table, board); each child (a drawing). Tool: past-environment model (light), show why, 10 minutes. By hand: pencil, paper. Evidence: E82, the first drawing, the heard list. Hand-backs out: retrieval misses to day 83 "Support groups"; E82's commonest letter to day 83 "Return and repair".

## Day 83


- **2 min. Return and repair.** E82's commonest letter: its wrong example beside the right one.
- **10 min. Support groups.** Readiness group (those who missed the day 81 item) with the teacher: 5 minutes on bare soil beside a planted patch, naming what is harmed, drawing a boundary and explaining the benefit; a missing harm link gets a picture-to-need match, a solution with no benefit gets a before-and-after arrow; 2 minutes on a fresh litter-cover case. Retrieval group (day 82 misses) joins the teacher for the last 3 minutes with a two-picture comparison. Everyone else works in pairs on one extra fossil card. If many missed the readiness item, the whole class does its 7 minutes and the two-picture card is done in pairs.
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine.
- **22 min. Interpreting finds.** 5 minutes: the teacher models seen and inferred on the trunk drawing, solid and dotted lines. 6 minutes: 15 pairs sort eight statement cards about a Big Stump drawing (12 feet across, 38 feet around, from park pages) into shows, does not show and cannot tell yet; a stump piece gives at least the tree's width. 6 minutes: the guide-card idea, that a fern or palm today tells what its fossil kind needed; pairs add a guide row to the table. 5 minutes: each child revises the model in a second color.
- **3 min. Exit E83.**
- **3 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has the teacher model seen and inferred, then pairs sort statement cards and draw lines from finds to what they show; the exit has each child alone write one thing seen and one inferred for a dragonfly wing print.

Held by: teacher (small group); pairs. Tool: past-environment model (light), show why, 5 minutes. By hand: second color. Evidence: E83, the revised model, the heard list. Hand-backs out: E83's letters to day 84.

## Day 84


- **2 min. Return and repair.** E83's commonest letter.
- **10 min. Desert evidence.** The teacher shows a schematic dry valley with whale bones and shell prints in rock, and a guide card: whales and these shells live in the sea today. Pairs fill rows of the table of finds. State plainly that the valley is desert now.
- **10 min. Comparison.** Pairs set the Arctic trunks and the desert valley side by side: what was found, what the guide card says, the place then, the place now. It is a very slow change over ages that no one watched; the steady patterns idea carries it.
- **14 min. Model revision.** Each child draws the valley when the whales lived and adds one line to the first model: "The place now does not tell the place then."
- **6 min. Exit E84.**
- **3 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice fills a desert table of whale bones and shells in pairs and revises the model; the exit gives a desert quarry with leaf prints and a guide card and asks alone what they support and what they do not show.

Held by: pairs, then each child. Tool: past-environment model (light), practice with feedback, 14 minutes. By hand: second color. Evidence: E84, the new drawing. Hand-backs out: E84's letters to day 85.

## Day 85


- **2 min. Return and repair.** E84's commonest letter.
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; after it, the teacher pins a one-week strip of the log beside the board.
- **10 min. Extinct forms.** An early whale form beside a living whale (original drawings): the guide says the early form is extinct and whales are not. Pairs sort eight cards: extinct (none alive anywhere) or only missing here.
- **12 min. Undisturbed layers.** 4 minutes: the teacher stacks cards oldest first and says "older" and "younger". 4 minutes: 15 pairs lay five cards in order, then flip the stack and say what they can no longer tell. 4 minutes: the time strip, one thin card for the week's log beside thick layers that each took very long; no numbers.
- **10 min. Model revision.** 7 minutes: revise the Arctic explanation in a second color with the trunks in a lower layer, older and younger labeled; a note box says new evidence can change an explanation. 3 minutes: Exit E85.
- **3 min. Prediction.** Pairs write what a face-down new find would change in the model, then see the card. Paper stands in for the spec-only viewer.
- **3 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts extinct and living cards and orders five undisturbed layers; the exit gives a landslide that jumbled the layers, which the practice never shows, and asks whether a kind missing from one lake is extinct.

Held by: pairs; each child. Tool: past-environment viewer (light), show why, 3 minutes, spec only; paper fits. Past-environment model (light), practice with feedback, 7 minutes. By hand: card stacks. Evidence: E85, the revised model. Hand-backs out: E85's letters to day 86.

## Day 86


- **2 min. Return and repair.** E85's commonest letter.
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine.
- **18 min. Regional record.** The class state's fossil, or the Florissant, Colorado record. 3 minutes: the teacher shows the map and one find card. 6 minutes: 15 pairs fill the table of finds for three points (kind, size, place; sizes printed "invented for practice"). 5 minutes: pairs write two claims and sort them as shows or does not show; the map's two places do not make the whole region one place. 4 minutes: six pairs each read one claim (40 seconds each).
- **12 min. Product F.** The teacher reads the card once; children work alone. Collected for the checklist after class (a six-tick look-for checklist and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child).
- **5 min. Exit E86.**
- **3 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice reads a regional fossil record in pairs and then completes product F alone; the exit gives a new map with snail fossils and tracks and asks what the kinds, sizes and places support and leave uncertain.

Held by: pairs; each child. Tool: past-environment model (light), practice with feedback, 12 minutes, checklist filled later. By hand: pencil. Evidence: product F, E86. Hand-backs out: E86's letter to day 87 "Retrieval and repair"; the product F checklist to day 93.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: fossil, kind, size, place, older, younger, extinct, layer. Frames: "The fossil shows ___. I infer ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged drawings; tactile layer cards; dictation; partners may read cards aloud. Weather helper roles can be taken seated.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn answers; a scribe. Do not supply the inference.
- **Reading load.** Cards 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, read aloud by the teacher.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one thing a find cannot show, and the find needed.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Make the table of finds together, then the child draws the model alone. On day 85, lay five cards, flip them, and talk. Product F: set the card, step back, give feedback after.
- **One projector.** Show the drawings and table; children draw on paper; layer cards are paper.
