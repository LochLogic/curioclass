Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 93 closes the anchor: fossil trees and the study's habitat interpretation support a formerly warm forest, unlike the comparison landscape. Students cite fossil records and distinguish ancient change from their short weather log.
>
> Group the question board into past evidence, changed needs and useful responses. Days 82 to 86 analyze fossil kinds, sizes and locations to infer past organisms and environments across long times. This raises how change affects living systems. Days 87 to 91 argue solution merit from changed interactions; day 92 asks whether replanting always helps. Days 93 and 94 connect both scales. Transfer uses new fossil sites and water-access problems.
> Carried: day 82 starts a past-environment drawing and evidence table, retaining patterns and data models. Day 85 adds extinct forms and relative layers, introducing long times and revising stability and change. Day 87 revises unit 4's system with condition-to-organism arrows. Days 88 and 89 revise unit 2's design cycle and evidence argument using crossing and passage records. Day 91 adds plant-food interactions. Day 85 compares extinct early whale forms with living whales; not all whales are extinct. Day 93 joins these in consensus; day 94 tests evidence limits. Both assessment tasks annotate these models. At day 88, define goals and limits, propose a crossing, inspect test records and improve the proposal; research supplies the needed habitat knowledge.

| 95 | 3-LS4-1 | Use fossil data as evidence of what lived long ago and what their environments were like. | F: the six-point product takes 12 minutes on day 86 and eight revision minutes on day 93, both outside this sitting. Score outside class, zero sitting minutes. Fresh diagnostic, 32 minutes: compare two outcrops on a dry plain, printed "invented for practice". At the same level in an undisturbed layer, the west outcrop has complete freshwater-shell fossils three centimetres long; the east has land-animal footprints 14 centimetres long. A guide identifies the shell form as extinct worldwide. Above the eastern tracks is a rooted trunk fragment 60 centimetres long. Compare claims of continuous water and a shoreline with nearby land; annotate a model with kinds, sizes, locations, relative order and evidence limits. Contrast this history with a week's weather. Answer: freshwater organisms at the west and land animals at the east favor nearby water and land over continuous water; transported shells remain possible. Upper roots are younger than the tracks here. Shell and footprint lengths are known, but tracks do not give body length and the trunk fragment gives only a minimum. This shell form is extinct, not all shellfish; a week's weather cannot explain the long history. Wrong: permanent present environment (A), local absence proves extinction (B), deeper is always older (C), size alone proves tropical climate (F). Four minutes directions, six independent checking and three cleanup complete 45. | section 5.3 |
| 96 | 3-LS4-4 | Judge, with a claim, how well a solution works for a problem caused when an environment changes and so may the plants and animals living there. | S: the six-point product takes 20 minutes on day 91 and eight revision minutes on day 94, both outside this sitting. Score outside class, zero sitting minutes. Fresh diagnostic, 32 minutes: a cold spell lowers a garden's temperature, printed "invented for practice". Some flowering plants stop flowering, moths move away, exposed caterpillars die and cold-tolerant beetles arrive and reproduce. Goals: retain four flowering plants and nine pollinator visits within ten tokens, with structures at most 50 centimetres tall. Matched model trials: a tall shelter retains six plants and 12 visits for eight tokens but stands 70 centimetres tall; a low cover retains four plants and nine visits for ten tokens and stands 40 centimetres tall. Make a merit claim with system arrows and a limit on the evidence. Answer: the low cover meets both goals, the budget and height limit; explain temperature effects on flowering and the plant-pollinator interaction, distinguishing organism outcomes without promising universal benefit. Wrong: all organisms respond alike (D); largest counts override constraints (E). Four minutes directions, six independent checking and three cleanup complete 45. | section 5.3 |
