## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3-ESS2-1** (days 113, 114, 115, 117): Show weather data in tables and graphs to describe the weather usually expected in a given season.
- **3-ESS2-2** (days 113, 114, 115, 117): Gather and combine information to describe the climates of different regions of the world.
- **3-ESS3-1** (days 113, 114, 116, 117): Judge, with a claim, how well a design such as a flood wall or lightning rod lessens the harm from a weather hazard.
- skill s3-weather-data (grade 3)
- skill s3-climates (grade 3)
- skill s3-weather-hazard (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

>
> Group the question board: what repeats, answered days 99 to 102; whether one year describes everywhere, days 103 to 107; how expected hazards cause less harm, days 108 to 112. Each answer raises the next question. Days 113 and 114 connect them.

Day 109 revises the design cycle with flood criteria; day 110 tests and improves one roof. Day 111 revises cause-and-effect arrows from lightning evidence. Day 113 joins these in consensus, used in transfer tasks.

Atlas card: region M is warm throughout the year; region N has cold winters and mild summers. Both have wetter and drier months. A 30-year station summary gives rainy July days: M ranges from 14 to 26, N from 1 to 9; selected years show M at 15, 23, 19 and N at 3, 8, 5. Describe both regions' usual conditions, compare their ranges, explain variation across years and link each claim to its source. Answer: M is warm throughout and usually wetter in July; N has greater seasonal temperature contrast; both July records vary across years. Neither promises tomorrow's weather. Wrong: this year's weather is the whole climate (C); uses one source only (D). Fresh diagnostic; earlier product C is scored outside class. | section 6.4 |
| 116 | 3-ESS3-1 | Argument with hazard cause and effect; make a claim about design merit | H, 30 minutes: an unseen wind-screen design protects a model waiting area. Goal: at least seven of nine lightweight markers stay in place; limit: at most 13 tokens. Matched trials retain 7, 8, 7 markers with the screen, and 2, 3, 2 without; cost is 12 tokens. Annotate the hazard model and judge merit, evidence and limits. Answer: meets goal in all three trials within cost; the screen changes wind reaching the markers, reducing displacement, without stopping the windstorm. Stronger winds remain untested. Wrong: a design stops the hazard (E); overlooks the budget (F); model success guarantees real protection (G). Fresh diagnostic; earlier H scored outside class. Three minutes directions, nine independent checking and three cleanup complete 45. | section 6.4 |
