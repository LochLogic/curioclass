## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3-PS2-1** (days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5): Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence.
- **3-PS2-2** (days 1, 2): Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next.
- skill s3-balanced-forces (grade 3)
- skill s3-motion-patterns (grade 3)
- skill sk-push-pull (grade K, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill s1-sky-patterns (grade 1, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill sk-weather-watch (grade K, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 1: watch an empty swing from outside its path, or an original drawing beside the pendulum. The teacher releases the pendulum, then holds it still at the bottom. Students draw an explanation.
> Model safe observation on day 1, fair comparison on day 3 and recording on day 9. Groups then choose tests and defend conclusions. Read notebooks daily to choose support.
>
> - **Earlier-grade rows in the days.csv skills column (retrieval or readiness, not owned; field 4):** sk-push-pull (grade K); s1-sky-patterns (grade 1); sk-weather-watch (grade K).
>
> Group notices and wonders into causes and patterns on the question board. Days 1 to 5 ask, "Can pushes act without movement?" Class controlled tests connect contact forces with cause and effect. Days 6 to 10 ask, "Will our explanation work in another test, and what repeats?" Group tests lead to position records and predictions. Days 11 to 13 ask, "When should we change a prediction?" Bowl observations and a changed pendulum test the pattern's limits. Mark answered questions with evidence; retain unresolved ones. Transfer uses a hanging delivery basket on day 14 and an unfamiliar cardboard delivery sled on day 15.
> Carried: Cause and effect and force arrows begin on day 1; day 4 adds opposing arrows from box evidence; day 8 distinguishes the string's pull from gravity using pendulum observations. Day 13 reaches consensus: arrows describe forces on one object, not its travel. Patterns and position tables begin on day 1; day 9 adds repeated ordered observations; day 12 checks predictions at a fixed observation beat after changing string length. Day 13 consensus separates repeating order from shrinking travel. Both assessments use these models; none is withdrawn.
