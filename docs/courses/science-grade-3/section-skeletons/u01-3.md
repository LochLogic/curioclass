## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3-PS2-2** (days 11, 12, 13, 14, 16): Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next.
- **3-PS2-1** (days 13, 15, 16): Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence.
- skill s3-motion-patterns (grade 3)
- skill m2-measure-with-tools (not a progression row)
- skill s3-balanced-forces (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> On day 13, students explain starting, stopping and returning using box-test evidence and motion records: pushes can change motion; gravity pulls down while the string guides the curved path. Motion carries it past the bottom. The path repeats while swings become smaller.
>
> Group notices and wonders into causes and patterns on the question board. Days 1 to 5 ask, "Can pushes act without movement?" Class controlled tests connect contact forces with cause and effect. Days 6 to 10 ask, "Will our explanation work in another test, and what repeats?" Group tests lead to position records and predictions. Days 11 to 13 ask, "When should we change a prediction?" Bowl observations and a changed pendulum test the pattern's limits. Mark answered questions with evidence; retain unresolved ones. Transfer uses a hanging delivery basket on day 14 and an unfamiliar cardboard delivery sled on day 15.
> Carried: Cause and effect and force arrows begin on day 1; day 4 adds opposing arrows from box evidence; day 8 distinguishes the string's pull from gravity using pendulum observations. Day 13 reaches consensus: arrows describe forces on one object, not its travel. Patterns and position tables begin on day 1; day 9 adds repeated ordered observations; day 12 checks predictions at a fixed observation beat after changing string length. Day 13 consensus separates repeating order from shrinking travel. Both assessments use these models; none is withdrawn.

| 14 | 3-PS2-2 | Observe or measure motion, find its repeating pattern and predict from evidence. | Items 1 and 2 score the day 12 changed-length pendulum record described below. Day 14 transfer practice is unscored: four minutes per group, six groups, 24 minutes total. Observe an unfamiliar miniature delivery basket suspended from two parallel strings. Individually record at least two cycles of turning positions and centre crossings. On the earlier record, award two points for an ordered record matching the observed motion. Then six independent practice minutes. Mark the repeating order, predict the next turning side after the last recorded turn and use two earlier cycles to explain. On the earlier record, award one point for order, one for prediction and two for evidence connecting the record to a repeating-motion model. Key: left turn, centre, right turn, centre repeats; the next turn is opposite the last. Use the actual first side observed, not a prefilled sequence. Wrong: "It stays on the left because that is where it began" (E); "It must always reach exactly the same spot" (F). | section 1.3 |
| 15 | 3-PS2-1 | Plan and conduct a controlled investigation of balanced and unbalanced forces, using motion evidence. | Items 3 to 5 score the day 7 group investigation record described below. Day 15 transfer practice is unscored: plan during the waiting portion of the 30-minute station block. Use an unfamiliar cardboard delivery sled in a tray: compare approximately equal opposing pushes with one deliberately stronger opposing push. Change only relative strength. Keep object, surface, starting position, push locations and directions fixed; repeat each condition. On the earlier record, award two points for one change and fixed conditions, one for repetition. Allow five live practice minutes per group within that same block. Each child applies an identifiable comparison of both conditions and records observations; on the earlier investigation, award two points for individually documented conducting and recording. Then six independent practice minutes. Use arrows on this sled and trial evidence to explain no change from rest compared with a change in motion; on the earlier record, award one point for balanced reasoning, one for unbalanced reasoning, one for arrows and two for evidence. Key: equal opposing forces cancel; an uncancelled force can change motion. Accept unexpected results honestly recorded with a proposed retest. Wrong: "Still means no forces" (A); "Equal opposite pushes start it moving" (B); "A longer arrow shows farther travel" (C); "Change strength and surface together" (D). | section 1.3 |
