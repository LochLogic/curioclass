## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3-LS1-1** (days 42): Make models showing that living things have many different life cycles, yet all are born, grow, reproduce and die.
- **3-LS3-1** (days 43, 44, 45, 46): Use data as evidence that plants and animals get traits from their parents and that members of a similar group differ in those traits.
- skill s3-life-cycles (grade 3)
- skill s3-inherited-traits (grade 3)
- skill s1-like-parents (grade 1, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill m2-measure-with-tools (not a progression row)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Model reading arrows on day 38, comparing records on day 44 and linking evidence to a claim on day 50. Then children propose revisions; ask which observation warrants each change.
>
> Group the question board into growth, resemblance and surroundings. Days 37 to 41 ask where seedlings and beetles came from and what follows; models identify shared life patterns. Day 42 asks whether harvest means reproduction, raising where the next generation comes from. Days 43 to 46 analyze likeness and variation in that generation. Days 47 to 52 ask whether inheritance explains every difference, using controlled records to explain environmental effects. Days 53 and 54 connect these answers. Transfer records use unfamiliar flowering plants and animals, detailed below.
> Carried: retain unit 2's cause-and-effect comparisons. Initial day 37 cycle drawings gain flowering-plant evidence on day 38, beetle stages on day 39 and shared patterns on day 41. Revise resemblance models on days 43 and 46 from plant and animal records; retain individual variation. Revise cause-and-effect arrows on days 48 and 49 from crowding evidence, including inconclusive results. Begin claim, evidence and reasoning on day 50; revise on day 52. Day 53 consensus joins cycles, trait graphs and explanations. Children use these models in transfer work; retain observed and supplied labels.
