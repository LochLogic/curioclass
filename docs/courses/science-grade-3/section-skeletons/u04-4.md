## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3-LS2-1** (days 74, 76, 78): Argue that some animals live in groups and that the group helps its members survive.
- **3-LS4-3** (days 74, 77, 78): Argue from evidence that in a given habitat some living things do well, some do poorly, and some cannot live at all.
- **3-LS4-2** (days 74, 75, 78): Explain, with evidence, how differences among members of one species can help some survive, find mates and reproduce.
- skill s3-animal-groups (grade 3)
- skill s3-habitat-survival (grade 3)
- skill s3-variation-advantage (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 74 closes the anchor: group actions can protect members, coloring can reduce detection, and survival depends on needs meeting conditions. Children write with evidence from readings, camouflage counts and habitat comparisons.
>
> Group the question board into groups, differences and places. Days 59 to 63 ask how grouping helps; that raises why individuals still differ in success. Days 64 to 68 explain variation, raising whether an advantage works everywhere. Days 69 to 73 argue from needs and conditions; day 74 joins the answers. Transfer uses unfamiliar plant and bird records, animal groups and a pond.
> Carried: initial day 59 system drawings gain group cause-and-effect arrows on day 62 from animal records. Revise unit 3's claim, evidence and reasoning frame on day 63 after challenges. Start structure-and-function comparisons on day 64; revise on days 66 and 68 from camouflage counts, thorn evidence and bird records. Day 69 revises habitat systems from needs and conditions; day 73 adds local evidence. Day 74 consensus retains these models for assessment annotation.

Fresh diagnostic, 30 minutes: a new invented shallow pond is sunny, warm and permanently flooded. A floating plant needs sunlight and floating roots, and produces many new leaves. A rooted plant needs shallower water than this pond provides; it stays alive but loses leaves. A land snail needs dry resting places, none are available, and it cannot remain. Argue which does well, poorly or cannot remain, using needs and conditions, and annotate a system model. Expected: the three judgments follow those records, with a cause for each. Wrong: all living things do equally well (F), or no organism survives a habitat that is difficult for one kind (G). Five weather minutes, four directions, three checking and three cleanup complete 45. | section 4.4 |
