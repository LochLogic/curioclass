# Scope and sequence: Science Grade 3

Course `science-grade-3`, grade 3, draft, written 2026-11-14, fixes applied 2026-11-15.

The frame's six units retain their order, 111 unit days, 15 owned progression rows and 16 performance expectations. Nine course-level days bring the total to 120 meetings of 45 minutes. The day decisions, section boundaries, standards, materials and controls live in [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-3/days.csv). School day is `ceil(course day x 1.25)`, four meetings per week across weeks 1 to 30. No unit or assessment is moved to fit another subject. Sections below are day groupings for later planning, not section plans.

Each unit starts with its frame's observable anchor and a question board. Evidence raises the next question, changes an initial model and supports a consensus explanation. An end-of-unit assessment uses an unfamiliar phenomenon or problem. Where an assessment spans two days, its codes are divided between the days; each code on a day requires all its parts, through short prompts or the performance/product named there. The unit plan will write the items, scoring and accommodations. Each child's contribution is identifiable; six groups of five take turns during live evidence while other children complete independent records. Reuse any oral/small-group record gathered within ten days unchanged, never another collection or score on the old work. No unit exceeds 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment is required or reserved. Model revisions and section exit tickets provide intermediate evidence without another assessment day.

The weather log has two explicit five-minute slots in every four-meeting week, including five minutes of the day 77 assessment and designated respond days where necessary. It starts in week 1, records temperature, rain or snow, wind direction and sky, and continues through day 120. Readings below zero are written in words and excluded from numerical graphs and temperature comparisons. Teacher-supplied local records cover seasons the class has not observed. Averages are supplied, never calculated by students. Planting takes 15 minutes on day 18. Days 27 and 29 reserve ten minutes each to hand-pollinate: brush each open flower with a cotton swab and exchange pollen between different plants. Check pollen allergies first and wash hands. The unit plan sets exact dates from the supplier calendar and actual flowering; adult care covers intervening dates. Check for seed ripening about course days 41 to 45. Two-minute care or observation slots through day 58 stay inside recorded days, with adult care daily between meetings. Observed and photograph-only stages remain distinct. Neither routine adds days or claims a complete live cycle when it was not observed.

## Unit 1. Pushes, pulls and swings

16 course days, 1 to 16; school days 2 to 20; weeks 1 to 4. Sections: 1.1, days 1 to 5; 1.2, days 6 to 10; 1.3, days 11 to 16.

The swing and safe class pendulum raise two connected questions: what changes motion, and what lets us predict its return? Class tests of equal and unequal pushes establish controlled variables and force arrows before groups plan and run their own tests. Motion records then support predictions, a second repeating motion in a bowl and an explanation of a failed prediction. The arrow model begins on day 1, changes with evidence on days 4 and 8, and reaches consensus on day 13 before transfer. No student rides a swing in an investigation. Force strength is compared, not measured in units; gravity is only a downward pull.

Readiness occupies the last ten minutes of day 1 after the hook: `sk-push-pull` and the prediction practice in `s1-sky-patterns`. Day 2 uses the results for one lesson of support or deeper evidence work. Both were last used in grade 1; the aligned frame follows the counted rule below. No push retrieval duplicates this readiness evidence. The later unit 2 use of pushes is retrieval because unit 1 has now taught it. Day 14 assesses only 3-PS2-2: observe or measure an unfamiliar repeating motion, record it, identify its pattern and justify a prediction. Day 15 assesses only 3-PS2-1: each child plans a controlled repeated comparison, contributes a recorded trial, and uses force arrows and motion evidence for balanced and unbalanced cases. Day 14's former practice folds into day 13. Day 16 is the single unit response allocation for both records. The CSV budgets each observed assessment for 30 children in six groups of five, with independent record work for waiting groups.

Candidate: drop the come-back can, zero additional days. A second pattern already appears in the bowl on day 11, while elastic construction adds set-up and invites an unnecessary stored-energy explanation. The owned motion reasoning remains.

## Unit 2. Pushes and pulls without touching

17 course days, 17 to 33; school days 22 to 42; weeks 5 to 9. Sections: 2.1, days 17 to 22; 2.2, days 23 to 28; 2.3, days 29 to 33.

The suspended clip and static paper attraction challenge contact-only explanations. Student questions drive tests of distance, object type, magnet number and orientation, followed by static attraction. The initial model on day 17 changes on days 20 and 22. The teacher-only electromagnet on day 24 prompts questions and connects a discovery to a useful device, without student circuits. Only after investigating magnetic effects does the class define the cupboard-latch need, several success criteria and constraints on materials, time and cost. Trying one solution sharpens the problem; changed users require changed criteria. The consensus model on day 29 links evidence to that definition. This load gives seven principal inquiry days, six definition/application days, two assessment days, an opener and a respond day, rather than equal shares of two skill rows.

Retrieve unit 1 forces on day 17 and grade 2 design comparison on day 25; no older idle prerequisite needs readiness. Planting on day 18 and ten-minute pollination slots on days 27 and 29 serve unit 3 without changing this unit's length; day 29 stays on the shorter route so the second pollination is not lost. Day 31 assesses the full electric and magnetic questioning expectation, including distance and orientation. Day 32 assesses defining the new toy-car magnetic problem, with a product carrying the complete need, criteria, constraints and changing-needs work. Day 33 responds once to both parts.

Candidate: drop the compass extension, zero additional days. Earth's magnetism is not needed to explain these interactions or define the problem. Keep the time for static as well as magnetic evidence and for the full engineering definition.

## Unit 3. Growing up and taking after

22 course days, 37 to 58; school days 47 to 73; weeks 10 to 15. Sections: 3.1, days 37 to 41; 3.2, days 42 to 46; 3.3, days 47 to 52; 3.4, days 53 to 58.

The class's plants and mealworms, already established on day 18, make life-cycle models answer a live question. Models begin on day 37 and change on days 39 and 41 as dated observations or identified photographs reveal omitted stages. Comparing the butterfly, frog and flowering plant establishes birth, growth, reproduction and death despite different stages and timing. Reproduction leads to the question of offspring likeness and within-kind variation. Plant and animal data both support inheritance reasoning before the hydrangea puzzle and crowding records introduce environmental influence. Days 48 and 49 read the existing comparison; they do not restart grade 2's light-and-water experiment. The consensus revision on day 53 connects the three rows without introducing genes. Days 37 to 42 develop cycles, 43 to 46 focus on inherited traits, and 47 to 52 develop environmental evidence and its distinction from inheritance; 53 to 54 integrate, 55 to 57 assess and 58 responds before readiness. Day 55's former evidence critique is retained on day 54.

Readiness on grade 1 like-parents content takes day 36's last ten minutes; grade 2 day 39 retrieves an evidence-account practice, but does not teach resemblance. Day 37 uses the results for grouping without delaying the hook, and day 38 provides one-lesson support through verified plant and animal family records alongside the cycle work. Retrieve grade 2 pollination/seed work on day 37, the now-supported resemblance on day 43, and grade 2 plant-needs evidence on day 47. Day 55 assesses 3-LS3-2 through new evidence of environmental and combined trait influences; day 56 assesses 3-LS1-1 through complete animal and flowering-plant cycle models; day 57 assesses 3-LS3-1 through data on plant and animal parent-young likeness and within-kind variation. The former day 55 evidence critique folds into day 54. Day 58 responds once to all three records in its first 35 minutes, including care and cleanup, then gives unit 4 readiness in its last ten minutes and records which late stages were actually seen. Failed or incomplete live cycles use dated photographs, with no invented observation. Non-human examples avoid requests about student family resemblance, adoption or bodies.

Candidate: keep seed-packet timing for 15 minutes on day 42, within its existing day. Compare the packet's germination and harvest timings with the dated record, while explicitly distinguishing harvest from reproduction. This adds a timing pattern, not a claim that the packet documents the whole life cycle.

## Unit 4. Surviving where you live

20 course days, 59 to 78; school days 74 to 98; weeks 15 to 20. Sections: 4.1, days 59 to 63; 4.2, days 64 to 68; 4.3, days 69 to 73; 4.4, days 74 to 78.

The tundra anchor asks why groups, individual differences and habitat fit can each matter. Five days develop group arguments using musk oxen, wolves, fish and bees, including evidence that some animals survive alone. Counts are sourced counts out of equal numbers of hunts, never percentages. Five days then use unit 3 variation to explain camouflage, thorn differences and a within-kind bird mate-choice case; survival alone does not complete the expectation. Five days connect needs and conditions in the tundra, desert/bog and local habitat. Initial system models on day 59 change with variation evidence on day 66 and habitat evidence on day 69, reaching consensus on day 74. Cause and effect remains explicit in the habitat argument even though the system model also helps.

Unit 4 has no readiness check: its earlier rows were all used in grade 2, so day 58 is response only. Day 59 retrieves grade 2 habitat diversity; day 60 retrieves parent-offspring survival behavior in a short young-signal and adult-response account before group arguments. Retrieve parts-mimic on day 64: its relevant structure/function content is taught by the grade 2 hook/attachment model on day 61 and assessed on day 64. Retrieve same-year variation there too. Day 75 assesses only 3-LS4-2: within-kind evidence for survival, finding mates and reproduction. Its former integration practice moves into day 74, whose skills and codes now include variation. Day 76 assesses only 3-LS2-1: a claim supported by group data and causal reasoning for food, protection and handling change, including group size and jobs. Six groups of five give spoken reasons while the rest complete their own evidence links; the CSV budgets 45 minutes for 30 children. Day 77 assesses all three habitat outcomes: doing well, doing poorly and not surviving, with a cause tied to needs and conditions. Its weather log leaves 40 minutes for the assessment. Day 78 responds once to the three assessment records. Paper moths are a table simulation, with no child cast as prey. Predator images are non-graphic. The wood frog's unusual freezing tolerance is not evidence that it survives poorly; describe partial freezing in winter, not complete cellular freezing.

Candidate: keep the local habitat as a second system, one day on day 73 plus ten minutes of preparation on day 72, all inside these 20 days. A public-place photograph inventory offers the same needs-and-conditions argument when access or weather prevents outdoor observation. No organism collection is required.

## Unit 5. Environments change

16 course days, 82 to 97; school days 103 to 122; weeks 21 to 25. Sections: 5.1, days 82 to 86; 5.2, days 87 to 91; 5.3, days 92 to 97.

Arctic tropical-plant fossils open the question of an environment unlike today's. Data on fossil kind, size and location, the desert whales of Wadi Al-Hitan, extinct forms and undisturbed layers supply evidence for a changing past. Relative ages and deep time serve interpretation, with no fossil-name memorization. Models begin on day 82 and change on day 85; students explain how new evidence could change their explanation. Present-day cases then use unit 4 habitat systems and unit 2 criteria: highway/crossing for land, dam/fish ladder for water, lost shade for temperature, a spreading plant for other organisms, and post-fire replanting for food resources. Each case isolates one environmental change. Five days establish fossil reasoning, six develop solution-merit reasoning, two integrate the models, and three assess and respond.

Readiness on `sk-reduce-impact` occupies the last ten minutes of course-level respond day 81, immediately before the unit. Day 82's grouping uses the evidence without delaying its hook; day 83 reserves a one-lesson supported route on harm-reducing solutions, alongside the fossil data, with extra evidence for ready students. Retrieve grade 2 fast/slow events and earlier fossil reading on day 82; topic familiarity does not substitute for interpreting new evidence. Day 95 assesses fossil data inference, day 96 assesses merit of a solution to a new single environmental change, and day 97 responds once to both. Consensus models on day 93 carry the evidence to transfer. Deep time and changes in life are stated plainly from evidence, without soliciting beliefs. Disaster and animal-harm cases remain non-graphic. Climate change is not assessed here; questions, if raised, receive evidence-based answers.

Candidate: keep the state fossil for 20 minutes on day 86, within that day. Where no state fossil is designated, use a documented regional find, with the same inference task. It is evidence about a former environment, not a naming exercise or another field trip.

## Unit 6. Weather, climate and hazards

20 course days, 98 to 117; school days 123 to 147; weeks 25 to 30. Sections: 6.1, days 98 to 102; 6.2, days 103 to 107; 6.3, days 108 to 112; 6.4, days 113 to 117.

The weather log raises the problem of expecting seasonal weather despite daily differences. Five days represent and interpret the class record beside supplied local seasonal records. Five days widen this to many-year climate range and year-to-year variation in the town, Manaus, Cairo and Utqiagvik. Climate is not inferred from one class year. Five days then ask how expected hazards can cause less harm: flood barriers, a wind-resistant roof and lightning rods. Only the roof uses a quick physical model; lightning uses paper evidence. The initial seasonal model on day 98 changes after atypical records on day 102, becomes a weather-and-climate consensus on day 107 and incorporates hazard evidence on day 113. This places representation before climate synthesis and criteria before merit claims.

Retrieve the same-year log on day 98, land/water and globe work on day 103, forecasts on day 108 and grade 2 erosion solutions on day 109. Grade 2 day 11 explicitly reteaches forecasts and community preparation after its own readiness, so day 108 retrieves taught content; no new readiness check is needed. Day 115 keeps the expert-settled data-based pair, 3-ESS2-1 and 3-ESS2-2: 20 minutes representing seasonal temperature, precipitation and wind records, 20 minutes gathering/combining sources on typical range and year-to-year variation across regions, and five minutes directions/cleanup. No individual oral collection is added. Day 116 assesses a design-merit claim for an unfamiliar hazard. Day 117 responds once to both. No negative-temperature arithmetic, means, climate-cause mechanisms or line graphs are required. Examples stay general; no student must recount a disaster. Fan controls include low speed, fingers clear and eye protection if anything can fly.

Candidate: keep a real forecast and warning for 15 minutes on day 108, inside its existing day. Use dated, located public weather-service information in original Curio Class wording. It provides a reason to consider a protection design, without turning a historical warning into current safety advice.
