# Section 5.3: Testing environmental explanations

## Header


Testing environmental explanations

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS4-4, this section's share (days 92, 93, 94, 96, 97).** "Judge, with a claim, how well a solution works for a problem caused when an environment changes and so may the plants and animals living there." Day 92 asks whether replanting always helps; days 93 and 94 join it to the fossil scale; day 96 checks it; day 97 repairs it. The practice is "Engaging in argument from evidence" ("Make a claim").
- **3-LS4-1, this section's share (days 93, 94, 95, 97).** "Use fossil data as evidence of what lived long ago and what their environments were like." Day 93 revises product F and closes the Svalbard anchor; day 94 compares fossil and modern records; day 95 checks it; day 97 repairs it. The practice is "Analyze and interpret data".
- **Limits that bite here.** Climate change is not assessed; if raised, answer plainly from the evidence. No species names, no dates, no plate-motion mechanism. Extinct means gone everywhere; size alone gives no climate. Fire can benefit an ecosystem and replanting is not always needed. Fresh check-day work is scored; the revised earlier products are recorded beside it as repair evidence, a separate mark. No feedback or reteach on days 95 and 96.
- **Connections.** Both class models joined on day 93. The weather log, days 93 and 94, as the short record beside the long one. The claim frame throughout. Unit 6's retrieval of the log and the goal-versus-limit idea, day 97.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The Svalbard close: rooted fossil trunks and the study's habitat reading support a formerly warm forest, unlike the treeless landscape now; the land has since moved, a later grade's question. (2) How the 12 points are earned (a point for each complete component), why feedback is a six-tick checklist with one question stamp (about 1 minute a child, 30 minutes a product for 30 children; scoring each check about 30 more), why checklists go back on day 97 and scored papers on day 98 and why a missed chance is unscored, not zero. (3) Why every item is fresh. (4) Fire and replanting, stated from the evidence. (5) The day 97 groups and the unit 6 handoff.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** the two rows above (days 95 and 96), on the unit plan. Each is scored from the fresh case in the table, on the same six components; the earlier revised product (item F from section 5.1, item S from section 5.2) is marked beside it as repair evidence, not the score. Wrong answers: A, B, C, F (day 95); D, E (day 96).

**Exit questions.** Each is on a fresh card, "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next teaching day's "Return and repair" opens with that letter's wrong example; E94's letters wait for day 97.
- **E92 (6 minutes).** "After a fire, three plots. Goal: at least 4 berry shrubs back, within 4 tokens. Plot P is left alone: 6 shrubs, 0 tokens. Plot Q is replanted: 5 shrubs, 3 tokens. Plot R is replanted and fenced: 9 shrubs, 7 tokens. Which choices have merit, and does the record show replanting always helps?" Answer: P and Q meet both; R breaks the token limit; one set of matched plots cannot show replanting always helps. Wrong: "R, because 9 is the most" (E); "Every animal gets its food back the same way" (D).
- **E93 (3 minutes).** "A sandy hill has prints of fan-shaped leaves in a rock layer. A guide says these leaves belong to warm, wet forests. The class log for one week shows cold days. Aman says: 'The log shows this place is cold, so it never had a forest.' Is he right?" Answer: No. The prints and the card show a warm, wet forest long ago; one week is a short record and cannot show ages. Wrong: "Yes, the log shows it is cold, so it always was" (A).
- **E94 (7 minutes, unit plan field 5).** A map shows marine-coral fossils at one ridge and rooted tree remains at another, with no matching layers to order them. A guide says this coral lived in seawater. A separate modern record shows lost pond shade warming the water; a shade trial costs four tokens and retains three water plants and two insect larvae, meeting goals of three plants and two larvae within four tokens. "What does each record support about environmental change, and what can neither establish?" Answer: seawater at one sampled place and land trees at the other long ago, without establishing which came first; restoring shade supports temperature-dependent pond life for the stated goals. Neither gives an exact fossil date or guarantees all organisms benefit. Wrong: today's environment always existed (A); all organisms respond alike (D).
- **T97 (10 minutes, day 97, unscored, two items).** (a) "An undisturbed cliff. Low layer: lily-leaf prints 30 centimetres across (a guide: still water). High layer: prints of a dry-soil shrub (a guide: dry ground). The class log shows one frosty week. Which layer is older, what was the place like each time, and why can one week not tell us?" Answer: the low layer is older; still water, then dry ground; one week is a short record. Wrong: "The lily leaves are big, so it was tropical" (F); "It is frosty now, so it was always frosty" (A). (b) "Rain washes soil from a hillside into a gully. Goal: at least 5 of 8 saplings stay, within 5 tokens. A brush fence costs 3 tokens and 6 of 8 stay. A concrete channel costs 8 tokens and 8 of 8 stay. Which has merit?" Answer: the brush fence; the channel breaks the limit. Wrong: "The channel, because 8 is the most" (E); "All the gully animals will do well" (D).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: evidence, record, explanation, extinct, merit, limit, short record, long record. Frame: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___. The record does not show ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged records; tactile cards; dictation; a partner may read aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts and records aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn models; a scribe. Do not supply links, arrows or the limit.
- **Reading load.** Records 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads each aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one claim the records cannot support, and the record needed.
