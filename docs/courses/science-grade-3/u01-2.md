# Section 1.2: Fair tests and repeating paths

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 3. Unit 1, Pushes, pulls and swings. "What makes a swing start, stop and keep coming back?" Anchor: a playground swing (and a class pendulum of string and washers) that one push sets going, that swings in a pattern we can predict, and that two equal pushes from opposite sides hold still (days 1 to 16): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 6 to 10.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (6, 8), fluency 1 (10), application 2 (7, 9). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->



## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3-PS2-1** (days 6, 7, 8): Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence.
- **3-PS2-2** (days 8, 9, 10): Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next.
- skill s3-balanced-forces (grade 3)
- skill s3-motion-patterns (grade 3)
- skill m2-measure-with-tools (not a progression row)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Group notices and wonders into causes and patterns on the question board. Days 1 to 5 ask, "Can pushes act without movement?" Class controlled tests connect contact forces with cause and effect. Days 6 to 10 ask, "Will our explanation work in another test, and what repeats?" Group tests lead to position records and predictions. Days 11 to 13 ask, "When should we change a prediction?" Bowl observations and a changed pendulum test the pattern's limits. Mark answered questions with evidence; retain unresolved ones. Transfer uses a hanging delivery basket on day 14 and an unfamiliar cardboard delivery sled on day 15.
> Carried: Cause and effect and force arrows begin on day 1; day 4 adds opposing arrows from box evidence; day 8 distinguishes the string's pull from gravity using pendulum observations. Day 13 reaches consensus: arrows describe forces on one object, not its travel. Patterns and position tables begin on day 1; day 9 adds repeated ordered observations; day 12 checks predictions at a fixed observation beat after changing string length. Day 13 consensus separates repeating order from shrinking travel. Both assessments use these models; none is withdrawn.
<!-- /skel -->

- **3-PS2-1, this section's share (days 6, 7, 8).** "Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence." Groups plan and run their own fair test of push direction, repeat it, and explain it with arrows. Day 8 separates the string's pull from gravity on the pendulum.
- **3-PS2-2, this section's share (days 8, 9, 10).** "Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next." Children record the pendulum's turns in a position table, find what repeats, and predict the next turn from earlier ones.
- **Limits that bite here.** One change at a time. Forces are compared, not measured. Hand pushes are only about equal. No period, frequency, timer or stopwatch: turns are counted, not timed. Positions are read in whole centimeters and a moving washer cannot be read closer than about 1 cm, so a drop of less than 1 cm between swings may not show. Gravity is only a downward pull. No scaled graphs; math unit 1 gives ordered tables.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1, whole centimeters and ruler zero, days 7, 9 and 10; halves and quarters wait for math unit 7. Math unit 1, ordered tables, day 10. Speaking and writing: "I changed ___ and kept ___" (review of grade 2), days 6 to 8.
- **Practices.** Planning and carrying out investigations: group plan day 6, test day 7, repeated record day 9. Patterns: first table day 9, prediction day 10.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a pendulum's weight is pulled down by gravity and held to a curved path by the string, so it speeds up toward the bottom and slows toward the top; it passes the bottom because it is already moving. The swings shrink slowly (air and string rub; from memory). The time of a swing depends on string length, not on weight; this is for the teacher only. A direction test is a fair comparison only if the number and strength of pushes are kept, which hand pushes can only roughly do. A trial that surprises is evidence, not a mistake.

## Day 7

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Carry out group fair tests and compare repeated observations.
- note: Nature of science: different methods can supply evidence for the same question.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Set-up.** Floor clear; equipment out.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 6, D and H, 2 minutes each.
- **4 min. Own plan.** Each child writes their own plan from the team plan: the one change, the kept things, the trials. This starts the record revised on day 8.
- **16 min. Run the group tests.** Six groups of five, two conditions, three trials each, about a minute a trial; roles rotate so every child has an identifiable trial, marked with their initials, and every child records every trial. An unclear trial is repeated once and both are kept. A box that moves 1 cm or more under the balanced pushes is recorded as it happened. If the unbalanced pair does not move the box, push harder and record both. If the kit fails, an invented record is used and said aloud to be invented.
- **7 min. Arrows and explanation.** Each child draws the arrows for both conditions and writes two sentences: what stayed or moved, and why.
- **5 min. Compare groups.** Groups put their results on the board. Different groups chose different unbalanced pairs and numbers of trials, yet the patterns agree: different methods can give evidence for the same question.
- **3 min. Exit.** E7.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Boxes and trays wiped and stacked.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice runs two push conditions and draws arrows; the exit reads three given tries of 0, 0 and 3 cm and says what they show, a reading of results the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (board); groups (team plan, results); children (own record). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): show why, 4 min (Arrows and explanation). By hand: ruler, record, arrows. Evidence: the day 7 record (first-teaching work, revised day 8, repair mark on day 15); E7. Hand-backs out: B and G to day 8.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 6: "Which feature are we changing, and what are we keeping?" Day 8: "What pulls on the washer when it is held at the side? When it hangs still?" Day 10: "What did you see that makes you say so?" Every child may pass in public talk.
- **Written task, day 7.** The group-test record: plan, trials with initials, arrows and two sentences.
- **Judged by the teacher with 5 look-fors.** One change named; the rest kept; trials repeated; arrows match what was seen; an explanation that uses balanced or unbalanced and a centimeter reading.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 6: fair test words and the weather row. Day 7: whole centimeters. Day 8: the question board and the day 1 drawing. Day 9: zero at the end of the strip (grade 2). Day 10: reading a table row and the weather row.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: feature, number, strength, direction, front, back, turn, center, cycle, repeat, table, predict. Frames: "I changed ___ and kept ___." "The next turn will be ___, because ___ and ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner moves the pendulum or box as directed; large tables; raised tape marks; roles that need no fine reading; a seat near the ring.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; for E10 the record may be read to the child.
- **Reading load.** Tables and labels; no sentence over 15 words; directions are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A group that finishes early tests a second feature, number or strength, with a plan like its first and the same checks; it is not scored.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A book on a smooth table, a ruler and two pencils for the direction test: pushes from the front and back, then both from the front, three tries each, and the arrows. For the swing, hold a key on a string at arm's length, low arc, and write the order of turns; or use a record you have drawn. Keep the washers or the key from the child's mouth.
- **One projector.** Show the menu card and the class table; each child writes their own record.
