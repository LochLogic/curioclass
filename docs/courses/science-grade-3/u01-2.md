# Section 1.2: Fair tests and repeating paths

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-PS2-1, this section's share (days 6, 7, 8).** "Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence." Groups plan and run their own fair test of push direction, repeat it, and explain it with arrows. Day 8 separates the string's pull from gravity on the pendulum.
- **3-PS2-2, this section's share (days 8, 9, 10).** "Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next." Children record the pendulum's turns in a position table, find what repeats, and predict the next turn from earlier ones.
- **Limits that bite here.** One change at a time. Forces are compared, not measured. Hand pushes are only about equal. No period, frequency, timer or stopwatch: turns are counted, not timed. Positions are read in whole centimeters and a moving washer cannot be read closer than about 1 cm, so a drop of less than 1 cm between swings may not show. Gravity is only a downward pull. No scaled graphs; math unit 1 gives ordered tables.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1, whole centimeters and ruler zero, days 7, 9 and 10; halves and quarters wait for math unit 7. Math unit 1, ordered tables, day 10. Speaking and writing: "I changed ___ and kept ___" (review of grade 2), days 6 to 8.
- **Practices.** Planning and carrying out investigations: group plan day 6, test day 7, repeated record day 9. Patterns: first table day 9, prediction day 10.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a pendulum's weight is pulled down by gravity and held to a curved path by the string, so it speeds up toward the bottom and slows toward the top; it passes the bottom because it is already moving. The swings shrink slowly (air and string rub; from memory). The time of a swing depends on string length, not on weight; this is for the teacher only. A direction test is a fair comparison only if the number and strength of pushes are kept, which hand pushes can only roughly do. A trial that surprises is evidence, not a mistake.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none scored in this section. The day 7 group-test record is first-teaching work. It gets a look-for checklist and one question stamp before day 8 (about 1 minute a child), is revised on day 8 with the original observations kept, and gets a repair mark beside items 3 to 5 on day 15, which section 1.3 owns; the score is the fresh day 15 work.

**Exit questions.** Asked aloud with cards labeled "invented for practice"; short stems; a rotating six heard. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. Rule 4: none of these cards is a case taught in the unit.
- **E6 (day 6).** **E6a.** Card: a plan pushes a block from the left with one finger, then from the right with two fingers, to find if direction matters. "What should the plan change?" Answer: use the same number of fingers; change only the direction. Wrong: "It is fine, the direction is different" (D). **E6b.** "The plan pushes each way once. What else does it need?" Answer: more trials of each. Wrong: "Nothing, it has both ways" (H).
- **E7 (day 7).** Card: a group's results for about equal pushes from the front and back: 0 cm, 0 cm, 3 cm. "What do the results show? What does the group do next?" Answer: it mostly stayed; the 3 cm trial may show the hands were not equal; run again and keep all three. Wrong: "Equal pushes make it move" (B); "Erase the 3, it must be wrong" (G).
- **E8 (day 8).** Card: a lantern hangs still from a hook by a rope. "Draw the arrows on the lantern." Answer: gravity down, rope up, same length. Wrong: "Nothing pulls it, it is still" (A); an arrow for the rope drawn as long as the rope (C).
- **E9 (day 9).** **E9a.** Card: a record of a hanging toy: toward the door, middle, toward the window, middle, toward the door, middle. "Where is it at the next turn?" Answer: toward the window. Wrong: "Toward the door, it started there" (E). **E9b.** Card: far turns of another swing: 15, 14, 14, 13 cm. "Will the next far turn be exactly 15? Why?" Answer: no; they have been getting a little smaller. Wrong: "Yes, it always goes the same" (F).
- **E10 (day 10, section exit ticket).** Card: a rocking-toy record, left turn, center, right turn, center, left turn, center, right turn, center, left turn, center, right turn. "What comes next? What did you see to say so?" Answer: center, because every right turn was followed by center. Wrong: "Right turn, it stays where it is now" (E); "Left, I like left" (G). A blank answer is filed as an incomplete component, not a letter.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Force arrows: on day 8 the pendulum drawing gets the string's pull and gravity, two arrows, and the class swing model from day 1 is revised; arrows show forces on the washer, not its path. Patterns: the position table begins on day 9 and adds repeated ordered observations; day 10 reads and predicts from it. Not withdrawn. Review, in the ledger's words: fair test (keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing); whole centimeter and ruler zero; trial; balanced, unbalanced and cancel (from section 1.1); the dated weather table; pattern; predict; question board; return and repair; group roles rotate; repeat an unclear try and keep both.
- **Key moment 1, day 7: the direction test on the tray.** Ideal instrument: a force reader for each hand, which a class does not have; the ruler shows the result. Spec: the Curio Class pendulum and balanced-push simulation is spec only, does not exist, and partly fits as a way to compare other directions after the real trials. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md#pendulum-and-balanced-push-simulation).
- **Key moment 2, day 9: positions of a swinging washer.** Ideal instrument: a motion sensor or slow video with a ruler grid, which a class does not have. The class pendulum with a taped strip marked every centimeter fits partly: it shows order well and size only to about 1 cm. The simulation partly fits for repeat records. Asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 7: six sets, one per group, six trials a group. Day 9: six pendulums with four counted washers, secured by the teacher; five runs of ten turns, about 10 seconds a run, rotating readers, 14 minutes in all. Day 8: one teacher pendulum. Eye protection: a class set, worn while a pendulum swings, wiped by captains in the clean-up part (30 pairs, five captains, 3 minutes). Washers counted out and in.
- **Fallback.** A reading that cannot be made (the washer too fast to read) is written as order only, "far right, center, far left", and the table keeps the side column. If a pendulum cannot be hung, an invented record is used and said aloud to be invented; it supports reading and predicting, not conducting.
- **A room with nothing.** The box test on a book and two pencils. For the pendulum, a key on a string held by an adult at arm's length, low arc, with a taped paper strip; or an invented record, said aloud to be invented.

## 6. Days

## Day 6


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine, run by a helper from the rotating six.
- **6 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 5, A, B and C, 2 minutes each.
- **2 min. Set-up.** Roles named; trays set on the floor.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Fair test words, keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing, said back.
- **7 min. The feature menu.** New. A push has three features to change: how many pushes, how strong, which way. A menu card lists them. With an invented crate, the class picks which to change and which to keep; for direction, two about equal pushes from the front and back (balanced) are compared with two from the front (unbalanced).
- **13 min. Group plans.** Six groups of five write a team plan for a direction test on the other axis of the tray (front and back): change direction; keep the box, tray, start line, two pushes, about equal strength and where they touch; watch the end place in whole centimeters; three trials of each. Groups choose their unbalanced pair (both from the front, or one from the front and one from the side), who plays each role, and a prediction with a reason. The teacher meets each group for 2 minutes, 12 minutes in all.
- **4 min. Plan swap.** Two groups read each other's plan, tick the one change and the kept things, and say one question aloud.
- **3 min. Exit.** E6a, E6b.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Trays stacked.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has teams write a direction plan and swap it; the exit gives a plan that changes two things and asks what it should change, a repair the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (menu card); groups (team plan); children (own prediction). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): practice with feedback, 3 min (inside Group plans). By hand: plan sheet. Evidence: E6a, E6b. Hand-backs out: D and H to day 7 (4 minutes).

## Day 7


- **2 min. Set-up.** Floor clear; equipment out.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 6, D and H, 2 minutes each.
- **4 min. Own plan.** Each child writes their own plan from the team plan: the one change, the kept things, the trials. This starts the record revised on day 8.
- **16 min. Run the group tests.** Six groups of five, two conditions, three trials each, about a minute a trial; roles rotate so every child has an identifiable trial, marked with their initials, and every child records every trial. An unclear trial is repeated once and both are kept. A box that moves 1 cm or more under the balanced pushes is recorded as it happened. If the unbalanced pair does not move the box, push harder and record both. If the kit fails, an invented record is used and said aloud to be invented.
- **7 min. Arrows and explanation.** Each child draws the arrows for both conditions and writes two sentences: what stayed or moved, and why.
- **5 min. Compare groups.** Groups put their results on the board. Different groups chose different unbalanced pairs and numbers of trials, yet the patterns agree: different methods can give evidence for the same question.
- **3 min. Exit.** E7.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Boxes and trays wiped and stacked.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice runs two push conditions and draws arrows; the exit reads three given tries of 0, 0 and 3 cm and says what they show, a reading of results the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (board); groups (team plan, results); children (own record). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): show why, 4 min (Arrows and explanation). By hand: ruler, record, arrows. Evidence: the day 7 record (first-teaching work, revised day 8, repair mark on day 15); E7. Hand-backs out: B and G to day 8.

## Day 8


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 7, B and G, 2 minutes each. The day 7 record's look-for checklist and question stamp are returned too.
- **8 min. Revise the record.** Each child revises the plan and explanation in a second color, keeping the original observations and initials.
- **12 min. The pendulum, two pulls.** The teacher pendulum, eye protection on. Held at the side and released, then hung still. The class draws arrows for each moment: gravity pulls the washer down; the string pulls it toward the support. Held still at the bottom, the two are the same length and cancel; released from the side, they do not cancel, so it moves. Arrows are for forces, not the path. Then: "What could tell us where it goes next?"
- **8 min. Revise the swing model.** Each child revises the day 1 drawing, adding the two arrows. The class model is updated.
- **5 min. Question board.** "Can pushes act without movement?" is marked answered with the box evidence. "Will our explanation work in another test, and what repeats?" stays open.
- **3 min. Exit.** E8.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Captains collect and wipe eye protection.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice revises the child's own swing drawing with two arrows after the pendulum demonstration; the exit draws two arrows on a hanging lantern at rest, a new object the class never drew.

Held by: teacher (pendulum, board); children (own drawing). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): show why, 8 min; practice with feedback, 8 min. By hand: arrows. Evidence: E8; revised day 7 record. Hand-backs out: A and C to day 9.

## Day 9


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Measuring warm-up.** Retrieval of grade 2: put zero at the end of a drawn strip and read a whole-centimeter length; a miss gets zero aligned and a second strip.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 8, A and C, 2 minutes each.
- **4 min. Set-up and safety.** Eye protection fitted; stop signal; the recorder counts out four washers; the teacher has secured each support; the paper strip is taped under the path with center marked and a mark each centimeter.
- **14 min. Record the swings.** Six groups of five. The releaser holds the washers still at the 10 cm mark and lets go gently, no push. Ten turns a run. Five runs, so each child reads in turn. Everyone writes the first six turns in their own table: turn number, side, mark in whole centimeters. Counts, not time, order the runs. A turn too fast to read is recorded as order only.
- **9 min. Table and pattern.** New: position table; turning point; cycle, one left turn, center, right turn, center. Each child marks one cycle on their table in color and writes what repeats and what changes: the sides repeat, the marks get slightly smaller. The word "pattern" is reviewed; one run is not a pattern.
- **3 min. Exit.** E9a, E9b.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Washers counted in; eye protection wiped; strips collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice records swings in a table with a group; the exit predicts the next turn from a given record and whether a far turn will repeat exactly, a prediction the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (secured supports); groups (pendulum); children (own table). Tool: None. By hand: ruler, strip, table. Evidence: E9a, E9b. Hand-backs out: E and F to day 10.

## Day 10


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 9, E and F, 2 minutes each.
- **5 min. Warm-up.** Read a table row aloud to a partner: turn, side, mark.
- **12 min. Pairs with practice cards.** Fifteen pairs. Six cards of other swings, labeled "invented for practice": a ceiling lamp, a hanging gate, a wind chime, a rope bridge and two more. Each pair covers the next entry, says "I think ___, because I saw ___", uncovers it, and says which turns supported them. The teacher hears a rotating six, 40 seconds each.
- **6 min. Own table.** Each child marks the repeating order on their day 9 table and predicts the next turn with two earlier turns as evidence.
- **5 min. Exit, section exit ticket.** E10, individual.
- **5 min. Section close.** The class pattern list: what repeats (the order of turns) and what changes (the size). The question board is updated.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Cards collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has pairs justify predictions from six swing cards; the exit has each child alone say what comes next in a new rocking record, the same act on a case no card shows.

Held by: teacher (class list); pairs (cards); children (own table). Tool: None. By hand: tables, cards. Evidence: E10. Hand-backs out: E and G to day 11 (section 1.3 holds that slot).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 6: "Which feature are we changing, and what are we keeping?" Day 8: "What pulls on the washer when it is held at the side? When it hangs still?" Day 10: "What did you see that makes you say so?" Every child may pass in public talk.
- **Written task, day 7.** The group-test record: plan, trials with initials, arrows and two sentences.
- **Judged by the teacher with 5 look-fors.** One change named; the rest kept; trials repeated; arrows match what was seen; an explanation that uses balanced or unbalanced and a centimeter reading.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: feature, number, strength, direction, front, back, turn, center, cycle, repeat, table, predict. Frames: "I changed ___ and kept ___." "The next turn will be ___, because ___ and ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner moves the pendulum or box as directed; large tables; raised tape marks; roles that need no fine reading; a seat near the ring.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; for E10 the record may be read to the child.
- **Reading load.** Tables and labels; no sentence over 15 words; directions are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A group that finishes early tests a second feature, number or strength, with a plan like its first and the same checks; it is not scored.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A book on a smooth table, a ruler and two pencils for the direction test: pushes from the front and back, then both from the front, three tries each, and the arrows. For the swing, hold a key on a string at arm's length, low arc, and write the order of turns; or use a record you have drawn. Keep the washers or the key from the child's mouth.
- **One projector.** Show the menu card and the class table; each child writes their own record.
