# Section 3.1: Different lives, a shared pattern

## Header


Different lives, a shared pattern

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS1-1, this section's share (days 37 to 41).** "Make models showing that living things have many different life cycles, yet all are born, grow, reproduce and die." This section carries all of it once, on a flowering plant and three animals: day 37 first drawings, day 38 the plant, day 39 the class beetle, day 40 the butterfly and the frog, day 41 the shared pattern. Section 3.4 returns to it on new records (days 53, 54, 56, 58).
- **3-LS3-1, this section's share (day 38 only).** "Use data as evidence that plants and animals get traits from their parents and that members of a similar group differ in those traits." Only the ten-minute support that reviews grade 1; the data work belongs to section 3.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Flowering plants only. No human reproduction: no person, baby or family of people is a case. No genes. Reproduction continues a kind, not one individual, and not every individual reproduces. Dates describe these individuals, not universal life spans. An unseen stage is never labelled observed. Children do not handle the mealworms, bran or habitat; nothing is released.
- **Connections.** Grade 2 seed and pollen routes (day 37). Whole-centimetre ruler for plant height (days 37 to 39, m2-measure-with-tools). The ELA frog reading (school days 40 to 77): day 40 uses pictures only and does not teach that text.
- **Practices.** Developing and using models (every day): each child changes their own drawing in a second colour and keeps the first.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a life cycle is the pattern of a kind, not one animal coming back; the adult makes new individuals and later dies. A flowering plant's new individual begins in a seed; germination is a growth stage, not birth. Mealworm beetles, butterflies and ants have egg, larva, pupa and adult; frogs and dragonflies change without a pupa. Stage names differ across kinds; the four events do not. Pollen is not a seed (grade 2).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Items 1 to 3 and the portfolio belong to section 3.4. Rule 4: the chicken, corn, ant and dragonfly cards below are new to the unit; the tomato, the class beetle, the butterfly and the frog are taught and never used as exits.

**Exit questions.** Each is a card, "invented for practice", read aloud. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E37 (chicken).** Cards: egg (day 0), chick (day 21), hen (day 150). "Put the cards in the order a chicken's life goes. What does your arrow mean?" Answer: egg, chick, hen; the arrow means "then", a later stage. Wrong: "The hen turns back into the same egg" (A).
- **E38 (corn, photographs only).** Cards: seed (day 0), seedling (day 8), tall plant (day 70), ears holding kernels (day 100), dry stalk (day 130). "Which stage shows the plant making seeds? How do you know? Is its box solid or dotted?" Answer: the ears; kernels are seeds that can begin new plants; dotted, because the class has only a photograph. Wrong: "Picking the ears shows new plants were made" (B); "The dry stalk starts again as the same plant" (A).
- **E39 (ant, photographs only).** Cards: egg, larva, pupa, an adult with eggs beside her. "What does the adult do that starts the next ant? Which card is the next ant's birth?" Answer: she lays eggs; the egg is the new ant, a different animal from her. Wrong: "The adult ant turns into an egg" (A).
- **E40 (dragonfly, photographs only).** Cards: egg, young in water, adult. "How many stages are drawn? Is a pupa needed? Where does the next young one come from?" Answer: three; no pupa; from an egg the adult lays. Wrong: "The adult changes back into the young one" (A). A wrong stage count is an incomplete component, not a letter.
- **E41 (section exit ticket, 10 minutes).** Given on the unit plan, field 5. Moth Z: day 0 egg, 5 larva, 20 pupa, 32 adult, 45 new eggs, 60 original adult dead. Poppy: day 0 seed, 6 seedling, 42 flowering adult, 60 seed pod, 70 new seeds growing, 76 new seedlings, 84 original adult dead. Printed models copy these stages but add an arrow from the dead moth to its own egg and label every interval six days. "Which arrows or labels lack support, and how would you repair the models to show the shared pattern despite different stages and timing?" Expected: separate parent and offspring labels, corrected unequal intervals, birth, growth, reproduction and death in both, a pupa only in the moth. Wrong: the dead moth returns as its own egg (A). Other unsupported labels get descriptive feedback.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The child's own life-cycle drawing (day 37) gains the plant's flowering and seeds on day 38, the beetle stages on day 39, the butterfly and the frog on day 40 and the shared pattern on day 41, each time in a second colour with the first kept. Review, in the ledger's words: flower, fruit and seed; pollen is not a seed; young share features with their parents but are not exact copies; dotted where unseen, solid where seen; the question board. Nothing is withdrawn. The stage arrow means "then", never a push, a carry or a needs arrow.
- **Key moment 1, day 39: a stage seen alive.** Ideal instrument: a hand lens on an open dish with a time-lapse of the whole cycle. Spec: the sealed habitat fits partly (stages show through the lid; nothing is touched or counted); a time-lapse does not exist. The Curio Class growth viewer (light, show why, spec only) partly fits on day 38, five minutes replacing part of the comparison; it must keep dates and seen or supplied labels and ask for a prediction first. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 41: the shared pattern.** Ideal instrument: a wall chart where every model lies beside the others. Paper fits.
- **Equipment and minutes.** One sealed habitat, six groups at one minute each: 6 minutes inside a 10-minute part (day 37) and an 8-minute part (day 39). Rulers read to the whole centimetre, step 1 cm; plants are not uprooted. Clean-up is 3 minutes on days 37 to 39: the adult empties cups, trays and rulers are wiped, hands are washed in two waves at two sinks with wipes for the wave that waits.
- **Fallback.** A plant or the habitat fails (mould, no pupae yet): the adult removes it and the day runs on the dated photograph cards, said aloud to be supplied, "invented for practice" where they are cards, and weaker than seeing.
- **A room with nothing.** Cards and the dated class record only; every box is dotted, and the class says so.

## 6. Days

## Day 37


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers look at the pots and habitat; adult care continues.
- **8 min. Hook: alike, yet not the same.** The labelled class seedlings beside the packet picture of their kind, then two bigleaf hydrangea pictures, blue and pink, captions hidden. "How can plants of one kind look alike yet differ?" Initial explanations are written on strips and kept, not corrected.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Review of grade 2, in the ledger's words: flower, fruit and seed; pollen is not a seed. Pairs find sowing and pollination in the dated class record. A miss gets separate pollen and seed arrows.
- **10 min. Look and measure.** The sealed habitat passes group to group, one minute each (6 minutes), while the other groups measure their plant pair to the whole centimetre and write the date (4 minutes each); the jobs rotate inside the 10.
- **8 min. First life-cycle drawing.** Worked example on the board (3 minutes): boxes for stages, a stage arrow that means "then", a solid box for a stage seen and a dotted box for one only pictured or not yet seen. Each child then draws a class plant's life and a mealworm's life.
- **3 min. Question board.** Notices become questions, grouped growth, resemblance, surroundings.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Exit.** E37; the rotating six say theirs to the teacher (6 children at 30 seconds).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws a first life-cycle of the class plant and mealworm from a worked example; the exit puts cards of a chicken's life in order alone and says what the stage arrow means.

Held by: teacher (board, habitat); children (own drawing). Tool: paper life-cycle model (light): show why, 8 min (first drawing). By hand: drawing, measure. Evidence: E37; the first drawing kept. Hand-backs out: A to day 38.

## Day 38


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Living records.** Pairs measure and water equally from adult-filled cups; others look.
- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 37, A.
- **10 min. Readiness support: like parents (review).** Whole class if more than a third missed day 36 item 1 or 2; otherwise a small group with the teacher while the rest compare a radish card (dated photographs, invented for practice). Two minutes rereading the grade 1 record, four matching one shared feature and circling one difference, two saying how both can be true, two on a fresh pair. Review, in the ledger's words: young share features with their parents but are not exact copies.
- **5 min. Reading the arrows.** What arrows and labels show: the stage arrow means "then", dates sit on boxes, solid means seen, dotted means pictured. The class plant's dated record is read aloud.
- **12 min. Compare two records.** Pairs lay the class plant's dated record beside a tomato photograph card (invented for practice, photograph only). They add the pictured stages dotted: flower, fruit holding seeds, dried plant. Taught: birth is the start of a new living thing (in a flowering plant, in a seed); the adult reproduces by making seeds; the seeds' arrow ends at a new plant, labelled "new plant", never at the dried one. The growth viewer, if it exists, replaces 5 of these minutes with a prediction first.
- **3 min. Revise your model.** Second colour; the plant drawing gains reproduction and death.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Exit.** E38.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has pairs lay the class plant record beside a tomato card and add dotted stages; the exit picks which of five corn cards shows seed making and says why a photograph box is dotted.

Held by: teacher (arrow routine); children (own drawing). Tool: paper life-cycle model (light): show why, 12 min. By hand: cards, drawing. Evidence: E38; the support sheet. Hand-backs out: B and A to day 39.

## Day 39


- **2 min. Living records.** Pairs measure their pair to the whole centimetre; others look.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 38, A and B, 2 minutes each.
- **8 min. Observe the habitat.** The sealed habitat passes group to group, one minute each (6 minutes) while the others sort photograph cards (egg, larva, pupa, adult); then 2 minutes to mark in the class table which stages were seen alive today.

The adult lays eggs: the arrow goes to a new box labelled "new beetle". Adults die after weeks (photograph card). Terms: egg, larva, pupa, adult, offspring.
- **8 min. Revise your animal model.** Second colour; each child's day 37 mealworm drawing gains the missing stages, dotted where unseen.
- **5 min. Beside the plant.** On the class chart the plant and the beetle stand side by side; pairs name one thing the same. This is a first look; day 41 builds the pattern.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Exit.** E39.
- **2 min. Question board.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice observes the habitat and adds missing stages to the child's own mealworm drawing; the exit reads four ant cards and says what the adult does to start the next ant, and which card is its birth.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own drawing). Tool: paper life-cycle model (light): show why, 10 min. By hand: cards, drawing. Evidence: E39; the revised drawing. Hand-backs out: A to day 40.

## Day 40


- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 39, A.
- **6 min. Hook: two animals that change a lot.** Butterfly and frog picture cards (Curio Class originals, photograph only); pairs sort the cards that belong to one animal.
- **12 min. Teach two more cycles.** Butterfly: egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, adult. Frog: egg, tadpole, froglet, adult frog. Both adults lay eggs. - **2 min. Question board.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E40.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (own set). Tool: paper life-cycle model (light): show why, 8 min. By hand: cards, chart. Evidence: E40; the revised set. Hand-backs out: A to day 41.

## Day 41


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** The two commonest letters from days 39 and 40, 2 minutes each.
- **3 min. Seed look.** A look, not a set-up: pairs check the pods for ripening; only stages actually seen are marked solid and supplied photographs are marked apart.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds the four-cycle pattern chart and revises each child's own models; the exit repairs two printed models with a wrong arrow and equal intervals, a repair the practice never asks.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 37: "How can plants of one kind look alike yet differ?" Day 39: "What do you see in the box, and what do you not see?" Day 41: "What is the same in a plant's life and a beetle's?" Every child may pass in public talk.
- **Written task, day 41.** The revised set of models, with the exit ticket on its own sheet.
- **Judged by the teacher with 4 look-fors.** All four events are in each model; the new individual is labelled apart from the adult and the arrow leaves the eggs or seeds, not the dead adult; solid means seen and dotted means pictured; a difference in stages or timing is named.

## 8. Practice that returns


- **Warm-up.** Day 37: flower, fruit and seed, and pollen (grade 2). Day 38: alike in ___; different in ___ (grade 1). Days 39 to 41: the last model.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: egg, larva, pupa, adult, stage, then, seen. Frames: "First ___, then ___." "We saw ___. We did not see ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Raised-outline stage cards; large-print cards; a partner describes the habitat aloud, and a photograph card is in hand.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card aloud; accept pointing, ordering cards or a drawing; a scribe.
- **Reading load.** Cards under 40 words, no sentence over 15; the teacher reads every card.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second animal from a card to the pattern chart and say what is the same.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 37: look at a plant and its seed packet picture; draw its life so far with arrows. Day 38: put three dated plant pictures in order and say what comes next. Day 39: order egg, larva, pupa and adult cards. Day 40: compare a butterfly and a frog. Day 41: fix one arrow so the new animal is not the old one.
- **One projector.** Show the cards and chart; children draw their own.
