# Course frame: Science Grade 3

Course id science-k5 (grade 3 row), grade 3, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6: forces and motion, magnets and a design problem, life cycles and traits, surviving where you live,
  changing environments past and present, then weather, climate and hazards.
- **Days.** 120 teaching days: 111 in units and 9 at course level (6 for three course-level checks, 3 respond
  days). Elementary science shares the day, so the course assumes one science lesson of 45 minutes on four days a
  week (180 minutes a week) for 30 of the year's 32 teaching weeks; the other two weeks hold the school's own
  testing and events. A science day is therefore not a school day: science day n falls near school day 4n/3, and
  the cross-subject calendar below uses both. The year owns 15 rows (16 performance expectations, with 3-PS2-4
  and 3-5-ETS1-1 in one row). A row that joins an investigation, a model revised on evidence and a written
  explanation or argument needs about six to eight 45-minute lessons, so 15 rows fill about 111 days; the full
  160-day yardstick is not needed.
- **Standard.** The grade 3 performance expectations of data/standards/ngss-pes.txt, each taught in full (every
  clarification example the standard names has a place in the unit that owns it), plus 3-5-ETS1-1, the band's
  engineering expectation this grade owns. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md. Practices and crosscutting concepts
  are named per unit in the C3 column of the Units table (C3 is the social studies framework; science uses that
  column for its own two other dimensions, see "For the lead"). Plain facts (BASELINE rule 5, values rule b): the
  age of fossils and the long history of life are taught as the evidence shows.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"): the elementary
  units in the source store are NonCommercial and reference only, read for sequence and ideas, never copied.
  Phenomena are chosen to be real, observable in a classroom or schoolyard where possible, puzzling at grade 3 and
  explainable with the unit's core ideas; each unit names its anchor below, and a teacher may swap in a local
  phenomenon that needs the same ideas. Investigations are our own designs. Data and pictures come from open and
  public-domain material only: NASA, USGS and NOAA pages and data, the National Weather Service's local climate
  records, public-domain images, and open simulations released before 2026-03-29, each checked on its own terms.
  Curio Class's own online tools serve as simulations where a class cannot run the investigation (listed per
  unit, built later through the tool pipeline).
- **Materials.** Classroom-cheap: paper, tape, string, washers, rulers, cups, seeds, soil, bar and ring magnets,
  balloons, a thermometer and a straight-sided jar for rain. Every investigation names a household substitute
  where one is safe, and a no-kit path where none is (a data set from a class that ran it, or a Curio Class
  simulation), said plainly to be weaker than doing it (PRODUCTION-PATH: substitutes, then no-kit). Each unit plan
  states its cost to the school.
- **Safety.** A course safety page for the year (eye protection where anything can fly, counting magnets out and
  back, hand washing after soil and living things, allergy checks); every hands-on day carries its own Safety and
  set-up section (LESSON-DAY.md).
- **The weather log.** From the first week, the class keeps a weather log (temperature, rain or snow, wind
  direction, sky) for about five minutes on two lesson days a week, inside other units' lessons. Unit 6 reads
  months of the class's own data beside local records. The plant investigations of unit 3 likewise run across
  weeks while other lessons go on.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor, builds a question board, and each lesson set answers a
  question the board or the last lesson raised. Each unit has an initial model, named revision points and a class
  consensus model; the end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has not met.
- **How science works.** Taught on purpose in unit 5 (explanations of the past come from evidence in fossils, and
  change when new evidence arrives) and in unit 1 (a fair test changes one thing at a time).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Units 5 (deep time, environmental
  change) and 6 (storms and floods that students may have lived through) are named in their rows. Every hands-on
  unit needs a science teacher's read of safety and materials before the course is called finished (FOUNDATION
  7.3); who, and any pay, is Cory's.
- **Reading level.** Science texts are short (one to two pages), written at a grade 2 to 3 band with diagrams; the
  science words of each row are taught when the class needs them, never front-loaded as a list.

## For the lead

1. **Time a week (audience).** 120 science days of 45 minutes, four a week. Many schools give grade 3 science less
   (two or three lessons a week); the social studies grade 3 frame assumes a daily 40-minute block, and the two
   together with ELA and math may not fit every school's day. Pick: keep 120; the scope and sequence marks about 15
   days as compressible (unit 1's come-back can, unit 3's second organism, unit 6's lightning rod model) for a
