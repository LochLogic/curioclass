# Section 6.4: Connecting evidence and revising claims

## Header


Connecting evidence and revising claims

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-ESS2-1, this section's share (days 113, 114, 115, 117).** "Show weather data in tables and graphs to describe the weather usually expected in a given season." Joined to the other two on days 113 and 114, checked on day 115, repaired on day 117. The practice is "Analyzing and Interpreting Data" ("Represent data").
- **3-ESS2-2, this section's share (days 113, 114, 115, 117).** "Gather and combine information to describe the climates of different regions of the world." Same days. The practice is "Obtaining, Evaluating, and Communicating Information" ("Obtain and combine information").
- **3-ESS3-1, this section's share (days 113, 114, 116, 117).** "Judge, with a claim, how well a design such as a flood wall or lightning rod lessens the harm from a weather hazard." Joined on days 113 and 114, checked on day 116, repaired on day 117. The practice is "Engaging in Argument from Evidence" ("Make a claim"). Crosscutting: patterns; cause and effect.
- **Limits that bite here.** Only bar graphs and picture graphs; averages supplied; no temperature below zero as a number; no climate causes; climate change is not assessed and is answered plainly if raised. A design lessens harm and never stops the hazard; model results support model claims. Fresh work on days 115 and 116 is the scored work; the revised earlier products W, C and H are recorded beside it as a repair mark, not the score. No feedback and no reteach on days 115 and 116.
- **Connections.** English W.3.1 (an opinion with reasons) in the merit claim; math 3.MD.B.3 in the graphs. Day 119 (course level) audits new weather displays and day 120 responds; nothing here pre-empts them.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The anchor closes on day 113: no two days are alike, yet each season has its own usual weather (seasonal graphs), a place's climate comes from many years (regional records), and protection evidence supports less harm despite unusual weather. (2) How the 18 points are earned from the fresh day 115 and 116 work (six for each of the W, C and H components, one for each complete component), with each revised product recorded beside the score as a repair mark, not the score; why feedback is a six-tick look-for checklist and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; and why a missed chance is unscored, not zero. (3) Why every fresh item is new. (4) How the day 117 groups run. (5) What is handed on: day 118 is a unit 5 check.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** the three rows above, on the unit plan field 5: item 115a (3-ESS2-1, Ridge records), item 115b (3-ESS2-2, regions M and N) and item 116 (3-ESS3-1, the wind screen). Each fresh case is scored on the components of the earlier product (W for 115a, C for 115b, H for 116; for 116 the step-free component becomes the goal check in every trial). Each revised earlier product is recorded beside the score as repair evidence, a separate mark. Wrong answers: A, B (115a); C, D (115b); E, F, G (116).

**Evidence form.** Printed cards, "invented for practice" said first; the teacher reads directions and cards aloud once and does not interpret the data.

**Exit questions.** Day 113 has no exit: the unit plan gives it none, so its evidence is the 2-minute notebook entry and the revised product H. If many miss a letter on E114, it is repaired on day 117 only.
- **E114 (day 114, 12 minutes).** A new town has spring temperature 20 and summer 28 degrees Celsius, rainy days 8 and 4 in 30-day samples, and usual winds east and west. Its 30-year atlas says warm summers, while station records show summer rainy days ranging from 2 to 9. A proposed wind shelter costs six tokens, within a six-token limit. Its goal is to retain at least five of six markers. Matched trials retain 6, 6, 6 markers with the shelter and 2, 1, 2 without it. "(a) Make a table of the weather data, graph the rainy days and explain the likely seasonal pattern using all three measures and both climate sources. (b) Draw arrows for the shelter. Write a merit claim. Say what is not shown." Answer: bars 8 and 4 with labels; warmer, usually less rainy summers with west winds, varying across years. The shelter meets the retention goal in every trial within cost; the comparison supports less marker displacement, not an end to storms or guaranteed safety. Wrong, each tagged by its own letter: the scale ignored (A); one day guaranteeing a season (B); one year as the whole climate (C); one source only (D); the shelter stops the storm (E); cost ignored (F); the model guarantees real protection (G).
- **Recheck cards, day 117 (5 minutes, unscored, fresh).** One card for each group.
  - **R117-A (display).** Stone Hill, rainy days in 30-day samples, spring, summer, autumn, winter: 16, 6, 12, 2. "Draw a bar graph with a scale of 2 and label it." Answer: bars at 16, 6, 12, 2, scale 0 to 16 by 2. Wrong: bars drawn by counting marks (A).
  - **R117-B (one day).** "Autumn at Pine Row is usually dry. One autumn day was stormy. Ro says autumn there is stormy. Is Ro right? One sentence." Answer: No; one day is not the season. Wrong: "Yes" (B).
  - **R117-C (one year).** "Last January had 4 snowy days. The town's 30-year January record runs from 3 to 15. Ro says last January is the town's climate. Is Ro right? One sentence." Answer: No; one year sits inside the range. Wrong: "Yes" (C).
  - **R117-D (two sources).** "Atlas: Region X is cold in winter. Station: it rains in only 2 months. Write one sentence about X using both." Answer: X has cold winters and rain in only 2 months. Wrong: one source only (D).
  - **R117-E (protection).** Goal: at least 4 of 6 paper figures stay dry; limit: at most 7 tokens. A bus-stop cover costs 7 tokens; dry figures 5, 5, 4 with it and 1, 1, 2 without. "Does it have merit? Name one thing the trials do not show." Answer: yes, it meets the goal within the limit; it does not stop the rain, and heavier rain was not tested. Wrong: "It stops the rain" (E); "Cost does not matter" (F); "Real bus stops are safe" (G).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The three class models, joined on the wall on day 113 into one consensus model: the seasonal expectation model (section 6.1), the climate model and world map (6.2), and the protection model with its design cycle and arrows (6.3). Day 114 gives each child a printed copy to annotate; days 115 and 116 are annotated unaided; day 117 revises in a second color. The weather record continues on days 113, 114 and 117.
- **Weather record, the real reading.** As in section 6.1: one thermometer (step: nearest printed mark) and one rain jar (first printed mark; less is "wet", never dry), read by the helper pair with an adult, who alone touch the equipment and wipe their hands (fragrance-free wipes) inside the 5 minutes; below zero in words; unsafe weather uses the adult's reading or the dated adult record.
- **Key moment 1, day 113: the joined wall model.** Ideal instrument: one shared wall every child adds to. Paper cards and a wall fit. The three models are light; no digital tool is planned.
- **Key moment 2, days 115 and 116: annotate unaided.** Ideal instrument: a printed copy to mark. Paper fits.
- **Turns.** Day 113: 30 children place one card or arrow, about 20 seconds each, 10 of the 25 minutes. Day 117: a group of 7 or fewer states one revision at 45 seconds each; a larger group has a rotating seven speak while the rest write; a record names who.
- **Equipment.** Printed evidence records for days 115 and 116, no kit.
- **A room with nothing.** The printed cards read aloud, said to be invented; the dated adult record for weather.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: pattern, range, source, hazard, harm, merit, limit, trial. Frames: "In ___ it is usually ___. The atlas says ___; the station says ___. ___ has merit because ___, so ___. The record does not show ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged and tactile graph grids; a scribe or recorded spoken reasoning, without a second individual interview; large print.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all directions and cards aloud without interpreting data; the same evidence demands. Do not supply the scale, the link between sources or the limit.
- **Reading load.** Cards of 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, read aloud by the teacher; no independent long reading.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write the one record that would change your climate account, or the one trial that would test your design's limit.
