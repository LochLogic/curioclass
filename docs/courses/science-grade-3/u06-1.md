- **3-ESS2-1, this section's share (days 98 to 102).** "Show weather data in tables and graphs to describe the weather usually expected in a given season." All of the standard sits here: tables (day 99), pictographs and bar graphs (day 100), an expectation built from temperature, rain or snow and wind together (day 101), and an expectation kept when one record differs (day 102). The practice is "Analyzing and Interpreting Data" ("Represent data"); the crosscutting concept is patterns.
- **Limits that bite here.** Only picture graphs and bar graphs; no line graphs. Climate change is left out; if a child raises it, answer plainly from the evidence and say it is a later grade's question. Children never calculate an average: monthly averages are supplied. A temperature below zero is written in words and kept out of numerical graphs and comparisons. A blank box is "not recorded", never a dry day. Scales here count by 2; the key and the scale were taught in math (3.MD.B.3) and are retrieved, not taught.
- **Connections.** Math 3.MD.B.3, scaled picture and bar graphs: retrieval day 99, use day 100. Patterns (crosscutting): days 98 and 102. Claim, evidence and reason: days 101 and 102.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) "Usual weather" is what most days of a season are like across many dated records; it says what is likely, never what will happen. (2) One day is not a season, and one odd day does not break a pattern. (3) How to read a scaled graph: a picture or a tick can stand for 2 days, and half a picture is 1. (4) Why supplied averages and a local station packet cover the seasons the class log has not reached yet. (5) Product W: how its six points are earned (unit plan field 5), why feedback is written after class, and why its revision on day 102 is never scored that day.

- **E98 (day 98).** Card: a town called Fenmoor. (a) One cold, rainy day in July. "Pia says: it was cold on that day, so all of Fenmoor's summer is cold. Is she right? Say why." (b) "Fenmoor's winter log: cold, cold, snow, cold, mild, cold. What weather should Fenmoor expect in winter? Use the word usually." Answer: (a) No. One day is not a season; we would look at many summer days. (b) Usually cold, because most days were cold. Wrong: "Yes, she saw it" (B); "Yes, summer weather is the same every day" (B).
- **E99 (day 99).** Card: six dated autumn rows for a town called Ashby. Rain: yes, no, yes, no, yes, blank. Wind: west, west, north, west, east, blank. "(a) How many rainy days are written? (b) The last row is blank. Was it a dry day?" Answer: (a) 3. (b) We do not know; blank means not recorded. Wrong: (b) "Yes, dry, nothing was written" (B).
- **E100 (day 100).** "(a) A town had rainy days in three months: May 9, June 3, July 6. Draw a picture graph or a bar graph. Write the key or scale first. (b) A bar graph is marked 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. A bar stops at the fourth mark. How many rainy days?" Answer: (a) key first; May 4 and a half clouds, June a half cloud, July 3 clouds, or bars at 9, 3 and 6. (b) 8. Wrong: (a) "May 9 clouds, one for each day" (A); (b) "4" (A).
- **W (day 101).** Evidence is product W, not an exit.
- **E102 (day 102).** "Ridgeway's winter record has 27 cold days and 3 warm days. (a) One winter day was warm. What is a Ridgeway winter day usually like? (b) In another town's 24-day record, 15 days were odd. Does the town change its mind about the usual? Say why." Answer: (a) usually cold; one warm day is one row. (b) Yes; 15 of 24 is more than half, so many days are odd, not one. Wrong: (a) "Tomorrow will be warm" (B); (b) "No, the usual never changes" (no letter).

- **3 min. Return and repair.** Day 97's retrieval handed forward: children who missed finding a dated wind record in the class log get a highlighted row and find the wind box. The goal-and-limit half returns on day 109.
- **10 min. Hook.** Four pages from the class log stand with their headings hidden: a cold day, a warm day, a windy day, a rainy day, picked from different months. Pairs talk for 3 minutes, then predict what a fifth page, face down, will show. Reveal the dates: the pages come from different seasons. "No two days are alike. Can we still tell the season?"
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Review of weather watch: pairs find the date, temperature, rain or snow, wind and sky in one earlier entry. A miss gets matching symbol and label cards during the weather record.
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine. Children who missed the retrieval work the symbol cards with the teacher while the helper pair reads.
- **12 min. Worked example.** Twenty-four log pages lie in four piles by month. The teacher models one pile: what repeats, not each day. New words: season (spring, summer, autumn, winter; autumn is also called fall), usual, expect. The class starts the seasonal expectation model: four season boxes, each with temperature, rain or snow, and wind, written with "usually". A cold day in a warm pile: one day is not a season. Where the log has not reached a season, a local station packet page stands in and is said to be from the packet.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts log pages by month and models what repeats in a season; the exit gives a one-day claim about a new town's summer and a winter log, and asks alone what to expect, using the word usually.

Held by: teacher (pages, board); pairs. Tool: none (paper). By hand: pencil, symbol cards. Evidence: E98. Hand-backs out: E98's letters to day 99.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: season, usual, expect, table, key, scale, rainy day, wind direction. Frame: "In ___ it is usually ___, because ___ days out of ___ were ___. One day can differ." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Large-print and tactile grids; keys with shapes, never colour alone; counts read aloud; a partner may scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards and stems aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers. Do not supply the scale or the limit.
- **Reading load.** Cards of 50 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words; tables hold numbers and one-word entries.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write the record that would make you change the seasonal claim, and how many days it would take.
