## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do we know places have changed, and what happens to living things when they do?"
Day 82: reveal original drawings of fossil tree trunks preserved where they grew on Svalbard, beside a map and a treeless Arctic landscape. Hide explanatory captions. Ask how both landscapes could belong to one place.
Day 93 closes the anchor: fossil trees and the study's habitat interpretation support a formerly warm forest, unlike the comparison landscape. Students cite fossil records and distinguish ancient change from their short weather log.
Model observation versus inference on day 83 and a merit argument on day 88. Then ask children which evidence warrants each revision.

**Fossil product F, day 86, 12 minutes; revise day 93, eight minutes.** An unfamiliar grassland site has undisturbed layers. The lower layer contains complete freshwater-fish fossils 18 centimetres long and water-plant leaves six centimetres long at two mapped locations. An identification guide states that this fish form is extinct worldwide, though other fish exist. The upper layer contains rooted tree-trunk pieces 80 centimetres long. The guide associates the lower finds with lake water and the upper rooted trunks with trees growing on land. Ask children to annotate their past-environment model and explain what the kinds, sizes and locations support, what changed, and which layer is older. Contrast this long history with a week's weather observations. Give written feedback after day 86. Day 93 revisions precede scoring after day 95.

Award six points, one each for: using fish and plant evidence to infer a former lake; using both mapped locations without claiming the whole region was lake; interpreting sizes as evidence of organism dimensions, with a trunk piece giving only a minimum length; ordering the lower lake before the upper land trees with the undisturbed condition; distinguishing the extinct form from all fish; and revising the model with evidence while distinguishing long environmental history from a week's weather. All require analyzing evidence with past-life ideas and scale. Meets: "Fish and water plants at both places support lake water there before the rooted trees grew. The fish were 18 centimetres long, but the tree could be longer than its 80-centimetre piece. The lower layer is older because these layers were not disturbed. This fish form is gone everywhere, not all fish. I changed my all-grassland model to lake, then trees, then the shown grassland. These fossils concern long ago; a week's log cannot explain that history." Does not: "It is grassland now, so it always was" (A).

Day 86 fits five weather minutes, 20 regional-fossil minutes, 12 product minutes, five exit minutes and three cleanup minutes. Day 93 fits five weather minutes, eight revision minutes, 29 consensus minutes and three cleanup minutes. Do not turn the regional worked example into scored work.

**Solution product S, day 91, 20 minutes; revise day 94, eight minutes.** A new wet-meadow case isolates water loss after a drainage ditch opens. Supplied records show wet-soil plants failing to make seeds, frogs moving to another pond, trapped water insects dying, and dry-ground beetles arriving and reproducing. Goals are restoring wet plants and frog breeding, within six material tokens and a rule keeping the path dry. In matched model sites, a ditch plug costs five tokens, restores eight of ten wet plants and six frog egg clusters, but floods the path. A side pool costs six tokens, restores seven plants and five egg clusters, and keeps the path dry. The goal card requires at least seven plants and five clusters. Ask for a merit claim comparing the solutions, evidence-linked system arrows for plant and animal changes, and one limit of the evidence. Feedback follows day 91; revise on day 94 before scoring after day 96. No solution is built in a real habitat.

Award six points: explain water loss affecting plant reproduction; use records to distinguish moving, dying and arrival with reproduction; model the water-to-plant and water-to-frog interactions; support the side pool with both goal counts; compare both costs and the path constraint; and qualify the claim because matched model-site results do not guarantee every organism benefits in the field. Meets: "The ditch removes water the wet plants need to make seeds. Frogs leave, trapped insects die, and beetles arrive and breed. My arrows show water supporting plants and frog breeding. The side pool meets seven plants and five egg clusters for six tokens and leaves the path dry. The plug costs five tokens and the pool costs six. Both fit the budget, but the plug floods the path. The pool has merit for these goals; these model results do not prove it helps every organism." Does not: "The plug is best because eight is biggest, even if the path floods" (E).

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Today's environment must always have existed here. | Treats present conditions as past evidence. Repair by matching fossils to their supported past conditions. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Absence from one place proves worldwide extinction. | Confuses local loss with global absence. Repair by comparing a local map with the supplied worldwide extinction statement. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Deeper fossils are always older. | Drops the undisturbed-layer condition. Repair by comparing an intact stack with a flipped stack of cards. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Environmental change affects every organism in the same way. | Ignores differing needs and responses. Repair by tracing one supplied need for each organism. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | The solution with the largest count is best even when it breaks a limit. | Ignores constraints. Repair by checking the goal and limit boxes before judging merit. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | A fossil's size alone proves a tropical climate. | Treats a dimension as sufficient environmental evidence. Repair by pairing size records with independently supported habitat information. | Anticipated, from memory. |

Use repairs in teaching slots or day 97, never on assessment days. These are anticipated ideas, not observed errors.
