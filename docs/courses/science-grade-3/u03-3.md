# Section 3.3: What surroundings can change

## Header


What surroundings can change

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS3-2, this section's share (days 47 to 52).** "Use evidence to back the explanation that surroundings can shape an organism's traits, such as a plant stunted by too little water." The whole code is carried here: plants (crowding, water) and animals (diet and exercise, food and color, a learned response). The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts. Section 3.4 assesses it (day 55, portfolio record 1).
- **3-LS3-1, this section's share (days 51, 52).** "Use data as evidence that plants and animals get traits from their parents and that members of a similar group differ in those traits." Used to sort what comes from the parents, from the surroundings, or from both.
- **Limits that bite here.** Surroundings means water, light, space, soil, food and what an animal learns. No soil chemistry for the hydrangea (the soil differs; the color follows). No genes. No people's bodies or weights. No new deprivation test; grade 2's test is retrieved once. Photographs alone cannot assign a cause. A result that shows no difference is reported as it is.
- **Connections.** Unit 2's fair test and cause-and-effect arrows (days 48, 49); the day 18 radish comparison; whole-centimeter ruler (day 48, m2-measure-with-tools); the grade 2 bar graph (day 48).
- **Practices.** Constructing explanations (days 50 to 52): claim, evidence and reason, in the ledger's words from grade 2.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a trait can come from the parents, from the surroundings, or from both. Two pictures show a difference but not its cause; a comparison that changes one thing and keeps the rest the same can. The hydrangea case is sourced from a university garden page that reports the same kind of bigleaf hydrangea bearing blue flowers in some soils and pink in others (reference only, paraphrased); we do not teach why. A learned response is not passed to young. A result with no difference is evidence too.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Item 1 (day 55) belongs to section 3.4. Rule 4: the rosebush, pots X and Y, pots A and B, parrot, houseplant and the four sorting cards below are new to the unit; the hydrangea, the class radishes, the kale, the sisters, the arctic fox, the clicker dog, the trout, fern, chickadee and grass are taught and never used as exits.

**Exit questions.** Cards, "invented for practice", read aloud. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E47 (rosebush).** Two photographs of one kind of rosebush: one in a sunny yard with tall stems, one beside a shed with short stems. Sam says the yard made it taller. "Do the two photographs prove it? What else could differ?" Answer: no; water, soil, age or space could differ; we need a comparison that changes one thing. Wrong: "Yes, you can see the yard made it taller" (E).
- **E48 (pots X and Y).** Pot X has 3 plants, 15, 16 and 15 centimeters tall. Pot Y has 9 plants, 9, 8, 10, 9, 9, 10, 8, 9 and 9 centimeters. Same kind, water, light and soil. "What does the comparison show about crowding? What was the one thing changed?" Answer: the crowded plants were shorter; the number of plants in the pot was changed; for these pots, crowding made a difference. Wrong: "The shorter plants were born smaller; crowding changed nothing" (C).
- **E49 (pots A and B).** Pot A has 2 plants on a sunny sill. Pot B has 8 plants on a dim shelf. A's plants are taller. "Does this show crowding made B's plants shorter? Why or why not?" Answer: no; the light also differs, so we cannot tell. Wrong: "Yes, B has more plants, so crowding did it" (E); "A's plants are taller because their parents were tall" (C).
- **E50 (parrot, houseplant).** (a) A parrot hatched from an egg. Its family says "hello" to it every morning, and now it says "hello". Lee says it was born knowing "hello". "Is that supported? Where did it get it?" Answer: no; it heard it, so it learned it; it is not passed to young. Wrong: "It inherited saying hello" (D). (b) A houseplant of a tall-growing kind sits behind a thick curtain with almost no daylight and is short and pale. "Why is it short?" Answer: too little light is part of its surroundings. Wrong: "That kind is only ever short" (C).
- **E51 (sort four cards).** (a) A calf has the white face of both its parents. (b) A geranium in a dim hall has long, pale stems. (c) Two pumpkin kinds grow in a garden: big weighs more than small in every plot, and both weigh more in sun. (d) A hen runs to the barn at a rattle; incubator chicks do not. "Sort each: parents, surroundings or both. Write the words that show (c)." Answer: (a) parents, (b) surroundings, (c) both, the kinds differ and sun changes both, (d) surroundings (learned). Wrong: "(b) is from the parents" (C); "(c) is only from the parents" (C); "(d) the hen was born knowing the rattle" (D).
- **E52 (section exit, 10 minutes).** Given on the unit plan, field 5. The record traces same-kind mint cuttings to parent M, which has toothed leaves; every cutting keeps toothed leaves. With age, water, light, soil, container size and temperature matched, spaced cuttings reach 14 and 15 centimeters and crowded cuttings 8 and 9. "How do these data support an environmental effect while allowing inherited likeness, and what remains uncertain?" Expected: the controlled spacing comparison, shared toothed leaves from the recorded parent, and no claim that spacing explains every difference. Wrong: spacing explains every difference (C). Other slips get descriptive feedback.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Unit 2's fair test and cause-and-effect arrows (review, in the ledger's words: fair test, keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing). The cause-effect arrow is revised on days 48 and 49 from the crowding evidence, including a result that shows no difference. Grade 2's "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___" (claim, evidence and reason) is revised on day 52. The 3.2 resemblance chart stays on the wall and is sorted on day 51. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, days 48 and 49: the crowding comparison.** Ideal instrument: matched pots with measured heights and a repeat. Spec: the day 18 radish pots and a ruler (step 1 cm) fit; heights may differ by less than the step or the plants may fail, so supplied dated data (invented for practice, labeled) sit beside the class record. The Curio Class growth viewer (light, show why, spec only) partly fits on day 49, 5 minutes replacing a duplicate comparison, with a prediction before playback. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 52: the explanation.** Ideal instrument: a class chart with claim, evidence and reason side by side. Paper fits.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 48: six groups with two pots each, one ruler a group; a 12-minute measure fits about 12 plants a group. Clean-up is 3 minutes on days 48 and 49. Weather 5 minutes on days 49 and 50. The rotating six give their verdict on day 49 (6 children at 30 seconds, 3 minutes inside 5).
- **Fallback.** Radish pots fail or show no difference: say so aloud, read the supplied record beside the class one, and label which is which.
- **A room with nothing.** Pot cards and the supplied records only; every reading is invented for practice, said aloud.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 47: "What else could differ between two gardens?" Day 49: "Do the pots show a difference, or can we not tell yet?" Day 52: "What does the evidence let us say, and what does it not?" Every child may pass in public talk.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: surroundings, trait, learned, evidence, reason, crowded. Frames: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." "The records do not show ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Large-print pot cards; a partner reads the ruler aloud; dictation for the explanation; tactile height strips.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card aloud; accept a spoken, pointed or drawn answer; a scribe.
- **Reading load.** Cards under 50 words, no sentence over 15; the teacher reads each case aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write what a second change to the pots would tell us, and what would then have to stay the same.

## 10. Two rooms

Day 49: say what the readings show and do not show. Day 50: one short case, with the claim, evidence and reason. Day 51: sort four cards. Day 52: write one explanation.
- **One projector.** Show the pot cards and the chart; children measure, graph and write on paper.
