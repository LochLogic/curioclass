# Science Grade 3 tool requests

These requests come from [unit 1](s01.md), field 8. They describe work to specify later. No tool is built here.

## Pendulum and balanced-push simulation

Mark: light. Role: show why. Verdict: does not exist; spec only. It partly fits the ideal instrument because simulated observations cannot demonstrate conducting a physical investigation. Real objects remain the first route. The teacher judges plans and explanations.

| Day | Part | Job | Minutes inside the existing lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | Balanced pushes on a resting box | Show why opposing equal pushes can leave motion unchanged. | 6, replacing one comparison report. |
| 8 | Force arrows on the suspended object | Show why arrows describe pushes and pulls, rather than the path of travel. | 5, replacing a display discussion. |
| 9 | Pendulum position recorder | Show why repeated observations support a prediction. | 6, replacing a second apparatus observation. |
| 12 | Changed string length | Show why a prediction from the old conditions needs new observations. | 6, replacing one comparison trial. |
| 16 | Matched and unmatched records | Practice with feedback by selecting evidence for a prediction or force explanation. | 5, inside the assigned response group's seven minutes. |

1. Keep the balanced-push and pendulum parts separate. On the box, let students choose one variable from number, relative strength or direction of applied forces while the other conditions remain fixed. Start from rest. Provide a realistic surface setting and a clearly labeled simplified setting without surface resistance. A still box must not falsely establish equal hand pushes when surface resistance can also balance them. Compare force strengths qualitatively; show no numerical force units.
2. Draw arrows on the object receiving each force and name its source. Distinguish the direction of movement from force direction. On the pendulum, show gravity downward and the string's pull along the string. Include the paused turning point without implying balanced forces. Do not require calculating combined forces or introduce energy, period, frequency or trigonometry.
3. Let children predict, run a trial and record positions before seeing an explanation. Record successive turning sides and center crossings as events, not as equal-time measurements. Keep shrinking distances visible. Compare whole-centimeter lengths only; give words and ordered tables, not scaled graphs. When string length changes, a fixed observation beat can sample different positions. Do not imply that changing length removes the alternating order of turning sides.
4. Preserve repeat trials and the conditions attached to each record. Feedback on day 16 checks a closed choice and explains the observation that supports it. Do not score open plans or explanations automatically. Do not present simulated data as measured classroom observations.
5. The default room uses one device per student; one projector also works when students record predictions on paper before the teacher runs their chosen condition. Export printable trial records with conditions and provenance. An offline paper sequence must preserve the question and reasoning, although it cannot supply physical conducting evidence. No account or tracking is used.
6. Assessments on days 14 and 15 do not use this tool. Supply actual classroom observation records and a sequence of original still images for a no-kit route, clearly identifying who observed what and when. Until those exist, do not present invented numbers as measurements. Mark the force assessment's conducting component as not observed when only records or simulation are available.

## Force-arrow drawing

This is a paper model on days 1 to 8 and 13 to 16, not a request for another digital tool. Students retain and revise their own drawings. On assessment days they draw without corrective feedback; on day 16 they revise from component feedback. No key digital tool is required by unit 1.

## Non-contact interactions

Requested by [unit 2](s02.md), field 8. Mark: light. Role: show why. Verdict: spec only, does not exist; partly fits because simulated observations are weaker than handling real objects. Do not use it during assessments.

| Day | Job | Minutes within existing work |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | Compare gaps with one magnet and steel, retaining the same apparatus and repeated observations. | 5, replacing a duplicate report. |
| 20 | Turn one of two magnets at a fixed gap and contrast steel attraction. | 5, replacing a duplicate report. |
| 21 | Ask about one versus two magnets, their arrangement and object type without assuming doubled force. | 5, replacing a duplicate report. |
| 23 | Ask how a rubbed comb's attraction of paper changes with distance. | 5, replacing a duplicate trial. |
| 24 | Interpret an on-off electromagnet record if a safe teacher demonstrator is unavailable. | 6, replacing the demonstration. |
| 26 | Connect magnetic observations with a latch problem's criteria and resource limits. | 5, replacing a second example. |
| 33 | Repair a chosen interaction question or problem definition from component feedback. | 4, inside a six-minute response group. |

Let students ask a question and select one change before seeing results. Preserve object identity, gap and orientation in every record; compare force size qualitatively, without force units. Magnet-to-magnet attraction can become repulsion after a turn; a steel clip must not do so. Static paper attraction must not imply all uncharged materials repel. Visible failure to move does not establish absence of force. Show source-labeled arrows on the receiving object, separate from its travel. Retain gravity and tether forces where relevant.

The teacher judges open questions and definitions. Closed-choice feedback gives evidence and a reason, never an automated score for open explanations. Keep particle models, fields, circuit construction, energy calculations, compass extensions and gram measurements out. Any simplified simulation is labeled as a model rather than measured classroom data.

Default to individual devices, with a single-projector route in which children direct the selected change and record predictions on paper. No accounts or tracking. Export the same conditions and evidence as printable records. A paper route preserves questioning and interpretation. Real, dated classroom records with apparatus and provenance are still required for a no-kit observation route; invented practice cards are never represented as measured data. Include actual on-off observations, with teacher control and safety context, rather than asking families to build an electromagnet. Living records and weather observations use the class notebook or dated photographs, not this tool.
