- **3-LS4-4, this section's share: all of it (days 87 to 91).** "Judge, with a claim, how well a solution works for a problem caused when an environment changes and so may the plants and animals living there." The practice is "Engaging in argument from evidence": children name the changed condition, trace what it does to each living thing, then make a claim about a response's merit against its goals and limits. Section 5.3 revises and checks it.
- **Limits that bite here.** One environmental change at a time. No greenhouse effect or climate change. Every trial is a matched model trial, printed "invented for practice"; no solution is built in a real habitat and no real animal is tested. Counts are whole numbers out of 10; no percentages. No graphic animal harm; no one is asked for a personal disaster account.
- **Connections.** Unit 2's design statement and tradeoff (need, criteria, limits, tokens), days 87 to 89 and 91. Unit 4's system model and needs meet conditions, day 87. The claim frame "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___", days 88 to 91. ELA RI.3.8 (links between sentences), day 88: the case text marks its because and so links. Engineering depends on science research, day 89.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Merit is how well a response meets its goals within its limits; the biggest count is not merit. (2) A change gives different results: some living things stay and reproduce, some move, some die, some arrive; each follows from a need. (3) A matched model trial shows what works in the model, so every claim ends with one limit on the evidence. (4) The fish ladder is explained by mechanism only: a staircase of small pools. (5) Stream shade is one change: water temperature rises where shade is lost. (6) Fire, a spreading plant and a drained ditch are changes to resources; some plants need fire, so replanting is not always needed (5.3).

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 5.3 owns the items on days 95 and 96. This section writes day 91's product S (20 minutes), scored only after its day 94 revision.

**Product S, day 91 (unit plan field 5 holds the points).** Card, "invented for practice": a wet-meadow case after a drainage ditch opens. Records: wet-soil plants fail to make seeds; frogs move to another pond; trapped water insects die; dry-ground beetles arrive and reproduce. Goal card: restore wet plants and frog breeding, within six material tokens and a rule that the path stays dry. Matched model sites: a ditch plug costs five tokens, restores eight of ten wet plants and six frog egg clusters, but floods the path; a side pool costs six tokens, restores seven plants and five egg clusters, and keeps the path dry. The goal card needs at least seven plants and five clusters. Children give a merit claim comparing the two, system arrows tied to the records, and one limit on the evidence. Feedback follows day 91; revision is day 94.

**Exit questions.** Each is on a fresh card, "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" opens with that letter's wrong example.
- **E87 (3 minutes).** "A farmer removes a hedge beside a small meadow. Wind now blows across the meadow. A tall wildflower that needs shelter stops flowering. A bird that nested in the hedge moves to a farther hedge. A wind-blown grass spreads. Why did the wildflower and the grass have different results?" Answer: the wind meets the grass's need and not the wildflower's; each follows its own need. Wrong: "The wind changed both, so both did the same" (D).
- **E88 (6 minutes).** "A new canal cuts a field from a pond where toads breed. Goal: at least 6 of 10 marked model toads cross, within 4 tokens. Planks across the canal cost 2 tokens and 2 of 10 cross. A tube under the canal costs 3 tokens and 8 of 10 cross. A wide bridge costs 6 tokens and 10 of 10 cross. Which has merit? Use the goal and the limit." Answer: the tube meets both; the planks miss the count; the bridge breaks the token limit. Wrong: "The wide bridge, because 10 is the most" (E); "Every animal will use the tube" (D).
- **E89 (5 minutes).** "A weir across a creek stops minnows from reaching an egg pool. Goal: at least 6 of 10 marked model minnows reach the pool, within 5 tokens. Wide steps cost 2 tokens and 3 of 10 reach it. A step-pool channel costs 4 tokens and 8 of 10 reach it. A long ramp costs 7 tokens and 10 of 10 reach it. Which has merit, and what does the record not show?" Answer: the step-pool channel meets both; the record covers only minnows, not other creek animals. Wrong: "The long ramp, because 10 is the biggest" (E); "The channel will help every creek animal" (D).
- **E90 (5 minutes).** "Tall reeds that shade the edge of a small pond are cut. The edge water warms by 3 degrees. Cold-water insect larvae at the edge fall from 9 to 2. Warm-water snails rise from 2 to 8. A card says the larvae need cool water and the snails need warm shallow water. Why did they change in different ways?" Answer: warmer water meets the snails' need and not the larvae's. Wrong: "The warmer water made all the animals do worse" (D).
- **E91 (7 minutes, unit plan field 5).** A new spreading vine replaces seed plants eaten by field mice. Removing strips of vine restores eight seed plants and six feeding visits, costs four tokens and leaves the vine elsewhere. Whole-patch removal restores ten seed plants and eight feeding visits for six tokens. Goal: at least seven seed plants and five visits, within five tokens, without clearing the whole patch. "Which removal has merit, using the changed plant-food interaction and the goals and limits?" Answer: strip removal meets both counts and limits, restoring a food link without proving full recovery; whole-patch removal exceeds the budget and clears the whole patch. Wrong: all organisms respond alike (D); whole-patch removal is best because its counts are largest despite broken limits (E).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: change, condition, result, arrive, move, goal, limit, token, merit. Frame: "I think ___ has merit because ___, so ___. The record does not show ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged cards; tactile token cards; dictation; partners may read aloud; helpers may read seated.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers; a scribe. Do not supply the limit on the evidence.
- **Reading load.** Cards 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, read aloud by the teacher.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a third response to a case and say what record would show its merit.
