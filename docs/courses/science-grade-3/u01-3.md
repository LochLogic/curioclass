# Section 1.3: Testing predictions and explanations

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-PS2-2, this section's share (days 11, 12, 13, 14, 16).** "Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next." Children find the same pattern in a rolling ball, test a prediction when the pendulum changes, record an unfamiliar repeating motion alone, and respond.
- **3-PS2-1, this section's share (days 13, 15, 16).** "Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence." Day 13 pulls the unit's force evidence into one class explanation. Day 15 is a fair test on an unfamiliar sled, and day 16 repairs what the checks show.
- **Limits that bite here.** Turns are counted, never timed: no period, frequency or stopwatch. Forces are compared, not measured; hand pushes are only about equal. A turning point is not balanced just because the motion pauses for an instant. A failed prediction calls for checking the observations as well as the conditions. Whole centimeters; a moving washer or ball reads to about 1 cm at best. Observed data is never replaced by expected data.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1, whole centimeters, retrieved day 12 and used days 11, 12 and 14. Math unit 1, ordered tables, days 12 and 14. Speaking: "I think ___, because I saw ___" (review), days 11 to 16.
- **Practices.** Planning and carrying out investigations: day 12 changed-length record, day 15 plan and trials. Cause and effect: the class explanation, day 13. Patterns: the bowl as a second pattern, day 11.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: the ball in a bowl and the washer on a string behave alike because gravity pulls each toward the lowest place while the bowl or string guides the path; each passes the bottom because it is already moving, so it keeps going until a force changes that. Each turn is a place where it stops for an instant and gravity pulls it back, so it is not balanced there. The swings shrink slowly (rub and air; from memory). Shortening the string changes how quickly the turns follow one another, but the order does not change; do not give a rule for it. Teacher use of the assessment: the fresh day 14 and 15 work is scored outside lesson minutes, and the revised records get a repair mark beside the score.

Evidence of the three teaching days, asked aloud with cards labeled "invented for practice"; short stems; a rotating six heard. If many miss one, the next teaching day or day 16 opens with that error's wrong example. Rule 4: none of these cards is a case taught in the unit, and the day 13 cart and track are not met before day 13.
- **E11 (day 11).** Card: a can rolls back and forth in a curved skate dip: left side, bottom, right side, bottom, left side. **E11a.** "Where is the can next?" Answer: the bottom. Wrong: "The right side again, it stays there" (E). **E11b.** "Will it reach exactly the same height on the left again? Why?" Answer: no, a little lower; each pass has been a bit smaller. Wrong: "Yes, it always goes the same" (F).
- **E12 (day 12).** **E12a.** Card: a hanging bucket on a porch hook is shortened. Pat predicts it will stay on the left because it started there. The record shows left, center, right, center. "Was Pat right? What do you think now?" Answer: no; it still goes left, center, right, center. Wrong: "Yes, it started on the left" (E). **E12b.** "Pat saw the order one time and says it always works. What should Pat do?" Answer: run it again and compare. Wrong: "Nothing, it worked" (H).
- **E13 (day 13, section exit ticket).** Three fresh cards. Card 1: a wheeled cart rests at the bottom of a U-shaped track, with two equal arrows from opposite sides. "Does it move? Why?" Answer: no; the equal pushes cancel. Wrong: "Yes, two pushes start it" (B); "No force is on it, it is still" (A). Card 2: the cart is moved to a raised end and let go; its record is start, middle, far end, middle, start, middle, far end, middle. "Where is it next, and why?" Answer: start (a little lower is fine), because the order has repeated. Wrong: "The far end, it stays there" (E). Card 3: as it passes the bottom moving right, a new long arrow to the right is drawn on it; no one has watched it since. "What could the new arrow change? Can the old record say where it goes next?" Answer: its speed or direction; no, nothing new has been seen. Wrong: "Yes, the old pattern still tells us" (G); "The long arrow means it goes far" (C). A blank answer is filed as an incomplete component, not a letter.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Force arrows: day 13 settles the class consensus, that arrows are for forces on one object, not for its travel; day 15 uses them on the sled. Patterns and position tables: day 11 adds the bowl as a second pattern; day 12 checks predictions at a fixed beat, turn 3 and turn 5, the far turns, after the string is changed; day 13 separates the repeating order from the shrinking travel. The class question board is closed out: answered questions are marked with their evidence, unresolved ones kept. Both assessments use these models; none is withdrawn. Review, in the ledger's words: pattern; predict; fair test (keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing); trial; balanced, unbalanced and cancel; position table, turning point, cycle; return and repair; question board.
- **Key moment 1, day 12: the pendulum after the string is shortened.** Ideal instrument: a motion sensor or slow video with a ruler grid, which a class does not have. The taped strip with whole-centimeter marks and counted turns fits partly: order is clear; sizes read to about 1 cm. The Curio Class pendulum and balanced-push simulation is spec only, does not exist and partly fits, as a way to repeat a record after the real one. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md#pendulum-and-balanced-push-simulation).
- **Key moment 2, days 14 and 15: records made alone.** Ideal instrument: a real pendulum or sled for each child, which the plan does not have; one station with turns is the plan, so the order and marks come from each child's own sheet. Spec: no tool; the same simulation partly fits only for response practice.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 11: six bowls, soft balls and rulers; each child releases twice, about 40 seconds a release, 7 minutes. Day 12: six pendulums, shortened by the teacher alone, four counted washers. Day 14: one two-string paper basket at a station, with a spare; six groups of five, 4 minutes a turn including a 30-second wipe of eye protection, 24 minutes; the other groups work quietly on their own notebook explanation of the compelling question, which is not scored. Day 15: one station with a tray, an unfamiliar cardboard sled and a ruler, six groups, 5 minutes each, 30 minutes; each child makes one trial of each condition, about 30 seconds each. A second station would halve the wait and is an option. Clean-up on every hands-on day: 4 minutes, washers counted, eye protection wiped by captains, trays and bowls dried.
- **Fallback.** A reading that fails is written as order only. An invented record is a named fallback on days 11 and 13, said aloud to be invented, never on days 12 and 14, where only the child's own observations count; a child who could not read a turn records what was seen and says so.
- **A room with nothing.** The bowl and the box on a table; a key on a string for the pendulum at arm's length, low arc. The no-kit route is not available for the day 12 record or the check days.

## 6. Days

## Day 11


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 10, E and G, 2 minutes each.
- **3 min. Set-up.** Bowls on trays; tape strip inside each bowl marked every centimeter up from the bottom; roles named.
- **6 min. Predict.** The class watches the teacher release one ball from the 6 cm mark on the left. Each child writes first: "On the right, will it turn higher, the same or lower than the start? Then what?" with "I think ___, because I saw ___."
- **14 min. Observe the bowl.** Six groups of five. Each child releases the soft ball from the same left mark twice, without pushing, about 40 seconds a release; the others watch. Everyone writes the first four turns in a table: turn, side, height on the tape in whole centimeters. An unclear turn is recorded as order only. If a ball will not roll cleanly, repeat once and keep both.
- **6 min. Compare with the pendulum.** Same reasoning on a second thing: the order repeats, the size shrinks. Gravity pulls it to the bottom, and it keeps going past because it is already moving. Never throw the ball.
- **5 min. Pattern list.** The class pattern list from day 10 gets the bowl added. Question: "When should we change a prediction?"
- **3 min. Exit.** E11a, E11b.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Bowls dried and stacked; balls wiped.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice predicts, then watches a ball in a bowl; the exit predicts where a new rolling can goes next from a written record, with nothing to watch.

Held by: teacher (pattern list, board); groups (bowl); children (own table). Tool: None. By hand: tape strip, table. Evidence: E11a, E11b. Hand-backs out: E and F to day 12.

## Day 12


- **2 min. Measuring warm-up.** Retrieval of grade 2: zero at the end of a strip, whole-centimeter length; a miss gets zero aligned and a second strip.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 11, E and F, 2 minutes each.
- **6 min. Safety and predict.** Eye protection fitted; stop signal. The teacher alone has shortened each string to 40 cm. Each child writes: "Will the order of turns stay the same? Will the turns reach about the same marks?"
- **14 min. Observe the changed pendulum.** Six groups of five. A releaser holds the four washers at the 10 cm mark and lets go gently. Ten turns a run, three runs, counts not time. Each child, on their own sheet, ticks L, C, R for at least two cycles including the center crossings and writes the mark at turn 3 and at turn 5, the far turns, in whole centimeters. This is the record revised on day 13, made alone. A turn too quick to read is left blank, never filled by guessing.
- **6 min. Predictions against observations.** Groups put the predicted and seen marks on the class table. If a prediction failed, first check the readings, then what changed: the string.
- **6 min. Mark and predict.** Each child marks the repeating order on their record and predicts the next turning side after their last recorded turn, with two earlier cycles as evidence.
- **3 min. Exit.** E12a, E12b.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Washers counted in; eye protection wiped; strips collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice predicts and watches the shortened pendulum; the exit judges a classmate's claim about a changed bucket from a given record, and says what to do after one try.

Held by: teacher (secured supports, class table); groups (pendulum); children (own record). Tool: None. By hand: strip, ruler, record. Evidence: the day 12 record (look-for checklist before day 13, revised day 13, repair mark beside items 1 and 2 on day 14); E12a, E12b. Hand-backs out: E and H to day 13.

## Day 13


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **6 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 12, E and H, 2 minutes each. The day 12 records go back with their look-for checklists and question stamps.
- **7 min. Revise the record.** Each child revises the day 12 record in a second color, keeping the original observations.
- **11 min. Class explanation.** The class builds one explanation from the box evidence and the motion records: pushes can change motion; two about equal pushes from opposite sides cancel; at the bottom the string's pull up and gravity's pull down cancel, which is why it hangs still; gravity pulls the washer down the curve and the string guides the path; it keeps moving past the bottom; the order of turns repeats while the swings get smaller. The arrows are for forces on one object, not for its travel.
- **5 min. Unfamiliar record.** An invented record of a hanging flowerpot, said aloud to be invented, claims "it will reach exactly the same mark forever." Pairs find what the table supports and what it does not.
- **8 min. Exit, section exit ticket.** E13, individual, one half sheet with two questions read aloud one at a time, then one spoken card.
- **3 min. Question board.** Answered questions are marked with their evidence; unresolved ones stay.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds one class explanation from the box and swing evidence; the exit asks alone about two new cases, equal pushes on a cart at rest and the next place on a track.

Held by: teacher (class explanation); children (own revised record). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): show why, 6 min (Class explanation); check unaided, 8 min (Exit). By hand: arrows, record. Evidence: E13; the revised day 12 record. Hand-backs out: to day 16 (days 14 and 15 allow none).

## Day 14


- **6 min. Launch and transitions.** Rules for an assessment day: own work, no help, no feedback. Eye protection fitted.
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **24 min. Station turns.** Six groups of five, 4 minutes each, at one two-string paper basket the teacher has secured. The teacher releases it. Each child, on their own sheet, ticks the turning positions and center crossings for at least two cycles; three runs of ten turns a turn. Waiting groups write their own explanation of the compelling question in their notebooks, unscored. A 30-second wipe of eye protection falls inside the 4 minutes.
- **6 min. Independent prediction.** Each child marks the repeating order on their sheet and predicts the next turning side after their last recorded turn, using two earlier cycles. Scored as items 1 and 2.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Washers counted; eye protection wiped; strips collected.

Held by: teacher (station, sheets); children (own sheet). Tool: None. By hand: sheet, strip. Evidence: items 1 and 2 score this fresh record, outside lesson minutes; the revised day 12 record gets a repair mark beside them. Hand-backs out: the checklist to day 16, the scored paper to day 17.

## Day 15


- **5 min. Launch and transitions.** Own work, no help, no feedback.
- **30 min. Station turns and plans.** Six groups of five, 5 minutes each, at one station with a tray, an unfamiliar cardboard sled and a ruler. Each child plans on their own sheet while waiting: compare about equal opposing pushes with one deliberately stronger push, changing only the relative strength; keep the sled, tray, start line, where the pushes touch and their directions; repeat each condition. At the station each child makes one trial of each condition, about 30 seconds each, and records what the sled did, read to the nearest cm. An unexpected result is recorded honestly with a retest idea.
- **6 min. Independent explanation.** Each child draws the arrows for both conditions on the sled and writes why one stayed and one moved. Scored with the plan and trials as items 3 to 5.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Trays and sleds wiped and stacked.

Held by: teacher (station); children (own sheet). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): check unaided, 6 min. By hand: plan, ruler, arrows. Evidence: items 3 to 5 score this fresh work, outside lesson minutes; the revised day 7 record gets a repair mark beside them. Hand-backs out: the checklist to day 16, the scored paper to day 17.

## Day 16


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The look-for checklists for the day 14 and 15 work go back, with the day 13 exit; the scored papers come back on day 17. Each child sees which group they will join.
- **28 min. Four groups, 7 minutes each with the teacher.** The class is sorted by the largest gap into four groups; the teacher meets each in turn, and the others work from written partner prompts. Forces (A, B and C): hold a box still between two hands, draw both arrows, say strength and distance apart. Fair testing (D and H): cross out all changes but one in a proposed test, then rerun the same condition. Alternating positions (E): lay turning-position cards in order and slide a finger along them. Evidence limits (F and G): compare successive turning marks on a table, then highlight two earlier matching turns that support a prediction.
- **8 min. Fresh individual transfer.** Unit 2 retrieval: draw the two contact pushes on a fresh resting-box picture, labeled "invented for practice"; equal opposing arrows predict no change. Then, on the same card, a new short motion record to continue with a reason. Unscored; used to plan later support.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Papers collected; trays wiped.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has groups hold a box still and draw both arrows with the teacher; the exit has each child alone draw them on a book picture, then continue a bead record, with no help.

Held by: teacher (groups); children (own work). Tool: None. By hand: cards, arrows. Evidence: the fresh transfer, unscored. Hand-backs out: none; unit 2 retrieves.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 11: "Does the ball do what the washer did? How do you know?" Day 13: "When should we change a prediction?" Day 16: "What did you check first when your prediction failed?" Every child may pass in public talk.
- **Written task, day 13.** The revised day 12 record plus one sentence for each of: how the swing starts, stops and comes back.
- **Judged by the teacher with 4 look-fors.** The record is in order with the center crossings; the repeating order is marked; the prediction uses two earlier cycles; the explanation names the string's pull and gravity as arrows on the washer. Honest odd readings are kept.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: bowl, swing, turn, center, cycle, repeat, shorter, changed, prediction, evidence, balanced, unbalanced. Frames: "I predicted ___, but I saw ___." "The order ___ because ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner releases the washer or ball as the child directs; large tick boxes; raised tape marks; the track and tray cards described aloud; extra station turns scheduled first on assessment days.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; a partner may tick the sheet while the child calls the turns, provided the child watches and calls; large print; a scribe for the explanation.
- **Reading load.** Tables and labels; no sentence over 15 words; directions are read aloud, not coached.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict, then test, whether a heavier washer load changes the order of the turns (it should not) and say what stays the same.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A deep bowl on a table, a soft ball and a ruler: release from the same side twice and write the order of the turns. For the pendulum, a key on a string at arm's length, low arc, order of turns only. Last day of the unit, day 16: draw the two pushes on a resting box and say what the arrows predict. No report about home is asked of the child.
- **One projector.** Show the class table, the explanation and the track card; each child writes their own record.
