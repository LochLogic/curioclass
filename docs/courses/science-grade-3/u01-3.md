# Section 1.3: Testing predictions and explanations

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 3. Unit 1, Pushes, pulls and swings. "What makes a swing start, stop and keep coming back?" Anchor: a playground swing (and a class pendulum of string and washers) that one push sets going, that swings in a pattern we can predict, and that two equal pushes from opposite sides hold still (days 1 to 16): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 11 to 16.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (11), fluency 1 (13), application 1 (12), check 2 (14, 15), respond 1 (16). Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> On day 13, students explain starting, stopping and returning using box-test evidence and motion records: pushes can change motion; gravity pulls down while the string guides the curved path. Motion carries it past the bottom. The path repeats while swings become smaller.
>
> Group notices and wonders into causes and patterns on the question board. Days 1 to 5 ask, "Can pushes act without movement?" Class controlled tests connect contact forces with cause and effect. Days 6 to 10 ask, "Will our explanation work in another test, and what repeats?" Group tests lead to position records and predictions. Days 11 to 13 ask, "When should we change a prediction?" Bowl observations and a changed pendulum test the pattern's limits. Mark answered questions with evidence; retain unresolved ones. Transfer uses a hanging delivery basket on day 14 and an unfamiliar cardboard delivery sled on day 15.
> Carried: Cause and effect and force arrows begin on day 1; day 4 adds opposing arrows from box evidence; day 8 distinguishes the string's pull from gravity using pendulum observations. Day 13 reaches consensus: arrows describe forces on one object, not its travel. Patterns and position tables begin on day 1; day 9 adds repeated ordered observations; day 12 checks predictions at a fixed observation beat after changing string length. Day 13 consensus separates repeating order from shrinking travel. Both assessments use these models; none is withdrawn.
<!-- /skel -->

- **3-PS2-2, this section's share (days 11, 12, 13, 14, 16).** "Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next." Children find the same pattern in a rolling ball, test a prediction when the pendulum changes, record an unfamiliar repeating motion alone, and respond.
- **3-PS2-1, this section's share (days 13, 15, 16).** "Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence." Day 13 pulls the unit's force evidence into one class explanation. Day 15 is a fair test on an unfamiliar sled, and day 16 repairs what the checks show.
- **Limits that bite here.** Turns are counted, never timed: no period, frequency or stopwatch. Forces are compared, not measured; hand pushes are only about equal. A turning point is not balanced just because the motion pauses for an instant. A failed prediction calls for checking the observations as well as the conditions. Whole centimetres; a moving washer or ball reads to about 1 cm at best. Observed data is never replaced by expected data.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1, whole centimetres, retrieved day 12 and used days 11, 12 and 14. Math unit 1, ordered tables, days 12 and 14. Speaking: "I think ___, because I saw ___" (review), days 11 to 16.
- **Practices.** Planning and carrying out investigations: day 12 changed-length record, day 15 plan and trials. Cause and effect: the class explanation, day 13. Patterns: the bowl as a second pattern, day 11.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: the ball in a bowl and the washer on a string behave alike because gravity pulls each toward the lowest place while the bowl or string guides the path; each passes the bottom because it is already moving, so it keeps going until a force changes that. Each turn is a place where it stops for an instant and gravity pulls it back, so it is not balanced there. The swings shrink slowly (rub and air; from memory). Shortening the string changes how quickly the turns follow one another, but the order does not change; do not give a rule for it. Teacher use of the assessment: scoring the revised records happens outside lesson minutes.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: bowl, swing, turn, centre, cycle, repeat, shorter, changed, prediction, evidence, balanced, unbalanced. Frames: "I predicted ___, but I saw ___." "The order ___ because ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner releases the washer or ball as the child directs; large tick boxes; raised tape marks; the track and tray cards described aloud; extra station turns scheduled first on assessment days.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; a partner may tick the sheet while the child calls the turns, provided the child watches and calls; large print; a scribe for the explanation.
- **Reading load.** Tables and labels; no sentence over 15 words; directions are read aloud, not coached.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict, then test, whether a heavier washer load changes the order of the turns (it should not) and say what stays the same.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A deep bowl on a table, a soft ball and a ruler: release from the same side twice and write the order of the turns. For the pendulum, a key on a string at arm's length, low arc, order of turns only. Last day of the unit, day 16: draw the two pushes on a resting box and say what the arrows predict. - **One projector.** Show the class table, the explanation and the track card; each child writes their own record.
