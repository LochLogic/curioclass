# Section 1.1: What changes motion?

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 3. Unit 1, Pushes, pulls and swings. "What makes a swing start, stop and keep coming back?" Anchor: a playground swing (and a class pendulum of string and washers) that one push sets going, that swings in a pattern we can predict, and that two equal pushes from opposite sides hold still (days 1 to 16): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 5.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (1, 2, 3), fluency 1 (5), application 1 (4). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.

- **3-PS2-1, this section's share (days 1 to 5).** "Investigate how balanced and unbalanced forces change, or do not change, the way an object moves, and use the results as evidence." Here the class learns the force arrow, runs one teacher-planned box test with repeated trials, and reads starts, stops and turns from arrows. Group-chosen tests are section 1.2.
- **3-PS2-2, this section's share (days 1, 2).** "Observe or measure an object's motion to show that a repeating pattern lets you predict how it will move next." Day 1 gives the first look at the pendulum. Day 2 teaches the dated weather table and prediction from repeated entries. Position tables of motion begin in section 1.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Forces are compared, never measured in numbers. "Equal" hand pushes are only about equal, judged by feel, so a box that stays still does not prove the hands are equal. Change one thing at a time. Gravity is only a downward pull. The ruler reads whole centimeters, so "stayed" means moved less than 1 cm. No period, frequency or friction talk. No child sits on the playground swing.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1, whole centimeters, days 3 and 4. Speaking: "I think ___, because I saw ___" (review), days 1 to 5. Weather record, days 1, 2 and 5.
- **Practices.** Planning and carrying out investigations: plan on day 3, test on day 4. Cause and effect: first stated day 1, arrows built from evidence day 4.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a force is a push or pull on one object, with a direction and a size. Forces on one object that cancel leave its motion as it was: at rest it stays at rest. Forces that do not cancel change its speed or direction. A resting box still has forces on it: gravity pulls it down and the tray pushes it up. An arrow starts on the thing pushed and shows the force, never the path traveled. Pendulum background is for section 1.2.

- **E3 (day 3).** **E3a.** Card: Jo rolls a red toy truck gently on the carpet. Kim rolls a blue toy truck hard on the tile. They say hard pushes go farther. "Is that a fair test? Why?" Answer: no, they changed the truck, the floor and the push; change only one. Wrong: "Yes, one gentle and one hard" (D). **E3b.** "Kim rolled hers once and says she is sure. Should she roll again? Why?" Answer: yes, to see if it happens again. Wrong: "No, once is enough" (H).
- **E4 (day 4).** **E4a.** Card: two children pull a toy boat with strings from opposite banks; the two arrows are the same length. "Does the boat start to move? Why?" Answer: no; equal pulls from opposite sides cancel. Wrong: "Yes, two pulls make more go" (B). **E4b.** Card: a girl holds a book still against a wall with her hand. "Is any force on the book? Why?" Answer: yes, her hand pushes it and the wall pushes back; they balance. Wrong: "No, it is not moving" (A).
- **E5 (day 5, section exit ticket).** Four fresh cards, each labeled "invented for practice", with arrows of different lengths: (1) a wagon at rest, one arrow to the left; (2) a wagon rolling left, one arrow to the right; (3) a wagon rolling left, one arrow toward the top of the page; (4) a package at rest, two equal arrows on opposite sides. "Say what each one does." Then "Which card has the strongest push?" and "Draw which way gravity points on card 4." Answers: (1) starts moving left; (2) slows down; (3) turns toward the top of the page; (4) stays still, the pushes cancel; the longest arrow is the strongest push; gravity points down toward the floor. Wrong: card 4 "It moves left" (B); card 4 "Nothing pushes it, it is still" (A); "The longest arrow's ball goes farthest" (C).

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding; assessment: readiness. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Observe the swing and class pendulum, raise questions and draw an initial force explanation.
- note: Hook first; reserve last ten minutes for readiness on sk-push-pull and s1-sky-patterns, last used before grade 2, and sk-weather-watch (last used grade 1), to shape day 2. No direct sun observation. Weather log: 5 minutes within this day, temperature, rain or snow, wind direction and sky; below zero in words only.

<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Eye protection and the stop signal.** Eye protection is fitted first and the stop signal is taught before anything is handled.
- **8 min. Hook.** The class sits in a ring 2 m back from the teacher's pendulum (four washers on a 1 m string, support secured by the teacher). The teacher releases it from a side mark for about ten swings, then holds it still at the bottom with a hand, then lets go. If a swing is in view, the class also watches the empty swing from the window; otherwise a Curio Class original drawing of a swing is shown beside the pendulum. No child sits on a swing. Compelling question: "What makes a swing start, stop and keep coming back?" No explanation is given.
- **4 min. Notice and wonder.** Children say what they saw; the teacher writes notices and turns them into questions on the question board (review).
- **5 min. The force arrow.** New. A push or a pull is a force. The teacher draws three: a hand pushing a ball to the right, a harder push, a string pulling a toy toward the puller. An arrow starts on the thing pushed, points the way the force goes, and is longer for a stronger force. Each child copies one.
- **6 min. First explanation.** Each child draws the washer and string and the arrows they think make it start, stop and come back, then says to a partner, "I think ___, because I saw ___." The teacher hears a rotating six, 30 seconds each, 3 minutes in all. The drawing is kept and revised on days 8 and 13.
- **3 min. Exit.** E1a, E1b.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Captains collect eye protection; wipes.
- **5 min. Weather record, modeled.** The teacher shows the thermometer, rain jar, direction card and sky and writes one dated row; the class copies it. Day 2 teaches the steps. Adult prechecks; never look at the sun; hand wipes for the last minute.
- **10 min. Readiness check.** One minute of directions, nine for three paper items read aloud, labeled "invented for practice". Not scored; papers kept.

Held by: teacher (question board, class pendulum, record sheet); children (own drawing). Tool: force arrow drawing (light, paper): show why, 5 min (The force arrow); practice with feedback, 6 min (First explanation). By hand: drawing, copied weather row. Evidence: E1a, E1b; readiness papers. Hand-backs out: C to day 2 (3 minutes).

## Day 3

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Plan a class fair test of equal and unequal pushes on one box.
- note: Choose one change, what stays fixed, observations and repeated trials.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 2, G and H, 2 minutes each.
- **4 min. Warm-up.** Fair test: keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing (review). An invented plan, said aloud to be invented: Rin rolls a marble down a board, then a heavy ball down a longer board. Pairs say what was changed.
- **10 min. The class plan.** Question: "Do equal pushes from opposite sides change how a box moves?" The teacher builds the plan on the board. Change: how strong one of two pushes is (about equal, then one stronger). Keep: box, tray, start line, where the pushes touch, and that both push toward the box from opposite sides. Watch: where the box ends, read to the nearest whole centimeter; "stayed" means less than 1 cm. Repeat: three trials of each condition (a trial is one try). New words: balanced (about equal pushes from opposite sides), unbalanced (one stronger), cancel (the pushes undo each other). Each child writes a prediction, "I think ___, because ___."
- **14 min. Practice the pushes, not recorded.** Groups of five, roles rotate (pusher, second pusher, measurer, recorder, watcher). Each pusher tries a gentle push they can repeat, about three pushes at 20 seconds, 5 minutes in all. The two pushers match their pushes by feel and the watcher draws the two arrows. One practice trial of each condition goes on the plan table. The teacher moves between six groups.
- **7 min. Own plan.** Each child copies the plan table and checks the one change, the kept things and the three trials.
- **3 min. Exit.** E3a, E3b.
- **3 min. Board and clean-up.** The board asks "Can pushes act without movement?" Boxes and trays wiped and stacked.

Held by: teacher (class plan, board); groups (plan table); children (own plan). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): practice with feedback, 4 min (inside the practice part). By hand: plan table. Evidence: E3a, E3b. Hand-backs out: D and H to day 4.

## Day 4

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Run the box test and revise force arrows using motion evidence.
- note: Equal opposing forces on the same resting object cancel; unequal forces change motion; compare strengths without units.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Set-up.** Roles named; trays on the floor, clear walking space.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 3, D and H, 2 minutes each.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Balanced, unbalanced and cancel said back with the day 3 plan on the board.
- **14 min. Run the box test.** Six groups of five. Condition 1, about equal pushes from opposite sides, three trials; condition 2, one push stronger, three trials. Six trials of about a minute: reset 15 seconds, push 5, read the ruler 15, record 20. Roles rotate each trial, so each child pushes at least twice and every child fills in their own record. Repeat an unclear trial once and keep both. A box that moves 1 cm or more under about equal pushes is recorded as it happened; the class later says why hand pushes are never exactly equal. If condition 2 shows no move, push harder and record both. If the kit fails, an invented record is used and said aloud to be invented.
- **5 min. Arrows from the evidence.** Each child draws the box and arrows for both conditions and writes one sentence: "The pushes are ___ because ___" (written task, with the cm read).
- **6 min. Start, stop, turn.** A short demonstration, not new words: a ball rolls in the tray; a push against it slows it; a push from the side turns it; an arrow for each. Then the resting box again: gravity pulls it down and the tray pushes it up, two equal arrows. Gravity is named only as a downward pull.
- **3 min. Exit.** E4a, E4b.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: push, pull, force, arrow, stronger, balanced, unbalanced, cancel, trial, gravity, weather. Frames: "The arrow points ___ because ___." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner applies the push the child directs; raised-line or large arrows; the pendulum and cards described aloud; a seat near the ring front.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; record help as "shown with help".
- **Reading load.** Labels and one short sentence at a time; no sentence over 15 words; everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict, with an arrow drawing, what a push from the side does to the resting box, then test it.
