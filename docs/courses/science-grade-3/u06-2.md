# Section 6.2: Describing climates across regions

## Header


Describing climates across regions

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-ESS2-2, this section's share (days 103 to 107).** "Gather and combine information to describe the climates of different regions of the world." All of the standard sits here: gather from a map and two sources (day 104), combine them into wet, dry, warm and cold comparisons (day 105), use maps, texts and records together (day 106), and revise (day 107). The practice is "Obtaining, Evaluating, and Communicating Information" ("Obtain and combine information"); the crosscutting concept is patterns.
- **3-ESS2-1, this section's share (day 107 only).** "Show weather data in tables and graphs to describe the weather usually expected in a given season." Nothing new: the exit asks the child to keep today's weather, a seasonal expectation and a climate range apart.
- **Limits that bite here.** Climate is the range of weather a place usually has and how much it changes from year to year. Places are compared in words, maps and whole-number counts (months below freezing, months with rain). No temperature below zero is graphed or compared. No sun-angle or air-circulation causes; a map fact such as "near the equator" is a pattern to use, not to explain. Climate change is left out; answer plainly from the evidence if raised, and say it is a later grade's question. Sources are supplied by the teacher; no student web search.
- **Connections.** Social studies ss3-globe-grid (equator, poles), retrieval day 103. English RI.3.9 (compare two texts on one topic), days 104 and 105, a two-column chart of what each source says. Math 3.MD.B.3 (month counts as bar graphs), days 105 and 106.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Weather is a day's or a year's conditions; climate is the usual range across many years (public normals use 30 years; from memory). (2) Why the class's one year is not the town's climate. (3) How the two-column chart works and why every sentence names its source. (4) Why a place can have a hot summer and a short rainy season at once, in words only. (5) Product C: how its six points are earned, why feedback is written after class, and why its revision on day 107 is not scored that day. (6) Care for the places named: Utqiagvik is a town on Alaska's north coast and is presented for its weather records only; a local voice may be invited (unit plan field 10) but nothing depends on one.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none; the end-of-unit rows belong to section 6.4. This section owns product C (3-ESS2-2): the six-point prompt is on the unit plan, field 5. It is done alone on day 106 (20 minutes), feedback is a six-tick look-for checklist and one question stamp after class, about 1 minute a child; it is revised on day 107 (6 minutes) and recorded after day 115 as a repair mark beside the score. The score comes from the fresh day 115 work on the same components.

**Evidence form.** Cards of 60 words or fewer, "invented for practice" said first; the teacher reads each card aloud once. Day 106's evidence is C itself, with a short exit, X106, after it.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next teaching day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E103 (day 103).** "Two towns counted rainy days in June for 30 years. Town A's counts run from 12 to 14. Town B's counts run from 3 to 20. Which town's rainy days change more from year to year? Say how you know." Answer: Town B; 3 to 20 is a wider range than 12 to 14. Wrong: "Town A, because it has 12 to 14 days" (C). **E103b (spoken).** "This June, Town A had 19 rainy days. Is 19 usual for Town A? Say why." Answer: no; 19 is outside the range of 12 to 14. Wrong: "Yes, 19 is Town A's climate" (C).
- **E104 (day 104).** Card 1, an atlas: "Region R is hot all year. Its map is brown, a dry place." Card 2, a station: "Rain falls on 1 to 4 days each month. Every year of the record looks alike." "Write one sentence about R's climate that uses both cards." Answer: R is hot all year and dry, with 1 to 4 rainy days a month. Wrong: "R is hot all year" (D); "R has few rainy days" (D).
- **E105 (day 105).** Region V: the atlas says desert; the station says 0 months below freezing and 1 wet month. Region W: the atlas says mountain town; the station says 6 months below freezing and 5 wet months. It rained today in V. "Which is colder? Which is drier? Name the card and the numbers." Answer: W is colder (6 months below freezing, not 0); V is drier (1 wet month, not 5; the atlas says desert); one rainy day does not change V. Wrong: "V is wet because it rained today" (B); "W is drier because it is cold" (D).
- **C (day 106).** Product C is the main evidence. A short exit, X106, follows it: an atlas card and a station card for Region M (cold plateau; 7 months below freezing, 2 wet months over 30 years; this year 3 wet months). "Write one sentence about M's climate that uses both cards. Does this year change it? Why?" Answer: M is cold and dry, and one year among 30 does not change it. Wrong: one card only (D); this year taken as the climate (C).
- **E107 (day 107).** A new region's atlas says warm throughout the year; a 30-year station card gives rainy September days ranging from 11 to 25, including two years with 12 and 22. "(a) Combine the sources to describe its climate. (b) Explain why one dry September day does not overturn that account." Answer: (a) Warm, often rainy in September, with year-to-year variation. (b) One day is weather, not climate. Wrong: uses one year as the whole climate (C); ignores one source (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The consensus weather-and-climate model: the seasonal expectation model from section 6.1 gains a climate strip on day 103 (the town's many-year range beside the class's one year). The two-column source chart grows from the town and Manaus (104) to Cairo and Utqiagvik (105) to region cards on a wall world map (106, 107), adding range and year-to-year variation. Review not re-taught: the map key and land-water model (grade 2).
- **Key moment 1, day 103: one year beside many.** Ideal instrument: paper strips, one year's bar beside the 30-year bars. Paper fits. The weather-data viewer (light, show why) partly fits, spec only; not relied on.
- **Key moment 2, day 104: two sources in two columns.** Ideal instrument: the printed two-column chart. Paper fits.
- **Equipment.** One globe is a demonstration for the teacher; each pair has a printed map (15 maps), so no turn on shared equipment. Weather record on days 105 and 106 as in section 6.1 (thermometer step: nearest mark; rain jar: first mark; too little is "wet", never dry; helper pair and adult only touch them).
- **Turns.** Day 104: six children heard, 1 minute each, in the 10-minute part. Day 107: 30 children place one card on the wall, about 20 seconds each, 10 minutes of the 20; six pairs describe a region, 1 minute each.
- **A room with nothing.** The printed map and the cards read aloud; the dated adult record for weather.

## 6. Days

## Day 103


- **5 min. Return and repair.** E102's commonest letter (A or B).
- **6 min. Retrieval and repair.** Pairs use a map key to find land, ocean, the equator and the poles, then two marked regions (4 minutes). Children who miss get an enlarged or tactile map and a teacher model in a small group (2 minutes) while the rest check each other.
- **11 min. Hook.** The class log's one year of a month's rainy days sits beside a strip of the town's same month over 30 years from the station packet, covered. "Does this year's log describe our climate?" Pairs guess, then the strip is uncovered with this year marked. What does it tell us that one year cannot?
- **14 min. Worked example.** The teacher builds the meaning with the strip: climate is the range of weather a place usually has over many years, and how much it changes from year to year. New words: climate, region, range (lowest to highest), year to year. Pairs find the range on the strip and mark where this year sits.
- **5 min. Model and question board.** The climate strip is added to the seasonal expectation model. New notices go on the board: does one year describe everywhere?
- **4 min. Exit E103.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds the meaning of climate from a covered 30-year strip of the town's rainy days; the exit gives two towns' 30-year June counts and asks alone which varies more, and whether a new year is usual.

Held by: teacher (strip, map); pairs. Tool: none (paper). By hand: pencil, printed map. Evidence: E103. Hand-backs out: E103's letters to day 104.

## Day 104


- **5 min. Return and repair.** E103's commonest letter (C): the class year laid beside the many-year strip.
- **6 min. Map.** On the globe and the printed map, find the town and Manaus. Locations only; why is not taught here.
- **9 min. Source one.** The teacher reads the atlas cards aloud (about 55 words each, short sentences). Pairs fill column one of the two-column chart: what the atlas says about each place.
- **9 min. Source two.** The teacher reads the station cards aloud (months with rain, temperature words, a short many-year strip). Pairs fill column two.
- **10 min. Combine.** Pairs write one sentence per place using both columns ("The atlas says ___. The station says ___. So ___."). The teacher hears six children from the rotating six, 1 minute each; the record names who.
- **4 min. Exit E104.**
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Cards collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice finds both places on a map and fills a two-column chart from atlas and station cards in pairs; the exit has each child alone write one climate sentence for a new region using both given cards.

Held by: pairs; each child. Tool: none (paper). By hand: pencil, two-column chart. Evidence: E104, the heard list. Hand-backs out: E104's letters to day 105.

## Day 105


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** E104's commonest letter (D): link one detail from each card.
- **5 min. Map and key.** Find Cairo and Utqiagvik on the map; say what the key shows.
- **10 min. Read the cards.** The teacher reads both places' atlas and station cards aloud (whole-number counts of months below freezing and months with rain; Cairo's January 2019 and 2020 beside the many-year table). Pairs fill the two-column chart.
- **14 min. Combine and compare.** Pairs build a wet, dry, warm, cold table for both places from the chart, then write two comparisons with the numbers. Cairo's two Januarys show year-to-year variation; cold is told in months below freezing, never in numbers below zero.
- **4 min. Exit E105.**
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds a wet, dry, warm and cold table for two places in pairs; the exit gives two new regions and has each child alone say which is drier, naming the card and the numbers, after a rainy day in one.

Held by: pairs; each child. Tool: none (paper). By hand: pencil, chart. Evidence: E105. Hand-backs out: E105's letters to day 106.

## Day 106


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine.
- **3 min. Return and repair.** E105's commonest letter, then the reading part opens with one example.
- **8 min. Source reading.** The teacher reads three cards aloud: a place near the equator, a place near a pole, and a middle place (Omaha). Pairs say the wet, dry, warm and cold pattern of each and tell one year's weather from many years' evidence.
- **20 min. Product C.** Alone, on paper, from the atlas and station cards (unit plan field 5). The teacher reads the directions and cards once, then does not help.
- **4 min. Model.** The class adds a region column to the model in a second colour: usual weather, range, variation, source.
- **3 min. Exit E106.** Alone, on the Region M cards.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Papers collected for scoring outside class.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice reads three region cards in pairs and writes product C alone; the exit gives a new plateau region with one wetter year and asks alone for a climate sentence and whether that year changes the climate.

Held by: pairs, then each child. Tool: none (paper). By hand: pencil, second colour. Evidence: product C, E106. Hand-backs out: C's checklist and question stamp, and E106, to day 107.

## Day 107


- **6 min. Return and repair: product C revision.** The checklist is returned; the commonest letter is shown first (2 minutes); each child revises one component in a second colour.
- **20 min. Synthesis.** 10 minutes: every child places one region card (place, kind of climate, range, source) on the wall world map, about 20 seconds each. 6 minutes: six pairs each describe one region from the wall in a sentence, 1 minute each; the record names who. 4 minutes: a three-row chart of today's weather, a seasonal expectation and a climate range, filled from one place.
- **12 min. Notebook.** Each child writes how today's goal links to the evidence and what changed in the model.
- **4 min. Exit E107.**
- **3 min. Clean-up.**
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has each child place a region card on the wall map and write a notebook link to the model; the exit gives a new warm region's cards and asks alone for its climate, and whether one dry day changes it.

Held by: teacher (wall); each child. Tool: none (paper). By hand: second colour. Evidence: E107, revised C, the heard list. Hand-backs out: E107's letters to day 108.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 103: "What does one year not tell us?" Day 104: "What does the map add that the atlas card does not?" Day 107: "Which of these is weather, which is a season's usual, which is climate?" Every child may pass.
- **Written task.** Product C (day 106).
- **Judged by the teacher with 3 look-fors.** Both sources are used and named; the range and the year-to-year change are stated; today's weather is kept apart from climate.

## 8. Practice that returns


- **Day 103.** The land-water model and the globe (grade 2; social studies). **Day 104.** The map key (grade 2). **Day 105.** The weather record and the table row (unit 1). **Day 106.** Short record, long record (unit 5). **Day 107.** The seasonal expectation model (section 6.1) and revising in a second colour (unit 1).
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure (day 107).** Each child places one card on the wall in turn and the class reads the wall back twice together; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: climate, region, range, year to year, source, atlas, station. Frame: "The atlas says ___. The station says ___. So ___ has a ___ climate. Tomorrow can differ." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged and tactile maps; large print; counts read aloud; a partner may scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards and stems aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers. Do not supply the link between sources.
- **Reading load.** Cards of 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, read aloud by the teacher; children read numbers and short labels only; no independent long reading.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one question a third source could answer about a region.
