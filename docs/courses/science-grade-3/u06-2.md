- **3-ESS2-2, this section's share (days 103 to 107).** "Gather and combine information to describe the climates of different regions of the world." All of the standard sits here: gather from a map and two sources (day 104), combine them into wet, dry, warm and cold comparisons (day 105), use maps, texts and records together (day 106), and revise (day 107). The practice is "Obtaining, Evaluating, and Communicating Information" ("Obtain and combine information"); the crosscutting concept is patterns.
- **3-ESS2-1, this section's share (day 107 only).** "Show weather data in tables and graphs to describe the weather usually expected in a given season." Nothing new: the exit asks the child to keep today's weather, a seasonal expectation and a climate range apart.
- **Limits that bite here.** Climate is the range of weather a place usually has and how much it changes from year to year. Places are compared in words, maps and whole-number counts (months below freezing, months with rain). No temperature below zero is graphed or compared. No sun-angle or air-circulation causes; a map fact such as "near the equator" is a pattern to use, not to explain. Climate change is left out; answer plainly from the evidence if raised, and say it is a later grade's question. Sources are supplied by the teacher; no student web search.
- **Connections.** Social studies ss3-globe-grid (equator, poles), retrieval day 103. English RI.3.9 (compare two texts on one topic), days 104 and 105, a two-column chart of what each source says. Math 3.MD.B.3 (month counts as bar graphs), days 105 and 106.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Weather is a day's or a year's conditions; climate is the usual range across many years (public normals use 30 years; from memory). (2) Why the class's one year is not the town's climate. (3) How the two-column chart works and why every sentence names its source. (4) Why a place can have a hot summer and a short rainy season at once, in words only. (5) Product C: how its six points are earned, why feedback is written after class, and why its revision on day 107 is not scored that day. (6) Care for the places named: Utqiagvik is a town on Alaska's north coast and is presented for its weather records only; a local voice may be invited (unit plan field 10) but nothing depends on one.

**Evidence form.** Cards of 60 words or fewer, "invented for practice" said first; the teacher reads each card aloud once. Day 106's evidence is C itself, with a short exit, X106, after it.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next teaching day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E103 (day 103).** "A town's 30-year record of snowy days in February runs from 4 to 14. This February had 18 snowy days. Lena says: this February shows our climate. Is she right? Say why." Answer: No. Climate is the usual range over many years; one February is one year's weather. Wrong: "Yes, 18 is our climate" (C); "Yes, it is what we saw" (C).
- **E104 (day 104).** Card 1, an atlas: "Region R is hot all year. Its map is brown, a dry place." Card 2, a station: "Rain falls on 1 to 4 days each month. Every year of the record looks alike." "Write one sentence about R's climate that uses both cards." Answer: R is hot all year and dry, with 1 to 4 rainy days a month. Wrong: "R is hot all year" (D); "R has few rainy days" (D).
- **E105 (day 105).** Region V: the atlas says desert; the station says 0 months below freezing and 1 wet month. Region W: the atlas says mountain town; the station says 6 months below freezing and 5 wet months. It rained today in V. "Which is colder? Which is drier? Name the card and the numbers." Answer: W is colder (6 months below freezing, not 0); V is drier (1 wet month, not 5; the atlas says desert); one rainy day does not change V. Wrong: "V is wet because it rained today" (B); "W is drier because it is cold" (D).
- **C (day 106).** Product C is the main evidence. A short exit, X106, follows it: an atlas card and a station card for Region M (cold plateau; 7 months below freezing, 2 wet months over 30 years; this year 3 wet months). "Write one sentence about M's climate that uses both cards. Does this year change it? Why?" Answer: M is cold and dry, and one year among 30 does not change it. Wrong: one card only (D); this year taken as the climate (C).
- **E107 (day 107).** A new region's atlas says warm throughout the year; a 30-year station card gives rainy September days ranging from 11 to 25, including two years with 12 and 22. "(a) Combine the sources to describe its climate. (b) Explain why one dry September day does not overturn that account." Answer: (a) Warm, often rainy in September, with year-to-year variation. (b) One day is weather, not climate. Wrong: uses one year as the whole climate (C); ignores one source (D).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: climate, region, range, year to year, source, atlas, station. Frame: "The atlas says ___. The station says ___. So ___ has a ___ climate. Tomorrow can differ." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged and tactile maps; large print; counts read aloud; a partner may scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards and stems aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers. Do not supply the link between sources.
- **Reading load.** Cards of 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, read aloud by the teacher; children read numbers and short labels only; no independent long reading.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one question a third source could answer about a region.
