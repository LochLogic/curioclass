## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS1-1, this section's share (days 53, 54, 56, 58).** "Make models showing that living things have many different life cycles, yet all are born, grow, reproduce and die." Day 53 joins the unit's cycle models into one consensus; day 56 is the assessed model (item 2); day 58 revises it once more.
- **3-LS3-1 (days 53, 54, 57, 58).** "Use data as evidence that plants and animals get traits from their parents and that members of a similar group differ in those traits." Taught last in section 3.2; assessed here (item 3, day 57).
- **3-LS3-2 (days 53, 54, 55, 58).** "Use evidence to back the explanation that surroundings can shape an organism's traits, such as a plant stunted by too little water." Taught last in section 3.3; assessed here (item 1, day 55).
- **Limits that bite here.** Flowering plants only. No genes, no trait prediction, no human reproduction, no people's bodies as examples, no soil chemistry. A date interval describes the recorded individual, not a universal life span. A graph mistake or a misread count gets descriptive feedback, not a letter. A missed check day means unscored, not zero.
- **Connections.** Unit 2's cause-and-effect comparisons (day 54); the bar graph from section 3.2 (day 57); whole-centimetre ruler (days 53, 58, m2-measure-with-tools); claim, evidence and reason from section 3.3 (every written record).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: what the three portfolio records, the three fresh items and the day 58 transfer each ask, how the 12 points are awarded (0 or 1 a component, a point only when the whole look-for is there), why the scored work is the fresh check-day work, with the revised portfolio record recorded beside it as repair evidence (a separate mark, not the score), and why feedback is a look-for checklist with one question stamp, about 1 minute a child, after class; scoring the fresh work takes about 1 minute a child a day. Day 58 hands back the checklists and exit tickets; the scored fresh papers come back on day 59, in its Return and repair. Teacher judges components; no self-marking.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** items 1, 2 and 3 (days 55, 56, 57), as in the table above. Rule 4: the fresh cases (marigold and duckling, zinnia and lady beetle, bean and guinea pig) differ from the portfolio cases below and from every taught case; nothing here repeats the cases of sections 3.1 to 3.3.

**Transfer portfolio, the repair evidence (day 53, 10 minutes a record, revised on day 54).** All records are new when produced and printed "invented for practice". Four look-fors a record, the same four components the day 55 to 57 items score; a look-for counts only when its complete look-for is there. Read prompts and captions aloud, enlarge records, give tactile stage cards, accept dictation. Do not supply causal links or model arrows.
- **Record 1 (item 1).** Basil seedlings from one seed family, equal in age, light, spacing, soil, container size and temperature, only water changed: 10 and 11 centimetres with adequate water, 5 and 6 with too little. Two same-kind chicks ignore a blue card, then approach it after repeated card-before-feed experiences. A short-basil and a tall-basil family keep their shorter or taller pattern in parent and offspring under matched adequate water; less water lowers both. Ask for one explanation of the water effect, the learned response and the combined influences. Points: water claim from matched records; learning claim from before-and-after; both family pattern and water named for height; reasoning that both can shape a feature without giving every difference one cause. Wrong: "The chicks inherited knowing the card" (D); "All the height differences are inherited" (C).
- **Record 2 (item 2).** Pea P: day 0 seed containing a new plant, day 7 seedling, day 35 flowering adult, day 49 closed pods, day 70 adult dead. Honeybee queen Q: day 0 egg, day 3 larva, day 8 pupa, day 16 adult, day 30 laying new eggs, day 730 queen dead. Two models, the shared pattern and the differences, and a prediction (a seedling after 7 days). Second card, revealed after the first draft: on day 49 P's opened pod holds new seeds; one begins growing that day and is a seedling on day 56. Revise to connect the parent with new individuals. Points: plant model has birth, growth, reproduction, death; animal model has them; comparison with supplied timing justifies the prediction; the revision links adult and new individuals. Wrong: "The dead bee turns into its own egg" (A).
- **Record 3 (item 3).** Sunflower parents and six offspring share leaf form; offspring leaf lengths 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5 centimetres. Two sheep parents and four lambs all have wool; lamb fleece shades vary. Organize both, explain inherited likeness and variation in each kind, citing observations, with no future prediction. Points: plant likeness, plant variation, animal likeness, animal variation, each justified by the organized data. Wrong: "Different lengths mean they cannot be related" (F); "The lamb looks like the ewe, so she is its parent" (G).

**Exit questions for the teaching days.** Days 53 and 54 are full: the portfolio records and their revision are the day's evidence, and there is no separate slip (plan point 1). The section exit is day 58's transfer, unscored, on a record not worked that day: mustard M (day 0 seed, 4 seedling, 30 flowering adult, 48 pod with new seeds, 70 original adult dead; a new seed begins growing on day 52 and is a seedling on day 56) and lacewing L (day 0 egg, 6 larva, 20 pupa, 31 adult, 42 new eggs, 65 original adult dead; an offspring hatches on day 48); documented mustard parents and all five offspring have yellow flowers, with leaf-width tallies narrow 2 and wide 3; documented lacewing parents and all four offspring have green bodies, with wing-length tallies short 3 and long 1; same-family mustard at matched conditions reaches 16 and 18 centimetres with adequate water, 9 and 10 with too little. "How do your revised models explain the common life pattern and differing stages? What evidence supports inherited likeness, variation and a watering effect?" Expected: both models show birth, growth, reproduction and death linked to new individuals, a pupa only in lacewings; yellow flowers and green bodies support likeness; 2 to 3 and 3 to 1 tallies show variation; the watered mustard being taller supports a water effect because other conditions match. Wrong: "Mustard dies and starts again as the same plant" (A); "The pods prove the mustard reproduced" (B); "All the height differences are inherited" (C); "Offspring must match in every feature" (F).

**Day 54 practice cards (pairs, 4 minutes, not scored).** (a) Cress seedlings matched but for water: 10 and 11 centimeters with enough, 4 and 5 with too little. (b) A crow that dropped nuts on a road after watching other crows; its chicks, never shown, do not. (c) Two photographs of one kind of pumpkin, one in a large garden with long vines, one in a small yard with short vines; a child says space caused it. Answers: (a) a water effect, controlled; (b) learned, not passed to young (D); (c) the photographs alone cannot show it; name one missing condition (E).

**Day 54 exit (alone, 4 minutes, not scored).** (a) Bean seedlings matched but for light: 12 and 13 centimeters in bright light, 5 and 6 in dim light. (b) A dog that opened a gate latch after watching its owner; its puppies, never shown, do not. (c) Two photographs of one kind of sunflower, one by a south fence and tall, one by a north corner and short; a child says the fence made it taller. Answers: (a) a light effect, controlled; (b) learned, not passed to puppies (D); (c) the photographs alone cannot show it; name one missing condition (E).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The unit's wall models stay up and are used, not retaken: the life-cycle drawings from days 37 to 41, the resemblance models from days 43 and 46, the cause-and-effect arrows and the claim, evidence and reason frames from days 48 to 52. Day 53 joins them in one consensus model, with observed and supplied labels kept apart. Portfolio drafts, look-for checklists and revisions are filed together and kept to day 58.
- **Key moment 1, days 53 and 54: the portfolio.** Ideal instrument: a printed record set and a paper life-cycle model the child draws on. Paper fits; no tool is built for it. Spec: whole-centimetre ruler (step 1 cm), the class plants for the living record.
- **Key moment 2, day 56: the revised model.** Ideal instrument: model drawn on paper after a second card. Paper fits.
- **Hands-on and weather.** Days 53 and 58: ruler and tray, one measurement of the class plants in the 2-minute living record, 3 minutes of clean-up on day 53 (2 on day 58). Days 53 and 54: weather record 5 minutes. A helper from the rotating six reads the thermometer (6 children at 30 seconds, 3 minutes inside the 5). Day 55 carries 2 minutes of care and 3 of clean-up.
- **Fallback.** Mould, escapes or unsafe weather: stop the physical work and use the dated record on the wall.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper models and the printed records only; every reading is invented for practice, read aloud. Growth viewer not used in this section.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: life cycle, offspring, trait, surroundings, evidence, reason. Frames: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." "Alike in ___; different in ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged records; tactile stage cards; dictation; a partner may read the ruler aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts and captions aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn models; a scribe. Do not supply causal links or arrows.
- **Reading load.** Records under 50 words, no sentence over 15; the teacher reads each record aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one prediction the records cannot support, and what record would be needed.
