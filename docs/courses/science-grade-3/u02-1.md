## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-PS2-3, this section's share (days 17 to 22).** "Ask questions to find what causes and changes electric or magnetic pushes and pulls between two objects that are not touching." Children ask and test questions about magnetic pushes and pulls: how the gap changes the pull, how turning a magnet changes its direction, and how the object and the number of magnets matter. Static attraction opens the unit on day 17 and returns in section 2.2 (day 23).
- **Limits that bite here.** Only forces from objects children handle; static electricity only, no student circuits. No poles as theory, no fields, no particles, no force numbers. No rule for how strength changes with distance: "in our trials, a closer gap gave a bigger lean." Two magnets are not promised to pull twice as hard. Not every metal is pulled. A lean of 0 cm means none seen, never no force. The lean strip is read in whole centimeters, a 1 cm step; a lean that differs by 1 cm between tries is about the same.
- **Connections.** 3-PS2-1 forces and arrows from unit 1 (retrieved day 17, revised days 19, 20 and 22). Whole centimeters from m2-measure-with-tools (day 19). ELA SL.3.1: listen, build on, ask, taught inside the question board routine (day 17).
- **Practices.** Asking questions: modeled day 18, children's own days 19 to 21, sorted day 22. Cause and effect: days 19 to 21, from controlled comparisons.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: each encased magnet has two ends (poles); unlike ends attract and like ends repel, which is why turning a magnet can flip the direction. A steel clip near either end becomes a temporary magnet, so it is pulled by either end and never pushed. Aluminum is not pulled. The pull gets weaker quickly as the gap grows, so a far gap can show nothing without the force being zero (from memory). Rubbed plastic attracts light paper by a different cause, static charge; day 17 only shows it. Children say "end" and "red end", not "pole".

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 2.3 owns items 1 to 3 (days 31 and 32). This section's evidence is the exit questions below, asked aloud with cards labeled "invented for practice", each child answering on their own sheet or by pointing; a rotating six heard each day, the record sheet naming who. If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that letter's wrong example. Rule 4: none of these cards is a case taught in the unit; the clip, thread, comb and tissue are never an exit.
- **E17 (day 17).** Card: a key hangs on a thread; a magnet is held near it with a gap; the key leans toward the magnet. **E17a.** "The magnet does not touch the key. Is the magnet pulling the key? How do you know?" Answer: yes; the key leans toward it, with a gap between. Wrong: "No, they are not touching" (A). **E17b.** "Draw an arrow for the pull on the key." Answer: an arrow that starts on the key and points to the magnet across the gap. Wrong: "No arrow, nothing touches it" (A).
- **E18 (day 18).** **E18a.** Card: Pat's plan is "I will hold the magnet closer and turn it around, then see if the key leans more." "What is wrong with Pat's plan?" Answer: two things change at once, so we could not tell which one did it. Wrong: "Nothing, two changes find the answer faster" (D). **E18b.** Card: the question "Does the kind of thread change how far the key leans?" "Fill in the strip: change, keep the same, watch." Answer: change the thread; keep the key, the magnet and the gap; watch the lean. Wrong: "Change the thread and use a bigger magnet" (D).
- **E19 (day 19).** **E19a.** Card: Pat changed the gap and also changed to a different magnet, and says the smaller gap made the bigger lean. "Can Pat say that?" Answer: no; two things changed. Wrong: "Yes, closer is stronger" (D). **E19b.** Card: at a far gap the hung key did not move. Pat says there is no pull there. "What can we say?" Answer: none was seen; a small pull might be too small to see; try a closer gap. Wrong: "Pat is right, it is too far for any pull" (A).
- **E20 (day 20).** **E20a.** Card: a magnet hangs on a string; its red end swings toward a held magnet and stays close. "Which end of the held magnet is close: red or blue? Say why." Answer: the blue end; unlike ends pull together. Wrong: "Red, because they match" (no letter). **E20b.** Card: a steel nail on a tray; a magnet's red end slides it toward the magnet; Pat says the blue end will push the nail away. "Is Pat right? Say why." Answer: no; steel is pulled by either end. Wrong: "Yes, the blue end pushes it" (C).
- **E21 (day 21).** **E21a.** Card: a magnet pulls a steel bolt. Pat says it will pull a silver tray made of aluminum, because both are metal. "What do you say?" Answer: not every metal is pulled; test it. Wrong: "Yes, all metals are pulled" (B). **E21b.** Card: Pat used two magnets instead of one and also swapped the key for a spoon, and says two magnets pull twice as hard. "Can Pat say that?" Answer: no; two things changed, and doubling is not promised. Wrong: "Yes, two magnets pull twice as hard" (D).
- **E22 (day 22, section exit ticket).** Card: an unfamiliar pair, two flat round magnets, one hung on a loop of thread, shown at two gaps and after one is turned; numbers the class did not record. **E22a.** "Fill in the strip for: does the gap change the pull?" Answer: change the gap; keep the same two magnets and the same ends facing; watch how far the hung magnet moves. Wrong: "Change the gap and turn one magnet" (D). **E22b.** "Fill in the strip for: does turning change the pull?" Answer: change which end faces; keep the gap and the two magnets; watch toward or away. Wrong: "Turn it and move it closer" (D). **E22c.** Card: at the far gap nothing moved. "Is there no push or pull there?" Answer: we cannot tell; none was seen; try closer. Wrong: "No force, it is too far" (A).

A blank or off-topic answer is filed as an incomplete component, not a letter.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Force arrows and cause and effect from unit 1. Day 17 adds the gap: each child draws an explanation first, then the arrow across the gap joins the class model. Day 19 revises arrow length from the distance table; day 20 revises direction from turning; day 22 consolidates both and keeps the day 17 drawings beside the revised model. Review, in the ledger's words: force, force arrow, balanced and cancel, fair test (keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing), trial, plan table, question board, return and repair, weather record, centimeter and ruler zero. Nothing is withdrawn. The section runs on hands-on work and paper; it has no key tool.
- **Key moment 1, day 19: the lean at three gaps.** Ideal instrument: a force sensor with a mounted ruler, which a class does not have. The taped lean strip under a hanging clip fits partly: order is clear, sizes read to about 1 cm. The Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, show why) is spec only, does not exist and partly fits, as a way to compare gaps after the real trials. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md#non-contact-interactions).
- **Key moment 2, day 20: toward or away at one fixed gap.** Ideal instrument: two low-friction carts, one magnet on each, which the class does not have. A hung magnet with a two-sided lean strip fits partly (direction is clear, size less so). The same simulation partly fits, for contrast with steel.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Six groups of five, each with two magnets, five counted clips, thread, a lean strip, a distance strip, and goggles for every child. Day 17: one 80-second turn each, 5 children is under 7 minutes. Days 19 to 21: six or nine trials of about 40 seconds shared across five children, 13 to 15 minutes. The teacher hears a rotating six: 6 children at about 40 seconds is 4 minutes. Clean-up is 5 minutes: magnets and clips counted, goggles wiped with a damp cloth by their owners (one cloth per group, about 30 seconds each), strips taped back.
- **Fallback.** A lean that fails (too weak, or a clip that sticks) is repeated once, both tries kept; a group with no readable lean uses the class table and says so. An invented record is the named fallback on days 19 to 21, said aloud to be invented, and the plan never scores it. On a damp day the comb may not lift tissue on day 17: the teacher tries a dry cloth once, then shows a record "invented for practice" and says so.
- **A room with nothing.** No magnets: supplied records, labeled with who, what and when, give weaker evidence and cannot show a gap in the hand; say so. Static needs only a comb and a cloth.

## 6. Days

## Day 17


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine from unit 1: a helper from the rotating six reads the thermometer to the nearest mark, the rain jar, the direction card and the sky; the last minute is clean-up and handwashing. An adult checked conditions first.
- **3 min. Retrieve unit 1.** A resting parcel with two equal opposing arrows, drawn on the board and said aloud to be invented for practice. "Why does it stay still? What could a longer arrow to the right change?" Day 16's unscored sheets show who needs a held-box demonstration; that small group gets it (1 minute) while the rest write one line.
- **6 min. Hook.** Teacher demonstration. A steel clip on a thread taped to the table leans toward a magnet held 3 cm away and then hangs in the air, tethered. Then the teacher rubs a comb on the wool cloth (20 strokes) and lifts tissue bits across a gap of about 2 cm. If the air is damp and nothing lifts: one try with a dry cloth, then a record "invented for practice", said aloud.
- **4 min. Draw first.** Each child draws what they think is making the clip lean, before any word is taught. Nothing is marked wrong; drawings are kept for day 22.
- **4 min. Talk: listen, build on, ask.** Pairs share drawings: the partner listens, builds on ("I agree, and I think ___") or asks one question ("What makes you think ___?"). Four voices from the rotating six then share. Any child may pass.
- **5 min. Gap, attract, repel, arrow.** Teach the gap (the space with nothing between), and the pull across it as an arrow that starts on the clip and points to the magnet. Show a hung magnet swung away by a second magnet's marked end: a push across a gap. Words: attract (a pull toward, across a gap) and repel (a push away, across a gap); each magnet has a red end and a blue end. Three arrows sit on the clip: the magnet's pull, gravity, the thread's pull.
- **7 min. Turns at the clip.** Six groups of five, goggles on. Each child has one turn of about 80 seconds: bring the magnet slowly toward the hung clip until it leans, hold, and draw the arrow across the gap on their own sheet. The teacher secured each tether.
- **3 min. Question board.** Notices become questions: each pair gives one "I wonder"; the teacher posts them unedited.
- **3 min. Exit.** E17a, E17b.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** Magnets and clips counted back; goggles wiped by their owners; comb, cloth and tissue collected; tethers off.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice has each child bring a magnet to a hung clip and draw the arrow across the gap; the exit draws that arrow alone for a bolt sliding on a tray, with no magnet in hand, and asks if touching is needed.

Held by: teacher (demonstrations, class model, board); groups (clip sets); children (own drawing and sheet). Tool: force arrow drawing (light): show why, 5 min (Gap, attract, repel, arrow). By hand: drawing, sheet. Evidence: E17a, E17b; the first drawing, kept. Hand-backs out: A to day 18.

## Day 18


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 17, A, 2 minutes.
- **15 min. Set-up for unit 3 (living records).** The teacher alone fills the pots, sets the wicks and reservoir and runs the lamp before class; allergies were checked the day before. Two children from the rotating six sow the two rapid-cycling flowering pots at the teacher's table. Each group of five sows two radish pots of six seeds, one crowded (about 1 cm apart) and one spaced (about 4 cm), using the ruler. Roles: planter (the only one who touches soil), spacer, labeler, date recorder, mealworm watcher; each group looks at the mealworm box for 30 seconds without handling. The six planters wash hands in turn at two sinks (3 minutes inside the 15). Care is kept equal.
- **6 min. Model a question.** The teacher turns a day 17 notice into a testable question: "Does the gap change how far the clip leans?" Change the gap; keep the magnet, the clip and the end facing; watch the lean. A question that cannot be tested ("Why is it magic?") is set aside beside it.
- **4 min. Sort the board.** Pairs sort the question cards into four piles: distance, materials, turning, uses. Wonders that fit none stay on the board.
- **7 min. Choose a change.** Each child writes the day 19 gap question on their own sheet, then the group agrees one strip: three gaps between 1 and 10 cm and what stays the same.
- **3 min. Hear the groups.** One child per group reads the strip aloud, 30 seconds each.
- **3 min. Exit.** E18a, E18b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts question cards and writes a gap strip for the class clip; the exit names change, keep and watch for a new case, a nut on a string where the string kind changes, and judges a test that changes two things.

Held by: teacher (model, board); groups (strip); children (own sheet). Tool: None. By hand: strips, cards. Evidence: E18a, E18b. Hand-backs out: D to day 19.

## Day 19


- **2 min. Measuring warm-up.** Retrieval of m2-measure-with-tools: read a whole-centimeter gap from a ruler picture that starts at zero, invented for practice. A miss gets zero aligned and one more strip.
- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 18, D, 2 minutes.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers date and note the pots and the mealworm box and wash hands at one sink; others look from their seats.
- **4 min. Ask the question.** Groups read their strip. Each needs one change, kept conditions and something to watch. Nothing new is taught.
- **3 min. Plan table.** Change, keep, watch; three gaps, three trials of each (review of the unit 1 plan table).
- **2 min. Safety and set-up.** Goggles fitted; two magnets counted out; no magnets near phones or tablets; fingers clear. The clip hangs from thread taped to the table edge with a lean strip under it and a distance strip beside it.
- **13 min. Investigate.** Six groups of five. Hold the red end at the first gap for 5 seconds, then read how far the clip tip moved toward the magnet, in whole centimeters. Nine trials of about 40 seconds shared by five children (four make two, one makes one); everyone records every reading. A lean too small to see is written "none seen", never zero force. An unclear try is repeated once and both kept.
- **5 min. Class table.** Each group posts its readings by gap. The teacher hears a rotating six, one child per group, 40 seconds each: a reading and "I think ___, because I saw ___." Simulation (light, spec only) may replace the groups' duplicate reports; without it, two groups' tables are put side by side.
- **4 min. Revise arrows.** The arrow across the gap is longer for the closer gap; it shows how strong the pull was in our trials, not how far the clip moved.
- **3 min. Exit.** E19a, E19b.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** Magnets (2 a group) and clips (5 a group) counted; goggles wiped; strips collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice runs the gap test and posts readings by gap; the exit reads two given results from other setups and asks what Pat can claim, including a far gap with no lean seen, which the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (class table, model); groups (clip set); children (own table). Tool: Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, spec only): show why, 5 min (Class table); force arrow drawing (light): show why, 4 min (Revise arrows). By hand: strips, table, arrows. Evidence: E19a, E19b. Hand-backs out: A and D to day 20.

## Day 20


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 19, A and D, 2 minutes each.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **4 min. Ask the question.** "Does turning one magnet change whether it pulls or pushes the other?" Change which end faces (red toward blue, red toward red); keep the gap (one whole-centimeter gap chosen by the group), the same two magnets and the same height; watch toward or away on a two-sided lean strip.
- **3 min. Plan table.** Two conditions, three trials each; then a steel check: the red end, then the blue end, brought to a hung clip at the same gap.
- **2 min. Safety and set-up.** One magnet hangs on a thread loop, the other is held. Keep the pair apart when stored; fingers clear; goggles.
- **12 min. Investigate.** Six trials of about 40 seconds shared by five children, then the steel check, two trials each end. Each child records toward or away with the reading. An unclear try is repeated and both kept.
- **5 min. Class table.** Readings posted; a rotating six heard. Simulation (light, spec only): turn one of two magnets at a fixed gap and contrast steel; without it, the teacher compares two tables.
- **5 min. Revise arrows.** Attraction: the arrow on the hung magnet points toward the held one. Repulsion: it points away. The arrow always starts on the thing acted on. Steel: toward, with either end. Class statement, said with "in our trials".
- **3 min. Exit.** E20a, E20b.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** As day 19.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice tests a hung magnet against a held one and runs a steel check; the exit works backward from a given swing to which end is close and judges a claim about steel, which the practice never asks.

Held by: teacher (table, model); groups (magnet pair); children (own sheet). Tool: Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, spec only): show why, 5 min (Class table); force arrow drawing (light): show why, 5 min (Revise arrows). By hand: strips, arrows. Evidence: E20a, E20b. Hand-backs out: C to day 21.

## Day 21


- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; a helper from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 20, C, 2 minutes.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **3 min. Choose the question.** Three are offered, two groups each: (1) does one magnet or two stacked pull a clip from farther? (2) does a hung steel clip or a hung magnet lean more toward the same red end at the same gap? (3) does a steel clip or a foil square lean more? Only the object or the number changes.
- **3 min. Plan table.** Two conditions, three trials each.
- **2 min. Safety.** Stacked magnets: slide apart, never pull; fingers clear.
- **15 min. Investigate.** Six trials of about 40 seconds shared by five children; everyone records. A foil lean of 0 cm is recorded as none seen.
- **5 min. Class table.** All three questions are posted. Simulation (light, spec only): one versus two magnets and object type, without assuming doubled force; without it, the three tables are read side by side. Not every metal is pulled; two magnets are not promised to be twice as strong.
- **3 min. Exit.** E21a, E21b.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** As day 19; foil squares collected.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice runs two conditions of stacked magnets or object types and posts them; the exit predicts for an aluminum tray the groups never tried and judges an unfair comparison, neither of which the practice asks.

Held by: teacher (table); groups (their question); children (own sheet). Tool: Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, spec only): show why, 5 min (Class table). By hand: strips, table. Evidence: E21a, E21b. Hand-backs out: B and D to day 22.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 17: "What do you think is making the clip lean?" Day 19: "What did you see change when the gap changed?" Day 22: "What did we ask that we could not answer yet?" Every child may pass in public talk.
- **Written task, day 22.** The day 17 drawing, redrawn with the revised arrows and one sentence: "When the gap gets smaller, ___, because I saw ___."
- **Judged by the teacher with 4 look-fors.** The arrow starts on the object pulled; it crosses the gap; its length matches the evidence; the sentence uses "in our trials" or a reading.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 17: unit 1 parcel arrows. Day 19: whole centimeters from zero. Days 18 to 22: the previous day's wrong example, when many made it. Days 17, 18, 21 and 22: the weather row.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Day 22 practices sorting questions until it is easy; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: gap, attract, repel, magnet, end, steel, closer, farther, lean, change, keep the same, watch. Frames: "I wonder if ___ when we change ___." "I saw ___, so I think ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner moves the magnet as the child directs; large print on the lean strip; the drawing may be dictated. A child who cannot see the lean hears it called and feels a thread tug.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; a scribe for the strips.
- **Reading load.** Strips and labels only; no sentence over 15 words; student pages stay under 350 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict, then test, how a card spacer between magnet and clip changes the lean.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A fridge magnet, a steel clip on a thread and a ruler: bring the magnet toward the clip at two gaps and say which leans more; then turn a second magnet and say whether it pulls or pushes. Last day of the section, day 22: write one testable question and what you would keep the same. No report about home is asked. Static needs only a comb and a cloth.
- **One projector.** Show the class table, the board and the arrow model; each child draws and writes on their own sheet.
