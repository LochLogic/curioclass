## Header


How groups help

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS2-1, this section's share (days 59 to 63).** "Argue that some animals live in groups and that the group helps its members survive." All three benefits (food, protection, handling change), small and large groups, different jobs, and the spoken then written claim, evidence and reason. Item G is produced on day 62 and revised on day 63. Day 76 assesses the code fresh.
- **3-LS4-3, day 59 only.** "Argue from evidence that in a given habitat some living things do well, some do poorly, and some cannot live at all." Day 59 raises the question and draws the first system model. Teaching is section 4.3.
- **Limits that bite here.** Records support an argument; they do not prove a cause. The wolf comparison says success rose up to four hunters, then leveled off; it is read as a pattern and never turned into counts. Hunting is non-graphic and no child plays prey. No requested stories about home or family. Counts are whole numbers out of 10, one square a trip, no percent. Every printed record is marked "invented for practice" unless it is a drawing of a sourced observation.
- **Connections.** Math 3.MD.B.3 bar graph (day 61). ELA W.3.1 opinion with reasons: science teaches the spoken then written claim, evidence and reason first (days 60 to 63); ELA unit 3 starts school day 81. Unit 3's cause and effect and claim, evidence and reason (days 62, 63).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: why a group can help (more eyes, shared work, shared warmth) and why it need not (more mouths to feed; a lone forager that does fine); what a claim, evidence and a reason that links them are; how the wolf result reads; why the musk ox ring protects calves without guaranteeing it; jobs in a colony and in a pack; how item G's four look-fors are checked without giving the answer (the same four components score the day 76 fresh work; the revision is repair evidence).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item G is produced here (day 62, revised day 63); its revision is marked as repair evidence after day 76, and its four components score the day 76 fresh work (unit plan field 5). The end-of-unit rows are owned by section 4.4. Rule 4: no case below reappears in an exit or in item G; the exits are held back and never taught.

**Item G, produced day 62, 12 minutes alone, printed "invented for practice".** Meerkat card: a small foraging party, one lookout and diggers; eight of ten trips with lookout calls end with food and safe return, four of ten without calls; one call is followed by a retreat to shelter. Penguin card: a much larger huddle; neighbors shelter one another as cold wind rises and individuals change places. Prompt, read aloud: "How can these groups help? Say food, staying safe and cold wind. Name the jobs. Which group is bigger?" Four points: a supported food link; a supported protection link; a supported cold-wind link; a model comparison of the small and large groups and their jobs. Feedback after class on day 62: a look-for checklist, four ticks, and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; revision 8 minutes on day 63. Wrong: "all animals must live in groups" (B); "the huddle always keeps every bird warm" (C).

**Exit questions.** Read aloud once; at most two short parts; answered on a slip, spoken, or pointed. If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E59.** Card: a meadow with tall grass with seeds, a small stream and one bush; a field mouse needs seeds, water and a place to hide. "Draw the mouse and a link line to each thing it needs." Answer: three link lines, to the seeds, the stream and the bush. Wrong: "The meadow gives the mouse everything, so no lines" (F). Spoken: "A pond turtle needs warm, open water and water plants. The winter tundra has ice, snow and no open water." "Could the turtle live on the winter tundra? Say why." Answer: no; what it needs is not there. Wrong: "Yes, turtles live anywhere" (F); "No, nothing can live on the tundra" (G).
- **E60.** Card: "Pelicans swim in a half circle toward shore. They push fish into shallow water." "How does the group help a pelican?" Answer: it herds fish into shallow water, so each can scoop food. Wrong: "All birds need a group to eat" (B); "The group always gets fish" (C).
- **E61.** Card: gull bar graph, fish trips out of 10: one gull 4, two gulls 6, three gulls 8, six gulls 3. "Would you add more gulls to a group of three? Use the graph." Answer: no; it helped up to three, and six did worse. Wrong: "More gulls always means more fish" (C); "One gull cannot catch fish" (B).
- **E62.** Card: "A prairie dog stands on the mound and barks. The others dive into burrows." "Write: I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." Answer: a group can help it stay safe, because one watched and called, so the others got away. Wrong: "All animals need groups" (B); "The warning always saves every one" (C).
- **E63 (section exit, 7 minutes).** Card: "Two mice carry seeds home together. A lone owl gets food, a nest and stays safe all season on its own." "What does grouping help? What shows that not every animal needs a group?" Answer: it can help the mice get food; the owl did well alone. Wrong: "Every animal needs a group" (B); "The mice always get enough, so groups never fail" (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The day 59 system drawing stays on the wall and in each child's file. It gains group cause-and-effect arrows on day 62, labeled "so", from the day 60 and 61 records. Unit 3's claim, evidence and reason frame is spoken on day 60, written on day 62 and revised on day 63 after a partner questions the evidence. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, day 59: the first system drawing.** Ideal instrument: a drawing surface where a child links an animal to what the place gives it and keeps the draft dated. Paper and pencil fit; the system model is light. Spec: fits; TOOL-ASKS.md holds any gap.
- **Key moment 2, day 62: the argument on a record.** Ideal instrument: the printed record with a copy of the drawing to mark in a second color. Paper fits.
- **Weather record, days 59 to 62 (5 minutes).** The thermometer is non-glass and is read to the nearest mark; a reading below zero is said in words. Two helpers from the rotating six read the thermometer and the rain jar (30 seconds each) while the class fills in the row. Only the helpers touch the equipment; the adult empties the jar and handles any ice. The helpers wash hands at two sinks in the one clean-up minute. The adult checks conditions first and the class stays indoors in a warning. If a reading fails (a frozen jar, no rain), read the dated adult record aloud and say it is supplied.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed cards, a drawn ladder and the dated adult weather record read aloud; every number said to be invented for practice.

## 6. Days

## Day 59


- **8 min. Hook.** Reveal two original drawings: musk oxen standing in a ring, calves inside, wolves at the edge, no injury drawn; a white hare on snow with a second hare nearly hidden. Add a winter conditions card, words only: "Cold. Snow. Strong wind. Few plants." No explaining captions. Pairs talk for one minute, then the class says which animals they noticed, which they nearly missed, and whether a rainforest tree frog could stay here. Any child may pass; no one is asked about home. Notices become questions on the board.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** The grade 2 pond card and forest A as a reminder only: which kinds live in both, which in only one (shared kind, different kind). A miss goes to day 60.
- **24 min. First system drawing.** 4 minutes: the teacher draws one link on the musk ox (it eats sedges; a link line from the animal to what the place gives). 13 minutes: pairs draw the musk ox, wolf and hare on the tundra with link lines, 15 pairs at once while the teacher circulates. 3 minutes: six children from the rotating six each name one link (30 seconds each). 4 minutes: sort the question board into groups, differences and places.
- **3 min. Exit E59.**
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine; two helpers from the rotating six.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Drawings filed, jar emptied by the adult, helpers' hands washed.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the musk ox, wolf and hare with link lines to what the place gives; the exit draws link lines for a meadow field mouse and judges whether a pond turtle could live on the tundra.

Held by: teacher (board, wall drawing); children (own drawings). Tool: system model, drawing part (light), show why, 17 minutes. By hand: paper, pencil. Evidence: E59, the first drawing, the heard list. Hand-backs out: the retrieval miss and E59's letters (F, G) to day 60, part "Return and repair".

## Day 60


- **3 min. Retrieval.** Grade 1 review: a chick calls and the parent brings food. Say the three links: the young does, the adult does, so the young stays alive. Why does that help it survive? Key: the food it needs to grow.
- **8 min. Return and repair.** Four short repairs, 2 minutes each, whole class if more than a third missed, otherwise a small group at the teacher's table while the others compare an extra group card: day 59's inventory miss (side-by-side picture matching), today's retrieval miss (order three pictures), and E59's letters F and G.
- **24 min. Four groups.** 6 minutes: the teacher models one evidence-to-cause link on the musk oxen: "I think the ring protects calves, because I saw horns facing out and calves inside, so wolves meet horns, not calves." Name three ways a group can help: food, protection, handling change. 13 minutes: six groups of five each read two of four invented cards (musk ox ring, wolf pack, school of fish, honeybee colony in winter), each card 45 words or fewer and read aloud once by the teacher, then each child reads one sentence aloud (30 seconds a turn, 5 minutes) and the group fills two rows of a chart: how it helps, small or large, jobs. 5 minutes: the class chart; six children each give one row.
- **3 min. Exit E60.**
- **5 min. Weather record.** The routine.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Cards collected, jar emptied.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice models and builds evidence-to-cause links on the musk oxen in four groups; the exit has each child alone say how pelicans swimming in a half circle help each pelican.

Held by: teacher (modeled link, class chart); groups (cards). Tool: none (paper cards). By hand: chart, pencil. Evidence: E60, the heard list. Hand-backs out: E60's letters (B, C) to day 61, part "Return and repair".

## Day 61


- **4 min. Return and repair.** E60's letters B and C, 2 minutes each, as in day 60.
- **31 min. Hunting counts.** 8 minutes: the wolf ladder, picture cards for groups of 1 to 6 wolves with bars that are only taller or the same, no counts; success rises up to four, then stays level. 12 minutes: invented heron record, fish trips out of 10: one heron 2, two herons 5, three herons 7, four herons 7; pairs draw a bar graph on squared paper, one square a trip (grade 2 bar graph), then state a claim. 8 minutes: compare the two records; what would you want to know before saying a bigger group is always better? (two records, not proof). 3 minutes: partner speaking, claim then evidence then reason, 45 seconds each way, all pairs at once; the teacher hears the rotating six.
- **3 min. Exit E61.**
- **5 min. Weather record.**
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Papers and squared paper collected, jar emptied.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice reads wolf-group bars and compares group sizes in pairs; the exit has each child alone read a gull graph of fish trips and decide whether to add more gulls to a group of three.

Held by: teacher (ladder); pairs (graph). Tool: none. By hand: squared paper, pencil. Evidence: E61, the graph. Hand-backs out: E61's letters (B, C) to day 62, part "Return and repair".

"Does your 'so' say how it helps?" 8 minutes: pairs, each child's day 62 frame sentence is questioned for 90 seconds and revised for 2 and a half minutes (4 minutes a child, all pairs at once; the teacher hears the rotating six). 6 minutes: revise the class frame on the wall. 4 minutes: three teacher-written revised sentences read aloud; no child's mistake is shown.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 59: "Which animal did you notice first, and which nearly disappeared?" Day 60: "What does the group do for each member, and how do you know?" Day 63: "What did you see that shows that?" Every child may pass in public talk; the teacher hears the rotating six each day and records who.
- **Written task.** Item G, day 62 (revised day 63).
- **Judged by the teacher with 4 look-fors.** A food link supported by the lookout counts; a protection link from the call and retreat; a cold-wind link from the huddle; a comparison of small and large groups and their jobs.

## 8. Practice that returns


- **Day 59.** Grade 2 inventories (shared kind, different kind). **Day 60.** Grade 1 young-and-adult survival chain. **Day 61.** Grade 2 bar graph, one square for one. **Days 62, 63.** Unit 3's claim, evidence and reason and its cause-and-effect arrows. **Days 59 to 62.** The weather record from unit 1.
- Fluency target: none; the standard sets none. Day 63 is fluency in speaking the frame, not in speed.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: group, protect, food, lookout, job, predator, evidence. Frames: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." "The group helps by ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged drawings; a tactile cutout of the musk ox ring; dictation; a partner may read the thermometer aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards and exits aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn answers; a scribe. Do not supply the link between evidence and benefit.
- **Reading load.** Cards 45 words or fewer; no sentence over 15 words; a student page holds at most 350 words (about 220 on day 60). The teacher reads each card aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say what record would show that a lone animal needs a group.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the cards aloud together; the child says the claim, evidence and reason; the child draws the bars on day 61 and works item G alone on day 62. Read the thermometer outdoors only when the adult judges it safe; otherwise use a dated local weather report.
- **One projector.** Show the drawings, cards and ladder; children draw and write on paper.
