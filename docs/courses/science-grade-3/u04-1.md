# Section 4.1: How groups help

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 3. Unit 4, Surviving where you live. "Why do some living things survive in a place while others do not?" Anchor: the Arctic tundra in winter: musk oxen form a ring against wolves, white-coated hares are hard to see on the snow, and no rainforest tree frog or cactus could live there (days 59 to 78): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 59 to 63.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (59, 60), fluency 1 (63), application 2 (61, 62). - **3-LS2-1, this section's share (days 59 to 63).** "Argue that some animals live in groups and that the group helps its members survive." All three benefits (food, protection, handling change), small and large groups, different jobs, and the spoken then written claim, evidence and reason. Item G is produced on day 62 and revised on day 63. Day 76 assesses the code fresh.
- **3-LS4-3, day 59 only.** "Argue from evidence that in a given habitat some living things do well, some do poorly, and some cannot live at all." Day 59 raises the question and draws the first system model. Teaching is section 4.3.
- **Limits that bite here.** Records support an argument; they do not prove a cause. The wolf comparison says success rose up to four hunters, then leveled off; it is read as a pattern and never turned into counts. Hunting is non-graphic and no child plays prey. No requested stories about home or family. Counts are whole numbers out of 10, one square a trip, no percent. Every printed record is marked "invented for practice" unless it is a drawing of a sourced observation.
- **Connections.** Math 3.MD.B.3 bar graph (day 61). ELA W.3.1 opinion with reasons: science teaches the spoken then written claim, evidence and reason first (days 60 to 63); ELA unit 3 starts school day 81. Unit 3's cause and effect and claim, evidence and reason (days 62, 63).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: why a group can help (more eyes, shared work, shared warmth) and why it need not (more mouths to feed; a lone forager that does fine); what a claim, evidence and a reason that links them are; how the wolf result reads; why the musk ox ring protects calves without guaranteeing it; jobs in a colony and in a pack; how item G is scored (4 points) without giving the answer.

**Scored item G, produced day 62, 12 minutes alone, printed "invented for practice".** Meerkat card: a small foraging party, one lookout and diggers; eight of ten trips with lookout calls end with food and safe return, four of ten without calls; one call is followed by a retreat to shelter. Penguin card: a much larger huddle; neighbors shelter one another as cold wind rises and individuals change places. Prompt, read aloud: "How can these groups help? Say food, staying safe and cold wind. Name the jobs. Which group is bigger?" Four points: a supported food link; a supported protection link; a supported cold-wind link; a model comparison of the small and large groups and their jobs. Written feedback after class on day 62 (3 minutes a child); revision 8 minutes on day 63. Wrong: "all animals must live in groups" (B); "the huddle always keeps every bird warm" (C).

**Exit questions.** Read aloud once; at most two short parts; answered on a slip, spoken, or pointed. If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E59.** Card: "A pond turtle needs warm, open water and water plants. The winter tundra has ice, snow and no open water." "Could the turtle live on the winter tundra? Say why." Answer: no; what it needs is not there. Wrong: "Yes, turtles live anywhere" (F); "No, nothing can live on the tundra" (G).
- **E60.** Card: "Pelicans swim in a line. They push fish into shallow water. A pelican alone often misses." "How does the line help a pelican?" Answer: it herds fish into shallow water, so each can scoop food. Wrong: "All birds need a line to eat" (B); "The line always gets fish" (C).
- **E61.** Card: gull bar graph, fish trips out of 10: one gull 4, two gulls 6, three gulls 8, six gulls 3. "Would you add more gulls to a group of three? Use the graph." Answer: no; it helped up to three, and six did worse. Wrong: "More gulls always means more fish" (C); "One gull cannot catch fish" (B).
- **E62.** Card: "A prairie dog stands on the mound and barks. The others dive into burrows." "Write: I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." Answer: a group can help it stay safe, because one watched and called, so the others got away. Wrong: "All animals need groups" (B); "The warning always saves every one" (C).
- **E63 (section exit, 7 minutes).** Card: "Two mice carry seeds home together. A lone owl gets food, a nest and stays safe all season on its own." "What does grouping help? What shows that not every animal needs a group?" Answer: it can help the mice get food; the owl did well alone. Wrong: "Every animal needs a group" (B); "The mice always get enough, so groups never fail" (C).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: group, protect, food, lookout, job, predator, evidence. Frames: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." "The group helps by ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged drawings; a tactile cutout of the musk ox ring; dictation; a partner may read the thermometer aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards and exits aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn answers; a scribe. Do not supply the link between evidence and benefit.
- **Reading load.** Cards 45 words or fewer; no sentence over 15 words; a student page holds at most 350 words (about 220 on day 60). The teacher reads each card aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say what record would show that a lone animal needs a group.
