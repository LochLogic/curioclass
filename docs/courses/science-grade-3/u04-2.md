## Header


Differences that matter

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS4-2, this section's share (days 64 to 68).** "Explain, with evidence, how differences among members of one species can help some survive, find mates and reproduce." All three parts: survive (coat, color, thorns), find mates (bird), reproduce (young seen in a separate record). Item V is produced on day 68 and revised on day 74. Day 75 assesses the code fresh.
- **Limits that bite here.** Compare within one kind only. A difference can help; it does not promise survival or a mate. No genetics, no change across generations, no named selection mechanism. Paper moths model detection, not survival, and one class trial does not establish animal outcomes. Thorn-clipping evidence supports protection, not inherited thorn size. A winter-dormant animal is not a poor survivor. Counts are ten of each, whole numbers.
- **Connections.** Unit 3's variation and "alike in ___; different in ___" (day 64). Grade 1 and 2 shape and job, structure and function (days 64, 66, 67). Math 3.MD.B.3 tally and bar graph (days 65, 66). ELA RI.3.3 cause and effect, signposts because and so (day 64; ELA unit 2 ended school day 77).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: why only same-kind comparisons count; why matched conditions and equal starting numbers matter; why a mate record and a young record are different observations; what the paper search does and does not model; why we say "can help" and not "will"; how item V's four look-fors are checked (the same four components score the day 75 fresh work; the revision is repair evidence).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item V is produced here on day 68; its revision is marked as repair evidence after day 75, and its four components score the day 75 fresh work (unit plan field 5). The end-of-unit row (day 75) is owned by section 4.4.

**Item V, produced day 68, 12 minutes alone, "invented for practice".** Barley plants of one kind, ten deeper-rooted and ten shallower-rooted, in matched dry plots: eight and three survive. One invented meadow-bird kind, ten longer-plumed and ten shorter-plumed males in matched conditions: seven and two find mates; six of the seven pairs and one of the two pairs later raise young. Prompt, read aloud: "How can each difference matter? Use the numbers. Mark your copy of the model." Four points: root and survival with eight against three; plume and mates with seven against two; young with six against one; model arrows that do not promise success. Meets: "More deeper-rooted barley survived, eight not three. Reaching water could help. Longer-plumed birds found more mates, seven not two, and six pairs raised young, not one. My arrows join the differences to those results, but some without the feature also did well." Does not: "Barley and birds are different kinds, so these records show variation within a kind" (A). Feedback after day 68: a look-for checklist, four ticks, and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; handed back day 69; revised 8 minutes on day 74.

**Exit questions.** Read aloud; at most two short parts.
- **E64.** Card: "Ten red foxes with big ears and ten with small ears, one hot summer. Weak from heat: 1 of the big-eared, 4 of the small-eared." (1) "Why can we compare these two groups?" (2) "What job could big ears do?" Answer: same kind; big ears can give off heat. Wrong: "Compare a fox with a rabbit" (A); "Big ears always keep a fox safe" (C).
- **E65.** Card: "White paper bugs on a white cloth are hard to find. Mina says red bugs on the white cloth are hardest, because red is bright." "Is Mina right? What would you test?" Answer: no; hiding depends on the cloth; test red bugs on a red-patterned cloth. Wrong: "Bright colours hide best on any cloth" (D).
- **E66.** Card: "Paper bugs on matching cloth: 4 of 20 picks. On a different cloth: 11 of 20." "What does this show? What does it not show?" Answer: matching color was picked less, which could help real bugs hide; paper is not alive and cannot show real survival. Wrong: "So matching real bugs always live longer" (C).
- **E67.** Card: "In one lizard kind, bright-throated males formed 11 of 15 pairs. No nests were checked yet." "What does this show? Does it show young?" Answer: finding mates; no, young need their own record. Wrong: "Pairs mean there are young" (E).
- **E68 (section exit, 8 minutes).** Card: "Ten shrubs with big thorns each lose 2 leaves. Ten with small thorns each lose 9. In one bird kind, strong bills open tough pods and weak bills do not. Males offer seeds; some are accepted. Later, chicks are seen eating seeds." (1) "How can these differences help survive, find mates and have young?" (2) "Do accepted seeds alone show chicks hatched?" Answer: thorns protect leaves; strong bills give food for survival, courtship and chicks; accepted seeds show mate-finding, only the chick record shows young. Wrong: "Pairing proves chicks hatched" (E); "Big thorns guarantee a shrub lives" (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The day 59 system drawing and the day 62 arrows stay on the wall. Structure-and-function rows (part, job, who it helps) start on day 64 and are revised on days 66 and 68 from the camouflage counts, thorn evidence and bird records. The cause-and-effect model is revised on days 66 and 68. Claim, evidence and reason is used each day. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, day 65: the pattern flips with the cloth.** Ideal instrument: a real search of paper shapes on a real patterned cloth, ten of each color, repeated, then the cloth reversed. Paper and cloth fit.
- **Key moment 2, day 66: predict, then see again.** Ideal instrument: the Curio Class camouflage viewer, which keeps the background, equal starting counts and repeated trials and asks for a prediction first. Spec only; partly fits. Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md. The cause-and-effect model is a light paper tool.
- **Equipment, day 65.** Six groups of five, each with 20 precut paper moths, two cloth squares (one matching each color), a tally sheet and a pencil. A turn is hide 30 seconds, pick the first six moths seen 30 seconds, tally 30 seconds: 90 seconds, five turns, 7 and a half minutes a cloth, inside an 8-minute round. Search seated, no racing, no child cast as prey. The adult checks cloth allergies and clears tables the day before. Clean-up: moths in bags, cloth folded, tables wiped, 3 minutes counting weather's one.
- **If a reading fails.** A group that loses trials uses a supplied tally, said aloud to be invented, shown in another color on the class table.
- **Weather record, days 65, 66 (5 minutes).** As in section 4.1.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed tallies read aloud as invented; no viewer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: kind, difference, hide, mate, chick, evidence. Frames: "The ___ helps because ___, so ___." "This does not show ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Cards enlarged; moths with a texture as well as a color; dictation; a partner may tally.
- **Check accommodations.** Read exits and item V aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn answers; a scribe; no causal links supplied.
- **Reading load.** Cards 50 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, a student page at most 350 words. The teacher reads each card aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say a difference that might help in one place and not in another, and what record would show it.

- **One projector.** Show the cards and the class table; children tally and write on paper.
