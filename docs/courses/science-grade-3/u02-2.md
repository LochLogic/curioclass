# Section 2.2: From unseen forces to a useful latch


## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-PS2-3, this section's share (days 23, 24).** "Ask questions to find what causes and changes electric or magnetic pushes and pulls between two objects that are not touching." Day 23 asks about static attraction and distance (the electric part, static only); day 24 asks questions about a switched magnet.
- **3-PS2-4, this section's share (days 25 to 28).** "Name a simple design problem that ideas about magnets could help solve, such as a door latch." The cupboard latch is the case; the need comes before any magnet.
- **3-5-ETS1-1, this section's share (days 25 to 28).** "Name a simple design problem that meets a need or want, with set measures of success and limits on materials, time or cost." Need on day 25, criteria and limits on day 26, one trial on day 27, a changed need on day 28.
- **Limits that bite here.** Static electricity only, comb and cloth, no student circuits; the electromagnet is a teacher-only demonstration and its construction is not assessed. One latch attempt on day 27; no comparing many solutions (grade 4) and no fair test of a prototype (grade 5). The holding criterion is a washer count, an equal-unit count with no grams. Users are hypothetical; no child reports on home or is the example of a mistake.
- **Connections.** s2-compare-designs (grade 2) retrieved day 25. Cause and effect and arrows from section 2.1 (days 23, 24, 27, 28). Math 3.MD.A.2 bridge: washers now, grams after math unit 7.
- **Practices.** Asking questions and defining problems: static and switch questions (days 23, 24); need, criteria, limits (days 25 to 28).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: rubbing moves charge, so a charged comb attracts light paper; it works best on dry days and weakens with distance (from memory). A coil of wire carrying current around a steel nail makes a magnet that stops when the current is off, and the wire warms. A design problem has a need (who needs what), criteria (checkable measures of success) and limits (called constraints by engineers: materials, time, cost); criteria can pull against each other, and a changed user changes the criteria. Children say "limit".

- **E23 (day 23).** **E23a.** Card: a plastic ruler rubbed on cloth lifts cereal bits from 1 cm but not from 5 cm. Pat says it has no pull at 5 cm. "What can we say?" Answer: none was seen at 5 cm; the pull may be too small to see; closer lifted more. Wrong: "Pat is right, no pull" (A). **E23b.** "We want to test whether more rubbing lifts more bits. Wrong: "Yes, it has to touch" (A). **E24b.** Card: Pat will turn the switch on and also use a bigger screw, to see if the switch matters. "What is wrong with Pat's plan?" Wrong: "Yes, it says what we need" (E). **E25b.** "The class plant tray slides off the windowsill when the window opens. Say the need in one sentence." - **E26 (day 26).** **E26a.** Card: the class needs a stand for a paper sign; sticks, tape and clay; 6 tokens and 5 minutes. "Write two criteria we can check, and the limits." Answer: two checkable criteria, such as holds the sign up for ten seconds; limits: the three materials, 5 minutes, 6 tokens. Wrong: "Use sticks, tape and clay; it looks good" (E). **E26b.** Card: the limit is 5 tokens; Pat's pencil holder costs 7 tokens and holds more pencils than needed. "Can Pat keep it?" Answer: no; it is over the limit. Wrong: "Yes, it works better, so the limit does not matter" (F).
- **E27 (day 27).** **E27a.** Card: a team's bag hook with the criterion "holds the bag"; the bag stayed up 1 second, then fell. "Write one criterion to change, and why." Answer: holds the bag for 10 seconds, because "holds" did not say how long. Wrong: "Change the hook, the tape and the time" (D). **E27b.** Card: a team used a third magnet although the problem allows two, and the door held. "Does this trial show their design meets the problem?" Answer: no; it broke a materials limit. Wrong: "Yes, it held, so it meets the problem" (F).
- **E28 (day 28, section exit ticket).** Card: a magnetic pickup tool lifts six steel bottle caps from a table into a box. Need: a helper needs the caps lifted so that the table is clear. ## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Arrows and cause and effect from section 2.1. Day 23 adds static evidence to the class model, labelled "rubbed": it pulls light paper across a gap, more when closer. Day 24 adds a cause that can be switched. Design records begin on day 25 (the need), take criteria and limits on day 26, change after the day 27 trial and again on day 28 (a new user). Review, in the ledger's words: limit (the most we allow), does well and does poorly, the design-cycle chart (define, sketch, build, test, compare), tradeoff, force arrow, fair test, return and repair. Nothing is withdrawn. The section runs on hands-on work and paper; it has no key tool.
- **Key moment 1, day 23: bits lifted at three gaps.** Ideal instrument: a charge sensor, which a class does not have. A count of bits lifted fits partly; it is whole bits, and damp air can lift none. The Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, show why) is spec only, does not exist and partly fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 24: the switch.** Ideal instrument: a student circuit with a current meter, which the safety rules forbid. The teacher demonstrator fits partly: the class sees on and off but does not control it. Records naming apparatus, observer and date, or the simulation (spec only, replacing the demonstration), are weaker evidence and are said to be.
- **Key moment 3, days 26 and 27: the hold.** Ideal instrument: a force meter in newtons, beyond grade 3. The washer count (an equal-unit count, 0 to 3) fits partly; it cannot show a hold stronger than 3. Asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md#non-contact-interactions).
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 23: six groups, each with a comb, cloth, paper bits and a distance strip; nine trials of about 60 seconds each (rubbing, holding, counting) shared by five children, 15 minutes. Day 24: one demonstrator; all 30 children watch seated, goggles fitted (2 minutes for 30 pairs, neighbours helping). Days 26 and 27: six cupboard models, 12 magnets, 18 washers, tape; day 26's hold measure is three runs of three adds at about 30 seconds each, 4.5 minutes in 9; day 27 is 8 minutes to build and 4 to test, five children one 15-second one-hand try each. Clean-up: 3 minutes on day 23 and 24, 4 on days 26 and 27 (parts counted, tape off, goggles wiped by owners).
- **Fallback.** Day 23: on a damp day, if no bits lift at any gap, record none seen, use a record "invented for practice" for the table, say so aloud, and try again another day. Day 24: no demonstrator, use a record naming apparatus, observer and date, or an invented one said to be. Day 26: a latch that holds 0 or all 3 washers is recorded as it is ("0", "3 or more"); a pair too weak to hold the door uses a lighter door of card. Day 27: a trial that fails to read is repeated once.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper cupboard sketches and supplied records; the day 27 trial becomes a paper judging of two sketches against the statement, and says it is weaker evidence.

## 6. Days

## Day 23


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 22, A and D, 2 minutes each.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **3 min. Retrieve the gap table.** The day 19 table is shown. "When the gap got smaller, what did the lean do?" One line each.
- **4 min. Ask the question.** "Does the rubbed comb lift more paper bits when it is closer?" Change the gap (three gaps on the distance strip, 1 to 6 cm); keep the comb, the cloth, 20 strokes and the same ten bits; watch the number lifted.
- **2 min. Safety and set-up.** Allergies checked. Comb and cloth are the default; a balloon on hair is by choice only, inflated by an adult, who removes fragments at once. Keep bits from mouths and devices away. No circuits. Dry hands.
- **15 min. Investigate.** Six groups of five. Each trial: rub 20 strokes, hold the comb at the gap over ten bits for 5 seconds, count the bits lifted or clinging. Three gaps, three trials each, about 60 seconds a trial; four children make two trials, one makes one; everyone records all nine. If nothing lifts at any gap, write "none seen" and use the invented record (said aloud) for the table.
- **5 min. Class table.** Counts posted by gap; a rotating six heard, 40 seconds each. Simulation (light, spec only) may replace a duplicate trial: ask how a rubbed comb's pull changes with distance.
- **4 min. Compare with the magnet.** Same question as day 19, a different cause: the comb lifts paper, not only steel, and only after rubbing. Arrows go from the paper toward the comb across the gap, longer for the closer gap, labelled "rubbed".
- **3 min. Exit.** E23a, E23b.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Combs and cloths collected; bits swept into a bag.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice rubs a comb and counts bits lifted at three gaps; the exit reads a given ruler result with nothing lifted at 5 cm and says what can be claimed, and names what to keep in a new test.

Held by: teacher (table, model); groups (comb sets); children (own sheet). Tool: Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, spec only): show why, 5 min (Class table); force arrow drawing (light): show why, 4 min (Compare). By hand: strip, table. Evidence: E23a, E23b. Hand-backs out: A and D to day 24.

## Day 24


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back day 23, A and D, 2 minutes each.
- **2 min. Living records.** The day's two observers; others look.
- **2 min. Goggles.** Every child fits goggles (30 pairs, neighbours help) and stays seated, devices put away.
- **9 min. Demonstration.** Teacher only: a cell, an insulated coil on a steel nail, a switch and a current limiter; clips on the table. Switch on: clips jump up to the nail tip from about 1 cm and cling; off: they fall. Three on-off cycles; the class counts clips held and the teacher writes the counts. The cell is disconnected between uses; stop if the wire warms. No demonstrator: a record naming apparatus, observer and date, or the simulation (6 minutes, replacing this part), said to be weaker.
- **4 min. Notice and wonder.** Pairs say what they saw; one wonder each goes on the board.
- **8 min. Questions.** Each child writes one question the switch could help answer. The class sorts them: switch, gap, uses, and ones we cannot test here. The teacher runs one: do the clips stay with the switch off? Only the switch changes.
- **6 min. Useful devices.** Where is a pull you can switch on and off useful? A crane that lifts steel and lets it go; a door held open until a switch is off. The class adds these to the "uses" pile. Nothing is built.
- **4 min. Claim.** Each child writes "When the switch is ___, the clips ___. I know because I saw ___."
- **3 min. Exit.** E24a, E24b.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Goggles wiped and returned; the teacher counts clips.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice watches a switched magnet demonstration and writes a question and a claim; the exit asks alone whether a switched magnet must touch a new screw to pull, and spots a two-change plan.

Held by: teacher (demonstrator, board); children (own claim). Tool: Curio Class non-contact simulation (light, spec only): show why, 6 min, only if the demonstrator is unavailable. By hand: claim, cards. Evidence: E24a, E24b. Hand-backs out: A and D to day 25.

## Day 26

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: rub, lift, static, switch, need, criterion, limit, token, cost, time. Frames: "___ needs ___ so that ___." "We can check it by ___." "The limit is ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** A partner holds the comb or places washers as the child directs; criteria on large-print strips; dictation allowed for the statement; the demonstration is described aloud for a child who cannot see it.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card aloud; accept pointing, drawing or dictation; a scribe for the statement strips.
- **Reading load.** Strips and cards, no sentence over 15 words; student pages under 350 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second criterion to the cupboard statement and say how it pulls against the first.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Static: a comb, a cloth and torn paper at two gaps; say what changed. Latch: a box lid, two fridge magnets and a few washers; write the need, two criteria you can check and three limits (magnets, minutes, tokens), then try one latch. No report about home is asked of the child.
- **One projector.** Show the class table, the criteria strip and the statement; each child writes their own.
