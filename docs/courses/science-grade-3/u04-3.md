# Section 4.3: Needs meet conditions

## Header


Needs meet conditions

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS4-3, this section's share (days 69 to 73).** "Argue from evidence that in a given habitat some living things do well, some do poorly, and some cannot live at all." All three outcomes: does well (hare, polar bear on coastal tundra, saguaro in a desert), does poorly (a musk ox bull with little fat left), cannot live (rainforest tree frog on the tundra, cactus in a bog). Item H is produced on day 71 and revised on day 72. Day 77 assesses the code fresh.
- **Limits that bite here.** Polar bear: coastal tundra with seal-bearing sea ice only. The wood frog is an interior forest and wetland comparison, with leaf-litter winter shelter and seasonal breeding ponds, never evidence for coastal tundra; part of its body water freezes and it thaws in spring. Fat-depleted bulls face greater risk, not certain death; do not call all musk oxen poor survivors. Dormancy is not poor survival. No collecting, no touching organisms or water. The schoolyard inventory is evidence of needs and conditions, not a species list. No "adaptation" or "natural selection".
- **Connections.** Grade 2 habitat and inventory (days 72, 73). System model and cause and effect (days 69, 71, 73). ELA frogs (day 69): the same frog words, no shared text assumed. Math 3.MD.B.3 is not used.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: a habitat is a system with needs on one side and conditions on the other; one place gives different outcomes to different kinds; "does poorly" needs evidence (lost leaves, little fat), not a guess; a missing link means a kind cannot remain; how item H's four look-fors are checked (the same four components score the day 77 fresh work; the revision is repair evidence).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item H is produced here on day 71; its revision is marked as repair evidence after day 77, and its four components score the day 77 fresh work (unit plan field 5). The end-of-unit row (day 77) is owned by section 4.4.

**Item H, produced day 71, 12 minutes alone, "invented for practice".** A garden pond with permanent fresh water, oxygen, sunlight, waterlogged edges and no dry ground. Waterweed needs light and wet roots and makes new shoots and flowers. A marsh flower needs wet soil but grows poorly when fully under water and has fewer leaves. A burrowing grassland mammal needs dry burrows; its burrows flood and it has no dry shelter. Prompt, read aloud: "Which does well, which does poorly, which cannot stay? Use needs and conditions. Mark your copy of the model." Four points: a thriving judgment from evidence; a poor-survival judgment; an inability-to-remain judgment; arrows that explain each need and condition link. Wrong: "They all do equally well because they are alive" (F); "No living thing can stay in a hard place" (G). Feedback after day 71: a look-for checklist, four ticks, and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; handed back day 72, revised 8 minutes on day 72.

**Exit questions.** Read aloud; at most two short parts.
- **E69.** Card: "A cold, windy place. No trees. A short summer. Dwarf willow grows low and needs short summers. A banana plant needs warm all year." "Which can live here? Say why." Answer: the willow; its needs meet the conditions. Wrong: "Both, plants grow anywhere" (F); "Neither, nothing can grow in the cold" (G).
- **E70.** Card: "A shady forest floor. A fern grows many new fronds. A sun-loving prairie flower has few small leaves. A desert lizard needs hot, dry rock and has none." "Which does well? Which does poorly?" Answer: the fern does well; the flower lives but does poorly; the lizard cannot stay. Wrong: "They all do the same" (F); "No living thing lives in a dark forest" (G).
- **E71.** Card: "A cranberry plant needs wet, sour soil. A dry sandy hill has none." "Would it do well on the hill? Say why." Answer: no; its need, wet soil, is not there. Wrong: "Yes, plants grow wherever there is soil" (F).
- **E72.** Card: "A class studies a damp, shady strip by the school gym wall. A fern grows there. A group writes its entry: 'Fern. By the wall. It is there.'" "Write the entry again. Add a condition of the place and a sign on the fern." Answer: fern and place kept; shade or damp; a sign on the plant such as many new fronds or brown tips; a photograph shows what is there, not how well. Wrong: "It is there, so it is doing well" (F).
- **E73 (section exit, 8 minutes).** Card: "A shaded wet hollow with damp soil. A fern has many new fronds. A sun-loving daisy has few small leaves. A dry-leaf beetle needs dry leaves to rest; every leaf here is soaked." "Argue which does well, poorly, or cannot stay. Link each to the evidence." Answer: the fern does well, the daisy poorly, the beetle cannot stay. Wrong: "All three do equally well" (F); "The hollow is too wet for any kind to live" (G).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The habitat system drawing (day 59, arrows day 62) is revised on day 69 with a conditions column and need-and-condition arrows; day 73 adds local evidence from the schoolyard. The cause-and-effect arrows read "needs met, so does well; a need missing, so cannot stay". Claim, evidence and reason is used on days 70, 71 and in E73. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, day 69: needs against conditions.** Ideal instrument: a system model in which a child links a kind's needs to a place's conditions and sees a missing link. Spec: the system model is light and paper fits; partly fits. Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, day 73: the schoolyard inventory.** Ideal instrument: the place itself, with a clipboard and inventory sheet, six groups of five, one spot each; a turn is one entry in 2 minutes, 5 entries in a 12-minute visit. Fallback: a local habitat photo inventory (no-kit), same sheet, indoors.
- **Safety, day 73.** The adult prechecks the route, weather, allergies and boundaries the day before. The class stays in sight, touches no organisms or water, wears closed shoes. Hands are cleaned at the door with fragrance-free wipes, one tub for each table of five, six tables at once: 5 children at 15 seconds is under 2 minutes. Clipboards are stiff card.
- **Weather record, days 69, 70, 73 (5 minutes).** As in section 4.1.
- **A room with nothing.** Cards read aloud as invented; the photo inventory; the dated adult weather record read aloud.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: need, condition, habitat, does well, does poorly, cannot stay. Frames: "It needs ___, and the place has ___, so ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged cards; tactile need and condition tokens; dictation; the photo inventory for any child who cannot walk the route.
- **Check accommodations.** Read exits and item H aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn answers; a scribe; no links supplied.
- **Reading load.** Cards 50 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words, a student page at most 350 words. The teacher reads each card aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say what change to a place would move a kind from "does poorly" to "cannot stay".

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the cards aloud; the child sorts need and condition cards; on day 73 walk one yard or street spot with the adult and use the same sheet, or use photographs.
- **One projector.** Show the cards and the habitat drawing; children sort and write on paper.
