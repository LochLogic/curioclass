- **3-ESS3-1, this section's share (days 108 to 112).** "Judge, with a claim, how well a design such as a flood wall or lightning rod lessens the harm from a weather hazard." All of the standard sits here: a hazard and the harm it does (day 108), a flood wall judged from paper evidence (109), a roof tested in a model (110), a lightning-rod system judged from paper evidence (111), and one design judged on its own evidence and limits (112). The practice is "Engaging in Argument from Evidence" ("Make a claim"); the crosscutting concept is cause and effect.
- **Limits that bite here.** Merit means how well a design meets its goal within its limits. A design lessens harm; it does not stop the hazard. A lightning rod belongs to a complete system (rod, wire, ground) and does not stop strikes; no atmospheric-electricity mechanism. Flood and lightning use paper evidence only: no flood demonstration, no storm watching, no outdoor lightning activity. Model results support model claims only. Building and refining prototypes belongs to grade 4; here the roof is quick evidence for one judgment. Counts and tokens are invented for practice and said so; no invented real-world rates. Climate change is left out.
- **Connections.** Unit 2's need, criterion, limit and token (day 109); unit 5's merit claim (days 109 to 112). English W.3.1 (an opinion with reasons) bridges the claim on day 112. Cause and effect: arrows revised day 111.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Hazard is the weather event; harm is what it does to people and things; a warning is an official statement that a hazard is expected. (2) Merit is checked goal first, then limit, then what the evidence cannot show. (3) How the lightning-rod system works as a design, without a mechanism (from memory). (4) The fan station: one adult, low setting, guard, impact eye protection for all, line for observers. (5) Product H: how its six points are earned, why feedback is written after class, why its revision on day 113 is not scored that day. (6) Care: some children have lived through a storm, flood or fire; examples stay general, no one is asked to recount, sharing is a choice.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next teaching day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E108 (day 108).** Card: "Weather service: strong wind expected Tuesday afternoon in the river valley. Get ready: bring in loose things; stay inside in the strongest gusts." "Pip says: if we get ready, the wind will stay away. Is he right? Say why." Answer: No. Getting ready does not stop wind; it lessens the harm from flying things. Wrong: "Yes, getting ready stops the wind" (E).
- **E109 (day 109).** Goal: at least 8 of 10 paper cars stay dry. Limit: at most 9 tokens. Ditch: 7 tokens; dry cars 9, 8, 9 with it and 4, 3, 4 without. Deep trench: 12 tokens; dry cars 10, 10, 10. "Which design has merit? What do the trials not show?" Answer: The ditch; it meets the goal in all three trials within the limit; the trench breaks the limit. The trials do not show heavier rain or other streets. Wrong: "The trench, it keeps the most cars dry" (F); "The ditch stops the flood" (E); "So every street with a ditch is safe" (G).
- **E110 (day 110, 3 minutes).** Goal: an awning stays on in at least 2 of 3 trials. Limit: at most 2 pieces of tape. Awning A: 1 piece, stayed on 1 of 3. Awning B: 2 pieces, 3 of 3. Awning C: 4 pieces, 3 of 3. "Which awning has merit? Say why." Answer: B; it meets the goal within the tape limit; C breaks the limit and A misses the goal. Wrong: "C, it is the strongest" (F); "B will stay on in any storm" (G); "B stops the wind" (E).
- **E111 (day 111).** A model greenhouse in a hail box, goal: at most 3 of 10 glass squares cracked; limit 9 tokens. Nothing: 8, 9, 8 cracked. Net alone, 4 tokens: 5, 5, 6. Net with frame and slope, 8 tokens: 2, 3, 2. Solid roof, 12 tokens: 0, 0, 1. "Which design has merit? What do the records not show?" Answer: the net with frame and slope meets the goal within the limit; the solid roof breaks the limit; the records do not show hail never cracks the glass, or that real greenhouses are safe. Wrong: "The net means hail never cracks the glass" (E); "So every real greenhouse is safe" (G).
- **E112 (day 112, 4 minutes).** A new roof fastening costs eight tokens, within a nine-token limit. In three wind trials its roof stays attached, while the unfastened roof detaches. "(a) Make a merit claim using the results and the budget. (b) Say what harm is reduced and what these trials cannot guarantee." Answer: The fastening has merit: the roof stays on within the budget, so less roof is lost. Wind still happens; stronger winds were not tested. Wrong: "The fastening stops wind" (E); "Cost does not matter if it works" (F); "It will protect real roofs from any storm" (G).
- **H (day 112).** Evidence is product H, with E112.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: hazard, harm, warning, design, goal, limit, merit, trial. Frame: "I think ___ has merit because ___, so ___. The record does not show ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Large-print tables; tactile counters for counts; a partner may read tables aloud; observers at the fan stand where they can see.
- **Check accommodations.** Read cards and stems aloud; accept spoken, drawn or pointed answers. Do not supply the goal, the limit or the evidence limit.
- **Reading load.** Cards of 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words; tables hold numbers; the warning reading is heard, not read.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write the one trial that would test your claim's limit, and what it would cost.
