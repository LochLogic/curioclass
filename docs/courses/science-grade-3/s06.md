## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What weather can we expect here, and how can people get ready for the dangerous kind?"
Day 98: display contrasting dated pages from the class log, with seasonal headings hidden. Children predict the next page. Reveal dates: why can days differ yet seasons have patterns?
Day 113 closes the anchor: seasonal graphs support likely conditions, many-year regional records establish climate, and protection evidence supports reduced harm despite unusual weather.
Model graph scales on day 100 and source comparison on day 104; then ask children to justify revisions before class agreement.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


Group the question board: what repeats, answered days 99 to 102; whether one year describes everywhere, days 103 to 107; how expected hazards cause less harm, days 108 to 112. Each answer raises the next question. Days 113 and 114 connect them.
Assessed verbs: "Represent data", "Obtain and combine information", "Make a claim". Supply averages; below-zero temperatures stay in words. No climate mechanisms, negative-number arithmetic or climate-change assessment. A lightning rod belongs to a complete protection system; it does not stop strikes.
Carried: day 98 starts a seasonal expectation model. Day 100 revises its displays from counts; day 102 adds exceptions. Day 107 adds regional climate range and variation from combined sources. Day 109 revises the design cycle with flood criteria; day 110 tests and improves one roof. Day 111 revises cause-and-effect arrows from lightning evidence. Day 113 joins these in consensus, used in transfer tasks.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Ignore the stated scale when representing data. | Scale is ignored. Rebuild one bar with counters grouped by the key. | Anticipated from memory. |
| B | One day's weather guarantees the season's weather. | Variation is ignored. Compare the unusual day with several seasonal records. | Anticipated from memory. |
| C | A short weather record determines the whole climate. | Evidence span is too short. Place a class year beside the many-year record. | Anticipated from memory. |
| D | A climate account can ignore the second source. | Information is not combined. Link one supported detail from each card. | Anticipated from memory. |
| E | A protection design stops the weather hazard. | Hazard and harm are confused. Draw hazard-to-design-to-harm arrows. | Anticipated from memory. |
| F | A successful design need not meet its budget. | A constraint is ignored. Check cost against the stated limit. | Anticipated from memory. |
| G | A model trial guarantees real-world protection. | Evidence limits are ignored. Identify one condition not tested. | Anticipated from memory. |

File wrong answers only under their matching letter. An inaccurate count or missing access criterion gets descriptive feedback, not a misleading misconception label. If many miss, use the corresponding repair for five to ten minutes in the next teaching day, or on day 117 for assessment evidence.
