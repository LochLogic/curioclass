## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can things push and pull without touching, and how can we put that to work?"
Day 17: show a tethered clip held above its anchor by a magnet across a gap, then rubbed balloon and paper attraction. Substitute a comb for latex. Children draw explanations first.
On day 29, explain both anchors from observations: objects interact across gaps; magnets attract or repel, while rubbed plastic attracts paper. Include gravity and the tether's pull.
Model questioning on day 18; children then choose changes. Use notebooks to choose support.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


Group the question board into distance, materials, turning and uses. Days 17 to 22 ask what changes the clip's pull, using controlled comparisons to identify causes. This raises whether paper attraction works similarly: days 23 and 24 ask static questions and observe switched magnetism. Days 25 to 28 ask how this evidence meets a cupboard need through problem definition, one trial and improvement. Days 29 and 30 ask whether that definition permits judging solutions. Transfer uses unfamiliar interaction records on day 31 and approaching toy cars on day 32.
Exclude particles, fields, circuits, magnetic Earth, force calculations, grams and promised doubling from two magnets. Formal design comparisons and prototype fair testing belong later.
Carried: retain arrows and cause-and-effect comparisons. Day 17 adds gaps; day 19 revises lengths from distance evidence; day 20 revises direction from turning magnets; day 22 consolidates both. Day 23 adds static evidence. Design records begin on day 25, add criteria and limits on day 26, and change after day 27 trials and day 28 user needs. Day 29 consensus joins these models for independent use on days 31 and 32.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A force requires contact. | Confuses a gap with no interaction. Repair by marking the visible gap in the tethered-clip observation. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Every metal responds magnetically in the same way. | Overgeneralizes from steel. Repair by comparing labelled steel and aluminium records under the same magnet. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Turning a magnet makes it repel steel. | Transfers magnet-to-magnet behavior to steel. Repair by comparing steel attraction with either end facing. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Changing two conditions isolates a cause. | Cannot identify which change mattered. Repair by crossing out one change in a proposed question. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | A materials list defines the design problem. | Substitutes equipment for a need and success measures. Repair by adding who needs what and how success can be checked. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | A solution may ignore a stated resource limit. | Treats constraints as optional. Repair by checking a proposal against the printed allowance. | Anticipated, from memory. |

These are anticipated ideas, not observed claims about children. Missing evidence is recorded by component, without inventing a misconception.
