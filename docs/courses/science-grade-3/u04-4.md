# Section 4.4: Explaining unfamiliar survival evidence

## Header


Explaining unfamiliar survival evidence

## 1. Standards and practices


- **3-LS2-1 (days 74, 76, 78).** "Argue that some animals live in groups and that the group helps its members survive." Assessed verb: "Construct an argument". Day 76 is the check.
- **3-LS4-3 (days 74, 77, 78).** "Argue from evidence that in a given habitat some living things do well, some do poorly, and some cannot live at all." Assessed verb: "Construct an argument". Day 77 is the check.
- **3-LS4-2 (days 74, 75, 78).** "Explain, with evidence, how differences among members of one species can help some survive, find mates and reproduce." Assessed verb: "Use evidence to construct an explanation". Day 75 is the check.
- **Limits that bite here.** Nothing new is taught. The fresh check-day work is scored (items V, G, H: 12 points, a point per complete component). The revised earlier products are recorded beside the score as repair evidence, a separate mark, not the score. No feedback and no reteach on days 75 to 77. A missed revision opportunity means no repair mark, not zero. Sparse answers get component feedback, not a letter. No genetics, no natural selection by name.
- **Connections.** Unit 3's inherited traits (day 75); unit 1's fair test and unit 3's evidence critique (day 74); ELA W.3.1 (unit 3 in progress): the argument frame stays available on the wall.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At an adult's level: what each fresh check asks and why it is the scored work (it shows what a child does alone on a new case); how the 12 points are awarded and how the repair mark sits beside them; why feedback is a look-for checklist, four ticks and one question stamp, about 1 minute a child; why the scored papers come back on day 81, a day after day 78's return of checklists; the day 76 oral record kept unchanged for day 80; the teacher judges components, no self-marking.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** the three end-of-unit rows above (days 75, 76, 77). Each scores the fresh case of the table on the four components of its earlier product (item V from section 4.2, item G from section 4.1, item H from section 4.3); the revised product is recorded beside it as repair evidence, not the score. Their wrong answers are A, C, E (day 75), B (day 76), F, G (day 77).

**Exit question E74 (9 minutes, synthesis evidence, not scored).** Card, invented for practice: "A cold meadow. A lookout calls as a hunter comes. The group runs into shelter. On brown ground, hunters find 9 of 10 white animals. They find 1 of 10 brown animals. A seed eater cannot stay: there are no seeds." (1) "How do the call and the brown colour each help?" (2) "Why can the seed eater not stay?" Answer: the call lets the group retreat; brown is found less on brown ground, which can help it survive; there are no seeds, so a need is missing. Wrong: "White hides best because it is bright" (D); "No animal can live in a meadow without seeds" (G).

**Day 78 fresh explanation (8 minutes, unscored, individual).** Card, invented for practice: "Sand lizards of one kind run from a hunter over loose sand. Ten with long toe fringes: 9 escape. Ten with short fringes: 4 escape." "How could long fringes help? Say one thing the card does not show." Answer: long fringes may help grip loose sand; it does not show young or other places. Wrong: "Lizards and snakes can be compared" (A); "Wide toes mean every lizard escapes" (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class models are joined on day 74 into one tundra consensus model: group arrows, structure-and-function rows, and need-and-condition arrows with the local evidence. The model stays on the wall for annotation in the checks (days 75 and 77) and for day 78. Item V is revised on day 74, 8 minutes.
- **Key moment 1, day 74: the consensus model.** Ideal instrument: one shared system model on the wall that every child adds to. Paper cards and a wall fit. Spec: the system model is light and paper fits.
- **Key moment 2, days 75 and 77: annotate unaided.** Ideal instrument: a printed copy of the model to mark. Paper fits. The system model is used as a check unaided, light, 32 and 30 minutes.
- **Day 76 turns.** Six groups of five, four minutes a group, about 45 seconds a child: 24 minutes; the other groups write evidence links. A heard list names each child.
- **Weather record, days 74, 77, 78 (5 minutes).** As in section 4.1. If a reading fails, read the dated adult record aloud.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed records read aloud as invented; no devices.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: group, difference, condition, need, evidence, cause. Frames: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___." Home-language answers count.
- **Access.** Enlarged records; tactile cards; dictation; a partner may read aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts and records aloud; accept spoken, pointed or drawn models; a scribe. Do not supply causal links or arrows.
- **Reading load.** Records 60 words or fewer, no sentence over 15 words; the teacher reads each aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one claim the records cannot support, and the record needed.
