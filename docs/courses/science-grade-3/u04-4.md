# Section 4.4: Explaining unfamiliar survival evidence

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 3. Unit 4, Surviving where you live. "Why do some living things survive in a place while others do not?" Anchor: the Arctic tundra in winter: musk oxen form a ring against wolves, white-coated hares are hard to see on the snow, and no rainforest tree frog or cactus could live there (days 59 to 78): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 74 to 78.** Days by kind: fluency 1 (74), check 3 (75, 76, 77), respond 1 (78). **Exit question E74 (9 minutes, synthesis evidence, not scored).** Card, invented for practice: "A cold meadow. A lookout calls as a hunter comes. The group runs into shelter. On brown ground, hunters find 9 of 10 white animals. They find 1 of 10 brown animals. A seed eater cannot stay: there are no seeds." (1) "How do the call and the brown colour each help?" (2) "Why can the seed eater not stay?" Answer: the call lets the group retreat; brown is found less on brown ground, which can help it survive; there are no seeds, so a need is missing. Wrong: "White hides best because it is bright" (D); "No animal can live in a meadow without seeds" (G).

**Day 78 fresh explanation (8 minutes, unscored, individual).** Card, invented for practice: "Sand lizards of one kind run from a hunter over loose sand. Ten with long toe fringes: 9 escape. Ten with short fringes: 4 escape." "How could long fringes help? Say one thing the card does not show." Answer: long fringes may help grip loose sand; it does not show young or other places. Wrong: "Lizards and snakes can be compared" (A); "Wide toes mean every lizard escapes" (C).
