Nine additional days hold three two-day course-level checks and their respond days. Course day and school day coincide, so `school_day` is blank in days.csv. The CSV, rather than this prose, is the day record. Section numbers group related work without adding section plans.

## Unit 1. Constructions and rigid transformations

**Calendar:** days 1 to 26, weeks 1 to 6. Sections: 1.1 constructions, 1.2 motions and symmetry, 1.3 proof and synthesis. Nine construction lessons precede nine motion and symmetry lessons; the final four base lessons turn conjectures into angle proofs and a justified design. Distances first become constraints, then definitions, then reasons in an argument. Day 23 gives the frame's additional argument practice; day 24 models a wall estimate, merging an eight-minute introduction to judging estimates. The modeling prompt moves later than its base placement so assumptions can be compared after students have precise geometric language.

**Evidence:** day 1 readiness covers the four rows in the table. Day 14 embeds the only required mid-unit assessment: five minutes retrieval, 25 for rotation reasoning and 20 for one construction with a short justification and brief motion prompts. Days 15 and 16 each use their opening five minutes for its response, replacing ordinary retrieval. Day 25 has one extended angle proof and short prompts on every taught construction and definition, the motion properties and sequences, and all named polygon symmetries. Include segment and angle copying and bisection, perpendiculars through on-line and external points, parallels, and the three inscribed polygons. G-CO.C.9's perpendicular-bisector conjecture continues into unit 2; day 25 does not claim its proof is finished. G-CO.C.10's angle sum is the first of four theorems. Day 26 is the single end-of-unit respond day and prepares unit 2's readiness.

**Candidate settled:** keep the progression comparison through construction-to-motion reasoning and days 19 to 23's proof work, zero added days. Keep a tiling choice within day 22 and a freshly written fictional service-location choice within day 9, zero added days. Drop the regular-pentagon demonstration because it adds an unsupported construction demand; drop the food-energy prompt's setting. Neither is required evidence. Use geometric patterns without sacred-symbol borrowing. Preserve the inclusive trapezoid definition when listing all symmetries.
