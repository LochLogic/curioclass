## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do angles and arcs reveal about the circles around us?"
**Hook, day 127:** For five minutes, compare two drawn wheels with different chords and spokes. Which segments must match, and what would establish that? These diagrams are invented for practice.
**Supporting questions:** How does an angle's position matter (day 128)? Which equal distances locate a circle (days 131 and 133)? What stays fixed under dilation (day 136)?
**Closing claim:** Circle definitions and similarity justify angle relationships, constructions and measurement. Day 142's tangent proof, constructed circles and sector derivation establish this.
**Teaching:** Separate measured conjectures from proofs on day 128, proving the inscribed-angle relation with isosceles triangles, including centre-inside and centre-outside cases. Model perpendicular distance on day 132, forward and inverse sector calculations on day 134, and on day 137 the radian definition, ending with 2 pi radians = 360 degrees. Day 127 finds a half-chord by the Pythagorean Theorem. Day 128 names circumscribed angles; day 129 proves their supplementary relationship with central angles. Step back during days 135 and 138's independent practice.

| 142 | G-C.A.2 | Identify and describe the listed angle, radius, chord and tangent relationships. | E1, 18 minutes, MP3. For centre O and point T on its circle, prove that a line through T is tangent if and only if it is perpendicular to OT. Do not assume the theorem. Answer: if perpendicular, every other point X on the line has OX squared = OT squared + TX squared greater than OT squared, so only T lies on the circle. Conversely, drop OF perpendicular to a tangent. If F differs from T, reflecting T across F gives another point T' on the line with OT' = OT, contradicting tangency. Thus F = T. Assuming the theorem as a premise is A. E2, 10 minutes, MP7. A minor central angle AOB is 132 degrees and C lies on the major arc AB. Let P be the intersection of the tangents at A and B. Find angle ACB and angle APB, explaining each relationship. Answers: 66 degrees, half the intercepted arc; 48 degrees, since two right angles in OAPB leave 180 - 132. UV is a diameter and W is a distinct point on the circle. Find angle UWV and justify it. Answer: 90 degrees, half 180. In a radius-5 circle a chord is 3 units from O; find its length and explain why the perpendicular from O bisects it. Answer: the half-chords are legs of right triangles with hypotenuse 5 and other leg 3, so each is the square root of 25 - 9, or 4; the foot is the midpoint and the chord is 8 units. Wrong: inscribed angle 132 (B), circumscribed angle 66 (C), diameter angle 180 (D), angle APB 138 from taking angle OAP and OBP as 45 degrees each (E), half-chord 2 from subtraction (F). | section 7.1 |
| 142 | G-C.A.3 | Construct both triangle circles and prove the cyclic angle relationship. | E3, 9 minutes, MP5. Provide an unmarked triangle with sides 6, 8 and 11 centimetres and a separate triangle with sides 5, 7 and 9 centimetres, printed at full size. Construct the first triangle's circumcircle and the second triangle's incircle. Keep construction traces, explain each centre and radius, and justify your method. No centre or bisector is supplied. Answer: intersect two perpendicular bisectors, use distance to any vertex; equal distances force the third vertex onto the circumcircle. Intersect two internal angle bisectors, use perpendicular distance to a side; equal side distances make all three sides tangent. Circumradius 5.6 centimetres, centre outside the triangle beyond the 11 centimetre side; inradius 1.7 centimetres; centres and circles within 2 millimetres of true. The print leaves 6 centimetres beyond the 11 centimetre side. Wrong: circumcentre located by angle bisectors (G); incircle radius taken to a vertex (H). E4, 3 minutes, MP3. Prove opposite angles of cyclic quadrilateral ABCD sum to 180 degrees; then find C when A is 76 degrees. Answer: their intercepted arcs partition the circle, so their half-measures sum to half 360, or 180; C = 104 degrees. Wrong: C = 76 because opposite angles are equal (I). | section 7.2 |
| 142 | G-C.B.5 | Derive arc proportionality, define radians and derive and apply sector area. | E5, 10 minutes, MP7. Explain with dilation why the same angle cuts arc lengths proportional to radius. Define its radian measure. Derive sector area from the fraction of a full turn, then, for radius 7 centimetres and angle 4 pi/7 radians (about 103 degrees), find arc length and area. A second sector has the same angle and radius 14 centimetres: how many times larger is its area? Answer: dilation factor k multiplies both s and r, giving ks/(kr) = s/r; theta = s/r is the constant of proportionality, independent of radius. A full turn is 2 pi radians, so area = (theta/(2 pi))(pi r squared) = r squared theta/2. Arc length r theta = 4 pi centimetres; area = 14 pi square centimetres; the radius-14 sector has area 56 pi, 4 times larger. Wrong: doubling radius doubles theta (J); putting 103 into r squared theta/2 (K); saying the area is 2 times larger (L). | section 7.3 |

All assessment cases are invented for practice. Reserve their numbers and diagrams from instruction. All thirty students work independently together. Nothing from first teaching is scored, and no reteaching occurs on day 142. Give written feedback after class; revise on day 143 and score after class that day.

**Teacher judgments:** E1 requires both implications without assuming tangency's perpendicularity. Meets: "A perpendicular makes every other line point farther than the radius by the Pythagorean Theorem, so only T meets the circle. Conversely, if the perpendicular foot F differs from T, reflecting T in F gives a second circle point. Hence F = T and OT is perpendicular." Does not: "Tangents are perpendicular because that is the tangent rule" (A). E2 requires each angle relationship and the congruent-half-chord explanation. Meets: "The inscribed angle is 66 degrees, half the central angle. The tangent angle is 48 degrees by the quadrilateral angle sum with two right angles. Angle UWV is 90 degrees, half the semicircle intercepted by diameter UV. Each half-chord is a leg of a right triangle with hypotenuse 5 and other leg 3, so it is the square root of 25 - 9, or 4, giving chord 8." Does not: "The half-chord is 5 - 3" (F). E3 requires executable traces, distinct centre definitions and correct radii. Meets: "My first construction retains perpendicular-bisector arcs meeting at O; radius OA passes through all vertices. My second retains two angle-bisector constructions meeting at I and a perpendicular IF to a side; radius IF touches each side. I chose a compass because it transfers equal distances without estimating." Does not: "I use a vertex distance for the incircle radius" (H). E4 requires the complementary arcs argument and C = 180 - 76 = 104 degrees. Meets: "The opposite intercepted arcs total 360, so half their sum is 180. Thus C = 180 - 76 = 104 degrees." Does not: "Opposite angles match" (I). E5 requires general dilation, the ratio definition, a derived area formula and units. Meets: "A dilation scales arc s and radius r by k, leaving s/r fixed for this angle. That constant is theta radians. The turn fraction is theta/(2 pi), so sector area is theta/(2 pi) times pi r squared, or r squared theta/2. With radius 7 and angle 4 pi/7, arc length is 4 pi centimetres and area is 14 pi square centimetres; radius 14 gives 56 pi, 4 times larger." Does not: "A larger radius makes the same angle have more radians" (J).

**Accommodations:** Read directions aloud, allow a scribe or recorded explanation, enlarged figures and adapted compass grips; preserve independent construction choices and proof.
