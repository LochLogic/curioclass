# Course frame: Geometry

Course `geometry` (catalogue id `geometry`), grade 10 (data/courses.csv lists grades 9 to 10; the accelerated path is parked, see For the lead), draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) now sets the daily record; operational day references below follow that record.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8 units, in the base text's own order (IM Geometry, units 1 to 8).
- **Days.** 160 course days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks at five lessons a week, one 50-minute lesson a day: a 5-minute opening of spaced, mixed practice on earlier work, then the base lesson (written for a class period of at least 45 minutes). Each unit's days are its base lessons, plus one modeling-prompt day, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day after it. Unit 1, the one unit over 25 days, also holds a mid-unit assessment of about 20 minutes inside a lesson (a construction and a short justification), its respond work in the openings of the next two lessons; no day moves. Days follow load: unit 1 (four rows, a new model and the first formal proofs) and unit 2 (the congruence proofs) each carry one more day for proof and construction practice on paper; unit 6 (five rows, the most of any unit) carries one more for coordinate proof writing. Units 4 and 8 are the shortest (11 base lessons each). An assumed row last taught or assessed in grade g gets retrieval in grade g+1 and a readiness check in grade g+2 or later. Passing retrieval does not reset last use. Checks precede the receiving unit, except unit 1's last ten minutes of day 1 after the hook; rows explicitly taught earlier this year get retrieval at later use.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, high school (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt), is the whole standard: every code a row owns sits in one unit below, and each unit names every lettered part and every theorem the standard lists. The standards cells use plain semicolon-separated codes; the teaching prose distinguishes ownership, begun parts and contributions finished elsewhere. The progression keeps its own notation. Begun parts assessed here have a named product; one-day contributions are labeled as unassessed contributions in days.csv. Math has no check set. The C3 column names the unit's practices; the "Check set adds" column names, as candidates, the Progressions check for the unit (the high school progressions of the Common Core Standards Writing Team, and for geometry the K to 8 geometry and geometric measurement progressions and the standards' own notes where no high school geometry progression is published) and extensions. The (+) codes are deferred to the electives (data/progressions/math-excluded.csv), with one exception kept from the progression: S-CP.B.8 (unit 8, see For the lead).
- **Focus, coherence and rigor.** The scope and sequence states the single planned-minutes focus account and its source interpretation. It uses the published high school prerequisite categories, including connected applications, per course; it does not use K to 8 percentage thresholds or code-count averages. Unit planning must verify the proposed instructional-minute allocations against actual tasks. Coherence joins rigid motions, similarity, equations, roots and quantity reasoning to the preceding and following courses. Proof and construction build understanding, repeated independent procedures build fluency, and one model in each unit develops application.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Geometry (CC BY 4.0; S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\hs-geometry): 8 units, 124 lessons, placed in the Source map at the end. Credit line on adapted days, in the form docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md sets: "Adapted from Illustrative Mathematics K-12 Math, Geometry, unit <n>, lesson <n>, CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Changed by Curio Class." The CC BY-NC edition is not used, and no login-gated material is: cool-downs, answer keys, unit assessments and modeling-prompt sample responses are gated, so Curio Class writes its own exit tickets, unit assessments, answer keys and modeling rubrics.
- **Proof.** Proof grows in steps: conjectures from constructions and informal justifications (unit 1), paragraph and flow-diagram proofs from rigid motions (units 1 and 2), proofs with similarity (unit 3), and coordinate proofs (unit 6). Two-column format is one choice, not the only one.
- **Readiness.** The last-use ledger in the scope and sequence governs each earlier row. Checks occupy the last ten minutes of respond days 26, 48, 81, 105 and 143 immediately before the receiving unit. Unit 1 checks its four older rows in the last ten minutes of day 1 after the hook. Units 4 and 7 retrieve work explicitly taught earlier this year. Unit 5 retrieves the cylinder component taught on day 43 and checks the still-older cone/sphere components on day 81, beside cross sections and circle formulas. Algebra I rows open with retrieval at first use, not readiness.
- **Practices.** All eight are placed (C3 column): MP1 (unit 5), MP2 (unit 4), MP3 (units 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 8), MP4 (units 3, 4, 5, 8), MP5 (units 1, 4, 7), MP6 (units 1, 2, 5, 8), MP7 (units 2, 3, 6, 7), MP8 (unit 6).
- **Fluency without a timer.** The procedures this course expects to be fluent: using the congruence and similarity criteria to solve problems and prove relationships in figures (G-SRT.B.5, units 2 and 3); coordinates in proofs, and perimeter and area from coordinates (G-GPE.B.4 and B.7, unit 6); the basic constructions (G-CO.D.12, unit 1). Each is built in the units named and kept up in the daily opening for the rest of the year, beside Algebra I's equation solving and graphing, which Geometry uses in units 3 to 6. No countdown timer is ever shown to a student. The year-end check directly samples all four expectations through untimed independent work without supplied solutions; the dated record goes to the next course.
- **Modeling.** Each unit has one modeling-prompt day, using the base text's public modeling prompts (the sample responses are gated, so the rubric is ours). The quantity standards N-Q.A.1 to N-Q.A.3, started in Algebra I, are finished here (units 4 and 5).
- **Constructions and the paper path.** Compass and straightedge, paper folding and tracing paper are the first tools in every construction and transformation lesson; dynamic geometry and graphing technology is a second representation, used on a free tool that needs no student account and named in no student page. Every task that uses it has a paper version.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). Unit 8's medical-test contexts are health content: before reading them the agent studies a national public health body's plain-language pages on screening tests (what a positive result means, base rates), a health journalists' association guide on reporting test accuracy, and a disability and illness language guide, and checks that the contexts stay general and that no student is asked about their own health. No other unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content that needs a named community study. The answer-key agent reads every check, assessment, proof rubric and key, solving each item cold beside the script check (BASELINE rule 27), before the course is called finished. Word problems use a range of names and settings; the unit plan lists the people and places its problems show.
