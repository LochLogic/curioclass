# Course frame: Geometry

Course id `geometry`, grade 10 (data/courses.csv lists grades 9 to 10; grade 9 on the accelerated path), draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8 units, in the base text's own order (IM Geometry, units 1 to 8).
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks at five lessons a week, one lesson of 45 to 50 minutes a day (the base text's lessons are written for a class period of at least 45 minutes). Each unit's days are its base lessons, plus one readiness day, one modeling-prompt day and one end-of-unit assessment day. Days follow load: unit 1 (four rows, a new model and the first formal proofs) and unit 2 (the congruence proofs) each carry one more day for proof and construction practice on paper; unit 6 (five rows, the most of any unit) carries one more for coordinate proof writing. Units 4 and 8 are the shortest (11 base lessons each).
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, high school (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt), is the whole standard: every code a row owns sits in one unit below, and each unit names every lettered part and every theorem the standard lists. A code marked with a tilde in a row is started there and owned by a later row (named in the unit). There is no check set in math, so the "Check set adds" column names candidate extensions only. The (+) codes are deferred to the electives (data/progressions/math-excluded.csv), with one exception kept from the progression: S-CP.B.8 (unit 8, see For the lead).
- **Focus, coherence and rigor.** docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md covers kindergarten to grade 8 only and no high school bar is filed (For the lead), so no K-8 percentage line is borrowed. (1) Focus is counted on teaching days, not rows: each base lesson by the standards the base text aligns to it (its "Standards by Lesson" table), against the widely applicable prerequisites the progression tags (N-RN, N-Q, A-SSE, A-APR, A-CED, A-REI, F-IF, G-SRT.B, G-SRT.C). That tagged list is the progression's own and has not yet been checked against the high school publishers' criteria table. Geometry: 52 of 124 base lessons address at least one listed code (42 percent); weighted by the share of each lesson's codes that are listed, 25 percent. By unit: 3 of 22, 1 of 15, 10 of 16, 11 of 11, 9 of 18, 11 of 17, 7 of 14, 0 of 11. Across Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II: 227 of 376 base lessons (60 percent, weighted 45). An independent read reports that the publishers' table also lists G-CO.A.1, G-CO.C.9 and G-CO.C.10 (and some function and statistics codes) and applies focus per course; on that list Geometry is 71 of 124 (57 percent, weighted 34). Which list and which reading (per course or across the sequence) apply is settled with the high school bar page. Readiness, modeling-prompt and assessment days are not counted; the scope and sequence counts each Curio Class day by the standards its plan names. The earlier row-share estimates (26 and 52 percent) are withdrawn. (2) Coherence: 35 of Geometry's 124 base lessons (28 percent) address an algebra or quantity code (N-, A- or F-), and 52 (42 percent) with the similarity and trigonometry clusters counted; the unit 6 coordinate proof-writing day adds to both. The algebra Geometry uses (equations of circles and lines, square and cube roots, two-way tables) is practised on Algebra I's major work, and every seam below is written at both ends. (3) Rigor: each unit names its concept work (proof and construction), its procedures and its applications (the modeling-prompt day).
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Geometry (CC BY 4.0; S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\hs-geometry): 8 units, 124 lessons, placed in the Source map at the end. Credit line on adapted days, in the form docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md sets: "Adapted from Illustrative Mathematics K-12 Math, Geometry, unit <n>, lesson <n>, CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Changed by Curio Class." The CC BY-NC edition is not used, and no login-gated material is: cool-downs, answer keys, unit assessments and modeling-prompt sample responses are gated, so Curio Class writes its own exit tickets, unit assessments, answer keys and modeling rubrics.
- **Proof.** Proof grows in steps: conjectures from constructions and informal justifications (unit 1), paragraph and flow-diagram proofs from rigid motions (units 1 and 2), proofs with similarity (unit 3), and coordinate proofs (unit 6). Two-column format is one choice, not the only one.
- **Modeling.** Each unit has one modeling-prompt day, using the base text's public modeling prompts (the sample responses are gated, so the rubric is ours). The quantity standards N-Q.A.1 to N-Q.A.3, started in Algebra I, are finished here (units 4 and 5).
- **Constructions and the paper path.** Compass and straightedge, paper folding and tracing paper are the first tools in every construction and transformation lesson; dynamic geometry and graphing technology is a second representation, used on a free tool that needs no student account and named in no student page. Every task that uses it has a paper version.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. No unit needs a community or qualified reader. Unit 8's medical-test contexts stay general; no student is asked about their own health.
