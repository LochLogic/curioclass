## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can one angle and one length give us a height we cannot reach?"

**Hook, day 68:** For five minutes, compare two scaled building outlines with equal sight-line angles, labeled "invented for practice". Can one measured horizontal distance determine either height? Sketch and conjecture before calculating.
**Supporting questions:** Why do ratios survive scaling (day 68)? What do exact triangles tell us (days 69 and 70)? Which measurements are necessary (day 74)? How does changing the reference angle change the ratios (day 75)?
**Closing claim:** Similarity fixes angle-dependent ratios; a height estimate needs defined quantities and justified precision. Day 79's design tests this claim; day 80 checks it independently.
**Teaching:** Model correspondence on day 68 and exact derivations on days 69 and 70. On day 71, demonstrate table-based side solving before day 72 practice. Day 73 connects names and calculator operations to the table, including inverse lookup; day 76 practices that taught operation. Step back during day 79's model revision.


**Practices:** MP2: day 68, explain rise divided by horizontal distance as steepness. MP5: day 74, choose table or calculator in the measurement-request task and defend precision. MP4: day 79, define quantities, draw a triangular flag, compute, compare with measured dimensions, revise and report a limitation.
**Fluency:** No new course fluency expectation is assigned here. Days 72 and 76 practice taught ratio selection and inverse lookup independently, with feedback and no speed score. Retrieve unit 3 criterion selection briefly in warm-ups.
**Limits:** Acute angles and degrees only; unit-circle functions and non-right-triangle laws wait. Begun G-GMD.A.1 teaches only the circumference limit on day 78, sampled by E7; retain day 80 evidence for unit 5, which completes the code. No cultural symbols or real construction specifications are used.
**Carried:** Keep unit 3's ratio table. Day 68 ties scaled columns to equal quotients; day 71 builds angle rows. Day 73 ties columns to cosine, sine and tangent. Withdraw supplied triangle labels on day 77; retain numerical ratio tables through assessment. Restore labels for repair, then retry independently. Similarity reasoning remains.

**Rigor and focus:** Follow seven understanding, two fluency and three application days, respectively 350, 100 and 150 instructional minutes. Independent exits show reasoning, procedure and modeling. Qualifying tasks take 525 of 600 minutes, 87.5 percent: 45 daily except day 78's 30-minute polygon trigonometry. Other minutes cover setup and earlier repairs, including 20 on circumference limits. Exclude assessment and response; count mixed work once. Use the high school prerequisite method; no supporting cluster is assigned.

**Language:** Section 4.1 explains corresponding sides and quotients; section 4.2 justifies requests, assumptions and precision. Offer labeled diagrams, home-language rehearsal and adjustable sentence openings, then fade them. Extension compares polygon estimates without new standards.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Opposite and adjacent sides stay fixed when the reference angle changes. | The angle is untracked. Repair: mark the chosen angle before labeling its two legs. | From memory. |
| B | Scaling a triangle changes its side ratios. | Common factors are not cancelled. Repair: divide both scaled lengths by their multiplier. | From memory. |
| C | The hypotenuse equals the sum of the legs. | Length addition replaces squared lengths. Repair: draw the three squares and write their area equation. | From memory. |
| D | Sine equals opposite leg divided by adjacent leg. | The denominator is misidentified. Repair: trace the hypotenuse before writing sine. | From memory. |
| E | Inverse tangent means the reciprocal of tangent. | Reciprocal and inverse operations are confused. Repair: verify the recovered angle by forward tangent. | From memory. |
| F | Sine and cosine of the same acute angle are always equal. | Complementary angles are omitted. Repair: mark both acute angles and exchange the opposite and adjacent roles. | From memory. |
| G | Extra output digits make a measurement more accurate. | Computation is mistaken for measurement resolution. Repair: compare the report with instrument increments. | From memory. |
| H | Ground distance alone determines a building's height. | The model lacks an angle or second length relationship. Repair: draw two different sight lines over the same ground segment. | From memory. |
| I | An inscribed polygon's perimeter exceeds the circle's circumference. | Chords and arcs are confused. Repair: compare each chord with its matching arc. | From memory. |
| J | The height above the sight line is the building's total height. | The vertical reference is misplaced. Repair: mark the eye-height segment below the triangle. | From memory. |
