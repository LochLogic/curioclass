## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How few facts do we need to be sure two triangles are the same?"
**Hook, day 27:** Two paper triangles have equal sides but different orientations. Can one sheet move onto the other without stretching? Collect proposed correspondences before revealing the labels.

**Supporting questions:** What guarantees that a vertex lands exactly (day 29)? Which three facts suffice (day 35)? When does a converse hold (day 39)?

**Closing claim:** Rigid motions justify triangle criteria, which justify relationships beyond triangles. The day 42 argument and day 44 assessment show which facts guarantee a conclusion.

**Teaching:** Model exact segment alignment on day 31; gradually remove proof frames. Derive the included-angle criterion on day 32; prove the isosceles theorem with it on day 34. Require reasons for every coincidence. On day 38 derive parallel-angle converses using the unique-parallel postulate. Step back during independent proofs.
