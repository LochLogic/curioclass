## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why do writers keep retelling Persephone and Nasreddin Hodja, and what makes a retelling new?"

**Hook, 93.** Read the modern story's first departure between households. Which details make this an ordinary journey, and which suggest a story behind it? Open the compelling question before retrieval.
**Inquiry.** On 95, which source events and character types survive the modern Persephone story? On 97, how does the cousin's language change the trickster's purpose? On 107, how do the poem's return and the play's scene sequence make different meanings?
**Closing explanation.** The author note on 120 explains how the retelling transforms a pattern from Hawthorne, Bulfinch or Borrow, beside a modern mentor. On 121 students independently explain another transformation.
**Teaching.** Model source tracing on 94, language on 96, structure on 99 and production judgment on 102. Withdraw comparison frames on 100; return them only for a recorded need.

**Underground, Curio Class, 2026, ours, invented for practice.** At our shared garden, my cousin Rami lifted an empty basket. He had promised beans for the autumn supper, but the bed was bare. "Perhaps the roots are keeping the whole harvest underground," he said. "We could serve invisible bean stew. Nasreddin made broth without catching his ducks."

"Splendid harvest," I said, looking through the basket's slats. The seed packet lay unopened beside his boot.

Rami turned it over. "A wish is a bridge drawn on paper. It takes nobody across until somebody builds it." He knelt beside the bed. "Time to come down to earth."

I had called his plans fanciful. Now he asked an experienced gardener what could still be planted before winter. He wrote down her answer, fetched the tools and began preparing the bed. There would be no beans at this supper. For once, he told the neighbours that before telling them his next idea.

**The Pruning Lesson, Curio Class, 2026, ours, invented for practice.** This complete miniature play is an assessment-only supplement to Pomegranate Season, outside the teaching script and recordings. **Constructed meeting narrative sample, invented for practice, Curio Class, 2026, ours.** This illustrates the five craft rows rather than replacing the student's source-based story: "I was checking invitations when my cousin Leila arrived in her patched jacket. The host pointed her toward the kitchen. She stopped with one hand still on the brass latch. 'Is my name missing?' she asked. Nobody answered. By evening she had borrowed a bright coat. At the door the same host bowed and offered her the first plate. Leila held the coat's sleeve above it. 'Your supper,' she said. I almost laughed; then I remembered my own silence that afternoon. Because I had checked her name myself, I could no longer pretend the invitation was the problem. I moved my chair beside hers. When the plates were cleared, I put the invitation next to the coat. The paper had not changed. Our welcome had." Author note: the feast tale's costume-based welcome becomes an invitation-checker's recognition of complicity. Its two arrivals preserve the source pattern, while the narrator's changed action supplies a new consequence.

Meets a: the invitation-checker narrates two linked arrivals in a clear situation. Meets b: the latch pause, question and internal recognition develop the conflict. Meets c: by evening shifts time, at the door shifts place, because gives a reason, and when closes the sequence. Meets d: patched jacket, brass latch and plate make the action concrete. Meets e: the unchanged invitation frames a changed welcome. Not yet for a: "I compared Leila's invitation with my guest list.
