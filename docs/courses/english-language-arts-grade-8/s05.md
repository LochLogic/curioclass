## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When a headline says a gene was found or edited, how do we know what to believe?"

**Hook, 129.** Set the federal gene definition beside "One gene explains every trait", a headline invented for practice. Ask what the definition permits that headline to claim; open the compelling question before retrieval.
**Inquiry.** On 131, how do the federal pages distinguish genes from traits? On 138, which sentence limits a research conclusion? On 145, what qualification survives in the article but disappears from the infographic?
**Closing claim.** Complete the food-label argument on 152 from the federal pages and research trail. Submit 153; speak 154.
**Teaching.** Model connections on 129, sentence roles on 130, questioning on 134 and medium choice on 144. Students take over maps on 132 and source decisions on 137.


**Text set.** Keep all six records; field 10 selects federal excerpts, including a wheat results report. Add a reserved Jewett assessment excerpt. Core works: seven whole short works and six short excerpts, counting each medium separately. The two anchor records comprise one whole cluster and one excerpt cluster; neither is long.
