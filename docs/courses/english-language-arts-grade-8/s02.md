**Cold A, report by the invented town's archive editor, day 60.** On Thursday, the archive withdrew fragile fever-relief papers from its public reading table. This was prudent protection. The papers are a storehouse of voices from 1793. Like a threadbare coat, an old page wears further with repeated handling. Some visitors argue that seeing an original provides a connection no copy can offer. That loss matters, so we should reserve monthly supervised viewings of originals while using copies for daily study. We must secure these records for our Posterity, echoing the founding promise studied in the exhibit. Here is the archivist sentence for punctuation: At last the archivist spoke. The preservation advice reads, "A facsimile, even when the image is sharp, does not restore words already lost."

**Cold B, report by the invented town's reading-room volunteer, day 60.** The fever-relief papers remained on the public reading table all Thursday; they were withdrawn on Friday morning. Their sudden removal was avoidable disruption. Readers arrived expecting to consult the originals, and no copies were ready. Preparing copies before withdrawal would have protected both the papers and readers' study time. I support preservation but object to the transition. Neither report includes the reading-room desk log, which could establish when the papers were withdrawn. The withdrawal date remains disputed.

**Cold C, editorial by an invented town's museum volunteer, day 61.** The museum should try one evening opening each month for its fever-relief exhibit. Letters from residents on daytime shifts ask for evening access; their employers confirm that work ends after the museum closes. These requests establish an access problem, though they do not predict attendance. Visits increased during a trial month with Thursday evening hours. A newspaper feature about the exhibit ran that same month. The increase proves that the evening schedule alone caused the rise. The museum's oldest display case is carved oak. A further three-month trial, with attendance recorded by time and visitor feedback, would help decide whether evening opening warrants the staffing cost.

**Cold D, spoken comment by an invented town's meeting participant, day 61.** I urge the council to lend the copied volunteer ledger to the river hall for a month. A signed insurance policy would repay the replacement cost if the copy were damaged. Therefore the loan poses no educational loss: money can replace the lessons visitors would miss while the copy is away. The loan agreement promises return before our school visits begin. That deadline matters, but no transport schedule has been supplied. The town's crest includes a heron. Some members want a delivery plan before approval. I say insurance already answers that concern. Approve the loan today.

Use pairs daily, groups on 40 and teacher-led discussion on 50; practice again after collection on 60. Record one specific next move. Field 5 supplies model and criteria; its W.8.1a to e components are the year's one argument rubric, reused unchanged on 126 and 153.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve weekly words in source sentences. On 58 the edit also checks one verbal, one voice choice and one mood shift in the student's own argument (unit 1, L.8.1a to d), so grammar returns before 119. Reread an anchor passage weekly for accuracy and expression, without a rate target. Keep choice-reading blocks, logs and fortnightly conferences; reuse recent records.

## U9. Supports and extension

Preview words, chunk clauses and mark referents. Model "Although the writer acknowledges ..., the response ...", then fade it. Pair audio with print. Extend by defending a rival interpretation.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, the parent supplies another text-based position to connect and question. With one projector, retain individual printed sources. Publish locally; Curio Class collects nothing.
