# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 8

Course id ela-k12 (grade 8 row), grade 8, draft frame, written 2026-11-14; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, each one specific topic and its set of texts: a mystery in a town historical society, the founding
  words and the people who answered them, the 1906 fight over poisoned food and false medicine, old myths made new,
  and how gene science reaches the public through the news.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units and 12 at course level (6 for three course-level checks of 2 days, 6
  respond days, 2 after each check). It assumes one ELA lesson of about 50 minutes every school day, five a week,
  for 32 weeks, about 20 minutes of independent reading on four nights a week, logged, and 15 minutes of in-class
  independent reading twice a week, when the teacher confers with each student about once every two weeks. Choice
  books come from the Curio Class Reading Room shelf, printable, or any book the student already has. The course
  places 29 progression rows: a long text read whole, three pieces through the full writing process and six shorter
  ones, two research projects (unit 3, and a second in unit 5 on a question the student generates), a findings talk
  in unit 3 and a formal argument talk to an audience beyond the class in unit 5, a staged play and a formal text discussion at least every two weeks. Five longer
  units, not six: grade 8's rows ask for longer texts and two staged productions, and the units are weighed by that
  load in the table.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 8 (RL, RI, W, SL and L). Every grade 8 code is
  owned by a row placed below, and every lettered part (W.8.1a to e, W.8.2a to f, W.8.3a to e, W.8.9a and b,
  SL.8.1a to d, L.8.1a to d, L.8.2a to c, L.8.3a, L.8.4a to d, L.8.5a to c) is named in the unit that teaches it.
  The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md and the approach is docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule governs every unit (rule
  a for the founding words: what each document said and who answered it, with no verdict handed to students; rule
  b, facts and backed sources, for the food, drug and gene texts).
- **Base text.** None: ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Texts are chosen
  by BASELINE rule 29: anchors are original Curio Class texts written to the grade 8 text spec and read by a
  qualified ELA teacher before use, and recent US government nonfiction, public domain as federal works (17 U.S.C.
  105). An older public-domain text (published 1930 or earlier) appears only as a paired or optional read, never
  the anchor; units 2 and 3 lean on such paired primary sources more than any other unit: each is excerpted short,
  and the standard is taught first on the original and federal anchors, then applied to the paired excerpt with
  full support. The grade 8 text spec gives each original literary text its own named narrator or speaker, voice
  and setting, and no two anchors in a unit share a voice. Each original is a commission to that
  spec; its text record (the measure where usable, the qualitative demands, reader and task, the placement
  reason) comes with the scope and sequence. Each unit's texts and their rights line are in the Units
  table's C3 column (ELA has no C3 framework; see "For the lead"). Images inside government pages are checked one
  by one.
- **Reading level.** The 2012 supplement's grades 6 to 8 band (925 to 1185 on its first measure), read alone by
  the end of the year. Working split, ours: grade 8 anchors in the upper half of the band; the paired eighteenth
  century letters and 1906 texts sit above it and are read with full support. "Independently" (RL.8.10, RI.8.10) is
  checked twice: one unseen literary text at the top of the band in unit 5 and one unseen informational text in
  check 3, read without before-reading help (directions only) and scored apart from supported reading; targeted
  supports continue during learning, and supported performance is recorded separately. A poem or play rests on the qualitative
  judgment and the task. Anchors aim at about 55 percent informational, counted in the scope and sequence.
- **Knowledge threads.** Thread 1 (e8-reading-range): grade 6 read the first law codes and grade 7 Athens; grade 8
  reads the founding words and who answered them in unit 2, beside social studies grade 8's Declaration unit, the
  muckraking press that changed a law in unit 3 and a free press checking science claims in unit 5; unit 2 names a
  Constitution excerpt (the Preamble) beside the Declaration, and unit 5 the First Amendment; words reused: law, citizen,
  democracy, with right, press, constitution and amendment added. Thread 2: grade 6 read hazards and grade 7
  ecosystems; grade 8 reads science claims in public life (1906 food and drugs in unit 3, gene science in the news
  in unit 5, beside science grade 8's genetics unit); words reused: data, evidence, population, impact, with trait,
  gene, source, claim and trial added.
- **Writing calendar.** Nine pieces: argument 3 (unit 2 process piece and a short on-demand piece first, unit 5
  argument talk with its written text), informative 3 (unit 1 analytical essay through the process, counted as
  informative as RUBRIC-ELA files literary analysis; unit 3 research article; unit 5 on-demand explanation),
  narrative 3 (unit 1 on-demand chapter as a grade 7 review, unit 4 on-demand retelling and process piece), a third
  each against the rubric's 35, 35 and 30, counted by pieces; the scope and sequence also reports them by days. Every reading day ends in an evidence paragraph or
  a written question (W.8.9, W.8.10). Typing: three pages in one sitting by unit 2.
- **Paper path and no accounts.** Every anchor has a print copy. Curio Class has no student accounts and hosts no
  student work. Unit 3's research is drafted with partner comments in a shared document and published in whatever
  the school already uses, with links, on a shared classroom device or in a lab session in the school day; a room
  without devices comments on printed drafts, writes by hand and lists the sources, and the plan says plainly that
  the paper path does not meet W.8.6, which is recorded unfinished for that class.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read, and every original text gets the
  qualified ELA teacher's read (rule 29). Unit 2 (slavery, the 1793 fever's deaths and the slander of Black
  volunteers) is named for a reader with expertise in African American history, and unit 4's Nasreddin Hodja
  tales for a reader from the Turkish or Persian tradition (who, and any pay, is Cory's). Unit 3's food and factory texts and unit 5's gene
  texts are flagged for care.
