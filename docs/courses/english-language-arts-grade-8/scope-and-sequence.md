# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 8

Course `english-language-arts-grade-8`, grade 8, draft, 2026-11-15.

The day authority is `data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-8/days.csv`: 160 course days, 148 inside five units and 12 at course level. ELA meets every school day for about 50 minutes, so course day equals school day and `school_day` stays blank. Unit order, lengths and owned progression rows remain the frame's. Section boundaries wait for unit planning; each unit therefore uses `<unit>.1` throughout. No section plans, assessment items, answer keys or lessons are written here.

## Decisions applying throughout

The current sequence brief governs two conflicts with the frame. Grade 7 prerequisites get retrieval tasks, not the frame's proposed readiness checks. Every unit has an end-of-unit assessment inside its own days, including units 3 to 5, whose frame had counted the following course-level check as their assessment. Those following checks retain their frame slots and skills but use cold texts on earlier units' topics. They do not repeat the unit's just-completed content or performances.

Every listed assessment code includes every part of that code. Lettered parts appear individually in the CSV, with the parent code alongside them for ownership. Reading assessments reserve brief evidence responses per demand; writing products and revision records can supply evidence instead of a new composition. Discussion observations cover preparation, rules and decisions, goals/deadlines/roles, connections among speakers, and qualifying or justifying views. The unit plan must provide a scoring row and response decision for each part. Broad-code mid-unit samples marked in the CSV use the existing draft or portfolio to cover its parts, not an unannounced full performance.

Daily partner talk precedes the written evidence paragraph or written question. The written response needs that day's text, including on a grammar day using anchor sentences. Unit plans will count all questions, tasks and exit tickets toward the minimum 80 percent requiring the text; this sequence cannot report an achieved percentage before items exist. Sentence teaching uses anchor sentences and then the student's draft. A model precedes each writing type, and each process piece has peer and teacher feedback followed by a named revision day.

Formal prepared text discussions occur on days 10, 20, 29, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 110, 120, 130, 140 and 150. Days 60 and 90 use a brief paired text discussion after the individual response has been collected; these are practice, not extra assessed performances or sources of individual check answers. Every gap is at most ten school days. The discussion routine is taught on day 10 and used before that informally with teacher modelling. Frames mostly fade after unit 1; retain an evidence-link prompt or role card for students who need it.

In-class independent reading takes 15 minutes on school days ending in 2 and 4, including during assessment weeks after the individual task or while other groups perform. On those days the remaining 35 minutes use a narrow anchor excerpt or existing product, not a second full-length task. Confer with each student about once every two weeks using a rotation and reading log. Home reading is about 20 minutes on four nights, logged; in unit 1 it includes the shared novella, with the choice book carried chiefly in the in-class block until the novella is finished. Later it is the student's long choice book from the Reading Room or home. No extra nightly reading block is assumed. A print copy accompanies every anchor.

For the whole novella, reserve days 1 to 18 for about 2,000 to 2,500 words a course day on average, adjusting the last instalment to the finished length. Shared close reading selects short passages while the remaining pages fall in the existing home and silent-reading time. Days 17 to 18 complete the whole book, not a summary. Whether 35,000 to 45,000 words fits actual readers within this reading budget must be checked once the manuscript exists; do not turn this estimate into an additional homework requirement. The one-minute oral-reading repeat for previously flagged and new students is scheduled in teacher conferences on days 2, 4, 7 and 9. No rate is displayed. Once each week, partners reread a short passage from the current anchor for accuracy and expression, including a previously read passage during a check week. These are routines within the existing time, not extra days or an intervention claim.

## Unit 1. The missing letter

**30 days, course days 1 to 30, weeks 1 to 6.** The whole mystery gives readers a common set of clues on which to weigh evidence, follow a theme and distinguish the reader's knowledge from the characters'. The federal archive pages bring the same evidence habit into nonfiction. Sentence work grows from phrases and clauses to verbals, voice and mood before students need those choices in a full analytical essay. The last third therefore carries more writing time than additional reading strategies.

Days 1 to 18 complete the novella and evidence models; day 11's single-sitting imagined chapter retrieves grade 7 narrative writing, with no claim of grade 8 narrative mastery. Day 19 models the W.8.2 analytical essay; days 20 to 27 take it through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing and submission. The question about what the letter said and why it stayed lost is answered through the text, without asking about students' own families. Civil War loss is previewed on days 11 and 17; no battle scenes or personal disclosure.

Readiness: none established, subject to the last-use evidence limit above. Retrieval is distributed before the relevant new work. Mid-unit assessment day 15 samples evidence in both text kinds, theme/summary, plot effects, irony and L.8.1a to d, with response on 16. End days 27 to 29 divide the existing essay/revision record, cold literary and archive reading, and observed small-group discussion plus short language explanations. Together they cover all ten owned rows and every owned part, including W.8.2a to f, W.8.9a/b, SL.8.1a to d, L.8.1a to d and L.8.3a. The reading log, routine writing and whole-text completion supply the extended-time evidence for W.8.10 and reading range; the early band checks do not establish year-end independent proficiency. Respond day 30 addresses these end parts once.

**Candidate cells:** keep the novella text record, 0 extra course days, written before student use; keep the discussion look-for sheet inside day 10's taught routine and days 20/29, 0 extra days; keep the theme task joining reading and writing inside days 27 to 28, which cost 2 of the unit's existing assessment days. The essay explains suspense and the cold response relates theme to character, setting and plot. Nothing waits for an extra course-level day.

## Unit 2. Founding words, answered

**32 days, course days 31 to 62, weeks 7 to 13.** Original features and editorials establish word choice, responses to viewpoints and argument judgments before the harder paired primary excerpts demand them. Banneker's feature precedes the Declaration and letters. The volunteers' feature and Jones and Allen's own reply prepare the supported reading of Carey. No student performs or defends the slander. The two original reports make factual conflict distinct from interpretation before students compare historical accounts. This order gives the year's hardest prose and its counterclaim process argument more time than a six-row equal split would.

Days 31 to 45 carry the main reading, day 48 teaches pause/omission punctuation, and day 49 is the shorter on-demand argument. Day 50 models the process argument about the marker; days 51 to 59 develop it with feedback and revision. All pause choices named by L.8.2a are taught, including the dash, but no dash exercise or glyph is written in this scope and sequence. Slavery, the Declaration's exclusions, fever deaths and the racist allegations are flagged on their reading days; no personal disclosures or reenactment.

Readiness: none established; the six grade 7 prerequisite groups get retrieval before their new demands. Mid-unit day 46 covers RI.8.4, L.8.6, RI.8.6/8/9 and the recorded argument in SL.8.3; respond on 47. End day 59 uses the existing W.8.1a to e product, day 60 samples cold nonfiction and L.8.2a to c, and day 61 checks short written and heard arguments. These cover all six owned rows, every part of the owned codes, with one unit response on day 62. L.8.6 includes using the words accurately, not only naming meanings.

**Candidate cells:** keep the text-dependent question count, 0 extra days, maintained by the unit writer over every question/task/exit ticket; keep the common argument scoring criteria and model piece, 1 existing teaching day, day 50, with subsequent reuse inside feedback and submission days. This does not claim a percentage before questions are written.

## Unit 3. Poison and promises: the 1906 fight over food and medicine

Context, roots, references and verification are taught together, then used while a student-generated question opens several research avenues. Days 72 to 76 explicitly teach questions, searching, judging, notes and citations before civics unit 6 needs that routine. The shorter article and linked display then require those decisions to work in practice, with a findings talk after publication.

The research set is the ledger's approximately fifteen fenced sources, still to choose and check. No student tests food or substances. Handwriting, printed comments and a drawn diagram preserve the thinking on paper but leave W.8.6 recorded unfinished without digital production/publication/collaboration evidence.

## Unit 4. Myths made new

**32 days, course days 93 to 124, weeks 19 to 25.** The modern stories lead, with supported Persephone and Nasreddin Hodja sources then making the transformations visible. The initial short retelling rehearses the narrative climb. The whole long poem and whole one-act script let students distinguish a text's structure from a production's choices. Two staged versions use short scene work in groups beside complete course-recorded productions. Planning these choices precedes the extended process retelling, which has substantial time for internal reflection, transitions among related experiences, feedback and revision.

Days 93 to 100 establish the retelling comparisons and one-sitting narrative; 101 to 107 read the whole script and compare/rehearse/stage both versions. Days 109 to 120 develop the process narrative, applying the mid-unit findings. A student may take a nonacting role and analyse the course recordings; nobody plays someone being harmed. The split-household story does not invite family disclosure. Seizure/bargain is not romance. Nasreddin Hodja is a living tradition shared across regions; sensitivity work must precede its use. The unconfirmed tale edition is an unresolved text dependency, not a settled source choice. No religious work is added while the frame's question remains open.

Readiness: none established; the myth and all grade 7 ELA prerequisites are retrieved, with printed source stories supplying missing knowledge. Mid-unit day 108 checks transformation, words, structures and production judgments, and samples the existing day 98 narrative; response and process planning meet once on 109. End day 121 divides short reading responses across RL.8.9/4/5 and L.8.5a/c, day 122 submits the existing narrative for all W.8.3a to e, and day 123 evaluates actor/director decisions against script and recalls L.8.5b. This covers all five owned rows and parent L.8.5 in full. Respond on day 124. The following check uses earlier missing-record/public-document topics and fresh sources, not Persephone, Nasreddin Hodja or another production performance.

**Candidate cells:** keep the play's text record, 0 extra days, prepared before use with qualitative/task evidence rather than an invented computed level; keep the staging speaking look-for inside rehearsals and versions on days 103 to 106, 4 existing days, no extra staging day. Speaking observation supports participation and evidence use; ownership of grade 8 formal presentation stays in unit 5.

## Unit 5. Gene stories: judging science claims in the news

**28 days, course days 129 to 156, weeks 26 to 32.** The federal gene paragraphs first teach sentence roles and distinctions among gene, trait and environment. The newsroom feature and First Amendment connect the science-claims thread to the free press. A short second research cycle supplies a one-sitting explanation and the eventual argument talk. Formal medium comparisons begin on day 145 after civics finishes its press unit on day 144; print, recorded explanation, infographic and invented advertisements then make tradeoffs and motives concrete. Existing argument and discussion routines carry into the written talk, while focused reasoning, integrated media and delivery are explicitly taught for grade 8.

Days 129 to 130 include the gene-definition bridge because science genetics continues through day 131. Research runs 134 to 140, after that unit ends; its substantial written output is the short explanation on day 142 rather than a second large published article. Days 148 to 153 plan, draft, receive feedback on and complete the written argument text. Day 154 reserves the formal talk to an audience beyond the class; its audience remains the lead's decision, with the frame's another-class option used only to estimate time. Three-minute talks run in parallel small groups, with teacher rotation and saved written/visual evidence, so a class does not wait through thirty serial talks in one short period. Do not treat every group's peer score as teacher evidence. The unit plan must budget teacher observation across rehearsal day 152 and performance day 154. No student is asked about family health, traits or genes; conditions are named respectfully, and advertisements name no real brands.

Readiness: none established; retrieve paragraph/interaction skills on 129 to 130, research from unit 3 on 134, medium skills on 144 and the earlier talk on 148. Mid-unit day 141 samples paragraph roles, connections/distinctions and the existing second research trail; respond on 143. Day 142's explanation uses the research but is not another mid-unit assessment. End part 1 on 147 assesses cold media tradeoffs and social/commercial/political motives; respond in the first ten minutes of 148. End day 153 uses the existing argument script and short cold paragraph analysis, 154 the formal talk for SL.8.4/5/6 in full, and 155 the unseen upper-band literary text read with directions only. Respond to 153 to 155 once on 156. This covers all four owned rows while the continuing unit 1 row receives its independent RL.8.10 evidence. Check 3 uses food-preservation science and food-law editorials from unit 3's topic, not another gene-text assessment.
