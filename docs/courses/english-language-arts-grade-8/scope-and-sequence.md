# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 8

Course `english-language-arts-grade-8`, grade 8, draft, 2026-11-15.

The day authority is `data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-8/days.csv`: 160 course days, 148 inside five units and 12 at course level. ELA meets every school day for about 50 minutes, so course day equals school day and `school_day` stays blank. Unit order, lengths and owned progression rows remain the frame's. Section boundaries wait for unit planning; each unit therefore uses `<unit>.1` throughout. No section plans, assessment items, answer keys or lessons are written here. The teacher times the one-minute oral-reading sample to bound observation; students see no rate or timer. Group-rotation timings serve classroom scheduling.

## Decisions applying throughout

The frame and day map agree: confirmed grade 7 prerequisites get retrieval at first use, and every unit has its own end-of-unit assessment inside its days. Following course-level checks retain their slots and use new cases from earlier units in forms not assessed in the last two weeks. They carry recent records explicitly instead of repeating or rescoring them. No unit, owned row or day count has moved.

Every listed assessment code includes every part of that code. A task sampling only lettered parts lists those parts and omits the broader parent; ownership remains in the frame, progression and skills. In particular, L.8.5b ends on day 86 and returns on 123, while L.8.5a/c end on 121; those records together complete L.8.5. Literary-only and nonfiction-only evidence uses W.8.9a or b respectively, never the whole parent by implication. Reading assessments reserve brief evidence responses per demand; writing products and revision records can supply evidence instead of a new composition. Discussion observations cover preparation, rules and decisions, goals/deadlines/roles, connections among speakers, and qualifying or justifying views. The unit plan must provide a scoring row and response decision for each part. Mid-unit assessments diagnose taught work in existing annotations, notebooks, drafts or research trails; their day rows specify the sampled parts. They do not give a second cold version of the end. Collect individual conference or group-observation evidence once per window; checks within ten days reuse it. Stored products may supply evidence without another performance. Response to a carried record addresses only an unresolved need, never repeats response already completed.

Daily partner talk precedes the written evidence paragraph or written question. The written response needs that day's text, including on a grammar day using anchor sentences. Unit plans will count all questions, tasks and exit tickets toward the minimum 80 percent requiring the text; this sequence cannot report an achieved percentage before items exist. Sentence teaching uses anchor sentences and then the student's draft. A model precedes each writing type, and each process piece has peer and teacher feedback followed by a named revision day.

Formal prepared text discussions occur on days 10, 20, 29, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, 100, 110, 120, 130, 140 and 150. Days 60 and 90 use a brief paired text discussion after the individual response has been collected; these are practice, not extra assessed performances or sources of individual check answers. Every gap is at most ten school days. The discussion routine is taught on day 10 and used before that informally with teacher modeling. Frames mostly fade after unit 1; retain an evidence-link prompt or role card for students who need it.

Reserve 15 minutes for independent reading on the second and fourth school days of every five-day week: days 2, 4, 7, 9, continuing in that pattern through day 159. This is 64 blocks, 960 minutes, including assessment weeks. The CSV marks every block; reading follows an independent assessment, never prepares its text. It runs separately from the teacher-observed talk segment. On those days the remaining 35 minutes use a narrow anchor excerpt or existing product, not a second full-length task. Confer with each student about once every two weeks using a reading log and a 30-student roster: 8, 7, 8, then 7 students across the four blocks; each contact takes at most 90 seconds including movement, leaving 3 to 4.5 minutes for recording. The first fortnight uses that rotation for the once-only one-minute reading samples of flagged/new students, with any remaining contacts used for conferences. This reading allocation is a planning judgment, with no achievement claim. Home reading is about 20 minutes on four nights, logged; in unit 1 it includes the shared novella, with the choice book carried chiefly in the in-class block until the novella is finished. Later it is the student's long choice book from the Reading Room or home. No extra nightly reading block is assumed. A print copy accompanies every anchor.

For the whole novella, reserve days 1 to 18 for about 2,000 to 2,500 words a course day on average, adjusting the last instalment to the finished length. Shared close reading selects short passages while the remaining pages fall in the existing home and silent-reading time. Days 17 to 18 complete the whole book, not a summary. Whether 35,000 to 45,000 words fits actual readers within this reading budget must be checked once the manuscript exists; do not turn this estimate into an additional homework requirement. The one-minute oral-reading repeat for previously flagged and new students is scheduled in teacher conferences on days 2, 4, 7 and 9. No rate is displayed. Once each week, partners reread a short passage from the current anchor for accuracy and expression, including a previously read passage during a check week. These are routines within the existing time, not extra days or an intervention claim.

## Unit 1. The missing letter

**30 days, course days 1 to 30, weeks 1 to 6.** The whole mystery gives readers a common set of clues on which to weigh evidence, follow a theme and distinguish the reader's knowledge from the characters'. The federal archive pages bring the same evidence habit into nonfiction. Sentence work grows from phrases and clauses to verbals, voice and mood before students need those choices in a full analytical essay. The last third therefore carries more writing time than additional reading strategies. Days 9 and 14 are Understanding; days 23, 25 and 26 are Application.

Days 1 to 18 complete the novella and evidence models; day 11's single-sitting imagined chapter retrieves grade 7 narrative writing, with no claim of grade 8 narrative mastery. Day 19 models the W.8.2 analytical essay; days 20 to 27 take it through planning, drafting, feedback, revision, editing and submission. The question about what the letter said and why it stayed lost is answered through the text, without asking about students' own families. Civil War loss is previewed on days 11 and 17; no battle scenes or personal disclosure.

Readiness: none; grade 7 use confirmed. Retrieval is distributed before the relevant new work. Mid-unit assessment day 15 diagnoses strongest explicit/inferred evidence in the already-read novella/archive texts and notebook evidence for L.8.1a/b, with response on 16. Theme, plot and irony are not retested at mid-unit; the cold end carries them. Remaining language parts wait for 29. End days 27 to 29 divide the existing essay/revision record, cold literary and archive reading, and observed small-group discussion plus short language formation/use/repair tasks. Day 29 gives each of six groups of five a four-minute observed discussion and one-minute transition, with five minutes of directions, 35 minutes total. Students complete fifteen-minute sentence work while waiting and the separate fifteen-minute reading block afterwards. Existing draft examples plus short transformations explain verbals generally/in sentences, form/use both voices and all five moods and recognize/correct shifts. Keep explanations within fifteen minutes; finish on 30 if trial timing requires it without collecting again. Together they cover all ten owned rows and every owned part, including W.8.2a to f, W.8.9a/b, SL.8.1a to d, L.8.1a to d and L.8.3a. The reading log, routine writing and whole-text completion supply the extended-time evidence for W.8.10 and reading range; the early band checks do not establish year-end independent proficiency. Respond day 30 addresses these end parts once.

**Candidate cells:** keep the novella text record, 0 extra course days, written before student use; keep the discussion look-for sheet inside day 10's taught routine and days 20/29, 0 extra days; keep the theme task joining reading and writing inside days 27 to 28, which cost 2 of the unit's existing assessment days. The essay explains suspense and the cold response relates theme to character, setting and plot. Nothing waits for an extra course-level day.

## Unit 2. Founding words, answered

**32 days, course days 31 to 62, weeks 7 to 13.** Original features and editorials establish word choice, responses to viewpoints and argument judgments before the harder paired primary excerpts demand them. Banneker's feature precedes the Declaration and letters. The volunteers' feature and Jones and Allen's own reply prepare the supported reading of Carey. No student performs or defends the slander. The two original reports make factual conflict distinct from interpretation before students compare historical accounts. This order gives the year's hardest prose and its counterclaim process argument more time than a six-row equal split would.

Days 31 to 45 carry the main reading, day 48 teaches pause/omission punctuation, and day 49 is the shorter on-demand argument. Day 50 models the process argument about the marker; days 51 to 59 develop it with feedback and revision. Days 54, 57 and 58 are Application. All pause choices named by L.8.2a are taught, including the dash, but no dash exercise or glyph is written in this scope and sequence. Slavery, the Declaration's exclusions, fever deaths and the racist allegations are flagged on their reading days; no personal disclosures or reenactment.

Readiness: none; grade 7 use confirmed; the six grade 7 prerequisite groups get retrieval before their new demands. Mid-unit day 46 diagnoses existing purpose/response and factual-conflict maps for RI.8.6/9 and accurate acquisition/use of vocabulary for L.8.6; respond on 47. Cold word-choice and written/heard argument judgments wait for 60 to 61. End day 59 uses the existing W.8.1a to e product, day 60 samples cold nonfiction and L.8.2a to c, and day 61 checks short written and heard arguments. These cover all six owned rows, every part of the owned codes, with one unit response on day 62. L.8.6 includes using the words accurately, not only naming meanings.

**Candidate cells:** keep the text-dependent question count, 0 extra days, maintained by the unit writer over every question/task/exit ticket; keep the common argument scoring criteria and model piece, 1 existing teaching day, day 50, with subsequent reuse inside feedback and submission days. This does not claim a percentage before questions are written.

## Unit 4. Myths made new

**32 days, course days 93 to 124, weeks 19 to 25.** The modern stories lead, with supported Persephone and Nasreddin Hodja sources then making the transformations visible. The initial short retelling rehearses the narrative climb. The whole long poem and whole one-act script let students distinguish a text's structure from a production's choices. Two staged versions use short scene work in groups beside complete course-recorded productions. Planning these choices precedes the extended process retelling, which has substantial time for internal reflection, transitions among related experiences, feedback and revision.

Days 93 to 100 establish the retelling comparisons and one-sitting narrative; 101 to 107 read the whole script and compare/rehearse/stage both versions. Days 109 to 120 develop the process narrative, applying the mid-unit findings. A student may take a nonacting role and analyse the course recordings; nobody plays someone being harmed. The split-household story does not invite family disclosure. Seizure/bargain is not romance. Nasreddin Hodja is a living tradition shared across regions; sensitivity work must precede its use. The unconfirmed tale edition is an unresolved text dependency, not a settled source choice. Myths and folk tales remain the RL.8.9 sources. The lead ruling is settled: religious texts, where a standard calls for one, are read as literature or history, never as scripture or an anchor; no student states or performs a belief.

Readiness: none; grade 7 use confirmed; the myth and all grade 7 ELA prerequisites are retrieved, with printed source stories supplying missing knowledge. Mid-unit day 108 diagnoses transformation in the already-read stories/source annotations and every W.8.3a to e part in the existing day 98 narrative; cold word/structure comparisons and production judgments wait for the end; response and process planning meet once on 109. End day 121 divides short reading responses across RL.8.9/4/5 and L.8.5a/c, day 122 submits the existing narrative for all W.8.3a to e, and day 123 evaluates actor/director decisions against script and recalls L.8.5b. The following check uses earlier missing-record/public-document topics and fresh sources, not Persephone, Nasreddin Hodja or another production performance.

## Unit 5. Gene stories: judging science claims in the news

**28 days, course days 129 to 156, weeks 26 to 32.** The federal gene paragraphs first teach sentence roles and distinctions among gene, trait and environment. The newsroom feature and First Amendment connect the science-claims thread to the free press. A short second research cycle supplies a one-sitting explanation and the eventual argument talk. Formal medium comparisons begin on day 145 after civics finishes its press unit on day 144; print, recorded explanation, infographic and invented advertisements then make tradeoffs and motives concrete. Existing argument and discussion routines carry into the written talk, while focused reasoning, integrated media and delivery are explicitly taught for grade 8.

Days 129 to 130 include the gene-definition bridge because science genetics continues through day 131. Research runs 134 to 140, after that unit ends; its substantial written output is the short explanation on day 142 rather than a second large published article. Days 148 to 153 plan, draft, receive feedback on and complete the written argument text. Day 154 reserves the formal talk to another class in the school, with families invited where the school allows, as settled by the lead. No separate family evening is required. Three-minute talks run in parallel small groups, with teacher rotation and saved written/visual evidence, so a class does not wait through thirty serial talks in one short period. Do not treat every group's peer score as teacher evidence. For 30 students, the individual evidence roster assigns students 1 to 15 to day 152 and 16 to 30 to day 154. Each has one 90-second teacher-observed segment containing a focused claim, reason/evidence, actual media integration and delivery. Each day budgets 22.5 minutes observation, 7.5 transitions and 5 directions/feedback, 35 minutes, with the 15-minute choice-reading block separate. Full organisation, support and details come from the script/visual saved on 153; actual eye contact, volume, pronunciation, integrated media and formal speech come from the named observed segment. Day 154 reuses the 152 records without another observation. Everyone still gives the complete three-minute group talk; the teacher does not claim to hear thirty complete talks. Day 151 informal-to-formal rehearsal comparison supplies contextual adaptation for SL.8.6. No student is asked about family health, traits or genes; conditions are named respectfully, and advertisements name no real brands.

Readiness: none; grade 7 use confirmed; retrieve paragraph/interaction skills on 129 to 130, research from unit 3 on 134, medium skills on 144 and the earlier talk on 148. Mid-unit day 141 diagnoses sentence roles on an already-annotated paragraph and current research decisions; the cold RI.8.3 connection/distinction task waits for end day 153. Respond on 143. Day 142's explanation uses the research but is not another mid-unit assessment. End part 1 on 147 assesses cold media tradeoffs and social/commercial/political motives; respond in the first ten minutes of 148. End day 153 uses the existing argument script and short cold paragraph analysis, 154 the formal talk for SL.8.4/5/6 in full, and 155 the unseen upper-band literary text read with directions only. Respond to 153 to 155 once on 156. This covers all four owned rows while the continuing unit 1 row receives its independent RL.8.10 evidence. It carries media/motive evidence from 147 and paragraph/connection evidence from 153 instead of repeating either task.
