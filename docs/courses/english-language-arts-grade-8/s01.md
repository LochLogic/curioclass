## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What did the missing letter say, and why did someone want it to stay lost?"

**Hook, day 1.** Read the commissioned opening at the empty letter file. Separate what the file establishes from what the volunteers suspect; open the compelling question without revealing its answer.

**Inquiry questions.** Q1, day 3: Which incident changes the search, and what dialogue explains the decision? Q2, day 10: Where does knowing more than the volunteers create suspense or humour? Q3, day 18: How do the archive readings change which clues deserve confidence?

**Closing explanation, days 21 to 27.** Explain how knowledge gaps and consequential decisions build suspense. Use beginning, turning point and ending evidence, including the letter's contents and concealment motive. The manuscript must supply these; this plan invents neither.

**Teaching.** Model evidence on 1, theme on 2, plot effects on 3, reader knowledge on 5 and nonfiction reasoning on 6. Withdraw completed models on 7; students lead the final reread on 18.

**Cold literary selection: The second voice. Curio Class, 2026. Ours; invented for practice.**

1. At the town archive, Nia practiced the introduction to an exhibition of recorded memories. "I can do it without notes," she told Owen. At the first unfamiliar name she stopped, then began again, louder. The visitors would enter in ten minutes. She pushed the pronunciation sheet beneath her chair.

2. Behind the partition, the keeper was testing a playback machine. It would repeat whatever the microphone captured, after a short delay. She switched it on, then went to fetch the visitors. Neither volunteer had heard her explanation from the other room.

3. Nia tried again. A voice beyond the partition repeated her mistake. She stiffened. "Someone thinks this is funny." Owen bent toward the grille. "Please stop copying us," he whispered. "Please stop copying us," the machine whispered back. He bowed gravely to the grille, as though politeness might improve its manners.

4. Nia reached for the switch that would silence the exhibition. Owen caught sight of the moving playback counter. "It copies the pause too," he said. Nia waited without speaking. The second voice fell silent. She released the switch. Instead of asking the keeper to remove a visitor, she asked Owen to read the pronunciation sheet with her.

5. When the doors opened, Nia held the sheet where everyone could see it. At one name she hesitated, checked and continued. The keeper asked whether she wanted the microphone louder. "Clearer will do," Nia said. This time she left the sheet on the table.
