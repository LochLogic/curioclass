# Section 3.6: Photographs, partner talk and a model paragraph

## Header


Children read the pictures and captions of the research articles, their own slips, and a model paragraph of about 55 words read aloud first; they read at most 150 printed words a day and the teacher reads every stem aloud. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: photographs of a bee and of pollen only; no sting or injury. The indoor path is complete. Any outdoor look is optional (field 5) and no child handles an insect.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.2.7**, "Participate in shared research and writing projects (e.g., read a number of books on a single topic to produce a report; record science observations)." Day 96 records an observation (confirm or correct a note against a photograph); day 97 shares notes with a partner and in a group of five; day 98 turns the class notes into a model report.
- **W.2.8**, "Recall information from experiences or gather information from provided sources to answer a question." Day 96 adds a fact from a photograph or its caption; day 97 answers a partner's question from the child's own slips and names the tag.
- **W.2.2**, "Write informative/explanatory texts in which they introduce a topic, use facts and definitions to develop points, and provide a concluding statement or section." Day 98 only: analyze a model and plan a paragraph. The paragraph itself is written and scored on days 99 to 101 (section 3.7), from new sources.
- **RI.2.7** (day 96): "Explain how specific images (e.g., a diagram showing how a machine works) contribute to and clarify a text", here a photograph and its caption. **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3** (day 97): "Ask and answer questions about what a speaker says in order to clarify comprehension, gather additional information, or deepen understanding of a topic or issue." Practice only; the day 99 and 100 group record (R6) is the check.
- **RF.2.3c, e, f; RF.2.3d; RF.2.4, 4a to 4c; L.2.2d, 2e** (cycle 16 days 96 and 97, cycle 17 day 98): -dge and -ge, -ch and -tch, -er and -est, reading to confirm meaning, and spelling by pattern.
- **Limits that bite.** A photograph shows what is there, not what happened next. The bumblebee photograph with pollen on its head is reserved for the day 96 exit and never appears in teaching. The Bee Visit and Butterfly Visit cards (R2), the paragraph feedback sheets of day 100, and the words ledge, wedge and brick (day 101) are never used in teaching. The word list for the cycle 17 check (stretch, kitchen, sweeter, smallest, ranch) is never used in teaching.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 verifying a note in a second source returns as review. Unit 1: ordered paragraph strips (day 17), the four-row feedback sheet (days 18 and 19), the talk card and tally child (day 25). Unit 2: the topic and focus chart (day 46).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The mining bee photograph (public domain, in 01-b) shows pollen on a bee at work; it does not show the pollen reaching the next flower. A model paragraph has a topic sentence, facts, a definition and a conclusion; the definition has a job. The paragraph is written later from new sources, never copied from the model.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned.** None are scored here. This section prepares R1, R2, R3 and R6 (days 99 to 101, section 3.7) and never uses the Bee Visit and Butterfly Visit cards, the paragraph feedback sheets, the "Meets" sample paragraph of the unit plan, the day 101 caption (ledge, wedge, brick) or the day 96 reserved photograph as an example.

**Cycle 16 written check** (day 97, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Five independent spellings, none a day 93 to 96 word and neither ledge nor wedge. (1) "We walked up the ____." ridge. (2) "The ____ gave a prize." judge. (3) "The actor stood on the ____." stage. (4) "I will ____ into the pool." plunge. (5) "That is ____ house." their. Wrong: "rij" and "ridg" omit the dge (Y); "juj" and "judg" omit the final e (Y or AN); "stag" omits the e so the vowel reads short (T); "plunj" omits the ge (Y); "ther" or "thier" writes the odd part as if regular (AM); "bridg" for any word swaps or omits letters of the ending (AN). After marking, dictate "pledge" and "hinge" as unscored practice.

**Exit questions.** Spoken and by picture, one ask per stem, answered by the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner and the teacher notes hands. Shown is the right answer in the child's words; shown with help is right after one prompt ("What does the caption say?"); not yet seen is another answer or none. Cards are invented for practice, except the photograph of E96.
- **E96** (day 96). Note: "The yellow grains are on a wing." The reserved photograph (a bumblebee with yellow pollen among the hairs of its head; Mike Budd, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, 2022, public domain) and its caption: "Yellow pollen grains lie among the hairs on this bee's head." Item (a): "Does the photograph and caption support the note? What would you change?" Key: no; change "wing" to "head hairs". Item (b): "What does the caption add to the picture?" Key: it says where the grains are and what they are; the picture alone does not prove they travel on. Wrong: keeping the wing claim (C); "the photograph shows the pollen reaching the next flower" (C).
- **E97** (day 97). Partner says: "An insect crawls over a flower." Note card (invented): "Pollen sticks to its back." Item: "Ask your partner one question about this, then answer it from the note card." Key: any question the card answers, such as "Where does the pollen stick?" answered "On its back"; the child names the tag (the card). Wrong: an unrelated reply (L); an answer that ignores the question asked (N); no question asked (M).
- **E98** (day 98). Card: "Hummingbirds visit red flowers. A bill is a bird's beak. A long, thin bill reaches nectar deep inside a flower. Pollen sticks to the bird's face. So the bird helps the flower." Item (a): "Point to the definition and say what it is for." Key: "A bill is a bird's beak"; it explains bill so the next fact makes sense. Item (b): "Say what the last sentence does." Key: it ends the paragraph by saying why the bird helps the flower. Wrong: "the definition is just another fact" (AF); no ending named (G); the topic sentence not found (F).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The research slips, group record sheets and joint chart (days 88 to 95) are held by the groups; the photograph pages (the mining bee in 01-b and the four article pictures) are held by pairs on day 96. The talk card and tally child (unit 1, day 25) return on day 97. The ordered paragraph strips with an unrelated fact crossed out (unit 1, day 17) and the four-row feedback sheet (unit 1, days 18 and 19) return on day 98; the plan page (new on day 98: four boxes, topic, two notes, a word to define, an ending idea) is held by each child and stays with the child. The sound-spelling-map, syllable-types chart, word wall, talk routine and heard-six column stay up.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles, sort and reread, ten children each; the day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations. Day 97 uses the 25-minute check form.
- **Key moment 1, day 96: a note checked against a photograph.** Ideal instrument: the printed photograph with its caption beside the slip. Spec: fits; paper or a screen.
- **Key moment 2, day 97: the partner question.** Ideal instrument: a talk card and the slips. Spec: fits; paper. No recording is made.
- **Optional outdoor look (day 96, not needed for the standard).** Only if the school allergy plan allows it, families and the office are told, an adult carries emergency communication, and the area is inspected and untreated. No child touches an insect; hands are washed.
- **Tools.** None. No account is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** Describe the photograph aloud; slips are torn paper; tiles are letters on card.

## 6. Days

## Day 96


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed D, E, C or AE on day 95, cover the article and say a fact in four words; or hold up the question card; or trace one fact back to its line.
- **3 min. Say the plan.** A photograph is evidence. Today we check our notes against pictures and captions: we confirm a note, or we correct it. A photograph shows what is there; it does not show everything that happened after.
- **10 min. Model with the mining bee.** Teacher shows the mining bee photograph and caption from 01-b ("A female mining bee collects pollen from willow flowers") and the class note "bees collect pollen". Models: "The caption confirms the note; the picture shows yellow on her body." Models what the picture does not show (where the pollen goes next).
- **14 min. Pairs check their notes.** Pairs take two of their own slips from days 92 to 95 and the pictures of 01-b to 01-e (art to be made) and say "The picture confirms ___" or "The picture corrects ___". They write a new slip tagged "photo" or "caption". The teacher hears six pairs (1 minute each).
- **10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "What does the photograph show that the words do not?" Children say "The picture shows ___ that the words ___". Hear the six.
- **12 min. Fix or add a note.** Each child fixes one slip or adds one, in four words, with the tag. The teacher scribes for a child who asks.
- **6 min. Exit.** E96 (field 3): the reserved photograph, shown once with its caption read aloud; the six answer aloud, the rest tell a partner.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** "Model with the mining bee" and "Pairs check their notes" (the picture confirms or corrects) are the exit's shape. Instead: sort given notes under "confirmed" and "corrected" cards, or find and mark on the photograph the part that supports a note.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Yesterday's commonest miss (S, T or Y) written beside the right word and read aloud.
- **15 min. Fresh passage and dictation.** Read the cycle 16 card, a fresh decodable passage of about 40 words (cycle 16 patterns only); confirm meaning after decoding. Dictate two words (fudge and hinge) and one sentence with their. Text to make: the cycle 16 passage (field 11).
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (section 2.1, field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the cycle 16 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 4 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which letter in their is the odd part?"

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the photograph page and slips; the teacher holds the joint chart. Tool: None. By hand: checking, writing four-word slips, dictation. Evidence: E96; the new slip; the dictation; the oral card; the heard column.

## Day 97


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed C or AE on day 96, reread the caption and put a finger on the grains; or trace one fact back to its line.
- **3 min. Say the plan.** Today you talk with a partner. One asks a question about the other's notes; the other answers from the slips and says the tag. We use the talk card: turn, pair, link, clarify.
- **8 min. Model the talk.** Teacher and one child model: "Where does the pollen go?" "To another flower, 01-a." "You said another flower; which part do you mean?" "The sticky stigma." The class names the four moves (turn, pair, link, clarify).
- **16 min. Partner rounds.** Two rounds of 8 minutes. Pairs ask and answer from their slips; in round 2 they swap partners. The teacher hears six pairs per round (1 minute each); the other 24 children work in pairs. Interrupting is K.
- **12 min. Science talk in fives.** Six groups of five with a talk card and a tally child (unit 1): "How do pollinators help plants grow and survive?" Children speak in turn from slips; the tally child counts who spoke. Practice only; no score.
- **9 min. Write.** Each child writes one sentence that answers a partner's question and gives the tag ("Pollen goes to another flower, 01-a"), a few words; teacher scribes for a child who asks.
- **7 min. Exit.** E97 (field 3): the insect card; the six answer aloud, the rest tell a partner.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** "Model the talk", the partner rounds and the written answer sentence ask a question and answer it from a card, the exit's shape. Instead: judge a given question (does the slip answer it?), or sort question strips into "the slips answer this" and "they do not".
- **15 min. Written check (foundations).** Cycle 16 written check (field 3), unscored.
- **5 min. Reader.** Reread A Package at the Bridge in pairs for expression.
- **25 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card for the whole cycle (12 minutes), the rest in stations. Group 5 of five.
- **15 min. Fresh response.** Day 93's six read cycle 16 words to the teacher (12 minutes, 2 minutes each) while the others partner-read a pattern card and spell two dictated words; no new teaching.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the slips and a talk card; the tally child holds the tally. Tool: None. By hand: asking, answering, tallying, writing a sentence, spelling five words. Evidence: E97; the written sentence; the cycle 16 check; the oral card records (group 5 of five, the last of the cycle); the heard column.

## Day 98


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed K, L, M or N on day 97, rehearse gaining the floor with the turn card, or say the partner's question back before answering.
- **3 min. Say the plan.** Today we look at how a good paragraph is built, with a topic sentence, facts, a definition and an ending. The definition has a job. You will write a paragraph on days 99 to 101 from sources you have not seen, so today we only study a model and plan.
- **10 min. Hear and read the model.** Teacher reads the model paragraph, "Bats Help Flowers at Night" (about 55 words), twice: Bats help flowers at night. Some bats drink nectar, a sweet juice inside a flower. Their long noses and tongues reach deep inside. Pollen sticks to the bat while it feeds. Some pollen brushes onto the next flower. So bats help flowers while they eat. Pairs reread it with a finger.
- **12 min. Analyze the model.** Pairs lay paragraph strips for the model in order and name the job of each: topic, fact, fact, definition, ending. An unrelated strip ("Bats sleep in caves") is crossed out with a reason. The class says why the definition is there (AF).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** "Analyze the model" (name each strip's job, say what the definition is for) is the shape of the day 98 exit items (point to the definition, say what it is for, say what the last sentence does). Instead: find and mark the jobs in a different given paragraph with colour, or fix a given paragraph with no definition; the point-to and say-what-for asks stay in the exit.
- **12 min. Shared writing from notes.** Teacher models turning the day 94 hummingbird slips into the four parts on the board: a topic sentence, two facts, a defined word (bill), an ending. Children offer the next word each time ("Which note goes here?").
- **10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "How does the definition help the explanation?" Children say "The definition of ___ helps because ___". Hear the six.
- **8 min. Plan page.** Each child fills the plan page for the group's day 95 pair of articles: topic idea, two notes, a word to define, an ending idea. Words only, no sentences; the plan stays with the child and is not scored.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Cycle 16 check repair: the commonest miss of the day 97 check (Y, T, AM or AN), the wrong spelling beside the right one, written on the board.
- **15 min. Teach: -ch, -tch and -er, -est.** Retrieve -dge and -ge endings. Teach -tch after a short vowel (catch, match, pitch, hatch, itch) and -ch after a consonant or long vowel (lunch, bench, teach). Teach the endings -er and -est (taller, tallest, faster, fastest, slower, slowest): the base word first, then add the ending and say what it adds. Model, map, blend and spell. Dictate a sentence with catch.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (section 2.1, field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the cycle 17 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 1 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which ending has the t, in match or in lunch?"

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the model strips and the board writing; each child holds a plan page. Tool: None. By hand: laying strips, naming jobs, filling a plan page, spelling. Evidence: E98; the plan page; the oral card; the heard column.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 96, "What does the photograph show that the words do not?"; day 97, "How do pollinators help plants grow and survive?"; day 98, "How does the definition help the explanation?"
- Written task, day 97: one sentence that answers a partner's question with its tag, a few words.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) the answer comes from the child's own slip, not the teacher; (2) the tag names where the fact was found; (3) the child asks or answers so the partner can tell the idea was heard (the question is linked).

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Each day opens with one repair of at most 5 minutes for the commonest miss of the day before; day 98 opens with the cycle 16 check repair.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence each day; the record sheet has a heard column. Day 97 closes cycle 16 with group 5 of five; day 98 begins cycle 17 with group 1 of five.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of A Package at the Bridge by day 96; accuracy first; no timer.
- **Earlier work returning.** Research slips and joint chart (days 88 to 95); ordered paragraph strips and the four-row feedback sheet (unit 1); the talk card and tally child (unit 1); the topic and focus chart (unit 2); the talk routine.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: photograph, caption, confirm, correct, evidence, tag, question, answer, definition, ending, topic. Frames: "The picture confirms ___." "The caption corrects ___." "Which part do you mean?" "The definition of ___ helps because ___."
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer; describe the photograph aloud when needed; a child who cannot write draws and the teacher scribes.
- **Check accommodations.** The cycle 16 check is read twice; a child may say the spelling aloud and the teacher writes it, recorded as help.
- **Reading load.** At most 150 printed words a day: four-word slips, two captions, the model paragraph (about 55, read aloud first), the cycle 17 card.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second photograph that confirms the same note.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look at the photograph together, read the caption aloud, and ask the child to fix one slip. Take the partner role in the question and answer. Spell three words with letter cards.
- **One projector.** Show photograph and slip side by side.
