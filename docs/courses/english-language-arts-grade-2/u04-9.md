# Section 4.9: Assembling the packet, reflecting and explaining a poem

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.2.4, days 150 and 152:** "Describe how words and phrases (e.g., regular beats, alliteration, rhymes, repeated lines) supply rhythm and meaning in a story, poem, or song." Taught 126 and 127 (section 4.3, review); on 150 and 152 each child describes all four effects on a poem they have not seen, P1 (18 topic minutes a day, a table of five at a time).
- **L.2.2d and L.2.2e, day 151:** spelling patterns and the printed dictionary, in the cycle 25 revision and the cycle 26 sort.
- **SL.2.4, SL.2.6 and SL.2.3, day 151:** "Tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking audibly in coherent sentences"; "Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification"; "Ask and answer questions about what a speaker says in order to clarify comprehension." The reflection on the packet is spoken in groups of five (review of the talk routine and the listener question of grade 1).
- **RI.2.3, day 150:** the planting steps are checked for a connection from one step to the next (taught 143, practiced 146).
- **Limits that bite here.** P1 is read at each group's table and nowhere else; children not at the table never see poem P, so the check stays cold. The packet holds paper seeds only; there is no planting or outdoor visit. A right retry on 151 is marked shown with help and never replaces the first mark. Day 152 rehearses the poem and song of 06 after P1 (never before).
- **Unit items owned.** P1 (150 and 152) and T6 (153, inside T4 and T5), below.
- **The teacher page must explain.** How to hear four effects, one at a time and in the child's words (beat, rhyme, beginning sounds, repetition) with what each does for the meaning; that a device named without its effect is O and a device left out is AF; why a spoken answer to a listener must be a whole sentence; and how to run a group table in six minutes.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned.**
- **P1, days 150 and 152, RL.2.4, 18 topic minutes each.** Children 1 to 15 on 150 (three groups of five, six minutes each); children 16 to 30 on 152 with the same prompt and groups. Poem P, "Room to Bloom", is read once to the group (30 seconds) and then each child describes four effects, one asked at a time, in about 65 seconds: the beat, the rhyme, the beginning sounds, the repeated line. Key: steady beats sound like repeated work; grow and fro rhyme and link lines; busy bees repeats a first sound and makes the bees stand out; the repeated request emphasizes making space. Any other convincing text-based effect is accepted. Each effect is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. A device named without its effect is O; a device left out is AF. Retain individual notes; the first 15 records are reused on 152 with no second performance.
- **T6, day 153, SL.2.6, 0 extra minutes** (section 4.10 runs the sitting, inside T4 and T5). Question: "Why does Heron ask Duck to splash less?" Key: a whole sentence such as "Heron asks because the reeds need the water." A fragment such as "Reeds" when a sentence is requested is P. Marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Exit questions** (E150 and E152 reread a passage of the unit's own texts; E151 is an invented case, said so aloud).
- **E150.** Passage: 05-a, paragraph 3 ("Here is the first reason"), reread aloud twice; not 05-d, whose steps the day checks. Reread aloud twice 05-a, Flowers Through the Seasons, paragraph 3, the first reason. Show three strips: A "Bees wake up hungry in early spring." B "Early flowers bloom, so more bees can eat." C "There are more bees later in the year." Say: "Mark the strip that comes right after the early flowers." Key: strip C (more bees later in the year). Wrong: strip A (bees wake up hungry in early spring) (Z). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same paragraph. Say: "Say why early flowers come before more bees later." Key: Early flowers feed the hungry bees, so more bees are there later in the year. Wrong: Because it is next. (Z); Because it is on the list. (Z).
- **E151.** Card: "Invented for practice. Before: Our poster is nice. Written feedback: Say what makes it nice. After: Our poster has a red kite in the corner. A listener asks: Why did you change the first sentence?" Paper: "Mark the answer that is a whole sentence" (A: "I changed it to say what makes the poster nice." B: "To say."). Spoken (E151b): a second listener asks "What did you put in the new sentence?"; "Answer the listener in a whole sentence." Key: "I put a red kite in the corner of the poster." Wrong: "To say" or "Because" alone (P: a fragment); "It is my packet" (W: the question ignored).
- **E152.** Passage: 06-a Room for Wings, verse 3, lines 1 and 2, printed and read aloud twice; not the sound cards the day sorts. Show the first two lines of verse 3 of Room for Wings (06-a), printed, and read them aloud twice: "Leave a leaf and leave a stem, Safe and warm, a home for them." Say: "Circle the two words that rhyme at the ends of the lines." Key: stem and them. Wrong: leave and leaf. Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same lines. Say: "Say what those two words do for the poem." Key: They rhyme, which ties the two lines together: the stem becomes a home for them. Wrong: Stem and them rhyme. (O).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The edited packet (envelope, drawing, edited card, typed invitation) is assembled on 150 and goes to 4.10 for the sharing on 155; the P1 notes and reflection notes are kept. Models carried by children: the talk routine and the listener question, the four-finger count of sound devices, the editing checklist, the heard-six column.
- **Key moment 1, days 150 and 152: a group table with one poem read aloud.** Ideal: a corner table with a printed poem the teacher holds. Spec: fits (one printed copy for the teacher, none for the children). **Key moment 2, day 151: a reflection card of two sentences per child.** Ideal: a sentence strip. Spec: fits.
- **A room with nothing.** The poem is read from the teacher's page; reflection sentences are said, not written; the packet is a folded sheet.

## 6. Days

## Day 150


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** Retrieve the four sound devices on four fingers (beat, rhyme, beginning sounds, repeated line) and the sequence words (first, next, then, after that, last).
- **35 min. Assemble and P1.** Each child puts the envelope, drawing, edited card and printed invitation together in the packet and checks that the class steps keep their order. P1 is run first, before any poem work: children 1 to 15 come to the teacher's table in three groups of five, six minutes each (18 minutes inside the 35); the 15 others assemble. Poem P is read once at each table (30 seconds), then each child answers four asks one at a time, about 65 seconds each (5 x 65 = 5 min 25 s).
- **10 min. Sequence check.** Pairs check the class steps against 05-d: say why one step must come before the next, with a because (RI.2.3, review of 143 and 146); the teacher listens to six pairs.
- **5 min. Read together.** Pairs reread the steps aloud with a finger under the words.
- **5 min. Exit.** E150.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The sequence check asks why a step comes first, as E150 does (the first reason in 05-a). Use a different kind of task: act the steps of a different task in the wrong order and say what goes wrong, in a few words.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before (Q, R or S), at most 5 minutes.
- **12 min. Read the matched decodable.** After pattern teaching; meaning confirmed from print and sentence (RF.2.4a, c).
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (7 to 12) read the cycle 26 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **16 min. Spell.** Words and sentences with -ous and -us words.
- **10 min. Map irregular words.** No guessing from pictures or first letters.
- **5 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: the group holds the four-finger count; pairs hold the steps. Tool: None. By hand: assembly, spelling, the oral card. Evidence: E150; P1 notes for children 1 to 15 (written feedback after class); the oral card, children 7 to 12.

## Day 151


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** The commonest E150 wrong answer (Z): act hand washing in the wrong order.
- **10 min. Model a reflection.** The teacher models a two-sentence reflection (what my packet is for; one change I made) and a listener question, with a fragment and a whole-sentence answer side by side (P, SL.2.6). A fragment is never marked wrong in conversation; it is a request for detail that needs a sentence.
- **20 min. Reflect in groups.** Six groups of five. Round one, each child gives the two-sentence reflection (5 children x 2 minutes = 10 minutes). Round two, each child asks one listener question and gets an answer (10 minutes). The teacher listens in four groups for about 3 minutes each (12 minutes) and records SL.2.3 and SL.2.4 on the sheet.
- **10 min. P1 retry.** Children 1 to 15 retry the missing effect with the teacher at the table, 40 seconds a child (15 x 40 = 10 minutes), after written feedback; poem P is not reread aloud. A right retry is marked shown with help. The other 15 reread poem 06-a in pairs.
- **10 min. Write.** Each child writes the two reflection sentences on a strip for the packet (written by hand).
- **5 min. Exit.** E151.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The model reflection and listener answers use a "changed it because" sentence, as E151 does. Use a different kind of task: choose between a whole sentence and a fragment for a reflection starter, then say the whole one.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before, at most 5 minutes.
- **8 min. Revise the cycle 25 record.** Written check (148) and any remaining oral turn, corrected after the note; scored afterward on 152.
- **10 min. Reread the cycle 26 reader.** For accuracy, then smoothly (RF.2.4b).
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (13 to 18) read the cycle 26 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **15 min. Sort.** Twelve -ous and -us words by ending; spell four on paper.
- **10 min. Use the pattern in sentences.** Each child writes one sentence with a cycle word, checks one spelling in the printed dictionary.

Who holds what is carried: the child holds the reflection sentences; the group holds the listener question. Tool: None. By hand: strips, the retry, sorting. Evidence: E151; the P1 retry marks; the heard column and SL.2.3 and SL.2.4 notes; the cycle 25 revision; the oral card, children 13 to 18.

## Day 152


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** The commonest E151 wrong answer (P or W): replay the question, answer again.
- **35 min. P1 and rehearsal.** P1 first, before any poem work: children 16 to 30 come to the teacher's table in three groups of five, six minutes each (18 minutes inside the 35), the same four asks one at a time. The 15 others rehearse 06-a and 06-b in groups of five, reading a line each in a steady beat and noting the rhyme. After P1, the second set rehearse 06-a and 06-b with the others.
- **10 min. Sing and read.** The class reads or sings 06-b with a steady beat; a partner taps the repeated line.
- **5 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "How does the repeated line help the song?"
- **5 min. Exit.** E152.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): P1 and the rehearsal ask what the sounds add, as E152 does. Use a different kind of task: tap the beat of a line, and sort sound cards into rhyme, repeated sound and repeated line.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before, at most 5 minutes.
- **15 min. A fresh decodable passage.** Not the reader; read, then meaning confirmed (RF.2.4c).
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (19 to 24) read the cycle 26 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **15 min. Dictation.** Words and a sentence with -ous and -us words, then compared with the pattern.
- **8 min. Dictionary.** Each pair finds one cycle word in the printed beginning dictionary and checks its spelling (L.2.2e).
- **5 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: the group holds the four-finger count. Tool: None. By hand: rehearsal, dictation, the dictionary. Evidence: E152; P1 notes for children 16 to 30; the cycle 25 score (written and oral, after class); the oral card, children 19 to 24.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: beat, rhyme, repeat, listener, reflect. Frames: "The ___ helps because ___." "I changed ___ so ___." "The listener asked ___." A child may describe an effect in the home language first and then in English.
- **Access.** Enlarge print; tap the beat on the table; accept a gesture or a drawing with an explanation; the exit strips are read aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Extra time at the table; a question repeated; a prompt "What does that do for the poem?" is recorded as help.
- **Reading load.** Poem P is four short lines and is read aloud; the exit cards are read aloud; reflection sentences are two.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say a fifth effect, such as a quiet last line, or answer two listener questions in whole sentences.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read a four-line poem aloud and ask the four asks one at a time; on 151 say two sentences about the packet and answer a question from the grown-up in a whole sentence; on 152 explain the poem 06-a. The checks are the same, one child at a time.
- **One projector.** Show the packet pieces and the reflection strip; keep poem P off the screen on 150 and 152.
