# Section 3.9: The bee-view story, the poster and the talk plan


## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


Days 104 to 108 start the unit's performance task: a poster and a talk (days 113 to 115) and a bee-view story (days 117 and 118). Day 104 is the one day that teaches (telling a story or a visit with facts and detail, SL.2.4); 105, 106 and 108 apply; 107 is fluency. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Children read at most 150 printed words a day; the story 02 is read aloud by the teacher (about 510 words), and the child page holds picture cards and short labels. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: bees, a girl on a bench, pollen, flowers; no sting, injury, death or illness. The bee's thoughts are imagined and the class says so.

- **SL.2.4** (104), "Tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking audibly in coherent sentences." **SL.2.6** (104, 106, 107), "Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification."
- **W.2.7, W.2.8** (105, 108): shared research and gathering facts from provided sources; the poster is built from 01 with the child's own notes.
- **L.2.6** (108), "Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading and being read to, and responding to texts, including using adjectives and adverbs to describe."
- **L.2.2d, L.2.2e, RF.2.3c to 3f, RF.2.4a to 4c** (every day): cycle 18 (-ible, -able) from 103 to 107, with its written check on 107; cycle 19 opens on 108 with au and aw review, and from 108 each oral turn also carries **RF.2.3a** (distinguish long and short vowels) and **RF.2.3b** (additional vowel teams).
- **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3**: the talk routine (turn, pair, link, clarify) on every day.
- **Limits that bite here.** The poster is scored on 113 (facts), not in this section: made 105, written feedback handed back 107, revised 108, scored 113 to 115. The cycle 18 check words (washable, bendable) and their passage and entry are reserved. The unit plan's exit cards for 104 to 108 are replaced here by new cases, so no exit repeats a taught case; the originals are not used in teaching.
- **Connections.** Unit 1 and 2 talk and revision; section 3.8, the typed sentence from 102 (revised 105); section 3.10 (the talk, the poems, the story plan).
- **The teacher page must explain.** The story is told by an imagined bee, so what the bee thinks is invented and what it does is checked against the articles (the pollen on its legs, the nectar). A good telling keeps order, adds a detail from the body ("pollen on my legs"), and uses complete sentences.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Items owned:** none of the unit's scored items (the poster and talk are scored in 3.11 and the story in 3.12). The cycle 18 written check is held on 107 (spelling 5, passage 5, reference correction 5); feedback after 107, correction 108, score 109.

**Cycle 18 check card (day 107 only, invented for practice, ours).** Dictation, two words and a sentence: "washable, bendable. This washable bag is bendable." Passage: "The bag bends to fit the box. We can wash it after the trip. Why can we clean it?" Key: it can be washed. Reference correction: draft "washabel"; entry "washable: able to be washed"; key washable. Wrong: washabel kept (Y); the letters of the ending written out of order, "bendabel" (AN); a passage answer that contradicts the words, "it breaks" (C); a neighbour's spelling copied (J). The unit's question "Why is it washable?" gives the spelling away, so it is changed here to the question above. Data note: the end items of the first data job use edible, terrible and stackable on day 107; they overrule the words printed here.

**Retrieval, day 104.** e1-describe-present: recount a familiar fossil discovery in ordered complete sentences with a drawing (4 minutes, pairs). A miss: the teacher models the missing detail in the day's story work.

**Exit questions** (each a new case, read aloud, said to be invented; the rotating six answer aloud, the rest tell a partner).
- **E104.** This card is invented for practice. Read aloud: "A moth rests on a pale flower at night. Pollen sticks to its body. Then it flies to a pink flower." Say: "Tell this visit as the moth, with I, in two sentences that keep the order." Key: Two complete sentences in order, as the moth, with the pollen on the body. Wrong: I fly to a pink flower. Pollen sticks to my body (B); On the pink flower (O); I went to a flower (Z).
- **E105.** "Source A: A butterfly sips nectar with a long tongue. Source B: Pollen sticks to its body when it lands." Two poster lines are shown: "Butterflies sleep at night." "Pollen sticks to its body." Two asks. Ask 1: "Point to the line the sources support." Key: the second. Wrong: the first, a fact in neither source (C). Ask 2: with the labels "night" and "body": "Point to the label that goes with that line." Key: body. Wrong: night (C).
- **E106.** This card is invented for practice. Show the plan words: "moth" and "pollen sticks to its body". Say: "Say one whole sentence a listener could understand, using the plan words." Key: One whole sentence with both plan words that is true, such as The moth has pollen stuck to its body. Wrong: Pollen sticks to body (O); A moth eats the pollen (C).
- **E107.** Printed line: "A dusty bug rests on the plant." Ask: "Read this line to me so a listener can follow." Key: accurate words and a pause at the full stop. Wrong: dusty read from the first letters, "dusk" (S); the vowel of rests misread, "roots" (T).
- **E108.** This card is invented for practice. Show the poster line: "The bee takes dust." Show Source A: "Pollen is the yellow powder on a flower." Show Source B: "Hairs on a bee carry it to the next flower." Say: "Say the line again with one word from Source A and one word from Source B." Key: A true line that uses pollen (or yellow, powder) from Source A and hairs, carry or next flower from Source B. Wrong: The bee takes dust (I); The bee eats the pollen (C).

## 6. Days

## Day 104


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **4 min. Retrieval (e1-describe-present).** Pairs recount a familiar fossil discovery in ordered complete sentences with a quick drawing; the teacher notes one miss.
- **8 min. Read aloud: One Bee, Two Flowers (02).** The teacher reads the whole story; children join the repeated lines. The class says aloud which parts are the bee's invented thoughts.
- **12 min. Check against the articles.** Pairs sort six strips from the story into "true of real bees (01)" and "imagined", with the article in hand; share and link.
- **10 min. Pairs retell.** Three picture cards of a new invented visit (a fly on a daisy, then a yellow flower; not the moth of E104); pairs lay the shuffled cards in order and say first, then, last; the partner checks the order with the cards (15 pairs at once, the teacher circulates).
- **8 min. Talk routine.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "What does the bee notice with its body?"
- **10 min. Sentence strips.** Each child writes or says one sentence with a detail, draws it and adds it to the class story line.
- **5 min. Exit.** E104.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The picture-card retell is changed to laying shuffled cards in order and saying first, then, last (sort); the exit asks for a two-sentence ordered telling.
- **3 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of the day 103 exit or the cycle 17 check.
- **15 min. Reread and spell.** Reread the cycle 18 reader for accuracy; spell five -ible or -able words and one sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; round 1: six at the teacher table, 2 minutes each (group 2 of five).
- **3 min. Close.** "Which end of the word says able to be?"

Who holds what: pairs hold the order cards; the teacher holds the record and the heard column. Tool: None. By hand: telling, sorting, drawing, spelling. Evidence: retrieval; E104; the oral card.

## Day 105

- **30 min. Stations.** Poster building by hand: a fact, a label, a drawing (nothing is traced). In parallel the shared keyboard: 15 pairs, 2 minutes each, revise the typed sentence from the written feedback. Each child gives the poster about 28 minutes.
- **12 min. Share at tables.** Pairs show the poster; the partner asks one clarifying question (turn, pair, link, clarify).
- **5 min. Exit.** E105.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** Stations have each child write a fact, a label and a drawing on the poster; the exit points to the line the sources support and its label (judge a given answer).
- **5 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Warm-up review: no repair is routed here (handbacks.csv routes none from day 104).
- **15 min. Reread, sort and use the pattern.** Reread the reader; sort ten words by -ible and -able; use one in a sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; group 3 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which of -ible or -able did you hear last?"

Who holds what: each child holds the poster and the note card; pairs hold the typed sentence. Tool: shared word processor, 30 minutes, 15 pairs. By hand: poster facts and labels, speaking. Evidence: the poster (made, kept for feedback); the typed revision; E105; the oral card.

## Day 106

- **5 min. Exit.** E106.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.

- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Warm-up review: no repair is routed here (handbacks.csv routes none from day 105).

Who holds what: each child holds the plan card; the teacher holds the heard column. Tool: None. By hand: planning, speaking, listening. Evidence: the plan card; E106; the oral card.

- **5 min. Exit.** E108.

- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of the cycle 18 check, beside the right word.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: visit, notice, label, detail, source. Frames: "I see ___." "The source says ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may plan in the home language and say the sentences in English.
- **Access.** Pointing to a card is an answer; a scribe for writing; large print; a child who cannot speak aloud may show the order cards.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud after a first independent try (recorded as help); extra time inside the same part.
- **Reading load.** The poster labels and the plan card only; the story is read aloud; no day asks for more than 150 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second detail that answers "what did the visitor feel?" in the bee's imagined voice and say it is imagined.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the story aloud and ask the child to tell back "what the bee did, in order". Build the poster from two articles at a table. Rehearse the three sentences while you ask for one more detail.
- **One projector.** Show the story pages once; the poster examples go up one at a time.
