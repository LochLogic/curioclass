## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole


The following five complete cold selections are original Curio Class texts in the 2026 plan edition. Print "invented for practice" beside each. **01-check11, The Class Sign.** After lunch on Monday, Mira's new class made a sign for its book corner. Mira stayed by the table while the others chose paint. "I can print the small letters," she said. The paper curled as she began. Mira stopped and waited. Then she pressed tape on each corner. Mira finished the words. She moved her chair so Leo could paint the border. They carried the sign to the book corner together.

Picture specification: Mira prints at a table with a long sheet of paper. Its far end curls up and tape rolls lie beside her. Leo paints at the side. The book corner is behind them. The image adds that the sheet is long and curls at the far end. Neither the scene nor this picture is taught before day 11.

**02-d, School Reading Partners.** At Bridge School, pupils read with partners each Tuesday after lunch. A partner is someone who works with another person. The teacher pairs pupils at a low shelf. Each pair chooses a book. They read in turns so both can follow the print. If a word is hard, they look at its letters and blend the sounds. Then they reread the sentence to check that it makes sense. At the end, each partner tells one thing the book explains.

**02-f, The Welcome Card.** At Cedar School, each new pupil receives a welcome card. One side shows a classroom map. The other side lists where to put a coat and where to find books. A class partner reads the card with the newcomer. Then both pupils walk the route shown on the map. The card stays in the pupil's desk so it can be checked again.

**02-e, The Return Shelf.** A return shelf holds books that pupils have finished reading. After reading, pupils put each book on this shelf with its cover facing out. This lets another reader see its title. A label hangs over the shelf. Each book has a title card tucked inside. If a card is missing, the pupil takes a blank card and writes the book's title. The cards help the class match each book to its name.

Diagram specification: reading mat at left, return shelf at right, an arrow from mat to shelf labeled "after reading". Show three covers facing the reader, the label above and blank cards beside the shelf. The arrow clarifies movement and order rather than adding a new factual answer.

**01-check31, The Paper Tower.** Nia's group set paper cups on the floor to build a tower. Their first tower fell when the top cup tipped. Nia looked at the bottom row. "These cups are too close together," she said. She spread the bottom cups farther apart and started again. Ben passed her one cup at a time. This tower stood while they counted all its cups. Nia drew the wide bottom row in her notebook so the group could remember what had changed.

Picture specification: two panels show the same group. In the first, a tower has fallen and two bottom cups touch. In the second, the tower stands on three bottom cups with a gap about one cup wide between each. This is a reading case, not a construction activity or a claim that every tower is steadier with wider spacing.
