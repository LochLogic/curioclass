## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.2.1, days 138 to 142:** "Write opinion pieces in which they introduce the topic or book they are writing about, state an opinion, supply reasons that support the opinion, use linking words (e.g., because, and, also) to connect opinion and reasons, and provide a concluding statement or section." Day 138 rereads a model opinion and names its five parts again (topic, opinion, reasons, links, ending); days 139 to 141 draft one piece in three sittings; day 142 revises it after written feedback and retries a missing part alone.
- **L.2.5b and L.2.6, day 138:** "Distinguish shades of meaning among closely related verbs ... and closely related adjectives"; "Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading and being read to, and responding to texts, including using adjectives and adverbs to describe." Exact verbs were met on the word ladder of unit 3 (review); the new work is choosing between near words for the picture in the head.
- **W.2.5, day 141:** "With guidance and support from adults and peers, focus on a topic and strengthen writing as needed by revising and editing." Revising changes meaning (140 to 142); editing fixes conventions in a separate pass (141, 142).
- **Limits that bite here.** Check days collect and give no help, no frame and no hint; the paragraph-frame was withdrawn after 136. The piece is about a class pot or school bed, never a home yard. Cards M and N are the only sources on 139 to 141 and carry the facts; a child writes no fact that is not on them. Written feedback is given after class, one line to a child.
- **Connections.** RI.2.8 (reasons that support a point), days 139 to 141, in the reasons drawn from M and N; L.2.2a to c (capital for a name, end mark, apostrophe), day 141 editing; SL.2.1a to c and SL.2.3, days 138 and 142.
- **The teacher page must explain.** That a precise word is chosen for what it lets a reader see, not for sounding grand; the difference between revising (a new or better idea) and editing (capitals, marks, spelling); that a link word joins a reason to the opinion; and why a check day gives no help.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned** (the pieces are collected, not scored on the day; cards M and N were first read on day 137):
- **W1, days 139, 140 and 141, W.2.1.** One piece in three sittings: 20 topic minutes (introduction and opinion), 30 (reasons with links, after written feedback on the first part), 30 (conclusion, and earlier reasons revised after feedback). Cards M and N (see the unit plan) are the only sources. Five parts, each marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen: a topic named (H), an opinion stated (I), relevant reasons (J), linking words (K), a conclusion (L). Model answer: "Our garden should include native plants because they fit our climate. Also, night blooms can feed night visitors. Let us include both." Scored after revision on 142 (the conclusion only after that revision).
- **W2, day 141, W.2.5.** 10 more topic minutes: a separate editing pass on the same piece (a capital for a name, an end mark, a spelling from cycles 20 to 24) using adult and peer notes. Keep the draft before and after. Copying the draft neatly is M; a flagged convention left unchanged is N.

**Record sheet.** One row a child: E138 and E142; the W1 sittings (three cells) and W2 (focus, content revision, editing); the heard column names the six asked to read one sentence aloud and say why it fits; the foundations column holds the cycle 23 written check (138), the cycle 23 oral card (children 25 to 30 on 138) and the cycle 24 oral card (1 to 6 on 139, 7 to 12 on 140, 13 to 18 on 141, 19 to 24 on 142; 25 to 30 on 143 in section 4.7).

**Exit questions** (cards invented for practice, said so aloud). If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E138.** Card: "Invented for practice. A: Pat set the full tub on the shelf. B: Pat tossed the full tub on the shelf." Two sentence strips, no pictures. Paper: "Mark the sentence for a careful helper carrying a tub full of water." Key: A. Spoken (E138b): "Say the word in that sentence that shows how the helper put the tub down." Key: set. Shown needs the sentence and the word; shown with help needs the word after the prompt "What does the helper do with the tub?" Wrong: B, or both marked as the same (X: near words treated as the same).
- **E142.** Card: "Invented for practice. Draft: Our pot should have flowers. Flowers can offer food." Two draft strips (one with a link, one without). Paper: "Mark the strip that links the reason to the opinion." Key: strip 2. Spoken (E142b): "Say one sentence that finishes the opinion." Key: "Let us make room for flowers in our pot." Wrong: strip 1 (K); the reason said again with no ending (L); a reason not on the card, such as "because pink is nice" (J).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The claim-evidence-reasoning model (a claim and the reason from the text, joined by an arrow or a link word) is held by pairs from 139; the paragraph-frame stays withdrawn; main-idea-and-details returns on 138 for the model opinion. The talk routine (turn, pair, link, clarify) and the heard-six column stay with pairs. New and carried to 4.7: the word ladder of exact words, held by the teacher on 138 and by children from 139 on a wall card. The sound chart and word-part work of unit 3 return in the cycle work.
- **Key moment 1, days 138 and 142: a piece open in front of the class.** Ideal: the model written large with room to add a link and an ending. Spec: fits (paper, two colours of pen). **Key moment 2, days 140 to 142: a draft with a one-line note beside it.** Ideal: the child's page with the note stapled on. Spec: fits (paper). No digital tool is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** The model copied on the board; children draft on folded paper; one pen color for the draft and a second for the revision, so the change shows.

## 6. Days

## Day 138


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **10 min. Return and repair.** First ten minutes, from the day 137 marks. A fresh invented case (a rule about bikes in the hall, with two points and two reasons): 4 minutes whole class on the commonest of F and G, then two 3-minute teacher turns (an F table and a G table) while the rest redo their answer from the written note. A right retry is marked shown with help.
- **15 min. Reread a model opinion.** The teacher reads the shared opinion from day 135 aloud (about 60 words, 1 minute), pairs read it by turns (6 minutes), then point to its parts with five colours: topic, opinion, reason, link word, ending (8 minutes). Words previewed: precise, shade.
- **10 min. Model exact words.** On the model the teacher swaps flat words for exact ones with two ladders: drink, sip, gulp (a bee at a flower) and cold, chilly, freezing (an early spring morning). Each word is acted. She says why one fits: choose the word that shows the picture in your head (L.2.5b). Describing words for a thing and for an action are review (unit 2).
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "Which word makes the picture clearer?" 30 children in pairs, 1 minute a turn, two rounds, the rest for the link and clarify steps.
- **10 min. Independent.** Each child rewrites two flat words in a three-sentence practice piece with exact ones from the word bank (words gathered from 05 and 06), adds one describing word (L.2.6) and circles the two; the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E138.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The ladder model and the independent rewrite ask the child to pick the near word that fits a described helper, as E138 does. Use a different kind of task: sort word cards onto a strong-to-weak line with no picture, or act each word and say which is faster, with no sentence pair.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before, at most 5 minutes.
- **15 min. Written check, cycle 23.** Every child writes four dictated words, then the sentence, then corrects the shown misspelling with the printed dictionary entry (5 minutes each); the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (25 to 30) read the cycle 23 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **15 min. A fresh response.** Each child writes or draws a sentence using a cycle word, then reads it to a partner.
- **13 min. Close.** The class reads the cycle 23 reader once more for ease.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the ladders and the parts of the model; pairs hold the model opinion. Tool: None. By hand: colours, rewriting, the written check. Evidence: E138; the retry on F or G; the cycle 23 written check; the oral card, children 25 to 30.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 138, "Which word makes the picture clearer?" Day 142, "What did the note ask you to change, and why?"
- **Written task, days 139 to 141.** The opinion piece on the garden question, in three sittings, revised on 142.
- **Judged by the teacher with five look-fors** for W1: (1) the topic is named; (2) an opinion is stated; (3) each reason is on the cards; (4) because, and or also joins each reason; (5) an ending completes the position. W2: the topic is kept, the meaning changes after the note, and the flagged convention is corrected in its own pass. Marked shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: opinion, reason, because, also, exact, tiny, sip. Frames: "I think ___ because ___." "Also, ___." "Let us ___." A child may say a reason in the home language first and have it scribed.
- **Access.** Enlarge print, read cards aloud, accept a scribe or a drawn picture with a labeled reason; the exit strips are described aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Extra time within the sitting; the card read again on request; a scribe for content, with the support recorded. Conventions are not scribed in the editing pass.
- **Reading load.** Children read the model opinion (about 60 words) and cards M and N (about 40 words each) with a partner; the exit cards are read aloud; the sheets have a few labels.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second reason that a source card supports and say why it fits; or choose an exact word for the ending and say what it shows.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** On 138 reread an opinion together, find its parts, and swap one flat word for an exact one. On 139 to 141 give the two cards, write an opening, then reasons, then an ending, one a day, with no help while the child writes. On 142 read the note together and make one change. The grown-up hears the cycle words aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the model with its five parts in five colours and the word ladders; the draft stays on paper.
