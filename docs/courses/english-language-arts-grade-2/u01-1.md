# Section 1.1: Entering the story

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-2/1/s/1.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-2 u01-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Children read short labels, the cycle reader and sentence cards; the teacher reads the story, the poem and every stem aloud. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.2.1: "Ask and answer such questions as who, what, where, when, why, and how to demonstrate understanding of key details in a text."** Day 2 models who and where; day 4 has each child write and answer a question with a chosen question word; day 5 applies why and how. Day 1 only retrieves. Answers cite a line or a picture detail; no question asks about a child's own school or home.
- **RL.2.3: "Describe how characters in a story respond to major events and challenges."** Day 1 introduces event and response with the doorway; day 3 adds the challenge and response boxes to the story-map; day 5 applies them to one why answer; day 6 teaches that a response can change.
- **RL.2.7, days 2 and 4: "Use information gained from the illustrations and words in a print or digital text to demonstrate understanding of its characters, setting, or plot."** Day 2: who is where; day 4: the setting from both sources.
- **Foundations.** RF.2.3a, "Distinguish long and short vowels when reading regularly spelled one-syllable words", is cycle 1 (days 1 to 5): closed syllables show short vowels, open and silent-e syllables show long ones. RF.2.3b, "Know spelling-sound correspondences for additional common vowel teams", opens on day 6 with ai and ay. L.2.2d, "Generalize learned spelling patterns when writing words", and RF.2.4 to RF.2.4c run daily through the cycle reader. L.2.2 is partial: d only here.
- **SL.2.1a to c and SL.2.3** run through one four-step talk routine (field 5), taught on day 1 and held by partners from day 3.
- **Limits that bite here.** The story is read aloud and is not decodable; children read the cycle reader and labels. Content in the story: a new school in winter, a child alone at lunch, a friend away with a cold, one hurt moment ("Not now", day 8); no death, injury, violence, religion or slur. The story never says why Mira moved, so no child is asked to say why they moved or where they live. Mira, Ada, Leo, Ms. Park, Mr. Davis and Maple Street School are invented; say so each time a chapter begins.
- **One word, one sense.** In the talk routine "turn" means face your partner; "take a turn" (grade 1 turn card) means have your go; the poem's "pages turn" means flip. Each is named once when it first comes up. "Response" means what a character says or does; a child's spoken reply is an "answer". "Share" in the poem is an act; Morning Share in the story is a class time.
- **Connections.** RL.2.10 and SL.2.2 are not taught here; the story reading is logged from day 10 (section 1.2).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A trait ("Mira is shy") is not a response; the response is what she says or does, and a feeling is inferred from it (error B). A picture names something only when the child says what it adds (error C). Closed, open and silent-e syllables are one system: the letters after the vowel decide whether it says its name. Words are read from the letters first, then checked against the picture (error P).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. The day 11 rows belong to section 1.2.

**Exit questions.** Each reading exit is one spoken ask on a part of the day's text that the day does not work through, reread aloud twice (named in each line); a talk or fluency exit stays on an invented card printed "invented for practice". Nothing in the question gives the answer, and a child cannot guess it from a choice. The day's rotating six answer aloud, about 20 seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means the answer is right and uses a word of the card; shown with help means it is right after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means a wrong answer or none. The cards and the answers are in items.csv (items.csv is truth where this list differs). If many miss one, the next day's opening repair shows its wrong example beside the right one.
- **E1** (day 1). Reread aloud twice the chapter 1 paragraph that begins "I wonder what it is like inside." Say: "Tell me what Mira wonders, in the order the story gives." Answer: She wonders what it is like inside, then who will sit next to her, then if the desks are big or small, and most of all if she will know what to do. Wrong: She wonders if she will know what to do, then what it is like inside (A); She wonders what is for lunch (A).
- **E2** (day 2). Reread aloud twice the chapter 2 lines that begin "On the desk was a small card." Say: "Where is the star on the card? Use the words of the story." Answer: Over the i in MIRA. Wrong: Over the M (A); On the window (A).
- **E3** (day 3). Reread aloud twice chapter 3 from "Ada thought for a moment" to "You can watch first." Say: "Tell me what Ada does when Mira says she will watch." Answer: Ada tells Mira it is easy, one thing each, and then says okay, Mira can watch first. Wrong: Ada is nice (B); Ada tells Ms. Park (A).
- **E4** (day 4). Reread aloud twice the first two paragraphs of chapter 3, the big blue chart and the sign. Show the chapter 3 picture. Say: "Tell me one thing the picture shows about the room that the words do not say." Answer: Any picture detail the two paragraphs leave out: the chart hangs right behind Mira and Ada's desk, other pairs lean in to talk, or Ada's hand is up as she talks. Wrong: There is a fish tank (A); It has a chart (C).
- **E5** (day 5). Reread aloud twice chapter 5 from "A boy with a striped hat" to "I remember." Say: "Tell me why Mira almost smiles." Answer: Leo says he is the one who did the thumbs-up on Monday, and Mira remembers him. Wrong: Because she won the card game (A); Because Ada sat with her (A).
- **E6** (day 6). Reread aloud twice chapter 6 from "Mira's hands started to fold" to "a warm desk." Say: "Tell me what Mira looks at that helps her change." Answer: The name card with the green star; it reminds her that somebody made it before she came and offered her a warm desk, so she stands up instead of watching. Wrong: She looks at Leo (A); Mira is brave (B).

**Cycle 1 written check** (day 5, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Four independent spellings; none is a day 1 to 4 word. (1) "Jo has a ripe ____." plum. (2) "Kim can ____ down the hill." slide. (3) "It is ____ late." so. (4) "Let us go ____ and sit." inside. Wrong: "plume", "slid", "sloide" and "insid" each show a long or short vowel misplaced (L) or a pattern not carried over (Q); tag the letter that fits the child's spelling. After the check is marked, dictate "stripe" and "napkin" as unscored practice.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The story-map (who, where and when, what happens; grade 1 review) is held by the teacher on days 1 to 3. On day 3 it gains two boxes, challenge and response; partners hold it from day 4, and it returns through day 32. The talk routine (turn: write or draw a detail; pair: share it; link: "I agree because" or "I would add"; clarify: "Which part do you mean?") is taught on day 1 and held by partners from day 3. The six question words card, the sound-spelling-map and the wait signal stay on the wall. The rotating six (the "heard" column) is the record for talk and for foundations.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles (spell the day's words), sort (card sort by pattern) and reread (partner reread of the reader or phrase cards), ten children each. The day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations. Fifteen pair tile sets; five in use at once.
- **Key moment 1, day 3: challenge and response on the story-map.** Ideal instrument: a large wall map with movable cards. Spec: fits; paper. Gaps and asks: none.
- **Key moment 2, day 6: before and after strip.** Ideal instrument: a two-box paper strip with picture cards. Spec: fits.
- **A room with nothing.** The story-map is a sheet folded in thirds; pictures are drawn by the teacher on scrap paper; tiles are letters on torn card.

## 6. Days

## Day 1


- **8 min. Open the school question.** Put the compelling question on the wall. Each child draws one thing a school has (a room, a thing, a helper's job) and writes one label word; pairs show each other. Never home, family or a move.
- **5 min. Words before reading.** Enter, notice and wonder, each said and acted out once.
- **12 min. Read chapter 1, The Doorway.** Teacher reads aloud with the picture (about 330 words, pauses at the snowflake, at "One, two, three" and at the open door). Say aloud that the story is invented.
- **8 min. Retrieval.** Reread the Mr. Davis paragraph. Each child names its character, answers one detail card, says what the picture adds and links to a partner's answer. Unscored notes.
- **12 min. What the words show about entering.** Teach the event-response card: the event is the new door; the response is what Mira says and does (hand on, off, on; "One, two, three"; she pushes). Model once with the class, then pairs tell what Mira does first and next.
- **10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** Teach the four steps on the question "What does Mira do before she enters?" Rehearse the wait signal.
- **5 min. Exit.** E1.
- **10 min. Retrieval (foundations).** Whole class, pair sets of sound counters: say ship, change the first sound to /ch/, move three counters, write chip; then read cap and cape and write cape. Record long and short recognition and spelling separately as unscored notes.
- **5 min. Sound sort.** The teacher says eight words (nest, bone, we, jump, tide, no, stop, tune); children hold up the short card or the long card.
- **10 min. Words rule.** Twelve printed words in three columns: closed (jump, stop, chin), silent-e (bone, smile, mule), open (go, she, no). Children find what the letters after the vowel do. Model smile: map /s/ /m/ /ī/ /l/, the e is silent, blend, spell with tiles.
- **32 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (field 5): round 1, 12 minutes, six at the teacher table for the oral card (grade 1 words only); rounds 2 and 3, 10 minutes each.
- **3 min. Close.** "What helps me read a word with e at the end?"

Who holds what is carried: the teacher. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, tiles, counters. Evidence: E1; the retrieval notes.

## Day 2


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E1 (A), show the wrong order beside the right and say the order together; otherwise retrieve the four talk steps.
- **4 min. Words.** Welcome, partner and classroom, used in a sentence each.
- **12 min. Read chapter 2, Welcome.** About 300 words with the picture. Say aloud that the story is invented.
- **8 min. Model questions.** The teacher shows two cards (Who stood up when Ms. Park asked? Where is Ada's desk?), points to the answering line and answers "The words say ...".
- **10 min. Ask a partner.** Each child draws a who or where card, asks it, partner answers with the line; swap; clarify with "Which part do you mean?" Hear the six.
- **8 min. The poem.** Teacher reads Our School Day once; the class joins the two refrain lines on the second reading; pairs answer "When does the poem say the school day begins?"
- **8 min. Words and picture.** Pairs point to the chapter 2 picture for three read-aloud sentences (Ada's desk is by the window; the card says MIRA; Ms. Park holds the door), then say one thing the picture shows that the words do not (the snow outside).
- **5 min. Exit.** E2.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Model the day 1 retrieval misses with counters and the cap and cape pair.
- **15 min. Reader.** Build two-syllable words from halves (nap + kin, in + vite, cup + cake, sun + shine); teacher models the first reading of the cycle 1 reader A Note for Sam, then the class reads it in chorus.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which word did I read by its letters?"

Who holds what is carried: the teacher models the question; pairs hold the question cards. Tool: None. By hand: card drawing, pointing. Evidence: E2; heard column.

## Day 3


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E2 (A), reread the card aloud with a finger on who and where; otherwise retrieve the six question words.
- **3 min. Words.** Respond and belong; challenge is taught below.
- **12 min. Read chapter 3, The Big Blue Chart.** About 320 words with the picture. Say aloud that the story is invented.
- **12 min. Challenge and response.** Add two boxes to the story-map. Challenge: a hard thing that happens that a character has to deal with. Response: what the character says or does about it. Model with Mira at the turn and talk: the challenge is that she does not know how; her words are "I will just watch" and her hands fold.
- **13 min. Pairs fill the map.** Pairs draw and label the challenge and response boxes for the Quiet Signal part of the chapter, then say "The challenge is ___, so Mira ___." Turn, pair, link, clarify; hear the six.
- **10 min. The poem again.** Choral reread of Our School Day; partners link: "Which stanza tells what pupils do together at lunch? I agree because."
- **5 min. Exit.** E3.
- **5 min. Opening (foundations).** Hold up long and short cards for eight spoken words.
- **15 min. Reread and sort.** Reread A Note for Sam; sort twelve words into closed, open and silent-e; spell two sentences; find the one wrong word in a printed sentence and say how to fix it.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the story-map; pairs hold the talk routine. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, sorting. Evidence: E3; heard column.

## Day 4


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E3 (B), lay the event card beside two action cards; otherwise retrieve challenge and response.
- **12 min. Read chapter 4, The Long Hall.** About 290 words with the picture.
- **10 min. Setting from two sources.** Teach the two-column setting chart (words say, picture shows). Model on the long hall: the words give the shiny floor and the cubbies; the picture adds that Mira now looks up and not at her feet.
- **13 min. Fill the chart.** Pairs fill it for the schoolyard and the library (words say, picture shows); link and clarify.
- **12 min. My own question.** Each child writes a question with a question word they have not yet used (teacher keeps the tally), then answers it by pointing to words and picture; swap with a partner.
- **3 min. Vary the words.** Teacher counts which of the six question words were used and sets one for tomorrow.
- **5 min. Exit.** E4.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** If many struggled, model a matched pair with the e hidden and shown (L).
- **15 min. Fresh passage.** Read the pattern passage "Jess and Pete hike up a hill. Then Pete sits and rests. He spots a bell on a gate. Jess taps the bell. It has a fine tone." Confirm meaning after decoding (who? what?); dictation "We can sit and rest."
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the story-map and setting chart. Tool: None. By hand: writing a question, drawing. Evidence: E4; heard column.

## Day 5


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E4 (A), reread the paragraph and the illustration together and replace the setting; otherwise retrieve the setting chart.
- **12 min. Read chapter 5, The Card Game.** About 290 words with the picture. Say aloud that the story is invented.
- **5 min. Reread.** Reread the napkin paragraph for the lines that tell why Mira says no.
- **15 min. Why and how stations.** Pairs choose three of six question cards (Why does Mira say "I am fine"? How can you tell she wants to go? ...), answer aloud, point to the line or picture, and say "I know because."
- **8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "Why does Mira twist her napkin?" Hear the six.
- **10 min. Write one answer.** Each child writes "Mira ___ because ___" with a few words and a drawing; the teacher scribes the rest.
- **5 min. Exit.** E5.
- **15 min. Written check (foundations).** Cycle 1 written check (field 3), unscored.
- **5 min. Reader.** Reread A Note for Sam for expression.
- **25 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card for the whole cycle (12 minutes), the rest in stations.
- **15 min. Fresh response.** Day 1's six read the grade 1 words plus the cycle patterns to the teacher (12 minutes, 2 minutes each) while the others partner-read a pattern card and spell two dictated words; no new teaching.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the map and the question cards. Tool: None. By hand: writing, pointing. Evidence: E5; the written task; check notes.

## Day 6


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E5 (A), reread the exit card aloud and replace the action; otherwise retrieve challenge and response.
- **3 min. Words.** Hesitate and approach, acted out.
- **12 min. Read chapter 6, Ada's Empty Desk.** About 330 words with the picture. Say aloud that the story is invented.
- **10 min. A response can change.** Teach the before and after strip: in chapter 3 Mira says "I will just watch"; here, with Ada away, she stands and walks to Leo. Model the strip, then say "Her response changes when ___."
- **15 min. Sort and place.** Pairs sort six line cards from chapters 3 and 6 onto the strip, three each, and read the chosen pair aloud.
- **10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On the day's question "How does her response change after the next challenge?" Hear the six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E6.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Repair the commonest day 5 check error (L or Q).
- **15 min. Ai and ay.** Retrieve open and closed syllables; teach the long a team spellings: ai in the middle (rain, wait, paid, chain), ay at the end (day, play, stay, may). Model: map, blend, spell. Say the words aloud with the class; add ai and ay to the sound-spelling-map.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the before and after strip. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, placing cards. Evidence: E6; heard column.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 2, "Who welcomes Mira, and where?"; day 3, "How does Mira respond to the turn and talk?"; day 6, "How does her response change after the next challenge?"
- Written task, day 5: "Mira ___ because ___", a few written words and a drawing.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) names what happened in the chapter; (2) says what Mira did, not what she is like; (3) points to the line or picture that tells.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 2 to 6 open with one repair of at most 5 minutes, for the commonest exit miss; if none, a retrieval of the day before. The foundations opening repairs the day before's commonest spelling or reading miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence on each day, using only patterns taught on an earlier day. The record sheet has a heard column.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of the cycle reader by day 5, with accuracy first; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: enter, notice, welcome, partner, classroom, respond, challenge, belong, hesitate. Frames: "Mira ___ because ___." "The words say ___ and the picture shows ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print cards; the A and B pictures differ in color and shape. A child who cannot write the label draws and the teacher scribes.
- **Check accommodations.** Cards are read aloud; the spelling check can be answered with tiles.
- **Reading load.** A child reads the cycle reader (about 60 words) and labels; the story, the poem and every stem are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Explain a second picture detail and what it shows about Mira's response.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read one chapter aloud, then ask "What does Mira do?" and "Where is she?" Let the child point. Do the event and response card on paper, and spell three words with letter cards.
- **One projector.** Show the picture beside the lines; mark on printed cards.
