- **RL.2.1: "Ask and answer such questions as who, what, where, when, why, and how to demonstrate understanding of key details in a text."** Day 2 models who and where; day 4 has each child write and answer a question with a chosen question word; day 5 applies why and how. Day 1 only retrieves. Answers cite a line or a picture detail; no question asks about a child's own school or home.
- **RL.2.3: "Describe how characters in a story respond to major events and challenges."** Day 1 introduces event and response with the doorway; day 3 adds the challenge and response boxes to the story-map; day 5 applies them to one why answer; day 6 teaches that a response can change.
- **RL.2.7, days 2 and 4: "Use information gained from the illustrations and words in a print or digital text to demonstrate understanding of its characters, setting, or plot."** Day 2: who is where; day 4: the setting from both sources.
- **Foundations.** RF.2.3a, "Distinguish long and short vowels when reading regularly spelled one-syllable words", is cycle 1 (days 1 to 5): closed syllables show short vowels, open and silent-e syllables show long ones. RF.2.3b, "Know spelling-sound correspondences for additional common vowel teams", opens on day 6 with ai and ay. L.2.2d, "Generalize learned spelling patterns when writing words", and RF.2.4 to RF.2.4c run daily through the cycle reader. L.2.2 is partial: d only here.
- **SL.2.1a to c and SL.2.3** run through one four-step talk routine (field 5), taught on day 1 and held by partners from day 3.
- **Limits that bite here.** The story is read aloud and is not decodable; children read the cycle reader and labels. Content in the story: a new school in winter, a child alone at lunch, a friend away with a cold, one hurt moment ("Not now", day 8); no death, injury, violence, religion or slur. The story never says why Mira moved, so no child is asked to say why they moved or where they live. Mira, Ada, Leo, Ms. Park, Mr. Davis and Maple Street School are invented; say so each time a chapter begins.
- **One word, one sense.** In the talk routine "turn" means face your partner; "take a turn" (grade 1 turn card) means have your go; the poem's "pages turn" means flip. Each is named once when it first comes up. "Response" means what a character says or does; a child's spoken reply is an "answer". "Share" in the poem is an act; Morning Share in the story is a class time.
- **Connections.** RL.2.10 and SL.2.2 are not taught here; the story reading is logged from day 10 (section 1.2).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A trait ("Mira is shy") is not a response; the response is what she says or does, and a feeling is inferred from it (error B). A picture names something only when the child says what it adds (error C). Closed, open and silent-e syllables are one system: the letters after the vowel decide whether it says its name. Words are read from the letters first, then checked against the picture (error P).

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. The day 11 rows belong to section 1.2.

**Exit questions.** Each exit is one spoken ask on a new invented card, read aloud twice and printed "invented for practice". Nothing in the question gives the answer, and a child cannot guess it from a choice. The day's rotating six answer aloud, about 20 seconds each; the rest tell a partner. Shown means the answer is right and uses a word of the card; shown with help means it is right after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means a wrong answer or none. The cards and the answers are in items.csv (items.csv is truth where this list differs). If many miss one, the next day's opening repair shows its wrong example beside the right one.
- **E1** (day 1). Read aloud twice: "Leo sorts word cards into two piles. He says each word out loud. Then he writes one new word on a slip." Say: "Tell me what Leo does, in the order the card gives." Answer: Leo sorts word cards into two piles, then says each word out loud, then writes one new word on a slip. Wrong: He writes a word first, then sorts the cards (A).
- **E2** (day 2). Read aloud twice: "Ada waits by the art-room sink. Mira is still at her desk." Say: "Say who waits by the sink, using the words of the card." Answer: Ada waits by the art-room sink. Wrong: Mira waits by the sink (A).
- **E3** (day 3). Read aloud twice: "The window is stuck and Leo cannot push it open. He asks Mira to push with him." Say: "Tell me what Leo does about the stuck window." Answer: He asks Mira to push the window with him. Wrong: Leo is shy (B); Leo is a good friend (B).
- **E4** (day 4). Read aloud twice: "Leo steps into the reading tent. Its cloth roof hangs above him." Show the picture: a tent with a blue cloth roof covered in painted stars, a floor lamp, and two cushions. Say: "Tell me one thing the picture shows about the tent that the words do not say." Answer: Any picture detail the words leave out: the stars on the blue roof, the lamp or the cushions. Wrong: There is a tree outside (A); It is a tent (C).
- **E5** (day 5). Read aloud twice: "A truck is too loud for Mira to hear Ada. Mira asks Ada to move to a quiet bench so they can talk." Say: "Tell me why Mira asks Ada to move." Answer: The truck is too loud, so Mira cannot hear Ada; she asks Ada to move so they can talk. Wrong: She wants to dance (A); Because Ada is mean (A).
- **E6** (day 6). Read aloud twice: "Leo waits quietly when the line is full. Ada makes room. Now Leo steps in and tells his idea." Say: "Tell me what changes for Leo after Ada makes room." Answer: Leo goes from waiting quietly to stepping in and telling his idea. Wrong: He leaves the line and goes home (A).

**Cycle 1 written check** (day 5, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Four independent spellings; none is a day 1 to 4 word. (1) "Jo has a ripe ____." plum. (2) "Kim can ____ down the hill." slide. (3) "It is ____ late." so. (4) "Let us go ____ and sit." inside. Wrong: "plume", "slid", "sloide" and "insid" each show a long or short vowel misplaced (L) or a pattern not carried over (Q); tag the letter that fits the child's spelling. After the check is marked, dictate "stripe" and "napkin" as unscored practice.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 2, "Who welcomes Mira, and where?"; day 3, "How does Mira respond to the turn and talk?"; day 6, "How does her response change after the next challenge?"
- Written task, day 5: "Mira ___ because ___", a few written words and a drawing.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) names what happened in the chapter; (2) says what Mira did, not what she is like; (3) points to the line or picture that tells.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 2 to 6 open with one repair of at most 5 minutes, for the commonest exit miss; if none, a retrieval of the day before. The foundations opening repairs the day before's commonest spelling or reading miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence on each day, using only patterns taught on an earlier day. The record sheet has a heard column.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of the cycle reader by day 5, with accuracy first; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: enter, notice, welcome, partner, classroom, respond, challenge, belong, hesitate. Frames: "Mira ___ because ___." "The words say ___ and the picture shows ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print cards; the A and B pictures differ in colour and shape. A child who cannot write the label draws and the teacher scribes.
- **Check accommodations.** Cards are read aloud; the spelling check can be answered with tiles.
- **Reading load.** A child reads the cycle reader (about 60 words) and labels; the story, the poem and every stem are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Explain a second picture detail and what it shows about Mira's response.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read one chapter aloud, then ask "What does Mira do?" and "Where is she?" Let the child point. Do the event and response card on paper, and spell three words with letter cards.
- **One projector.** Show the picture beside the lines; mark on printed cards.
