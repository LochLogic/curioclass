# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 2

Course id ela-k12 (grade 2 year; slug english-language-arts-grade-2), grade 2. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text, in its order: schools and community, fossils, pollination, providing for pollinators. Each unit is one topic with its own set of texts; units 3 and 4 share one topic (pollinators) at two depths.
- **Days.** 160: 154 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 2, 2 after unit 4) and 2 respond days (one after each check). The time a day is about 120 minutes, five days a week, in two blocks: a topic block of about 60 minutes (reading, talk, writing and language on the unit's texts) and a foundations block of about 60 minutes (phonics, spelling, fluency, decodable and grade-level reading in small groups). Grade 2 shares the day with math, science and social studies, so the course assumes two hours, not the base text's three, and leaves out its Labs hour (source map). Why the total fits: the base text's four modules hold 143 topic lessons and its Skills Block 130 foundations lessons; 154 unit days give every topic lesson a day with 11 over for the end tasks and rereading, and the foundations block 24 days over for re-teaching and fluency (For the lead, item 1).
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 2 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Every grade 2 standard and lettered part is owned by one of the 30 rows and each row sits in one unit below; where a standard is split across rows (RF.2.3, L.2.1, L.2.2), the rows name the split. Check set: each unit plan compares the unit with the base text's unit assessments for its module and the published K to 2 review criteria lines on phonics, decodables and fluency; the Units table's "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects. The bar is the lens (docs/agents/lenses/ela.md) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md, with RUBRIC-ELA.md for its text, question and writing lines. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in order: higher court, backed sources, dignity and openness, grade and life experience.
- **Base text.** EL Education K-5 Language Arts, 2017 edition, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017, docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md): the four grade 2 modules and the 2017 K-2 Reading Foundations Skills Block, grade 2 (cycles 1 to 26). Every trade book is replaced by a Curio Class text written to the per-grade text spec; the short texts the base wrote itself are adapted with the credit line. The 2025 edition and the 2024 Skills Block (CC BY-NC) are never used. Day credit: "EL Education K-5 Language Arts Curriculum (2017), grade 2, module M, unit U, lesson L", and the Skills Block cycle and lesson for the foundations block.
- **Where a row sits.** A row sits in the unit that teaches it to the grade 2 standard and checks it, following the base text's unit assessments. Before that unit the class works the thread at the grade 1 level; after it, the skill returns as review on new texts. Foundations rows run in the Skills Block's pattern order and later units name them as continued.
- **Texts and the reading level.** By the end of the year students read grade 2 texts and the low end of the grades 2 to 3 band on their own; read-alouds sit above it. Each unit plan writes a text record (measure, what makes it hard, the task, why here), a question set and a word list. Each unit mixes stories, poems and informational texts. The year's long text read whole is unit 1's chapter-length Curio Class school story, read across two weeks.
- **Decodable texts.** Curio Class writes its own for each cycle's patterns, checked against the sound-spelling-map, alongside grade 2 texts. No guessing from the picture or the first letter.
- **Writing calendar.** Unit 1: informative (how a school works for its community), through the process. Unit 2: narrative (one well-elaborated event about finding a fossil), revised and edited as two steps. Unit 3: research from provided sources and a recorded spoken piece about a pollinator. Unit 4: opinion (the best way to help pollinators, with reasons, linking words and a conclusion), through the process.
- **Talk.** A named discussion protocol on the unit's text most days, at least two a week (e2-discussion).
- **Paper path and devices.** Every task has a paper route. No student accounts. W.2.6 and SL.2.5 are met on a teacher-held or shared classroom device (a class digital piece in unit 2, audio recordings made with the teacher in unit 3); the paper route alone does not complete them. No timer is shown to a child; the teacher holds any watch for fluency.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 1's schools around the world and unit 4's folktales from living cultures also need a qualified reader from or expert on each place and tradition; who, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 2 (2 days, mid-year).** One-to-one and small-group: a grade 2 text not seen before read aloud for accuracy and expression; vowel-team, irregular and prefixed and suffixed words read and spelled; a cold informational text read by the student with main-topic, paragraph-focus and text-feature questions; a short written response on how a character met a challenge. No timer shown; the teacher holds any watch.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Small-group re-teaching of what the check shows, ending with a short fresh task. Children still decoding below grade 2 get a named catch-up routine in unit 3's foundations block.
- **After unit 4 (2 days, end of year).** A text at the low end of the grades 2 to 3 band read aloud; two-syllable and grade 2 pattern words read and spelled; a cold pair of short texts on one topic with questions comparing their important points; an opinion paragraph about a familiar tale with reasons and a conclusion.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teaching of the gaps; the record goes on to the grade 3 teacher with what the respond day changed.
