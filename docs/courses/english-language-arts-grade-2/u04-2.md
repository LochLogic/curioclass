# Section 4.2: Tales told in many places

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.2.2, this section's share:** "Recount stories, including fables and folktales from diverse cultures, and determine their central message, lesson, or moral." Day 123 recounts the hummingbird tale (03) and what each animal does; day 124 recounts the lizard tale (04) and what each animal believes. The moral is worked out from events, as in section 4.1.
- **RL.2.6, this section's share:** "Acknowledge differences in the points of view of characters, including by speaking in a different voice for each character when reading dialogue aloud." Day 124 reads 04 in parts, one voice for each animal; day 125 applies it to a new pair of lines.
- **L.2.2d** and **L.2.2e**: cycle 20 closes on day 123 (y to ies or s, written check); cycle 21 opens on 124 and runs through 125 (schwa spelled a; de- and dis-). Words are checked in the printed beginning dictionary on day 125.
- **RF.2.3c to f, RF.2.4a to c, SL.2.1a to c and SL.2.3** are carried each day: two-syllable words with long vowels, the prefixes de- and dis-, the cycle's irregular words mapped, reading again for meaning, and the talk routine.
- **Limits that bite here.** Tale 03 has a forest fire told with animals only; no person is in it, no animal is hurt, nobody acts out fire, and no child is asked about fire, evacuation or home. Tale 04 is invented for practice and claims no culture; say so aloud on day 124. No mocking accents or costumes in either tale. The origin of 03 is unknown: say that plainly, never attach it to one people.
- **The teacher page must explain.** What the credit on 03 says and does not say (Maathai told it as a hummingbird doing her best; a film in Hatxa Kuĩ shared by a heritage group in Spain; no source confirmed a Quechua origin, so none is claimed); why 04 is invented (the Mexican tale in the unit plan could not be cleared); and how a point of view differs from what a character does.

## 3. Checks, written first

T4 key: Duck splashes, Heron asks her to stop, the pond runs low, Duck apologizes and carries water back; play can affect a shared place (A, B). This section's days rehearse what they sample: recount in order and a moral held up by events.

**Record sheet.** As in section 4.1: one row a child, each exit marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen; the heard column names the six asked "How do you know?"; the foundations column holds the cycle 20 written check (123) and the oral-record turns (cycle 20: children 24 to 30 on 123; cycle 21: children 1 to 6 on 124 and 7 to 12 on 125).

**Exit questions** (E123 rereads a passage of the unit's own texts; E124 and E125 are cases invented for practice, said so aloud; one ask each; "How do you know?" goes to the six).
- **E123.** Passage: the note "Where this fable comes from" after the second telling (its first two sentences), reread aloud twice; not the hummingbird credit the day works through. Reread aloud twice the note after the second telling, "Where this fable comes from." Show two picture cards: La Fontaine writing the fable in French; La Fontaine at the start of a long road beside a banner that reads The Beginning. Say: "Mark the picture that shows what the note tells us." Key: the picture of La Fontaine writing the fable in French. Wrong: the picture of La Fontaine at the start of the long road. Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same note. Say: "Say who told this telling, as the note says." Key: La Fontaine, a French poet, told it; he took his ideas from older fables credited to Aesop. Wrong: La Fontaine made up the fable first..
- **E124.** Card, invented for practice and said so aloud. Read aloud twice: "Owl says, The cave is safe. Mouse says, The cave is not safe. Mouse runs out." Show two picture cards: a cave with a smile sign and a small owl; a cave with a warning sign and a running mouse. Say: "Mark the picture that shows what Mouse believes about the cave." Key: the cave with the warning sign and the running mouse. Wrong: the cave with the smile sign (C). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Say: "Say what Mouse believes about the cave." Key: The cave is not safe. Wrong: The cave is safe (C).
- **E125.** Card, invented for practice and said so aloud. Read aloud twice: "Snail says, Let us wait for the rain. Beetle says, Let us move before the rain." Show two picture cards: a waiting snail; a moving beetle. Say: "Mark the picture that matches what Beetle wants." Key: the picture of the moving beetle. Wrong: the picture of the waiting snail (C). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Say: "Say what Snail wants." Key: To wait for the rain. Wrong: To move before the rain (C).

## 6. Days

## Day 123


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **10 min. Cultural introduction and credit.** The teacher shows where the tale 03 is told on a map, in the credit's own words: Maathai in Kenya told it as a hummingbird doing her best; a film in Hatxa Kuĩ, a language of the Amazon forest in Brazil, shared by a language-heritage group at a university in Spain. "Nobody has shown where it began." Children name the credit as part of the reading. No accents, no costumes, no guesses about a people.
- **12 min. Read 03.** The teacher reads it whole (3 minutes: 280 words); pairs read it again by turns, a paragraph each (6 minutes); the class rereads the hummingbird's lines together (3 minutes). Words previewed: flames, perched, scooped, part.
- **8 min. Model.** The teacher recounts it with the story-map and finds the event that holds up the message: the others help only after they see the hummingbird try. She shows how to say what the hummingbird does and what she says. Fire is a story event only.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "What do the other animals do after they watch the hummingbird?"
- **15 min. Pairs.** Each pair fills the story-map for 03 and puts a dot on the event that shows the message; the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E123.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The credit talk on the map asks who told the tale, as the exit does. Instead: sort three given sentences about a tale into "the book says" and "nobody knows".

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair** from cycle 20 notes and the commonest wrong answer on E122 (E), 3 minutes, then the commonest miss of the oral records of days 120 to 122 (AB), 2 minutes.
- **15 min. Written check, cycle 20.** Every child writes the cycle's plurals from the spoken words on the cycle 20 check sheet (taught patterns only), with the printed dictionary available for one word; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **14 min. Rotation.** Seven children (24 to 30) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the other twenty-three are at two stations of eleven and twelve (sort and reread).
- **15 min. A fresh response.** Each child writes or draws a sentence using a plural from the check, then reads it to a partner.
- **11 min. Close.** The class reads the cycle 20 reader once more for ease.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the story-map and the talk routine. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, writing, sorting. Evidence: E123; the written check; the oral card, children 24 to 30.

## Day 124


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest answer on E123 that claimed more than the source.
- **10 min. Cultural introduction.** The teacher says plainly that 04 is a story we made up, and that the tale first thought of for this lesson could not be cleared; a made-up tale can still teach how to read. Children hear that Curio Class wrote it and claim no people.
- **15 min. Read 04 in parts.** Six groups of five, each child one of Narrator, Lizard, Crow, Squirrel and Tortoise; the teacher reads the whole script first (3 minutes: 312 words), then groups read it once together (6 minutes) and swap parts for a second pass of the last page (6 minutes). Words previewed: mist, gathered, shiny, hollow.
- **8 min. Model: what each animal believes.** On the beliefs table the teacher fills two rows: Crow believes the sun is angry, so brings gifts; the Lizard goes to look. A point of view is what a character thinks and wants.
- **8 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "Which animal's idea does the Lizard test?"
- **9 min. Pairs.** Fill the other rows (Squirrel, Tortoise); then say which event shows the message; the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E124.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** Filling the beliefs-table rows is the exit's ask. Instead: hand out four quote strips and have the pair match each to the animal that says it, or label each strip with what that animal wants.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Retrieve.** Cycle 20 words and the plural rule, briefly.
- **15 min. Model, map, blend (cycle 21).** The schwa spelled a (ahead, around, alone), and the prefixes de- and dis- (decode, disagree, dislike). Tiles on the table.
- **15 min. First reading of the cycle 21 reader** (a pattern-matched replacement, unless rights clear); pairs echo-read.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (1 to 6) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the rest at stations.
- **13 min. Spell** words and a sentence from dictation; map the cycle's irregular words with no guessing from pictures.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the beliefs table. Tool: None. By hand: parts, table, spelling. Evidence: E124; the oral card, children 1 to 6.

## Day 125


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest answer on E124 (C or B).
- **8 min. Reread 04** silently by pairs, finding each animal's own line.
- **10 min. Rehearse in groups.** Six groups of five, the same parts as 124, each marking the line where each animal's view shows and how the voice changes (higher, slower, louder).
- **14 min. Perform.** Six groups each read the first eight lines (2 minutes each: 12 minutes), with 2 minutes of changeover; the class names one difference in voice after each group.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "How is Tortoise's voice different from Squirrel's?"
- **8 min. Pairs.** The beliefs table for the Snail and Beetle lines: say each line in a voice that shows what it wants.
- **5 min. Exit.** E125.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** Saying each line "in a voice that shows what it wants" repeats the exit's ask. Instead: a pair hears a given line in one voice and judges which animal it fits, then fixes the voice.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Retrieve** cycle 21 words.
- **12 min. Reread the cycle 21 reader** for accuracy, then smoothly (RF.2.4b).
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (7 to 12) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the rest at stations.
- **16 min. Spell** words and sentences with the schwa a and de-/dis-.
- **10 min. Dictionary.** Each pair finds one cycle word in the printed beginning dictionary, checks its spelling and says the meaning of the prefix (L.2.2e).
- **5 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: groups hold the parts; pairs hold the beliefs table. Tool: None. By hand: reading in voices, spelling, finding. Evidence: E125; the oral card, children 7 to 12.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 123, "What do the other animals do after they watch the hummingbird?" Day 124, "Which animal's idea does the Lizard test?" Day 125, "How is Tortoise's voice different from Squirrel's?"
- **Written task, day 125.** The beliefs table for the Snail and Beetle lines: a few words and a drawing for what each wants. Short writing, one task for the section.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: mist, gathered, believe, voice. Frames: "Crow thinks ___ because ___." "I read Squirrel's line ___ because ___." A child may say a belief in the home language first.
- **Access.** Enlarge print, read directions aloud, accept pointing or a scribe; the two exit pictures are described aloud for a child who cannot see them.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing or drawing for the exit; the reason may be said to a scribe; extra time within the part.
- **Reading load.** 03 (280 words) and 04 (312 words) are read with teacher support; the exits are read aloud; the cycle 21 reader and the check sheet are the only other print.
- **Extension, same standard.** Voice Crow's lines in two different ways and say which fits what Crow believes; or defend a second message for 04 from its events.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the tale together; ask the child to recount it and to say what each animal wants. On day 124 take two parts yourself and give the child two. The grown-up hears the cycle words aloud.
- **One projector.** Show 03 or 04 with the lines in colors by speaker; the class reads their part when its color appears.
