# Section 2.3: Facts, imagined thoughts and a closing that fits

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-2/2/s/2.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-2 u02-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Children read short labels, cards, one short passage at a time and the cycle reader; the teacher reads the account, the story and every stem aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.2.3**, "Write narratives in which they recount a well-elaborated event or short sequence of events, include details to describe actions, thoughts, and feelings, use temporal words to signal event order, and provide a sense of closure." Day 43 recounts one Anning discovery with actions and time words taken from the account, and keeps her facts apart from the thoughts and feelings of an invented narrator; day 45 models one elaborated event of the child's own with a closing that fits. Full drafting waits for section 2.6.
- **RL.2.10 and RI.2.10**, "read and comprehend literature... / informational texts... in the grades 2-3 text complexity band proficiently, with scaffolding as needed." Day 44 only: partners reread a story passage, a biography passage and a short poem with support; no new teaching.
- **RF.2.3, 2.3e, 2.3f; RF.2.4, 4a to 4c; L.2.2d, 2e** (days 43 to 45, cycle 8): ild, old, ind, ost; the words together, whole, hello, weird; is-contractions (where's, there's, it's, she's) read and spelled as whole words; reading the cycle reader. **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3**: the talk routine on days 43 to 45.
- Skills carried: e2-narrative, e2-spelling, e2-fluency, e2-tricky-spellings, e2-discussion.
- **Limits that bite.** The Anning account never says what Mary thought or felt, because no source tells us; any thought or feeling in a child's writing belongs to an invented narrator and is labeled so. Mary's family and her exclusion from the Geological Society are not dwelt on. A child reads no more than 150 printed words a day (day 44: the poem, one story page and one account section total 143). The year may be shortened: day 44 is the day that folds into day 43 (field 11).
- **Connections.** Section 2.1 supplied the story and the account; section 2.2 the story map. Section 2.4 opens on day 46 with the articles. The narrative pieces here feed section 2.6.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between a fact the account states and an idea a reader imagines; the invented narrator is never Mary and never says what she felt. Week 9 words (account, collect, compare, observe, support, describe, explain, detail) are taught as vocabulary in use; collect is not used as a root example (that is a day 57 item).

## 3. Checks, written first



**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 2.3 owns no end-of-unit row.

**Exit questions.** Each exit is two spoken items, each one ask, except as noted. Item (a): "Point to the picture that goes with the card I read." All children show A or B on a two-card hold-up. Item (b): the day's one ask, answered aloud by the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner. Shown means the right picture and an answer using a word from the card; shown with help means the right picture and an answer after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means the other picture or no answer. Cards are printed "invented for practice" and read aloud twice. None reuses a story page, a worked example or a check case.
- **E43** (day 43). Card: "Fact card: Anning collected fossils and sold her finds. Separate card from an invented reader: First I read about her collecting. Next I wondered how she noticed tiny details. At last I felt eager to look closely at a fossil picture." Ask: "Which feeling belongs to the invented reader and not to Anning?" Answer: Eager; it is the reader's feeling ("I felt eager"). The fact card only says she collected fossils and sold her finds.
- **E44** (day 44). Card: "Yusuf found a fine line. He stopped. Was it part of the shell? He checked the picture once more." Ask: "Read the card aloud for a listener." Answer: Reads accurately, pausing at each full stop and at the question mark, with a rising voice for the question; the second reading is smoother.
- **E45** (day 45). Card: "First I saw a tiny mark. I leaned close and wondered what it was. I felt puzzled. Finally my labelled drawing showed the mark clearly." Ask: "Write one ending sentence that fits what happened." Answer: An ending sentence that stays with the actions, thought and feeling, such as "Now I felt ready to show my drawing."

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The talk routine, the heard-six column and the link card are unit 1 routines. The fact and idea chart (two columns: "What the account says", "What a reader can imagine") is built on day 43 and held by the teacher; the event checklist (time word, action, thought, feeling, closing) is made on day 45 and held by partners; both return in sections 2.6 and 2.7. The word wall holds together, whole, hello, weird. The six question words card stays up.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles (spell the day's words), sort (card sort by pattern) and reread (partner reread of the reader or phrase cards), ten children each. The day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations.
- **Key moment 1, day 43: the fact and idea chart.** Ideal instrument: a large wall chart with movable sentence strips. Spec: fits; paper. Gaps and asks: none.
- **Key moment 2, day 45: the event checklist.** Ideal instrument: a five-box card with a pencil mark. Spec: fits.
- **Tools.** None. No account is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** The chart is two columns on folded paper; the account is read from a printed copy; tiles are letters on torn card.

## 6. Days

## Day 43


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E42 or the retry (B), reread the line, cover the choice and find the words; otherwise retrieve the story map.
- **10 min. Words.** Account, collect, compare, observe, support, describe, explain, detail: each said and used once in a sentence about Mary or the story (for example "An account tells what happened."); partners say one back.
- **10 min. Reread Big Finds.** Pairs read the 104-word Big Finds part and mark with a finger each action Mary did and each time word (1811, 1823, 1828, later).
- **10 min. Fact or imagined.** Two chart columns: "What the account says" and "What a reader can imagine". Model with the learning part: facts (her father showed her how to look, she looked closely at the stones) against an invented reader's "First I read about the stones. Next I wondered how small they were. At last I felt curious." Say aloud that the reader is made up and that no source tells what Mary felt.
- **12 min. Recount one discovery.** Each child writes in a few words two sentences from the account with a time word and an action ("In 1811 ___.", "Later ___."), then one sentence for the invented reader, starting "I" and labeled "my idea", with a thought or a feeling. The teacher scribes the rest.
- **8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "Which words are Mary's facts and which are our idea?" Hear the six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E43.
- **5 min. Retrieval (foundations).** Read oi, oy, ou, ow word cards from the cycle 7 check misses (Y); reread any irregular word missed.
- **15 min. Teach: ild, old, ind, ost.** Child, wild; gold, cold, hold; find, kind, mind; most, post: the vowel says its name before these chunks. Model child: map /ch/ /ild/, blend, spell with tiles. Together and whole are mapped irregular words; read where's, there's, it's and she's as whole words (not scored).
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 1 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which chunk makes the vowel say its name?"

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the chart; pairs hold the account section. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, writing a few words, tiles. Evidence: E43; the written sentences; the heard column.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 43, "Which words are Mary's facts and which are our idea?"; day 44, "Which reading helped you understand the most?"; day 45, "Does the ending fit the event?"
- Written task, day 45: an event with a time word, an action, a thought, a feeling and a closing, in a few words. Day 43 also collects two fact sentences and one labeled idea.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) uses a time word and an action; (2) a thought and a feeling are present and labeled as an idea where about Mary; (3) the ending fits the last event.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 44 and 45 open with one repair of at most 5 minutes, for the commonest exit miss; day 43 takes E42 and the cycle 7 check. The foundations opening repairs the day before's commonest spelling or reading miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence each day, using only patterns taught on an earlier day. The record sheet has a heard column.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of the cycle 8 reader; pauses at full stops and question marks; no timer.
- **Story routines.** The story map returns on day 45 (the closing of Amara's story); the question words card on day 43.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: account, collect, compare, observe, support, describe, explain, detail, fact, idea. Frames: "The account says ___." "A reader can imagine ___." "First ___, then ___, at last ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print cards; the A and B pictures differ in color and shape. A child who cannot write the sentences draws and the teacher scribes.
- **Check accommodations.** Cards are read aloud twice; the spelling work can be answered with tiles.
- **Reading load.** A child reads at most 150 printed words a day (day 44: 143). The account and the story are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second event to the day 45 piece with a new time word and an action, and keep the closing fitting the last event.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the Big Finds part aloud and ask "What did Mary do?" Write two action words on cards with 1811 and later. Then the child tells one imagined sentence, "I wondered ___", and says whose idea it is. Spell three words with letter cards.
- **One projector.** Show the chart and the picture cards; mark on printed cards.
