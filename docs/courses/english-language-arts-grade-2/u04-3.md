- **Limits that bite here.** The poem and the song are read, chanted and sung by the class together; no solo is required and no child is made to sing alone. No tune is fixed; any steady four-beat tune will do. The unit plan names the song's effect (a steady beat like wings), but the children say it in their own words. Neither the poem nor the song asks a child to report on a home garden.
- **The teacher page must explain.** What a beat is (a clapped steady pulse); why a repeated line carries meaning (here, the request for room for wings); how rhyme and alliteration differ (end sounds against beginning sounds); and the poem and song are Curio Class originals with no source needed.

Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest answer on E127 (O).
- **8 min. Reread.** Pairs reread the ant's two requests in 01 and 02 aloud from the side-by-side print.
- **12 min. Compare.** Pairs sort six strips into only in 01, only in 02 and in both, then name one shared event and one difference; a sentence frame, "Both ___. But ___." is on the board.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "How does the ant sound different in the two tellings?"
- **20 min. Voices.** Six groups of five each read a short piece of the dialogue in the two tellings (2 minutes each: 12 minutes), changing voice for each speaker; after each group the class names one difference; the last 8 minutes pairs revise their chart.
- **5 min. Exit.** E128.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The compare step ("Both ___. But ___.") is the exit's ask. Instead: give a comparison that leaves out a difference, and have the pair find and mark what is missing.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.**
- **15 min. Written check, cycle 21.** Every child writes the cycle's words from the spoken words on the check sheet (taught patterns only), with the printed dictionary for one word; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (25 to 30) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the rest at stations.
- **15 min. A fresh response.** Each child writes or draws a sentence using a cycle word, then reads it to a partner.
- **13 min. Close.** The class reads the cycle 21 reader once more for ease.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the side-by-side print, the story-map and the beliefs table. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, reading in voices, writing. Evidence: E128; the written check; the oral card, children 25 to 30.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: beat, rhyme, repeat, bloom, voice. Frames: "The repeated line asks for ___." "The sound ___ is repeated, so ___." "In the second telling, the ant ___." A child may say the effect in the home language first.
- **Access.** Enlarge print, read directions aloud, accept clapping or pointing for the beat; the exit pictures are described aloud for a child who cannot see them.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing for the picture; the reason may be said to a scribe or drawn; extra time within the part; a quiet corner for the oral card.
- **Reading load.** 06-a and 06-b (192 words in all) are chanted and sung with teacher support; the exits are read aloud; the reader, the check sheet and the strips are the only other print.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second repeated line or a pair of rhyming words to the poem and say what it adds; or compare the poem and the song for what each repeats.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Clap the beat together, chant the repeated line, and ask what it asks for. On 127 read the song and find two rhyming words. On 128 read both tellings and find one thing the same and one different. The grown-up hears the cycle words aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the poem and the song with the repeated lines and rhyming words in two colors; the class claps along and the teacher marks as the class names.
