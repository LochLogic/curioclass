## 1. Standards and practices


Days 109 to 112 get the talk ready and open the story. Day 109 rehearses the poster talk and asks for detail; 110 is fluency (the poems, describing words, an unclear sentence made clearer by talk); 111 compares careful presentation talk with talk to a friend; 112 plans the bee-view story, shows the recorder and holds the cycle 19 check. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Children read at most 150 printed words a day: the poems (about 150 words in all) are read together on 110 and the cards of 111 are short lines; every stem is read aloud. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: bees, beetles, moths, flowers; no sting, injury, death or illness. No bee is touched.

- **SL.2.6** (109 to 111), "Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification." **SL.2.3** (109 to 112), "Ask and answer questions about what a speaker says in order to clarify comprehension, gather additional information, or deepen understanding of a topic or issue."
- **L.2.6** (110), "Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading and being read to, and responding to texts, including using adjectives and adverbs to describe."
- **L.2.3a** (111), "Compare formal and informal uses of English" (the unit's code L.2.3 is asked through this part).
- **SL.2.4** (112), "Tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking audibly in coherent sentences." **SL.2.5** (112), "Create audio recordings of stories or poems; add drawings or other visual displays to stories or recounts of experiences when appropriate to clarify ideas, thoughts, and feelings." **W.2.3** (112), the story plan: "Write narratives in which they recount a well-elaborated event or short sequence of events."
- **RF.2.3a, 3b, 3c, 3e, 3f; RF.2.4a to 4c; L.2.2d, L.2.2e; SL.2.1a to 1c** every day: cycle 19 (au and aw review), with the vowel card (RF.2.3a, b) and the irregular-word card (RF.2.3e, f) in each oral turn; the written check on 112.
- **Limits that bite here.** The unit's day 111 and 112 exit and comparison lines are reserved: the day 111 lines "I will explain how pollen travels" and "Here is what happened at the flower", and the day 112 pair "I will describe our next flower visit" and "Here is our next stop". ## 3. Checks, written first


**Items owned:** none scored here. The cycle 19 written check is held on 112 (spelling 5, passage 5, reference correction 5; feedback after 112, correction 113, score 114). The 112 register comparison is collected for feedback and scored on 118.

**Cycle 19 check card (day 112 only, invented for practice, ours).** Dictation: "lawn, haul. We haul a log across the lawn." Passage: "A dog licks its paw. Paul puts straw in the box for the dog. What does the dog lick?" Key: its paw. Reference correction: draft "laun"; entry "lawn: grass kept short"; key lawn. The unit's passage is changed here because it prints lawn and hauls, which would give the dictation spelling away. Wrong: laun (AO, a vowel team swapped for another with the same sound); the final letters out of order, "lwan" (AN); a passage answer that contradicts the words, "the box" (C); a neighbor's spelling copied (J); a picture guess from the first letter, "pup" for paw (S). Data note: the end items of the first data job use jaw, sauce, saucer and haunt on day 112; they overrule the words printed here.

**Exit questions** (each a new case, read aloud, said to be invented; the rotating six answer aloud, the rest tell a partner).
- **E109.** This card is invented for practice. A speaker says: "The beetle walks on the flower and the pollen sticks." Say: "Ask the speaker one question to get one more detail." Key: A question about the beetle, the flower or the pollen, such as Where does the pollen stick? Wrong: What is your favorite color? (M); I like beetles (L).
- **E110.** "The beetle came to the flower." Two asks. Ask 1: "Say it again with one word that tells how the beetle looks." Key: a describing word before beetle, such as "The shiny beetle came to the flower." Wrong: the sentence said again with no new word (Z). Ask 2: "Say it again with one word that tells how the beetle came." Key: a word with came, such as "The beetle came slowly to the flower." Wrong: the word put on the beetle, "The slowly beetle came." (R).
- **E111.** Two lines for the same idea, said to be invented: "Let me tell you about the moth's visit." and "The moth went there, you know." Two asks. Ask 1: "Which line would you use to open a talk for a visitor who has not seen your poster?" Key: the first. Wrong: the second (Z). Ask 2: "Say why that line suits the visitor." Key: a visitor who has not seen the poster needs to be told what the talk is about. Wrong: "The other is bad English." (X); "Because it is first." (Z).
- **E112.** Plan line: "Then the bee hung under the leaf while the rain tapped." Two drawings are described: a close view of the bee's feet gripping the leaf, a wide view of the whole hedge. Two asks. Ask 1: "Say which drawing would help a listener." Key: the close view. Wrong: the hedge view (V). Ask 2: "Say why." Key: it shows how the bee holds on. Wrong: "I like it." (E); a reason about how pretty the hedge is (V).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The revised poster and the plan card (3.9) stay with the child; the typed sentence and written feedback of 102 and 105 stay in the folder; the talk card, tally child and the chart of talk criteria made on 109 stay up. The sound-spelling map, word wall and the oral record with its heard column stay up.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Section 1.1, field 5. The oral card is heard for six children a day (groups 2, 3, 4 and 5 on 109 to 112); from 108 each turn carries the vowel card (30 seconds) and three irregular words.
- **The recorder (day 112, show why).** One teacher-held device, a recording corner at a side table. Demonstration to the class, 4 minutes: the teacher records one sentence, plays it back, and the class says what they can hear and what would make it clearer. Then each child records one planned sentence for 1 minute; changeovers are counted: 30 children at 1 minute each plus 5 minutes of changeovers is 35 minutes, the whole station window, so each child leaves the planning table for one minute only. Recordings stay in the class; a child may record in a pair or be heard live instead (the 112 exit is a plan, not a score). If the device fails, the class practices into a paper tube and the teacher listens live; the 116 and 117 recordings wait for a working device.
- **Key moment 1, day 109: the rehearsal.** Ideal: a listener who asks for one detail. Spec fits; paper and voice. Key moment 2, day 112: a recording the child can hear back. Ideal: the recorder; spec fits (key tool, show why).
- **Tools.** Audio recorder (key), 112 show why. A room with nothing: the practice is live to a partner and the audio is added when a device is found; recording standards stay incomplete until then.

## 6. Days

## Day 109


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **15 min. Build the criteria.** The class makes a three-line chart of a good talk (a voice the back row can hear, whole sentences, facts from the source), with the teacher drawing out each from a model good and a model weak talk, both invented. Looking toward the listener or the poster is allowed.
- **20 min. Rehearse in fives.** Six groups of five, simultaneous; each speaker 2 minutes plus 1 minute for one listener question (3 minutes a child, 15 minutes a group), then 5 minutes for each group to give one star and one next step against the chart. The teacher moves between groups and hears the day's rotating six.
- **8 min. Volunteers.** Three or four children give the first two sentences to the class; the class links or asks. Any child may pass.
- **10 min. Independent reading.** Child-read band texts 01-b to 01-e; teacher circulating.
- **5 min. Exit.** E109.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.
- **2 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of the cycle 18 check or of day 108.
- **15 min. Reader.** Read Dawn in the Garden (cycle 19) after the pattern model; map the odd words; spell five words and a sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; group 2 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which sound do au and aw share?"

Who holds what: each child holds the poster and plan card; the teacher holds the chart and the heard column. Tool: None. By hand: speaking, listening, spelling. Evidence: E109; the oral card.

## Day 110


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **18 min. Poems.** Choral reading of 03-b, A Dusty Visitor, then by line with volunteers; 03-a, Land, Cling, Carry, for its chorus and rhythm only. The class says what makes the picture clear (the describing words).
- **15 min. Describing words.** Sort word cards (fuzzy, yellow, round, small, sticky, bright, soft, quick) onto a picture of the visitor and onto its action; each child says a whole sentence with one describing word and one word for how it moves.
- **15 min. Make it clearer, by talk.** Pairs take three pairs of invented lines, one that tells more in each ("A fly went." and "A tiny fly went quickly to a daisy."), choose the line that tells more and say what the added words tell. Nothing is written.
- **5 min. Independent reading.** Child-read band texts.
- **5 min. Exit.** E110.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The describing-word sort and the three pairs of lines (choose the line that tells more) are sort and judge tasks; the exit asks the child to say one sentence again with one new word (a fresh prompt).
- **2 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of 109.
- **15 min. Reread and use the pattern.** Reread Dawn in the Garden for expression; sort ten au and aw words; spell four and write one sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; group 3 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Say an aw word you heard today."

Who holds what: each child holds a word card set; the teacher holds the heard column. Tool: None. By hand: choral reading, sorting, speaking. Evidence: the reading observation; E110; the oral card.

## Day 111


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **8 min. Two lines, one idea.** The teacher shows the invented pairs: "Let me tell you about the beetle's visit." and "The beetle went there, you know." Who is each said to? What does the listener need? No line is called bad.
- **12 min. Sort the cards.** Fix the friend lines: pairs take three invented friend lines ("Hey, look at this daisy!") and say each again for a visitor who has not seen the poster; the partner says what words were added.
- **15 min. Say it both ways.** Each child says a poster sentence one way for a visitor and one way for a friend, to a partner, who says which was which.
- **8 min. Link.** Whole class: what changed? (The visitor line says more.) Turn, pair, link, clarify.
- **10 min. Independent reading.** Child-read band texts.
- **5 min. Exit.** E111.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice now says three friend lines again for a visitor (fix); the exit chooses between two given lines and says why (judge).
- **2 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of 110.
- **15 min. Fresh passage and dictation.** A fresh cycle 19 passage of about 40 words; confirm meaning after decoding. Dictate two words and one sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; group 4 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which letters make the sound in saw?"

Who holds what: pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: sorting and speaking. Evidence: E111; the oral card.

## Day 112


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **8 min. The model.** Reread part of One Bee, Two Flowers (02): where the bee is, what it wants, what it does in order, what it notices with its body, how it ends.
- **4 min. Show why.** The teacher records one sentence and plays it back; the class says what can be heard.
- **35 min. Plan and record.** Each child fills a five-box plan page (where, wants, does, notices with the body, ending) with a word or drawing a box and sketches a drawing for the notices box and one other box. Children go one at a time to the recording corner for 1 minute to record one planned sentence (30 minutes plus 5 for changeovers).
- **5 min. Comparison collected.** Each child answers the fresh register question on paper: a prepared line and a partner line for the next flower visit are read aloud; the child says or writes how each suits its listener. Collected; feedback after class, revision on 113, score on 118.
- **5 min. Exit.** E112.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The plan page has each child sketch drawings for two boxes; the exit judges between two described drawings.
- **3 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of 111.
- **15 min. Cycle 19 written check.** Spelling 5, passage 5, reference correction 5, from the card in field 3.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; group 5 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Say the word you spelled with aw."

Who holds what: each child holds the plan page; the teacher holds the device and the heard column. Tool: audio recorder, 35 minutes, 30 children at 1 minute plus 5 minutes changeovers; by hand: planning, choosing a drawing, spelling. Evidence: the plan page; the comparison (collected); E112; the cycle 19 check; the oral card.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: rehearse, listener, detail, careful, friend, plan. Frames: "Where does it ___?" "I will tell you about ___." "I see ___ in my head." A child may rehearse in the home language first; the home language is never ranked.
- **Access.** Pointing to a card is an answer; a scribe; large print; a child who prefers not to speak aloud may rehearse to one listener and be heard live later; a child may record in a pair.
- **Check accommodations.** The check is read aloud after a first independent try (recorded as help); extra time inside the same part.
- **Reading load.** The poems (about 150 words) are read together on 110, never alone; cards of 111 are short lines read aloud; no day asks a child to read more than 150 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second detail from the senses to the plan and say what a listener should see.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Be the listener: ask "Where does it ___?" after each of the child's sentences, then swap. On 111 say a line to a friend and the same line to a visitor, and ask which was which. On 112 record one sentence on a phone or a recorder and play it back.
- **One projector.** Show the chart of talk criteria and the plan page once; the sort cards are on paper.
