## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.2.8, days 132 to 135 and 137:** "Describe how reasons support specific points the author makes in a text." Days 132 and 133 take 05-a and 05-b and draw an arrow from each reason to the point it supports; day 135 uses the same link to build an opinion; day 137 checks it on new articles.
- **RI.2.9, days 134 and 137:** "Compare and contrast the most important points presented by two texts on the same topic." Day 133 models the compare with 05-a and 05-b; day 134 applies it to 05-a and 05-c; day 137 checks it. The most important point, not a minor detail, is what is compared.
- **W.2.1, days 132, 135 and 136:** "Write opinion pieces in which they introduce the topic or book they are writing about, state an opinion, supply reasons that support the opinion, use linking words (e.g., because, and, also) to connect opinion and reasons, and provide a concluding statement or section." Day 135 builds a shared model, and day 136 drafts one with the paragraph frame fading; a child's own piece is in section 4.6.
- **L.2.2d and L.2.2e, days 132 to 136; SL.2.3, days 132 to 136:** cycle 22 closes on 133 (written check), cycle 23 (-ate) opens on 134; the dictionary is used on 132 and 137. Talk: ask and answer questions about what a speaker says.
- **Limits that bite here.** The articles were written by Curio Class from the sources on the text's own page; no child is asked about a home yard, a purchase, a spray or an insect. Children never use a spray or handle an insect, and planting means a class pot, a window box, a school bed or a park. The shallow-water, change-every-few-days and "less is more" points of 05-c rest on search summaries and must be confirmed before they are taught as fact (source to check). Neither the checks nor their cards are used before 137.
- **The teacher page must explain.** The difference between a point and a reason; that because, also and so join a reason to a point; what "most important point" means beside a minor detail; and why the compare uses points, not facts, from each article.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The main-idea-details and claim-evidence-reasoning models (new on 132 and 133: the point, its reasons and the arrow from each reason) are held by the teacher on 132 and 133, and by pairs from 134; the paragraph-frame is held by the teacher on 135 and withdrawn after 136. The talk routine and heard-six column stay with pairs. The sound-spelling-map, syllable-types and word-parts of unit 3 return in the cycle work.
- **Key moment 1, days 132 and 133: arrows from reasons to a point.** Ideal: the article printed once with space to draw the arrows. Spec: fits (paper, a pencil). Gaps and asks: none. **Key moment 2, days 135 and 136: the opinion strips.** Ideal: a strip each for topic, opinion, reasons and ending, laid in order. Spec: fits (paper strips). No digital tool is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** The article copied large on the board; children draw arrows on their own copy or on a folded paper; pairs share a copy.

## 6. Days

## Day 132


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Retrieval (practice only, no check evidence).** Pairs say one thing an author wants (a point) and one reason from a grade 1 text (e1-author-reasons, e1-opinion); the teacher notes one miss.
- **10 min. Read 05-a.** The teacher reads the unit question and the article aloud (2 minutes: about 250 words), pairs read it by turns (5 minutes), the class rereads the first and last paragraphs together (3 minutes). Words previewed: pollinator, nectar, bloom, clump.
- **10 min. Model.** On the printed article the teacher finds the point ("plant flowers that bloom in every season"), then draws an arrow from each reason to it: early bees need food; fall flowers give food before cold; a clump is easy to find. A reason tells why the point is good.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "Which reasons support the point?"
- **20 min. Pairs.** Each pair draws the arrows on their own copy, then says each reason with because; the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E132.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The pairs' arrow drawing and "say each reason with because" are the exit's ask. Instead: give an arrow already drawn to the wrong point, and have the pair judge and fix it.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.**
- **15 min. A fresh decodable passage** (not the reader), read and meaning confirmed.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (19 to 24) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the rest at stations.
- **15 min. Dictation** of words and a sentence, then compare with the pattern.
- **8 min. Dictionary.** Each pair finds one cycle word in the printed beginning dictionary and checks it (L.2.2e).
- **5 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the arrows and the point. Tool: None. By hand: arrows, dictation. Evidence: E132; the oral card, children 19 to 24.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: point, reason, because, also, habitat, spray. Frames: "My reason is ___ because ___." "The author wants ___." "Both articles want ___." A child may say the reason in the home language first.
- **Access.** Enlarge print, read directions aloud, accept pointing or a scribe for the arrows and chart; the exit pictures are described aloud for a child who cannot see them.
- **Check accommodations.** Extra time within the part; the reason may be said to a scribe; the card read aloud again on request. The answer is marked the same.
- **Reading load.** 05-a, 05-b and 05-c (about 230 to 250 words each) are read by the children with teacher support; the exits and cards M and N (about 30 words each) are read aloud; the sheets have a few labels.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second reason for the point in 05-a and say why it fits; or write a reason that does not fit and say why.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the article together; ask what the author wants (the point), then what makes the point a good idea (the reasons) and draw the arrows. On 134 read two articles and ask what both want. On 135 and 136 write an opinion with one reason. The grown-up hears the cycle words aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the article with the point and the reasons in two colors; the class says each reason aloud; the child's sheet is on paper.
