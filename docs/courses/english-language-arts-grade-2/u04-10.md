## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit items run here, not owned.** Day 153: T4 (RL.2.2), T5 (RL.2.6) and T6 (SL.2.6). Day 154: T7 (RL.2.9) and W3 scoring. Day 156: I3 (RI.2.9) and I4 (RI.2.8). Their keys are in unit plan field 5 and in sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.5, 4.7 and 4.9.
- **Time on 153 (30 minutes).** F2 is read aloud once to the whole class (30 seconds); a printed copy is on each group's table. Six groups of five, 5 minutes a group: each child recounts and connects the moral (T4, 3 minutes a group, 36 seconds a child), then reads Heron's request and Duck's answer in two voices and says what each wants (T5, 2 minutes a group, 24 seconds a child). A complete sentence is asked for the one question "Why does Heron ask Duck to splash less?" (T6, no extra time).
- **Time on 154 (30 minutes).** V3 and V4 are read aloud once; six groups of five at 5 minutes: each child gives one similarity and one difference (about 1 minute each). W3: 30 teacher minutes of scoring, one a child, while the class works independently (below).
- **Time on 156 (25 minutes).** One written page: I3 15 minutes (strips: "Both ___." "Q ___, but R ___."), I4 10 minutes (strips: "A clump helps because ___." "Caterpillars need ___ because ___."). No help. The six of the heard column read their strips aloud (1 minute each, inside the 25).
- **E155.** One spoken ask, heard from the six after the class hears the winter talk of the first ant telling (paragraphs 9 and 10) read twice in two voices with the speech tags left out: "Say how you could tell who was speaking." Key: the voice changed; the small, asking voice was the grasshopper and the sorry, firm voice was the ant. Wrong: "You read it louder" (no tie to a character); "Both were the ant" (C). **E157** is the closing exit and also the fresh opinion retry. Card: "Invented for practice. Choice: set a shallow water dish with stones by the school bed. A: Pollinators need a safe place to sip. B: The dish is round." Paper: "Mark the fact that helps the choice." Key: A. Spoken (E157b): "Say one sentence with an opinion, a link word and the fact that helps." Key: "We should set a shallow water dish because pollinators need a safe place to sip." Shown needs opinion, link and fact A; shown with help after "Which fact helps the pollinators?" Wrong: B as the reason (J); no link word (K); a fragment such as "Because they sip" (P).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The packet, the P1 notes, the heard column and the cycle 26 record all stay for the record sheet. Nothing is carried past 157 except the records.
- **Key moment 1, days 153 and 154: a group table with a printed card.** Ideal: a corner table with the teacher holding the card, a timer, and a record sheet. Spec: fits (paper; a kitchen timer or a classroom clock). **Key moment 2, day 156: strips for the written comparison.** Ideal: sentence strips and a pencil. Spec: fits. **Key moment 3, day 157: tables of 10-minute teacher turns.** Ideal: four tables with small whiteboards. Spec: fits (paper).

## 6. Days

## Day 153


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Briefing.** The teacher says what is checked (a recount, a moral, two voices, whole sentences), that the card is new and that there is no help in the group.
- **5 min. Read F2 aloud.** F2, Splash (about 60 words), is read aloud once to the whole class (30 seconds); the briefing tells children where each group's printed copy waits. The rest is quiet reading.
- **30 min. Group sittings (T4, T5, T6).** Six groups of five come to the teacher's table for 5 minutes each (6 x 5 = 30 minutes): recount in order and connect the moral to an event (3 minutes a group, 36 seconds a child), then read Heron's request and Duck's answer in two voices and say what each wants (2 minutes a group, 24 seconds a child); one request for detail is answered in a whole sentence. The other groups read a chosen tale from 01 to 04 or 06 alone.
- **15 min. Quiet reading.** Each child reads a tale or poem of their own choice; the teacher records the heard column.
- **5 min. Close.** Each child names one tale they enjoyed to a partner.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before, at most 5 minutes.
- **15 min. Written check, cycle 26.** Every child writes four dictated words, then the sentence, then corrects the shown misspelling with the printed dictionary entry (5 minutes each); the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (25 to 30) read the cycle 26 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modelled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **15 min. A fresh response.** Each child writes or draws a sentence using a cycle word, then reads it to a partner.
- **13 min. Close.** Pairs read the cycle 26 reader aloud once more for ease.

Who holds what is carried: the child holds the story-map; the teacher holds the card and record. Tool: None. By hand: the card at the table, quiet reading, the written check. Evidence: T4, T5 and T6 (individual marks, with the 150 and 152 P1 records retained); the cycle 26 written check; the oral card, children 25 to 30.

## Day 154


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Briefing.** The teacher says what is checked (a similarity and a difference between two versions) and that no help is given.
- **5 min. Read V3 and V4 aloud.** V3 and V4, Fox in the Well (about 35 words each), are read aloud once; a printed copy of both waits at each group's table.
- **30 min. Group sittings (T7).** Six groups of five come to the teacher's table for 5 minutes each (6 x 5 = 30 minutes): each child says one thing both versions share and one difference in the endings, with the words that show it (about 1 minute each). The other groups read quietly.
- **15 min. Quiet reading.** Each child reads a tale or article of their own choice; the teacher records the heard column.
- **5 min. Close.** Pairs say one thing that stays the same and one thing that changes between two stories they know.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error from day 153, at most 5 minutes.
- **30 min. Independent work and W3 scoring.** Pairs reread the cycle 26 reader and spell words with the printed dictionary at their desks, with no help; the teacher scores the W3 packets at one minute a child (30 minutes, 30 children) and records H to L separately.

Groups of ten by the latest recorded letter (R, S, Q, AB to AE): one group with the teacher (map, blend, read the matching decodable, spell), the other twenty at two stations, partner reread and tile spelling. Rotation continues so each group has the teacher once.
- **7 min. Close.** Pairs read one new cycle word sentence aloud.

Who holds what is carried: the packet is in its envelope. Tool: None. By hand: the card at the table, independent work. Evidence: T7 (individual marks); W3 (scored by H to L from the packet, owned by section 4.7); the heard column.

## Day 155


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** Set out the packets; the class agrees how to listen (gain the floor, link, ask).

- **20 min. Read for classmates.** Six groups of five choose a tale from 01 to 04 or the poem or song in 06 and read it in parts, a line or character each: preparation 8 minutes, then each group reads for the class for 2 minutes (6 x 2 = 12 minutes) (RL.2.10).
- **5 min. Close.** The class says one thing it noticed from another group.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **8 min. Revise the cycle 26 record.** Written check (153) and any remaining oral turn, corrected after the note; scored afterward on 156.
- **42 min. Flexible groups.** Three rounds of 14 minutes. Groups of ten by the latest recorded letter (R, S, Q, AB to AE): one group with the teacher (map, blend, read the matching decodable, spell, reread), the other twenty at two stations: partner reread of the cycle 26 reader, and tile spelling with the printed dictionary.

Who holds what is carried: the child holds the packet; the group holds the chosen tale. Tool: None. By hand: sharing, reading in parts, spelling. Evidence: no exit, no new assessed evidence; the P1 retry marks for children 16 to 30; the cycle 26 revision.

## Day 157


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** Each child sees their own written marks and notes; groups are formed by the largest recorded error counts (at most four tables).
- **40 min. Tables and retry.** Four tables of up to ten children each take a 10-minute teacher turn (4 x 10 = 40 minutes) on the error with the largest count (among A to G, O, AF, P, H to L, in that order of count): a wrong example beside a right one, on a new invented case (see field 4). While a table waits, children retry one fresh reading task in pairs: a new two-card pair (S and T, about 40 words each) with a similarity, a difference and a reason for one point.
- **10 min. Exit and opinion retry.** E157, the fresh opinion retry, each child says or writes one sentence; the six of the heard column read theirs aloud (1 minute each).
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The tables and the retry ask for an opinion with a link and a fact, as E157 does. Use a different kind of task: match each wrong sentence to its right pair, then read the right one aloud.
- **5 min. Close.** Each child tells a partner one thing they can now do. The unit ends.

- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error from day 156, at most 5 minutes.

Groups of ten by the latest recorded letter (R, S, Q, AB to AE): one group with the teacher (map, blend, read the matching decodable, spell, reread), the other twenty at two stations: partner reread of the cycle 26 reader, and tile spelling with the printed dictionary. Who holds what is carried: the child holds the recorded marks; the table holds the wrong-and-right pair. Tool: None. By hand: the retry cards, E157, spelling. Evidence: E157; the retry marks for reading and opinion (a right retry is marked shown with help and never replaces the first mark); the table records.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 153, "Why does Heron ask Duck to splash less?" (a whole sentence). Day 154, "What is the same and what is different at the end of each version?" Day 156, "How does the reason help the point?"
- **Written task, day 156.** The four strips on cards Q and R. **Retry, day 157:** one opinion sentence on a fresh case.
- **Judged by the teacher with look-fors:** (1) the recount is in order and the moral rests on an event; (2) two voices show what each character wants; (3) a similarity and a difference are both given with evidence; (4) a comparison names the most important point, not a minor detail; (5) each reason is tied to its own point. Marked shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: recount, moral, version, similar, different, reason. Frames: "First ___, then ___." "The lesson is ___ because ___." "Both ___, but ___." "I should ___ because ___."
- **Access.** Enlarge print; cards read aloud; a drawing or a scribe for strips; a voice can be shown by a gesture card.
- **Check accommodations.** Extra time within the sitting; the card read again; a partner reads the first line; a scribe for content, with the support recorded.
- **Reading load.** F2 is about 60 words and V3 and V4 about 35 each, read aloud once; Q and R about 40 each, read by the child; no longer text is needed.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second difference with its evidence; or voice a third character in a chosen tale.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read each card aloud and ask the same questions one at a time; on 154 score a packet with the five look-fors together; on 155 share the packet with a grown-up and read a favorite tale aloud in two voices; on 156 read the two articles and fill four strips; on 157 take the one error that shows most and try a fresh case.
- **The last day of the unit.** On 157 the child tells the grown-up one thing they can now do and one thing to try again next unit; the packet is kept. Nothing from the last day carries past the records.
- **One projector.** Show the strips and the cards (one at a time); the sittings stay at the table.
