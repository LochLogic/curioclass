# Section 2.9: The end-of-unit checks, the class piece and sharing

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-2/2/s/2.9), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-2 u02-9.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Children read a short cold text, their own narrative, and printed cards of fewer than 60 words (the grammar card, the sentence card and the letter card); the teacher reads every stem and the cold texts aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Days 69 to 72 are the end-of-unit check on writing and sentence skills, day 73 refines illustrations, and day 74 answers the check and shares the narratives. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Foundations on days 69 to 74 are flexible groups through cycle 12 only; the cumulative collection is days 75 and 76.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.2.3** (days 69, 73, 74): the cold narrative (day 69), the illustration that serves the event (day 73) and the shared reading (day 74).
- **L.2.1a to 1e** (day 69): a collective noun, an irregular plural, a reflexive pronoun, an irregular past verb, and an adjective or adverb chosen by what it modifies, on the day 69 grammar card. Section 2.6 taught them; this section runs them.
- **W.2.5** (days 70, 71, 74): revise from written feedback and a peer suggestion (day 70), edit separately (day 71), and one targeted retry (day 74).
- **L.2.1f** (day 70): produce, expand and rearrange simple and compound sentences, on the sentence card; section 2.7 taught it.
- **L.2.2a, 2b, 2c** (day 71): capitals for a holiday, a place and an invented product name; commas in a greeting and closing; an apostrophe in a contraction and a possessive, on the letter card; section 2.8 taught them.
- **W.2.6** (day 72): the teacher types and displays a class piece while pairs choose wording and order; the observation is the item.
- **RL.2.7** (day 73), "Use information gained from the illustrations and words in a print or digital text to demonstrate understanding of its characters, setting, or plot." On the story pictures and the child's own illustration.
- **L.2.2d, 2e; RF.2.4a to 4c; SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3** (days 73, 74); **L.2.4** (day 74): the word-meaning retry.
- Skills carried: e2-narrative, e2-grammar, e2-revise-edit, e2-sentences, e2-images-and-text, e2-spelling, e2-fluency, e2-discussion, e2-word-strategies.
- **Limits that bite.** The three cards are printed fewer than 60 words and the teacher reads them aloud. Children do not type. No new instruction on days 69 to 72. Day 69 uses a cold text, 03-check69 (A Shape in the Stone), and the day 69 problem is a label that falls, a shadow or a smudge. Spelling and decoding are collected on days 75 and 76, not here.
- **Connections.** Sections 2.6, 2.7 and 2.8 supplied every skill checked here.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Each check day is assessment, not teaching; written feedback happens after class and returns the next day; a right retry never replaces the first mark.

Same-day fresh practice is progress evidence.

**Exit questions.** A reading exit marked Passage asks on a named part of the unit's own texts, read aloud twice, never on a card and never on the passage the day works as its example; for those exits, "the card" below means that passage. Each exit is one ask on an invented card, with two items on the days that test two things (a and b, 54, 55, 61, 66 and 67). The teacher reads each card twice (days 65 to 67 and 71 use a printed card), asks the one question, and hears the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner. Shown means the answer is right alone; shown with help means it is right after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means no right answer. There are no hold-up pictures: the ask alone carries the check. No card reuses an article sentence, a worked example, a shared piece or an end item. Items.csv holds the keys.
- **E69** (day 69, one ask). Card: "Ned tripped on a loose cord by the stage. A helper plugged it back in. Now the lights came on." Ask: "Tell me how the story ends." Answer: The cord is plugged back in and the lights come on. Wrong: "Ned tripped on a cord" (P).
- **E70** (day 70, one ask). Card: "Draft: Mia ran. Feedback: Tell where." Ask: "Say the sentence again with a detail that tells where." Answer: A whole sentence with a place, such as "Mia ran across the yard.". Wrong: "Across the yard" (AD).
- **E71** (day 71, one ask). Printed card: "dear gus; On labor day in maine, the bakers lamp isnt on the shelf.; take care; Jude" Ask: "Fix the word bakers so the card says whose lamp it is." Answer: baker's. Wrong: "bakers lamp" (AG).
- **E72** (day 72, one ask). Card: "Our pair wants the sentence directly under the picture so the next reader sees both." Ask: "Tell us one choice you would offer for the typed piece, and why." Answer: Any wording or placement choice with a reason, such as "Put the title above the picture so readers see it first.". Wrong: "I just watched" (AI).
- **E73** (day 73, one ask). Passage: The Mark in the Stone, page 6 (Amara brings the stone to Dr. Lee), words read aloud twice with the picture covered; not a page worked today (g2-u2-03). Read page 6 of The Mark in the Stone aloud twice, picture covered. Ask: "Tell me one detail a picture could show that these words leave out." Answer: A detail the words do not give that fits page 6, such as Dr. Lee kneeling on the ground or Amara rocking on her toes as she waits. Wrong: "Amara runs away with the stone" (B).
- **E74** (day 74, one ask). Card: "Hal replanted the seed because the wind blew it away. Now it is in the soil again." Ask: "Use re- and plant to say what replanted means here." Answer: Planted again. Wrong: "Not planted" (H).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Each child's practice narrative with its two sets of notes (day 63 feedback, day 64 revision) is kept; on day 69 the cold narrative and the grammar card are new; the day 70 sentence card and the day 71 letter card are new each day; the five-box sheet and the time-word cards sit on the wall; the class piece is the teacher's typed page.
- **Standard grouping (foundations).** Three groups of ten by the latest evidence; three rounds of 15 minutes: teacher group (map, blend, read, spell), partner reread, tiles. Regroup in the last 5 minutes.
- **Key moment 1, day 69: the check.** Ideal instrument: a printed narrative prompt and a ruled draft page with a space for a picture, a printed grammar card. Spec: fits; paper.
- **Key moment 2, day 72: digital production and publishing.** Ideal instrument: a shared text editor on a projector, the teacher typing while pairs give words and order, and a locally displayed page with no account. Spec: partly fits (the unit plan names shared typing as light, partly fits); gaps: no device per five children and no child types. The record sheet lists 15 pairs; paper alone leaves W.2.6 open.
- **Tools.** Shared typing, day 72 (30 of the 40 topic minutes, the observation); no search engine and no account.
- **A room with nothing.** The day 72 piece is written by the teacher on large paper while pairs call words; mark each child's contribution on the paper sheet; record the digital part as open.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 73, "What does your picture show that the words leave out?"; day 74, in the sharing block, "What did you want your reader to see?" and "Which part do you mean?" in the talk routine.
- Written task, day 69: the cold narrative of one event (with actions, thoughts, feelings, time words and an ending). Revised on day 70, edited on day 71, scored on day 72 after class.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: (1) one event with a start and a finish; (2) an action, a thought and a feeling; (3) time words in order; (4) a closing.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 73 opens with one repair of at most 5 minutes for the commonest E72 miss; days 69 to 72 have no topic repair slot (their minutes are the checks) and day 74 opens with Return and repair. The foundations opening repairs the day before's evidence.

- **Earlier work returning.** The talk routine and the heard-six column on days 73 and 74; the day 65 to 67 conventions and the day 60 sentence skill are the cards on days 70 and 71; the digital page on day 72 returns from day 66.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: evidence, detail, support, clarify, caption, rewrote. Frames: "First I ___. Next I ___." "I felt ___." "I would put ___ here because ___." "The picture shows ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer; the cards are large print; the narrative can be drawn and dictated, the teacher scribing. A child who cannot say a contribution aloud points to a two-choice card.
- **Check accommodations.** The cold text and the three cards are read aloud twice; answers may be drawn, spoken or pointed; the retry gets one prompt.
- **Reading load.** At most 150 printed words a day: the cards, the checklist and the child's own draft; the cold text is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second event after the first, or write a caption that gives a second detail the picture does not show.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the cold text aloud, write the narrative, revise the next day from the adult's written note, edit the day after, and the adult acts as the second reader; skip the typed piece and the child reads a finished page aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the cards one at a time; show the typed piece with the teacher typing on day 72.
