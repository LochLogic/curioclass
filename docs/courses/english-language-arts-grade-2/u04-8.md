# Section 4.8: The class invitation and a clear reading

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **L.2.2a, day 147:** "Capitalize holidays, product names, and geographic names." Practiced on the invitation: names of places and days (a street, a park, a library). Met in unit 2 (review of section u02-8, day 65).
- **L.2.2b, day 147:** "Use commas in greetings and closings of letters." The comma after "Dear families" and after the closing (review of section u02-8, day 66).
- **W.2.6, days 147 and 148:** "With guidance and support from adults, use a variety of digital tools to produce and publish writing, including in collaboration with peers." One shared device, light use: each pair types a short part and chooses a placement, 2 minutes a pair, 15 pairs (review of the shared keyboard of section u03-8, day 102).
- **W.2.5, day 148:** "With guidance and support from adults and peers, focus on a topic and strengthen writing as needed by revising and editing." Peer revision of the invitation on 148.
- **RL.2.6, day 149:** "Acknowledge differences in the points of view of characters, including by speaking in a different voice for each character when reading dialogue aloud." Rehearsed in groups of five; taught 123 and 124 (section 4.2).
- **L.2.2d and L.2.2e, days 147 to 149:** spelling patterns and the printed dictionary, as in the cycle work.
- **Limits that bite here.** The invitation is to a classroom showing of the packets; the place and day are invented for practice and the teacher fills the real ones. Work and names stay inside class; nothing is posted. The device is one shared one, so every child's turn is 2 minutes; the invitation is completed by hand on paper either way. Day 149 is a fluency day: nothing is drafted or edited.
- **The teacher page must explain.** That a capital marks the name of one particular place, not every noun; that a comma separates the greeting and closing from the lines; how a pair's typing turn is recorded; and that a different voice shows what a character wants, not an accent.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The edited packet (envelope, drawing, edited card) comes from 4.7 and goes to 4.9 on 150; the typed invitation is printed and stays in class. Models carried by children: the editing checklist, peer feedback that is kind, specific and helpful, the talk routine and the heard-six column.
- **Key moment 1, days 147 and 148: a shared device.** Ideal: one device with a large screen and a keyboard, each pair at it for 2 minutes. Spec: fits, light use (15 pairs x 2 minutes = 30 minutes per day). Paper meets the writing goal but leaves W.2.6 incomplete. **Key moment 2, day 149: a printed pair of lines with the character names.** Ideal: strips. Spec: fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Pairs write the invitation by hand with a ruler for the layout and say the digital choice aloud; W.2.6 is then left incomplete and noted.

## 6. Days

## Day 147


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** Retrieve the parts of a letter (greeting, body, closing) and what a capital marks, using the unit 2 wall card; pairs say one part each.
- **10 min. The invitation.** The teacher shows a model line on a strip with the place and day invented for practice (review of section u02-8): where the greeting comma goes, where the closing comma goes, which words name a particular place. Not taught as new.
- **30 min. Write and type.** Each child writes the invitation by hand on paper (greeting, one sentence naming the place and day, closing). Fifteen pairs take the shared device in turn, 2 minutes each, and type the greeting and the place line (W.2.6); the teacher stays at the device for the 30 minutes. Pairs not at the device keep writing, then check the place names against a printed street and park card.
- **10 min. Pairs check.** Pairs check each other's invitation against the checklist: capital for a name, comma after the greeting, comma after the closing, a spelling checked in the printed dictionary.
- **5 min. Exit.** E147.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The model line and the checklist ask for capitals and commas on one strip, as E147 does. Use a different kind of task: tick the capitals and commas already on a finished model, or sort name cards into place and person names.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before, at most 5 minutes.
- **15 min. A fresh decodable passage.** Not the reader; read, then meaning confirmed (RF.2.4c).
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (19 to 24) read the cycle 25 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **15 min. Dictation.** Words and a sentence with -cal and -cle words, then compared with the pattern.
- **8 min. Dictionary.** Each pair finds one cycle word in the printed beginning dictionary and checks its spelling (L.2.2e).
- **5 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the letter-part card and the checklist. Tool: shared device, light. Part: Write and type (30 minutes). Mark: shared device; job: typing the greeting and the place line, 2 minutes a pair. By hand: the written invitation, the checklist, spelling. Evidence: E147; the invitation draft with the contribution recorded (AA); the oral card, children 19 to 24.

## Day 148


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** AA: each pair says the choice it made yesterday, one example.
- **10 min. Peer feedback.** Pairs swap invitations; each says one kind, specific, helpful thing and asks one question about whether a guest could find the room: 15 pairs, 2 minutes each way, 6 minutes to write the notes.
- **30 min. Revise and lay out.** Each child revises after the notes and chooses a word order and where the picture goes. Fifteen pairs take the shared device in turn, 2 minutes each, to move the room line or enlarge it and place the picture (W.2.6); each pair states the choice and why for the contribution record. Others work by hand on the layout.
- **5 min. Share.** Pairs read their invitation aloud; the listener says where the room line is.
- **5 min. Print and place.** Final invitations are printed and folded into the packet envelope.
- **5 min. Exit.** E148.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The practice moves or enlarges the room line, as E148 does. Use a different kind of task: choose the larger of two words for a title, or ask which line a guest reads first on the printed model.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest foundations error of the day before, at most 5 minutes.
- **15 min. Written check, cycle 25.** Every child writes four dictated words, then the sentence, then corrects the shown misspelling with the printed dictionary entry (5 minutes each); the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (25 to 30) read the cycle 25 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **15 min. A fresh response.** Each child writes or draws a sentence using a cycle word, then reads it to a partner.
- **13 min. Close.** The class reads the cycle 25 reader once more for ease.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the feedback and the contribution card. Tool: shared device, light. Part: Revise and lay out (30 minutes). Mark: shared device; job: moving or enlarging the room line, 2 minutes a pair. By hand: notes, the revised invitation. Evidence: E148; the contribution record (AA); the cycle 25 written check; the oral card, children 25 to 30.

## Day 149


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening.** Retrieve what a different voice shows (what a character wants) with the two-voice card; no new teaching.
- **10 min. Reread the invitation.** Pairs reread their printed invitation aloud, finger under the words, for accuracy and then smoothly (RF.2.4b).
- **25 min. Rehearse in parts.** Six groups of five rehearse a chosen tale from texts 01 to 04, each child taking one character or the narrator, and the teacher listens to each group for 4 minutes (6 x 4 = 24). While waiting, groups rehearse alone and a partner ticks voices.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "How did the voice show what the character wanted?"
- **5 min. Exit.** E149: pairs read the two lines to each other (30 seconds each); the teacher hears the six of the heard column.
- Shared shape (note, not timed): The rehearsal reads two speakers in two voices, as E149 does. Use a different kind of task: match each voice mark to a character the group names, and clap the beat of the lines, with no read-aloud contrast.
- **5 min. Close.** The class names one voice that helped a listener.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Retrieve.** Cycle 25 words (-cal and -cle).
- **15 min. Model, map, blend (cycle 26).** The -ous ending says /us/ (enormous, dangerous, marvelous) and the -us ending also says /us/ (circus, bonus, chorus, octopus); the s is not a plural. Tiles on the table: tap the syllables, blend the whole, then spell. Each word is checked in a sentence.
- **15 min. First reading of the cycle 26 reader.** A pattern-matched replacement written by us (the base reader on animals big and small is used only where text and picture rights clear); read after the pattern is taught; pairs echo-read.
- **12 min. Rotation.** Six children (1 to 6, first-day samples, diagnostic) read the cycle 26 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); the rest at stations. New material is modeled before a child's turn; the reserved check words stay unseen.
- **13 min. Spell.** Words and a sentence from dictation; map the cycle's irregular words with no guessing from pictures or first letters.

Who holds what is carried: groups hold their tale and the two-voice card. Tool: None. By hand: finger-tracking, rehearsal, tiles. Evidence: E149; the heard column (six children); the oral card, children 1 to 6.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 148, "How can a guest find the room quickly?" Day 149, "How did the voice show what the character wanted?"
- **Written task, days 147 and 148.** The class invitation (greeting, a sentence naming the place and day, closing), typed in part and finished by hand.
- **Judged by the teacher with look-fors:** (1) the greeting and the closing each have a comma; (2) every place and day name has a capital; (3) the room and time are easy to find; (4) the pair made and explained one wording or placement choice. Marked shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 147.** Capitals and commas (unit 2); the editing checklist (4.6, 4.7). **148.** Peer feedback; the invitation. **149.** Reading in parts (4.2); a smooth reading with a partner. Cycle words each day in foundations.
- **Fluency target.** Accurate, expressive rereading of the invitation and a chosen tale by 149; no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: invitation, greeting, closing, guest, capital. Frames: "Please come to ___." "I moved ___ because ___." "Owl sounds ___." A child may rehearse a character's line in the home language first.
- **Access.** Enlarge print; read cards aloud; allow a scribe for the invitation and a partner to type; voices may be shown by a gesture card.
- **Check accommodations.** Extra time for the exit; the line read again; a partner reads a line first.
- **Reading load.** The invitation is about 30 words; the voice lines are 12 words or fewer; no new text is read.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second sentence to the invitation naming something to see; or read the narrator and a character.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Write a three-line invitation, fix the capitals and commas with the checklist, decide with the grown-up where the room line goes; on 149 read two characters in two voices. Skip the device and set the layout with a ruler.
- **One projector.** Show the invitation and the pair of lines; typing is at one keyboard, the others stay on paper.
