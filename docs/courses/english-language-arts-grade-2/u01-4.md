# Section 1.4: Facts and definitions for a paragraph

## Header


Children read the cycle 4 reader, the cold selection 02-d, the note slip and the frame card; the teacher reads 02-a and 02-b aloud again and children follow the words to know. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cards are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.2.2: "Write informative/explanatory texts in which they introduce a topic, use facts and definitions to develop points, and provide a concluding statement or section."** Day 16 gathers a fact, its source tag and a definition from 02-b. Day 17 teaches the four parts (introduce, develop with facts and a definition, conclude) on the grade 1 paragraph-frame. Days 18 and 19 are one paragraph on reading partners, begun, given written feedback, then revised and finished. Day 20 responds to both.
- **RI.2.1: "Ask and answer such questions as who, what, where, when, why, and how to demonstrate understanding of key details in a text."** Day 16 asks a new article question before gathering facts; days 18 and 19 ask and answer cold (field 3).
- **Foundations.** RF.2.3a, RF.2.3b and L.2.2d: cycle 4 (igh, ie, y as long i) on days 16 to 20, written check on day 20. This is review of grade 1 (y as long i; ie and igh as long i), met now as one set. RF.2.4 to RF.2.4c and SL.2.1a to c, SL.2.3 run daily through the reader and the talk routine.
- **Limits that bite here.** A definition is told in the article's own words, never from outside knowledge. The articles say "many" and "some"; read them so. No country is ranked. The paragraph is about reading partners in 02-d, not the child's own school or home. Present-day claims in 02-b were checked on 2026-11-19 and need a recheck before launch. Children read 02-d (79 words) themselves; a child who cannot has it read aloud.
- **One word, one sense.** A "fact" is something the article says, not an opinion. To "define" a word is to tell what it means; "definition" is that meaning. To "conclude" here is to end a paragraph with what the facts show, not to decide. "Light" in the reader is only the word to read.
- **Connections.** Section 1.3 gave the articles, the recount strip and the question words; this section gathers facts from them. Section 1.5 turns the same notes into a book from day 22.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A paragraph has a topic that says what is explained (D), facts that match the source (E), a hard word told in the article's meaning (F) and an ending that follows from the facts (G). Feedback is written by these four rows and returned before scoring. A fact copied from nowhere, an ending that ignores the facts and a picture used to guess a word are different errors with different repairs.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** day 18 RI.2.1 and W.2.2; day 19 W.2.2 (RI.2.1 is retained, no new minutes). All four are in the table above.

**Day 18 RI.2.1, how it runs (15 minutes).** Children read 02-d alone or with the teacher reading it aloud (4 minutes), write one question the passage answers and its answer (3 minutes), then the teacher reads six single-ask stems twice each and children write a few words for each (1 minute each, 6 minutes), 2 minutes to hand in. Stems: "Who reads together?" "What do they choose?" "Where does the teacher pair them?" "When do they read?" "Why do the partners read in turns?" "How do pupils read a hard word?" Marking is shown, shown with help (the stem read a third time), not yet seen. The six question words each get a cell. Wrong "when" answers, such as "Every morning", contradict the passage (A).

**Day 18 and 19 W.2.2, how it runs.** The frame card (topic, fact, fact, definition, conclusion) stays on the desk. The four rows are introduces the topic (D), develops with facts from 02-d (E), defines (F) and concludes (G). The unit plan's 0, 1, 2 is marked on the one scale: 2 is shown, 1 is shown with help, 0 is not yet seen. Day 18 is unscored. Written feedback follows after class, one sentence per row, in words the child can act on; day 19 revises, then the finished paragraph is scored once. No reteaching on day 19.

**Exit questions.** Each exit is two spoken items, each one ask: (a) "Point to the picture that goes with the card I read." (A or B hold-up); (b) one reason from the rotating six, the rest tell a partner. Shown means the right picture and a reason from the card; shown with help means the right picture and a reason after one prompt; not yet seen means the other picture or no reason. Cards are invented, read twice and printed "invented for practice". None reuses an article scene.
- **E16** (day 16). Card (02-a insert): "Pupils keep their drawing books in a cubby. A cubby is a small open space with a shelf for one person's things. Each cubby has a name card." One ask: "Tell me what a cubby is, using the card." Answer: a small open space with a shelf for one person's things. Wrong: "A kind of bag", the meaning contradicts the card (E). items.csv is the truth over this line.
- **E17** (day 17). Card (02-b insert): "The job board lists names. It names who wipes the tables each day. Pupils check it every morning." One ask: "These facts have no first sentence. Say a first sentence that tells what the paragraph explains." Answer: a sentence that names the topic, such as "A job board helps pupils share the table jobs". Wrong: "Tables are nice", no topic that explains anything (D).
- **E20** (day 20, in the fresh retry for children with no recorded letter). Draft (invented): "Pupils save used envelopes in a box. They draw on the blank backs." Printed feedback: "End by explaining what saving the envelopes helps pupils do." One ask: "Say an ending for the draft so that a listener understands." Answer: an ending that follows the facts, said in phrases, such as "Saving envelopes gives pupils more paper to draw on". Wrong: "I like apples" ignores the facts (E); an ending said so fast that it is lost is (O). The apron retry card of the unit plan is items X20a, X20d, X20f and X20g, one ask each by recorded letter (A, D, F, G).

**Cycle 4 written check** (day 20, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write it in a printed gap. None is a teaching word. (1) "The cat got a ____ and ran." fright. (2) "Dad ____ an egg in the pan." fried. (3) "The wet cloth will ____ soon." dry. (4) "The lid is too ____ to open." tight. Wrong spellings tagged: "frite", "frid", "dri", "tite" (Q, spelled by sound, pattern not carried over). Changed from the first plan, whose bright is a word of the reader.

## 6. Days

## Day 16


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E15 (A), follow the arrows with a finger and say the labels in order; otherwise retrieve the diagram card.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Grade 1 task e1-informative: pairs order four strips (topic, fact, fact, closing) of an invented paragraph about a reading corner and name each part. Practice only.
- **4 min. Words.** Fact, topic and define, with a sentence each.
- **10 min. Reread 02-b, A Place to Read, and ask a new question.** Teacher reads it aloud (say the teacher line first); pairs ask one text-dependent question and point to the line that answers it.
- **10 min. Gather a fact.** Model one note slip: the fact in a few words, the source tag "A Place to Read", and what the article says "library" means.
- **14 min. Pairs gather.** Each pair fills one slip for a fact and one for a word (library or volunteer) from the article. Hear the six.
- **7 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "Which fact tells what a library is for?"
- **5 min. Exit.** E16.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Repair the commonest day 15 check miss (Q, M or L) with the sound-spelling-map.
- **15 min. Teach igh, ie and y.** Review of grade 1: contrast y in my with y in funny; map light, night, high, right, pie, cried, tried, my, fly, try, sky, by, why; blend, then spell two words with tiles.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card of grade 1 words and cycle 3 patterns, since no cycle 4 pattern was taught before today.
- **3 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the note slips; the teacher holds the article. Tool: None. By hand: writing a few words, tiles. Evidence: E16; heard column.

## Day 19


- **25 min. Revise and finish.** Return the written feedback and read it aloud to those who need it (5 minutes); children revise and complete the paragraph alone (20 minutes). Score the finished paragraph once.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: define, fact, topic, introduce, develop, conclude, partner, library, volunteer. Frames: "A ___ is ___." "The article says ___." "This fact comes from ___." "The facts show ___."
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print strips and slips. A child who cannot write dictates to the teacher or an approved scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Stems and cards are read aloud; the spelling check can be answered with tiles; extra time on day 19.
- **Reading load.** A child reads 02-d (79 words), the reader (about 60 words), strips and slips; 02-a and 02-b are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second definition and say which fact it supports.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read 02-d aloud, let the child tell it back, then write a topic, two facts, a meaning and an ending on four lines of paper.
- **One projector.** Show the frame card and the strips; mark on printed copies.
