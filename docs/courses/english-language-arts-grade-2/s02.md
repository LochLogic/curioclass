**01-check57, Finding Fossil Clues.**

Page 1, heading **Keeping a Shape**. Paragraph 1: A shell can leave a hollow shape in sediment. Sediment is material such as mud or sand. If the shell dissolves, the hollow shape may remain. It is a mold. Other material can fill that mold and harden into a cast. The cast is a copy of the shell's shape. This is one way fossils can form.

Diagram specification: show shell buried in sediment, then an empty shell-shaped hollow, then material filling it. Print beneath it, "The middle picture shows the mold. The last picture shows the cast." Bold **mold** and **cast** in the paragraph. No numerical ages.

Page 2, heading **Recording a Find**. Paragraph 2: A fossil collector looks closely at a find. To collect is to gather things. The collector writes its location in a notebook. A notebook is a book for notes. An unmarked bag has no label. The collector adds a label before putting the find in the bag. These notes help keep the find connected with its place.

Page 3, heading **Reading a Trace**. Paragraph 3: A fossil can also record an action. A footprint may leave a trace in soft sediment. If that trace is preserved, it can become a fossil. A track tells us where an animal stepped. It does not tell us the animal's name.

Page 4, **Glossary**: cast, a copy of a shape made when material fills a mold; location, the place where something is; trace, a sign left by an action. **Index**: cast 1; collector 2; footprint 3; mold 1; notebook 2. Print-menu cards: a shell icon labelled Shapes, page 1; a bag icon labelled Find Records, page 2; a footprint icon labelled Traces, page 3. The teacher-held digital copy has the same labelled menu links and pages; children choose a link orally and find the answer on paper. Show both formats during the reference task, without demonstrating the answer.

Beginning dictionary card: cast, noun, a copy of a shape; a hard cover that protects a broken bone. Example: "The cast filled the shell-shaped hollow." Select the first meaning in this article. This card and its digital counterpart are original practice references, not outside dictionary extracts.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | The opening and ending functions are confused. | Reread opening and ending together to trace the completed action. | From memory. |
| B | A detail contradicts the text. | Replace it with the detail in its answering sentence. | From memory. |
| C | One paragraph detail is named as the whole topic. | Compare all paragraph focuses before naming what joins them. | From memory. |
| D | An author purpose is unsupported by the text. | Point to what the author actually explains or describes. | From memory. |
| E | A useful text feature is ignored during a search. | Choose the feature that leads directly to the requested fact. | From memory. |
| F | A mold is confused with a cast. | Match the hollow and the filled shape to their captioned names. | From memory. |
| G | An irrelevant word meaning is chosen. | Test each meaning in the actual sentence. | From memory. |
| H | The prefix meaning is mistaken. | Join the known prefix meaning to the known base meaning. | From memory. |
| I | The known root meaning is lost. | Connect the new word to its root in the sentence. | From memory. |
| J | Compound parts are joined with a meaning that does not fit. | Combine the parts, then check the result in context. | From memory. |
| K | The narrative has no elaborated event. | Expand one moment rather than listing unrelated facts. | From memory. |
| L | The narrative gives no character action. | Add what the character does in that event. | From memory. |
| M | The narrative gives no character thought. | Add what the invented character wonders or thinks. | From memory. |
| N | The narrative gives no character feeling. | Add a feeling tied to the event. | From memory. |
| O | The narrative has no time words. | Add temporal words to show event order. | From memory. |
| P | The narrative has no closure. | Finish the event opened at the beginning. | From memory. |
| Q | A collective noun is replaced by a plural noun. | Name the people as one group. | From memory. |
| R | An irregular plural is regularized. | Rehearse the singular and irregular plural pair in context. | From memory. |
| S | A reflexive pronoun mismatches its referent. | Match the pronoun to the person acting. | From memory. |
| T | An irregular past verb is regularized. | Rehearse the present and past pair in context. | From memory. |
| U | An adjective modifies an action in place of an adverb. | Ask whether the description modifies the thing or the action. | From memory. |
| V | A revision loses the narrative focus. | Keep changes connected to the chosen event. | From memory. |
| W | Rushing obscures sentence meaning. | Reread in phrases for a listener. | From memory. |
| X | A picture supplies a guess instead of decoding. | Read the letters before checking meaning. | From memory. |
| Y | A taught vowel correspondence is misread. | Map the spelling to its taught sound before blending. | From memory. |
| Z | A suffix is omitted in reading. | Blend the base and its ending as one complete word. | From memory. |
| AA | A mapped irregular word is regularized. | Remap its unexpected letters and reread the word. | From memory. |
| AB | Doubling is omitted before a vowel suffix. | Compare the short-vowel base with its suffixed form. | From memory. |
| AC | A spelling is copied without consulting its reference. | Locate the entry and compare its letters with the draft. | From memory. |
| AD | A sentence is incomplete. | Add the missing subject or predicate before expanding it. | From memory. |
| AE | A proper name is left lowercase. | Capitalize each word in the particular name. | From memory. |
| AF | A letter greeting or closing lacks its comma. | Place the comma after the greeting or closing. | From memory. |
| AG | A possessive apostrophe is omitted. | Mark who owns the named thing with the apostrophe. | From memory. |
| AH | A contraction apostrophe is omitted. | Mark the missing letters where the words join. | From memory. |
| AI | A child observes publishing without contributing. | Offer a choice of wording or placement to discuss with a partner. | From memory. |
| AJ | An imagined response is attributed to a historical person without evidence. | Separate the source's stated facts from the invented narrator's response. | From memory. |
