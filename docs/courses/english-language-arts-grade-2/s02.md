**01-check57, Fossil Notes.**

Page 1, heading **Keeping a Shape**. Paragraph 1: A shell can leave a hollow shape in sediment. Sediment is material such as mud or sand. If the shell dissolves, the hollow shape may remain. It is a mold. Other material can fill that mold and harden into a cast. The cast is a copy of the shell's shape. This is one way fossils can form.

Diagram specification: show shell buried in sediment, then an empty shell-shaped hollow, then material filling it. Print beneath it, "The middle picture shows the mold. The last picture shows the cast." Bold **mold** and **cast** in the paragraph. No numerical ages.

Page 2, heading **Recording a Find**. Paragraph 2: People collect fossils to study them. A fossil collector looks closely at a find. The collector writes its location in a notebook. Another bag in the box is unmarked, so nobody can tell where its find was taken. The collector adds a label before putting each find in a bag. These notes help keep the find connected with its place.

Page 3, heading **Reading a Trace**. Paragraph 3: A fossil can also record an action. A footprint may leave a trace in soft sediment. If that trace is preserved, it can become a fossil. A track tells us where an animal stepped. A track alone may not tell us exactly which animal made it.

Page 4, **Glossary**: cast, a copy of a shape made when material fills a mold; location, the place where something is; trace, a sign left by an action. **Index**: cast 1; collector 2; footprint 3; mold 1; notebook 2. Print-menu cards: a shell icon labeled Shapes, page 1; a bag icon labeled Find Records, page 2; a footprint icon labeled Traces, page 3. The teacher-held digital copy has the same labeled menu links and pages; children choose a link orally and find the answer on paper. Show both formats during the reference task, without demonstrating the answer.

Beginning dictionary card: cast, noun, a copy of a shape; a hard cover that protects a broken bone. Example: "The cast filled the shell-shaped hollow." Select the first meaning in this article. This card and its digital counterpart are original practice references, not outside dictionary extracts.

Sources for 01-check57: molds, casts and fossil formation, the two government pages in field 10. The collecting practice (location noted, bag labeled) is general science teaching, ours; fossil collecting is restricted in many parks and protected lands, so the text never tells a child to collect anywhere (a government page on this is to locate, read and date). The trace-fossil statements need a government or museum page, to locate, read and date; none is read yet.

**01-check68, Notes From the Rock.** Paragraph 1: An animal dies near a river. Mud slowly covers its bones. The mud protects them from wind and rain. Paragraph 2: Water seeps through the mud. Tiny bits of mineral from the water fill spaces in the bones. Over a long time the bones turn hard like stone. Paragraph 3: Much later, the rock above wears away. A walker may spot the fossil bone at the surface. A scientist then digs it out with care. Print no headings. Original; facts follow the field 10 government pages on how fossils form.
