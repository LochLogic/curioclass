# Section 2.4: Topic, text features and the author's purpose

## Header


Children read short labels, cards, one printed page of an article at a time and the cycle reader; the teacher reads the articles and every stem aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.2.2**, "Identify the main topic of a multiparagraph text as well as the focus of specific paragraphs within the text." Day 46 teaches it on article B of *Fossil Clues*, with the model for paragraph focus; the model is withdrawn on day 52 (section 2.5).
- **RI.2.5**, "Know and use various text features (e.g., captions, bold print, subheadings, glossaries, indexes, electronic menus, icons) to locate key facts or information in a text efficiently." Day 47: subheadings, bold print and captions; day 49: glossary, index, menu and icons.
- **RI.2.6**, "Identify the main purpose of a text, including what the author wants to answer, explain, or describe." Day 48.
- **L.2.4e**, "Use glossaries and beginning dictionaries, both print and digital, to determine or clarify the meaning of words and phrases." Day 49, on the glossary of article C and a beginning dictionary card.
- **RF.2.3d**, "Decode words with common prefixes and suffixes", days 48 and 49 through the cycle 9 doubling words (rubbing, hopping). **RF.2.3e, 2.3f; RF.2.4, 4a to 4c; L.2.2d, 2e** (days 46 to 49): ild, old, ind, ost, the cycle 8 words and the cycle 9 words even, finally, wrong, won; reading the readers with accuracy and checking spellings with the word wall and the dictionary. **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3**: the talk routine on days 46 to 49.
- Skills carried: e2-main-topic-paragraphs, e2-text-features, e2-author-purpose, e2-prefixes-suffixes, e2-word-strategies, e2-spelling, e2-fluency, e2-tricky-spellings, e2-discussion.
- **Limits that bite.** A child reads no more than 150 printed words a day: the articles are read aloud and children read a subheading part, or page 4 of article C (about 100 words). Article B tells that an animal dies near a river; say it plainly and briefly, with no gloom. Article C says fossils on public land belong to everyone and may not be taken; say aloud that children never dig or take fossils and ask a ranger or a teacher. On day 49 a shared device shows the same page as the print; no search engine and no account.
- **Connections.** Section 2.1 gave the fossil words and article A; section 2.3 the cycle 8 start. Section 2.5 opens on day 50 with a prefix and a base word, and day 52 repeats the topic work with the model withdrawn.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Topic is the whole article; focus is what one paragraph says; both are named by the child. Mold and cast appear in article C, which is read aloud on day 49; they are not worked examples for the day 57 items (those use a cold text).

## 3. Checks, written first



**Unit assessment items owned:** none. The day 57 rows on RI.2.2, RI.2.5 and RI.2.6 belong to section 2.5, which re-teaches nothing but repeats the practice.

**Exit questions.** A reading exit marked Passage asks on a named part of the unit's own texts, read aloud twice, never on a card and never on the passage the day works as its example; for those exits, "the card" below means that passage. Each exit is two spoken items, each one ask, except as noted. Item (a): "Point to the picture that goes with the card I read." All children show A or B on a two-card hold-up. Item (b): the day's one ask, answered aloud by the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner. Shown means the right picture and an answer using a word from the card; shown with help means the right picture and an answer after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means the other picture or no answer. Cards are printed "invented for practice" and read aloud twice. None reuses an article sentence or a worked example.
- **E46** (day 46). Passage: article C, page 2, the part Tracks and Traces (two paragraphs), read aloud twice; article C is first read on day 49 (g2-u2-01). Read aloud the part of article C called Tracks and Traces, on page 2, twice. Ask: "Tell me one topic that fits both paragraphs." Answer: Fossil tracks: how a footprint becomes a fossil and what a track can show. Then s02-X46bv2: "What is paragraph two about?" Answer: What a track can tell: where an animal stepped and how it moved, though not always which animal made it.
- **E47** (day 47). Passage: article A, page 1, shown: its four subheadings, bold words and diagram captions (g2-u2-01). Show page 1 of article A. Ask: "Which feature leads you fastest to the part about why few living things become fossils?" Answer: The subheading Why Fossils Are Rare. Then s02-X47bv2: "Which feature shows that trace is a key word?" Answer: The bold print. Then s02-X47cv2: "Which feature tells what the right-hand picture shows?" Answer: The caption under it: A footprint fossil is a trace of what an animal did.
- **E48** (day 48). Passage: article C, page 1, the part Shapes in Stone (two paragraphs), read aloud twice; article C is first read on day 49 (g2-u2-01). Read aloud the part of article C called Shapes in Stone twice. Ask: "Tell me what the author wants to do." Answer: Explain how: it tells the steps by which a shell's shape becomes a fossil, a mold or a cast. Then s02-X48bv2: "Does the author want to answer a question, explain how, or describe what?" Answer: Describe what: it tells what Lyme Regis is like, a town on the coast with tall cliffs full of fossils.
- **E49** (day 49). Passage: article C, page 4, shown: the glossary, the index and the menu (g2-u2-01). Show page 4 of article C. Ask: "Which tool gives you the page number for location?" Answer: The index: location, page 3. Then s02-X49cv2: "Which icon takes you to the part about tracks?" Answer: The footprint icon, labeled Tracks: page 2.

**Cycle 8 written check** (day 47, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Four independent spellings; none is a day 43 to 46 word. (1) "The soup is ____, not hot." mild. (2) "She was ____ and not shy." bold. (3) "We ____ the corn into flour." grind. (4) "I will ____ the party." host. Wrong: "mield", "bowld", "grined" and "hoast" each write a taught vowel correspondence wrongly (Y); tag Y. After the check is marked, dictate "behind" and "told" as unscored practice.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The topic and focus chart (a topic box above paragraph-focus boxes, joined by arrows) is built by the teacher on day 46 and held by partners from day 47; it returns on day 52. The feature route card (subheading, bold term, caption) is made on day 47; the purpose cards (answer, explain, describe) on day 48; the reference route cards on day 49. The word wall holds even, finally, wrong, won and the cycle 8 words. The talk routine, the heard-six column and the six question words card stay up.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles (spell the day's words), sort (card sort by pattern) and reread (partner reread of the reader or phrase cards), ten children each. The day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations.
- **Key moment 1, day 46: the topic and focus chart.** Ideal instrument: a large wall chart with movable paragraph strips. Spec: fits; paper. Gaps and asks: none.
- **Key moment 2, day 49: reference tools on screen and on paper.** Ideal instrument: a teacher-held device showing the article with a clickable icon menu and a pop-up glossary, children choosing a link orally, checked against an identical printed page. Spec: does not exist (the unit plan names reference screens as light use, practice and then check unaided, and the ask goes in TOOL-ASKS.md; this plan does not edit it). Gaps: the menu links and the pop-up glossary. The printed menu, glossary and dictionary pages do the whole job when no screen exists.
- **Tools.** Reference screen, day 49 only, in the model part (6 minutes), to show why the icon works; children practice on paper; unaided check is later. No search engine and no account.
- **A room with nothing.** The chart is two rows on folded paper; the articles are read from printed copies; the menu is a hand-drawn row of three icons; tiles are letters on torn card.

## 6. Days

## Day 46


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E45 (P), reread the whole event aloud and ask what fits the last thing that happened; otherwise retrieve the five-box checklist.
- **6 min. Words.** Topic, focus, bury, layer: each said and shown on a picture card; use "bury" and "layer" in a sentence.
- **10 min. Read aloud: article B.** Teacher reads How a Bone Becomes a Fossil (226 words) with its diagram. Say plainly that an animal dies near a river; it is a fact about how fossils start. Say aloud that fossils are protected and children never dig or take them.
- **10 min. Model: topic and paragraph focus.** Topic chart: the whole topic box above four paragraph-focus boxes. Model Buried Fast (focus: the sediment covers the bones) and Minerals Move In (focus: minerals make the bone hard); then name the topic (how a bone becomes a fossil).
- **12 min. Pairs do the other two.** Pairs read Rock Wears Away and Uncovered With Care (about 110 words) turn about, name each focus in a few words, and check that both fit the topic. Teacher moves between pairs.
- **7 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "How do the paragraph focuses fit the topic?" Children say "The topic is ___; this paragraph is about ___". Hear the six.
- **5 min. Write one sentence.** Each child writes "The topic is ___. One focus is ___." with a few words.
- **5 min. Exit.** E46.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Retrieve ild, old, ind, ost: read six word cards; map any missed together or whole.
- **15 min. Transfer to a fresh passage.** Pairs read a short fresh passage that uses only cycle 8 patterns and the words hello and weird (to make); confirm meaning after decoding; the teacher dictates one sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 4 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which word did you confirm after you decoded it?"

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the chart; pairs hold the paragraph strips. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, writing a few words, tiles. Evidence: E46; the written sentence; the heard column.

## Day 48


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E47 (E), set the question beside the route cards and walk the finger from subheading to bold word to caption; otherwise retrieve the feature route.
- **8 min. Reread the opening of article B.** The teacher rereads Five Steps: "This article tells the steps in order." Children say what the author wants to tell.
- **10 min. Model: three purposes.** Purpose cards: answer a question, explain how, describe what. Model with article B: the author wants to explain how a bone becomes a fossil, because the opening says it tells the steps in order and the diagram has five boxes.
- **10 min. Sort invented blurbs.** Pairs sort three invented blurbs by purpose and say the words that show it: a recipe card (explains how), a zoo sign about an animal (describes what), a quiz page (answers a question). Say aloud they are made up.
- **10 min. Pairs find the line.** Pairs find in article A (What Is a Fossil?) the line that shows its purpose and decide: answer, explain or describe.
- **8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "How do you know what the author wants to do?" Hear the six.
- **4 min. Write one sentence.** Each child writes "The author wants to ___. I know because ___." with a few words.
- **5 min. Exit.** E48.
- **5 min. Retrieval (foundations).** Retrieve dropping the silent e: hope to hoping, bake to baking; read the cycle 8 check misses (Y).
- **15 min. Teach: doubling before a vowel suffix.** One short vowel and one consonant at the end: double the consonant before an ending that starts with a vowel: rub to rubbing, hop to hopping, stop to stopped, big to bigger. Compare hope to hoping. Even and finally are mapped irregular words.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 1 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Why is it hopping and not hoping?"

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the purpose cards. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, sorting, writing a few words, tiles. Evidence: E48; the sorted blurbs; the heard column.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 46, "How do the paragraph focuses fit the topic?"; day 47, "Which feature found the answer fastest?"; day 48, "How do you know what the author wants to do?"; day 49, "Which tool do you use first, and why?"
- Written task, day 49: "I found ___ by using ___", a few written words. Days 46 and 48 also collect one sentence each.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) names the topic, the feature or the purpose asked about; (2) gives a word or line from the text as the reason; (3) uses the feature or tool for its job.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 47 to 49 open with one repair of at most 5 minutes, for the commonest exit miss; day 46 takes E45. The foundations opening repairs the day before's commonest spelling or reading miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence each day, using only patterns taught on an earlier day. The record sheet has a heard column.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of the cycle 9 reader by day 52 (section 2.5); no timer.
- **Earlier work returning.** The story map and the question words card appear in the day 46 and 49 retrievals; Article A is reread on day 48.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: topic, focus, bury, layer, process, caption, glossary, index, purpose, explain, describe. Frames: "The topic is ___." "This paragraph is about ___." "The author wants to ___." "I used the ___ to find ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print cards; the A and B pictures differ in color and shape. A child who cannot write the sentence draws and the teacher scribes. The menu icons are labeled with words.
- **Check accommodations.** Cards are read aloud twice; the spelling check can be answered with tiles.
- **Reading load.** A child reads at most 150 printed words a day (a subheading part, page 4 of article C, cards); the articles are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a one-line question for a classmate that needs two features to answer, or name the purpose of a second article part.

## 10. Two rooms

- **One projector.** Show the article page and the menu together; the child answers aloud and the teacher marks on the printed card.
