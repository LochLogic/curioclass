## 1. Standards and practices


Days 113 to 115 are the poster talks: ten children a day present, three minutes each including a listener question (30 teacher minutes); the other twenty are the panel. Day 116 is the single response for all three days. Nothing new is taught on a Check day. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Children read at most 150 printed words a day: the poem C2 (about 30 words) on 113 and their own posters; every other stem is read aloud. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen; in the unit plan's 0 and 1, shown is 1. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: bees, flowers, pollen; no sting, injury, death or illness; no insect is touched.

- **W.2.7, W.2.8** (113 to 116), "Participate in shared research and writing projects"; "Recall information from experiences or gather information from provided sources to answer a question." P1: the poster's facts (made 105, revised 108) are scored as their own cell; R1's shared-research mark is retained, never retested.
- **SL.2.6** (113 to 116), "Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification." P2, observed in the three-minute turn; scored on 117 (section 3.12).
- **L.2.5a, L.2.5b, L.2.6** (113 to 115): P3 a real-life connection for the day's word, P4 the two contrasts of the day's reserved verse, P5 one adjective and one adverb; each observed in the same turn, scored on 117.
- **RI.2.3** (115, 116), "Describe the connection between a series of historical events, scientific ideas or concepts, or steps in technical procedures in a text": the connected three-to-five-step explanation, kept from each child across 113 to 115.
- **RL.2.10** (113, 115): C2, "Independently comprehend a whole short literary text"; read 113, feedback, revised 114, scored 115.
- **RF.2.3c** (every day), P6: six children a day read fresh words for two minutes each; **L.2.2d, L.2.2e, RF.2.4a to 4c, SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3** are practiced on 116.
- **Limits that bite here.** The reserved verses (forms 1 to 3) and the C2 poem are read only on their days and never taught. Each child is observed once. Eye contact is not scored; the child may look at the poster. Written feedback follows every turn; nothing is scored on the day.
- **Connections.** 3.9 (the poster and plan card), 3.10 (the criteria chart, the comparison), 3.8 (the response routine). **The teacher page must explain:** why the same twelve cells are marked on every child and how the panel works (below).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Items owned:** P1 (W.2.7, W.2.8; the poster's facts), P2 to P5 (observed, scored 117), P6 (RF.2.3c; six children a day), C2 (RL.2.10, scored 115), the connected process explanation (RI.2.3, 115), the 116 retry. Keys, look-fors and samples are on the unit plan, field 5; each cell is marked separately.

**Record sheet.** One row a child: P1 facts, P2 sentence, P3 word, P4 verb contrast, P4 adjective contrast, P5 adjective and adverb, process (RI.2.3), C2 event and feeling, P6, 116 retry, and a heard column.

**Exit questions** (unscored practice; read aloud, said to be invented; the rotating six answer aloud, the rest tell a partner).
- **E113.** This card is invented for practice. Show the poster note: "A beetle walks on the petals. Pollen sticks to its body." A listener asks: "What does the beetle carry?" Say: "Answer the listener in a whole sentence." Key: The beetle carries pollen on its body. Wrong: On its body (O); It carries seeds (A).
- **E114.** Say: "Tell about a real time you would use the word scratchy." Key: A real thing or time the word fits, such as a wool sweater or a rough towel. Wrong: Scratchy is when it is scratchy (P); Scratchy is something to eat (AP).
- **E115.** Read aloud twice article 01-d (A Bat Visits at Night), paragraph 2 (A long nose and a long tongue), from g2-u3-01. Say: "Tell how the bat helps the next flower, joining the steps with so or because." Key: A sentence that joins the steps, such as Pollen sticks to the bat while it feeds, so some brushes onto the next flower. Wrong: It feeds. It flies. Pollen brushes (B); The bat plants seeds (A).
- **E116.** This card is invented for practice. Show the feedback: "Say where the pollen goes." Show the source: "The beetle lands on the next flower and some pollen rubs off there." Say: "Say one whole sentence that answers the feedback, using the source." Key: A whole sentence that says where the pollen goes, such as Some pollen rubs off on the next flower. Wrong: At the flower (O); The pollen is yellow (E).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The revised poster, the plan page, the register comparison (answered 112) and the written story stay with the child; the criteria chart and talk card stay up. The record sheet and the heard column go with the teacher.
- **Presentation turns (counted).** Ten children a day, 3 minutes each (2 minutes for the explanation and the word contrasts, 1 for one listener question) is 30 minutes. A panel of ten from the other twenty sits at the front and rotates each day; each panelist asks one question, so every child is on the panel once. The teacher keeps the sheet and does nothing else. Another class or a staff member joins as a second listener when possible; if not, the fallback is two panelists who have not seen the poster.
- **Equipment.** The recorder is used only for the ten children's rehearsal recordings on 116 (foundations): 10 children at 2 minutes each plus 5 minutes of changeovers is 25 minutes; the other twenty work a named task in the room. If it fails, those ten rehearse live and record on 117 with the others.
- **Key moment 1, days 113 to 115: the talk.** Ideal: a live listener who asks for detail. Spec fits; no recording. Key moment 2, day 116: recording the planned story. Ideal: the recorder; spec fits.
- **Tools.** Audio recorder (key), 116 practice with feedback. A room with nothing: live telling to the teacher, recording standards left incomplete.

## 6. Days

## Day 113


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **30 min. Ten presentation turns (group 1).** Children 1 to 10, 3 minutes each: the poster explained in whole sentences, a real-life use of the day's word (dusty) and one word from the poster, the contrast of the two pairs in the reserved verse (form 1, unit plan field 5), one adjective and one adverb, and one panel question. The panel marks the criteria chart; the teacher fills the sheet.
- **5 min. Exit.** E113.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.
- **25 min. Quiet work.** C2, 5 minutes: each child reads the new poem alone (independent first; a read-aloud only after a first try, recorded as help), then draws or writes the event and one feeling with two details. Register revision, 5 minutes: the 112 comparison answer after written feedback. Story draft, 15 minutes: the written story drafted from the 112 plan page.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **12 min. P6 decoding sample.** Six different children read fresh taught-pattern words, 2 minutes each, at the teacher table.
- **23 min. Independent matched reading.** Each child reads a matched passage to answer its meaning question, then rereads to explain one self-correction (8 minutes of the 23).
- **25 min. Flexible groups.** Four groups made from the cycle checks (S and T; AM; AO and AN; Y and J), 6 minutes each with the teacher; the others reread.

Who holds what: each child holds the poster and the poem sheet; the teacher holds the record sheet. Tool: None. By hand: speaking, reading alone, drafting. Evidence: P1; P2 to P5 (observed); C2 (read); P6; E113.

## Day 116


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Hand back.** Each child gets the written feedback on the talk, with the one component to retry.
- **32 min. Response groups.** Four teacher turns of 8 minutes: meaning (P, Q, AP), sentences (O, R), facts (A, C, D, E) and process (B); the groups come from the record sheet. The others work a named task: rewrite one sentence, redraw one label, or sort fact cards.
- **3 min. Retry.** Parallel pairs: each child retries the selected P2 to P5 component with a partner; the partner keeps a checklist. The original observation stays; the retry is marked shown with help.
- **15 min. Story.** Revise the written story again from the feedback (7 minutes); then rehearse the planned story aloud to a partner with its drawing (8 minutes). The first twenty do not record yet.
- **5 min. Exit.** E116.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 113, "What does the poster tell the listener?"; day 114, "What did the listener want to know more about?"; day 115, "How does one step lead to the next?"; day 116, "What will you change?"
- Written task, day 113 and 116: the story drafted from the plan page and revised after feedback (scored as C3 on 117).
- Look-fors for the talk: (1) whole sentences that answer the listener; (2) facts from the sources; (3) a real-life word connection; (4) the two contrasts; (5) an adjective and an adverb used for the right things.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: poster, listener, contrast, step, real use. Frames: "The poster says ___." "A ___ is slower than a ___." "First ___, so ___." A child may rehearse in the home language first.
- **Access.** A scribe for the story; pointing to a poster label is a start; a child who cannot speak to a group may talk to one panelist; large print; the recorder is offered in a pair.
- **Check accommodations.** Poem and cards are read aloud after a first independent try (recorded as help); extra time inside the same part; a panel question may be given in writing.
- **Reading load.** C2 is about 30 words, read alone first; nothing else printed exceeds a poster label.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second contrast of the child's own to the talk.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Be the panel: sit as the second listener, ask one question after each explanation, and keep the same twelve cells on paper. On 116 retry one component. Record the story on a phone if one is at hand.
- **One projector.** Show the criteria chart; the panel sheet is on paper.
