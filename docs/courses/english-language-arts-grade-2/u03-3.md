# Section 3.3: Words with real uses, exact verbs and two pollinator poems

## Header


Children hear and read two short poems (about 152 words in all) and short cards; the teacher reads each poem aloud first and every stem aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: poems about a bee, butterfly, hummingbird and bat; no sting, injury or fear; the bee's feelings in any poem are imagined.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **L.2.5a**, "Identify real-life connections between words and their use (e.g., describe foods that are spicy or juicy)." Day 85 teaches it with the words fuzzy, sticky, cling and tremble (a real place or thing each fits); day 86 uses it in the child's own sentence. The words dusty, slender and settle are reserved for the day 113 to 115 checks and are never drawn out as real-life examples here, though settle and dusty appear in the poems.
- **L.2.5b**, "Distinguish shades of meaning among closely related verbs ... and closely related adjectives." Day 85 teaches sip and gulp, stroll and dash, and damp, wet and soaked; day 86 chooses among them; day 87 hears them in a poem. The pairs of the day 113 to 115 checks (glide and swoop, warm and hot, drift and dart, cool and cold, creep and rush, big and huge) are never contrasted here, though glide and swoop are in the poem; the poem is read for its sound and picture only.
- **L.2.6**, "Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading and being read to, and responding to texts, including using adjectives and adverbs to describe." Day 86: the thing card and action card (unit 2) decide whether the describing word is an adjective or an adverb. Only a describing word for a noun and one for an action are met.
- **RL.2.10** (day 87): the child rereads two short poems and tells how the speaker sees the visitor; a range record, not a verdict. **RF.2.4b** (day 87): a smooth, expressive second reading; no timer.
- **RF.2.3c, e, f; RF.2.4, 4a, 4c; L.2.2d, 2e; SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3** (cycle 14, days 85 to 87): consonant-le continued and two-syllable long vowels, rereading to confirm meaning, spelling by pattern, and turn, pair, link, clarify.
- **Limits that bite.** The poems are read aloud then chorally; a child decodes only the chorus and the cards. The check poem *Before I Fly* and the verses of day 113 to 115 are never shown. Bees and others are not handled; pictures are the close look.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 shades of meaning (tap, knock, bang; tiny, little) and look, peek, glance return as review. The unit 2 thing card and action card returns on day 86; perform-a-stanza (unit 1, day 21) on day 87. The step chain and the 01-a steps return on day 87 to join poem lines to steps.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A poem is not retold like an article. Tarry means stay and waste time; the refrain says do not tarry. The poems' facts match 01-a: pollen clings to a bee's fuzz, some rubs off on the stigma.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned.** None are scored here. The unit plan's rows for L.2.5, L.2.5a, L.2.5b and RL.2.10 are owned by sections 3.11 and 3.12. This section reserves their cases and never uses them: dusty, slender and settle as form words with real-life uses; the verse pairs glide and swoop, warm and hot, drift and dart, cool and cold, creep and rush, big and huge; and the poem *Before I Fly*.

**Exit questions.** Spoken, one ask per stem, answered by the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner and the teacher notes hands. Shown is the right answer in the child's words; shown with help is right after one prompt; not yet seen is another answer or none. None reuses a taught worked example or a check case.
- **E85** (day 85). Item (a): "Say where you could find something bumpy, and why the word fits." Key: any real place or thing that has bumps, such as a stony road or an orange peel, with the reason. Wrong: the word repeated with no place (P). Item (b): "Which is faster, stroll or dash? Show with your arms." Key: dash; a slow walk and a quick run shown. Wrong: the same speed shown (Q). Item (c): "Which is colder, chilly or freezing? How do you know?" Key: freezing; ice or snow, or "chilly is a little cold". Wrong: both the same (Q).
- **E86** (day 86). Card: "A slug crawls onto a leaf." Item (one ask each): "Say one describing word for the slug." then "Say one describing word for how the slug crawls." Key: a word for the slug (slimy, small, gray) and a word for the crawl (slowly, quickly, carefully). Wrong: "crawls slow" written without -ly, or a describing word for the leaf instead of the slug (R).
- Day 87 has no exit; the oral reading record and the heard column are the evidence.

**Cycle 14 written check** (day 87, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Five independent spellings, none a day 83 to 86 word. (1) "A ____ has a sharp point." needle. (2) "Do not ____ on the rock." stumble. (3) "She has a ____ on her nose." freckle. (4) "The ____ flew the plane." pilot. (5) "Say it ____." again. Wrong: "needl" and "freckl" omit the final syllable (Y); "needel" and "freckel" swap the last two letters (AN); "pilat" and "pilit" misspell the second syllable (T); "agin" and "agen" write the odd part as if regular (AM). After marking, dictate "saddle" and "tiny" as unscored practice.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The word ladder (new, day 85): a row of three picture cards from least to most (damp, wet, soaked), kept by the teacher on day 85 and pairs from day 86. The thing card and action card (unit 2, day 56) return on day 86. The step chain and arrow cards (day 79) return on day 87 to join poem lines to steps. The poems are printed large, one page each; the chorus is also on a strip on the wall. The talk routine, heard-six column, word wall, sound-spelling-map and syllable-types chart stay up.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles, sort and reread, ten children each; the day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations. Day 87 uses the 25-minute check form.
- **Key moment 1, day 85: the word ladder.** Ideal instrument: three real or pictured objects in a row. Spec: fits; paper.
- **Key moment 2, day 87: perform a stanza.** Ideal instrument: six groups of five and the printed stanza. Spec: fits; no recording needed.
- **Tools.** None. No account is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** The poem is written on the board; the ladder is three drawings; pairs act the verbs; tiles are letters on card.

## 6. Days

## Day 85


- **5 min. Retrieval miss.** If many missed day 84's retrieval, model it now: say a real use of tiny (a tiny seed) and show look, peek and stare with the picture cards; otherwise hear the six say a real use of one grade 1 word.
- **8 min. Hear a poem.** Teacher reads 03-a, Land, Cling, Carry (about 75 words), twice with the pictures. Children echo the chorus "Land, cling, carry". Tarry is said to mean stay and waste time. No decoding of the stanzas; the chorus is read from the wall strip.
- **12 min. Words and real uses (L.2.5a).** Four words from the poem and 01-a: fuzzy, sticky, cling, tremble. For each: say it, act it, then "Where would you find something fuzzy?" Children give a real place or thing (a fuzzy blanket, sticky tape, a wet leaf clings to a boot, a leaf trembles in wind). Pairs add one of their own.
- **12 min. Near words (L.2.5b).** Teacher builds the word ladder: damp, wet, soaked with three towel pictures; acts sip and gulp with a cup; stroll and dash with arms. Pairs place the cards and say "___ is more than ___". No verse pair of the checks is used.
- **8 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "Which word fits the hummingbird better, hover or land? Why?" Children say "The word ___ fits because ___". Hear the six.
- **5 min. Write.** Each child completes "The ___ bee ___ on the flower" with one describing word and one exact verb from the wall, a few words each; teacher scribes for a child who asks.
- **5 min. Exit.** E85 (field 3).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** "Words and real uses" ("Where would you find something fuzzy?"), the ladder with sip and gulp, stroll and dash acted, and the write line ask what the exit asks (a real use, why a word fits, which is more). Instead: sort picture cards under the words fuzzy, sticky, cling and tremble, or judge a given use ("a fuzzy ice cube") and fix it.
- **5 min. Choral reread.** Everyone reads the chorus again from the wall strip with the poem page open; no new words.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Yesterday's commonest miss (S, T or Y) written beside the right word and read aloud.
- **15 min. Reread and sort.** Reread the cycle 14 card in pairs for accuracy. Sort twelve -le cards by the letters before le: ble (marble, humble, noble), dle (cradle, noodle, saddle), kle (wrinkle, sparkle), zle (nozzle, drizzle, dazzle). Spell cradle and sparkle on paper and use each in a sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (section 2.1, field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the cycle 14 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 3 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which two letters come before le in cradle?"

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the word ladder and the wall strip; pairs hold the near-word cards. Tool: None. By hand: acting verbs, placing cards, writing a few words, sorting. Evidence: E85; the written sentence; the heard column (heard-six and oral card).

## Day 86


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E85 (P or Q), show a fuzzy peach card and a smooth plum card and have pairs say where each word fits; or lay stroll and dash on the ladder.
- **8 min. Reread the poem.** Teacher and class reread 03-a chorally; children hear the exact words (hover, swoop) and the pictures. They do not contrast verbs of the day 113 to 115 checks.
- **14 min. Pick the exact word.** On cards, four plain sentences with a weak verb or word ("The bird goes to the flower." "The bee is on the petal."). Pairs choose a stronger word from the wall (hovers, lands, tremble, sticky) and say "I chose ___ because it shows ___". The teacher models one; the 15 pairs do the rest. Hear the six pairs.
- **12 min. Describe a pollinator.** Each pair chooses one of the four pictures of 01-a (bee, butterfly, bat, hummingbird). They say two sentences with one describing word for the animal and one exact word for what it does, deciding thing or action with the thing card and action card (unit 2).
- **10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "Why is your word better than a plainer one?" Children say "My word ___ shows ___". Hear the six.
- **6 min. Write.** Each child writes their best sentence on a card, a few words, and draws the picture it makes.
- **5 min. Exit.** E86 (field 3).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** "Pick the exact word" and "Describe a pollinator" ask for one describing word and one word for the action, the exit's shape. Instead: find and mark describing words and action words in given sentences, or sort word cards on the thing card and action card.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Yesterday's commonest miss (S, T or Y) written beside the right word and read aloud.
- **15 min. Fresh passage and dictation.** Read the cycle 14 card, a fresh decodable passage of about 40 words (cycle 14 patterns only); confirm meaning after decoding. Dictate two words (pilot and needle) and one sentence with again or said. Text to make: the cycle 14 passage (field 11).
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (section 2.1, field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the cycle 14 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 4 of five at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which letter in again is the odd part?"

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the word cards and the thing and action cards; the teacher holds the ladder. Tool: None. By hand: choosing cards, writing a sentence, dictation. Evidence: E86; the sentence card; the dictation; the heard column (heard-six and oral card).

## Day 87


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E86 (R), use the thing card and action card on "The slug crawls ___": name the action, then choose its word.
- **12 min. Hear and name the visitor.** Teacher reads 03-b, A Dusty Visitor (about 77 words), twice with the pictures. Children name the visitor from the clues in the words (fuzzy, yellow, round, small, buzzed) and point to the line. The real-life use of the title word is not drawn out.
- **12 min. Rehearse for expression.** Pairs reread 03-b stanza by stanza and the 03-a chorus, making the voice fit each line. The teacher hears the six (1 minute each) for accuracy and expression (RF.2.4b record) while the others rehearse in pairs.
- **12 min. Poem lines and steps.** Pairs match three lines of 03-b (dipped in a flower; gold on its legs; dusted the stigma) to steps of the chain from 01-a and say "This line is step ___ because ___". The class checks one match aloud.
- **10 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "Which describing word in 03-b tells you most about the next flower?" Children say "The word ___ tells me ___". Hear the six.
- **9 min. Perform a stanza.** Six groups of five each read two lines of one stanza of 03-a or 03-b for the class while everyone joins the refrain (unit 1, day 21). Listeners say one thing they heard.
- **15 min. Written check (foundations).** Cycle 14 written check (field 3), unscored.
- **5 min. Reader.** Reread the cycle 14 card in pairs for expression.
- **25 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card for the whole cycle (12 minutes), the rest in stations. Group 5 of five.
- **15 min. Fresh response.** Day 83's six read cycle 14 words to the teacher (12 minutes, 2 minutes each) while the others partner-read a pattern card and spell two dictated words; no new teaching.

Who holds what is carried: groups hold the printed stanza; the teacher holds the step chain. Tool: None. By hand: matching, rehearsing, spelling five words. Evidence: the oral reading record; the cycle 14 check; the cycle 14 oral card records (group 5 of five, the last of the cycle); the heard column.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 85, "Which word fits the hummingbird better, hover or land? Why?"; day 86, "Why is your word better than a plainer one?"; day 87, "Which describing word in 03-b tells you most about the next flower?"
- Written task, day 86: one sentence with a describing word and an exact word, a few words each, with a drawing.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) the exact verb or describing word is stronger than a plain one; (2) the word fits the animal in the picture; (3) the child can say a real thing the word fits.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Each day opens with one repair of at most 5 minutes for the commonest miss of the day before; day 85 models the day 84 retrieval miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence each day; the record sheet has a heard column. Day 87 closes cycle 14 with group 5 of five.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of both poems by day 87; accuracy first; no timer.
- **Earlier work returning.** Shades of meaning (grade 1, tap, knock, bang); look, peek, glance; the thing card and action card (unit 2, day 56); perform a stanza (unit 1, day 21); the step chain (day 79).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: fuzzy, sticky, cling, tremble, hover, damp, wet, soaked, sip, gulp, stroll, dash, tarry. Frames: "___ is more than ___." "I chose ___ because it shows ___." "You can find something ___ in ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Acting a verb is an answer; picture cards sit beside every word; a child who cannot write draws and the teacher scribes; the poem is heard before it is seen.
- **Check accommodations.** The cycle 14 check is read twice; a child may say the spelling aloud and the teacher writes it, recorded as help.
- **Reading load.** At most 150 printed words a day: the chorus strip (about 10 words), the cards (about 40), the cycle 14 card (about 40), the poem pages (read aloud first).
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a third rung for the ladder with a new word set, or find a poem line whose word could be swapped for a stronger or weaker one and say what changes.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read each poem aloud twice. Act the words together with a cup and a towel. Spell three -le words with letter cards.
- **One projector.** Show the poem page and the ladder side by side; the child answers aloud and the teacher marks on the printed card.
