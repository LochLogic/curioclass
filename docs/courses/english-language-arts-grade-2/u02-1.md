# Section 2.1: Beginnings, endings and the language of fossils

## Header


Children read short labels, cards and the cycle reader; the teacher reads the story, the account and every stem aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words on a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.2.5**, "Describe the overall structure of a story, including describing how the beginning introduces the story and the ending concludes the action." Day 33 hears the whole shape (the beginning, the middle steps First, Next, Then, At last, the ending) in *The Mark in the Stone*; day 34 teaches how the beginning introduces; day 36 how the ending concludes. Section 2.2 builds the story map on it.
- **W.2.3**, "Write narratives in which they recount a well-elaborated event or short sequence of events..." Day 33 only: two ordered events and an ending in a few words, as a retrieval of grade 1; elaboration, thoughts and feelings wait for section 2.6.
- **RI.2.1**, "Ask and answer such questions as who, what, where, when, why, and how." Day 35, on the Mary Anning account, with the six question words card.
- **RI.2.4**, "Determine the meaning of words and phrases in a text relevant to a grade 2 topic or subject area." Day 37, fossil words in article 01-a.
- **L.2.4 / L.2.4a / L.2.4c**, "Use sentence-level context as a clue" and "Use a known root word as a clue." Day 37: multiple-meaning words pick, brush, fan, mark; roots care and hunt. The day 57 items on mold, cast and collector belong here (field 3) but are not taught as examples: cast and collect come later.
- **RF.2.3a, b, f; RF.2.4, 4a to 4c; L.2.2d, 2e** (days 33 to 37, cycle 6): r-controlled vowels, open and closed syllables, the cycle's irregular words, reading the cycle reader with accuracy and rereading to confirm meaning, spelling by pattern and checking with the word wall and a beginning dictionary. **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3**: the talk routine (turn, pair, link, clarify) runs on days 33, 34, 35, 36 and 37.
- Skills carried: e2-story-structure, e2-narrative, e2-spelling, e2-fluency, e2-vowel-teams, e2-tricky-spellings, e2-discussion, e2-wh-questions, e2-word-strategies.
- **Limits that bite.** A child reads no more than 150 printed words a day, so the story, the account and the article are read aloud and children read one page, a section or a card. Fossil time is only "very long ago", with no numbers and no comparison with any family belief. Anning's poverty and exclusion are told as plain facts and not dwelt on. Say aloud on day 33 that Amara and Dr. Lee are invented and that collecting fossils is not allowed in many places, so children ask first.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 (story parts, wh-questions, the five syllable types, inflections) is retrieved on day 33. Unit 1 supplies the talk routine, the heard-six column, the story-map and Mira's story. Section 2.2 opens on day 38 with the story map.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The text leaves the kind of shell open on purpose; do not name one. Day 35's facts come from the sources named in text 02 (the Oxford museum page, the Geological Society page); "said she learned" is Mary's own later account.

## 3. Checks, written first



**Unit assessment items owned.** Item names: 57-RI.2.4, 57-L.2.4a and 57-L.2.4c (rows above, four minutes each, on the day 57 cold text). This section prepares them by the strategies of day 37 (sentence clue, known root) and by the page 8 definition of a mold; it never uses mold, cast or collector as a worked example. The day 40 and 41 rows are in section 2.2.

**Exit questions.** A reading exit marked Passage asks on a named part of the unit's own texts, read aloud twice, never on a card and never on the passage the day works as its example; for those exits, "the card" below means that passage. Each exit is two spoken items, each one ask. Item (a): "Point to the picture that goes with the card I read." All children show A or B on a two-card hold-up. Item (b): the day's one ask, answered aloud by the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner. Shown means the right picture and an answer that uses a word from the card; shown with help means the right picture and an answer after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means the other picture or no answer. Cards are printed "invented for practice" (day 35 carries true facts, so it says so) and read aloud twice. None reuses a story page, a worked example or a check case. If many miss one, the next day's opening repair shows the wrong example beside the right one.
- **E33** (day 33). Passage: The Mark in the Stone, page 10 (Amara draws, wraps and packs her stone), reread aloud twice; not a page worked today (g2-u2-03). Reread page 10 of The Mark in the Stone aloud twice. Ask: "Tell me what Amara does on page 10, in order." Answer: Amara draws her stone in her notebook, then wraps it in soft paper, then lays it in her bag.
- **E34** (day 34). Passage: The Park Cart, pages 1 to 3 (Barb, Dad and the cart set off up the path), read aloud twice (g2-u2-cr6). Read pages 1 to 3 of The Park Cart aloud twice. Ask: "Tell me what the beginning of The Park Cart introduces." Answer: Barb and her big yellow cart, Dad loading jars of corn and a fern, and the start of the trip up the path.
- **E35** (day 35). Card: "Mary Anning said she learned fossil collecting from her father. She became a fossil collector and dealer. Many people who knew fossils wanted her advice." Ask: "Say one question about Mary that this card answers." Answer: A question the card answers, such as "Who did Mary learn fossil collecting from?" (her father) or "What did Mary become?" (a fossil collector and dealer).
- **E36** (day 36). Passage: The Park Cart, pages 1 to 6, read aloud twice: the beginning (the trip up the path starts) and the ending (they can go on) (g2-u2-cr6). Read The Park Cart aloud twice. Ask: "Tell me how the ending finishes what the beginning started." Answer: The beginning starts Barb and Dad taking the cart up the path; a jar falls, but the corn is not hurt, so at the end they can go on up the path.
- **E37** (day 37). Card: "A scientist will record the find. The recorder in the lab keeps all the notes." Ask: "Use a small word you know to say what recorder means here." Answer: A recorder is a person who records; the small word is record.

**Cycle 6 written check** (day 37, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Four independent spellings; none is a day 33 to 36 word. (1) "The cow sleeps in the ____." barn. (2) "Please ____ the soup with a spoon." stir. (3) "This rope is ____, not long." short. (4) "Do not ____ your hand on the hot pan." hurt. Wrong: "bern", "ster", "shurt" and "hirt" each write a taught vowel correspondence wrongly (Y); tag Y. After the check is marked, dictate "market" and "tiger" as unscored practice (closed and open syllables).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The story-map (who, where and when, what happens) from unit 1 is held by the teacher on days 33 and 34 and gains a beginning box and an ending box on days 34 and 36; partners hold it from day 36. The six question words card, the sound-spelling-map, the word wall (yellow, orange, great, colors, turns, sing, that, start) and the wait signal stay on the wall. The talk routine (turn, pair, link, clarify) and the heard-six column are unit 1 routines, practised here. The oral card for cycle 6 is read by six children a day (heard column). Beginning dictionaries are shared by pairs from day 35.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles (spell the day's words), sort (card sort by pattern) and reread (partner reread of the reader or phrase cards), ten children each. The day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations.
- **Key moment 1, day 34: the beginning box on the story-map.** Ideal instrument: a large wall map with movable cards. Spec: fits; paper. Gaps and asks: none.
- **Key moment 2, day 37: the sentence-clue and root card.** Ideal instrument: a two-step card (read the sentence; find the small word). Spec: fits; paper.
- **Tools.** None. Reference screens for later days are not needed here. No account is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** The story map is a sheet folded in thirds; the story is read from a printed copy; tiles are letters on torn card; the word wall is a sheet on the board.

## 6. Days

- **12 min. What the text tells and what it leaves open.** Two chart columns, "The text tells" and "The text leaves open". Model one of each (it tells: a curved mark was pressed into the stone; it leaves open: what kind of shell it was). Pairs add one of each in turn; turn, pair, link (a link card), clarify. Hear the six.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 33, "What does the text tell, and what does it leave open?"; day 34, "What does the beginning start that the rest of the story must finish?"; day 35, "What did Anning find, and how?"; day 36, "How does the ending finish what the beginning started?"

- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) names an event or fact from the text; (2) uses the word beginning or ending for its job; (3) points to the line or picture that tells.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 34 to 37 open with one repair of at most 5 minutes, for the commonest exit miss; if none, a retrieval of the day before. The foundations opening repairs the day before's commonest spelling or reading miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence each day, using only patterns taught on an earlier day. The record sheet has a heard column.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of The Park Cart by day 37, with accuracy first; no timer.
- **Story routines.** Mira's unit 1 story returns on days 33 and 36 as review; grade 1 whole-story parts return through the story map.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: fossil, discover, notice, examine, wonder, careful, clue, evidence, beginning, ending. Frames: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." "The text tells ___; it does not tell ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print cards; the A and B pictures differ in color and shape. A child who cannot write the sentence draws and the teacher scribes.
- **Check accommodations.** Cards are read aloud; the spelling check can be answered with tiles.
- **Reading load.** A child reads one reader page, one 100-word excerpt, and cards (under 150 words a day); the story, the account and the article are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second line in the ending that closes a different part of the beginning, or write a different ending for the story and say what it concludes.
