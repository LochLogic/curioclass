## 1. Standards and practices


Days 99 to 101 are the unit's first check. Nothing new is taught and no day opens with a repair; a Check day only collects, and repairs wait for day 103 (section 3.8). Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Children read at most 150 printed words a day: the two check cards of day 99 (about 145 words) are the whole load, and every stem and exit is read aloud. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen; in the unit plan's 0 and 1, shown is 1. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: the cards hold bees, butterflies and a moth at flowers; no sting, injury, death or illness. No child touches an insect.

- **W.2.7**, "Participate in shared research and writing projects (e.g., read a number of books on a single topic to produce a report; record science observations)." Day 99 scores R1, the child's own located fact in the joint report.
- **W.2.8**, "Recall information from experiences or gather information from provided sources to answer a question." Day 99 collects R2: the child reads the two new cards alone and writes one fact from each that answers "How does each one carry pollen?", with its card named. Corrected on 100, scored on 101.
- **W.2.2**, "Write informative/explanatory texts in which they introduce a topic, use facts and definitions to develop points, and provide a concluding statement or section." R3 is begun on 99, completed on 100, scored on 101 as four separate cells (topic, facts, definition, conclusion).
- **W.2.5**, "With guidance and support from adults and peers, focus on a topic and strengthen writing as needed by revising and editing." Revision on 100; editing on 101.
- **SL.2.1a to 1c and SL.2.3** (R6): "Ask and answer questions about what a speaker says in order to clarify comprehension, gather additional information, or deepen understanding of a topic or issue." Fifteen children are observed on 99 and fifteen on 100; all groups retry on 101; scored on 103.
- **L.2.2d, L.2.2e** (day 101, R4 and R5): spell one word by a taught pattern, then check it in a printed beginning-dictionary entry.
- **RF.2.3c to 3f; RF.2.4; L.2.2d, 2e** (foundations, cycle 17): -ch and -tch, -er and -est, reading to confirm meaning, spelling by pattern.
- **Limits that bite here.** The Bee Visit and Butterfly Visit cards, the day 100 feedback sheets, the day 101 caption and entry, and the five cycle 17 check words (section 3.8) are reserved and never appear in teaching. No child is told which fact answers the question (error AD). The paragraph frame is withdrawn on day 99; the chart of the four jobs of the day 98 model stays up.
- **Connections.** Unit 1: the four-row feedback sheet. Unit 2: revise, then score. Section 3.6: the model paragraph, the plan page, the talk card.
- **The teacher page must explain.** The cards are invented for practice; a bee that shakes pollen loose and a butterfly whose tongue touches it are two ways of carrying it, so a good R2 names both. A wrong fact is not helped on the day: it is recorded, fed back in writing, and repaired on day 103.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Items owned:** R1 (scored 99), R2 (collected 99, corrected 100, scored 101), R3 (scored 101), R4 and R5 (collected 101, scored 103), R6 (observed 99 and 100, retried 101, scored 103). Keys, look-fors and samples are on the unit plan, field 5; each cell is marked separately.

**Record sheet.** One row a child: R1, R2 (relevant fact; card named), R3 cells, R3 revision, R6 cells, R4, R5, and a heard column naming who was heard.

**Exit questions.** Unscored practice. The teacher reads the card aloud and says it is invented. One ask; the rotating six answer aloud (about 20 seconds each), the rest tell a partner. Shown is the right answer in the child's words; shown with help is right after one prompt; not yet seen is another answer or none.
- **E99.** This card is invented for practice. Read aloud twice: "A moth visits a flower at night. Its body touches the pollen. It flies to a new flower and some pollen stays there." Say: "Say one sentence that tells what a paragraph about this moth is about." Key: A topic sentence that names the moth and what it does for flowers, such as A moth carries pollen from flower to flower. Wrong: Its body touches the pollen (F); The flower is pale (E); A moth (O).
- **E100.** This card is invented for practice. Show the draft: "A moth helps flowers." Show the written feedback: "Say how the pollen gets to the new flower." Show the source line: "Pollen sticks to its body and goes with the moth." Say: "Say one sentence you would add." Key: A new sentence that answers the feedback by joining the sticking to the new flower, such as Pollen sticks to its body, so it goes with the moth to the new flower. Wrong: Moths are helpers for flowers (I); Moths are gray (E).
- **E101.** This card is invented for practice. Show the caption: "A moth rests on the lach." Show the entry: "latch: a catch that holds a gate shut." Say: "Use the entry. Say the word the way it should be spelled, letter by letter." Key: l, a, t, c, h. Wrong: l, a, c, h (J); l, a, t, h (Y). Same caption and entry. Say: "Say which letter the caption was missing." Key: The t before ch. Wrong: The first letter (Y).

## 6. Days

## Day 99


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **10 min. R2: gather alone.** Each child reads the Bee Visit and Butterfly Visit cards (invented for practice, about 145 words) and writes two slips: how each carries pollen, card named. A child who asks has the card read aloud after a first try (shown with help).
- **25 min. R3: begin the paragraph.** From the slips each child writes a topic sentence, two or more facts and a word to define, and notes an ending idea. The frame is withdrawn; the chart of four jobs is up. Drafts are kept for written feedback after class.
- **18 min. R6: discussion in fives, children 1 to 15.** Three groups of five at the teacher table, 6 minutes each: round 1 (3 minutes) each says one fact and its card, about 35 seconds; round 2 (3 minutes) each links to the last speaker or asks a clarifying question and is answered. The teacher fills four cells a child. The other 25 draft in pairs, quietly.
- **5 min. Exit.** E99.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.
- **2 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of the day 98 foundations exit, written beside the right word and read aloud.
- **15 min. Reader.** Pattern model, 3 minutes: -tch and -ch; -er and -est. Then read Pitch and Catch in the Garden (cycle 17 reader, section 3.6), map the odd words, spell five words and one sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation: round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the cycle 17 oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which letters sit before the e in taller?"

Who holds what: each child holds the two slips and the draft; the teacher holds the talk card, tally and record sheet. Tool: None. By hand: reading alone, slips, drafting, speaking in turn, spelling. Evidence: R1; R2; R3; R6 (children 1 to 15); E99; the oral card.

## Day 100


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. R2: correct against the cards.** Hand back the written feedback. Each child checks each slip against its card line and corrects it (C, E, AE); kept for scoring on 101.
- **15 min. R3: complete the paragraph.** Add facts, definition and conclusion from the corrected slips. A child who asks has the feedback line read aloud.
- **5 min. Partner feedback.** Pairs read each other's paragraph and fill the four-row feedback sheet, a few words a row.
- **10 min. R3: revise.** Each child changes one idea using the teacher's feedback and the partner's sheet. Before and after pages are stapled.
- **18 min. R6: discussion in fives, children 16 to 30.** As day 99; the other 25 write quietly.
- **5 min. Exit.** E100.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.
- **2 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of day 99 beside the right word.
- **15 min. Reread and use the pattern.** Reread Pitch and Catch in the Garden for expression, 4 minutes. Sort ten words by -tch, -ch, -er, -est; spell four and write one sentence.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; six at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which ending makes a word mean the most?"

Who holds what: each child holds the draft and both versions; pairs hold the feedback sheet. Tool: None. By hand: correcting, finishing, peer feedback, revising, spelling. Evidence: R2 corrected; R3 complete; revision; R6 (children 16 to 30); E100; the oral card.

## Day 101


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **15 min. R4 and R5: edit and check.** Each child edits the revised paragraph from a checklist (capital, end mark, spelling), then writes the missing word of a fresh caption by the taught pattern and checks it in a printed beginning-dictionary entry (read aloud to a child who asks: shown with help). Collected for scoring on 103.
- **18 min. R6 retry in six groups.** One group of five at a time at the teacher table, 3 minutes, to retry a missed turn, link, clarification or answer, written feedback in hand. A right retry is shown with help and never replaces the first mark. The other 25 rehearse reading their paragraph aloud to a partner.
- **5 min. Exit.** E101.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.
- **22 min. Quiet work.** Independent reading of band texts 01-b to 01-e, 10 minutes; shared-project record, 7 minutes (each child tells what was added to the joint question and a partner or the teacher writes it); reread the paragraph for sharing, 5 minutes.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** The commonest miss of day 100.
- **15 min. Fresh passage and dictation.** A fresh cycle 17 passage of about 40 words; confirm the meaning after decoding. Dictate two words and one sentence with taller.
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; six at the oral card.
- **3 min. Close.** "Which letter in taller is doubled?"

Who holds what: the teacher holds the retry record; each child holds the revised paragraph. Tool: None. By hand: editing, spelling with an entry, retry, independent reading. Evidence: R4 and R5; R6 retry; R1 to R3 retained; E101; the oral card.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 99, "How does each visitor carry pollen?"; day 100, "Which of your partner's facts answers the question?"; day 101, "What did you add after the feedback?"
- Written task, day 100: the complete paragraph with its revision, from the child's own slips.
- Look-fors, the unit plan's R3 rows, scored separately: (1) an introduced topic; (2) accurate facts from the cards, each with its card named; (3) a definition that helps a reader; (4) a conclusion that completes the explanation. The revision is a fifth cell: one idea changed after written feedback.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** None on Check days; the foundations opening repair is the only repair slot.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and a sentence each day (record sheet heard column).
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of Pitch and Catch in the Garden by day 100; no timer.
- **Earlier work returning.** Source tags and the joint chart (days 88 to 95); the model paragraph's four jobs (day 98); the talk card (unit 1); revision after written feedback (unit 2).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: pollen, carry, tongue, topic, fact, ending. Frames: "The card says ___." "I add ___ because ___." "Which part do you mean?" A child may say the fact in the home language first; the card named is still required.
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer; a child who cannot write dictates to a scribe and names the card; large print on request; the discussion record counts speech, not handwriting.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud after the first independent try, recorded as help; oral answers for R2; extra time inside the same part.
- **Reading load.** Two cards (about 145 printed words) on day 99; later texts are the child's own paragraph and a caption; every stem is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second card line that supports the same fact and name both cards.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Put the two cards on a table, read nothing, and let the child write one fact from each with its card named. Take the partner role in discussion: say a fact, then ask "Which part do you mean?" Give written feedback the next morning and let the child fix one idea. On day 101 check one spelling in the entry strip together.
- **One projector.** Show the cards one at a time, then the day's record sheet; groups of five meet at a table while the rest face their drafts.
