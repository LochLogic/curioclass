# Section 3.12: The recorded stories and the end of the unit


## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


Days 117 and 118 close the unit: the bee-view stories are recorded with a drawing (117, first twenty; 118, last ten), the child's typing and audio contribution is kept as evidence, and the formal and informal story lines are revised. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Children read at most 150 printed words a day: their own story plan and drawing labels; every stem is read aloud. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: a bee or butterfly visits flowers; the bee's thoughts are imagined and the class says so; no sting, injury, death or illness. Recordings stay in the class and are not posted.

- **SL.2.4** (117, 118), "Tell a story or recount an experience with appropriate facts and relevant, descriptive details, speaking audibly in coherent sentences." **SL.2.5** (117, 118), "Create audio recordings of stories or poems; add drawings or other visual displays to stories or recounts of experiences when appropriate to clarify ideas, thoughts, and feelings." **SL.2.6** (117, 118), "Produce complete sentences when appropriate to task and situation in order to provide requested detail or clarification."
- **W.2.6** (117), "With guidance and support from adults, use a variety of digital tools to produce and publish writing, including in collaboration with peers": the typing contribution of 102 and its revision on 105 are retained beside the audio contribution.
- **W.2.3** (117), "Write narratives in which they recount a well-elaborated event or short sequence of events": C3, the written story drafted on 113, revised on 114 and 116, scored on 117.
- **L.2.3a** (118), "Compare formal and informal uses of English": the 112 comparison, revised after feedback, scored on 118.
- **RF.2.3c** (117, 118): the last six P6 records; 118 retains all thirty. **RF.2.3b**: an insecure pattern gets a fresh word on 118, never a new test.
- **Limits that bite here.** No new teaching and no new same-day score on 118: the earlier work, after written feedback and a chance to revise, is scored. A first recording made on 118 is never the scored one. The 118 comparison lines and the E117 and E118 cards are used nowhere before. Home ways of speaking are never ranked.
- **Connections.** 3.10 (the recorder shown, the comparison collected), 3.11 (the rehearsal recordings, the response routine), the bee-view story 02 (the model read on 104 and 112, not reread here), sections 3.8 and 3.9 (typing, feedback).
- **The teacher page must explain.** How the thirty children move through the recorder in two days (counted below) and what a child with no working device does.

**Record sheet.** One row a child: S1 (order, facts, detail, audible, whole sentences), S2 (audio saved, drawing linked), S3, S4 (typing contribution from 102 and 105, audio contribution), S5, C3 cells (events, actions, thoughts, feelings, time words, closure), P6, and a heard column.

**Exit questions** (unscored practice; read aloud, said to be invented; the rotating six answer aloud).
- **E117.** This card is invented for practice. Read aloud: "A butterfly story. First I landed on a leaf. Then a drop of water fell on my wing." Say: "Say the next line of the story so it tells what the butterfly feels." Key: A line that names a feeling, such as That made me feel scared. Wrong: Next I flew to a red flower (AI); Then the water dried (AI).
- **E118.** A line said to be invented: "Hey, wait till you hear this!" Two asks. Ask 1: "Say that line again so it suits a class visitor you do not know." Key: a line that tells what the story is about, such as "Today my story is about a butterfly." Wrong: the line said again unchanged (Z); "That way of talking is wrong." (X). Ask 2: "Say what you added for the visitor." Key: what the story is about. Wrong: "Nothing." (Z).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The written story with its feedback trail, the plan page and the drawing, the register comparison with feedback, the typed sentence of 102 and 105 with its feedback, the rehearsal recordings of the final ten (116), and the thirty P6 records. The recorder's files stay on the class device.
- **Recording windows (counted).** Day 117 topic: 20 children at 2 minutes each plus 10 minutes of changeovers is 50 minutes, the first twenty in turn; the other ten, in 117 foundations, revise and rerecord in 25 minutes (10 at 2 minutes plus 5 of changeovers). Day 118 topic: the remaining ten at 2 minutes each plus 5 minutes of changeovers end by minute 25; the feedback window of 15 minutes lets up to 20 children rerecord a short phrase at 30 seconds each (10 minutes) with 5 minutes of changeovers. If more need a rerecord, the correction is made on paper.
- **Fallback.** If the device fails, the story is told live to the teacher and a partner, the recording standards are marked incomplete (U), and the recording is made when a device is found; the unit is not held up.
- **Key moment 1, days 117 and 118: the recording.** Ideal: the recorder at recording distance. Spec fits (key tool, check unaided). Key moment 2, day 118: the revised comparison. Ideal: before and after sheets. Spec fits.
- **Tools.** Audio recorder (key), 117 check unaided. A room with nothing: live telling and a signed teacher checklist.

## 6. Days

## Day 117


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **50 min. Recording, first twenty.** Children 1 to 20 record in turn: the bee-view story in order with facts, descriptive detail and whole sentences, and explain how the drawing clarifies it (2 minutes each). A partner chooses a wording or a place of the drawing with the child (one decision, aloud). The other children read independently, rehearse in pairs and wait; changeovers total 10 minutes.
- **5 min. Last story revision (C3).** Everyone revises the written story once, from the feedback.
- **5 min. Exit.** E117.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The exit asks the child to produce (restored 2026-11-20 from a given-answer pick); the practice takes a different shape, with a given card or line to work from and the answer made, not picked.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **12 min. P6 decoding sample.** The last six children.
- **23 min. Independent matched reading.** As days 113 to 116.
- **25 min. Final ten revise and rerecord.** Children who made rehearsal recordings on 116 revise after the written feedback and rerecord, 2 minutes each plus 5 minutes of changeovers; the other twenty read or rehearse.

Who holds what: the teacher holds the device and the sheet. Tool: audio recorder, 50 minutes in the topic block and 25 in foundations. By hand: telling, drawing, revising. Evidence: S1 to S4; C3 (scored); P6; E117.

## Day 118


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **25 min. Recording, last ten.** The remaining ten children record, 2 minutes each plus 5 minutes of changeovers, done by minute 25. These are the publication recordings: unscored today.
- **18 min. Formal and informal story lines.** Six groups of five, 3 minutes each with the teacher; each group revises its 112 comparison using the written feedback and says which line fits a presentation and which fits familiar talk. The other children rehearse or read.
- **15 min. Feedback window.** Every child has a written note on the 117 recording; up to 20 children rerecord a short phrase (30 seconds each, 10 minutes, 5 minutes of changeovers) or correct a drawing or caption.
- **2 min. Exit.** E118.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The comparison chooses which line fits a listener; the exit says a given line again for a stranger (fix).

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **15 min. Fresh word.** For a child with an insecure vowel-team pattern (RF.2.3b), a fresh word is read in a short turn; never a whole new test.
- **30 min. Flexible groups.** Map, blend, matched decodable, spell and reread, from the P6 and cycle records.
- **15 min. Independent reading.** Matched reading and reread.

Who holds what: the teacher holds the device, the sheet and the heard column. Tool: audio recorder, 25 + 15 minutes. By hand: comparison, drawing correction. Evidence: S1 to S5 scored from retained work; E118.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: story, record, listen, feel, imagine. Frames: "First ___, then ___." "I felt ___." "The drawing shows ___." A child may record in the home language first and again in English; the English recording is the scored one.
- **Access.** A scribe; the recorder in a pair; pointing to the drawing; large print; a child may record a shorter story; the recording is never required to be perfect.
- **Check accommodations.** Rerecording is offered once; a prompt is read aloud; extra time inside the same part.
- **Reading load.** The child's own plan and drawing labels only.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second visit and a closing that answers the start.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Tell the story to the parent and record it on a phone; draw one picture that helps a listener; the parent writes one decision the child made. On day 118 compare two lines and say who each suits.
- **One projector.** Show the child's drawing while the recording plays.
