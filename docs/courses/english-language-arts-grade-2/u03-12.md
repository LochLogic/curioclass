Days 117 and 118 close the unit: the bee-view stories are recorded with a drawing (117, first twenty; 118, last ten), the child's typing and audio contribution is kept as evidence, and the formal and informal story lines are revised. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Children read at most 150 printed words a day: their own story plan and drawing labels; every stem is read aloud. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud. Content note: a bee or butterfly visits flowers; the bee's thoughts are imagined and the class says so; no sting, injury, death or illness. Recordings stay in the class and are not posted.

Ask 2: "Say what you added for the visitor." Key: what the story is about. Wrong: "Nothing." (Z).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: story, record, listen, feel, imagine. Frames: "First ___, then ___." "I felt ___." "The drawing shows ___." A child may record in the home language first and again in English; the English recording is the scored one.
- **Access.** A scribe; the recorder in a pair; pointing to the drawing; large print; a child may record a shorter story; the recording is never required to be perfect.
- **Check accommodations.** Rerecording is offered once; a prompt is read aloud; extra time inside the same part.
- **Reading load.** The child's own plan and drawing labels only.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second visit and a closing that answers the start.
