# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts, Grade 2

Course `english-language-arts-grade-2`, grade 2, draft, 2026-11-14.

The four units retain the frame's order, owned rows, and 154 unit days. Six course-level days bring the year to 160. ELA meets every school day, so course day equals school day and `school_day` stays blank in `days.csv`. A day has a 60-minute topic block and a 60-minute foundations block. Its kind describes the main topic work; the note locates the other block. The `unit` values remain 1.1 to 4.1 as placeholders until sections are planned.

## Reading, writing, and foundations across the year

The topic texts, rather than isolated standards, organize the work. Day notes name the exact text ids in `data/plans/ela-texts.csv`; those ids refer to planned texts, not books already on file. Reading comes before discussing or writing about the passage. A brief written response to the day's reading accompanies teaching days, alongside the longer products named below. The unit plan must make at least 80 percent of its questions and tasks depend on the texts, including rereading difficult parts; this sequence reserves the work but writes no questions or items.

The school story is read whole on days 1 to 10, with rereading after completion. Early rich read-alouds can exceed students' decoding ability, while daily child-read school and fossil selections establish grade 2 reading. In units 3 and 4 students themselves read harder texts within the grades 2 to 3 band, with support at its high end. Teacher reading never substitutes for this opportunity. Records distinguish independent understanding from supported understanding. Grade 3 receives that distinction, rather than an unsupported claim of independent reading at the band's high end.

The provisional text records below give the demands, use, and reason for placement. No quantitative measure can be reported for an unwritten text. For prose the intended range is the grades 2 to 3 band, approximately 420 to 820, with the final measure, qualitative judgment, reader/task fit, and matched supports established before use. A richer read-aloud version may exceed that range, but each set also needs child-read selections. Verse has no useful prose measure. Read-aloud and child-read versions must be identified in the unit plan, with a teacher note for each support.

Informational anchors occur in every unit, with literary anchors in units 1 and 4 and literary paired texts throughout. Each listed work is read whole or as an explicitly credited traditional retelling. The catalogue groups several articles into single rows, so a numerical literary/informational balance would be misleading until those component texts exist. The frame's writing products remain informative, narrative, research-based informative, and opinion, with a recorded narrative added in unit 3. No grades 3 to 12 writing-share or gateway score is asserted for grade 2.

The named discussion routine is **turn, pair, link, clarify**. It begins with gaining the floor and taking turns, adds linking comments, and asks speakers for clarification, information, and deeper explanation. It runs briefly on most teaching days and at least twice each week, including weeks with assessments. Frames fade; they do not replace text evidence. Sentence work uses the anchor text and then the child's draft. Topic vocabulary is met before, during, and after reading; general words such as respond, describe, explain, and support return across topics.

Foundations follows all 130 lessons in source order. Each five-lesson cycle models and maps a pattern before the matched decodable is read, then rereads, spells, transfers, and checks it. Lesson 5 holds a 15-minute cycle check and a final 15-minute response slot inside the foundations hour. These embedded checks do not add course days or become additional unit assessments. Twenty-four further foundations blocks provide re-teaching, fluency, observation, and response. Irregular words are mapped; context confirms a decoded word rather than supplying a guess. The teacher holds any watch; no timer is shown to a child.

The source map has one row for each of the 143 topic lessons and 130 foundations lessons, plus the added blocks, the 25 decodable readers and poem, and all 16 omitted Labs stage guides. Archive members identify the held source exactly. The combined module 4 unit 3 guides for lessons 5 to 7 and 12 to 13 are represented by separate lesson rows. Topic order is retained; module 2 unit 3 lesson 12 is split to separate revising and editing. Trade books, third-party images, logos, and videos are replaced. The third-hour Labs stages are cut because the frame provides two hours. Overviews and handwriting resources are teacher reference and optional review, not additional scheduled lessons. Later-edition crosswalks are unused. Day writing must supply the frame's full base credit for each block.

## Assessment and retrieval decisions

Every assumed ELA row is from grade 1 or was used earlier this year. Under this job's rule 2, those rows receive retrieval tasks inside teaching days, not readiness checks. Consequently no day carries `assess=readiness`: unit 1 retrieves its grade 1 routines after the hook on day 1; units 2, 3, and 4 retrieve their particular prerequisites as the work begins. The specific grade 1 ids are in the day notes. No older, unrefreshed ELA prerequisite was found. The frame's older language calling these checks is flagged below, not silently implemented against the newer rule.

On an assessment row, `codes` names the evidence reserved for that task. Other foundations instruction is still identified by its cycle and lesson in the note. A multi-day assessment is one evidence collection across its named components: every listed parent code receives all applicable lettered parts, and the day notes identify products or observed performances where a written response alone would miss a part. Parts not yet taught in the continued foundations sequence are not prematurely scored. RF.2.3b finishes in unit 3, RF.2.3 finishes in unit 4, and year-end reading range is demonstrated after a year of growth. L.2.2d and L.2.2e belong to unit 1, a through c to unit 2; L.2.1a through L.2.1e are in unit 2, with f on its sentence row. Parent codes do not transfer ownership to a different unit.

## Unit 1. Schools and community

**Calendar:** course days 1 to 32, weeks 1 to 7. The question is how schools around the world are alike and different. The chapter story first makes character response and belonging concrete; school articles then supply facts, definitions, and images for an informational book. Reading and talk precede the book draft, and its conclusion follows its factual pages. Ten days sustain the whole story instead of treating a long text as disconnected excerpts.

Cycles 1 to 5 occupy days 1 to 25: syllable-type review, ai/ay, ee/ea/y, igh/ie/y, then oa/ow. Days 26 to 32 retain foundations time for rereading, spelling transfer, dictionary use, and response. This is a launch and review unit, not an attempt to teach all additional vowel teams before their later cycles.

**Assessment and response:** retrieval on day 1 replaces a marked readiness check. Day 11 checks questions, character response, and illustrations; day 12 responds. Days 18 and 19 check reading and an informative paragraph; day 20 responds before rehearsal. The closing observation window is days 26, 30, and 31: discussion, the informational book, all six question words in literature and information, oral key ideas, images with words, reviewed vowel patterns, spelling and dictionary use, and literary/informational reading with all three fluency parts. The whole book supplies topic, facts, definitions, and conclusion. Day 32 is the single closing response day, with a fresh retry for the observed gap.

**Candidate settled: kept.** The first two base-unit assessments occupy days 11, 18, and 19 as mid-unit assessments; the third base-unit discussion assessment remains on day 26 and is joined by the book and closing observations on days 30 and 31. Cost: six topic assessment-bearing days within the 32, with response on days 12, 20, and 32. Five cycle checks each cost 15 minutes plus 15 minutes response within their fifth foundations lesson, on days 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25. No day is added outside the unit. The two additional topic blocks are days 12 and 32. Three sub-ready days and the three base-unit record sheets/family notes belong to later unit planning, without extra course days.

This unit needs no later same-year content. Social studies' interviews are a later use of ELA questioning, and science's plant writing is a later use of informative writing. Schools abroad must be presented as ordinary, varied places after the frame's country-specific sensitivity study. No task probes a child's move, shelter, or home life.

## Unit 2. Fossils tell of Earth's changes

**Calendar:** course days 33 to 74, weeks 7 to 15. A discovery story teaches beginning and ending functions; the Anning account supplies factual knowledge without fabricated inner thoughts. The articles then make paragraph focus, purpose, features, and word strategies useful. Sentence and grammar work precede narrative practice. A practice draft receives feedback before the new discovery narrative is assessed; revising and editing get separate days, followed by collaborative digital publication.

This unit has 11 owned rows, including five word-strategy parts, six grammar parts, conventions, narrative elaboration, and digital collaboration. It therefore keeps the longest allocation, 42 days. Cycles 6 to 12 run on days 33 to 67: r-controlled review; oi/oy/ou/ow and not-contractions; ild/old/ind/ost and is-contractions; doubling; three -ed sounds and would-contractions; oo/ou/ui/ue/ew; -tion/-sion. Days 68 to 74 retain the foundations hour for observation and response. Knowing a familiar prefix's meaning is taught on day 50; productive prefix decoding waits for the later foundations cycles.

**Assessment and response:** grade 1 routines are retrieved on day 33, with no readiness check on this first day. Days 40 and 41 retain the first base-unit assessment of story structure and text evidence; day 42 responds. Day 57 retains the second base-unit assessment, covering topic and paragraph focus, author purpose, efficient features, and all five word-learning strategies; day 58 responds. The closing assessment is days 68 to 72: a fresh article and story excerpt, the discovery narrative, sentence performances, separate revising and editing, and class digital publication. The product and short supplementary performances cover every grammar and convention part, including letter commas and invented product names where a fossil narrative would not elicit them. The decoding/spelling evidence covers only patterns through cycle 12. Day 74 is the single closing response day; day 73 refines illustrations and adds no assessment.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Three mid-unit assessment days, four closing assessment days, and one digital-publication observation day are inside the 42 days. Response occupies days 42, 58, and 74. Seven cycle checks cost 15 minutes each, with 15-minute responses inside their foundations block, on days 37, 42, 47, 52, 57, 62, and 67. The three extra topic slots are days 42 and 58 and the additional editing day created by splitting base module 2 unit 3 lesson 12 across days 70 and 71. No prefix-decoding assessment is imported early.

Science's land-change unit occupies school days 24 to 55, weeks 5 to 11. It overlaps the first fossil work rather than guaranteeing all its content beforehand. This is a topic connection, not a prerequisite: the ELA articles teach fossil formation themselves and say very long ago without a number scale. Anning's poverty and exclusion are explained at grade level without dwelling on loss or asking children to compare science with family beliefs. Typed publication on day 72 uses a shared device, with every child's peer collaboration and contribution recorded; paper alone does not complete W.2.6.

## Unit 3. The secret world of pollination

**Calendar:** course days 78 to 118, weeks 16 to 24, after the mid-year response day. Students first read and explain linked pollination steps, then move from a shared research model to gathering independently from provided sources. Notes become an informative paragraph before the poster and oral presentation. The bee-view story supplies a model for a separate recorded narrative after students have facts and presentation experience. This order avoids asking for a research product or coherent story before the reading, notes, and rehearsal that make it possible.

Cycles 13 to 19 run on days 78 to 112: consonant-le across two cycles; -k/-ck/-ic with un-/re-; -dge/-ge; -ch/-tch and -er/-est; -ible/-able; then review with au/aw. Two-syllable decoding gets ten foundations lessons before its later applications, while the 15-lesson second base unit protects the heavier research and paragraph process. Days 113 to 118 retain the foundations hour for grouping, fluency, and the final decoding observation. Below-grade readers get the named map, blend, matched decodable, spell, reread routine, followed by regrouping from evidence; they also keep supported child-reading of the topic texts.

**Assessment and response:** day 77 retrieves applicable grade 1 routines; day 78 retrieves connection and syllable work, day 84 word relationships, day 88 research, and day 104 presentation. None is marked readiness. Day 83 retains base unit 1's reading/process assessment, with response on day 84. Days 99 to 101 adapt base unit 2's paragraph-writing sequence into a new-source research and informative-writing assessment, with revision on day 100 and separate editing evidence on day 101; day 102 shares, and day 103 gives the single response slot for those assessed components. The ending observations are days 113 to 115 for research posters and oral presentation, followed by response on day 116. Days 117 and 118 collect the recorded pollinator stories, clarifying drawings, coherent complete speech, formal/informal comparison, and final two-syllable long-vowel decoding including consonant-le. The final 15 minutes of day 118 is the single response slot for these two days, including a short rerecording or word retry.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Cost: four mid-unit assessment-bearing topic days, three presentation observation days, and two recorded-story observation days inside the 41. Response is days 84, 103, and 116 and the final 15 minutes of day 118, each attached only to its named evidence collection. Seven cycle checks and responses cost 15 plus 15 minutes inside foundations on days 82, 87, 92, 97, 102, 107, and 112. The three added topic blocks are day 103 and the two recording days. The second base unit's writing is made an explicit assessment allocation; the scope does not invent a missing source assessment lesson number.

Science's pollinator model is later: school days 116 to 123, weeks 24 to 25, rather than the ELA frame's approximate week 27. ELA needs the steps from day 78 and research from day 88. Its bridges therefore teach the facts and diagram links for 15 minutes on day 78 and model gathering from supplied articles, diagrams, and observation photographs for 15 minutes on day 88, within the topic hours. Science's plant-needs investigation on school days 102 to 114 also comes after the research begins; the ELA texts supply those facts without assuming an experiment. Photographs provide a complete indoor path. Optional outdoor looks require supervision, checked allergies, and distance from insects. Recordings stay in class; a partner or group recording preserves an option for children who do not want to record alone. A live telling without a recording leaves SL.2.5 open. Audio creation is the second digital tool kind after the typed class piece; individual contributions remain on the record.

## Unit 4. Providing for pollinators

**Calendar:** course days 119 to 157, weeks 24 to 32. Recounting a fable and determining its moral precedes comparing versions; voiced dialogue makes character viewpoints observable. Poems and songs add sound effects and meaning. Helping-pollinators articles then teach how reasons support points before students write opinions. A complete practice opinion receives feedback before the seed packet, which is drafted, revised, edited, illustrated, and explained. The final observations return to all literary and informational strands rather than treating the attractive packet as evidence for every reading code.

Cycles 20 to 26 run on days 119 to 153: y to ies; schwa a with de-/dis-; schwa e/o with im-/in-; -ate; compounds; -cal/-cle; -ous/-us. The last four foundations blocks give cumulative reading, spelling, and response. The 39 days protect the six owned rows' different demands: literature comparison and performance, informative-text comparison and reasons, full opinion writing, and poem/song effects. They are not equal shares of six isolated skills.

**Assessment and response:** retrieve grade 1 tale, character, comparison, and word-effect work on day 119, not a marked readiness check. Day 132 retrieves author reasons and opinion writing. Days 129 and 130 retain the first base-unit assessment, adapted to credited tales, voiced dialogue, and two fable versions; day 131 responds. Day 137 retains the second base unit's article comparison and reason-support assessment; the first 10 minutes of day 138 responds. Days 139 to 141 retain its opinion assessment as one product with all parts; day 142 responds. Closing observations on days 153, 154, and 156 cover voiced viewpoints; all four sound devices linked to rhythm and meaning; the complete packet opinion; recounting and moral; both literary and informational comparison; reasons supporting points; and all six decoding parts plus spelling and band reading. Day 155 is an unassessed sharing day. Day 157 gives the single closing response slot with a fresh task for the observed gap.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Cost: six mid-unit assessment-bearing topic days and three closing observation days inside the 39. Response is days 131, 142, and 157 plus 10 minutes on day 138. Seven cycle checks and their responses cost 15 plus 15 minutes inside foundations on days 123, 128, 133, 138, 143, 148, and 153; cycle 26 is included, not skipped to make room for year-end events. The three added topic blocks are days 131, 156, and 157. Final fluency and all-pattern evidence is collected in the foundations block on day 156, with a fresh response on day 157.

Social studies' two-account work, school days 89 to 112, and sourced claims, days 26 to 43, precede these uses. ELA still teaches whole-story version comparison and complete written opinions. Culture work spans social studies days 114 to 129, weeks 23 to 26, and is not guaranteed complete when ELA opens. Day 119 reserves 10 minutes for people, place, and living story traditions; days 123 and 124 reserve 10 minutes each for the particular tale's cultural introduction. These are bridges inside existing topic days. The Quechua attribution, Mexican tale source, and both Aesop tellings require the frame's source and sensitivity work before use. No mocking voices or costumes, danger to people in the forest-fire telling, home-yard questions, or required seed purchases.
