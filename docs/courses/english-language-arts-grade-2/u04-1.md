## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.2.2, this section's share:** "Recount stories, including fables and folktales from diverse cultures, and determine their central message, lesson, or moral." Days 119 to 121 recount fable 01 and the second telling 02 and work out a moral from what the characters do. Folktales from other cultures come in section 4.2.
- **RL.2.9, this section's share:** "Compare and contrast two or more versions of the same story." Day 121 tracks what is different between 01 and 02; day 122 compares characters, events and the moral across both.
- **L.2.2d** "Generalize learned spelling patterns when writing words" and **L.2.2e** "Consult reference materials, including beginning dictionaries, as needed to check and correct spellings": cycle 20, y to ies or s (a consonant before the y: the y becomes i and es is added; a vowel before the y: only s is added). Day 121 checks one plural in the printed beginning dictionary.
- **RF.2.3d** (the endings -ies and -s), **RF.2.3c** (two-syllable long-vowel words such as pony and lady), **RF.2.3e** and **RF.2.3f** (the cycle's irregular words, mapped), **RF.2.4a to c** (reading for a purpose, rereading, confirming a word from context), **SL.2.1a to c** and **SL.2.3** (the talk routine) are carried every day.
- **Limits that bite here.** The fables are read; the class does not act out hunger or cold. Hunger never proves a person is lazy, and no child is cast as the grasshopper or the ant. No child reports on home food or money. The moral is worked out from events, never copied from a printed line (01 and 02 print none). Neither check card of the unit (F1, V1 to V4, F2) is used on these days.
- **Connections.** RL.2.6, voices for characters, day 122 (the ant's two answers read aloud once); W.2.1 is not yet taught (section 4.5).
- **The teacher page must explain.** Why the moral is a claim tested against events, not a fact; that a second moral is accepted when events support it (the extension); why 01 and 02 differ (Jacobs 1894 and La Fontaine in Wright's translation, retold by Curio Class); and how y to ies works in sound and spelling.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Record sheet.** One row a child: each exit as shown, shown with help, not yet seen; a heard column naming the six rotating children asked "How do you know?" each day; a foundations column for the first-day sample (119) and the oral turns (120 to 122). An exit is picked up by every child; the reason is heard from six, and an unheard reason is never marked as shown.

**Exit questions** (each on a passage of the unit's own texts, reread aloud, never the worked passage; each is one ask; the follow-up "How do you know?" goes to the six).
- **E119.** Passage: the winter part, paragraphs 8 to 11 (from "One cold morning" to the end), reread aloud twice; not the story-map model, which maps the whole fable. Reread aloud twice the winter part of the fable, from "One cold morning" to the end. Show the three picture cards in a mixed row: the ants handing out grain, the grasshopper asking, the grasshopper wrapping her wings. Say: "Put the pictures in the order the story tells." Key: the ants handing out grain, the grasshopper asking, the grasshopper wrapping her wings. Wrong: the grasshopper asking, the ants handing out grain, the wings (A); the wings, the ants handing out grain, the grasshopper asking (A). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same part and pictures. Say: "Tell how you know the wings come last." Key: The story says she wrapped her wings after the ant said no and she watched the ants carry the grain inside. Wrong: The wings are last because that picture is the biggest. (A); She wrapped her wings first, then she asked. (A).
- **E120.** Passage: the summer talk, paragraphs 3 to 5 (from "Come and sing with me!" to "Why should I worry?"), reread aloud twice; the day's model tests a different lesson on the gathering and the winter store. Reread aloud twice the summer talk, from "Come and sing with me!" to "Why should I worry?" Show two picture cards: the ant telling the grasshopper to store food; the ants opening their store in winter. Say: "A fable says: listen when a friend warns you. Mark the picture of the event that shows it." Key: the picture of the ant telling the grasshopper to store food. Wrong: the picture of the ants opening their store (B). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same part. Say: "Tell why that event teaches it." Key: The ant warned her to store food, but the grasshopper said winter was far away, so she had nothing when winter came. Wrong: The grasshopper was hungry. (B); Because the grasshopper sang. (B).
- **E121.** Passage: the ant's winter words in each telling: 01 paragraph 10 and 02 paragraphs 5 and 6 (the ant's question and the grasshopper's answer), reread aloud twice; not the place and speakers row the day models. Reread aloud twice what the ant says in winter in each telling: the first telling, paragraph 10, then the second telling, from "What were you doing all summer?" to "for anyone who came by." Show two picture cards: the ant asking the grasshopper a question; the grasshopper asking for grain. Say: "Mark the picture that shows what only the second telling has." Key: the picture of the ant asking the grasshopper a question. Wrong: the picture of the grasshopper asking for grain (D). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same parts. Say: "Say the question the ant asks in the second telling." Key: What were you doing all summer?. Wrong: May I have a few grains? (D); In summer we gathered, and you sang. (D).
- **E122.** Passage: where winter comes: 01 paragraph 7 ("Then the season changed") and 02 paragraph 2 ("Then the cold north wind came"), reread aloud twice; not the whole-version chart the day models. Reread aloud twice the part where winter comes in each telling: the first telling, paragraph 7, then the second telling, paragraph 2. Show two picture cards: a cold wind blowing over a bare field; the ants handing out grain at their nest. Say: "Mark the picture that shows what is the same in both tellings." Key: the picture of the cold wind over a bare field. Wrong: the picture of the ants handing out grain (E). Second ask, spoken, heard from the six: Same parts. Say: "Say what changes in that part." Key: In the second telling the grasshopper walks to the ant's house and knocks; the first telling says the leaves fell and the fields were bare. Wrong: Nothing changes. (D); The wind is cold. (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The story-map (who, where and when, what happens, challenge and response; grade 1 and unit 1 review) is held by the teacher on 119 and 120, and by pairs from 121. The talk routine (turn, pair, link, clarify) and the heard-six column are held by pairs. The sound-spelling-map, syllable types and word parts of unit 3 return in the cycle work. The side-by-side print of 01 and 02 (new) is held by the teacher on 121 and by pairs on 122.
- **Key moment 1, days 121 and 122: two versions seen at once.** Ideal: the two tellings printed side by side with the same lines level, and a highlighter for each version. Spec: fits (paper). Gaps and asks: none. **Key moment 2, day 120: the winter passage reread to test a moral.** Ideal: the passage on one page with the events numbered. Spec: fits (paper). Both need no digital tool.
- **A room with nothing.** One printed copy of each text held up, and the events copied on the board; pairs share a copy.

## 6. Days

## Day 119


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **7 min. Retrieval (practice only, no check evidence).** Pairs recount a grade 1 story in three events, say what it teaches, and name one feeling word; the teacher notes one miss for the story-map on 120.
- **8 min. Hook.** The teacher reads the first part of 01 aloud, to the grasshopper's "Why should I worry?" and asks what the ant means by getting ready, then posts the question of the unit: How can people help pollinators thrive? Children draw one way to get ready for a hard time; they report nothing from home.
- **10 min. Where fables come from.** Fables are short animal stories that show something to think about; 01 follows a telling credited to Aesop, a storyteller of ancient Greece, in a book by Joseph Jacobs (1894), and Curio Class wrote the words. Point to Greece on the map. Fables are still told today in many languages; no costume and no accent.
- **12 min. Read 01.** The teacher reads it whole (2 minutes); pairs read it again by turns, a paragraph each (6 minutes: 250 words, two readings); the class rereads the last four paragraphs together. Words previewed first: prepare, gather, store, season, plenty.
- **8 min. Model the story-map.** The teacher fills it: who and where (grasshopper, ant, a field), the summer talk, the ants gathering, the season changing, the request and the ant's reply. The ant says what she thinks; the grasshopper says what she thinks. Recount is the key events in order, dropping small details.
- **7 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "What does each animal think about winter?"
- **5 min. Exit.** E119.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The model fills the story-map in order, and the talk asks the same order-the-events ask as the exit. Instead: give the pair a recount of the story-map with two events swapped, and have them judge it and fix the given answer.
- **3 min. Transitions.**

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Retrieve.** Earlier plurals (-s, -es) and y as a vowel sound from cycles 3 and 4.
- **15 min. Model, map, blend (cycle 20).** Consonant before the y: city, cities; story, stories; cry, cries. Vowel before the y: day, days; play, plays. Tiles on the table and board.
- **10 min. First-day sample (diagnostic, unscored).** Each child builds three new plurals on a slate (pony, lady, monkey); the teacher walks and marks shown, shown with help, not yet seen for the groups.
- **12 min. Map the cycle's irregular words** with no guessing from pictures or first letters.
- **13 min. Spell** six words and a sentence on paper with the tiles.
- **5 min. Close.** "Look at the letter before the y."

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the story-map. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, tiles, spelling. Evidence: retrieval (unscored); E119; the first-day sample.

## Day 120


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest wrong order on E119 (A).
- **15 min. Reread 01.** The teacher reads the winter passage (from "Then the season changed") aloud while pairs follow; then pairs read it twice, the second time like talking (RF.2.4b).
- **10 min. Model: which events support the moral?** The teacher says a moral is the lesson a fable teaches (grade 1: the central message). She recounts the events in order, offers "getting ready before a hard time can help," and tests it against two events: the ants gathering and the ants having grain in winter. Then she tests "never sing" and finds no event that holds it up. A moral is accepted only when events support it.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "Which events support the moral?"
- **15 min. Pairs.** Each pair fills a three-event story-map strip (summer, season changes, winter) and puts a dot on the event they think shows the moral; the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E120.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The pairs' dot on the event that shows the moral is the exit's ask. Instead: give the pair a lesson ("never sing") and two events, and have them find and mark which event, if any, holds it up.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair** (foundations): the commonest miss of the first-day sample.
- **15 min. First reading of the cycle 20 reader.** Teacher reads page 1; pairs echo-read pages 2 to 4. Confirm meaning after decoding.
- **16 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation: eight children (1 to 8) read the oral card at the teacher table, 2 minutes each (16 teacher minutes); the other twenty-two use tiles, sort and reread in two stations of eleven.
- **14 min. Spell** words and two sentences from dictation.
- **10 min. Sort and reread** with the eight heard.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the story-map. Tool: None. By hand: dots, drawing, spelling. Evidence: E120; the oral card, children 1 to 8.

## Day 121


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest unsupported moral on E120 (B).
- **15 min. Read 02.** The teacher reads it whole (2 minutes); pairs read it twice (169 words), then underline who speaks. Credit it aloud: it follows La Fontaine as put into English by Elizur Wright, a translation from the 1800s, and Curio Class wrote the words. Words previewed: version, request, borrow, repay, promise, narrator.
- **10 min. Model: what changed.** On the side-by-side print the teacher compares one row: place and who speaks. 01 has two scenes and the ant speaks only; 02 has one scene at the ant's door and a narrator who says something about the ant. Version is one telling of a story; another telling is another version.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "What does the ant say in each telling?"
- **15 min. Pairs.** Six strips are sorted into only in 01, only in 02 and in both (differences first); the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E121.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The sort of six strips into only in 01, only in 02 and in both asks the exit's "what is different" ask. Instead: give a partner's note that lists something both versions have as a difference, and have the pair judge and fix it.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.**
- **10 min. Reread the cycle 20 reader** for accuracy, then smoothly (RF.2.4b).
- **16 min. Rotation.** Eight children (9 to 16) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the rest at stations.
- **12 min. Sort** twelve words by consonant-y or vowel-y; spell four on paper.
- **12 min. Dictionary.** Each pair finds one plural word in the printed beginning dictionary, checks its spelling and says what changed (L.2.2e).
- **5 min. Close.**

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the story-map and the side-by-side print. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, finding, spelling. Evidence: E121; the oral card, children 9 to 16.

## Day 122


Topic block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest wrong answer on E121 (D).
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Pairs say one thing that was the same and one that was different between two characters they met in unit 3.
- **14 min. Reread both tellings** side by side in pairs, with a highlighter for each version (RF.2.4b).
- **12 min. Model: compare whole versions.** Characters (the same two), events (what both have: the grasshopper sings and has nothing in winter; what only one has) and the moral each supports. The request changes: in 01 she asks for a few grains; in 02 she asks to borrow and promises to repay. The ant's answer differs too. The class accepts different morals if each rests on events; sentence frame: Both ___. But ___.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn, pair, link, clarify on "How does the request change between tellings?"
- **11 min. Pairs.** Complete a chart: one thing the same, one different, and a moral for each version; the teacher confers with the six heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E122.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The chart row "one thing the same" repeats the exit's ask. Instead: give a chart row where a difference sits in the same box, and have the pair judge and fix the given answer.

Foundations block (60 minutes).
- **5 min. Opening repair.**
- **15 min. A fresh decodable passage** (not the reader), read and meaning confirmed.
- **14 min. Rotation.** Seven children (17 to 23) read the oral card, 2 minutes each; the rest at stations.
- **14 min. Dictation** of words and a sentence, then compare with the pattern.
- **12 min. Stations** and reread for the other children.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the story-map, the chart and the talk routine. Tool: None. By hand: highlighting, chart, dictation. Evidence: E122; the oral card, children 17 to 23.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 120, "Which events support the moral?" Day 121, "What does the ant say in each telling?" Day 122, "How does the request change between tellings?"
- **Written task, day 122.** The chart: a few words and a drawing for one thing the same, one different and a moral for each version. Short writing, one task for the section.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors.** (1) one real similarity from the texts; (2) one real difference; (3) a moral that rests on an event named in the chart; (4) the children's own words. Marked shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 119.** Retrieval of the grade 1 recount, character and feeling work. **120.** Story-map (119). **121.** Moral supported by events (120). **122.** Request and answer difference (121); y to ies or s each day, with one earlier plural, in foundations.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of the cycle 20 reader by day 123; accuracy first, no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: prepare, gather, store, borrow, repay, version. Frames: "The ant thinks ___ because ___." "In 02 the grasshopper asks to ___." "The lesson is ___ because ___." A child may say the moral in the home language first.
- **Access.** Enlarge print, read directions aloud, accept pointing or a scribe for the chart; two pictures per exit are described aloud for a child who cannot see them.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit pictures may be pointed to; the reason may be said to a scribe or drawn; extra time within the part.
- **Reading load.** 01 (250 words) and 02 (169 words) are read by the children with teacher support; the exits are read aloud; the cycle 20 reader and passage are the only other print.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend a second moral for 01 from its events, or find the event that does not fit a proposed moral.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the fable together, then ask the child to tell it in order with first, next and last; ask which event shows the lesson. On day 122 read the second telling and find one thing the same and one different. The grown-up hears the cycle words aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the two tellings side by side with a different highlighter color for each; the class recounts to a partner, and the teacher fills the chart on screen.
