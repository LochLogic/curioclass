# Section 2.2: The story map, evidence and the mid-unit check

## Header


Children read short labels, cards, one story page at a time and the cycle reader; the teacher reads every stem and the cold check text aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.2.5**, "Describe the overall structure of a story, including describing how the beginning introduces the story and the ending concludes the action." Taught in section 2.1; here partners build the story map for *The Mark in the Stone* on day 38 and lead the explanation, then the mid-unit check on day 40 and the repair on day 42 apply it to a cold story.
- **RL.2.1**, "Ask and answer such questions as who, what, where, when, why, and how to demonstrate understanding of key details in a text." Day 39 teaches the return-to-the-text routine for choice questions; day 41 checks it (all six question words, plus a question the child composes); day 42 repairs it.
- **RF.2.3a, b, f; RF.2.4, 4a to 4c; L.2.2d, 2e** (days 38, 39, 42, cycle 7; days 40 and 41 continue the cycle with no new teaching): oi, oy, ou, ow; their and people; read and spell not-contractions as whole words. **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3**: the talk routine on days 38, 39 and 42.
- Skills carried: e2-story-structure, e2-spelling, e2-fluency, e2-vowel-teams, e2-tricky-spellings, e2-discussion, e2-wh-questions.
- **Limits that bite.** Days 40 and 41 are check days: nothing is reteaching, and the exit budget is inside the topic minutes (25 on day 40, 30 on day 41). A child reads no more than 150 printed words a day; the cold check text (97 words) is read aloud twice while children follow. The text leaves the shell kind open; do not name one. Fossil time is only "very long ago".
- **Connections.** Section 2.1 gave the beginning and ending functions and the six question words card. The talk routine, the heard-six column and the four-minute reading conference come from unit 1. Day 43 (section 2.3) moves to the Anning account.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Marking on check days is shown, shown with help, not yet seen, kept apart from any grade; the response on day 42 is by recorded letter; the day 40 and 41 results are the only record read on day 42.

Each child fills a three-box strip (beginning, middle, ending) with a few words or a drawing (8 minutes), then two sentences on the back: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." Partner talk 4 minutes; the day's six explain aloud to the teacher (5 minutes). Wrong: "The ending starts the wind problem" (A). Shown, shown with help, not yet seen: shown means both sentences hold with a detail; with help means one holds.
- **41-RL.2.1** (25 topic minutes). Children follow the text again (2 minutes). Each composes a question with who, what, where, when, why or how and writes its answer in a few words (8 minutes). The teacher then reads six questions, one at a time, and children write or draw a short answer in six boxes (10 minutes, no choices): Who catches the pages? What does she want to read? Where is she? When is it? Why does she need the box? How does she finish? Key: Lina; her fossil notes; the outdoor fossil table; Tuesday morning; the box blocks the wind; she reads one sheltered page at a time. Wrong: "She lets every page blow away" (B). The last 5 minutes finish the day 40 record. Marking per question.

Item (b): the day's one ask, answered aloud by the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner. Shown means the right picture and an answer using a word from the card; shown with help means the right picture and an answer after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means the other picture or no answer. Cards are printed "invented for practice" and read aloud twice. None reuses a story page, a worked example or the check text. Exits on days 40 and 41 are inside the check budgets.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The full story map (who, where and when, what happens, with challenge and response boxes, from unit 1, and the beginning and ending boxes from section 2.1) is built by partners on day 38 for *The Mark in the Stone* and stays on the wall for days 40 to 42. The six question words card, the sentence frames "The beginning introduces ___" and "The ending concludes ___", the word wall (their, people, and the cycle 7 words) and the wait signal stay up. The heard-six column records talk and foundations. On days 40 and 41 nothing new is carried; the strips and the six-box response strip are collected as evidence.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles (spell the day's words), sort (card sort by pattern) and reread (partner reread of the reader or phrase cards), ten children each. The day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations.
- **Key moment 1, day 38: the partner-built story map.** Ideal instrument: a large wall map with movable cards. Spec: fits; paper. Gaps and asks: none.
- **Key moment 2, day 41: the six-box response strip.** Ideal instrument: a paper strip with six numbered boxes. Spec: fits.
- **Tools.** None. No account is needed.
- **A room with nothing.** The story map is a folded sheet; the check text is read from a printed copy; tiles are letters on torn card; the response strip is a sheet folded into six.

## 6. Days

## Day 38


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E37 (G or I), say the sentence aloud and reject the meaning that does not fit, or cover the ending and say the small word; otherwise retrieve the six question words.
- **10 min. Reread with a partner.** Partners lead: pairs read pages 4, 5 and 7 of the story (141 words) turn about and mark with a finger the line that tells the next event. The teacher listens for accuracy.
- **15 min. Build the story map.** Each pair fills a story map with card strips: beginning (who, where and when, what starts), the middle steps First, Next, Then, At last, and the ending. The teacher checks the beginning and the ending boxes first.
- **10 min. Explain the two jobs.** Pairs tell another pair "The beginning introduces ___; the ending concludes ___". Turn, pair, link ("You said ___; I would add ___"), clarify. Hear the six.
- **5 min. Compare two maps.** The teacher holds up two pairs' maps and the class names one thing both beginning boxes hold.
- **10 min. Write two sentences.** Each child writes "The beginning introduces ___ and the ending concludes ___" with one detail from the map; the teacher scribes the rest. Check a capital and an end mark.
- **5 min. Exit.** E38.
- **5 min. Opening repair (foundations).** Retrieve the cycle 6 patterns and the check misses (Y); read six word cards.
- **15 min. Teach: oi, oy, ou, ow.** Oi and oy say /oi/ (coin, oil, point; boy, toy, Roy); ou and ow say /ou/ (out, loud, house; cow, town, how). Oy and ow come at the end of a word, oi and ou in the middle. Model coin: map /k/ /oi/ /n/, blend, spell with tiles. Their and people are mapped irregular words; read don't and doesn't as whole words (the apostrophe stands in for a letter; not scored).
- **37 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation (field 5): round 1, 13 minutes, six at the teacher table for the oral card, 2 minutes each (12 teacher minutes); rounds 2 and 3, 12 minutes each at tiles, sort and reread. Group 1 of five at the oral card; grade 1 and cycle 6 words only today.
- **3 min. Close.** "Where does oy come in a word?"

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the story map and the card strips. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, ordering cards, writing a few words, tiles. Evidence: E38; the story maps; the heard column.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 38, "How do the beginning and the ending work together?"; day 39, "Which line proves your choice?"; day 42, "What does the text leave open?"
- Written task, day 38: "The beginning introduces ___ and the ending concludes ___", a few written words with one detail from the map.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) names an event or fact from the story; (2) says what the beginning introduces and what the ending concludes; (3) points to the line that tells.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 39 and 42 open with one repair of at most 5 minutes, for the commonest exit or check miss; day 38 takes E36 and E37 misses and the cycle 6 check. The foundations opening repairs the day before's commonest spelling or reading miss.
- **Oral card.** The rotating six read six words and one sentence each day, using only patterns taught on an earlier day. The record sheet has a heard column.
- **Fluency target.** A smooth second reading of the cycle 7 reader by day 42, with accuracy first; no timer.
- **Story routines.** The story map from section 2.1 returns on day 38 and day 42; the question words card on days 39 to 42; the talk routine on days 38, 39 and 42.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: story map, beginning, ending, introduce, conclude, because, line, choose. Frames: "The beginning introduces ___." "The ending concludes ___." "I know because the text says ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer. Large print cards; the A and B pictures differ in color and shape. A child who cannot write the sentence draws and the teacher scribes, and the scribe is named on the check strip.
- **Check accommodations.** The cold text and every question are read aloud; the six-box strip can be answered by pointing at picture cards for the child to name; the spelling check can be answered with tiles. Extra time is allowed on days 40 and 41.
- **Reading load.** A child reads at most 150 printed words a day (a story page of at most 96 words, cards, the cycle reader). The cold text is read aloud while children follow.
- **Extension, same standard.** Compose a question that needs two lines of the story to answer, or draw the beginning and ending of a different story and say what each does.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read a page aloud, ask "What does this start?" and "What finishes it?" and let the child point. On the check days, read the text twice and let the child answer the six questions by pointing and saying one word. Spell three words with letter cards.
- **One projector.** Show the story page beside its picture; the story map is printed, marked on paper.
