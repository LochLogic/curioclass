Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-2/2/s/2.8), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-2 u02-8.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Children read their own drafts, short cards, a model letter of about 20 words and the cycle reader; the teacher reads every stem aloud, and children read at most 150 printed words a day. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Days 64 to 67 revise and edit the practice narrative from section 2.7, one convention a day; day 68 is the end-of-unit check on two cold texts. Practice cases are invented and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.2.5**, "With guidance and support from adults and peers, focus on a topic and strengthen writing as needed by revising and editing." Day 64 revises the practice narrative after written feedback and a partner suggestion; days 65 to 67 edit it. The day 70 and day 71 items are owned here.
- **W.2.3** (day 64): the narrative is the same event; revision adds or sharpens an action, thought, feeling, time word or closing.
- **L.2.2a**, "Capitalize holidays, product names, and geographic names." Day 65. Product names are invented (Zipfold, Brightmark); holiday and place names use Thanksgiving, Fourth of July, Lake Erie and Utah.
- **L.2.2b**, "Use commas in greetings and closings of letters." Day 66, on a model letter and the child's own short letter.
- **W.2.6**, "With guidance and support from adults, use a variety of digital tools to produce and publish writing, including in collaboration with peers." Day 66 shows why (10 minutes): the teacher types a greeting and one sentence while pairs choose the words and their order on a shared device. The day 72 item is owned here.
- **L.2.2c**, "Use an apostrophe to form contractions and frequently occurring possessives." Day 67, with can't, isn't, it's, we're and Kofi's bag and the guide's brush.
- **RI.2.2 and RL.2.5** (day 68): the check on two cold texts; the section 2.5 row owns 68-RI.2.2 and the section 2.7 row owns 68-RL.2.5, and this section runs the day.
- **RF.2.3d, 2.3e, 2.3f, 2.4 to 4c; L.2.2d, 2e** (days 64 to 67): cycle 12 (-tion, -sion; module review). **SL.2.1a to 1c, SL.2.3**: the talk routine on days 64 to 67.
- Skills carried: e2-revise-edit, e2-narrative, e2-spelling, e2-fluency, e2-tricky-spellings, e2-prefixes-suffixes, e2-discussion, e2-sentences, e2-story-structure, e2-main-topic-paragraphs.
- **Limits that bite.** A child reads no more than 150 printed words a day. The teacher types on day 66; children do not type. Prefix decoding stays unfinished (unit 3). No real product names are used; every product name is invented.
- **Connections.** Section 2.7 supplied the drafts; section 2.9 uses these skills on cold cards on days 70 and 71 and on a published piece on day 72.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Each of days 65 to 67 teaches one convention only; editing is separated from revising, and the editing checklist card appears on day 67.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned** (rows above; names): 69-W.2.3, 70-W.2.5, 71-W.2.5, 71-L.2.2a, 71-L.2.2b, 71-L.2.2c and 72-W.2.6. They sit on the day 69 narrative prompt, the day 71 letter card and the day 72 class piece, so nothing here uses them. Teaching avoids the letter card's cases: it does not use Arbor Day, Cardiff, Quillwick, Nora, didn't, Dear Jo, Dear Sam, Your friend, Best wishes or Kai; the narrative practice uses finds and not the day 69 problems (a label that falls, a shadow, a smudge). Keys and error letters are as the table gives them; marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

**Exit questions.** A reading exit marked Passage asks on a named part of the unit's own texts, read aloud twice, never on a card and never on the passage the day works as its example; for those exits, "the card" below means that passage. Each exit is one ask on an invented card, with two items on the days that test two things (a and b, 54, 55, 61, 66 and 67). The teacher reads each card twice (days 65 to 67 and 71 use a printed card), asks the one question, and hears the rotating six (about 20 seconds each); the rest tell a partner. Shown means the answer is right alone; shown with help means it is right after one prompt ("What does the card say?"); not yet seen means no right answer. There are no hold-up pictures: the ask alone carries the check. No card reuses an article sentence, a worked example, a shared piece or an end item. Items.csv holds the keys.
- **E64** (day 64, one ask). Card: "Draft: I saw a bird. It flew away. Feedback: Tell what the bird looked like." Ask: "Write one sentence to add that follows the feedback." Answer: A sentence that says how the bird looked, such as "It had red wings.". Wrong: "I like cake" (V).
- **E65** (day 65, one ask). Printed card: "Hugo and Rita saw a flinkbrush kit at lake huron on earth day." Ask: "Say which words need capital letters, and how each is written." Answer: Lake Huron, Earth Day and Flinkbrush. Wrong: "lake huron, earth day, flinkbrush" (AE).
- **E66** (day 66, two items, each one ask). Item (a) Printed card: "dear wes; The fossil trays came today.; see you soon; Opal" Ask: "Say the greeting as it should be written." Answer: Dear Wes,. Wrong: "Dear Wes" (AF); "Dear wes," (AE). Item (b) Printed card: "dear wes; The fossil trays came today.; see you soon; Opal" Ask: "Say the closing as it should be written." Answer: See you soon,. Wrong: "See you soon" (AF).
- **E67** (day 67, two items, each one ask). Item (a) Printed card: "This is Finns tray. We havent used it yet." Ask: "Write Finns so it shows the tray belongs to him." Answer: Finn's. Wrong: "Finns" (AG). Item (b) Printed card: "This is Finns tray. We havent used it yet." Ask: "Write havent the correct way." Answer: haven't. Wrong: "havent" (AH).
- **E68** (day 68, one ask). Passage: A Choice of Actions, pages 1 to 6, read aloud twice: the beginning (Dan must decide) and the ending (a fine decision) (g2-u2-cr12). Read A Choice of Actions aloud twice. Ask: "Say how the ending finishes what the beginning started." Answer: The beginning says Dan must make a decision and pick a game for his team; the ending finishes it: the class picks the map game, so Dan made a fine decision. Wrong: "The ending is that Dan must make a decision" (A).

**Cycle 12 written check** (day 67, 15 minutes, unscored). The teacher reads each sentence twice and says its missing word; children write the word in a printed gap. Four independent spellings; none is a day 63 to 66 word and none is action, vision, motion, mission, expression or fiction (mission and expression are in the reader or the day 64 model). (1) "We got off at the ____." station. (2) "That ____ is new." invention. (3) "We had a ____ in class." discussion. (4) "He had a look of ____." confusion. Wrong: "stashun", "inventshun", "discushun" or "confuzhun" put the wrong letters on the ending sound (Y); "stat" or "invent" omit the ending (Z). Tag the letter that fits. After marking, dictate "fiction" as unscored practice.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The draft page with the teacher's written feedback line and a partner suggestion strip ("Explain why ___", from section 2.1's peer strip and grade 1's) is held by each child on day 64; the capital cards (common name and special name) by pairs on day 65; the model letter and a printed letter frame (greeting, line, closing, name) on day 66; the apostrophe cards (two words squeezed, belongs to someone) and the four-line editing checklist (capital, comma, apostrophe, spelling checked) on day 67. The word wall holds the cycle 12 words.
- **Standard rotation (foundations).** Three stations, tiles, sort and reread, ten children each. The day's six sit at the teacher table for round 1, two minutes each, then take the other stations. Day 68 is flexible groups (see Day 68).
- **Key moment 1, day 64: revision after feedback.** Ideal instrument: a draft page with a space beside each line for a partner suggestion. Spec: fits; paper.
- **Key moment 2, day 66: shared typing.** Ideal instrument: a shared text editor on a projector with the teacher typing and pairs giving words and order. Spec: partly fits (the unit plan names shared typing as light, partly fits); gaps: no device per five children, and no child types. The paper letter does the conventions; the typing shows why a shared piece needs agreement, and the observation of each child is day 72's.
- **Tools.** Shared typing, day 66 (10 minutes, show why; the teacher types). No account, no search engine.
- **A room with nothing.** The letter is written on lined paper; the shared piece is the teacher writing on a board while pairs call out words; the capital cards are two folded cards.

## Day 66


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E65 (AE), hold up a common-name card and a special-name card; otherwise retrieve the capital cards.

## Day 67


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many missed E66 (AF, AE), walk a finger down the model letter and stop at each comma; otherwise retrieve the letter frames.
- **3 min. Words.** Apostrophe, contraction, belongs to: each said once and shown on a card.
- **6 min. Reread.** The teacher rereads the story lines "Amara's class went to a fossil park" and "I can't tell you for sure"; children point to each apostrophe and say which kind it is.
- **12 min. Model: two kinds of apostrophe.** A contraction squeezes two words and the apostrophe stands where letters are gone (can't, isn't, it's, we're). A possessive shows who has something (Kofi's bag, the guide's brush). Ask "gone letters, or belongs to someone?" each time.
- **10 min. Pairs sort and fix.** Pairs sort 8 cards into contractions and belongs-to words and fix 4 printed sentences by adding the missing apostrophe.
- **8 min. Edit with a checklist.** Each child uses the four-line editing checklist card (capital, comma, apostrophe, spelling checked) on their narrative, marking each box.
- **6 min. Turn, pair, link, clarify.** On "How can you tell a contraction from a belongs-to word?" Hear the six.
- **5 min. Write.** Each child writes one sentence with a belongs-to word and one with a contraction, with a few words.
- **5 min. Exit.** E67.
- **15 min. Written check (foundations).** Cycle 12 written check (field 3), unscored.
- **5 min. Reader.** Reread the cycle 12 reader for expression.
- **25 min. Rotation.** Standard rotation; the six read the oral card for the whole cycle (12 minutes), the rest in stations. Group 5 of five.
- **15 min. Fresh response.** Day 63's six read the cycle words to the teacher (12 minutes, 2 minutes each) while the others partner-read a pattern card and spell two dictated words; no new teaching.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the apostrophe cards; each child holds the editing checklist. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, fixing sentences, marking a checklist, spelling on paper. Evidence: E67; the checklist marks on the narrative; the check notes.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: revise, feedback, capital, holiday, letter, greeting, closing, apostrophe, contraction. Frames: "I added ___." "Dear ___," "Your ___," "It is ___'s ___." "Which part do you mean?"
- **Access.** Pointing is an answer; the letter frame has the commas pre-printed in grey for a child who needs it; the cards are large print. A child who cannot write the letter dictates it and the teacher scribes.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 68 texts are read aloud; answers may be drawn or spoken; the spelling check can be answered with tiles.
- **Reading load.** At most 150 printed words a day: the cards, the model letter and the child's own draft.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second letter to another reader and check each comma, or sort ten words for the apostrophe kind.
