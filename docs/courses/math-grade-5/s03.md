## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does it mean to take a fraction of a fraction?"
**Hook, day 22:** Four people share seven meters of paper ribbon equally, invented for practice. During eight minutes, retrieve sharing and ask how one share is one fourth of seven. Pose the compelling question; teach the new multiplication connection immediately afterward.
**Supporting questions:** How does division give a fraction of a quantity (day 24)? What does a fractional square tile measure (day 25)? How does decomposition appear in an expression (day 29)? Why do both denominators matter (day 38)?
**Closing claim:** I can explain fraction products by partitioning, create matching stories, and equate square tiling with multiplying side lengths. Saved tasks S1 to S3 establish this on day 40; fresh tasks T1 and T2 are unscored practice.
**Teaching:** Model partitioning on day 22 and square tiling on day 25. Compare decompositions on day 28; explicitly connect double partitions to multiplication on day 33. Students choose drawings on day 37. Teach every part of 5.NF.B.4a through days 22 to 38 and 4b through days 25 to 39. Day 29 revisits all three grouping symbols within five minutes; unit 1 retains ownership and assessment of 5.OA.A.1.

<!-- skel -->
- **5.NF.B.4a:** Interpret the product (a/b) × q as a parts of a partition of q into b equal parts; equivalently, as the result of a sequence of operations a × q ÷ b. For example, use a visual fraction model to show (2/3) × 4 = 8/3, and create a story context for this equation. Do the same with (2/3) × (4/5) = 8/15. (In general, (a/b) × (c/d) = ac/bd.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **5.NF.B.4b:** Find the area of a rectangle with fractional side lengths by tiling it with unit squares of the appropriate unit fraction side lengths, and show that the area is the same as would be found by multiplying the side lengths. Multiply fractional side lengths to find areas of rectangles, and represent fraction products as rectangular areas. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **5.OA.A.1:** Use parentheses, brackets, or braces in numerical expressions, and evaluate expressions with these symbols. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Multiplies numerators but retains the second denominator. | Ignores the first factor's partition. Repair: repartition each part of the second factor into the first denominator's equal shares. | From memory. |
| B | Adds denominators in multiplication. | Transfers an additive rule to partitions. Repair: count rows times columns in the unchanged whole. | From memory. |
| C | Uses a square tile's side length as its area. | Confuses length with coverage. Repair: tile one unit square and name each tile's area from its share of that square. | From memory. |
| D | Labels area with length units. | Does not track the measured attribute. Repair: contrast a traced side with a covered interior. | From memory. |
| E | Reverses a fraction when connecting a product to equal shares. | Swaps the partition count and selected-share count. Repair: label the denominator's equal shares before selecting the numerator's number of shares. | From memory. |
| F | Concatenates a mixed number's whole part and numerator. | Loses the whole's equal parts. Repair: count the denominator's parts in each whole before adding the remaining parts. | From memory. |
| G | Multiplies only the first term of a grouped sum. | Ignores the group as one side length. Repair: match the grouped length to both partial rectangles. | From memory. |
| H | Insists a fraction product cannot exceed the other factor. | Assumes every fraction is less than one. Repair: compare the fractional factor with one using equal whole strips. | From memory. |
| I | Treats the starting fractional quantity as one original whole. | Changes the reference whole. Repair: keep the original whole outlined while repartitioning only the starting quantity. | From memory. |

Predictions are untested. Use one repair for the identified error; do not infer a second misconception from the same wrong answer.
