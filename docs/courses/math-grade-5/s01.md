## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How much space does a solid take up, and how can we write how we found it?"
**Hook, day 1:** Compare two differently shaped solids built from twelve equal cubes. Which occupies more space? Students build or inspect complete layer drawings for eight minutes, then discuss the compelling question. This is an original task.
**Supporting questions:** How does packing measure space (day 2)? Why do layers give a product (day 5)? Why do different cuts preserve volume (day 9)? How does grouping record our reasoning (day 10)?
**Closing claim:** I can measure space with equal cubes, explain both formulas and add disjoint prism volumes. Saved tasks P and Q and fresh items 1 to 8 check this claim on day 13, including writing, interpreting and evaluating expressions.
**Teaching:** Model square versus cubic units on day 2 and label each factor on day 5. Compare students' decompositions on day 9; step back for independent model choice on day 11. Later section plans give each teaching day one fresh exit question, at most two parts, covering its whole goal.

<!-- skel -->
- **5.MD.C.3a:** A cube with side length 1 unit, called a “unit cube,” is said to have “one cubic unit” of volume, and can be used to measure volume. Parts, by verb: (a) identify a one-unit-edge cube as one cubic unit; (b) use it to measure volume. Teach on day 2, practise through day 4, check with item 1 on day 13.
- **3.MD.C.5a:** A square with side length 1 unit, called “a unit square,” is said to have “one square unit” of area, and can be used to measure area. Parts, by verb: (a) identify a one-unit-edge square as one square unit; (b) use it to measure area. Readiness item 1 checks these on day 1; repair and practise on day 2 as needed.
- **3.MD.C.5b:** A plane figure which can be covered without gaps or overlaps by n unit squares is said to have an area of n square units. Parts, by verb: cover without gaps or overlaps and relate the square count to area. Readiness item 2 checks this on day 1; repair and practise on day 2 as needed.
- **3.MD.C.6:** Measure areas by counting unit squares (square cm, square m, square in, square ft, and improvised units). Parts, by verb: measure area by counting unit squares. Readiness item 1 checks this on day 1; repair on day 2 and retrieve on day 5.
- **5.MD.C.3b:** A solid figure which can be packed without gaps or overlaps using n unit cubes is said to have a volume of n cubic units. Parts, by verb: pack without gaps or overlaps and relate the cube count to volume. Teach on day 2, practise through day 4, check with item 2 on day 13.
- **5.MD.C.4:** Measure volumes by counting unit cubes, using cubic cm, cubic in, cubic ft, and improvised units. Parts, by verb: measure volume by counting cubes in each named or improvised cubic unit. Teach counting on day 2 and the units on day 7; practise on day 7, check with item 3 on day 13.
- **5.MD.C.5a:** Find the volume of a right rectangular prism with whole-number side lengths by packing it with unit cubes, and show that the volume is the same as would be found by multiplying the edge lengths, equivalently by multiplying the height by the area of the base. Represent threefold whole-number products as volumes, e.g., to represent the associative property of multiplication. Parts, by verb: (a) find volume by packing; (b) show equivalence with the three-edge product and base area times height; (c) represent three-factor products as volumes, including equivalent groupings. Teach packing and products on day 3 and both formulas on day 5; practise groupings on day 6 and all parts in P on day 11; check with item 4 on day 13.
- **5.MD.C.5b:** Apply the formulas V = l × w × h and V = b × h for rectangular prisms to find volumes of right rectangular prisms with whole-number edge lengths in the context of solving real world and mathematical problems. Parts, by verb: apply each formula to mathematical and real-world volume problems. Teach on day 5, practise on days 6, 7 and 11, check with item 5 on day 13.
- **5.OA.A.1:** Use parentheses, brackets, or braces in numerical expressions, and evaluate expressions with these symbols. Parts, by verb: (a) use each grouping symbol; (b) evaluate expressions with each symbol. Teach parentheses on day 6 and brackets and braces on day 10; practise on day 10, check with item 7 on day 13.
- **5.OA.A.2:** Write simple expressions that record calculations with numbers, and interpret numerical expressions without evaluating them. For example, express the calculation “add 8 and 7, then multiply by 2” as 2 × (8 + 7). Recognize that 3 × (18932 + 921) is three times as large as 18932 + 921, without having to calculate the indicated sum or product. Parts, by verb: (a) write expressions recording calculations; (b) interpret expressions without evaluation; (c) recognize multiplicative comparisons without calculating. Teach on day 6, practise on day 10, check with item 8 on day 13.
- **5.MD.C.5c:** Recognize volume as additive. Find volumes of solid figures composed of two non-overlapping right rectangular prisms by adding the volumes of the non-overlapping parts, applying this technique to solve real world problems. Parts, by verb: (a) recognize additivity; (b) find volume by adding two disjoint prism volumes; (c) apply this to real-world problems. Teach on day 8, practise on days 9 to 12, check with item 6 on day 13.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Reports volume in square units. | Confuses covering with packing. Repair: contrast one square with one cube, then label the cube's volume. | From memory. |
| B | Accepts gaps in a volume packing. | Does not require complete coverage of the solid's space. Repair: fill a missing cube position before counting. | From memory. |
| C | Counts only visible cubes. | Treats a drawing as a complete inventory. Repair: reveal and count one hidden layer. | From memory. |
| D | Reports base area as prism volume. | Omits the number of layers. Repair: stack equal base layers and match each to a factor. | From memory. |
| E | Uses an enclosing prism's volume for a stepped solid. | Includes empty space. Repair: shade the two occupied prisms and add their volumes. | From memory. |
| F | Omits grouping needed to record a sum multiplied by a factor. | Does not preserve the stated calculation. Repair: box the sum before writing the multiplication. | From memory. |
| G | Adds within a multiplication group. | Misreads the written operation. Repair: match each operation sign to its spoken instruction, then calculate. | From memory. |
| H | Interprets a multiplicative comparison as an additive one. | Reads "times as large" as "more." Repair: draw five copies of the entire sum as one quantity. | From memory. |
