## Unit 1. Volume and expressions

14 days, course days 1 to 14, weeks 1 to 3. Sections 1.1 to 1.4 cover cubes and layers, dimensions and formulas, composed solids and expressions, then applications and assessment. Packing and counting precede arrays of layers, which precede both volume formulas; decomposition comes only after a single prism is understood. Grade 4 multiplication strategies suffice here.

The day 1 area readiness check comes after the hook; day 2 holds ten minutes for the needed square-unit reminder before cube packing. Day 6 introduces parentheses with writing, evaluation and interpretation without computation. Day 10 introduces brackets and braces separately, without nested groups. This moves first exposure to braces ahead of the unit assessment; day 29 later revisits them. Volume measurement includes all named cubic units and an improvised cubic unit.

Assessment products on days 11 and 12 carry model choice, packing-to-formula reasoning and additive-volume application. Day 13 completes the end-of-unit assessment with short concept and expression prompts, including each grouping symbol; saved products are reused. Day 14 is the unit's single response allocation. No mid-unit assessment is required.

Candidate settled: keep the packing-to-layers-to-formulas and expression guidance inside days 1 to 10, zero added days. Keep the fixed-cube-count box extension as a five-minute alternate case on day 11, within its application time. MP5 tool choice and MP7 structure are visible in that day and its saved assessment product. Day 12 uses town trash, with no family habits or money requested.

## Unit 2. Fractions as quotients

7 days, course days 15 to 21, weeks 3 to 5. Sections 2.1 and 2.2 move from concrete sharing through diagrams and equations to general quotient meaning and its application. The short unit owns one row, so it receives five teaching days rather than an equal share of the year. Fraction strips, a tape diagram and a number line connect a share, its equation and its location.

Day 15 retrieves grade 4 division and multistep quantities inside the hook. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed. Day 20 assesses a/b as a divided by b through fractional and mixed-number shares, models, equations and whole-number bounds; day 21 responds. MP2 quantity reasoning and MP4 modeling are part of both teaching and assessment. Food contexts avoid diet talk.

Candidate settled: keep the sharing explanation of a/b on days 15 to 19, zero added days. Keep the joining-person extension as a five-minute alternative on day 19; it replaces a parallel sharing case.

## Unit 3. Fraction multiplication

20 days, course days 22 to 41, weeks 5 to 9. Sections 3.1 to 3.5 connect division to a fraction of a whole number, extend area to one fractional side, use operation properties and mixed-number sides, partition a fraction again, then generalize and apply fraction products. The eighteen teaching days preserve the base order across its two source units. This gives tiling and model-to-symbol reasoning more time than the single quotient row received.

Grade 4 multiplication of a fraction by a whole number is retrieved on day 22, rectangle area on day 25 and mixed numbers on day 31. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed. Mixed numbers become single fractions; unlike-denominator addition is not assumed. Day 29 revisits an unnested brace expression. Day 38 generalizes from repeated partitions. Day 39 retains independent partition-and-story explanations for both whole and fractional factors and fractional-area tiling and side-product reasoning. Day 40 reuses them and completes short prompts on the partition equation, fraction products and products represented as rectangular areas; both parts of 5.NF.B.4 are covered without stacking fresh performances. Day 41 responds. Application begins the later-owned fraction-problem work without transferring ownership from unit 4.

Candidate settled: keep partition meaning and tiling on days 22 to 38, zero added days. Keep the garden-area extension as a ten-minute alternate application on day 39. MP8 repeated reasoning and MP7 structure carry into the assessment. The optional estimation, mosaic and flag days remain cut as the frame directs.

Day 51 gathers independent real-context multiplication, including mixed numbers, and both division-context solutions. Day 53 reuses that evidence for 5.NF.B.6 and 7c while assessing interpretation, story, model and inverse multiplication checks for 7a and 7b. Day 54 responds once. General fraction-by-fraction division waits for grade 6.

Candidate settled: keep the unit-fraction limits and multiplication checks throughout days 43 to 48, zero added days. Keep the partner-written pair of division situations as a five-minute alternative on day 52. MP1 sense-making and MP4 model choice are recorded in day 51 evidence and day 53 explanations. Read-aloud and explicit quantity words support the year's longer problem texts.

## Unit 5. Multi-digit multiplication and division

22 days, course days 57 to 78, weeks 12 to 16. Sections 5.1 to 5.5 cover partial products, the standard algorithm and fluency, partial quotients, missing dimensions and fractional quotients, then applications and assessment. Nine source lessons build multiplication, eight develop division and its quotient connections, and three apply them. The load follows composing, estimating a useful partial quotient and explaining zeros, not equal time per owned row.

Day 57 retrieves grade 4 partial products and day 66 retrieves partial quotients. The standard multiplication algorithm starts on day 60 after its expanded record is understood. Division stays within four-digit dividends and two-digit divisors with explained strategies. Day 73's fractional partial quotients use a common denominator or an already-known whole quotient; a source sum needing unlike denominators is replaced because that teaching comes in unit 9. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed.

Day 75 saves an explained division application. Day 76 takes one independent, untimed multiplication-algorithm sample inside its first ten minutes. Day 77 reuses those products and adds the explained quotient and multiplication repair; day 78 responds. Later teaching days reserve five-minute mixed multiplication warm-ups at the explicit intervals in the CSV, varying zeros and factor lengths, with feedback and no visible countdown. This continues fluency after the algorithm's initial unit.

Candidate settled: keep the partial-products-to-algorithm connection and the division boundary in the twenty teaching days, zero added days. Keep the three-digit by two-digit verification extension as a five-minute alternate on day 64. MP6 precision and MP7 structure are checked through the explained records. Trash and recycling examples use supplied school or town data, never a family's habits or money.

## Unit 6. Decimal place value

12 days, course days 79 to 90, weeks 16 to 18. Sections 6.1 to 6.3 establish thousandths and their forms, compare and round with place units and number lines, then order, apply and assess. Four teaching days establish representations, including the kept optional relationship lesson; six use those representations to compare, round and solve problems. This precedes operations so students can explain the unit they are combining or splitting.

Days 79, 80 and 85 retrieve the specified grade 4 place-value, notation, number-reading and rounding rows. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed. Day 88 retains independent rounding and precision evidence. Day 89 reuses it for 5.NBT.A.4 and takes three brief fresh blocks: both adjacent-place relationships; numerals, words and expanded form; comparison with symbols and justification. Day 90 responds. MP6 and MP7 require place-unit language and a correction to the longer-is-larger error. Pretend pirate coins are a game context, not history.

Candidate settled: keep both-direction place value and comparison by place inside days 79 to 88, zero added days. Keep the between-decimals extension as a five-minute alternative on day 87.

## Unit 7. Decimal operations
