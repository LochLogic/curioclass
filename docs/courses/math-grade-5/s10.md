## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can two number lines locate a point, sort shapes and show a pattern?"

**Hook, day 135:** Ask the compelling question. Show a dot on unnumbered crossing lines. What information would let someone reproduce its location? Allow five minutes, then teach perpendicular numbered axes and their common zero.
**Supporting questions:** Why does coordinate order matter (day 136)? Can a square have several category names (day 141)? How do corresponding terms become points (day 145)? What does a point mean here (day 146)?
**Closing claim:** I can locate points, explain inherited shape properties, graph paired patterns and interpret coordinates. C1, H1, P1 and C2 check this on day 148.
**Teaching:** Model axis distances before shortcuts. Compare shape properties before drawing inclusion arrows. Model corresponding table rows before graphing. Step back for independent explanations and ask which property or coordinate supports each claim.

- **5.G.A.1, parts by verb:** (a) Use perpendicular number lines with a shared zero to define a coordinate system and locate points with ordered pairs; (b) understand how coordinate order names travel along the corresponding axes.
- **5.OA.B.3, parts by verb:** (a) Generate two numerical patterns from two given rules; (b) identify relationships between corresponding terms and explain informally why they hold; (c) form ordered pairs of corresponding terms; (d) graph the ordered pairs.
- **5.G.A.2, parts by verb:** (a) Represent real world and mathematical problems by graphing first-quadrant points; (b) interpret coordinate values in context.

**Practices:** On day 145, MP6 develops through checking a partner's table-to-graph coordinates and scales; MP7 develops through explaining the paired pattern's relationship. On day 141, MP7 develops through placing squares in both rectangle and rhombus categories. P1 and H1 retain this evidence.
**Rigor:** Keep eleven understanding days, zero fluency days and two application days, 660, zero and 120 minutes. Required 5.NBT.B.5 fluency continues in five-minute mixed algorithm warm-ups on days 136, 139, 142 and 145, with zeros, varied factor lengths and feedback, without a countdown. The course's conservative focus budget credits this unit with zero of 780 major-work minutes, zero percent; no supporting cluster is assigned.
**Limits:** Use the first quadrant and its boundary axes, whole numbers and simple fractions. Trapezoids have at least one parallel pair; isosceles triangles have at least two equal sides. No negative coordinates, slope, formal functions or hidden-rule guessing.
**Carried:** Retain unit 9's number line, resting its fraction models. Tie each axis to coordinate notation on day 135; fade tracing on day 136 and withdraw route arrows on day 137. Tie the coordinate plane to pairs on day 136; fade supplied labels on day 145 and withdraw completed grids on day 147, keeping blank grids.
