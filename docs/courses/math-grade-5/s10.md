## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can two number lines locate a point, sort shapes and show a pattern?"
**Hook, day 135:** Ask the compelling question. Show a dot on unnumbered crossing lines. What information would let someone reproduce its location? Allow five minutes, then teach perpendicular numbered axes and their common zero.
**Supporting questions:** Why does coordinate order matter (day 136)? Can a square have several category names (day 141)? How do corresponding terms become points (day 145)? What does a point mean here (day 146)?
**Closing claim:** I can locate points, explain inherited shape properties, graph paired patterns and interpret coordinates. C1, H1, P1 and C2 check this on day 148.
**Teaching:** Model axis distances before shortcuts. Compare shape properties before drawing inclusion arrows. Model corresponding table rows before graphing. Step back for independent explanations and ask which property or coordinate supports each claim.

<!-- skel -->
- **5.G.A.1:** Use a pair of perpendicular number lines, called axes, to define a coordinate system, with the intersection of the lines (the origin) arranged to coincide with the 0 on each line and a given point in the plane located by using an ordered pair of numbers, called its coordinates. Understand that the first number indicates how far to travel from the origin in the direction of one axis, and the second number indicates how far to travel in the direction of the second axis, with the convention that the names of the two axes and the coordinates correspond (e.g., x-axis and x-coordinate, y-axis and y-coordinate). Parts, by verb: (a) Use perpendicular number lines with a shared zero to define a coordinate system and locate points with ordered pairs; (b) understand how coordinate order names travel along the corresponding axes.
- **5.NBT.B.5:** Fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm. Parts, by verb: (a) Fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm.
- **5.G.B.3:** Understand that attributes belonging to a category of two-dimensional figures also belong to all subcategories of that category. For example, all rectangles have four right angles and squares are rectangles, so all squares have four right angles. Parts, by verb: (a) Understand that category attributes belong to every subcategory.
- **5.G.B.4:** Classify two-dimensional figures in a hierarchy based on properties. Parts, by verb: (a) Classify two-dimensional figures in a property-based hierarchy.
- **5.OA.B.3:** Generate two numerical patterns using two given rules. Identify apparent relationships between corresponding terms. Form ordered pairs consisting of corresponding terms from the two patterns, and graph the ordered pairs on a coordinate plane. For example, given the rule “Add 3” and the starting number 0, and given the rule “Add 6” and the starting number 0, generate terms in the resulting sequences, and observe that the terms in one sequence are twice the corresponding terms in the other sequence. Explain informally why this is so. Parts, by verb: (a) Generate two numerical patterns from two given rules; (b) identify relationships between corresponding terms and explain informally why they hold; (c) form ordered pairs of corresponding terms; (d) graph the ordered pairs.
- **5.G.A.2:** Represent real world and mathematical problems by graphing points in the first quadrant of the coordinate plane, and interpret coordinate values of points in the context of the situation. Parts, by verb: (a) Represent real world and mathematical problems by graphing first-quadrant points; (b) interpret coordinate values in context.
