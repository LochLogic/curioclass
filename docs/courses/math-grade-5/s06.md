## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do thousandths extend the place value we know?"

**Hook, day 79:** Ask the compelling question. Within eight minutes, including retrieval, partition one hundredth of a fixed whole into ten equal pieces. Ask what one piece represents. Model its name and notation after students conjecture.

**Supporting questions:** How can one amount have several names (day 80)? What changes between adjacent places (day 82)? Why can fewer digits name more (day 84)? When does rounding hide a difference (day 88)?

**Closing claim:** I can read, write, compare and round thousandths and explain adjacent places in both directions. Tasks T1 to T3 and saved R1 check this on day 89; day 90 records revisions.

**Teaching:** Model subdivisions before connecting written forms. On day 82, match a drawn balance to exchanges. On day 84, model zooming without changing the whole. Step back during practice; compare explanations before correcting them.

- **5.NBT.A.1:** Recognize that in a multi-digit number, a digit in one place represents 10 times as much as it represents in the place to its right and 1/10 of what it represents in the place to its left. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **5.NBT.B.5:** Fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
