## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How does the standard algorithm record what partial products show?"

**Hook, day 57:** Pose the compelling question. Estimate 186 x 24, then find it with partial products: 4,464. In eight minutes, including retrieval below, compare records of the same product. Ask how a shorter record could preserve every part; introduce the standard algorithm on day 60.

**Supporting questions:** Where are the rectangle's parts recorded (day 59)? What does a composed digit represent (day 62)? Which multiple should we remove (day 68)? What does the remainder measure (day 72)?

**Closing claim:** I can multiply with the standard algorithm and explain division using useful multiples. Saved work S1 and A1, division task T1 and repair T2 provide evidence on day 77, completed through day 78 revisions.

**Teaching:** Connect rectangles to expanded records before modeling compact multiplication on day 60. Compare digit values on day 62; step back during mixed practice. Day 65 retrieves unit 1 volume. Model choosing and adjusting partial quotients on day 66, then compare students' decompositions before organizing them on day 68.


**Practices:** Day 63's digit-value audit names composed units and explains zeros (MP6). Day 64's record match connects compact rows to partial products (MP7). T1 and T2 check these independently.

**Rigor:** The 1,200 teaching minutes allocate 540 to understanding (45 percent), 240 to fluency (20 percent) and 420 to application (35 percent). Only 5.NBT.B.5 requires fluency: use spaced, mixed algorithm practice with feedback, without time pressure. Division practice consolidates explained strategies. Major work: one of one units and twenty of twenty teaching lessons, 100 percent; reserving thirty-five minutes for retrieval and repair leaves at least 1,165 minutes, 97.1 percent. No supporting cluster is assigned.

**Limits:** Multiplication has no standard digit ceiling; vary factor lengths through four digits by two. Division uses at most four-digit dividends and two-digit divisors. Standard division fluency belongs to grade 6. Fractional shares retrieve unit 2; unlike-denominator sums wait until unit 9.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Treats the multiplier's tens digit as ones. | The second row loses its place value. Repair: expand the multiplier and label the tens row before adding. | From memory. |
| B | Drops a newly composed unit. | Regrouping has lost value. Repair: exchange ten equal units for one next-place unit and attach it to the next step. | From memory. |
| C | Omits one partial product. | The factors have not been fully distributed. Repair: match each region to exactly one written product. | From memory. |
| D | Reports an estimate as an exact result. | Approximate and exact quantities are confused. Repair: calculate after estimating and label both results. | From memory. |
| E | Treats an object count as a group count without dividing by the group size. | Objects and groups have different units. Repair: divide the object count by the divisor to find its group count. | From memory. |
| F | Removes a multiple larger than the remaining dividend. | A proposed partial quotient has not been checked. Repair: compare its product with the remaining amount and choose a smaller multiple. | From memory. |
| G | Omits an internal zero from the whole quotient. | Summed partial quotients have lost their place values. Repair: write the sum in expanded form before compressing it. | From memory. |
| H | Adds the remainder as a whole share. | The remainder has not been shared among all groups. Repair: partition that remainder into divisor-many equal shares. | From memory. |
| I | Multiplies area by a known width to find length. | The unknown dimension is not linked to the area product. Repair: label the missing side and write width x length = area before dividing. | From memory. |
