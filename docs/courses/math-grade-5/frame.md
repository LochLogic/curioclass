# Course frame: Math Grade 5

Course id math-grade-5, grade 5, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10, following the base text's units in its order: its unit 2 is split (fractions as quotients, then
  fraction multiplication joined to its unit 3's), its unit 3 is split at fraction division, its unit 5 is split
  (decimal place value, then decimal operations), its unit 6 is split (powers of ten and conversion, then fractions
  and scaling), and its unit 8 (a year review) becomes course-level review days.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 145 in units and 15 at course level (3 course-level checks on 4 days, 4 respond
  days, 7 year-review days). Math is daily: one lesson of about 60 minutes every school day, five a week (the base
  text's lesson length: a warm-up of 5 to 10 minutes that mixes earlier work, the activities, a synthesis and our own
  exit question). 160 days is the full-year yardstick. The base text's year is 148 lessons (135 required) with 2
  assessment days a unit; the units here keep 123 lesson days (every required lesson through its unit 7, and five
  optional lessons kept for load) and add 2 days of our own where the rows' load notes ask for them. Each unit's
  days are its lessons, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, grade 5 (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt),
  taught in full, every lettered part placed (5.MD.C.3a and b, 5.MD.C.5a to c, 5.NF.B.4a and b, 5.NF.B.5a and b,
  5.NF.B.7a to c, 5.NBT.A.3a and b). The 25 progression rows are the contract; each unit names the rows it owns.
  There is no check set: the standard is used as written. The unit plan checks each unit against the Progressions
  documents, which the rows cite by page; the "Check set adds" column names the candidate check. Eureka Math is a
  reference for sequence and models only, never copied.
- **Major work.** The major clusters (5.NBT.A and B, 5.NF.A and B, 5.MD.C) hold units 1 to 7 outright and most of
  units 8 and 9: 9 of 10 units are mainly major work (90 percent by units). By days, about 133 of 160 (83 percent),
  counting a unit's lessons whose main standard is major and splitting its assessment and respond days in the same
  share; the year review is all major work. The line is 65 percent (RUBRIC-MATH-K8). The supporting and additional
  work practises the major work: expressions are written for volumes (unit 1), conversions practise powers of ten
  (unit 8), line plots practise adding and multiplying fractions (unit 9), and the coordinate grid graphs numerical
  patterns and perimeters (unit 10).
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, grade 5 (CC BY 4.0), adapted into our days with a
  credit line on each (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the store is S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\grade-5). Its
  order is kept; the source map at the end places every lesson. Only the free public pages are used: cool-downs,
  answer keys, section checkpoints and end-of-unit assessments sit behind a login and are not taken, so every exit
  question and assessment is our own. The 2nd edition (CC BY-NC) is never used.
- **Fluency.** 5.NBT.B.5 (multiplying multi-digit whole numbers with the standard algorithm) is the grade's required
  fluency, taught in unit 5 from grade 4's partial products and kept up after it in the warm-ups: short, spaced,
  mixed practice with feedback, never a countdown timer shown to a student. Division by two-digit divisors
  (5.NBT.B.6) stays with partial quotients and area models, every step explained; the division algorithm is grade 6's
  fluency. Volume in unit 1 multiplies with grade 4 strategies, since the algorithm comes in unit 5.
- **Fractions.** Fractions read in unit-fraction language ("three fifths of a pan"); one tape diagram and one area
  picture carried through units 2 to 4 and 9. Unlike denominators are added in unit 9, as the base text does, so
  units 3 and 4 multiply and divide without them; a product with a mixed number is rewritten as a fraction (grade 4
  skill) until unit 9.
- **Practices.** Each unit names one or two Standards for Mathematical Practice in the C3 column (C3 is a social
  studies framework and does not apply); the unit plan names the task where students do them.
- **Readiness.** The rows mark one weak handoff that skips grade 4: m3-area-concepts (area as unit squares) opens
  unit 1 with a readiness check before volume as unit cubes. Each unit's first base lesson revisits the grade 4 row
  it builds on as its readiness check.
- **Trapezoid.** The inclusive definition (a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides), the base
  text's own convention (its lesson 7.5), kept from grade 4 and stated again in unit 10.
- **Paper path.** Every manipulative has a paper form (unit cubes drawn on isometric dot paper, fraction strips, 10
  by 10 and thousandths grids, base-ten drawings, coordinate grids), and every practice generator prints. No student
  account.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. No unit needs a community or qualified
  reader; contexts are food, building, trash and recycling, sports records and maps. Food-waste and trash contexts
  stay about the class and the town, never about a family's habits or money.
- **Reading load.** Word problems in units 4 and 9 are the longest of the year; every word problem may be read
  aloud, and the unit plan names the words to teach (quotient, product, factor, scale).
