## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can what we know about whole numbers and fractions help us compute with decimals?"
**Hook, day 91:** Ask the compelling question. Show drawings of 0.46 and 0.38 with the same whole. Ask whether joining them makes a whole. After eight minutes including retrieval, model exchanging hundredths for tenths and connect to a written sum.
**Supporting questions:** Why align units (day 93)? Why does a tenth times a tenth make a hundredth (day 100)? Why can division produce more groups than wholes (day 102)?
**Closing claim:** I can compute all four decimal operations and connect drawings, place units and written methods. Records A1, S1, D1 and M1 establish this on day 106; C1 critiques reasoning.
**Teaching:** Model exchanges on days 91 and 94, connect area to products on day 100, and name group size before quotient notation on day 102. Step back during independent work; compare explanations before correcting.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows and one repair | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Right-aligns digits of unequal decimal lengths. | Unlike units are combined. Repair: place each digit in a labeled column before computing. | From memory. |
| B | Subtracts the smaller digit from the larger in each column. | Subtraction ignores quantity order. Repair: exchange one larger unit before subtracting. | From memory. |
| C | Calls a tenth times a tenth a tenth. | The product unit is too large. Repair: label the overlap in a unit square as one hundredth. | From memory. |
| D | Multiplies by the decimal divisor to find a quotient. | Group size replaces group counting. Repair: count divisor-sized groups in one whole before scaling the count. | From memory. |
| E | Renames a count of hundredths as tenths. | A computed count loses its original unit. Repair: write the count over 100 before decimal notation. | From memory. |
| F | Claims multiplying positive numbers always increases either factor. | A whole-number generalization is overextended. Repair: draw a fraction of a quantity with a factor below one. | From memory. |
| G | Writes the tens partial product as ones. | The algorithm loses factor place value. Repair: label the tens row with its full multiplier. | From memory. |
| H | Omits a newly composed unit in addition. | The exchange is not preserved in writing. Repair: circle ten smaller units and record their replacement in the next column. | From memory. |
