## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we divide with unit fractions, and when do we multiply or divide?"
**Hook, day 42:** After posing the compelling question, compare sharing one fourth of a paper sheet between two people with making quarter-sheet panels from two sheets, invented for practice. Use eight minutes, including retrieval below. Then explicitly teach the two meanings.
**Supporting questions:** What quantity is unknown (day 42)? Why does repartitioning change the denominator (day 44)? Why can division give more groups than whole units (day 46)? Which operation answers this question (day 50)?
**Closing claim:** I can choose multiplication or either permitted division type, represent the quantities and check my answer. Day 51's saved solutions and day 53's fresh explanations provide evidence, completed on day 54.
**Teaching:** Model sharing on day 43 and counting groups on day 45; connect each to inverse multiplication on days 44 and 46. Compare meanings on day 47. On day 50, ask students to name the unknown before calculating; step back for independent choices on day 51.

<!-- skel -->
- **5.NF.B.7a:** Interpret division of a unit fraction by a non-zero whole number, and compute such quotients. For example, create a story context for (1/3) ÷ 4, and use a visual fraction model to show the quotient. Use the relationship between multiplication and division to explain that (1/3) ÷ 4 = 1/12 because (1/12) × 4 = 1/3. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **5.NF.B.7b:** Interpret division of a whole number by a unit fraction, and compute such quotients. For example, create a story context for 4 ÷ (1/5), and use a visual fraction model to show the quotient. Use the relationship between multiplication and division to explain that 4 ÷ (1/5) = 20 because 20 × (1/5) = 4. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **5.NF.B.7c:** Solve real world problems involving division of unit fractions by non-zero whole numbers and division of whole numbers by unit fractions, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations to represent the problem. For example, how much chocolate will each person get if 3 people share 1/2 lb of chocolate equally? How many 1/3-cup servings are in 2 cups of raisins? Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **5.NF.B.6:** Solve real world problems involving multiplication of fractions and mixed numbers, e.g., by using visual fraction models or equations to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
