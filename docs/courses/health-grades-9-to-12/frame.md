# Course frame: Health, grades 9 to 12

Course `health-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `health-9-12`), one semester course for high school, typically taken in grade 9, that owns every grade 9 row of the health progression (the progression places the whole 9 to 12 span at grade 9). Draft frame, 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 21: 18 non-sensitive units (1 to 18) and three sensitive units (19, 20, 21), each removable. Every unit
  runs two to four days (Cory, 2026-11-20, Desk q-prework-health-pe-2: short units of two to four days, the goal
  rows as one weekly thread).
- **Days.** 80: 76 days in units and 4 course-level days (a mid-course check and its respond day, an end-of-course
  check and its respond day). One lesson of 45 minutes a day, five a week, for one semester of 16 weeks (the fall
  half of the school year, school weeks 1 to 16; a school that teaches it in the spring half keeps the same order
  and weeks 17 to 32). The 45 minutes hold the opening scenario or norm question (five minutes at most), the work,
  collecting sheets, and on food and hand-washing days five minutes of clean-up. The dose is the health approach
  page's default (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, Pacing: 9 to 12 about 80 days of 45 minutes, one semester,
  Desk q-later-arts-health-pe; kept by Cory 2026-11-21, Desk q-prework-open-2), so 60 hours. The national figure is
  higher: the 2007 National Health Education Standards (second edition, page 76) recommend at least 80 hours a year
  in grades 3 to 12; the course page names that figure and the gap so a school can add time. The load is about twice
  any earlier grade's (54 owned indicators; progression read 2026-11-19, finding 1), accepted by Cory (Desk
  q-prework-health-pe-2): days are weighed by load, so a row that owns three indicators gets a four-day unit or
  shares one with a one-indicator row of the same model, and every unit's last day is its end-of-unit assessment.
  Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (no unit runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit
  assessment). **The goal thread:** the two goal rows are one running thread. Unit 1 writes the goal (days 3 and
  4); then a ten-minute goal slot opens the first lesson of each week from week 2 to week 11 (ten slots, about two
  days in all), inside whatever unit is running, where students carry out, log, monitor and revise their goal
  (NHES.S6.12.4, NHES.S6.12.5, owned by unit 16); unit 16 gathers the log and evaluates it. A unit holding a slot
  plans 35 minutes of its own work that day, and units 2 to 15 are sized with that in mind. A sensitive unit's
  days count as optional (STANDARD.md section 5 rule 6): a school that leaves one out uses its days to extend the
  non-sensitive unit named in its row. These are course days: docs/courses/CALENDAR.md places them on school weeks.
- **Standard.** National Health Education Standards, 2024, SHAPE America: the 54 indicators of the 9 to 12 span
  (`NHES.S<n>.12.<n>`), each owned by one of the 25 non-sensitive grade 9 rows and placed in one unit here; ids and
  our own words only (data/standards/nhes-2024.txt), never the indicator text. Review tool: CDC's Health Education
  Curriculum Analysis Tool (HECAT), 2021 edition, keyed to the 2007 standards
  (docs/standards-notes/health-2007-to-2024.md); each unit names the healthy behavior outcomes it serves
  (`HECAT.<topic>.HBO<n>`) and every day ties to one (bar criterion 1). The course carries the 9 to 12 column of the
  coverage map (STANDARD.md section 4): outcomes marked "met in 6 to 8" are not taught again; any other outcome
  grade 6 to 8 already taught returns as a short review inside a unit. All eight standards are taught, and every skill
  standard (2 to 8) is practiced in at least two topics, counted with the sensitive rows taken out so the claim holds
  in a school that leaves them out: standard 2 in personal health (unit 3) and mental health (unit 7); 3 in personal
  health (unit 4), food (unit 6) and mental health (unit 6); 4 in mental health (units 8, 9, 10), violence prevention
  (unit 10) and alcohol and other drugs (unit 12); 5 in tobacco (unit 11), alcohol and other drugs (unit 12), safety
  (unit 13) and physical activity (unit 15); 6 in personal health (units 1, 16) and physical activity (unit 15's
  activity-plan revision); 7 in food (unit 14) and physical activity (unit 15); 8 in safety (unit 17), violence
  prevention and tobacco (unit 18). The C3 column of the Units table holds, for health, the skill standard the unit
  teaches or practices and its step card (the model). Check set: each unit planner reads the HECAT 2021 module of its
  topic, 9 to 12 knowledge and skill expectations, before planning (STANDARD.md section 3); the "Check set adds"
  column names what that read is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds, with the lines of
  "What a health day never does" (APPROACH.md).
- **Base text.** None. Health is Curio Class's own: cases, scenarios, step cards, data cards (invented for practice
  unless a source is named), source packets and family notes are written by Curio Class. Facts and figures come from
  public health agencies (CDC, including its Youth Risk Behavior Survey and, with FDA, the National Youth Tobacco
  Survey; NIH; FDA; the national highway traffic safety agency; the federal Department of Labor; SAMHSA), each with
  its source and year, checked by the health expert agent (below). No trade book, paid video or library visit.
  Sexual health curricula sold under license are reference only and never copied (docs/reviews/health-open-material-2026-11-16.md).
- **The health unit shape.** Each unit names its behavior outcomes, three objectives (knowing, valuing, doing) and
  one skill with its steps (APPROACH.md). Every skill here was first taught in an earlier grade, so the unit retrieves
  its step card on day 1, models it at the span-12 depth (the teacher says each step aloud on a new case), gives it
  to pairs for the fluency practice, withdraws it on the application day, and the end-of-unit assessment is taken
  without it. Norms and pressure (bar criterion 4): three non-sensitive Understanding days use a current national
  survey figure for high school students, with source and year, about what most peers really do: unit 2 (CDC Youth
  Risk Behavior Survey, daily physical activity), unit 11 (National Youth Tobacco Survey, past-month e-cigarette use;
  most high school students do not vape) and unit 12 (Youth Risk Behavior Survey, past-month alcohol use; most do
  not drink). The figures are picked at the unit plan from the latest edition and checked at the expert read.
  Pressure is met in the post in unit 6, the party, ride and job in unit 12, and the flavored-product ads in unit 11.
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day"): Understanding (first teaching of the span-12 idea, ending in
  a choice and a reason that uses a fact from the day), Fluency (practice only: pair rehearsals of a step card on
  short scenarios, a refusal line, a help request, a negotiation), Application (the skill on a new case, a team
  product or the student's own goal), Check (the end-of-unit assessment) and Respond (the course-level respond days).
  Fluency here is short: at most one whole Fluency day a unit, often a fifteen-minute pair rehearsal inside an
  Understanding day; the weekly goal slot is Application.
- **End-of-unit evidence.** Every unit ends on a check day: a sitting with an unseen case or scenarios, answered alone
  in writing (or by card, tool or with an adult; no student must speak aloud), scored on the skill steps with a
  guide, plus one facts-in-use item per fact the unit lists and one valuing item (what would be a good choice for
  this person and why, scored on whether the reason uses a unit fact, never on which choice; APPROACH.md). In units
  whose skill is a spoken exchange (8, 9, 10, 12), the rehearsal is heard from every student on a class record sheet
  across the unit's last two days, half on the day before the check and half on the check day while others write.
  Where an owned indicator asks the student to do something (NHES.S2.12.5 in unit 7, NHES.S7.12.4 in unit 14,
  NHES.S7.12.3 and NHES.S5.12.8 in unit 15, NHES.S6.12.4 and NHES.S6.12.5 in unit 16, NHES.S8.12.2 in unit 17,
  NHES.S8.12.5 in unit 18), the evidence includes the student doing it: a goal log, a team product delivered to a
  real audience, or a teacher record of an in-class demonstration, scored on the steps, never on health, home habits
  or whether a goal worked. A character's case shows understanding only and never stands alone for a doing
  indicator. A personal goal log may have its content covered; a student who keeps a goal wholly private runs a
  school-day goal from the safe list (water, a screen break during study, a walk at lunch, time outside) and logs
  that. Products (units 17 and 18) get a draft day, written feedback and a revision before the score, inside the
  units' days. Twice a unit the student rates their own use of the steps and names one next step (bar criterion 8).
  The teacher marks met, partly or not yet. Never a fact-recall test; no score on a day a topic is first taught.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** The count starts from grade 9. Rows last used in grade 8 (health grades 6 to 8) open
  as a retrieval task inside a day of the unit that needs them (sequence-write.md rule 2), named in each unit's row.
  Three assumed rows were last used two or more grades back and get a readiness check: g7-phw-living-with-conditions
  (grade 7) before unit 5, in the last ten minutes of unit 4's last day; g7-s-online-boundaries (grade 7) before unit
  10, in the last ten minutes of the mid-course respond day; g6-fn-eating-patterns (grade 6) before unit 14, in the
  last ten minutes of unit 13's last day. Each skips on purpose (the progression's own notes). A school that teaches
  the course in grade 10, 11 or 12 counts from that grade: every grade 8 row then gets a readiness check at the same
  points, and the scope and sequence says so in its short path. There are no routes.
- **Sensitive units and the leave-out rule.** STANDARD.md section 5: sensitive content lives only in its own units
  (sexual health and consent: unit 19; sexual violence, abuse and dating violence: unit 20; suicide and self-harm:
  unit 21). No skill lives only in a sensitive unit (each indicator a sensitive row touches is owned by a
  non-sensitive row: NHES.S4.12.4 and NHES.S4.12.6 in unit 10, NHES.S3.12.1 in unit 4, NHES.S4.12.3 and
  NHES.S4.12.10 in unit 8). Getting help is taught in non-sensitive units (4 and 8). No non-sensitive unit assumes a
  sensitive one. Each sensitive unit has a leave-out note on its first page and a longer family note (English and
  Spanish) sent home two weeks before; a student 18 or over gets the note and makes the leave-out choice themselves,
  under the school's rules (AGENTS.md, Desk q-prog-sel-1). The three sit after the non-sensitive units and right
  before the end-of-course check, and no course-level check samples them, so leaving one out changes no check. In
  sensitive units rehearsal uses written scripts of the refusal, telling or help line only; the teacher or a card
  plays any pressure; no student plays a person who pressures, abuses or threatens (APPROACH.md).
- **Reading level.** Grade 9 students read a case of about one page alone, and a source packet of two to four short
  agency pages in pairs; anything longer is read aloud or summarized on a card. Each unit teaches six to ten health
  words in depth (the rows' words) with a sentence frame for each skill step. The right idea said in another language
  is a language gap, not a content gap. No student must speak aloud: every rehearsal and check item can be done in
  writing, by card or with an adult.
- **Family notes.** Every unit sends a home task (a talk with a trusted adult the student chooses, a school adult
  counts, or a short list to read together), written in English and Spanish when the unit is finished. No task asks
  a student about their own drug use, sexual activity, diet, weight, mental health, abuse or family; cases are about
  characters, and personal plans stay with the student unless they choose to share.
- **Devices.** None needed; no owned code needs technology. NHES.S4.12.1 (many channels and media) is met on paper
  message templates (a text, a post, an email, a clinic portal message) that are never sent; NHES.S3.12.3 and
  NHES.S3.12.4 are met on printed source packets. A class set of devices, where a school has one, may open the same
  agency pages; any Curio Class practice tool runs with no student account, collects no health information and has
  the same items on paper.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper cases, step cards, source packets and logs; any electronic tool has the
  same task on paper beside it, and no student account is ever made.
- **Safety.** Hands-on days and their controls are named in the last column of the Units table: hand washing and
  the optional no-cook food demonstration in unit 14. Guides the safety read uses: CDC, "Clean Hands"
  (https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/); the federal food safety site (https://www.foodsafety.gov/); the school's own
  food, allergy and kitchen rules. Links named from memory, opened at the safety and expert reads. No real
  cigarette, vape, alcohol, medicine, pill or naloxone device is brought to class; pictures only.
- **Expert read (bar criterion 7).** The Curio Class health expert agent reads every unit plan and day for medical
  and scientific accuracy against current public health sources and records the date (STANDARD.md section 6). The
  figures in units 2, 3, 11 and 12, the drug and medicine facts in unit 12 and every fact in units 19 to 21 are read
  against their latest editions. The course page may say figures were last checked against their sources on a date,
  and that a school's health staff should read it too.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14), and the safety and answer-key reads are agent reads too. Units
  that need the agent to study named sources first are named in their last column; the communities: students with
  chronic conditions and disabilities (units 1, 2, 5, 15), students and families with low income, in neighborhoods
  named in public data, or without insurance (units 3, 4, 14), students with eating disorders or body image concerns
  (units 6, 14, 15), students who use nicotine, alcohol or other drugs or live with someone who does (units 11, 12),
  students who have lost someone on the road or at work (unit 13), LGBTQ students and students of every faith and
  culture (units 9, 19), students who have been harmed (unit 20), and students who have lost someone to suicide or
  are struggling now (unit 21). Every check gets the answer-key agent's read, and unit 14 the safety agent's read.
- **Grade 12 and older students.** The course is planned for grade 9. A school that teaches it to seniors in the
  spring half keeps every unit; the end-of-course check sits in finals week (week 32), and if seniors leave early
  the respond day folds into the check day's second half and the sensitive units' optional days give way first.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 9 to 12 have no stagger rule except finals weeks (CALENDAR.md rule 4: week 16 is
  the finals week of a half-year course). The mid-course check is in week 7 (day 35) with its respond day in week 8
  (day 36); the end-of-course check and its respond day are in week 16 (days 79 and 80), the finals week.

### Handed on: where the threads end

This is the last health course; no grade 9 row hands on to a later health row. The threads end here: the step cards,
the goal thread and the advocacy thread close in units 16 and 18, and the end-of-course check's results go to the
student. Three lines carry on outside health: physical activity goes on in PE grades 9 to 12 (the moving and training
plans), and the SEL grades 9 to 12 rows lean on nine of these rows, including grade 10 and 11 rows on
g9-meh-boundaries-consent and g9-meh-help-and-concern (Cross-subject).
