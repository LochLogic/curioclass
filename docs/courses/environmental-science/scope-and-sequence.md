# Scope and sequence: Environmental Science

Course `environmental-science`, grade 12 elective (grades 11 to 12 may take it), draft, 2026-11-23.

The year is 160 course days, five 50-minute lessons a week for 32 weeks: 149 days in the frame's nine units and 11 course-level check and respond days. Course day and school day coincide, so `school_day` is blank. The day order, sections, goals, skills, codes, tools, supplies and safety live in `data/plans/environmental-science/days.csv`; the base text placements live in `data/plans/environmental-science/source-map.csv`. No unit runs over 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment is reserved.

Each unit opens on its anchor, a question board and a first class model, names the day its model is revised, and ends on a transfer case the class has not seen. This sequence reserves assessment slots and their purposes, not items or keys. Every end-of-unit and check task asks about each part of each code it lists, a short prompt per part, or names the product that stands for it. No score comes from the day a topic is first taught; a product is drafted, gets written feedback, is revised in a named lesson and is scored after that revision. Each unit's response time is counted once, on its last day, a Respond day. The course owns only CUR-ENV.1 to CUR-ENV.21; each "Deepens" code of a core course is named in the day note as review, and that core course checks it. Literacy codes (RST.11-12, WHST.11-12) sit on the days that practice them and add no days.

Values rule, kept on every policy day: science says what can happen, values and law decide what should, and no student is asked to declare a political view. Climate is taught as the evidence shows (day 61).

## Unit 1. What sets the number of species?

**Days 1 to 18, weeks 1 to 4, 18 days.** Park Grass plots fed since 1856 hold far fewer species than unfed ones; the class asks why, and how many kinds share its schoolyard. Section 1.1 (days 1 to 5) plans and runs the field counts while the season allows: day 2 has each student propose placement, amount and accuracy before the class agrees one protocol, day 3 rehearses cover and plant names on photographs, days 4 and 5 are field days, the second resampling one plot so variation is seen before it is named. Section 1.2 (days 6 to 8) turns counts into richness and Simpson's index and judges a site difference against that variation. Section 1.3 (days 9 to 18) sets the class's correlation against the designed experiment (nature of science, day 9), reads the nutrient, acidity and competition records (days 10 and 11) and revises the model on day 12. Sampling comes first because every later unit reuses it; the drivers come after the class has its own counts to set against the experiment.

**Readiness:** day 1, last ten minutes after the hook, on bio-biodiversity-math, bio-ecosystem-stability-change and bio-population-carrying-capacity; repair in day 2's first 15 minutes.

**End-of-unit assessment, two end days.** The field sampling report (CUR-ENV.1, CUR-ENV.2) is drafted on day 13, written feedback comes back on day 14 while pairs argue a Park Grass prediction, and day 15 is the revision lesson; the revised report is scored. Day 17 is the sitting on an unseen long-term grassland experiment (CUR-ENV.3). The frame names the sitting for the last day; it sits on day 17 so the response, day 18, stays inside the unit. Day 18 responds to both once, then gives unit 2's readiness check.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** The planning element (individual and collaborative, deciding kind, amount and accuracy of data) is day 2 and days 4 to 5. The nature of science element (diverse methods) is day 9, revisited on day 12.

## Unit 2. Holding the soil

**Days 19 to 33, weeks 4 to 7, 15 days.** The Yellow River's sediment fell by about nine tenths as the Loess Plateau was terraced, replanted and dammed. Section 2.1 (days 19 to 24) builds what a soil is before asking why it moves: formation and profile (20), settling jar and feel (21), percent of each layer and infiltration (22), pH and organic matter (23), and an explanation of water holding from structure (24, the model revision). Section 2.2 (days 25 to 33) explains erosion (25), compares practices with yield and the farmer's costs (26), practices percent change (27), reads the plateau's own records (28) and argues the river's change against its rivals, dams and less rain (29). Day 30 names what else runs off a field, the bridge into unit 3.

**Readiness:** chem-resource-cost-benefit on day 18; repaired on day 26 where it is used. chem-water-earth-materials and bio-evaluate-impact-solutions are retrieval (19, 26).

**End-of-unit assessment.** Day 32, a sitting with an unseen soil test set and an unseen erosion record with rival causes (CUR-ENV.4, CUR-ENV.5); day 33 responds once and gives unit 3's readiness check.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Structure to function (properties inferred from structure) is days 22 to 24; evaluating competing solutions from evidence is days 28 and 29.

**Sensitivity:** days 19 and 28 name the farming families and the people moved from the steepest slopes, through Chinese researchers' own studies and the accounts of those who ran and lived under the program.

## Unit 3. The water you drink

**Days 34 to 51, weeks 7 to 11, 18 days.** About 400,000 people around Toledo were told not to drink their tap water in August 2014. Section 3.1 (days 34 to 39) follows the nutrients: cycles as stocks and flows (35), the watershed part-lesson (36), blooms and low oxygen (37), Lake Erie's load against bloom size (38) and a predicted load cut (39). Section 3.2 (days 40 to 44) teaches the tests and the tolerance index in class before the stream day (42) so field time is for sampling, then scores the stream (43). Section 3.3 (days 45 to 51) turns to supply: where water comes from and flow toward wells (45), the Aral Sea (46), treatment and its failure points (47), and the whole Toledo chain on day 48, the model revision.

**Readiness:** bio-carbon-cycle, chem-human-earth-systems and s7-human-impact-design on day 33; repaired on days 35 and 40.

**End-of-unit assessment.** Day 50, a sitting: the low-oxygen zone off the Mississippi mouth from federal records, and a stream's tests and animals scored by the index (CUR-ENV.6, CUR-ENV.7); the class's stream record is reused unscored as one item's context. Day 51 responds once.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Using a model to predict relationships between systems is days 36 and 39; matter conserved in a system is day 39, framed as a phosphorus budget in which every kilogram of the load is accounted for.

**Sensitivity:** days 34, 45 and 48 (families who have had unsafe water or rely on wells; Flint is not used).

## Unit 4. Turning off coal

**Days 55 to 69, weeks 11 to 14, 15 days.** Britain made about 40 percent of its power from coal in 2012 and closed its last coal station in September 2024. Section 4.1 (days 55 to 60) compares sources before the case: energy flows in a power station (56), a solar cell's output (57), sources per unit of electricity (58), reliability and cost (59) and practice (60). Section 4.2 (days 61 to 69) reads the climate records first (61), then Britain's records (62), lists the rival causes (63), weighs them by timing (64), tests a different mix (65) and writes the explanation (66).

**Readiness:** chem-climate-forecast and chem-carbon-cycle-quantitative on day 54, the respond day after check 1; repaired on day 56 and used on day 61.

**End-of-unit assessment.** Day 68, a sitting: an unseen country's grid record against rival causes and a supplied source comparison (CUR-ENV.8, CUR-ENV.9); day 69 responds once and gives unit 5's readiness check.

**Candidates settled, all three kept, no days added.** Evaluating the claims and reasoning behind an accepted explanation is day 64; energy and matter flows into, out of and within a system is days 56 and 65; weighing alternative explanations is days 63 and 64.

**Evidence meets belief and sensitivity:** day 61 (climate change: the measured records first, taught plainly and respectfully as the evidence shows). Days 55 and 66 name coal communities and families in fuel industries.

## Unit 5. The mines left behind

**Days 70 to 85, weeks 14 to 17, 16 days.** More than 500 abandoned uranium mines remain on the Navajo Nation. Section 5.1 (days 70 to 73) traces a mined material and its pathways, with the groundwater part-lesson (72) and the leaching model (73). Section 5.2 (days 74 to 79) judges exposure and dose (74), dose-response, biomagnification and each pathway's own harm (75), cause against correlation in health studies (76), who decides the cleanup (77) and a town's own waste (78). Section 5.3 (days 80 to 85) writes the evidence report. Pathways come before risk because a dose needs a route; the decision day comes after the risk so the class knows what the choices weigh.

**Readiness:** s7-resource-distribution (and, for a grade 11 taker, bio-sources-of-variation) on day 69; repaired on day 71. chem-nuclear-processes and bio-sources-of-variation are retrieval (71, 75).

**End-of-unit assessment, two end days.** The evidence report on one mine's pathway and risk (CUR-ENV.10, CUR-ENV.11) is drafted on day 80, feedback returns on day 81, and day 82 is the revision lesson; the revised report is scored. It is due school day 82, off ELA 12's check days 83 to 85. Day 84 is the sitting on an unseen hazardous-waste case (Love Canal, its facts rewritten as our own items). Day 85 responds to both once and gives unit 6's readiness check.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Reading adapted scientific literature for its conclusions is days 76 and 80; telling cause from correlation is day 76.

**Sensitivity:** every day of the unit, above all 70, 71, 75, 76, 77 and 80. Only material the Navajo Nation has released publicly; no contact with the Nation; "Diné" and "Navajo Nation" as the Nation uses them; its scientists, leaders, 2005 ban and its own cleanup decisions shown; no student shares a family health history. A fresh sensitivity read comes before the unit plan (later.csv L68).

## Unit 6. Two countries, two futures

**Days 86 to 100, weeks 18 to 20, 15 days.** Japan is shrinking while Niger grows by more than 3 percent a year. Section 6.1 (days 86 to 92) builds the model: rates (87), age pyramids and the transition (88), stepping by age group (89), projections with assumptions (90), practice (91) and what a projection can and cannot say (92). Section 6.2 (days 93 to 100) turns the energy pyramid into land (93), estimates per-person footprints (94), splits impact into people and use per person (95), works a class footprint (96) and writes the answer (97).

**Readiness:** s7-population-consumption and bio-energy-pyramids on day 85; repaired on days 87 and 93. bio-population-carrying-capacity is retrieval (87).

**End-of-unit assessment.** Day 99, a sitting: an unseen country's pyramid projected with stated assumptions and two footprints compared (CUR-ENV.12, CUR-ENV.13); day 100 responds once.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Mathematical and computational representations to support claims is days 89 and 90; significance depending on scale is day 95.

**Sensitivity:** days 86, 88, 90, 92, 94 and 97: family size, faith and culture never judged, no prescription, no blame, both countries through their own statistics offices, and no question about a household's income.

## Unit 7. Snakes in the Everglades

**Days 104 to 117, weeks 21 to 24, 14 days.** Road surveys counted about 99 percent fewer raccoons and opossums as pythons spread. Section 7.1 (days 104 to 110) tests the cause: threats (105), counting and its uncertainty with beans (106), before-and-after and the north-of-park comparison (107), rival causes (108), practice (109) and the written claim (110). Section 7.2 (days 111 to 117) judges what to do: strategies (111), removal data and a removal rate tested (112), a strategy chosen on a budget (113) and a second case (114). The cause is settled before the remedy because a strategy aimed at the wrong cause fails.

**Readiness:** bio-environment-change-species and bio-biodiversity-simulation on day 103, the respond day after check 2; repaired on days 105 and 112. bio-define-human-impact-problem is retrieval (111).

**End-of-unit assessment.** Day 116, a sitting: an unseen decline weighed against rival causes and a strategy chosen for an unseen case (CUR-ENV.14, CUR-ENV.15); day 117 responds once and gives unit 8's readiness check.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Weighing alternative explanations is days 107 and 108; evaluating a solution on prioritized criteria and trade-offs is day 113.

**Sensitivity:** days 104 and 111 (removal programs kill animals; released pets; students who keep reptiles).

## Unit 8. The hottest blocks

**Days 118 to 135, weeks 24 to 27, 18 days.** Some Richmond blocks ran about 9 degrees Celsius hotter than others, many inside 1930s hazardous grades. Section 8.1 (days 118 to 123) measures heat: why surfaces differ (119), the thermometers on school surfaces (120), the heat walk (121), mapping (122) and the explanation (123, the model revision). Section 8.2 (days 124 to 126) argues each link from a 1930s grade to today's heat on its own evidence and drafts the report. Section 8.3 (days 127 to 135) reaches air through the anchor's question: pollutants and ozone (127), inversions (128), trends (130), what rules changed (131) and why heat and bad air share blocks (132). Heat comes first because the class's own walk is the unit's evidence and needs the season; air follows once the class has a map to set it on.

**Readiness:** chem-reaction-rates and chem-resources-hazards-climate-human on day 117; repaired on days 127 and 124. chem-thermal-equilibrium and chem-earth-energy-climate are retrieval (119).

**End-of-unit assessment, two end days.** The heat walk report (CUR-ENV.17, CUR-ENV.18) is drafted on day 126, feedback returns over days 127 and 128, and day 129 is the revision lesson; the revised report is scored. Day 134 is the sitting on an unseen city's air record (CUR-ENV.16). Day 135 responds to both once.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Making and defending a claim from student-generated evidence is days 125 and 129; systems designed to cause a desired effect is day 132.

**Sensitivity:** days 118, 124, 125, 126 and 127 (race, housing and history; students may live in such neighborhoods; asthma, no student singled out).

## Unit 9. Designing for our own place

**Days 136 to 155, weeks 28 to 31, 20 days.** Philadelphia chose green infrastructure over bigger tunnels in 2011. Section 9.1 (days 136 to 142) is the investigation: the plan and its records (137), a team problem (138), a sampling plan approved for safety (139), two field days (140, 141) and a claim with its limits (142). Section 9.2 (days 143 to 145) designs, compares and refines a solution with a monitoring plan. Section 9.3 (days 146 to 155) explains how decisions are made, chooses a route and writes, revises and presents.

**Readiness:** none; every assumed row was checked earlier this year and returns as retrieval (138, 143, 147).

**End-of-unit assessment, two end days.** The team report (CUR-ENV.19 to CUR-ENV.21) is drafted on day 148, written feedback returns on day 149 during a peer critique, and days 150 and 151 are the two revision lessons; the report is scored on day 151. Day 153 holds the presentations with the route chosen, about seven teams at about six minutes. Day 155 responds to both once.

**Candidates settled, both kept, no days added.** Design, evaluate and refine a solution is days 143 to 145 and 150 to 151; feedback stabilizing or destabilizing a system is day 145.

**Sensitivity:** days 136 and 147 (local disputes; records chosen by the teacher; no student required to contact an official).
