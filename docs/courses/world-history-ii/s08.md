## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Which means of changing a society have worked best: nonviolence, negotiation, armed struggle or outside pressure?"
**Hook:** Day 108 pairs Nkrumah's independence speech with Lumumba's independence address and a dated colonial map. Why could declaring freedom leave unfinished work?
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: Independence for whom (109)? How do tactics change power (110)? What ended apartheid (112)? How do accounts and maps explain displacement (114)? What can peace agreements settle (116)? Which assumptions changed, for whom (118)?
**Closing claim:** Day 119 answers the compelling question using Nkrumah, Mandela, the Green Belt Movement and one counterexample. Judge outcomes, human costs and whose rights changed; answer a counterclaim. Any supported conclusion is valid.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing on 108, causal reasoning on 110 and corroboration on 114; step back on 118 and withdraw writing frames on 119. Teach decolonization, nationalism, partition, apartheid, sanctions, Zionism, antisemitism, Nakba and self-determination in context. Avoid reenactments, graphic images and family disclosure. Separate people, faiths, governments and armed groups. Correct false claims through evidence; students never declare a side.
