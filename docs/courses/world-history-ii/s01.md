## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the revolutions of 1776 to 1830 keep the promises they made, and for whom?"

**Hook:** First compare France's 1789 promise of equality with de Gouges's demand that women share it. Ask who could claim the promise.

**Supporting questions:** Co-write and revise these: Who held power (day 3)? Why did France revolt (4)? Which promises survived (7)? What changed in each Atlantic revolution (9)? Did independence change ordinary lives (11)? How do experts define success (12)?

**Closing claim:** On day 13, students answer the compelling question using the three founding declarations, de Gouges, Bolívar and community accounts. They qualify their answer by group and date and answer a counterclaim.

**Teaching:** Model sourcing on day 1, causation on 4 and interpretations on 7. Revisit questions; withdraw the argument frame on 12. Name executions and slavery plainly, without graphic images or role play. Allow private source discussion without family disclosures.

**Central America:** In day 10's source work, model federation and breakup against restored European dynasties; pairs practise. On day 11, model Salvadoran land loss and coffee exports against French exclusions and British manufactures; pairs practise social and economic comparisons. Reserve exit passages.


- **D1.2.9-12:** Explain experts' agreements and disagreements about interpreting and applying concepts within a compelling question. Parts: explain agreement; explain disagreement; address interpretation and application.
- **D1.3.9-12:** Explain those same parts for a supporting question, not just identify an ambiguous word. The local inventory holds codes only; the [published indicators](https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.pdf), pages 23 and 24, supply the verbs.

**Limits:** Retrieve earlier government and Enlightenment content. Latin America continues through 1888. Industry, unification and action belong to later units. Westphalia did not instantly create modern states. Trace Sweden's rise and decline and Poland's prominence and partitions.

**Carried:** Use the timeline on days 2 to 11, qualifying by region on 9. Change map scales on 8 and 10. Revise causal chains on 4 and 9; test perspective on 7 and 11. Connect claims to sources on 1; withdraw the frame on 12, retaining sources and maps.
