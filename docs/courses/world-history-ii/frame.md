# Course frame: World History II (about 1800 to today)

Course `world-history-ii` (catalogue id `world-hist-2`), grade 10, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 11 units, in time order (about 1800 to today, opening with the absolutism and revolutions of the late 1600s and 1700s as context).
- **Days.** 160 course days: 150 in units and 10 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year. Checked by days, not rows (the cross read): 43 rows in 150 unit days is about 3.5 days a row, so no unit is set by its row count. The heavy rows were weighed one by one: wh2-postwar-world (ten Massachusetts codes plus two C3), wh2-cold-war-europe and wh2-cold-war-world (one development in depth for each student, chosen across both rows, and a short survey of the rest), wh2-israel-palestine (eight lettered parts, more than one account of each), wh2-genocide-atrocity (one anchor case and a survey) and wh2-research-argument (a sustained project). Light rows (wh2-states-and-absolutism, short recall for its second code; wh2-terrorism; wh2-power-shifts-21st-century) take two or three days. The year has 10 check and respond days, two fewer than World History I, because the rows need the unit days. After the reads, units 8 and 10 gained 3 days (unit 8 13 to 14, unit 10 9 to 11) from units 1 to 3 (16 to 15, 12 to 11, 14 to 13): unit 1's Louis XIV and Peter the Great are recall of World History I, unit 2 has three rows, and wh2-colonial-culture is the lightest row of unit 3. The time a day: one lesson of about 50 minutes a day, five a week, across 32 weeks, so the 160 days fill the year. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and one respond day (a unit over 25 days also holds a mid-unit assessment); a row a unit assumes from the year before, or from earlier this year, opens as a retrieval task inside a day; any other earlier-grade row it assumes (last used two or more grades back) opens the unit with a readiness check on its first day (here, unit 8's grade 5 and 8 civil rights rows).
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 World History II, topics WHII.T1 to WHII.T7, is the content anchor: every WHII code is owned by one row and every row sits in one unit below. MA.WHII.T1.2 (England as the exception) repeats grade 9's MA.WHI.T6.3 and is a short recall in unit 1, not a reteach. C3 9 to 12 indicators carry the skills. Each unit plan is checked against California, Tennessee and New York and the college-level world history course framework; the "Check set adds" column lists candidates with their source, confirmed or dropped in the unit plan's comparison table, within the unit's days. Check set names never appear in student, teacher or family copy. The values rule governs contested claims; where a court has ruled (the Rwanda tribunal, the International Court of Justice and the Yugoslavia tribunal on Srebrenica, Nuremberg) the unit sides with the court.
- **Base text.** None. Partial base: East Tennessee State University, World History Since 1500 (CC BY 4.0, college level). Its chapters can serve these units: chapter 6 (revolutions) unit 1; chapter 7 (Industrial Revolution) unit 2; chapters 8 to 10 (nation building, Asia 1700 to 1900, the second wave of imperialism) unit 3; chapter 11 (the First World War, Indian independence movement, Russian Revolution) units 4 and 5; chapter 12 (interwar years, fascism) unit 5; chapter 13 (the Second World War) unit 6; chapter 14 (Cold War and decolonization) units 7 and 8; chapter 15 (the modern world) units 9 to 11. Passages are adapted down to the grade 9 to 10 band. The CC BY license covers the book's text and its own maps only: every image is checked on its own terms (several are non-commercial or stock), and current events after 2022 come from other dated sources. Chapters 1 to 5 belong principally to World History I. The chapter guide above is a list of candidates, not an adoption: the scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) selects passages and records each in the course's source map with its destination unit, whether it is adapted or left out and why, and its rights status (the book's text and own maps apart from third-party images). Where the base is thin (genocide cases, Israel and Palestine, Latin America) sources are primary documents, court records, survivors' testimony from open archives and each community's own published materials.
- **Inquiry arc.** Every unit has a compelling question that more than one answer can defend. Unit 1 teaches how to build an inquiry around a live disagreement among scholars (wh2-contested-questions), and unit 4 teaches how to critique a historian's interpretation (wh2-historians-interpretations); later units reuse both. Writing starts from the multi-page sourced argument with a counterclaim handed on by World History I (retrieved in unit 1, with the five source checks) and builds to the research argument of unit 11. Grade 10 adds: students find their own sources, answer classmates' critique, and present to the class and one other class; grade 12 goes further (an outside audience, two contrasting forms).
- **One example in depth.** wh2-wwii-war-and-diplomacy (one battle), wh2-cold-war-europe and wh2-cold-war-world (one development for each student, chosen across both rows together), wh2-decolonization (one movement), wh2-science-20th-century (one case) and wh2-genocide-atrocity (one case) follow the same route as World History I: a short survey of all options for everyone, one studied in depth, and survey pages complete enough that a student working alone needs no classmates' reports.
- **Genocide anchor.** wh2-genocide-atrocity anchors on the 1994 genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda, with Srebrenica (1995) as the second case (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13, q-ss-progression-1). The other cases on the Massachusetts list are met briefly; the unit first teaches the 1948 Convention's definition so mass violence that courts have not found to be genocide is named accurately. A student with family ties to a case may choose another.
- **Informed action.** Once in the year, in unit 11 (wh2-global-problems): each student chooses whether and how to act on one global problem, informed by evidence on what has worked; the course picks no cause. Each student takes the action through an adult (or records a reasoned choice not to act) and looks back on it; no visible change is needed for it to count. Any outside communication goes through the teacher or parent, using adult contact details.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Review roles follow FOUNDATION section 7.3, revised 2026-11-14: the sensitivity, safety and answer-key agents do the content reads, each cold and after studying its sources; the cases and sources named here stay binding. Before it reads units 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 (slavery and Indigenous content, colonial violence, genocide, the Holocaust, persecution and famine, civilian deaths, Israel and Palestine) the agent studies the community and sources each unit's last column names, the community's own published voice first. The course's checks get the answer-key read before the course is called finished.
- **Paper path.** Every source, map, data set and task has a printed version, and no day needs a device, an internet connection or a student account. Testimony and documentary segments come with a printed transcript; the unit 11 source search uses a bounded printed archive or index, and the talk a printed captioned map or panel.
- **Literacy standards (also met).** The unit work also meets these Common Core literacy standards for history and social studies (data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt; each row's notes name them), with no added days: unit 3, RH.9-10.7; unit 4, RH.9-10.3, RH.9-10.6, RH.9-10.7, RH.9-10.8 and RH.9-10.9; unit 5, RH.9-10.7; unit 9, RH.9-10.7; unit 11, WHST.9-10.1, WHST.9-10.4, WHST.9-10.5, WHST.9-10.7, WHST.9-10.8 and WHST.9-10.9. Across the year: RH.9-10.2, RH.9-10.4 and RH.9-10.10 (every unit reads, glosses and summarizes sources in the grade 9 to 10 band) and WHST.9-10.10 (short writing in every unit, the research argument in unit 11). WHST.9-10.6 is not claimed: the paper path keeps every product off a device.
- **Reading level.** Grade 9 to 10 band; excerpts glossed; every source in a unit plan has a stated difficulty, a reason and a rights line. Period texts that hold slurs are excerpted around them (rule 6).
- **Current events.** Units 9 to 11 reach the present. Every current claim is dated, and the unit plan names what to recheck each year.
