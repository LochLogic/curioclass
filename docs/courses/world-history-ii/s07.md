## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was the Cold War decided more by what happened inside the Soviet bloc or by pressure from outside it?"
**Hook:** Day 93 pairs Truman's 1947 address with Novikov's 1946 telegram and a dated European occupation map. Ask how each speaker makes the other's power seem threatening.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: How did rival systems organize power (94)? What restrained or escalated conflict (96)? How did local aims shape interventions (100)? Could economic reform coexist with repression (102)? Why did bloc governments lose control (105)?
**Closing claim:** Day 105 answers the compelling question from Gdańsk's demands, the corrected collapse chronology and the arms-race and Afghan-war records. Weigh an opposing claim; either weighting needs a causal mechanism and a limit.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing and mapped inference on 93, causal comparison on 97 and corroboration on 102. Teach containment, nonalignment, deterrence, detente, glasnost, perestroika and oligarchy in context. Day 100 pairs investigate one chosen development; complete printed surveys serve one child. Day 101 teaches the civil war and 1949 victory before Mao's campaigns. Model causal weighting on 103; withdraw argument frames on 105. Name civilian deaths, famine and repression without graphic images, reenactments or family disclosure. Students never represent nationalities; distinguish peoples from governments.
