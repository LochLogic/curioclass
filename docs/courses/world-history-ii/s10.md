Item 2 uses the Akayesu tribunal release's public-incitement finding, in a nongraphic paraphrase with attribution. Item 4 uses unused 2014 telephone-records executive-summary findings, a separate member's statement, and an unused families' association recovery account. Teach from other passages. Do not reuse assessment labels or transfer situations in teaching or exits.

**Item 1, teacher judged:** Look for a listed act, intent to destroy, a protected group and a source-based explanation. Meets: "The court's evidence supports intent to destroy a substantial part of the Bosnian Muslim group, rather than simply to remove residents; a local part can qualify." Does not: "Any large death total proves genocide" (A). The seven descriptions are: killing group members; serious bodily or mental harm; deliberately imposed living conditions calculated to destroy the group physically; measures intended to prevent births; forcibly transferring children to another group; destroying buildings without evidence of destructive intent; ordinary political disagreement. Select the first five. Apply the intent and protected-group requirement to each, not a requirement that all five occur.

**Item 2, teacher judged:** Look for interacting structural causes and deliberate choices, one international failure, and the source's purpose and limits. Meets: "Colonial administration hardened identities, but extremist leaders chose organized killing. The tribunal finding connects an official's incitement with violence. International warnings and reduced protection show decisions failed victims; the release cannot represent every survivor's experience." Does not: "Ancient hatred made killing inevitable, and nobody could act" (B, N). Alternative cases require their own causes and legal records, not a substituted place name.

**Item 3, teacher judged:** Look for the two distinct groups in the sitting (the Taliban from day 142's revised response), people, events, enabling conditions and consequences. Meets: "Bin Laden's al-Qaeda grew from Afghan-war networks and planned September 11. Mullah Omar's Taliban rose during Afghan civil war and sheltered al-Qaeda. Zarqawi's Iraqi network and Baghdadi's later expansion exploited war and state breakdown, developing into ISIS and territorial rule." Does not: "The Taliban and ISIS jointly organized September 11 because all Muslims supported them" (F). Accept other accurately sourced people and links. Distinguish Afghanistan's 2001 intervention from Iraq's 2003 invasion; Iraq did not organize September 11.

**Item 4, teacher judged:** Look for an intended security benefit, documented result, privacy cost, societal response, counterclaim and evidentiary limits. Meets: "The oversight majority found little additional demonstrated benefit from bulk records and identified privacy risks; a separate member disputed parts of that evaluation. Peer support addressed families' recovery needs, but a mission statement cannot establish recovery rates. More targeted collection with oversight is defensible." Does not: "Every measure worked, and a support group's existence proves all families recovered" (G). Other evidence-based judgments earn equal credit. Keep this a historical 2014 policy case, not a statement of current surveillance law. The bulk program ended under the USA FREEDOM Act of 2015.

**Survey scoring:** Each survey row requires a picked answer and an underlined evidence cue. They examine breadth; item 2 tests depth. Do not require ten paragraphs. Mark factual distinctions against the key; teacher-judge explanations as secure, partial or needs teaching on the record sheet. Plausible conclusions without evidence are partial. Correct institutional attribution matters: criminal tribunals judge individuals; the International Court of Justice judges state responsibility. The 2007 judgment found Serbia failed to prevent Srebrenica genocide, not that Serbia itself committed it.
