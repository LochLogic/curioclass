## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why did the world's promise of 'never again' fail in Rwanda and Srebrenica, and what has it learned?"

**Hook:** On 134, pair Article I's prevention promise with Kigali and Srebrenica memorial descriptions of remembrance. Ask what a promise needs to protect people.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: What distinguishes genocide (134)? How did exclusion become organized killing (135 to 136)? Who could have acted (137)? What can justice repair (138)? Why did Srebrenica's protection fail (139)? Which warnings recur (140)? How should responses protect lives and liberties (142)?
**Closing claim:** On 142, write 120 words answering the compelling question from Kigali testimony, the United Nations inquiry into Rwanda, Srebrenica memorial accounts and court findings. Explain two failures, evaluate one later response and answer a counterclaim. Court findings are evidence, never subjects for a denial debate.
**Teaching:** Model classification on 134, sourcing testimony on 136 and causal argument on 137; step back on 138 and withdraw the writing frame on 142. Teach genocide, mass atrocity, ethnic cleansing, intent, tribunal, prevention, terrorism and civil liberties. Give content notes, nongraphic transcripts and equivalent case choices without requiring disclosure. No reenactments, atrocity simulations, propaganda replay or collective blame.


**Limits:** Rwanda carries depth; everyone studies Srebrenica and every survey case. Alternative depth cases get equivalent sources and criteria. Mass atrocity is an umbrella term; genocide needs a protected group, a listed act and destructive intent. Forced removal alone does not establish genocide. Prevention need not await judgment. Informed action belongs to unit 11.
**Carried:** Keep unit 9's source-sourcing, corroboration, cause-effect-chain, perspective, timeline, region, map-scale, continuity-change and claim-evidence-reasoning. Tie the causal chain to Rwanda on 135; remove prompts on 138. Return perspective on 136, chronology and maps on 139, and continuity-change on 142. Supply-demand and latitude-longitude rest. Retain source labels and access supports.

**Item 1, teacher judged:** Look for a listed act, intent to destroy, a protected group and a source-based explanation. Meets: "The court's evidence supports intent to destroy a substantial part of the Bosnian Muslim group, rather than simply to remove residents; a local part can qualify." Does not: "Any large death total proves genocide" (A). The seven descriptions are: killing group members; serious bodily or mental harm; deliberately imposed living conditions calculated to destroy the group physically; measures intended to prevent births; forcibly transferring children to another group; destroying buildings without evidence of destructive intent; ordinary political disagreement. Select the first five. Apply the intent and protected-group requirement to each, not a requirement that all five occur.

**Item 2, teacher judged:** Look for interacting structural causes and deliberate choices, one international failure, and the source's purpose and limits. Meets: "Colonial administration hardened identities, but extremist leaders chose organized killing. The tribunal finding connects an official's incitement with violence. International warnings and reduced protection show decisions failed victims; the release cannot represent every survivor's experience." Does not: "Ancient hatred made killing inevitable" (B). Separately: "Nobody could choose protection" (N); "The United Nations acted independently of states" (C); "This tribunal release tells every survivor's experience" (J). Alternative cases require their own causes and legal records, not a substituted place name.

**Item 3, teacher judged:** Look for the two distinct groups in the sitting (the Taliban from day 142's revised response), people, events, enabling conditions and consequences. Meets: "Bin Laden's al-Qaeda grew from Afghan-war networks and planned September 11, prompting the 2001 United States intervention in Afghanistan. Mullah Omar's Taliban rose during Afghan civil war and sheltered al-Qaeda, bringing international pressure. Zarqawi's Iraqi network and Baghdadi's later expansion exploited war and state breakdown, developing into ISIS and territorial rule." Does not: "The Taliban and ISIS jointly organized September 11" (F). Accept other accurately sourced people and links. Distinguish Afghanistan's 2001 intervention from Iraq's 2003 invasion; Iraq did not organize September 11.

**Item 4, teacher judged:** Look for an intended security benefit, documented result, privacy cost, societal response, counterclaim and evidentiary limits. Meets: "The oversight majority found little additional demonstrated benefit from bulk records and identified privacy risks. A defender might argue that keeping all records enables fast connections during emergencies. That possibility deserves testing, but does not outweigh documented privacy costs without evidence of added security. This report cannot settle every future emergency. The families' association describes peer support addressing recovery needs, but its account cannot establish recovery rates. Targeted collection with oversight is defensible." Does not: "Every measure worked, and a support group's existence proves all families recovered" (G). Other evidence-based judgments earn equal credit. Keep this a historical 2014 policy case, not a statement of current surveillance law. The bulk program ended under the USA FREEDOM Act of 2015.

**Survey scoring:** Each survey row requires a picked answer and an underlined evidence cue, scored separately by the teacher as 0 or 1. They examine breadth; item 2 tests depth. Do not require ten paragraphs. A plausible conclusion without evidence earns only its choice cell. Correct institutional attribution matters: criminal tribunals judge individuals; the International Court of Justice judges state responsibility. The 2007 judgment found Serbia failed to prevent Srebrenica genocide, not that Serbia itself committed it.
