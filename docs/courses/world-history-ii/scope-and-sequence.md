# Scope and sequence: World History II (1800 to today)

Course `world-history-ii` (catalogue id `world-hist-2`), grade 10, full year (160 days), draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/world-history-ii/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment built on unseen sources (bar item 7), with its respond day after it (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Days with genocide, war, slavery, religious, Native, LGBTQ or hard-history content carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. Personal finance is touched once, inside day 62 (a bank run where deposits had no insurance), with no added day. Every end-of-unit assessment and course-level check asks about each part of each code it lists, with a short prompt per part; the day's goal in days.csv names the parts (the blueprint), with no day added.

The base text is partial: John Rankin and Constanze Weise, World History Since 1500 (ETSU, CC BY 4.0), chapters 6 to 15. data/plans/world-history-ii/source-map.csv records every chapter section, the day it lands on, merges and splits, each cut with its reason, and (in `order_conflict`) why a section is used out of the book's order; every other unit day has an "ours" row; days that adapt it name the section in the note. Credit line on each adapted day: "Adapted from John Rankin and Constanze Weise, World History Since 1500, ETSU, CC BY 4.0." The book's images are not covered by that licence and are checked one by one. The book is read against each community's own scholarship where the frame asks (Haiti, the Congo, the Holocaust, Nanjing, the Holodomor, partition, Kenya), and its last chapter is rechecked for events after it was written.

Unit 8 opens with a readiness check (day 108) on the grade 5 and 8 civil rights rows day 117 uses (ss5-civil-rights-movements, ss8-civil-rights-laws), which grade 9 did not use. Every other earlier row is from World History I (the year before) or was used there (ss8-civic-action, World History I's action cycle), and opens as a retrieval task inside a day; cross-subject leans (chemistry, biology, grade 7 science, grades 6 to 8 math) are retrieval too. Grade 9's argument, source checks and multi-cause chain are retrieved where they are reused (days 1, 17, 43, 54).

The course shares one research product with ELA 10: ELA builds the reading on rising seas in weeks 26 to 28, history supplies the question set, the project runs in weeks 29 to 31 (the report and talks in week 31), and history's audience adaptation comes in week 32. Unit 11's days are placed to match.

## Unit 1. States, rights and revolutions, 1648 to 1830 (days 1 to 15, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** an inquiry where historians disagree about the revolutions of 1776 to 1830; the Thirty Years War and the Peace of Westphalia; Louis XIV and England recalled, Peter the Great, serfdom's spread, Prussia, Poland and Sweden; the causes of the French Revolution, its course through the Terror, Napoleon and the Congress of Vienna; the Revolution judged against its own goals, with Olympe de Gouges; the Haitian Revolution as self-liberation and France's indemnity from 1825; the American, French and Haitian Revolutions compared; Latin American independence with Bolivar's Jamaica Letter; strongmen, export economies, the end of slavery in Brazil (1888) and Indigenous peoples in the new nations; a sourced argument with a counterclaim.

Arc: one day framing the contested question, two on the state system as background, four on France, two on Haiti and the comparison, two on Latin America, then two to plan and write the argument. France before Haiti so Haiti can be seen answering France's promises.

- Readiness: none. Day 1 opens with a retrieval task on grade 9's source checks and argument; days 3, 4, 8, 9 and 10 retrieve World History I rows.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 14: absolutism, the French, Haitian and Latin American revolutions, and a contested-question task on unseen sources. Respond day 15.
- Candidate: the Congress of Vienna, kept, part of day 6, 0 added days. Candidate: Bolivar's Jamaica Letter, kept, the source of day 10, 0 added days.

## Unit 2. The Industrial Revolution, 1750 to 1900 (days 16 to 26, weeks 4 to 6)

**Topics taught:** why industry began in Britain (enclosure, coal, capital, colonial markets and cotton grown by enslaved people, the steam engine); the causes weighed in a chain; industry's spread to Europe, the United States and Meiji Japan; population and wage data on migration, the middle class and inequality; factory inspectors' reports and workers' testimony (dangerous work, child labour, pollution); laws and rail lines reshaping city and countryside; early feminism, socialism, communism, and liberal and conservative replies; British reform (unions, factory acts, the Chartists, the vote).

Arc: three days on causes and spread, three on effects from data and testimony, two on the ideas and reforms that answered them, then the sourced claim. Causes before effects so the testimony is read against a known cause chain.

- Readiness: none. Day 16 retrieves World History I's cotton economy and Scientific Revolution.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 25: causes and spread of industry, its effects and the reform ideas, with a data and testimony task on unseen sources. Respond day 26.
- Candidate: Meiji industrialization, kept, part of day 18, 0 added days.

## Unit 3. Nations and empires, 1850 to 1914 (days 27 to 39, weeks 6 to 8)

**Topics taught:** Italian and German unification on before and after maps, with 1848 as context; European control outside Europe mapped at two scales; the causes of 19th century imperialism (rivalry, slavery's profits and the debate over how much they financed industry, the Iberian empires' goals); race theories and Social Darwinism shown false; empire in Asia (British India and 1857, the Opium Wars, the Taiping and self-strengthening, Japan's own empire); empire in Africa (the Berlin Conference, the Congo Free State, the Herero and Nama genocide, Adwa); how context shaped colonizers' and colonized views, and Latin America's dependence; how empire changed Western culture; a colonial text beside a colonized writer's response; resisting, negotiating and governing as answers to empire.

Arc: one day on unification, three on causes and justifications, three on impact, two on culture and writing, then two on the answers of the ruled and the compelling question. Causes before impact; the ruled answer last so they have the final word. The Herero and Nama genocide and Adwa sit on day 36 among the answers to empire, so a genocide is not a fifth of a crowded Africa day (day 32 keeps African states before the scramble, Berlin and the Congo).

- Readiness: none. Days 28, 29 and 31 retrieve World History I's empire map and slave trade rows.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 38: unification, the causes and impact of empire and its cultural effects, with an unseen source on context and writers' views. The two-scale map is tested once, in check 1 (day 41). Respond day 39, with review of units 1 to 3.
- Candidate: the Taiping Rebellion and self-strengthening, kept, part of day 31, 0 added days. Candidate: the Ottoman Tanzimat, dropped: no day free; Ottoman decline returns as background to the fall of empires (day 50), which does not teach the Tanzimat. Candidate: indentured labour from India and China, dropped: no day free; US History I carries Asian migration.

## Course-level check 1 (days 40 to 42, weeks 8 to 9)

Day 40 (week 8): a sourced argument from an unseen document set on revolution or industry. Day 41 (week 9): a map task at two scales and a data table. Day 42 (week 9) responds by group. Unit 3 was assessed on day 38, so the check's items for unit 3's skills (the two-scale map, slavery's capital) draw their content from units 1 and 2.

## Unit 4. The First World War and its peace, 1914 to 1923 (days 43 to 58, weeks 9 to 12)

**Topics taught:** the long-term causes (Germany's rise, nationalism, imperial rivalry, militarism, alliances) told from the trigger at Sarajevo; industrial weapons, trenches and casualties; the war worldwide, with soldiers and labourers from India, Africa and China, and United States entry; propaganda posters and production charts (conscription, rationing, state control); the Armenian genocide of 1915 to 1923 with Assyrians and Greeks, from survivors' testimony, and the Turkish state's denial studied as denial; economic ruin, the Lost Generation, the fall of the Russian, Ottoman and Austrian empires; Ataturk's Turkey, the mandates and new Middle East borders with Balfour and Sykes-Picot; the Paris Peace Conference and Versailles; the influenza pandemic and modernism; two historians' readings of Versailles critiqued; a written interpretation set against a documentary segment or museum display.

Arc: two days on causes, three on the war, two on the genocide, four on the postwar world, then three on interpretation ending in the compelling question. The critique of historians sits here so it is taught before ELA 10's argument unit (weeks 13 to 17).

- Readiness: none. Day 43 retrieves grade 9's multi-cause chain with a worked example; day 54 retrieves grade 9's sources and interpretations.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 57: causes, total war, the Armenian genocide and the postwar world, with a critique of an unseen historian's account. Respond day 58.
- Candidate: the Balfour Declaration and Sykes-Picot, kept, part of day 51, 0 added days.

## Unit 5. Revolution, depression and dictators, 1905 to 1939 (days 59 to 72, weeks 12 to 15)

**Topics taught:** Russia from Nicholas II to 1917 and a 1905 to 1928 timeline (civil war, the USSR, the New Economic Policy, Stalin's rise); the Depression's causes (tight money, the gold standard); unemployment and price data, German hyperinflation, a bank run; instability in weak democracies (Germany), Britain as the democracy that survived, Keynes, von Mises, Hayek and Friedman read with data; Mussolini's Italy; Stalin's purges, the Gulag, and the famines in Ukraine (the Holodomor) and Kazakhstan; the Nazi rise and the Nazi laws against Jews; why democracy failed or survived; the roads to war (Ethiopia, Spain, Japan in Manchuria and China with the Nanjing Massacre and its denial, the Philippines, the Rhineland to Poland, the Nazi-Soviet Pact) and appeasement judged with what leaders knew.

Arc: two days on Russia, three on the Depression, four on the dictatorships ending in the compelling question, then three on the three roads to war. The dated timeline distinguishes the routes to dictatorship. Mussolini's rule and Stalin's consolidation began before the Depression; the Depression intensified instability and helped the Nazi rise. Britain supplies the comparison with a surviving democracy.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 71: the Russian revolutions, the Depression, the totalitarian regimes and the road to war, with a dated-evidence task on unseen sources. Respond day 72.
- Candidate: the three roads to war as the frame for Japan's militarism, kept, the frame of days 68 to 70, 0 added days. Candidate: Gandhi's interwar campaigns and the Salt March, dropped: unit 8's India option carries Gandhi (day 109) and no day is free.

## Unit 6. The Second World War and the Holocaust, 1939 to 1945 (days 73 to 88, weeks 15 to 18)

**Topics taught:** the war's course in Europe and the Pacific; one battle in depth (Britain, Midway, Stalingrad, Normandy or Iwo Jima); occupation, forced labour and the "comfort women" system in Asia; Churchill, Roosevelt and Stalin through the wartime conferences; Jewish life before the war and the roots of the Holocaust; Nazi laws, ghettos, mass shootings, the death camps and the murder of about six million Jews; the persecution and murder of Roma people, disabled people, gay men and others; testimony on resistance (the Warsaw Ghetto uprising), rescuers, bystanders and perpetrators; Hiroshima and Nagasaki, the hibakusha, and how present-day views shape interpretation; the dead and uprooted; reform and decolonization, the superpowers and the arms race; the UN, the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Nuremberg and the Geneva Conventions.

Arc: four days on the war, four on the Holocaust, two on the atomic bombs, then four on consequences ending in the compelling question. No simulations or "what would you do" games.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 87: the war, the Holocaust, the atomic bombs and the war's consequences, with an unseen source on interpretation. Respond day 88, with review of units 4 to 6.
- Candidate: the war in Asia beyond Nanjing, forced labour and the "comfort women" system, kept, part of day 75, 0 added days.

## Unit 7. The Cold War, 1945 to 1991 (days 93 to 107, weeks 19 to 22)

**Topics taught:** rival worldviews and Europe's split from each side's own statements; the UN, NATO, the Warsaw Pact and the non-aligned movement; divided Germany, the Berlin crises of 1948 and 1961, Soviet life after Stalin, Hungary (1956) and Czechoslovakia (1968); the arms race, ABM and SALT, detente and the space race; the Marshall Plan and containment in Korea and Vietnam; United States backing for coups in Iran (1953) and Guatemala (1954), military rule in Latin America and Chile 1973; the Middle East, the Soviet war in Afghanistan, the Cuban Revolution and the Missile Crisis; one development in depth; China from 1949 through the Great Leap Forward and its famine to the Cultural Revolution; official Chinese accounts of Deng's reforms and Tiananmen against other records; why the Soviet bloc fell (Solidarity, Gorbachev, 1989, 1991) and the effects of the breakup.

Arc: two days on the blocs, two on Europe, three on the wider world, one in depth, two on China, then three on the collapse and the compelling question.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 106: the blocs, the Cold War in Europe and beyond, China and the Soviet collapse, with an official account to corroborate. Respond day 107.
- Candidate: the space race, kept, part of day 96, 0 added days. Candidate: Latin American military rule and the 1973 coup in Chile, kept, part of day 98, 0 added days.

## Unit 8. Independence and equal rights after 1945 (days 108 to 121, weeks 22 to 25)

**Topics taught:** nationalist movements in Asia, Africa, the Middle East and Latin America, with Congo's independence and Patrice Lumumba; one movement in depth (India and partition, Ghana, Kenya, Algeria or Vietnam); the means movements used, then and now; apartheid as its government defended it, and South Africans' resistance to the negotiated end (Sharpeville, Soweto, the ANC, Mandela, sanctions); Zionism, Jewish immigration and antisemitism as background; the 1947 partition vote and the war of 1948 from more than one account (the War of Independence and the Nakba; the Arab states' rejection and invasion; Palestinians' loss of land and homes through expulsion by Israeli forces and flight from the fighting; Jewish refugees from Arab countries); the wars since 1948; the region's cultures and faiths today and the peace attempts; late 20th century movements (feminism, LGBTQ rights, environmentalism, racial equality) with the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007).

Arc: three days on decolonization, two on South Africa, four on Israel and Palestine, then three on social movements ending in the compelling question about means. Every contested event carries more than one account; students are never asked to state a side.

- Readiness: day 108 opens with a readiness check on ss8-civil-rights-laws and ss5-civil-rights-movements (not used in grade 9), used on day 117.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 120: decolonization, apartheid, Israel and Palestine (one short prompt for each of the eight parts) and the social movements, with two unseen accounts of one event. Respond day 121.
- Candidate: Congo's independence and Lumumba, kept, part of day 108, 0 added days. Candidate: the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, kept, part of day 117, 0 added days.

## Unit 9. A connected world since 1945 (days 122 to 133, weeks 25 to 27)

**Topics taught:** the postwar boom in Western Europe and Japan from growth data; 20th century science cases (Einstein to Jane Goodall), one in depth; why globalization grew after 1991, with the Green Revolution as an earlier connection; globalization's effects on workers, prices and the environment; competition for scarce resources; the Iranian Revolution, the Saudi-Iranian rivalry, the Iran-Iraq War and the Gulf and Iraq wars; the Arab uprisings, Syria, Yemen, ISIS, Kurdish autonomy and the Gulf states; populism in Europe and China's growing power from their own statements and data.

Arc: one day on the boom, two on science, three on globalization with the compelling question answered from data on day 126, two on the Middle East, then one day each on European populism (day 130) and China's growing power (day 131), the two power standards.

- Readiness: none. Day 123 retrieves chemistry's nuclear processes and grade 9 biology's DNA.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 132: the postwar boom, one science case, globalization, the Middle East and 21st century power, with an unseen data set. Respond day 133.
- Candidate: the Green Revolution, kept, part of day 125, 0 added days. Candidate: recent pandemics, dropped: unit 11 carries disease (day 147). Candidate: tradition and modernization in Turkey and Iran, dropped: Ataturk (day 51) and the Iranian Revolution (day 128) carry it.

## Unit 10. Genocide, atrocity and terror since 1945 (days 134 to 144, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** the 1948 Genocide Convention's definition and how genocide differs from other mass atrocity; the genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda (its colonial roots, survivors' testimony, the world's failure to act, the tribunal and gacaca courts, the Congo wars after); Srebrenica inside the Bosnian War, and Kosovo, with the tribunals' findings; a brief survey (Northern Ireland, Cambodia, Darfur and South Sudan, Kashmir, Sri Lanka, Guatemala, Syria, Shi'a and Sunni conflict, the Rohingya) for causes and warning signs; international terrorism (al-Qaeda, September 11, the Taliban, ISIS); one government response, with the civil liberties debate.

Arc: the frame's budget exactly: definition 1 day, Rwanda 4, Srebrenica 1, survey 1, terrorism 2, end 1, respond 1. The definition first so every case is named against it.

- Readiness: none. The Convention's definition is first taught here.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 143: the definition applied, the Rwanda and Srebrenica cases, and one response to terrorism, from unseen sources. Respond day 144.
- Candidate: none in the frame.

## Unit 11. Global problems and the research argument (days 145 to 157, weeks 29 to 32)

**Topics taught:** a research question from the paired-school question set and a judged source list of at least five; international efforts on climate change, habitat and farm output, and climate's effects at local to global scales, with the UN Sustainable Development Goals as the yardstick; humanitarian efforts on disease and AIDS, child mortality, refugees, girls' education and rural economies; the report (explanation and argued recommendation with counterclaims), peer critique and revision; a short talk with a captioned map or panel; the informed action; the argument adapted for a second audience.

Arc: placed on the shared brief's milestones. Day 145 (week 29) launches the research with a modelled search, then each student's own search of a bounded printed archive or index (at least five useful sources, primary and interpretive, each kept or rejected with a reason); days 146 and 147 build the content and double as research time (each student reads two of their own sources against the day's content); day 148 drafts (by mid week 30), day 149 critiques and revises, day 150 prepares the talk from a modelled talk (week 30, when ELA 10 teaches it); day 151 hands in the report and day 152 gives the talks in groups of four (week 31); days 153 and 154 run the informed action: compare efforts across time and place, assess options, decide, then take the action through an adult or record a reasoned choice not to act; day 155 is the end-of-unit assessment; day 156 (week 32) is history's audience adaptation; day 157 responds and looks back on the action. A school teaching this course alone runs the whole cycle on its own sources and the history-only question set, ready at launch.

- Readiness: none. Day 146 retrieves chemistry's climate forecast and grade 7's climate evidence; day 153 retrieves grade 8's action cycle (ss8-civic-action, used in grade 9).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 155, a composite: the unseen-source task on how well international efforts have met global problems, explained with more than one discipline, scored with the argued recommendation (day 151), the critique and revision (day 149), the talk and the adaptation (days 152 and 156) and the action reasoning (days 153 and 154). Respond day 157, after the week 32 adaptation day.
- Candidate: the UN Sustainable Development Goals, kept, part of day 146, 0 added days.

## Shorter path

The year is 160 days of 50 minutes. Two shorter routes keep every owned code, skill and assessment, every contrasting account and every required performance; the unit plans check them against real minutes, so neither is yet shown to fit.

- **45-minute periods, same 160 days.** Each teaching day keeps its source work, its product and its exit ticket, and uses the shorter excerpt of each source and trims the second worked example or the whole-class share; assessment and respond days stay whole, and no day drops its second account of a contested event.
- **About 154 days.** These days fold into a neighbour, because each code or skill they carry is taught on another day too: 12 (planning the answer, into 13), 17 (the cause chain, into 16), 24 (the unit 2 claim, into 23 and 25), 67 (the democracy comparison, into 66), 105 (the Cold War claim, into 104 and 106), and 119 (the means-of-change claim, into 118).
