## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why did a few states come to rule most of the world by 1914, and how did the people they ruled answer?"
**Hook:** Compare Europe's borders before and after unification with Sambourne's imperial giant over Africa. Ask whether national independence and ruling others could grow together.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: How did unification change power (27)? What do maps hide (28)? Why expand (30)? Who gained and lost (33)? Who told the story (35)? How did people answer conquest (36)?
**Closing claim:** Day 37 answers the compelling question using dated maps, the base accounts, Kipling and Tagore. Explain interacting causes, compare regional responses and answer a counterclaim.
**Teaching:** Model sourcing and causation on 27, scale on 28, context on 33 and selection on 35; withdraw the argument frame on 37. Teach the political terms in context. Name slavery, forced labour, mutilation and genocide plainly. No graphic images, slurs or victim or perpetrator role play. Offer private discussion without personal disclosure. Explain missionary aims without religious assent; distinguish Christianity from colonial government.
