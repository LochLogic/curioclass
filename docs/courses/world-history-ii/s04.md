## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was the peace of 1919 bound to fail?"
**Hook:** Day 43 juxtaposes a dated alliance map with Wilson's proposal to reduce armaments. Ask what a peace would have to change; retain first answers for day 56.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: Why did a crisis become war (44)? Whose lives did total war mobilize (47)? How do we establish genocide despite denial (49)? What changed after war (53)? What can historians establish about the peace (56)?
**Closing claim:** Day 56 answers the compelling question using Wilson, treaty provisions, maps, Rankin and Weise, and MacMillan. Judge solutions against problems, answer a counterclaim and distinguish contributing conditions from inevitability.
**Teaching:** Model causal ranking on 43, corroboration on 48 and source critique on 54; partners test claims on 55, then write independently on 56. Source before reading; revise questions afterward. Teach militarism, total war, genocide, mandate, reparations, modernism and credibility in context. Distinguish governments from peoples. Require no family disclosure, graphic images, slurs or victim or perpetrator role play. Study denial against evidence, never as an equally supported account.

- **P1, unseen historian's account:** Rankin and Weise, World History Since 1500, PDF page 152 (printed 145), the paragraph continuing from the previous page, before the account of wartime opposition. Supply this original summary under the book's CC BY 4.0 licence: "The peace excluded Germany and Soviet Russia, demanded reparations and burdened Germans with taxes. These decisions helped prepare the conditions for another war." Label it an adapted interpretation, with its authors, date and credit. Correct the original's anachronistic "USSR" to Soviet Russia, noting the correction in the credit. This is a causal claim to test, not the answer key. Teaching on 54 uses the preceding League discussion and MacMillan's different interpretation, never this paragraph.
- **P2, original Curio Class museum display:** Title: "An attempt at security." Mount an original map locating northern France and the Saar beside two document panels and a timeline. First panel: Versailles article 45 transferred the Saar coal mines to France to compensate for destroyed French mines. Article 49 placed the territory under League administration; mine ownership did not equal French sovereignty. Include the treaty annex's provisions protecting inhabitants' religious practices, schools and languages. These attempted safeguards let students evaluate cultural consequences separately from economic control. Second panel: the Covenant provided peaceful dispute procedures and collective responses whose implementation depended on governments. The timeline places the armistice in 1918, Versailles in 1919, Senate rejection in 1919 and 1920, and the Soviet Union's formation in 1922. Central argument: "The settlement linked resource compensation with institutions for security; neither guaranteed lasting agreement." Identify Curio Class as the display's author and link the [treaty](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv13/ch12subch4) and [Covenant](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv13/ch10subch1). This is a new secondary work, not an attributed institutional exhibit. Everyone receives the same printed layout, captions and image descriptions. Day 55 uses a different display about colonial mandates.
- **P3, expert appraisal:** Give MacMillan's historian credentials and the interview date, December 29, 2002. Summarize her discussion of Frances Stevenson's diary and record keeping: the secretary's diary and improved preservation of records help a historian reconstruct Lloyd George's working environment. The appraisal values access and preservation; it does not promise impartiality. This passage is withheld from day 54's discussion of peace aims. Use an original factual summary, not copied copyrighted dialogue.
- **P4, unseen physical evidence:** Use Kennedy's photograph "A Mine Crater on the Ridge", plate 46, in [Ypres to Verdun](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57144/pg57144-images.html), 1921, public-domain original. Credit Kennedy and give the collection's 1919 to 1920 photographic period rather than inventing a precise exposure date. Its caption identifies the Vimy Ridge location and visible damage. Pair it with his preface's observation that fields and villages remained devastated in 1919 and reconstruction had advanced by spring 1920. The preface and plate caption were read; the final image and description still need visual checking. State that the mine crater records military excavation and explosives, not coal extraction. Its effect on land use must be inferred cautiously. Use a different northern French photograph on day 50.

Locate Iraq, Palestine, Syria and Lebanon; distinguish Transjordan's administration. ## 9. Family note

Students investigate war, genocide and peace through evidence, without reenacting suffering. El alumnado investiga la guerra, el genocidio y la paz mediante fuentes, sin representar el sufrimiento.
