## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was the peace of 1919 bound to fail?"
**Hook:** Day 43 juxtaposes a dated alliance map with Wilson's proposal to reduce armaments. Ask what a peace would have to change; retain first answers for day 56.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: Why did a crisis become war (44)? Whose lives did total war mobilize (47)? How do we establish genocide despite denial (49)? What changed after war (53)? What can historians establish about the peace (56)?
**Closing claim:** Day 56 answers the compelling question using Wilson, treaty provisions, maps, Rankin and Weise, and MacMillan. Judge solutions against problems, answer a counterclaim and distinguish contributing conditions from inevitability.
**Teaching:** Model causal ranking on 43, corroboration on 48 and source critique on 54. On 51, model and practise evaluating mandate choices' cultural and environmental effects in pairs during map work. Partners test claims on 55, then write independently on 56. Source before reading; revise questions afterward. Teach militarism, total war, genocide, mandate, reparations, modernism and credibility in context. Distinguish governments from peoples. Require no family disclosure, graphic images, slurs or victim or perpetrator role play. Study denial against evidence, never as an equally supported account.

- **P1, unseen historian's account:** Rankin and Weise, World History Since 1500 (2022), PDF page 152, printed 145, paragraph continuing from page 144, before wartime opposition. - **P2, original Curio Class museum display:** Title: "An attempt at security." Mount an original map locating northern France and the Saar beside two document panels and a timeline. First panel: Versailles article 45 transferred the Saar coal mines to France to compensate for destroyed French mines. Article 49 placed the territory under League administration; mine ownership did not equal French sovereignty. Include the treaty annex's provisions protecting inhabitants' religious practices, schools and languages. These attempted safeguards let students evaluate cultural consequences separately from economic control. Second panel: the Covenant provided peaceful dispute procedures and collective responses whose implementation depended on governments. The timeline places the armistice in 1918, Versailles in 1919, Senate rejection in 1919 and 1920, and the Soviet Union's formation in 1922. Central argument: "The settlement linked resource compensation with institutions for security; neither guaranteed lasting agreement." Identify Curio Class as the display's author and link the [treaty](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv13/ch12subch4) and [Covenant](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1919Parisv13/ch10subch1). ## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools:** None. Supply printed maps, sources, displays, graphs, timelines, record sheets and pencils; projection is optional. Describe images and label patterns without relying on colour.
