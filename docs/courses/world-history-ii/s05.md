## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why did democracy fail in some countries between the wars and survive in others?"

**Hook:** Day 59 opens with dated cards showing Mussolini appointed in 1922, Britain leaving gold in 1931 and Hitler appointed in 1933. Ask whether economic crisis alone explains these different outcomes.
**Supporting questions:** Co-write: How did revolution change power (60)? Why did economies collapse (63)? How did institutions and choices shape dictatorship (67)? Why did collective security fail (70)?
**Closing claim:** On 67, write two paragraphs answering the compelling question from Lenin, British recovery evidence, persecution laws and the dated regime comparison. Explain a counterclaim. On 70, add how domestic power affected international choices.
**Teaching:** Model causation on 59. On 63, use the kept Weimar reading to teach institutional instability beside British parliamentary continuity and model policy judgment. Practice enabling choices on 64 to 66; apply the comparison and withdraw writing frames on 67. Source before reading; revise questions afterward. Teach soviet, monetary policy, deflation, fascism, totalitarianism, collectivization and appeasement in context. No slurs, graphic images, family disclosure or victim or perpetrator role play. Distinguish regimes from peoples; denial is tested against evidence, never balanced against established atrocities.


**Framework detail (Massachusetts 2018, printed page 155):** T4.6 includes Lenin's personality cult (day 60, item 1b). T4.9 names political murder, censorship, propaganda, paramilitary and youth organizations, the Enabling Act, the Night of the Long Knives and Nazi judgments of art (days 64 to 66, item 3b).
**Limits:** The Philippines extends the dated timeline to December 1941; unit 6 teaches the wider war and Holocaust. Friedman and Schwartz explain the Depression retrospectively in 1963. Do not attribute their ideas to 1930s policymakers. Informed action belongs to unit 11.
**Carried:** Keep unit 4's nine models: timeline, cause-effect chain, supply-demand, map-scale, coordinates, region, perspective, source-sourcing and corroboration. Reuse timeline and causation on 60; reactivate supply-demand on 61, distinguishing a market from a whole economy. Revisit sourcing and corroboration on 65, map models on 69 and perspective on 70. Withdraw comparison prompts on 67, retaining source labels.
