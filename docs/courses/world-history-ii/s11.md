- **Item 2:** Look for all four patterns, three scales and qualified judgment. Meets: "Vunidogoloa relocated after repeated tidal flooding, changing settlement and land use. - **Item 3:** Look for characteristics and causes of both problems, contrasting contexts, obstacles, opportunities and connected disciplines. Meets: "Malaria control meets a biological hazard shaped by local water and service access; regional cooperation supports prevention. Refugee protection also needs funding, but borders and host-state decisions constrain access. Global agencies support both while local institutions shape results." Does not: "Both are global, so one donation solves them everywhere" (H).
- **P1:** Look for a historical claim, chronological explanation, integration rather than five summaries, source usefulness, counterclaim and justified recommendation. Meets: "Cooperation worked through negotiation and local adaptation. The health agency's 2010 overview establishes eradication in 1980, but is an institutional success account. Bhattacharya and Campani's 2020 abstract traces the 1958 to 1967 negotiations; Bhattacharya and Dasgupta's 2009 local-worker discussion explains how cooperation changed implementation in the 1970s. Their interpretations complicate, rather than deny, the agency account. The HIV network's strategy identifies stigma and unequal access; the refugee-led network's participation account independently supports including affected people, though it cannot prove HIV outcomes. Against the counterclaim that one successful method should simply be repeated, these differences support a next step: health agencies should co-design access reviews with community representatives and compare treatment access before and after. These five sources support testing participation, not assuming it causes improvement." Does not: "Five websites agree, so no appraisal is needed" (D). "Smallpox's strategy works unchanged for HIV" (H).
- **P2:** Look for a located claim, credibility and relevance tested against another source, and a concrete revision. Meets: "Your claim, 'international staff alone eradicated smallpox', overreads the agency's 2010 account: it describes countries collaborating. Bhattacharya and Dasgupta's local-worker passage corroborates cooperation and identifies local partners. The agency is relevant but credits its own program; the historians explain participation. Revise to 'international and local workers cooperated'." Does not: "I agree with your opinion, so your source is credible" (D).
- **P3:** Look for reasoning, sequence and detail, with repair or justified confirmation. Meets: "You place eradication before surveillance expanded. Correct the sequence and explain how finding cases helped containment, citing the dated program record." Does not: "Both partners agree, so the causal explanation needs no evidence check" (D).
- **P4:** Look for explanation and argument, accurate captioned evidence, engaging ideas, contrasting perspectives, audience-specific changes and an answer to a question. Meets: "My panel caption reads: 'Smallpox declared eradicated in 1980; health agency overview, 2010.' I explain that finding cases supported containment and recommend community participation, while acknowledging that different diseases need different methods. The historians emphasize local workers alongside the agency's coordination account. For the other class, I open with 'How would a team find every case?', define surveillance as finding and recording cases, and replace specialist terms. Does not: "I reread my report faster and removed the explanation" (E).
- **P5:** Look for feasible individual and collective options, a decision-maker, several causal steps, an obstacle or unintended effect, and self-reflection.
