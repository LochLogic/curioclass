## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Agreements, spending or reach prove the intended outcome. | Confuses commitment, activity, outcome and impact. | From memory; test against development-effectiveness reporting categories. |
| B | An outcome proves a single cause; lessons transfer unchanged. | Omits alternatives, chronology and context. | Bhattacharya and Campani, 2020, abstract. |
| C | Climate determines every move or land-use change. | Treats conditional influences as destiny. | Climate assessment, small-islands search extracts. |
| D | Five summaries or agreement with a partner establish credibility. | Does not integrate sources or critique evidence and reasoning. | From memory. |
| E | Repeat the report unchanged or use an uncited decorative map. | Audience, explanation and evidence have separated. | From memory. |
| F | Everyone in a category shares one experience. | Erases unequal access, agency and within-group differences. | Refugee-led network and people living with HIV network, own accounts. |
| G | The teacher's choice or a promised result justifies action. | Lacks capacity, strategy, causal reasoning and self-reflection. | From memory. |

If at least a quarter of the class misses an exit criterion, use the next day's first five minutes from its existing opening or modelling block. A: sort pledge, activity and outcome. B: add a rival cause to a dated chain. C: connect scales on a map and qualify causation. D: repair a claim or explanation against two sources. E: revise an opening for an audience. F: compare disaggregated evidence and a community voice. G: connect decision-maker, strategy, effects and limits. Recheck with a fresh example; preserve research and product time.
