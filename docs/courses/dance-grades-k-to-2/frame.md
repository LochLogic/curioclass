# Course frame: Dance, grades K to 2

Course `dance-grades-k-to-2` (catalogue id `dance-k-2`), a band course for kindergarten, grade 1 and grade 2, run as three years one after another (year K, year 1, year 2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a thread comes back one step
  harder a year later in the same place: a body-and-space unit (technique and the dance elements), a making unit
  (ideas and the shape of a dance), a watching-and-changing unit (revising and responding to dances), and a
  performance unit that ends the year with the class's dance for families (staging and connecting). Each unit joins
  two progression threads, and every unit dances, watches another artist's dance and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5).
  Units 1, 5 and 9 claim only performing ids; they touch responding through the works they watch and creating
  through the short patterns the class makes up, so every unit touches at least two artistic processes.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up (tables pushed back, floor checked and the space marked, about 3
  minutes), a warm-up (about 5), the teaching and dancing, a cool-down and the closing line (about 4) and putting the
  room back and collecting props and cards (about 3); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes of its own on day 2, after the warm-up, taken from the teaching time (day 2: set-up 3, warm-up 5, retrieval 10, teaching 15, cool-down 4, room back 3). Each unit's
  days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is
  over 25 days) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the first unit of each year is
  longest because it owns six ids and sets up the dance space, the start and stop cue and the warm-up (20 days in
  year K, where every routine is new; 18 in years 1 and 2, where they return); the watching unit owns six ids, four
  of them response ids heard in talk; the making unit owns four ids; the performance unit owns five ids and holds
  the rehearsals for the family showing (12 days in year K, whose showing is one short dance beside others in a
  marked area; 14 in years 1 and 2, with a seated audience and then separate zones and a staging extra). These are
  course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year
  falls in that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the dance educators'
  association's opportunity-to-learn standards (2017 revision, posted on nationalartsstandards.org) ask for at least
  60 minutes of dance a week in K to 2, 90 at the quality level. This course gives 80 minutes a week, 20 over the 60
  and 10 short of the 90; on the once-a-week path it gives 40, 20 short of the 60. A school on that path can add
  time. The course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a dance specialist uses that
  teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the watch of a dance that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one rehearsal day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into
  the warm-ups, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path: year K, units of 10, 7,
  6 and 5 sessions; years 1 and 2, units of 9, 7, 6 and 6; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1
  respond session. The check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (week 18 and week 26 in year K;
  week 17 and week 25 in years 1 and 2); the respond sessions fall one week later (week 19 and week 27 in year K;
  week 18 and week 26 in years 1 and 2). A respond session is not a check day; in year 2, week 18 also holds math's
  mid-year check, and the respond session asks no test of any child. A merged day never drops a critique, a
  rehearsal before a showing or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge. Year K unit 4's five
  sessions run: 1, the works studied, the technique shown and the dance begun; 2, making, the critique and the
  change rehearsed in the same session; 3, the connecting items at the teacher's spot; 4, the family or classroom
  showing with its look-fors; 5, respond. On this merged path the rehearsal of the change follows the critique inside
  session 2; the next-day rehearsal in Day kinds describes the full path.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, dance performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year K claims only the 21 kindergarten ids (`.Ka`, `.Kb`, `.Kc`), year 1 only the 21
  grade 1 ids, year 2 only the 21 grade 2 ids: 63 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and
  assessed in its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. No
  pre-kindergarten id is claimed. Every year touches all four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding,
  connecting; bar criterion 1). In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the
  artistic processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and the dances it studies. Each id's action was
  checked against the national standard's text, not only the words file; the rows' descriptions now carry the full ids,
  and the units below teach and check each id in full (the second read's additions of 2026-11-26 are in units 1, 5
  and 9). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file
  lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list
  (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule
  (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file; each year's scope and
  sequence still states its id count (21) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a child's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Dance here, motor skill in physical education.** Dance in this course is the art: making, performing,
  responding to and connecting dance. The traveling, in-place and dance moves as motor skills are physical
  education's (STANDARD.md 6; physical education K to 2 units 1, 2, 13 and 21). Dance teaches the technique its own artistic standards
  need inside this course and assumes no earlier physical education instruction; it uses the moves a child already
  has as material for shapes, patterns, formations and dances, and teaches
  choreography, performing for an audience and response. Units 1, 5 and 9 name the physical education rows they lean
  on (Cross-subject, below).
- **Base text.** None. Dance is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open dance recordings). Dances studied are shown from
  public domain films (early films in the Library of Congress's collections) and, for living or recent makers and
  for every tradition, from a description in our own words with a link to the maker's, the company's or a museum's
  or cultural body's own page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Each dance named below is a pick the unit
  plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped dance must keep the unit's ids
  met and the band's range. The band's dances come from Africa (Ghana), the Americas (the United States, Mexico,
  and Native nations through the hoop dance), Asia (China, Korea, India, the Philippines, Japan) and Europe (Spain,
  Russia, France, the United Kingdom), from 1894 to today, with living makers or living traditions in every year
  (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). The unseen dance in every check is a short Curio Class original danced live
  by the teacher from a picture step card, or a short written description read aloud, never an embed (APPROACH
  "Scoring creative work"). Curio Class writes every step card, work card, starting-point card, symbol card, record
  sheet, family note and every unseen dance.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Body and space: copying a pattern, stopping on the cue and the
  group circle (K), traveling curved and zigzag paths in lines and circles with a steady beat (1), traveling
  pathways in formations, landing on the strong beat in two- and three-beat music (2). Making: a short dance with a
  start, middle and finish (K), a sequence whose moves are picked for a feeling or a bit of music (1), a linked
  phrase whose middle holds the central idea (2). Changing and recording: a change on the teacher's suggestion and
  one drawn symbol (K), a change to a remembered sequence and symbols for jump, turn and slide (1), a chosen change
  and symbols for high, middle and low (2). Performing: a marked area beside others with one prop (K), a seated
  audience and props tried (1), separate zones and a staging extra (2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a move pattern, an
  element or a dance form shown in named steps with the step card, or a dance watched with the unit's words (the
  artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated practice with
  feedback (a warm-up, the start and stop game, a pattern run three times, a symbol drill); in K to 2 it is mostly
  the five-minute warm-up and one or two fluency days a unit. Application: making and rehearsing a dance with some
  choice; the second application day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's
  rehearsal of the change is application too. Check: a showing scored live on the guide, or the talk and show items
  of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second
  showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique,
  rehearsal, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for, named id by id in the unit's
  "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one item for
  each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live by the teacher as each group dances; a show item, where the child
  shows a move at the teacher's spot; a talk item, at most two a child on any check day; and a kept card (a symbol
  card or an idea card) made on the unit's last making days and scored after the session. Two kinds of check day,
  never mixed on one day. A showing day: set-up 3 minutes, warm-up 5, six groups of five dance in the marked zone
  for about 4 minutes each (24: about two minutes of dancing, then each child's one spoken line where the unit asks
  one) while the rest sit in the audience zone and watch (the class is the audience, nothing else is asked of it),
  cool-down and the self-check on the picture guide 5, room back 3: 40 minutes. A watching day: set-up 3, the
  teacher dances the unseen Curio Class phrase for the class twice (about 3), six groups of five at the teacher's
  spot for about 4 minutes each (24) for the show and talk items, while the others draw move symbols or finish a kept
  card at tables, seated and quiet, room back 3: 33 minutes, so the day has slack for a second showing of the phrase
  at the table. Either way the teacher watches one group, never a room and a group at once; a group the teacher
  wants to see again may be recorded on the school's device that day (hands and feet, or the whole body with the
  family's yes) and the recording stays there (APPROACH "Kept work"). Units 1 to 9 and 11 have two check days;
  unit 10 has one showing day and a kept card. The rehearsal, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks
  on the record sheet and the re-rehearsal come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a
  move or an element is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first
  practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every child has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a
  unit goes into the portfolio folder (a symbol card, an idea card or a drawing of the child's dance, or a recording
  kept on the school's device) with a one-line reflection spoken to the teacher or a partner (bar criterion 8).
- **Taking part without dancing alone.** No child dances alone for the class: every showing is in a group of five,
  and a child may dance beside the teacher or a partner, or show at the teacher's spot (APPROACH "Supports"). A child
  who cannot move freely dances a seated or one-limb version, or takes the caller's or drummer's role for a pattern.
  Each unit plan names the child's movement version and which look-fors it shows. A caller's or drummer's role is a
  sound way to take part but counts only for what it shows (a beat kept, a pattern called); where a role shows
  nothing of an owned movement action, the teacher sees a short movement version with the child's usual supports at
  the teacher's spot and records the support. A child who does not want to be recorded is marked live.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Year K assumes no earlier dance learning (pre-kindergarten ids are not taught; a
  kindergarten unit may use one as a readiness reference). Every year 1 and year 2 unit assumes the year before's
  row in the same thread, last used one grade back, so it opens with a ten-minute retrieval task on day 2, after the warm-up, never
  a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from the band's order (K,
  then 1, then 2). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a child may meet year 2 before year 1: each retrieval
  task then becomes one ten-minute reteach of the earlier steps of both threads together, inside the same block (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline
  look"). The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (K: 14 to 17 and 29 to 32; grade 1: 14 to 16, 19 and 29 to 32; grade 2: 14 to 16, 18 to
  20, 28, 29, 31 and 32). Mid-year: week 18 in year K (course days 35 and 36 of the year), week 17 in years 1 and 2
  (days 33 and 34). Year-end: week 26 in year K (days 51 and 52), week 25 in years 1 and 2 (days 49 and 50). The
  weeks follow from the unit days by load, not from the pilot's. Weeks 29 to 32 hold every core subject's year-end
  check in K to 2, so the year-end check comes before the performance unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and the
  performance unit's family showing, in the year's last weeks, is its own end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a dance for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 6 (Revelations is set to spirituals, religious songs; a
  second starting-point dance keeps every id met), unit 7 (Bharatanatyam grew in part from temple dance; the unit uses
  a non-devotional piece and Kpanlogo keeps every id met), and unit 12 (the hoop dance, which some nations' teachings
  tie to ceremony; it is watched only, and Kpanlogo's origin keeps DA:Cn11.1.2a met). Swapping a dance keeps the
  unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that
  year. A unit that builds on an earlier unit's dance carries a fallback for a class that left that unit out: units 3,
  7 and 11 change a short Curio Class original sequence on a step card; units 4, 8 and 12 start from a fresh,
  neutral starting point. The class learns or makes that material inside the receiving unit's days before it
  revises or stages it; the receiving unit claims only its own ids, and the omitted unit's ids stay unclaimed.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 in a cleared classroom, hall or gym with room for every child to stretch both
  arms out without touching another, floor tape or chalk to mark spots and zones, a hand drum or a pot and a wooden
  spoon, the voice and clapping for the beat; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in
  (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One child at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, the
  group showing has a solo-with-parent version (the parent dances the partner part or watches), and the showing is to
  the family. The teacher needs no dance training: each unit's teacher page says what to look for, in plain words,
  and every unseen dance is on a picture step card the teacher can learn in a few minutes.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as scarves and
  a hand drum counted once, printing of step, work and symbol cards), all optional beside the free path: year K $15,
  $5, $5, $20 ($45); year 1 $5, $5, $5, $15 ($30); year 2 $5, $5, $5, $10 ($25). Planning figures, not quotes; any
  ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher device may play music and show a linked dance; every day runs without
  it, from the printed work card (a picture and a description read aloud), the teacher showing the moves, and the
  voice, claps or a drum for the beat. No owned id needs technology. DA:Pr6.1.2b allows a projected image as one
  staging extra among others; a paper-path class meets it with a small prop or a painted backdrop, in full. A
  paper-path student meets every id in full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH
  "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on printed cards, the floor and the body; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  beat keeper, a symbol card maker) has a printed version that does the same job and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards and symbol cards are picture-led with the words read aloud for K to 2; no child reads
  a card alone. English learners: each unit teaches its three to six words in depth, with a picture, a movement and
  a frame ("My move is ___." "I saw a ___ move."), a child may show the word instead of saying it, and the check
  marks what the child danced apart from what they said (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is a moving unit, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the Nova
  Scotia Department of Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades Primary-12" (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf, its dance section on printed page 43), its sections on dance and on the activity space, and the dance educators' association's
  opportunity-to-learn standards (2017 revision, https://nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/NDEO%20Opportunity%20to%20Learn%20Standards%202017%20rev.pdf), its lines on the dance space and floor. Floor rule:
  jumps, landings and soft stamping use a level, resilient surface the school confirms suitable; a wooden finish over concrete, tile or a
  loose mat on a hard floor is not enough by itself. On an unsuitable floor the class does those moves low and
  without impact, and their look-for is seen in a suitable school space before it is marked met, never from a
  written stand-in.
  Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): a clear floor with room
  to move arms out, checked for spills and objects before each session; bare feet or soft-soled shoes on a clean
  floor, never socks on a smooth floor; no lifts; no jumping from heights; no inverted moves (no head spins,
  handstands or flips); partner contact only by agreement and never required; the stop cue taught first and used
  every session; props soft and short (no ribbon or scarf tied round a neck, no hard sticks, nothing small enough to
  mouth); water and rest offered. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will
  copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the dances with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 2 (the Dai
  people of Yunnan, China, and the peacock dance), unit 3 (Korean fan dance), unit 5 (Mexican folklórico), unit 6
  (spirituals in Revelations; Bangladeshi heritage in Chotto Desh), unit 7 (the Ga people of Accra, Ghana; Indian
  classical dance), unit 8 (Chinese ribbon dance), unit 9 (Filipino tinikling), unit 11 (breaking in the Bronx;
  flamenco and the Roma people of Andalusia), unit 12 (Native nations and the hoop dance; the Ga people again).
  Every task that ties dance to a child's own life (units 4, 8, 12) offers an invented or public option marked the
  same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). A dance from another culture is watched, and learned only where the
  community's own teaching says it is open to be taught; no costume, no accent, no imitation of a people, and no
  sacred or ceremonial dance as an exercise or warm-up (the rows' teacher note; Cory, 2026-11-20). No task asks about
  a child's body, weight or diet. Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, a cleared floor, step
  cards, work cards, a drum or the voice. Activities: moves and elements taught in steps, dances watched with their
  setting, making and rehearsing with a critique and a change, a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12
  pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per child on the main look-for. Outcome
  claimed: met marks on the year's 21 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with children, and
  Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten opens the band and the subject: there is no course before and no pre-kindergarten course. The eight
kindergarten threads that start here with no earlier row are da-k-ideas, da-k-form, da-k-revise, da-k-elements,
da-k-technique, da-k-stage, da-k-respond and da-k-connect (their `assumes` cells are empty); each is first used in
