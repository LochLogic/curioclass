# Scope and sequence: Library and Research Skills, grade 4

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-4`, grade 4, draft 2026-11-26. The days are in
data/plans/library-and-research-skills-grade-4/days.csv; this page holds the judgment.

## How the year sits

- **30 course days**, one session of about 35 minutes (about 29 working minutes) a week for 30 weeks, as the frame
  sets: 26 unit days (6, 7, 6, 7), two course-level check days and two respond days. Base text: none; every day is
  Curio Class's own, on paper, with no library access, device or account needed.
- **school_day = 5 x course day**, so course day n falls in week n ((school_day - 1) // 5 + 1 = n), the last day of
  that week. A school may move the session to any day of the week; the week is what counts.
- **Calendar.** Library is a special (CALENDAR.md rule 1, lead calendar pass 2026-11-26), so its checks do not count
  in the K to 5 stagger. The mid-year check (week 14) and the year-end check (week 29) still miss every grade 4 core
  check week (social studies 8, 21, 27; science 10, 19, 30; math 12, 23, 31; ELA 16, 32), as CALENDAR.md's table
  gives (weeks 14 and 29). Both weeks also hold health grade 4's checks; health counts in the stagger and library
  does not, so this is no break (settled by CALENDAR.md rule 1, kept by Cory 2026-11-26).
- **Kinds** in library's words: Understanding is the show-me day, Fluency the practice day, Application the inquiry
  day, Check the assessment day, Respond the respond day. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the
  compelling question. The frame's "show-me with practice" days (9, 17, 26) are Understanding, since each first
  teaches a move. Fluency is two days (3 and 10), each practicing only moves the days before it showed; the
  accuracy check practiced on day 10 is modeled on day 8 first.
- **Daily evidence:** an exit ticket doing the move once on a new case (about 5 minutes); working notes on inquiry
  days; one self-check per unit after the share (days 4, 11, 19, 28).
- **Readiness and retrieval (rule 2).** Every row the course assumes is a library grade 3 row, the year before, and
  each is used on a grade 3 scope day: g3-key-words and g3-safe-searching (retrieved day 2, before the three-way
  search), g3-research-questions (day 3, before the narrowing practice), g3-source-kinds-and-makers and
  g3-source-list (day 4's judge step and source line, again day 8 and day 9, before the comparison and honest use),
  g3-notes-in-boxes (day 10, before the own-box notes round), g3-research-team (day 17), g3-make-product (day 23),
  g3-gallery-feedback (day 25). Each opens as a retrieval task on or before the day that builds on it, never on a
  unit's day 1, so no readiness check is placed; day 7's model may start from a grade 3 key-word box as its "before"
  picture, inside the show-me. Rows used earlier this year come back too (the
  `retrieves` column): the search plan in unit 2's inquiry, the plan in unit 3's inquiry, and the plan, search, notes
  and honest use in unit 4. Unit 4's revisited AASL.V.C.1 (kindergarten's k-wonder) is no assumed row of any grade 4
  progression row, so it needs no readiness check; the day 22 show-me starts from a wonder itself.
- **Sources not loaded (later.csv L149).** Grade 4 leans on none of the online-reasoning lessons or the web-literacy
  book (lateral reading starts in grade 5). The check-set cells the Candidate days use (days 1, 7, 10, 11, 17, 19,
  22, 24, 28) come from the continuum's 3 to 5 band file: the band file is on disk and was read by the frame and
  checked by the expert 2026-11-26 (every grade 4 cell used here matches); its store entry waits on L149. No day
  leans on the digital-citizenship presentation; it is a sensitivity-read source only (browser check: download needs
  a sign-in). No source-map.csv is written (waits on L149).
- **Host tasks.** Each unit names an in-step grade 4 host and a stand-alone question with a closed Curio Class
  source set (below and in each unit's notes).

## Year 4 (the only year): units

### Unit 1. Planning a research path (days 1 to 6, weeks 1 to 6)

*How do we turn a big topic into a plan, and which kinds of source help most?* Owned: AASL.I.B.2, AASL.IV.B.1.
Revisited: AASL.I.A.1, AASL.VI.A.1 (grade 3). Two owned rows, six days: one show-me per row, so the search across
kinds gets its own model.

Arc: day 1 models narrowing, questions with a guess and the plan; day 2 models one question searched three ways and
the contents, index and glossary; day 3 practices both; day 4's inquiry completes the cycle on the host question
(plan, search two kinds, read the answering part, judge its maker, a one-line answer with its source line, partner
share, self-check), as the codex read asked; day 5 is the sitting, after practice and inquiry; day 6 the respond day.

- Retrieval: day 2, key words and a synonym plus one scenario card (g3-key-words, g3-safe-searching), before the
  three-way search; day 3, three questions on a picture, one thick (g3-research-questions), before the narrowing
  practice; day 4, maker and purpose and the source line.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 5, written sitting about 25 minutes on a new topic: a narrowed part, questions with a
  guess, a plan that fits, with the rubric line "the plan names what I still do not know, and each step fills one of
  those" (I.B.2, I.A.1); a search plan carried out on two new printed kinds with a located result from each
  (IV.B.1). Response time: day 6.
- Host: social studies grade 4 unit 2 (ss4-canada-mexico-research) opens in week 4, and a research project usually
  sits in its weeks 5 to 8, so day 4 most often runs on the stand-alone Arctic question and the plan carries into the
  social studies project; kept to land, climate and animals. ELA's e4-research is weeks 8 to 16, later: unit 1
  teaches the plan itself. Stand-alone: "How do Arctic animals get through the winter?" Day 4's minutes: plan about
  6, search two kinds about 10, judge and answer about 5, partner about 3, self-check about 4.
- Sensitivity: the stop rule on every search day (2 to 4); printed kinds or a teacher-checked page only.
- Candidates: gaps in what one knows and what an overview said, **kept** as the plan sheet's first two lines on day 1
  (about 2 minutes); a template and a timeline, **kept** as a "when" box beside each plan step (about 1 minute).

### Unit 2. Notes that answer (days 7 to 13, weeks 7 to 13)

*What do beavers really do to a stream, and how do we use what sources say honestly?* Owned: AASL.I.B.1,
AASL.IV.B.4, AASL.VI.A.2. Begun: AASL.IV.B.3, AASL.IV.A.3 (grade 5). Revisited: AASL.VI.B.2 (grade 2). Three rows,
seven days, the year's heaviest reading and writing: three show-me days, one per row, then practice, inquiry,
sitting, respond.

Arc: notes first (day 7), because a note must answer before its source is weighed; then source kinds, purpose, the
ad and the accuracy check (day 8); then honest use (day 9), where copying starts; day 10 practices all three; day
11's inquiry uses them on the host question; day 12 is the sitting; day 13 the respond day.

- Retrieval: day 8, label two sources and name each maker (g3-source-kinds-and-makers), before the comparison;
  day 9, a source list line for one cover (g3-source-list), before honest use; day 10, two key-word notes from a
  short paragraph (g3-notes-in-boxes), before the own-box notes round; day 11 brings back the search plan. Day 7
  stays clean for the hook; its model may start from a grade 3 key-word box as the "before" picture.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 12, written sitting about 27 minutes on a new otter case: notes rubric (about 12
  minutes, I.B.1 and IV.B.4), items (about 7, IV.A.3, IV.B.3 with the accuracy check, its second source given as two
  printed sentences beside the statement), honest-use rubric (about 8, VI.A.2). Response time: day 13.
- Leans: e4-evidence-inference (ELA unit 1, weeks 1 to 8) before, named again on day 7; e4-research (ELA unit 2,
  weeks 8 to 16) the same weeks; e4-firsthand-secondhand (ELA unit 4, weeks 24 to 32) and ss4-primary-source (social
  studies unit 4, weeks 12 to 16) at the end of this unit or later, so day 8 carries the bridge: it teaches the
  comparison itself on beaver cases and borrows none of the voyages content.
- Host: ELA grade 4 unit 2's animal defenses research (weeks 8 to 16) is the in-step host; science unit 2's energy
  resources question ended in week 10 and may be reused as a finished question, kept to how a resource is made and
  used, not to policy. Stand-alone: "What do beavers do to a stream when they build a dam?" Day 11's minutes: notes
  about 13, the two lines about 4, source list about 3, share about 3, self-check about 4.
- Sensitivity: honest use without shaming (days 9 to 12); the ad page names no brand.
- Candidates: facts and details that support a main idea and a conclusion from evidence, **kept** as the "what my
  notes show" line, modeled day 7 and used day 11 (about 2 minutes each); where two sources differ, **kept** as one
  line on day 11 (about 2 minutes). The accuracy check (codex finding 3) is the day 10 exit ticket and one item on
  day 12, inside their minutes.

### Unit 3. When no book has the answer (days 16 to 21, weeks 16 to 21)

*How can we find out something no source can tell us yet?* Owned: AASL.V.C.2, AASL.II.D.2. Revisited:
AASL.III.D.1, AASL.II.A.1 (grade 3). Two owned rows, six days.

Arc: day 16 models a survey and an observation plan; day 17 models open questions, listening and restating, with
practice; day 18 runs the survey or starts the week-long observation and the interviews; day 19, a week later,
tallies, shares and self-checks; day 20 is the assessment; day 21 the respond day. The unit sits after the
mid-year check so the observation runs between sessions in the middle of the year.

- Retrieval: day 17, what each of three sample members added (g3-research-team); day 18 brings back unit 1's plan.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 20, about 28 minutes: written part about 10 minutes (the plan explained, and for a
  new neutral question no source holds, which kind of plan and its first step, V.C.2; three open questions,
  II.D.2); observed exchanges about 18 minutes, 70 seconds a pair for 15 pairs, three results
  recorded separately (open question and follow-up for II.D.2, restatement for II.A.1), as the codex read set.
  Response time: day 21.
- Host, in step: science grade 4 unit 4, the changing land (weeks 15 to 19). Stand-alone: "Which read-aloud book
  does our class most want to hear again?", with the home branch (the invented schoolyard picture sequence, one
  student and the teaching adult) on day 18. The tally uses grade 3 math, the year before.
- Weather: weeks 18 and 19 are mid-winter. If no rain falls or the ground is frozen, the observation group records
  what the week offers at three set spots (where frost, puddles or snow last longest), or works from the printed
  schoolyard picture sequence in class; the steps and the tally are the same. Soil after rain stays the case.
- Sensitivity: survey and interview rules on days 16 to 20 (APPROACH Sensitive ground item 8); answers by role,
  never by name; the observation from a window or a set spot with the teacher.
- Candidates: group research to a shared result, **kept** as one class tally chart on day 19 (about 3 minutes);
  restating another's idea accurately, **kept** on day 17 and in the day 20 exchange (about 2 minutes on day 17).

### Unit 4. A question of my own (days 22 to 28, weeks 22 to 28)

*What do I want to find out, just to learn, and how can advice make my answer better?* Owned: AASL.V.A.3,
AASL.I.C.3. Revisited: AASL.V.C.1 (kindergarten), AASL.I.C.2 (grade 3). Two owned rows plus the year's product,
its feedback round and its revision, seven days.

Arc: day 22 chooses the question and the audience; days 23 and 24 plan, search, note and draft, with the rubric
read before drafting and the self-check against it; day 25 is the feedback round; day 26 models choosing advice and
the change log, then students revise; day 27 hands in the revised sheet and holds the sitting; day 28 shares in a
gallery, reteaches and self-checks.

- Retrieval: day 23, the unbacked sentence (g3-make-product) and the plan and search; day 24, notes and honest use;
  day 25, one kind suggestion (g3-gallery-feedback).
- End-of-unit assessment: day 27, the revised sheet and its log scored on their rubric (V.A.3, I.C.3), then a
  written sitting about 15 minutes on a new sample sheet (I.C.3). Response time: day 28's regrouped reteach (about
  10 minutes), the frame's respond step.
- Host: ELA grade 4 unit 1's writing-process revision (e4-writing-process, weeks 1 to 8), before, used as the
  feedback habit. Stand-alone: each student's own question from the neutral list.
- Sensitivity: questions on neutral ground, a sensitive choice steered privately (day 22); slips read by the
  teacher before day 26.
- Candidates: a format for an audience and a purpose, **kept** as day 22's audience line (about 2 minutes); what
  makes a good product, **kept** as the rubric read beside a sample on day 24 (about 4 minutes); presenting with
  care for content and delivery, **kept** as one gallery line on day 28 (about 1 minute).

## Shorter path

- **A shorter session (30 minutes, about 26 working minutes).** Teaching days cut the opening retrieval and the exit
  ticket to about 3 minutes each. Assessment days skip both, with the setup cut to 2 minutes at each end; on sitting
  days (5, 12, 14, 27, 29) the papers are on the desks before the class arrives, so the opening setup is about 1
  minute and the 27-minute sittings (days 12 and 29) fit. Day 20's observed part runs at about 60 seconds a pair (its
  written part stays about 10 minutes), and day 27's hand-in time shrinks to about 9 minutes.
- **A year about 10 school days short (two weekly sessions lost).** Two fold-in days are named; nothing assessed
  and no owned part is cut, and day 10 stays, so unit 2 keeps its practice before the day 12 sitting:
  1. Day 3 (unit 1 practice), about 29 minutes saved: one narrowing try moves to day 1's exit ticket (already
     there), one three-way search try to day 2's exit ticket, and day 3's grade 3 retrieval
     (g3-research-questions) to day 4's opening.
  2. Day 13 (unit 2 respond), about 29 minutes saved: unit 2's regrouped reteach joins the mid-year respond day (day
     15), which already works from unit 2's class count.
  Total about 58 minutes, two sessions, leaving 28 of the frame's 30 days; the check weeks move with the school's
  own calendar. If a school loses a third session, fold day 6 (unit 1 respond) into day 15's regrouped reteach,
  which already works from unit 1's mid-year results.
- **No other reduced path is named in the frame.** A school meeting twice a week finishes in about 15 weeks; that is
  a faster pace, not a shorter path, and its checks keep their place after units 2 and 4.
