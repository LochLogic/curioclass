# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, grade 4

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-4` (catalog id `library-4`), grade 4. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the library pilot frame (kindergarten) beside the grade 3 frame; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one year of library and research skills, each unit one full inquiry cycle (ask, find, judge, use,
  share, reflect) on a small question, in the order a grade 4 class needs the moves: narrowing a topic into a plan and
  searching more than one kind of source first, then taking notes that answer from judged sources and using them
  honestly, then finding out what no source holds by asking others, then a free-choice inquiry of one's own, revised
  from advice.
- **Days.** 30: 26 in units, 2 course-level check days (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond days (1 after
  each check). One session of about 35 minutes, one session a week, for 30 weeks (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: the
  3 to 5 default of 30 sessions of 35 minutes, about 17.5 hours a year, at the one-a-week end of the settled "one or
  two sessions a week"). The 35 minutes hold getting out notebooks and source sets, the session, putting books and
  cards back and collecting work; a lesson plan budgets about 3 minutes at each end for that, so about 29 working
  minutes, and every assessment form below is costed against those 29 minutes for a class of 30. One adult runs the
  room (the classroom teacher or a librarian). Nine grade 4 rows, seven of them owned (nine ids), fit 26 unit days by
  load: unit 1 carries two owned rows and gets 6 days; unit 2 three rows (two owned, three ids, the year's heaviest
  reading and writing) and gets 7; unit 3 two owned rows and gets 6; unit 4 two owned rows and the year's product,
  its feedback round and its revision, and gets 7. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (no unit is
  over 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment); units 1 to 3 also hold their respond day, and unit 4's respond step is
  its revision day, with its regrouped reteach in the year-end check's respond day. These are course days: with 30
  days over 30 weeks, course day n falls in week n of docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, and the scope and sequence places
  them on school days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day):
  Understanding is the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through the move on an invented case, holding the unit's
  skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, varied tries of the same move on new cases, with feedback: narrow
  two topics, search one question three ways, sort four pages by purpose, rewrite closed questions as open ones);
  Application is the inquiry day (the class runs part of the cycle on its host question: plan, search, judge, note,
  make, share). Fluency is modest in grade 4: one practice day in units 1 and 2, a practice half-day in units 2 and 3, plus the five-minute opening
  retrieval on later days. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the compelling question.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words
  only, data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone).
  Ownership rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone.
  Grade 4 owns nine ids (AASL.I.B.2; AASL.IV.B.1; AASL.I.B.1 and AASL.IV.B.4; AASL.VI.A.2; AASL.V.C.2; AASL.II.D.2;
  AASL.V.A.3; AASL.I.C.3) and carries nine as `~`, each named in its unit's What students learn with its owner.
  Skills: the nine progression rows, each in one unit. Check set: the Empire State Information Fluency Continuum, 3
  to 5 band file, grade 4 column and its bold priority skills (read cell by cell for this frame, later.csv L103;
  result in the Check set read bullet); the "Check set adds" cells name what each unit plan should weigh from it.
  Values: K to 5 case sets stay neutral (no politics, elections, religion, identity groups or conflict; Cory,
  2026-11-21).
- **Base text.** None. Every day is Curio Class's own. Curio Class writes: a short background passage and a source set
  for each unit's stand-alone question (passages at grade 4 level, a photo with caption, a log or letter, a diagram),
  an invented printed catalog page, an invented children's encyclopedia entry and an invented results page for unit 1,
  four invented web pages with differing purposes (one with an ad), the honest-use case cards, the survey and
  observation plan sheets, the interview record sheet, the "I found out" sheet, the change log, one skill card a unit
  and the research notebook pages. No day needs a trade book, a paid source, a library visit or a database (no library
  access is assumed; Cory, 2026-11-11).
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from another grade 4 course, named by its row id for the
  teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question with a closed
  source set) so the unit runs when no core task is in step or at home (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence records
  both. A host is used for its question only when its topic keeps the K to 5 neutral rule; otherwise the unit borrows
  only the skill and runs on its stand-alone case (unit 2, below).
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question and closes with the share step and the
  self-check: each student rates their own use of the skill card's steps, names one step they did well and one thing
  to do differently next time (APPROACH.md, the form of daily evidence; the continuum's grade 4 reflect cells, own
  strengths and a goal, and what makes a good product). The six steps on the cycle card (ask, find, judge, use, share,
  reflect) are met each unit: ask and find in the plan and search (unit 1), judge in purpose and kind (unit 2), use in
  answer-only notes (unit 2) and the survey tally (unit 3), share and reflect in each unit's close and in unit 4's
  feedback round.
- **Evidence.** Daily: an exit ticket that asks the student to do the unit's move once on a new case (about 5
  minutes, all at once), with error letters for the usual slips; on inquiry days, the working notes in the research
  notebook (APPROACH.md). End-of-unit assessment: a written sitting on a case not seen before, all 30 at once, scored
  on a short teacher-judged rubric plus a few one-answer items checked on the page, in about 20 to 27 minutes; unit
  4 adds its revised product, scored on its rubric after the feedback round and revision counted in its days. Each
  unit names its form and total below; no unit assessment or course-level check spills into its respond day. It is
  scored on a day after the move was practiced, never on a show-me day. The respond day's groups come from the class
  record sheet by the rubric step missed (APPROACH.md, unit field 7).
- **Reading level.** Source passages at about grade 4 level, 200 to 400 words, with a read-aloud copy on the teacher
  page and a shorter set for students who read below it (APPROACH.md, section field 9). Students write notes, plans,
  questions, short records and a one-page sheet.
- **Paper path.** The whole course runs on paper: printed source sets, the invented catalog, encyclopedia and results
  pages, case cards, plan and record sheets, notebooks. No day needs a device, an internet connection or a student
  account.
- **Devices.** None per student, and no owned or carried code needs technology. Unit 1's search across kinds uses
  three printed kinds (a catalog page, an encyclopedia entry, a results page); where a school has one shared device or
  a projector, the teacher may show a teacher-checked results page instead, under the stop rule, and the printed
  pages do the same job in full, so a paper-path student meets AASL.IV.B.1 completely.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond books, paper and pencils, no field work and no trip; unit 3's
  observation plan watches the schoolyard from a window or a set spot with the teacher, so no laboratory or field guide
  applies. The screen-safety side is the stop rule (g3-safe-searching, kept in unit 1), checked by the sensitivity read
  (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, items 2 and 3).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned
  (Cory, 2026-11-14). No unit carries cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content: the case sets are
  Arctic animals and land, beavers and their dams, the schoolyard after rain and a class book choice, and each
  student's own neutral question. What the agent studies first: APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground" items 1 (neutral K to 5
  case sets; unit 1 keeps social studies' Canada and Mexico host to land, climate and animals; unit 2 does not borrow
  social studies unit 4's voyages and conquest content), 2 and 3 (the stop rule and privacy, unit 1), 4 (honest use
  without shaming, unit 2), 5 (whose voices a source set shows, C12) and 8 (surveys and asking people, unit 3); and,
  for unit 1's search, the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children's free grades 3 to 5 presentation "Being
  a Good Digital Citizen: Grades 3-5", listed at https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/resources (listing read
  2026-11-25; the kit was not opened, and a download may need a sign-in). No organization's name goes on a student,
  teacher or family page.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30: unit 1 about $8 (the
  three printed kinds in pairs, plan sheets), unit 2 about $10 (two source sets, the invented pages, case cards), unit
  3 about $5 (plan and record sheets), unit 4 about $8 (the "I found out" sheet, change log, the neutral question list);
  about $31 a year.
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The 3 to 5 band file's grade 4 column and priority boxes, every indicator
  row (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-3-5\esifc-priority-3-5.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, nothing copied). Met by the rows: picking an interesting part of a broad topic and following a planned
  path (g4-narrow-and-plan); questions for a topic (g4-narrow-and-plan); comparing primary and secondary sources the
  teacher chose, and a media source's purpose (g4-primary-secondary); answer-only notes in own words, organized with
  help (g4-evidence-notes); avoiding plagiarism by putting ideas in one's own words and crediting them
  (g4-honest-use); a source list with author, title and date (g4-honest-use, as review of g3-source-list); revising
  own work to criteria and advice (g4-revise-from-advice); group research to a shared result (g4-class-survey);
  restating others' ideas accurately (g4-asking-others); seeking information on personal interests by the catalog
  (g4-own-question). Differences, each a row change listed in For the lead item 1: predicting an answer before
  research (g4-narrow-and-plan note); using contents, index and glossary to reach the answering part
  (g4-search-more-kinds note); marking what in a source is opinion while noting (g4-evidence-notes note); a short
  quotation in quotation marks with its source as one honest way (g4-honest-use note; the continuum lists quoting in
  grade 4, and the note cards that keep quotation apart stay in grade 6, g6-quote-paraphrase); naming the audience
  before choosing the product's form (g4-own-question note; sharing to a real audience is owned in grade 5,
  g5-share-real-audience). Met without a row change: reading headings and pictures on a page (unit 1's invented pages);
  own strengths and a goal (the self-check). Left out: inferences about implicit meaning, visual information against
  text, and story and genre skills (ELA grade 4, e4-evidence-inference and e4-visual-info; STANDARD.md section 4);
  online messaging behavior and cyberbullying (no student account or online space in Curio Class; health grade 3,
  g3-v-bullying, and grade 5, g5-s-online-safety); identity and cultural-difference cells and the problem-for-change
  cells (K to 5 case sets stay neutral; SEL and social studies); design thinking (science, STANDARD.md section 4).
  Beyond the band file: a survey or observation plan when no source holds the answer (g4-class-survey, AASL.V.C.2,
  which the band places nowhere). No row is two or more grades from its cell.
