# Section 4.2: Why countries trade, and one claim about it

<!-- skel -->
Grade 2: Places and Peoples of the World. Unit 4, Countries and trade. *Why do countries need each other?* (days 48 to 57): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 52 to 57.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (52, 54), application 2 (53, 55), check 1 (56), respond 1 (57). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 2 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption, story and prompt aloud, and the whole day 56 packet. Answers are circled, drawn as an arrow, sorted, said or a few written words. The claim is said first, then recorded with a frame, with transcription support only. Days run 34 to 36 minutes. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time; one actor in each story scene. Kelda and Tarn are invented countries from 4.1. Every trade story and model label is ours, invented, and printed "invented for practice"; where a real country is named (Japan, Saudi Arabia, Peru, the United States, Vietnam, Italy, Kenya, Madagascar) the card says it is a model, not a record of real shipments or of what a country is known for. No brand name appears; no child is asked what is in their home. Trade Stories A, Trade Stories B, Country Cards C, Source Card D and Africa Map Pair B are held back and unseen until their day.

- **D2.Eco.14.K-2, this section's share (days 52, 54, 55, 56).** "Describe why people in one country trade goods and services with people in other countries." Day 52: four reasons (a resource missing, a skill, a different product, a lower price, including a thing both can make). Day 54: a choice gives up another (opportunity cost) and countries make agreements. Day 55: a claim. Day 56: item 3.
- **D2.Eco.15.K-2 (days 53, 56, 57).** "Describe products made abroad and sold at home, and products made at home and sold abroad." Day 52 names import and export with arrows; day 53 traces them; day 56 item 4.
- **D2.Geo.11.K-2 (days 53, 54, 56).** "Explain how using products links people to faraway places." The maker, carrier, seller and user chain (day 52 names it, day 53 uses it); day 56 item 5.
- **MA.2.T4.2 (days 55, 56, 57).** Explain the characteristics of a country. Here: countries differ in what they have and can do, so they trade (day 55); day 54 revises the 4.1 comparison; item 2 and item 2b are sat on day 56.
- **Limits that bite here.** No disputed borders, tariff calculations, brands or rankings of countries. Countries can trade things both make. Trade stories about real countries are model stories, never a claim about actual shipments. Opportunity cost is the best alternative given up, not everything wanted. An agreement makes two countries share a rule, never one country. No child reports what their family buys or has.
- **Connections.** Unit 3: good and service, resource, skill, producer, consumer, cost, region (days 52 to 55). Grade 1: choose and give up (day 54). 4.1: map key and the three questions (days 52, 53); the four-row chart (day 54 revision); unit 2's claim frame (day 55).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Import and export from one country's viewpoint: the same good is an import in one place and an export in another. Why trade happens: different resources, skills and prices, and variety; it does not mean a country is poor or weak, and both sides gain by choosing. A service crosses borders as work or knowledge. Opportunity cost, with its one-line adult definition and the child's "the best other thing we gave up". Agreements between countries: real, named only if the page is opened in full (source to check). No tariffs; no officeholder is named.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: trade, import, export, maker, carrier, seller, user, choose, give up, agreement, source. Frames: "Made in ___, used in ___, so ___." "We chose ___ and gave up ___." "I think ___ because ___. ___ shows ___, made by ___." Pointing and home-language reasons first.
- **Access.** Raised-line maps; a described label; a scribe; a partner holds a card. The day 56 sitting may be taken in two short parts for a child who tires.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts and the packet neutrally; accept pointing, drawing, speech or a scribe; enlarge maps; record the support used. Do not assess handwriting.
- **Reading load.** Labels of one to three words; frames of at most nine words; at most 150 words a child reads in a day.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 55 turns a story round: the same trade told from the other country's viewpoint, import and export swapped.
