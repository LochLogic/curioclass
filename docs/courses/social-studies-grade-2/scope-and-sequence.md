# Scope and sequence: Grade 2: Places and Peoples of the World

Course `social-studies-grade-2`, grade 2, draft 2026-11-14. 96 course days (91 in seven units, 3 course-level check
days, 2 respond days), three lessons a week of 30 to 40 minutes over 31 weeks, with a fourth lesson in some weeks
before the mid-year check (course day 46 is pinned to school day 68, week 14, in scripts/frame.py; this sequence
keeps the pin). The days are in data/plans/social-studies-grade-2/days.csv; `school_day` follows the calendar's
spread with that pin, so days.csv and data/plans/calendar.csv agree. No base text, so no source map.

**What holds every day.** Every map, source set and task is printed, including each country's source set in unit 7: a
day assumes at most one shared classroom device and always has a paper path. Every unit opens with its compelling
question and supporting questions the class writes together; from unit 2 every closing claim names the source its
reason came from. The end-of-unit assessment in every unit is a short task done from a source not seen before
(assess `end`), beside the teacher's observation over the unit's last lessons, with one respond day after it (the
response, counted once). The year's informed action is in unit 3 (days 34 to 36 and 42).

**Readiness.** Every grade 1 row grade 2 builds on was used the year before, so no unit holds a readiness check
(frame-write lesson 16); each opens with a retrieval task inside its first day, named in the day's note. The frame's
Days bullet still says "readiness check" (see For the lead).

**A full year (later.csv L8).** Grade 2 handed the rest of the shared economics topic (MA.2.T5.4 to MA.2.T5.9) to
grade 1, and Ghana, Canada and Mexico to grades 3 and 4. The year stays full inside the frame's units and days: each
unit below names the topics it teaches in full, and the days those topics would have used go to the made map, the
action cycle, two accounts, the interviews and the country report.

## Unit 1. Maps tell stories (days 1 to 16, weeks 1 to 5)

*Can a map ever show the world just as it is?* Anchor MA.2.T1.1 to MA.2.T1.3; C3 D2.Geo.1, D2.His.11, D3.2.

Topics taught in full: the title, key, compass rose and scale and what each tells; scale counted in whole units;
maps that show how people live with the land; who made a source, when and where, from the source itself; fact and
opinion; why a round Earth cannot be drawn flat; the Mercator map, the Peters map and the globe compared; North
America labeled; a map of a familiar place with all four parts.

Order: the four map parts first (days 1 to 5), since every later map task reads them; the sourcing card next (6 to 8),
taught on maps so it is ready for every source in the year; projections after (9 to 11), because "who made this map
and what does it bend" needs both; North America (12); the made map last (13 and 14), using everything before it.
Load: ss2-map-parts carries five days, the made map two (a made product), sourcing and projections three each.

- Assessment: day 15, on a map not seen before (the four parts, maker and date, fact or opinion, which projection and
  what it bends), with the maps made on days 13 and 14; respond day 16.
- Candidates settled: **kept**, North America labeled (California 2.2.2), one day (day 12), from a word bank rather
  than from memory, since recall of the whole map is beyond the row's grade 2 depth. Maps and globes (Tennessee 2.10)
  are met by the owned days, no cost.
- Lean: RI.2.6 (ELA unit 2 from week 7, later): days 6 to 8 carry the frame's bridge ("What is this map for?").

## Unit 2. Land, water and climate (days 17 to 29, weeks 6 to 9)

*Where on Earth would you choose to live, and why?* Anchor MA.2.T2.1 to MA.2.T2.3; C3 D2.Geo.4, D2.Geo.10, D4.1.

Topics taught in full: the seven continents and five oceans placed on a world map and globe; major features of every
continent (the Rocky Mountains and the Great Lakes, the Andes and the Amazon, the Alps and the Danube, the Sahara and
the Nile, the Himalayas, the Outback and the Great Barrier Reef, the Antarctic ice sheet); why oceans matter; weather
and climate; how land and water shape climate and what grows; regions; physical change in one region and cultural
change in a town in another region, each at two dates; a claim with a reason that names its source.

Order: continents, features and oceans (days 17 to 20), then climate and regions (21 to 23), then change at two dates
(24 and 25), then the claim with a named source (26 and 27), taught last on what the class has just read.

- Assessment: day 28, from a map and a photograph not seen before (continents, oceans, a feature, climate and why, a
  claim naming its source), with the observation of days 24 to 27; respond day 29.
- Candidate settled: **none to keep**; no check state names an addition, and present-day photographs of people and
  places on each continent are a Curio Class illustration inside days 18 and 19, not an addition.
- Leans: 2-ESS2-2 (science unit 1, already taught). W.2.1 (ELA unit 4, weeks 24 to 32, later): days 26 and 27 carry the
  bridge (shared writing with a frame). K-ESS2-1 (science kindergarten): weather and climate retaught in brief, day 21.

## Unit 3. Living with the land (days 30 to 45, weeks 9 to 14)

*Should people change the land, or change their ways to fit it?* Anchor MA.2.T2.4, MA.2.T5.1 to MA.2.T5.3; C3
D2.Geo.5, D2.Geo.8, D2.Geo.9, D2.Geo.12, D2.Eco.4, D2.Eco.7, D2.Eco.13.

Topics taught in full: how people use, change and adapt to their environment; how city, suburban, town and farm
communities meet daily needs from near and far; how floods, earthquakes and hurricanes affect people and how they
recover; getting ready for a storm (the action cycle); natural resources and the industries and jobs they support, in
the student's own region and elsewhere; renewable and non-renewable resources; one food from farm to table; goods made
here and elsewhere and what it costs to make a good; people as a resource, tools and machines.

Order: people and the land (days 30 to 33) lead into the readiness action (34 to 36), placed early in the unit so the
action has two weeks to run before the look back (42). Resources and industries fill those two weeks (37 to 41),
then the look back and the written answer (42 and 43). Load: ss2-people-environment carries eight days (four
standards and the action cycle), ss2-resources-industries four, ss2-people-resource one (one standard, one idea).

- Assessment: day 44, from a source set not seen before about a pictured place, with the observation of the action
  cycle; respond day 45.
- Candidates settled: **kept**, urban, suburban and rural communities compared (New York 2.1), inside day 32 at no
  added day; **kept**, one food from farm to table (California 2.4.1), one day (day 39). Industries (Tennessee 2.04)
  are met by the owned row, no cost.
- Own-state method: day 37 (the student's own region beside the places studied).
- Leans: K-ESS3-3 and K-ESS3-1 (science kindergarten), one-question reviews on days 30 and 37.
- Sensitivity: days 33 to 35 (disasters; a child may have lived through one).

## Unit 4. Countries and trade (days 48 to 57, weeks 15 to 18)

*Why do countries need each other?* Anchor MA.2.T4.1, MA.2.T4.2; C3 D2.Eco.14, D2.Eco.15, D2.Geo.11.

Topics taught in full: physical and political geography (Africa as one continent of more than 50 countries); what
makes a country; the purposes of government, and that not every government is chosen in elections; two countries
compared by history, culture, language and way of governing; why countries trade; goods made abroad and goods made
here and sold abroad, traced on a map; opportunity cost in trade; how countries work together.

Order: what a country is (days 48 to 51) before why countries trade (52 to 54), since trade is between countries;
the written answer (55).

- Assessment: day 56, from a physical and a political map not seen before and a set of product labels; respond day 57.
- Candidates settled: **kept**, countries working together through trade agreements and treaties (California
  2.3.2), inside day 54 at no added day. Maps and trade (Tennessee 2.07, 2.08, 2.11) are met by the owned days, no
  cost.

## Unit 5. People on the move (days 58 to 71, weeks 18 to 23)

*Why do people leave home for a new place?* Anchor MA.2.T3.1, MA.2.T3.2; C3 D2.Geo.7, D2.His.4, D2.His.6, D2.His.14,
D2.His.16.

Topics taught in full: why people move (work, family, safety, land, freedom); chosen and forced moves; the
cause-effect chain; Africans captured and enslaved; the Cherokee and other Native nations forced from their homelands
in the 1830s, and the Cherokee Nation and the Eastern Band today; refugees fleeing war today; "a nation of immigrants"
and its honest limits; Ellis Island and Angel Island newcomers in their own words; newcomers today; two accounts of
one arrival and the check that each teller had their own way of knowing.

Order: reasons and the cause-effect chain (days 58 and 59), the forced moves named plainly (60 and 61), reasons
weighed and moves traced on a map (62); then "a nation of immigrants" and its limits (63 and 64), newcomers long ago
and today (65 to 67), and two accounts last (68 and 69), on the arrivals the class has just read. The past runs in
time order inside each part.

- Assessment: day 70, from two short accounts not seen before of one move; respond day 71.
- Candidate settled: **none to keep**; no check state names an addition. Ellis Island and Angel Island side by side
  (days 65 to 67) is a Curio Class example, sourced in the unit plan.
- Lean: RI.2.9 (ELA unit 4, weeks 24 to 32, later): days 68 and 69 carry the bridge (a two-column "both say, only one
  says" chart).
- Sensitivity: days 58, 60, 61, 64 and 66 (slavery, Cherokee removal and the nations today, refugees, the Chinese
  exclusion law). No role play or simulation of capture, removal or flight.

## Unit 6. Family stories and culture (days 72 to 81, weeks 23 to 26)

*When people move to a new place, do they change the place more, or does the place change them more?* Anchor
MA.2.T3.3, MA.2.T3.4; C3 D2.His.12.

Topics taught in full: what makes up a culture; writing interview questions; interviewing a trusted adult about
where a family came from, how, why and when it came to this town or state; an interview as a source and its limits;
answers on a timeline; what people bring when they move; how migration changes newcomers and the place; how all of a
community's people make it richer.

Order: culture and the questions (days 72 and 73), a practice interview with a staff member before the real one (74
and 75), the interview read as a source and placed on a timeline (76), then what moves with people and what changes
(77 and 78), and the modeled claim (79), never a ranking of cultures.

- Assessment: day 80, from a short memory and a photograph not seen before, with the observation of the interviews;
  respond day 81.
- Candidates settled: **kept**, a class history time line from sources (California 2.1.1), inside day 76 at no added
  day. Cultural comparison (Tennessee 2.01, 2.02) is met by the owned row, no cost.
- Own-state method: day 73 (this town or state, where the anchor names Massachusetts).
- Sensitivity: days 75 and 78 (family structures, immigrant families, Native nations today).

## Unit 7. A country up close (days 82 to 93, weeks 27 to 30)

*What matters most about how people live in the country I chose?* Anchor MA.2.T4.3; C3 D1.4, D3.1, D4.3.

Topics taught in full: choosing a country from a menu of about 16 (at least two from each inhabited continent; not
Ghana, Canada or Mexico); supporting questions tied to the big one; picking sources for what they are and how they are
built; facts on land and water, resources and work, and people today; one custom compared with the student's own
community; a claim backed by two facts from named sources; a short report with a map, presented.

Order: the question, the choice and the supporting questions (days 82 and 83), sources (84), three gathering days by
supporting question (85 to 87), the claim (88), drafting, finishing and presenting (89 to 91).

- Assessment: day 92, the report scored, plus two facts found on a short source page not seen before about a country
  off the menu; respond day 93.
- Candidate settled: **none to keep**; MA.2.T4.3 is met by the report itself.
- Lean: W.2.7 (ELA unit 3, weeks 16 to 24, already taught).
- Sensitivity: day 82 (each menu country from its own government pages; no stereotypes).
