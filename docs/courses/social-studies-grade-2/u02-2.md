# Section 2.2: Weather, climate and regions

<!-- skel -->
Grade 2: Places and Peoples of the World. Unit 2, Land, water and climate. *Where on Earth would you choose to live, and why?* (days 17 to 29): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 21 to 23.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (21, 22, 23). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 4 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, sorted or a few written words. Days run 37 to 39 minutes, each with a 12-minute supported-work block. Every place in the teaching and the exits is invented and printed "invented for practice"; real places appear only through the checked pages named below. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time.

- **MA.2.T2.3, this section's share (days 21 to 23).** "Explain how the location of landforms and bodies of water shapes climate, weather, and vegetation for living." Day 21: weather is today, climate is the pattern over many years. Day 22: four places and what each does to the air and to what grows (high up, by the sea with a ridge inland, near the equator, in a desert). Day 23: the same ideas used on regions. Item 3 (day 28) is taught here and owned by section 2.4.
- **D2.Geo.4.K-2, this section's share (days 22, 23).** "Explain how weather, climate and other environmental features affect people's lives in a place." Day 22: what a worker wears or grows. Day 23: how life in a region fits its conditions, and that jobs and choices still differ. Item 4 (day 28) is owned here.
- **Limits that bite here.** No numbers worked out and no latitude grid; the equator is only the line round the globe's middle. Geography shapes conditions and does not decide anyone's life or work. A card cannot prove a climate, and one day is never a climate. Near the equator is often warm, but a high mountain there is cold. A desert is dry, not always hot. A coast may be wet or dry. The cause the children give for rain on one side of a ridge is "wet air from the sea goes up the hill, cools, and the water falls", from a source to check (the NASA rain shadow page). No child reports on a home, a job or the weather where family lives.
- **Connections.** Grade 1: environmental feature (land, water, weather, plants) and the two bins (review, days 21 and 22). Science kindergarten K-ESS2-1, weather patterns, retaught briefly on day 21 and not assumed. Section 2.1: the oceans and rain (day 21 repair), the globe and compass words (days 22 and 23). Grade 1's region, "a big area made of many towns", is the review on day 23.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Weather is what the air is like on one day; climate is what a place is usually like over many years (source to check: the NOAA weather and climate page). Air is colder higher up a mountain, and the Sun is not closer in a way that warms it (source to check). Sea air carries water; pushed up a ridge it cools and rain falls on the side facing the sea, leaving the far side drier (source to check). Some deserts are cold (source to check). A region is an area that shares something; its edges can move as features change.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 21: "How do you know that is weather and not climate?" Day 22: "Why does the high town need the warmer coat?" Day 23: "What do these places share, and what could people do about it?"
- **Written task, day 23.** Complete one sentence from a frame: "These places share ___, so ___." (a few words, or a drawing and one word). A child who cannot write dictates.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) the shared condition is named from the cards, not from a feeling; (2) the "so" is a possible effect on plants or on what people do; (3) it does not say everyone there does one job.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: weather, climate, pattern, region, coast, ridge, desert, rain, dry, wet. Frames: "That is weather because ___." "These places share ___, so ___." Pointing and home-language answers first.
- **Access.** Raised-line ridge and sea cards for a child who cannot see, neutral description of each card, the teacher moves the paper arrow for a child who cannot.
- **Check accommodations.** Prompts read aloud; circle, point, scribe; sentences on X21 read aloud, never explained; record the support used.
- **Reading load.** Labels of one to three words; the three X21 sentences are read aloud; at most 150 words a child reads in a day.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 23 says what would change if the sea were on the other side of the ridge.
