# Section 1.4: Making our own map

<!-- skel -->
Grade 2: Places and Peoples of the World. Unit 1, Maps tell stories. *Which map shows the world best, and best for what?* ("Can a map ever show the world just as it is?" stays as a supporting question) (days 1 to 16): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 13 to 16.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (13), application 1 (14), check 1 (15), respond 1 (16). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 4 of 4 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, drawn, counted or a few written words. Day lengths: 35 minutes on days 13 and 14, 33 on day 15 and 30 on day 16 (30 to 40). Practice cards print "invented for practice". Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time. The teacher says aloud that a child's map is never held up as a mistake.

- **MA.2.T1.3, this section's share (days 13 to 15).** "Make a map of a familiar place such as the school, neighborhood, or a park." The familiar place is our classroom by default, or a school part the class names (the library, the playground), never a child's home or street. Day 13 plans and drafts; day 14 revises with written feedback; day 15 scores only the revised map (unit assessment item 3).
- **D2.Geo.1.K-2, this section's share (days 13 to 15).** "Make maps, graphs and other pictures of familiar places." The class counts two kinds of features together on day 13 and each child draws a picture graph beside the map plan; on day 14 each child counts fresh and draws a new graph (unit assessment item 4). One mark for each counted feature.
- **MA.2.T1.1, MA.2.T1.2, D2.His.11.K-2 and D3.2.K-2 (days 15 and 16).** Their verbs are quoted in sections 1.1 to 1.3. Day 15 samples them (items 1, 2, 5 and 6); day 16 responds to what it shows (MA.2.T1.1 and D2.His.11.K-2).
- **Limits that bite here.** Paper only. Whole-number counts; the scale is counted in whole units. The teacher paces the long wall once while the class counts aloud; no child must pace. No child maps a home, a family or anything owned. Day 15's packet is read neutrally once and never taught; no help with answers. Scoring is by component: shown, shown with help, not yet seen; nothing is totalled.
- **Connections.** Kindergarten u03-3 (a picture map with a key; a two-row picture graph with a place for zero) and grade 1 u05-3 (a map made from a place, revised from written feedback) are review. Math 2.MD.D.10 lean (a picture graph with up to four categories), from memory; source to check. Day 14 asks "what does our map leave out and what is it best for?" back to the compelling question.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to pace a wall and choose a scale length in whole paces; how to write one feedback cue a child can act on (a count that does not match, a feature in the wrong place, a missing part); why drafts are practice and only the revision is scored; how to hear children not yet heard on days 14 and 15; and the day 15 scoring after class (about 3 minutes a child, so about 90 minutes, ours, untested).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Paper maps, a globe and equal strips provide the comparisons; a printed globe net and a soft paper sphere substitute for a globe. The teacher opens the paper shell on day 9 by tearing a printed line; no blade or student cutting. Gather pencils, paper, string, counters, equal strips, a north reference, map cards and a self-check strip per child. Everything prints in black and white; one shared display is optional. No student timers.
> **Language support.** Teach title, key, compass rose, scale, maker, date and place with labelled pictures and gestures. Rehearse "The key tells me ___" and "Each scale length stands for ___, so the distance is ___." Accept pointing and home-language answers first. Judge map reasoning separately from English.
<!-- /skel -->

- **Carried.** All four models are kept and withdrawn only as teaching help. The map-key model is made by each child on day 13 (symbols and a key box) and used on day 14; the compass-directions model is made on day 14 (a rose set by the wall north); the map-scale model is made on day 13 (a whole-unit scale bar from the class's wall pacing) and checked on day 14; the source-sourcing card is applied to our own map's credit line ("Made by ___ in our classroom, 2026") on day 14. Teacher pointing is withdrawn: day 13 children trace key to map themselves; day 14 has only a written cue; day 15 is independent. The question board closes with "What should our map show?" on day 13 and the compelling question's first claim on day 14. Blank frames and the four-part card stay available.
- **Key moment 1, day 13: the class paces the wall and counts together.** Ideal instrument: the class counting two kinds of features and the teacher's counted pacing. Spec: print fits (no tool). Fallback: if the room cannot be counted or paced (a visitor, a closed door), count from the Curio Class photograph card of the same room and say so; a child who cannot pace uses the teacher's pace count. The scale length is two paces if the wall's count is even, one pace if it is odd.
- **Key moment 2, day 14: a child revises from a written cue.** Ideal instrument: the day 13 draft with one written cue, and a clean sheet kept beside the draft. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Blank paper, a pencil, a taped line on the floor for the teacher's pace, counters for the graph.

## 6. Days

## Day 13

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Plan a map of a familiar place (the school or a park): what to show, symbols for the key, a scale in paces; a picture graph of two kinds of features counted together
- note: Depth: the kindergarten picture map becomes a map with all four parts. Map and graph drafts are practice; written feedback is returned for day 14 revision
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Error H (from E12): trace a country inside a continent inside the world on three nested outlines. This is the one repair the warm-up holds. Retrieval (one question inside it): "Which land or water features belong on our map?" (grade 1).
- **5 min. What should our map show?** Name the place (our classroom) and what a visitor needs to find; list what to show, what to leave out and why; post "What should our map show?".
- **6 min. Scale in paces.** The teacher paces the long wall once while the class counts aloud (about 2 minutes); the class picks the scale length (two paces, or one if the count is odd); each child draws a scale bar of equal boxes and counts how many bars the wall is.
- **5 min. Count and graph.** The class counts two kinds of features together (two minutes); each child draws a picture graph, one mark for each feature (three minutes). Fallback: count from the room photograph card.
- **10 min. Draft the map.** Each child drafts a map of the room: title, symbols and a key box, a rose, the scale bar, features in their observed places. The teacher circles and hears the child not yet heard. Drafts are practice.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E13: mark 1 minute; group heard 3 minutes.

Who holds what is carried: children now hold key and scale; the teacher no longer points. After class: one written cue on each draft and graph (about 1.5 minutes a child, ours, untested). Tool: None. By hand: counting, drawing, circling. Evidence: E13; the drafts (practice, not scored).

## Day 14

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Make the map with a title, key, compass rose and a scale counted in whole units; then ask what our own map leaves out and what it is best for
- note: Paper only; no device needed. Within twelve working minutes, make a graph from fresh room counts (four) and revise the map (eight); retain written feedback and revised versions before scoring
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return each draft with its written cue; error I (from E13): retrace the room route, then place the feature. This is the one repair the part holds.
- **12 min. Working time.** Four minutes: fresh counts of two other features and a new picture graph (one mark for each feature; a small group recounts with the teacher). Eight minutes: revise the map from its written cue, set the rose by the wall north, and write the credit line "Made by ___ in our classroom, 2026". The teacher adds a written cue for any remaining missing part or count; children revise before collection. Keep the draft and the revision.
- **9 min. Closing claim.** On a frame, each child circles one map card (the trail map, our map, the Mercator card, the Peters card or the globe) and one purpose card, and writes or draws one because. The six children of the claim group say theirs aloud (5 minutes, about 50 seconds each); the other children's claims are heard on day 15.
- **5 min. What our map leaves out.** Pairs: "What could our map help someone find? What does it leave out?" Revisit the question board: "What should our map show?" and a first answer to "Which map shows the world best, and best for what?"
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E14: mark 1 minute; a second group of six heard 3 minutes (the exit group is not the claim group).

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the key, rose and scale on their own map. Tool: None. By hand: counting, drawing, writing, circling. Evidence: E14; the revised map and graph (scored on day 15).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 13: "What should our map show, and what can it leave out?" Day 14: "What could our map help someone find? What does it leave out?" and "Which map is best for what you want to do, and why?" Day 16: "What did you improve?"
- **Written task, day 14.** The closing claim frame: circle a map card and a purpose card, then write one because (a word or a short phrase) or draw it. A child who cannot write the because says it to the teacher.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) the map and the purpose match; (2) the because points to a feature of the map (something we can show); (3) the child says what the map leaves out or bends.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: map, title, key, compass rose, scale, graph, mark, count, maker, date, place, leaves out. Frames: "Our map shows ___." "It leaves out ___." "I think the ___ is best for ___ because ___." "Made by ___ in ___, 2026." Pointing and home-language answers first; a scribe keeps the child's ideas, not the English.
- **Access.** Large-print or raised packet maps; neutral description of each map for a child who cannot see it; a partner may move counters; a child who cannot pace uses the teacher's count; a child who cannot draw small marks makes bigger ones or places counters.
- **Check accommodations.** Read labels and prompts aloud without locating answers; enlarged high-contrast maps, tactile symbols, pointing, oral answers or a scribe. Record the child's reasoning, not handwriting or reading speed.
- **Reading load.** Labels and a few words; at most 150 words a student reads in a day; the packet is read aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** A secure child on day 14 adds a second scale on a map of a bigger place and says why the label matters.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 13: choose one room, count two kinds of things in it together, draw a graph with one mark for each, then draft a map with a title, key, rose and scale. Day 14: revise from your written cue and write the credit line. Day 15: the same packet, read once, then step back. Day 16, the last day of the unit: look at what day 15 showed, re-teach one part, try a fresh card, and hear the child's claim about which map is best for which job.
- **One projector.** Show the packet maps once at day 15's start; children answer on their own pages; the paper map, strips and counters stay paper.
