# Section 2.4: A claim with a named source

<!-- skel -->
Grade 2: Places and Peoples of the World. Unit 2, Land, water and climate. *Where on Earth would you choose to live, and why?* (days 17 to 29): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 26 to 29.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (26), application 1 (27), check 1 (28), respond 1 (29). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 4 of 4 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption, credit and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, said, placed or a few written words; the claim is said first, then recorded with a frame, with transcription support. Days run 32 to 40 minutes. The Place Cards, Ocean Cards and every practice card are ours, with invented children and places, and printed "invented for practice"; the teacher says so aloud. Each card names who made it. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time.

- **D4.1.K-2, this section's share (days 26 to 29).** "Make an argument and give reasons for it." Here the reason names its source: "I think ___ because ___. The ___ shows ___." Day 26: modeled, then shared writing. Day 27: said to a partner, then recorded alone with the frame. Day 28: item 6. Day 29: revised. Item 6 (day 28) is owned here.
- **MA.2.T2.3 (days 27, 28).** "Explain how the location of landforms and bodies of water shapes climate, weather, and vegetation for living." The reasons for a choice come from the day 22 and 23 ideas. Item 3 (day 28) is owned here.
- **Day 28 and day 29 carry the whole unit.** The assessment samples MA.2.T2.1 (item 1), MA.2.T2.2 (item 2), D2.Geo.4.K-2 (item 4) and D2.Geo.10.K-2 (item 5) taught in sections 2.1 to 2.3; day 29 responds to all six.
- **Limits that bite here.** A reason is about land, water, weather, climate, plants or how people live with them; "I like it" is a first idea, not a reason. Either place is a defensible choice. No child chooses against where their family lives, and none reports on a home. No place is ranked above another, and a place is not "better" for being warm. A source is a named card and who made it, not a feeling. Spelling and handwriting are never judged; the reason and the source are each child's own words.
- **Connections.** Grade 1: a claim with a reason (retrieval, day 26). Unit 1: the closing claim frame, a map card, a purpose card and a because (day 26); the three questions on a card (the "who made it"). English language arts W.2.1, opinion writing, comes later in ELA (bridge: shared writing, then a frame).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A claim says what you think; a reason says why; the source says where the reason can be seen and who made it. A claim that names its source can be checked by someone else. Two children may choose differently from the same evidence and both be right. Marking is shown, shown with help or not yet seen by part; a missing continent, a missing representation or a missing change type is never averaged away. Day 29's groups come from the record sheet.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: claim, reason, source, because, choose, shows, made by. Frames: "I choose ___ because ___." "The ___ shows ___." "It was made by ___." Pointing and home-language answers first; judge reasoning apart from English.
- **Access.** A scribe writes the child's own words; raised-line and described cards for a child who cannot see; a partner holds the card for a child who cannot.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts aloud, accept pointing, enlarged outlines, tactile coastlines, speech or a scribe. Describe spatial relationships neutrally. Read word-bank labels aloud without locating them. Do not assess handwriting, decoding speed or spelling. Record the support used.
- **Reading load.** Labels of one to three words; frames of at most seven words; at most 150 words a child reads in a day.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 27 defends a second choice from the same evidence.
