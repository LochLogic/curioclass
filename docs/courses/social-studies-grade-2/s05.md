**Look-fors and samples.**

- Item 1 requires an evidence-based distinction between chosen and forced movement. Meets: "The Antin family arranged a move to join Father. The officials' order forced the other family out because they could not stay." Does not: "Both chose because they traveled." Accept recognition of pressures on Antin's family; do not require a claim of unlimited freedom.
- Item 2 requires why, how and the mapped route. Meets: "They crossed from Europe over the Atlantic to North America by ship to join Father." Does not: "They moved because the arrow points west."
- Item 3 requires possible reasons, distinguished from certainty. Meets: "Family reunion and a better living might explain it; A gives evidence for both." Does not: "Every family moved for treasure."
- Item 4 requires selecting and supporting a more likely reason, and the chain. Meets: "Joining Father is better supported than a holiday: he sent for them, they traveled, and he met them." Does not: "A holiday, because ships are fun."
- Item 5 (circle task plus a spoken line) requires two concrete immigrant examples and both limits. Meets: "Antin came from Europe and Lau from Asia. Many families came from abroad. Native nations were here first, and enslaved Africans were brought by force." Does not: "Everybody is an immigrant who chose to come."
- Item 6 requires comparing viewpoints using both sources. Meets: "Mary felt excited to see land and family. Rahaf wanted safety and education. Both hoped the move would help, but their stated hopes differ." Does not: "Then ships, now planes." Do not infer Rahaf's transport from the card.
- Item 7 requires agreement, a difference and source dependence. Meets: "Both say Father met them in Boston. Only A says Mary felt excited. B used A, so there is only one witness here. C is Mary again, so still one witness." Does not: "Two sheets prove everything twice."
- Day 69's closing claim requires an answer, two named sources and a link from evidence to reason. Meets: "People sometimes leave because staying is unsafe. Cherokee Today shows people forced from home; Arrival Voices describes a search for safety. These moves were not just trips for fun." Does not: "Everyone moves for work because I think so."

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "Everyone moves for work." | One possible reason is treated as universal or certain. On 59 compare two invented motives; ask which source supports each. | From memory. |
| B | "Arrival caused the departure" or reversed arrow. | Cause, effect or route is confused. On 60 reorder a fresh chain, then trace origin to destination. | From memory. |
| C | "They chose because they got on a ship." | Transport or later agency is confused with freedom to stay. On 60 and 63 compare freely arranged travel with imposed departure or unsafe staying. | From memory. |
| D | "Native people arrived as immigrants" or "everyone freely came." | The phrase erases prior Native presence and enslavement. On 64 use a before-and-after map and distinguish the histories without ranking belonging. | From memory. |
| E | "Then boats, now planes" answers a question about hopes. | Travel facts replace perspective. On 68 underline what each person hoped or felt before comparing. | From memory. |
| F | "One leaves out a detail, so somebody lied." | Difference is mistaken for contradiction. On 69 compare a complete invented notice with its accurate short version. | From memory. |
| G | "Two sheets prove it twice." | Dependence is missed. On 69 trace a retelling back to its witness; contrast two independently observing tellers. | From memory. |
| H | "It is true because I like it" or unnamed evidence. | Opinion, claim and evidence are confused. On 70's opening feedback use a fresh caption to distinguish checkable fact from preference. | From memory. |
