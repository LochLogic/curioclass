## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 58.** Show Anna Arrival and its route map. Read her reunion memory (the food memory is held for the day 65 exit), then ask the compelling question. Collect possibilities without asking about children's families.
**Supporting questions.** Shape students' questions into: What reasons explain a move (58 and 59)? Who could stay, and who could not (60 to 62)? Whose history does the immigrant phrase describe or miss (63 and 64)? Did arrival feel the same to everyone (65 to 67)? Does agreement mean two witnesses (68 and 69)?
**Closing claim.** On 69, children answer the question using Smith Life, Cherokee Today and Arrival Voices. They may argue that safety, family or several reasons matter most, with evidence and reasoning, while distinguishing choice from force.
**Teaching.** Model the chain on 59, perspective comparison on 67 and the two-column account chart with a copied retelling on 68. On 69 model a sourced claim on a separate example before students compose independently. Revisit questions daily; source each text before using it: who, when, where, and why it was made.


**Parts by verb.** MA.2.T3.1: investigate reasons and recognize choice and force. MA.2.T3.2: give examples explaining the phrase.
- **D2.His.14.K-2:** Generate possible reasons for something that happened in the past.
- **D2.His.16.K-2:** Select the reasons that best explain a past event.
- **D2.Geo.7.K-2:** Explain why and how people, goods and ideas move between places; people here, goods in unit 4 and ideas in unit 6.
- **D2.His.4.K-2:** Compare past people's viewpoints with present people's viewpoints.
- **D2.His.6.K-2:** Compare accounts of one historical event.
Each skill's verb names its part. Sections 5.1 to 5.4 own the parts their days teach.
**Limits and teacher background.** No legal-status questions, immigration-policy debate or graphic accounts. Teacher note on the Antins: field 5, Antin A. Forced movement includes flight when staying is unsafe. Choosing a route does not make flight freely chosen. Distinguish enslavers' motives from captive people's wishes. The inquiry groups cases by reason, then arrival, following the scope and sequence.
**Carried.** Keep unit 4's maps, sourcing card and claim frame. Tie the chain to because, moved and afterward on 59; withdraw filled examples on 62. Revise perspective and continuity-change charts on 67. Tie sourcing to account comparison on 68 and independence on 69. Retain these models. On 65 bridge grade 1's before-and-after ordering into dated cards; unit 6 develops the timeline. Withdraw teacher composition on 69, retaining transcription.

**Look-fors and samples.**

- Item 1 requires an evidence-based distinction between chosen and forced movement. Meets: "The Antin move was chosen: Father sent for them and they traveled. The other family was forced because they were not allowed to stay." Does not: "Both chose because they traveled." Accept recognition of pressures on Antin's family; do not require a claim of unlimited freedom.
- Item 2 requires why, how and the mapped route. Meets: "They crossed from Europe over the Atlantic to North America by ship to join Father." Does not: "They moved because the arrow points west."
- Item 3 requires possible reasons, distinguished from certainty. Meets: "Joining Father and a better life for the family might explain it; A gives evidence for both." Does not: "Every family moved for treasure."
- Item 4 requires selecting and supporting a more likely reason, and the chain. Meets: "Joining Father is better supported: A says he sent for us, they traveled, and he met them. Mary Antin made A." Does not: "More money, because I think so."
- Item 5 (circle task plus a spoken line) requires two concrete immigrant examples and both limits. Meets: "Antin came from Europe and Lau from Asia. Many families came from abroad. Native nations were here first, and enslaved Africans were brought by force." Does not: "Everybody is an immigrant who chose to come."
- Item 6 requires comparing viewpoints using both sources. Meets: "Mary wanted to join Father and felt excited to see land and family. Rahaf wanted safety and education. Both hoped the move would help, but their wants differ." Does not: "Then ships, now planes." Do not infer Rahaf's transport from the card.
- Item 7 requires agreement, a difference and source dependence. Meets: "Both say Father met them in Boston. Only A says Mary felt excited. B used A, so it had no way of knowing for itself. D saw it for itself, so A and D agreeing is stronger. One teller wrote A, B and C." Does not: "Two sheets prove everything twice."
- Day 69's closing claim requires an answer, two named sources and a link from evidence to reason. Meets: "People sometimes leave because staying is unsafe. Cherokee Today shows people forced from home; Arrival Voices describes a search for safety. These moves were not just trips for fun." Does not: "Everyone moves for work because I think so."
