## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 72.** Show the Music Here map and read Posadas remembering gatherings in Mexico and California. Ask the compelling question before retrieving grade 1 learning. Can music travel without fitting in a suitcase?
**Supporting questions.** Shape children's questions into: What is culture (72)? What can we ask a teller (73 to 75)? What does a memory show or leave unknown (76)? What travels with people (77)? What changes for whom, and what goal can people share (78)?
**Closing claim, day 79.** Answer the compelling question from Music Here and an interview the teller permits sharing. A child may use Language Today instead. Defend either emphasis or explain why these sources cannot measure which changes more; never rank cultures.
**Teaching.** Model a respectful question on 73, listening and a follow-up on 74, timeline placement on 76 and a claim on 79 using a separate example. Withdraw composed questions on 75 and the completed claim on 79. Source every card before reading. On 77, compare music gatherings with language classes. On 78, teach Posadas and the Cherokee Nation's language school first as worked examples, each in its own place; then the class's own town is the community.

**Interview prompts, days 73 to 75.** Children write or dictate: "Where did you or someone in your family live before? How did they travel here? Why did they move? When did they arrive in this town or state? What would you like to tell us about that move?" Practise "Could you explain that part?" "We have always lived here" (a Native family, or a family rooted here for generations) is a full answer to the where and when questions; record it as given. The adult chooses a safe move within or between places and may decline any question. Record the teller, interview date and place, event date, exact remembered words where possible, and permission to share. Do not collect recordings or legal documents. Each child asks a question and a follow-up directly, including in a small-group school interview. For the complete school path on 75, the teacher is the teller for one rotating group of six, five minutes, a record column naming who was heard, while others match each printed source card to a day 73 question. Reserve five minutes for recording and reflection. The teacher notes individual asking and listening. Home interviews are assigned on day 74 and come back by day 76; a child with no home record uses the school record.
