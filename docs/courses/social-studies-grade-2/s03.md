## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 30.** Show the Terraced Field photograph beside an original raised-house drawing. Ask the compelling question; record competing answers before explaining either choice.
**Supporting questions.** Shape students' questions into: Why live here (day 30)? How do communities meet needs (day 32)? How can we help our school prepare (days 34 to 36)? How do resources and learning support work (days 37 to 41)? What did our action accomplish (day 42)?
**Closing claim.** Day 43 answers the compelling question using Terraced Field and Changing Land Cards. Defend changing land, adapting, or combining both for a stated place; explain a benefit and a drawback.
**Teaching.** Model reading maps and captions on day 30, checking advice on day 34, resource chains on day 37, and sourced reasoning on day 41. Step back during action and day 43. Source each document: who, when, where and why. Revisit the question board.

**Held-back packet, still to make.** Cedar Inlet is an explicitly invented harbor city. Draw and caption four panels, each with at most three short sentences. Keep the whole packet unseen until day 44.

1. **Harbor Map.** Show a sheltered bay, homes and a boat landing, a forest, a boatyard and a new pier replacing marsh. Caption: "People settled by this sheltered bay because boats could land away from strong waves. Boat crews change their travel times to fit the rising and falling water. A new pier gives boats more landing space but replaces marsh where birds fed."
2. **Work Card.** "Local trees supply timber for building wooden boats; trees can grow again when forests are cared for. Oil used for fuel takes far longer to form than a human lifetime. Builders pay for timber, saws and workers' time."
3. **Learning Card.** "A boatbuilder learned to measure at school and to join wood at work. Using this knowledge, she invented a clamp that holds curved boards, helping the team make more boats. A saw cuts the boards."
4. **Connections Map.** Show Cedar Inlet and an inland sheep-farming community. Caption: "The city gets timber nearby and wool blankets from the farming community. The farming community gets wool nearby and boats from Cedar Inlet to cross its lake. Cedar Inlet offers boat repair; an animal health worker in the farming community gives advice to the city's animal keepers by telephone."

**Look-fors and sample answers.**

- Item 1 requires settlement reasoning and an adaptation linked to conditions. Meets: "The sheltered bay makes landing easier. Crews adapt their travel times to water levels." Does not: "People live here because every town has boats."
- Item 2 requires both environmental and cultural effects. Meets: "The pier replaces bird habitat and changes where people land boats." Does not: "There is water," without describing human effects.
- Item 3 requires a comparison across community types, using near and far for both. Meets: "The city uses nearby timber and distant blankets; the farming community uses nearby wool and distant boats. Both depend on other places." Does not: "Farms get everything nearby."
- Item 4 requires a disaster effect on people and evidence from a recovery account. Meets: "The Flood Guide says roads flooded, so travel was interrupted. The city's account describes teams working on recovery." Does not: "Floods are bad," without identifying an effect. Retain individual notes from days 33 to 36; revisit gently before day 44 if evidence is missing.
- Item 5 requires resource, industry, job and renewal reasoning. Meets: "Forest trees supply boatbuilding; builders shape timber. Trees can regrow with care, but oil cannot be replaced during our lives." Does not: "Boats grow in forests; oil returns when the tank is filled."
- Item 6 requires local and distant goods and services plus a production cost. Meets: "Boats and boat repair come from the city; blankets and animal health advice come from the farming community. Paying for timber is a cost of making boats." Does not: "The boat's selling price is the only cost; advice is a good."
- Item 7 requires a tool, learned skill and the learning-to-invention-to-production connection. Meets: "The saw is equipment; measuring is learned. Learning helped her invent a clamp, which helps the team make more boats." Does not: "The saw knows how to measure; people need no learning."
- Item 8 requires a claim, named source and a reason linking evidence to the choice. Meets: "People should combine changes and adaptations here. Harbor Map shows adjusted boat schedules and a pier; Work Card shows why boats matter, but the pier costs bird habitat." Does not: "Always change the land because I say so." Other supported positions meet.

**Accommodations.** Read all captions and prompts neutrally; supply enlarged high-contrast or tactile diagrams, oral description, pointing, speech or a scribe. Keep reasons and source choices the child's own; do not score decoding or handwriting.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** No specifications or asks. Supply printed sources, original diagrams, advice, cards, Action Record, paper, pencils and voting counters. All tasks print; one shared display is optional. Each 30-minute teaching core keeps five minutes to open, eight to teach, twelve to work and five for evidence; optional ten minutes extend discussion. Show no timers.

**Action.** Days 34 to 36 use class-proposed options, a fair vote and school procedures; day 42 checks delivery and response. No drills or cleanup. At home, one child and adult compare options and deliver a household readiness message; record the response.

**Language.** Teach adapt, change, region, resource, renewable, industry, skill and source with pictures. Rehearse "The source shows ... , so ... ." Adults read captions; children read labels. Extend by defending a different choice.
