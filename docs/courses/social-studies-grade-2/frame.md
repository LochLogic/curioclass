# Course frame: Grade 2: Places and Peoples of the World

Course id ss-k5, grade 2. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7, a world geography year in Massachusetts 2018 grade 2 order (maps; how the Earth shapes people's
  lives; migrations and cultures; countries and governments; resources), with maps first because every later unit
  reads a map, and the country report last because it gathers the whole year.
- **Days.** 96: 91 in units, 3 for course-level checks (1 after unit 3, 2 after unit 7) and 2 respond days
  (after the mid-year check). Social studies shares the grade 2 day with reading and math, so the course assumes
  three lessons a week of 30 to 40 minutes (about 90 to 120 minutes a week) across 32 teaching weeks, not the
  full-year yardstick of 160. Grade 2 has 18 rows, fewer than grade 1, but heavier ones (a map made to four
  parts, the year's action cycle, interviews, a research report), so it keeps grade 1's days.
- **Standard.** Content: Massachusetts 2018 grade 2, MA.2.T1 to MA.2.T5, every standard owned by a row and placed
  below, with one planned split: Massachusetts prints grade 1 T4 and grade 2 T5 as one economics topic, and Curio
  Class teaches it once (data/progressions/social-studies-excluded.csv). Grade 2 teaches resources, people as a
  resource and goods made here and elsewhere (MA.2.T5.1 to MA.2.T5.3); grade 1 taught employment, goods and
  services, choices and saving, so MA.2.T5.4 to MA.2.T5.9 are excluded here with that reason, and choices are kept
  alive as review in unit 4 (opportunity cost in trade). Skills: C3 K-2 indicators as owned by the rows, two of
  them deepening a kindergarten or grade 1 row (D2.Geo.1 in ss2-make-map, D4.1 in ss2-claim-sources; the depth
  lines are in the rows). Check set: California, Tennessee and New York compared in each unit plan, additions
  recorded with their source; the Units table's "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects. Values
  rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in order: higher court, backed sources, dignity and openness, grade and life
  experience.
- **Base text.** None (pre-work P2). Every day is Curio Class's own. Sources: open and public-domain maps,
  photographs, letters and memories (Library of Congress, National Archives, National Park Service, USGS maps,
  NOAA and FEMA readiness pages, Ellis Island and Angel Island records and memories in the public domain,
  public-domain images), adapted with a credit line, and Curio Class's own read-alouds. Country facts come from
  open government and public-domain sources, each with its rights line. No day needs a trade book or a library
  visit.
- **Reading level.** Grade 2 students read short texts on their own with support. Sources are short (a captioned
  photograph, a labeled map, a few sentences of a memory), read together first; each has a stated difficulty in
  the section plan. From unit 1 every source gets the three sourcing questions (who made it, when, where).
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit opens with a compelling question and supporting questions the class writes
  together (grade 1's skill); from unit 2 every closing claim names the source its reason came from
  (ss2-claim-sources). Unit 7 runs the whole arc as each student's own short research report.
- **Informed action.** Once in the year, unit 3 (ss2-people-environment): after learning how people get ready for
  and recover from a storm or flood, the class chooses one way to help its own school get ready, checks it against
  the town's or state's published advice, decides by a fair vote, carries it out through the school and talks
  about what happened. The class chooses; the course never picks the cause.
- **Own-state method.** Where the anchor names Massachusetts (MA.2.T3.3, when families came to the state), the
  course asks when families came to this town or state, and the student's own region is used beside the
  countries studied (ss2-resources-industries).
- **Hard history at grade 2.** Unit 5 names forced moves plainly (Africans captured and enslaved; the Cherokee
  and other Native nations forced from their homelands; refugees fleeing war today), with the human side and
  present-day Native nations, and no role play or simulation of capture, removal or flight.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 3 (1 day, mid-year).** On a map not seen before: name what the title, key, compass rose and scale
  tell; find two continents and two oceans; answer who made the map and when from the map itself; write a claim
  about how people live with the land in a pictured place, with a reason that names the source.
- **2 respond days after the mid-year check.** Re-teach in small groups what the check shows, most often scale and
  naming the source in a claim.
- **After unit 7 (2 days, end of year).** Day one: two short accounts not seen before of one event (a town flood,
  a ship's arrival): what both say, where they differ, whether each teller had their own way of knowing. Day two:
  from a short source set not seen before about a place, give a cause-effect chain for one move and a claim with
  two facts from named sources. The record goes on to the grade 3 teacher.
