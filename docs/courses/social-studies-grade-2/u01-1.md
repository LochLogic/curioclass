# Section 1.1: Reading a map's parts

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-2/1/s/1.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-2 u01-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every stem, caption and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, placed as a token, drawn or a few written words. Each day runs 35 minutes (30 to 40). Every map and card in this section is ours and invented, the teacher says so aloud, and the card prints "invented for practice". Pip the puppet (review of grade 1) says each wrong idea aloud, one line at a time; no child's work is ever the example of a mistake.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.2.T1.1, this section's share.** "Explain what map parts such as the compass rose, scale, key, and title tell us, and give examples of maps showing how people relate to their environment." The title and the key on day 2, the compass rose on day 3, scale on day 4, and "give examples" of people and their land and water on day 5, with all four parts. "Explain" here means a child says or shows what each part tells, with the frames in field 9. Unit assessment item 1 (day 15) is owned here. "Environment" is the teacher's word; children say land and water.
- **Limits that bite here.** Scale is counted in whole units (blocks, paces, steps) with equal paper strips; no ruler, ratio or latitude. Four directions only, no "northeast". North is where the rose points, never "the top of the page". A symbol means what this map's key says, on this map only. Every taught map is invented, shows no real child's home, street or family, and names no real place. The flood map shows a made-up town where no one is hurt and nothing is lost. No place or people are ranked by drawn size.
- **Connections.** MA.2.T1.3, days 2 and 3: the key and the rose are the parts students make on day 14. Math 1.MD.A.2 lean (equal units end to end, grade 1), day 4; counting by fives on day 4 (2.NBT.A.2 lean, from memory).
- **Review, not new (same words as the ledger).** Map, key, symbol, globe, land, water, cardinal directions (north, south, east, west) and direction indicator (grade 1 u05-1); the question board; the exit routine (mark a picture, then say why, heard from a rotating six); Pip. Grade 1's "direction indicator" gets its second name on day 3: compass rose.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Each map part has one job: the title says what the map shows; the key says what each symbol stands for; the compass rose shows directions and may point anywhere on the page; the scale ties a length on the map to a distance on the ground, counted in whole units. A symbol is not the place. The best map depends on its purpose. A compass needle points toward north (from memory; source to check); if the room's steel spoils the needle, the class names one wall north for the day and says so.

## 2. Where it sits


Opens the unit and the year, with no earlier unit in this course. All four items of the start-of-year check (unit plan field 4) serve this section: item 1 (key) is repaired on day 2, item 2 (directions) on day 3, item 4 (land and water) on day 5, and item 3 (an old map as a source) is asked of the rotating six on days 2 and 3 and repaired on day 6, in section 1.2. Results go on the record sheet, which also names the rotating groups.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 1 (MA.2.T1.1: title, key, rose, scale, a counted distance, two examples of people using land or water), asked on day 15 on the Willow Garden Map. Nothing taught on days 1 to 5 uses that map.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks one picture in the last minutes; the teacher reads the prompt aloud. The day's rotating six (about 6 children, 20 to 30 seconds each, one spoken ask) are heard after the mark, and the record sheet names who was heard; a child not yet heard is "not yet seen", never "missed". Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Each card is new and prints "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E1 (day 1).** Mark: on a new card, a trail map of a nature preserve and a world map. "Circle the map that helps you find the path to the lookout tower." Spoken: "Why that map?" Answer: the trail map; it shows the paths. Wrong: the world map because it is bigger, prettier or has more color (N).
- **E2 (day 2).** Mark: a new card titled Orchard Paths; a diamond means a well; the card has a title, a key, a compass rose and a scale bar, each in its own box. "Circle the part that tells what the diamond means." Spoken: "What does the title tell us?" Answer: the key; the title tells the place and subject (paths in an orchard). Wrong: circles the title, the rose or the scale bar (A).
- **E3 (day 3).** Mark: a new fairground card; the rose points right for north; the stage is left of the snack stand (the skeleton's "kiosk" is printed "snack stand"; it is the one-ask exit X3, the answer south). "Circle the direction word from the snack stand to the stage: north, south, east or west." Spoken: "How do you know?" Answer: south; north points right, so left is south. Wrong: west, north or east, from reading the page and not the rose (B).
- **E4 (day 4).** Mark: a new card; a path is six equal scale lengths; each length means three blocks. "Circle how far the path is: 6, 9 or 18 blocks." Spoken: "What does the scale tell us?" Answer: 18 blocks; each map length stands for three blocks of ground. Wrong: 6 (counts the lengths and ignores the label) or 9 (adds one length of three to six) (C).
- **E5 (day 5).** Mark: a new Lake Edge card with five numbered places: a boat ramp with a fish stall beside it, garden steps cut into a hillside, a bare hill, open water, a rock. "Circle two places where people use or change the land or water." Spoken: "How do people use it?" Answer: the boat ramp and the garden steps; boats are launched at the ramp, food grows on the cut slope. Wrong: circles the bare hill, the water or the rock, or names a feature and no action (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The four-part card (title, key, rose, scale, each in its own box) is introduced piece by piece and kept on the wall through the unit. The map-key model (grade 1: trace from a symbol to the key and back; "The map shows ___. I can find it because the key says ___.") is read on day 2 with teacher pointing, then by partners, and is read without help on day 5. The compass-directions model (the paper rose turned on the desk, wall signs set by compass) is read on day 3 and carried to day 14, when students make a rose. The map-scale model (equal strips laid end to end, one box is one unit) starts on day 4 and is used on day 5. The question board stays posted; each answer is added after the day it answers. Teacher pointing is withdrawn, not access to the map's information.
- **Key moment 1, day 2: a child traces from symbol to key and back.** Ideal instrument: a printed map with a key box and tokens to cover symbols. Spec: print fits; no tool. Gaps and asks: none (this section runs on print and has no key tool).
- **Key moment 2, day 3: the rose turns and a direction changes with it.** Ideal instrument: a paper rose on a paper fastener, one for each pair. Spec: fits.
- **Key moment 3, day 4: equal strips counted end to end along a path.** Ideal instrument: paper strips cut equal to one box of the scale bar, six for each pair. Spec: fits; a strip is not a ruler.
- **A room with nothing.** Sketch each map on scrap paper; fold strips from one sheet; set the wall north by a compass or, failing that, by naming a wall north; a turned sheet of paper is the rose.

## 6. Days

## Day 1


- **15 min. Hook and question.** Say the maker first: "We made these cards for our class; they are drawn from real coastlines." Show the globe and two World Projection Cards, called Card 1 and Card 2 (a reminder of kindergarten's two world maps beside the globe). Ask, "Which would help us compare how big lands are?" Each child puts a counter in one of two cups on the way back to the mat, or passes. Read the totals aloud as numbers, never as names, and do not say which is right. Post the compelling question. Ask, "What do we want to find out?" and write the class's supporting questions on the board; if no one asks whether a map can show the world just as it is, the teacher offers it: "I wonder if any map can."
- **3 min. Retrieval.** "What smaller question could help us choose a useful map?" Answer: a relevant question such as "What does the map show?" An unrelated question goes to the side of the board.
- **10 min. Start-of-year check.** The four-picture page (unit plan field 4), read aloud, items 1 to 4; every child marks; not scored; record shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E1: mark 1 minute; group 1 (six children) heard 2 minutes; collect 2 minutes.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board and the cards; each child holds a counter and the check page. Tool: None. By hand: counters, marking the page, circling. Evidence: E1; the check page (record only, not scored).

## Day 2


- **6 min. Return and repair.** Two live routes: check item 1 (a symbol stands for a thing) and error N (from E1). The more common is taught to the whole class in 5 minutes; the other goes to a small group in Pairs work. Item 1: pair a block with its drawn symbol, then match a new key. Error N: Pip's wrong idea, then three job cards matched to three map cards.
- **11 min. Title and key.** Lantern Lane Market (invented): the title, the key (a star is a stall, a wave is a pond), a symbol traced to the key and back with the teacher pointing. Then the same map with the title hidden and with the key hidden, to show what each part is for. Model "The title tells me ___. The key tells me ___." Pip's wrong idea A.
- **12 min. Pairs work.** Harvest Hill (invented), one printed copy for each pair: choose a title card from three; match three symbols to their key words. The teacher hears eight pairs say item 3's why (why an old map of Boulder could show what Boulder looked like then; 40 seconds a pair, about 5 minutes) and takes the small group's repair (4 minutes).
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** E2: mark 2 minutes; group 2 heard 3 minutes; add the day's answer to the question board 1 minute.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher points on day 2; each pair holds the key. Tool: None. By hand: tracing, matching cards, circling. Evidence: E2; item 3's why for eight pairs.

## Day 3


- **6 min. Return and repair.** Check item 2 (a direction word from a pointing arrow) and error A (from E2). The more common is taught to the whole class in 5 minutes, the other goes to a small group. Item 2: turn a paper compass and locate a new pair of places. Error A: pairs match four new titles and four new keys.
- **11 min. Compass rose.** Name the compass rose: the direction indicator of grade 1, with all four directions drawn. Birch Camp (invented) has its rose pointing down. Set the wall signs by compass (fallback: name one wall north, said aloud). Say "From the ___ to the ___ is ___, because the rose points ___." Pip's wrong idea B.
- **12 min. Pairs work.** Quarry Hill (invented) and a paper rose on a fastener for each pair; turn the rose and name directions between places. The teacher hears seven pairs say item 3's why (40 seconds a pair, about 5 minutes) and takes the small group's repair (4 minutes).
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** E3: mark 2 minutes; group 3 heard 3 minutes; board 1 minute.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the rose; the teacher no longer points. Tool: None. By hand: turning the rose, naming directions, circling. Evidence: E3; item 3's why for seven pairs.

## Day 4


- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Error B (from E3): turn a paper rose on the desk; pairs name the direction between two places on fresh cards. This is the one repair the warm-up holds.
- **12 min. Scale in whole units.** Sunny Street (invented): the scale bar is a row of equal boxes and one box means one block. Lay a paper strip as long as one box along the path from the bakery to the library, counting strips aloud. Say "Each scale length stands for one block, so the distance is ___." Then Cove Road (invented): one box means five steps; count by fives. Pip's wrong idea C.
- **12 min. Pairs work.** Six equal strips for each pair and two fresh paths with different labels; count and say the frame. The teacher circles; a small group takes any child still counting marks and not units.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** E4: mark 2 minutes; group 4 heard 3 minutes; board 1 minute.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the strips and say the frame. Tool: None. By hand: laying strips, counting, circling. Evidence: E4. Hand-off: day 5 uses the strips again on a path.

## Day 5


- **8 min. Return and repair.** Two live routes: check item 4 (land and water: trace a river beside land on a paper map and find another example) and error C (from E4: lay strips along a path on a fresh map and count the ground units). The more common is taught to the whole class in 5 minutes; the other goes to a small group in Pairs work. Section 1.2 day 6 receives error D.
- **7 min. Model: Alder Farm Map.** Read all four parts, then ask how people use or change the land or water (a ditch from the stream to the field). Frame: "People ___ the ___ so ___."
- **11 min. Pairs work.** Riverbend Park (invented) and Lowfield (invented; a raised bank beside a creek that can overflow): each pair reads each map's four parts and says one way people use or change land or water. The small group takes its repair (4 minutes).
- **3 min. Question board.** "What do map parts tell us?" Add the class's answer in the children's words.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** E5: mark 2 minutes; group 5 heard 3 minutes; board 1 minute.

Who holds what is carried: pairs read the four-part card with no teacher pointing. Tool: None. By hand: reading the maps, writing one label word (field 7), circling. Evidence: E5; the written label (field 7).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 2: "What does the title tell us? What does the key tell us?" Day 4: "What does the scale tell us?" Day 5: "How do people use or change the land or water here?"
- **Written task, day 5.** On Riverbend Park, circle one place where people use or change land or water and write one word (a label such as bridge or path), or draw the action. A child who cannot write the word draws it and says it.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) the child points to a mapped feature and not to blank paper; (2) the answer names what people do there, not only what is there; (3) the child says which map part told them.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 1.** Retrieval: the grade 1 question words (a smaller question for a big one). Day 2: the key (grade 1, check item 1) and the question board. Day 3: day 2's title and key; grade 1's direction indicator. Day 4: day 3's rose. Day 5: day 4's strips and grade 1's land and water.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words, taught with a picture and a gesture: title, key, symbol, compass rose, north, south, east, west, scale, block, distance, purpose. Frames: "The title tells me ___." "The key tells me ___." "From the ___ to the ___ is ___." "Each scale length stands for ___, so the distance is ___." Pointing and home-language answers come first; judge map reasoning apart from English.
- **Access.** Large-print or raised maps (a string path, raised key marks, a raised rose); describe a map neutrally for a child who cannot see it; paper strips are tactile. No child must pace or turn the rose alone; a partner may hold it.
- **Check accommodations.** The exit prompt is read aloud. A child may circle, point, place a token, draw one mark or answer to a scribe, or answer privately. Record the support used.
- **Reading load.** Printed labels of one to three words; at most 150 words a student reads in a day; every caption and stem is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 4 explains why the same bar could stand for blocks on one map and steps on another, using Cove Road beside Sunny Street.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 2: one printed map with the key covered; ask what the symbols might mean, uncover the key, then cover the title. Day 3: turn a sheet of paper and ask which way north points now. Day 4: lay strips of paper along a path and count them. Day 5: ask where people changed the land or water on a map and how the map shows it. The wrong ideas are said by a toy or a drawn friend.
- **One projector.** Show each map on the screen; children still mark their own printed card. A shared screen never replaces a child's own mark, and the rose and strips are paper.
