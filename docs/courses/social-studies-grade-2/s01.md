## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 1.** Show the same world on a globe and the two projection cards. Ask which would help compare land sizes. Record competing choices before teaching what maps select.
**Supporting questions.** Shape the class's questions into: What do map parts tell us (day 5)? Who made this source, and what can we check (day 8)? Can a map show the world just as it is (day 11)? What should our map show (day 14)? Keep students' wording beside these questions.
**Closing claim.** On day 14, students dictate or write which map best serves a chosen purpose, using the World Projection Cards or their Familiar Place Map. Day 15 asks for a fresh spoken choice.
**Teaching.** Model reading map parts on days 2 to 4 and sourcing on day 6. Bridge RI.2.6 with "What is this source for?" Model claim, evidence and reason on day 11; partners try a different purpose. Step back during days 13 and 14. Revisit the question board after each answer.

**Limits.** Count equal scale lengths in blocks or paces; no ruler, ratios or latitude calculations. Mercator keeps local angles, not whole continents' shapes or sizes; Peters keeps relative areas, not shapes or all distances. Navigation means a constant compass direction, not necessarily the shortest route. No ranking peoples by mapped size. Named continents are supported here; unit 2 teaches their locations.
**Carried.** Read the key on day 2 and directions on day 3; students create both on day 14. Tie scale to repeated equal strips on day 4; revise the idea of one world-wide scale on day 11. Introduce the sourcing card on day 6 and use it independently from day 8. Retain all four models; withdraw teacher pointing, not access to map information.

- Item 1 requires all four functions, a counted distance and two human-environment examples. ## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** Paper maps, a globe and equal strips provide the comparisons; a printed globe net and a soft paper sphere substitute for a globe. The teacher opens the paper shell on day 9; no blade or student cutting. Gather pencils, paper, string, counters, a north reference, map cards and a self-check strip per child. Everything prints in black and white; one shared display is optional. No student timers.
**Language support.** Teach title, key, compass rose, scale, maker, date and place with labelled pictures and gestures. Rehearse "The key tells me ___" and "Each scale length stands for ___, so the distance is ___." Accept pointing and home-language answers first. Judge map reasoning separately from English.
