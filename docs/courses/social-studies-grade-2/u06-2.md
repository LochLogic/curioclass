# Section 6.2: A telling as a source, what people bring, and how a place and its people change

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, boxed, put on a strip, tokened, said or drawn. Days run 35 to 37 minutes. Pip says each wrong idea, one line at a time, one actor in each scene. Every person in a practice card (Ines, Tavo, Tavan, Pell, the cook, Eddyford's families) is invented and printed "invented for practice"; the teacher says so aloud. Real tellings appear only as Curio Class summaries in our words with one credit line: the Posadas Card (day 72, again on 78), Language Today and the local nation's own page (day 78). No sources.csv rows exist for this course yet, so each real fact is marked "source to check". No child reports on home, family, money, religion or how they came here; any child may pass.

- **MA.2.T3.3 (day 76).** "Interview family, neighbors, friends, or school staff ..." Day 76: read what was recorded, put its dates on a timeline, and keep unknown dates unknown. Children not yet heard ask on this day.
- **D2.His.12.K-2 (day 76).** "Ask questions about a source as it relates to a particular event or development." The visitor's account is the source; the move is the event. Item 2 on day 80 asks the same of a new source.
- **MA.2.T3.4 (days 77, 78).** "Identify what people bring when they move, the effects of migration ... and explain how all of a community's people enrich it." Day 77: what people bring, in a bag and in a person. Day 78: the effects of migration on newcomers and on the place, and how newcomers, long-time residents and the Native nation of this place each add.
- **Limits that bite here.** A memory shows what one teller said and leaves the rest unknown. A date not given is unknown and sits in a box, not between years. Culture moves in people, not only in bags. Learning English need not replace another language. Native nations are living governments with their own languages, named by their own names, never an immigrant group and never in the past tense. Belonging never depends on adding a tradition. A feeling is not read from a date. No ranking of cultures.
- **Connections.** D2.Geo.7.K-2, ideas travel too (day 77; goods and ideas, with 4.2). Unit 5: the chain (day 77), perspective (day 78), the teller's own way of knowing and the copy (day 76). Unit 2: physical change and cultural change (day 78). Tennessee 2.01 customs compared (day 77) and 2.02 a shared community goal (day 78), each inside its day.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the class copy is a copy and the visitor's own words are firsthand. Why a source's limits are as useful as its contents. That an unfamiliar practice is a custom, not a problem. How the Posadas Card, Language Today and the local nation's page were found and matched (source to check). Why the town cards use only lines a teller allowed to share, with names off.

- **X77b (day 77).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. The cook told the neighbors a folktale from her old town. How did the folktale get to the new town? Answer: It moved with the cook: she remembered it and told it. A story can travel in a person. Wrong: The folktale came in her bag (A); The neighbors already knew it before she came (H).
- **X77c (day 77).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. The cook tells the neighbors a folktale from her old town. A neighbor shows the cook a family cooking custom. Say one way the folktale and the cooking custom are alike. Answer: Both are shared with other people, or both are parts of culture that people pass on. Wrong: The folktale is better than the cooking (K); Only the cook has culture; the neighbor just lives there (M).
- **X78 (day 78).** Mark, paper: Four cards. Cards 1, 2 and 4 are about Eddyford, an invented town. Card 1: a family that came this year started a night market for foods. Card 2: families who have always lived in Eddyford keep the old orchard open to all. Card 3 is real, from the Seneca Nation of Indians' own pages: the Seneca Nation is a government whose constitution was established in 1848, and its language departments offer Seneca language classes. Card 4: the town hall bell rings at noon. Put a token on the card that shows what the Seneca Nation adds today. Answer: Card 3. Wrong: The token is on card 1 (L); The token is on card 2 (L); The token is on card 4 (H).
- **X78b (day 78).** Spoken, the six: Look again at the four cards. Say what the families who have always lived in Eddyford added to the town. Answer: They keep the old orchard open to all. Wrong: Nothing, they just live there (M).
- **X78c (day 78).** Spoken, the six: Look again at the four cards. Say one change to Eddyford because of the family that came this year. Answer: Eddyford got a night market. Wrong: The place did not change, only the family did (F); Nothing changed in Eddyford (F).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with a picture: memory, source, timeline, unknown, bring, share, teach, newcomer, add. Frames: "The source says ___; it cannot tell ___." "___ added ___." "We could all ___ so that ___." Home-language rehearsal allowed; a partner may translate.
- **Access.** Large print; movable, cut cards; described pictures; a child may point, draw or dictate; a standing table strip for a child who cannot see a small strip.
- **Check accommodations.** Every ask is read aloud, neutrally; a child who passes in public answers to the teacher alone; pointing, drawing and home-language answers are accepted; no timers are shown.
- **Reading load.** Labels only, about 50 words a day; the teacher reads every card, the visitor's account and the three town sources.
- **Extension, same standard.** "What source would check the visitor's memory, and who could tell it for themselves?"
