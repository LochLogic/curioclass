## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.2.T1.3** (days 13, 14, 15): Make a map of a familiar place such as the school, neighborhood, or a park.
- **D2.Geo.1.K-2** (days 13, 14, 15): Make maps, graphs and other pictures of familiar places.
- **MA.2.T1.1** (days 15, 16): Explain what map parts such as the compass rose, scale, key, and title tell us, and give examples of maps showing how people relate to their environment.
- **MA.2.T1.2** (days 15): Compare map projections such as Mercator and Peters and explain how each shows the world differently.
- **D2.His.11.K-2** (days 15, 16): Identify who made a source, when and where, using clues in the source itself.
- **D3.2.K-2** (days 15): Judge a source by telling fact from opinion.
- skill ss2-make-map (grade 2)
- skill ss2-map-parts (grade 2)
- skill ss2-projections (grade 2)
- skill ss2-source-maker (grade 2)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Shape the class's questions into: What do map parts tell us (day 5)? Who made this source, and what can we check (day 8)? Can a map show the world just as it is (day 11)? What should our map show (day 13)? Keep students' wording beside these questions.
> **Closing claim.** On day 14, students dictate or write which map best serves a chosen purpose, using the World Projection Cards or their Familiar Place Map. Day 15 hears children not yet heard.
> **Teaching.** Model reading map parts on days 2 to 4 and sourcing on day 6. Bridge RI.2.6 with "What is this source for?" Model claim, evidence and reason on day 11; partners try a different purpose. Step back during days 13 and 14. Revisit the question board after each answer.
>
> - **D2.Geo.1.K-2:** Construct maps, graphs and other representations of familiar places. On day 13 each child draws a picture graph from counts the class makes together, beside the map plan.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Paper maps, a globe and equal strips provide the comparisons; a printed globe net and a soft paper sphere substitute for a globe. The teacher opens the paper shell on day 9 by tearing a printed line; no blade or student cutting. Gather pencils, paper, string, counters, equal strips, a north reference, map cards and a self-check strip per child. Everything prints in black and white; one shared display is optional. No student timers.
> **Language support.** Teach title, key, compass rose, scale, maker, date and place with labelled pictures and gestures. Rehearse "The key tells me ___" and "Each scale length stands for ___, so the distance is ___." Accept pointing and home-language answers first. Judge map reasoning separately from English.
