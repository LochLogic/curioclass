## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.2.T3.3** (days 76): Interview family, neighbors, friends, or school staff to learn where their families came from, how and why they moved, and when they came to Massachusetts.
- **D2.His.12.K-2** (days 76): Ask questions about a source as it relates to a particular event or development.
- **MA.2.T3.4** (days 77, 78): Identify what people bring when they move, the effects of migration, and the elements that define a culture, and explain how all of a community's people enrich it.
- skill ss2-family-interviews (grade 2)
- skill ss2-source-maker (grade 2)
- skill ss2-culture-migration (grade 2)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Shape children's questions into: What is culture (72)? What can we ask a teller (73 to 75)? What does a memory show or leave unknown (76)? What travels with people (77)? What changes for whom, and what goal can people share (78)?
> **Teaching.** Model a respectful question on 73, listening and a follow-up on 74, timeline placement on 76 and a claim on 79 using a separate example. Withdraw composed questions on 75 and the completed claim on 79. Source every card before reading. On 77, compare music gatherings with language classes. On 78, teach Posadas and the Cherokee Nation's language school first as worked examples, each in its own place; then the class's own town is the community.
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 5's sourcing card, claim frame and continuity-change chart. Revisit cause-effect-chain on 77, then revise it for changes to newcomers and place on 78. Return to perspective on 78, distinguishing a teller's experience from everyone's. Revisit corroboration on 76: compare the teacher's account with the staff member's written account from day 74; check firsthand knowledge before comparing. Tie timeline dates to events on 76; retain unknown dates as unknown, not invented positions. Keep the models, withdrawing filled examples on 79.
