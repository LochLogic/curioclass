## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 48.** Show Africa Map Pair and an unbranded imported classroom object. Ask the compelling question. Record possible answers before explaining borders or trade.
**Supporting questions.** Shape students' questions into: Is a continent a country (48)? What makes a country, and what does government do (49 and 50)? How do two countries differ (51)? Why exchange goods and services (52 and 53)? What do choices and agreements change (54)?
**Closing claim.** Day 55 answers the question using Trade Stories A. Students may emphasize resources, learned skills or cooperation; explain why their evidence supports that emphasis and consider another answer.
**Teaching.** Model map comparison on day 48, sourcing on 51, trade arrows on 52 and sourced reasoning on 54. Step back on 55. Day 49 names all six characteristics with pictures. Ask who made each source, when, where and why; check whether another source supports it. Revisit the question board.

**Look-fors and sample answers.**

- Item 1 requires a feature-based map choice and country-continent distinction. Meets: "The plateau is physical. Borders are political. Botswana is one country in Africa, which contains many countries." Does not: "Africa is a big country; the blue map is physical."
- Item 2 requires country characteristics, a purpose connected to public need and an accurate government contrast. Meets: "Japan has people, land with borders, laws and a government. A fire station helps keep people safe. Japan's elected representatives choose the prime minister; Saudi kings are not chosen by popular vote." Does not: "A flag is enough. Every leader wins a vote." Day 50 notes must show purposes, not simply a building name.
- Item 2b (day 54 revision) requires a supported comparison in all four categories, without treating a country as one uniform culture. Meets: "Edo was renamed Tokyo in 1868; the first Saudi state began at Diriyah in 1727. The cards show puppet theater in Japan and Al-Sadu weaving in Saudi Arabia, examples that do not describe everyone's life. Japanese and Arabic differ. Japan's elected representatives choose its prime minister; Saudi kings inherit rule." Does not: "One country has culture and the other has none." Score only the revised comparison and day 56's fresh trade claim. Day 55's trade claim is practice; for feedback, meets: "I think countries need each other's skills. Trade Stories A shows a visitor paying a guide for knowledge of a place, so buying help connects them." Does not: "Countries are alike because I say so."
- Item 3 requires reasons for goods in both directions and the service. Meets: "Each buyer wants the other workshop's product and skill. The translator knows the language needed for the instructions." Does not: "Only poor countries buy things; services stay at home."
- Item 4 requires both making and selling places with correct direction. Meets: "The cap comes into the United States from Peru. The pump goes out to Peru from the United States." Does not: "The cap is American because an American buys it."
- Item 5 requires a person, a distant place and a causal link through use. Meets: "The child uses a cap that a maker in Peru wove. Sellers and carriers bring that maker's work to the child." Does not: "Peru is far away."
- **Item 6, day 56:** Look for claim, a named source, a reason that links them, and the maker or "unknown" stated. Meets: "They need each other's skills. ## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** No specifications or asks. Supply printed maps, country and trade cards, sourcing cards, unbranded label transcripts, paper and pencils. Every source and task prints; one shared display is optional. One imported classroom object with a readable origin label, or a Curio Class label photograph marked "invented for practice." Nothing is bought or brought from home.
**Core path.** Keep five minutes opening, eight teaching, twelve working and five gathering evidence. Optional ten minutes extend comparisons. Show no timers. At home, the adult replaces partner talk and records the child's own reasons.
**Access and language.** Teach country, continent, border, government, trade, import, export and opportunity cost with pictures. Rehearse "Made in ... , used in ... , so ... ." Adults read captions; children read labels. Accept home-language reasoning. Extend by reversing the trading viewpoint.
