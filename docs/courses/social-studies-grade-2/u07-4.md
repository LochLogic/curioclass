# Section 7.4: Presenting, the check and the response

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


Grade 2 children read short labels and sentences. Every stem is spoken, one ask at a time. Answers are marked, tokened, said, drawn or a few written words. Pip says each wrong idea. Practice places are invented and printed "invented for practice". The held-back Page I and Postcard P are in the unit plan (field 5) and are never shown before day 92. No child reports on home, family, money, religion, body or immigration status; any child may pass; no child must say why they chose a country.

- **D4.3.K-2 (days 91, 92, 93).** "Share a short summary of an argument in print, speech or digital form" (the unit plan: present in print, speech and a digital form). Day 91: the final spoken record and corrections to the digital entry, in a fixed circle order. Day 92: scored item 4 from retained evidence. Day 93: a fresh claim.
- **MA.2.T4.3 (days 91, 92).** "Gather information and present a short research report on the physical features, resources, and people of a country outside the United States." Day 91 presents; day 92 scores item 1 from the retained portfolio.
- **D1.4.K-2 (day 92).** "Link the smaller supporting questions to the big question they help answer." Item 2 on an unseen page.
- **D3.1.K-2 (days 92, 93).** "Gather useful information from one or two sources, using who made them and how they are built to choose." Item 3 on day 92; a fresh source on 93.
- **Limits that bite here.** A claim may differ from another and still be supported. No ranking of countries. A showing is about thirty seconds and no child is the example of a mistake. Day 92 asks nothing about home or family.
- **Connections.** Claim frame (units 1 to 6) and the three questions card (7.1) on 92 and 93. Corroboration is not assessed here.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The fixed circle order with a record column for who was heard; the second adult or older helper for the two circles; how items 1 and 4 are scored from retained work with no sitting minutes; reading Page I and Postcard P aloud neutrally, once; the component-to-move table (unit plan field 6); the four response groups.

## 2. Where it sits


Follows 7.3, whose reports, digital entries and note cards day 91 uses. Unit 7 ends on 93. Next is the course-level check on day 94 (two accounts of one event), which draws on the source-comparison and claim work, not on a country's facts. Results go on the record sheet; scores are kept apart by component.

## 3. Checks, written first


Unit assessment items owned by last teaching: item 1 (MA.2.T4.3) and item 4 (D4.3.K-2), both from retained evidence. Items 2 and 3 are sat on day 92 (items 2 and 3 are in 7.3's and 7.2's tables). Scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen, for every part and every medium.

**Sitting, day 92.** Opening 5 minutes: the teacher reads each source strip, Page I and Postcard P aloud once, then each step one ask at a time. 20 minutes of individual work on a printed sheet. Item 2 (about 8 minutes): underline two facts on Page I; put a token on the supporting-question card each fact answers; say or write one line, "How could those answers help us decide what matters about life in Iceland?" Item 3 (about 8 minutes): circle Page I or Postcard P for the work question; point to who made it and to one helping part; underline two facts. About 4 minutes spare for a dictated line. A child who cannot mark says the line to the teacher, at most 4 children at about 1 minute each while the rest work, named on the sheet. Reflection 5 minutes: mark the picture of the part the child wants to revisit; not scored. Items 1 and 4 need no sitting minutes. The fresh transfer for item 4 is a short claim about Iceland written or dictated as the item 2 line, with no digital turn.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks once in the last 3 minutes. Day 92 has no exit ticket; the reflection mark stands. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E91a (day 91).** Mark, three pictured listener questions after a showing: "Where did that fact come from?"; "What color is your paper?"; "How long was your talk?" "Put a token on the question that helps make a report stronger." Answer: "Where did that fact come from?" Wrong: either other question (H). The taught question on 90 and 91 is "Which page says that?"; the exit words it differently so the exit is a transfer.
- **E91b (day 91, six).** Spoken: "Say how that question helps the report." Answer: it shows where the fact came from, so a reader can check it. Wrong: "it asks about the paper" (H).
- **E93a (day 93).** Mark, a fresh invented source, "invented for practice": a garden record from a school garden club, "People grow pears here. A weekly stall sells the pears." Two parts: a source line, "Made by the Garden Club, 2026", and a flower picture. "Put a token on the part that tells who made the record." Answer: the source line. Wrong: the flower picture (B).
- **E93b (day 93, six).** Spoken: "Say a claim about what matters in this place, with two facts." Answer: a claim such as "Growing food matters here because people grow pears and a stall sells them." Wrong: one fact or none, "People grow pears" (D).

- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E91a.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 91: "Which page says that?" (the listener's). Day 93: "What claim can you make, and which two facts support it?"
- **Written task, day 92.** The one-line answer to "How could those answers help us decide what matters?" in a few written words or dictated.
- **Judged by the teacher with 3 look-fors.** Two facts from the page; each linked to a different supporting question; the line says how both help the big question.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Cards: claim, fact, source, maker, because, listener, question. Frames: "I think ___ because ___." and "The ___ says ___." Bilingual rehearsal allowed on 91 and the reason may be said in any language the teacher can record.
- **Access.** A scribe; pointing; large print; a helper reads the sheet; a child may direct an adult to enter a digital summary and the support level stays visible.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts, captions and source strips aloud without selecting evidence; large print; image descriptions; the first language for the reason.
- **Reading load.** About 60 words a child reads on 92 (Page I and Postcard P labels with the stems); the teacher reads everything aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask what evidence could weaken the claim.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Say the claim and two facts aloud to a grown-up, who asks "Which page says that?" On day 92, do the sheet together with the stems read aloud; on day 93, try a fresh record. Any day may be skipped.
- **One projector.** One circle only, run in order, the display shown on the screen; extra children show on day 90 as the unit plan says.
