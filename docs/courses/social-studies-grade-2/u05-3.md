# Section 5.3: The immigrant phrase, and what newcomers said

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-2/5/s/5.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-2 u05-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption and story aloud. Answers are circled, boxed, pointed to, sorted, said or a few written words. Days run 35 minutes. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time; one actor in each story scene. Kit, Lark, Moss, Pell, Finn, Kai and every child in a story card are invented and printed "invented for practice"; the teacher says so aloud. Real people (Anna, Catherine, Mock Ging Sing, Rahaf) appear only as Curio Class summaries in our words with one credit line each; no transcript line is quoted and no translated poem is copied. Real nations are named by their own names and live in the present tense. Every real fact is marked "source to check" until its page is opened in full (this course has no sources.csv yet). No child is asked where they or their family came from, how, or under what status; any child may pass.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.2.T3.2, this section's share (days 63, 64, 65, 67).** "Give examples of why the United States is called 'a nation of immigrants.'" Day 63: examples of families arriving from other countries, at many times and still today. Day 64: the honest limits of the phrase. Days 65 and 67: what newcomers said about arriving.
- **D2.His.4.K-2 (days 64, 65, 66, 67).** "Compare the views of people in the past with the views of people today." A view is what a person hoped, wanted or felt, in their own telling. Day 64: the government's reason and the nation's wish; days 65 and 66: past voices; day 67: past beside today.
- **Limits that bite here.** No questions about status, and no policy debate. No claim that every newcomer passed through Ellis Island or Angel Island, or that one voice speaks for a group. Recent accounts are dated and are not what a person thinks today. Native nations are not immigrants and are living nations. No child reports their own origins.
- **Connections.** Grade 1: citizens and diverse (day 63, retrieval), Lenape towns and nations today (day 64). 5.2: Smith Life and Cherokee Today (day 64). Unit 2: changed, stayed the same (day 67). Kindergarten: "Both ___, but ___" (day 67). Grade 1: the timeline of dated cards (day 65). Unit 1: the three questions, now with who made it and when told (days 65 to 67).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** An immigrant is a person who comes from another country to live; a visitor goes home. The phrase is true of many families and says nothing of those whose arrival was not a free choice or who were here first. Anna's page names her country by its later name; in 1920 it was the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes, called Yugoslavia from 1929 (source to check). A memory is told long after the event: the event date and the interview date are different. A law kept most Chinese people out; Angel Island held some newcomers, most of them Chinese, for weeks, months, and for some up to two years (source to check: the Angel Island Immigration Station Foundation pages; Chinese American historians). A Curio Class summary is not a quotation.

**Unit assessment items owned:** item 5 (MA.2.T3.2, Example Cards) and item 6 (D2.His.4.K-2, Antin A beside the Present Card), both sat on day 70. Day 63's sort and day 67's comparison prepare them; none of their cards or cases appears on day 70.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks once in the last 3 minutes; the teacher reads each ask aloud, one ask at a time. The rotating six (about 45 seconds each, one spoken ask) are heard in the pairs work. Every case is new (rule 4).
- **X63 (day 63).** Mark, paper: Invented for practice. Wei came from China to join an uncle in the United States in 1990. Circle the word for Wei: immigrant or Native. Answer: Immigrant. Wrong: Native is circled (D).
- **X63b (day 63).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. Wei came from China to join an uncle in the United States in 1990. Say how Wei's card helps show why this country is called a nation of immigrants. Answer: It is one example of a person coming from another country to live here. Wrong: It shows everyone came like Wei (J).
- **X64 (day 64).** Mark, paper: On the Keetoowah Card, the United Keetoowah Band says it is a sovereign nation headquartered in Tahlequah, Oklahoma. On the Removal Caption, from 1837 to 1839 the United States government removed thousands of Cherokee people from their homelands in the Appalachian Mountains. Did Cherokee people become immigrants by being forced west? Circle yes or no. Answer: No. Wrong: Yes is circled (D).
- **X64b (day 64).** Spoken, the six: Enslaved African families were brought to the Americas by force. Say whether the phrase a nation of immigrants describes how they came. Answer: No. They were brought by force; they did not choose to come. Wrong: Yes, they immigrated (J).
- **X64c (day 64).** Spoken, the six: The Keetoowah Card is dated November 2026. Is the United Keetoowah Band in the past, or here today? Answer: Here today. The dated card says it is a sovereign nation. Wrong: Only in the past (M).
- **X65 (day 65).** Mark, paper: On the Cookie Card, Anna remembers being so hungry for cookies that she took four big cookies the next morning. Her mother told her not to be embarrassed and to eat all she wanted. Anna came to America in 1920. Circle what Anna wanted in her memory: cookies, a hat, a boat. Then put an A on the timeline at the year she came. Answer: Cookies; the A at 1920. Wrong: A boat is circled (E); A hat is circled (K); the A at 1991, the telling taken for the move.
- **X65b (day 65).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. On the Newcomer Card, Zeb is new in town and has a cousin who lives there. Zeb says, I hope my cousin will be my friend here. Say what Zeb hopes for. Answer: That his cousin will be his friend. Wrong: He came by plane (E); The same as Anna (L).
- **X66 (day 66).** Mark, paper: Invented for practice. On the Lin Card, Lin hoped to find work. A law was made against Chinese workers coming, so officers held Lin on the island for months. Circle why Lin was held: a law, a storm, Lin's wish. Answer: A law. Wrong: Lin's wish is circled (N).
- **X66b (day 66).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. Lin hoped to find work. A new girl hopes to join her mother. Say how their hopes are alike. Answer: Both hoped coming would bring something good: work for Lin, her mother for the girl. Any likeness that rests on the two hopes. Wrong: Lin chose to be held (N); Lin came by ship and she came by plane (E).
- **X67 (day 67).** Mark, paper: On the two Arrival Voices paragraphs, circle the paragraph that tells what Catherine expected before she came. Answer: Paragraph 1. Wrong: Paragraph 2 is circled (L).
- **X67b (day 67).** Spoken, the six: Say what Rahaf said in her 2018 interview about going back to Syria. Answer: She would probably go back to visit, but not to live there again. Wrong: She expected the city to be beautiful and clean (L); She came by plane (E).
- **X67c (day 67).** Spoken, the six: Say one way that what Catherine expected and what Rahaf said are alike or different. Answer: A supported likeness or difference, such as Catherine looked ahead to a new place and Rahaf looked back at her home; not a claim about every newcomer. Wrong: Ships then, planes now (E); They felt the same (L).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The phrase "a nation of immigrants", tied on day 63 to examples, then to its limits on day 64. Perspective, new on day 65: what a person hoped, wanted or felt, in their own telling, with who made it and when; revised on day 67 beside a recent account. Continuity and change, new on day 67 as "alike and different" for a newcomer long ago and one told more recently (not a claim about all newcomers). The timeline of dated cards, review on day 65. The three questions on a card, with a new line: when was it told. The chain from 5.1 returns on day 66 with one added box, the law.
- **Key moment 1, day 65: the dated strip.** Ideal instrument: a wall strip on which a child places dated cards. Spec: none (Tools: None); a taped paper strip and cards fit.
- **Key moment 2, day 67: the alike-and-different chart.** Ideal instrument: a two-column chart a pair fills together. Spec: none; a paper chart fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed cards, maps, strip, charts, pencils, two colors of crayon. Tools: None.

## 6. Days

## Day 63


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The letter most missed from day 62 (C or I): a trip planned freely beside a family told they cannot stay, or a fresh origin-to-destination arrow followed with a finger. The other goes to a small group.
- **3 min. Retrieval, grade 1.** The unit's retrieval task, spoken in pairs and scanned by the teacher: "Can someone belong to our community without being a citizen? How can people share a community and have different languages or traditions?" No classmate is named. A miss is repaired in day 64's Return and repair.
- **8 min. Two examples.** Anna's move (Europe to North America; day 58's summary again, with its credit line) and Catherine (a Curio Class summary, Poland to New York in 1920, credited to her National Park Service oral history; no checked free page for a second continent was found, so the teacher says families came from many places and the invented cards show other continents; not Lau, Rahaf or Smith). Terms: immigrant (a person who comes from another country to live), visitor (goes home). Each card gets the three questions and an arrow.
- **6 min. The phrase.** "Nation" is grade 1's (a people with their own government). The phrase "a nation of immigrants": many families here have a story of coming from another country, at many times, and families arrive today. One family is an example; the phrase is about many. An invented card of a family who arrived last month shows "still".
- **9 min. Pairs work.** Each pair sorts six invented cards under immigrant, visitor and born here (Kit, from Africa, comes to study and stays; Lark, from South America, arrived last year to live; Moss, from Europe, came long ago; Wren, from Asia, visits for a week and goes home; Finn was born here; and the family that arrived last month). The six are heard (about 4.5 minutes). A small group of at most 6 meets the teacher for a retrieval miss or a repair letter.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** X63; X63b was heard in the pairs work.
- **1 min. Question board.** "Whose history does the phrase describe?" is added.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The six sorting cards share X63's shape, one word for a person. Changed so they do not copy it: practice uses Kit, Lark, Moss, Wren, Finn and a family who arrived last month; Wei and China appear on the exit only. ## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with a picture or map: immigrant, visitor, nation, homeland, hoped, felt, alike, different. Frames: "An immigrant ___; a visitor ___." "The phrase describes ___; it does not describe ___." "Both ___, but ___." Home-language rehearsal allowed.
- **Access.** Raised or taped arrows; two colors plus a symbol for underlining; described pictures; large print; any child may point, draw or dictate.
- **Check accommodations.** The ask is repeated aloud; a drawn, boxed or pointed answer is a mark; a child who passes in public answers to the teacher alone.
- **Reading load.** Labels only (about 60 words a day); the teacher reads every card (about 220 words a day).
- **Extension, same standard.** "What other voice would show a different view of the same arrival, and where could we find it?"
