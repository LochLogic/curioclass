## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


Grade 2 children read short labels and sentences. Claims are said first, then recorded with a frame, with transcription support only. Every stem is spoken, one ask at a time. Pip says each wrong idea. Japan is the model; the Japan claim uses only the hook fact (islands, seas around them, mountains over much of the land; source to check, the foreign ministry page read 14 November 2026) plus a hedged reason marked as thinking, not fact. Practice places are invented and printed "invented for practice". No child reports on home, family, money, religion, body or immigration status; any child may pass.

- **D4.3.K-2 (days 88 to 90).** "Share a short summary of an argument in print, speech or digital form" (the unit plan's line: present the main argument in print, speech and a digital form). Print on 89 and 90; speech practiced on 90 and scored on 91; digital on 89 and 90 in a rotation, about 15 children a day.
- **D1.4.K-2 (day 88).** "Link the smaller supporting questions to the big question they help answer." The claim answers the big question and each fact names the supporting question it answers.
- **MA.2.T4.3 (day 89).** "Gather information and present a short research report on the physical features, resources, and people of a country." Day 89 drafts the report from the note page: map, three parts, claim card.
- **Limits that bite here.** Different claims can fit the evidence; none is ranked. A claim names two facts from named sources and says how they help. No country is the "best". The display is one offline device run by an adult, no account, no network, no upload; a child directs it and is never required to type.
- **Connections.** The claim frame "I think ___ because ___. The ___ shows ___, made by ___" (units 1 to 6), extended with two facts on 88. Map key on the report's map, 89. Lean W.2.7 (ELA unit 3, already taught), 89.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Claim versus list of facts; how two facts and a "because" make an argument; the hedged Japan reason; the digital entry is the child's own words directed to an adult; how to give written feedback (a one-line note card per child, about 30 minutes after class on 89 and on 90); a child with no device turn is recorded not yet seen.

## 2. Where it sits


Follows 7.2, whose note pages and returned source-choice feedback day 89 uses. Precedes 7.4, where reports are shown. Retrieval of grade 1 reasoned claims on 88 is a record-sheet note. Written source-choice feedback is revised in day 89's first 4 working minutes; report feedback from 89 is revised on 90; the digital note card from 90 is used on 91.

## 3. Checks, written first


Unit assessment item owned: item 2 (D1.4.K-2, on the unit plan). Item 4 (D4.3.K-2) is built here and scored on 92 from retained evidence (in 7.4's table). Scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A child's mark is recorded; the reason is heard on 88 only (on 89 and 90 the rotation takes the teacher's time, so the paper mark and the draft are the evidence).

**Exit questions.** Each child marks once in the last 3 minutes. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E88a (day 88).** Mark, two cards about the invented Alder Ford river barge, "invented for practice": three fact cards from the Alder Ford page, "Boats carry vegetables to town.", "Children fish in the river for supper." and "The river is long." "Finish the claim with two facts that answer the question: The river matters for living in Alder Ford because ___ and ___." Answer: the vegetables and fishing facts. Wrong: facts with no because, or the long river (D).
- **E88b (day 88, six).** Spoken: "Say how your claim helps us learn what matters about living there." Answer: boats carrying vegetables means the river helps people get food. Wrong: repeats "boats carry vegetables" (D).
- **E89a (day 89).** Mark, a new invented fact card, "The lake gives people water.", with its source label "Lake Page, made by Curio Class", and one empty report part box. "Fill the part box: write the fact, then write its page name in the label slot." Answer: the fact in the box, Lake Page in the slot. Wrong: a blank slot or a border (H). The child's draft is collected as additional evidence.
- **E90a (day 90).** Mark, two display panels, "invented for practice": one shows only a country name and a drawing; the other shows a claim and two facts with labels. "Put a token on the panel that tells what matters and why." Answer: the second panel. Wrong: the first panel (D).
- **Digital record, days 89 and 90.** Each child's own one-minute turn, three asks: E89b or E90b, say the claim for the adult to enter; E89c or E90c, say the two facts; E89d or E90d, point to the entry on the screen and say if it is right. Record shown, shown with help (the adult supplied words) or not yet seen. A child who only watched is not yet seen (F).

## 6. Days

## Day 88


- **3 min. Retrieval.** "Say what you think and why" (grade 1 reasoned claims). A miss: add "because" and name the source. No score.
- **6 min. A claim, Japan.** The teacher says and records (cards-japan-claim-day-88.md): "I think the sea matters for how people live in Japan because Japan is islands with sea all around. The Japan page shows islands, seas and mountains, made by Curio Class. So people there may live and work near the sea" (the last line is thinking, said aloud as thinking). Two facts, the supporting question each answers (both land and water here), and the link to the big question. Pip says D.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** E from day 87: replace "everyone" in two fresh captions with what the page supports (cards-repairs-86-88.md); any other letter goes to a small group of at most 6 during the pairs.
- **17 min. Claims by pairs.** Each child picks two facts from their note page that answer different supporting questions, says a claim to a partner, then records it with the frame (blank-claim-frame.md), with transcription support only. The teacher hears the rotating six, 4.5 minutes, in this part.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E88a; E88b was heard in the pairs.
- **1 min. Board.** "Which answers best support our claim?" is answered aloud once.

Total 35. Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the Japan claim, each child holds a frame and two facts. Tool: None. By hand: saying, recording. Evidence: E88a, E88b; the claim is a draft, scored through day 92 item 4.

**Shared shape (note, not timed).**
- The Japan claim and the pairs claims share their shape with E88a and E88b (a claim, not the bare fact; say how it answers). The exit's Alder Ford barge appears on no card.

## Day 89


- **3 min. Return and repair.** D, one repair with a fresh card (cards-repairs-89-91.md).
- **4 min. Revise.** The written feedback from 87 returns (blank-feedback-note.md); each child revises one source choice on the note page. Both versions kept; only the revision is scored.
- **6 min. Teaching, the display.** With Japan, the teacher directs an adult to enter a claim and two facts into the offline display (blank-display-sheet.md) and checks the screen. Pip says nothing new; F is met on 90.

**Shared shape (note, not timed).**

## Day 90


- **3 min. Return and repair.** H, with fresh unlabeled facts (cards-repairs-89-91.md); any other letter to a small group.
- **5 min. Teaching, presenting.** With Japan (cards-listener-japan-day-90.md): show the map, say the claim, say two facts and name their sources; a listener asks "Which page says that?" Pip says F: "I watched a screen."
- **7 min. Practice in pairs.** Each child says their claim and two facts to a partner, then the partner asks one question; the pair changes places. The day's 15 children with a display turn go to the device in turn, one minute each, run by an adult.

Total 35. Who holds what is carried: each child holds the report and the feedback note. Tool: one offline display (light), enter a claim, one minute a child. By hand: revising, speaking. Evidence: E90a and the digital record (E90b to E90d).

**Shared shape (note, not timed).**
- The pairs practice and the Japan presenting share their shape with E90b to E90d and the day 91 showing (say the claim, two facts and sources); they use the Japan and the children's own claims, never the exit's two panels.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 88: "How does that answer what matters about living there?" Day 90: "Which page says that?" and "What would you change after hearing that?"
- **Written task, day 89.** The report page: the map with its key, a fact and sticker in each of three boxes, and the claim card with two facts and a "because". Dictation allowed.
- **Judged by the teacher with 4 look-fors.** The claim answers the big question; two facts come from named sources; the "because" links them to the claim; all three report parts are present.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 88.** Grade 1 reasoned claims; the claim frame (units 1 to 6); the "some, many, not everyone" wording from 87.
- **Day 89.** The note pages; source-choice revision; the question board.
- **Day 90.** H repair; the three questions card; presenting in pairs.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Cards: claim, fact, source, because, matters, report, summary. Frames: "I think ___ because ___." and "The ___ shows ___, made by ___." Bilingual rehearsal allowed.
- **Access.** Dictation to the adult at the display; large print frames; a pointing option; a pre-cut claim card.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a point; the reason may be said in any language the teacher can record.
- **Reading load.** About 60 words a child reads alone (the frame, labels, own notes); the teacher reads the Japan claim and every stem aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask what evidence would weaken the claim.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Pick two facts, say a claim with "because", record it on the frame. Show it to the grown-up on a device or on paper. Any day may be skipped.
- **One projector.** Show the Japan claim and the display on the screen; one device serves the rotation.
