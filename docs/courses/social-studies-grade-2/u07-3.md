Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Write together: What land and water are here (85)? What resources support people's work (86)? How do people live today, and what varies (87)? Which answers best support our claim (88)?
> **Closing claim, day 89.** Each child answers the compelling question using their country's Map, Land and Work page, and People Today page, naming two supporting facts and explaining the connection. Revise on 90; present on 91. Different claims can fit the evidence.
> **Teaching.** Model question connections on 83, source selection on 84 and claim writing on 88, using Japan. Step back during gathering and drafting. Teach before observing; retain prompts but remove completed examples on 89.
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 6's sourcing card and claim frame. Revisit map-key and region on 85; tie map symbols to notes. Revisit opportunity-cost on 86 with an invented trade choice, without a new assessment. Tie claim-evidence-reasoning to source labels on 88; withdraw filled examples on 89, retaining the frame only where needed. Other models remain available.

Grade 2 children read short labels and sentences. Claims are said first, then recorded with a frame, with transcription support only. Every stem is spoken, one ask at a time. Pip says each wrong idea. Japan is the model; the Japan claim uses only the hook fact (islands, seas around them, mountains over much of the land; source to check, the foreign ministry page read 14 November 2026) plus a hedged reason marked as thinking, not fact. Practice places are invented and printed "invented for practice". No child reports on home, family, money, religion, body or immigration status; any child may pass.

- **D4.3.K-2 (days 88 to 90).** "Share a short summary of an argument in print, speech or digital form" (the unit plan's line: present the main argument in print, speech and a digital form). Print on 89 and 90; speech practised on 90 and scored on 91; digital on 89 and 90 in a rotation, about 15 children a day.
- **D1.4.K-2 (day 88).** "Link the smaller supporting questions to the big question they help answer." The claim answers the big question and each fact names the supporting question it answers.
- **MA.2.T4.3 (day 89).** "Gather information and present a short research report on the physical features, resources, and people of a country." Day 89 drafts the report from the note page: map, three parts, claim card.
- **Limits that bite here.** Different claims can fit the evidence; none is ranked. A claim names two facts from named sources and says how they help. No country is the "best". The display is one offline device run by an adult, no account, no network, no upload; a child directs it and is never required to type.
- **Connections.** The claim frame "I think ___ because ___. The ___ shows ___, made by ___" (units 1 to 6), extended with two facts on 88. Map key on the report's map, 89. Lean W.2.7 (ELA unit 3, already taught), 89.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Claim versus list of facts; how two facts and a "because" make an argument; the hedged Japan reason; the digital entry is the child's own words directed to an adult; how to give written feedback (a one-line note card per child, about 30 minutes after class on 89 and on 90); a child with no device turn is recorded not yet seen.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 88: "How does that answer what matters about living there?" Day 90: "Which page says that?" and "What would you change after hearing that?"
- **Written task, day 89.** The report page: the map with its key, a fact and sticker in each of three boxes, and the claim card with two facts and a "because". Dictation allowed.
- **Judged by the teacher with 4 look-fors.** The claim answers the big question; two facts come from named sources; the "because" links them to the claim; all three report parts are present.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Cards: claim, fact, source, because, matters, report, summary. Frames: "I think ___ because ___." and "The ___ shows ___, made by ___." Bilingual rehearsal allowed.
- **Access.** Dictation to the adult at the display; large print frames; a pointing option; a pre-cut claim card.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud; accept a point; the reason may be said in any language the teacher can record.
- **Reading load.** About 60 words a child reads alone (the frame, labels, own notes); the teacher reads the Japan claim and every stem aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask what evidence would weaken the claim.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Pick two facts, say a claim with "because", record it on the frame. Show it to the grown-up on a device or on paper. Any day may be skipped.
- **One projector.** Show the Japan claim and the display on the screen; one device serves the rotation.
