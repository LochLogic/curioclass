# Section 1.2: Who made it, and what can we check?


Grade 2 children read short labels and credit lines; the teacher reads every caption, credit and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, placed as a token or a few written words. Each day runs 35 minutes. Photograph cards read "Photograph by Curio Class, 2026, made in a school room" (or hallway) and show no identifying child. Every other card is invented and prints "invented for practice". Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.His.11.K-2, this section's share (days 6 and 8).** "Identify who made a source, when and where, using clues in the source itself." Three questions on a card, answered from the source's own words: who made it, when, where it was made. The place it was made is kept apart from the place it shows, and from the library or archive that keeps it. "The record does not say" is a full answer. Unit assessment item 5 (the Vermont map) is taught here and asked on day 15; the skeleton lists it under section 1.3 as well, which meets it again on day 11.
- **D3.2.K-2, this section's share (days 7 and 8).** "Judge a source by telling fact from opinion." A fact is something we can check; an opinion is what someone thinks. A source with an opinion in it can still be used for what can be checked. Unit assessment item 6 (the Willow Garden caption) is owned here.
- **Limits that bite here.** Sources are maps, photograph cards and their captions only. No child sources anything from home. A credit is read as printed; nothing is guessed. No real person is judged. A real source's maker, date and place are read off the full image before the card is laid out, and the archive's description is kept apart from the printed words (unit plan field 5). The Vermont map and the Willow Garden caption are held back for day 15.
- **Connections.** RI.2.6 bridge, day 6 (ELA unit 2 comes later): "What is this map for?" is asked of a map and a captioned photograph, with the teacher modeling. Day 8's old map reuses the title and key of section 1.1 (MA.2.T1.1).
- **Review, not new.** "Who made it, when and why (and 'the record does not say' when it does not)" (kindergarten u04-2, grade 1 u02-2); source, photograph, old and new; fact ("something we can find out", grade 1 u01-1); claim and evidence (grade 1 u06-2). This grade's card swaps "why" for "where made", and keeps "why" as the bridge question "What is this map for?"
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Three different places can be named on one source: where it was made (origin), what it shows, and who keeps it. A maker can be a person, a firm or a group. An old map is evidence of what its maker knew and chose, not a photograph of the place. "Checkable" may mean checkable on this source or by looking somewhere else. An opinion tells us what someone thinks; it does not make the source useless.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 6 (D3.2.K-2: fact and opinion in the Willow Garden caption) and, taught here, item 5 (D2.His.11.K-2: maker, date and place on the Vermont map); both asked on day 15. Nothing taught on days 6 to 8 uses those cards.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks; the teacher reads each prompt aloud. The day's rotating six (the next six not yet heard on the record sheet) are heard after the mark; where a spoken part has two asks, they are two stems asked one after the other and recorded apart. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E6 (day 6).** Mark: reserved Room Photograph A, an empty classroom made in a school doorway, with its maker, date and making place printed. "Circle the date." Spoken, two stems: "Point to who made it." "Point to where it was made." Answer: the printed maker, and the printed making place (the credit line, not the picture). Wrong: points to the library or archive that keeps it (E); says a name or place that is not printed (M); points to the room in the picture as the place it was made, not the credit (J).
- **E7 (day 7).** Mark: a fresh photograph card of a boot tray with four boots and two printed statements, "Four boots sit on the mat." and "These boots are the coolest." Place the token "I can check this" on the picture on the statement that can be checked. Spoken: "What can this source help us find?" Answer: the token on "Four boots sit on the mat"; anything the picture shows that can be counted or seen, such as how many boots, not what everyone thinks is coolest. Wrong: the check token on both statements (F); says the picture cannot help because it has an opinion on it (L).
- **E8 (day 8).** Mark, two marks on one new card (a window and door photograph made in a school lobby, with its photographer, date and making place printed; two statements, "A window sits beside a door." and "This is our best room."): circle who made it; place the check token on the statement that can be checked. Spoken: "Point to where it was made." Answer: the printed maker; the window statement; the printed making place. Wrong: circles the archive or card holder as maker (E); names an unprinted place (M); points to the picture, not the credit, for the place made (J); puts the check token on both statements (F).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The three questions on a card (who made it, when, where) are introduced on day 6 with the teacher modeling, used by pairs on day 7's warm-up, and used independently on day 8; from here every source in the year gets the card. The four-part map card from section 1.1 stays on the wall; on day 8 the old map is read for its title and key as well. The question board gets "Who made this source, and what can we check?" on day 8. Teacher modeling is withdrawn by day 8; the card stays.
- **Key moment 1, day 6: the credit read from the source itself.** Ideal instrument: the full-size image with its margin enlarged, and a printed transcript of the words as printed. Spec: print fits; a projector helps with the margin. Gaps and asks: none; there is no tool and none is spec only.
- **Key moment 2, day 8: two credits sorted.** Ideal instrument: credit cards sorted by hand. Spec: fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Copy the credit line onto paper in large print; the three questions go on a folded card; a photograph is a drawing with a printed credit line.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 6: "Who made it? When? Where?" and "What is this map for?" Day 7: "Which can we check, and which is what someone thinks?" Day 8: "What is this source good for, and what can it not tell us?"
- **Written task, day 8.** On the source card, copy the maker's name as printed, or write "not printed", and the date as printed. A child who cannot copy the word points to it and says it to the teacher.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) the words copied come from the credit line, not from the picture; (2) "not printed" is written where the credit is missing, and nothing is guessed; (3) the maker, the place made and the place shown are kept apart.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 6.** Grade 1's old map as a source (check item 3), day 5's land use. Day 7: the three questions on a fresh card. Day 8: the key and title from section 1.1; day 6's credit sort; day 7's tokens.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words, taught with a picture: maker, date, place, made, keeps, fact, opinion, check. Frames: "It was made by ___." "It was made in ___." "I can check ___ on the picture." "This is what someone thinks." Home-language answers first; the credit line is read aloud.
- **Access.** Enlarge every credit; a child who cannot see the card has the credit read aloud and the photograph described neutrally; a partner may move tokens.
- **Check accommodations.** Prompts read aloud; point, circle, token or scribe; one stem at a time. Record the support used.
- **Reading load.** Credit lines and statements are one short line; the teacher reads every one aloud; at most 150 words a student reads in a day.
- **Extension, same standard.** A secure child checks one more statement on a card and says what else could be used to check it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 6: look at one printed credit and ask who made it, when and where; ask where the picture shows and where it was made. Day 7: write two statements about one photograph and sort them with two pieces of paper. Day 8: repeat with a second source and write the maker's name. A toy says the wrong idea.
- **One projector.** Show the margin and credit enlarged; children still circle and place tokens on their own card.
