# Section 5.2: Forced moves, then and now



Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption and story aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, sorted, drawn, said or a few written words. Days run 35 minutes. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time; one actor in each story scene. Bel and every other child in a story card are invented and printed "invented for practice". Real people appear only as Curio Class summaries in our words with one credit line each; real nations are named by their own names, in the present tense where they live on. No role play, no acting out, no pictures of chains or of a ship's hold: maps and words only. Every real fact is marked "source to check" until its page is opened in full (this course has no sources.csv yet). No child is asked about their own family, where they came from or how; any child may pass, and a quiet drawing task is always open.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.2.T3.1, this section's share (days 60, 61, 62).** "Investigate why people move to new places around the world, recognizing that some migration is chosen and some is forced." Three forced moves, told plainly: Africans captured, taken across the ocean and enslaved (day 60); the Cherokee and other Native nations forced from their homelands by the United States government (day 61); refugees fleeing war today (day 62). Each is asked with 5.1's test, "Could they have stayed?"
- **D2.His.14.K-2 (days 60, 61, 62).** "Suggest possible reasons for a past event or development." Here: who decided, and what they wanted, kept apart from what the moved people wanted.
- **D2.His.16.K-2 (day 62).** "Pick which reasons are more likely than others to explain a past event." For each move studied, the reason a source line supports.
- **D2.Geo.7.K-2 (day 62).** "Explain why and how people, goods and ideas travel from place to place." People only; goods are unit 4, ideas unit 6.
- **Limits that bite here.** No role play or simulation of capture, removal or flight. No graphic passages; Smith's birthplace is given only as Africa. Say "enslaved people" and "enslavers", never "slaves" or "owners". Native nations are named specifically and are living nations. Refugees are people, not a nationality; no child is asked whether they or their family are refugees. No legal status or policy debate. Hard history is not ranked: no "worst".
- **Connections.** Grade 1: nation, and the Cherokee Nation's own pages (day 61). Unit 2: the continents (day 62). Unit 4: the arrow (day 62). 5.1: the chain and the test question (all three days). Unit 2's claim frame with a named card (day 60, K).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Enslavers' motives and captive people's wishes are different: the card says who decided and what each wanted (source to check: Jeffries's Teaching Hard History K-5 framework, the unit plan's read). The lives enslaved people built, families, songs and stories, skilled work, resistance, are part of the history, not an afterthought. Removal was a decision of the United States government, and the Cherokee people did not choose to leave (the Cherokee Nation's own pages; source to check). Three Cherokee governments exist today, named on day 61. Refugee means a person who had to leave their country because staying was not safe (UNHCR age 6 to 9 guidance; source to check). Choosing a route does not make flight chosen.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks once in the last 3 minutes; the teacher reads each ask aloud, one ask at a time. The rotating six (about 45 seconds each, one spoken ask) are heard in the pairs work. Every case is new (rule 4).
- **X60 (day 60).** Mark, paper: On the Smith Life later-life card, Smith bought his freedom, then owned land by a river. Circle the picture of Smith's life after he was free: owned land by a river, sailed for fun, lived in a palace. Answer: Owned land by a river. Wrong: Sailed for fun is circled (K); Lived in a palace is circled (K).
- **X60b (day 60).** Spoken, the six: Smith had to work for the men who held him. Whose choice was that, Smith's or the enslavers'? Answer: The enslavers' choice. Wrong: Smith chose it (C).
- **X61 (day 61).** Mark, paper: On the Seminole Card, officers of the United States government told Seminole families to leave their homes in the Southeast, and the families were made to go west. Circle chosen or forced. Answer: Forced. Wrong: Chosen is circled (C).
- **X61b (day 61).** Spoken, the six: The Seminole families were forced west. Is the Seminole Nation gone, or here today? Answer: Here today. Wrong: The Seminole Nation is gone (M).
- **X62 (day 62).** Mark, paper: Invented for practice. On the Route Card, a family fled Syria to Jordan, then flew to Luxembourg. On the world map, draw the route. Answer: An arrow from Syria to Jordan, then an arrow from Jordan to Luxembourg. Wrong: The arrows run from Luxembourg back to Syria (I); An arrow runs from Syria to Luxembourg only (I).
- **X62b (day 62).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. On the Route Card, say why the family left Syria. Answer: War made staying unsafe. Wrong: They went to see Luxembourg (A); They chose to go (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The chain "Because ___, they moved ___. Afterward ___." is filled by the teacher on day 60, half filled by pairs on day 61, and a blank strip on day 62: filled examples withdrawn. The test "Could they have stayed?" and "Who decided?" on every day. Unit 2's claim frame with a named card: "The ___ card says ___." The arrow and the three questions on a card, kept on the wall. The question board.
- **Key moment 1, day 60: whose wish.** Ideal instrument: statement cards a child drags into "Smith decided" and "others decided". Spec: none (Tools: None); paper cards and two sheets fit.
- **Key moment 2, day 62: a route traced between continents.** Ideal instrument: a world map on a screen where a child draws the path. Spec: none; a printed world map and a pencil fit.
- **A room with nothing.** Printed cards, maps, the strip, pencils. Tools: None.

## 6. Days

## Day 60


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The letter most missed from day 59 (B, C or K): for B, reorder a fresh reason, move and afterward chain; the other letters go to a small group in the pairs work.
- **3 min. The source.** Smith Life, a Curio Class summary in our words of Venture Smith's own account, printed in 1798 (who wrote it down, and the paragraphs used, are read off the PDF's title page at plan data). The three questions on a card: who made it, when, where. Africa and North America are found on the world map (review of unit 2).
- **9 min. Smith Life, told once, calmly.** Three short paragraphs (listed at layout; paragraph 4 is held for the exit). One: Smith was a child; men captured him and took him from his home and family in Africa; they put him on a ship and he was brought across the ocean to the Americas; men took him by force (said once, no picture). Two: other people said he belonged to them; they are called enslavers. Three: he worked and saved and bought his own freedom. It ends on how he lived on. No acting; any child may draw instead.
- **4 min. Whose wish.** On the chain strip: because the men wanted him to work for them, they took him; afterward he was enslaved. Two columns: what the enslavers wanted, what Smith wanted (to go home, to be free). Pip says C.
- **4 min. The lives they built.** A card in our words that rests only on Smith's own account (the wider lives enslaved people built are not on a page read for this unit and stay out): enslaved people were people with families and skills; Smith worked, saved and bought freedom for himself and later for his wife and children. The class does not rank hardships.
- **7 min. Pairs work.** Each pair sorts six statement cards into "Smith decided" and "others decided", then points to the line of the Smith Life card for one: "The Smith Life card says ___." The six are heard (about 4.5 minutes). A small group of at most 6 meets the teacher for a repair letter.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** X60; X60b was heard in the pairs work. The board is read: "Who could stay, and who could not?"

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The six statement cards share X60b's shape, whose choice it was. Changed so they do not copy it: no statement says Smith worked for the men who held him; the later-life paragraph is on the exit card only.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the filled chain; each pair holds its statement cards. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, circling. Evidence: X60, X60b.

## Day 61


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The letter most missed from day 60 (C, or K if more missed): a trip a family plans freely beside a family told they cannot stay; or a fresh card with the supporting line pointed to. The other goes to a small group.
- **3 min. Retrieval, grade 1.** "A nation is a people with their own government." In pairs, say one thing the Cherokee Nation does today, from grade 1's own pages (governs itself, teaches its language). A reminder, not a test.
- **9 min. Cherokee Today.** A Curio Class text, credited to the Cherokee Nation's pages, the United States National Park Service trail page, the United Keetoowah Band and the federal list of recognized tribes (urls in the unit plan, field 10): "In the summer of 1838 soldiers rounded up Cherokee families and forced them from their homes; an estimated 4,000 of an estimated 16,000 people died; families grieved and rebuilt. The Muscogee (Creek), Choctaw and Chickasaw nations were forced out too. As of November 2026, three Cherokee governments govern themselves: the Cherokee Nation, with its capital in Tahlequah, and the United Keetoowah Band, both in Oklahoma, and the Eastern Band in North Carolina." The fifth nation named on the trail pages, the Seminole, is held for the day 61 exit. A map shows the homeland in the Southeast and an arrow west (places matched to the pages at plan data). Told once, ending on rebuilding and today.
- **4 min. Who decided.** The United States government decided; the Cherokee people did not choose to leave their homeland. On the chain strip, half filled: "Because the government ___, they ___. Afterward ___ and families rebuilt." Pip says C.
- **4 min. Past and today.** Two bins, "forced out in the 1830s" and "governs itself today"; the same nation is in both. Pip says M. Each of the five nations is its own nation with its own name; day 64 reads their own pages.
- **7 min. Pairs work.** Each pair finishes the half-filled strip, then places the three Cherokee governments on a card map. The six are heard (about 4.5 minutes). No acting out; a quiet drawing of the map is open to any child.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** X61; X61b was heard in the pairs work.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The half-filled strip for the Cherokee shares X61's card shape, officers sending families west, and the two bins share X61b's shape, past or here today. Changed so they do not copy them: the strip asks where soldiers came, where the families were made to go and what came afterward, never chosen or forced; practice uses the Cherokee Nation, the United Keetoowah Band and the Eastern Band, never the Seminole Nation, and the Seminole Card is on the exit only.

Who holds what is carried: each pair holds its strip; the class holds the two bins. Tool: None. By hand: filling the strip, placing names. Evidence: X61, X61b.

## Day 62


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The letter most missed from day 61, C or M: the three Cherokee governments on a map card beside the removal arrow, for M. The other goes to a small group.
- **9 min. A word met, then a chain.** "Refugee: a person who had to leave their country because staying was not safe", taught from Refugee Card, invented for practice: fighting reaches Bel's town; it is not safe to stay; Bel's family go to another country to be safe. The class fills a blank chain strip with no example in view (filled examples withdrawn), then answers "Could they have stayed?" No one is asked whether they or their family are refugees. Pip says C.
- **9 min. Which reason is better supported?** Three cards, each with two possible reasons: Anna (to be with family, or to see a big city; day 59); Smith (men took him and made him work for them, or Smith wanted to travel); the Cherokee (the government decided, or the people wanted to see the west). Each pair points to the supporting line: "The ___ card says ___." The six are heard (about 4.5 minutes), one ask each.
- **8 min. Trace the moves.** Each pair traces three moves on the world map with a pencil and says "from ___ to ___, by ___, because ___": Anna, Europe to North America by ship; Smith, Africa to the Americas by ship; the Cherokee, east to west on one continent, so an arrow that does not cross an ocean (places and means matched to the pages at plan data). Pip says I.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** X62; X62b was heard in the pairs work.
- **1 min. Question board.** "Who could stay, and who could not?" is read. Tomorrow: why one country is called a nation of immigrants.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The tracing of Anna, Smith and the Cherokee shares X62's shape, an arrow from where people left to where they arrived. Changed so they do not copy it: the practice map names continents only, and Syria, Jordan and Luxembourg appear on the exit only. The three reason pairs share X62b's shape, why they left: Bel's card gives the reason in its first line.

Who holds what is carried: each pair holds its map and a blank strip; the teacher holds the three-question card. Tool: None. By hand: tracing, pointing. Evidence: X62, X62b.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with a picture or map: forced, enslaved, enslavers, nation, government, refugee, route. Frames: "___ decided. They could not stay because ___." "The ___ card says ___." "From ___ to ___ by ___." Home-language rehearsal allowed.
- **Access.** Raised or taped arrows on the maps; described pictures; large print; any child may point, draw or dictate; a child may leave to a quiet drawing task at any time.
- **Check accommodations.** The ask is repeated aloud; a drawn or pointed answer is a mark; a child who passes in public answers to the teacher alone.
- **Reading load.** Labels only (about 50 words a day); the teacher reads every card (about 200 words a day).
- **Extension, same standard.** "What source would tell us what Smith or Bel wanted, in their own words?"

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read each card once, calmly; the child orders the chain, points to the line, and says who decided. Skip nothing in the told parts; stop and draw if the child wishes.
- **One projector.** Show the maps and the chain strip enlarged; the pairs work uses printed cards and maps; no image of chains is shown.
