# Section 7.1: Choosing a country, asking questions, choosing sources

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


Grade 2 children read short labels and sentences. Every stem is spoken, one ask at a time. Answers are marked, tokened, said, drawn or a few written words. Days run 35 minutes (30 to 40). Pip says each wrong idea, one line at a time. Real facts about a country are Curio Class summaries in our own words with one credit line. No sources.csv rows exist for this course yet, so every real fact below is marked "source to check". Japan is the teaching example and is not on the menu. Reedmark, Kestrel Bend and every practice place and family are invented and printed "invented for practice". No child reports on home, family, money, religion, body or immigration status; any child may pass.

- **MA.2.T4.3 (day 82).** "Gather information and present a short research report on the physical features, resources, and people of a country outside the United States." This section's share: choose the country and name the three parts to gather (land and water, resources and work, people today). Gathering is days 85 to 87, the report is made on 89 and 90, and it is presented on 91.
- **D1.4.K-2 (day 83).** "Link the smaller supporting questions to the big question they help answer." Whole indicator: sort questions that help from ones that do not, say how one helps, and write the class's three supporting questions.
- **D3.1.K-2 (day 84).** "Gather useful information from one or two sources, using who made them and how they are built to choose." Day 84 chooses a source by its maker and one useful feature. The gathering is section 7.2. First choices are practice, never scored.
- **Limits that bite here.** Study examples of life, not one national lifestyle. No country is ranked and none is "the best". A child's country choice needs no reason. A family country off the menu is welcome only with the teacher's help and a printed set made by day 84; otherwise the child studies a menu country and no one asks why.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 supporting questions (review, days 82 and 83). The three questions card, who made it, when, where made (unit 1, day 84). Continents on a locator card (unit 2, day 82). The claim frame stays blank on the wall from unit 6 and is not used until 88.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How the three supporting questions map to the three report parts; that the Japan hook is the only real country fact taught and is "source to check" (the foreign ministry's Nature and Climate page, read 14 November 2026, an adult reference with no open licence, so our own words: Japan is a country of islands, seas surround them, mountains cover much of the land); that Curio Class makes every page and map, so the maker is Curio Class and the office whose records it used goes in the credit line; that choices on 84 are practice.

## 3. Checks, written first


Unit assessment items owned by last teaching: none. Item 2 (D1.4.K-2) is built here on 83 and sits in section 7.3's table; item 3 (D3.1.K-2) starts here and sits in 7.2's. The scale on every record is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A mark alone is recorded; "shown" needs the child's reason, heard from the day's rotating six, so a child not yet heard is "not yet seen" until their turn. The six are named on the record sheet.

**Exit questions.** Marked in the last 3 minutes, asked aloud one ask at a time. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E82a (day 82).** Mark, the exit card with two questions: "What do people eat?" and "Is my shirt red?" "Put a token on the question that helps our big question." Answer: the eating question. Wrong: a token on the shirt question (A).
- **E82b (day 82, six).** Spoken: "Say one thing you hope to learn about how people live in your country." Answer: any hope about how people live ("What are the mountains like?"). Wrong: "I like their flag" or "What toys do they have?" (A); "I only want to see the animals" (C). Never ask why the country was chosen.
- **E83a (day 83).** Mark, three question pictures, "invented for practice": a tap and a bucket, "Where do people get water?"; a pencil, "What color is my pencil?"; a number, "What is my favorite number?" "Put a token on the question that helps us learn how people live." Answer: the water question. Wrong: the pencil or the number question (A).
- **E83b (day 83, six).** Spoken: "Say how the question Where do people get water? helps us learn how people live." Answer: it tells how people meet a need ("people need water"). Wrong: "it is a question" or "I like it" (A).
- **E84a (day 84).** Mark, three cards about the invented Kestrel Bend: a town map with a title, a key and labels, made by "Curio Class, 2026"; a visitor's note, "I loved the river, it was lovely"; a plain decorated border. "Put a token on the card that can show where the river is." Answer: the map. Wrong: the note or the border because it looks nice (B).
- **E84b (day 84, six).** Spoken: "Point to who made the town map." Answer: the maker line on the card, Curio Class, 2026. Wrong: "the nicest picture" (B).
- **E84c (day 84, six).** Spoken: "Point to the part of the town map that helps you find the river." Answer: the key or a label. Wrong: "the colors" (B). Split from one stem of two asks, 2026 data pass.

## 6. Days

## Day 82


- **5 min. Hook, Japan.** The Japan card (three sentences, our own words, one credit line) read beside the original locator map (cards-japan-hook-day-82.md). The big question goes on the board: "What matters most about how people live in the country I chose?" Children say aloud, "Could land and water matter?" Japan is the only real fact taught.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** After the hook: "Our big question asks what matters about life in a country. Would 'What work do people do?' help? How?" A reason that work meets needs is shown. "It is another question" signals A. Record, no score. The repair is day 83's. Unit 6's day 81 results (s06-X81, s06-P81-3, s06-P81-7) go on the record sheet only; no unit 6 work is repaired today.
- **5 min. The menu.** The teacher reads the sixteen country names on the locator map by continent area (review of unit 2). Each country card shows its outline and name only; the three small pictures are on the exit card.
- **7 min. Choosing.** Tables of six go to the sign-up chart one table a minute, five minutes, with two minutes for a full country (at most 4 children each; second picks) or a family country off the menu, which is the teacher's job by day 84 and never asked about.
- **11 min. First look and exit.** Each child opens their country card with its outline, finds the country on the locator map, then the exit card with two questions and places a token (E82a). The rotating six are heard one at a time at 45 seconds each (E82b), 4.5 minutes. The rest draw one thing they hope to learn.
- **3 min. Question board.** The big question and the three pictures go up; supporting questions come tomorrow.

Total 35. Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board. Tool: None. By hand: tokens, drawing. Evidence: E82a, E82b.

**Shared shape (note, not timed).**
- The retrieval question shares its shape with E82b (a question about land, work or people); its case, a work question, is not on the exit.
- The first-look drawing shares E82b's shape; the exit card's three pictures print only on the exit.

## Day 83


- **5 min. Return and repair.** A, from the day 82 retrieval miss (and E82). Pip says "Every question helps"; the right is "helps because ___". More than a third missed: the whole class, and the first 5 minutes of the teaching part continue it (ten together). Otherwise a small group of at most 6 while the rest begin the pair cards.
- **8 min. Question connections, Japan.** The teacher models four Japan question cards on the wall (cards-japan-questions-day-83.md): "What are the mountains like?" (land and water), "What can people do with the sea?" (resources and work), "Which places do many people live in today?" (people today), and an unrelated "How many windows are in our room?" For each: "Does the answer help us decide what matters about life in Japan? Say how." Teach before observing. Pip says A.
- **9 min. Pairs sort.** Each pair sorts six invented question cards (cards-sort-day-83.md) into "helps" and "does not", tags the three that help with the land, work or people picture, and says "helps because ___" to the partner. The teacher hears the rotating six, 4.5 minutes.
- **8 min. Our three questions.** The class writes them together: What land and water are here? What resources support people's work? How do people live today, and what varies? Each goes on the board with a drawn link to the big question.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E83a; E83b was heard during the pairs.

Total 33. Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the sort cards, the teacher holds the board. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, tagging. Evidence: E83a, E83b.

**Shared shape (note, not timed).**
- The pairs sort and the "helps because ___" talk share their shape with E83a and E83b (choose the question that helps, then say how). The sort cards (jobs, rivers, homes) and the wall cards carry none of the exit's water, pencil or number questions.

## Day 84


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The one letter most missed from day 83 (A) with a fresh card; any other goes to a small group of at most 6 during the pairs part.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** "Name two kinds of source." Two answers from grade 1 (a photograph, a letter, a map, a story people tell). A miss: two cards side by side, one maker named; the grade 1 page is still to locate. No score.
- **7 min. Choose a source, Japan.** The teacher shows the Japan set (cards-japan-set-day-84.md): the map (made by Curio Class from public-domain map data), a Land and Water page with headings (made by Curio Class in 2026) and an invented visitor's note, "I loved the boat ride". The need: where are Japan's mountains? Read the sourcing card on each (blank-sourcing-card.md): who made it, when, where made, and a new line, "What part helps?" (a title, key, label, heading or caption). Pip says B. Teach before observing.
- **17 min. Pairs choose.** Country groups of one to four open their own printed packet: map, Land and Work page, People Today page and an original illustration (planned only, not yet written; see the packet layout sheet). The need: "What land and water are here?" Pick one source and fill its sourcing card. Practice, not scored. The teacher hears the rotating six, two asks, about 9 minutes. A child who needs the page read aloud sits with the teacher, at most 6 children at about 1 minute each, named on the sheet.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** E84a; E84b and E84c were heard during the pairs.

Total 36. Who holds what is carried: each pair holds its sourcing card; the teacher holds the Japan set. Tool: None. By hand: card filling. Evidence: E84a, E84b, E84c.

**Shared shape (note, not timed).**
- The Japan choice (map, page, note) and the pairs choice share their shape with E84a to E84c (choose by maker and helping part). The exit's Kestrel Bend map, note and border print only on the exit.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 83: "How does that question help us learn about life there?" Day 84: "Which source will you use for the land and water, and who made it?"
- **Written task, day 84.** The sourcing card for the chosen source: maker, date, place made, and one helping part (circle or a few words). Dictation allowed. Practice, not scored.
- **Judged by the teacher with 3 look-fors.** The maker is named; the part that helps is a heading, key, label or caption, not a color; the source fits the need.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 82.** Continents on the locator card (unit 2) and grade 1 supporting questions (retrieval).
- **Day 83.** The big question and Pip's routine (units 1 to 6); sorting what helps from what does not (grade 1 supporting questions).
- **Day 84.** The three questions card (unit 1); source kinds (grade 1); the repair of A.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Cards with pictures: country, land, water, work, people, question, source, maker. Frames: "This question helps because ___." and "I pick ___ because ___ made it." Bilingual rehearsal allowed. Country names are said as the country says them where known (source to check).
- **Access.** Large movable cards, tokens, pointing, a scribe, a helper to read a page.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each stem aloud, one ask at a time; accept a point or a drawing; the heard reason may be said in any language the teacher can record.
- **Reading load.** About 40 words a child reads alone on 84 (the sourcing card and the packet map labels); the teacher reads the hook, Japan and every stem aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask which question two different sources could answer, and how the child could tell.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look at one country card together. Say the big question, ask which of three pictures to learn about first, and sort six question cards. The grown-up may ask what the child hopes to learn but never why the country. Any day may be skipped.
- **One projector.** Show the Japan map, the sort cards and the sourcing card in turn; the board becomes a sheet of paper on the wall.
