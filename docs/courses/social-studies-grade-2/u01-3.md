# Section 1.3: Flat maps of a round world

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-2/1/s/1.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-2 u01-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every caption, credit and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to or a few written words. Each day runs 35 minutes. World Projection Cards are ours, drawn by Curio Class from a public-domain coastline dataset (source to check), and each carries a credit line. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time. No place or people are ranked by how big a map draws them.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.2.T1.2, this section's share (days 9 to 11).** "Compare map projections such as Mercator and Peters and explain how each shows the world differently." Day 9: a round skin cannot lie flat unless it is stretched, folded or opened, so every flat map changes something. Day 10: the Mercator map keeps the shapes of very small areas and draws lands far from the equator too big. Day 11: the Peters map keeps sizes and stretches shapes; each map shows one thing well; choose for a stated purpose. Unit assessment item 2 (day 15) is owned here. Children say "the map that keeps sizes" and "the map that keeps small shapes"; the teacher says Mercator and Peters, and "projection" (a way to draw the round Earth on flat paper).
- **D2.His.11.K-2, this section's share (day 11).** "Identify who made a source, when and where, using clues in the source itself." On day 11 the three questions are asked of each World Projection Card's own credit line. The people who first made each kind of map, and when, are withheld until a source is checked (source to check: the federal projection page named in unit plan field 10, not yet read).
- **MA.2.T1.1, this section's share (day 12).** "Explain what map parts such as the compass rose, scale, key, and title tell us": the title and the key (and the rose) on a labeled outline of North America. Practice only; no unit item.
- **Limits that bite here.** No ruler, ratio or latitude calculation; the word "latitude" is not taught. "Equator" is met as the line round the middle of the globe, not tested. Mercator keeps local angles, not whole continents' shapes or sizes; Peters keeps relative areas, not shapes or all distances. A ship holds one compass direction, which is not always the shortest route. No flat map is "wrong"; no map is the answer for every job. Named continents are supported on the cards; unit 2 teaches where they lie. The day 12 map names three countries and the waters and ranges of the unit's list, not every country.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 and kindergarten: the globe and two world maps (kindergarten u03-2) may come back as a reminder on day 9, never as an exit; "Both ___, but ___" is review. Claim, evidence and because (day 11): grade 1's claim frame. Section 1.1: the rose (day 11's ship) and scale (day 11 revises it).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Any flat map changes sizes, shapes, directions or distances somewhere. Mercator keeps small shapes and lets a ship hold one compass direction (so it suits sailing a course) but makes lands far from the equator look too big; Greenland looks as big as Africa on it, though Africa is far bigger (from memory; source to check). Peters keeps relative sizes but stretches shapes. Neither keeps everything the globe does. No single scale bar holds across a flat world map. Native nations live across North America today; the day 12 map shows countries and waters only and does not say the land was empty.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 2 (MA.2.T1.2: which map is Mercator and which is Peters from a taught clue; how each bends the world; which helps compare areas; one limitation), asked on day 15 on Map X and Map Y with a new task. Item 5 (D2.His.11.K-2, the Vermont map) is taught in section 1.2 and met again on day 11. Neither uses a card or coast taught on days 9 to 12 as the task.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks; the teacher reads each prompt aloud. The day's rotating six are heard after the mark. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Cards are new and print "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E9 (day 9).** Mark: a new Pictures Card with three pictures, a flat paper star, a balloon and a salad bowl. "Circle the one that can lie flat with no gap and no stretch." Spoken: "Why can a balloon's skin not lie flat as it is?" Answer: the flat paper star; to lie flat, a round skin has to be stretched, folded or opened. Wrong: circles the balloon or the salad bowl (G); says Earth would get holes (Q).
- **E10 (day 10).** Mark: a new Mercator card with two made-up lakes: the lake near the top edge is drawn wider than the lake near the middle. "Does the drawing prove the top lake is bigger? Circle yes or no." Spoken, two asks: "Why not?" and "What does this kind of map keep right?" Answer: no; this map stretches lands far from the middle; it keeps the shapes of very small places. Wrong: yes, or "it looks bigger so it is bigger" (G); says it keeps all the sizes right (G).
- **E11 (day 11).** Mark: a fresh pair of world cards. "Circle the map that keeps the shapes of small places near us right." Answer: the Mercator card. Spoken: "Read me the credit line on one card." Answer: the printed maker, date and place. Wrong: circles the other card because the first map is useless (K); names the holder of the data as maker (E); says it was made in the place it shows (J).
- **E12 (day 12).** Mark: a fresh blank outline of North America with five arrows and a printed word bank of fourteen words (Canada, United States, Mexico, Pacific Ocean, Atlantic Ocean, Arctic Ocean, Great Lakes, Mississippi River, St. Lawrence River, Rocky Mountains, Appalachian Mountains, North America, Amazon River, Brazil). "Write one country, one ocean, the Great Lakes, one river and one mountain range." Spoken: "How did the key help you find the river?" Answer: five fitting labels; the key's wavy line means river. Wrong: reads a symbol as the wrong feature (A); writes "North America" or a continent as a country (H). Practice only; the Pacific, the Rocky Mountains and the United States are the labels modeled, so a modeled label may be chosen.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The globe stays beside every map; "it stands for" (kindergarten) is said of each card. Scale: the strip model of section 1.1 is revised on day 11 (no one scale bar holds across a flat world map: a paper cut-out of Greenland fits the globe but not the Mercator card). The compass rose is carried to day 11's ship. The three questions (section 1.2) are used on each card's credit on day 11. The question board gets "Can a map show the world just as it is?" on days 9 and 11. Teacher modeling of the claim, evidence and because frame ends on day 11; partners use it.
- **Key moment 1, day 9: the shell will not lie flat.** Ideal instrument: a soft ball with land printed on it and a flat sheet, and a real peel as a teacher demonstration. Spec: a printed paper shell torn along a printed line by the teacher, and a paper cup pressed flat by each pair, fit. The teacher alone handles a real peel (citrus is not handled by children). No blade or student cutting. This section has no key tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 10: true-size cut-outs laid on the Mercator card.** Ideal instrument: a transparent overlay of land areas kept true. Spec: partly fits. Paper cut-outs made from the equal-area card keep area and bend shape, which the teacher says aloud ("true size, bent shape").
- **A room with nothing.** A ball of paper and a flat sheet; a drawn globe beside two hand-drawn maps; the pairs flatten a paper cup.

## 6. Days

## Day 9


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 8's exit misses: one repair for the most common letter among E, J, M, F and L, taught to the whole class only if more than a third made it; otherwise a small group in Pairs work.
- **11 min. Hook and shell.** The globe beside a flat World Projection Card: "Can this round skin lie flat?" Name "projection". The teacher opens the printed paper shell by tearing along the printed line and lays it flat: gaps, or stretch. Kindergarten's Australia on globe and map may be recalled. Pip's wrong idea Q.
- **11 min. Pairs work.** A paper cup for each pair: press it flat, say what happens (it wrinkles, folds or tears), and say the frame "To lie flat, the skin must ___."
- **2 min. Question board.** "Can a map ever show the world just as it is?" The class's first answer, in their words, is not final.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** E9: mark 2 minutes; group heard 3 minutes; board 1 minute.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the shell; pairs hold the cups. Tool: None. By hand: pressing the cup, circling. Evidence: E9.

## Day 10


- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** Error Q (from E9): an intact globe beside separated paper strips; children point to which has gaps. This is the one repair the warm-up holds.
- **14 min. The Mercator map.** The Mercator card beside the globe; name the equator. Small shapes near us look right; lands far from the equator are drawn too big. Lay the paper cut-out of Greenland (true size, bent shape) on the card, then the cut-out of Africa. Say "On this map Greenland looks as big as Africa; on the globe Africa is much bigger" (source to check).
- **11 min. Pairs work.** Pairs repeat the cut-out test with Greenland and Africa, then read a new invented-island card and say whether the drawing proves which island is bigger.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E10: mark 1 minute; group heard 3 minutes; board 1 minute.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the cut-outs. Tool: None. By hand: laying cut-outs, circling. Evidence: E10.

## Day 11


- **4 min. Warm-up.** Retrieval: which card draws Greenland too big, and what is true on the globe? No repair is routed to this day.
- **12 min. The Peters map.** The Peters card beside the Mercator card and the globe: sizes kept, shapes stretched; the Greenland cut-out fits. Frame "Both ___, but ___." Read each card's credit line with the three questions (maker, date, place); say why each card bends what it bends. Revise scale: no one bar holds across a flat world map.
- **8 min. Claim, evidence, because.** Model: "I think the Peters map is best for comparing sizes because Greenland's cut-out fits it and the sizes match the globe."
- **5 min. Pairs work.** Partners try a different purpose, "a ship holds one compass direction", and say the claim, the evidence and the because.
- **2 min. Question board.** "Can a map show the world just as it is?" The class revises its answer: no flat map keeps everything; the best map depends on the job.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E11: mark 1 minute; group heard 3 minutes (reading a credit line, 30 seconds each).

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the claim frame. Tool: None. By hand: circling, saying a claim. Evidence: E11.

## Day 12


- **9 min. Return and repair.** Two parts: error G (from E9 and E10): trace one land on the globe and on a flat map and say what changed (5 minutes); error K (from E11): match two job cards to the two maps (4 minutes). The more common is for the whole class; the other goes to a small group in Pairs work.
- **8 min. Model: North America.** A simple outline with a title, a key (a wavy line is a river, triangles are a mountain range, blue ripples are an ocean or lake) and a rose. Model labeling the three countries, one ocean and one range from the word bank. Pip's wrong idea H: North America is not a country.
- **12 min. Pairs work.** Each child labels a blank outline from the word bank, partners check; the teacher takes the small group.
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** E12: writing five labels 4 minutes; group heard 2 minutes while the others write.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the key. Tool: None. By hand: writing labels from a bank. Evidence: E12 (practice only).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 9: "What happens when a round skin is pressed flat?" Day 10: "Does the drawing prove the island is bigger?" Day 11: "Which map is best for this job, and why?"
- **Written task, day 12.** Label five parts of a blank North America from a word bank (E12). A child who cannot write the word points to it on the bank and the teacher writes it.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) each label sits at the feature it names; (2) the country, ocean, lakes, river and range are each a fitting kind; (3) no country label sits on the whole continent.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 9.** Day 8's credits; the globe and map from kindergarten. Day 10: day 9's shell. Day 11: day 10's cut-outs and day 6's three questions. Day 12: the key and rose from section 1.1.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: round, flat, stretch, fold, tear, globe, projection, Mercator, Peters, size, shape, ocean, country, river, mountains. Frames: "To lie flat, the skin must ___." "Both ___, but ___." "I think the ___ map is best for ___ because ___." Pointing first.
- **Access.** Raised outline cards; a ball or a papier-mache shell for a child who cannot see; neutral description of each map; the teacher hands the cup to a child who cannot press it.
- **Check accommodations.** Prompts read aloud; circle, point, scribe; the word bank on E12 may be read aloud; the writing is shortened to the labels a child can write. Record the support used.
- **Reading load.** A label of one to three words; the word bank is the longest read; at most 150 words a student reads in a day.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 11 says what the Peters card bends that the Mercator card keeps.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 9: press a paper cup flat and a ball of paper flat; say what had to change. Day 10: compare two pictures of one land. Day 11: pick one of two maps for a job and say why. Day 12: label a printed outline from a word bank. The wrong ideas are said by a toy.
- **One projector.** Show the two cards side by side with the globe on the table; children still circle on their own card; the cup and cut-outs are paper.
