- **MA.2.T4.1, this section's share (day 48).** "Tell physical geography from political geography, such as Africa as a continent that holds many independent countries." One continent, two maps with two keys; Africa is not a country.
- **MA.2.T4.2, this section's share (days 49 to 51).** "Explain the characteristics of a country: its people, land and borders, history, culture, languages, and type of government, and the purposes of government." Day 49: the six characteristics and what makes a country. Day 50: purposes, and that leaders are not always chosen by voting. Day 51: a four-part comparison of two countries from sources. The comparison is revised on day 54 and scored on day 56 (item 2b).
- **Limits that bite here.** No disputed borders: production verifies every border on a map and uses none that is in dispute. Government purposes are responsibilities, not proof that every government does them well. No ranking of countries or peoples. A card shows examples of a country's life, never everyone's. No religion line on any card; a child's question about belief gets "people in every country follow many beliefs, and a card does not say what one person believes". No child is asked about their home, family, money, religion or where they are from.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 groups, rules and elected leaders (retrieval, day 48). Unit 1: map key and the three questions (days 48, 51). Unit 2: continent and region (days 48, 49). Unit 3: claim frame (carried to 4.2).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A continent is a land mass; a country is a governed place with people and borders; one continent holds many countries. Physical and political maps answer different questions about one place. "More than 50" African countries is the count on the United Nations member list (source to check; the page and date are named before production); never ask children to count them. A government's purposes are what it is responsible for. Inherited and elected leadership are both met as facts, with no judgment of either. Japan's emperor has a role set by its constitution and does not govern; elected representatives choose the prime minister (source to check). Saudi leadership passes within the ruling family and is not decided by popular vote (source to check). Neither country stands for a faith or a people.
