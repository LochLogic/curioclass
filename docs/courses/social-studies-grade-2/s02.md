## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 17.** Compare the dated Aletsch images with a warm coastal Place Card beside the globe. Ask where students might choose to live and what they need to find out. Choosing their current home is welcome.
**Supporting questions.** Shape students' questions into: Where are these places (days 17 to 20)? How do land and water shape life (days 21 to 23)? What changes over time (days 24 and 25)? How can sources support our choice (days 26 and 27)?
**Closing claim.** Day 27 answers the compelling question from the World Feature Atlas and Place Cards. Students explain a choice with a geographic reason and named source; partners consider another defensible choice.
**Teaching.** Model locating on day 17, climate on day 21, comparison on day 24, and sourced reasoning on day 26. Step back on day 27. Revisit the question board daily. Say claims before writing; retain the frame until independent reasoning is demonstrated.

**Look-fors and sample answers.**

- Item 1 requires all seven continents and at least one named major feature per continent on map and globe. Days 18 and 19 maps are practice. Return written location feedback and let children revise maps and retry globe placements on new outlines and shuffled location prompts during days 21 to 27. Retain the written cue, revisions and later globe notes; record each required location before removing labels. Locate Rocky Mountains in western North America, Great Lakes along the United States and Canada border, Andes in western South America, Amazon across northern South America, Alps in south-central Europe, Danube across central and southeastern Europe, Sahara in northern Africa, Nile in northeastern Africa, Himalayas in southern Asia, Outback in Australia's interior, Great Barrier Reef off northeastern Australia, and the Antarctic ice sheet on Antarctica. Teach every named feature; record at least one per continent on both representations. Meets: "The Andes run along western South America; here they are on my map and globe." Does not: "The Andes are somewhere in America" without locating. Day 28 adds all seven continent labels and two features on the new map; the later globe record supplies globe evidence. A missed globe feature remains a gap.
- Item 2 requires five ocean locations on both representations and an explanation of habitability. Pacific lies between the Americas and Asia and Australia; Atlantic between the Americas and Europe and Africa; Indian between Africa, Asia and Australia; Arctic surrounds the North Pole; Southern surrounds Antarctica. Meets: "Oceans are homes for living things. Water evaporates from them and later falls as rain." Does not: "Oceans matter because I like swimming." Trade is important but alone does not explain habitability.
- Item 3 requires a location-to-weather link, a long-term climate link and a vegetation link. Meets: "Air from the sea rises at the ridge and drops rain on the west today. The caption says that side is wet over many years, so forest can grow; the drier valley has scattered shrubs." The cause must come from the child, not the caption. Does not: "It rains today, so it always rains everywhere here."
- Item 4 requires an environmental condition and its practical consequence for each place, without a universal cultural claim. Meets: "People working outside on the wet slope may need rain protection; valley farmers need plants that can grow with little rain." Does not: "Everyone near water fishes."
- Item 5 requires two located regions, physical and cultural change at two dates and a continuity. Meets: "The Europe map shows a smaller lake in 2020 than 1990. Its caption says less rain fell. The Asia street adds a tram between 2000 and 2020, but people still travel along it." Does not: "Both show cultural change" or "the first diagram is today."
- Item 6 requires a choice, a reason from a named source and the link between them. Meets: "I choose the wet slope because I want to walk among trees. Coast and Ridge shows forest there because it gets more rain." Does not: "The valley is best because I like it." Different supported choices also meet.

A class globe is required; with none, use a ball and record the shortfall.
