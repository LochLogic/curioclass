## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 17.** Compare the dated Aletsch images with a warm coastal Place Card beside the globe. Ask where students might choose to live and what they need to find out. Choosing their current home is welcome.
**Supporting questions.** Shape students' questions into: Where are these places (days 17 to 20)? How do land and water shape life (days 21 to 23)? What changes over time (days 24 and 25)? How can sources support our choice (days 26 and 27)?
**Closing claim.** Day 27 answers the compelling question from the World Feature Atlas and Place Cards. Students explain a choice with a geographic reason and named source; partners consider another defensible choice.
**Teaching.** Model locating on day 17, climate on day 21, comparison on day 24, and sourced reasoning on day 26. Step back on day 27. Revisit the question board daily. Say claims before writing; retain the frame until independent reasoning is demonstrated.

**Held-back packet, still to make.** Coast and Ridge is explicitly invented. Its map shows a sea west of a ridge, wet western slopes and a drier eastern valley. Caption: "Air from the sea carries water. As it rises over these mountains, it cools and rain falls. Less rain reaches the valley beyond. Over many years the western slopes have been wet and the eastern valley dry. Higher slopes are usually colder. Today rain is falling on the western slope." Draw forest west and scattered shrubs east. Add a fresh, accurately credited coastal photograph of land and water, withheld from teaching, for this prompt: "Point to one land feature and one water feature the photograph shows. Can this single picture tell the climate?" Answer: identify visible features; no, use long-term evidence. This is further evidence for MA.2.T2.3, error D. Its location need not be the invented place; label the distinction. The climate information comes from the caption, not guessing at the photograph.

**Look-fors and sample answers.**

- Item 1 requires all seven continents and at least one named major feature per continent on map and globe. Days 18 and 19 retain individual annotated maps and paper globes; check placements before labels are removed. Locate Rocky Mountains in western North America, Great Lakes along the United States and Canada border, Andes in western South America, Amazon across northern South America, Alps in south-central Europe, Danube across central and southeastern Europe, Sahara in northern Africa, Nile in northeastern Africa, Himalayas in southern Asia, Outback in Australia's interior, Great Barrier Reef off northeastern Australia, and the Antarctic ice sheet on Antarctica. Teach every named feature; record at least one per continent on both representations. Meets: "The Andes run along western South America; here they are on my map and globe." Does not: "The Andes are somewhere in America" without locating. Day 28 adds all seven continent locations on both and two features on the new map. A missed globe feature remains a gap.
- Item 2 requires five ocean locations on both representations and an explanation of habitability. Pacific lies between the Americas and Asia and Australia; Atlantic between the Americas and Europe and Africa; Indian between Africa, Asia and Australia; Arctic surrounds the North Pole; Southern surrounds Antarctica. Meets: "Oceans are homes for living things. Water evaporates from them and later falls as rain." Does not: "Oceans matter because I like swimming." Trade is important but alone does not explain habitability.
- Item 3 requires a location-to-weather link, a long-term climate link and a vegetation link. Meets: "Air rises at the ridge and drops rain on the west today. The caption says that side is wet over many years, so forest can grow; the drier valley has scattered shrubs." Does not: "It rains today, so it always rains everywhere here."
- Item 4 requires an environmental condition and its practical consequence for each place, without a universal cultural claim. Meets: "People working outside on the wet slope may need rain protection; valley farmers need plants that can grow with little rain." Does not: "Everyone near water fishes."
- Item 5 requires two located regions, two dated comparisons, physical and cultural change, and a continuity. Cause must come from the explanation, not the image alone. Meets: "Aletsch in Europe has less ice in 2024 than 1984. The explanation links ice loss with warming. Main Street in North America shows changed transport between about 1905 and 1941, while people still use the street to travel." Require visible transport evidence in both images; finalize that detail after crop inspection. Does not: "The glacier caused the town to change" or "1941 is today."
- Item 6 requires a choice, a reason from a named source and the link between them. Meets: "I choose the wet slope because I want to walk among trees. Coast and Ridge shows forest there because it gets more rain." Does not: "The valley is best because I like it." Different supported choices also meet.
