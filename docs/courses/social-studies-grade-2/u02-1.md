# Section 2.1: Where on Earth: continents, features and oceans


Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every caption, credit and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, placed on a map or a few written words. Days run 36 to 40 minutes. The World Outline Cards, the World Feature Atlas, the Ocean Cards and the Place Cards are ours, made from public-domain outlines and credited present-day photographs (source to check), and each card carries a credit line. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time. Practice cards print "invented for practice".

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.2.T2.1, this section's share (days 17 to 19).** "Locate all the continents and some major physical features on each one on a world map and a globe." Day 17: the seven continents on both, and where each lies by compass direction. Day 18: four features of the Americas. Day 19: eight features of the other five continents. Item 1 (day 28) is owned here; its globe evidence is gathered in sections 2.2 to 2.4.
- **MA.2.T2.2, this section's share (day 20).** "Locate the world's oceans on a map and globe, and explain why oceans matter and help make the world livable." Five oceans, and why they matter: homes and food for living things, ships, rain, most of the planet's water. "Livable" means a place where living things can live. Item 2 (day 28) is owned here.
- **Limits that bite here.** Place by compass words (west, northeastern), never by latitude, longitude or any grid; the equator is only the line round the globe's middle (unit 1). The count is seven continents and five oceans (source to check: NOAA ocean facts); the Southern Ocean is the water round Antarctica. A feature is placed within a continent, not measured. No photograph stands for a whole continent: every continent shows cities, homes, farms and wild places, and no feature page is the only picture of its continent (not "Africa is desert and river", not "Asia is rural"). Antarctica is shown as ice and research stations. People are named by their own names; any Native nation named is in the present tense. No child reports on a home or a trip.
- **Connections.** Grade 1: continent, ocean, river, mountain (review, day 17). Unit 1: the compass rose and the key (days 17 to 19), and the three questions on a card, who made it, when, where made (days 18 and 19). Science 2-ESS2-2, land and water on maps, already taught (retrieval, day 17).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A continent is a very large land area (grade 1's words) and countries lie inside it; Africa is a continent with many countries. The Pacific is one ocean that goes round the back of the globe, so a flat map cuts it at its edges. The five oceans are one connected body of salt water. A feature's place is "in the west of South America", not a dot. Ships carry goods across oceans; sea water warms, rises as vapor and falls again as rain (source to check: NOAA ocean water and weather pages). Every place or count named here is from memory until its page is read.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 1 (MA.2.T2.1, continents and two features, on fresh World Map B) and item 2 (MA.2.T2.2, five oceans and why they matter, on a fresh map). Neither uses a card taught on days 17 to 20.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks; the teacher reads each prompt aloud, one ask at a time. The day's rotating six are heard after the mark at the class globe. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Each exit uses a new card, crop or globe turn. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **X17 to X17e (day 17).** Mark, the fresh World Outline Card F (seven lettered shapes and a north arrow): X17 "Circle Africa." X17b "Draw a box round the land that lies north of Africa." Answer: Africa; Europe. Spoken, the six at the globe, one ask each: X17c "Show me Antarctica." X17d "Is Antarctica north or south of Africa?" X17e "What is Africa?" Answer: the land at the bottom; south; a continent, a very large land area. Wrong: Africa called a country (A). A land misplaced or north and south reversed has no letter in the register: record not yet seen and repair with the north arrow (plan point).
- **X18 to X18c (day 18).** Mark, a blank crop of the Americas and the edge of Africa, not used in teaching: X18 "Circle where the Andes are" from three marked positions (west of South America, east of South America, the edge of Africa). Answer: west of South America. Spoken, the six on a globe turned a new way: X18b "Show me the Amazon." X18c "Is the Amazon in the north or the south of South America?" Answer: the long river across the north; north. The plan's "land or water" ask is replaced: a river is water, so it could not separate a right answer from a wrong one. Wrong: a feature in the east or on another continent (J).
- **X19 to X19c (day 19).** Mark, a new coastal crop of Australia: X19 "Circle where the Great Barrier Reef is" from three marked positions (offshore northeast, inland, offshore west). Answer: offshore, northeastern Australia. Spoken, the six on a new globe view, one feature drawn from the eight-card pack: X19b "Show me this feature." X19c "Is the feature drawn land, water or ice?" Wrong: the feature on the wrong continent or the wrong side (J).
- **X20 to X20d (day 20).** Mark, a fresh map centred on the Indian Ocean (the Pacific shows as two pieces at the edges), six numbered shapes, six words in the bank (the five oceans and Great Lakes): X20 "Write the number of each ocean beside its name." Answer: both Pacific pieces named, every ocean matched, Great Lakes left alone. Spoken, the six: X20b "Show me the Southern Ocean." X20c "Tell me one way oceans help living things." X20d "Are the five oceans joined or apart?" (added: the plan named L but had no ask for it). Answer: round Antarctica; homes, food or rain; joined. Wrong: a cut ocean named once or its far piece left unnamed (B); only four oceans (M); holidays only (C); apart (L).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The compass rose and key from unit 1: the teacher reads directions with the north arrow on day 17, pairs say them on day 18, each child says one on day 19, and children place a feature by compass word alone on day 20. The Atlas key adds three symbols of ours (dots for desert, a ring for reef, white hatching for ice sheet), read from the key as in unit 1. The three questions (who made it, when, where made; "the record does not say" when it does not) are asked of an Atlas credit line on days 18 and 19. The question board holds the compelling question from day 17 and its first supporting question, "Where are these places?", with answers added each day. The globe is on the table every day.
- **Key moment 1, day 17: the seven shapes on the globe and the flat card together.** Ideal instrument: a class globe with a matching flat card. Spec: fits. A printed card and a globe are enough; the teacher turns the globe so the north arrow stays the same.
- **Key moment 2, day 20: the Pacific across the paper edge.** Ideal instrument: a globe and a flat card read edge to edge. Spec: partly fits; two printed copies taped edge to edge show the join, then the globe shows it whole.
- **A room with nothing.** A ball with seven continents drawn on it, a printed globe net, or the Atlas page alone, with the shortfall written on the record sheet. No tool is used; Tools: None.

## Day 17

Hook.** The two dated Aletsch images (1984 and 2024, a month apart, so compare ice not snow; credit read aloud: federal imagery, Michala Garrison, United States Geological Survey data) beside a warm coastal Place Card and the globe. Pairs work.** Each pair places seven name tags on a blank card with lettered shapes (P17-1), then says one direction sentence from a deal of cards that use only the North America, South America, Asia and Australia pairs (P17-2). ## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 17: "What is Africa, and how do you know?" Day 18: "Where do the Andes lie in South America?" Day 20: "Why do oceans matter?"
- **Written task, day 20.** Write the number of each ocean beside its name (X20, from a word bank). A child who cannot write a name points to it and the teacher writes it.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** (1) each number sits beside the matching ocean; (2) all five oceans are matched and the lakes word is left alone; (3) a map edge does not split one ocean into two.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 17.** Grade 1's continent and ocean words; unit 1's arrow and key. **Day 18.** Unit 1's key; day 17's seven names. **Day 19.** Day 18's four features and the three questions. **Day 20.** Day 19's Nile and Alps; every day's direction words.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: continent, ocean, mountains, river, desert, ice, north, south, east, west. Frames: "___ lies north of ___." "The ___ is in the ___ of ___." "Oceans matter because ___." Pointing and home-language answers first.
- **Access.** Raised outline cards and a globe with raised land for a child who cannot see; neutral spoken description of each map; the teacher places a tag for a child who cannot.
- **Check accommodations.** Prompts read aloud; circle, point, scribe; word banks read aloud; record the support used.
- **Reading load.** Labels of one to three words and Atlas captions of at most three short sentences read aloud; at most 150 words a child reads in a day.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 19 says two directions to go from one continent to another round the globe.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A globe or a ball and a printed card. Day 17: name the continents and say where one lies from another. Days 18 and 19: place the features. Day 20: follow the Pacific round the ball and say one reason oceans matter. The wrong ideas are said by a toy.
- **One projector.** Show the Atlas page with the globe on the table; each child still marks their own card.
