## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 82.** Read Japan's Nature and Climate facts about mountains and surrounding seas beside an original locator map. Ask the compelling question: could different features matter for different ways of living? Japan is the teaching example, outside the research menu.
**Supporting questions.** Write together: What land and water are here (85)? What resources support people's work (86)? How do people live today, and what varies (87)? Which answers best support our claim (88)?
**Closing claim, day 89.** Each child answers the compelling question using their country's Map, Land and Work page, and People Today page, naming two supporting facts and explaining the connection. Revise on 90; present on 91. Different claims can fit the evidence.
**Teaching.** Model question connections on 83, source selection on 84 and claim writing on 88, using Japan. Step back during gathering and drafting. Teach before observing; retain prompts but remove completed examples on 89.

**Look-fors and sample answers.** Record independent, prompted or not yet for each part, including every presentation medium. These are illustrative samples, not additional country facts to teach.

- Item 1 meets when notes show gathering, the report covers all three categories, and the child presents it. Meets: "My map shows a river. Land and Work tells how people use its water for crops. People Today shows a city and a smaller town and names languages people use." Accept only details actually supported by that child's packet. Does not: "Here are animals I like," with no resource or people information.
- Item 2 meets when two found facts are linked to different supporting questions and the big question. Meets: "Catching fish answers what work people do. Most people living near Reykjavík answers how people live today. Fish work and city life both matter there." Does not: "Both answers are Iceland."
- Item 3 requires relevant facts plus attention to maker and organization, not the assertion that every government source is always right. Meets: "I choose Page I. The statistics office gathers work records, and this heading helps me find catching and preparing fish." Does not: "I pick the postcard because the mountain is pretty."
- Item 4 requires a defensible answer, two accurate attributed facts, a connection, and evidence in each medium. Meets: "Fish matter for some people's work: Page I says people catch fish and other people prepare them for sale. Fish support more than one kind of work." The child's own country claim must appear in all three media. Does not: "My country is the best," with unrelated pictures. Neither paper nor speech substitutes for unseen digital evidence.

Each child says the claim and two facts aloud at the device, and the adult records that as the child's spoken presentation. Children bring a finished claim card, direct its entry into a prepared offline display, choose or point to evidence, and check the result; others revise paper reports while waiting. On 91, keep ten minutes for the question, teaching or any response, then use sixteen for thirty short showings of about thirty seconds (the child reads the claim and a listener asks one question about it), two for transitions and two for the exit: ten, sixteen, two and two make thirty. A child who needs a longer turn or access support has it during supported work on 89 or 90, or in the optional ten minutes; nothing scored depends on those minutes. Revisions are allowed through day 91, and the latest work the child made is scored. No timer is shown to children.

**Next teaching moves.** More than a third missing an exit means a class response in the next day's warm-up and first teaching minutes, ten total; otherwise meet that group while others continue research. For A, connect a question to a need. For B, compare makers and headings, then find a new fact. For C, sort three report categories and reread the map key. For D, link two details to a claim and explain why. For E, replace "everyone" with what the evidence actually supports. For F, have the child direct and check their own summary on the shared device. Use new instructional examples, preserving the reserved tasks. Extend by asking what evidence could weaken a claim. On 93, use the response allocation below rather than adding another slot.
