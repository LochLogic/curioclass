## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

**Hook, day 82.** Read Japan's Nature and Climate facts about mountains and surrounding seas beside an original locator map. Ask the compelling question: could different features matter for different ways of living? Japan is the teaching example, outside the research menu.
**Supporting questions.** Write together: What land and water are here (85)? What resources support people's work (86)? How do people live today, and what varies (87)? Which answers best support our claim (88)?
**Closing claim, day 89.** Each child answers the compelling question using their country's Map, Land and Work page, and People Today page, naming two supporting facts and explaining the connection. Revise on 90; present on 91. Different claims can fit the evidence.
**Teaching.** Model question connections on 83, source selection on 84 and claim writing on 88, using Japan. Step back during gathering and drafting. Teach before observing; retain prompts but remove completed examples on 89.


**Parts by verb, section 7.1.** MA.2.T4.3: gather information; present a short report covering physical features, resources and people.
- **D1.4.K-2:** Connect smaller research questions to the unit's big question.
- **D3.1.K-2:** Gather relevant information from one or two sources; use their makers and organization to choose them.
- **D4.3.K-2:** Present the main argument in print, speech and a digital form.
The local C3 file lists codes only; the linked framework supplies these indicators.
**Limits.** Study examples of life, not a single national lifestyle. No country rankings, costume performances or required family disclosures. Comparing an everyday custom means describing two practices without making our community the norm. No new action cycle or finance lesson; unit 3 holds the year's action.

**Next teaching moves.** More than a third missing an exit means a class response in the next day's warm-up and first teaching minutes, ten total; otherwise meet that group while others continue research. For A, connect a question to a need. For B, compare makers and headings, then find a new fact. For C, sort three report categories. For I, sort things made from what they come from. For G, match symbols to the key; for H, add source labels. For D, link two details to a claim and explain why. For E, replace "everyone" with what the evidence actually supports. For F, have the child direct and check their own summary on the shared device. Use new instructional examples, preserving the reserved tasks. Extend by asking what evidence could weaken a claim.
