# Section 6.1: What culture is, and how to ask a teller

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption, story and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, boxed, pointed to, put on a token, said, drawn or a few written words. Days run 35 to 37 minutes. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time. Every person in a practice card (Hana, Beck, Ms. Bell, Ms. Dale, Mr. Quill) is invented and printed "invented for practice"; the teacher says so aloud. The one real telling is the Posadas Card, a summary in our words with one credit line. No sources.csv rows exist for this course yet, so every real fact is marked "source to check". No child is asked about their own home, family, money, religion or how they came here; any child may pass.

- **MA.2.T3.4, this section's share (day 72).** "Identify ... the elements that define a culture." The seven parts: language, food, music, beliefs, holidays, art, ways of dress. What people bring, the effects of migration and how all of a community's people enrich it belong to 6.2.
- **MA.2.T3.3 (days 73, 74, 75).** "Interview family, neighbors, friends, or school staff to learn where their families came from, how and why they moved, and when they came ..." Day 73: write the four questions. Day 74: listen and ask a follow-up with a visitor. Day 75: each child asks, listens and keeps faithful notes. This town or state stands for Massachusetts.
- **D2.His.12.K-2 (days 73, 74).** "Ask questions about a source as it relates to a particular event or development." A teller's spoken telling is the source; the event is the move.
- **Limits that bite here.** Culture is a name for what people share and do, with differences inside every group. No ancestry chart, costume, tasting or religious observance. Beliefs is a word on a card, defined as "what people hold to be important", with one non-religious example; no child names a belief. A teller picks a safe move, within or between places, and may decline any question; a pass is recorded as a full answer. "We have always lived here" is a full answer to where and when. Nobody asks about papers, money or why a family is sad.
- **Connections.** Unit 4, culture as "what people in a place make, do and share" (day 72, review). Unit 5, the chain "because, moved, afterward" and the three questions (days 73, 74). ELA unit 1, speaking and listening (SL.2.3, taught). Grade 1 u06-1 custom, language, tradition (day 72) and u02-1 the calendar (day 73).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** An interview is a spoken source: the teller made it, the child writes it down. A faithful note holds only what the teller said; unknown stays unknown. A follow-up asks about a part the teller already said. Why the four topics, and why the teller chooses what to share. How to find a willing school visitor, and the fallback if none can come (day 74).

## 2. Where it sits


Follows unit 5, whose chain (a reason, the move, what happened next) the four questions ask. Day 72 opens with the unit's one retrieval of grade 1's ways of living and day 73 with the calendar and kinds of sources; a miss goes to the next day's repair. Section 6.2 opens on day 76 with the notes and dates from days 74 and 75. Results go on the record sheet; no readiness check is reserved.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none by number. Items 1 and 3 are owned by 6.3 and item 2 by 6.2, which teach them last. The day 75 observation is retained evidence for item 1; day 73 and 74 work is practice and is not scored.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks once in the last 3 minutes; the teacher reads each ask aloud, one ask at a time. A spoken "(six)" item is heard from the rotating six, about 45 seconds each, during the pairs work. If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair takes that letter. Every case is new (rule 4); none is a card taught.
- **X72 (day 72).** Mark, paper: Invented for practice. At the Aspen Street Fair a family serves a stew, a neighbor hangs a painted tile and a neighbor sings a song. Three pictures: the stew, the painted tile, the song. Put a token on the one part of culture you cannot hold. Answer: The song. Wrong: The token is on the stew (A); The token is on the painted tile (A).
- **X72b (day 72).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. Two families at the Aspen Street Fair keep the same holiday. Say one way they could keep it differently. Answer: Any difference, such as a different dish, song or game. Wrong: Everyone keeps it the same way (G); One family's way is better (K).
- **X73 (day 73).** Mark, paper: Invented for practice. School cook Ms. Dale remembers her move to this town in 2018; interviewed today. Three question cards: Why did you come here? How did you get here? What year did you come to this town? Circle the question that asks when. Answer: What year did you come to this town? Wrong: The card Why did you come here? is circled (B); The card How did you get here? is circled (B).
- **X73b (day 73).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. School cook Ms. Dale remembers her move to this town. Ask Ms. Dale a question about where she lived before. Answer: A question about where she lived before, such as Where did you live before you came here? Wrong: What is your favorite color? (D); How did you get here? (B).
- **X74 (day 74).** Mark, paper: Invented for practice. Mr. Quill says: I came to this town by bus. I came for a job. Three notes: He came by bus for a job. He came by bus for a job at a big factory. He came by train for a job. Circle the note that holds only what Mr. Quill said. Answer: He came by bus for a job. Wrong: The note about a big factory is circled (C); The note about a train is circled (C).
- **X74b (day 74).** Spoken, the six: Invented for practice. Mr. Quill said he came to this town for a job. Ask him one follow-up question about that. Answer: A question about the job, such as What kind of job? or Could you explain that part? Wrong: What is your favorite food? (D); How old are you? (D).
- **X75 (day 75).** Spoken, the six: Ask the teller the question for your topic: where, how, why or when. Answer: A question that asks the child's own topic of the teller's move, such as Where did you live before? How did you get here? Why did you move? When did you come to this town? Wrong: Where are you from? That is all (B); A topic a partner already asked is asked again and one topic is left out (B).
- **X75b (day 75).** Spoken, the six: Ask the teller one follow-up question about something the teller just said. Answer: A question about a part the teller just said, such as Could you explain that part? Wrong: Do you like pizza? (D); Thank you. (no follow-up asked) (D).
- **X75c (day 75).** Mark, paper: In your box write or draw only what the teller said. If the teller passed or did not say, write passed or unknown. Answer: A word or a drawing of what the teller said; passed or unknown where it applies. Wrong: A detail the teller did not say is written (C); A guessed year is written where the teller passed (C).

## 6. Days

## Day 72


- **5 min. Hook, Music Here.** The Music Here map (Mexico and California locator maps with an arrow) and the Posadas Card read aloud, the first three sentences: he grew up in Mexico, a fandango there lasts from evening to dawn with everyone joining, and in California local laws limit all-night events so he organizes shorter ones at homes and garages (a summary in our words; credit speaker and interpreter Russell Rodriguez; sources 72B to 72D, checked). "Can music travel without fitting in a suitcase?" is asked and left open. The unit's question goes on the question board.
- **4 min. Retrieval, grade 1's ways of living.** Two neighbors speak different languages and both help at school. Thumbs: can both belong? Say one thing people may share or do differently. A miss goes to day 73's repair, with two original neighbor cards.

Seven picture cards go on the culture chart: language, food, music, beliefs, holidays, art, ways of dress. Terms: culture (review of unit 4: what people make, do and share), part of culture. Pip says A.
- **7 min. Sort in pairs.** Each pair sorts eight small cards from Beck's street (invented) onto the chart. The rotating six are heard on X72b (about 4.5 minutes), one spoken ask: "Say one way they could keep it differently."
- **4 min. Same or different.** Pip says G, then K. The class says: people who share something still do it differently; different is not better.
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** X72 on paper; X72b was heard in the pairs work.
- **2 min. Question board.** "What is culture?" is added.

- **Print and before class.** Print: Music Here map and Posadas Card, culture chart, seven parts, Hana's street cards, Beck's street cards, exit cards, days 72 to 75 (unit 6) (the day 72 card only), question board, unit 6, record sheet (unit 6). ## Day 74


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Sheets come back with the cue (2 minutes). The letter most missed from day 73 (B or D) with a fresh case (3 minutes).
- **4 min. Listening and a follow-up, a model.** Mr. Okoye (invented, "invented for practice") answers "Why did you come?" with "To be near my sister." The teacher asks "Could you explain that part?" and writes only what he says. Pip says C. Terms: follow-up, faithful note.
- **14 min. The visitor.** A willing school adult tells a safe move to the whole class (consent asked before; the visitor may decline any question and chooses what is shared). The rotating six ask: four the topic questions, two a follow-up; the record sheet names them. Every child records the answer in their own four boxes with a word or a drawing. The teacher writes a class copy on chart paper. The visitor writes, or dictates for the teacher to write, a short account in their own words for day 76 and approves it.
- **6 min. Check our notes.** Pairs check their boxes against the class copy: underline what the teller said. Anything guessed is rubbed out. The rotating six are heard on X74b (about 4.5 minutes): "Mr. Quill said he came for a job. Ask him one follow-up question about that."
- **3 min. Exit ticket.** X74 on paper; X74b was heard while the pairs checked.
- **2 min. Revise.** Each child changes one question on their sheet, using the cue.
- **3 min. Home interview.** Home sheets go out (any trusted adult; a child with no home path uses the school round; back by day 76).

Fallback if no visitor can come: the teacher tells their own safe move, or reads a teller card printed "invented for practice"; then day 76's written account is also invented, said aloud.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Which of our four questions asks when?" (day 73); "What did the teller say, and what did I guess?" (day 74); "Could you explain that part?" (day 75, said to the teller).
- Written task, day 73: the interview sheet, four question frames and one question of the child's own; practice, with a written cue.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: one question for each of where, how, why and when; every question is about the move; one question of the child's own; each can be asked of a teller who may pass. Marked shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Meets: "Where did you live before? How did you get here? Why did you come? What year did you come to this town? What do you remember best about the trip?" Does not: "Where are you from? That is all." and "How old are you?"

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with a picture: culture, interview, teller, question, follow-up, faithful note, pass. Frames: "My question is ___." "The teller said ___." "Could you explain that part?" "I would rather not say." Home-language questions allowed; the teller or a partner may translate.
- **Access.** Large print; movable cards; described pictures; a child may point, draw or dictate; a seated or standing round; a child who cannot write holds a partner's pencil or dictates.
- **Check accommodations.** Every ask is read aloud, neutrally; a child who passes in public answers to the teacher alone; pointing, drawing and home-language answers are accepted; no timers are shown.
- **Reading load.** Labels only, about 40 words a day; the teacher reads every card, caption and prompt.
- **Extension, same standard.** "What is a fifth question that would help us understand a move, and who could answer it?"

Day 75: the child interviews the grown-up and writes only what was said.
