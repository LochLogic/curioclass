# Section 3.1: People and the land they live with


Grade 2 children read short labels; the teacher reads every card, caption, credit and prompt aloud. Answers are circled, pointed to, said, drawn or a few written words. Days run 36 to 38 minutes. Marlow, Gap Town, the Place Cards, the Changing Land Cards and every practice card are ours, with invented places and children, printed "invented for practice"; the teacher says so aloud. The Terraced Field photograph and the flood and recovery cards rest on pages the unit plan names (field 10). No sources.csv exists for this course yet, so every real line is marked source to check and is worded only to what the checked page will support. Pip the puppet says each wrong idea, one line at a time.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.2.T2.4, this section's share (days 30, 31).** "Explain and describe how people interact with the physical environment, including why people settle near particular bodies of water and landforms." Day 30: why people settle by a river and at a mountain gap. Day 31: how people adapt. Explain and describe are said and drawn, with a reason.
- **D2.Geo.5.K-2 (days 30, 31).** "Describe how what people do changes the cultural and environmental character of places." Day 30: changing the land (a terrace, a dam). Day 31: adapting, and where the two overlap.
- **D2.Geo.8.K-2 (day 32).** "Compare how people in different kinds of communities use nearby and faraway environments to meet daily needs." Four communities, near and far.
- **D2.Geo.12.K-2 (day 33).** "Identify ways a disaster can affect the people living in a place." A flood, an earthquake, a hurricane: one effect on people and one help.
- **Limits that bite here.** No disaster simulation and no engineering design. A place offers possibilities; it does not decide what every person there does. Disasters are told calmly with pictures of places and helpers, never of hurt people; no child is asked about their own experience, home or family. No community type is ranked above another.
- **Connections.** Grade 1 landforms and community problems (retrieval, day 30). Unit 2: region (day 31), map key and compass rose on the cards (day 32), the three questions for sources (days 30 and 33). Section 3.3 picks up day 32's near and far (D2.Geo.9, D2.Eco.4).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Adapt (change what we do, wear or build to fit a place), change the land (reshape land or water) and their overlap: a house raised on posts is both. Settling has reasons; a pretty view is not one, and naming a feature does not explain its effects. Why disasters are told calmly, and how day 33's practice explanation feeds item 4 (cue on day 34, revised on days 35 and 36 in section 3.2).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** items 1 (MA.2.T2.4), 2 (D2.Geo.5.K-2) and 3 (D2.Geo.8.K-2), and with section 3.2 item 4 (D2.Geo.12.K-2). All sit on day 44; the sitting is written in section 3.4. Days 32 and 33 are practice, never scored as items 3 and 4.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks one picture in the last two minutes; the teacher reads the prompt aloud, one ask at a time. The day's rotating six (about 45 seconds each, one spoken ask) are heard in the supported-work block. Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. Every case is new (rule 4).
- **X30 and X30b (day 30).** Mark, Reed Lake Town (invented; renamed from Pine Lake Town, which clashes with unit 1's Pine Lake Campground): three pictures, a lake with fish and a boat, a field of bright flowers, a pink sunset. X30 "Circle the picture that shows a reason people could settle here." Answer: the lake. Wrong: the flowers or the sunset, a view only (B). Spoken, the six, X30b, on a new card, "a town drains a wet, muddy field and plants grass there for a ball field": "What did draining change?" Answer: the field is dry now, so children can play on it. Wrong: "It is a field" or names the thing and nothing it did (P).
- **X31 and X31b (day 31).** Mark, Windy Shore Card (invented; replaces Dry Flats Card, whose hat and shirt were adapting cards of a hot place already taught on Hot Flats): a cap with ear flaps, a scarf wound round the face, a bridge over a creek. X31 "Circle the picture that shows people changing the land." Answer: the bridge. Wrong: the cap or the scarf, adapting taken for changing the land (A). Spoken, the six, X31b: "A school in a rainy place builds a covered walkway between two buildings. Did the school adapt, change the land, or both?" Answer: both (a roof over the walk fits the rain; a new walkway is built on the land). Wrong: only one of the two (A). The plan's hot-place roof is replaced because shade is taught on Hot Flats.
- **X32 and X32b (day 32).** Mark, Hill Farm Card (invented): a small map with a key; the farm, an orchard one square away, a cloth town twelve squares away; two arrows of equal length toward the farm, pears from the orchard and cloth from the town; the farm's own field grows oats. X32 "Hill Farm's own field grows oats. Circle one thing the farm gets from outside its own fields." Answer: the pears or the cloth. Wrong: the oats field, as if the farm needed nothing from elsewhere (C). Spoken, the six, X32b: "Which thing comes from far away?" Answer: the cloth, because the map shows the town far off. Wrong: "all of it is from near" (C). The stem is split into one ask each, and the oats field is a distractor so that a child who does not read the arrows cannot guess by circling all.
- **X33 and X33b (day 33).** Mark, Library Flood Case (invented for practice): three pictures, a wet library floor, a muddy chair, a child at a closed library door holding a book bag. X33 "Circle the picture that shows how the flood changed what a person could do." Answer: the child at the door. Wrong: the floor or the chair, damage only (D). Spoken, the six, X33b: "A flood closes the bridge in a town. Who is affected?" Answer: the people who cross it (to school, the shop, the doctor). Wrong: "the bridge is broken" (D).

## 6. Days

## Day 30

Three asks, one at a time, answered to a partner then shared: "Name the land and water." "How could people make this smaller?" "How could a class choose together?" - **11 min. Supported work.** The day's six at the teacher's table, one ask each (about 5 minutes). The rest, in pairs, draw a line from each of six picture cards to the place or Changing Land Card it belongs with, and say why to a partner.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** X30 mark; X30b spoken to the six.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The six picture cards in supported work share X30's shape, a picture sorted by whether it shows a reason to settle. Changed so they do not copy it: they are six cards drawn to four place or Changing Land cards with a because said to a partner, never three pictures on one new town; the pretty sight (leaves by a lake) is taught as no reason, and neither the flowers nor a sunset appears. The terrace and dam cards share X30b's shape, what a change did; neither shows a ditch, and the day 31 sort uses a levelled school garden, not a ditch.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the question board and the three questions; the children say "because". Tool: None. By hand: lines, circling. Evidence: X30 and X30b.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 30: "What helped people settle here?" Day 31: "Must every home look alike?" Day 32: "What does this place get from near and what from far?" Day 33: "What helped people afterward?"
- **Written task, day 33.** The practice explanation, drawn or labeled: "The Flood Card shows ___ (an effect on people). It shows ___ (a help)." A child who cannot write dictates.

Meets: a drawing of a closed road with a child turned away, and "neighbors help", with "Flood Card" written or said. Does not: a drawing of a wet building and "bad".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: settle, river, gap, adapt, change, flood, help. Frames: "People settled here because ___." "They adapt by ___." "A flood can ___ people." Pointing and home-language answers first; judge reasoning apart from English.
- **Access.** Raised-line or tactile terrace and dam cards and a described photograph for a child who cannot see; a partner holds a card for a child who cannot; a scribe writes a child's own words.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts neutrally; accept pointing, speech or a scribe; enlarge pictures. Do not assess handwriting or decoding. Record the support used.
- **Reading load.** Labels of one to three words; at most 150 words a child reads in a day; every caption is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child secure on day 31 finds a place on a card where people both adapt and change the land and says how.
