# Year spine: grade 6 ELA (draft for Cory's review)

Drafted 2026-11-11. Not approved, not in data/units.csv, nothing here is built or public. This is item 1 of "What a
finished course holds" in docs/ROADMAP.md for the second proving course: every teaching day of the year, in order,
with the reason for the order. It follows the layout of docs/courses/math-4-spine.md and applies the lessons of
docs/reviews/math-4-spine-2026-11-11.md from the start.

**How to read the numbers.** Counts of standards, tools and Reading Room pieces come from the repo files. Every day
count, minute count and share is my estimate, marked (est.) where it first appears. None is copied from a program.

## What to check

1. **The shape.** The year is six topic stretches, each built on a set of texts, with reading, writing, speaking
   and language taught through those texts. This is not the order of data/sequence.csv, which lists modules one
   skill at a time. The five differences are marked **changed** below.
2. **The topics.** I chose them to fit what the Reading Room can hold (public domain texts). Three of the six need
   texts the Reading Room does not have today. The topics are proposals, and any of them can be swapped without
   changing the standards or the day counts.
3. **The 50-minute period and the 150-day year.** Both are estimates. See "Least sure" at the end.
4. **Unit size.** Units are 1 to 5 days as the roadmap asks, but an ELA unit cannot be lifted out and taught alone
   (see "The units inside each stretch").

## The year at a glance

- **150 teaching days** (est.): 142 in units, 8 cumulative check and respond days.
- **Period: 50 minutes** (est.). A usual day: a 10-minute opener (independent reading three days a week, language
  or vocabulary two days a week), a 35-minute lesson, a 5-minute written exit question. The year is 125 hours.
- **8 stretches, 55 units.** An opening stretch, six topic stretches, a closing stretch. A stretch is one topic and
  one set of texts, 18 to 24 days. A unit is a package of 1 to 5 days inside it.
- **How the 150 days split by the main work of the day** (est.; most days mix strands, so this counts the lead one):

| Main work | Days | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Reading literature | 33 | 22 percent |
| Reading informational text and literary nonfiction | 36 | 24 percent |
| Writing: narrative 12, argument 11, informative 10, research 9 | 42 | 28 percent |
| Speaking and listening (seminars, a debate, listening to a speech, presentations) | 10 | 7 percent |
| Language (whole days; the openers are extra) | 10 | 7 percent |
| Checks and respond days (start of year 2, end of stretch 5, cumulative 8) | 15 | 10 percent |
| Class routines 2, year-end mixed work 2 | 4 | 3 percent |
| **Total** | **150** | |

- **Reading days are 48 percent literature and 52 percent informational** (33 and 36 of 69). The outside criteria
  ask for about 45 and 55 in grades 6 to 8, counted by texts. The count by texts cannot be made until texts are
  chosen.
- **Writing: 13 pieces**, 6 taken through the full process and 7 written in one sitting. By type: 4 argument,
  5 informative, 4 narrative (31, 38 and 31 percent). The process pieces are 2, 2 and 2. The outside criteria ask
  for about 35, 35 and 30. Three short research projects.
- **Most days are print or discussion lessons, not tool time.** The 23 tools plus one new tool add up to about
  12 hours (est., from the minutes in the shape files), under a tenth of the 125 hours. The tool column below is
  not the course. The texts, the questions about them and the writing are the course.
- **What exists today:** 23 tools, all at spec stage and none built (9 grade 6 specs fixed, 14 band specs not yet
  fixed), 1 new tool approved with no spec, no unit, and no Reading Room text that has been through the Enrich
  step. Every day below is a gap day until its unit is written.

**The limits of grade 6.** Checks and tasks stay inside these, so nothing tests grade 7: one theme or central
idea, not two (RL.6.2, RI.6.2); how the author builds one narrator's point of view, not a contrast of several
(RL.6.6); telling supported claims from unsupported ones, not judging whether the reasoning is sound (RI.6.8,
SL.6.3); arguments with claims, reasons and evidence, with no counterclaim required (W.6.1); basic source details,
not a formal citation style (W.6.8); short research projects (W.6.7).

## The units inside each stretch

Units are 1 to 5 days, as the roadmap asks. ELA differs from math in one way that matters: a unit here reads part
of a stretch's text set and cannot be lifted out and taught alone. The stretch is the real teaching package: one
topic, one set of texts, one word list, one main piece of writing. The readiness check and the end check sit at
the stretch, not in each unit. Whether data/units.csv should hold these 55 rows or the 8 stretches is a question
for Cory.

## The checks

Checking is 15 of 150 days, 10 percent (est.): 2 at the start of the year, 5 end-of-stretch check days, and 4
cumulative checks each followed by a respond day.

**At the stretch, not in every unit.** Each of the six topic stretches opens with a 10-minute readiness check on
the standards in its "Builds on" row. Each ends with an end check inside its last unit: a short new text on the
stretch's topic with questions, about half a period, and the other half spent going over it. The stretch's process
piece, scored with a rubric, is the other half of the evidence. Every day ends with a written exit question.

**Five cumulative checks.** Each is one period: about 20 minutes on a new text or pair of texts, about 30 minutes
of writing in one sitting about the same texts (est.; see "Least sure"). The next day is for responding: the class
goes over it, and the key says "if many missed this, do that".

What each needs, and how ELA differs from the math checks:

- **Not from generators alone.** A reading check needs a fixed new text and questions written for that text.
  Each check needs two forms (A and B) so it can be given again. Only the language and vocabulary items can be
  redrawn from generators.
- **Every item names its standard**, and every key says what a wrong answer suggests and what to do next.
- **Speaking and listening is not on paper.** It is checked with a one-page look-for sheet at the seminar (4.9,
  7.6), the debate (5.9), the speech (5.4) and the presentations (6.8).
- **Accommodations.** Directions and writing prompts may be read aloud and time extended. Reading the passage
  aloud changes what a reading check tests, so the key says when that is and is not allowed.
- **Results** live on a printable class record sheet, as in math. There are no accounts.

## What is missing today, said plainly

- **No unit is written.** All 150 days are gap days.
- **No tool is built.** All 23 are specs. Fourteen are band specs that are not yet fixed and have no grade 6 path;
  they carry the homes of 25 standards (the shape file's count). The argument drafting tool has no spec.
- **Tools are about 12 of 125 hours.** The rest is texts, questions, discussion and writing, all of which a unit
  must supply in print. A text-dependent question set for each anchor text is the largest piece of unwritten work
  in this course, and no shelf holds it today.
- **Texts.** No grade 6 text has been through Enrich, so none has a text record. Stretches 3, 5 and 6 have no
  text on file at all, and stretch 6 probably needs explainers that Curio Class writes itself. Stretches 2, 4 and
  7 have anchors on file but each lacks a second text (a play scene, poems, a second account). There is no modern
  nonfiction and no modern fiction, because of copyright. A reviewer may mark a year of texts all written before
  1930, and may ask where authors and people of many backgrounds appear. The topic choices here do not settle that.
- **No audio or video** for RL.6.7, RI.6.7 and SL.6.2 beyond photographs, maps and tables.
- **No writing rubrics, model pieces or student samples** for any of the three types. The outside criteria ask
  for all three.
- **No cold-read check texts.** The five cumulative checks and five end checks need about 15 short new texts in
  two forms each (est.).
- **Word lists.** No stretch word list exists, and I did not check which grade 6 words the Word Wall holds.
- **Grade 5 underneath.** "Builds on" is true of the standards. I did not check the grade 5 ELA ledger, and the
  grade 3 to 5 band tools are not fixed.
- **English learner supports, extensions, family notes and supply lists** belong to the units and are not planned
  here. ELA leans harder on language than math does, so the sentence frames and word support matter more.
- **Sensitivity.** The novel (animal violence), the child labor sources (children hurt at work) and two of the
  three memoirs on file (boarding schools) need a fresh sensitivity read before they are fixed in a unit.
- **No ELA rubric is on file.** docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md has no ELA twin. The criteria I read are linked below; a
  summary in our own words should be filed before the expert reads this spine.
- **The day counts are untested**, and no teacher of grade 6 has seen them.

## Least sure

1. **The topics for stretches 3, 5 and 6.** They fit public domain sources well, but none of those sources is on
   file or verified, and child labor as the argument topic is a judgment call. If the texts cannot be found, the
   stretches keep their standards and days and take a new topic.
2. **Six stretches of 18 to 24 days.** The well-reviewed programs I read about use four long modules or six units
   of five to seven weeks. Six shorter stretches cover all three writing types twice but may be too short to
   build deep knowledge. Five stretches of about 27 days is the other honest option.
3. **One period for a cumulative check, and 50 minutes for everything.** Twenty minutes of reading and thirty of
   writing is tight. If the pilot shows it does not fit, each check needs two days, which costs four days from the
   stretches. One program I read about assumes 90 minutes a day; in a 50-minute period this spine has less
   reading time than that program does.
