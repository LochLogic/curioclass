# Scope and sequence: Career Pathways, grades 9 to 12

Course id career-pathways-grades-9-to-12. Grade 10 standards (the grade 10 rows of data/progressions/cte.csv), taught to
any student in grades 9 to 12. Draft 2026-11-26, written from the frame (docs/courses/career-pathways-grades-9-to-12/frame.md),
its three reviews and their fixes, and the Desk answers of 2026-11-26. The day-by-day plan is
data/plans/career-pathways-grades-9-to-12/days.csv (96 rows).

## Calendar

96 sessions of 45 minutes, three a week for 32 weeks. A course day n sits on school day ceil(n x 160 / 96), and week =
(school day - 1) // 5 + 1. Checks fall where the frame and CALENDAR.md put them: course-level check 1 on days 42 and 43
(school days 70 and 72, weeks 14 and 15), course-level check 2 on days 95 and 96 (school days 159 and 160, week 32, the
finals week). Grades 9 to 12 follow the finals-week rule only; there is no stagger rule.

## Every day

- A field task day closes with five minutes to collect the piece, the job card and the plan page. The next session opens
  with a five-minute fix: each student revises the last piece's decision or data line from the ticked look-for strip.
- Every plan is private, read only by the teacher. It is scored on its reasoning, never on the job, route or circumstances
  chosen. No task asks about family income, work, immigration, record or health.
- Every figure carries its source and year. The data sheets are dated for the school year and say "check these figures
  this school year".
- Paper path every day. Practice 5's shared-device rule stays: five pairs at the one shared device, about five minutes
  each, the rest on paper (days 55 to 57). A student who cannot use any device that year has the digital part of practice
  5 recorded as not met (Cory, 2026-11-26).
- A sensitive sub-cluster keeps the frame's family note, health's steps for when a student tells you something on its
  teacher page, and the leave-out swap to another field task in the same cluster. A swapped id is named as not claimed.
  Unit 3 carries the pay-gap flag on every day.
- No row owns a first job's safety and rights; they are practiced on the teacher page on day 72 (Cory, 2026-11-26).
- Supplies and safe use of space are named in each day's note. Field tasks are paper plans. The only hands-on test is
  card and school coins, done seated, with the coins counted out and back in and five minutes of clean-up (days 16, 17,
  25 and 26 where Advanced Manufacturing is a shared cluster, and packet days 34 and 35 for a student whose own cluster
  it is). Unit 6's tests use paper, card or school stock only, with the
  hazard written on the brief and checked first (day 83).
- Read aloud, a scribe, an oral or written answer, and a card option are offered on every Check.

## Unit 1. Where my plan stands (days 1 to 12)

Compelling question: "What do I know now about myself and the world of work, and which three clusters will I study?"
The plan is reopened or begun, and the class chooses its two shared clusters. Why the days fall in this order: the self
comes before the world of work (the lens's order), and the plan, goals, values and sorts come before the ballot because
the choice of clusters uses them all.

- Day 1 hook and the six models; family note; readiness. Day 2 dated goals (CD1.A3).
- Day 3 values (CD2.A5, R5). Day 4 learner moments (ALL2.A2, R2), both private.
- Day 5 study log begun and school plan reviewed (ALL1.R5).
- Days 6 and 7 three sorts and the grouping that helps (CD3.R4).
- Days 8 and 9 field task: the class ballot, then each student's own cluster by both decision methods (CD2.R2); the
  study log rated in draft before day 9's collect (ALL1.R3).
- Day 10 plan day; the study log rating revised from its feedback. Day 11 end. Day 12 respond, unit 2 readiness and
  family note.

## Unit 2. Three clusters, broad to narrow (days 13 to 41)

Compelling question: "What work is done in each of my three clusters, how do people train for it, and which part of it
could be mine?" Day 13 opens with it (two minutes); the readiness reteach is on day 14. Why the days fall in this order:
the two shared blocks come before the packet, so the routine is taught twice with the teacher before a student runs it
alone, and the unit comes before the pay-gap unit and the job pick because a student meets real work in three fields
before judging who does it or narrowing to one job.

Two shared cluster blocks (block 1 days 13 to 21, block 2 days 22 to 30), then each student's own cluster packet (days 31
to 41). A block is an explore day, six field task sessions, a plan day and a mid-unit assessment. The packet is the same
steps self-guided, with a job study step on day 38. Check-ins are split over packet days 2 and 3, 5 and 6, and 7 and 8
(days 32 and 33, 35 and 36, 37 and 38), about 90 seconds a student.

Learner rows ride on fixed sessions in every block and the packet:

- Session 3: the failed-first-try task (CD1.A4).
- Session 4: several ideas, two combined against the field's constraint (practice 8).
- Session 5: the learning strategy judged (ALL1.R4).
- Plan days: steering habits rated (CD1.R4), a piece improved and the next step (ALL1.R2), the skills log judged (CD4.R3),
  and the goals reread with one that shifted changed and its reason on the change log (CD1.R3 carried; days 20 and 29).
- Practice 9 runs on every explore day and every task with a budget.

## Course-level check 1 (days 42 and 43)

Day 42 gives a new job card from a cluster outside each student's three. The prepared cards are Travel and Leisure,
Marine Transportation, Insurance, and Ecological Research and Development. The student places the job on the cluster map
by training level, then answers "which of the three groupings (cluster, training level, work setting) helps you most to
see where this job fits a plan, and why" (CD3.R4), and reads its route, pay and outlook. Two dated data items cover a
rate and a percent (practice 9). The decision steps are run both ways on an invented choice of program and defended with
one figure, then "which method suited this choice better, and why" (CD2.R2). Check 1 samples units 1 and 2's two shared
blocks; the packet was assessed on day 40, so no packet item is set (the non-repeat rule). Day 43 is the respond day,
with unit 3's readiness.

## Unit 3. Who does which job, and why (days 44 to 54)

Compelling question: "What shapes who does each job, and what shapes my own choices?" This is the pay-gap unit. Dated
who-holds and earnings figures are read as patterns, never verdicts. The unit is built around day 47. Why the days fall
in this order: who holds the jobs and how open the student has been come first, from the student's own job cards; the
forces on a choice (luck, experience, the uncommon job) follow; society, the economy and place come after; the
stereotype case closes the teaching, so the job pick in unit 4 comes after the student has checked their own snap
judgments.

- Day 44 who holds jobs. Day 45 earnings comparison and a private count of openness (CD3.R5).
- Day 46 luck and experience (CD2.A8, R8, A6, R6). Day 47 the uncommon-job decision (CD3.A6).
- Day 48 society's needs and a downturn (CD5.A1, A2). Day 49 projections as percent change (CD5.A3) and changing work
  (ALL2.A1, R1).
- Day 50 moving or staying (CD4.A5, R5). Day 51 field task: the stereotype case and the ad rewrite (CD2.A7, R7).
- Day 52 plan day. Day 53 end. Day 54 respond, unit 4 readiness.

Sources not loaded yet: the federal tables of employment and of earnings by occupation, sex, race and ethnicity
(cpsaat11, cpsaat37, cpsaat39), the monthly jobs survey extract and the projections. The unit 3 data sheet is written
from them at the unit plan.

## Unit 4. One job, the full plan (days 55 to 67)

Compelling question: "Which one job will I study in depth, and what is my plan to its first credential?" One
sub-cluster from the three clusters and one named job in it, studied in depth and planned to the first credential. Why
the days fall in this order: the pick and the job study come before the routes, the routes before the trade-offs and the
fallback, and all of them before the full plan is drafted, because each is a line of the plan.

- Day 55 the three-cluster comparison table and chart, and the pick (practice 5). Days 56 and 57 the job study and the
  fact matrix (CD3.R2). Device turns run on days 55 to 57.
- Day 58 at least three routes (CD2.R4); no route is the default, and the military is one real route from dated federal
  pages. Day 59 the steps taken on a route against another (ALL2.A5, R5).
- Day 60 school results and the private progress check (ALL1.A1, R1), and readiness for the step after high school
  (ALL2.R4).
- Day 61 trade-offs (CD2.R9). Day 62 the fallback (practice 6).
- Days 63 and 64 field task: the full plan drafted, then checked against the data sheets with the change log (ALL2.A6,
  R6, practice 10, CD3.R1, CD1.R5); day 64 also drafts the two reflection lines (CD1.R1, CD2.R1).
- Day 65 plan day (CD1.R2; CD1.R1 and CD2.R1 revised from their feedback). Day 66 end: the scored set names a product
  per code (the full plan, the fact matrix, the routes sheet, the route-steps line, the readiness checklist, the
  trade-offs line, the reflection lines, the attachments). Day 67 respond, unit 5 readiness.

## Unit 5. Getting work (days 68 to 80)

Compelling question: "How do I show an employer, or a client, that I can do the job I chose?" Why the days fall in this
order: the resume and cover letter come first because the field task, the interview and the portfolio all use them; the
workplace week and self-employment follow; the two interview days come last before the plan day so the teacher has heard
every student once before the scored mock interview.

- Day 68 the job search and a model resume. Day 69 the resume in full (CD4.A2).
- Day 70 a cover letter and an invented application (CD4.R2). Day 71 learning outside class (ALL2.A7, R7).
- Days 72 and 73 field task: a first week in an invented workplace (CD4.A1, R1), the skills that carry (CD4.A4, R4) and
  how school widens options (ALL1.A7, R7).
- Day 74 self-employment (CD4.A6, R6). Days 75 and 76 interview skill.
- Day 77 plan day. Day 78 written end; its scored set names a product per code (the written pieces, the supervisor card
  week and skills plan, the carry-over line, the school-widens line, the outside-class entry, the self-employment line).
  Day 79 mock interview end (about 70 seconds and a ten-second changeover a student).
- Day 80 respond, unit 6 readiness.

The teacher page on day 75 states the guidance's own terms: before a conditional job offer an employer may not ask
disability-related questions, though it may ask whether you can do the job's tasks, with or without a reasonable change
(the federal equal employment agency's guidance, opened 2026-11-26). The teacher page on day 72 cites the federal safety
agency's young workers page for a safe workplace and the Wage and Hour Division's YouthRules and child labor pages for
hours and barred tasks, a stricter state law applying where there is one. These sources are not loaded yet.

## Unit 6. The applied project (days 81 to 94)

Compelling question: "Can I solve a real problem from my chosen job's field and defend it to a panel?" Teams of three or
four solve a problem from their chosen jobs' fields over five weeks, then each student defends a portfolio built around
it. Why the days fall in this order: the project uses every earlier unit, so it comes last; the problem, teams and
milestones open it, the work sessions test and check the decision, the evidence and portfolio days come before the plan
day, and the panel is the course's closing demonstration.

- Day 81 problem, teams, norms and milestones (practices 12, 4, 7, 1). Day 82 options weighed (practice 3). Day 83 the
  test and a second try.
- Day 84 the lateral check on paper (CD3.R3, ALL1.A8). It uses the reusable source sheets Curio Class writes: three
  invented sources, each with an invented owner's about page, two invented reports on it and the dated table it should
  match. One class set, one per pair, about $9 (Cory, 2026-11-26).
- Day 85 effects (practice 11). Day 86 status notes and the replan. Day 87 learning alone and information use, with the
  two reflections drafted (ALL2.R3, ALL1.R8). Day 88 evidence (CD2.R3), with the habits reflection drafted (ALL1.R6).
- Day 89 the portfolio and a model defense (CD4.K7), then a paired run-through of each defense's opening minute on the
  panel card (CD4.A7 practiced). Day 90 plan day: the three reflections revised from their feedback.
- Days 91 to 93 panels: four-minute slots, ten a session (CD4.A7). The scored set names the portfolio's list of ways to
  present oneself (CD4.K7) and the evidence line on the project brief (CD2.R3) beside the source log, reflections and
  practice attachments. Day 94 respond.

## Course-level check 2 (days 95 and 96)

Check 2 samples units 3 to 5; unit 6 was assessed on days 91 to 93, so no project item is set (the non-repeat rule).
Day 95 gives six short prompts, six to seven minutes each:
- an unseen percent change for two jobs from two dated projections, and the one plan line it would change, and how
  (CD5.A3);
- a regional pay item for an invented student's stated goal: would this move fit the goal, and why (CD4.A5, R5;
  private ground kept);
- an invented posting read against a resume line, the line judged against the posting's criteria and revised (CD4.R2;
  CD4.A2 was scored whole in unit 5 and is not claimed here);

- how the program prepares for that change and how it fits the plan (ALL2.R6, A6);
- a trade-off named, then how well the plan handles it and what the student would change (CD2.R9).

Day 96 is the respond day; the plan and portfolio go home.
