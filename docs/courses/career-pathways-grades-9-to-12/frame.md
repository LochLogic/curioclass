# Course frame: Career Pathways, grades 9 to 12

Course `career-pathways-grades-9-to-12` (catalogue id `cte-pathways`), one year, placed in grade 10 (open to grades 9 to 12 in the catalogue, typical grade 10), draft frame (2026-11-25); the scope and sequence sets the days later. It copies the shape of the career education pilot frame, docs/courses/career-exploration-grades-6-to-8/frame.md, and the pattern notes of docs/reviews/frame-career-exploration-grades-6-to-8-fixes-2026-11-25.md.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6 units: one opening unit that reopens (or starts) the plan and chooses the clusters; one long cluster unit in three blocks (two shared clusters taught whole-class, then each student's own cluster through the self-guided packet); one unit on what shapes who does which job (the pay-gap unit); one unit that narrows to one named job and writes the full plan; one unit on getting work; and the applied project with the portfolio defense.
- **Days.** 96 course days: 92 in units and 4 for the two course-level checks and their respond days. Three sessions a week of 45 minutes, 32 weeks (96 sessions, 72 hours a year), the default time of docs/courses/career-education/APPROACH.md ("Pacing", settled 2026-11-16 by Cory, two or three sessions a week; the session length and count set by the expert reader 2026-11-20, ours and untested). No national body publishes a time figure for this course that we found (APPROACH.md, searched 2026-11-20), so none is cited. Unit days: 12, 29, 11, 13, 13 and 14. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment; unit 2 runs over 25 days, so it also holds two mid-unit assessments (one after each shared cluster block). The 45 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting the work: every field task day and packet day ends with five minutes to collect the piece, the job card and the plan page, and the card-and-coin test in the Advanced Manufacturing block names five minutes of clean-up (coins counted back in). Readiness checks take ten minutes. These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them evenly over the 32 weeks, course day n on school day ceil(n x 160 / 96).
  **Why 96 fits.** Per student the load is three clusters, not fourteen: a cluster block is about nine sessions (an explore day, six field task sessions holding the cluster's four to seven sub-clusters, a plan day and the block's assessment), so the three blocks and the unit's closing days take 29 sessions, about a third of the year. The other 51 rows (the learner goals and the twelve practices) sit in five units of 11 to 14 days, weighed by load: the full plan (unit 4, 15 rows) and the applied project (unit 6, 12 rows, three panel days) are the longest; the pay-gap unit (3) is the shortest, since its ten rows are judged on short pieces and the plan. One student meets about 70 of the course's 143 rows (STANDARD.md section 3, "Pathways' width").
  **Shorter path.** A school at two sessions a week runs 64 sessions (48 hours, APPROACH.md): 60 in units (8, 20, 7, 8, 8 and 9) and the same 4 course-level days. Built from the floor up: the sessions that never fold are every unit's closing days (18: plan day, assessment and respond day in each unit, two assessment days in unit 5 and three panel days in unit 6), one shared cluster block and the packet (20, the short path's one shared cluster, settled by the lead 2026-11-22, Desk q-prog-cte-1), and one field task session in every other unit (4). That leaves 18 sessions for the other units' own teaching, so the short path has little slack, and its unit-by-unit folds are in the table after these bullets ("Shorter path, unit by unit"). No fold takes a feedback or revision day (lesson 21). The scope and sequence sets each folded session by day; if its read finds a session overloaded, the fix is a change of breadth or dose, which is Cory's call ("For the lead").
- **Day kinds.** In the words of docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md: an **explore day** (a cluster's dated data sheet and map, a worker's account, a dated table) is Understanding; a **skill day** that first teaches a career task here (the first resume, cover letter, application, the first mock interview answer, the weighted criteria table) is Understanding, because first teaching wins; a **field task day**, a **packet day** (a field task done self-guided from the packet) or a **plan day** is Application (every day that drafts or revises the piece or the plan); the assessment day and the panel days are Check; the respond day is Respond. Fluency is small: rereading a pay or outlook figure on a new field (the practice that returns, APPROACH section field 8), a second interview answer and the five-minute fix at the start of each field task session, a few minutes inside other days, never a whole day.
- **Standard.** The 2024 national career clusters framework (14 clusters, 72 sub-clusters, 12 career ready practices) as content and the 2024 National Career Development Guidelines as learner goals (STANDARD.md sections 1 to 3; ids in data/standards/cte-career-clusters-2024.txt and data/standards/ncdg-2024.txt, our own lines in ncdg-2024-words.txt). The course has 143 rows: it owns the 58 sub-clusters Career Exploration only names and 64 learner-goal indicators (on 45 rows), and carries at higher demand the 14 clusters, the 14 first-look sub-clusters and the 12 practices that Exploration owns, with the 66 Exploration indicators carried on the same 45 rows. Owned here means the course supplies every sub-cluster's teaching, field task and check for the students who meet its cluster; a student meets the sub-clusters of their three clusters, and the course page says so (STANDARD.md section 3, "Ownership"). Cluster ids are names, not verbs, and no practice or indicator sentence is quoted (STANDARD.md section 1). The values rule: no route is the default; the plan and the applied project are scored on their reasoning, never on the job or path chosen or the student's circumstances. The 5 adult-only indicators (CD3.R6, CD4.R7, CD5.R1, CD5.R2, CD5.R3) are left out with their reasons (STANDARD.md section 5).
- **Base text.** None. Sources are public labor data, each figure with its source and year: the US Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook (public domain, https://www.bls.gov/ooh/, typical entry level and training), its state and metro wage tables (Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, https://www.bls.gov/oes/, the figures by region), its employment projections (https://www.bls.gov/emp/, the ten-year outlook) and its monthly jobs survey (Current Employment Statistics, https://www.bls.gov/ces/, a field's jobs through a recent downturn); the federal occupational database O*NET (CC BY 4.0, https://www.onetonline.org/, attribution in the credit line only); the federal apprenticeship pages (https://www.apprenticeship.gov/); and a state labor office where the school adds one. Every data sheet is dated with the school year it was built for and carries the line "check these figures this school year"; each training route on a job card is checked against the handbook's typical entry level that year (STANDARD.md section 3). Figures are national unless marked local or regional. Everything else (the 14 cluster data sheets, the 72 sub-cluster field task sheets, the course's task template, the 14 packets, the full plan template, the weighted criteria table, the invented workers, postings, cases and every invented data set) Curio Class writes, each invented set marked "invented for practice".
- **Running it with one teacher** (STANDARD.md section 3, ours, untested). The two shared clusters are taught whole-class: one room on one field task with one data sheet on the same day. The own-cluster packet is self-guided: the cluster's dated data sheet, its sub-clusters' field task sheets on the course's task template, and the steps of the job study, each with its model, so each of the 14 packets stands alone. On packet days the whole class works in its own packets at once; packet days 2, 5 and 8 are check-in days, when the teacher meets each student for about 90 seconds (the step done, one figure, the next step, logged on the packet's check-in line), about 45 minutes for a class of 30. Skill days (resume, interview, the full plan) and the applied project are whole-class. The class chooses its two shared clusters in unit 1 with a weighted criteria table (g10-cd2-r2-compare-models); a school that adds a lab, an employer or a program for a cluster may put that cluster on the ballot first, never in place of the vote. A student whose own first choice is a shared cluster takes their own from the other twelve.
- **Broad to narrow.** Every student goes from a cluster to a sub-cluster to one named job studied in depth (STANDARD.md section 3, "Broad to narrow"): each cluster block ends with one sub-cluster and one job the student might narrow to; unit 4 opens with the student's pick from any of their three clusters, one sub-cluster and then one named job; the full plan (g10-all2-full-plan) narrows to that job's first credential, and the applied project (g10-prac-12-academic-skills, unit 6) takes its problem from that job's field. No job is the default.
- **Sub-cluster checks.** Each sub-cluster row's evidence is its field task piece: the decision and one line of data on the job card and the plan's changed line drafted. Every piece gets written feedback on two or three look-fors after class; the next session opens with a five-minute fix, when the student revises that piece's decision or data line from the feedback, so no piece is scored on the day it was first done. The block's plan day revises one chosen piece in full and the block's assessment scores it on the rubric, records every fixed piece against its look-fors (the sub-cluster's check, unscored beyond the record), and gives one unseen short item per sub-cluster from its data sheet (four to seven items, about three minutes each).
- **The plan and the portfolio.** The career plan and the portfolio are carried all year. A student who took Career Exploration may bring the grade 8 plan and portfolio, never required; every student builds this course's plan on the full plan template in unit 1. Every unit's plan day returns written feedback on the unit's pieces and plan lines, and the student revises one piece of their choice and writes the plan's changed lines. The plan is private: it stays with the student, is read only by the teacher, and is never shown to the class.
- **Placement and grade.** Placed in grade 10, the typical grade (Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-career-1). A school may run it in grade 9 (STANDARD.md sections 3 and 7): it then swaps the grade 10 ELA leans for `e9-w-informative` and `e9-sl-presentation`, treats Exploration (grade 8) as the year before, so its rows open as retrieval, not readiness, and teaches the Algebra I leans alongside rather than before (each task then carries its own worked example of the display or percent change it needs). A school may run it in grade 11 or 12: the leans are then all taught before, and the counts below still start from grade 10. The counts in this frame start from grade 10.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Career Exploration is an optional course, so no student is assumed to have taken it (lesson 32): every Pathways row's handoff from Exploration is a seam by course order with a fallback. Each row carries its grade 8 part at higher demand, so the K and A parts are taught again in context here, and unit 1 introduces every model from nothing for a student who never met it (the plan, the job card, the cluster map, the decision steps card, the data table and the portfolio; the lateral-reading model in unit 6). For a student who took Exploration, its rows were last used in grade 8, two grades back (the Exploration unit and days of each are in Seams), so each unit opens with a readiness check, in the words of sequence-write.md rule 2: ten minutes, given before the unit opens so its results shape day 1, with a ten-minute reteach that models the same reading. Unit 1's sits in the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook; unit 3's in the last ten minutes of check 1's respond day (course day 43); every other unit's in the last ten minutes of the previous unit's respond day. Day 1 of units 2 to 6 stays clean for the hook and the compelling question. Each readiness check samples the grade 8 rows its unit's rows assume and always holds the two items of APPROACH.md unit field 4 (a table or a percent, and a dated figure). The cross-subject leans count the same way: the math grade 7 rows (`m7-percent-problems`, `m7-unit-rate-fractions`, `m7-compare-populations`, last used in grade 7) and social studies grade 8 `ss8-public-budget` are two or more grades back, so they get readiness items (units 2 and 3); the Algebra I rows (grade 9, the year before) open as retrieval tasks inside the first day that uses each (units 2, 3, 4 and 6, named in the Units table); ELA grade 9's `e9-w-informative` and `e9-sl-presentation` are retrieval where ELA grade 10 has not yet taught its own (Cross-subject). The computer science, library and SEL leans are optional courses' rows, so each is a bridge the unit teaches (lesson 32). The scope gate's count governs, and the scope fix pass updates this bullet.
- **Scored evidence.** Each unit's end-of-unit assessment is a product, never a quiz about clusters: the piece the student chose and revised and the plan's changed lines, scored on a rubric with two or three look-fors per practice and two sample pieces at different levels, plus three to five unseen short items on reading data (unit 2's blocks: one per sub-cluster, above). The piece is drafted on a field task, packet or skill day, gets written feedback after class, and is revised on the plan day before the assessment day scores it; no score comes from a day a topic is first taught. One full performance per assessment day: unit 5 has two assessment days (the written pieces, then the mock interview) and unit 6 three panel days (each student defends once). **Practice evidence.** Portfolio choice never waives a practice: the scored set is the chosen revised piece, the revised plan lines, and a short evidence attachment for each practice placed in the unit that the chosen piece does not show (an excerpt of the other piece or the teacher's record of it, with its look-fors). The attachments by unit: unit 2, the idea list, the combination and its test against the field's constraint (practice 8) and the budget and wage-against-training sheet (9) from whichever field task holds them; unit 4, the comparison table and chart (5) and the fallback (6), if the full plan's scored lines do not show them; unit 5, the interview record (2) beside the written pieces; unit 6, the weekly status notes and the slip with its fix (1), the team's norms and the disagreement record (4) and the milestone plan with its buffer and replan (7). Units 1 and 3 place no practice. Each attachment gets written feedback with the pieces and one revision on the plan day, so it is never scored on the day it was first taught.
- **Paper path.** Every task runs on paper: printed data sheets, field task sheets, the packets, the cluster map, the job cards, the weighted criteria table, the full plan template, the resume and cover letter templates. Where a day offers a tool (a dated job profile, a pay or outlook table reader, a private plan template, a mock-interview question bank), the paper path does the same task in full, and no tool needs a student account or stores a plan, an interest or an answer off the device (APPROACH.md, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Devices.** None per student; one shared device for practice 5. One shared teacher screen or projector shows a profile or table on some explore days (the projector room of APPROACH section field 10). Practice 5 (digital tools and data) is the one carried code whose task needs technology: in unit 4 each pair takes a turn of about five minutes at one shared device with a free offline spreadsheet (enter the three-cluster comparison rows with source and year, make one chart, read the decision it supports) while the class builds and charts the same table on paper, and each student names one privacy step (no names or personal facts in the file, saved only on the school device). A school with a class set runs it in pairs at the devices. The paper path does the whole table, chart and decision by hand; a student who cannot use any device that year meets the digital operation part of practice 5 as not met that year, and the teacher record says so (this is the last course that carries it; "For the lead"). No generated-text tool is used: unit 6 checks a supplied machine-made summary on paper (g10-cd3-r3-rate-sources), and Data Science and Artificial Intelligence describes automated tools and checks a supplied output, never runs one.
- **Family note.** One per unit in English and Spanish (APPROACH.md unit field 9): what the unit explores, what a student may take home (the job card, the plan page, the packet), and one question about a person's own work, to ask a family member or instead any trusted adult, or to answer for an invented worker or from a public interview or profile, so no student is made to reveal a family's work status (Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-career-1); sharing is the student's choice. A student 18 or over gets the family note themselves and makes their own leave-out choice (AGENTS.md).
- **What a school or family may leave out.** A section on one of the six sensitive sub-clusters of STANDARD.md section 5 (Military and National Security, Behavioral and Mental Health, Emergency Response, Judicial Systems, Public Safety, Resource Extraction). Each is a field task session in a shared block, or a packet step; the student left out does a same-cluster swap in that session (another sub-cluster's field task of the same cluster at greater depth, or its program-of-study map for a second job), and that sub-cluster's id is named on the course page and in the unit's family note as not claimed that year for that student or class. If the class's shared clusters include Public Service and Safety or Healthcare and Human Services, the family note for unit 2 names the sensitive sub-clusters ahead of the block. A packet on one of these sub-clusters follows the school's choices, with the same-cluster swap offered.
- **Sensitive ground.** No task asks about a student's or family's income, immigration or work status, a criminal record or health (STANDARD.md section 6). Job models are invented workers from many groups; one worker from a group seldom shown in each field is met in every cluster block, disabled workers doing the work among them across the year. The full plan's costs are a program's published costs, never a family's ability to pay. A student's resume uses a school address or none. Unit 3 is the pay-gap unit (it holds g10-cd3-a6-uncommon-in-plan with g10-cd3-r5-open-to-jobs and g10-cd2-stereotype-check), so every one of its sections carries health's steps for when a student tells you something (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page, as does every section or packet step in unit 2 on Behavioral and Mental Health, Emergency Response, Judicial Systems or Public Safety, and any unit 4 job study or unit 6 project a student takes in those fields.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md, kind `sensitivity`); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Communities and sources per unit are in the last column of the Units table: disabled workers (every unit), first-generation students (units 1 and 4), the six sensitive sub-clusters and their communities in all 14 packets and both shared blocks (2), the pay-gap unit (3), rural and tribal students who stay close to home (3), the military route (2 and 4), job seekers' rights and students who already work (5), and any applied project in a sensitive field (6).
- **Safety read.** Unit 2 (the field task sheets of all 14 clusters, read once as a set: the card-and-coin bracket test, the paper drone survey, the paper lighting cue sheet, the paper dilution series, any seed or soil task a school adds, the food science batch sheet with no tasting) and unit 6 (any test a student's applied project runs) get the safety agent's read before the course is called finished, against these guides by title, each free to read: Talking Safety, the young worker safety curriculum of the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/talkingsafety/); the young workers pages of the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (https://www.osha.gov/young-workers); the position statement Safety and School Science Instruction (National Science Teaching Association, https://www.nsta.org/nstas-official-positions/safety-and-school-science-instruction) for hands-on hazards in general; and, for soil and seeds, Food Safety for Your School Gardens, Classrooms, and Greenhouses (University of Maryland Extension, FS-1077, https://dev.extension.umd.edu/resource/food-safety-your-school-gardens-classrooms-and-greenhouses-fs-1077). All four were opened 2026-11-25 by the pilot frame's two reads; the safety agent opens each again before its read and replaces any that has moved, keeping the title. A student sign-off form is never cited as guidance. The last column of the Units table gives each hands-on unit's hazards and controls in a "Safety (days.csv words)" sentence.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** A grade 12 section teaches to week 32: unit 6's panels fall in weeks 31 and 32 and check 2 on school days 159 and 160, the finals week. Where seniors leave up to two weeks early, the six sessions of weeks 31 and 32 fold: unit 6's panel days and respond day move one week earlier into the place of its two last work sessions (the short path's unit 6 folds), and check 2 runs on the school's last senior days, its respond day as the plan and portfolio sent home. A section with seniors and others runs the full calendar and gives the seniors check 2 early.
- **Literacy and other side standards.** None claimed. A resume, a cover letter, an application, a factual report, an interview answer and a defense to a panel are practiced here, not owned; ELA grades 9 and 10 own the leans named in Cross-subject.
- **Reading level.** A job profile or worker account is at most 150 words at grade 10 level; longer sources are read aloud (APPROACH.md section field 9).
