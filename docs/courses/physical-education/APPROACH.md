# Physical education: approach

**Approved by Cory 2026-11-21 (Desk q-prework-open-1). Written 2026-11-16, fixed 2026-11-19 after a cold read
(docs/reviews/prework-physical-education-read-2026-11-19.md); fields placed by number and sensitive ground gathered

physical education. It fills the unit and section plans' subject slots (docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md) under the shared
page docs/APPROACH.md. The standard, the grade-outcome rule and the appropriate practice points are in
docs/courses/physical-education/STANDARD.md; the bar is the PE part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md; the lens is
docs/agents/lenses/physical-education.md. Fields not named on this page (unit fields 1, 3, 4 and 6; section fields

## What a PE unit carries (unit field 2)

- **The standards**: each SHAPE indicator id with its action in our own words (data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt,
  second column; the indicator's wording is never quoted).
- **The skill or skill theme** and its key parts (cues): for example, overhand throw with side to target, step
  with the opposite foot, follow through.
- **The setting it moves to**: from practice alone, to changing conditions, to a small-sided game, a dance or a
  routine.
- **The grade outcome per standard** the unit serves (STANDARD.md 3), and how each is checked.

## Kinds of day (principle 2)

- **Understanding.** A skill or concept shown and named in cues, then tried at once. Talk is short; moving is
  most of the lesson.
- **Fluency.** Many tries of a skill with feedback on one cue: stations, partner tasks, task cards. No lines.
- **Application.** The skill used in a changing setting: a small-sided game, a dance sequence, a fitness
  circuit, a tactic in play.
- **Check.** The unit's check (unit field 5).
- **Respond.** Acts on a check: more practice on the cue marked "not yet", in a new task.

## The end-of-unit assessment (unit field 5)

Each unit's check has a skill task (the main cue observed with a short guide) and a knowledge task on movement or
fitness concepts; conduct is marked once in the unit. Growth is against the student's own baseline, never against
classmates (criterion 7). The skill task repeats the cue of the unit's baseline watch (section field 3).

## Marking and response (unit field 7)

The skill task and conduct are marked by the teacher watching against the cue guide, never self-marked by hands up.
The respond-day groups come from the teacher's met, partly and not yet marks by cue.

## Space, supplies and tools (unit field 8; instruments and a room with nothing in section field 5)

- **Space and equipment**: a gym, a field or a small room, with a low-equipment version and a wet-weather version
  (bar criterion 5).
- **An ordinary room** (C14): a clear space about the size of a room, a few soft balls or rolled socks, a rope or
  string, and a line (tape, string or chalk). Nothing to buy. Class of thirty: stations in groups of three or four,
  one item a pair. The teacher needs no special training; each unit's cue card says what to look for.
- **Safe space**: the clear-space size (arm's length all round, one throwing direction everyone faces); the
  surface (soft or hard) and what changes on a hard floor or wet grass; a water break in warm weather; equipment
  checked before the lesson. Safety facts are sourced; a fact with no source is marked "from memory" (criterion 4).
- **What a tool may and may not do.** A tool may show a skill's cues by video or picture, run a timer or a music
  track for a lesson with a stated purpose, hold task cards, and give practice with feedback on fitness knowledge.
  It may not replace moving, judge a student's movement, rank students, or collect fitness data about them.

## Family note (unit field 9)

A family note per unit, in English and Spanish, is written when the unit is finished. It offers short active breaks
and home activity (Pacing, below).

## Sources (unit field 10)

Course pages say the course has not been tried with children and claim no effect on fitness or health. Each course
has a one-page logic model. What this page rests on: the appropriate practice guidelines (2009), pages 1 to 12 and
22 to 25, and PECAT 2019's grade tables for K to 5, 6 to 8 and 9 to 12 (read 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-19; STANDARD.md
sections 3 and 4); the rest is from memory and marked so.

## Standards and practices (section field 1)

Cue cards take the place of math's practices. Each section names the SHAPE standard and indicator ids it works on
(our wording in data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt), and the cue or concept of the day. What the teacher page must
explain includes the teacher note for any sensitive ground the section touches (below).

## The daily evidence (section field 3)

- **Closure** (the last minutes): each student shows or says one thing: the day's cue performed once while the
  teacher watches a named group, a self-check held privately (a card on the desk, a mark on the student's own
  sheet), or a one-line exit ticket on a concept in grades 3 to 12.
- **The teacher's mark**: met, partly or not yet on the day's one cue for a rotating group of students. No grade,
  no rank.
- **Baseline and growth**: the unit's first skill day has a short watch of the unit's main cue for the whole class
  in rotating groups, marked met, partly or not yet. The Check day repeats the same cue, so each student has two
  marks and the growth is the change.
- **Conduct** is marked against the unit's stated behaviours (taking turns, using equipment safely, helping a
  partner), taken from the S3 indicators, never against dress, attendance or effort alone.
- **Fitness**: when fitness is measured, the student keeps their own record, on paper or on their own device
  only, and sets a goal. Results are private and never graded.

The next lesson's instant activity is chosen for the "not yet" cue.

## What is carried (section field 5)

The model is the teacher's demonstration with the cue card. Students then hold it with the cue card or a partner
who watches for one cue. Each unit plan names the day the cue card goes away: the student performs and names the
cues from memory. A new skill is first tried with the teacher watching, never first met in a game (C7). What a room
with nothing does: the low-equipment and wet-weather versions named in unit field 8.

## How a day runs (section field 6)

Every day, whatever its kind, follows the lesson shape in STANDARD.md 4: instant activity, warm-up, focus,
cool-down, closure. At least half of each lesson is moderate to vigorous activity.

## Practice that returns (section field 8)

Skills return in later units in new settings (the overhand throw returns in a striking unit and a target game).
Fitness concepts return in every warm-up and cool-down. Each grade's outcomes build on last grade's.

## Supports (section field 9)

- Every lesson, and every closure and check task, has an adaptation for disabilities and for a range of skill:
  equipment size, distance, speed, rules, roles (criterion 6).
- A student with a short-term injury has a role that fits what the family or the school nurse has said they may
  do: a moving task for the parts that are fine, otherwise a helping or observing role (cue watcher, keeper of
  their own count). If they have been told to rest, they rest and get a seated role. Nobody is sent to the bench
  as a punishment.
- **English learners** (C11): each unit names its movement words (cues, body parts, direction words), taught with a
  picture or demonstration and a sentence frame ("My step is with the ___ foot."). A student slow to say a cue can
  show it; the check marks movement and understanding apart, so a language gap is not marked as a skill gap.
- **Reading load**: task cards are picture-led, read aloud in K to 2. **Extension**: the same cue in a harder
  setting (distance, speed, a moving partner), never the next grade's outcome (C10).
- Groups are made by the teacher, quickly and privately; no public team picks.
- No exercise as punishment, no elimination, no human targets.

## Two rooms (section field 10)

One child at home: partner tasks use a parent, a sibling or a wall, a target, or the child's own count against
yesterday; no team game is needed. One projector or none: every day runs from a printed cue card, and a screen only
shows a skill picture.

## Sensitive ground

These rules gather what the PE pages and progression rows already say; the cultural and emotion rows point here. A
section that touches one carries its teacher note in section field 1. Units with cultural or disability content are read by the sensitivity agent before they go
live (docs/FOUNDATION.md section 7; docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md).

- **Bodies, weight and food.** No weighing and no body measurements; body composition is not taught as a body
  measure. No body weight, calorie or diet talk. Food rows use foods a class commonly sees and respect allergies,
  and name the health row they share. Fitness results follow section field 3: private, never graded, posted or
  compared.
- **Dress.** A grade never rests on dress (STANDARD.md 4).
- **Injury and medical conditions.** A student with a medical condition (asthma, a heart or breathing condition, a
  neck or back concern, an allergy) follows the school nurse's and family's guidance, and the unit names the
  alternative (a log roll for a forward roll). The injury role is in section field 9. Individual adapted plans
  are the school's specialists' (STANDARD.md 5).
- **Water.** Dry land only, as in "Water safety" below.
- **Cultural dance and games.** Each cultural claim is checked against a source before a unit names it. A sacred or
  ceremonial dance or game is never used as a game or a warm-up; a unit uses an activity only when its source says
  it is open to be taught (Cory, 2026-11-20, Desk q-prework-health-pe-2).
- **Gender.** No activity is split by gender, and the language is gender neutral (STANDARD.md 4).
- **Emotions.** Calming steps are offered, never a punishment, and a student may step out for a reset with a role.
  PE does not treat mental health conditions; the teacher page names the school counselor.

## Pacing

Settled 2026-11-16 (Cory, Desk card q-later-arts-health-pe): elementary PE (K to 5) is about 2 to 3 sessions a week.
Middle and high school PE minutes vary widely by school; each band frame states its sessions a year and their
length, and each band's scope and sequence names its short path (bar C2). The course says openly (Cory, 2026-11-20,
Desk q-prework-health-pe-1) that 2 to 3 sessions a week is below the weekly minutes the national body recommends:
SHAPE America's guidelines (shapeamerica.org/standards/guidelines) set 150 minutes a week in elementary school and
225 in middle and high school, for the whole year. The course page, the teacher page and the family note offer
short active breaks and home activity to help close the gap.

## Water safety

Curio Class gives dry-land safety and skill tasks every school can teach: a reach rescue, a throw rescue to a
target and fastening a life jacket. In-water tasks belong to a school's own aquatics program, with a qualified
lifeguard on site and the school's swim policy; a pool and a lifeguard are the school's call. No child is made to
enter water. The course page says this (Cory, 2026-11-20, Desk q-prework-health-pe-2).
