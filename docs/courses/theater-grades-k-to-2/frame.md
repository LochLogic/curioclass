# Course frame: Theater, grades K to 2

Course `theater-grades-k-to-2` (catalogue id `theater-k-2`), a band course for kindergarten, grade 1 and grade 2, run as three years one after another (year K, year 1, year 2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a pretend-together unit (imagining and group play), a voice and
  body unit (the actor's tools and the audience's reactions), a props and ties unit (making things for a drama and
  tying it to the child's life and other subjects), and a story unit that ends the year with a tale acted for
  families. The first three units each join two progression threads, one of creating or performing and one of
  responding or connecting; the story unit holds one thread with four ids, since kindergarten to grade 2 theater has
  seven rows a year, not eight. Every unit makes, performs, watches another artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4
  and 5).
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes: chairs to the walls, the clear floor checked,
  the group agreements card shown), the warm-up (5), the teaching, playing and rehearsing, and the close (about 4:
  chairs back, props to the class box, the day's evidence collected); a making session with glue and card takes 6
  for clean-up. A readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment
  (two check days: a showing day and a teacher's-table day; no unit is over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit
  assessment) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the voice and body unit of years 1
  and 2 owns seven or eight ids and is longest in year 1; the props unit of year 2 is longer because scenery joins
  props; the story unit is shortest because its tale is short and its showing uses the year's props and warm-ups.
  These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a
  year falls in that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): none is stated: the
  Educational Theatre Association's 2016 opportunity-to-learn standards, checked at source in the second-model read of
  2026-11-25, address grades 6 to 12 and give no K to 5 weekly-time figure (For the lead item 2). The course gives 80
  minutes a week, and 40 on the once-a-week path. The course is written so a classroom teacher
  can teach it; a school with a drama teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the study of the
  works and the demonstration with a step card, opened by the look or listen at a work that carries the unit's
  response and its connecting sentence; rehearsal with the critique and the revision; the showing check; a separate
  teacher's-table check session; and a respond session that starts from marks already made, so no first-time evidence
  is collected in a respond session (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). A five-session unit runs: 1 study and
  demonstration, 2 rehearsal, critique and revision, 3 showing check, 4 teacher's-table check, 5 respond; a story
  unit's study session holds only its study and teaching days, and its acting starts in session 2. Fluency
  days fold into the openers, never the demonstration, the critique, the revision, either check or the respond
  session. Sessions on that path: year K, units of 8, 8, 7 and 5; year 1, 7, 9, 7 and 5; year 2, 8, 8, 7 and 5 (28
  unit sessions a year; a making day keeps a session of its own); each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond session. Check sessions: year K
  weeks 17 and 26, year 1 weeks 17 and 26, year 2 weeks 17 and 26; each respond session falls one week later. Year K's
  week 17 session sits inside the kindergarten core weeks 14 to 18, which CALENDAR.md rule 1 allows for an arts special;
  the others are off the core check weeks. On this path a story unit's showing check is its family showing (with an in-class version for
  any child without a family yes). The scope and sequence writes out each unit's session order and marks which days
  merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, theater performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule binds
  (STANDARD.md 3.1): year K claims only the 17 kindergarten ids (`.Ka`, `.Kb`), year 1 only the 24 grade 1 ids, year
  2 only the 24 grade 2 ids: 65 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2 (kindergarten is uneven, 17 against 24), every
  one taught and assessed in its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a
  claim. No pre-kindergarten id is claimed. Every year touches all four artistic processes (creating, performing,
  responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at least two. In the Units table the C3 column is
  headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and the works
  it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of
  the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check
  set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence
  still states its id count (17, 24, 24) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a child's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Theater is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open theater recordings). Works studied are of three
  kinds: traditional tales whose source tellings are public domain, retold by Curio Class in its own words with the
  source credited (scripts the class performs are these retellings, Curio Class originals or the children's own,
  APPROACH "Rights"); theater forms and makers, living and past, described in our own words and linked to the maker's,
  a museum's or a cultural body's own page, never embedded or copied; and, for unseen check work, a short written
  description, a live teacher demonstration or a Curio Class original (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). Each work
  named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped
  work keeps the unit's ids met and the year's range. Range across the band (checked across the three years, not
  only within one): Africa (Ghana, South Africa), the Americas (a Cherokee story, Brazil, the United States), Asia
  (Japan, China, India), Europe (Norway, Russia, France, ancient Greece) and Oceania (Australia), with living makers
  each year. Curio Class writes every step card, tale retelling, scene card, audience card, record sheet and family
  note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Voice: a sound and a character's voice with the teacher leading
  (K), voice joined to face, body and gesture to show a feeling (1), voice and movement adjusted to show small
  differences in one character (2). Story building: entering an imagined place and adding one idea (K), adding the
  next step to a plot built in order and changing it (1), making up dialogue and changing lines (2). Making: a
  paper-bag puppet or prop and a flashlight or sound added (K), a planned costume piece and prop, one object standing
  for another (1), a planned simple set, a tried light or sound effect, a place or event shown by one prop (2).
  Story: who and where in a short tale, acted with help (K), what a character does and why, a scene from a fiction
  source (1), causes and results, a scene from a nonfiction source (2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown in
  named steps with the step card (a sound, a frozen picture, a gesture), or a tale or work heard, watched or
  described and talked about with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a
  respond day). Fluency: the warm-up routine and a scene beat run several times with feedback (a voice warm-up, a
  freeze and melt, a gesture game); in K to 2 it is mostly the five-minute warm-up and one or two fluency days a unit.
  Application: guided drama and rehearsal with some choice of role, line or prop; the second rehearsal day holds the
  unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: the showing
  day and the teacher's-table day of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet", or
  a re-rehearsal before a second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency,
  application with the critique, revision, two check days, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide
  (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live as each group performs; a kept card (an audience
  card, a prop-plan card, a story map) made on the unit's last rehearsal or making days or on the showing day as the
  audience, scored after the session; and talk or table items at the teacher's table on a description, a teacher
  demonstration or a Curio Class original, at most two talk items a child. The end-of-unit assessment is two check
  days near the unit's end, so the teacher never marks a performance and hears talk at once. The showing day's 40
  minutes: set-up 3; warm-up 5; six groups of five perform for about 4 minutes each (24) while the others are the
  audience and fill their audience card where the unit uses one; each child's self-check on the picture guide (thumbs
  up, sideways, down for each look-for) 3; close 4. That is 39 minutes. The teacher's-table day: set-up 3; in
  units 6 and 10 the teacher's short original scene (3), answered by every child on audience cards; six groups of five
  at the table for about 4 minutes each (24), where each child answers the talk items and does any table item; the
  others draw in their audience journal or play with the unit's puppets in a quiet corner, no glue or paint; close 4.
  That is 31 minutes, 34 in units 6 and 10, with the rest spare for the groups that run long. A group the teacher cannot watch live
  is recorded on the school's device that day, hands, props and sound only where the family has said no to faces, and
  marked before the respond day (APPROACH "Kept work"). The showing's rehearsals, its spoken critique with the
  teacher's look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score
  comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the
  first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every child has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece
  a unit goes into the portfolio folder (an audience card, a prop-plan card, a story map or a photo of a prop) with a
  one-line reflection, spoken to the teacher or a partner in K to 2 (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Year K assumes no earlier theater learning (pre-kindergarten ids are not taught; a
  kindergarten unit may use one as a readiness reference). Every year 1 and year 2 unit assumes the year before's
  row in the same thread, last used one grade back, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never a
  readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from the band's order (K,
  then 1, then 2). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a child may meet year 2 before year 1: each retrieval
  task then carries its ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline
  look"). The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a core
  check or respond day for the grade (K: 14 to 18 and 29 to 32; grade 1: 14 to 16, 19 and 29 to 32; grade 2: 14 to 16,
  18 to 20 and 28 to 32). Weeks chosen by the units' load, not to dodge other after-core courses (the lead's calendar
  pass does that): mid-year in week 19 in year K (course days 37 and 38 of the year), week 20 in year 1 (days 39 and
  40) and week 17 in year 2 (days 33 and 34); year-end in week 27 in every year (days 53 and 54). Weeks 29 to 32 hold
  every core subject's year-end check in K to 2, so the year-end check comes before the story unit (CALENDAR.md
  stagger tool c), and the story unit's showing, in weeks 28 to 32, is its own end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Every unit of this course uses play-acting, which STANDARD.md 5 names as a reason a community may
  leave a unit out; a school that leaves out play-acting leaves out the course, and the course page says so plainly.
  Units that study a cultural or ceremonial tradition, named on the course page: unit 3 (Chinese shadow puppetry),
  unit 7 (Brazilian mamulengo and Japanese bunraku), unit 10 (Peking opera) and unit 12 (a Cherokee story, studied by
  hearing and comparing only). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and
  the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 with a clear floor (desks pushed back or a hall), chairs, paper bags, card,
  socks, found objects, crayons and the voice; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in
  (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One child at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, a
  group scene has a doubled version (parent and child, or the child with two puppets), and the showing is to the
  family or a recorded take. The teacher needs no drama training: each unit's teacher page says what to look and
  listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items counted once,
  printing of step cards, scene cards and audience cards), all optional beside the free path: year K $5, $5, $25 (six
  small LED flashlights, reusable, and paper bags), $5 ($40); year 1 $5, $5, $15, $5 ($30); year 2 $5, $5, $20
  (colored film for the flashlights, card for a set), $5 ($35). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For
  the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work's page or a step; every day runs from the
  printed step card, scene card and description without it. A school device may record a group the teacher cannot
  watch live (Scored evidence). No owned id needs technology, so a paper-path student meets every id in full. Kept
  work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on the floor, the voice, paper, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit
  plan adds (a stage-directions practice tool, a sound-effects board) has a printed version that does the same job and
  needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards, scene cards and audience cards are picture-led with the words read aloud for K to 2;
  no child reads a card alone, and an audience card is answered by circling a face, drawing or pointing. English
  learners: each unit teaches its six words in depth, with a picture, a gesture or a sound and a frame ("My character
  wants ___." "I felt ___ when ___."), a child may show or act the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what
  the child performed apart from what they said (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on (movement on a shared floor; puppet, prop and set making), so every unit
  gets the fresh safety agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against free guides, named here and
  opened by the agent: the federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov) and
  California's environmental health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for schools, with its list of
  materials not to buy for grades K to 6 (oehha.ca.gov), for every making day; and the same federal agency's button
  battery safety pages (cpsc.gov), for the flashlights. Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" and marked
  "from memory" until that read checks them: a clear floor with room for arms out, chairs and bags to the walls; bare
  feet or soft soles on a clean floor; no lifts, no falls rehearsed, no contact stage fights and no real or toy
  weapons; partner contact only by agreement and never required; voice rest and no forced loud sound; props checked by
  the teacher before use (no sharp edges, nothing small enough to swallow, no cords long enough to wrap a neck);
  flashlights LED only, with screw-closed battery doors and no coin or button batteries; no light shone into eyes and
  no flashing; supplies labeled non-toxic; blades used by an adult only; hands washed after glue and found objects.
  Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (Japanese
  kamishibai), unit 3 (Chinese shadow puppetry), unit 4 (an Akan tale from Ghana), unit 6 (Handspring's horse
  puppet, from a war story), unit 7 (mamulengo of northeast Brazil; bunraku of Japan), unit 8 (the class's own
  community stories), unit 9 (a Panchatantra tale from India), unit 10 (Peking opera) and unit 12 (a Cherokee story).
  No child plays a character by an accent, a costume of another culture or a stereotype; no child is cast against
  their wish, and a villain is played by the teacher or a puppet; no role play puts a child in a victim's or a
  harmer's place (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). Every task that ties a character to a child's own life
  (units 3, 7, 11, and the community stories of unit 8) offers an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH
  "Sensitive ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, a clear floor, step
  cards, scene cards, audience cards, found and low-cost materials. Activities: the voice and body taught in steps,
  tales and works heard or watched with their setting, guided drama and rehearsal with a critique and a revision, a
  showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks a
  unit per child on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's ids (17, 24, 24) and growth on the
  look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with children, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten opens the band and the subject: there is no course before and no pre-kindergarten course. The seven
kindergarten threads that start here with no earlier row are th-k-imagine, th-k-show, th-k-actor, th-k-design,
th-k-story, th-k-world and th-k-notice (their `assumes` cells are empty); each is first used in the unit named in the
table below.
