## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Could the United States have acted sooner against fascism and the Holocaust?"
**Hook, day 42.** Compare the Neutrality Acts' arms restriction with Lend-Lease's authority to supply arms. Ask whether staying out of combat meant staying out of world affairs.
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: What did neutrality protect or risk (44)? How did dictatorship and aggression spread (43 and 47)? Why did the United States enter (45)? What changed the war (46 to 50)? What did officials know, choose and leave undone (51 to 53)?
**Closing claim, day 54.** Answer the compelling question from the neutrality and aid records, refugee testimony, rescue records and trial evidence. Distinguish earlier aid or refuge from earlier military intervention; answer a counterclaim.
**Teaching.** Model policy argument on 44, map reasoning on 46 and sourcing on 47. Students select evidence on 49 and write independently on 54. Practise D2.His.1.9-12 on 44 and D2.Geo.1.9-12 on 46. Teacher notes distinguish fascism from any strong government, neutrality from disengagement, and knowledge from capacity. Date Yalta during the war, February 1945; Potsdam follows Germany's surrender but precedes Japan's.

**A1 reserved packet and prompt.** Supply at most 240 words altogether from: Secretary Hull's March 23, 1938 telegram to Kennedy, paragraphs 1 and 2, proposing refugee cooperation within existing immigration laws; the December 17, 1942 Allied declaration, opening and concluding paragraphs, publicly identifying mass murder and promising punishment; and the October 1, 1946 Nuremberg judgment, Goering's finding of guilt, with a brief neutral explanation of the counts. These passages are never used in teaching or exits. Ask: Who made each, when, for which audience and purpose? What did each show officials knew, what did it do, and what did it leave undone? Compare the three stages, bring in the 1944 rescue agency, and identify one earlier action that the evidence supports considering without promising it would have prevented the Holocaust. Use details from every record.

**A1 key and look-fors.** Hull's diplomatic proposal acknowledged a refugee crisis while preserving existing entry limits. The Allied public declaration establishes acknowledged knowledge of systematic murder by December 1942, not the first date anyone knew. Condemnation and promised prosecution did not themselves provide refuge. The War Refugee Board organized rescue and relief in 1944, late in the killing; military liberation ended imprisonment for survivors, and the Allied tribunal judged individual criminal responsibility after the war. Earlier admission within unused quotas was an available policy avenue; military control and access constrained wartime rescue. Sources do not establish that every official knew the same things or that a different policy would have saved everyone. Judge accurate chronology, sourcing, specific evidence, feasible alternatives and limits. Meets: "Hull sought cooperation without expanding legal admissions. By 1942 the Allies publicly named mass murder, yet their declaration promised punishment rather than entry. The 1944 board attempted rescue; the 1946 verdict shows later justice, which could not restore murdered lives." Does not: "Nobody knew until the trials, and the declaration rescued all the refugees."

**S49 task and look-fors.** Choose Munich, Midway or Yalta, representing a road to war, battle or conference. From its curated packet, independently source two primary records, test one agreement or discrepancy against a secondary history, and explain what happened and what it changed. A battle study explains military results; a conference study distinguishes commitments from subsequent implementation. Differing purposes need not produce factual disagreement. Look for provenance, context, corroboration, causal reasoning and a limitation. Meets, for Munich: "The agreement transfers territory and regulates evacuation. Chamberlain's public assurance sought confidence at home; it does not prove security was achieved. The later occupation shows the settlement did not contain German ambitions." Does not: "Chamberlain said peace, so both sources prove there was lasting peace." This illustrative pair is not a model taught before the independent task.

**S49 packet choices.** Munich pairs the territorial agreement with Chamberlain's September 30, 1938 return statement. Midway pairs Nimitz's June 6, 1942 communique with the translated Japanese Mobile Force battle report, published by naval intelligence in 1947. Yalta pairs its protocol's Poland section with Churchill's February 27, 1945 Commons statement. Supply a dated secondary account for each, including subsequent developments; identify the translation and retrospective reporting dates. Use different clauses for day 48's teaching and day 55. The protocol and agreement were read; final speech and battle-report selections remain to verify before lesson writing. Three curated choices keep one period for independent analysis without requiring twelve research projects.

## U3. Daily evidence for section 4.1

Every invented caption or display is labeled "invented for practice". New source selections are withheld from teaching and other checks. Use different passages for modeling, practice, these exits and day 55. Fixed historical names and dates recur; the source selection and reasoning task must be fresh. Each answer below states the evidence sought, not a prescribed judgment. Record error letters and the student's revision.

- **E42, MA.USII.T3.1.** Case, invented for practice: an embargo bars arms sales to both an aggressor and the country it attacks, while trade in food continues. Explain its neutrality aim and one consequence. Answer: avoiding involvement can also restrict a threatened country's defense; continued trade contradicts complete disengagement (A). Add an open capstone question.
- **E43, MA.USII.T3.2.** A new caption dates Italian fascism to 1933 and calls exclusion from public employment a private prejudice. Correct both errors and explain how state action expanded persecution. Answer: Mussolini took office in 1922 and consolidated dictatorship afterward; Hitler became chancellor in 1933. The German civil-service law used state power to remove many Jewish employees; later Italian racial laws also institutionalized antisemitism. War, political choices and escalating violence connect exclusion to genocide, not an automatic single step (B, P).
- **E44, MA.USII.T3.1; D2.His.1.9-12.** Two policy cards, invented for practice: an aid plan lends ships without sending combat troops; an opposing memo warns of retaliation. Judge the plan's security mechanism and answer the objection. Answer: aid may strengthen a partner while increasing exposure; neither aim nor warning establishes the actual result (G).
- **E45, MA.USII.T3.3.** A museum display, written by Curio Class for practice, has three separate spaces: "Longer conflict," "Immediate attack," and "War with Germany." Choose two of the three, write one linked explanation for each, and identify which institution declared war. Answer: expansion in China and the Pacific, economic restrictions and failed diplomacy form context; Pearl Harbor prompted the December 8 declaration against Japan; Germany's December 11 declaration preceded Congress's response against Germany. Congress, not an automatic alliance mechanism, declared war (C, S).
- **E46, MA.USII.T3.4; D2.Geo.1.9-12.** On a fresh dated outline locate and key Britain, France, the Soviet Union, the United States, Germany, Italy and Japan. Explain one supply problem by comparing an Atlantic route with a Pacific route using the scale and regional inset maps. Answer: all seven correctly located and aligned for 1942; route length, sea access and fronts shape supply. A flat map's apparent spacing alone is insufficient; scale varies with projection (D).
- **E47, MA.USII.T3.5a to d.** Four new captions say: "Rearming and entering the Rhineland were the same act"; "Austria joined Germany at Munich"; "The pact protected Poland from both signers"; "Nanjing shows only battlefield deaths." Correct each, explaining one consequence. Answer: expanding forces differed from remilitarizing a restricted zone; Austrian annexation preceded Munich; the pact and protocol facilitated partition; Japanese forces murdered civilians and disarmed soldiers and committed sexual violence. Connect weakened restraints, further conquest, partition and civilian suffering to the appropriate event (E, K, Q). No graphic description is required.
- **E48, MA.USII.T3.5e and f.** Choose an exit-only battle logistics card, separate from the six-battle teaching cards and explain what it changed and what it did not settle. Then place Yalta and Potsdam relative to Germany's surrender and explain one decision from each. Answer: a specific military consequence with a limit; Yalta planned occupation and postwar arrangements before surrender, while Potsdam addressed occupation and Germany's treatment after it. Both preceded Japan's surrender. Check the selected battle against its card, not against a required choice (F, L).
- **E49, MA.USII.T3.5.** Submit S49. Which particular detail in your second source most changes or limits the claim you could make from the first? Answer: name its provenance, the detail and the resulting qualification; agreement can strengthen a bounded claim without manufacturing disagreement (G).
- **E50, check-set addition.** A display says, "Special units prove the armed forces offered everyone equal treatment." Revise it with exit-only unused passages from two of the three community accounts (your choice), plus a separate Navajo Nation proclamation paragraph. What does that paragraph add? Answer key, all three: distinguish Black airmen's flying and ground work, Japanese American soldiers' European service, and Diné communications expertise; cite a specific contribution and unequal condition without making the three experiences identical. The present nation speaks for its own continuing community (H, N).
- **E51, MA.USII.T3.6.** A draft account says every St. Louis passenger returned directly to Germany and refugees made no efforts themselves. Revise it from the fresh voyage record and testimony; explain how admissions policy shaped the alternatives. Answer: passengers sought refuge and negotiated; Britain, France, Belgium and the Netherlands received passengers on return to Europe, and many later faced German occupation. Refusal and quota barriers mattered; one later fate cannot stand for every passenger (G).
- **E52, MA.USII.T3.6.** On a new 1944 rescue record, distinguish an authorized action from evidence that people were reached. What would you need to judge its timing? Answer: the War Refugee Board's authority or plan differs from documented implementation; compare dated earlier reports, obstacles and rescue results. A late rescue effort could save lives without proving either an adequate response or total inaction (G).
- **E53, MA.USII.T3.6.** An unused liberation account records survivors' immediate needs; a separate trial record names individual charges. Explain the different responses and one limit of each. Answer: liberation and medical or material relief addressed captivity and survival; prosecution sought accountability for specific crimes. Neither restored murdered relatives or automatically secured a permanent home for survivors (I).
- **E54, MA.USII.T3.1 and T3.6.** Collect W54 separately. Case, invented for practice: an official rescue announcement is followed by a dated report of delays and limited arrivals. What does the second record change? Answer: it qualifies implementation and reach without proving total inaction; announced intent alone is insufficient (G). Add an open capstone question.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach neutrality, fascism, antisemitism, refugee, quota, liberation and prosecution in context. Offer "By __, this source establishes __, but cannot establish __." Accept home-language planning and oral rehearsal; compare an oral explanation with writing to identify language gaps. Limit independent readings to 250 words per sitting; read dense laws aloud. Provide large print, transcripts, described or tactile maps and scribed responses. Extension tests a contrary source within the same question.

Give advance content notes. Jewish life, resistance and relief efforts accompany persecution; name Nazi responsibility and the six million Jews murdered. Explain other targeted groups distinctly. At Nanjing, name murder and sexual violence without graphic detail. Frame propaganda as false racial ideology; stop dehumanizing claims and return to evidence. Permit a quiet equivalent text task without forced disclosure or reenactment.
