## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Could the United States have acted sooner against fascism and the Holocaust?"

**Hook, day 42.** Compare the Neutrality Acts' arms restriction with Lend-Lease's authority to supply arms. Ask whether staying out of combat meant staying out of world affairs.
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: What did neutrality protect or risk (44)? How did dictatorship and aggression spread (43 and 47)? Why did the United States enter (45)? What changed the war (46 to 50)? What did officials know, choose and leave undone (51 to 53)?
**Closing claim, day 54.** Answer the compelling question from the neutrality and aid records, refugee testimony, rescue records and trial evidence. Distinguish earlier aid or refuge from earlier military intervention; answer a counterclaim.
**Teaching.** Model policy argument on 44, map reasoning on 46 and sourcing on 47. Students select evidence on 49 and write independently on 54. Practice D2.His.1.9-12 on 44 and D2.Geo.1.9-12 on 46. Teacher notes distinguish fascism from any strong government, neutrality from disengagement, and knowledge from capacity. Date Yalta during the war, February 1945; Potsdam follows Germany's surrender but precedes Japan's.


**Limits.** Survey every road, battle and conference listed; study Munich, Midway or Yalta deeply by choice. Home-front policy and the bomb belong to unit 5; sustained informed action belongs to unit 12. No combat games, simulations, slurs or graphic images.
**Carried.** Retain unit 3's source record, perspective, causal chain and claim reasoning; set aside its economic graphs. On 46, connect map scale, region and timeline to dated fronts and supply distances. Return on 47 and 48; students revise independently on 49. On 52, connect perspective to dated knowledge; withdraw writing prompts on 54.

- **E42, MA.USII.T3.1.** Case, invented for practice: an embargo bars arms sales to both an aggressor and the country it attacks, while trade in food continues. Explain its neutrality aim and one consequence. Answer: avoiding involvement can also restrict a threatened country's defense; continued trade contradicts complete disengagement (A). Add an open capstone question.
- **E43, MA.USII.T3.2.** A new caption dates Italian fascism to 1933 and calls exclusion from public employment a private prejudice. Correct both errors and explain how state action expanded persecution. Answer: Mussolini took office in 1922 and consolidated dictatorship afterward; Hitler became chancellor in 1933. The German civil-service law used state power to remove many Jewish employees; later Italian racial laws also institutionalized antisemitism. War, political choices and escalating violence connect exclusion to genocide, not an automatic single step (B, T).
- **E44, MA.USII.T3.1; D2.His.1.9-12.** Two policy cards, invented for practice: an aid plan lends ships without sending combat troops; an opposing memo warns of retaliation.
