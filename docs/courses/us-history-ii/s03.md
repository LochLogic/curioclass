## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the New Deal fix the Depression, and for whom?"

**Hook, day 26.** Compare the Bonus Army photograph with Roosevelt's 1933 inaugural promise of work. Ask what would count as recovery for the people shown.
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: Why did hardship spread (26 and 27)? How did people respond (28 to 31)? What changed government's role (32 to 35)? Who benefited, who remained excluded, and what counts as success (36 and 37)?
**Closing claim, day 37.** Answer the compelling question using the two economic accounts, migrant and removal accounts, the chosen case and the Social Security law. Distinguish relief, recovery and reform; answer a counterclaim. Either qualified judgment can succeed.
**Teaching.** Teach chronology before interpretation. Model causal chains on 27, sourcing on 28, geographic comparison on 29 and counterclaims on 36. Students choose evidence on 31 and 34 and write without frames on 37. Teacher notes distinguish a crash from a contraction, deposits from income, and legal eligibility from actual access.


**D2.Geo.9.9-12, own words.** Evaluate how climate varies over long periods and influences migration, settlement, resource use and land use, from local places to worldwide patterns. Parts: evaluate each of those four relationships across scales.

**Content parts.** T2.2 asks students to describe causes and effects and compare explanations. T2.3 asks them to gather and analyze sources and create a report. T2.4 asks them to analyze one case from primary sources. T2.5 asks them to evaluate programs and social responses.
**Limits.** Survey all eleven options; assess one deeply. America First began in 1940: signpost that forward look before returning to 1934. War policy belongs to unit 4, formal macroeconomic graphs to Economics, and informed action to unit 12.
**Carried.** Keep unit 2's sourcing, perspective and claim record, and unit 1's opportunity cost, supply and demand, and causal chain. Retrieve on 26; revise chains on 27 and source records on 30. Withdraw prompts on 31 and 37; retain models for transfer.

- **E26, MA.USII.T2.2.** A foreign factory loses American orders while its lender withdraws credit. Explain two links from these events to workers' hardship. Would the stock crash alone explain them? Answer: lost sales reduce production and wages; restricted credit can deepen contraction; financial and trade links transmit hardship. A crash alone misses these mechanisms (A). Add an open question for the capstone.
- **E27, MA.USII.T2.2.** A case (invented for practice): a record shows deposits contracting while households postpone purchases. Explain how each economic account interprets this combination and identify evidence that could distinguish their emphasis. Answer: money contraction limits payments and credit; reduced spending lowers sales and employment. Timing, banking records and spending evidence help test emphasis; co-occurrence alone cannot rank causes (B).
- **E28, MA.USII.T2.3; D2.His.10.9-12.** Case, invented for practice: a sheriff's 1932 report to a governor calls a farm protest disorderly; a farmer's 1932 relief application describes threatened foreclosure. What must you record before using these accounts, and what could corroborate either? Answer: maker, date, audience, purpose and context; seek court, farm-price or other participant records. These fictional claims establish no historical event. Wrong: listing records without analysis (Y); one account represents all farmers (C).
- **E29, MA.USII.T2.3; D2.Geo.9.9-12.** Two districts have similarly dry years but different cultivation and out-migration. What can climate explain, what else must be checked, and why does a county pattern not establish a world pattern? Answer: dryness can pressure yields and water use; check soil cover, jobs, debt and policy. Compare longer records and global settlement and land-use evidence; scale and timing constrain inference (D).
- **E30, MA.USII.T2.3.** Case, invented for practice: an official caption calls a family's departure voluntary; a later family account describes threatened loss of relief and a United States citizen child's removal. What claim can you defend about this case, and what remains uncertain? Answer: the supplied account suggests coercion despite citizenship; corroborate timing and circumstances. The case establishes no actual family's history or frequency of removal. Wrong: all departures were voluntary (E); only noncitizens could be removed (K); one family represents everyone (C).
- **E31, MA.USII.T2.3; D2.Geo.9.9-12; D2.His.13.9-12.** Collect R31 and G31 separately. Case, invented for practice: two equally dry districts show different migration after one factory closes. Identify an alternative explanation and a source limit. Answer: employment may affect movement; local records do not establish global climate causation. Seek dated employment and migration evidence (D).
- **E32, MA.USII.T2.4 and its options.** Case, invented for practice: four unused records describe a dues-funded bargaining association, a legislature's workplace rule, a public employment project and a citizen petition against intervention. Classify each by kind, then explain why an unsupported accusation against a religious group cannot supply an economic explanation. Answer: union, law, program and movement; purpose and funding distinguish them. Scapegoating supplies no tested causal mechanism. Wrong: all are government agencies (F); the accusation is evidence (I).
- **E33, MA.USII.T2.4.** Name one detail in an unused primary record from your chosen case that challenges the case's official aim or your first judgment. Source it and explain how your explanation must change. Answer: precise provenance, a relevant contrary detail, and a revision distinguishing intention from experience (C, T).
- **E34, MA.USII.T2.4.** Collect C34 separately. Case, invented for practice: a program archive contains only managers favorable reports. A gallery caption calls it every residents experience. What further evidence is needed, and how must the claim change? Answer: seek differently situated residents records and limit the claim to the reports provenance (C).
- **E35, check-set addition.** Case, invented for practice: two Native councils already exercise authority in 1933. In 1935 one accepts a federal reorganization proposal to protect land; the other rejects it to retain its form of government. What do these decisions show about sovereignty and responses to federal policy? Answer: their authority predates the proposal; different decisions show distinct priorities, not one universal Native response. These are fictional councils, not attributed tribal testimony. Wrong: sovereignty began with the federal act (G); every nation welcomed it (M).
- **E36, MA.USII.T2.5.** Case, invented for practice: a sample of 20 Black and 20 white workers lists 8 and 16 respectively in occupations covered by old-age insurance. What does this show about access, and what does it not establish about legal eligibility? Separately, a bank customer's statement lists a stock investment whose market value fell; does deposit insurance repay that fall? Answer: unequal sample coverage, 40 and 80 percent, does not establish a racial eligibility rule or exclusion from every program; inspect occupations. Insurance does not cover stock-market losses. Wrong: every Black worker was barred from all programs (H); stocks are insured deposits (N).
- **E37, MA.USII.T2.2 and T2.5.** Collect W37 separately. Case, invented for practice: town relief wages rise, bank deposits shrink and employers continue cutting jobs. Give a qualified recovery judgment using each economic account, then name evidence that could distinguish their emphasis. Answer: public wages may support demand while deposit contraction restricts payments and credit; local relief does not prove recovery. Compare dated spending, lending and job records to test timing and rival explanations. Wrong: the accounts are interchangeable (B); local wages settle national recovery (J). Add an open capstone question.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach contraction, money supply, relief, recovery, reform, coercion, sovereignty and coverage in context. Offer "The record supports __, but cannot establish __." Newcomers rehearse with labeled visuals; more fluent learners qualify causes. Accept home-language notes and oral explanations to distinguish language from content gaps. Read at most 250 words independently per sitting; read harder legal passages aloud. Provide large print, described maps, tactile outlines, oral or scribed answers and transcripts. Extension tests a competing explanation on the same evidence.

## U10. Two rooms

One adult supplies counterclaims and compares sources with one child. A projector can show labels and maps; paper copies carry the identical work. No device or family disclosure is needed.
