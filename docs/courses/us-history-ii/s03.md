## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the New Deal fix the Depression, and for whom?"
**Hook, day 26.** Compare the Bonus Army photograph with Roosevelt's 1933 inaugural promise of work. Ask what would count as recovery for the people shown.
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: Why did hardship spread (26 and 27)? How did people respond (28 to 31)? What changed government's role (32 to 35)? Who benefited, who remained excluded, and what counts as success (36 and 37)?
**Closing claim, day 37.** Answer the compelling question using the two economic accounts, migrant and removal accounts, the chosen case and the Social Security law. Distinguish relief, recovery and reform; answer a counterclaim. Either qualified judgment can succeed.
**Teaching.** Teach chronology before interpretation. Model causal chains on 27, sourcing on 28, geographic comparison on 29 and counterclaims on 36. Students choose evidence on 31 and 34 and write without frames on 37. Teacher notes distinguish a crash from a contraction, deposits from income, and legal eligibility from actual access.

**Assessment architecture.** A1, A2 and A3 are new on day 38. R31, G31, C34 and W37 are individual evidence already collected, scored once as parts of this composite. Shared research and group presentations are permitted, but each student submits an independent report, geographic judgment and case explanation. Reserve A1's whole report selection and numerical series from teaching and exits. Use different years and passages for practice. **A1 packet and item.** Supply the Federal Works Agency's 1940 First Annual Report, its two paragraphs describing work for women and sewing projects, with maker, date and purpose. Pair these with historical unemployment estimates: 1935, 20.1 percent; 1937, 14.3 percent; 1938, 19.0 percent, from the labor statistics table in field 10. Its pre-1940 estimates count emergency work-relief workers among the unemployed; explain that definition on the packet. Ask: What result does the program record support, for whom? What does the series establish about recovery? Explain a mechanism using either economic account, identify a response or criticism, and state what these sources cannot establish about causation or excluded workers. Answer: work and useful goods benefited participants and recipients; unemployment fell then rose, so neither steady recovery nor universal coverage follows. Public wages could support spending; monetary stabilization is another mechanism but is not measured here. Administrative reporting has an institutional purpose. Benefits do not prove the program alone caused national recovery.

**A2.** Explain two linked causes and one consequence in the United States and one abroad. Compare how Keynesian and monetarist accounts explain the contraction and what each emphasizes in responding. Answer: lost spending and bank failures reinforced falling employment; reduced foreign lending and trade transmitted hardship abroad. Keynesian reasoning emphasizes inadequate aggregate spending and fiscal support; monetarist reasoning emphasizes monetary contraction, bank failures and monetary stabilization. These can be reinforcing mechanisms; the crash alone is insufficient (A, B).

**A3 (11 minutes; A1 takes 17 and A2 12).** Match each of the eleven new situations above to its institution, law or movement. Then explain why the menu includes both government policies and organizations that supported or opposed government. These are teacher-written situations, not quotations. Each row supplies its key; no second set of eleven case studies is required. The final explanation meets if it distinguishes agencies or statutes from independent organizations; it does not meet if it calls every option a Roosevelt agency (F).

**R31, report task.** Gather four primary sources from the curated archive: a Bonus Army demand, a farm protest account, a family survival or migrant account, and a removal account. Record provenance, ask a supporting question, corroborate one account with a secondary history, and explain responses through protest, mutual aid, migration or resistance to coercion. Explain differing circumstances; do not treat the sources as a representative poll. Look for purposeful gathering, accurate sourcing, specific evidence and analysis across responses. Attach G31.

**G31, geographic evaluation.** G31 completes the evaluation drafted on day 29 (day 31's ten minutes finish it; they do not start it). Use day 29's dated climate and map packet. Evaluate how longer climate variability influenced migration, settlement, water and soil use, and cultivated land. Compare a county, the Great Plains and worldwide patterns; distinguish farming and policy effects. Cite one long-term climate series and two mapped or tabulated observations. At world scale, test whether similar climate conditions imply similar movement or land use. State where evidence cannot establish a relationship. A world locator alone does not meet the task. Look for judgments on all four relationships, connections across three scales, and limits on causal claims.

**G31 packet specification.** Print a Plains precipitation series for 1895 to 1940 beside the dated drought map; Cimarron County, Oklahoma, cultivation and farm records; and the 1940 census's 1935 to 1940 internal-migration tables, including receiving California communities. For the world comparison, use the precipitation archive's 1901 to 1940 observations beside 1930 and 1940 global population, cropland and irrigated-land reconstructions. Label reconstructions as estimates, distinguish rainfall from drought indices, and show station gaps and matching spatial scales. Compare the Plains with another agricultural region visible in those records, without assuming an identical response. Population stocks do not measure migration flows: a justified judgment that global migration causation remains unestablished must specify the missing flow and policy evidence. These data sources are selected; final extraction and map preparation remain lesson-production work.

**C34, case task.** Explain the chosen option's origins, aims, mechanism, relevant people, results, reach and an objection. Use two primary records with provenance and one secondary account; distinguish intention from experience. Submit an individual explanation with the presentation. The option rows fix the focus; score only the chosen option deeply. Antisemitic claims are evidence of propaganda, never explanations students adopt. Offer another case without removing this history from the survey. Look for an accurate mechanism and chronology, sourced perspectives, evidence of results and a qualified judgment.

**W37, argument task.** Answer the compelling question in about 350 words using four named sources across economic explanation, lived experience and policy. Compare both accounts, address the strongest counterclaim and uneven effects, and distinguish relief, recovery and enduring reform. Use field 2's source set and independent case evidence. Look for accurate mechanisms, source-based reasoning, qualification and an answered counterclaim.

**Scoring.** Record each task's look-fors separately as absent, partial or secure. Secure means accurate and supported by the specific evidence requested; partial means present but incompletely supported. Do not let fluent writing compensate for an omitted mechanism or geographic relationship. Score A3 one point per match and one for its distinction. Retain originals when students revise.

## U3. Daily evidence for section 3.1

Use distinct source selections for models, these exits and the assessment. The report archive and case packets separate modeled records from students' independent choices. The unit assessment sits in field 5. Answers below state look-fors, not a required political judgment.

- **E26, MA.USII.T2.2.** A foreign factory loses American orders while its lender withdraws credit. Explain two links from these events to workers' hardship. Would the stock crash alone explain them? Answer: lost sales reduce production and wages; restricted credit can deepen contraction; financial and trade links transmit hardship. A crash alone misses these mechanisms (A). Add an open question for the capstone.
- **E27, MA.USII.T2.2.** A fictional record shows deposits contracting while households postpone purchases. Explain how each economic account interprets this combination and identify evidence that could distinguish their emphasis. Answer: money contraction limits payments and credit; reduced spending lowers sales and employment. Timing, banking records and spending evidence help test emphasis; co-occurrence alone cannot rank causes (A, B).
- **E28, MA.USII.T2.3; D2.His.10.9-12.** An official calls a farm protest disorderly; a farmer describes threatened foreclosure. What must you know before using these accounts, and what could corroborate either? Answer: maker, date, audience, purpose and context; court, farm-price or additional participant records can test specific claims. Neither label settles what happened (C).
- **E29, MA.USII.T2.3; D2.Geo.9.9-12.** Two districts have similarly dry years but different cultivation and out-migration. What can climate explain, what else must be checked, and why does a county pattern not establish a world pattern? Answer: dryness can pressure yields and water use; check soil cover, jobs, debt and policy. Compare longer records and global settlement and land-use evidence; scale and timing constrain inference (D).
- **E30, MA.USII.T2.3.** An official caption calls a family's departure voluntary; a later family account describes threatened loss of relief and a United States citizen child's removal. What claim can you defend, and what remains uncertain? Answer: coercion can operate without formal deportation, and citizenship did not prevent removal. Corroborate circumstances and timing; do not generalize every departure from one family or accept the official label uncritically (C, E).
- **E31, MA.USII.T2.3; D2.Geo.9.9-12; D2.His.13.9-12.** Submit R31 and G31. Identify one competing explanation your report tested and one source limitation that changes its conclusion. Answer: a specific alternative such as labor demand versus drought, a relevant evidence comparison, and an appropriately limited conclusion (C, D, E).
- **E32, MA.USII.T2.4 and its options.** A label says every option was a relief agency founded in 1933. Correct it with one union, one law and the later antiwar movement; explain why an antisemitic accusation cannot count as economic evidence. Answer: the craft federation predated the New Deal, the labor standards law dates to 1938, and America First formed in 1940. Scapegoating is an unsupported accusation, not a causal mechanism (F, I).
- **E33, MA.USII.T2.4.** Name one detail in an unused primary record from your chosen case that challenges the case's official aim or your first judgment. Source it and explain how your explanation must change. Answer: precise provenance, a relevant contrary detail, and a revision distinguishing intention from experience (C, F).
- **E34, MA.USII.T2.4.** Submit C34, then answer a partner's question about one person or group your case does not represent. Answer: evidence-supported limit on reach or perspective, not an invented universal experience (C, H).
- **E35, check-set addition.** A museum caption says the 1934 act created Native sovereignty and received unanimous support. Revise it using the act and the contrasting Native accounts. Answer: sovereignty predates the act; it changed federal land and government policy, while nations and individuals judged its effects differently. Name the sources and distinguish nations rather than treating one voice as universal (G).
- **E36, MA.USII.T2.5.** A fictional covered factory worker and an excluded domestic worker both seek protection against old-age income loss in 1935. Explain the difference without claiming a racial eligibility rule. Then distinguish an insured bank deposit from a stock investment. Answer: occupational coverage limited old-age insurance and disproportionately affected Black workers; neither all Black workers nor all Social Security programs were excluded. Deposit insurance protects eligible deposits under its rules, not stock losses (B, H).
- **E37, MA.USII.T2.2 and T2.5.** Submit W37. What evidence would most weaken your conclusion, and what would it change? Answer: a relevant missing or contrary record, its effect on the claim, and a distinction between economic mechanisms and policy outcomes (B, C). Add an open capstone question.
