## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the fear of communism make the country stronger or weaker?"
**Hook, day 67.** Compare Kennan and Novikov in 1946: Why did former allies describe each other as threatening?
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: Why did allies divide (67)? How did containment work (68 and 69)? Who decides production (70)? Where did powers compete (71)? Why did science funding grow (72)? Who gained (73 to 77)? How did nations resist termination (78)? What proves guilt (79 and 80)?
**Closing claim, day 81.** Judge strength by security, prosperity and rights, using Truman, Marshall, the alliance record, Detroit evidence and Smith or Kameny. Answer contrary evidence; more than one qualified judgment is defensible.
**Teaching.** Model sourcing and perspective on 67, causation on 68, map comparison on 76 and a counterclaim on 80. Students independently research on 73 and argue on 81. Practise D2.His.5.9-12 on 79 and RH.11-12.9 on 81. Teacher notes must distinguish communist ideals from Soviet institutions, policy aims from outcomes, and association from evidence.

## U3. Checks, written first

Every caption or display an exit asks students to correct is written by Curio Class for practice, and the page labels it so. Use distinct source selections for modelling, practice, exit tickets and assessment. Source labels name maker, date, audience and purpose. The following original claims and tasks are reserved for exits; do not teach their wording. Short answers require a reason or evidence, not just a correction. Passage and map preparation remains in field 10.

- **E67, MA.USII.T3.9.** A new caption says, "Former allies disagreed only because their leaders disliked each other." Revise it with two causes and explain why one diplomat's account cannot prove every motive. Answer: incompatible systems, Soviet control in Eastern Europe, security fears and competing influence interacted; a diplomat has a particular position and purpose (A). Add an open capstone question.
- **E68, MA.USII.T3.9.** A fresh claim says, "Aid to Greece and Turkey and European recovery aid were the same military alliance." Correct it, explaining how each could serve containment. Answer: the Truman Doctrine backed military and economic assistance; Marshall's recovery program supported economic stability. Neither was NATO. Explain the mechanism, not only the name (B).
- **E69, MA.USII.T3.9.** Correct: "Both blocs formed their alliances in 1949, and Korea reunited after American victory." Answer: NATO formed in 1949, the Warsaw Pact in 1955; North Korean invasion, international intervention and Chinese intervention led to an armistice and continuing division. Explain one connection between containment and intervention, without reducing Korean aims to superpower orders (B, D).
- **E70, MA.USII.T3.10.** A fictional exhibit says that government regulation of a privately owned bakery makes its economy communist. Explain the error using ownership, allocation, prices and incentives. Answer: regulation alone does not replace private ownership and market allocation. Contrast state production targets and administered prices with market decisions and profit incentives, while acknowledging Soviet bonuses and American government rules. Communist ideals and actual Soviet institutions differ (C).
- **E71, MA.USII.T3.10.** On a new outline map locate two of Berlin, Cuba and Afghanistan (your choice) and attach a dated conflict note to each. Explain a local aim and superpower involvement for one. Answer key, all three: Germany, Caribbean and south-central Asia; Berlin division and the 1961 wall, the 1962 Cuban missile crisis, and Soviet intervention in Afghanistan in 1979 and the 1980s. Accept accurate local aims supported by the source cards, not passive labels (D).
- **E72, MA.USII.T3.11.** A new caption reads, "One satellite immediately made American education and space leadership secure." Revise it with two institutional responses and a causal link. Answer: Soviet achievement raised security and technical concerns; Eisenhower backed the civilian space agency and signed the 1958 education act. These supported longer-term exploration and scientific training rather than guaranteeing superiority (E).
- **E73, MA.USII.T4.1.** From a previously unused paragraph of the highway statute, explain one mechanism and identify separate evidence needed to judge its local effects. Answer: identify the selected funding or construction provision accurately; seek a dated local map or community record rather than infer outcomes from the law's aim (F).
- **E74, MA.USII.T4.1.** Submit P74. Which source most challenged your initial conclusion, and why? Answer: attribute a specific detail and explain the resulting qualification. An unsupported statement that every group benefited does not suffice (F, G).
- **E75, MA.USII.T4.3.** Explain why an increase in television purchases could be both an effect and a cause of economic growth. Include veterans' benefits and suburban housing in a connected explanation. Answer: incomes and expanded education and housing access supported demand; purchases and construction supported production and employment. Advertising encouraged consumption, but access remained unequal (G).
- **E76, MA.USII.T4.3; D2.Geo.8.9-12.** A fresh claim says that a veteran's eligibility proves access to any suburban home. Use a reserved Detroit neighborhood description and the map legend to evaluate it. Answer: identify a mapped lending classification and a documented discriminatory practice; formal eligibility did not remove lender, seller and neighborhood barriers. A map alone cannot decide an individual's loan outcome (G, H).
- **E77, MA.USII.T4.3; D2.Geo.8.9-12.** A new planning memo says, "More roads improve every place equally." Evaluate it with one place within the city or a suburb, plus one link to the rural fringe. Answer: locate evidence; explain how public routing and housing investment can redistribute access, divide neighborhoods and convert fringe land. Distinguish proposed from observed change and weigh benefits against costs (G, H).
- **E78, check-set addition.** Explain why termination and relocation were different and why neither means Native nations disappeared. Name an action toward restoration and one limit of restoration. Answer: termination withdrew federal recognition and obligations; relocation encouraged movement to cities. Menominee organizing secured restoration in 1973; the Klamath Tribes regained recognition in 1986 without automatic return of their land base. Both continue as governments today (I).
- **E79, MA.USII.T4.2; D2.His.5.9-12.** A fresh claim says that one proven espionage case establishes the guilt of everyone who attended the same meeting. Explain the error, a source of real security concern and a civil-liberties cost of generalized suspicion. Answer: conduct requires individual evidence; documented spying and Soviet ties heightened fears, but association alone cannot prove spying. Investigations and blacklists could damage employment and speech (J).
- **E80, MA.USII.T4.2.** Submit R80. Explain why Senate censure of McCarthy did not establish that federal discrimination against gay workers had ended. Answer: distinguish a senator's decline from separate employment policies; cite Kameny's challenge and its limits. Sexual orientation is no evidence of disloyalty or espionage (J).
- **E81, MA.USII.T3.9 and T4.2; RH.11-12.9.** Submit W81. Identify two sources that pull your argument in different directions; explain how you weighed them. Answer: attributed foreign and domestic evidence, a causal judgment and a reasoned response to contrary evidence (A, J). Add an open capstone question.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach containment, planning, incentive, redlining, covenant, recognition, termination, relocation and due process. Offer: "This establishes __, but cannot establish __." Allow home-language rehearsal; compare speech with writing to distinguish language from content gaps. Read at most 250 words independently per sitting; read laws aloud. Provide described maps, transcripts and scribed checks. Extension tests competing causes.

Name discrimination and civilian harms plainly; omit slurs and graphic material. Require no personal disclosures. Show community organizing alongside exclusion. Native nations continued; relocation and termination differed. No victim or perpetrator role play.
