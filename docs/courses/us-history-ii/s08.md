**Collected work and time.** P110 is individual work, scored as part of the unit assessment: five minutes to add Cuba, Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia to the flashpoint map, sixteen for four timeline rows, and nine for comparison. Each row gives the president's Vietnam decision, intended purpose, one Vietnamese consequence and one American consequence, with dated source references. Teach with different passages; no completed row is supplied. The remaining day 110 minutes are retrieval 5, instruction 10 and exit ticket 5.

P110's answer distinguishes Eisenhower's direct aid to Diem, Kennedy's increased advisers and support for counterinsurgency, Johnson's bombing and ground-force escalation, and Nixon's troop withdrawals, Vietnamization, expanded regional attacks and negotiations. Intended preservation of a noncommunist South was not achieved in 1975. Effects varied: aid strengthened some South Vietnamese institutions while repression and instability alienated others; advisory commitments deepened American involvement; escalation brought civilian deaths and displacement alongside American casualties and dissent; withdrawal reduced American ground exposure while fighting and insecurity continued for Vietnamese. Classify a result as intended or unintended relative to a named actor, not as a synonym for good or bad. Sources can support several bounded judgments.

**P110 look-fors:** accurate four-policy chronology; comparison rather than four isolated labels; intentions distinguished from results; Vietnamese and American consequences supported by evidence; local agency and uncertainty retained. Meets: "Eisenhower offered conditional direct aid; Johnson later committed combat forces. Both sought a durable noncommunist South, but escalation increased Vietnamese displacement and American casualties. Aid did not give either president control over Vietnamese decisions." This illustrates comparison; a complete answer still supplies all four rows. Does not meet: "All four invaded to help everyone, and Nixon won in 1975." Missing or inaccurate rows receive separate feedback, not a single impression score.

Choose Kennedy, Johnson or Nixon to supply T5.1 evidence; an Eisenhower argument is also allowed, with B112 carrying T5.1. Day 111 uses retrieval 5, criteria and final source selection 10, writing 30 and exit ticket 5. Instruction on argument precedes this day; no draft-specific coaching during collection.

**W111 look-fors:** defensible judgment; sources interpreted in context; feasible alternative; evidence linking decision and consequences; answered counterclaim. **B112 key against these panels.** Kennedy used maritime interception in Cuba, sought legislation at home and expanded military air support in Vietnam; an announced proposal is not an enacted law or a measured result. Johnson's Medicare insurance and Job Corps training had different mechanisms and intended beneficiaries; both demanded resources alongside escalation. Nixon's negotiated opening and instruction to halt investigation require separate judgments. The 1972 arms agreements are background, not an additional required answer without a supplied source. Other bounded judgments supported by the excerpts can meet the look-fors.

**A112 look-fors:** correct date and context; stated purpose distinguished from an established fact; comparison of feasible choices; two populations' consequences; justified judgment with a limitation. Meets: "Nixon addressed Americans in 1970 to defend attacks in Cambodia while withdrawing troops. He rejected inaction and described aid as insufficient. Those claims show his case for expansion, not its necessity. Later protest and continuing Vietnamese fighting qualify the promised protection; the speech alone cannot measure civilian effects." Does not meet: "This is Johnson at Tonkin, proving withdrawal ended the war."

**B112 look-fors:** all three presidents analyzed through primary evidence; Kennedy's domestic and foreign choices distinguished; Medicare and Job Corps accurately separated and set against escalation; Nixon's China opening and the tape weighed without cancelling either. Each panel earns separate feedback. Meets: "Kennedy's quarantine speech supports a bounded confrontation; his civil rights address calls for domestic legislation, not proof it had passed. Johnson's Medicare insured older beneficiaries and Job Corps offered vocational training to young people; escalation drew money and attention from those commitments. Nixon's China opening reduced some tensions; his private Watergate conduct still raises a separate abuse-of-power judgment." Does not meet: "Kennedy invaded Cuba in 1962, Johnson gave everyone Medicare, and Nixon's China visit made the cover-up acceptable."

**C112, check-set coverage:** A new exhibit says the draft and the Twenty-sixth Amendment were the same law and that federal troops fired at Kent State. Correct both claims and explain one connection between military policy and civic participation. Answer: conscription inducted people into service; the amendment protected citizens aged eighteen and older against age-based voting denial; Ohio National Guard members killed four students and wounded nine on May 4, 1970. Draft-age arguments and protest connected foreign policy with participation, without proving a single cause for amendment ratification. Wrong answers: voting ended the draft (F); federal troops fired at Kent State (I).

**C112 look-fors:** distinguish legal mechanisms, identify the shooting's agents, and make a bounded connection. Meets: "Voting protection did not end induction. The Ohio National Guard fired at Kent State; opposition to the war and draft-age arguments helped connect foreign policy to participation." Does not meet: "The amendment stopped induction, and federal troops fought enemy soldiers at Kent State."
