## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How should the choices open at the time and their later consequences shape our judgments of the presidents from Eisenhower to Nixon?"
**Hook, day 103.** Read Eisenhower's aid letter beside a map of Vietnam's temporary 1954 division. Whose choices could aid not control?
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: Why commit (103 and 104)? How did domestic promises coexist with escalation (105 and 106)? Who challenged policy (107)? What did withdrawal change (108)? How should diplomacy and abuses of power affect judgment (109 to 111)?
**Closing claim, day 111.** Judge one president using two primary sources and one outcome record from field 10, an available alternative and an answered counterclaim.
**Teaching.** Model sourcing on 103, public justification versus private options on 104, and causation on 106. Compare accounts on 107 and 108; research independently on 110 and argue on 111. Practise D3.1.9-12 and D2.His.10.9-12. Section 8.1 owns both standards: summarizing policies, explaining consequences and analyzing presidential records.

**Collected work and time.** P110 is individual work, scored as part of the unit assessment: five minutes to add Cuba, Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia to the flashpoint map, sixteen for four timeline rows, and nine for comparison. Each row gives the president's Vietnam decision, intended purpose, one Vietnamese consequence and one American consequence, with dated source references. Use Eisenhower's aid letter, Kennedy's Vietnam directive, Tonkin and Johnson's escalation records, Nixon's Vietnamization statement, and independently selected outcome passages. Teach with different passages; no completed row is supplied. The remaining day 110 minutes are retrieval 5, instruction 10 and exit ticket 5.

P110's answer distinguishes Eisenhower's direct aid to Diem, Kennedy's increased advisers and support for counterinsurgency, Johnson's bombing and ground-force escalation, and Nixon's troop withdrawals, Vietnamization, expanded regional attacks and negotiations. Intended preservation of a noncommunist South was not achieved in 1975. Effects varied: aid strengthened some South Vietnamese institutions while repression and instability alienated others; advisory commitments deepened American involvement; escalation brought civilian deaths and displacement alongside American casualties and dissent; withdrawal reduced American ground exposure while fighting and insecurity continued for Vietnamese. Classify a result as intended or unintended relative to a named actor, not as a synonym for good or bad. Sources can support several bounded judgments.

**P110 look-fors:** accurate four-policy chronology; comparison rather than four isolated labels; intentions distinguished from results; Vietnamese and American consequences supported by evidence; local agency and uncertainty retained. Meets: "Eisenhower offered conditional direct aid; Johnson later committed combat forces. Both sought a durable noncommunist South, but escalation increased Vietnamese displacement and American casualties. Aid did not give either president control over Vietnamese decisions." This illustrates comparison; a complete answer still supplies all four rows. Does not meet: "All four invaded to help everyone, and Nixon won in 1975." Missing or inaccurate rows receive separate feedback, not a single impression score.

**W111, 30 minutes:** Judge one president in 250 to 350 words using two attributed primary sources and one outcome record. Weigh an alternative available then, explain a consequence for Vietnamese and Americans where Vietnam is the case, and answer the strongest counterclaim. Choose Kennedy, Johnson or Nixon to supply T5.1 evidence; an Eisenhower argument is also allowed, with B112 carrying T5.1. Day 111 uses retrieval 5, criteria and final source selection 10, writing 30 and exit ticket 5. Instruction on argument precedes this day; no draft-specific coaching during collection.

**W111 look-fors:** defensible judgment; sources interpreted in context; feasible alternative; evidence linking decision and consequences; answered counterclaim. Meets: "Johnson argued escalation would protect South Vietnam. Negotiation remained an alternative, though he feared its strategic costs. Tonkin's authorization did not establish that bombing would secure political loyalty. The later record of casualties and displacement weakens that expectation, even allowing his concern about abandoning an ally." Does not meet: "Johnson was bad because I dislike war." A sample illustrates reasoning, not a prescribed verdict; other judgments can meet the same criteria.

**Day 112:** A112 takes 22 minutes, B112 takes 18, and C112 takes 5. Leave five minutes for marking short components. These are three tasks, with B112's three brief panels, not three essays. Provide source metadata, approximately 180 words for A112 and no more than 80 words per B112 panel, with concise response boxes. B112 allows five minutes for Kennedy, seven for Johnson and six for Nixon. P110 and W111 are already collected, not rewritten on assessment day.

**A112 source reservation.** Use the April 30 address's paragraphs introducing three options through the announcement of joint American and South Vietnamese attacks. Preserve paragraph breaks and label omissions; exclude material outside those decision paragraphs. Supply an original context card on the Cambodian war, the prior secret bombing, subsequent protest and continued Vietnamese fighting. Reserve this document and these questions from teaching and practice; day 108 uses the November 3, 1969 statement instead. Historical people and dates recur; the evidence selection and response situation are new.

**A112 look-fors:** correct date and context; stated purpose distinguished from an established fact; comparison of feasible choices; two populations' consequences; justified judgment with a limitation. Meets: "Nixon addressed Americans in 1970 to defend attacks in Cambodia while withdrawing troops. He rejected inaction and described aid as insufficient. Those claims show his case for expansion, not its necessity. Later protest and continuing Vietnamese fighting qualify the promised protection; the speech alone cannot measure civilian effects." Does not meet: "This is Johnson at Tonkin, proving withdrawal ended the war."

**B112 look-fors:** all three presidents analyzed through primary evidence; Kennedy's domestic and foreign choices distinguished; two Johnson programs accurately separated and set against escalation; Nixon's China opening and the tape weighed without cancelling either. Each panel earns separate feedback. Meets: "Kennedy's quarantine speech supports a bounded confrontation; his civil rights address calls for domestic legislation, not proof it had passed. Johnson's Medicare covered older people and Medicaid assisted eligible people with limited income; escalation drew money and attention from those commitments. Nixon's China opening reduced some tensions; his private Watergate conduct still raises a separate abuse-of-power judgment." Does not meet: "Kennedy invaded Cuba in 1962, Johnson gave everyone Medicare, and Nixon's China visit made the cover-up acceptable."

**C112, check-set coverage:** A new exhibit says the draft and the Twenty-sixth Amendment were the same law and that federal troops fired at Kent State. Correct both claims and explain one connection between military policy and civic participation. Answer: conscription inducted people into service; the amendment protected citizens aged eighteen and older against age-based voting denial; Ohio National Guard members killed four students and wounded nine on May 4, 1970. Draft-age arguments and protest connected foreign policy with participation, without proving a single cause for amendment ratification. Wrong answers: voting ended the draft (F); all protesters and veterans agreed (H).

**C112 look-fors:** distinguish legal mechanisms, identify the shooting's agents, and make a bounded connection. Meets: "Voting protection did not end induction. The Ohio National Guard fired at Kent State; opposition to the war and draft-age arguments helped connect foreign policy to participation." Does not meet: "The amendment stopped induction, and federal troops fought enemy soldiers at Kent State."
