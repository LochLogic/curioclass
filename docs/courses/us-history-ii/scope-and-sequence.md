# Scope and sequence: US History II (the 1920s to today)

Course `us-history-ii` (catalogue id `us-hist-hs`), grade 12, full year (160 days), draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/us-history-ii/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment built on unseen sources, and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Days with Native, hard-history, LGBTQ, disability or health content carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. There is no base text, so there is no source-map.csv; the partial bases (Since Time Immemorial in units 3, 6, 9 and 11; Principles of Economics 2e in units 1 and 3) are credited on the days that adapt them. Since Time Immemorial days keep the content as written, in our structure and tone, with one credit line saying the content is the tribes'.

The course is thin after the redraw (data/plans/later.csv L8), so each unit below names the topics it teaches in full; together they fill the year inside the frame's units and days. Lettered codes sit on the days that teach them. MA.USII.T2.4 is read as 11 single options, a to k (L2 done): the class surveys all eleven on day 32 and each student or group takes one in depth on days 33 and 34.

Readiness checks open units 1, 7 and 9, the units that assume a grade 8 row (idle three grades). Units that assume a grade 10 or 11 row open with a retrieval task instead. Every unit's first and last teaching days end with the exit routine (one open question noted), so the capstone question can be chosen on day 123.

## Unit 1. The economic toolkit (days 1 to 12, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** scarcity and trade-offs; the goals of economic policy and why they conflict; supply and demand on a simple graph and what moves it; profit and risk; financial markets, investment and a made-up asset tracked on paper; asset prices and returns; national income, employment and prices with GDP and supplied indicators; banks and the panics of 1907, 1933 and 2008; the Federal Reserve since 1913, its structure and tools; wars and technologies in business cycles (the Civil War, canals and railroads, the space age, the internet); government's roles in a market economy; fiscal and monetary policy in the 1930s, 1970s and 2008 (first pass).

Arc: six toolkit days (choice, goals, the graph, profit and investment, asset prices, the whole economy), then four on money and government (banks and panics, the Fed, business cycles and government's roles, policy at work and an argument on who should steer). The tools come before the history that uses them; the Fed follows the panics that made it.

- Readiness: day 1, on grade 8's opportunity cost, budget and data panel (ss8-public-budget). The reteach is a station on day 2.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 11: an unseen panic or war boom with a data table, the chain traced and the response judged. Respond day 12.
- Candidate: none in the frame.

## Unit 2. Tradition and modernity, about 1913 to 1930 (days 13 to 25, weeks 3 to 5)

**Topics taught:** women's war service and women voting after 1920; the Harlem Renaissance (Hughes, Hurston, its patrons); the Armory Show; the Scopes trial and fundamentalism; Prohibition and the Jazz Age; gay and lesbian life in cities; the consumer economy, Harding, Coolidge and Teapot Dome; refugees, the Red Scare, the 1924 quotas and the Indian Citizenship Act; the revived Klan, the Tulsa Race Massacre and the first Great Migration; eugenics, forced sterilization and Buck v. Bell; the Bread and Roses strike, the Boston police strike, Sacco and Vanzetti.

Arc: five days on the decade's new freedoms (culture, art, faith, nightlife, the consumer economy), four on its fears (immigration, racial terror, eugenics, labor and radicals), then two days to plan and write the year's first full sourced argument on both sides of one question. Freedoms first so the fears read as the reaction to them.

- Readiness: none. Day 13 opens with a retrieval task on World War I and Jim Crow (grade 11).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 24: two unseen 1920s sources, one modern and one traditional. Respond day 25.
- Candidate: women voting after the 19th Amendment, kept inside day 13, 0 added days. The Indian Citizenship Act, kept inside the quotas day (day 18), 0 added days. The consumer economy and Harding, Coolidge and Teapot Dome, kept together, 1 day (day 17).

## Unit 3. Depression and New Deal (days 26 to 39, weeks 6 to 8)

**Topics taught:** the Depression's causes and effects worldwide; Keynesian and monetarist accounts compared; a primary source report on how Americans responded (the Bonus Army, farm protests, family survival, Dust Bowl migrants with a climate record and maps, the forced removal of Mexican Americans); the New Deal and its people (Hoover, the Roosevelts, Perkins, Long, Coughlin); the eleven case study options surveyed (the FDIC, the SEC, the TVA, Social Security, the labor relations act, the WPA, the labor standards act, the AFL, the CIO, the Communist Party, America First and antisemitism) with one in depth; bank runs and deposit insurance; the 1937 court plan in brief; the Indian Reorganization Act; how well the New Deal worked and for whom.

Arc: two days on causes and the two accounts, four on the primary source report, then the New Deal survey, a two-day case study, the Indian Reorganization Act, a day on results and for whom, and the argument. The report comes before the New Deal so students know who needed relief before judging the relief.

- Readiness: none. Day 26 opens with a retrieval task on the Depression worldwide (grade 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 38: an unseen program record and a Depression data set. Respond day 39.
- Candidate: the Indian Reorganization Act, kept, 1 day (day 35, Since Time Immemorial). Roosevelt's 1937 court plan, kept in brief inside the survey day (day 32), 0 added days.

## Course-level check 1 (days 40 to 41, weeks 8 to 9)

Day 40 (week 8) checks units 1 to 3: an unseen task on the 1920s and 1930s with the unit 1 chain used on a real event, plus a short knowledge section. Day 41 is its respond day.

## Unit 4. Isolation, fascism and the Holocaust (days 42 to 56, weeks 9 to 12)

**Topics taught:** the League's rejection, isolationism and the Neutrality Acts; fascism and Nazism and the laws that led to the Holocaust; Lend-Lease; why the United States entered the war; the Allied and Axis powers on the world map; the roads to war (the Rhineland, Munich, Austria and Czechoslovakia, the Nazi-Soviet Pact and Poland, Japan's invasion of China and the Nanjing Massacre); the major battles and the Yalta and Potsdam conferences, one studied from sources; the Tuskegee Airmen, the 442nd and the Navajo Code Talkers; the Allied response to the Holocaust (quotas, Evian, the St. Louis, what officials knew, the War Refugee Board, liberation, Nuremberg).

Arc: four days from isolation to Pearl Harbor, four on the war's course (map, roads to war, battles and conferences, a source study), one on soldiers and special units, three on the Holocaust response, and the argument. The response follows the war's course so students judge what was known against what was possible.

- Readiness: none. Day 42 opens with a retrieval task on fascism and the road to war (grade 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 55: unseen dated documents from 1938 to 1944. Respond day 56.
- Candidate: soldiers and the segregated and special units, kept, 1 day (day 50).

## Unit 5. The home front and the bomb (days 57 to 66, weeks 12 to 14)

**Topics taught:** wartime growth and the second Great Migration; Randolph and the 1941 order; women in munitions and military jobs and the first programmers; the incarceration of Japanese Americans and Japanese Canadians, Korematsu and its 2018 repudiation, the 1988 redress; two wars and civil rights demands; the Bracero program and the Zoot Suit riots; the bomb's domestic decision record, survivors' accounts, the nuclear debate to the early 1960s and the 1995 exhibit dispute.

Arc: four home front days, three on the bomb, then the argument on what winning cost. The home front closes the war at home; the nuclear debate hands to the Cold War.

- Readiness: none. Day 57 opens with a retrieval task on women's work and immigration (grade 11).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 65: an unseen home front source and an unseen later account of the bomb. Respond day 66.
- Candidate: the Bracero program and the Zoot Suit riots, kept inside the T3.8f day (day 60), 0 added days.

## Unit 6. The Cold War at home and abroad, 1945 to about 1960 (days 67 to 83, weeks 14 to 17)

**Topics taught:** the Cold War's causes from Soviet and American sources; Eastern Europe, the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, NATO and the Warsaw Pact, Korea; communism compared with a market economy; flashpoints on one running world map; Sputnik and science education; one Truman or Eisenhower policy researched; the GI Bill, suburbs, television and consumerism; redlining and covenants; highways and housing reshaping one metropolitan area; termination and relocation of Native nations; espionage, loyalty programs and blacklists; McCarthy, the Lavender Scare and McCarthyism's opponents.

Arc: six days abroad (causes, containment, alliances and Korea, the two systems, the map, Sputnik), six at home (a policy researched and presented, the boom, who was shut out, the metropolitan maps, termination), two on the Red Scare, and the argument. One decade seen abroad, at home and in politics, with the map work running across it.

- Readiness: none. Day 67 opens with a retrieval task on the two blocs (grade 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 82: an unseen Cold War document and an unseen metropolitan map. Respond day 83.
- Candidate: termination and relocation, kept, 1 day (day 78, Since Time Immemorial unit 4).

## Course-level check 2 (days 84 to 85, week 17)

Day 84 is the mid-year check on units 1 to 6, weighted to 4 to 6: an unseen document set on the war or postwar years and a sourced argument with a counterclaim. Day 85, its respond day, closes the fall half.

## Unit 7. The civil rights movement and its laws (days 86 to 102, weeks 18 to 21)

**Topics taught:** the movement's roots and goals; King, Parks, Baker, Hamer, Malcolm X; the NAACP, SNCC and CORE; Mendez v. Westminster and Mexican American civil rights; Brown, Massive Resistance and Emmett Till; Montgomery, Little Rock and Eisenhower's record; nonviolence, Gandhi, the sit-ins and Freedom Rides; Birmingham, the March on Washington, Selma and King's assassination; how the 1964 and 1965 acts were won; demographic data from 1964 to 2010; fair housing, affirmative action and the 2023 ruling, Shelby County, the Warren Court in brief; gaps in education, health, wealth and incarceration and Obama's election; Massachusetts leaders, METCO and the 1974 Boston order; Detroit, Denver and Charlotte compared.

Arc: seven days on the movement (roots, people, Mendez, Brown, Montgomery, nonviolence, the great campaigns), four on its laws and what followed in data, three on the Boston case beside other regions, and the argument. The movement first, then its laws, then a Northern case, so the law is read as the movement's result and its limits.

- Readiness: day 86, on grade 8's civil rights laws (ss8-civil-rights-laws). The reteach is a station on day 87.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 101: an unseen protest document and an unseen court excerpt. Respond day 102.
- Candidate: Mendez v. Westminster, kept, 1 day (day 88). The Warren Court rulings, kept in brief inside the T4.9 day (day 95), 0 added days.

## Unit 8. Presidents and the war in Vietnam, 1954 to 1975 (days 103 to 113, weeks 21 to 23)

**Topics taught:** Eisenhower's commitment after 1954 (Geneva, Diem); Kennedy and the Cuban missile crisis; the Great Society (Medicare, Medicaid, the War on Poverty, the 1965 immigration law); the Gulf of Tonkin, escalation, Tet and My Lai; the antiwar movement and veterans, the draft, the 26th Amendment and Kent State; Vietnamization, Laos and Cambodia, the fall of Saigon and Vietnamese, Hmong and Cambodian refugees; detente, the opening to China and Watergate; the flashpoint map added to.

Arc: one president at a time on one timeline (Eisenhower, Kennedy, Johnson at home and at war, the antiwar movement, Nixon at war and in diplomacy), then the map and the weighing of results, and the argument judging one president.

- Readiness: none. Day 103 opens with a retrieval task on the Cold War in Asia (grade 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 112: an unseen presidential document from 1954 to 1975. Respond day 113.
- Candidate: the draft, the 26th Amendment and Kent State, kept inside the antiwar day (day 107), 0 added days.

## Unit 9. Movements for rights since the 1960s (days 114 to 123, weeks 23 to 25)

**Topics taught:** what makes a movement succeed; women's rights (Title IX, the ERA, Roe and Dobbs, O'Connor, feminist writers, the pill, NOW); LGBTQ civil rights (Stonewall, Pride, AIDS activism, Goodridge, Obergefell); disability rights (the 1977 sit-ins, deinstitutionalization, independent living, the 1975 education law, the ADA, IDEA); the environment (Silent Spring, the clean air, water and wetlands laws); farmworkers and workers' rights (Chavez, Huerta, protection against discrimination and harassment); Native nations' rights and sovereignty (the Indian Civil Rights Act, Alcatraz, AIM, Wounded Knee 1973, self-determination laws, preserving cultures); the Chicano and Asian American movements; one movement in depth and the comparison across movements.

Arc: two survey days, three in-depth days (causes, course and tactics, opponents and results), a comparison, presentations and the argument. The survey comes first so every student chooses a movement knowing the field.

- Readiness: day 114, on grade 8's protest and change (ss8-protest-change). The reteach is a station on day 115.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 122: an unseen document from a movement not studied in depth. Respond day 123, which is also the day each student chooses the capstone question from the exit-routine notes.
- Candidate: the Chicano and Asian American movements, kept inside survey day 2 (day 115), 0 added days.

## Unit 10. Liberals, conservatives and the end of the Cold War, about 1968 to 1991 (days 124 to 134, weeks 25 to 27)

**Topics taught:** mid-century liberalism (the New Deal legacy, unions, welfare programs, regulation) and its results; the 1970s (stagflation, the oil shocks, the Iran hostage crisis, Carter); the conservative movement and Reagan (tax cuts, deregulation, anti-communism, the 1981 air traffic controllers' strike); the two traditions weighed by results; failing communist economies and Solidarity; US policy, arms control, the Berlin Wall and the Soviet collapse; the flashpoint map completed.

Arc: two days on liberalism, the 1970s as the hinge, two on conservatism, a day weighing both, then three on the Cold War's end. Each tradition is explained in terms its supporters accept before it is weighed. Part of day 128 goes to each student's capstone source set.

- Readiness: none. Day 130 opens the Cold War's end with a retrieval task on the Soviet collapse (grade 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 133: two unseen sources, one liberal and one conservative. Respond day 134.
- Candidate: the 1970s, kept, 1 day (day 126).

## Unit 11. The United States since 1990 (days 135 to 145, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** the computer and internet revolution and the human genome; Asian and Latin American immigration after 1965 compared with 1880 to 1920; the elections since 1992, the Affordable Care Act and the COVID-19 pandemic on one timeline; the 2008 financial crisis in full; Native nations' self-government today; the Gulf War; the September 11 attacks; Homeland Security, the Patriot Act and Freedom Act, Hamdi and Boumediene, the backlash against Muslim Americans; the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq judged against their goals.

Arc: two trend days, three check set days, three on terrorism and the response, and the argument. Part of day 135 goes to each student's capstone source set (ELA 12's source list day where paired).

- Readiness: none. Day 140 opens with a retrieval task on terrorism in the world since 1990 (grade 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 144: an unseen recent policy record. Respond day 145.
- Candidate, in the frame's order of keeping: the 2008 crisis, kept, 1 day (day 138); Native self-government today, kept, 1 day (day 139, Since Time Immemorial unit 6); the Gulf War, folded into the September 11 day (day 140), 0 added days; the survey of elections, the Affordable Care Act and COVID-19, kept, 1 day (day 137). In a short senior year the cut order is the survey (day 137), then Native self-government (day 139), then 2008 (day 138), as the frame sets.

## Course-level check 3 (days 146 to 147, week 30)

Day 146 is the year-end check: the year's knowledge and a continuity-and-change essay across at least two units from unseen sources. Day 147, its respond day, reviews each student's capstone question and source set.

## Unit 12. Capstone: a researched argument (days 148 to 160, weeks 30 to 32)

**Topics taught:** the student's own question from the 1920s to today; the source set audited and corroborated; the strongest counterclaim; drafting, peer critique for credibility, critique of a historical explanation, revision; two forms for two audiences, one beyond the classroom; the year's informed action.

Arc: question and sources, corroboration, draft, two critiques, revision, two forms, rehearsal, presentation, then the action and the final argument. Days fall against ELA 12's milestones where the two are paired: talks inside days 150 to 156 (day 156 here) and the paper on day 158.

- Readiness: none. Day 148 opens with a retrieval task on the research cycle and argument (grade 11).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 159: critique an unseen historical explanation, with the capstone scored on the course rubric against D4.3 to D4.5. Respond day 160, with the look back on the action.
- Candidate: none in the frame.
- Short senior year: the unit runs in 10 days by folding the rehearsal (day 155) into the forms day, the two critiques (days 151 and 152) into one, and planning and taking the action (days 157 and 158) into one. The audience and the action stay.
