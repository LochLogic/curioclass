# Scope and sequence: US History II (the 1920s to today)

Course `us-history-ii` (catalogue id `us-hist-hs`), grade 12, full year (160 days), draft (2026-11-14), revised after the expert and Codex reads of 2026-11-14. The days live in data/plans/us-history-ii/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment built on unseen sources, and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Days with Native, hard-history, LGBTQ, disability or health content carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. There is no base text, so there is no source-map.csv; the partial bases (Since Time Immemorial in units 3, 6, 9 and 11; Principles of Economics 2e in units 1 and 3, by chapter or section: 2, 3, 17, 19.1, 21.1, 22.1, 27, 28 and 30.4 in unit 1, chapter 25 for the Keynesian account in unit 3, with the monetary account from the Federal Reserve's dated history of the Great Depression) are credited on the days that adapt them. Day 78's Since Time Immemorial guide replaces its role play with document analysis. Since Time Immemorial days keep the content as written, in our structure and tone, with one credit line saying the content is the tribes'.

The course is thin after the redraw (data/plans/later.csv L8), so each unit below names the topics it teaches in full; together they fill the year inside the frame's units and days. Lettered codes sit on the days that teach them. MA.USII.T2.4 is read as 11 single options, a to k (L2 done): the class meets all eleven as an orientation menu on day 32 and each student or group takes one in depth on days 33 and 34, where the era's people are read from primary sources as the case takes them. Survey days orient; the case chosen is the one taught in depth, with every item its option names.

Every unit's first and last teaching days end with the exit routine (one open question noted), so the capstone question can be chosen on day 123.

## End-of-unit assessments

Each end-of-unit assessment is a composite: an unseen task for what one sitting can test, plus the unit's own products (a source study, a report, a presentation, an argument, a timeline) scored on the course rubric for what a sitting cannot. Each end-of-unit day's note lists every part of every code under "Parts asked", with a short prompt for each part the main task misses (settled rule 4). No day is added.

## Unit 1. The economic toolkit (days 1 to 12, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** scarcity and trade-offs; the goals of economic policy and why they conflict; supply and demand on a simple graph and what moves it; profit and risk; financial markets, investment and a made-up asset tracked on paper; asset prices and returns; national income, employment and prices with GDP and supplied indicators; banks and the panics of 1907, 1933 and 2008; the Federal Reserve since 1913, its structure and tools; wars and technologies in business cycles (the Civil War, canals and railroads, the space age, the internet); government's roles in a market economy; fiscal and monetary policy in the 1930s, 1970s and 2008 (first pass).

Arc: six toolkit days (choice, goals with government's roles as the categories policies fall into, the graph, profit and investment, asset prices tracked across several years of a made-up series, the whole economy), then four on money and government (banks and panics, the Fed, business cycles, policy at work and an argument on who should steer). The tools come before the history that uses them; the Fed follows the panics that made it.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook, on grade 8's opportunity cost, budget and data panel (ss8-public-budget). The reteach is a station on day 2.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 11: an unseen panic or war boom with a data table, the chain traced and the response judged, with day 5's tracking sheet and day 10's argument scored. Respond day 12.
- Candidate: none in the frame.

## Unit 2. Tradition and modernity, about 1913 to 1930 (days 13 to 25, weeks 3 to 5)

**Topics taught:** women's war service and women voting after 1920; the Harlem Renaissance (Hughes, Hurston, its patrons); the Armory Show; the Scopes trial and fundamentalism; Prohibition and the Jazz Age; gay and lesbian life in cities; the consumer economy, Harding, Coolidge and Teapot Dome; refugees, the Red Scare, the 1924 quotas and the Indian Citizenship Act; the revived Klan, the Tulsa Race Massacre and the first Great Migration; eugenics, forced sterilization and Buck v. Bell; the Bread and Roses strike, the Boston police strike, Sacco and Vanzetti.

Arc: five days on the decade's new freedoms (culture, art, faith, nightlife, the consumer economy), four on its fears (immigration, racial terror, eugenics, labor and radicals), then two days to plan and write the year's first full sourced argument on both sides of one question. Freedoms first so the fears read as the reaction to them.

- Readiness: none. Day 13 opens with a retrieval task on World War I and Jim Crow (grade 11).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 24: two unseen 1920s sources, one modern and one traditional. Respond day 25.
- Candidate: women voting after the 19th Amendment, kept inside day 13, 0 added days. The Indian Citizenship Act, kept inside the quotas day (day 18), 0 added days. The consumer economy and Harding, Coolidge and Teapot Dome, kept together, 1 day (day 17).

## Unit 3. Depression and New Deal (days 26 to 39, weeks 6 to 8)

**Topics taught:** the Depression's causes and effects worldwide; Keynesian and monetarist accounts compared; a primary source report on how Americans responded (the Bonus Army, farm protests, family survival, Dust Bowl migrants with a long-term climate series, a dated drought record and maps at local, regional and world scales for migration, settlement, resource use and land use, the forced removal of Mexican Americans); the New Deal and its people (Hoover, the Roosevelts, Perkins, Long, Coughlin, Lindbergh); the eleven case study options as an orientation menu (the FDIC, the SEC, the TVA, Social Security, the labor relations act, the WPA, the labor standards act, the AFL, the CIO, the Communist Party, America First and antisemitism) with one in depth; the 1930 to 1933 bank runs (day 26) and deposit insurance today (day 36); the 1937 court plan in brief; the Indian Reorganization Act; how well the New Deal worked and for whom.

Arc: two days on causes and the two accounts, four on the primary source report, then the New Deal survey, a two-day case study, the Indian Reorganization Act, a day on results and for whom, and the argument. The report comes before the New Deal so students know who needed relief before judging the relief.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 25, on the Depression worldwide (wh2-great-depression, last used in grade 10). The reteach is a station on day 28.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 38: an unseen program record and a Depression data set, with day 31's report and its geographic evaluation (T2.3, D2.Geo.9), day 34's case presentation (T2.4) and day 37's argument comparing the two accounts (T2.2) scored. Respond day 39.
- Candidate: the Indian Reorganization Act, kept, 1 day (day 35, Since Time Immemorial). Roosevelt's 1937 court plan, kept in brief inside the survey day (day 32), 0 added days.

## Course-level check 1 (days 40 to 41, weeks 8 to 9)

Day 40 (week 8) checks units 1 to 3: an unseen task with the unit 1 chain used on a real event, plus a short knowledge section. It falls right after unit 3's assessment (day 38), so it does not repeat it (settled rule 3): its items on unit 3's skills draw content from units 1 and 2 (a 1920s case or a unit 1 panic), not the Depression and New Deal sources. Day 41 is its respond day.

## Unit 4. Isolation, fascism and the Holocaust (days 42 to 56, weeks 9 to 12)

**Topics taught:** the League's rejection, isolationism and the Neutrality Acts; fascism and Nazism and the laws that led to the Holocaust; Lend-Lease; why the United States entered the war; the Allied and Axis powers on the world map; the roads to war (the Rhineland, Munich, Austria and Czechoslovakia, the Nazi-Soviet Pact and Poland, Japan's invasion of China and the Nanjing Massacre); the major battles and the Yalta and Potsdam conferences, one studied from sources; the Tuskegee Airmen, the 442nd and the Navajo Code Talkers; the Allied response to the Holocaust (quotas, Evian, the St. Louis, what officials knew, the War Refugee Board, liberation, Nuremberg).

Arc: four days from isolation to Pearl Harbor, four on the war's course (map, roads to war, battles and conferences, a source study), one on soldiers and special units, three on the Holocaust response, and the argument. The response follows the war's course so students judge what was known against what was possible. Day 47 is signposted as a step back from 1941 to the 1930s.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 41 (course-level check 1's respond day), one check on the four grade 10 rows (wh2-totalitarian-regimes, wh2-road-to-war, wh2-wwii-war-and-diplomacy, wh2-holocaust); day 42 retrieves World War I. The reteach is a station on day 44; days 46 and 51 use the war and Holocaust items for targeted support.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 55: unseen dated documents from 1938 to 1946 (before, during and after the war), with short items on entry, the map and one conference, and day 49's source study scored. Respond day 56.
- Candidate: soldiers and the segregated and special units, kept, 1 day (day 50).

## Unit 5. The home front and the bomb (days 57 to 66, weeks 12 to 14)

**Topics taught:** wartime growth and the second Great Migration; Randolph and the 1941 order; women in munitions and military jobs and the first programmers; the incarceration of Japanese Americans and Japanese Canadians, Korematsu and its 2018 repudiation, the 1988 redress; two wars and civil rights demands; the Bracero program and the Zoot Suit riots; the bomb's domestic decision record, survivors' accounts, the nuclear debate to the early 1960s and the 1995 exhibit dispute.

Arc: four home front days, three on the bomb, then the argument on what winning cost. The home front closes the war at home; the nuclear debate hands to the Cold War.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 56, on the bomb decision (wh2-atomic-bombs, last used in grade 10); day 57 retrieves women's work and immigration from grade 11. The reteach is a station on day 61.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 65: an unseen home front source and an unseen later account of the bomb, with a short prompt on each home front part and on the reasons given at the time and the early nuclear debate. Respond day 66.
- Candidate: the Bracero program and the Zoot Suit riots, kept inside the T3.8f day (day 60), 0 added days.

## Unit 6. The Cold War at home and abroad, 1945 to about 1960 (days 67 to 83, weeks 14 to 17)

**Topics taught:** the Cold War's causes from Soviet and American sources; Eastern Europe, the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, NATO and the Warsaw Pact, Korea; communism compared with a market economy; flashpoints on one running world map; Sputnik and science education; one Truman or Eisenhower policy researched; the GI Bill, suburbs, television and consumerism; redlining and covenants; highways and housing reshaping one metropolitan area; termination and relocation of Native nations; espionage, loyalty programs and blacklists; McCarthy, the Lavender Scare and McCarthyism's opponents.

Arc: six days abroad (causes, containment, alliances and Korea, the two systems, the map, Sputnik), six at home (a policy researched and presented, the boom, who was shut out, the metropolitan maps, termination), two on the Red Scare, and the argument. One decade seen abroad, at home and in politics, with the map work running across it.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 66, on the two blocs (wh2-cold-war-blocs, last used in grade 10). The reteach is a station on day 68. Day 75 retrieves how industry reshaped cities (US History I used it).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 82: an unseen Cold War document and an unseen metropolitan map, with day 74's policy presentation and day 81's argument scored and short prompts on the two systems, a flashpoint, Sputnik and McCarthyism. Respond day 83.
- Candidate: termination and relocation, kept, 1 day (day 78, Since Time Immemorial unit 4).

## Unit 7. The civil rights movement and its laws (days 86 to 102, weeks 18 to 21)

**Topics taught:** the movement's roots and goals; King, Parks, Baker, Hamer, Malcolm X; the NAACP, SNCC and CORE; Mendez v. Westminster and Mexican American civil rights; Brown, Massive Resistance and Emmett Till; Montgomery, Little Rock and Eisenhower's record; nonviolence, Gandhi, the sit-ins and Freedom Rides; Birmingham, the March on Washington, Selma and King's assassination; how the 1964 and 1965 acts were won; demographic data from 1964 to 2010; fair housing, affirmative action and the 2023 ruling, Shelby County, the Warren Court in brief; gaps in education, health, wealth and incarceration and Obama's election; Massachusetts leaders, METCO and the 1974 Boston order; Detroit, Denver and Charlotte compared.

Arc: seven days on the movement (roots, people, Mendez, Brown, Montgomery, nonviolence, the great campaigns), four on its laws and what followed in data, three on the Boston case beside other regions, and the argument. The movement first, then its laws, then a Northern case, so the law is read as the movement's result and its limits.

- Readiness: none. Day 86 opens with a retrieval task on civil rights law (ss8-civil-rights-laws, checked in Government's unit 2 this fall), early organizing and Jim Crow.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 101: an unseen protest document and an unseen court excerpt, with day 94's data reading, day 99's comparison and day 100's argument scored and a short prompt on each lettered part. Respond day 102.
- Candidate: Mendez v. Westminster, kept, 1 day (day 88). The Warren Court rulings, kept in brief inside the T4.9 day (day 95), 0 added days.

## Unit 8. Presidents and the war in Vietnam, 1954 to 1975 (days 103 to 113, weeks 21 to 23)

**Topics taught:** Eisenhower's commitment after 1954 (Geneva, Diem); Kennedy and the Cuban missile crisis; the Great Society (Medicare, Medicaid, the War on Poverty, the 1965 immigration law); the Gulf of Tonkin, escalation, Tet and My Lai; the antiwar movement and veterans, the draft, the 26th Amendment and Kent State; Vietnamization, Laos and Cambodia, the fall of Saigon and Vietnamese, Hmong and Cambodian refugees; detente, the opening to China and Watergate; the flashpoint map added to.

Arc: one president at a time on one timeline (Eisenhower, Kennedy, Johnson at home and at war, the antiwar movement, Nixon at war and in diplomacy), then the map and the weighing of results, and the argument judging one president.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 102, on the Cold War in Asia (wh2-cold-war-world, last used in grade 10). The reteach is a station on day 104.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 112: an unseen presidential document from 1954 to 1975, with day 110's four-presidency timeline and consequences for Vietnamese and Americans scored. Respond day 113.
- Candidate: the draft, the 26th Amendment and Kent State, kept inside the antiwar day (day 107), 0 added days.

## Unit 9. Movements for rights since the 1960s (days 114 to 123, weeks 23 to 25)

**Topics taught:** what makes a movement succeed; women's rights (Title IX, the ERA, Roe and Dobbs, O'Connor, feminist writers, the pill, NOW); LGBTQ civil rights (Stonewall, Pride, AIDS activism, Goodridge, Obergefell); disability rights (the 1977 sit-ins, deinstitutionalization, independent living, the 1975 education law, the ADA, IDEA); the environment (Silent Spring, the clean air, water and wetlands laws); farmworkers and workers' rights (Chavez, Huerta, protection against discrimination and harassment); Native nations' rights and sovereignty (the Indian Civil Rights Act, Alcatraz, AIM, Wounded Knee 1973, self-determination laws, preserving cultures); the Chicano and Asian American movements; one movement in depth and the comparison across movements.

Arc: three survey days (women's and LGBTQ movements; disability and environmental movements; workers' and Native movements with the Chicano and Asian American movements), three in-depth days (causes, course and tactics, opponents and results), presentations with the comparison, and the argument. The survey comes first so every student chooses a movement knowing the field.

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 113, on grade 8's protest and change and grade 10's decolonization (ss8-protest-change, wh2-decolonization). The reteach is a station on day 115. Day 120 retrieves earlier reform movements (grade 11) for the comparison.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 122: an unseen document from a movement not studied in depth, with the movement studied in depth scored from day 120's presentation against its option. Respond day 123, which is also the day each student chooses the capstone question from the exit-routine notes.
- Candidate: the Chicano and Asian American movements, kept inside survey day 3 (day 116), 0 added days.

## Unit 10. Liberals, conservatives and the end of the Cold War, about 1968 to 1991 (days 124 to 134, weeks 25 to 27)

**Topics taught:** mid-century liberalism (the New Deal legacy, unions, welfare programs, regulation) and its results; the 1970s (stagflation, the oil shocks, the Iran hostage crisis, Carter); the conservative movement and Reagan (tax cuts, deregulation, anti-communism, the 1981 air traffic controllers' strike); the two traditions weighed by results; failing communist economies and Solidarity; US policy, arms control, the Berlin Wall and the Soviet collapse; the flashpoint map completed.

Arc: two days on liberalism, the 1970s as the hinge, two on conservatism, a day weighing both, then three on the Cold War's end. Each tradition is explained in terms its supporters accept before it is weighed. Part of day 128 goes to each student's capstone source set.

- Readiness: none. Day 130 opens the Cold War's end with a retrieval task on the Soviet collapse (Economics' unit 1 readiness check checked it this spring; the items are printed for a student not taking Economics).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 133: two unseen sources, one liberal and one conservative, then a causal judgment on the Cold War's end from internal and external evidence. Respond day 134.
- Paired with ELA 12: day 132's exit routine adds one source that argues against the capstone claim to the shared set.
- Candidate: the 1970s, kept, 1 day (day 126).

## Unit 11. The United States since 1990 (days 135 to 145, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** the computer and internet revolution and the human genome; Asian and Latin American immigration after 1965 compared with 1880 to 1920; the elections since 1992, the Affordable Care Act and the COVID-19 pandemic on one timeline; the 2008 financial crisis in full; Native nations' self-government today; the Gulf War; the September 11 attacks; Homeland Security, the Patriot Act and Freedom Act, Hamdi and Boumediene, the backlash against Muslim Americans; the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq judged against their goals.

Arc: two trend days, three check set days, three on terrorism and the response, and the argument. Part of day 135 goes to each student's capstone source set (ELA 12's source list day where paired).

- Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 134, on terrorism in the world since 1990 (wh2-terrorism, last used in grade 10). The reteach is a station on day 140, where the unit reaches it.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 144: an unseen recent policy record, with day 136's immigration comparison and a short prompt on one technological trend. Respond day 145.
- Paired with ELA 12: day 143's exit routine adds a second source against the claim, so the draft due on day 145 already answers it.
- Candidate, in the frame's order of keeping: the 2008 crisis, kept, 1 day (day 138); Native self-government today, kept, 1 day (day 139, Since Time Immemorial unit 6); the Gulf War, folded into the September 11 day (day 140), 0 added days; the survey of elections, the Affordable Care Act and COVID-19, kept, 1 day (day 137). In a short senior year the cut order is the survey (day 137), then Native self-government (day 139), then 2008 (day 138), as the frame sets.

## Unit 12. Capstone: a researched argument (days 148 to 160, weeks 30 to 32)

**Topics taught:** the student's own question from the 1920s to today; the source set audited and corroborated; the strongest counterclaim; drafting, peer critique for credibility, critique of a historical explanation, revision; two forms for two audiences, one beyond the classroom; the year's informed action.

Arc: question and sources, corroboration, draft, two critiques, revision, two forms, rehearsal, presentation, then the action planned and taken on day 157 and the final argument with the action record handed in on day 158, so the look back on day 160 has three days for a response. Days fall against ELA 12's milestones where the two are paired: talks inside days 150 to 156 (day 156 here) and the paper on day 158.

Paired with ELA 12: the draft is ELA's, due day 145 with the counterclaim sources added in units 10 and 11. Day 149 corroborates inside that draft and day 150 checks its historical evidence, corroboration and counterclaim and marks the revision (no second draft). Day 154 adapts the talk ELA taught and rehearsed by day 147 to history's two audiences; days 155 and 156 rehearse and give it, one talk serving both courses. Alone: day 150 drafts, and day 154 teaches the talk.

- Readiness: none. Day 148 opens with a retrieval task on the research cycle and argument (grade 11).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 159: critique an unseen historical explanation, with the capstone scored on the course rubric against D4.3 to D4.5: the two forms and audiences (days 154 to 156), the peer critique record (day 151) and the explanation critique record (day 152). Respond day 160, with the look back: the response or its absence so far, and what the student would do next.
- Candidate: none in the frame.
- Short senior year, alone or paired: the unit runs in 10 days by folding the rehearsal (day 155) into the forms day, the two critiques (days 151 and 152) into one day kept as two separate tasks, and the hand-in (day 158) into the presentation day. Kept in every case: the two forms for two contrasting audiences, both critiques, the action, the assessment and the respond day. Where paired, ELA 12's own cuts (its revision days) do not remove any of these.
