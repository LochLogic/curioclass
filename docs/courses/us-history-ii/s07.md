## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How did ordinary people use and challenge the law to change it?"

**Hook, day 86.** Compare Parks's earlier organizing with Brown's holding. Why did a ruling need organized action?
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: Who organized (86 to 88)? Why did rulings meet resistance (89 and 90)? How did direct action work (91 and 92)? What changed (93 to 96)? Why did Northern schools require remedies (97 to 99)?
**Closing claim, day 100.** Explain legal change and enforcement using Brown, the acts, Baker, the international petition and Boston evidence. Answer a counterclaim.
**Teaching.** Model sourcing on 86, corroboration on 90, causation on 91, data comparison on 94 and a counterclaim on 98. Research follows on 87 and 97; independent argument follows on 100.


**D2.Civ.12.9-12.** Analyze people's use and challenge of laws for different public issues. Parts: analyze local, state, national and international law. Sections 7.1 to 7.6 respectively own origins, T4.5a to c, T4.5d and laws, demographics and T4.9, T4.7, and synthesis. They develop D2.Civ.12 through international appeals, local protest, national law, legal limits, state remedies and argument, respectively. Practice D2.His.5 on 90 and RH.11-12.7 on 94.
**Content parts.** T4.4 analyzes origins, development and goals through research. T4.5 researches and analyzes resistance, protests and decisions through primary sources. T4.6 evaluates achievements, later influence and demographics. T4.9 researches housing, affirmative action, four disparity fields and Obama's election. T4.7 researches leaders and school controversies through primary sources.
**Limits.** Unit 9 owns later movements; 93 previews borrowing and independent roots. Unit 12 owns informed action. Date later law. Mark chronological returns on 88 and 97.
**Carried.** Keep source records, perspective, causal chains and evidence reasoning. Introduce continuity and change on 94; revise it with disparities on 96 and remedies on 99. Withdraw writing frames on 100; retain evidence.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach segregation, integration, civil disobedience, disparity and demographic. Expand the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, Student Nonviolent Coordinating Committee, Congress of Racial Equality and Metropolitan Council for Educational Opportunity before initials. Offer home-language rehearsal, described maps and oral responses. Extension tests alternative causes.

Name racist violence plainly; omit slurs and graphic images. No segregation simulations, personal disclosures or compelled religious participation. Teach organizing alongside harms.
