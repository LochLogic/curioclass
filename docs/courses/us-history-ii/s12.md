## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What question from the past century still needs an answer, and what do the sources say?"
**Hook, day 148.** Read the Library of Congress description of Marian Anderson's 1939 concert beside this proposed claim: "One concert ended segregation." What could the records establish?
**Supporting questions.** Co-write: Which sources deserve confidence (148 and 149)? How do argument and explanation differ (151 and 152)? What must change for another audience (154 to 156)? What action follows, if any (157 and 160)?
**Closing claim, day 158.** Each student answers the chosen question using the named sources in their approved register and answers counterevidence. The teaching example weighs Anderson's concert as a challenge to exclusion, using White's letter record, the concert description and the museum biography, without claiming segregation ended.
**Teaching.** Model source independence on 149, argument critique on 151, causal explanation on 152 and audience adaptation on 154. Withdraw the model during each independent task. Keep unit 11's perspective, causal chains, continuity and change, and evidence reasoning. Source every record before use. Corroboration returns in revision on 153 and independent presentation on 156; withdraw its organizer on 158.

**C151 model, not assessed.** Critique: "The concert attracted an integrated audience, so exclusion had ended everywhere." The dated concert description supports the first clause but cannot support the universal conclusion. Students then critique their peer's distinct argument, not this example. Return the critique on 153 for revision.

**E152, original teaching explanation.** "Anderson's concert became a public challenge to racial exclusion because her artistry and organized support made an alternative venue possible. Roosevelt alone caused its success. Anderson's April concert caused Eleanor Roosevelt's earlier February resignation from the Daughters of the American Revolution, and the large audience proves discrimination disappeared."

Prompt: Using the museum biography, the library account and the concert description, critique its reasoning, sequence and details, then repair it. Answer: preserve the explanation's link between exclusion, organizing and an alternative venue; reject sole causation and the reversed sequence: Roosevelt resigned on February 26, 1939, and Anderson sang on April 9, 1939. Audience evidence establishes public reach, not the disappearance of discrimination. The teacher models the procedure first with a different original explanation about Anderson's earlier musical training, using the museum biography.

**Reserved U159 packet, never used on days 148 to 158, including students' research, models, critiques and exits.** Use [Executive Order 9981, July 26, 1948](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/executive-order-9981), sections 1 to 3, and [Freedom to Serve, May 22, 1950](https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/freedom-to-serve), chapter I's Marine Corps implementation paragraph, printed page 6. Read both online. These federal authored records are public domain. Limit the packet to 400 words, including dates, provenance and glossary. Excerpt the order without losing its implementation qualification. The report passage distinguishes integrated training from continuing separate assignments. Explain outdated terminology in a note; use clearly labeled paraphrases where needed, never silently modernize a quotation. Reserve both whole documents for this unit; familiar background from earlier units is allowed.

**U159, deliberately flawed explanation written for this assessment.** "Federal leadership mattered: Truman announced equal treatment and established a committee. The committee's May 1950 report caused his July 1948 order. Once he signed, every service integrated immediately because presidential intent alone changed daily practice. The report proves that every Black service member experienced equality."

Apply the E152 prompt to this new explanation and the two reserved records. Students need no facts outside the packet. The first sentence has documentary support. The remaining claims require a corrected sequence and a distinction between policy and implementation. The report cannot establish every person's experience.

**Scoring.** For each look-for, record 0 for absent or incorrect, 1 for partial, or 2 for accurate, specific evidence and reasoning. Keep D4.3, D4.4 and D4.5 separate. Record all three explanation dimensions separately on both E152 and U159. A strong portfolio cannot erase a missing unseen critique. Agreement with the teacher is not a criterion.
