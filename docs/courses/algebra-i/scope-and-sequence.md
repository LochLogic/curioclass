# Scope and sequence: Algebra I

Course `algebra-i`, grade 9, draft, 2026-11-15.

The seven units keep the frame's order, ownership and 151 days. Three two-day course-level checks, each followed by one respond day, bring the year to 160 course days. The course meets every school day, so course day and school day have the same number; `school_day` stays empty in `days.csv`. The CSV holds the days. Section numbers ending in `.1` are placeholders until unit planning divides the work into sections.

The load is deliberately uneven. Unit 2 has six rows and 26 base lessons for the linear reasoning used throughout the year. Unit 7 has six rows and 24 base lessons for several genuinely different quadratic methods. Unit 3 has three rows and ten base lessons. Unit 1 has 16 base lessons in 14 meetings: the three spreadsheet introductions share day 6. Each unit also has one modeling day, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day. The two mid-unit assessments occupy part of existing days.

## Unit 1. One-variable statistics

Days 1 to 17, weeks 1 to 4, 17 days. A question about variation comes before choosing a display. Students rebuild dot plots, histograms and box plots, describe shape, and only then decide which summaries are useful. Day 6 compresses spreadsheet mechanics using one table rather than three unrelated introductions. Days 7 to 14 move from changing data to standard deviation, unusual values and comparisons. Day 10 retrieves a perfect-square root before standard deviation begins with a small squared-distance calculation; technology handles larger sets. Data are anonymous or supplied, with no student body measurements, health, income or family details.

Day 1 holds the ten-minute readiness window described above. Day 15 is the extended modeling performance: an individual annotated display and a comparison supported by shape, center and spread. Its first eight minutes introduce model critique using an original sample. Day 14 retains each student's independent dot plot, histogram and box plot from one small supplied data set. Day 16 scores that record once alongside day 15's comparison for S-ID.A.1 to S-ID.A.3. Short items ask about display choice, shape-dependent summaries, outliers and contextual meaning; no second three-display construction is collected. Day 17 responds once, with its final ten minutes reserved for unit 2 readiness. No mid-unit assessment is required.

Candidate settled: keep the Statistics and Probability progression comparison as a planning check, zero added student days. The three displays and the move from informal variability to standard deviation are its concrete consequence. Drop the separate standard-deviation extension and commute-time comparison: days 10 to 14 already provide the needed comparison and outlier work, and another full task would displace response time. No extra standard is added.

## Unit 2. Linear equations, inequalities and systems

Days 18 to 46, weeks 4 to 10, 29 days. Quantities, units and constraints precede transformations of equations. Days 21 to 28 establish equivalence, formula rearrangement and a graph as all solution pairs. Days 29 to 34 develop systems from intersections through substitution and elimination, including why replacing an equation preserves the solution set in both directions. Days 35 to 43 move from one-variable inequalities to half-planes, boundary conventions and simultaneous constraints. This order lets the final modeling task use both systems and feasible regions.

Readiness is on day 17 for the grade 7 inequality row; day 18 retrieves grade 8 equations. Before the once-squared formula, day 26 retrieves both equation roots and distinguishes the principal square root from contextually allowed solutions. Response occupies the five-minute openings on days 35 and 36, not another full day. The public base assessment listing confirms that this unit has a mid-unit assessment, but its items and detailed placement are gated; no gated content is used.

## Evidence, practice and source decisions

Distribution retrieval is inside the core comparison time. After linear solving is taught here, openings retrieve the current-year algebra rather than declaring a new use of an older-grade row. Mid-unit response replaces, rather than adds to, the next two openings.
