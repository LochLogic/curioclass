# Scope and sequence: Algebra I

Course `algebra-i`, grade 9, draft, 2026-11-15.

The seven units keep the frame's order, ownership and 151 days. Three two-day course-level checks, each followed by one respond day, bring the year to 160 course days. The course meets every school day, so course day and school day have the same number; `school_day` stays empty in `days.csv`. The CSV holds the days. Section numbers ending in `.1` are placeholders until unit planning divides the work into sections.

The load is deliberately uneven. Unit 2 has six rows and 26 base lessons for the linear reasoning used throughout the year. Unit 7 has six rows and 24 base lessons for several genuinely different quadratic methods. Unit 3 has three rows and ten base lessons. Unit 1 has 16 base lessons in 14 meetings: the three spreadsheet introductions share day 6. Each unit also has one modeling day, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day. The two mid-unit assessments occupy part of existing days.

## Unit 1. One-variable statistics

Days 1 to 17, weeks 1 to 4, 17 days. A question about variation comes before choosing a display. Students rebuild dot plots, histograms and box plots, describe shape, and only then decide which summaries are useful. Day 6 compresses spreadsheet mechanics using one table rather than three unrelated introductions. Days 7 to 14 move from changing data to standard deviation, unusual values and comparisons. Standard deviation begins with a small squared-distance calculation; technology handles larger sets. Data are anonymous or supplied, with no student body measurements, health, income or family details.

Day 1 holds the ten-minute readiness window described above. Day 15 is the extended modeling performance: an individual annotated display and a comparison supported by shape, center and spread. Its first eight minutes introduce model critique using an original sample. Day 16 checks S-ID.A.1 to S-ID.A.3, including display choice, the shape-dependent choice of summaries, outliers and interpretation in context; the day 15 product carries the full performance. Day 17 responds once, with its final ten minutes reserved for unit 2 readiness. No mid-unit assessment is required.

Candidate settled: keep the Statistics and Probability progression comparison as a planning check, zero added student days. The three displays and the move from informal variability to standard deviation are its concrete consequence. Drop the separate standard-deviation extension and commute-time comparison: days 10 to 14 already provide the needed comparison and outlier work, and another full task would displace response time. No extra standard is added.

## Unit 2. Linear equations, inequalities and systems

Days 18 to 46, weeks 4 to 10, 29 days. Quantities, units and constraints precede transformations of equations. Days 21 to 28 establish equivalence, formula rearrangement and a graph as all solution pairs. Days 29 to 34 develop systems from intersections through substitution and elimination, including why replacing an equation preserves the solution set in both directions. Days 35 to 43 move from one-variable inequalities to half-planes, boundary conventions and simultaneous constraints. This order lets the final modeling task use both systems and feasible regions.

Readiness is on day 17 for the grade 7 inequality row; day 18 retrieves grade 8 equations. Day 34 has a 20-minute original mid-unit assessment on equations, formulas, solution graphs and systems. Its lesson core still distinguishes systems with zero, one or infinitely many solutions. Response occupies the five-minute openings on days 35 and 36, not another full day. The public base assessment listing confirms that this unit has a mid-unit assessment, but its items and detailed placement are gated; no gated content is used.

Day 44 supplies the extended heating-plan performance. Day 45 checks A-REI.B.3, A-CED.A.4, A-REI.D.10, A-REI.C.5, A-REI.C.6, A-REI.D.12 and A-CED.A.3. It includes rational and letter coefficients, equations and inequalities, a squared-quantity formula, proof of elimination, strict and non-strict boundaries and viable contextual values. The planning product prevents a second extended performance from crowding the written assessment. Day 46 is the single full end-assessment response allocation. All financial quantities are given, with no request for family information.

Candidate settled: keep the Algebra progression comparison as a planning check, zero added days, especially solving as reversible reasoning. Drop the extra elimination extension and corner-based best-plan task: core proof is already on days 31 to 33, and optimization of a feasible region would exceed this unit's intended boundary.

## Unit 3. Two-variable statistics

Days 47 to 59, weeks 10 to 12, 13 days. Frequency tables come first, including the choice of denominator for joint, marginal and row or column conditional frequencies. Linear fitting then uses unit 2 equations. Residuals precede numerical correlation so students have a reason to question a fit even when a single summary looks impressive. Causal claims come last, after students can describe association precisely. Day 50 retrieves grade 8 scatter plots and fitted lines; day 47 retrieves two-way tables. There is no older-grade readiness requirement or mid-unit assessment.

Days 53 to 55 distribute individual coefficient computation over three days for a class of 30, with ten students per day using a shared device while others complete independent representation work. Each computation record and its separate interpretation are retained. Day 55 also contains the eight-minute argument bridge because ELA's argument evaluation is later. Day 57 is the extended supplied-institution-data performance. Day 58 checks frequencies, linear fitting, slope and intercept units, residuals, correlation computation and interpretation, and causal limitations. It reuses the individual device record rather than collecting it again. Day 59 responds once. Published data need a source line; no health or body data about students, personal admissions plans or family finances are collected.

Candidate settled: keep the bivariate Statistics and Probability progression comparison as a planning check, zero added days. Its consequences are explicit conditional denominators, residual evidence and separate computation of r. Drop the second closing data set and separate news-graph extension: the closing model and causal-claim day already carry those purposes, and another performance would crowd the 13-day unit.

## Unit 4. Functions

Days 63 to 83, weeks 13 to 17, 21 days. Day 63 retrieves the grade 8 definition before adding notation. Notation and rules precede interval rates; rates precede comparisons, allowable inputs and piecewise rules. Absolute value grows from distance, not an unexplained graph template. Linear inverses then reuse equation rearrangement. Days 79 to 80 connect undoing a rule with the meaning of the quantities and with a model's limits.

There is no readiness check for this unit: its assumed rows are grade 8 or current-year work. Day 81 is the bonus-rule modeling performance. Day 82 checks function uniqueness and graph pairs, notation in context, average rate from equations, tables and estimated graphs, contextual domains and linear inverses. It also uses the piecewise and absolute-value products from days 74 to 76 for those started expectations. Day 83 responds once and gives unit 5 readiness in its final ten minutes. There is no mid-unit assessment. Given rates and pay amounts replace any personal financial questions.

Candidate settled: keep the Functions progression comparison as a planning check, zero added days, with input-output meaning preserved across representations. Drop separate step-function extensions, the nonlinear-rule preview and the extra vacation-planning prompt. Core piecewise work and linear inverses already need their allotted days; nonlinear inverse teaching remains with the later course as the frame states.

## Unit 5. Introduction to exponential functions

Days 84 to 107, weeks 17 to 22, 24 days. Equal differences and equal factors lead into repeated multiplication, decay and integer-input exponential functions. Zero and negative inputs are explained by extending a table backward. Graphs, interval rates and parameters then support repeated percent change and interest. Days 102 to 104 return to growth comparisons and prove the equal-interval properties before choosing a population model. The delayed proof now has examples to explain, not just rules to memorize.

Readiness for grade 7 percent problems is on day 83; day 84 retrieves grade 8 function comparison and exponents. Day 97 rebuilds the tape diagram without administering another readiness check. Day 105 holds the data-only debt modeling performance, using version 3, no launch video or policy question, and national income alongside debt. Day 106 checks F-LE.A.1 and each of 1a, 1b and 1c through equal-interval arguments and situation choices. Exponential and interest products supply evidence of the started codes. F-LE.A.3 is still partial at this point: its linear comparison is retained, and its quadratic and higher-power parts are taught on day 114 and assessed on day 129. Ownership stays in unit 5. Day 107 responds once. There is no mid-unit assessment.

Candidate settled: keep the Functions progression comparison as a planning check, zero added days, distinguishing a factor from an additive rate. Drop separate doubling-time, half-life and savings-plan extensions: decay and repeated interest already occur in the core, and the frame's chosen debt model occupies the one modeling day. No family savings, borrowing or income is requested.

## Unit 6. Introduction to quadratic functions

Days 111 to 130, weeks 23 to 26, 20 days. Current-year functions and grade 8 comparison are retrieved. Visual patterns and second differences precede falling-object and area models. Day 114 completes the F-LE.A.3 comparison against quadratics and simple higher powers within its existing 50 minutes. Area diagrams then connect products to standard and factored forms; graphs show why different forms reveal different features. Mixed-representation comparisons begin on day 124. Grade 8 coordinate transformations are retrieved on day 125 before vertex form is used to move a graph.

No readiness check or mid-unit assessment is needed. Day 128 is an individual drawn fountain design; no physical fountain or live throw is required. Day 129 checks F-IF.C.7a, including hand graphs of both lines and quadratics, intercepts and extrema, and uses the retained day 114 tables and graphs to assess the completed F-LE.A.3 expectation. The design carries the extended graphing performance. Day 130 responds once. Coefficients for falling-object models are supplied, and the objects are never people.

Candidate settled: keep the Functions and Algebra progression comparison as a planning check, zero added days, tying area expansion to equivalent forms and their features. Drop a second-fountain extension and live projectile data collection. A second design would duplicate the performance, and measurements are unnecessary for the owned graphing expectation.

## Unit 7. Quadratic equations

Days 131 to 157, weeks 27 to 32, 27 days. Grade 8 roots and irrational numbers open as retrieval, not readiness. Equations grow from situations and estimated graph intersections to square-root reasoning, zero product and factoring. Perfect-square structure leads to completing the square. Only then do exact radicals and the formula become useful, followed by its derivation, number-system arguments and vertex rewrites. Day 153 completes mixed-representation comparisons across linear, exponential, quadratic and absolute value functions. Day 154 connects linear and quadratic equations in a system.

Day 144 has a 20-minute original mid-unit assessment on real roots, factoring and square-completion work so far. It does not yet assess the later general formula derivation. The next two five-minute openings, days 145 and 146, are its only response allocation. Day 155 is the extended ticket-price modeling performance. Day 156 checks method choice, completing the square and deriving the formula, rational and irrational sum and product arguments, and the interpretation of zeros, symmetry and extrema. Retained individual rewrites on day 152, comparisons on day 153 and the day 155 model spread the performances across the closing days. No child-by-child interview is required. Day 157 responds once, before the course-level check. All price and attendance values are supplied.

Candidate settled: keep the Algebra and Number progression comparison as a planning check, zero added days, especially both square-root signs and the nonzero condition in the irrational-product argument. Drop separate sums-and-products-of-roots and extended concert-optimization tasks. The first exceeds the intended core load; the second repeats the kept model's maximum-revenue reasoning.

## Evidence, practice and source decisions

Every teaching day has a five-minute spaced opening. Days 2 to 17 mix grade 8 linear equations with the rebuilt data displays. After unit 2, linear equations, inequalities and formula rearrangement stay in the rotation twice each week. After unit 4, notation, contextual domains and interval rates join it; after unit 5, integer exponents and factor-versus-difference decisions recur; after unit 6, hand sketches and expression forms recur. Use two earlier strands per opening and revisit a corrected error after two days and after a week. No student countdown is displayed. Mid-unit response replaces, rather than adds to, the next two openings.

The practices have named work, not just unit labels: MP1 on choosing a quadratic method on day 148; MP2 on interpreting units in notation on day 65; MP3 on the reversible elimination proof on day 33 and number arguments on day 151; MP4 on each modeling day; MP5 on choosing displays on day 3 and comparing a hand fit with regression on day 51; MP6 on frequency denominators on day 48 and endpoints on day 73; MP7 on area expansion on day 118 and zero product on day 134; MP8 on equal-interval arguments on day 103. Assessment products and short written explanations carry the corresponding reasoning.

`source-map.csv` inventories all 132 main-course base lessons, nine public modeling prompts and 128 optional supports lessons. The main lessons remain in order, with lessons 1.6 to 1.8 merged onto day 6. The seven modeling tasks are placed after the corresponding unit's last base lesson, where their methods are available; the introductory critique shares unit 1's modeling day. The extra vacation prompt is cut with its reason. Original end assessments, response days and course-level checks have `ours` rows. Mid-unit assessments are embedded in their mapped lesson days, not added meetings. Optional supports rows have `track=optional` and a blank day, with the matching core day named in their note; they do not silently add 128 days to this year.

Only public first-edition pages in the licensed local base-text store were used. Adapted days will receive the credit required by `docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md`. Gated exits, assessment items, answer keys and sample responses are not sources. Goals, notes and source decisions are written in Curio Class words. Unit plans will write items, rubrics and answer keys.

The tool names in the CSV are consistent generic names for a spreadsheet, graphing tool, statistics calculator, scientific calculator and basic calculator, plus coordinate-plane, data-display, area-model and tape-diagram drawings. No course tools file exists yet; the unit plans must bind those names to the eventual tools file. Digital graphing and table work has a grid-and-data paper path with a basic calculator. Correlation coefficient computation still requires individual access to a shared or individual device with no student account; paper interpretation alone cannot satisfy S-ID.C.8. Supplies name no-kit or household substitutes. Supplied bounce records replace a live measurement setup. Math safety cells remain empty as required.

Started standards retain their frame boundaries: nonlinear fitting continues in Algebra II; quantity and precision expectations finish in Geometry; root graphs finish in Geometry; general expression structure, function construction and transformations continue in Algebra II. ## Shorter path

For a 40-minute period, use a five-minute spaced opening, twenty-minute core, ten-minute independent application and five-minute exit. Reduce repeated examples, copying and full-class reports; retain every new representation and owned part. Give supplied tables and pre-drawn axes where drawing them is not the goal. Modeling days use five minutes to launch, five to plan, twenty for individual work, five for partner critique and five to revise. A mid-unit day uses five minutes retrieval, fifteen core teaching and twenty assessment; the assessment serves as that day's exit. End assessment days use three minutes directions, thirty-two individual work and five evidence review, with the extended performance already retained. Response days use five minutes triage, fifteen targeted examples, fifteen independent retry and five record; days 17 and 83 instead use five triage, ten examples, fifteen retry and ten readiness. For individual device work, use days 53 to 56 with cohorts of seven, seven, eight and eight, at three minutes per student. Days 53 and 54 have five minutes opening, nine instruction, twenty-one rotations with independent work and five exit. Day 55 has five opening, eight argument bridge, twenty-four rotations and three exit. Day 56 has five opening, six task preparation, twenty-four rotations and five exit. These are planning budgets to test in unit writing, not a claim that an unmodified base lesson fits a shorter period.
