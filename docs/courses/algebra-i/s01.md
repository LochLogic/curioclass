## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What is typical of our class, and how much do we differ?"
Day 1 opens with an eight-minute question sort: does "How many counters are in this sealed packet?" anticipate variation? Does "How many counters do packets contain?" Students anonymously report counts from supplied packet cards labeled "invented for practice". Ask the compelling question about the resulting class collection, not personal characteristics.

Ask what each display reveals on day 3, which summaries suit a shape on day 5, what changes spread on day 10, and whether an unusual observation should be excluded on day 12.

By day 16, students can defend a bounded comparison using shape, appropriate center and spread, and three accurate displays, evidenced by the day 14 construction, day 15 model and fresh short items.

Model scales and quartiles on day 2 and squared distances on day 10. Contrast examples before independent work. Step back during days 3, 13 and 15; ask which evidence would change a claim.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask "What does this picture conceal?" on day 3 and "Does a flagged value prove a mistake?" on day 12. Day 13's comparison must connect shape, a justified center and spread, and context.

Day 15's eight-minute original critique uses tile counts 38, 39, 47, invented for practice. A sample dot plot places them on equally spaced ticks. Students repair the numerical spacing and explain why equal tick intervals must represent equal differences before beginning their independent model.

## U8. Practice that returns

Use field 4's retrieval rotation. Add distribution retrieval inside comparison work. Days 5, 6 and 11 end with independent evidence, not a group fluency score.

## U9. Supports and extension

Section 1.1's language goal grows from describing variation to justifying a comparison. Teach cluster, tail, median, quartile and spread through labeled examples. Offer "Compared with __, __ has __ because __." Allow home-language rehearsal, partner listening, oral description and written revision. Fade frames by day 15; record vocabulary and reasoning separately. Read short prompts aloud; enlarge grids, describe plots verbally and allow a scribe without choosing summaries. Extension: construct two unequal distributions with identical five-number summaries and explain the limitation.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, supplied records replace class collection; the parent becomes the discussion partner, then observes independent work. With one projector, demonstrate cell calculations while students calculate and plot on paper. Keep day 17's independent retry and readiness record.
