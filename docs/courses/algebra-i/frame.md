# Course frame: Algebra I

Course id `algebra-1`, grade 9 (taken in grade 8 on an accelerated path: see For the lead), draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7 units, in the base text's own order (IM Algebra 1, units 1 to 7).
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks at five lessons a week, one lesson of 45 to 50 minutes a day (the base text's lessons are written for a class period of at least 45 minutes). Each unit's days are its base lessons kept, plus one readiness day (the readiness checks on earlier grades' skills and the reactivated models), one modeling-prompt day and one end-of-unit assessment day. Days follow load, not equal shares: unit 2 (six rows, the algebra the year runs on) and unit 7 (six rows, four solving methods) are the longest; unit 3 (three statistics rows) is the shortest.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, high school (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt), is the whole standard: every code a row owns sits in one unit below, and each unit names every lettered part it teaches. A code marked with a tilde in a row is started here and owned by a later row (named in the unit). There is no check set in math, so the "Check set adds" column names candidate extensions only (base-text extension tasks, never a (+) standard taught as core). The 53 (+) codes are deferred to the electives (data/progressions/math-excluded.csv).
- **Focus, coherence and rigor.** docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md covers kindergarten to grade 8 only and no high school bar is filed (For the lead), so no K-8 percentage line is borrowed. (1) Focus is counted on teaching days, not rows: each base lesson by the standards the base text aligns to it (its "Standards by Lesson" table), against the widely applicable prerequisites the progression tags (N-RN, N-Q, A-SSE, A-APR, A-CED, A-REI, F-IF, G-SRT.B, G-SRT.C). That tagged list is the progression's own and has not yet been checked against the high school publishers' criteria table. Algebra I: 102 of 132 base lessons address at least one listed code (77 percent); weighted by the share of each lesson's codes that are listed, 65 percent; with F-BF and F-LE counted too, 107 (81 percent, weighted 80). By unit: 0 of 16, 26 of 26, 1 of 10, 17 of 18, 18 of 21, 16 of 17, 24 of 24. Across Algebra I, Geometry and Algebra II: 227 of 376 base lessons (60 percent, weighted 45). An independent read reports that the publishers' table also names F-LE.A.1, F-BF.A.1, S-ID.A.2, S-ID.C.7 and S-IC.A.1 (and three G-CO codes) and applies focus per course; on that list Algebra I is 121 of 132 (92 percent, weighted 76) and the series 276 of 376 (73 percent, weighted 54). Which list and which reading apply is settled with the high school bar page. Readiness, modeling-prompt and assessment days are not counted; the scope and sequence counts each Curio Class day by the standards its plan names. The earlier row-share estimate (72 percent) is withdrawn. (2) Coherence: the statistics units practise the major work (unit 3 fits lines with unit 2's equations; unit 1 sets the data routines of unit 3), and every seam below is written at both ends. (3) Rigor: each unit names its concept work, its procedures and its applications (the modeling-prompt day).
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Algebra 1 (CC BY 4.0; S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\hs-algebra-1): 7 units, 132 lessons, placed in the Source map at the end. Credit line on adapted days, in the form docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md sets: "Adapted from Illustrative Mathematics K-12 Math, Algebra 1, unit <n>, lesson <n>, CC BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Changed by Curio Class." (supports lessons and modeling prompts name their own source the same way). The CC BY-NC edition is not used, and no login-gated material is: cool-downs, answer keys, unit assessments and modeling-prompt sample responses are gated, so Curio Class writes its own exit tickets, unit assessments, answer keys and modeling rubrics. Base lessons are adapted, not copied whole.
- **Algebra 1 supports.** The base text's supports course (hs-algebra-1-supports, 128 lessons, each a warm-up and two activities numbered to match the Algebra 1 lesson it comes before) is the source for two things: the brief reminders a student gets after missing a readiness check, and an optional extra-time track for a double block or a two-year Algebra I. It is never counted in the 160 days and never replaces the grade-level lesson.
- **Modeling.** Each unit has one modeling-prompt day, using the base text's public modeling prompts (the task pages are free; the sample responses are gated, so the rubric is ours). Every statistics standard and the starred standards are modeling standards; the prompt day is where they are done whole.
- **Technology and the paper path.** Spreadsheets, graphing and regression appear in units 1, 3, 4 and 5. Each has a paper path: printed data tables, grid paper and a basic calculator; lines are fitted by eye and checked against a teacher-displayed fit. The paper path keeps graphing, fitting and interpreting; for S-ID.C.8, which asks for the correlation coefficient computed with technology, each student also computes it from paired data on a shared or individual device (a classroom computer or a statistics calculator, no student account), and unit 3 checks that computation apart from the interpretation. Any electronic tool is a free one that needs no student account. No tool is named in student pages.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. No unit needs a community or qualified reader. Class surveys collect nothing that can single a student out (no body measurements, health, income or family details), and money contexts never ask about a student's own family.
