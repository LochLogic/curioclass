## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why do their ages differ, and how do the oldest records tell us how old the Earth is and what it was like when it formed?"
Anchor: day 146 opens with field 10's sample cards, showing dated materials beside meteorite fall years. Students order the cards and ask why one body contains different ages.
Day 152's account explains common formation, later activity and missing records using ages, compositions and crater evidence. Day 153 challenges its reasoning.
Model one evidence-to-claim link on day 148; thereafter students choose evidence and challenge unsupported inferences.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


The question board follows the puzzle: "Where did Earth's records go?" (148) raises "What event does an age date?" (149), then "Could these bodies share an origin?" (150), "Can surfaces preserve a different history?" (151), and "Which account explains all records?" (152 to 153). Revisit unresolved questions daily. Assessment transfers to an unfamiliar sample and Martian resurfacing dossier.

Limits: qualitative reasoning from supplied ages, repeated halving and simple rates, without logarithms, isotope laboratory methods or radioactive sources. Rock age need not equal body age. Meteorites and lunar surfaces can also change. Crater diameter alone cannot date a crater; counts require comparable areas, size ranges and impact histories. No engineering expectation is claimed.

Carried: retain unit 9's plate, rock-cycle, matter-cycle and scale models, Chemistry's half-life work and unit 3's orbit model. Add an evidence-linked timeline on day 146. Revise missing-record mechanisms with erosion and recycling (148), dated events with mineral evidence (149), common-origin mechanisms with size and composition (150), and surface histories with craters (151). Day 152 joins accretion, heating, differentiation, cooling and later change; day 153 revises confidence after counterarguments. Use these mechanisms on the new assessment case; models remain revisable.
