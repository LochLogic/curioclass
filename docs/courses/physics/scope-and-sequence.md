# Scope and sequence: Physics

Course `physics`, grade 11, draft.

The frame's ten units keep their order, ownership and 149 days. Seven course-level check days and four course-level respond days bring the year to 160 days, five 50-minute meetings a week. Course day and school day are identical, so `school_day` stays blank. The day purposes, skill placements, standard codes, assessment slots and materials are in [days.csv](../../../data/plans/physics/days.csv). The section ranges below group those days for later planning, without writing section plans or assessment items.

Every hook opens a question board and an initial model. Evidence revises that model at the points named below. Daily notebook evidence informs teaching, and each section closes with a brief exit ticket inside its last day. A readiness check occupies ten minutes of the preceding unit's respond day, except unit 1's check after its first hook. The next unit's hook is adjusted from those results. Each affected unit reserves one full reteach lesson on its second day, with an extension for students already ready. Units 2 and 5 use retrieval instead. No unit exceeds 25 days, so no separate mid-unit assessment is required or reserved.

Every end-of-unit assessment uses a new case and covers every part of every code it lists, including the practice, all core idea elements and the crosscutting elements. A separate short demand for each part can share one case. At least 80 percent of its points require a practice used with an idea on the new case. Named products and performance records supplement transfer evidence; they do not replace the unseen case. Each unit's final day is its response time, counted once, including a fresh attempt. Course-level response is separate.

## Unit 1. Push, pull, bounce

18 days, course days 1 to 18, weeks 1 to 4. Sections: 1.1, days 1 to 4, motion representations; 1.2, days 5 to 9, force evidence; 1.3, days 10 to 14, momentum and boundaries; 1.4, days 15 to 18, synthesis, assessment and response.

Unequal skater speeds raise the question before an equation is supplied. Position and velocity records make acceleration meaningful before controlled cart comparisons establish the force law. Falling-object data test its reach. The model changes on day 8, then signed momentum, reference frames and external interactions explain why unequal speeds can coexist with equal interaction forces. Day 14 revises the skater model using both laws. Nine days through day 9 serve the quantitative force strand, five develop momentum explicitly, and four consolidate and assess both, rather than dividing the two rows equally.

Readiness: the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook, on `s8-forces-motion-mass`, `s8-collision-forces` and force-diagram, last used in grade 8. Day 2 is the reserved reteach. Algebra I slopes, equation writing and rearranging are retrieved in days 4, 9 and 12. End-of-unit assessment: day 17, analysing new motion records and representing a new two-object collision mathematically and verbally, including boundaries and outside momentum exchange. Respond: day 18. Unit 2 needs retrieval of this work, not another readiness check.

Candidate decisions: keep reliable data analysis with tools and models, embedded in days 8 and 15, two existing days and no added days. Drop the additional designed-system element here because unit 2 develops and assesses it through an actual design cycle; unit 1 retains its required cause-and-effect and systems elements.

## Unit 2. Softer landings

12 days, course days 19 to 30, weeks 4 to 6. Sections: 2.1, days 19 to 22, defining and planning; 2.2, days 23 to 27, making, evaluating and refining; 2.3, days 28 to 30, justification, assessment and response.

The crumpling test vehicle makes protection a problem students can improve. Criteria, constraints and sub-problems precede material choices. Collision-time evidence then motivates a controlled prototype test, an evaluation, one refinement and a retest. The initial protection model changes after the first test on day 25 and again with day 27's retest. The single owned expectation needs a complete cycle, which explains its twelve days. Neither the purpose nor success of the design is reduced to making an attractive object.

Retrieve unit 1 momentum, Chemistry device sub-problems and Biology criteria and trade-offs inside days 18 to 20. No readiness slot or mid-unit assessment. Day 29 combines the design record, including evaluation and refinement evidence from days 20 to 28, with an unfamiliar protection case requiring transfer. Day 30 responds to that evidence; its last ten minutes hold unit 3's readiness check on `s8-electromagnetic-forces`, `s8-fields`, `s8-gravity-mass`, `s8-gravity-solar-system` and field-model.

Candidate decisions: keep prioritised criteria and trade-offs on days 20 and 25, two existing days; keep technology shaped by needs and values on days 20 and 28, two existing days, sharing day 20. The combined cost is three existing days, no additions. Use test-crash records without injury narratives, personal disclosure or images of injured people. A non-personal protection case preserves the science for students needing it.

## Unit 3. Where will the spacecraft be?

16 days, course days 31 to 46, weeks 7 to 10. Sections: 3.1, days 31 to 35, quantitative forces; 3.2, days 36 to 40, fields and orbit laws; 3.3, days 41 to 46, computation, explanation, assessment and response.

Precise spacecraft arrival raises a prediction problem. Readiness evidence from day 30 shapes the opening; day 32 reteaches the idle field and gravity rows. The two force laws build from Chemistry's electric attraction and unit 1's force model before the orbit work uses gravity. Field evidence on day 36 includes currents, magnets and changing fields, which unit 5 later retrieves. Ellipses and equal-area sectors precede period calculations. The supplied step model follows the relationships students can explain, and day 43 revises the spacecraft model by distinguishing observed laws from explanations. Six days establish force and field concepts, six develop orbital representations, and four integrate, assess and respond.

Retrieve Chemistry's computational and Earth-Sun models and grade 9 Scientific Revolution context. All orbit calculations stay within two-body systems: Sun-centred `T squared = a cubed` in years and astronomical units; supplied `k` or a same-body reference orbit for moons and satellites. Square and cube roots use a calculator. No fractional exponents, vector components or calculus are prerequisites. Readiness reteach: day 32. End-of-unit assessment: day 45, both force laws in numerical and conceptual field representations and predictions for planets, moons and launched satellites, including all three orbit laws and prediction limits. Respond: day 46, whose last ten minutes check unit 4's `s8-kinetic-energy-graphs` and `s8-potential-energy`.

Candidate decisions: keep laws versus theories on day 43, one existing day; keep algebraic prediction on days 35 and 39, two existing days. These three days already serve the owned expectations. No extra days.

## Unit 4. The first hill

16 days, course days 47 to 62, weeks 10 to 13. Sections: 4.1, days 47 to 51, track evidence and particles; 4.2, days 52 to 56, stored and field energy; 4.3, days 57 to 62, joined models, assessment and response.

The cart's missing return height and a moving magnet require one energy account. Day 48 reteaches the idle kinetic and position rows after day 46's readiness check, while retrieving Chemistry energy accounting. Height and speed evidence leads to the first model revision on day 51: apparent loss is transfer, with thermal change explained through particle motion. Springs and charged plates extend arrangement energy. Two-magnet evidence then revises the field account on day 56, including both objects' motions. Sound, light and a supplied accounting table join the macroscopic and particle descriptions before transfer. The broader particle-energy expectation has days 49 to 54 and 58; the two-object field expectation has focused days 55 and 57, with shared synthesis thereafter.

End-of-unit assessment: day 61, a new everyday energy system and a new interacting electric or magnetic pair. Evidence includes particle motion and arrangement, energy carried by radiation, both force directions, both objects' changes and conservation across a stated boundary. The field's interaction energy is not arbitrarily assigned to a single object. Respond: day 62. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep energy conservation on day 60 and evidence-based relationship modelling on day 56, two existing days, no addition. These are also required elements, so settling the candidates does not make them optional.

## Unit 5. Spin a wheel, light a lamp

11 days, course days 66 to 76, weeks 14 to 16. Sections: 5.1, days 66 to 68, dynamo model and investigation plan; 5.2, days 69 to 72, conduct, improvement and causal evidence; 5.3, days 73 to 76, generator explanation, assessment and response.

The dynamo's changing brightness motivates the link between motion and current. Day 67 retrieves electromagnetic forces and field sources used in unit 3, including day 36, so no new readiness check is appropriate. Planning comes before conducting both directions: current making a field, then a changing field making current. Students choose controls, data quantity, precision, repetitions and practical limits, improve the plan and repeat selected trials. Causal evidence revises the dynamo model on day 73, distinguishing battery storage from energy carried by current. One expectation receives four investigation days because conducting and improving an investigation cannot be replaced by a reading.

Day 75 assesses the planning and conduct record from days 68 to 71 plus an unseen generator case, both causal directions, controls, repeated evidence and limitations. Day 76 responds; its last ten minutes check unit 6's `s8-wave-amplitude-energy` and `s6-digital-signals`. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep planning and conducting on day 68 and causal prediction on day 72, two existing days, no addition. These remain required practice and crosscutting work.

## Unit 6. Messages made of waves

16 days, course days 77 to 92, weeks 16 to 19. Sections: 6.1, days 77 to 81, wave relationships; 6.2, days 82 to 86, causal evidence, digital questions and detection; 6.3, days 87 to 92, devices, communication, assessment and response.

The voice carried by light raises questions about both waves and encoding. Day 78 reteaches from day 76's readiness evidence. Travel evidence and the algebraic relationship precede comparisons across light, sound and seismic media. Students distinguish a supported relationship from an accidental association. Pixel numbers and pulses make transmission and storage concrete before students evaluate and sharpen questions about benefits and risks. Absorption and photoelectric threshold evidence precede explanations of solar cells, medical imaging and fibre communication, each tracing interactions with matter. Day 90 revises the initial message model through that mechanism. Wave relationships have four focused days, digital representation and questions three, device mechanisms four, with the remaining five serving the hook, reteach, joined evidence, assessment and response.

Day 91 assesses all three expectations on a new medium, a digital-system question and a device not previously explained. It requires mathematical support, evaluation of the question's premise, both transmission and storage trade-offs, and visual, spoken and written technical communication. Respond: day 92; its last ten minutes check unit 7's `s6-light-paths`. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep designed stability on day 84 and challenging questions on day 85, two existing days, no added time.

## Unit 7. Light, two ways

14 days, course days 93 to 106, weeks 19 to 22. Sections: 7.1, days 93 to 97, evidence for light models; 7.2, days 98 to 102, model limits and published claims; 7.3, days 103 to 106, joined explanation, assessment and response.

Contrasting sunlight and radio effects begin the model question without requiring exposure experiments. Day 94 reteaches light paths from day 92's check and retrieves Biology's ultraviolet-DNA idea. Interference, diffraction and resonance evidence establish wave usefulness before the photoelectric threshold motivates photons. Day 98 revises the model by retaining each account where useful. Photon energy, absorption and total exposure then support judging published claims for evidence quality, bias and reliability. Day 103 joins mechanism and source judgment. Four focused days develop and compare models, four develop and judge radiation claims, and six open, reteach, integrate, consolidate, assess and respond.

Day 105 assesses arguments for both light models on new evidence and the validity and reliability of an unfamiliar published radiation claim. It distinguishes frequency per photon from intensity, duration and absorption, and heating from potential tissue damage. Qualitative only, no quantum theory calculations. Day 106 responds. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep incomplete but useful models on day 98 and published-claim evaluation on days 101 to 102, three existing days, no addition. Cancer, sun exposure and radiation worries require evidence-based, non-mocking discussion, no family-illness questions and an equivalent non-health detector case when needed.

## Unit 8. Where your atoms came from

18 days, course days 110 to 127, weeks 22 to 26. Sections: 8.1, days 110 to 114, spectra and the Sun; 8.2, days 115 to 119, timescales and stellar elements; 8.3, days 120 to 123, cosmic evidence; 8.4, days 124 to 127, explanation, assessment and response.

Contrasting laboratory and stellar spectra begin as observations, not an announced explanation. Day 111 reteaches scale from day 109's check and retrieves Chemistry atoms, fusion and Earth's energy budget; gravity and light are already available. Element signatures lead to solar energy transport, mass-lifetime evidence, flares and short and long solar cycles. Day 116 revises the Sun model before stellar mass and life stage explain element production. Simple nuclei diagrams distinguish atoms from conserved total nucleons. Redshift, background radiation and hydrogen-helium proportions then converge on an early-universe explanation. Day 123 revises the joined model and day 124 examines why independently checked, convergent evidence supports a theory. Solar lifespan and variability get four focused days, element formation four including spectral inference, cosmic evidence four, and six days open, support, integrate, assess and respond.

Day 126 assesses a new stellar system and cosmic evidence set: Sun model and energy path, mass and lifespan, all specified solar variations, element production and nuclear accounting, and all three Big Bang evidence lines. Day 127 responds; its last ten minutes check unit 9's `s6-surface-change-scales`. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep the supported-theory element on day 124 and scale comparison on days 111 and 123, three existing days. Required energy-and-matter reasoning remains: trace stellar radiation transfers without asserting a simple conserved total energy for an expanding universe. Teach the age of the universe from evidence without requiring statements about belief.

## Unit 9. The restless Earth

18 days, course days 128 to 145, weeks 26 to 29. Sections: 9.1, days 128 to 133, interior evidence and convection; 9.2, days 134 to 137, surface feedback and analogue limits; 9.3, days 138 to 141, crust ages and evidence; 9.4, days 142 to 145, joined models, assessment and response.

Atlantic age stripes raise the question of what moves and renews crust. Day 129 reteaches idle surface-change scales from day 127's check, and retrieves the plate and rock models maintained in Biology and Chemistry, thermal transfer, weathering and half-life. Unit 6 waves now make seismic evidence usable. Seismic boundaries, magnetic history and high-pressure rock evidence constrain the interior model before three-dimensional mantle convection connects heat to moving matter on day 133. Surface-process feedback and scales follow; a warm-water analogue is explicitly limited because the mantle is mostly solid. Ocean and continental age patterns, present motion and radiometric evidence then revise the joined model on day 142. Interior evidence and its analogue have five focused days, surface processes three, ages and evaluation four, and six open, reteach, synthesise, consolidate, assess and respond.

Day 144 assesses a new interior evidence set, a new landform-feedback system and unfamiliar crust-age evidence. It covers layered density structure, heat-driven matter cycling, every specified evidence type, interacting surface and interior processes across scales, ocean and North American continental age patterns, and the dating evidence behind the argument. Day 145 responds; its last ten minutes check unit 10's `s6-geologic-time`. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep conservation with a defined system on day 133 and evaluation of accepted explanations on day 140, two existing days, no addition. Empirical age-pattern evidence remains required. Do not solicit earthquake or volcano loss accounts; retain the deep-time sensitivity flag.

## Unit 10. Older than Earth

10 days, course days 146 to 155, weeks 30 to 31. Sections: 10.1, days 146 to 150, preserved and reset records; 10.2, days 151 to 155, craters, explanation, assessment and response.

The age table includes ancient meteorites, old Earth minerals, old lunar samples and some younger lunar rocks, including dated Allende and Murchison samples. It raises a problem of differing records. Day 147 reteaches geologic-time ordering from day 145's readiness evidence; half-life, scale and orbital models are retrieved. Earth record loss explains why other bodies matter. Radiometric ages precede comparisons of planetary sizes and composition; controlled crater records then support relative histories. Day 152 revises the formation timeline by combining these independent constraints. Seven days through day 152 build and join the evidence, one repeats and strengthens reasoning, and two assess and respond. Ages are supplied, with qualitative reasoning, no radioactive sources or logarithms.

Day 154 assesses an unfamiliar collection of ancient-material and planetary-surface evidence: dating and preservation, solar-system sizes and composition, crater sizes and ages, and a reasoned account of formation about 4.6 billion years ago and early history. Day 155 responds. No mid-unit assessment. Candidate decisions: keep consistent natural laws on day 149 and rates over long times on day 151, two existing days, no addition. Both serve the formation account. Teach Earth's age as evidence shows without asking a student to affirm or renounce a belief.

## Leans, access and materials

The grade 11 rows of `calendar.csv` and the three companion frames were checked. ELA informative writing ends on day 34, week 7; its argument unit ends on day 55, week 11. Both precede unit 6 technical communication, day 90, and unit 8 explanations, days 119 and 124. A model technical explainer and a claim-evidence-reasoning frame remain in those days as supports, including when the courses are not paired. No same-year prerequisite is late, so no extra bridge day is needed. Algebra II rational exponents, days 46 to 69, are avoided in unit 3. Physics wave mathematics, days 79 to 82, precedes Algebra II periodic modelling in days 113 to 136. Algebra I rearranging, slope, squared terms and fitted lines and grade 8 calculator roots are retrieved at their first use, with representations supplied when necessary. History's Scientific Revolution is earlier-year context, never a prerequisite for the physics explanation.

Science literacy is taught here, not delegated to ELA. Unit 7's claim evaluation stays a short in-class product in week 21, and unit 8's stellar communication a short in-class product in week 24. Neither creates a process-essay due week alongside ELA's pieces or course-level checks. The long explanation grows through notebook models, not a simultaneous new writing project. The end-of-unit assessment still reserves time for each required mode of communication.

Every hands-on day lists physical supplies, a no-kit alternative and a hazard/control judgment in the CSV. Recorded anchors and provided-data days require no physical enactment. Carts stay on a floor or low table with a stop; nobody rides or climbs. Collision materials are teacher-cut, with eye protection. Springs stay at low stretch with eye protection. Avoid strong magnets, pinching and interference with medical devices, phones or cards. Electricity is battery voltage only, alkaline 1.5-volt cells only, teacher-supervised short taps with current limiting, no lithium, rechargeable or phone battery shorting and nothing plugged into the wall. A sensitive meter makes induction detectable; an unverified single-coil lamp response is not assumed. Warm water is teacher-poured at safe hand-washing temperature, with eye protection and wiped spills. Damp sand is the household stand-in for dusty flour/cocoa crater trays, with low releases, eye protection and contained cleanup.

Force-time sensor records are optional classroom equipment evidence. The no-kit design path uses supplied calibrated force-time runs; a stopwatch does not magically supply force. Unit 5's selectable recorded-run path retains student choices, controls, repetitions and plan revision, but is expressly weaker than physical conduct and cannot silently establish that performance. Space, stellar and interior work uses provided data and paper models rather than inaccessible experiments. Simulations can later supplement these paths under the frame's names: motion and collision tool, crash cushion tool, orbit step model tool, energy track tool, coil and magnet tool, wave and signal tool, photon and wave tool, star life tool, plate and sea floor tool, and crater counting tool. No simulation is presumed built. The orbit spreadsheet has printed update rules; every other digital representation has a drawn or supplied-data equivalent. The CSV's actual tool names describe those drawn tools, calculator or supplied spreadsheet, with no required account or named product.

There is no base text, so no source-map.csv is needed. The sequence is original; reference curricula supply no wording, problems or images. Unit plans will specify sourced record sets and quantities, school cost, teacher setup and cleanup, safety-page content and the required fresh sensitivity and safety reads. These are future deliverables, not claims that a read has occurred.
