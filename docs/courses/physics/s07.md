**Item 1, reserved transfer.** An inspection instrument sends light through two narrow openings to an evacuated photocathode. All instrument records below are invented for practice, not measurements. A supplied field diagram represents linked oscillating electric and magnetic fields crossing the gap. With both openings uncovered, a screen shows alternating bright and dark bands; either opening alone gives a broader, smooth pattern. A narrow opening spreads light into a wider central region with weaker side regions. Separate equal-height wave pulses overlap peak-to-peak or peak-to-trough, then emerge unchanged. A driven receiver responds most strongly near its natural frequency. In equal-duration photocathode trials, 430 trillion cycles per second gives no emission at either low or high intensity; 760 trillion gives emission at both, with more electrons at high intensity.

Claims: W says that continuous wave energy alone explains every record if the beam is bright enough. P says that independent particles following fixed straight paths explain every record, so the wave model can be discarded. Evaluate each claim's evidence and reasoning; use your revised model to explain which account is useful for field propagation, spatial patterns and individual absorption, and what must change when new evidence exceeds a model's reach.

**Key and scoring:** two points for explaining reinforcement, cancellation and unchanged onward pulses, without claiming energy is destroyed (A); two for using interference, diffraction and resonance to challenge P's fixed-path account (B); two for using the threshold and intensity comparison to challenge W (C); two for linking electromagnetic field propagation to the wave representation and discrete absorption to photons at different scales; two for retaining each useful account while revising the explanation when repeatable evidence challenges it (D). The simple particle picture's failure does not disprove photons. No quantum account of interference is required. Missing evidence receives feedback without being forced into a misconception letter.

Meets: "Overlapping peaks add their displacements to reinforce a larger peak. The dark overlap is cancellation, and the pulses continue afterward. Bands, spreading and selective resonance support waves; fixed straight paths do not explain the bands. Brightness failed below the emission threshold, so continuous energy accumulation is insufficient. Photons help explain individual absorption, while linked fields describe propagation. Keep both models within their demonstrated reach and revise the larger explanation when evidence demands it." Does not: "Waves are obsolete because the detector emits electrons" (D).

**Item 2, reserved transfer.** Provide the following freely checkable published claims with title, authoring body, page address, access date, section and a short surrounding-context extract. These pages are withheld from instruction until day 105. The claims are authentic; the accompanying trial is invented for practice.

- [Microwave Ovens](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens), section "Microwave Ovens and Health": "Microwave radiation can heat body tissue". The surrounding text limits burn claims to high exposure.
- [The Risks of Tanning](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/tanning/risks-tanning), section "Skin Cancer", explains a mechanism involving "damaging the DNA in skin cells" and discusses both major solar ultraviolet bands.

A technician argues that these claims conflict because lower-frequency photons cannot carry enough energy to cause any harm. Evaluate both published claims and this inference. Use the following sealed-chamber absorber record to check the reasoning behind the first claim: identical absorbers have equal mass and initial temperature. With cooling prevented, three repeats each show a 1-degree rise at low power for one minute, a 2-degree rise at low power for two minutes, and a 2-degree rise at double power for one minute. A matched unexposed control shows no rise. These constructed values illustrate a possible test; they do not independently verify a published claim. No cells or patients were tested. Explain what frequency, intensity, duration and absorption each contribute; compare thermal transfer with ultraviolet DNA changes and higher-frequency ionisation. Judge the sources' validity and reliability, including purpose, missing methods and possible bias, and name independent evidence needed to strengthen the conclusions.

**Key and scoring:** two points for higher frequency meaning more energy per photon, distinct from intensity and duration (E); two for checking the repeats and controlled comparisons, noting that absorber heating supports thermal transfer but not a clinical risk estimate (F). Wrong: "The unexposed comparison is unnecessary" (J); two for linking absorbed microwave energy to heating and absorbed ultraviolet to molecular DNA changes, with X-rays able to ionise, without requiring all ultraviolet to ionise (G); two for finding the claims compatible and rejecting the technician's claim that nonionising radiation cannot harm (H); two for judging each source's preventive purpose, context and evidence limits, and proposing corroboration beyond its institutional label (I). The safety-oriented purpose can select emphasis without making either claim false. Mechanistic ultraviolet damage is not proof that every exposure causes cancer. Independent controlled absorption measurements and published cellular evidence would address different links. Every point requires using a practice with an idea on this new case: twenty of twenty assessment points.

Meets: "The heating repeats agree, and doubled power or duration gives the same larger rise here. This checks energy transfer to an absorber, not cancer outcomes. A microwave photon has less energy, but many absorbed photons transfer thermal energy. Ultraviolet can change DNA chemically without all ultraviolet being ionising; X-rays can ionise. The two claims describe different mechanisms. Both sources aim to prevent harm, but these extracts omit study methods, so I would trace their supporting studies and compare an independent absorption investigation and cellular evidence." Does not: "The agency published both, so no evidence needs checking" (I).
