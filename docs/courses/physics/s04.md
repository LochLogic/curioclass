## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why can the cart not regain its release height when energy moves to the surroundings, and where does the energy of a leaping magnet come from?"

Day 59's explanation answers both puzzles using height and motion records, friction evidence and magnet comparisons: energy spreads into surroundings; preparing the magnetic arrangement required energy beforehand.
Model boundaries on day 48 and particle representations on day 51. Thereafter require predictions before reveals and evidence for revisions; students increasingly choose representations.

**Transfer case 1, invented for practice.** A descending counterweight powers a signal box. A supplied pathway diagram shows the cord turning a converter, separating charge onto two metal plates, then discharging through a lamp and sounding a chime. Students need only the stated pathway, not the converter's internal operation. Nothing is enacted. Choose the final instant after the weight and mechanism stop, the plates discharge and the light and sound leave the box. Relative to one unchanged height reference, initial Earth-weight gravitational energy is 36 joules and final gravitational energy is 18. The box gains 7 joules of thermal energy and external supports gain 2; 3 joules leave as sound and 6 as light. Other changes are negligible in this idealised account. Print the prose beside a table and incomplete diagram; students reconcile all three.

**Teacher judgment.** Item 1 meets: "My boundary encloses Earth, weight and box; arrows carry two joules to supports, three as sound and six as light. Falling reduces Earth-weight arrangement energy and drives the mechanism; its particle inset shows the weight's particles moving together. The box retains seven joules thermally: its inset shows particles vibrating more, changing motion and interaction energy. Sound involves coordinated material vibrations; light carries field energy through space. Charge separation stores arrangement energy in the plates and field even at rest. A stretched spring also stores energy through changed particle arrangement and interactions. My balance is 36 = 18 + 7 + 2 + 3 + 6. With supports and outgoing signals included, all 36 remain accounted for. Energy dispersed into surroundings does not automatically lift the weight again; none is destroyed." Does not: "Nine joules disappear as friction" (A). Item 2 meets: "Inside my boundary are both pucks and their field. Force arrows point right on the positive left puck and left on the negative right puck. Charge produces the interacting field; equal opposite forces accelerate both inward. Making the left puck more massive would reduce its acceleration, not make its interaction force larger. The shared field loses 12 joules: five goes to each puck and an outward arrow carries two to the guides. Neither puck gains the entire field decrease. Slow separation against inward forces requires outside work and raises field energy. Resetting supplies the energy released next time." Does not: "The field creates twelve new joules" (D). Award partial credit for each correct model link, not vocabulary alone. **Earlier product meets:** "**Where the energy goes.** Both records can conserve energy, but their boundaries matter. For the cart, Earth-cart arrangement energy falls from 24 to 15 joules. Because the table excludes the track, its missing nine joules need a transfer arrow. The diagram supplies that evidence: the track warms as its particles vibrate more, changing motion and interaction energy. Thus 24 = 15 + 9.

**A shared field store.** Similarly, the magnetic row starts with 11 joules in the pair's field. After release, three remain there and six appear as motion. The runners receive two thermally, so 11 = 3 + 6 + 2. Energy belonged to the arrangement of both magnets and their field; the field did not create it. My diagram encloses the pair and field, labels field energy decreasing and motion energy increasing, and shows a thermal-transfer arrow to the runners outside.

**Comparing the records.** The table makes amounts easy to compare, but without transfers it cannot alone show conservation. In contrast, the diagram identifies destinations and suggests particle mechanisms; its qualitative insets cannot establish numerical amounts or prove a measured mechanism. Together these supplied records support both balances. In conclusion, an apparent shortfall can be a boundary crossing rather than destruction: evaluate the amounts and the destinations together."

**Earlier product does not:** "The cart loses energy, so magnets create it. The table and picture look right." It supplies neither evidence-linked account, integrated balance, evaluation of limits nor developed explanatory structure; D applies only to its creation claim.

**Section 4.1 exit, day 51, four minutes.** Invented for practice. A fresh record shows a motor-driven rotor holding constant speed while its solid bearing warms. The motor transfers energy in; transfers beyond the rotor-bearing boundary are negligible during this interval. A proposed model shows the rotor losing motion energy to the bearing. Revise it with a boundary, transfer arrow and particle inset to explain the record. Answer: rotation energy stays constant; input increases thermal energy through disordered particle vibrations and interactions. Look-fors: evidence-linked revision, boundary, conservation and particle mechanism. Meets: "My boundary encloses rotor and bearing, with the motor outside. Its inward arrow supplies the bearing's thermal increase; rotation energy is unchanged. Bearing particles vibrate more, changing motion and interaction energy. Initial energy plus motor input equals final energy; none disappears." Does not: "The motor's energy vanishes" (A). Reserve day 51's final four minutes.

**Section 4.2 exit, day 56, four minutes.** Invented for practice; records only. Two magnets repel. A fresh sequence shows the left magnet clamped motionless, the right sled speeding rightward as separation increases, and its solid runners warming. The clamp does no work; other transfers are negligible. Revise a model that assigns the entire field store to the moving magnet: (a) show both motions, both forces and the shared field-energy change; (b) add a particle inset and conserve energy. Answer: equal opposite outward forces act, but only the right magnet gains kinetic energy; shared field energy decreases, supplying motion and warming. Look-fors: both motions and force directions, field decrease, thermal mechanism and boundary. Meets: "I enclose both magnets, field and runners. Force arrows point left on the clamped magnet and right on the moving one. The clamp prevents leftward motion without work; only the right magnet gains kinetic energy. Decreasing shared field energy supplies that gain and the runners' thermal increase, with more disordered particle vibration and changed interaction energy. Those gains equal the field loss; the total is conserved." Does not: "All field energy was inside the moving magnet" (E). Reserve day 56's final four minutes.
