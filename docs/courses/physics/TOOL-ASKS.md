# Physics tool asks

## Motion and collision tool

Unit 1 requests a light tool, spec only. The unit's paper paths stand on their own.

| Day | Part and job | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Hold mass fixed and change net force to show why acceleration changes. | Partly fits: simulated results cannot independently establish a programmed law. |
| 6 | Hold net force fixed and change mass to show why acceleration changes. | Partly fits: pair with measured records and their uncertainties. |
| 7 | Compare a supplied falling-object record with a model prediction. | Partly fits: label the record and prediction separately. |
| 11 | Set two masses and signed velocities, predict, then compare total momentum before and after contact. | Partly fits: a mathematical model supplements physical measurement. |
| 13 | Move the system boundary to classify internal and external interactions. | Partly fits: a teacher judges the explanation of outside exchange. |

The specification must keep one-dimensional motion and two interacting objects. It must show position and velocity against time, net force and mass, signed momentum tables, and an explicit frame and boundary. Changing an applied pull must not silently stand for changing net force. Supplied measurements need provenance, units, repetition and uncertainty; model-generated records must say "invented for practice" and must never appear as measured evidence. Keep simulated cases distinct from assessment and exit values in s01.md.

Students predict before changing a variable and explain the resulting pattern on paper. Use the same cases as printed tables when devices are unavailable, with predictions written before a shared display reveals results. The section plans will allocate minutes within the existing days. No tool is built or presumed available. It must work offline without accounts, judge only numerical responses, and leave open explanations to the teacher.

The drawn motion graph on days 4 to 8 and 15 to 17 and the drawn momentum table on day 11 remain paper instruments. Calculators on days 9 and 12 are light practice aids. They fit their stated jobs and need no new software. No simulation is required during the end-of-unit assessment.

## Coil and magnet tool

Unit 5 requests a light tool, spec only. Nothing is built or presumed available. Its job on days 68 to 71 is to support student choices about controls, measurements, repetitions, precision, limits and plan revision in both causal directions. It partly fits: model outputs cannot independently establish the physical mechanism or physical conduct.

Provide two separate configurations: a current-limited battery circuit beside a compass, and a closed coil with a moving magnet and sensitive current meter. Keep the battery out of the induction circuit. Never connect the sensitive induction meter across a cell; the compass detects the powered circuit's magnetic effect. Students choose one changed variable, hold others fixed, predict, select measurements and repeat before revising a plan. Record their decisions and individual actions. Include disconnected, stationary, reversed-connection, reversed-motion and different-speed comparisons with fixed geometry when appropriate. Show signed current with units, instrument resolution and a zero check. Do not imply that a zero current means no magnetic field. Use a current meter rather than assuming a single coil lights a lamp.

Distinguish model-generated values, labelled "invented for practice", from measured selectable records carrying apparatus, method, date, units, resolution and provenance. Real records must be collected and checked before use. Keep the unseen assessment and exit cases separate from practice. Do not display automatic prose judgments; open causal explanations belong to the teacher.

Run offline without accounts. All controls must work by keyboard and show their state in text without relying on colour. Printed branching records and a shared display must preserve the same student decisions, repetitions and plan revisions. This is partial coverage of HS-PS2-5: neither the simulation nor the recorded-data path establishes physical conduct. Retain the dated individual physical investigation record when suitable equipment is available.
