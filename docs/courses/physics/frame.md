# Course frame: Physics

Course `physics` (catalogue id `physics`), grade 11, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10 units, each driven by one anchor phenomenon or design problem.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 149 in units and 11 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year: 50-minute lessons, five a week, 32 weeks. Every investigation fits one lesson; a school with double periods may join two. Physics owns 20 expectations in 20 rows, fewer than Biology (30) or Chemistry (21), yet keeps the same 149 unit days for two reasons: it is the most quantitative of the three (every unit from 1 to 6 carries measured labs and algebra), and most of its handoffs come from grade 6 and grade 8 rows idle two or more grades, so eight units (1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10) have readiness before opening (unit 1 after its first hook) whose one-lesson reteach is counted inside the unit's days (Seams). About 7 days an expectation; units 1, 8 and 9 carry the most. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments).
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards high school performance expectations Physics owns: HS-PS2-1 to HS-PS2-5, HS-PS3-2, HS-PS3-5, HS-PS4 (all five), and the Earth and space expectations folded in under the modified science domains model (HS-ESS1-1 to HS-ESS1-6, HS-ESS2-1, HS-ESS2-3; Desk card q-hs-science-core). Each expectation is taught in full: practice, core idea and crosscutting concept together, every clarification example named in the row, and inside its assessment boundary. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md (high school Gateway 2). In the Units table the **C3** column, a social studies column, holds for science the practices and crosscutting concepts the unit develops. **Check set adds** holds candidate practice, crosscutting concept and nature of science elements (NGSS appendices F, G and H) that the scope and sequence confirms or drops; the C3 column's practices and crosscutting concepts are required, and a dropped candidate never removes one. "NGSS" never appears in student, teacher or family copy. Values rule (b): facts and backed sources decide claims; the age of the universe and of the Earth are taught as the evidence shows (BASELINE rule 5).
- **Base text.** None. High school science is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"); the open high school units listed there are reference only, for sequence and ideas, never copied (no shared run of 8 words). **How phenomena are chosen:** something a class can see, measure or read as data; puzzling at grade 11 and explainable with the unit's core ideas; not an anchor, design problem or check case any other science course uses, kindergarten to Physics, unless the unit says it returns on purpose and what is new (the dropped phone, the truck playing music, the speaker, the Everest rocks, the Japan 2011 tsunami, the hand-crank flashlight of grade 4 and the Arctic sea ice of Chemistry unit 9 among them); a local swap is allowed if it needs the same ideas. **Sources:** public-domain data and images from federal science agencies (spacecraft, telescope, earthquake and sea floor records), open-licence (CC BY) material checked on its own terms, published numbers redrawn as our own, and data the class collects, including from a phone's free built-in sensors where a student has one (never required). No paid text, no library trip, no student account.
- **Labs and materials.** Every investigation has three paths: hands-on with classroom-cheap materials (carts or toy cars, ramps from shelving, masking tape, metre sticks, a stopwatch, springs, magnets, a compass, wire, a 1.5-volt cell, a slinky), a safe substitute named where one exists; a no-kit path from a provided data set or a recorded video that students measure frame by frame; and, where no class could run it (orbits, stars, the mantle), a Curio Class simulation tool (listed per unit, built later through the tool pipeline, DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Every simulation runs with no account and has a paper path. Electricity stays at battery voltage; nothing plugs into the wall. Alkaline 1.5-volt cells only; never short a lithium, rechargeable or phone battery. A unit's provisional cost is stated in the scope and sequence and refined in its unit plan; each hands-on day carries a Safety and set-up section (rubric row S5).
- **Storyline and models.** Each unit opens on its anchor, builds a question board, and ends on a transfer task with a phenomenon the class has not seen. Every unit names its initial class model and the points where evidence revises it.
- **Nature of science, taught on purpose.** Unit 3 (Kepler's laws found in data before Newton explained them), unit 7 (two models of light, each useful where the other fails) and unit 8 (the Big Bang theory as the account several lines of evidence agree on).
- **Engineering.** Unit 2 runs the course's design cycle with the practices Biology and Chemistry taught (HS-ETS1-1 to HS-ETS1-4 are owned there). Physics owns no engineering expectation; unit 6 uses the trade-off weighing again for digital information.
- **Math readiness.** Math leans get retrieval by default, with readiness when the performance rests on an older row Physics uses before its next math use. Physics day 4 uses slope-as-rate and equation construction before Algebra II days 72 and 7, so day 1 checks these and day 2 supports them. Geometry day 114 used formula rearrangement and day 99 used square/cube roots in grade 10; these are retrieval. Algebra II days 15 to 16 use expression terms before Physics day 33, and days 96 to 108 use fitting before Physics day 138, so those are retrieval. Orbits use Kepler's laws, Newton's law of gravitation and a supplied step-by-step spreadsheet, never vectors or trigonometry beyond Geometry; motion stays in one dimension (the progression review's warning that orbits must not become an untaught vector course). Kepler's third law is used as T squared = a cubed (years and astronomical units, a the semimajor axis) for Sun-centred orbits only; for a moon or satellite the unit supplies the coefficient in T squared = k times a cubed, or compares two orbits around the same body from a supplied reference orbit, and students do not derive k. Students square or cube, then take a square root or cube root with a calculator (grade 8, m8-roots); no fractional exponents. Algebra II runs the same year and teaches rational exponents in its unit 3 (weeks 10 to 14), after this course's unit 3, so no unit needs it.
- **Reading level.** Grade 11 to 12 band; every reading is our own or adapted public-domain text, with words taught before they are needed.
- **Literacy standards.** The science and technical reading and writing standards for grades 11 and 12 (RST.11-12, WHST.11-12; data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt) are met alongside the science where the work already does them (Cory, Desk 2026-11-14: "Load them", no extra days). Each unit's "What students learn" cell ends with the codes it meets; they are never owned standards and add no days. Unit 7's judging of published claims about radiation meets RST.11-12.8.
- **Safety read.** Every unit with a hands-on day (1 to 6, 9 and 10) gets the safety agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) before the course is called finished, above all units 1, 2 and 5, against published school physics safety guidance (battery and magnet handling, drop heights, eye protection) and the safety data sheet of any material named; the course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). Unit 2 (car crashes): the agent first studies a national road safety agency's and road crash victims' families' organisations' published guidance on talking about crashes, and published guidance for schools on loss. Unit 7 (sun exposure and cancer, phone radiation worries): it first studies a national cancer institute's and a skin cancer foundation's public pages, cancer patients' own organisations' writing, and a national radiation protection body's pages on phone radiation. Units 8 to 10 (the age of the universe and the Earth, with students of faith): it first studies science teachers' associations' guidance on teaching cosmic and Earth history to students of faith and a national science academy's guide to science and religion, with the values rule in hand.

A row last used in grade g gets retrieval in grade g+1 and a readiness check in grade g+2 or later; use means a day teaches or assesses it, not a passing revisit. The check is in the preceding unit's last ten minutes or the intervening respond day; unit 1 uses the last ten minutes of day 1 after its hook. Affected units reserve one support lesson on their second day. Grade 8 force, energy, field, wave and scale rows, grade 6 signals and surface-change scales, grade 8 light/geologic-time returns and grade 9 Biology/plate evidence need readiness at first use. Chemistry rock-cycle and plate-model use for melting stays retrieval, but does not establish past plate-motion evidence. Light paths are checked before unit 6 devices; unit 7 retrieves that current-year work.

## Units

The order runs from motion on the ground (forces, momentum, a crash design) to motion in the sky (gravitation and orbits), then to energy, which ties the two together, then to the electric and magnetic side of fields. Waves come next because light carries the evidence for the last three units: stars and the Big Bang are known through light, and Earth's interior is mapped with seismic waves (unit 9 needs unit 6's wave math). The year ends on the age of the Earth, the end of the deep-time thread that started in grade 6.
