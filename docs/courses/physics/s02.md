## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we design something that makes a collision gentler on what it protects?"
Day 19 opens with a teacher-only slow cart collision: folded card crumples ahead of an intact passenger space. Students sketch why damage might protect something. Offer a quiet switch to a non-personal parcel-buffer case, without asking why. A historical road-death-rate graph raises the larger question without claiming one cause. Field 10 supplies the sources.
On day 28, students explain the crumpling model through their tests and collision records: longer stopping time reduces average force for equal momentum change, while protecting space still matters.
Model reading force traces on day 21. On day 22, ask groups to justify controls; thereafter step back from material choices and require evidence before revisions.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Average force equals momentum change multiplied by collision time. | The inverse relation is reversed. Repair: divide the same impulse among short and long time intervals. | From memory. |
| B | The cushion makes momentum disappear. | Outside exchange is missing. Repair: expand the boundary to include barrier and Earth. | From memory. |
| C | Longer contact always means lower average force, whatever the momentum change. | A comparison condition is missing. Repair: compare equal and unequal impulse examples. | From memory. |
| D | Protection can be judged without a measurable criterion. | Success cannot be checked. Repair: replace "good" with an observable numerical limit. | From memory. |
| E | The smallest force is automatically the best design. | Other constraints are ignored. Repair: check every candidate against the full criteria table. | From memory. |
| F | A slower revised-design trial isolates the cushion's effect. | The comparison changes two variables. Repair: match approach speed before comparing cushions. | From memory. |
| G | Three successful trials guarantee future protection. | Limited evidence is treated as certainty. Repair: compare a repeat range with the criterion and state remaining uncertainty. | From memory. |
| H | Average force is the peak force. | The shape of the force trace is ignored. Repair: locate its highest point separately from its time average. | From memory. |
| I | Elapsed travel time alone determines impact force. | Travel timing is mistaken for sufficient collision evidence. Repair: distinguish elapsed travel time from collision duration; use momentum change with collision duration for average force and calibrated force-time records for peak force. | From memory. |

These are anticipated errors, not observed frequencies. Assign a letter only when the student's explanation supports it. Blank work needs a follow-up, not an inferred error.
