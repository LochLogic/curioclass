# Course frame: Computer Science, Kindergarten

Course `computer-science-kindergarten` (catalogue id `cs-k12`), kindergarten, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, the pilot for the computer science grades; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, one for each concept area at its kindergarten depth, in the order the ideas build: our tools and
  their rules (systems and security, so device rules exist before the first device day), step by step (algorithms,
  with the first sorting rule for machine learning), robot paths (programming, using unit 2's step-cards), ask,
  count and decide (data, reusing unit 2's sorting), and computers around us, then and now (computing and society,
  the year's closer).
- **Days.** 32 course days: 28 in units, 2 for course-level checks (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). One lesson a week of about 30 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  kindergarten to grade 2 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a specials
  rotation, so course day n falls in week n. The 30 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens
  with a three-minute retrieval task from the second session on (APPROACH.md, Pacing), and floor-grid and device
  sessions keep four minutes at the end to put cards, tape and devices away, so a session teaches for about 23
  minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing), with
  one shape for a unit that a course-level check follows directly: in units 2 and 4 the check stands for the unit's
  own end-of-unit assessment and the check's respond day is the unit's respond day, as math kindergarten's year-end
  check stands for its unit 8's own (data/plans/calendar.csv). So no week holds an assessment and a respond day just
  before a check, and units 1 to 5 teach on 4, 5, 7, 4 and 2 days, 22 of the 32. Days follow the load of the rows:
  unit 3 owns four programming rows with device versions, the heaviest load, and gets 9 days; the two society rows of
  unit 5 are single unplugged talk-and-sort sessions and get 4. 32 sessions for 14 rows is the progression read's
  "about two sessions a row", counted with checks (docs/reviews/progression-computer-science-2026-11-19.md); 22 of
  them teach. Each course-level check takes one check session and one respond session, since a 30-minute session
  holds a whole-class paper task and the spoken answers of five groups. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are no
  mid-unit assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The kindergarten core checks take weeks 14 to 17 and 29 to 32 (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, target
  weeks), so this course's checks sit in week 12 (mid-year, after unit 2) and week 27 (year-end, after unit 4), with
  their respond days in weeks 13 and 28 (respond days are not check days). Unit 5 runs in weeks 29 to 32 after the
  year-end check, as math kindergarten's unit 8 keeps days after its year-end check; its own end-of-unit assessment
  checks its two rows. Overlaps with other after-core subjects' check weeks are the lead's calendar pass across the
  after-core frames (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** The 16 EK ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, all taught here, since Curio Class
  has no pre-K (STANDARD.md section 2): EK-ALG-PS-01, EK-ALG-ML-02, EK-ALG-IM-03, EK-PRO-PD-04, EK-PRO-VD-05,
  EK-PRO-RD-06, EK-PRO-TR-07, EK-DAT-DC-08, EK-DAT-DI-09, EK-DAT-IM-10, EK-SYS-HW-11, EK-SYS-SE-12, EK-SYS-IM-13,
  EK-SOC-HI-14, EK-SOC-HU-15, EK-SOC-CE-16. Each is taught at the depth its owner row's notes set in our own words
  (STANDARD.md "Depth"; the boundary statements are never copied or quoted), and "What students learn" says what is
  out. Plans cite ids and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md "Quoting"); the credit line
  "Standards: 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any day that leans on a statement.
  Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the computer science bar (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5,
  items 1 to 9) and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and reads the partial base unit it adapts
  for order; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS
  2026-11-12) holds. No Specialty id, and no X + CS id, is claimed.
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's fifth column (the C3 column of the core frames) names, for
  computer science, the concept area each unit teaches and the CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code
  and our label (STANDARD.md section 3), as bar items 1 and 2 ask. Each unit plan names the practice's day and task.
  Across the year: CT6 define the problem (units 2, 3, 4), CT8 build and plan (units 2, 3), CT9 test, debug and
  refine (unit 3), CT7 find patterns (units 2, 4), IC3 explain and give feedback (units 2, 3, 4), IC5 be a reliable
  teammate (units 1, 3, pair roles), HCD10 learn from and include different users (units 1, 5), ESR1 weigh who gains
  (units 2, 4, 5), ESR2 respect people and protect information (unit 1). Dispositions are named in the unit plans,
  not here.
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, archived edition
  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived, key stage 1, resources dated 2023), which its publisher no longer maintains.
  England's current computing lessons are served from Oak National Academy, whose Year 1 list keeps only the three
  units this frame leaves out, so the frames map the archived edition as a fixed text (For the lead 4). Year 1 is the
  kindergarten age (docs/reviews/computer-science-open-material-2026-11-16.md). License: the scout read the Open
  Government Licence v3.0 "unless expressly stated otherwise" on the publisher's terms page; the archive pages carry no
  license line of their own, so each file's license line is read when the store folder is made. Adapted unit by unit
  and rephrased for an American audience (STANDARD.md section 2, Cory 2026-11-16): US spelling, school years named as
  US grades, no money in pounds, no product or company name, our own browser tool or paper in place of named apps and
  floor robots. Three of its six Year 1 units feed units 1, 3 and 2 with 4; its two creating-media units and its
  animation unit are left out (see Source map). Units 4 and 5 are partly and wholly our own. The source's name
  appears only in a day's credit line, once a day. Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#teach-computing-curriculum): the
  source store holds no copy of this base (BASE-TEXTS.md lists it with no store folder), so the Source map below rests
  on the public unit pages (lesson titles, opened 2026-11-25), and each adapted lesson's check for third-party images,
  text and tool screenshots waits on the store folder (For the lead 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is an unplugged or trace session, the first teaching of a construct or idea (arrow cards on a floor
  grid, a card sort, a picture program predicted before it runs). Fluency is a modify session: change one card or one
  block of a working set or path and check it; kindergarten has little of it (unit 2 session 2, unit 3 session 6, and
  the retrieval openings). Application is a make or design session: plan a step-card set or a path to a goal, test it
  and fix it (unit 2 session 5, unit 3 session 7), or run a small investigation and use its graph to make a real
  choice (unit 4 session 4; session 3 first teaches k-data-decide, so it is Understanding). Check is the end-of-unit assessment or a course-level check; Respond acts on it by
  error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make, and no make day before a trace or modify day
  on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Kindergarten answers by pointing, sorting, ordering or saying aloud to a partner; the teacher notes
  by exception on a class checklist (APPROACH.md, "The form of daily evidence"). Every end-of-unit assessment is a
  sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: a whole-class paper task of about 15 minutes (glue or lay
  picture cards in order, circle the first wrong arrow, sort a new card set, place picture answers on a graph mat)
  collected from every child, plus one spoken answer per child heard in five groups of six while the others finish the
  paper task, about 4 minutes a group for 30 children; groups not reached are heard in the first 10 minutes of the
  respond day, at every session length, still one sitting's evidence. Each item asks every part of its id (a tool
  matched to a job and a person, a picture algorithm followed, a dot drawn where the robot is after each card,
  devices at home, at school and at work). Each owned id has its own look-for: a "make" id (EK-PRO-PD-04) is met by a child arranging a sequence,
  a "describe" or "explain" id (EK-ALG-IM-03, EK-SOC-HU-15) by a spoken answer, a "pose a question" id (EK-DAT-DI-09)
  by the child's own question, never by recognition of a supplied one. Units 2 and 4 are assessed in the course-level
  check that follows them (Days). No score comes from a session that first teaches a row. The respond day reteaches by
  error letter and repeats the missed item in a new form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per child. Device days: unit 1
  sessions 1 and 3 (the parts of a device we touch, a first turn at a station under the rules), unit 3 sessions 3 and
  7 (the picture-block path program). That leaves about five sixths of the year's time away from a device, more than
  APPROACH.md's "about two thirds" (ours, untested): the EK rows are written unplugged first, and the device days
  carry the parts that gain from a screen; grades 1 and 2 may raise the share as event and repeat blocks arrive.
  Every device day is written four ways (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at
  stations with the other pairs on the paper version of the same idea, one screen with a child at the device for each
  step, or no device. No EK id needs a device to be met in full: the paper arrow cards and a person or toy on the
  floor grid meet EK-PRO-PD-04, EK-PRO-RD-06 and EK-PRO-TR-07 in full, and the picture cards of devices meet
  EK-SYS-HW-11, so the course record never marks a kindergarten id part met for lack of a device.
- **Paper path.** Every session runs on paper, cards, tape and objects; the picture-block tool of unit 3 has the
  arrow-card program beside it, laid on the same floor grid. No tool asks for a student account, stores a child's
  name or sends anything off the device (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety").
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Kindergarten children are pre-readers or early readers. No code or word is read
  alone: blocks and cards are icons, every word is said aloud and shown with a picture or an object, and the teacher
  writes. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth, with the everyday words that have a computing meaning
  taught as two meanings (bug, run, mouse, program). The word algorithm is taught in context in unit 2 (EK-ALG-PS-01)
  and used again in units 3 and 4. Sentence frames: "First it ___, then it ___" and "I think it will ___ because ___".
  A child may answer with arrows, pointing or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. Unit 5 may be left out (homes, jobs and family devices are its ground): EK-SOC-CE-16 and EK-SOC-HI-14 are
  then not claimed that year, and the course still teaches computing and society through EK-SOC-HU-15 in unit 2.
  Unit 4 may be swapped for a different set of neutral class questions, not left out, so the course always claims
  EK-DAT-DC-08, EK-DAT-DI-09 and EK-DAT-IM-10 (the bar part 5 fails a course with nothing on data). Units 1 to 3
  carry the device rules and the models the rest of the year uses and are not swapped or left out.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Floor-grid sessions run in a
  cleared space with tape that lies flat, children walk the grid (no running, no blindfolds), and device cords stay
  behind the table; devices are carried with two hands. The scope and sequence copies these lines into each
  floor-grid and device day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $3 (printing tool and device
  picture cards and the public-and-private sort sets), unit 2 $4 (step-card and attribute-card sets), unit 3 $8
  (two rolls of masking tape for a floor grid, printing arrow cards and the picture-block cards; a soft toy the
  class already has), unit 4 $5 (sticky dots and chart paper for the floor picture graph, five small graph mats for
  group work), unit 5 $2 (printing the older-and-newer device strips and place pictures), about $22 in all; the
  sorting objects are classroom items.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 has disability content (tools that work for some people and
  not others: a pencil grip, a magnifier, a screen reader) and online-safety content. Before it reads, the agent
  studies a starting pack: the Autistic Self Advocacy Network's "Identity-First Language" page
  (autisticadvocacy.org/about-asan/identity-first-language/), one community's own account of language and identity,
  not taken as every community's; the National Center on Disability and Journalism's Disability Language Style Guide
  (ncdj.org/style-guide/, free, English and Spanish); and, as background on accessible hardware only, Section508.gov's
  "Hardware ICT Overview" (section508.gov/test/ict-hardware-overview/). The agent widens the pack to the brief's five
  sources, two of them owned by the communities whose tools the finished cards show, and records them. It then checks
  the public-and-private section carries health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground). Units 4 and 5 touch homes and families (class
  questions, devices at home, family jobs): the agent checks no question asks about a child's home, food, pets,
  devices or a family member's work. The course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on in the
  laboratory sense, so no safety agent read is planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity read
  confirms are on each day.

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten has no course before it in Curio Class (STANDARD.md section 3: no pre-K course; the EK set is taught
whole here). It assumes no computing; it assumes children who can listen, take turns, point and talk with a partner
with help, which unit 1 builds on as it sets the class's device rules. No row's assumes cell is filled, so no unit
opens with a readiness check; earlier units of the same year open as retrieval inside a session (lesson 16).
