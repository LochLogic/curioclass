# Course frame: Computer Science, Kindergarten

Course `computer-science-kindergarten` (catalogue id `cs-k12`), kindergarten, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, the pilot for the computer science grades; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, one for each concept area at its kindergarten depth, in the order the ideas build: our tools and
  their rules (systems and security, so device rules exist before the first device day), step by step (algorithms,
  with the first sorting rule for machine learning), robot paths (programming, using unit 2's step-cards), ask,
  count and decide (data, reusing unit 2's sorting), and computers around us, then and now (computing and society,
  the year's closer).
- **Days.** 32 course days: 28 in units, 2 for course-level checks (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). One lesson a week of about 30 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  kindergarten to grade 2 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a specials
  rotation, so course day n falls in week n. The 30 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens
  with a three-minute retrieval task from the second session on (APPROACH.md, Pacing), and floor-grid and device
  sessions keep four minutes at the end to put cards, tape and devices away. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit
  assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing: once-a-week courses end every unit with one), so each
  unit carries two days beyond its teaching days: units 1 to 5 teach on 3, 4, 6, 3 and 2 days. Days follow the load
  of the rows: unit 3 owns four programming rows with device versions, the heaviest load, and gets 8 days; the two
  society rows of unit 5 are single unplugged talk-and-sort sessions and get 4. The 14 rows are short; 18 teaching
  sessions for 14 rows is about the "two sessions a row" the progression read settled (docs/reviews/progression-computer-science-2026-11-19.md),
  less the end and respond days. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are no mid-unit assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The kindergarten core checks take weeks 14 to 17 and 29 to 32 (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, target
  weeks), so this course's checks sit in week 12 (mid-year, after unit 2) and week 27 (year-end, after unit 4), with
  their respond days in weeks 13 and 28 (respond days are not check days). Unit 5 runs in weeks 29 to 32 after the
  year-end check, as math kindergarten's unit 8 keeps days after its year-end check; its own end-of-unit assessment
  checks its two rows.
- **Standard.** The 16 EK ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, all taught here, since Curio Class
  has no pre-K (STANDARD.md section 2): EK-ALG-PS-01, EK-ALG-ML-02, EK-ALG-IM-03, EK-PRO-PD-04, EK-PRO-VD-05,
  EK-PRO-RD-06, EK-PRO-TR-07, EK-DAT-DC-08, EK-DAT-DI-09, EK-DAT-IM-10, EK-SYS-HW-11, EK-SYS-SE-12, EK-SYS-IM-13,
  EK-SOC-HI-14, EK-SOC-HU-15, EK-SOC-CE-16. Each is taught at the depth its owner row's notes set in our own words
  (STANDARD.md "Depth"; the boundary statements are never copied or quoted), and "What students learn" says what is
  out. Plans cite ids and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md "Quoting"); the credit line
  "Standards: 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any day that leans on a statement.
  Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the computer science bar (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5,
  items 1 to 9) and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and reads the partial base unit it adapts
  for order; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS
  2026-11-12) holds. No Specialty id, and no X + CS id, is claimed.
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's fifth column (the C3 column of the core frames) names, for
  computer science, the concept area each unit teaches and the CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code
  and our label (STANDARD.md section 3), as bar items 1 and 2 ask. Each unit plan names the practice's day and task.
  Across the year: CT6 define the problem (units 2, 3), CT8 build and plan (units 2, 3), CT9 test, debug and refine
  (unit 3), CT7 find patterns (units 2, 4), IC3 explain and give feedback (units 2, 3, 4), IC5 be a reliable teammate
  (units 1, 3, pair roles), HCD10 learn from and include different users (units 1, 5), ESR1 weigh who gains (units
  4, 5), ESR2 respect people and protect information (unit 1). Dispositions are named in the unit plans, not here.
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, Year 1 (England's
  Year 1 is the kindergarten age, docs/reviews/computer-science-open-material-2026-11-16.md), under the Open
  Government Licence v3.0 "unless expressly stated otherwise", adapted unit by unit and rephrased for an American
  audience (STANDARD.md section 2, Cory 2026-11-16): US spelling, school years named as US grades, no money in
  pounds, no product or company name, our own browser tool or paper in place of named apps and floor robots. Three
  of its six Year 1 units feed units 1, 3 and 2 with 4; its two creating-media units and its animation unit are left
  out (see Source map). Units 4 and 5 are partly and wholly our own. The source's name appears only in a day's credit
  line, once a day. Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#Teach Computing Curriculum): the source store holds no copy of
  this base and BASE-TEXTS.md does not list it, so the Source map below rests on the public unit pages (lesson titles,
  opened 2026-11-25), and each adapted lesson's check for third-party images, text and tool screenshots waits on the
  store folder (see For the lead 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is an unplugged or trace session, the first teaching of a construct or idea (arrow cards on a floor
  grid, a card sort, a picture program predicted before it runs). Fluency is a modify session: change one card or one
  block of a working path and check it; kindergarten has little of it (one in unit 2, one in unit 3, and the retrieval
  openings). Application is a make or design session: plan a path or a step-card set to a goal, test it and fix it
  (units 2 and 3), or use the class graph to make a real choice (unit 4). Check is the end-of-unit assessment or a
  course-level check; Respond acts on it by error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make,
  and no make day before a trace or modify day on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Kindergarten answers by pointing, sorting, ordering or saying aloud to a partner; the teacher notes
  by exception on a class checklist (APPROACH.md, "The form of daily evidence"). Every end-of-unit assessment is a
  sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: a whole-class paper task of about 15 minutes (glue or lay
  picture cards in order, circle the first wrong arrow, sort a new card set, read a new picture graph) collected from
  every child, plus one spoken answer per child heard in five groups of six while the others finish the paper task.
  No score comes from a session that first teaches a row. The respond day reteaches by error letter and repeats the
  missed item in a new form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per child; about two thirds of the
  year's time is away from a device (APPROACH.md, Pacing). Device days: unit 1 (the parts of a device we touch, the
  rules at a station), unit 3 (the picture-block path program, sessions 3 and 5 of the unit). Every device day is
  written four ways (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at stations with the other
  pairs on the paper version of the same idea, one screen with a child at the device for each step, or no device.
  No EK id needs a device to be met in full: the paper arrow cards and a person or toy on the floor grid meet
  EK-PRO-PD-04, EK-PRO-RD-06 and EK-PRO-TR-07 in full, and the picture cards of devices meet EK-SYS-HW-11, so the
  course record never marks a kindergarten id part met for lack of a device.
- **Paper path.** Every session runs on paper, cards, tape and objects; the picture-block tool of unit 3 has the
  arrow-card program beside it, laid on the same floor grid. No tool asks for a student account, stores a child's
  name or sends anything off the device (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety").
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Kindergarten children are pre-readers or early readers. No code or word is read
  alone: blocks and cards are icons, every word is said aloud and shown with a picture or an object, and the teacher
  writes. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth, with the everyday words that have a computing meaning
  taught as two meanings (bug, run, mouse, program). Sentence frames: "First it ___, then it ___" and "I think it will
  ___ because ___". A child may answer with arrows, pointing or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** A school may swap or leave out unit 4 or unit 5 for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md
  section 3); no later kindergarten unit needs them. Leaving out unit 4 means EK-DAT-DC-08, EK-DAT-DI-09 and
  EK-DAT-IM-10 are not claimed that year; leaving out unit 5 means EK-SOC-CE-16 and EK-SOC-HI-14 are not claimed.
  Units 1 to 3 carry the device rules and the models the rest of the year uses and are not left out.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Floor-grid sessions run in a
  cleared space with tape that lies flat, children walk the grid (no running, no blindfolds), and device cords stay
  behind the table; devices are carried with two hands. The scope and sequence copies these lines into each
  floor-grid and device day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $3 (printing tool and device
  picture cards and the public-and-private sort sets), unit 2 $4 (step-card and attribute-card sets), unit 3 $8
  (two rolls of masking tape for a floor grid, printing arrow cards and the picture-block cards; a soft toy the
  class already has), unit 4 $4 (sticky dots and chart paper for the floor picture graph), unit 5 $2 (printing the
  then-and-now strips and place pictures), about $21 in all; the sorting objects are classroom items.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 has disability content (tools that work for some people and
  not others: a pencil grip, a magnifier, a screen reader) and online-safety content: before it reads, the agent
  studies disability-led guidance on describing assistive technology and disabled people's own choice of words, and
  the federal Section 508 program's plain-language pages on accessible technology, then checks the public-and-private
  section carries health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md;
  APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground). Units 4 and 5 touch homes and families (class questions, devices at home, family
  jobs): the agent checks no question asks about a child's home, food, pets, devices or a family member's work. The
  course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on in the laboratory sense, so no safety agent
  read is planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity read confirms are on each day.

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten has no course before it in Curio Class (STANDARD.md section 3: no pre-K course; the EK set is taught
whole here). It assumes no computing; it assumes children who can listen, take turns, point and talk with a partner
with help, which unit 1 builds on as it sets the class's device rules. No row's assumes cell is filled, so no unit
opens with a readiness check; earlier units of the same year open as retrieval inside a session (lesson 16).
