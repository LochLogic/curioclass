# Course frame: Visual Arts, grades 3 to 5

Course `visual-arts-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `visual-arts-3-5`), a band course for grade 3, grade 4 and grade 5, run as three years one after another (year 3, year 4, year 5). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year keeps the K to 2 band's four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread
  come back one step harder a year later in the same place: a looking and studio unit, an ideas and critique unit,
  a making and judging unit, and a showing and museum unit that ends the year with the class exhibit. Each unit
  joins two progression threads, one of creating and one of responding, presenting or connecting, so every unit
  makes, studies another artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5).
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes, led by student monitors from year 3 on),
  the teaching and making, and clean-up and collecting work (about 6 minutes in a paint, ink or clay session, 3 in
  a drawing, writing or talk session); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and
  one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: year 3 runs 18, 16, 14 and 12 (unit 1 joins three
  processes in one finished work and sets the year's studio; the exhibit unit's making is mounting and an artist
  note); year 4 runs 16, 16, 14 and 14, because its museum unit owns five ids (va-g4-museum carries VA:Cn10.1.4a,
  a made piece) and its design unit only one; year 5 runs 16, 18, 14 and 12, because unit 10 drafts, critiques and
  revises both a work and a written artist statement. These are course days: the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2,
  rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the visual arts educators' association's checklist for
  elementary visual arts asks for at least 50 minutes of art a week through the year, taught by a certified visual
  arts educator (2025 posting on arteducators.org, pages 3 and 4, read 2026-11-25). This course gives 80 minutes a
  week, 30 over that figure, and 40 on the once-a-week path, 10 short of it; a school on that path can add time.
  The course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a certified art teacher uses that
  teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look at a work that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one making day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into the
  openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path: year 3, units of 9, 8, 6 and
  5 sessions; year 4, 8, 8, 6 and 6; year 5, 8, 9, 6 and 5; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1
  respond session. The check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (weeks 18 and 26 in years 3 and 5,
  weeks 17 and 25 in year 4); the respond sessions fall one week later (weeks 19 and 27 in years 3 and 5, weeks 18
  and 26 in year 4), none of them a core check week for its grade. A merged day never drops a critique, a revision
  or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, visual arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 3 claims only the 15 grade 3 ids (`.3a`), year 4 only the 15 grade 4 ids (`.4a`),
  year 5 only the 15 grade 5 ids (`.5a`): 45 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in
  its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches
  all four artistic processes (creating, presenting, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches
  at least two. Where a words-file line is shorter than the national standard's action, the unit teaches the
  national action in full and says so in its learning line (for example VA:Pr4.1.4a's past, present and emerging
  technologies). The VA:Pr5.1.4a and VA:Re8.1.5a lines were rewritten to the full action on 2026-11-26. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic
  processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks
  itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the arts part of
  docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column
  names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every
  id against the id file; each year's scope and sequence still states its id count (15) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a child's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Visual arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied come
  from museum and library open-access collections (public domain works released by the holding institution) and,
  for living or recent makers, from a description in our own words with a link to the maker's or museum's page,
  never a copy (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line,
  checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work must keep the unit's ids met and the year's range. Range is
  checked across the band as well as in each year (the K to 2 read's pattern note): every year has works from
  Africa, Asia, Europe and the Americas, a Native American maker or community, and living makers; no Japanese
  woodblock print is used, since the K to 2 band leaned on them, and no work of the K to 2 band is studied again.
  No work studied in another visual arts band is studied again as new work; a revisit is named as one, with its new
  task (the 2026-11-26 fix pass swapped the works the 6 to 8 and 9 to 12 frames also named). A work another arts
  discipline of the band also uses is named in both units as a planned link.
  Curio Class writes every step card, work card, setting card, reading card, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Drawing: observing to add detail and depth to an invented idea
  (3), mark-making with tested and invented marks, and buildings drawn from looking with overlap and a horizon line
  (4), several techniques practiced over a dated log, and a labeled sketch map with symmetry (5). Paint and ink:
  printing ink rolled on a foam plate (3), ink and watered paint with brush and stick, and a group mural panel with a
  shared color plan (4), watercolor washes and oil pastel scratch-through logged as practice (5). Form: a modeled
  clay or dough piece joined to a printed and collaged panel (3), a box-and-paper model of a place (3), a mixed-media
  relief or soft sculpture from merged ideas (5). Presenting: a chosen show space and a short artist note (3), a plan
  for two places that protects the work (4), the curator's argued choice of safe mounting (5).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills of a technique with feedback (a mark drill, a
  two-minute sketch, a wash strip, a tool check, a practice-log entry); in 3 to 5 it is the five-minute opener and
  one or two fluency days a unit. Application: making with some choice, and drafting or editing an artist note or
  statement; the second making day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest; from year 4 the
  critique-protocol's set steps), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: the self-check, the written
  sitting and any answers given aloud at the teacher's table; the showing is scored from the kept work, and the
  handling items from the making-day observations. Respond: a
  second try at the part marked "not yet". A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency,
  application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide (three
  to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept work after the session; a kept card or sheet made
  on the unit's last making days (a plan card, a reaction card, an argument card), scored with the showing; written
  items on an unseen open-licensed work or a Curio Class original, in a short sitting; and making-day observations,
  used only where an id asks a child to show handling (units 1, 5 and 9): the teacher marks each child on the record
  sheet's rotating list on the unit's last two making days, never on a check day. The end-of-unit
  assessment is a check day near the unit's end (two in units 3, 8 and 11, which own two products or five ids). Its
  40 minutes: set-up 3; each child's self-check on the guide 5; the written sitting, two or three short items on an
  unseen work shown on the printed work card, 15, with the teacher reading every prompt aloud once to the whole
  class before the table opens; the teacher's table 12, where only children who answer aloud are heard and their
  words written down (a child who writes with difficulty, or one who asks for a prompt again); collecting and
  clean-up 4. That is 39 minutes. The others finish the sitting, then mount or label work with drawing tools, so a
  check day has no paint, ink or clay, and the teacher watches one table with one job. The product's draft, its critique with the teacher's look-for marks on
  the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a
  technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice
  days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every child has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes
  into the portfolio folder with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every year 3 unit assumes the grade 2 row in the same thread, last used one grade
  back in Visual Arts, grades K to 2 (its year 2, units 9 to 12), so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's
  opener, never a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. Every year 4 and year 5 unit
  assumes the year before's row, also one grade back, and opens the same way. The count starts from the band's order
  (grade 2 in the course before, then 3, then 4, then 5). A child new to visual arts, or in a mixed class meeting a
  later year first, gets each retrieval task's ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH
  "Readiness and the baseline look"). Unit 11's symmetry leans on math grade 4 (Cross-subject) and opens with its
  own retrieval task. The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (grade 3: 9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28, 30, 31, 32; grade 4: 8, 9, 10, 12, 16, 19, 21,
  23, 27, 30, 31, 32, counting both of math's and social studies' "or" weeks; grade 5: 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29,
  30, 31, 32). Year 3: mid-year week 18 (course days 35 and 36 of the year), year-end week 26 (days 51 and 52). Year
  4: mid-year week 17 (days 33 and 34), year-end week 25 (days 49 and 50), one week earlier than years 3 and 5
  because unit 8 owns five ids and needs 14 days. Year 5: mid-year week 18 (days 35 and 36), year-end week 26 (days
  51 and 52). Weeks 27 to 32 hold most core year-end checks in grades 3 to 5, so the year-end check comes before the
  exhibit unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and the exhibit unit's showing, in weeks 27 to 32 (26 to 32 in year 4), is its own
  end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 4 (a Lakota winter count and the Great Migration, hard
  history), unit 8 (it studies Buddhist cave temples, by looking only, and asks for a piece on a community
  tradition) and unit 12 (photographs of child labor). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's
  family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, crayons, found material; nothing must be bought,
  and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One child at home: the parent is the
  audience and the critique partner, every group or partner task has a doubled version (parent and child), and the
  showing is to the family. The teacher needs no art training: each unit's teacher page says what to look for, in
  plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as brushes,
  brayers and pointed scissors counted once, printing of step, work and setting cards), all optional beside the free
  path: year 3 $30, $10, $10, $10 ($60); year 4 $20, $15, $5, $10 ($50); year 5 $15, $15, $10, $10 ($50). Planning
  figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work, a step, an exhibit's pages or the school's own
  class web page; every day runs from the printed step card and work card without it. Three ids touch technology:
  VA:Cr1.2.3a (tools and technology on hand to pursue an idea), VA:Pr4.1.3a (spaces, online ones included) and
  VA:Pr4.1.4a with VA:Pr5.1.4a (technology in keeping and showing art, physical and digital formats). Each is met in
  full on paper: the child chooses among paper tools (a mirror, a magnifier, a viewfinder, reference cards) for
  VA:Cr1.2.3a, judges a paper mock-up of a class web page beside the room and the hallway for VA:Pr4.1.3a, and plans a
  wall and a printed slide set from setting cards on old and new technology for the year 4 ids. A shared device, if
  the school has one, is used in turns, and only the teacher posts to any class page. Kept work stays on paper or on
  the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  color-mixing practice tool, a work viewer, a mark sampler) has a printed version that does the same job and needs
  no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards are picture-led with short words; a child reads at most one short card alone (APPROACH
  "Supports"), and setting cards, reading cards and sitting prompts are read aloud on request. English learners:
  each unit selects three to five of its words for teaching in depth, with a picture, a gesture and a frame ("I see ___, so I think
  ___." "One clue is ___."), and its other words are met in context and revisited; a child may show or say the word instead of writing it, and the check marks what the
  child made apart from what they wrote or said (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov, publication 5015) and
  California's environmental health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for schools, with its list
  of materials not to buy for grades K to 6 (oehha.ca.gov/risk-assessment/art-hazards). Rules for every unit, from
  APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): only supplies labeled non-toxic and conforming to
  the art materials labeling standard; blades (craft knives, lino cutters) used by an adult only; no heat source for
  children (no hot glue, no kiln); no powdered clay, dry pigment or spray for children (premixed paint, moist
  air-dry clay or dough, water-based printing ink); pointed scissors and any pointed tool only after the child passes
  the safe-handling check (year 4 on; year 3 uses blunt-tip scissors); hands washed after paint, ink, clay and found
  objects; food and dough allergies checked; no sharing of face-contact items. Each unit's last column gives its
  hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition, hard history or a sacred use name the communities and the sources the agent
  studies first: unit 1 (Asante adinkra cloth makers, Ghana; Elizabeth Catlett's print of a Black farm worker), unit
  2 (the Mughal court workshop of the Hamzanama), unit 3 (Marshallese navigators' stick charts), unit 4 (Lakota
  winter-count keepers; the Great Migration; the Norman conquest), unit 6 (the Chicano Park community, San Diego, and
  the Kassena women who paint the walls of Tiébélé, Burkina Faso), unit 8 (Diné weavers; the Buddhist cave temples of Dunhuang; the class's own
  community's traditions), unit 9 (Anishinaabe beadworkers and their bandolier bags; the New Year print makers
  of Yangliuqing, Tianjin; Mexican flower vendors in Diego Rivera's work), unit 10 (Romuald Hazoumè's Porto-Novo,
  Benin; the enslaved and Indigenous people of Suriname whose knowledge Maria Sibylla Merian recorded), unit 11
  (Ottoman tile makers of Iznik) and unit 12 (child workers photographed by Lewis Hine; the Native nations of the
  Yellowstone lands). Every task that ties art to a child's own life (units 6, 8, 10 and 11) offers
  an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Each section carries health's steps for
  "If a student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, work
  cards, setting cards, found and low-cost materials. Activities: technique taught in steps, works studied with their
  setting, making with a critique and a revision, written responses to unseen works, a showing each unit. Outputs: a
  portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with written reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per child on
  the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 15 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has
  been tried with children, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH
  "Sources").
