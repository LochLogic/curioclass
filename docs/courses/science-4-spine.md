## How science differs from the math spine

- **A performance expectation is an end point, not a lesson.** Each one joins three things: a core idea, a
  science or engineering practice, and a crosscutting concept. A stretch teaches toward its expectations over
  many days and checks them with a task at the end.
- **Each stretch starts from something to figure out.** Six stretches open with a phenomenon (a real event to
  explain). Two open with a design problem. The class keeps a question board and a class model, and returns
  to both.
- **Students work with real materials.** A screen tool can show an idea or give practice. It does not replace
  rolling the marble or pouring the water.
- **There is no "major work" weighting in science.** The balance to watch is across the three domains, across
  the eight practices and across the seven crosscutting concepts.

## The year at a glance

- **108 science sessions of 45 minutes: 4,860 minutes, 81 hours.** That is three sessions a week for 36 weeks.
- **Why 108 and not 150.** The 2018 national survey of science and math teaching reports an average of 27
  minutes of science a day in self-contained grades 4 to 6, and only 35 percent of those classes teach science
  on all or most days (source 5). 27 minutes over 180 days is 81 hours, the same total as this spine. Amplify
  Science plans four units of 22 sixty-minute sessions, 88 hours (source 7). Mystery Science plans 19 core
  lessons of about an hour each, with optional extras that can triple that (source 8). So 81 hours is inside
  the range real programs plan for, and it is a year an ordinary school can actually give.
- **A school with less time (90 sessions)** cuts in this order and loses no performance expectation: the 3
  catch-up days, the 2 Year Together days, the second day of the five two-day end tasks, then one day from
  each of lesson sets 2.2, 2.4, 3.2, 4.4, 5.3 and 8.2 and two from 8.5. That is 18 days.
- **A school with more time** spends it on longer investigations and on the writing. No new content is added.
- **How the 108 days split by domain.**

| Domain | Days | Share | Performance expectations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical science | 43 | 40 percent | 7 (4-PS3-1 to 4, 4-PS4-1 to 3) |
| Life science | 12 | 11 percent | 2 (4-LS1-1, 4-LS1-2) |
| Earth and space science | 40 | 37 percent | 5 (4-ESS1-1, 4-ESS2-1, 4-ESS2-2, 4-ESS3-1, 4-ESS3-2) |
| Cumulative checks, respond days, catch-up days, The Year Together | 13 | 12 percent | all |
| **Total** | **108** | **100 percent** | **14, plus 3 engineering** |

  Physical science counts stretches 1 to 3 (39 days) and the 4 days on light and seeing inside stretch 4.
  Life science is small because grade 4 has only two life science expectations. Engineering design is not a
  separate block: 20 of the days above (19 percent) are design work, inside stretches 2, 3 and 8.

| Reading, talk, drawing models, working with data and maps, writing (print) | 32 | 30 percent |

- **Tool time, said plainly.** The eight tools are 235 minutes by their specs: about 4 hours of an 81-hour
  year, 5 percent. Nobody should read the tool column as the course. Most of the year is print lessons and
  investigations that do not exist yet.
- **Checking takes 12 days, 11 percent:** 8 task days and 4 cumulative check days. Every cumulative check has a
  respond day after it.
- **9 stretches, 36 lesson sets.** A stretch is one topic and one module. A lesson set is a package of 1 to 5
  days that answers one question from the class question board.
- **What exists today:** 8 tools (7 specs, 1 live), no unit, no investigation sheet, no task. Every one of the
  108 days is a gap day until its lesson set is written.

appears.

## The checks

- **Start-of-year check, day 1, 20 minutes.** Grade 3 ideas the year leans on (3-PS2-1, 3-PS2-2, 3-LS4-1,
  3-ESS3-1) and one fair-test item (the practice 3-5-ETS1-3 needs). Not graded. It tells the teacher where to
  slow down.
- **First ideas probe, 10 minutes at the start of each stretch.** A drawing and two questions on the earlier
  expectations the stretch builds on. It is kept and compared with the end task.
- **Exit question, 5 minutes a day.**
- **End-of-stretch task.** A new phenomenon or problem the class has not seen, so it cannot be answered from
  memory. Each task asks for a core idea, a practice and a crosscutting concept together, names all three on
  the scoring guide beside each part, and ends with "if many missed this, do that". Where the task is two
  days, the second day is feedback and revising the model or explanation.
- **Cumulative checks 1 to 4.** One session each. Two parts: short items on the stretches since the last check
  plus a few from earlier, and one fixed task that crosses stretches. Checks 1 to 3 are followed by a respond
  day and a catch-up day. Check 4 is on day 107 so that day 108 can go over it.

## What is missing today, said plainly

- **No lesson set is written.** All 108 days are gap days.
- **The tools are a small part and mostly not built.** Seven are specs. The one live tool, How Animals See, is
  marked for rework and its three codes read as shown, not taught. The shape file calls its own science reads
  rough.
- **A screen cannot meet these standards alone.** 42 of the days need real materials. Curio Class has no
  investigation sheet, no materials list, no safety note and no teacher page for any of them.
- **Contraption Workshop carries five codes in 35 minutes.** The spine spreads those codes over 16 days of
  print and hands-on work, but the tool itself still needs the look the shape file calls for.
- **Three places have no tool at all:** the evidence stations for 4-PS3-2, plant structures for 4-LS1-1, and
  energy resources for 4-ESS3-1 (the Town Planner read is practice only, and the tool is about hazards).
- **The standard text is not wired in.** data/standards/ngss-pes.txt holds codes only. The practices,
  crosscutting concepts, core idea statements, clarification statements and assessment limits are in no data
  file, so nothing can check a spec or a task against them by script.
- **No science rubric is on file.** docs/ADOPTION-BAR.md gives science two lines. The EdReports K-5 science
  criteria were read for this draft (source 4) and are not filed or summarised in docs/.
- **Three crosscutting concepts and one practice are placed by this spine, not by the standards:** structure
  and function, stability and change, scale, and using mathematics and computational thinking. A reviewer
  would want to see each in a real task before believing it.
- **Nature of science and the engineering connections** (for example "science findings are based on
  recognizing patterns") are in the standards and are not placed on this spine yet.
- **No science generator or task bank.** The cumulative checks cannot be "built from the generators so they
  can be redrawn" as ROADMAP item 4 asks. The fixed tasks above are proposals with no scoring guide.
- **The Reading Room has no science text sets matched to stretches,** and 4-ESS3-1 is a reading standard in
  all but name.
- **Grades 3 and 5 are not spined.** "Builds on" is true of the standards and not yet of Curio Class.
- **The prerequisites in data/sequence.csv need correcting** (see above).
- **The time assumption is untested.** 81 hours matches the national average. Many classes get less, and some
  states do not use NGSS as written.
- **The day counts and the materials cost are untested.** No teacher has read this.
- **English learner supports, access notes and family notes** are owed per lesson set and are not started.

## Least sure of

1. **108 sessions of 45 minutes.** A reviewer may expect a program sized like Amplify Science (88 hours) or
   may say a real class has half that. The cut list above is the hedge.
2. **4-ESS3-1 at the end of the year.** Mystery Science puts it at the end of its energy unit. Placing it late
   gives a spaced return to energy and a research piece when students write best, but it sits about 60 days
   from the ideas it uses.
3. **Life science at 12 days, with plants inside a stretch anchored on animals.** OpenSciEd gives structure
   and function its own unit and re-anchors on plants part way through (source 6). Plants may need a second
   anchor here too.
