# Course frame: Computer Science, Grade 6

Course `computer-science-grade-6` (catalog id `cs-k12`), grade 6, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written on the shape of the kindergarten pilot (docs/courses/computer-science-kindergarten/frame.md and its fix report); the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6, in the order the ideas build: programs with memory (programming, where the text view arrives beside
  the blocks and every later device day uses it), models of an algorithm (algorithms, with the careers row, built on
  unit 1's procedures), data with care (data, the four rows grade 7's machine learning needs), learning machines and
  who they serve (algorithms, reusing unit 3's tables as a training set), packets and protection (networks and
  security), and systems, history and choices (systems and society, the year's closer).
- **Days.** 64 course days: 58 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 3, 2 after unit 6) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). Each course-level check takes two lessons: lesson one scores the unit right before it
  in full, standing for that unit's end-of-unit assessment, and lesson two samples earlier units with short new items
  (frame-write.md rule 20); the respond day follows. Two lessons a week of about 45 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  grades 6 to 12 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a real elective, so
  course day n falls on school day 2.5 n, rounded up (week n/2, rounded up). The 45 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and
  collecting work: a session opens with a retrieval trace or a predict task (three to ten minutes, APPROACH.md "How a
  day opens"), and device sessions keep three minutes at the start to get devices out and open the page and four at the end to save
  or print work and shut devices, so a device session teaches for about 33 minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond
  day, with one shape for a unit that a course-level check follows directly: in units 3 and 6 the check stands for
  the unit's own end-of-unit assessment and the check's respond day is the unit's respond day (the kindergarten
  pilot's pattern). Units 1 to 6 teach on 13, 9, 10, 5, 5 and 8 days, 50 teaching days. Days follow the load of the
  rows: unit 1 owns three device rows, introduces the text view and runs a team program with a draft, written feedback
  and a revision, so it gets 15, the largest; it gives the mid-year check's second lesson by tracing the choice and
  the count-controlled repeat, both met in grade 5, in one session. Unit 6 owns four single unplugged rows (system
  comparison, industry devices, eras, decisions) and gets 8; it gives the year-end check's second lesson by folding
  "who decided and who was affected" into the decision-cases session, since the check's lesson one now scores its
  four ids in a sitting of their own. Unit 5 owns one row and begins one, and gets 7. 58 unit days for 18 rows is
  about 3.2 a row. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are no mid-unit assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The grade 6 core checks fall on school days 59 and 60 (ELA, week 12), 71 and 72 (social studies,
  week 15), 80 (math, week 16), 90 (science, week 18), 109 and 110 (ELA, week 22), 114 and 115 (social studies, week
  23) and 118 (math, week 24), with week 32 the finals week (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md stagger rule 3,
  data/plans/calendar.csv). This course's mid-year check is course days 37 and 38, pinned (data/plans/course-list.csv `pins`, lead calendar
  pass 2026-11-26) to school days 87 and 89, both in week 18 and off every core and after-core check day (science's
  day 90 is the next day); its respond day is course day 39, school day 92, week 19. The year-end check is
  course days 62 and 63, school days 155 and 158, weeks 31 and 32 (week 32 the finals week), off every core check day;
  its respond day is course day 64, school day 160. Overlaps with other after-core
  subjects' check days were settled in the lead's calendar pass (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** 17 of the 45 MS ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, the grade 6 share of the
  middle school band set by the progression (STANDARD.md "Ownership of MS and HS ids", 17/15/13): MS-ALG-PS-02,
  MS-ALG-PS-03, MS-ALG-ML-06, MS-ALG-IM-09, MS-DAT-DC-21, MS-DAT-DC-22, MS-DAT-DC-23, MS-DAT-DC-24, MS-PRO-VD-16,
  MS-PRO-PD-12, MS-PRO-PD-15, MS-SOC-HI-38, MS-SOC-HU-43, MS-SOC-CE-44, MS-SYS-HW-30, MS-SYS-HW-31, MS-SYS-NT-34
  (ALG 4, DAT 4, PRO 3, SOC 3, SYS 3). Two more are begun here and owned in grade 7: MS-DAT-IM-28 (unit 3) and
  MS-SYS-SE-32 (unit 5). Each owned id is taught at the depth its owner row's notes and its boundary statement set,
  in our own words (STANDARD.md "Depth"; the boundaries were read in the source store,
  S:\CurioClass-sources\computer-science\csta-2026-11-21, for this frame and are never copied or quoted), and "What
  students learn" says what is out. Plans cite ids and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md
  "Quoting"); the credit line "Standards: 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any
  day that leans on a statement. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the computer science bar
  (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5, items 1 to 9) and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and
  reads the partial base unit it adapts for order; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to
  add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. No Specialty id, and no X + CS id, is claimed. Days outside the
  standards (bar C1): none of its 50 teaching days (64 course days less 4 end-of-unit assessment days, 4 course-level
  check days and 6 respond days).
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's Practices and concept areas column names, for computer science, the concept area each
  unit teaches and the CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code and our label (STANDARD.md section 3),
  as bar items 1 and 2 ask. Each unit plan names the practice's day and task. Across the year: CT7 abstract and find
  patterns (units 1 to 5), CT8 build and plan (units 1, 2), CT9 test, debug and refine (units 1 to 4), CT6 define the
  problem (units 2, 5, 6), IC3 explain and give feedback (units 1, 3, 5, 6), IC4 run a project (units 1, 2), IC5 be a
  reliable teammate (unit 1 and every pair session), HCD10 include different users (units 4, 6), HCD11 test and
  refine for use (units 2, 3), HCD12 keep users informed and in control (unit 6), ESR1 weigh who gains and who is
  harmed (units 4, 6), ESR2 respect people and protect their data (units 3, 6). Dispositions are named in the unit
  plans, not here.
- **Blocks to text.** Grade 6 shows blocks and Python text side by side (STANDARD.md "Blocks to text"): every program
  a student reads or writes this year is shown in both views in our own editor, and a student may build in either
  view. Unit 1 introduces the text view with a short program that already holds a loop and a choice (row
  g6-variable-types). Python is named on teacher pages as a subject term; student copy says "the text view".
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, archived edition
  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived/key-stage-3, Year 7, resources dated 2023, with 2024 resource updates listed
  on the networks unit page), which its publisher no longer
  maintains; England's current lessons, on Oak National Academy, differ by unit. Year 7 is the grade 6 age (the
  kindergarten pilot maps Year 1 to kindergarten). License: the scout read the Open Government Licence v3.0 "unless
  expressly stated otherwise" on the publisher's terms page; the archived Year 7 pages carry no license line of their
  own (opened 2026-11-25), so each file's license line is read when the store folder is made. Adapted unit by unit and
  rephrased for an American audience (STANDARD.md section 2, Cory 2026-11-16): US spelling, school years named as US
  grades, no money in pounds, no product or company name, our own editor in place of the named block language and the
  named spreadsheet program. Three of its six Year 7 units feed units 1, 3 and 5; its two media units are left out
  (see Source map). Units 2, 4 and 6 are our own. The source's name appears only in a day's credit line, once a day.
  Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#teach-computing-curriculum): the source store holds no copy of this base, so the
  Source map below rests on the public unit pages (lesson titles and unit overviews, opened 2026-11-25), and each
  adapted lesson's check for third-party images, text and tool screenshots waits on the store folder (For the lead 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is an unplugged or trace session, the first teaching of a construct or idea (be the computer with a
  trace-table, packets on cards, a flowchart built from words, a case sort), a predict-and-watch session that makes nothing (unit 5
  session 3), and a worked session that introduces a new operation, programming pattern or math bridge (unit 1
  sessions 3 and 5, unit 3 session 6). Fluency practices
  previously taught constructs and operations: change a working program, model or table in small checked steps (unit
  1 session 8, unit 2 session 3, unit 4 session 3) plus the retrieval openings; there is no speed target. A session
  that introduces a new operation, programming pattern or math bridge is Understanding, even when students modify a
  working example. Application is a make, design or society session: build a program to a brief, model a new
  algorithm, answer questions of a table, evaluate a technology, or analyze a case with two views (unit 1 sessions 6,
  9 and 11 to 13, unit 2 sessions 4, 7 and 9, unit 3 sessions 7 and 10, unit 4 session 5, unit 5 session 5, unit 6
  sessions 2, 4, 6 and 8). Check is the end-of-unit
  assessment or a course-level check; Respond acts on it by error letter. Inside every unit: understanding before
  modify before make, and no make session before a trace or modify session on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Daily evidence follows APPROACH.md "The form of daily evidence": an exit ticket on trace days ("what
  happens?", wrong answers by error letter), the program with two or three look-fors and one line "what I tested and
  what I changed" on modify and make days, the paper artifact on unplugged days, a short claim with two dated views on
  society days. Every end-of-unit assessment is a sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: read and
  trace items plus a short build, debug, model or audit task with look-fors and a paper version. Each owned id has its
  own look-for: a "use" or "develop" id (MS-PRO-VD-16, MS-PRO-PD-12, MS-DAT-DC-23) is met by the student's own
  program or tool steps (MS-DAT-DC-22's viewing part by the student opening a file's properties and using its
  metadata), an "explain", "analyze" or "compare" id by a written answer (MS-SOC-HI-38's comparison across two eras),
  a "hypothesize" id (MS-ALG-ML-06) by the student's own hypothesis, the new inputs they chose, the outputs obtained,
  whether those support it and the revision. MS-PRO-PD-15 is met by unit 1's team program, a product whose draft
  (session 11), written feedback from another team (session 12) and revision with the team's reflection (session 13)
  are counted in the unit's days before it is scored. Units 3 and 6 are assessed in the
  course-level check that follows them (Days). No score comes from a session that first teaches a row. The respond
  day reteaches by error letter and repeats the missed item in a new form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per student; pairs keep driver and
  navigator roles that switch at the halfway signal. Unit 1's team sessions 11 to 13 run teams of four on one device, with
  driver, navigator, tester and recorder rotating every eight minutes (the named exception for teams of four) (on paper the recorder writes and the same
  rotation holds). Device days: unit 1 sessions 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11 to 13 (our
  editor), unit 2 session 6 (a given program run on the test inputs), unit 3 sessions 3 and 5 to 7 and 10 (file
  properties, the table tool), unit 4 sessions 2 and 3 (a toy classifier in the page), unit 5 session 3 (a toy network
  in the page, one screen allowed), unit 6 session 4 (a toy sensor and controller loop, one screen). That keeps at
  least one unplugged session in every unit (APPROACH.md, Pacing). Every device day is written four ways
  (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at stations with the other pairs on the paper
  version, one screen with a student at the device for each step, or no device. Codes that need a device to be met in
  full: MS-DAT-DC-23 (a computational tool), the viewing-tool part of MS-DAT-DC-22 (viewing a file's metadata),
  MS-ALG-ML-06 (observing a real model's outputs) and the shared digital tool part of MS-PRO-PD-15. On the no-device
  path those days teach the idea in full on paper (a printed table sorted and filtered by hand, a printed properties
  panel, a hand-sorting card model, one shared paper program) and the course record marks the id "part met without a
  device"; the course never says it was met. MS-PRO-VD-16 and MS-PRO-PD-12 are met in full on paper: programs written
  in the text view on paper, traced with a trace-table and checked by a partner.
- **Paper path.** Every session runs on paper: trace-tables, programs printed in both views, flowchart and
  pseudocode templates, printed tables, packet cards and case cards. No tool asks for a student account, stores a
  student's name or sends anything off the device; work is saved on the device or printed (STANDARD.md "Privacy and
  safety").
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 6 readers; code read alone is twenty lines at most (APPROACH.md, Supports),
  and every error message is plain words we write. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth, with the
  everyday words that have a computing meaning taught as two meanings (string, run, call, model, filter, packet). The
  word variable is taught as a named box that holds one value at a time, and its math meaning is named beside it
  (Cross-subject); assignment is read "score becomes score plus 1", never "score equals score plus 1". Sentence frames:
  "First the program ___, then it ___", "I predict ___ because line ___ says ___", "I am filtering ___ so that ___". A
  student may answer a trace in numbers, arrows or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. Unit 4 may be left out (machine learning is ground some communities ask to skip): MS-ALG-ML-06 and
  MS-ALG-IM-09 are then not claimed that year, the family note names them, and grade 7's g7-pick-approach,
  g7-improve-training-data and g7-examine-algorithm-harm open with a readiness check in place of their retrieval. Days 40 to 46 then stay in the calendar for the school's own use, so units 5 and 6 and the pinned checks do not move, and the year-end check's two unit 4 items are swapped (Course-level checks).
  Unit 6's decision case set (sessions 7 and 8) may be swapped for another set of cases on the same id, not left out,
  since it carries two of the three society ids and the year-end check; society is still met through MS-SOC-CE-44
  in unit 2. Units 1, 2, 3 and 5 carry the models and the data rows the later units and grade 7 use and are not
  swapped or left out.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Unit 5's packet game runs in a
  cleared room: students walk (no running), routes keep clear of desks and cords, and a student who cannot walk the
  route passes cards from a seat as a fixed router. Device cords stay behind the table. The scope and sequence copies
  these lines into each packet-game and device day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $5 (printed programs in both
  views, trace-tables, the team rubric and feedback checklist), unit 2 $3 (flowchart and pseudocode templates, test
  tables, worker case cards), unit 3 $4 (printed tables and properties panels for the paper path, the messy table),
  unit 4 $3 (sorter card sets, screen printouts for the design audit), unit 5 $5 (eight sets of packet cards,
  masking tape for the routes, code cards), unit 6 $4 (system and device cards, the era source pack, case cards),
  about $24 in all.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 3 touches personal data (invented profile cards, metadata a
  device adds without asking), unit 4 has disability and language content (who a design leaves out, users with
  disabilities, multilingual users) and a classifier that fails for some inputs, and unit 6 touches online choices
  (sharing a private photo) and the history of who shaped computing, including women and Black mathematicians whose
  work was long uncredited. Before it reads, the agent studies a starting pack: the Autistic Self Advocacy Network's
  "Identity-First Language" page (autisticadvocacy.org/about-asan/identity-first-language/), one community's own
  account, not taken as every community's; the National Center on Disability and Journalism's Disability Language
  Style Guide (ncdj.org/style-guide/); NASA's own biography pages for the mathematicians unit 6 names
  (nasa.gov, Dorothy Vaughan); and the Federal Trade Commission's children's privacy guidance
  (ftc.gov, as of the scope's date) for unit 3's and unit 6's privacy cases. The agent widens the pack to the brief's
  five sources, two of them owned by the communities the units show, and records them. It then checks that unit 3
  session 4 and unit 6 sessions 7 and 8 carry health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground), and that no session asks about a student's own
  data, devices, accounts or family. The course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on in the
  laboratory sense, so no safety agent read is planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity read
  confirms are on each day.
