# Scope and sequence: Grade 2 Math

Course `math-grade-2`, grade 2, draft, 2026-11-15.

The 160 course days meet daily for 60 minutes, so course day and school day are the same. `days.csv` is the day authority. Its nine units retain the frame's order, 153 unit days and owned rows. Days 90 to 92 and 144 to 147 are the seven course-level check and respond days. No unit exceeds 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment is required. Section boundaries below package at most six days without changing a unit.

The sequence builds mental strategies before written fluency, rulers before number lines, hundreds before three-digit computation, and arrays before partitioning rectangles into rows and columns. The six added lessons are days 16, 26, 35, 82, 126 and 128. The source map accounts for all 146 base lessons: 139 kept, seven optional lessons cut, six added teaching days and eight separate assessment days. Unit 9's closing observation is embedded in its review work. Only public first-edition pages were used. Source metadata and URLs live in `source-map.csv`; login-gated assessments, responses and exit tickets are not sources for our items.

## Time, evidence and mathematical practices

Each unit day embeds five minutes of spaced, mixed mental addition and subtraction within 20. From day 30 onward, alternate this with written within-100 strategies. Center games from the cut lessons supply practice without restoring full center days. Track the inventory of all sums of two one-digit numbers across the year, alongside mental subtraction within 20. The end-of-year memory conclusion uses that inventory and observation, not a single sampled card set. Record just knows, quick strategy or counts. No student sees a timer, and blocks do not count as fluency evidence. Blocks and drawings remain available to teach or repair understanding, tied to a written method; no standard algorithm is required.

`note` records planned minutes on major clusters or supporting work that explicitly practises them. The budget totals 7,930 of 9,600 minutes, 82.6 percent, above the K to 2 planning line of 75 percent. Geometry days 93 to 100 count only their five-minute facts warm-up. Initial graph days count only 15 minutes of facts, totals and differences. Time days count skip-counting and calculation, money days count within-100 computation, and array days count doubles and equal addition, rather than counting an entire supporting day automatically. This is a planned allocation, not measured classroom time. Units 6 and 8 are supporting/additional units, so the unit count remains seven of nine centered on major work. The unit plans must preserve these minute floors when they time their activities.

All eight practices have content tasks and closing evidence: MP.1 plans two steps on day 34 and partner stories on day 159; MP.2 translates quantities among story, equation and number line on day 70 and in day 73's assessment; MP.3 compares subtraction methods on day 26 and written three-digit methods on day 128, with evidence on days 38 and 131; MP.4 models a comparison with a tape on day 13 and in day 19's evidence; MP.5 chooses measuring tools on day 43 and number-line jumps in unit 4, with evidence on days 55 and 73; MP.6 attends to unit intervals on day 41 and zero placeholders on day 77, with evidence on days 55 and 89; MP.7 uses related facts on day 3, place-value structure on day 76 and array rows on day 140, with evidence on days 19, 89 and 143; MP.8 explains why paired totals are always even on day 136 and in day 143's closing argument. These practice choices cost no additional day.

Closing assessments ask for each part of each listed code. A code shared by contributing and owning rows is assessed to the contributor's stated reach first, then in full by its owner: one-step 2.OA.A.1 in unit 1, complete one- and two-step coverage in unit 2; supported within-100 strategies before written fluency in unit 2; addition before complete addition/subtraction 2.NBT.B.7 in unit 7. The observation-window notes specify products and short prompts for missing parts. Do not infer a whole standard from a single successful calculation.

At a class size of 30, a two-minute individual record takes 60 adult minutes per window. Where needed, the day notes split this into two groups of 15 on different days, or four groups of at most eight, while classmates complete independent work. A smaller class or one child uses the same record once. Record reuse includes each child's product and what it shows, not a class-level impression. A course-level check or end day within ten days uses that record. Written and product tasks can be read aloud and answered with drawings or oral explanation without changing the mathematical demand; supports are recorded separately. The unit plans write the prompts, coverage roster and response decisions.

## Unit 3. Measuring length

**19 days, course days 39 to 57, weeks 8 to 12.** Sections 3.1 to 3.4 occupy days 39 to 43, 44 to 48, 49 to 53 and 54 to 57.

Iterated equal units become a ruler, followed by centimeter and meter benchmarks, then inches and feet. Measuring the same object in different units gives the inverse relation between unit size and count. Length differences and stories apply subtraction before the class generates measurement data and constructs a whole-number line plot. The scale is built from ruler units; an abstract number line is held until unit 4. Day 56's constructed yardstick tests whether a made tool preserves equal units.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Gather each child's tool explanation once. Response is ten minutes on days 58 and 59, counted once.

**Candidate settled:** keep the geometric-measurement check of unit size and plot scale as planning work, zero days. Keep a made and checked yardstick on day 56 as that day's work, one existing day and no additional day. Keep base lesson 3.17 on day 55, as the frame requires. ## Shorter path

For a 45-minute period, keep the five-minute retrieval/practice, ten minutes of explicit model-to-written connection, twenty minutes of the main task, five minutes of comparison/synthesis and five minutes of exit evidence. Select fewer repetitions, preserve each new case and owned part, and continue a center as a later warm-up. On observation days, gather a smaller group each day across the named closing window while peers work independently; the same record is taken once. The 50-minute independent check packets must be divided between the two already reserved check days, with teacher observation happening alongside written work. A unit plan that cannot fit its full evidence must flag the time problem rather than omit a part.

For about ten fewer meetings, these ten unassessed days are fold-in candidates. Their mathematical cases remain in the named neighbor; reduce repetitions or setup there, rather than remove content. None is an end day, observation day, course-level check or respond day.
