# Scope and sequence: Grade 2 Math

Course `math-grade-2`, grade 2, draft, 2026-11-15.

The 160 course days meet daily for 60 minutes, so course day and school day are the same. `days.csv` is the day authority. Its nine units retain the frame's order, 153 unit days and owned rows. Days 90 to 92 and 144 to 147 are the seven course-level check and respond days. No unit exceeds 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment is required. Section boundaries below package at most six days without changing a unit.

The sequence builds mental strategies before written fluency, rulers before number lines, hundreds before three-digit computation, and arrays before partitioning rectangles into rows and columns. The six added lessons are days 16, 26, 35, 82, 126 and 128. The source map accounts for all 146 base lessons: 139 kept, seven optional lessons cut, six added teaching days and eight separate assessment days. Unit 9's closing observation is embedded in its review work. Only public first-edition pages were used. Source metadata and URLs live in `source-map.csv`; login-gated assessments, responses and exit tickets are not sources for our items.

## Time, evidence and mathematical practices

Each unit day embeds five minutes of spaced, mixed mental addition and subtraction within 20. From day 30 onward, alternate this with written within-100 strategies. Center games from the cut lessons supply practice without restoring full center days. Track the inventory of all sums of two one-digit numbers across the year, alongside mental subtraction within 20. The end-of-year memory conclusion uses that inventory and observation, not a single sampled card set. Record just knows, quick strategy or counts. No student sees a timer, and blocks do not count as fluency evidence. Blocks and drawings remain available to teach or repair understanding, tied to a written method; no standard algorithm is required.

Focus is minutes directly on major-cluster codes divided by instructional minutes, with assessment and respond days excluded. For a conservative whole-day ledger, every `observe` row is also excluded in full even where teaching continues alongside the observation. The remaining 124 days provide 7,440 instructional minutes; the `Focus allocation` entries total 6,015 major-cluster minutes, or 80.8 percent, above the K to 2 line of 75 percent. Supporting contexts count only their explicitly taught major-code work: facts and totals/differences, place-value calculation, skip-counting or length measurement. Geometry contributes only its five-minute facts practice; graph setup and array layout do not count as major work. Days 39, 40, 58 and 59 each reserve ten response minutes within the existing 60, leaving 45 major-work minutes and five setup minutes; response is excluded from the numerator. Days 52 and 53 each allocate 25 major-work minutes (five facts, twenty estimating and measuring), thirty supporting plot minutes and five setup minutes. Day 54 allocates fifteen major-work minutes (five facts, ten length-story calculation), forty supporting plot minutes and five setup minutes. Each minute is counted once. This is a planning allocation; unit plans must preserve the stated arithmetic and measurement time. The frame points here rather than using a second count.

All eight practices have content tasks and closing evidence: MP.1 plans two steps on day 34 and partner stories on day 159; MP.2 translates quantities among story, equation and number line on day 70 and in day 73's assessment; MP.3 compares subtraction methods on day 26 and written three-digit methods on day 128, with evidence on days 38 and 131; MP.4 models a comparison with a tape on day 13 and in day 19's evidence; MP.5 chooses measuring tools on day 43 and number-line jumps in unit 4, with evidence on days 55 and 73; MP.6 attends to unit intervals on day 41 and zero placeholders on day 77, with evidence on days 55 and 89; MP.7 uses related facts on day 3, place-value structure on day 76 and array rows on day 140, with evidence on days 19, 89 and 143; MP.8 explains why paired totals are always even in day 136's retained argument, used at day 143's close. These practice choices cost no additional day.

On embedded observation days, `codes` lists the evidence being collected; notes distinguish concurrent instruction that is not yet assessed. Closing assessment windows gather each part of each listed code once, in the products and short prompts named below. End days hold at most one extended modeling or multistep task with explanation; their other items are brief. Course-level lists contain only newly sampled codes, with all parts of those codes named in their tasks. Reused individual records inform support and the handoff without another collection or score. A retrieval opening supplies practice, never assessment evidence. A code shared by contributing and owning rows is assessed to the contributor's stated reach first, then in full by its owner: one-step 2.OA.A.1 in unit 1, complete one- and two-step coverage in unit 2; supported within-100 strategies before written fluency in unit 2; addition before complete addition/subtraction 2.NBT.B.7 in unit 7. The observation-window notes specify products and short prompts for missing parts. Do not infer a whole standard from a single successful calculation.

At a class size of 30, a two-minute individual record takes 60 adult minutes per window. Where needed, the day notes split this into two groups of 15 on different days, or four groups of at most eight, while classmates complete independent work. A smaller class or one child uses the same record once. Record reuse includes each child's product and what it shows, not a class-level impression. A course-level check or end day within ten days uses that record without re-collecting it; a course-level check never re-scores the unit work. New check cases also change task form from any assessment in the previous ten school days. Written and product tasks can be read aloud and answered with drawings or oral explanation without changing the mathematical demand; supports are recorded separately. The unit plans write the prompts, coverage roster and response decisions.

## Unit 3. Measuring length

**19 days, course days 39 to 57, weeks 8 to 12.** Sections 3.1 to 3.4 occupy days 39 to 43, 44 to 48, 49 to 53 and 54 to 57.

Iterated equal units become a ruler, followed by centimeter and meter benchmarks, then inches and feet. Measuring the same object in different units gives the inverse relation between unit size and count. Length differences and stories apply subtraction before the class generates measurement data and constructs a whole-number line plot. The scale is built from ruler units; an abstract number line is held until unit 4. Day 56's constructed yardstick tests whether a made tool preserves equal units.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Days 55 and 56 are Check days with embedded observation, not separate fluency or application lessons; unit 3 totals ten understanding, two fluency, four application and three check days. Base lesson 17 remains as adapted measuring and plotting stations plus ten unscored arithmetic center minutes on day 55. Base lesson 18 retains thirty-five unscored yardstick-construction minutes on day 56 alongside the remaining explanations. Days 55 to 56 form the practical closing window: select and use ruler, yardstick, meter stick and measuring tape; estimate in inches, feet, centimeters and meters; generate data and draw the line plot. Gather each child's tool explanation once. Day 56's final five-minute estimate-and-check comparison also retains two measurements of the same object in differently sized units and the fewer-larger-units explanation for 2.MD.A.2. Day 57 reuses these records. Its one fresh performance measures two lengths and solves the difference story with a drawing and unknown equation in a standard unit; a short same-unit addition item completes the addition/subtraction story coverage within 100. Response is ten minutes on days 58 and 59, recorded on both receiving day rows and counted once inside the existing periods.

**Candidate settled:** keep the geometric-measurement check of unit size and plot scale as planning work, zero days. Keep a made and checked yardstick on day 56 as that day's work, one existing day and no additional day. Keep base lesson 3.17 on day 55, as the frame requires.
