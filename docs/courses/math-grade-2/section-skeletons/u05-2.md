## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **2.NBT.A.1** (days 79, 80): Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent amounts of hundreds, tens, and ones; e.g., 706 equals 7 hundreds, 0 tens, and 6 ones. Understand the following as special cases:
- **2.NBT.A.3** (days 79, 80): Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form.
- **2.NBT.B.5** (days 80): Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **2.NBT.A.4** (days 81, 83): Compare two three-digit numbers based on meanings of the hundreds, tens, and ones digits, using >, =, and < symbols to record the results of comparisons.
- **2.MD.B.6** (days 81, 82): Represent whole numbers as lengths from 0 on a number line diagram with equally spaced points corresponding to the numbers 0, 1, 2, ..., and represent whole-number sums and differences within 100 on a number line diagram.
- **2.NBT.A.2** (days 82): Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s.
- skill m2-three-digit-place-value (grade 2)
- skill m2-fluency-within-100 (grade 2)
- skill m2-compare-three-digit (grade 2)
- skill m2-number-line (grade 2)
- skill m2-count-skip-1000 (grade 2)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions:** How many tens make a hundred, day 74? What does zero preserve, day 77? How do reference hundreds help locate a number, day 81? Which place decides a comparison, day 85?
>
> - **2.NBT.A.2:** Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s. Parts, by verb: count within 1000; skip-count by 5, 10 and 100. Taught on days 75 and 82; checked in C.

> **Fluency:** Maintain 2.NBT.B.5 through five-minute mixed warm-ups alternating mental facts within 20 and written calculations within 100. Day 80 collects fresh F before feedback and rehearsal, without blocks or time pressure.
> **Limits:** Count, read and write through 1000; compare three-digit numbers. Mental changes start from 100 to 900. General three-digit calculation waits until unit 7; no standard algorithm or rounding. On day 79 spend five minutes naming 1000 as the endpoint; extend by finding different representations within this range.

Withdraw supplied lines on day 85. Keep paper models available for repairs; park tapes and graphs.
