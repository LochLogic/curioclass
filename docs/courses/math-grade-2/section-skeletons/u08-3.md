## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **2.G.A.2** (days 141, 142, 143): Partition a rectangle into rows and columns of same-size squares and count to find the total number of them.
- **2.OA.C.4** (days 141, 143): Use addition to find the total number of objects arranged in rectangular arrays with up to 5 rows and up to 5 columns; write an equation to express the total as a sum of equal addends.
- **2.OA.C.3** (days 143): Determine whether a group of objects (up to 20) has an odd or even number of members, e.g., by pairing objects or counting them by 2s; write an equation to express an even number as a sum of two equal addends.
- skill m2-partition-rows-columns (grade 2)
- skill m2-arrays-equal-addends (grade 2)
- skill m2-odd-even (grade 2)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Questions:** What remains after pairing, day 133? Why do doubles give even totals, day 135? How can rows and columns describe one total, day 139? How do equal squares fill a rectangle, day 142?
> **Teaching:** Model pairing and record the matching sum. Compare a complete pairing with one leftover. On day 135, rehearse why another pair preserves two equal groups. Step back for explanations on day 136. Model rows before columns; on day 141 demonstrate and practise a complete rectangle partition before independent work on day 142.
>
> **Practices:** MP.8, day 136: task P explains why adding another pair preserves evenness. MP.7, day 140: describe the same array by row sums and column sums, pointing to each addend's objects. Day 143 task A retains row-structure evidence.
> **Carried:** Park unit 7's base-ten pieces, number line and graph after its response groups. Tie the new array to written addition on day 137, then compare row and column sums. Withdraw supplied counters on day 140; retain student drawings. Restore equal square tiles for partitioning on day 141; withdraw supplied tiles on day 142, with tracing available as recorded access support. Keep the array drawing for grade 3.
