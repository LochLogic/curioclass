## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **2.NBT.A.1** (days 152, 153): Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent amounts of hundreds, tens, and ones; e.g., 706 equals 7 hundreds, 0 tens, and 6 ones. Understand the following as special cases:
- **2.NBT.A.3** (days 152, 153): Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form.
- **2.NBT.A.1a** (days 152): 100 can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens - called a “hundred.”
- **2.NBT.A.1b** (days 152): The numbers 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine hundreds (and 0 tens and 0 ones).
- **2.NBT.B.7** (days 154, 155): Add and subtract within 1000, using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method. Understand that in adding or subtracting three-digit numbers, one adds or subtracts hundreds and hundreds, tens and tens, ones and ones; and sometimes it is necessary to compose or decompose tens or hundreds.
- **2.NBT.B.5** (days 154, 155): Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- skill m2-three-digit-place-value (grade 2)
- skill m2-hundreds-unit (grade 2)
- skill m2-add-within-1000 (grade 2)
- skill m2-subtract-within-1000 (grade 2)
- skill m2-fluency-within-100 (grade 2)
