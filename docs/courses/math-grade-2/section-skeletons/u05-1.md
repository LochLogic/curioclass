## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **2.NBT.A.1a** (days 74, 75, 76): 100 can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens - called a “hundred.”
- **2.NBT.A.1b** (days 74, 75, 76): The numbers 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine hundreds (and 0 tens and 0 ones).
- **2.NBT.A.2** (days 75): Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s.
- **2.NBT.A.1** (days 76, 77, 78): Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent amounts of hundreds, tens, and ones; e.g., 706 equals 7 hundreds, 0 tens, and 6 ones. Understand the following as special cases:
- **2.NBT.A.3** (days 76, 77, 78): Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 74:** Show nine paper tens and ten paper ones. Can these become one piece without changing the amount? Children mark possible groups; the teacher introduces the hundred. Allow five minutes.
> **Supporting questions:** How many tens make a hundred, day 74? What does zero preserve, day 77? How do reference hundreds help locate a number, day 81? Which place decides a comparison, day 85?
> **Teaching:** Model exchanges on day 74, zero placeholders on day 77 and all four mental changes, including crossings, on day 84. Rehearse those changes on day 85. Step back during ordering on day 87 and collection work on day 88; ask how the representation proves the claim.
>
> - **2.NBT.A.2:** Count within 1000; skip-count by 5s, 10s, and 100s. Parts, by verb: count within 1000; skip-count by 5, 10 and 100. Taught on days 75 and 82; checked in C.
>
> **Practices:** MP.7, day 76: exchange ten tens in a mixed collection, then explain each digit in its numeral. MP.6, day 77: distinguish 506 from 560 using drawings and precise names for the empty place.
> **Carried:** Bring back unit 2's pieces; tie exchanges to numerals on day 74 and expanded sums on day 78. Withdraw physical pieces on day 79 and supplied drawings on day 85; children draw when explaining. Tie unit 4's equal-interval line to three-digit labels on day 81, then jumps on day 82. Withdraw supplied lines on day 85. Keep paper models available for repairs; park tapes and graphs.
>
> Balance follows the frame: thirteen understanding, one fluency and one application day, plus one check. Days 74 to 85 allocate 640 of 720 minutes to major work, 88.9 percent; days 86 to 89 are excluded. Days 74 and 75 each reserve ten response minutes outside that numerator. All fifteen teaching lessons primarily address major work, making one major-work unit. No supporting cluster is assigned.
