Grade 2 Math. Unit 4, Adding and subtracting on the number line, [unit plan](s04.md). **Days 63 to 67.** Days by kind: fluency 1, understanding 4. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.B.6.** “Represent whole numbers” as lengths from zero in the day 63 review, then “represent whole-number sums and differences within 100” with jumps on equally spaced lines. The tail is the start, the tip is the finish and the traveled spaces give the distance. The drawn interval and its label agree.
- **2.NBT.B.5, days 63 and 67.** “Fluently add and subtract within 100” using place value, properties or related operations. Show how the tens and ones can be moved in either order and keep the same result.
- **2.MD.B.5, day 66.** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” length problems in the same unit. Tie the missing part of an aligned tape drawing to the square in an equation and to the matching distance on the line.
- **MP.5, day 66.** Choose a route for a difference and explain what its traveled distance represents.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned.** Q1 and Q4 for the 2.MD.B.5 connection. Section 4.3 administers and files s04-Q1 and s04-Q4 within the day 70 Q1 through Q8 block. See its check shapes and screened cases. Data ids for the exits below are s04-X63 through s04-X67.

Read one cue aloud. Every child marks a separate paper picture and gets private feedback. Record shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Code only work that shows a specific error.

- **X63, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Give a line with single-unit ticks and decade labels. “Where on this line does the answer to seventy-two plus twenty belong?” Key: mark 92, two tens beyond 72. Marks 74 after adding two ones (G). Marks 91 by counting 72 as the first traveled unit (A).
  - Shape: Place a result found through calculation on a line. Practice locates announced numbers and does calculations on separate sheets.
- **X64, 2.MD.B.6.** Show an arrow with tail at 86 and tip at 75 on an equally spaced unit line. Leave the distance unprinted. “What move does this arrow show?” Key: take 11 away from 86. Accept a minus sign and 11 on the arc. Marks +11 and traces left as an increase (F). Labels the removal 12 by counting both ends (A).
  - Shape: Translate an unlabeled arrow into an operation and amount. In practice draw arrows for stated moves with the operation already given.
- **X65, 2.MD.B.6.** Print 77 = 48 + 29 beside an otherwise neutral equally spaced unit line with decade labels. “What jumps would show this equation?” Key: 48 to 68 to 77, right 20 then right 9. Accept any correct rightward partition of 29, including one labeled jump. Draws 0 to 29 (E). Draws 48 to 19 as the addition move (F).
  - Shape: Draw jumps for an end-first equation. Practice writes equations under supplied diagrams or completes one missing annotation on a modeled line.
- **X66, 2.MD.B.6.** Print 91 − 88 and two blank unit lines tagged “Back” and “Up.” Read the tags. “Which way travels fewer spaces for this difference?” The paper response shows each route and circles the shorter one. Key: 91 to 3 travels back 88 spaces. 88 to 91 travels up 3 spaces. Both show a difference of 3, and the up route travels less. One labeled arc per route suffices. Rejects the rightward route because subtraction must go left (C). Reports 91 as the gap on the up route (D).
  - Shape: Compare distances on two self-produced routes. Practice connects a count-back diagram to a missing-addend tape and discusses the result in each. Keep a two-line comparison of traveled distance off practice cards.
- **X67, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Print a unit line with arrows from 87 to 67 to 64. Give a separate unit line for the response. “What different route shows the same take-away?” Key: 87 to 84 to 64, left 3 then left 20. Accept any distinct valid partition removing 23. Goes to 84 then 82 by treating two tens as two ones (G). Lands at 65 by counting 87 first in the three-space jump (A).
  - Shape: Supply an unseen second route for a given diagram. In practice annotate or compare two completed routes.

## 9. Supports and extension

Teach **jump** as the move shown by an arrow on the line, and **distance** as how many spaces the move covers. Point to the **start** and the arrow tip. Use “I started at ___ because ___.” Read or sign every stem. Use enlarged or tactile ticks and record a child's directed marks with a scribe when needed. Children read only short labels independently. Extend day 67 by explaining where the ones move is visible in two already drawn routes.

## 10. Two rooms

At home take turns tracing a known jump and writing a matching equation. Use grid paper or repeated paper edges for ticks. Project a practice diagram if helpful, and let everyone draw or mark privately on paper.
