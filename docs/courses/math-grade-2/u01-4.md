Grade 2 Math. Unit 1, Adding, subtracting and working with data, [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 17 to 19.** Days by kind: fluency 1, application 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.OA.B.2.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 20 with mental strategies. The year-end target is to “know from memory” every sum of two one-digit numbers. The teacher distinguishes recall, a quick method and counting, and keeps initial accuracy separate from revision.
- **2.MD.D.10.** “Draw” picture and bar graphs for the same data, one unit per response and up to four categories. “Solve” put-together, take-apart and compare questions from the bars.
- **2.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction” to “solve” one-step problems within 20, with all unknown positions. Unit 2 expands the range and adds two-step problems. Paper drawings and equations show the situation.
- **Day 19 practices.** MP.4 in S11: show both lengths and the extra part. MP.7 in R: show how a known sum preserves the parts when one part is removed.

## 3. Checks, written first

### Exit questions

- **E17.** Show 39 scattered bookplates beside a packing label of 40. “What groups of five or ten show the true count?” Key: 39, such as three tens and nine singles or seven fives and four singles. Circle the groups and put the total with the circled picture. Wrong: 38 after starting the nine counts with 30 (A). Accepts 40 with three tens and nine ones as if the parts matched the label (G).
  - Shape: Group an unmarked collection to check a label. Practice uses familiar collections without a disputed label and oral fact games. Keep this collection out of F.
- **E18.** Supply pictured, invented game votes: spin 6, slide 4, stack 1. “What bar drawing shows how many more chose spin than slide?” Key: bars 6, 4 and 1 from zero in common units, with a mark on the extra two cells of spin. Wrong: 10 after joining the two bars (E). 8 extra after using two cells per spin vote and one per slide vote (C).
  - Shape: Turn a supplied vote list into one graph with a visible comparison. Practice collects actual votes to produce both graphs and three separate calculations for G. Keep the game vote slip separate.

### Closing records and paper forms

The following keys and shapes identify the check stock to reserve. Use the unit plan's prompts with one cue per turn. Children mark a story picture or graph, using a square on their equation where appropriate. Accept equivalent story equations that preserve the unknown.

- **F, days 17 and 18.** One private two-minute hearing per child. Read 8 + 7, 6 + 6, 9 + 5, 16 - 9, 15 - 7, 12 - 6 one at a time. Key: 15, 12, 14, 7, 8, 6. Ask for a method on one response. For example, 14 for the first fact after counting the starting 8 as a step shows A. An accurate answer reached by counting all shows B when observed. Save spoken responses on the paper record. The child may mark where a chosen strategy began on a clean picture space after the fact responses are saved.
  - Shape: Six fixed bare facts followed by one private method with no supplied model. Practice uses different facts, games and collections.
- **G, day 18.** With actual circle, square, triangle and star counts c, q, t and s, both labeled graphs must show c, q, t, s, including a labeled place for any zero. Read separately the circle-and-triangle total, the count of responses other than circles, and the difference between triangles and stars. Keys: c + t; q + t + s; larger of t or s minus smaller, or 0 for a tie. Teacher example for 6, 3, 5, 2: 11, 10, 3. Accept matching key and labels. Wrong: unequal units (C), bars begun at 1 (D), adding the comparison counts (E).
  - Shape: Graph one actual anonymous ballot in two forms and answer three specific bar questions. Practice uses object counts or a supplied question set. Reserve the options and full product sequence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Children carry their independent sketches and findings from sections 1.1 to 1.3. Keep graph forms for G. The private F turns have only spoken facts until the fact responses are saved. Day 19 children choose or draw a tape themselves. Teacher-held copies of the first marks remain alongside revisions.

- **Day 18.** Ideal: equal graph units tied to anonymous slips. Paper graphs fit because each vote gets one symbol and one equal cell in the two forms. If a tally is lost, recount the slips. If both are missing, leave G open until a fresh vote can be collected.
- **Day 19.** Ideal: a plain story picture and room to mark or draw the unknown. Scrap paper and hand-drawn blank grids and tape outlines fit. Use large paper squares in place of counters and handwritten slips in place of number cards, ballots or prepared pictures. Digital tools: None.

## Day 17

**Fluency.** I can group a collection and use useful facts. Children hold their practice papers. Teacher holds the private F record. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix mental sums and differences.
- **5 min. Directions.** Recall familiar group boundaries on scrap. Show separate pictures for collections and the adult facts record.
- **15 min. Rehearsal.** Return E16 to everyone. Use five minutes each for H and K on fresh comparison stock. Use five minutes for addition practice such as 22 + 16 = 38.
- **30 min. Observation.** F for roster 1 through 15, two adult minutes per child while peers use paper fact games and familiar collection tasks. In each turn allow about 60 seconds for six short replies, 40 seconds for one method and 20 seconds to log and switch. Record missing prompts for an actual later dated hearing. Keep the clock with the adult.
- **5 min. Exit.** E17. Start with two minutes for private marks. Hear a rotating six for up to 30 seconds apiece on a grouping reason while peers finish marks or review. Inspect each slip. After class, spend 15 minutes on written F feedback.

## Day 18

**Application.** I can show votes and answer a question about them. Children hold both graphs and their bar calculations. Paper graph forms (light), check unaided, 35 minutes of Graph work.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return E17 with one cue and mix mental facts.
- **5 min. Ballot.** Agree to the drawing-mark question. Two groups of 15 vote simultaneously, one anonymous slip each. Tally all four headings for each group before graphing.
- **40 min. Graph work.** Give everyone their group's tally. In the first 24 minutes allow ten per graph and use any extra time for labels. Hear ten F turns during that block. Pause the interviews for six minutes to read the three G calculations, one cue and paper mark at a time. Use the last ten minutes for labels and explanation while hearing the final five F turns. This gives each child 35 G minutes and five minutes for interruptions including their two-minute interview. Use roster 16 through 30 and the day 17 turn budget. Keep the group's tally as its key.
- **10 min. Share and exit.** E18, every child marks a graph. Hear one reason from a rotating six (4½ minutes) while peers finish paper marks. Collect both G graphs, calculations and E18. After class, spend 45 minutes on F and G feedback.

## Day 19

**Check.** I can show how pictures and equations help me add, subtract and compare. Children hold the first marks and new paper. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return E18 with a private written cue. Mix facts using separate stock.
- **5 min. Directions.** Distribute unlabeled or neutral pictures and read the procedure aloud. Show where to keep each first mark before the next cue.
- **7 min. Revision.** Return F and G with feedback. Give each child the shared seven minutes to mark changes before scoring. Preserve originals and help used. Only initial F responses can document unaided fact recall.
- **37 min. Check.** Read S1 to S10 and S12 to S14 at two minutes each, S11 at six and R at five. Pause to save between cues. Give all children the full S11 block to draw and mark a reason. Keep written and scribed responses with the picture.
- **5 min. Marking.** Give out paper keys for the short items with a place to mark items for follow-up. While children use the keys independently, hear a reason from the rotating six (4½ minutes) using their saved S11 pictures. File those reasons alongside the first marks and put pens down at the close.
- **1 min. File.** Collect original and revised F and G, the tally, and S and R. After class, spend 45 minutes marking the teacher-judged work. Day 20 begins with five minutes of feedback, then uses return slots on days 20 and 21.

## 8. Practice that returns

Grade 1's private facts hearing and revision routines are planned only. Demonstrate the turn and the separate revision space with fresh paper. Day 17 returns day 6's sums and day 16's comparison reasoning. Day 18 returns the graph forms of days 9 and 10. Day 19 returns the records from days 17 and 18 before the fresh items. ## 9. Supports and extension

Use survey for asking a group one question and counting the replies. Say total, fewer and difference while pointing to the quantity or gap. Use “___ has ___ extra.” Read every cue aloud and offer large paper, tactile cells or strips, and a neutral scribe or an oral response filed beside the child's mark. Document support used. Extend after collection with a different question about a graph already drawn.

## 10. Two rooms

At home use invented vote slips, draw two graphs, and give the one private fact hearing once. Split day 19 into two 15 to 30 minute sittings. Keep feedback, revisions and fresh responses together. With one projector, share directions and hand out all work and keys on paper.
