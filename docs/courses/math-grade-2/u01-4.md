Grade 2 Math. Unit 1, Adding, subtracting and working with data, [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 17 to 19.** Days by kind: fluency 1, application 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.OA.B.2.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 20 with mental strategies. The year-end target is to “know from memory” every sum of two one-digit numbers. The teacher distinguishes recall, a quick method and counting, and keeps initial accuracy separate from revision.
- **2.MD.D.10.** “Draw” picture and bar graphs for the same data, one unit per response and up to four categories. “Solve” put-together, take-apart and compare questions from the bars.
- **2.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction” to “solve” one-step problems within 20, with all unknown positions. Unit 2 expands the range and adds two-step problems. Paper drawings and equations show the situation.
- **Day 19 practices.** MP.4 in S11: show both lengths and the extra part. MP.7 in R: show how a known sum preserves the parts when one part is removed.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** F, G, S1 to S14 and R, as specified in [s01.md](s01.md). Keep every full case reserved. Read every cue aloud. Provide unmarked paper pictures and short labels. Show the children only the current cue. Keep F private through the last interview.

### Exit questions

- **E17.** Show 39 scattered bookplates and a packing label that says 40. “How many bookplates are really here?” Children answer on the picture by circling fives or tens and labeling their total. Key: 39, such as three tens and nine singles or seven fives and four singles. Wrong: 38 after counting 30 as the first of nine advances (A). Accepts 40 while showing three tens and nine singles because the parts were never checked against the label (G).
  - Shape: Group an unmarked collection to check a claim. Practice uses familiar collections without a disputed label. Keep grouping evidence separate from F.
- **E18.** Give a pictured table of invented game votes: spin 13, slide 2, stack 1. “What bar graph shows these votes?” Save the graph before asking “How many more chose spin than slide?” Key: bars of 13, 2 and 1 with equal units from zero. The difference is 11. Wrong: 15 from joining the spin and slide counts (E). Nine from giving each slide vote two cells and each spin vote one (C).
  - Shape: Graph supplied votes, then settle one comparison from the saved graph. G uses real ballots, two graph forms and three questions.

E17 records grouping in practice. F supplies the 2.OA.B.2 evidence on days 17 and 18. E18 uses 2.MD.D.10. Day 19 reuses F and G, with fresh 2.OA.A.1 stories and the 2.OA.B.2 related-fact task.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Children carry their independent sketches and findings from sections 1.1 to 1.3. Keep graph forms for G. Give F one bare fact at a time and save the answer before speaking again. Day 19 children choose or draw a tape themselves. Teacher-held copies of the first marks remain alongside revisions.

- **Day 18.** Ideal: equal graph units tied to anonymous slips. Paper graphs fit because each vote gets one symbol and one equal cell in the two forms. If a tally is lost, recount the slips. If both are missing, leave G open until a fresh vote can be collected.
- **Day 19.** Ideal: a plain story picture and room to mark or draw the unknown. Scrap paper for a child's own tape and blank paper grids for graph work fit. Use large paper squares in place of counters and handwritten slips in place of number cards, ballots or prepared pictures. Digital tools: None.

## Day 17

**Fluency.** I can group a collection and use useful facts. Children hold their practice papers. Teacher holds the private F record. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix mental sums and differences.
- **5 min. Directions.** Recall familiar group boundaries on scrap. Show where to keep a collection record. Use a blank sample for the interview directions. Keep F cards face down. Seat the waiting pairs away from the private hearing so they cannot hear the facts.
- **15 min. Rehearsal.** Return E16 to everyone. Use five minutes each for H and K on fresh comparison stock. Use five minutes for addition practice such as 22 + 16 = 38.
- **30 min. Observation.** F for roster 1 through 15, two adult minutes per child while peers use prepared fact games and group familiar collections into fives or tens. Pick matched game cards before class so no turn deals a reserved fact. In each turn allow about 60 seconds for six short replies, 40 seconds for one method and 20 seconds to log and switch. Record missing prompts for an actual later dated hearing. Keep the clock with the adult.
- **5 min. Exit.** E17. Start with two minutes for private marks. Hear a rotating six for up to 30 seconds apiece on a grouping reason while peers finish marks or review. Inspect each slip. After class, spend 15 minutes on written F feedback.

## Day 18

**Application.** I can show votes and answer a question about them. Children hold both graphs and their bar calculations. Paper graph forms (light), check unaided, 35 minutes of Graph work.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return each E17 paper with a short written or pictured cue. Rehearse mixed mental facts. Put any class reteaching of A or G in the named response round on day 20.
- **5 min. Ballot.** Recall the one-vote survey from day 7. Read the drawing-mark question and its four choices aloud. Two groups of 15 vote simultaneously, one anonymous slip each. Tally all four headings for each group before graphing.
- **40 min. Graph work.** Give everyone their group's tally. In the first 24 minutes set out one blank of each kind. Allow ten minutes of drawing per graph plus four minutes for interruptions or labels. Read the two directions separately before starting private F turns. Leave a pictured direction by each blank graph. Hear ten F turns in these 24 minutes. Pause the interviews for six minutes to read the three G calculations, one cue and paper mark at a time. Use the last ten minutes for labels and explanation while hearing the final five F turns. This gives each child 35 G minutes and five minutes for interruptions including their two-minute interview. Use roster 16 through 30 and the day 17 turn budget. Keep the group's tally as its key.
- **10 min. Share and exit.** E18, every child marks a graph. Hear one reason from a rotating six (4½ minutes) while peers finish paper marks. Collect both G graphs, calculations and E18. After class, spend 45 minutes on F and G feedback.

## Day 19

**Check.** I can show how pictures and equations help me add, subtract and compare. Children hold the first marks and new paper. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return E18 with a private written cue. Mix facts using separate stock.
- **5 min. Directions.** Distribute unlabeled or neutral pictures and read the procedure aloud. Show where to keep each first mark before the next cue.
- **7 min. Revision.** Return F and G with feedback. Give each child the shared seven minutes to mark changes before scoring. Preserve originals and help used. Only initial F responses can document unaided fact recall.
- **37 min. Check.** Read S1 to S10 and S12 to S14 at two minutes each, S11 at six and R at five. Pause to save between cues. Give all children the full S11 block to draw and mark a reason. Keep written and scribed responses with the picture.
- **5 min. Marking.** Have children turn over a key for the short items only. Have children mark a separate follow-up box, keeping each original answer visible. Keep the S11 and R keys with the teacher. While children use the keys independently, hear a reason from the rotating six (4½ minutes) using their saved S11 pictures. File those reasons alongside the first marks and put pens down at the close.
- **1 min. File.** Collect original and revised F and G, the tally, and S and R. After class, spend 45 minutes marking the teacher-judged work. Day 20 begins with five minutes of feedback, during the first of two five-minute return rounds as children start their pictured directions. Day 21 has two more five-minute rounds.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use survey for asking a group one question and counting the replies. Say total, fewer and difference while pointing to the quantity or gap. Use “___ has ___ extra.” Read each cue aloud or sign the same cue. For F, offer bare numerals and signs without a model. Offer large paper, tactile cells or strips, and a neutral scribe or an oral response filed beside the child's mark. Document support used. Extend after collection with a different question about a graph already drawn.

## 10. Two rooms

At home use invented vote slips, draw two graphs, and give the one private fact hearing once. Split day 19 into two 15 to 30 minute sittings. Keep feedback, revisions and fresh responses together. With one projector, share directions and hand out all work and keys on paper.
