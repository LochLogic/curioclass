Grade 2 Math. Unit 4, Adding and subtracting on the number line, [unit plan](s04.md). **Days 58 to 62.** Days by kind: understanding 5. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.B.6.** “Represent whole numbers as lengths from 0” with equally spaced points. Work within 100. A tick mark locates a boundary. Each space between consecutive whole numbers is one unit long, and a point's label counts the spaces from zero. On a cropped line a known label anchors the same scale.
- **Connection, day 61, 2.NBT.B.5.** “Fluently add and subtract within 100” using place value, properties or related facts. Written calculations describe changes in position before the class learns arrow notation.
- **MP.5, day 58.** Choose one repeatable length and use it to turn ruler spaces into a labeled diagram.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned.** None. Section 4.3 administers s04-N on day 73. Keep its two scale choices and its point-and-shading task reserved. Data ids for the exits below are s04-X58 through s04-X62.

Read one cue aloud. Every child marks a private paper picture. Record shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Attach a letter only when a mark or explanation shows the named idea.

- **X58, 2.MD.B.6.** Blank squared paper. “Make a number line from zero to fourteen.” Key: 14 equal spaces, 15 boundaries and consecutive labels from 0 through 14. Ends at 13 by counting the starting tick as a unit (A). Writes 0 through 14 with widely varying consecutive widths (B).
  - Shape: Build the whole line from an endpoint request. Practice transfers and labels existing ruler boundaries.
- **X59, 2.MD.B.6.** Draw a line segment with its interior covered on the page. Leave both end ticks visible, labeled 86 and 97. Below, give a blank strip and spaces for a space count and a tick count. “How would you show what belongs under the cover?” Key: 11 spaces and 12 ticks including the two ends. Counts all 12 ticks as traveled spaces (A). Draws 11 ticks for 11 spaces, treating the starting tick as the first space (A).
  - Shape: Reconstruct the hidden part and show both counts. In practice all the gaps and boundaries are visible and can be touched. Reserve the covered scene and paired reconstruction.
- **X60, 2.MD.B.6.** Give an equally spaced, unit-ticked line labeled only at 84 and 93. “Where would you put the flag for ninety?” Key: sixth space right of 84, third left of 93. Marks 89 by counting 84 first (A). Marks 91 by counting 93 first on the way left (A).
  - Shape: Place a supplied label between two nondecade references. Practice gives the position and children supply a missing value.
- **X61, 2.MD.B.6.** Print two cropped unit lines at an offset: a dot one space left of 92 and a star two spaces right of 90. Set the dot farther right on the page. “How do the dot and star numbers compare?” Key: 91 is less than 92. A child can show why by marking the steps from each printed label and writing the two values or using a comparison sign. Marks both 91 by counting 90 first (A). Marks the dot 92 and the star 91 by counting the starting tick first in both crops (A).
  - Shape: Compare two stationary positions across offset windows. Practice moves one counter or pencil tip on one aligned line.
- **X62, 2.MD.B.6.** Show a line labeled 80 and 100 at the ends with a dot 45 percent across and no interior ticks. “About what number is the dot?” Key: about 89. Accept 88 through 90 with a mark near halfway as evidence. Writes 84 with four oversized unit spaces followed by much smaller ones (B). Writes 96 with sixteen crowded unit spaces before the dot (B).
  - Shape: Estimate a hidden value from a fixed point and two ends. Practice places a supplied value before revealing the grid. Retain the first estimate.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Without a ruler, repeat the same paper edge and name its unit. Handwrite number cards or write directly on the strip. Use flat paper in place of string and a pencil tip in place of a counter. Adults prepare any cut strips and oversee tabletop use.

## Day 58

**Understanding.** I can make a number line from ruler spaces. Children hold rulers and paper. Paper strip (light), show why, ten minutes of Model. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Hook.** Compare a ruler and a bare equal-spaced line. Children mark two matching features.
- **5 min. Warm-up.** Rehearse written strategies within 100.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Recall the pictured nine-space strip from zero as unscored review. Ask “How long is this strip?” before showing the trace. Notice who counts marks. Then trace one equal paper span per space to show nine units.
- **10 min. Model.** Match ten ruler spaces to ten copied spaces, one at a time. Label zero and the far boundary 10. Show A and B once the right diagram is clear.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Give private Unit 3 feedback and run both rounds inside independent work.
- **20 min. Practice.** Children transfer premarked ruler boundaries, then label finished distances. Hear the six and use remaining teacher turns for missed T, as needed.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Collect X58.

## 9. Supports and extension

Show **number line** as a line with numbers spaced by equal steps and a **tick mark** as the small mark locating a point. On day 60 show a reference point as a labeled place that helps locate another number. Use “From ___ I moved ___ spaces.” Read or sign every prompt. Offer large grids, tactile ticks and a scribe who follows the child's directions. Students read only short labels on their own. Extend day 62 by testing two possible placements for the same supplied number and showing which fits the references.
