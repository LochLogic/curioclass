## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.D.9.** “Generate measurement data” from repeated readings of the same object on day 54 and measurements of several objects on day 55. “Show the measurements by making a line plot” with an even whole-number scale.
- **2.MD.B.5.** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” same-unit length problems with drawings and equations. **2.MD.A.4** “Measure to determine how much longer,” using a standard unit for the difference.
- **2.MD.A.1.** “Measure” by selecting and using a suitable tool. MP.5 and MP.6 on days 55 and 56: justify a tool choice and show where the equal units start and end. The adult records one explanation per child.
- **2.MD.A.3.** “Estimate” using benchmarks in the four units. Preserve the forecast made before measuring. **2.MD.A.2** “Measure” one length twice and “describe” the relation between unit size and count. The endpoints stay fixed.
- **Connections.** Day 57 maintains 2.NBT.B.5 “Fluently add and subtract within 100” with a place-value strategy, properties or a related fact. It also maintains 2.OA.A.1 “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” one- and two-step stories across situations and unknown positions. File that evidence separately from this unit's measurement scores.

### Closing records

- **T, 55 to 57.** Cue at each station: “How would you measure this path?” The station picture accepts start, end and unit marks. Use the four tools and prepared lengths in the unit, checking every setup before class. Adult key only: card 19 cm, straight routes 6 ft and 2 m, curved route 17 in. Print the assigned unit and hide the measured answers. A card read as 20 cm because it starts at 1 (B), 20 cm from counting boundary marks (C), or a tape stretched straight across the curve (J) each shows a different error. Accept an accurate choice supported by how it reads that route. In the private conference ask “How does your tool show this length?” Use one chosen picture to capture the tool reason and evidence of equal units from the start. Score after revision.
  - Shape: Independently choose and use tools on the four reserved tabletop routes. Practice uses supplied tools, open-surface reach or a single demonstration edge, apart from these stations and their record format.
- **E, 55 to 57.** With tools covered ask “About how long is this path in [unit]?” Read the assigned unit for each station. Mark the benchmark on the paper picture. Defensible samples using checked references: about 20 cm, 6 ft, 2 m and 18 in. Keep initial predictions. Marking a centimeter as an inch benchmark shows I. A guess alone needs a follow-up. On day 57, only fresh guesses on unseen objects can improve the estimation evidence. Actual lengths are 23 cm, 4 ft, 3 m and 14 in. Sample predictions: about 20 cm, 4 ft, 3 m and 12 in. For the last sample, two six-inch references fit about the length. Accept defensible alternatives. Keep both attempts and withhold readings until after forecasts are sealed.
  - Shape: Reserve the four independent predictions before T, and a fresh-object prediction in each affected unit on day 57. Practice gives partner feedback on other objects and keeps these records covered.
- **P, 55 and 56, retained on 57.** Cue: “How would a line plot show these strip lengths?” Each child measures the five reserved strips to the nearest centimeter. Adults cut strips of 78, 109, 82, 98 and 112 millimeters and verify them before class. Children see only the centimeter scale. The nearest-centimeter readings are 8, 11, 8, 10, 11 cm. Look for a title, unit and consecutive equal steps covering 8 through 11. Extra empty ticks at either end are fine. Frequencies at 8 through 11 are 2, 0, 1, 2. One mark per distinct reading (F) loses two observations. Skipping the empty 9 on equally spaced ticks (E) changes the scale.
  - Shape: Generate a private five-strip record and build its plot from a blank grid without supplied ticks. Practice uses other sets, supplied scale support or a pooled plot.
- **C, 56 and 57.** Cue: “Why do both counts fit this same strip?” Give the untouched strip, forecast space and two unit tools. Children estimate before measuring, mark both counts on the picture and show why they cover the same span. The checked strip is 24 inches or 2 feet. Feet give fewer units because each unit is longer. Saying the foot count must be greater because feet are larger shows D. Revise the explanation from this evidence on 57.
  - Shape: Independently measure one untouched strip in two units and explain the relation on one picture. In practice partners compare readings and can correct placement together. Keep the C strip separate from construction.
- **D, 57.** Cue: “How much longer is this track than the other?” Point to the longer cardboard track without giving a reading. Children measure both with a checked centimeter ruler, mark the excess on the picture and record an unknown equation. Key: 29 and 12 cm, with 12 + □ = 29 or 29 − 12 = □, giving 17 cm. Bracketing the longer whole as the difference gives 29 (H). Counting boundaries gives readings of 30 and 13 (C), even though they also differ by 17.
  - Shape: Independently choose the excess after measuring two specific objects. Practice rehearses one reading, a known excess, or comparisons from supplied values.
- **L, 57.** Show the two reserved strip lengths in separate nonoverlapping shapes. Cue: “How long is the joined strip?” The drawing and equation share one answer. Key: 37 + 26 = □, for 63 cm. Subtracting the two parts to get 11 cm shows G.
  - Shape: Draw and solve the reserved join from two given lengths on an independent story sheet. Practice moves paper strips or traces supplied worked labels with other cases.
- **F, 57.** Separately judge the calculation in D, L, S4 and S9. Key: D gives 17, L gives 63, S4 gives 37 and S9 gives 35. Look for accurate and independent strategy use. Self-drawn marks are fine. For 37 + 26, recording 53 after leaving out the new ten shows M.
  - Shape: Judge calculation and visible independent strategy from four reserved contexts, with addition and subtraction both with and without making or exchanging a ten. Practice uses other facts or allows partner feedback.

## Day 54

**Application.** I can read and compare measurements from a plot. Children draw the scale. Paper plot (light), practice with feedback, five minutes of Plot and repair. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Written within-100 strategy.
- **5 min. Setup.** Issue blank grids and check rulers.
- **35 min. Plot and repair.** Give five minutes to X53 E, using one fresh blank scale. Give each pair one pencil case and a ruler. Both children measure that same case twice and pool only their four readings. Keep each pair's plot with its own case. Build a plot and compare counts at chosen positions. Model a length difference by bracketing the spaces between two occupied positions. If every reading on a plot is the same, the longest and shortest lengths differ by zero. For another comparison, use one real reading from each of two different-sized cases. Hear six. If a tool slips, remeasure and keep only valid readings. If all readings agree, stack them at one position and preserve equal ticks on either side.
- **10 min. Length stories.** Draw an unknown equation for a comparison using the plot readings, asking how much shorter one length is. On separate paper join lengths of 50 and 38 cm for a total of 88 cm. Mark □ on the whole and use a screened partner case to check the unit and shape.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X54. This completes the 40 minutes for plots.
