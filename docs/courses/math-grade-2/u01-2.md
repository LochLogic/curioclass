Grade 2 Math. Unit 1, Adding, subtracting and working with data, [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 7 to 11.** Days by kind: understanding 3, application 2. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.D.10.** “Draw” a picture graph and a bar graph to represent the same data set with up to four categories and a single-unit scale. “Solve” simple put-together, take-apart and compare problems from a bar graph.
- **2.OA.B.2, day 11.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 20 mentally. Use known sums, make a ten and related differences. The year-end target is to “know from memory” all sums of two one-digit numbers.
- **Connections.** Day 9 ties bars to equations. Day 11 uses a fixed total with two parts for the one-step 2.OA.A.1 work.
- **MP.4, day 10.** Trace the same count through two graphs. Explain that a bar starts at zero and measures intervals. A row label and a graph key describe the record. Each plotted symbol or cell represents one response.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. E7 to E11 use 2.MD.D.10. Day 11 also samples 2.OA.B.2 during mental practice. Section 1.4 runs G. Reserve the drawing-mark ballot and the paired graphs with their three specific calculations. Read each exit cue aloud. Each child marks a picture on paper. Label and say “invented for practice” on supplied cases. Save first marks and check methods before assigning letters.

- **E7.** Give ten mixed pictures: three flags, one drum, four hats and two cups. Read the four headings on a chart with empty same-size cells. “Where does each picture go on this chart?” Draw links or place the cards and mark their cells. Key: flags 3, drums 1, hats 4, cups 2. Wrong: accepts 3, 1, 4, 1 as all ten after tallying (G). Gives a wide hat two cells because it looks big (C). Record ordinary mis-sorts descriptively.
  - Shape: File a mixed set of supplied icons onto a pictured chart. Practice sorts physical classroom objects and records tally marks.
- **E8.** Give a picture graph titled “Zippers,” with diamonds in the metal, cloth and paper rows, 3, 2 and 4 respectively. A separate strip shows one metal zipper beside one diamond. Circle one diamond in the cloth row. “What does just this diamond stand for?” On the circled graph mark or dictate the quantity it represents. Key: one cloth zipper. Wrong: two because of a thick outline (C). Half a zipper because a nearby icon looks wider (C).
  - Shape: Interpret one mark in a new category using the sample strip, without a printed answer legend. Practice reads row counts and draws repeated marks.
- **E9.** Give horizontal bars for folded maps, 11, and flat maps, 8. Use equal cells and a numbered axis from zero. Leave the bar ends unlabeled. “How many more maps are folded than flat?” Key: bracket the extra three cells or write 3 beside the bars, since 11 - 8 = 3. Wrong: 19 after joining both counts (E). 4 after reading the flat bar as seven cells from tick 1 to tick 8 (D).
  - Shape: Identify the unmatched span from finished bars. Practice builds bars and reads category counts, with arithmetic from counts tallied off the graph.
- **E10.** Read the pictured sign-count table: stop 0, arrow 4, bend 2, cross 7. Give blank axes with rows in the order cross, bend, stop, arrow. “What bar graph shows these counts?” Key: 7, 2, 0, 4 in axis order, equal cells from zero, empty labeled stop row. Wrong: starts cross at 1 and ends at 7, leaving six cells (D). Uses two cells per bend and one per arrow (C).
  - Shape: Convert a table to bars in a changed row order with a labeled row with zero responses. Practice converts finished picture rows and bars with the same labels in the same order.
- **E11.** Show tread-pattern bars for ridged 12, smooth 5 and bumpy 1. Point to the ridged and smooth bars. “What ‘how many more’ question could you leave for a friend about these two bars?” Save the child's question. “What answer goes on the back?” Key: how many more have ridges than are smooth? Seven. Accept equivalent wording. Wrong: 17 after joining both counts (E). Eight after reading the smooth bar as four units from tick 1 while reading the ridged bar from zero (D).
  - Shape: Write or dictate a question for a named pair on a new graph, then make its answer key. Practice uses objects or swaps questions with a partner who can ask for clarification.

## Day 7

**Understanding.** I can sort objects and show how many go in each group. Children hold objects, then their own tallies. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix mental signs with fresh pictured counts: caps 5, pencils 2, pads 1. Total 8, caps exceed pencils by 3.
- **5 min. Data repair.** Revisit the day 1 data skill with fresh marks. Match each object to one tally. Give separate cues for a total and a difference.
- **23 min. Sort.** Name a category as a group under one heading. Model one tally mark for each object. Use four labeled bins and reserve the exit card set. Before sorting, show how to take a survey. Ask which to sort first, pencils or rulers. Each child drops one unsigned slip. Count one vote per slip and keep the tally. Choose arithmetic pairs from the object tallies after checking the exit and assessment facts. If they coincide, use the prepared practice counts. Each pair sorts a tray of up to 20 objects. Show G and C on separate examples and check that the tallies account for the tray.
- **10 min. Check bins.** Partners audit one tally per object and exchange roles. Record each group's actual counts.
- **10 min. Category arithmetic and return.** Return E6. Five minutes for A, count on two from five to reach seven. Five minutes for J, cover five of seven to see two. Peers compute a total and a difference from their tray, staying within 20.
- **7 min. Exit.** E7. Collect the picture sorting chart.

## Day 8

**Understanding.** I can tell what a mark in a picture graph means. Children hold their symbol rows. Paper picture graph (light), show why, first 5 minutes of Model.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix both mental signs.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** Give five minutes each to E7's G and C moves.
- **15 min. Model.** Match equal stamps to erasers 4, brushes 5 and rulers 3. Put a key outside the counted rows and explain why the sample symbol there is outside the data. Cover the objects, then read the row counts.
- **13 min. Pairs.** Read completed rows, then make a picture graph from one tally. Each child draws and checks a row, then the pair swaps.
- **10 min. Category arithmetic.** Use the class categories, or the pictured brush and ruler counts for the fallback: together 8, difference 2.
- **7 min. Exit.** E8. Save each child's mark for the circled symbol.

## Day 9

**Understanding.** I can use two bars to tell how many more. Children hold and shade equal-cell grids. Paper bar graph (light), show why, first 5 minutes of Model.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix mental sums and differences.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Use the E8 C move with matching paper pieces.
- **18 min. Model.** Place 6 red and 4 blue paper folders in aligned rows, draw bars from zero, then tie the lengths to 6 + 4 = 10 and 6 - 4 = 2. Show D and E. Add a green label for the empty row and leave it blank. Cover the four folders. With the total ten still visible, ask how many are hidden. Have pairs ask one question of their own about these bars. Demonstrate two ways to split a fixed 12 using loose paper pieces: 10 and 2 or 8 and 4. Rejoin each to prepare for day 11.
- **15 min. Bars.** Partners color bars from a count table and read individual bar lengths. Each child makes a graph.
- **10 min. Category arithmetic.** Work from the class's written category counts, with 6 and 4 giving 10 together and a difference of 2 on the fallback sheets.
- **7 min. Exit.** E9. Collect one difference mark per child.

## Day 10

**Application.** I can show the same counts in two graphs. Partners hold picture rows and grids. Paper graph pair (light), practice with feedback, last 5 minutes of Pairs.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix both signs.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E9 D and E, five minutes each.
- **28 min. Pairs.** Each child draws picture rows for 2 red, 0 blue, 6 green and 3 yellow pencil stubs, then draws bars in the same order. Partners swap sheets and trace one category across both forms. Save both graphs on each sheet.
- **10 min. Category arithmetic.** Return to the class's tally. On the fallback stub counts, red and green make 8 and differ by 4.
- **7 min. Exit.** E10. Save the reordered bars and the labeled empty row.

## Day 11

**Application.** I can ask and answer questions about the counts in a graph. Children hold their bar graph and question cards. Paper bar graph (light), practice with feedback, first 5 minutes of Pairs.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mix mental sums and differences.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** E10 D and C, five minutes each.
- **12 min. Parts.** Revisit day 9's fixed whole. Use 16 folders and two open bins. Test proposed allocations of 13 and 3, or 12 and 4, by rejoining. Then each child chooses two parts of 16 and checks that they rejoin to make 16. Save mismatches for day 12.
- **26 min. Pairs.** For the bars paste 10, chalk 3, ink 2, sort supplied questions by total, part or difference. Keys for the full total, non-paste count and paste over ink are 15, 5 and 8. Check each answer with the bars. Each partner makes a question about a total, a missing part and a difference, one at a time. The other partner solves it and points to the matching bars. Switch jobs. Keep each child's three calculations.
- **7 min. Exit.** E11. Keep the authored question and key for day 12's return.

## 8. Practice that returns

The Grade 1 three-category tally display, matching pairs model and word difference are planned only. Revisit the day 1 meaning of difference by matching actual objects. Rebuild the tally and matching actions on classroom stock. Day 7 returns the day 1 retrieval and day 6 facts. Day 8 returns day 7's one-to-one tally. Day 9 carries a category count into a bar. Day 10 checks matching totals from day 9. Day 11 returns day 9's fixed whole. Days 7 to 10 keep five minutes of mixed facts and ten of category arithmetic. Day 11 uses known totals to derive differences. Keep the yearlong fluency target, with short untimed practice and private feedback.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use category for a group with the same label, tally for the count marks, scale for what each step counts, and key for what a symbol stands for. A picture graph shows counts with repeated pictures. A bar graph shows counts with the lengths of bars from zero. Use “One mark stands for one ___.” Offer large raised cells, pictured labels, pointing and a neutral scribe. Read or sign each cue with its printed label visible. Extend by writing another question that the same bars can answer.

## 10. Two rooms

At home, sort and tally paper scraps and make two graphs from the same counts. A projector can share the model. Each child works on a paper chart or draws one on scrap.
