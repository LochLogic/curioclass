## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do we measure, and how do measurements help us compare?"
**Hook, day 39:** Invented for practice: two pictures show the same path tiled by six short pieces or three longer pieces. Is either count wrong? Children mark pictures before discussing why the unit matters. Allow five minutes.

**Supporting questions:** What does a ruler count, day 41? Which tool fits, day 43? Why do larger units give fewer units, day 47? Why keep empty positions on a plot, day 53?

**Closing claim, day 57:** "I can choose tools, estimate, measure, compare and show measurements." T, E, P, C, D and L provide evidence.

**Teaching:** Model unit placement on day 40, intervals on day 41 and comparison unknowns on day 44. Step back during partner stories on day 50. On day 52, model nearest-mark readings for objects ending between marks before plotting. Practise on day 53 without fractions.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Gaps between successive measuring units still give a valid length. | Units do not cover the length. Repair: slide equal paper pieces until their edges touch. | Geometric Measurement, PDF page 12. |
| B | Measurement begins at the numeral 1. | The zero point is not understood. Repair: align the object's beginning to zero and trace the first unit. | Geometric Measurement, PDF page 12. |
| C | Count ruler marks rather than intervals. | Boundary marks are mistaken for length units. Repair: shade and count spaces between marks. | Geometric Measurement, PDF page 12. |
| D | A larger unit requires a larger count for the same length. | Unit size and count are conflated. Repair: cover one strip with two unit sizes and match endpoints. | Geometric Measurement, PDF page 13. |
| E | Empty whole-number values can be omitted from a plot scale. | Numerical positions are treated as categories. Repair: rebuild consecutive positions from a ruler. | Measurement Data, PDF page 9. |
| F | A repeated length needs only one plot mark. | A mark represents a distinct value rather than an observation. Repair: match one paper counter to each recorded measurement. | From memory. |
| G | A joined-length story is represented by subtraction. | Joining is not connected to a total. Repair: place labelled tapes end to end and bracket the whole. | From memory. |
| H | The longer object's length is its difference from the shorter object. | Whole and excess are confused. Repair: align drawn tapes and bracket only the excess. | From memory. |
| I | An inch and a centimeter name equal-sized units. | Unit names lack distinct benchmarks. Repair: overlay verified inch and centimeter strips. | From memory. |
| J | Measuring straight across a curve gives the curved path's length. | The measured route differs from the named route. Repair: lay a flexible paper tape along the path. | From memory. |
| K | Put the unknown at the result position regardless of its place in the story. | The unknown's position in the story is ignored. Repair: label before, change and after on a tape, placing the square before choosing the operation. | From memory. |
| L | Stop after the first calculation in a two-step story. | The intermediate quantity is mistaken for the requested quantity. Repair: draw both changes and circle the final question's quantity. | From memory. |
| M | Drop the new ten when composing ten ones in addition. | The composed unit is lost. Repair: regroup ten drawn ones as one ten and include it in the tens sum. | From memory. |
