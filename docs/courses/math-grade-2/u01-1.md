## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.OA.B.2.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 20 using mental strategies. The year-end target is to “know from memory” all sums of two one-digit numbers. Use count-on, make a ten, doubles and nearby doubles, and a known sum to find a difference.
- **2.NBT.B.5, days 5 and 6.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 100 is the full target. This section adds through 50 by combining tens and ones. Unit 2 extends both operations through 100.
- **Connections.** 2.OA.A.1 on days 3 and 6, locate the missing change before choosing a calculation in a one-step story within 20. 2.MD.D.10 on day 1, retrieve counts in categories for the work in section 1.2.
- **MP.7, day 3.** Use a known sum to derive either difference. The teacher page shows why a sum and its differences preserve the same two parts and total, why equals is “is the same quantity as,” and how ten ones can be regrouped without changing the total.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. Section 1.4 holds F and R. Reserve their full cases and fact sets. Print and say “invented for practice” on invented story stock. Each child marks the paper picture as the teacher reads aloud. Save each first response before another cue. A wrong numeral alone leaves the error letter open.

- **E1.** Give linked blank join and undo panels labeled 5 and 1 at the start of the join. “What fact chain joins these parts and undoes the join?” Key: 5 + 1 = 6 and either matching undo, such as 6 - 1 = 5. Accept changed addend or equality order. Wrong: 5 + 1 = 5 from counting the starting five as the step (A). 6 - 1 = 1 because the removed one is named (J).
  - Shape: Make a linked join-and-undo record on one picture. Practice uses individual spoken fact cards and a target strip without linked panels.
- **E2.** Put 7 + 1 = 8 above a part picture with separate blank addition and subtraction lines. Read the line labels “total first” and “take the smaller part away.” “What related equations fit this picture?” Key: 8 = 7 + 1 or 8 = 1 + 7, and 8 - 1 = 7 or 7 = 8 - 1. Wrong: Rejects the first line because it starts with 8 (I). 8 - 1 = 1 because the one taken away is named (J).
  - Shape: Author two specified relatives from one supplied sum. Keep paired equation-writing panels off practice and card stock. Practice slides and removes counters and explains aloud.
- **E3.** Show two torn labels in a pictured two-part record, 18 = 3 + □ and 18 - 3 = □. “What number goes under both tears?” Key: 15, since 3 + 15 = 18 and 18 - 3 = 15. Wrong: Says the record cannot start with 18 (I). Writes 3 because that part was removed (J).
  - Shape: Recover one label shared across two records. Practice flips one missing part at a time and has no dual torn-label record.
- **E4.** Give a pictured record labeled 10 + 3 = 13 with three margin moves read aloud: take one off 13, count all the dots, or count three with 9 as the first count. “Which move uses the known sum to find 9 + 3?” Have the child mark the move on the picture with the resulting count. Key: 13 - 1 = 12. Wrong: Counts every dot as the shortcut (B). Marks 11 and starts the three counts with 9 (A).
  - Shape: Select and show an adjustment from a supplied result. Practice compares children's own routes, with no supplied route menu.
- **E5.** Read three pictured envelope orders, 14, 16 and 13. “What grouped picture shows the envelopes needed?” Key: 43, shown as four tens and three ones or equivalent correct grouping, since 14 + 16 = 30 and 30 + 13 = 43. Wrong: 42, counting 30 as the first of thirteen advances (A). 44 after drawing 13 as ten plus four (G).
  - Shape: Group a total from three written orders. Practice uses two physical piles and keeps the three-order slip reserved.
- **E6.** Give a folded slip with three pictured card pockets. “What numbers complete this trail?” Reveal and read one card at a time: 4 + 7; 20 - 3; 15 - 9. Each child marks its pocket before the next is read. Key: 11, 17, 6. Wrong: 10 after counting 7 as the first of four advances (A). 9 for the last card because it names the removed part (J).
  - Shape: Complete an individual three-pocket mental record with sequential cues. Reserve its three facts. Practice uses a partner game and oral change stories.

- **5 min. Mixed facts.** Revisit both signs privately, using prior evidence to choose stock.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use part for a group within the whole, total for how many in the whole group, and change for what joins or leaves a group. A mental strategy is a way to figure out a fact in your head. Say just know when the answer comes to mind. Count on by starting with the amount you have and counting just the new ones. Doubles add two equal amounts. To make a ten, use enough of one part to bring the other to ten. A helpful pair is two amounts that are easy to combine. A related fact uses the same parts and total. Use “The other part is ___.” Read the short labels with the picture. Offer raised frames, wide paper spaces, pointing and a neutral scribe who records the child's mark without supplying a move. Extend by switching the equation sides and checking that it stays true.
