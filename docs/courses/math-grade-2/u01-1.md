Grade 2 Math. Unit 1, Adding, subtracting and working with data, [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 6.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 3. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.OA.B.2.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 20 using mental strategies. The year-end target is to “know from memory” all sums of two one-digit numbers. Use counting on, making ten, doubles, a nearby double and a known sum to find a difference.
- **2.NBT.B.5, days 5 and 6.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 100 is the full target. This section adds through 50 by combining tens and ones. Unit 2 extends both operations through 100.
- **Connections.** 2.OA.A.1 on days 3 and 6, locate the missing change before choosing a calculation in a one-step story within 20. 2.MD.D.10 on day 1, retrieve counts in categories for the work in section 1.2.
- **MP.7, day 3.** Use a known sum to derive either difference. The teacher page shows why a sum and its differences preserve the same two parts and total, why equals is “is the same quantity as,” and how ten ones can be regrouped without changing the total.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. Section 1.4 holds F and R. Reserve their full cases and fact sets. E1 to E4 and E6 use 2.OA.B.2. E5 uses 2.NBT.B.5. Print and say “invented for practice” on invented story stock. Each child marks the paper picture as the teacher reads aloud. Save each first response before another cue. A wrong numeral alone leaves the error letter open.

- **E1.** On the first panel put a flap labeled 1 over each of two single dots. Keep the dots covered. “What sum joins these groups?” Save 1 + 1 = 2. Uncover a second panel with the same total and a covered part, 2 = 1 + □. “What number is hiding?” Key: 1. Wrong: 1 for the sum after counting the starting dot as the added step (A). Rejects the second panel because the total is on the left (I). If a child needs to uncover the dots, record that help separately.
  - Shape: Use a recalled double to recover a covered part on a second panel. Partners practice single facts without a linked picture.
- **E2.** Give 6 + 0 = 6 and a picture with one group of six and one empty space. “What addition sentence puts the total by itself on the left?” Save 6 = 6 + 0 or 6 = 0 + 6. “What subtraction sentence takes all six away?” Key: 6 - 6 = 0 or 0 = 6 - 6. Wrong: rejects the addition because the total is on the left (I). Writes 6 - 6 = 6 because the six taken away stay (J).
  - Shape: Write related equations for an empty part. Practice slides and removes two nonempty groups and says their equations aloud.
- **E3.** Show two torn labels, 19 = 16 + □ and 19 - 16 = □. “What number goes under both tears?” Key: 3, since 16 + 3 = 19 and 19 - 16 = 3. Wrong: says the record cannot start with 19 (I). Writes 16 because the part taken away stays (J).
  - Shape: Recover one label shared across two records. Practice flips one missing part at a time.
- **E4.** Show 15 + 5 = 20. Read three choices for finding 14 + 5: take one off 20, count all the dots, or count five numbers with 14 first. “Which move uses the sum on the card?” The child marks one choice. Key: take one off 20, giving 19. Wrong: chooses to recount all the dots (B). Counts 14, 15, 16, 17, 18 as five advances (A).
  - Shape: Recognize an adjustment to a supplied fact. Practice compares two strategies children choose themselves.
- **E5.** Read three envelope orders, 14, 16 and 13. “How many envelopes do we need altogether?” Give space to answer with a drawing of tens and ones. Key: 43, shown as four tens and three ones or equivalent correct grouping, since 14 + 16 = 30 and 30 + 13 = 43. Wrong: 42, counting 30 as the first of thirteen advances (A). 44 after drawing 13 as ten plus four (G).
  - Shape: Group a total from three written orders. Practice uses two physical piles and keeps the three-order slip reserved.
- **E6.** Read one fact at a time. “What is 16 + 4?” “What is 19 - 1?” “What is 15 - 9?” Each child marks one empty pocket before the next cue. Keys: 20, 18, 6. Wrong: 19 for the sum after counting 16 as the first advance (A). Says 1 for the second fact because the removed one stays (J). Says 9 for the third because the removed nine stays (J).
  - Shape: Give three mental answers independently, then say each saved answer to a partner. Practice uses a roll-and-move game and change stories.

## Day 1

**Fluency.** I can use a fact I know. Children hold their own pictures and recall cards. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Hook.** Show dot groups of 4 and 2. Ask “How many altogether?” (6), then “How many dots are left without a partner?” (2). Call these the total and the difference. A sum is how many there are when groups are put together. Call a number we need to find the unknown. Point to its square. Read the equals sign as “is the same amount as” before retrieval.
- **15 min. Retrieval.** Use the nine unscored retrieval tasks in s01, one cue at a time. Keys in order: 8 and 5; 13 and 4; 9; 7; 3; 7; true; 4; 6 and 2. Hear six brief reasons while peers finish their marks. File misses for the named days.
- **5 min. Mixed facts.** Revisit both signs privately, using prior evidence to choose stock.
- **28 min. Partner practice.** Draw a prepared pair, make its sum or difference and check the target strip. Use 5 + 2 = 7 and the A/B contrasts from retrieval. A folded-paper puppet voices one invented mistake at a time. The teacher records just knows, quick strategy or counts when observed.
- **7 min. Exit.** E1. Save both marks.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use part for a group within the whole, total for how many in the whole group, and change for what joins or leaves a group. An equation says the amounts on the two sides are equal. A mental strategy is a way to figure out a fact in your head. Say just know when the answer comes to mind. Count on by starting with the amount you have and counting just the new ones. A sum tells how many there are altogether. Doubles add two equal amounts. To make a ten, use enough of one part to bring the other to ten. A helpful pair is two amounts that are easy to combine. A related fact uses the same parts and total. Use “The other part is ___.” Face the children. Read or sign each short cue while pointing to its printed label. Offer raised frames, wide paper spaces, pointing and a neutral scribe who records the child's mark without supplying a move. Extend by switching the equation sides and checking that it stays true.
