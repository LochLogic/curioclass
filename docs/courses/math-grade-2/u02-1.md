## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.NBT.B.5.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 100. Combine or remove like units without exchange and use the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- **2.NBT.B.9.** “Explain why” the strategies work on days 20 and 21. Each sum contains the same tens and ones regardless of which parts are added first. Subtracting a known part from a whole recovers the other part.
- **2.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction” to “solve” one-step stories on day 22. A square stands for the unknown start, change or result. Choose a written strategy from the quantities and their relationship. A standard algorithm is unnecessary.
- **Connections and practice.** 2.OA.B.2 returns in mental facts. On day 20, MP.3 compares reasons about the same sum. SL.2.1 gives children ways to agree or offer another method with a reason.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. The unit plan holds E, F and S1 to S14. Read each short cue aloud. Every child marks an individual paper picture. - **X20 (s02-X20), 2.NBT.B.5 and 2.NBT.B.9.** A screen has 98 holes. A cover hides 61. Give a plain screen outline with no loose holes marked. “How do tens and ones show how many holes are open?” Key: 9 tens less 6 tens and 8 ones less 1 one leave 3 tens and 7 ones, or 37. Wrong: 159 from joining the covered amount to all 98 (D). “37 because I subtracted” gives no value reason (H).
  - Shape: Reconstruct the visible part from a hidden count on a screen. Practice handles separate amounts or compares completed explanations. Keep the covered-screen picture out of both.
- **X21 (s02-X21), 2.NBT.B.5.** Sol's design needs 89 paper feathers. Sol has attached 56. Give an unfinished design picture with space for a calculation. “What calculation finds how many Sol still needs?” Key: 89 − 56 = 33. The teacher's check is 56 + 33 = 89. Wrong: 145 from adding the target and the known part (D). Marks 34 missing and accepts 56 + 34 as 89 (I).
  - Shape: Supply a calculation from a target and an unfinished design. Practice starts with a blank-addend equation. Keep unfinished collection pictures off practice sheets.
- **X22 (s02-X22), 2.OA.A.1.** At the end of rehearsal 45 masks hang on hooks. Earlier, the director took down 32. Give an end picture with the remaining amount labeled and space to mark the earlier amount. “What number sentence shows how many hung there at first?” Key: □ − 32 = 45, solved as 45 + 32 = 77. Accept an equivalent equation that identifies the original amount as unknown. Wrong: 13 from subtracting 32 from the remainder (D). Labels the start 76 while marking 45 and 32 as its parts (I).
  - Shape: Recover a start from an end-first account. Practice begins with visibly enacted forward changes. Keep the end-first mask record off all cards.
- **X23 (s02-X23), 2.NBT.B.5.** Show two sample page icons labeled 52 lines and 35 lines. “What calculation labels the two pages together?” Key: 50 + 30 = 80 and 2 + 5 = 7, for 87. Equivalent written strategies fit. Wrong: 17 from finding the difference (D). Writes “87, I added” without showing how the parts combine (H).
  - Shape: Produce a single combined label with written work. Practice checks supplied or partner calculations in mixed rounds. Keep combined page labels off the rounds.

## Day 22

**Application.** I can use a story to choose a calculation. The teacher holds the model, then children draw or label their own paper. Paper tape (light), show why, five minutes in Pairs.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Related facts within 20.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** X21 D and I, five minutes each. Use different pictured directions if an earlier missing-part prerequisite still needs repair.
- **15 min. Model.** Recall Day 3 Mika paper seals by name. Re-establish the relationships with a fresh case. Mika hangs 26 seals and adds 13, reaching 39. Act the events forward, and then act the removal from 39 to 26. Cover result, change or start one at a time. For the removal, 39 − 13 = 26, 39 − □ = 26 gives 13, and □ − 13 = 26 gives 39. For the arrival, 26 + 13 = 39, 26 + □ = 39 gives 13, and □ + 13 = 39 gives 26. Build each situation before covering a number.
- **25 min. Pairs.** In the first five minutes connect paper segments to the square. Then underline known quantities and mark a tape from a forward scene before calculating. Children draw any comparison tapes independently. Hear positions 7 to 12, adjusted for missed turns.

## 9. Supports and extension

Point to the complete group for **total** and to the square for the **unknown**, the amount still to find. An **equation** shows equal quantities on both sides. A **strategy** is your way to find the answer. Say it while pointing to the work. Rehearse “___ and ___ make ___” with marks for the parts. Read or sign cues, offer tactile marks or large blanks, and record scribing or other help. Keep only short labels on the page. Extend day 23 by comparing two ways to check a difference.
