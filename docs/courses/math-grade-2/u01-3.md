- **E12.** Show two bars from zero, ten wide notebook covers and ten narrow ones. “How many extra wide covers are there?” Let each child mark the matching lengths. Save the drawing. “What number sentence fits your picture?” Key: all cells match, so 10 - 10 = 0. Wrong: 20 after joining the bars (E). One after counting the narrow bar from tick 1 to 10 as nine cells while reading the wide bar from zero (D).
  - Shape: Show a difference when all the cells match. Practice uses unequal bars and marks off their shared portion.
- **E13.** Hold a horizontal bar graph for a teacher's spoken report. State that the cone bar ends at 16 and the hoop bar at 5, both from zero. Give blank paper with category icons and a space for a tape sketch. “The cone bar ends at 16. The hoop bar ends at 5. Both start at zero. What two strips show where the missing extra cones belong?” Key: common start, cone tape 16, hoop tape 5, square on the extra cone span. The teacher can confirm the span is 11 since 5 + 11 = 16. Wrong: labels a joined 21-unit run as the extra (E). Writes 16 - 5 = 11 on the whole cone tape because those are the two numbers in the report, without finding the extra part (F).
  - Shape: Reconstruct and mark a diagram from a spoken graph report. Practice traces existing lengths and then labels supplied strips. Reserve the graph report and open sketch for this exit.
- **E14.** Use two still pictures, Kit's spools and Paz's spools. “Kit has 2 more spools than Paz. Kit has 11. Where is Paz's amount on the tape diagram that fits?” Read all three sets of labels. A: Kit's 11 spans Paz's square plus 2. B: Paz's square spans Kit's 11 plus 2. C: Kit's 11 spans Paz's 2 plus a square. Key: mark Paz's full shorter tape on A, worth 9 since 11 - 2 = 9. A correct card letter by itself supplies no quantity mark. Wrong: B, naming Paz as larger (K). C, calculating 11 - 2 to fill the gap and labeling Paz's whole strip 2 without checking what is missing (F).
  - Shape: Find and mark the named missing quantity on one of three labeled diagrams. Practice uncovers one amount on a single shared diagram.
- **E15.** Picture a sleeve with its starting contents hidden. Read “Ari loses two tickets. Fifteen tickets stay in the sleeve. How many tickets were there at first?” Give blank space for the child to answer on a labeled tape. Key: the whole is 17, with parts 2 lost and 15 left, so □ - 2 = 15. Wrong: 13 from subtracting the two given numbers without locating the start (F). Sixteen after counting 15 as the first of two added steps (A).
  - Shape: Reconstruct the starting whole on a tape from a loss report. Practice acts out a change with visible pieces and tests a supplied equation.
- **E16.** Give blank paper and separate name labels. “Ivo made 12 folds. That is 5 fewer than Bea made. Who made more folds?” Save Bea. “How many folds did Bea make?” Key: 17, since 12 + 5 = 17. Children may draw their own strips. Wrong: names Bea but gives 7 because fewer meant subtract (H). Names Ivo as having more because the extra five were assigned to Ivo (K).
  - Shape: Identify the larger owner before finding the missing amount, without supplied lengths. Practice compares explanations with both amounts visible.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Children start with bars and link the extra cells to subtraction on day 12. On day 13 align and turn strips while their cells are visible. Tie both tapes and the gap to an equation, then withdraw the supplied graph. On days 14 and 15 children place labels and the square. Day 16 withdraws supplied practice tapes. Children may draw their own.

- **Day 12.** Ideal: two equally divided bars that align at zero. Paper bars fit. Hand-rule cells on scrap paper in place of a graph master.
- **Days 13 to 15.** Ideal: movable strips whose lengths retain meaning when the axis is removed. Paper tapes fit. Draw parallel strips or use folded scrap in place of prepared pieces. Use large paper squares for any unit counters. Digital tools: None.

## 9. Supports and extension

Compare means look for what is alike and what differs. A tape diagram uses strips whose lengths show amounts. In these comparisons the difference is how many are left unmatched. The unknown is the amount to find. Say larger, fewer and extra while pointing to the relevant lengths. Use “___ has the longer tape because ___.” Give tactile strips, wide label spaces and a neutral scribe. Read or sign every cue with the printed labels visible. Extend by stating the same difference from the other person's perspective.

## 10. Two rooms
