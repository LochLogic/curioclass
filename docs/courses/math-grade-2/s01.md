## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can a picture help us add, subtract and compare?"

**Hook, day 1:** Show two dot groups, 4 and 2. Ask how the picture helps find their total and difference. Children annotate paper copies before sharing. This five-minute original task opens the compelling question.

**Supporting questions:** How does a sum reveal a difference, day 3? How can two graphs show the same counts, day 10? Which quantity is missing, day 14? Why can "fewer" require addition, day 16?

**Closing claim, day 19:** "I can use pictures and equations to combine, separate and compare quantities." The graph investigation, facts record and story tasks below supply evidence.

**Teaching:** Model matching quantities to equations on days 2 and 9. Model add-to and take-from stories with unknown changes on day 3; practice both on day 6. Model two unknown parts on day 11; practice on day 15. Replace repeated practice. On day 13, align bars before removing the grid. On days 14 to 16, name who has more before choosing operations and drawing independently.

- **~2.OA.A.1, parts by verb:** one-step situations within 20 here; two-step work and numbers through 100 follow in unit 2.

**Practices:** MP.7, day 3: derive subtraction equations from a known sum and explain the shared parts and total. MP.4, day 13: build a tape diagram from two bars, label every length and interpret the unknown. Both return in day 19 evidence.

**Fluency:** 2.OA.B.2 and 2.NBT.B.5 set yearlong targets. Use five-minute spaced, mixed practice daily, partner explanation and feedback without time pressure. Days 1, 4, 6 and 17 rehearse familiar methods. Record just knows, quick strategy or counts; objects support teaching, never fluency evidence.

**Limits:** Here, 2.NBT.B.5 contributes addition through 50 on days 5 and 6; unit 2 completes within-100 addition and subtraction. The 2.OA.A.1 contribution is one-step situations within 20, including all unknown positions, start unknown and opposite-language comparisons. Two-step work and larger numbers follow in unit 2. No standard algorithm, scaled graphs or year-end memory verdict is required here.

**Carried:** Tie ten-frames to equations on day 2, use two frames on day 3 and withdraw supplied frames on day 4. Tie bars to subtraction on day 12; withdraw the supplied graph on day 13 as children make tapes. Tie tapes to equations that day; fade supplied tapes by day 16. Student drawings remain available. Retain both graph forms for data tasks.

Balance follows the frame: ten understanding, four fluency and four application days, plus one assessment day. Major work receives 720 of 960 instructional minutes, exactly 75 percent; days 17 to 19 are excluded. Days 7 to 10 retain fifteen arithmetic minutes each. Twelve of sixteen instructional lessons primarily address major work, also 75 percent.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A counting-on answer is one too small because the starting number was counted as a step. | The first addend is not held as a complete quantity. Repair: mark only added counts. | From memory. |
| B | Counts every object to find each fact. | Mental fluency is still developing. Repair: reconstruct one known fact, then derive a nearby fact mentally. | From memory. |
| C | Uses unequal graph units. | Equal counts are not represented by equal lengths. Repair: align one symbol or grid cell with each response. | From memory. |
| D | Starts bar lengths at 1 instead of 0. | A tick label is being counted as an interval. Repair: trace the first unit from 0 to 1. | From memory. |
| E | Adds the two counts when asked for their difference. | Comparison is treated as a combined total. Repair: match the shared portion and circle only the extra. | From memory. |
| F | Calculates directly with the given numbers without locating the unknown in the situation. | The missing quantity's role is not identified. Repair: draw the action and place the unknown before calculating. | From memory. |
| G | The proposed parts do not sum to the given total. | Decomposition is not checked against the whole. Repair: join the proposed parts and compare their sum with the total. | From memory. |
| H | Chooses an operation from the word more or fewer alone. | Comparative language replaces reasoning about quantities. Repair: label the larger quantity before selecting an operation. | From memory. |
| I | Rejects an equation solely because its total is on the left. | Equality is treated as an instruction to compute. Repair: represent the same quantity on both sides. | From memory. |
| K | Reverses which person has the larger quantity. | The comparison statement is attached to the wrong person. Repair: name and label both people before drawing the extra portion. | From memory. |
| J | Subtracting one known part leaves that same part. | The complementary part is not distinguished. Repair: cover the named part in a two-part drawing and name what remains. | From memory. |

A numeral alone does not establish an error letter. Check the child's method; record an unexplained arithmetic error separately until evidence identifies its cause.
