## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How is a number line like a ruler?"
**Hook, day 58:** Compare a ruler with an unlabelled line of equal intervals. What could the line tell us? Children mark matching features on paper. Allow five minutes.
**Supporting questions:** What do marks and spaces count, day 59? How do reference points locate numbers, day 60? Can subtraction travel right, day 66? How does a story match its diagrams, day 70?
**Closing claim, day 73:** "I can show numbers as lengths from zero and show sums and differences with jumps." Records R, N and D below establish each part.
**Teaching:** Model equal intervals on day 58 and arrow conventions on day 64. Compare counting back and finding a missing addend on day 66. Step back during day 70's matching task; ask what each number means.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Counting the starting mark as the first travelled unit. | Endpoints are being counted as lengths. Repair: cover each travelled interval with one equal strip. | Geometric Measurement, PDF pages 12 to 14. |
| B | Consecutive whole numbers can have unequal intervals. | Number order is being used without a fixed unit. Repair: rebuild consecutive positions with one repeated paper unit. | Geometric Measurement, PDF page 14. |
| C | Every subtraction strategy must move left. | Subtraction as a missing addend is unavailable. Repair: connect a rightward distance to its missing-addend equation. | Base lesson 2.4.9 narrative. |
| D | The subtraction answer is the larger endpoint. | A position is being reported instead of a difference. Repair: bracket the distance between the known endpoints. | From memory. |
| E | Every addition jump starts at zero. | The initial addend is omitted. Repair: mark the initial quantity before drawing the added distance. | From memory. |
| F | Jumping left makes an addition equation true. | The arrow's direction is disconnected from the operation. Repair: trace a rightward increase beside its addition equation. | From memory. |
| G | A tens jump changes the ones digit. | The size of ten is confused with one. Repair: compare one ten-length jump with ten unit intervals. | From memory. |
| H | Copies a given quantity into the unknown. | Known and unknown quantities are not distinguished. Repair: cover the unknown, label the known parts and test the proposed answer in the story. | From memory. |
| I | Stops after the first step of a two-step story. | The second change is omitted. Repair: draw both changes in order before finding the final amount. | From memory. |
