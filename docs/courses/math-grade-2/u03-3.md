Grade 2 Math. Unit 3, Measuring length, [unit plan](s03.md). **Days 49 to 53.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 1, application 2. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.B.5 and 2.OA.A.1.** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” same-unit stories. In one- and two-step cases, mark the unknown at its story position. Carry an intermediate length into the next action. Revisit joining, separating, parts and comparisons with different unknown positions on days 49 and 50.
- **2.MD.A.1, 2.MD.A.3 and 2.MD.A.4.** “Measure” with a suitable tool, “Estimate” before touching it and “Measure to determine how much longer” on day 51. Compare lengths in the same unit.
- **2.NBT.B.5.** “Fluently add and subtract within 100” on day 51 through place value or an inverse relationship. Keep the new ten after composing. No standard algorithm is required.
- **2.MD.D.9.** “Generate measurement data” by measuring several objects to the nearest whole unit and “Show the measurements by making a line plot.” MP.6 on days 52 and 53: choose the nearer mark, keep consecutive equal whole-number spaces and place one X per reading. Explain that the number is the length and the stack is the count of readings. Use ends clearly closer to one mark and no fractional labels.

- **X49 (s03-X49), 2.MD.B.5.** A 92 cm stage border has lost a 23 cm end. Give two aligned outlines, one for the original border and one for the broken piece. Show unnumbered centimeter ticks on the original and room to draw the surviving part. “What length of border is still hanging?” Key: 92 − 23 = □ or 23 + □ = 92, with □ = 69 cm. Bracket the surviving span. Brackets the original 92 cm whole as the excess beyond 23 (H). Marks 70 after counting the ends and all unit boundaries of the remaining span (C).
  - Shape: Reconstruct a remainder from damage on a fresh picture. Practice labels a known before-and-after pair and locates the cut.
- **X50 (s03-X50), 2.MD.B.5 and 2.OA.A.1.** A sliding pointer reaches 91 cm. It slides in 33 cm and then out 38 cm. Give a blank pointer picture and work space. “How far does the pointer reach now?” Key: 91 − 33 = 58, then 58 + 38 = □, with □ = 96 cm. Show both actions on a self-drawn tape. Stops at 58 (L). Writes 86 after dropping the composed ten (M).
  - Shape: Produce a whole two-action plan with a reversal on a blank picture. Practice sorts textile cards and sketches their first action. Model two removals.
- **X51 (s03-X51-estimate, then s03-X51), 2.MD.A.1, 2.MD.A.3, 2.MD.A.4 and 2.NBT.B.5.** A display recess is 85 cm long. Give each child an unmarked 34 cm foam or paper insert, a checked 5 cm reference, a covered 40 cm ruler and the recess picture. Explain that the recess is the space where the insert will go. First cue, s03-X51-estimate: “About how long is the insert in centimeters?” Key: about 35 cm, using about seven spans of the 5 cm reference. Save the estimate before uncovering the ruler. Leaves gaps between five reference copies for about 25 cm (A). Counts eight boundary marks around seven spans for about 40 cm (C). Second cue, s03-X51: “How much empty space will be left beside the insert?” Key: 51 cm. The insert measures 34 cm. 85 − 34 = 51. Labels the whole opening 85 (H). Counts 35 boundary marks on the insert and gets 85 − 35 = 50 cm (C).
  - Shape: Find the empty length using a new measurement and the size of the opening. Practice calculates from completed pairs of readings.
- **X52 (s03-X52), 2.MD.D.9.** Show a line plot of tassel lengths in centimeters. Consecutive scale 30 through 33. One X at 30, three at 32 and one at 33. Circle the top X at 32. “What does this circled X tell us about a tassel?” Key: trace to 32 cm and indicate one tassel measured about 32 cm. Claims the one X stands for all three readings at that position (F). Labels it 31 cm after skipping the empty position when counting from 30 (E).
  - Shape: Decode one selected mark in an unfamiliar plot. Practice builds stacks and matches marks to readings.
- **X53 (s03-X53), 2.MD.D.9.** Wicks measure 64, 64, 67 and 68 cm. Show three scales with equally spaced ticks: A 64, 65, 66, 67, 68. B 64, 67, 68. C 64, 66, 67, 68. “Which scale gives every centimeter its own place?” Key: A. There are three equal steps from 64 to 67. Circling or pointing to A answers the question. B skips two empty positions (E). C skips one (E).
  - Shape: Evaluate unfamiliar scales whose ticks are equally spaced. Practice constructs its own scale and places marks.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Return to the link made on day 44 between the unit 2 tapes and unknown equations. Withdraw supplied tapes on 50 and allow self-drawn models. On 52 show categorical bars briefly and withdraw them as soon as the ruler guides the plot. Tie each X above a number to one actual reading. Withdraw supplied scales on 53. Preserve the actual data and let children draw their own scales.

**Ideal for 49 showing why:** a paper before-and-after tape with its parts visible. Fits because an unknown can be marked on the cut. **Ideal for 52 to 53:** a checked ruler, movable paper Xs and a blank grid. Fits because children can transfer each reading to an even scale. This section has no key tool or digital spec.

Use cut paper for textile strips and Xs, plain firm strips for classroom objects, and checked actual-size paper rulers. Adults prepare pieces and keep long objects supported. Replace misprinted scales and halfway endpoints before class. Keep benchmark aids separate from the evidence papers.

- **15 min. Worked review.** A pictured textile was 98 cm before a single maker trimmed it. It measures 68 cm now. Label start, removed piece and remainder on the tape. Mark 98 − □ = 68. The missing amount is 30 cm. Place the square before solving.
- **20 min. Pairs.** Mark start, cut and remainder in supplied textile diagrams. Uncover one quantity to check after choosing a relation. Include all three take-from unknown positions. Hear positions 1 to 6.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X49.
- **Shared shape.** Shape: Reconstruct a remainder from damage on a fresh picture. Practice labels a known before-and-after pair and locates the cut.

## Day 50

## Day 52

**Understanding.** I can put a measurement on a line plot. Teacher provides a scale and children hold measured slips. Paper plot (light), show why, five minutes in Plot. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Written within-100 strategy.
- **5 min. Setup.** Verify ends and keep answers covered.
- **20 min. Measure and repair.** In the first ten minutes use two five-minute X51 rounds. Children in either round predict and measure fresh edges: bracket the excess for H or trace each centimeter space for C. Return s03-X51-estimate A or C privately on a parallel pictured card in either round. Peers estimate and measure alongside them. Before children measure the new strips, align the start of a strip at zero and point to where its other end falls between two marks. Name the nearer mark. Show one end nearer the lower mark and one nearer the higher mark. Children then estimate and measure three strips. Ends just short of 40, just past 40 and just short of 42 give readings 40, 40 and 42 cm. Give checked tapes long enough for all spans.
- **25 min. Plot.** Recall a categorical bar graph with a small unrelated example, then put it away. Transfer ticks to a centimeter scale from 40 through 42. Move one paper mark from each measured slip to its place and stack repeats. Use a title and unit. Hear positions 19 to 24.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X52. This completes the 30 minutes for plots.
- **Shared shape.** Shape: Decode one selected mark in an unfamiliar plot. Practice builds stacks and matches marks to readings.

## Day 53

**Understanding.** I can draw a scale that leaves room for every measured length. Children keep their own scales. Paper plot (light), practice with feedback, five minutes in Plot and repair. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mixed mental facts.
- **5 min. Setup.** Remove supplied scales and give blank grids.
- **20 min. Measure.** Predict, then check new between-mark strips. Set their ends nearer 44, 46 and 46 cm, respectively. Each child measures and labels a list.
- **25 min. Plot and repair.** First ten address X52 F and E. For the new readings draw equal steps from 44 through 46. Stack one X at 44 and two at 46, leave 45 empty and add a title and centimeter label. Compare with a scale that has extra empty ticks and still preserves equal intervals. Hear positions 25 to 30 and keep first work.

## 9. Supports and extension

Say **measurement** for one reading, **data** for the collected readings and **line plot** for a row of number positions with one mark per reading. Say “One mark means ___.” Use raised ticks, a large grid, paper marks that can be felt and a neutral scribe. Read one cue aloud at a time and keep solo reading to labels. Extend by comparing two consecutive scales with different starting points that display the same readings.
