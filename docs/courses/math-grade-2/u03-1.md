## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.A.1.** “Measure” with touching, equal pieces, then a centimeter ruler and a meter stick. MP.6 on day 41: construct a ruler by marking the joints, then show the distance from zero. MP.5 on day 43: choose tools for a comb and a long tabletop strip.
- **2.MD.A.3.** “Estimate” in centimeters and meters before checking. A benchmark is a known length you can picture along the object.
- **2.MD.A.2.** “Measure” a fixed span in two unit sizes and “describe” why the larger unit takes fewer copies. Keep the endpoints fixed.
- **2.MD.A.4.** “Measure to determine how much longer” on days 40 and 41. Align the starts and label the extra length in centimeters. On day 39, connect the extra span to a unit 2 unknown equation, 2.OA.A.1.
- Use whole units. Explain how two marks bound each interval, why zero marks the start even when the ruler has a margin, and how a common unit makes two people's counts comparable.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- Equal loose pieces become centimeter units on day 40. On day 41 mark their joints on paper, tie the space count to the reading and remove the cubes. Children keep the ruler. Day 42 benchmark pictures carry a known span into an estimate before the ruler comes out.
- **Ideal for showing why on 41:** loose units and a blank strip that shows each joint. Fits on paper. **Ideal for 43 tool choice:** a checked ruler and a meter stick that can lie beside the objects. Fits with verified paper measures. The paper path has no key tool or digital spec.
- Use cut paper for tiles and cubes, verified actual-size paper rulers and a verified full-length paper meter. Substitute plain cardboard objects or strips with checked lengths. Adults cut the pieces and set long objects flat where aisles stay clear. Replace a bad print scale with a checked strip and date any deferred standard-unit evidence. Measure objects.

## 9. Supports and extension

Say and show **unit**, one piece size used to cover the length. A **centimeter** is the length of one edge of a checked centimeter cube. Model its **cm** label on day 40. A **ruler** marks out equal spaces for measuring. A **meter** is the length of the checked long reference. Use **m** on day 43. Have children say “My unit is ___” while pointing. Point to the extra span for “how much longer.” Provide enlarged diagrams, tactile edges and signed or pointed reasons. Keep real measures at their verified size. Read every cue aloud and keep solo reading to short labels. Extend by changing a practice ruler's margin and comparing readings from the same zero.
