## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do equal parts help us read clocks and count money?"
**Hook, day 93:** In five minutes, sort rotated paper triangles and nontriangles. Ask what stays true when a shape turns. Introduce the compelling question; save a paper circle for the clock connection. Adapt the first source lesson's shape sort.
**Questions:** Which attributes decide a name, day 96? Must equal shares look alike, day 99? How do five-minute groups locate the hands, day 102? Why are four quarters one dollar, day 106?
**Closing claim:** Equal shares name wholes; clock groups count minutes, while coins have assigned values. Show this through shape record S, share record P, clock record C and money tasks M1 and M2, completed on day 110.
**Teaching:** Model attributes before drawing; compare equal and unequal partitions. Demonstrate both hands moving, then release matching to partners. Model coin values before totals. Require explanations of units when comparing strategies.
