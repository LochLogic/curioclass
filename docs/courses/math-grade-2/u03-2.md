Grade 2 Math. Unit 3, Measuring length, [unit plan](s03.md). **Days 44 to 48.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1, application 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.B.5.** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” same-unit stories. Label the known lengths and put □ at the missing length in a tape diagram and equation.
- **2.MD.A.4.** “Measure to determine how much longer” by aligning starts and measuring the extra span. On day 48, find a span between two scale readings. Endpoint readings name positions, and their difference names the length.
- **2.MD.A.1 and 2.MD.A.3.** “Measure” with suitable tools and “Estimate” from a known length before checking. MP.5 on day 46: trace a curved rim with flexible tape after choosing a tool for a straight edge. MP.6 on day 48: show which spaces give the distance when zero is missing.
- **2.MD.A.2.** “Measure” the same span twice and “describe” why feet give fewer units than inches. Match endpoints. Teach whole units and keep conversions for later.
- **2.NBT.B.5.** “Fluently add and subtract within 100” on day 45 using place value, properties or related facts. This supports 2.OA.A.1 in the day 44 comparison. Show how the same picture can support an unknown addition equation or a subtraction equation. Use strategies.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment item owned.** C for 2.MD.A.2, administered on day 56 and revised on day 57 ([unit field 5](s03.md)). Read “Why do both counts fit this same strip?” from the comparison master.

- Shape: Measure an untouched straight paper strip independently in each of two units after estimating, then account for both counts on one picture. Keep this strip and the independent two-count record out of practice and yardstick construction.

Read each cue aloud. Children mark their own paper pictures. Save the first response, support and any dated revision. Say and print “invented for practice” on the scenes. Mark shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Attach a letter when the work makes the reasoning visible.

- **X44 (s03-X44), 2.MD.B.5 and 2.MD.A.4.** Two blinds are 73 cm and 42 cm long with tops aligned. Give top-aligned outlines with unnumbered centimeter ticks along the gap and space for a tape diagram. “What length of fringe would make the blinds match?” Key: 42 + □ = 73 or 73 − 42 = □, with □ = 31 cm. Bracket the missing span. Brackets the 73 cm whole (H). Counts both endpoints and the interior boundary marks of the gap for 32 cm (C).
  - Shape: Draw the extension needed to match an aligned object. Practice uses a known excess to recover a whole length, or traces a supplied excess.
- **X45 (s03-X45), 2.MD.A.1, 2.MD.A.3 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Give an unmarked rail extension secretly 28 cm, an installed rail pictured with a 59 cm label, a known 10 cm reference, an estimate box and a covered ruler. Save the extension forecast before uncovering the ruler. “How long will the rail be with its extension?” Key: a defensible estimate such as about 30 cm from three spans, then 28 cm from the tool and 59 + 28 = 87 cm on the joined picture. Subtracts and gets 31 (G). Drops the composed ten and gets 77 (M).
  - Shape: Use a newly measured input in an installation decision. Centers rehearse estimating, measuring and calculating on separate cards.
- **X46 (s03-X46), 2.MD.A.3.** A paper model flute comes in two pieces stored side by side in a case. Show their full-size impressions, secretly 4 and 9 inches long, with a verified inch-wide latch. Show how the two ends meet without overlap. “About how many inches long is the joined flute?” Key: about 13 inches, using the inch reference along each piece and combining those spans. Accept defensible estimates. Uses centimeter spaces as inches and gets about 33 inches (I). Shows about 10 copies separated by gaps (A).
  - Shape: Estimate a joined length from two separate impressions and one inch reference. Withhold the ruler. Practice estimates and measures actual straight objects.
- **X47 (s03-X47), 2.MD.A.2.** Give an unmarked ramp picture and two tally records made from the same endpoints, one in inches and one in feet. Draw seven tallies on one and 84 on the other, grouped for easy comparison. Leave the unit labels blank. “How can you tell which record counts inches?” Key: trace short and long spans on the ramp to show that each inch covers less, so more fit in the same length. Assigns the larger count to feet because feet are bigger (D). Explains fewer feet by leaving gaps (A).
  - Shape: Explain different counts on an anonymous pair of records. Practice builds a foot reference and compares known units with labels visible.
- **X48 (s03-X48), 2.MD.A.1 and 2.MD.A.4.** Both panels begin at zero under a cover, with only the right part of the ruler exposed. Their right tips meet 33 and 76 cm. Give a picture with equal spaces on the ruler. “How long is the part that sticks out?” Key: mark the exposed span, 76 − 33 = 43 cm. Brackets the entire longer panel and labels the gap 76 (H). Counts 44 boundary lines (C).
  - Shape: Recover only the exposed part of overlapping objects from two readings. Practice uses one object and a torn ruler to compare strategies.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Tie unit 2 paper tapes to length equations on day 44 and keep them available through day 49. Supplied tapes leave on day 50. Day 48 links the two ruler readings to a written difference or missing addend. Remove the temporary loose tracing strip when children can mark just the spaces.

**Ideal for showing why on 44:** aligned paper tapes with a removable label. Fits because the square can name the uncovered quantity. **Ideal for 46 to 48:** checked inch and foot references, a flexible tape and a partial scale with equal spaces. Paper equivalents fit. This print section has no key tool or digital spec.

Use checked paper rulers, a verified paper meter, a verified foot strip and a verified paper yardstick with inch and foot marks. A narrow checked paper tape can bend around a route. Choose plain cardboard shapes or strips if the named objects are unavailable. Keep long materials supported flat. Replace a bad scale before scoring.

## Day 44

**Application.** I can draw a length comparison and find its missing part. Teacher supplies tapes and children label them. Paper tape (light), show why, five minutes in Worked review. Digital tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Written within-100 strategy.
- **5 min. Setup.** Lay out aligned blanks.
- **10 min. Repair.** X43 B and C, five minutes each as needed.
- **15 min. Worked review.** Mika's invented pennant rod is 53 cm and is 36 cm shorter than a second rod. Align tails at the common start and put □ on the second rod's full length. Write 53 + 36 = □, giving 89 cm. Check the supplied excess by following its brackets. Reread “shorter” while pointing to each rod.
- **20 min. Pairs.** Measure rods and use the known excess to recover the covered longer or shorter label. Uncover and check it. Work from screened rod and excess cards. Hear positions 1 to 6.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X44.
- **Shared shape.** Shape: Draw the extension needed to match an aligned object. Practice uses a known excess to recover a whole length, or traces a supplied excess.

## Day 47

**Understanding.** I can tell why one length takes more inches than feet. Partners start with different units, then switch tools. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mixed mental facts.
- **5 min. Setup.** Verify foot strips and lay tools flat.
- **10 min. Repair.** X46 I and A. If needed offer J at a parallel pictured repair station, one move per child per round.
- **15 min. Model.** Lay twelve checked inches along a foot and point to the common ends. Read foot marks on the yardstick. Show a yard as the full span of the yardstick and rehearse choosing a tool that fits an edge.
- **20 min. Pairs.** Give each pair a flat five-foot paper border and both marked tools. Each child predicts and measures the border using the yardstick's foot marks, then the inch tape, or in reverse order. Switch after six minutes. Compare the two readings, 5 feet and 60 inches, and explain how both reach the same end. Hear positions 19 to 24. Keep both readings as practice. C uses a separate unseen strip on day 56.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X47.
- **Shared shape.** Shape: Explain different counts on an anonymous pair of records. Practice builds a foot reference and compares known units with labels visible.

## Day 48

**Understanding.** I can find length when the start is away from zero. Children hold torn-scale drawings and trace their intervals. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Written within-100 strategy.
- **5 min. Setup.** Cover zero on a verified ruler or paper scale.
- **10 min. Repair.** X47 D and A, five minutes each as needed.
- **15 min. Model.** Between 54 and 74 on a centimeter tape, the covered spaces total 20 cm. Compare 74 − 54 = 20 and 54 + 20 = 74. Count only the spaces the object spans.
- **20 min. Pairs.** Predict the object's length from a known span before uncovering the torn ruler. Slide the ruler along the object and compare readings. Show why shifting both endpoint readings equally leaves the same distance. Hear positions 25 to 30.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X48.
- **Shared shape.** Shape: Recover only the exposed part of overlapping objects from two readings. Practice uses one object and a torn ruler to compare strategies.

## 9. Supports and extension

Show **inch** and **foot** on checked pieces, and **yard** on the whole yardstick. Model **in** on day 46 and **ft** on day 47. Point to **difference**, the extra span after the starts line up. Label the **missing length** on the uncovered part and the **total length** across the whole. Say “The unit is larger, so ___.” Read aloud. Keep print labels short and offer tactile intervals, enlarged pictures and a neutral scribe. Extend by shifting both ends of a drawn ruler span while preserving its length.
