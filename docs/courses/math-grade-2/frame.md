# Course frame: Grade 2 Math

Course id math-k8, grade 2. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9, Illustrative Mathematics Grade 2's nine units in its own order, with no unit moved: the base text's
  order teaches the ruler before the number line and the number line before three-digit numbers are placed on it,
  which the progression requires (reasons in the Units table).
- **Days.** 160: 154 in units (each unit's days include one end-of-unit assessment day), 4 for course-level checks
  (2 after unit 5, 2 after unit 9) and 2 respond days (one after each check). Math is daily: one lesson of 60
  minutes a day, five days a week, 32 weeks, the base text's own lesson length (warm-up, activities, lesson
  synthesis and exit ticket). The base text's 146 lessons, 9 assessment days and 6 check and respond days already
  exceed 160, so seven optional center and puzzle lessons are cut for the day budget (139 kept; the base text marks
  them optional, and their games move into warm-ups), and six Curio Class lessons go where the load is heaviest:
  compare problems with opposite language (unit 1); subtraction with and without a decomposed ten side by side, and
  two-step problems written for a partner (unit 2); counting across hundreds on the number line (unit 5); subtracting
  across a zero, and comparing two written methods (unit 7). 139 kept, 6 added and 9 assessment days make the 154.
  The base text budgets two assessment days a unit; Curio Class keeps one, because its own end-of-unit assessment
  (the base text's is behind a login) is one period of short items, and the one-to-one evidence (facts watched in a
  game, an explanation said aloud) is gathered during the unit's center days and lessons. Each unit plan names what is
  gathered on the assessment day, what is gathered in the lessons before it, the observation minutes and how every
  child is reached, and that every owned standard is covered. If a unit's evidence cannot fit, the extra day goes to
  Cory before the scope and sequence fixes the year; no standard is cut to keep the total.
- **Major work.** A provisional allocation, with the 75 percent line for K to 2 (docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md), counted as
  the evidence guide counts it: major clusters plus supporting work that practises them. The supporting units
  practise major work on purpose: money problems add within 100 (2.NBT.B.5, B.6), time skip-counts by 5s
  (2.NBT.A.2), odd and even and arrays use doubles and repeated addition (2.OA.B.2), line plots use rulers and
  subtraction. By days, about 150 of 160 (about 94 percent): only the geometry lessons (2.G, additional: 6.1 to 6.4,
  6.6 to 6.9 and 8.12) and a share of unit 6's assessment day stay out; on the stricter count of major clusters
  alone, 121 of 160 (75.6 percent) sit outside units 6 and 8 and the data and line plot lessons of units 1 and 3. By
  units, 7 of 9 are major work. By base lessons (a tag inventory, not a time count), about 121 of 139 kept lessons
  carry a major-cluster standard and about 131 a major or supporting one. By time, at 60 minutes a day the line is
  7,200 of 9,600 minutes; each unit plan records minutes by lesson part, and the scope and sequence confirms the line
  from those minutes. The cuts are made for the day budget, not for the line.
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, grade 2: every grade 2 standard and every lettered part
  is owned by a progression row and placed in a unit below (2.NBT.A.1a and b in unit 5; 2.MD.A.1 and A.3 together in
  unit 3). 2.OA.A.1, 2.NBT.B.5 and 2.NBT.B.7 are each begun by a contributing row and finished by their owning row,
  as the Units table says. Skills: the eight Standards for Mathematical Practice, one or two named per unit
  (candidates in the Check set adds column; the unit plan settles them with the task where students do them). Check
  set: each unit plan checks its order, limits and models against the Progressions documents (CC BY 4.0) and the
  Eureka Math grade 2 reference (sequence and models only, never its problems or wording), and records any addition
  with its source.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Grade 2 (CC BY 4.0), adapted into Curio Class days with
  a credit line on each day; the source map at the end places every base unit and lesson. Only the free public pages
  are used: cool-downs, keys and assessments are behind a login and are not taken, so every exit ticket and
  assessment is Curio Class's own. The second edition (CC BY-NC) is never used.
- **Fluency without a timer.** Two fluency targets: 2.OA.B.2 (add and subtract within 20 with mental strategies, and
  all sums of two one-digit numbers from memory by the end of the year) and 2.NBT.B.5 (add and subtract within 100).
  Both are built with short spaced, mixed practice in warm-ups all year (the cut center days' games move there) and
  checked by watching and listening and by short untimed written items: the teacher records just knows, quick
  strategy or counts. No timer is ever shown and no sheet is timed.
- **Concrete to written.** Base-ten blocks and quick drawings are tied to a written method in the same lesson, and
  methods that record new units below the line are welcome; blocks are not part of fluency. No standard algorithm is
  required (grade 4). The number line is built from the ruler, not given.
- **Readiness.** Unit 1 opens with a short readiness check on grade 1 facts within 10, problem types and a Compare
  problem shown with a tape diagram; units 2, 3
  and 5 open with one on the grade 1 piece they rest on (adding within 100 and subtracting tens; measuring in
  same-size units; tens and ones).
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs with objects, printed rulers, frames and paper; any Curio Class tool is an extra,
  with no student account.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent read. Unit 3's two saree silk lessons (base lessons 3.11 and
  3.12, a South Asian textile context) are read by someone who knows that context if one is found; who, and any pay,
  is Cory's. Teacher notes in the last column cover money, surveys, food and pairing children.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 5 (2 days, about week 18).** Short written items with a one-to-one part: facts within 20 (watched in a
  game and on a short untimed card set, recorded as just knows, quick strategy or counts); one- and two-step
  problems within 100 with a tape diagram or equation, a start-unknown and an opposite-language Compare included;
  add and subtract within 100, one with a decomposed ten, with an explanation; measure an object in two units and
  explain the counts; a length word problem; a number line jump problem; three-digit numbers in expanded form,
  compared and placed on a number line; 10 and 100 more and less.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Re-teach the gaps (most often decomposing a ten and Compare problems),
  in small groups, ending with a short fresh task.
- **After unit 9 (2 days, end of year).** Sums of two one-digit numbers from memory and differences within 20
  (watched, untimed); add and subtract within 100 fluently and within 1000 with a written method and an explanation;
  four two-digit addends; a bar graph and a line plot read and answered; time to five minutes; a money problem;
  odd or even; an array as equal addends; thirds and fourths of a shape.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teach the gaps the check shows; the record goes on to the grade 3
  teacher with what the respond day changed.
