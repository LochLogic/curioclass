## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does each digit of a three-digit number tell us?"

**Hook, day 74:** Show nine paper tens and ten paper ones. Can these become one piece without changing the amount? Children mark possible groups; the teacher introduces the hundred. Allow five minutes.
**Supporting questions:** How many tens make a hundred, day 74? What does zero preserve, day 77? How do reference hundreds help locate a number, day 81? Which place decides a comparison, day 85?
**Closing claim, day 89:** "I can explain each digit, rename a quantity, count through hundreds, compare quantities and mentally change them by ten or a hundred." Records H, W, P, N, C, O and M below show these parts.
**Teaching:** Model exchanges on day 74, zero placeholders on day 77 and all four mental changes, including crossings, on day 84. Rehearse those changes on day 85. Step back during ordering on day 87 and collection work on day 88; ask how the representation proves the claim.

- **2.NBT.A.2, parts by verb:** count within 1000; skip-count by 5, 10 and 100. Taught on days 75 and 82; checked in C.
- **2.NBT.A.1, parts by verb:** explain hundreds, tens and ones, including zero (P); a (2.NBT.A.1a) 100 can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens - called a “hundred.”; b (2.NBT.A.1b) The numbers 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine hundreds (and 0 tens and 0 ones).
- **2.NBT.A.3, parts by verb:** read and write each form through 1000 (N).
- **2.NBT.B.5, parts by verb:** fluently add; fluently subtract within 100 (F).
- **2.NBT.A.4, parts by verb:** compare by digit values; record with all three symbols (O).
- **2.MD.B.6, parts by verb:** represent lengths from zero; represent sums; represent differences within 100 (L).
- **2.NBT.B.8, parts by verb:** mentally add ten; add one hundred; subtract ten; subtract one hundred (M).

**Practices:** MP.7, day 76: exchange ten tens in a mixed collection, then explain each digit in its numeral. MP.6, day 77: distinguish 506 from 560 using drawings and precise names for the empty place.
**Fluency:** Maintain 2.NBT.B.5 through five-minute mixed warm-ups alternating mental facts within 20 and written calculations within 100. Day 80 collects fresh F before feedback and rehearsal, without blocks or time pressure.
**Limits:** Count, read and write through 1000; compare three-digit numbers. Mental changes start from 100 to 900. General three-digit calculation waits until unit 7; no standard algorithm or rounding. On day 79 spend five minutes naming 1000 as the endpoint; extend by finding different representations within this range.
**Carried:** Bring back unit 2's pieces; tie exchanges to numerals on day 74 and expanded sums on day 78. Withdraw physical pieces on day 79 and supplied drawings on day 85; children draw when explaining. Tie unit 4's equal-interval line to three-digit labels on day 81, then jumps on day 82. Withdraw supplied lines on day 85. Keep paper models available for repairs; park tapes and graphs.

Balance follows the frame: thirteen understanding, one fluency and one application day, plus one check. Days 74 to 85 allocate 640 of 720 minutes to major work, 88.9 percent; days 86 to 89 are excluded. Days 74 and 75 each reserve ten response minutes outside that numerator. All fifteen teaching lessons primarily address major work, making one major-work unit. No supporting cluster is assigned.
