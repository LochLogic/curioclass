## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can hundreds, tens and ones help us add and subtract bigger numbers?"

**Hook, day 111:** In five minutes, mark jumps from 397 to 403 on a paper number line. Could knowing the next hundred help? Connect the six-unit distance to the compelling question. Adapt the first source lesson's nearby-number comparison.
**Questions:** Why combine like units, day 114? Where does a newly made ten go, day 118? What must change before subtraction, day 124? Can different written records describe equal exchanges, day 128?
**Closing claim:** Exchanging units preserves quantity. Use drawings and written methods to explain both operations in record P, then solve the remaining cases in S and mental task M on day 131.
**Teaching:** Model each new exchange beside its written step. Compare an exchange case with a case needing none. Step back during rehearsals; select contrasting methods for discussion, then require an individual record.


**Practices:** MP.7, day 118: match grouped ones and tens to each new unit in a sum. MP.3, day 128: compare decomposing first with decomposing as needed for 573 - 286 = 287; explain why both preserve 573 and subtract identical units.
**Fluency:** No within-1000 fluency target. Maintain within-20 facts and 2.NBT.B.5 through alternating five-minute warm-ups, without blocks or timing. Record just knows, quick strategy or counts. Fluency days rehearse taught methods; introduce no new exchange.
**Limits:** Within 1000; mental starting numbers 100 to 900. No required standard algorithm or formal property names. Follow the scope and sequence's dropped three-addend extension; compare methods for depth.
**Carried:** Keep unit 6's parked models. Tie number-line jumps to equations on day 111; withdraw supplied lines on day 114. Tie pieces to written like-unit calculations on day 112; drawings replace pieces on day 119. Restore pieces for decomposition on day 122. On day 124, predict and model both exchanges in 562 - 278 = 284 with pieces and matching written steps; day 125 rehearses this case type. Withdraw supplied pieces on day 125, briefly restoring them across zero on day 126 and withdrawing on day 127. Children may draw throughout. On day 130 connect bar lengths to labeled quantities and equations, then cover the graph for calculation; retain the data table.

Balance: fourteen understanding days, five fluency, one application and one check. Major work: 990 of 1080 counted minutes, 91.7 percent; days 111 to 128 each allocate 55 of 60. Observation days 129 and 130 and check day 131 are excluded. Eighteen of eighteen counted lessons and one of one units are predominantly major work. Day 130's supporting graph retrieval practices major addition and subtraction; graph setup contributes no major minutes.
