## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.NBT.B.5.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 100. Build efficient subtraction strategies by exchanging a ten or adding on to find the missing part.
- **2.NBT.B.9.** “Explain why” both subtraction strategies work. One ten is replaced by ten ones. The tens count decreases by one when ten ones appear. The missing addend measures the same difference.
- **2.NBT.B.6.** “Add up to four two-digit numbers” on day 27 by reordering and grouping the addends. Four-addend totals may exceed 100. Use written strategies based on the groups. A standard algorithm is unnecessary.
- **Connections and practice.** Retrieve 2.OA.B.2 facts for the ones on days 24 and 25. MP.3, day 26: compare the exchanged-ten drawing with adding on and defend why both measure the same amount.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- Ideal on days 24 to 27: a ten rod that can be matched to ten single pieces, one to one. The pieces fit because every single keeps its value through the exchange. Mark one crossed-out rod beside ten new dots. Children tie that exchange to the equality on day 24 and carry it into drawings and checked equations. The teacher holds the pieces for repair on day 28. From day 29 children use written work and their own sketches.
- For the bank use one tray per pair made from a folded sheet, or draw two boxes on scrap. Cut paper rods with ten marked units and matching squares, or sketch rods and dots. If a trade looks like a change in amount, match ten ones to one rod before removing any. Digital specification does not exist. No key tool.

## 9. Supports and extension

Show **exchange** as trading a ten for ten ones of equal value. Have children point to and name one **addend** when the four numbers are grouped on day 27. Use **decompose a ten** beside the action and “These stay equal because ___.” Offer a tactile rod and matching marks, large paper, pointing or a neutral scribe. Read or sign every cue. Record help on checks and leave labels short. Extend by checking a subtraction through adding on.

## 10. Two rooms
