Grade 2 Math. Unit 2, Adding and subtracting within 100, [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 24 to 28.** Days by kind: understanding 5. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.NBT.B.5.** “Fluently add and subtract” within 100. Build efficient subtraction strategies by exchanging a ten or adding on to find the missing part.
- **2.NBT.B.9.** “Explain why” both subtraction strategies work. One ten is replaced by ten ones. The tens count decreases by one when ten ones appear. The missing addend measures the same difference.
- **2.NBT.B.6.** “Add up to four two-digit numbers” on day 27 by reordering and grouping the addends. Four-addend totals may exceed 100. Use written strategies based on the groups. A standard algorithm is unnecessary.
- **Connections and practice.** Retrieve 2.OA.B.2 facts for the ones on days 24 and 25. MP.3, day 26: compare the exchanged-ten drawing with adding on and defend why both measure the same amount.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. The unit plan holds E, F and M. Give every child a picture to mark and read each cue aloud. Mark story quantities “invented for practice.” A letter requires evidence from the marks or explanation.

- **X24 (s02-X24), 2.NBT.B.5 and 2.NBT.B.9.** A board has 71 magnetic letters. Jo takes down eight. Give an empty board outline. “What picture shows how many letters stay on the board?” Key: one of seven tens becomes ten ones. The picture shows 6 tens and 11 ones, then crosses off 8, leaving 63. Wrong: 77 from taking 1 from 8 (B). 73 from retaining seven tens beside three ones (C).
  - Shape: Reconstruct an exchange and removal on an unmarked board. Practice exchanges pieces at the paper bank. Keep intact board pictures off the bank cards.
- **X25 (s02-X25), 2.NBT.B.9.** Two strips start with 85 and 92 marks. Cross five off each. Give two strip outlines. “Which strip runs short of loose ones?” Each child circles a strip and shows the available ones on it. Key: 92, since 2 loose ones cannot supply 5. Teacher verification: 85 − 5 = 80 and 92 − 5 = 87. Wrong: “Neither, 5 minus 2 works” (B). Circles 92 with “that's the rule” in place of the ones evidence (H).
  - Shape: Identify an exchange need with two different starts and one fixed removal. Comparison practice uses the same start with different removals.
- **X26 (s02-X26), 2.NBT.B.5 and 2.NBT.B.9.** Give 86 − 49 above an outline for 8 tens and 6 ones, and space to show the written work. “What calculation shows where the opened ten went?” Key: 86 = 70 + 16, then remove 40 and 9. The 3 tens and 7 ones give 37. Wrong: 43 from 9 − 6 in the ones (B). 47 from using 80 + 16 before removing 49 (C).
  - Shape: Supply the missing written record for one exchange picture. Practice annotates finished side-by-side calculations. Keep single missing records out of practice.
- **X27 (s02-X27), 2.NBT.B.5.** A fringe has 90 loops. Pat crosses off 54. Give an unmarked fringe outline. “What drawing shows how many loops stay?” Key: 9 tens become 8 tens and 10 ones. Cross off 5 tens and 4 ones. The 3 tens and 6 ones give 36. Wrong: 44 from 4 − 0 in the ones (B). 46 from keeping nine tens during exchange (C).
  - Shape: Draw an exchange from a zero-ones start and remove the given amount. Practice annotates a started exchange or tracks four addends. Keep round-ten exchange starts and the fringe off practice cards.
- **X28 (s02-X28), 2.NBT.B.9.** Give two unfinished paper notes for 74 − 39, with blank drawing space. One starts 74 = 60 + 14. The other starts 74 − 30 = 44. “Where does a ten become ten ones in these notes?” Children mark the exchange in each. Key: before either removal in the first note, 74 becomes 60 + 14. After removing 30 in the second, 44 becomes 30 + 14. Both leave 35 when the full amount is removed. Wrong: “Both get 35” alone (H). “44 becomes 40 + 14, so 45 remain” (C).
  - Shape: Reconcile two incomplete notes whose exchanges occur at different moments. Practice traces complete equations and pictures. Keep interrupted calculations off practice paper.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- Ideal on days 24 to 27: a ten rod that can be matched to ten single pieces, one to one. The pieces fit because every single keeps its value through the exchange. Mark one crossed-out rod beside ten new dots. Children tie that exchange to the equality on day 24 and carry it into drawings and checked equations. The teacher holds the pieces for repair on day 28. From day 29 children use written work and their own sketches.
- For the bank use one tray per pair made from a folded sheet, or draw two boxes on scrap. Cut paper rods with ten marked units and matching squares, or sketch rods and dots. If a trade looks like a change in amount, match ten ones to one rod before removing any. Digital specification does not exist. No key tool.

## Day 24

**Understanding.** I can open a ten to get enough ones. The teacher models with pieces, then children hold them.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Related facts within 20.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** X23 D and H, five minutes each.
- **15 min. Model.** Trade a rod to subtract 7 from 53. Connect the rod and dots to 53 = 40 + 13 and 40 + 6 = 46. Compare this with putting ten ones together for addition. Count the amount before and after the trade.
- **25 min. Bank practice.** Fifteen paired banks exchange one rod, remove the required singles and check the balance. Switch jobs. Hear roster 19 to 24, adjusted for missed turns.
- **5 min. Exit.** X24.

## Day 26

**Understanding.** I can show what happens to the tens and ones in two subtractions. Teacher shows pieces, children record equations.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Mixed mental facts.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** X25 B and H, five minutes each.
- **15 min. Model.** Put 64 − 22 = 42 beside 64 − 27 = 37. In the first, use 60 + 4. In the second, use 50 + 14. Name the same starting amount before removing the tens and ones.
- **25 min. Method comparison.** Mark differences on the two completed records. Check the second by adding on: 27 + 3 + 30 + 4 = 64. The parts added make 37. Select a drawing showing the exchange and an adding-on record. Connect both to the gap between 27 and 64. Hear positions 1 to 6 defend the equal value.
- **5 min. Exit.** X26.

## Day 27

**Understanding.** I can subtract two-digit amounts and keep track of four addends. Children carry the exchange sketch and marked grouping.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Helpful pairs. If the grouping retrieval was weak, start a ten-minute repair with 2 + 8 + 8 = 18, continued in the first five of Grouping model.
- **10 min. Grouping model.** Pair 18 with 42 and 35 with 15 to get 60 + 50 = 110. Tick each starting addend. Show the same parts by place value as 90 + 20 = 110. Count the total as eleven groups of ten using paper marks.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** X26 B and C, five minutes each.
- **30 min. Practice.** Match two-digit removals to already started exchanges, including 82 = 70 + 12 for 82 − 56 = 26. Children mark what is removed on the supplied teaching drawing and connect it to a written step. Partners then track each addend in a grouping. Demonstrate a second pairing of the grouping case, 18 + 35 = 53 and 42 + 15 = 57, so 110 again. Hear positions 7 to 12.
- **5 min. Exit.** X27.

## Day 28

**Understanding.** I can follow where a ten becomes ones in two methods. Children hold drawings and equations.

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Inverse facts.
- **15 min. Return and repair.** Five minutes each for X27 B and C and grouping G.
- **15 min. Model.** Use 95 − 58 = 37. Draw and compare 95 = 80 + 15 before taking 50 and 8 with 95 − 50 = 45, then 45 = 30 + 15 before taking 8. In both, exchange one ten and remove the same 5 tens and 8 ones. Verify by adding on: 58 + 2 + 30 + 5 = 95.
- **20 min. Trace.** Put the supplied pieces away. Trace two complete drawings and link their exchanged and removed units to their equations. Hear positions 13 to 18.
- **5 min. Exit.** X28. Send C and H to day 29 Return and repair.

## 9. Supports and extension

Show **exchange** as trading a ten for ten ones of equal value. Use **decompose a ten** beside the action and “These stay equal because ___.” Offer a tactile rod and matching marks, large paper, pointing or a neutral scribe. Read or sign every cue. Record help on checks and leave labels short. Extend by checking a subtraction through adding on.

## 10. Two rooms
