- **2.OA.B.2:** Fluently add and subtract within 20 using mental strategies. By end of Grade 2, know from memory all sums of two one-digit numbers. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.MD.A.1:** Measure the length of an object by selecting and using appropriate tools such as rulers, yardsticks, meter sticks, and measuring tapes. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.MD.A.3:** Estimate lengths using units of inches, feet, centimeters, and meters. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.MD.A.4:** Measure to determine how much longer one object is than another, expressing the length difference in terms of a standard length unit. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.MD.B.5:** Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve word problems involving lengths that are given in the same units, e.g., by using drawings (such as drawings of rulers) and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.MD.D.9:** Generate measurement data by measuring lengths of several objects to the nearest whole unit, or by making repeated measurements of the same object. Show the measurements by making a line plot, where the horizontal scale is marked off in whole-number units. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.NBT.B.5:** Fluently add and subtract within 100 using strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.NBT.A.1:** Understand that the three digits of a three-digit number represent amounts of hundreds, tens, and ones; e.g., 706 equals 7 hundreds, 0 tens, and 6 ones. Understand the following as special cases: Parts, by verb: a (2.NBT.A.1a) 100 can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens - called a “hundred.”; b (2.NBT.A.1b) The numbers 100, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 700, 800, 900 refer to one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine hundreds (and 0 tens and 0 ones).
- **2.NBT.A.3:** Read and write numbers to 1000 using base-ten numerals, number names, and expanded form. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.NBT.B.7:** Add and subtract within 1000, using concrete models or drawings and strategies based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction; relate the strategy to a written method. Understand that in adding or subtracting three-digit numbers, one adds or subtracts hundreds and hundreds, tens and tens, ones and ones; and sometimes it is necessary to compose or decompose tens or hundreds. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.OA.A.1:** Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve one- and two-step word problems involving situations of adding to, taking from, putting together, taking apart, and comparing, with unknowns in all positions, e.g., by using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.NBT.B.9:** Explain why addition and subtraction strategies work, using place value and the properties of operations. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **2.MD.B.6:** Represent whole numbers as lengths from 0 on a number line diagram with equally spaced points corresponding to the numbers 0, 1, 2, ..., and represent whole-number sums and differences within 100 on a number line diagram. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
