Grade 2 Math. Unit 4, Adding and subtracting on the number line, [unit plan](s04.md). **Days 68 to 73.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 1, application 2, check 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.B.6.** “Represent whole numbers as lengths from 0” and “represent whole-number sums and differences within 100” on equally spaced number lines. On day 68 compare an open sketch with the equal-tick line. On the sketch labels give the jump sizes. Use equal-interval diagrams for evidence of the standard.
- **2.NBT.B.5, days 68 and 71.** “Fluently add and subtract within 100” using place value, properties or related operations, without a required standard algorithm.
- **2.OA.A.1, days 69, 70 and 72.** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” one- and two-step stories. Rehearse joining, removing, putting together, taking apart and comparing. Draw the quantities and use a square for the unknown in each position. **2.MD.B.5, day 70:** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” same-unit length problems, carried by Q1 and Q4.
- **MP.5, day 68.** Compare a route through a ten with unit jumps. **MP.2, day 70.** Point to the quantity each label represents in a tape, an equation and a line.

- **X68, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Print 54 + 39 and an equally spaced line with the departure at 54 and an intermediate tick marked 60. Give a two-jump answer area with blank distances and finish. “How do two jumps through 60 show 54 + 39?” Key: 54 to 60 to 93, right 6 then right 33. - **X69, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.OA.A.1.** Show an invented classroom sign on which 98 dots were lit. After some go dark, 81 glow. - **X70, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.OA.A.1.** The class art wall has room for 89 portraits. It already holds 66. Match the labels to the start, tip and jump. Reports 89 as the space left by reading the tip (D). Copies 66 into the space for portraits still to hang (H).
  - Shape: Explain the meanings in one unfamiliar static picture where the distance is unlabeled. Practice sorts complete story, tape, equation and line sets, and uses separate sets for independent story solving.
- **X71, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Print “74 + 23 = 96?” beside a unit line and space for written steps. “How can jumps check the answer on this note?” Key: 74 + 3 = 77 and 77 + 20 = 97, so the claim falls one short. Draw the jumps from 74 through 77 to 97 or another valid route with matching steps. Ends at 96 after counting 74 as the first of three spaces, then adding 20 (A). Writes 74 + 2 + 3 = 79 by treating two tens as two ones (G).
  - Shape: Audit a supplied claim with a self-drawn route and written reasoning. Practice calculates directly and checks an inverse or a supplied route with no claimed answer to audit.
- **X72, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.OA.A.1.** Show two jumps on a line with equal unit spaces, from 70 to 95 to 86, with lengths unprinted. “What classroom story fits this line?” Key example: Mika made 70 prints, printed 25 more, then recycled 9 and kept 86. Accept another one-actor classroom story tied to both changes. Children add quick scene marks and change labels to both arcs. A child may label two quick sketches, dictate to a scribe or point for a neutral adult to record. Read back a dictated story. Describes only the increase and stops at 95 (I). Adds nine for the leftward jump (F).
  - Shape: Invent a linked-change story from two unfamiliar arrows and mark its actions. Partner practice checks someone else's solution to a supplied story. Keep R's single jump and X72's two jumps separate.

- **R, day 72.** Show the equal-tick arrow from 58 to 87. Read each cue after the child saves the preceding mark. “How far did the arrow travel?” “What equation fits?” “What classroom story could this arrow tell?” Key: 58 + 29 = 87. Sample: “Mika put 58 tags on a board. She put on 29 more. Now it has 87.” A second sample can join 29 cm to a 58 cm ribbon for 87 cm. Ask separately “How could your own jumps show that sum?” On its own line, a correct model goes right 20 to 78 and right 9 to 87. Shows only 0 to 29 for the whole sum (E). Draws a leftward jump for the 29 added to 58 (F).
  - Shape: Interpret one arrow in an invented scene and independently represent the same sum on a separate line. Practice checks a partner's drawing for a supplied story.
- **N, day 73.** Ask “Which picture works as a number line?” on the two consecutive 0 through 6 diagrams, only one equally spaced. Circle it. On a separate 0 to 40 line with equal unit ticks and labels every five, ask “Show 37 units of length from 0.” Have children shade the length and mark its end. Key: 37 spaces, ending at 37. Selects the line with unequal consecutive spaces (B). Ends the shading at 36 by counting zero as the first unit (A).
  - Shape: Judge one complete scale choice, then independently locate and shade a length from zero on the second sheet. Practice traces or labels an existing equal scale without this choice-and-shade pair.
- **D, day 73.** Ask “How do jumps show 92 − 53?” Key: from 92 left 50 to 42 and left 3 to 39. Or count up from 53 to 60 to 90 to 92, with distances 7, 30 and 2 totaling 39. Ask separately “What addition equation checks your difference?” Key: 39 + 53 = 92, or 53 + 39 = 92. Calling the difference 92 reads the endpoint (D).
  - Shape: Draw a subtraction route and independently check its result with inverse addition. Practice reads completed routes or rehearses the calculation separately from the diagram.
- **S, day 73 if needed.** “How can jumps show 48 + 37?” Key: 48 + 37 = 85, for example right 30 to 78, right 7 to 85. A correct alternative partition is fine. Shows only 0 to 37 for the whole sum (E).
  - Shape: Independently represent a named sum on an unmarked unit line supplied only with reference labels. Practice annotates given moves or reads routes.
- **F, day 71.** Give four separate tens-and-ones pictures. Read “How much is ...?” once per calculation. Provide a tens-and-ones diagram that children can mark to show their own method. Keys: 67 + 30 = 97, 56 + 35 = 91, 79 − 20 = 59, 92 − 28 = 64. Read the tens-and-ones marks before coding. Uses 3 ones for 30 and gets 70 (G). Uses 3 ones for the tens in 35 and gets 64 (G).
