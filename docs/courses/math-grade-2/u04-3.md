Grade 2 Math. Unit 4, Adding and subtracting on the number line, [unit plan](s04.md). **Days 68 to 73.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 1, application 2, check 1. A plan, not a lesson. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.MD.B.6.** “Represent whole numbers as lengths from 0” and “represent whole-number sums and differences within 100” on equally spaced number lines. On day 68 compare an open sketch with the equal-tick line. On the sketch labels give the jump sizes. Use equal-interval diagrams for evidence of the standard.
- **2.NBT.B.5, days 68 and 71.** “Fluently add and subtract within 100” using place value, properties or related operations, without a required standard algorithm.
- **2.OA.A.1, days 69, 70 and 72.** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” one- and two-step stories. Rehearse joining, removing, putting together, taking apart and comparing. Draw the quantities and use a square for the unknown in each position. **2.MD.B.5, day 70:** “Use addition and subtraction within 100 to solve” same-unit length problems, carried by Q1 and Q4.
- **MP.5, day 68.** Compare a route through a ten with unit jumps. **MP.2, day 70.** Point to the quantity each label represents in a tape, an equation and a line.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read every cue aloud. Everyone makes independent marks on a paper picture. Label invented classroom scenes “invented for practice” and say it aloud. Use shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Give a letter only when the work shows that idea. With an item that asks for an equation, the key gives the equation first and the square's value second. Data ids for exits are s04-X68 through s04-X72.

- **X68, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Print 54 + 39 and an equally spaced line with the departure at 54 and an intermediate tick marked 60. Give a two-jump answer area with blank distances and finish. “How do two jumps through sixty show this sum?” Key: 54 to 60 to 93, right 6 then right 33. Marks 7 then 32 by counting 54 first (A). Starts at 0 and jumps to 39 (E).
  - Shape: Restore the unlabeled route through a fixed stop. Practice compares complete routes, with the distances supplied and checked against the equal-interval line.
- **X69, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.OA.A.1.** Show an invented classroom sign on which 98 dots were lit. After some go dark, 81 glow. Print these two known counts beside the picture and give a unit line with reference ticks. “How can your line show how many went dark?” Key: leftward distance between 98 and 81, marked 17. Counts both ends and writes 18 (A). Copies the 81 still glowing onto the change arrow (H).
  - Shape: Locate and label an unobserved removal on a line. Practice traces visible changes and fills only a missing start or result, or discusses a movement already shown.
- **X70, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.OA.A.1.** Show an invented classroom mosaic with 89 spots, 66 shining. Give the jump from 66 to 89 on a unit line with small icon spaces for the meanings. “How does this jump show the mosaic?” Key: 66 shining, 89 total, 23 still dark, matched to start, tip and traveled distance. Reports 89 as the difference by reading the tip (D). Copies the 66 shining spots into the missing-count box (H).
  - Shape: Explain the meanings in one unfamiliar static picture where the distance is unlabeled. Practice sorts complete story, tape, equation and line sets, and uses separate sets for independent story solving.
- **X71, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.NBT.B.5.** Print “74 + 23 = 96?” beside a unit line and space for written steps. “How can jumps check the answer on this note?” Key: 74 + 20 = 94 and 94 + 3 = 97, so the claim falls one short. Draw the jumps from 74 through 94 to 97 or another valid route with matching steps. Ends at 96 by counting 94 as the first of three spaces (A). Writes 74 + 2 + 3 = 79 by treating two tens as two ones (G).
  - Shape: Audit a supplied claim with a self-drawn route and written reasoning. Practice calculates directly and checks an inverse or a supplied route with no claimed answer to audit.
- **X72, 2.MD.B.6 and 2.OA.A.1.** Show two equally spaced jumps, from 70 to 95 to 86, with lengths unprinted. “What classroom story fits this line?” Key example: Mika made 70 prints, printed 25 more, then recycled 9 and kept 86. Accept another one-actor classroom story tied to both changes. Children add quick scene marks and change labels to both arcs. If needed a scribe records the child's story. Describes only the increase and stops at 95 (I). Adds nine for the leftward jump (F).
  - Shape: Invent a linked-change story from two unfamiliar arrows and mark its actions. Partner practice checks someone else's solution to a supplied story. Keep R's single jump and X72's two jumps separate.

**Unit assessment items owned.** R, N, D and conditional S for 2.MD.B.6; F and Q1 through Q16 as maintenance. Q1 and Q4 also supply the section 4.2 length evidence within the same minutes. Use [unit field 5](s04.md) for administration and original master reservations. The screened cases below govern the day sheets where the original family overlaps prior work. - **R, day 72.** Show the equal-tick arrow from 58 to 87. Ask “What classroom story could this arrow tell?” Give room to mark the known quantities and change in the picture and equation. Key: 58 + 29 = 87. Sample: “Mika put 58 tags on a board. She put on 29 more. Now it has 87.” A second sample can join 29 cm to a 58 cm ribbon for 87 cm. Ask separately “How could your own jumps show that sum?” On its own line, a correct model goes right 20 to 78 and right 9 to 87. Starting at 0 and ending at 29 shows E. Adding with a leftward jump shows F.
  - Shape: Interpret one arrow in an invented scene and independently represent the same sum on a separate line. Practice checks supplied, complete story solutions with partners.
- **N, day 73.** Ask “Which picture works as a number line?” on the two consecutive 0 through 6 diagrams, only one equally spaced. Circle it. On the separate 0 to 20 unit line labeled every five, ask “Show the length from zero for thirteen.” Mark 13 and shade all 13 intervals from zero. Choosing unequal widths shows B. Ends at 12 by counting zero as the first unit (A).
  - Shape: Judge one complete scale choice, then independently locate and shade a length from zero on the second sheet. Practice traces or labels an existing equal scale without this choice-and-shade pair.
- **D, day 73.** Ask “How do jumps show eighty-three minus forty-seven?” Key: from 83 left 40 to 43 and left 7 to 36, or right from 47 to 50 to 80 to 83 with distances 3, 30 and 3 totaling 36. Ask separately “What addition equation checks your difference?” Key: 36 + 47 = 83, or 47 + 36 = 83. Calling the difference 83 reads the endpoint (D).
  - Shape: Draw a subtraction route and independently check its result with inverse addition. Practice reads completed routes or rehearses the calculation separately from the diagram.
- **S, day 73 if needed.** “How can jumps show forty-eight plus thirty-seven?” Key: 48 + 37 = 85, for example right 30 to 78, right 7 to 85. A correct alternative partition is fine. Starting at zero and ending at 37 shows E.
  - Shape: Independently represent a named sum on an unmarked unit line supplied only with reference labels. Practice annotates given moves or reads routes.
- **F, day 71.** Give four separate tens-and-ones pictures. Read “How much is ...?” once per calculation. Keys: 67 + 30 = 97, 56 + 35 = 91, 79 − 20 = 59, 92 − 28 = 64. Wrong answers 70, 64, 77 and 82 respectively show G when marks replace each tens move with ones. Record accuracy and efficient strategy separately. Paper marks alone leave fluency open.
  - Shape: Four independent written calculations with a tens-and-ones record. Practice alternates calculations and diagram turns with feedback and keeps this mixed set covered.
