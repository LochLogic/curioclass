## 1. Standards and practices

- **2.NBT.A.1a, days 74 to 76.** One hundred “can be thought of as a bundle of ten tens.” Match squares to strips and strips to grids to show that an exchange keeps the amount.
- **2.NBT.A.1b, days 74 to 76.** The whole hundreds “refer to” one to nine hundreds, with zero tens and zero ones remaining after grouping.
- **2.NBT.A.2, day 75.** “Count” and “skip-count” by hundreds. Section 5.2 continues with ones, fives and tens.
- **2.NBT.A.1 and 2.NBT.A.3, days 76 to 78.** “Understand” what each digit represents. “Read and write” three-digit names and numerals, then expanded form. Explain how position sets value and how zero records an empty place. Section 5.2 takes up 1000.
- **MP.7, day 76.** Explain how ten tens exchanged for one hundred appear in the numeral. **MP.6, day 77.** Name the empty places in 506 and 560.

- **X74, 2.NBT.A.1a.** Show fifteen loose strips, each with ten dots, and a paper band with ten strip slots and a blank tag. “What amount belongs on the tag when you fill one band?” Key: fit ten strips, leave five loose and label the full band 100 or one hundred. Labels the full band ten because it holds ten pieces (A). Leaves the band empty because “there are only fifteen ones” (A).
  - Shape: Select one hundred from surplus stock and label the selected bundle. Practice fits an exact set to a grid or matches a finished bundle to a caption.
- **X75, 2.NBT.A.1b.** Show two stacks of three hundred-grids each, with all six edges visible, and one loose grid. Give one empty build box with a label slot. “What labeled build shows all these grids?” Key: draws seven hundred-grids and writes 700. Writes 70 and says one zero is enough for the two empty places (C). Points to the 7 in the hundreds column and says it means seven ones (B).
  - Shape: Unpack separate stacks into a single labeled build. Practice builds from a single numeral card or counts a single row of grids.
- **X76, 2.NBT.A.1 and 2.NBT.A.3.** Show unlabeled trays left to right with six squares, six grids and four strips. Give a three-place label with a value box below each digit. “How should we label the whole picture?” Key: 646 above 600, 40, 6. Writes 6, 40, 6 (B). Writes 600, 4, 6 (B).
  - Shape: Recover a numeral and its digit values from scrambled trays with a repeated digit. Practice uses ordered mats and distinct digits.
- **X77, 2.NBT.A.1.** Show 870 beside a copy with the final zero rubbed out, 87. Supply blank place boxes under both. “Why does 870 need that zero?” Key: zero shows no ones and keeps the 7 in the tens place, worth 70. In 87 the 7 is worth seven ones. Accept marked empty places and arrows showing the change in the 7's place. Says the erasure leaves the number unchanged (C). Says the 7 means seven ones in both copies (B).
  - Shape: Explain the effect of erasing a digit. Practice builds and reads intact numerals.
- **X78, 2.NBT.A.3.** Show two damaged labels. One has 582 beside 500 + 8 + 2 and a clean sum space. The printed mistake treats the 8 tens as eight ones (D). The other has a blank numeral beside 4 + 900 + 30. Ask one label at a time. “What sum belongs beside 582?” Key: 500 + 80 + 2. Writes 5 + 8 + 2 (D). Cover that card. “What number belongs beside this sum?” Key: 934. Writes 16 after treating the shuffled addends as 4, 9 and 3 ones (D).
  - Shape: Mend an incorrect expansion and recover a numeral from shuffled addends. Practice builds and links ordered sums and numerals with aligned pieces.

## 9. Supports and extension

Review **digit**, a written mark from 0 to 9 used to write numbers, and **bundle** for pieces held together, **ten tens** for ten groups of ten, and **hundred** for their total. The **hundreds** place counts those units. A **three-digit number** has three digits and here names hundreds, tens and ones. **Expanded form** is a sum of the values in those places. Say, point to and use the words. Frame: “The digit means ___ because ___.” Offer enlarged or tactile grids, pre-cut shapes and a scribe for directed marks. Read or sign cues. Children read short labels. Extend by renaming whole hundreds in tens.
