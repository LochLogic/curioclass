## 1. Standards and practices

**Share.** 4-PS3-2: "Make observations to provide evidence" for current transfer, integrated with earlier modes day 23. Investigation, PS3.B and energy and matter join when a connection changes output.

**Teacher explanation.** Current needs a conducting path through load and both terminals. Energy arrows run battery to outputs, not unchanged energy returning to the cell. Teach circuit: a complete conducting loop; current: electric charge moving along a path; terminal: a connection point. Stored energy is energy kept for later transfer. These belong on Understanding day 20; generator: a device converting motion energy to electrical energy, on Understanding day 22; earlier grades did not teach these terms. Review switch on/off from grade 3 u02-2 day 24; hands-on wiring is new. No electrons, chemistry, circuit arithmetic or numerical energy. A generator converts motion energy into electrical energy, creating none.

- **E20, day 20, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. This lamp is dark. Complete the drawing with an energy-path caption."
  Shape: finish a one-terminal diagram with transfer caption. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: Connect the free lamp terminal to the other cell terminal, making a complete loop through the lamp. Current carries stored battery energy to light.
  Wrong: "One lead from one terminal completes the path." (B).

- **E21, day 21, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. A report says one lead powered a buzzer because sound was heard. A nearby buzzer also sounded. Draw the circuit and comparison needed to test this report."
  Shape: circuit and comparison design controlling competing sound. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: Draw a complete loop through both cell terminals and the target buzzer. Silence the nearby buzzer; compare target sound with its path complete and interrupted. Require the control; hearing nearby sound proves no target path.
  Wrong: "One lead is enough to power this buzzer." (B).

- **E22, day 22, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. A lamp loop has an uncut wire. A switch joins two points on that wire. The lamp stays lit when the switch opens. Explain this result with a corrected switch drawing."
  Shape: explain unexpected light by relocating a bypassed switch. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: The intact wire still completes the path when the parallel switch opens. Put the switch in a gap in that wire, so opening interrupts the loop.
  Wrong: "A switch across an uncut wire must control the lamp." (L).

- **E23, day 23, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. Pedaling turns a generator. Current reaches a lamp and speaker. The beam warms cloth. The warmer casing touches a cooler clip. Sound moves a separate receiver. Revise an energy-path model for these four transfers. Compare its input with our crank."
  Shape: integrated four-mode model revision and input comparison. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: Current to lamp/speaker; light to cloth; heat warmer casing to cooler clip; sound to receiver. Pedaling and cranking supply motion converted to electrical energy. No energy is created.
  Wrong: "The generator creates energy without an input." (A).

**Worked case s02-K22-1.** "A turning handle drives a generator. What supplies its energy?" Key: turning supplies motion energy, converted to electrical energy. Wrong: "It creates energy without input" (A). Shape: locate a known device's input orally. This is teaching, not another exit; do not add the unseen pull-cord failure packet.
