# Section 2.2: Controlling current

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 4. Unit 2, Energy on the move. "How does energy get from place to place, and where does the energy we use come from?" Anchor: a hand-crank flashlight that lights when the handle turns, with no battery to change (days 16 to 38): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 20 to 23.** Days by kind: fluency 1 (23), application 3 (20, 21, 22). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 5 in the unit; the unit plan holds its section fields as U3 to U11 (a one-section unit until the 2026-11-16 cuts).
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.

**Share.** 4-PS3-2: "Make observations to provide evidence" for current transfer, integrated with earlier modes day 23. Investigation, PS3.B and energy and matter join when a connection changes output.

**Teacher explanation.** Current needs a conducting path through load and both terminals. Energy arrows run battery to outputs, not unchanged energy returning to the cell. First teach circuit, current, terminal and stored energy on Understanding day 20, and generator on Understanding day 22; earlier grades did not teach these terms. Review switch on/off from grade 3 u02-2 day 24; hands-on wiring is new. No electrons, chemistry, circuit arithmetic or numerical energy. A generator converts motion energy into electrical energy, creating none.

- **E20, day 20, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. This lamp is dark. Complete the drawing with an energy-path caption."
  Shape: finish a one-terminal diagram with transfer caption.
  Key: Connect the free lamp terminal to the other cell terminal, making a complete loop through the lamp. Current carries stored battery energy to light.
  Wrong: "One lead from one terminal completes the path." (B).
  Print the corresponding exit-20.md only that day. 

- **E21, day 21, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. A report says one lead powered a buzzer because sound was heard. A nearby buzzer also sounded. Draw the circuit and comparison needed to test this report."
  Shape: circuit and comparison design controlling competing sound.
  Key: Draw a complete loop through both cell terminals and the target buzzer. Silence the nearby buzzer; compare target sound with its path complete and interrupted. Require the control; hearing nearby sound proves no target path.
  Wrong: "One lead is enough to power this buzzer." (B).
  Print the corresponding exit-21.md only that day. 

- **E22, day 22, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. A lamp loop has an uncut wire. A switch joins two points on that wire. The lamp stays lit when the switch opens. Explain this result with a corrected switch drawing."
  Shape: explain unexpected light by relocating a bypassed switch.
  Key: The intact wire still completes the path when the parallel switch opens. Put the switch in a gap in that wire, so opening interrupts the loop.
  Wrong: "A switch across an uncut wire must control the lamp." (L).
  Print the corresponding exit-22.md only that day. 

- **E23, day 23, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. Pedaling turns a generator. Current reaches a lamp and speaker. The beam warms cloth. The warmer casing touches a cooler clip. Sound moves a separate receiver. Revise an energy-path model for these four transfers, comparing its input with our crank."
  Shape: integrated four-mode model revision and input comparison.
  Key: Current to lamp/speaker; light to cloth; heat warmer casing to cooler clip; sound to receiver. Pedaling and cranking supply motion converted to electrical energy. No energy is created.
  Wrong: "The generator creates energy without an input." (A).
  Print the corresponding exit-23.md only that day.
