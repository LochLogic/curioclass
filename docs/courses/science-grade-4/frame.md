# Course frame: Science Grade 4

Course id science-k5 (grade 4 row), grade 4, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6: speed and collisions, energy on the move, waves and codes, the changing land, Earth's patterns and
  hazards, then senses and structures.
- **Days.** 120 teaching days: 111 in units and 9 at course level (6 for three course-level checks, 3 respond
  days). Elementary science shares the day, so the course assumes one science lesson of 45 minutes on four days a
  week (180 minutes a week) across 30 weeks (a school year of about 36 weeks, of which about 30 carry science
  lessons here; the rest hold breaks, the school's own testing and events). Science day n falls near school day 4n/3; the cross-subject calendar uses both. The year owns
  14 rows (15 performance expectations, with 4-PS4-3 and 3-5-ETS1-2 in one row). Two rows are design work with
  building and testing (s4-energy-device, s4-information-codes) and one is a third design comparison
  (s4-hazard-solutions), so the year weighs a little more per row than grade 3; about 111 days in units fits it, and
  the full 160-day yardstick is not needed.
- **Standard.** The grade 4 performance expectations of data/standards/ngss-pes.txt, each taught in full, plus
  3-5-ETS1-2, the band's engineering expectation this grade owns; 3-5-ETS1-3 is started here in s4-energy-device
  (test and improve) and completed in grade 5. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md. Practices and crosscutting
  concepts are named per unit in the C3 column (see "For the lead"). Plain facts (BASELINE rule 5, values rule b):
  the great age of the Earth is taught as the evidence shows; energy resources are neither cheered nor blamed
  beyond what backed sources show.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"): the elementary
  units in the source store are NonCommercial and reference only. Phenomena are chosen to be real, observable or
  measurable in class where possible, puzzling at grade 4 and explainable with the unit's core ideas; each unit
  names its anchor, and a teacher may swap in a local phenomenon that needs the same ideas (a local road cut or
  river bank for unit 4, the state's own power plants for unit 2). Investigations are our own designs. Data, maps
  and pictures come from open and public-domain material only: USGS earthquake and volcano data and topographic
  maps, NOAA ocean-floor maps and tsunami pages, the US Energy Information Administration's state energy data,
  NASA images, public-domain photographs, and open simulations released before 2026-03-29. Curio Class's own
  online tools serve as simulations where a class cannot run the investigation (listed per unit, built later).
- **Materials.** Classroom-cheap: marbles, toy cars, cardboard ramps, cups, AA batteries, wire, small bulbs or
  buzzers, rubber bands, slinkies or rope, trays of water, flashlights, mirrors, sand, soil, a sloped tray as a
  stream table. Every investigation names a household substitute where safe (a baking tray and sand for a stream
  table; aluminum foil strips for wire) and a no-kit path where not (a class data set or a Curio Class
  simulation), said plainly to be weaker (PRODUCTION-PATH: substitutes, then no-kit). Every simulation has a paper
  path (the same data on a printed sheet) and needs no student account. Each unit plan states its cost.
- **Safety.** A course safety page for the year: low-voltage batteries only, never outlets, a bulb or buzzer always
  in the loop (a short circuit gets hot); never look at the sun, even in a mirror, and no lasers; rolling objects
  slow and on the floor, no throwing; water and sand on covered tables, spills wiped at once; hand washing after
  soil. Every hands-on day carries its own Safety and set-up section.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor and builds a question board; each lesson set answers a question
  the last one raised. One energy story runs through units 1 to 3: the energy transfer arrows start in unit 1,
  follow energy through sound, light, heat and current in unit 2, and follow a wave across water in unit 3. Each
  unit has an initial model, named revision points and a consensus model; the end-of-unit assessment uses a
  phenomenon the class has not met.
- **Design work.** Grade 4 owns 3-5-ETS1-2 (generate and compare several solutions): taught in full in unit 3
  (codes: research the problem first, generate at least two solutions, share proposals, test them under two
  conditions and compare them on the data), used again in unit 5 (hazards). Unit 2's device begins 3-5-ETS1-3 (test, find where it fails, improve)
  without the planned fair test that grade 5 completes.
- **How science works.** Taught on purpose in unit 4 (the rock record is read by assuming the same processes worked
  in the past as now) and unit 6 (a common idea, that light goes out from the eye, tested and revised by evidence).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 5 (earthquakes, tsunamis and floods
  students may have lived through) and unit 2 (communities whose work depends on one energy source) are named in
  their rows. Every hands-on unit needs a science teacher's read of safety and materials before the course is
  called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3); who, and any pay, is Cory's.
- **Reading level.** Science texts are short (one to three pages), written at a grade 3 to 4 band with diagrams;
  words are taught when the class needs them.
