## 1. Standards and practices

**4-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Make observations and/or measurements" of how ice, wind, plants and rain change land and how fast water carries sediment. Day 61 observes thawed clay and enclosed stones. Day 62 graphs repeated trials. Day 63 judges a cause from repeats. Day 64 compares ice, wind, roots, burrows and rain one record at a time. Day 65 measures a plant-cover comparison.

**4-ESS1-1, day 66 only.** "Identify evidence": the revised canyon model ends in a question about earlier landscapes. Nothing is taught or checked about layers here.

Limits that bite: one process per record; loose sand in a tray and clay are analogues, never a rate for a solid-rock canyon; a river contributes to canyon cutting beside weathering and gravity; turf differs from bare sand in more than roots, so the day 65 comparison cannot isolate roots. Supplied records are invented for practice and never pooled with live readings.

Connections: data in tables and bar graphs (grade 2 and 3, day 62); ruler reading and subtraction for travel (day 65); fair test (day 52). Math unit 6 uses whole centimeters and equal cupfuls, no conversions.

Review in the ledger's words: "erosion", "weathering: breaking rock material down" (day 58), "baseline" (day 59), "fair test: keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing" (u03-4 day 52), "centimeter, ruler zero, whole centimeter" (u01-1 day 3), "scaled bar graph, scale of 2" (grade 3 u06-1 day 100), "cause and effect" (u02-4 day 32, revised here as land arrows), "gravity: a pull down" (grade 3 u01-1 day 4) and "layer" (grade 3 u05-1 day 85), "two-column notes: source statement and source location" (u02-4 day 30). New terms: "abrasion: rubbing that wears material down" and "control: a matched comparison without the tested treatment" (day 61), "graph interval: amount between neighboring scale marks" (day 62), "burrow: an animal-dug opening" (day 64), "turf: grass with roots and attached soil" (day 65).

Teacher page must explain: breaking, carrying and settling are three jobs; a distance measures carrying, never breaking. Repeats cannot remove a confound. A tray result limits itself to the tested conditions. Rain shapes land and decides which plants can live there.

Planning/carrying out investigations joins ESS2.A/ESS2.E with cause and effect: children connect measured material movement to the tested condition. The canyon question adds stability and change; the tray gives no geological rate.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment ownership:** teach item 1 with section 4.1; section 4.4 administers it. Unscored days 65/66 practice receives written feedback and day 70 revision. All supplied exits are printed and announced "Invented for practice". Each child returns one paper exit in five minutes. Formative scale only: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keys were solved independently before writing.

**E61.** "Clay cracks widen while pieces still touch. Stones move without visible size or shape changes. Explain the weathering evidence, including what the stone record lacks."
Key: widening cracks support breakage in place; movement alone does not show stone weathering. New chips or changed edges would supply that evidence. Wrong: "Touching pieces cannot weather because they did not travel." (A).
Shape: compose a comparison of two incomplete records with the needed extra observation. Teaching records changes during demonstrations; no mixed clay/stone comparison response card.

**E62.** Stimulus: low flow 4, 5, 8 centimeters; high flow 6, 7, 6. Print: "Graph these six sediment-front distances. Add a carrying-or-breaking conclusion about the claim that high flow always carries farther."
Key: six correctly labeled bars, equal intervals and centimeters; distances measure carrying, not breakage. Low-flow 8 exceeds every high-flow reading, so "always" fails. Wrong: "Distance cannot show erosion unless rock broke." (A).
Shape: six-bar construction plus one process-qualified claim. Practice uses a live line plot, isolated bar/axis components and observation arrows; no six-bar trial graph with a causal conclusion.

**E63.** "A steep pebble tray loses less sand than a gentle bare tray twice. Evaluate the pebble claim by proposing a fair comparison."
Key: repetitions do not isolate pebbles because slope also differs. Compare pebble/bare at the same slope, water, starting sand and pour position. Wrong: "Repeats isolate pebbles even with different slopes." (C).
Shape: written causal verdict through a repaired experimental comparison. Practice sorts condition labels and connects observations to already matched tests; no confounded repeated-claim repair sheet.

**E64.** Reserved panels: ice opens a pavement crack with new chips; dust gathers behind a windbreak; a root lifts a slab; a tunnel roof collapses. A separate rain-fed hollow has grooves and water-needing plants. Print: "Label the land change in each panel. Plan a comparison testing rain's effect on the hollow's grooves and plants."
Key: ice breaking, wind carrying/settling, root lifting, collapse above an animal opening. Compare matched exposed/excluded rain conditions before and after; track groove change and plant condition separately with starting soil, plants and light matched. Co-occurrence alone does not prove causes. Wrong: "Living things cannot change land." (D); "Rain cannot affect which plants grow." (E).
Shape: one evidence sheet, process labels plus a paired rain investigation plan. Practice uses mechanism cutaways and a regional rainfall/plant map; no process-identification array or paired rain exclusion design.

**E65.** Stimulus: planted front starts at 3 and ends at 10 centimeters; bare starts at 1 and ends at 5. Planted receives more water. Print: "Calculate both travel distances. Use them to judge whether this record isolates plant cover."
Key: 7 and 4 centimeters; unequal water prevents isolating cover. This does not mean plants have no effects. Wrong: "Cover caused it even though water also changed." (C).
Shape: two nonzero-start calculations and a confounding verdict. Practice reads live fronts from zero on separate ruler strips; no nonzero pair, unequal-water turf comparison or verdict frame.

**E66.** Reserved coast sequence: cracked ledge, blocks fallen across a stream mouth, diverted stream, later waves exposing stripes. Print: "Revise this sequence model to explain the changed coast. Include one question about the exposed layers' earlier history."
Key: cracks precede falling blocks; gravity moves blocks; blockage diverts stream; waves later remove loose grains and expose layers. The record does not show what first made cracks. A question asks about fossil evidence in the layers. Wrong: "Rock cannot change, so these events cannot happen." (B).
Shape: integrated changed-coast sequence and layer question. Teaching sorts separate canyon evidence into representation folders; no rockfall/diversion sequence or complete sequence-plus-question model.

No auxiliary exit boxes: former process/root prompts become unscored teaching observations, never extra exit asks. Retain their concepts in the main work.
