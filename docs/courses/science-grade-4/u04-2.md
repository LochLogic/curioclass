## 1. Standards and practices

**4-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Make observations and/or measurements" of how ice, wind, plants and rain change land and how fast water carries sediment. Day 61 observes thawed clay and enclosed stones. Day 62 graphs repeated trials. Day 63 judges a cause from repeats. Day 64 compares ice, wind, roots, burrows and rain one record at a time. Day 65 measures a plant-cover comparison.

**4-ESS1-1, day 66 only.** "Identify evidence": the revised canyon model ends in a question about earlier landscapes. Nothing is taught or checked about layers here.

Limits that bite: one process per record; loose sand in a tray and clay are analogues, never a rate for a solid-rock canyon; a river contributes to canyon cutting beside weathering and gravity; turf differs from bare sand in more than roots, so the day 65 comparison cannot isolate roots. Supplied records are invented for practice and never pooled with live readings.

Connections: data in tables and bar graphs (grade 2 and 3, day 62); ruler reading and subtraction for travel (day 65); fair test (day 52). Math unit 6 uses whole centimeters and equal cupfuls, no conversions.

Review in the ledger's words: "erosion", "weathering: breaking rock material down" (day 58), "baseline" (day 59), "fair test" (day 52), "read whole centimeters from ruler marks" (day 3), "bar graph" (grade 3 weather table), "cause-and-effect arrows" (day 57), "gravity" and "layer" (grade 3), "two-column source notes" (day 30). New terms: "abrasion: rubbing that wears material down" and "control: a matched comparison left untreated" (day 61), "graph interval: amount between neighboring scale marks" (day 62), "burrow: an animal-dug opening" (day 64), "turf: grass with roots and attached soil" (day 65).

Teacher page must explain: breaking, carrying and settling are three jobs; a distance measures carrying, never breaking. Repeats cannot remove a confound. A tray result limits itself to the tested conditions. Rain shapes land and decides which plants can live there.

- **E61.** [s04-X61] "Invented for practice. Clay cracks widen, but its pieces remain touching. Stones change position without visible size or shape changes. Compare their weathering evidence, including what the stone record lacks." Key: clay cracks show breakage in place; stone movement alone does not show weathering; new chips or changed edges would help. Wrong: "Clay cannot weather while its pieces stay together." (A).
- **E62.** [s04-X62-graph] "Invented for practice. Low-flow distances: 4, 5, 8 centimeters. High-flow: 6, 7, 6. Graph all six results on equal intervals." Key: bars 4, 5, 8 and 6, 7, 6, zero start, conditions and trials labeled. Wrong: "High flow always goes farther, so omit low-flow 8." (C). [s04-X62-claim] "Evaluate the claim that higher flow always carries sediment farther." Key: not supported; low reaches 8 where high reaches 6; the results overlap; water can still carry sediment. Wrong: "Repeats prove high flow always carries farther." (C). [s04-XB62-process] "Which process do the sediment-front distances measure directly?" Key: carrying, or erosion; distance does not show breakage. Wrong: "Distance cannot show erosion because no rock broke." (A).
- **E63.** [s04-X63] "Invented for practice. A steep pebble-covered tray loses less sand than a gentle bare tray twice. Evaluate the pebble claim with a corrected comparison." Key: slope also differs; repeats do not remove that; match slope, water, sand and pouring, change only pebbles. Wrong: "Two repeats prove pebbles caused it." (C).
- **E64.** [s04-X64] "Invented for practice. A rain-fed hollow has fresh runoff grooves and water-needing plants. Propose a comparison that tests rain's effect on both." Key: matched rain-exposed and rain-excluded plots before and after; grooves and plant condition tracked separately; matched starting plants, soil and light; recovery after watering strengthens the plant evidence. Wrong: "Rain can reshape soil but cannot affect the plants that grow." (E). Four brief labels in the Exit box: [s04-XB64-ice] chips beside a new crack on icy pavement. Key: breakage, weathering. Wrong: "No weathering because nothing traveled." (A). [s04-XB64-wind] dust gathers behind a windbreak. Key: dust arrived and settled; its origin is not shown. Wrong: "Dust must have been newly broken by wind." (x). [s04-XB64-root] a root lifts a slab. Key: a living thing changed land. Wrong: "Living things cannot change land." (D). [s04-XB64-burrow] a tunnel roof collapses. Key: land above the opening fell; the animal made the opening; the trigger is not shown. Wrong: "Animals cannot change land." (D).
- **E65.** [s04-X65-distance] "Invented for practice. Planted sediment moves from ruler mark 3 to 10 centimeters. Bare sediment moves from 1 to 5. Find both travel distances." Key: planted 7, bare 4 (end minus start). Wrong: "Planted moved 10 and bare moved 5." (x: end reading taken as travel). [s04-X65-cause] "The planted slope also received more water. Does the record isolate a plant-cover effect?" Key: no, water differs; this does not show plants cannot affect land. Wrong: "Water does not matter; cover alone caused it." (C). [s04-XB65-roots] "Predict how established roots could affect sediment under equal water." Key: roots could hold sediment, reducing what moves; not a guarantee for every slope. Wrong: "Plants cannot change land." (D).
- **E66.** [s04-X66-model] "Invented for practice. A coast sequence: a cracked cliff ledge, then fallen blocks on a beach, then waves carry loose grains away and expose striped rock. Revise a model to explain it." Key: cracks came first; gravity moved blocks; waves carried loose grains; the record does not show what first made the cracks. Wrong: "Rock can never change, so the sequence cannot show changes." (B). [s04-X66-question] "Ask one question about earlier landscape history." Key: Do fossils in the lower stripe show an earlier environment? Any answerable layer question. Wrong: "The picture gives every layer an exact age." (x).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Carried: the cause-and-effect arrows (revised day 62 from the child's own trial notes, unsupported links marked uncertain); the data graph begun on day 62 (one actual pair graphed, the rest tabled); the canyon model, revised on day 66 for scale and stability: small repeated changes add up over a long time. Original records stay beside revisions; practice from days 65 and 66 waits for day 70.

- **Key moment 1, day 65: rooted turf against bare sand under matched water.** Ideal instrument: matched stream tables with a ruler at each sediment front. Spec: two household trays, damp sand, turf, equal cupfuls and rulers fit; the ruler's step is 1 centimeter, so small movement reads as no detected movement. Turf also changes structure and water soaking, so roots alone are not isolated, said aloud. Gaps: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, day 61: enclosed abrasion.** Ideal instrument: a tumbler and a fine scale. Spec: a secured jar, soft stone and water, shaken by an adult 100 times, partly fits; a visible change may not appear and is not promised. The unfrozen control and the unshaken jar are matched.
- **Day 63, optional: stream-table simulation (light), practice with feedback, five minutes, replacing the Paper comparison.** Verdict: does not exist; spec only; partly fits until built. Paper claim cards are the complete route and the default. Gaps: TOOL-ASKS.md.

Shared equipment fits. Day 65: six groups of five, four minutes each (45 seconds pour, 150 seconds of five readings at 30 seconds, 45 seconds reset), 24 minutes in all. Day 61: two demonstrations of ten minutes each, seen by all, one adult handling. Clean-up (days 61 and 65): children wipe their own goggles at once, one minute, wipes supplied; an adult and one handler contain solids, wipe spills and wash, three minutes; papers are filed, one minute. Runoff settles in the bucket, never the sink. Children who handled plants or soil wash in two staggered rounds of the last minutes, not at once.

A room with nothing: dated photographs and invented records, said aloud to be invented, give no firsthand credit. Day 62 graphs the invented slope panel if no day 60 readings exist, one pair only.
