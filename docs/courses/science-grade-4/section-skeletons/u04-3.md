Reserved case, invented for practice: a new valley has layers P, Q and R in that undisturbed bottom-to-top order. P holds rooted land plants, Q holds marine shells in place, R has no diagnostic fossils. The same distinctive three-layer sequence and fossil pattern occurs across the valley and at a distant exposure. A side exposure shows all three layers bent together and displaced along a break. A river channel lies in a notch in the valley wall; the bent, displaced layers end at the notch walls, and gravel on the notch floor lies flat. A second distant exposure has layers of the same colours as P, Q and R in a different order, with no rooted-plant layer. Explain a possible relative history, identifying evidence for each claim, and reject the claim that all these features appeared at once. Use the revised landscape model. Key: land conditions preceded marine conditions; P preceded Q then R; correlated sequences support formerly connected or regionally extensive layers, with uncertainty rather than colour alone, and the second exposure does not match because order and fossils differ; bending and displacement followed the layers, and channel cutting followed that displacement.
