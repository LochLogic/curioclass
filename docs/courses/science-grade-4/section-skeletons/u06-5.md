## 1. Standards and practices

- **4-LS1-2** (days 113, 114, 115, 117): Use a model to show how animals take in information through their senses, process it in the brain, and react in different ways.

| 115 | 4-PS4-2 | Develop a seeing model and test a cause by changing illumination. | Item 1, 17 minutes, 10 points. Reserved case, invented for practice: a screen stops a lamp lighting a dull tile directly. A mirror redirects lamp light around the screen onto the tile; the viewer sees the tile directly. Supply a labeled plan drawing. The tile and mirror sit inside a closed box with a viewing hole; the lamp is the only light in the box. Draw and explain the full light path. Predict what happens when an opaque card blocks lamp-to-mirror light while tile-to-eye stays clear. Evidence flap: the tile disappears; removing the card restores it. Revise or confirm the model. Answer: lamp to mirror to tile to eye; the mirror illuminates the tile rather than showing its image. An open tile-to-eye route is insufficient without illumination. Two points each: source-to-mirror link, mirror-to-tile-to-eye links, reflection explanation, blocked-illumination prediction, evidence-based revision or confirmation. Wrong: arrows leave the eye (A); tile remains visible without illumination (B); light crosses the opaque card (I). | section 6.1 |
| 115 | 4-LS1-2 | Use a model to test information, processing and different responses. | Item 2, 17 minutes, 10 points. Reserved records, invented for practice: two animals smell the same odor. P previously found food with it; Q previously met danger with it. P approaches and Q retreats. Model what each animal's body and brain do from smelling the odor to moving. Predict Q's response after repeated safe food experiences with that odor. Evidence flap: Q now approaches although the odor is unchanged. Test and revise or confirm the model. Answer: odor information reaches noses; brains process it with remembered experience and guide different actions. Changed experience can change action without changing input or sense organ. Do not infer all animals always respond identically. Two points each: information and receiving organs, brain processing link, different response links, memory-based interpretation, tested prediction with evidence. Wrong: nose directly commands legs (D); remembered experience cannot change action (E). | section 6.1 |
| 116 | 4-LS1-1 | Construct a plant argument covering four functions and interacting structures. | Item 3, 18 minutes, 12 points. Reserved sensitive-plant record, invented for practice: roots absorb water and anchor the plant; a dated record shows leaves drooping when the soil is dry and firm after watering; internal paths carry water to leaves. A dated sequence shows leaves folding after a touch and opening later. A drawing shows a bee carrying pollen between flowers and a later whole pod. Argue how interacting inside and outside structures support survival, growth, behavior and reproduction; name an evidence limit. Answer: roots, internal paths and leaves supply resources for survival and growth; folding after touch is behavior that protects the leaves; flowers, a pollinator and the pod cooperate in seed production. The whole pod does not show the seeds inside or every step of seed formation. Two points each: evidence-linked survival, growth, behavior, reproduction, internal-external interactions, and evidence relevance and limits. One point means partial support, zero absent or incorrect. Wrong: only outside parts matter (F); petals alone make seeds (G). | section 6.1 |
| 116 | 4-LS1-1 | Construct an animal argument covering four functions and interacting structures. | Item 4, 18 minutes, 12 points. Reserved sea-otter record, invented for practice, with grounded facts and original diagrams: dense fur traps insulating air; paws bring food to a mouth and internal digestive parts; lungs and heart supply the body; eyes, brain and paws cooperate in grooming. Diagrams show a pup developing in the uterus before live birth and milk-producing glands nourishing it afterward. The mother grooms and nurses a growing pup. Argue how inside and outside structures support all four functions, naming a limit. Answer: fur retains warmth but milk and digestion supply nourishment; sense-brain-paw interactions guide grooming; uterus and milk glands support reproduction and young growth. The records establish neither that fur alone suffices nor that every pup survives. Award two points each for evidence-linked survival, growth, behavior, reproduction, internal-external interactions, and evidence relevance and limits. One point means partial support, zero absent or incorrect. Wrong: fur is the only structure that matters (F). | section 6.1 |
