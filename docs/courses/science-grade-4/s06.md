## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Can a cat see in complete darkness, and how does a plant that cannot move get water, stay safe and spread its young?"
Day 94 reveals an original illustrated cat-at-night sequence: eyes shine during a flash; the cat crosses a dim yard and turns at a sound. Ask the compelling question before explanations. Day 104 reveals yesterday's white flower beside a newly colored one and starts the class comparison.
Day 113 closes both anchors: explain reflected light, sensed information guiding action, and interacting structures using light-box records, signal trials, cut stems and flowers. A cut stem shows transport, not root uptake; source cards supply that missing evidence.
Model an evidence-linked arrow on day 96 and an evidence-based argument on day 110. Then ask which observation challenges an account; step back as children test and revise.

<!-- skel -->
- **4-PS4-2:** Model how we see an object when light bounces off it and enters the eye. Limit: Leaves out which colors are reflected and seen, how eye cells work, and how the retina works.
- **4-LS1-2:** Use a model to show how animals take in information through their senses, process it in the brain, and react in different ways. Emphasis: the system that moves information from senses to brain to response. Limit: Leaves out how the brain stores and recalls memories and how sense receptors work.
- **4-LS1-1:** Argue that the inside and outside parts of plants and animals do jobs that help them survive, grow, behave and reproduce. Limit: Only parts large enough to see without a microscope.
