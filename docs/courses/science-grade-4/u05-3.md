Key: Two causal site-specific sketches, such as intercepting above, diverting beside or raising the entrance. Wrong: "Draw the same idea twice."   Shape: s05-X86-ideas: Two distinct feasible water-route changes. s05-X86-control: One relevant shared starting condition.
- **E87.** [s05-X87-compare] "Invented for practice. Goal: both house clusters stay dry. Small flow leaves a barrier's upstream houses dry and downstream houses wet. A channel leaves both clusters dry. Compare the solutions against the goal." Key: The channel better meets both-clusters-dry under this small flow. Barrier protects only upstream. Wrong: "The channel must protect at every larger flow." (E). [s05-X87-failure] "Use the same record. Record the barrier's failure evidence." Key: Downstream houses wet despite upstream houses dry. The cause is not established by wetness alone. Wrong: "No failure occurred because every barrier guarantees protection." (D).
  Shape: s05-X87-compare: Use both clusters and limit the claim to supplied condition. s05-X87-failure: Retain downstream wetting without inventing overflow/diversion.
- **E88.** [s05-X88-revise] "Invented for practice. A channel protects at small flow. Larger flow bypasses its end and wets houses. A platform protects at both flows. Revise the recommendation using both flow records." Key: Prefer platform on these tested records, or justify a conditional extended-channel proposal requiring retesting. Wrong: "Small-flow success proves larger-flow success." (E); "Any wall guarantees protection in every flood." (D). [s05-X88-improve] "Use the same record. Name an improvement to test against the recorded failure." Key: Extend the channel against end bypass, then retest matched flows; accept another change addressing that failure. Wrong: "No improvement is needed because any wall guarantees protection." (D).
  Shape: s05-X88-revise: Use larger-flow counterevidence without universal claims. s05-X88-improve: Improvement linked to observed end bypass.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Teacher holds tray/board; each child keeps dated logs. Day 86 prepares models, day 87 small-flow log, day 88 larger-flow revision. Review "revise the record in a second color, keeping the first drawing" (science-grade-3 u01-2 day 8; US spelling in print), retaining actual observations.

Ideal instrument: matched stream-table trials with visible routes/controlled input. Baking tray partly fits: observable routes, no natural discharge measurement. One adult-only wet station: damp sand, clay, cardboard houses, equal cups, catch bucket, cloth, splash goggles. Ask sand/clay allergies first. Children handle dry templates/guides only; adult installs wet models. All near splashing wear goggles for setup/work/cleanup. Pour below faces, wipe spills immediately, no tasting. Adult settles sediment and disposes outdoors where permitted, never in drains.

Small flow: one level cup; larger: two successive level cups, same inlet/height/pace. Whole cupful is input step; counts are whole houses. Pretest sight lines and restore the same starting bed each run. If water soaks in or route is unreadable, one safe reset inside reset budget, then announce Flood fallback W, invented for practice. Keep its observations separate, flagging missing practical evidence.

Fallback W, two trials per condition, three houses each: wall small 3/3 and 3/3 dry, large 1/3 and 1/3 with overtopping; channel small 3/3 and 2/3, large 1/3 and 0/3 with bank spill; platform small 3/3 and 3/3, large 2/3 and 2/3 with edge wetting. These suggest condition-dependent protection and improvement at recorded failure points, not a real-place claim. No digital/key tool. No-kit uses guides and W, never claims physical testing.
