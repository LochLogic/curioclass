## Header

Science Grade 4. Unit 6, [Senses and structures](s06.md). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is tried with children. The facts link supplies this section's days and kinds.

## 1. Standards and practices

**4-LS1-1: "Construct an argument".** Bring animals to equal depth with plants: inside/outside parts support survival, growth, behavior and reproduction through interactions. Argument joins LS1.A and systems on diagrams and dated records. Organ lists alone are not arguments.

Day 109 teaches heart, lungs, stomach, brain and skin as visible-scale parts. Bird diagrams show ovaries, internal egg formation, shell/contents and development; not every egg develops. Also teach nonhuman mammal uterus (womb) and milk glands before unfamiliar assessment use, using a generic mammal diagram. No human organs or student-body comparisons. Day 110 models a complete single-function argument from familiar plant records: claim, located evidence and a reason joining them. Children develop the other function links separately; the full four-function synthesis stays in the exit. Day 111 retrieves reasons/evidence, W.4.1. Define interaction as parts working together, and fertile egg as an egg capable of development. Explain that one observation can support survival and growth, but a confident label is not sufficient evidence. Review cause/effect and grade 3 claim/evidence/reason; no new cellular mechanism.

- **E111, s06-X111.** "Bird F's beak supplies digestive parts; lungs, heart and skin support the body. Eyes and brain guide shelter-seeking. Inside reproductive parts form a fertile egg.   Key: beak-digestion links nourish for survival and growth; sensing-brain links guide shelter-seeking; reproductive parts, shell and contents support development. Sample: "Beak and digestion nourish the growing bird. Eyes and brain guide shelter-seeking. Inside parts and the egg support developing young." Wrong: "The beak and feathers alone do every job" (F).

- **E112, s06-X112 / s06-X112-animal.** One two-panel task: "Repair both arguments using the diagrams. Include one evidence limit in the plant repair."
  Panel A: "Outside leaves alone supply the whole water route." Diagram A links roots, inside stem paths and leaves. Matched rooted seedlings have equally damp soil. After an inside-path break, one droops; the intact seedling stays firm.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 109: "Which inside part helps this job?" Day 110: "What evidence would strengthen that claim?" Day 112: "Which observation supports this sentence?"

Day 111 writing adds locators to already-valid separate claims. Judge source linkage, a functioning interaction and honest limits. Complete short arguments occur on exits, not matching practice sheets. Pairs take two simultaneous 45-second turns. Teacher hears a rotating six, 45 seconds each, within five minutes during work; record names. Every other child hands back paper. No thirty-child speaking round.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: survival, growth, behavior, reproduction, interaction, sufficient. Frame: "The ___ works with ___, as shown by ___." Read source labels aloud; independent load at most 180 words. Read directions and evidence aloud without supplying links. Offer enlarged diagrams, tactile arrows, a scribe and home-language explanations. Keep an individual paper response; a recognition-only answer cannot replace an explanation. Printed sentences have at most 15 words within DEFAULTS.md caps. Public sharing is optional; no personal disclosures. Extend by proposing evidence that could reject a plausible claim.
