## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Teacher holds the dated display and question board; children keep traces, observations and tags. Review "question board: notices become questions" (science-grade-3 u01-1 day 1). Day 77 starts movable-strip models; day 81 changes evidence tags, keeping originals.

Ideal map instrument: aligned dated layers retaining keys and selection limits. Map plotter (light), day 81, show why, five minutes; verdict: does not exist, spec only, [asks](TOOL-ASKS.md). Paper overlays fit and are the default. No key tool. Ideal topographic instrument: relief and slice traces. Adult clay demonstration partly fits: relief, not a measured landscape. Slice at one-centimeter ruler marks, ruler step one centimeter; record actual count, never fractional heights. Teacher alone handles clay/blunt slicer on a tray; children shape dry paper models and trace prepared outlines. Ask clay allergies beforehand; all wear splash goggles. If outlines smear, stop and use slice photographs or an original outline sequence, labeled invented for practice.

No-kit: paper relief and printed maps, missing firsthand evidence named. If extracts are absent, announce invented Map fallback U, showing land, ridge, trench, coastal/interior belts. Never label it real or recent. No classroom download dependency.

## 9. Supports and extension

Read source cards aloud. Student print, including labels and tables, follows DEFAULTS.md: at most 350 words, sentences of 15 words or fewer, normal punctuation.
