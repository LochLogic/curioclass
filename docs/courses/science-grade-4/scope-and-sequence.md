# Scope and sequence: Science Grade 4

Course `science-grade-4`, grade 4, draft, fixed 2026-11-15.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-4/days.csv). The frame's six units retain their order, 14 owned progression rows, 15 owned expectations and 111 days. Nine course-level days make 120. Each course day is 45 minutes. School day is `ceil(course day x 5 / 4)`, giving four meetings per five school days through week 30. Weeks 31 and 32 remain available to other subjects. This follows the grade 4 entries in `data/plans/calendar.csv`; it does not use the older draft calendar. Every unit uses section id `<unit>.1` pending section planning.

These are original Curio Class investigations, with no base text and no source-map file. The science performance expectations are the base; a state framework is only a crosswalk and adds no owned content. The dimension elements were checked against `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv`. Assessment descriptions reserve evidence and purposes, not items or keys. Every assessment covers each part of every code it lists, through a short prompt per part or an explicitly named observed performance or product. End-of-unit records score each performance once. Course-level checks use new cases and a task form not given in the preceding two weeks, claim only sampled codes, and never re-score a unit portfolio. An individual observation or conference within ten course days is reused unchanged, with no second collection or score on that record. Minute budgets plan for 30 students. All goals and notes are original Curio Class wording; there is no adapted lesson purpose to carry over. No unit exceeds 25 days, so none requires a mid-unit assessment. Daily notebook and model evidence guides adjustment; section plans will place section-closing exit tickets.

## Unit 1: Faster and harder

15 days, course days 1 to 15, school days 2 to 19, weeks 1 to 4. The marble rolling into a cup opens the question of why the faster ball does more. Students first decide what evidence would distinguish faster and slower rolls, collect repeated cup-distance measurements, and explain the pattern. Contact collisions then raise questions about where energy goes. Initial explanations on day 1 become energy-transfer arrows on day 10 and a consensus explanation on day 13. Speed stays qualitative and energy is never assigned a numerical value. Cup distance, rather than speed or energy, is measured.

The load is six days on establishing the speed-energy explanation, six on questioning, predicting and tracing collision changes, and three shared synthesis, assessment and response days. This gives the unfamiliar energy idea and its evidence more time than vocabulary. Grade 3 motion patterns and contact-force arrows are retrieval tasks on days 1 and 7; no older unused science row needs a readiness check. A worked line-plot example on day 3 retrieves earlier mathematics. ELA's informative writing is still being taught in unit 1, school days 1 to 38, so day 5 provides the frame's three-to-five-sentence science explanation support.

Day 14 uses three minutes to launch, 17 for the evidence explanation, 15 for a question and prediction, seven to review and three to collect for all 30, without an oral round. It assesses an unseen rolling-object case: evidence linking speed with energy, a testable question, and a prediction about collision energy changes. Students account for motion and sound and interpret supplied evidence of slight warming without claiming they measured tiny temperature changes. Day 15 responds once to that evidence, with its final ten minutes reserved for unit 2's sunlight-warming readiness check. The readiness result selects day 18 support; day 16 remains with its hook and question board.

Candidate settled: **keep collision sounds at two speeds**, one day, day 9, already inside the 15. Listening tallies provide a second observation stream without requiring recording equipment or quantitative sound analysis.

## Unit 2: Energy on the move

23 days, course days 16 to 38, school days 20 to 48, weeks 4 to 10. The hand-crank flashlight raises the need to trace energy beyond moving objects. Eight opening days connect observations of sound, light, heat and current. Days 20 to 22 give every student a circuit-wiring turn before a team chooses a device. An adult first tests the battery, bulb and buzzer together at their rated voltages; salvaged flashlight parts are not assumed compatible. Six design days then establish criteria, compare proposals, assemble the default lid alarm, identify failure and refine it. Day 24 includes ten minutes of reading about stored-energy signal mechanisms before selecting a proposal. The shared criterion is a signal detected from an agreed back-wall observation point on five of five lid openings, within one desk, the available materials, budget and time. Days 26 and 28 record comparable five-opening trials before and after refinement. The particular threshold and classroom limits are judgment calls; an equivalent accessible criterion may replace them if recorded before testing. Five resource days answer the device's next question, where useful energy and fuels come from: the old claim critique folds into day 34. The remaining four days synthesize, assess twice and respond. Models begin on day 16, are revised after transfer observations on day 23, and reach consensus on day 35.

The readiness check is on day 15, not the unit opener: `sk-sun-warms` was last explicitly taught in kindergarten; grade 1 day 38 only retrieves it. Day 16 preserves the flashlight hook and question board; the identified group gets its one-lesson warming reteach inside day 18's investigation. Day 24 retrieves grade 3 problem definition; day 30 retrieves environmental-change reasoning. Day 18 teaches the materials-light part of `s1-light-materials` through passing, blocking and redirecting a beam. It does not teach seeing by reflected light, so it supplies no maintenance claim for `s1-see-with-light`. Day 17 observes energy transfer by sound without requiring the causal vibration model that unit 3 will check. Day 18 uses direct sunlight for warming, never assumes a cool lamp will warm the surface, and carries a weather fallback: dated warming records plus a pretested solar calculator observation where one is already available, with a live sunlight retry within days 19 to 23 where possible. The record identifies the limitation if live warming cannot be observed. The same sunlight condition applies to day 18 reteaching.

Bridges are counted in the relevant days. Math's multistep work comes later, units 6 and 8 at school days 77 to 95 and 113 to 127: day 24 uses two steps of whole-number addition and subtraction. Large-number work starts school day 58: day 34 uses teacher-prepared shares out of ten, labels within 1,000 and adult reading of larger totals, with no percent. ELA note-taking spans school days 39 to 77 and overlaps this work, so day 31 supplies the two-column source sheet. Regional resources in social studies come on school days 107 to 132; day 30 supplies the resource facts itself.

Day 36 assesses `4-PS3-2` through new sound, light, heat and current observation stations, and `4-PS3-4` through previously collected individual design-test-refine evidence plus an unfamiliar converting-device trace. The actual build record is scored once here; a new build or interview is not required. Each child independently identifies starting and ending forms and the converting part. For 30 students, six groups of five use four five-minute observation stations and two five-minute independent device/portfolio tasks, with three minutes to launch, seven to synthesize and five to clean up. The adult pretests the sunlight and circuit evidence and places those stations together for supervision. The sunlight contingency records its limitation and reserves a live retry.

Day 37 independently assesses `4-ESS3-1` using two new sources: natural origin, energy need, renewal, technology extracting or using the resource, environmental effects, and how an improvement changes one effect while others remain. Day 33 teaches this with a sourced before-and-after pollution-control comparison, kept qualitative. Day 38 supplies the single response allocation for both assessment days. The lid alarm uses stored battery energy for sound within the device boundary; an optional rubber-band car needs its own controls and the same evidence. A solar-heater observation can support transfer but cannot replace the qualifying device. Neither choice adds a day.

Candidate settled: **keep the state energy profile**, one day, day 34. It makes the source-combination work local without adding advanced mathematics. Sensitivity notes on days 32 and 34 protect students from judgments about family employment. Evidence settles environmental claims; no resource or occupation is cheered or blamed. Team design on day 27 makes explicit that scientists and engineers collaborate and improve everyday technologies; day 33 connects changing needs to technology choices.

## Unit 3: Waves and messages

15 days, course days 42 to 56, school days 53 to 70, weeks 11 to 14. The floating cork opens a puzzle about movement without travel. Six days establish repeating water and rope patterns, amplitude, peak spacing and object motion; the lengthwise coil provides a second mechanical-wave example. Six days then address failed communication: research and an encoding reading precede generation of two solutions, peer proposals, tests under two conditions, and comparison against criteria and constraints. Three closing days synthesize, assess and respond. This protects the design practice rather than dividing days equally between standard codes.

Day 41's last ten minutes check `s1-sound-vibrates`, last taught in grade 1. Signal devices are not in the check: grade 3 day 25 builds on them (one-hop use), so they were last used in grade 3 and day 43 retrieves `s1-signal-device`. Day 42 keeps the cork hook and question board together. Day 43 gives the one-lesson response within the repeating-wave investigation: a secured vibrating ruler answers the readiness evidence and a distant tap or flash retrieves the signal idea, while ready students compare wave evidence. Grade 3 problem definition and this year's energy arrows receive retrieval. The wave drawing begins on day 42, is revised from contrasting observations on days 44 and 46, and reaches consensus on day 54. Day 46 explicitly connects scientific findings with observed patterns.

Math patterns come on school days 96 to 110 and geometry on days 128 to 149. Days 44 and 49 therefore carry the frame's bridges: ordinary peak and trough language and a stated code rule. Drawings and an unnumbered strip compare spacing qualitatively. Numerical amplitude or wavelength is neither required nor assessed; the frame now states the same qualitative boundary. Only repeating waves are used; no interference, electromagnetic waves or technical names for the two mechanical-wave kinds.

Day 55 keeps one end day: an unfamiliar wave-model revision, an unfamiliar-code comparison and digital encoding, transmission and decoding. Each child's research, generated proposals, peer exchange and two-condition test record is scored once for `3-5-ETS1-2`; no new build or oral round is added. The 45-minute budget is three minutes to launch, 15 for the wave model, 20 for the new-code comparison and digital task, four to identify individual portfolio evidence and three to collect. Actual test participation is recorded during days 50 to 53, not collected again on day 55. Day 56 responds once. Candidate settled: **drop the string-and-cup telephone**, zero days. It would compete with research, peer sharing and the two-condition comparison already needed inside 15 days; rope, coil and drums provide the sound and wave evidence.

## Unit 4: The changing land

17 days, course days 57 to 73, school days 72 to 92, weeks 15 to 19. The canyon's river and matching walls launch an initial landscape model. Ten days separate breaking rock from carrying sediment, plan and measure a one-variable comparison, graph results and connect rain and organisms with land change. Three days then reconstruct landscape history using fossils, matching strata and a bent or broken layer case; the old second claim critique folds into day 69. Four closing days hold consensus on day 70, assessments on days 71 and 72 and response on day 73. Day 66 connects the two content blocks. This weighting gives first-hand measurements and data interpretation more time than reading the already familiar fossil evidence.

Day 56's last ten minutes check `s2-erosion-solutions`: grade 3 day 109 retrieves that row beside flood-harm criteria but does not teach its erosion causes. Day 59 gives the one-lesson response before trials. Day 57 retrieves only the long-time part of `s2-fast-slow-events` taught by grade 3 fossil days 84 to 85. Weather tables and bar graphs retrieve on day 62, erosion solutions on day 65 after response, and fossils on day 67. The hook day no longer carries the other retrievals. The distinction between weathering and erosion is revised on day 62, connected back to the canyon on day 66 and synthesized on day 70. Freezing starts on day 59 for observation on day 61, with dated photographs if it fails. The model landscape is bent and broken on day 68 before students interpret real deformed strata. Day 69 explicitly uses the assumption that past and present natural processes work consistently. Day 67 also reads a layered-rock card set with marine shells above plant fossils, evidence that land became sea. Day 69 compares two landscape accounts and identifies the evidence for each claim and the unsupported alternative, absorbing old day 70. This reuses argument before unit 6's plant and animal work.

Math measurement spans school days 77 to 95, overlapping this unit, so day 59 keeps equal cupfuls and whole centimetres without conversion throughout the investigations. ELA research spans school days 39 to 77; day 69 uses its source routine with a brief refresher because the beginning overlaps. Day 71 assesses `4-ESS2-1`: new first-hand one-variable erosion observations and measurements, plus short weathering, rainfall and living-thing evidence. Six groups of five use one tray for four minutes each; others independently annotate process records. Three minutes to launch, 24 for turns, 13 for explanation and five for cleanup make 45. Day 72 assesses `4-ESS1-1` only: a new history case with fossil order, matching formations near and far and bent or broken layers, identifying the evidence for each change. Day 73 responds once to both assessments. Earth's great age is stated plainly from evidence, with relative order rather than numerical dating.

Candidate settled: **keep a photographed local landform**, one day, day 58. A teacher photograph set gives local evidence without requiring a trip, access to dangerous exposures or a change of audience or budget.

## Unit 5: Earth's patterns and hazards

17 days, course days 77 to 93, school days 97 to 117, weeks 20 to 24. Earthquake and volcano map belts open an initial pattern model. Six days build land, ice and seafloor map reading, topographic meaning and the table-before-pattern routine. Eight days then research hazards, generate alternatives and compare earthquake, flood, volcano-monitoring and tsunami-warning solutions. Three days synthesize, assess and respond. More time goes to design comparison than locating places because testing across likely conditions requires repeated observations.

Day 76's last ten minutes check `s2-water-where`, including solid ice and liquid water, last taught in grade 2. The land-water model is not in the check: grade 3 days 104 to 117 build on it (one-hop use), so day 78 retrieves `s2-land-water-model`. Day 77 keeps its hook and question board intact. Day 78 provides the one-lesson response on water locations and retrieves the constructed land-water model before topography; ready students compare ice and seafloor maps. Social studies unit 1 physical maps are also retrieved; its state maps overlap this unit, school days 107 to 132, so day 78 teaches topography itself. ELA maps, diagrams and two-source integration were taught by school day 77. The unit retrieves grade 3 hazard criteria, unit 3's compare-several-solutions routine and this year's erosion evidence; no second erosion-readiness check is needed.

Students revise the map model on day 81, use actual shake-table and small/large-flow evidence on days 85 to 88, and reach consensus on day 91. Day 92 assesses new land and seafloor map data organized before inference, and several solutions for a new town against criteria, constraints and a range of conditions. The portfolio supplies testing evidence, scored once here; students distinguish reducing harm from stopping Earth processes. For 30 students, use three minutes to launch, 20 for table-and-map analysis, 18 for the several-solution comparison and four to collect; no practical or oral rotation is added. Day 93 responds once, then its last ten minutes give unit 6 readiness. No plate mechanism is taught. Community and government response comes later in social studies, school days 137 to 160; day 84 keeps science's bridge to engineering, monitoring and warnings. Science's resource and hazard explanations are available before social studies needs them.

Candidate settled: **keep one recent event in a dated public data extract**, one day, day 82. Compare it with the longer record rather than infer a belt from one event. The unit writer chooses and verifies the event then; none is invented here. Disaster examples remain general, with no graphic loss scenes, simulated victims or required personal disclosure. Days 82 to 92 carry the relevant sensitivity notes. Day 90 makes technology's role in lowering risk explicit.

## Unit 6: Senses and structures

24 days, course days 94 to 117, school days 118 to 147, weeks 24 to 30. Five days investigate the cat-at-night question and source-object-eye paths. Five extend that model through different senses, brain processing, perception, memory and action. Nine days then open the plant anchor and argue from both internal and external plant and animal structures. Five shared days integrate, transfer, assess and respond. The heavier plant-and-animal argument load receives the largest share, including both required plant cases rather than an animal-only explanation.

Day 93's final ten minutes check `s1-see-with-light` and the sensing/response part of `s1-parts-mimic`, both last taught in grade 1: grade 2 only retrieves seeing and grade 3 variation teaches survival structures, not sense-organ response. Day 94 preserves the cat hook and retrieves only the materials-light content taught on day 18. Day 95 responds within the light-box lesson, before the ray model. Grade 3 variation really teaches survival structure-function reasoning, so that part retrieves on days 99 and 104 alongside grade 3 life cycles. Initial models on days 94 and 99 are revised on days 97 and 102. A colored stem is set up on day 104 and read internally on day 107; day 108 dissects a flower for reproduction. Roots, internal transport and leaves are argued as an interacting system, not evidence that plants get most matter from soil. The paired arguments develop on days 110 and 111 and join the consensus account on day 113.

Math geometry starts school day 128, after the early light work, so day 96 draws straight paths with direction arrows without requiring the geometry term. ELA visual explanations were taught by school day 38; its argument writing ends around day 119, before the plant and animal arguments. The science evidence frame remains available. Day 97 explicitly tests and revises the common idea that eyes emit sight. Learned signals on day 101 carry the perception-and-memory element without a mechanism of memory storage or receptors.

Day 114 absorbs old day 115's transfer rehearsal. Day 115 assesses two unfamiliar paper-model cases: seeing with a mirror and opaque barrier, including a prediction and revision when the path is blocked, and sense-brain-response interactions including perception and memory. Three minutes to launch, 17 per model, five to compare and three to collect make 45. Day 116 separately assesses plant and animal arguments: inside and outside structures, survival, growth, behavior and reproduction, interacting parts and relevant, sufficient evidence. Its budget is three minutes to launch, 18 per argument and six to review and collect. Day 117 is the one response allocation for both end days. No cells, retina, reflected colors, human reproductive organs or comparisons of students' bodies are required. Adults cut stems; pollen allergies are checked before dissection. Cactus spines appear in photographs only.

Candidate settled: **drop the cut-fruit extension**, zero days. The required flower dissection and seed-dispersal evidence already supply plant reproduction; time stays with internal transport and balanced plant and animal arguments.
