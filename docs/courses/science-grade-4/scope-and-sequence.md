# Scope and sequence: Science Grade 4

Course `science-grade-4`, grade 4, draft, 2026-11-14.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-4/days.csv). The frame's six units retain their order, 14 owned progression rows, 15 owned expectations and 111 days. Nine course-level days make 120. Each course day is 45 minutes. School day is `ceil(course day x 5 / 4)`, giving four meetings per five school days through week 30. Weeks 31 and 32 remain available to other subjects. This follows the grade 4 entries in `data/plans/calendar.csv`; it does not use the older draft calendar. Every unit uses section id `<unit>.1` pending section planning.

These are original Curio Class investigations, with no base text and no source-map file. The science performance expectations are the base; a state framework is only a crosswalk and adds no owned content. The dimension elements were checked against `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv`. Assessment descriptions reserve evidence and purposes, not items or keys. Every assessment covers each part of every code it lists, through a short prompt per part or the explicitly named observed performance or portfolio. No unit exceeds 25 days, so none requires a mid-unit assessment. Daily notebook and model evidence guides adjustment; section plans will place section-closing exit tickets.

## Unit 1: Faster and harder

15 days, course days 1 to 15, school days 2 to 19, weeks 1 to 4. The marble rolling into a cup opens the question of why the faster ball does more. Students first decide what evidence would distinguish faster and slower rolls, collect repeated cup-distance measurements, and explain the pattern. Contact collisions then raise questions about where energy goes. Initial explanations on day 1 become energy-transfer arrows on day 10 and a consensus explanation on day 13. Speed stays qualitative and energy is never assigned a numerical value. Cup distance, rather than speed or energy, is measured.

The load is six days on establishing the speed-energy explanation, six on questioning, predicting and tracing collision changes, and three shared synthesis, assessment and response days. This gives the unfamiliar energy idea and its evidence more time than vocabulary. Grade 3 motion patterns and contact-force arrows are retrieval tasks on days 1 and 7; no older unused science row needs a readiness check. A worked line-plot example on day 3 retrieves earlier mathematics. ELA's informative writing is still being taught in unit 1, school days 1 to 38, so day 5 provides the frame's three-to-five-sentence science explanation support.

Day 14 assesses an unseen rolling-object case: evidence linking speed with energy, a testable question, and a prediction about collision energy changes. Students account for motion and sound and interpret supplied evidence of slight warming without claiming they measured tiny temperature changes. Day 15 responds once to that evidence, with its final ten minutes reserved for unit 2's sunlight-warming readiness check. The readiness result selects day 16 support.

Candidate settled: **keep collision sounds at two speeds**, one day, day 9, already inside the 15. Listening tallies provide a second observation stream without requiring recording equipment or quantitative sound analysis.

## Unit 2: Energy on the move

23 days, course days 16 to 38, school days 20 to 48, weeks 4 to 10. The hand-crank flashlight raises the need to trace energy beyond moving objects. Eight opening days connect observations of sound, light, heat and current. Days 20 to 22 give every student a circuit-wiring turn before a team chooses a device. An adult first tests the battery, bulb and buzzer together at their rated voltages; salvaged flashlight parts are not assumed compatible. Six design days then establish criteria, compare proposals, assemble the default lid alarm, identify failure and refine it. Day 24 includes ten minutes of reading about stored-energy signal mechanisms before selecting a proposal. The shared criterion is a signal detected from an agreed back-wall observation point on five of five lid openings, within one desk, the available materials, budget and time. Days 26 and 28 record comparable five-opening trials before and after refinement. The particular threshold and classroom limits are judgment calls; an equivalent accessible criterion may replace them if recorded before testing. Six resource days answer the device's next question, where useful energy and fuels come from. The remaining three days synthesize, assess and respond. Models begin on day 16, are revised after transfer observations on day 23, and reach consensus on day 36.

The readiness check is on day 15, not the unit opener: `sk-sun-warms` was last used in grade 1. Day 16 preserves the flashlight hook and includes a one-lesson warming reteach for the identified group. Day 24 retrieves grade 3 problem definition; day 30 retrieves environmental-change reasoning. Day 18 deliberately refreshes the two grade 1 light rows for unit 6. Day 17 observes energy transfer by sound without requiring the causal vibration model that unit 3 will check. Day 18 uses direct sunlight for warming, never assumes a cool lamp will warm the surface, and carries a weather fallback: dated warming records plus a pretested solar calculator observation where one is already available, with a live sunlight retry within days 19 to 23 where possible. The record identifies the limitation if live warming cannot be observed. The same sunlight condition applies to day 16 reteaching.

Bridges are counted in the relevant days. Math's multistep work comes later, units 6 and 8 at school days 77 to 95 and 113 to 127: day 24 uses two steps of whole-number addition and subtraction. Large-number work starts school day 58: day 34 uses teacher-prepared shares out of ten, labels within 1,000 and adult reading of larger totals, with no percent. ELA note-taking spans school days 39 to 77 and overlaps this work, so day 31 supplies the two-column source sheet. Regional resources in social studies come on school days 107 to 132; day 30 supplies the resource facts itself.

Day 37 assesses all four energy-transfer modes through observation records, the actual design-test-refine portfolio plus a new device case, and a combined-source explanation of natural origins, renewability and environmental effects. Each student independently traces the starting and ending energy forms and identifies the converting part in the unfamiliar device; the team portfolio provides actual build and test evidence. Day 38 responds once. No mid-unit assessment is required. The lid alarm uses stored battery energy for sound within the device expectation's boundary. An optional rubber-band car must retain the same design evidence and its own controls. A solar-heater observation can support energy transfer but cannot replace the assessed qualifying device; neither option adds a day.

Candidate settled: **keep the state energy profile**, one day, day 34. It makes the source-combination work local without adding advanced mathematics. Sensitivity notes on days 32 and 35 protect students from judgments about family employment. Evidence settles environmental claims; no resource or occupation is cheered or blamed. Team design on day 27 makes explicit that scientists and engineers collaborate and improve everyday technologies; day 33 connects changing needs to technology choices.

## Unit 3: Waves and messages

15 days, course days 42 to 56, school days 53 to 70, weeks 11 to 14. The floating cork opens a puzzle about movement without travel. Six days establish repeating water and rope patterns, amplitude, peak spacing and object motion; the lengthwise coil provides a second mechanical-wave example. Six days then address failed communication: research and an encoding reading precede generation of two solutions, peer proposals, tests under two conditions, and comparison against criteria and constraints. Three closing days synthesize, assess and respond. This protects the design practice rather than dividing days equally between standard codes.

Day 41's last ten minutes check `s1-sound-vibrates`, last used in grade 1 for the causal vibration idea. Day 42 includes the one-lesson reteach after the cork hook where needed. Grade 3 problem definition, the maintained signal-device row, and this year's energy arrows get retrieval tasks. The wave drawing begins on day 42, is revised from contrasting observations on days 44 and 46, and reaches consensus on day 54. Day 46 explicitly connects scientific findings with observed patterns.

Math patterns come on school days 96 to 110 and geometry on days 128 to 149. Days 44 and 49 therefore carry the frame's bridges: ordinary peak and trough language and a stated code rule. Drawings and an unnumbered strip compare spacing qualitatively. Numerical amplitude or wavelength is neither required nor assessed, respecting the expectation's boundary despite the frame's ruler language. Only repeating waves are used; no interference, electromagnetic waves or technical names for the two mechanical-wave kinds.

Day 55 assesses unfamiliar waves through model revision, and unfamiliar codes through comparison supported by each team's research, generated proposals and two-condition test portfolio. Digital encoding, transmission and decoding are included. Day 56 responds once. Candidate settled: **drop the string-and-cup telephone**, zero days. It would compete with research, peer sharing and the two-condition comparison already needed inside 15 days; rope, coil and drums provide the sound and wave evidence.

## Unit 4: The changing land

17 days, course days 57 to 73, school days 72 to 92, weeks 15 to 19. The canyon's river and matching walls launch an initial landscape model. Ten days separate breaking rock from carrying sediment, plan and measure a one-variable comparison, graph results and connect rain and organisms with land change. Five days then reconstruct landscape history using fossils, matching strata and a bent or broken layer case. Two days assess and respond. This weighting gives first-hand measurements and data interpretation more time than reading the already familiar fossil evidence.

Day 57 retrieves grade 3 weather data and fossils, grade 2 fast-slow events maintained by the fossils row, and erosion solutions maintained by grade 3 weather-hazard work. No readiness slot is needed. The distinction between weathering and erosion is revised on day 62, connected back to the canyon on day 66 and synthesized on day 71. Freezing starts on day 59 for observation on day 61, with dated photographs if it fails. The model landscape is bent and broken on day 68 before students interpret real deformed strata. Day 69 explicitly uses the assumption that past and present natural processes work consistently. Days 69 and 70 compare two landscape accounts, argue which is supported and identify unsupported claims. This reuses argument before unit 6 gives it a fuller plant and animal treatment; the day 70 fold retains the comparison within day 69.

Math measurement spans school days 77 to 95, overlapping this unit, so day 59 keeps equal cupfuls and whole centimetres without conversion throughout the investigations. ELA research spans school days 39 to 77; day 69 uses its source routine with a brief refresher because the beginning overlaps. Day 72 assesses fresh erosion observations and a new fossil-layer landscape: every explanation identifies evidence for its claimed changes, including deformation. Day 73 responds once. Earth's great age is stated plainly from evidence, with relative order rather than numerical dating.

Candidate settled: **keep a photographed local landform**, one day, day 58. A teacher photograph set gives local evidence without requiring a trip, access to dangerous exposures or a change of audience or budget.

## Unit 5: Earth's patterns and hazards

17 days, course days 77 to 93, school days 97 to 117, weeks 20 to 24. Earthquake and volcano map belts open an initial pattern model. Six days build land, ice and seafloor map reading, topographic meaning and the table-before-pattern routine. Eight days then research hazards, generate alternatives and compare earthquake, flood, volcano-monitoring and tsunami-warning solutions. Three days synthesize, assess and respond. More time goes to design comparison than locating places because testing across likely conditions requires repeated observations.

Day 76's last ten minutes check `s2-water-where`, including solid ice and liquid water. The inspected grade 3 rows do not explicitly maintain that water-location content. Day 77 includes a one-lesson reteach after the hook for students identified, preserving the first-day phenomenon. The land-water model was maintained by grade 3 climates and gets retrieval instead. Social studies unit 1 physical maps are also retrieved; its state maps overlap this unit, school days 107 to 132, so day 78 teaches topography itself. ELA maps, diagrams and two-source integration were taught by school day 77. The unit retrieves grade 3 hazard criteria and unit 3's compare-several-solutions routine.

Students revise the map model on day 81, use actual shake-table and small/large-flow evidence on days 85 to 88, and reach consensus on day 91. Day 92 assesses new land and seafloor map data organized before inference, and several solutions for a new town against criteria, constraints and a range of conditions. The portfolio supplies observed testing evidence; students distinguish reducing harm from stopping Earth processes. Day 93 responds once. No plate mechanism is taught. Community and government response comes later in social studies, school days 137 to 160; day 84 keeps science's bridge to engineering, monitoring and warnings. Science's resource and hazard explanations are available before social studies needs them.

Candidate settled: **keep one recent event in a dated public data extract**, one day, day 82. Compare it with the longer record rather than infer a belt from one event. The unit writer chooses and verifies the event then; none is invented here. Disaster examples remain general, with no graphic loss scenes, simulated victims or required personal disclosure. Days 82 to 92 carry the relevant sensitivity notes. Day 90 makes technology's role in lowering risk explicit.

## Unit 6: Senses and structures

24 days, course days 94 to 117, school days 118 to 147, weeks 24 to 30. Five days investigate the cat-at-night question and source-object-eye paths. Five extend that model through different senses, brain processing, perception, memory and action. Nine days then open the plant anchor and argue from both internal and external plant and animal structures. Five shared days integrate, transfer, assess and respond. The heavier plant-and-animal argument load receives the largest share, including both required plant cases rather than an animal-only explanation.

Unit 2 explicitly refreshed the two earlier light rows on day 18, so day 94 retrieves them rather than checks readiness. Parts and their functions were maintained by grade 3 variation work; days 99 and 104 retrieve those and grade 3 life cycles. Initial models on days 94 and 99 are revised on days 97 and 102. A colored stem is set up on day 104 and read internally on day 107; day 108 dissects a flower for reproduction. Roots, internal transport and leaves are argued as an interacting system, not evidence that plants get most matter from soil. The paired arguments develop on days 110 and 111 and join the consensus account on day 113.

Math geometry starts school day 128, after the early light work, so day 96 draws straight paths with direction arrows without requiring the geometry term. ELA visual explanations were taught by school day 38; its argument writing ends around day 119, before the plant and animal arguments. The science evidence frame remains available. Day 97 explicitly tests and revises the common idea that eyes emit sight. Learned signals on day 101 carry the perception-and-memory element without a mechanism of memory storage or receptors.

Day 116 assesses unfamiliar seeing and animal-response cases and separate plant and animal arguments. The seeing case includes a mirror and an opaque barrier, requiring a prediction and model revision when the path is blocked. Both arguments address inside and outside structures supporting survival, growth, behavior and reproduction, with interacting parts and relevant, sufficient evidence. Day 117 responds once. No cells, retina, reflected colors, human reproductive organs or comparisons of students' bodies are required. Adults cut stems; pollen allergies are checked before dissection. Cactus spines appear in photographs only.

Candidate settled: **drop the cut-fruit extension**, zero days. The required flower dissection and seed-dispersal evidence already supply plant reproduction; time stays with internal transport and balanced plant and animal arguments.

## Materials, tools and access

The optional columns are filled on every planned hands-on day. The core route uses physical observations, paper models and printed evidence, so the digital/drawn-tool column is empty; ordinary notebook drawing is not a separate application. The frame's proposed ramp-and-cup collision tool, circuit builder, wave tank tool, stream-table tool, map plotter and light-ray box tool remain possible no-kit alternatives to be specified with unit plans, not assumed working software. All digital alternatives must have the same printed evidence without a student account.

Use the settled ceiling of about 30 dollars per unit beyond classroom stock, for a class of 30, with reuse and one shared apparatus where needed. Units 1 and 3 reuse cups, tubs and ramps; unit 2 prioritizes one reusable battery, bulb, buzzer and wire set over team-specific purchased devices; units 4 and 5 share the baking-tray stream table; unit 6 uses acrylic mirrors, one shared colored-stem setup and flowers. Detailed quantities and actual cost checks belong to unit plans. Safe household stand-ins are identified in the day record. A one-set teacher demonstration is the minimum fallback for required observations, device tests, code tests, weathering/erosion work and hazard tests. Every student still predicts, records and explains, and takes a circuit-wiring turn. Supplied data alone are weaker and cannot satisfy first-hand expectations alone. Allergy-related flower access uses an adult-prepared sealed specimen first, with dated photographs where handling is impossible and the limitation recorded.

For schools alternating science and social studies in blocks, preserve the unit order and the calendar dependencies above. After a gap, retrieve the last model and question board inside the next teaching day. Give an older-row readiness check at the last actual meeting just before its unit, not weeks before a break; its response remains inside the allocated opener. Keep the course-level checks in weeks 10, 19 and 30 using the calendar adjustment order, and verify that hazards still precede social studies school day 137. This is scheduling guidance, not an additional teaching day.
