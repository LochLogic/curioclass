## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How does energy get from place to place, and where does the energy we use come from?"
Day 16: turn the sealed flashlight handle and watch the light appear. Stop and observe what happens. Students draw where energy might start and go, then pose the compelling question without receiving its answer.
On day 35, students explain the crank using transfer observations, circuit records and device tests, then connect useful energy to natural resources and environmental effects using source notes.
Demonstrate safe handling and recording, then let children predict paths, choose comparisons and challenge explanations. Model combining sources on day 31; step back for the independent resource critique on day 34.

- **4-PS3-2:** Observe to gather evidence that sound, light, heat and electric current carry energy from one place to another. Limit: Energy is not measured in numbers.
- **4-PS3-4:** Use science ideas to design, test and improve a device that turns one form of energy into another. Limit: Devices either change motion into electricity, or draw on stored energy to cause motion, light or sound.
- **4-ESS3-1:** Gather and combine information to show that energy and fuels come from natural resources and that using them affects the environment.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask "What reached the receiver?" and "Which record justifies that change?" Day 29 writing links a plan, test and refinement. Look for energy reasoning, evidence and criteria.

## U8. Practice that returns

Open later work with a fresh path or claim. Day 23 rehearses explanation without speed pressure. Shorter path: retain its different case for five minutes on day 22, replacing paper comparison; omit 40 minutes of rehearsal.

## U9. Supports and extension

Supply diagrams, read-aloud cards and source-note frames; fade completed examples by day 34. Use tactile circuit layouts and visible signal evidence. Extend by evaluating another refinement against the same criterion.

## U10. Two rooms

One child predicts with the adult and keeps the same individual records. One projector displays comparisons; paper carries every response. Shared apparatus remains necessary for firsthand evidence.
