## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How did a river make a canyon a mile deep, and what do its layers tell us?"
Day 57: show the canyon photograph in field 10 without explanatory captions. Trace the river and matching stripes. Ask the compelling question; children draw tentative explanations and post questions.
Day 70: explain how moving water removes material and exposes older layers, using tray measurements, fossil order and matching walls. Distinguish evidence of long change from an exact duration.
Model safe measurement on day 59 and evidence notes on day 69. Ask what would change a claim, then step back while children choose controls and compare accounts.

Transfer uses unfamiliar slopes, separate process records and an unfamiliar layered valley in field 5.

A tray models loose sediment, not solid-rock canyon cutting. Teacher background distinguishes breaking, carrying and settling; a river contributes to canyon cutting alongside weathering and gravity.

All 28 points require observation, measurement or evidence-based explanation with an Earth idea on cases reserved from teaching. Do not score attendance, terminology alone or initial models. Look-fors are the component keys in the tables; award one of two points for relevant but incomplete evidence.
Item 1 sample measurements, invented for practice, not supplied during the check: gentle slope trials 2 and 3 centimeters; steep slope trials 6 and 5 centimeters. Meets: "My gentle slope measured 2 centimeters and steep slope 6 centimeters from their start marks. I saw grains leave the patch and move downstream. The repeat measured 3 and 5 centimeters, so both trials support more carrying on the steeper slope with equal water and starting sand. A tray does not prove a solid-rock canyon's rate. Both stones' cracks widened, so weathering occurred but freezing alone is not established. The marked wind-blown grains changed position without shown breakage. The animal brought soil onto the surface while the excluded patch stayed unchanged. Rain cut new channels and supported upright seedlings; excluded seedlings wilted and recovered with watering, showing that water affects plant life too." Does not meet: "Both trays changed, so all causes are the same." This gives no measurements or controlled comparison and ignores the process records.
Item 2 meets: "In the undisturbed sequence, P came before Q, then R. P's rooted plants show land before Q's marine shells show sea; R's environment is uncertain. The same ordered layers and fossils nearby and far away support once-connected or regionally extensive layers, not certainty from color; the second exposure has the same colors but a different order and no plant layer, so it does not match. All three layers existed before they bent and shifted together. The river cut the already shifted layers, so cutting came later. These features cannot all have appeared together: deformation needs earlier layers, and cutting crosses the deformation. We cannot tell the exact duration or what uniquely caused the break." Does not meet: "The river has always been here and all layers are the same age." This ignores the sequence evidence.
Read prompts aloud, enlarge or provide tactile diagrams, and accept dictated explanations with each child's own measurement decisions. Day 75 reuses day 71's individual practical record unchanged, without another turn or score.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask "Where did the material go?" on day 62 and "Which layer pattern supports that history?" on day 69. Judge evidence-linked explanations, not length.

## U8. Practice that returns

Day 63 rehearses causal judgment without speed pressure. The shorter path keeps its distinct case for five minutes on day 62 and removes 40 minutes of separate rehearsal.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach weathering, erosion, layer and evidence with pictures. Offer "The observation ___ supports ___ because ___." Read cards aloud; provide large or tactile sequences and a scribe. Fade frames by day 69. Extend by comparing two defensible histories within the evidence limits.

## U10. Two rooms

One child measures with an adult, using the same controls and records. One projector displays photographs; everyone writes on paper. At home, day 73 revises one explanation together.
