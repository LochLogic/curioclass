## 1. Standards and practices

**Share.** 4-PS3-1: "Use evidence to construct an explanation" now connects repeated effects to greater motion energy. Practice: constructing explanations; PS3.A; energy and matter, with cause and effect.

**Adult explanation.** Greater target movement repeatedly supports more energy available for transfer when rolling object, target, surface and contact conditions stay fixed. Distance indicates an effect, not energy units. Inspect conditions when a trial differs; never hide it. No speed formula or different-mass ranking. Day 5 bridges W.4.2 through supplied claim, evidence and reason labels and short written evidence notes.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** instructional ownership of item 1; section 1.4 administers it. All exit cases are invented for practice, printed and said so. Five minutes, every child on paper. Keep cases and response forms out of practice. Record shown, shown with help, not yet seen; return written feedback next lesson. More than ten misses is many.

- **E5, day 5, 4-PS3-1.** Caption: "The same cylinder hits the same folded target." Record: "Every faster trial moved the target farther than every slower trial. Each speed was tried three times." Prompt: "Explain which roll had more motion energy. Use the trials and explain why target motion supports your idea." Answer: faster cylinder motion has more energy; all three faster effects exceed all three slower effects; energy passing at contact changes target motion, so controlled differences support more energy available to transfer. Wrong: unchanged cylinder means equal energy (A).
  Shape: complete written evidence-to-energy explanation of a supplied repeated record. Practice sorts supplied observation and transfer labels, then matches one record to each. It never composes a whole oral or written speed-energy explanation, uses a cylinder/folded target, or combines two numerical trial sets with a full explanation.
- **E6, day 6, 4-PS3-1.** Caption: "A hoop hits the same cardboard tube. Faster rolls move it farther in repeated trials." Draft: "The faster hoop has more energy because fast means more energy." Prompt: "Strengthen this explanation with trial evidence and a transfer reason." Answer: repeated greater tube movement supplies evidence; transfer from hoop motion makes the tube move, so greater effects in this controlled comparison support more energy available in the faster hoop. Wrong: unchanged hoop means unchanged energy (A); tube travel measures energy (B).
  Shape: strengthen an inadequate written explanation with evidence and mechanism. Practice sorts complete statements and matches evidence; no flawed draft repair card or hoop.

**Item 1 Shape:** explain speed and energy from two repeated numerical sets for an unseen swinging receiver. No practice sheet or model card combines two numerical sets and a complete written explanation. Spool and hanging flap stay sealed until day 14.

## Day 5

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return E4: A five minutes, B five, from retained marble evidence.
- **10 min. Connect evidence labels.** Teach transfer through one observed cup movement. Sort supplied labels into observed change or energy account. Review the grade 3 claim frame orally, one part at a time; do not model a complete comparative explanation.
- **15 min. Partner evidence matching.** Partners match supplied labels to specific class records. Each has two 45-second turns naming a match and its source. Teacher hears six at 45 seconds while others continue. Everyone submits a matched set; no complete comparative explanation.
- **5 min. Evidence notes.** Each child submits three short record-based notes, including those not heard.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5, individual paper. Tool: None. A to day 6 Return and repair.

**Shape:** Practice matches supplied labels to records. E5 alone composes a complete evidence-to-energy explanation; no complete comparative explanation model, cylinder or folded target in practice.

## Day 6

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Return E5 and evidence-note feedback; repair A using marble evidence.
- **10 min. Read the tire case.** Invented for practice: same toy tire hits an index card; all three faster trials move it farther than all three slower trials. Sort three supplied statements: "The tire moved faster." "The card moved farther." "Energy passed into card motion." The first two report observations; the third explains transfer. Keep the scientific account separate from measurements; no incomplete draft.
- **15 min. Match evidence.** Pairs match repeated trial strips to complete claims. Supply both: "The faster tire had more motion energy" and "The tire had equal energy" (A). Match the first to repeated greater card movement; reject the second. Each child attaches evidence; no flawed-draft repair.
- **10 min. Say the link.** Two 45-second turns per partner; teacher hears six for 45 seconds. Everyone hands in matched evidence and a brief note. Name those heard on the roster; return written next steps on day 7. Carry the transfer question forward.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E6, individual paper. Tool: None. A/B to day 7 Return and repair, ten minutes.

**Shape:** Practice sorts supplied statements and matches evidence. E6 alone strengthens a flawed written explanation; no hoop or incomplete explanation card in practice.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 5: "Why keep the target unchanged?" and "How does target movement support the claim?" Day 6: "Which repeated observation supports this reason?" Partners alternate 45-second evidence-label reports, two turns each in three minutes. Teacher hears six at 45 seconds during work; everyone submits evidence notes. Written task day 5: three short notes from three repeats, without a complete explanation template. Look for relevant observations, conditions and units where measured.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 5 reviews section 1.1's written fair test, motion energy and repeated-trial records, days 2 to 4. Day 6 reviews repeated patterns and day 5's transfer reason. Grade 3 claim, evidence and reason is review. No earlier unit of this grade. Fluency uses repeated oral linking without time pressure, no timed target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: motion energy, evidence, transfer. Oral frame: "I think __, because I saw __, so __." At most 110 independent reading words daily. Read records and stems aloud as needed. Offer enlarged records, labeled sketches or a scribe for independent explanations. Extension: discuss what overlapping trial results would and would not justify, using the same object.

## 10. Two rooms

One child: retained records or R, with adult asking for evidence without supplying the explanation. One projector: one trial at a time for oral linking; each child keeps evidence notes. No purchase or live roll required.
