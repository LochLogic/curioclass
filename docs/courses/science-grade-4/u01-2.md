## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **4-PS3-1** (days 5, 6): Explain, using evidence, how an object's speed is related to how much energy it has.
- skill s4-speed-energy (grade 4)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Model safe release on day 1 and measurement on day 3. Let students propose controls on day 2, justify patterns on days 4 to 6 and question predictions on days 7 to 12. Use notebooks to select the next explanation to discuss.
>
> Group the question board by evidence and destinations. Days 1 to 3 ask, "Was speed the difference?" Fair comparisons raise "Does it happen repeatedly?" Days 4 to 6 use repeated observations to explain speed, energy and transfer. That raises "Where does the energy go?" Days 7 to 10 ask testable questions and predict motion, sound and warming destinations. Days 11 to 13 test the explanation elsewhere and attach evidence to answered questions; retain unresolved questions. Transfer: a rolling spool strikes a hanging cardboard flap, unseen before day 14.
> Carried: cause and effect and claim, evidence and reasoning begin on day 1; days 4 and 5 revise them from repeated-trial patterns. Force arrows return on day 7; day 8 observations locate pushes at contact. Energy arrows begin on day 7; days 8 and 10 add receiving-object motion, sound and warming from collision evidence and the sourced account. Day 12 distinguishes force arrows acting on an object from energy arrows between destinations. All reach consensus on day 13 and inform day 14 models; none is withdrawn.
<!-- /skel -->

**Share.** 4-PS3-1: "Use evidence to construct an explanation" now connects repeated effects to greater motion energy. Practice: constructing explanations; PS3.A; energy and matter, with cause and effect.

**Adult explanation.** Greater target movement repeatedly supports more energy available for transfer when rolling object, target, surface and contact conditions stay fixed. Distance indicates an effect, not energy units. Inspect conditions when a trial differs; never hide it. No speed formula or different-mass ranking. Day 5 bridges W.4.2 with an oral three-to-five-sentence frame and short written evidence notes.

- **E5, day 5, 4-PS3-1.** Caption: "The same cylinder hits the same folded target." Record: "Every faster trial moved the target farther than every slower trial. Each speed was tried three times." Prompt: "Explain which roll had more motion energy. Use the trials and explain why target motion supports your idea." Answer: faster cylinder motion has more energy; all three faster effects exceed all three slower effects; energy passing at contact changes target motion, so controlled differences support more energy available to transfer. Wrong: unchanged cylinder means equal energy (A).
  Shape: complete written evidence-to-energy explanation of a supplied repeated record. Practice uses oral chains and separate evidence notes; no complete written explanation frame, cylinder or folded target.
- **E6, day 6, 4-PS3-1.** Caption: "A hoop hits the same cardboard tube. Faster rolls move it farther in repeated trials." Draft: "The faster hoop has more energy because fast means more energy." Prompt: "Strengthen this explanation with trial evidence and a transfer reason." Answer: repeated greater tube movement supplies evidence; transfer from hoop motion makes the tube move, so greater effects in this controlled comparison support more energy available in the faster hoop. Wrong: unchanged hoop means unchanged energy (A); tube travel measures energy (B).
  Shape: strengthen an inadequate written explanation with evidence and mechanism. Practice sorts complete statements and matches evidence; no flawed draft repair card or hoop.
