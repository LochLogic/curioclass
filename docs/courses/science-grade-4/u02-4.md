## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **4-ESS3-1** (days 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35): Gather and combine information to show that energy and fuels come from natural resources and that using them affects the environment.
- **4-PS3-2** (days 35): Observe to gather evidence that sound, light, heat and electric current carry energy from one place to another.
- **4-PS3-4** (days 35): Use science ideas to design, test and improve a device that turns one form of energy into another.
- skill s4-energy-resources (grade 4)
- skill s4-energy-transfer (grade 4)
- skill s4-energy-device (grade 4)

>

Day 35 forms consensus; individuals apply these models in the end task. Retain the explanation frame; force arrows remain distinct from energy paths.

**Share.** 4-ESS3-1: "Obtain and combine information" about natural origins, uses and environmental effects. Source evaluation/communication, ESS3.A and cause/effect are inseparable: cite a resource, useful energy and causal effect. Day 35 also revisits "Make observations to provide evidence" (4-PS3-2) and "Apply scientific ideas to design, test, and refine" (4-PS3-4).

**Adult explanation.** Replenishment is different from impact and from constant local availability. Electricity/batteries are manufactured carriers/stores, not natural origins. Particle filters reduce particles, not every emission or extraction effect. Nuclear fuel is nonrenewable, with waste requiring management; no atomic model. Review grade 3 u05-2 changed condition/needs reasoning and u06-2 source wording. No family employment, energy bills, illness or disaster disclosures; evidence evaluates claims without praising/blaming jobs. Connections: supported source notes W.4.8 day 31; ten-share graph day 34, without percentages or numbers above 1,000.

## 3. Checks, written first

All exits use individual paper, path blank, on a half slip. No separate spoken judged round. Every held card prints the data stem exactly; private keys are in teacher-key.md. Cases and counts labeled invented are not factual source evidence. Each exit takes the minutes in its numbered Day block.

- **E30, day 30, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. Card A describes coal burned at a plant for town lighting. These lamps use town electricity. The caption calls a battery the natural resource. Repair the caption using a resource-to-use explanation."
  Shape: repair a caption by tracing natural origin to useful energy.
  Key: Coal is the natural resource; a battery is made. Coal stores energy, converted at the generating plant into electricity for lamps. Do not insert a battery into this town-lighting path.
  Wrong: "The manufactured battery is the natural resource." (I).
  Print the corresponding exit-30.md only that day. Add exit-sources-30.md, read neutrally; source summaries remain separate from invented cases.

- **E31, day 31, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A coastal community considers wind or a natural-gas deposit. Combine Sources A and B to explain renewal and its importance for continued use."
  Shape: two-source renewal comparison linked to continued community use.
  Key: A identifies wind as naturally replenished, although output varies. B explains limited gas deposits and very long formation. Extraction uses up the deposit, not replaced on human timescales. Identify both sources in the comparison.
  Wrong: "Natural gas is replenished within human lifetimes." (J).
  Print the corresponding exit-31.md only that day. Add exit-sources-31.md, read neutrally; source summaries remain separate from invented cases.

- **E32, day 32, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A community uses coal from mountaintop removal and valley-fill mining. Combine the mining and burning sources into two environmental cause-and-effect links."
  Shape: combine extraction/use sources into two causal links.
  Key: A: this mining changes land; polluted drainage may harm downstream aquatic wildlife. B: burning releases air pollutants, including gases causing acid rain that harms plants/aquatic animals. One supported causal link per source; not every mine has identical effects.
  Wrong: "Burning fuel releases no air pollutants." (K).
  Print the corresponding exit-32.md only that day. Add exit-sources-32.md, read neutrally; source summaries remain separate from invented cases.

- **E33, day 33, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A wind generator supplies clinic lights. Matched blade trials show less noise afterward. Bird collisions stay unchanged. Use both sources and the trial record to explain improvement and a remaining effect."
  Shape: source-and-trial synthesis of useful output and changed/remaining effects.
  Key: A connects wind motion, generator and electricity/light. B identifies blade noise and component-production effects. Matched invented trials support reduced noise, not fewer collisions. Component-production effects remain; bird outcomes come from the invented record.
  Wrong: "This improvement removes every environmental effect." (F).
  Print the corresponding exit-33.md only that day. Add exit-sources-33.md, read neutrally; source summaries remain separate from invented cases.

- **E34, day 34, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. Regional chart: gas six shares, wind three, sunlight one. Claim: all this electricity uses resources with no environmental effects. Draw an ordered picture graph with a key. Use the chart and wind-effects source to judge the claim."
  Shape: ordered graph plus two-source claim judgment.
  Key: Descending rows gas six, wind three, sunlight one; key one picture per share. Chart includes wind; effects source names bird/bat collision risk, contradicting claim. Chart alone cannot show effects.
  Wrong: "Wind has no environmental effects because it is renewable." (E).
  Print the corresponding exit-34.md only that day. Add exit-sources-34.md, read neutrally; source summaries remain separate from invented cases.

- **E35, day 35, 4-PS3-2.** Print: "Invented for practice. A hand-wound buzzer toy uses a generator. Bent contact: sound on one of five winds. Straightened contact: sound on five of five. Operator, winds, toy and listening point stay the same. A case card reports mining its materials cleared habitat. Model its energy path and improvement evidence, including one resource consequence."
  Shape: new-device model joining matched refinement evidence and resource consequence.
  Key: Winding supplies motion converted by generator to electrical energy; current carries it to sound. Straighter contact maintains loop more reliably; matched one-of-five versus five-of-five supports improvement. Mining cleared habitat; contact repair does not restore it.
  Wrong: "A better contact removes the effects of mining its materials." (F); "The generator needs no input." (A); "Improvement needs no tests." (D).
  Print the corresponding exit-35.md only that day.
