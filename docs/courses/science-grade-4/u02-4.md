## 1. Standards and practices

**Share.** 4-ESS3-1: "Obtain and combine information" about natural origins, uses and environmental effects. Source evaluation/communication, ESS3.A and cause/effect are inseparable: cite a resource, useful energy and causal effect. Day 35 also revisits "Make observations to provide evidence" (4-PS3-2) and "Apply scientific ideas to design, test, and refine" (4-PS3-4).

**Adult explanation.** Replenishment is different from impact and from constant local availability. Electricity/batteries are manufactured carriers/stores, not natural origins. Particle filters reduce particles, not every emission or extraction effect. Nuclear fuel is nonrenewable, with waste requiring management; no atomic model. Review grade 3 u05-2 changed condition/needs reasoning and u06-2 source wording. No family employment, energy bills, illness or disaster disclosures; evidence evaluates claims without praising/blaming jobs. Connections: supported source notes W.4.8 day 31; ten-share graph day 34, without percentages or numbers above 1,000.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** Item 3, resource teaching; section 2.5 administers it.

Every teaching-day exit is an individual paper response, read aloud without hints. Allow sentences, labeled drawings or tables. Require the explanation, not merely a choice. No extra spoken round. Return exit and practice feedback in the next named repair slot; day 35 returns on day 38. Blank or unrelated work gets a conference, not an error letter.

- **E30, day 30, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. Card A describes coal burned at a plant for town lighting. These lamps use town electricity. The caption calls a battery the natural resource. Repair the caption using a resource-to-use explanation."
  Shape: repair a caption by tracing natural origin to useful energy. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: Coal is the natural resource; a battery is made. Coal stores energy, converted at the generating plant into electricity for lamps. Do not insert a battery into this town-lighting path.
  Wrong: "The manufactured battery is the natural resource." (I).

- **E31, day 31, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A coastal community considers wind or a natural-gas deposit. Combine Sources A and B to explain renewal and its importance for continued use."
  Shape: two-source renewal comparison linked to continued community use. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: A identifies wind as naturally replenished, although output varies. B explains limited gas deposits and very long formation. Extraction uses up the deposit, not replaced on human timescales. Identify both sources in the comparison.
  Wrong: "Natural gas is replenished within human lifetimes." (J).

- **E32, day 32, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A community uses coal from mountaintop removal and valley-fill mining. Combine the mining and burning sources into two environmental cause-and-effect links."
  Shape: combine extraction/use sources into two causal links. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: A: this mining changes land; polluted drainage may harm downstream aquatic wildlife. B: burning releases air pollutants, including gases causing acid rain that harms plants/aquatic animals. One supported causal link per source; not every mine has identical effects.
  Wrong: "Burning fuel releases no air pollutants." (K).

- **E33, day 33, 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A wind generator supplies clinic lights. Matched blade trials show less noise afterward. Bird collisions stay unchanged. Use both sources and the trial record to explain improvement and a remaining effect."
  Shape: source-and-trial synthesis of useful output and changed/remaining effects. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: A connects wind motion, generator and electricity/light. B identifies blade noise and component-production effects. Matched invented trials support reduced noise, not fewer collisions. Component-production effects remain; bird outcomes come from the invented record.
  Wrong: "This improvement removes every environmental effect." (F).

- **E34, day 34, 4-ESS3-1, judged Exit box (check practice, paper).** Print: "Invented for practice. Regional chart: gas six shares, wind three, sunlight one. Claim: all this electricity uses resources with no environmental effects. Use this chart and the wind-effects source to judge the claim."
  Shape: evidence-linked judgment using a supplied graph and source. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Supply descending graph rows gas six, wind three, sunlight one, with one picture per share. Key: Chart includes wind; effects source names bird/bat collision risk, contradicting claim. Chart alone cannot show effects.
  Wrong: "Wind has no environmental effects because it is renewable." (E).

- **E35, day 35, 4-PS3-2; 4-PS3-4; 4-ESS3-1.** Print: "Invented for practice. A hand-wound buzzer toy uses a generator. Bent contact: sound on one of five winds. Straightened contact: sound on five of five. Operator, winds, toy and listening point stay the same. A case card reports mining its materials cleared habitat. Model its energy path and improvement evidence using the matched trials."
  Shape: new-device improvement model justified by matched refinement evidence. Reserve this response structure and case; use the different practice in field 6.
  Key: Winding supplies motion converted by generator to electrical energy; current carries it to sound. Straighter contact maintains loop more reliably; matched one-of-five versus five-of-five supports improvement. Collect the mining consequence separately during Resource branches; do not score it in E35.
  Wrong: "The generator needs no input." (A); "Improvement needs no tests." (D). Resource-branch claim that contact repair removes mining effects is F, recorded in work only.

**E34-graph, day 34, graph production.** Print: "Draw an ordered bar graph with a key for this invented generation record. Gas: four shares; sunlight: four shares; wind: two shares. One grid square represents one share."

Shape: construct an ordered bar graph from a fresh three-resource record. Practice constructs a state picture graph; the preceding day follows particle paths. Keep this new record concealed.

Key: bars four, four and two; the tied bars may appear in either order before wind. Label resources and the share scale; key one square per share. Wrong: "Wind has four shares" (x), confer rather than assign a science error.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

**Carried.** Grade 3 u05-2 condition-to-organism reasoning returns on a new invented road/shrub retrieval, never a lower-grade case. Two-column notes hold statement/source location, then resource/use/effect labels extend the model day 34. Day 35 attaches evidence to familiar crank questions. Teacher preserves each original portfolio and day 30 feedback through day 35 revision.

**Instruments/tools.** Ideal: attributable source cards and a dated local generation record. Printed cards fit; unsupported local claims do not. Use the prepared Colorado 2024 example from U2-state, not an assumed school location. Its current check and ten-share rounding are source to check by the data job. Keep the title and data year with the record. Zero may mean rounded below one share, not absent; generation is not all energy use or a household mix. No percentages for children. If state data are unavailable, fallback P is invented: five coal, three gas, two water shares; never call it local. Resource paths (light), specification only, partly fits particle capture, day 33 four minutes show why; paper diagrams replace it. [Asks](TOOL-ASKS.md#unit-2-circuit-paths). No key tool.

No fuels, pollutants, soil, foods or heated apparatus are handled. Source paragraphs and case records remain separate. There is no visitor/live-page dependency: printed summaries and fallback P keep every day usable.

## 6. Days

- **10 min. Return and repair.** First five E29 D; next five privately read written individual design feedback and identify revision target for day 35.

## Day 31

**Goal:** I can combine sources to compare resource renewal. **Kind:** Understanding.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** E30 I, manufactured object versus natural origin.
- **8 min. Teach combining.** Model source-statement/location notes using sunlight and oil summaries. Define renewable: replenished naturally on human timescales. Define nonrenewable: not replaced as fast as people use it. Explain variable availability separately.

Teacher reads at most 160 words, children annotate labels. Gloss turbine as a wheel turned by flowing water or air, coupled to a generator. No written wind/gas community comparison.

- **7 min. Exit ticket.** E31, both reserved summaries neutrally read aloud.

Held: children, two-column notes; teacher, resource labels. Tool: None. E31 J to day 32.

**Shape:** Practice categorizes individual cards and records source locations. E31 combines two reserved summaries into a community-supply explanation; no paired wind/gas response in practice.

## Day 32
