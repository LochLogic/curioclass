> Six groups of five share one apparatus in turns; waiting groups predict and record. Each child takes a trial role. One demonstrating group runs three slower and three faster trials a day while every child records each trial on their own sheet; the demonstrating group changes each day, and the pooled class records serve days 4 to 6 and 13. Materials per active group:
>
> | Days | Supplies and purpose | Preparation and cleanup |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1, 2, 3 | One marble, cardboard ramp, cup, ruler, tape and edge guard; compare rolls and measure cup travel. | Teacher prepares guarded floor lane the day before, checks smooth edges and pretests low releases. Reserve 5 minutes daily to count objects, store supplies and clear tape. |
> | 7, 8, 9, 11 | Two toy cars, one smooth wooden block and floor tape; compare moving-to-still impacts. | Teacher checks wheels, block splinters and lane containment the day before. Reserve 5 minutes daily to count, store and clear the lane. |
>
> Every listed day: flying or escaping objects, small-object ingestion and trips are hazards. Keep faces back, use gentle floor rolls inside a catch barrier, never throw, keep objects from mouths and count out and back. Active handlers wear impact eye protection, including during setup and cleanup; everyone else stays outside the marked work area and watches or records. Teacher alone cuts thin cardboard with blunt-tip scissors and retrieves escaped objects; stop all rolling first. Stop for broken guards, flying pieces or missing protection. No chemicals, heat or intentional warming tests.
>
> Household tier: recycled cardboard and cups; an existing wheeled toy replaces a purchased car. No-kit tier for both investigations: dated class trial records and motion sequences, weaker because students cannot plan and conduct their own comparison. Never invent measured warming.
