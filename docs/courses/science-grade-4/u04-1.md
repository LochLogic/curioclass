## 1. Standards and practices

**4-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Make observations and/or measurements" of how water and ice change rock. Day 58 separates breaking from carrying. Day 59 plans a one-variable tray comparison and starts a freezing comparison. Day 60 measures repeated tray trials in whole centimeters. Wind and plants wait for section 4.2.

**4-ESS1-1, day 57 only.** "Identify evidence": the hook asks what matching stripes on both walls could mean. Nothing about layers is taught or checked here; section 4.3 owns it.

Limits that bite: one process at a time, but the two can happen together (a boulder can split and roll). A tray moves loose sand and cannot give a canyon's cutting rate. Clay is an analogue, not rock. Earth's great age is stated plainly from evidence; no exact age follows from any record here. No child is asked what they or their family believe about it.

Connections: planning and carrying out investigations (days 59 and 60); cause and effect (arrows begin day 57, carried to day 62); math unit 6 uses whole centimeters and equal cupfuls, no conversions.

Review, in the ledger's words: "erosion" (grade 2), "slow change" (grade 3 day 84, day 57), "observation, inference" (day 16), "question board: notices become questions" (day 42), "fair test: keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing" (day 52), "read whole centimeters from ruler marks" (day 3), "barrier" and "freeze" (earlier grades). New: "weathering" (day 58), "baseline: a starting record for comparison" (day 59).

Teacher page must explain, at an adult's level: weathering is breakage in place and needs no travel; erosion is carrying; a marked grain's new position shows carrying and says nothing about size. A baseline is why a final crack cannot be called new. A tied or conflicting repeat stays in the record.

- **E58.** [s04-X58] "Invented for practice. Before: rock V has a pointed corner beside a marked ledge. After: V lacks that corner; its matching fragment lies below the ledge. Which features support breaking, carrying, or both?" Key: both; the missing corner supports breaking, the fragment below the ledge supports carrying. Wrong: "Only carrying occurred because weathering must move material." (A).
- **E59.** [s04-X59-plan] "Invented for practice. A plan changes grain size and pouring position between two trays. Rewrite it as one changed condition with a sediment measurement." Key: trial 1 center 9 cm, edge 12 cm; trial 2 center 13 cm, edge 10 cm; all four from starting marks. Wrong: "Keep only trial 1 because it supports the cause." (C). [s04-X60-cause] "Use the same record. Judge the claim that edge pouring always moves sediment farther." Key: the trials disagree; no consistent effect is established. Wrong: "One supporting trial proves the edge effect." (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Carried: the question board (children's questions grouped under missing material, wall stripes, time); the initial canyon model, kept unchanged for revision on day 66; cause-and-effect arrows, started day 57 and revised day 62. Original records stay with each child. Feedback never overwrites an original.

- **Key moment 1, day 60: the sediment front moves a measurable distance.** Ideal instrument: a stream table with a ruler along its edge. Spec: the household tray with damp sand, equal cupfuls and a ruler fits; the ruler's step is 1 centimeter, so a difference under 1 centimeter reads as "no detected movement", which is recorded as such. No tool is used; the simulation is spec only (day 63). Gaps: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, day 59: matched clay before and after freezing.** Ideal instrument: matched stone samples frozen and thawed in a freezer. Spec: clay in plastic containers with headspace partly fits; clay is not rock. A failed or empty freezing result is recorded, not repaired. No freezer: keep the starting records and mark the comparison "not run".

Shared equipment fits the minutes. Day 60: one tray pair, six groups of five, four minutes each (45 seconds pour, 150 seconds for five children at 30 seconds each, 45 seconds reset), 24 minutes in all. Day 59: one adult demonstration in the 15-minute Response, with pairs of 45 seconds working at once.

Clean-up fits: the day files give goggle cleaning to an adult and one handler; thirty pairs do not fit in five minutes that way. This plan has every child wipe their own goggles at the same moment (one minute, wipes supplied), the adult and one handler contain solids and wash (three minutes), and papers are filed (one minute). Runoff settles in the bucket, never the sink.

A room with nothing: dated photographs and invented records are the weaker route; they are said aloud to be invented, never pooled with live readings, and give no firsthand credit.

## 6. Days

## Day 57

- **10 min. Opening.** Return day 56's written notes without repair time. Show the canyon photograph with no caption and say its credit aloud (Alex Demas, public domain; the invented hook drawing only if it is unclear). Children look silently, then write two things they can see. Give the family note.
- **15 min. Notice and draw.** Alone: finish two observations, draw a first explanation and mark one uncertain part. Cause-and-effect arrows begin here and are tentative.

- **12 min. Group questions.** Each child writes one canyon question. Pairs speak at once, 45 seconds each. Hear a rotating six, 45 seconds each, within 5 minutes. Group the slips under missing material, wall stripes and time.
- **5 min. Exit.** E57 on a half slip and in the Exit box.
Tool: None. Evidence: E57, the observation sheet. Goes to day 58 Return.

## Day 59

- **15 min. Response.** Return day 58 and the day 56 readiness paper. ## 7. Talk and writing

Planned questions: day 57, "What can you actually see, and what is a guess?"; day 58, "Which feature shows breaking, and which shows carrying?"; day 60, "Do the trials agree?" Public talk hears a named rotating six at 45 seconds each within 5 minutes; every child may pass. Partner turns are 45 seconds each, all at once.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: weathering, erosion, sediment, baseline, trial. Frame: "The observation ___ supports ___ because ___." Access: large print and tactile sequences, a scribe for the child's own reasoning, a dry reading position beside the ruler. Check accommodations: read stems neutrally, dictated answers count, drawings count. Reading load: student pages stay within DEFAULTS.md; the teacher reads the invented cards aloud. Extension, same standard: design a second one-variable plan for a different condition and say what it would measure.
