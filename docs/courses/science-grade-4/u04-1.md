## 1. Standards and practices

**4-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Make observations and/or measurements" of how water and ice change rock. Day 58 separates breaking from carrying. Day 59 plans a one-variable tray comparison and starts a freezing comparison. Day 60 measures repeated water-amount trials in whole centimeters. Wind and plants wait for section 4.2.

**4-ESS1-1, day 57 only.** "Identify evidence": the hook asks what matching stripes on both walls could mean. Nothing about layers is taught or checked here; section 4.3 owns it.

Limits that bite: one process at a time, but the two can happen together (a boulder can split and roll). A tray moves loose sand and cannot give a canyon's cutting rate. Clay is an analog, not rock. Earth's great age is stated plainly from evidence; no exact age follows from any record here. No child is asked what they or their family believe about it.

Connections: planning and carrying out investigations (days 59 and 60); cause and effect (arrows begin day 57, carried to day 62); math unit 6 uses whole centimeters and equal cupfuls, no conversions.

Review, in the ledger's words: "erosion" (grade 2), "slow change" (grade 3 u05-1 day 84, reviewed day 57), "observation, inference" (u02-1 day 16), "question board: notices become questions" (u03-1 day 42), "fair test: keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing" (u03-4 day 52), "centimeter, ruler zero, whole centimeter" (u01-1 day 3), "barrier: something put on the path to slow or stop soil leaving" (grade 2 u02-2 day 16) and "freeze" (grade 2 u04-2 day 43). New: "weathering: breaking rock material down" and "sediment: loose rock material" (day 58); "baseline: a starting record for comparison" (day 59). Review "trial: one run of a test" from science-grade-4 u01-1 day 2; retrieve that meaning before repeats.

Teacher page must explain, at an adult's level: weathering is breakdown and needs no travel; erosion is carrying; a marked grain's new position shows carrying and says nothing about size. A baseline is why a final crack cannot be called new. A tied or conflicting repeat stays in the record.

Planning/carrying out investigations joins ESS2.A/ESS2.E with cause and effect: children connect measured material movement to the tested condition. The canyon question adds stability and change; the tray gives no geological rate.

**E57.** Drawing stimulus: a tributary ends behind a fan-shaped rubble pile; the main river remains open. Print: "Make a tentative blockage model with two observation labels."
Key: labels locate the stopped tributary and spreading rubble; an inferred arrow proposes material blocking water. Collect questions about rubble and wall rock during Group questions. Accept other observation-linked possibilities; initial correctness is not scored. Wrong: "Rock cannot change, so rubble could never come from the wall." (B).
Shape: one annotated tentative blockage model. Teaching keeps feature tallies, an unannotated sketch, separate arrows and pooled oral questions separate; no completed annotated blockage model.

**E58.** Stimulus: before, rock V has a pointed corner beside a marked ledge. After, V lacks the corner; its matching fragment lies below. Print: "Mark the features supporting breaking, carrying, or both."
Key: the missing corner supports breaking; the matching fragment's changed position supports carrying, so both. Wrong: "Only carrying counts; weathering needs travel." (A).
Shape: identify diagnostic features in a previously unseen paired record. Teaching supplies process labels on Gully G and Path T, then traces routes; no unlabeled feature-identification pair.

**E59.** A proposed tray comparison changes grain size and pouring position. Print: "Repair this tray plan so only one condition changes."
Key: hold grain size fixed and vary pouring position, measuring sediment movement in centimeters; or fix position and vary grain size. Check both freezing baselines in Freezer setup, outside E59. Wrong: "Keep both changes; either one can prove the cause." (C).
Shape: repair one confounded tray comparison. Practice creates correct plans and actual starting records; no flawed plan or missing-baseline diagnosis.

**E60.** Stimulus: only pouring position changes. Trial one: center 9 centimeters, edge 12. Trial two: center 13, edge 10. Print: "What conclusion about pouring position does this conflicting record support?"
Supply all four labeled distances in a table with centimeters. Key: one trial favors edge, another center. No consistent effect is established. Wrong: "Changing water as well would still isolate pouring position." (C).
Shape: one qualified causal conclusion from a supplied conflicting-results table. Practice records live single-condition ruler annotations and pools them; no supplied conflicting table or table-plus-verdict worksheet.

**Readiness R56:** the cloth-versus-bare item stays whole on s04 field 4. Day 59 returns it and checks the repaired explanation, without points.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Carried: the question board (children's questions grouped under missing material, wall stripes, time); the initial canyon sketch and separate arrows, kept for revision on day 66; cause-and-effect arrows, reviewing u02-4 day 32 cause and effect, revised for land on day 57 and revised day 62. Original records stay with each child. Feedback never overwrites an original.

- **Key moment 1, day 60: the sediment front moves a measurable distance.** Ideal instrument: a stream table with a ruler along its edge. Spec: the household tray with damp sand, equal cupfuls and a ruler fits; the ruler's step is 1 centimeter, so movement below one ruler step is recorded "less than 1 centimeter, unresolved", not zero. No tool is used; the simulation is spec only (day 63). Gaps: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, day 59: matched clay before and after freezing.** Ideal instrument: matched stone samples frozen and thawed in a freezer. Spec: clay in plastic containers with headspace partly fits; clay is not rock. A failed or empty freezing result is recorded, not repaired. No freezer: keep starting records, mark "not run", and use announced invented F on day 61.

Shared equipment fits the minutes. Day 60: one tray pair, six groups of five, four minutes each (45 seconds pour, 150 seconds for five children at 30 seconds each, 45 seconds reset), 24 minutes in all. Day 59: one adult demonstration in the 15-minute Erosion response, with pairs of 45 seconds working at once.

Safety for these wet days: goggles for everyone in splash range from setup through takedown. Adult pours, resets and handles clay/freezer containers; only one child at a time approaches the dry ruler edge. Ask about sand/clay ingredient allergies first; no tasting, glass or dry blowing. Five Cleanup minutes: adult/one handler contain solids and wash at two sinks, three minutes; everyone wipes goggles simultaneously with supplied wipes, one; file papers, one. Wet handling ends before goggles come off. Dry observers do not handle sand. Runoff settles in the bucket, never the sink.

A room with nothing: dated photographs and invented records are the weaker route; they are said aloud to be invented, never pooled with live readings, and give no firsthand credit. Fallback R59 shows fewer grains leaving the cloth-protected patch under equal pouring. R60 gives smaller/larger water amounts: trial one 18/24 centimeters, trial two 19/24. Both are printed and announced invented for practice; neither replaces a missing live observation.
