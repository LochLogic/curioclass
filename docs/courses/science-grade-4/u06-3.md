## 1. Standards and practices

**4-LS1-1: "Construct an argument".** Carry the plant half equally: internal/external interactions support survival, growth, behavior and reproduction. Argument joins LS1.A and systems on sealed specimens and readable records. Root uptake is not observed in a rootless cutting. Celery is a leaf stalk; use white-flower stems for a stem claim.

Review grade 3 u03-1 "flower, fruit, seed; pollen is not a seed" and life cycle; u04-2 variation and structure/function. Teach internal (inside), external (outside) and transport (movement) on day 104. Day 105 defines survival (staying alive), growth (getting larger), behavior (responding), reproduction (making new living things), relevant evidence (bearing on this claim) and sufficient evidence (enough support). Day 106 rehearses them. Behavior includes shoot bending, without a plant brain. Day 108 teaches stamen (pollen-bearing part), pistil (part receiving pollen), ovule (part that can become a seed), and fertilization (the joining step before seed development), through visible-scale diagrams. Teach ovary as the pistil's ovule-holding part, which can become fruit. Petals may attract visitors; pollen transfer and ovule development are involved in seeds. No cellular mechanism. W.4.1 supports day 107 evidence-linked reasons.

**Unit assessment item owned jointly with section 6.4:** item 3, administered on day 116. Its touch-folding plant and whole-pod package remain reserved.

- **E104, s06-X104 / s06-X104-question.** "Matched intact plants have firm leaves after root watering, or drooping leaves without watering. Draw your first water-movement model and add one unanswered question."
  Key: roots through an inferred inside route toward leaves; ask which paths carry water or another question the record leaves open. Retain tentative drawings descriptively. Wrong first idea: "Only outside surfaces move water" (F).
  Shape: initial intact-plant route plus open question in one artifact. Practice records color and conditions of rootless specimens and labels inside/outside views, without a complete route or composing questions.
- **E105, s06-X105.** "Invented composite plant: deep roots reach damp soil; waxy leaves lose less water. A tendril coils around a post; seed-bearing burrs catch fur. Explain how these structures support survival, growth, behavior and reproduction."
  Key: roots and water-saving leaves support resource supply for survival and growth; coiling is a response that can aid climbing; burrs disperse existing seeds, helping the next generation. Dispersal does not make seeds or guarantee new plants. Internal transport may strengthen the answer but is not required by this supplied card. Wrong: "Only outside parts can help a plant function" (F).
  Shape: one four-function account of a composite plant. Teaching uses separate single-job matches, not a four-function synthesis card.
- **E106, s06-X106.** "Record A: a rootless stem starts uncolored, then has inside color above dyed water. Record B: leaves droop without water. Explain which record supports internal water transport and what remains untested."
  Key: A locates the transported marker inside; B does not locate a path. With no roots, neither record tests root uptake. Wrong: "A proves the roots took up water" (H).
  Shape: judge two transport records and name the root limit. Rehearsal matches protection or dispersal claims to complete reasons; no paired transport-evidence judgment.
- **E107, s06-X107.** "A rooted diagram shows uptake, inside paths and leaves. A separate rootless flower-stem record shows color above the waterline. Explain their joint support for interacting parts, including the cutting's evidence limit."
  Key: the diagram supplies uptake; the cutting supports internal transport toward leaves. Together they support connected parts moving water. The cutting cannot directly test roots absorbing water. Wrong: "The rootless cutting proves what roots did" (H); "Inside paths have no role" (F).
  Shape: joint-source system argument with a limit. Practice tags each source separately or explains a single interaction orally; no combined-source argument.
- **E108, s06-X108.** "A flower sequence shows pollen-bearing stamens, a pistil containing ovules, and later fruit containing seeds. Explain reproductive cooperation, including why petals alone provide insufficient evidence."
  Key: stamens supply pollen that reaches the pistil; after successful fertilization, ovules can develop into seeds and the ovary into fruit. Petals may attract visitors, but alone establish neither transfer nor seed production. Wrong: "Petals alone produce seeds" (G).
  Shape: cooperating reproductive parts plus an insufficiency judgment. Practice labels separate parts and orders events, without explaining their cooperation or judging a petals-only claim.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Keep dated observations; transport models distinguish observed paths from source-supplied uptake.

Ideal 104/107 instrument compares actual inside paths before/after exposure. Adult-prepared sealed flower pairs partly fit: color is a marker, not a root observation. Celery stays a leaf-stalk comparison. Check pollen/celery allergies before plants arrive; verify thorn-free white snapdragon identity and untreated stock. Adult prepares pairs/sections with splash goggles, table cover and adult-only cutting tool. Follow actual dye instructions, including gloves where required; otherwise use diagrams. Children never open, taste or cut. Adult stores/wipes/washes hands in five cleanup minutes; paper-only observers need no thirty-child washing queue.

Six groups view one set two minutes each plus three setup minutes inside fifteen; day 104 shortens views for its repair. Observe colored/uncolored/unclear, not concentration. Day 107 measures color reach with a ruler outside the container, nearest whole centimeter, step 1 centimeter. Record under 1 centimeter as below this step; never invent a smaller reading. Keep unclear color as unclear rather than zero. Failed color: record unclear, then Plant P, printed/announced invented: start uncolored, later colored bands above water in flower stem; plain-water comparison uncolored. Preserve actual observations.

Day 108 ideal shows parts without pollen exposure. Adult dissects before class with goggles/blunt tweezers and seals six containers; original diagram supplies hidden ovules. No digital tool. No-kit room uses P and diagrams, marked supplied.
