## 1. Standards and practices

**4-ESS1-1, this section's share.** "Identify evidence" in rock layers and fossils to explain how a landscape has changed. Day 67 builds an undisturbed layered model and reads separate fossil-environment guides. Day 68 bends and offsets the model and traces a real exposure. Day 69 compares two accounts of one canyon with a cross-cutting channel. Day 70 joins all of it in one consensus explanation.

**4-ESS2-1, day 70.** "Make observations and/or measurements" returns as the class joins its tray measurements to the layer story and revises days 65 and 66 practice. No new measuring.

Limits that bite: relative order only, never a number of years; no named formations or rock-formation mechanisms to memorize; lower is older only in undisturbed layers; clay is an analogue, not rock; marine means sea-living and tells conditions, not an exact age. Separate finished models cannot show which change came first. The folded-rock photograph is a real exposure that is not the canyon and has no original-top marker. The canyon cards are original Curio Class prose based on the unit sources; no adapted lesson. No child gives a personal home or disaster story.

Connections: grade 3 fossils (retrieved day 67); nature of science, past and present processes assumed consistent (day 69, from the frame); two-column source notes (day 30) as the ELA research unit's note routine overlaps; consensus (day 13).

Review in the ledger's words: "guide card" (u05-1 day 83), "steady patterns" (day 84), and "short record, long record" (day 85) name how habitat guides support earlier conditions; layers record far more than one week. Review also: "fossil" (grade 3 u05-1 day 82), "layer, older, younger" and "undisturbed" (u05-1 day 85), "inferred" (day 66), "lake" (grade 2), "two optional forty-five-second peer evidence turns and individual written response" (u03-4 day 53). New: "marine: sea-living" (day 67); "bend: change a layer's curve", "offset: shift parts past each other", and "deformation: changed shape or position" (day 68).

Teacher page must explain: a layer must exist before it is bent or cut; a channel crossing a displaced layer is later than the displacement; matching color alone proves nothing, but matching order plus fossils supports a tentative link; the age of Earth is great and stated plainly, but no record here gives a number. No child is asked for a personal belief about it, and family belief is not weighed as evidence.

Constructing explanations joins ESS1.C with patterns: children connect fossil/layer order to landscape change. ESS2.A/ESS2.E and cause and effect return through retained observations, with stability and change over long times.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment ownership:** item 2, administered by section 4.4; full cases and scoring stay on s04 field 5. Day 69 remains unscored. Print and announce all supplied exits as "Invented for practice". Each is one individual five-minute paper item. Formative scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keys were solved before writing.

**E67.** Undisturbed exposure: marine shells below a middle layer without useful fossils, rooted land plants above. Print: "Draw a three-layer history model explaining the supported environment change and the uncertain middle."
Key: sea before land; bottom older, top younger; middle environment unknown. Use a supplied guide identifying in-place shells as sea-living and rooted plants as land evidence. Wrong: "The lowest layer is always oldest, even after overturning." (F).
Shape: one three-layer environmental-history drawing with an uncertain middle. Practice constructs plain/token clay and attaches separate habitat labels; no layered sea-to-land model. E67 is not the lower grade's fish/water-plant-to-trunk case.

**E68.** Print diagrams U and V with an intact J-below-K-below-L reference: U folds back until its limb is upside down; V shifts sideways without overturning. Continuous band labels remain traceable. Print: "Evaluate 'lowest means oldest' for both diagrams, giving each supported layer order."
Key: original J, K, L order survives tracing in both; U's overturned limb has J above L, so screen height fails. V retains upright order. Deformation follows layers; the separate diagrams do not order the two changes against each other. Wrong: "The lowest visible band is oldest in every bent sequence." (F).
Shape: evaluate one rule across two deformation diagrams and reconstruct each order. Practice handles a single marked-top stack at a time, recording movement, never the paired rule-evaluation worksheet.

**E69.** Record: a break shifts layers; an unshifted channel cuts across both displaced sides. Account A puts cutting before the shift, B afterward. Print: "Choose the better-supported account with evidence. State one timing limit."
Key: B, because the unshifted cut crosses shifted layers; exact duration unknown. Wrong: "The river always occupied this channel, before every layer existed." (H).
Shape: compare two channel/displacement accounts and bound timing. Practice compares fossil-habitat accounts and source locations, without a channel/break account pair or timing-limit template.

**E70.** Record: an abandoned upper channel above today's sediment-carrying stream; matching wall layers face across the canyon. Accounts: unchanged stream position, or continued cutting after abandonment. Print: "Compare these accounts using the upper channel, lower stream and matching layers."
Key: continued cutting better explains an older high channel and current lower carrying. Matching walls support removed material between once-continuous or regionally extensive layers. No exact duration or unique complete cause follows. Wrong: "The river has always occupied exactly its current position." (H).
Shape: evidence-linked comparison of static-position/continued-cutting accounts. Practice assembles consensus evidence tags without competing channel-position accounts. Keep the upper-channel diagram concealed.

Item 2 Shape: reconstruct a complete unfamiliar valley history from fossil order, near/far matches, deformation and a cut, rejecting simultaneity. Teaching uses separate construction logs, habitat labels and source notes; never the P/Q/R valley or a completed whole-history assessment template.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Key moment 1, days 67 and 68: a marked-top layered stack, built, cut, bent and offset.** Ideal instrument: real rock layers or a cut core. Spec: modeling clay in three bands with large buttons, a blunt slicer and a tray partly fits; clay is an analogue and its bends cannot tell which change came first. No tool used. Gaps: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, day 68: a real folded exposure.** Ideal instrument: a field visit. Spec: the public-domain photograph, no caption shown, with the invented fold drawing as the labeled fallback; no visit.
- **Key moment 3, day 70: five joined representations.** Ideal instrument: a shared display the class edits. Spec: paper display fits; no tool.

Equipment/time: six stacks, three large tokens per stack, adult blunt slicer. Five children each handle for 30 seconds within five minutes, leaving 150 seconds for assembly/recording. Ask about clay ingredient allergies first; no food specimens, soil or latex. No tasting. Adult counts tokens out/back and cuts away from hands. Handwashing uses two sinks: fifteen pairs, 30 seconds per pair including transfer, eight minutes allowed. Start five minutes before Cleanup during quiet guide/trace work, finish its first three; retain two Cleanup minutes for tools/tables. Children move only when called. With unsuitable handwashing, use paper layers instead. Day 69 is paper only.

A room with nothing: draw the layered stack and the bend on paper, said aloud to be invented; no firsthand credit for the clay turn.
