## 1. Standards and practices

**4-ESS2-1, days 71 and 73.** "Make observations and/or measurements": on day 71 each child observes and measures a new one-variable slope comparison in whole centimeters, then explains it and four separate process records (ice, wind, an animal, rain with plants). Day 73 repairs by the recorded gap.

**4-ESS1-1, days 72 and 73.** "Identify evidence": on day 72 each child explains the relative history of a new layered valley from fossil order, matching layers, bending and displacement, and a later river cut, then rejects "all at once". Day 73 repairs by the recorded gap.

Limits that bite: only cases reserved from teaching; one process per record; relative order and no number of years; a tray gives no rate for a solid-rock canyon; the day 72 records do not fix a duration or the unique cause of the break. No reteaching on days 71 and 72, no practical retry on day 73, and no change to an original score after repair.

Connections: item 1 parts and item 2 sit on the unit plan; day 73 uses the unit's whole error register. Days 71 and 72 follow the standing rule that work scored on a day was made on an earlier day or is made unaided on that day without feedback; the day 65 to 70 practice is the unscored feedback loop.

Routines: independent evidence then delayed component feedback; self-check against the stimulus before collection (day 72); four simultaneous paper repair groups with one assigned move (day 73).

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment ownership:** administer items 1 and 2, whole on s04 field 5. Item 1 has 16 points; item 2 has 12. Use one points scale on that record only; formative response uses shown, shown with help, not yet seen elsewhere. No new teaching before collection. Keys below were independently solved; actual distances have no predetermined correct value.

Each numbered prompt is one check item with at most two parts. Print and say "Invented for practice" on supplied records, not live observations. Keep reserved stimuli sealed. These short stems replace the older long/leading page directions in the data pass.

**1a, live measuring, day 71.** "Record both sediment movements with conditions and centimeters. Mark what you directly observed being carried."
Key: each child's actual two readings and observed movement, retained even if tied. A smaller-than-step movement is not an invented zero. Wrong: "Nothing eroded because no rock broke." (A). Missing/incorrect readings are measurement gaps, not C.
Shape: unaided paired slope observation/measurement with a carrying-evidence mark. Practice uses water amount or cover with single ruler annotations; no slope trial, combined measurement/evidence answer sheet or promised values.

**1b, cause, day 71.** "Explain what all repeats support about slope. Include one matched condition or a limit."
Key: qualify the effect using all valid repeats and equal water/sand/moisture/pour position; disagreement cannot establish a consistent effect. Tray sediment cannot give a solid-rock canyon rate. Wrong: "Unequal water would still isolate slope." (C).
Shape: independently explain a slope effect from the pooled live record. Teaching never rehearses a full pooled-slope causal report.

**1c, ice.** Matched damp stones widen cracks equally when frozen/thawed and unfrozen. Print: "Does this isolate freezing as the cause? Use the crack observations."
Key: no; both changed, including control. Weathering occurred without travel. Wrong: "No travel means neither stone weathered." (A).
Shape: judge causal isolation from an equally changing control. Day 61 keeps its actual outcomes and observation drawings; no equal-change stone verdict card.

**1d, wind.** New pile contains tracked marked grains, unchanged in visible size. Print: "Which process does this record establish? Use the grain evidence."
Key: carrying; position changed, visible breakage is not established. Wrong: "Erosion requires freshly broken rock." (A).
Shape: distinguish established carrying from unestablished breaking using grain identity. Teaching uses loose-material cutaways, never marked grains tracked into a pile.

**1e, animal.** Repeated pictures show a burrowing animal bringing soil onto a marked bare surface; excluded patch unchanged. Print: "Can land change without plants? Use this record."
Key: yes; animal redistributed soil, unlike excluded patch. Wrong: "Living things cannot change land." (D).
Shape: reject a plant-required claim from an animal-exclusion record. Practice uses an underground opening/collapse cutaway, no matched animal-excluded patch or plant-required claim.

**1f, rain.** Same seedlings/soil/light; exposed plot gains grooves and upright seedlings. Excluded plot has no grooves and wilting seedlings that recover when watered. Print: "Explain rain's effects on land and plant life using this record."
Key: grooves show reshaping; wilt/recovery support water's effect on plant life. Wrong: "Rain cannot affect which plants grow." (E).
Shape: one two-link causal explanation from a complete controlled rain/plant record. Practice maps regional water availability and routes; E64 plans a test but supplies no completed matched seedling/wilt/recovery record or two-link answer.

**2a, layer order/environment, day 72.** Reserved P/Q/R valley: rooted plants in P, marine shells in Q, R uninformative; undisturbed original order P below Q below R. Print: "Model the relative layer and environment history. Mark uncertainty where the fossils give no environment."
Key: P, Q, R; land then sea, R unknown. Wrong: "Even overturned layers must have their oldest layer lowest." (F).
Shape: initial part of a whole-valley history, land-to-sea in P/Q/R. No teaching sequence uses those fossil/order combinations; E67 is a different sea-to-land case.

**2b, matching.** Same distinctive ordered fossil sequence across the valley and far away; another exposure has same colors, wrong order, missing rooted plants. Print: "Judge the near and far layer matches using the evidence."
Key: sequence/fossils support tentative correlation of first exposures, not the color-only alternative. Wrong: "Matching color alone proves a match." (G).
Shape: evaluate three exposures including a color-only competitor. Practice makes separate freshwater-horizon/source notes, no complete near/far/competitor answer sheet.

**2c, deformation.** All three layers bend together and shift at a break. Print: "Place layer formation, bending and shifting in their supported relative order."
Key: layers before both changes; no order between bending and shifting follows from these facts alone. Wrong: "Lowest is always oldest, regardless of overturning." (F). Assign F only if stated; incomplete event order is an evidence gap.
Shape: bounded event-order inference from a whole-valley side exposure. Practice logs observed clay movements one at a time, no retrospective multi-event ordering packet.

**2d, cutting.** Bent/displaced layer ends meet notch walls; flat gravel covers its floor. Print: "Place channel cutting relative to the displaced layers using the notch evidence."
Key: cutting follows displacement; flat gravel is later than the cut it occupies. Wrong: "This river always occupied this notch, before the layers." (H).
Shape: use terminating bands and a gravel floor for relative order. No practice card includes this notch/floor relation; E69 concerns a different unshifted crossing-channel map.

**2e, account/limit.** Print: "Reject the claim that every feature appeared together using evidence. State one limit of this history."
Key: layers precede deformation; cut crosses deformed material. Duration and unique cause of break are unresolved. Wrong: "The river has always occupied its present valley." (H).
Shape: synthesize the whole reserved history against simultaneity with a limit. No completed multi-evidence history or simultaneity rebuttal is modeled in practice.

Score item 2's six components: order, environment, correlation, deformation, cutting, rejection/limit. Each is 0, 1 or 2, using s04's keys and samples; 2a carries two separate components. Item 1 retains s04's eight components, two points each. No word-count or vocabulary points. Blank/unseen evidence remains an identified gap. No altered reading earns credit.

**E73, unscored fresh paper.** New picture: a stream carrying grains cuts a notch through already tilted bands. Print: "Explain why 'the stream was always in this notch' fails, using the tilted band ends."
Key: bands existed and tilted before the notch cut their ends; the present notch is later. Wrong: "The river always occupied this notch before those bands existed." (H).
Shape: one refutation of an always-present-stream claim from tilted band endings. Repair work uses intact clay, prior papers and separate evidence labels; no new tilted-notch refutation. This is not the earlier footpath repair card.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Carried: the five familiar representations remain available before the checks, then are covered throughout days 71 and 72; every child's original work, readings and scores stay visible beside any revision. Day 71's practical record is kept unchanged for day 75.

- **Key moment 1, day 71: a new matched pair of slopes measured by each child.** Ideal instrument: a stream table and a ruler at the sediment front. Spec: two household trays with new liners, damp sand, equal cupfuls and a ruler fits; the ruler's step is 1 centimeter, so smaller movement is recorded less than 1 centimeter, unresolved, not zero and expected numbers are never entered. Gaps: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, day 72: a new layered valley.** Ideal instrument: a real exposure. Spec: the invented valley drawing with printed side records partly fits; it is a drawing, not an exposure, said aloud. Paper only, no tool.

Shared equipment fits: six groups of five, four minutes each (45 seconds pour, five 30-second readings, 45 seconds reset), 24 minutes in all on one shared tray pair with fresh liners and sand each group; adults pour and reset, children read at the dry edge. Every group's distances are posted without comment. If a reading fails, one retry inside the slot, then unresolved; if the apparatus fails, the sealed fallback is used separately and never pooled, with no firsthand credit. The sealed invented reasoning fallback has gentle slopes 14/15 centimeters and steep slopes 20/21, one centimeter ruler steps and matched water. It is never displayed during live measuring and earns no firsthand-measurement credit. Clean-up (5 minutes): the adult contains solids and washes; dry children clean their goggles at once with the supplied supplies; papers are filed. Keep settled solids in the bucket for the adult's established disposal route, never down a sink. Goggles protect everyone in splash range throughout setup/takedown; no tasting. Adult/one handler wash at two sinks within three cleanup minutes; simultaneous wiping/storage takes two. Dry observers never handle sand.

A room with nothing: the sealed fallback records carry the reasoning items; the practical performance is marked not run, with points only for separately demonstrated reasoning. Never treat absent observation as demonstrated performance.

## 6. Days

## Day 71

**Goal:** I can gather and explain land-change evidence independently. **Kind:** Check.
- **3 min. Launch.** Show matched conditions and identify slope as the changed condition. Read directions neutrally, reveal no outcomes. Tools: None.
- **24 min. Observation turns.** Six groups of five, four minutes each; adult pours/resets new slopes, every child reads both fronts alone. Waiting children silently annotate 1c to 1f. Post readings without comment; preserve missing/contrary observations.
- **13 min. Independent explanation.** Four minutes 1b from all valid repeats, nine complete 1c to 1f. Neutral rereading only. Collect all papers before feedback.
- **5 min. Cleanup.** Adult/handler contain sediment and wash; dry observers clean goggles simultaneously, store and file.
Evidence: item 1 alone, original personal practical record retained for day 75. Mark after class, three minutes per child, 90 minutes total. Return written component feedback day 73 Revision. If apparatus fails, reasoning uses sealed invented fallback; practical evidence remains not run/unobserved, never fabricated.

## Day 72

**Goal:** I can explain an unfamiliar valley from evidence. **Kind:** Check.
- **3 min. Launch.** Reveal P/Q/R valley, announce invented provenance and read neutral directions.
- **32 min. History explanation.** Ten minutes 2a, seven 2b, five each 2c, 2d and 2e. Paper alone, no peer/adult evaluation.
- **7 min. Evidence check.** Children reread their own response and stimulus, checking links without a displayed class answer or completed model. Retain additions beside originals; no content feedback before scoring.
- **3 min. Collection.** Collect every page, clear desks.
Tool: None. Evidence: item 2, mark outside class at two minutes a child, 60 minutes. Return with feedback day 73 Revision. Model drawings are instruments pupils construct, not supplied solution outlines.

## Day 73

**Goal:** I can repair an explanation using better evidence. **Kind:** Respond.
- **5 min. Feedback.** Read the private component feedback summary from days 71/72. Assign one paper move per child; original scores/readings stay fixed. Tools: None.
- **10 min. Targeted repair.** First five return every E70 and day 70 practice page, H move if needed. Next five four simultaneous paper groups, one assigned correction each; teacher meets only commonest need, everyone submits a correction. No four-group rotation or hidden extra return.
- **20 min. Revision.** Ten minutes return/read/revise day 71 papers; ten day 72. Each repairs one named evidence link, not the full packet. Keep originals visible; no practical retry or rescoring.
- **5 min. Explain change.** Partners each have 45 seconds simultaneously, then everyone writes an evidence-change note. Public sharing optional; no serial interviews.
- **5 min. Fresh case.** E73 alone, all papers collected, unscored.
All day 73 papers get written feedback in day 74 Launch, zero new repair minutes promised. Preserve unresolved gaps for the next scheduled response; no new readiness assessment here.

## 7. Talk and writing

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: the words of the unit, with pictures; frame "The observation ___ supports ___ because ___." Access: read prompts aloud once, enlarged print or tactile diagrams, a scribe or an oral answer with each child's own measuring and evidence decisions, a dry reading position at the ruler. Check accommodations: dictated answers count; access help is recorded apart from content. Reading load: the stems are read aloud neutrally; no passage is read alone beyond the stems. Extension, same standard: evaluate a second history for the valley and say what evidence would separate the two.
