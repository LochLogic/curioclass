**Unit assessment item owned:** item 2, administered on day 115. Its two histories under one odor remain reserved.

- **E99, s06-X99.** "A hamster's whiskers brush a wall in the dark. It steps sideways. Draw how this information guides its movement."
  Key: touch information reaches the brain, which processes it and guides movement. Receptor details are unnecessary. Wrong: "Whiskers command the legs directly" (D).
  Shape: complete touch-to-brain-to-movement drawing. Teaching matches separate roles and moves one token between roles; no complete touch-path model.
- **E100, s06-X100.** "A dog hears a gate click, then turns its head. A model links ears directly to neck movement. Evaluate this model using the record and its limits."
  Key: hearing precedes turning, but that order fits both the direct model and a brain-processing model. Add brain processing from the taught account; the observation alone does not reveal the brain. Never propose harming or blocking a brain as a test. Wrong: "The order proves ears directly command the neck" (D).
  Shape: flawed-model evaluation with an evidence limit. Catch practice tallies events and labels observations or inferences, without repairing a flawed model.
- **E101, s06-X101.** "A dog learned two cards: triangle means food left; circle means food right. It sees triangle, which is hidden, then chooses left. Explain the choice after the card disappears using your model."
  Key: eyes first receive light information; the brain uses remembered signal meaning to guide the later action. Wrong: "Hiding the card erases its remembered meaning" (E).
  Shape: delayed action after a vanished visual cue. Clap practice counts immediate responses while cues and meanings remain available; no hidden-card or delayed-choice account.
- **E102, s06-X102.** "A rabbit sniffs hidden food and approaches. A deer hears a hidden rustle and retreats. Revise your model to explain both animals' information and actions."
  Key: nose receives odor information, ears receive sound information; each animal's brain processes its input and guides its action. Processing is modeled, not observed directly. Wrong: "Nose and ears command legs directly" (D).
  Shape: revision across two different input-action records. Owl and snake practice labels one input or source fact at a time; no two-record model revision.
- **E103, s06-X103 / s06-X103-test.** "An animal learned to approach after a bell but ignores a new buzz. Predict its response to a hidden, audible bell using your model." Record the prediction before revealing: "New record: the animal approaches the hidden bell. Test your prediction against this evidence."
  Key: approach is supported by hearing and remembered meaning; justified uncertainty is acceptable before reveal. Confirm or revise using the approach record, without claiming every future response is certain. Wrong: "A hidden bell cannot guide action through remembered meaning" (E); "Ears command movement without processing" (D).
  Shape: one prediction-and-test task with a withheld observation. Practice matches already-revealed flag meanings and completed actions; no hidden cue, prediction or evidence flap.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Retain dated system-model links. Structure/function is review; processing and remembered meaning revise the class role chart.

Day 100 ideal shows information before action without comparing ability. Soft ruler, mat and clear enclosure partly fit: count catches/misses in whole events, step 1, not reaction times or catch distances. Adult plus one volunteering child, both wearing impact goggles, act with faces outside and hands below rim. Six groups have three minutes each, including goggle fitting, manufacturer-suitable wiping and drying. One handler per group, all thirty record; others sit outside boundary. Pretest containment/hygiene. If either fails or no child volunteers, use Catch C, printed and said invented: six releases, four catches, two misses. Mark supplied, no ranking. Adult cleanup three minutes.

Day 101 uses action cards/tallies in six simultaneous groups. One adult gives the shared cues, avoiding overlapping claps. Each group places one agreed action card per cue; its recorder tallies that group action, not five individual scores. Five equal cue opportunities early and five later; count one matched group action per cue, in whole actions (step 1), never one result per child. Gentle claps, seated pointing, no blindfolds. If records fail use Signal S, printed and said invented: early two of five, later four of five. Never replace valid non-improvement. No digital tool. No-kit room uses C/S and paper organ cards.
