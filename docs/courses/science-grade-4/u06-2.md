- **E99.** "A hamster's whiskers brush a wall in the dark. It steps sideways. Draw how this information guides its movement."
  Key: touch information from whisker-associated sensing structures to brain processing to movement. No receptor details needed. Wrong: "Whiskers command legs directly" (D).
  Shape: full touch-to-brain-to-movement drawing. Practice matches isolated organs, inputs and jobs, without a complete pathway drawing.
- **E100.** "A dog hears a gate click, then turns its head. Explain what this record shows about an ears-to-neck model and its needed revision."
  Key: hearing precedes turning, but order alone cannot distinguish direct command from brain processing. Include brain from taught model; record does not directly show brain. Other accurate evidence-limit accounts count. Wrong: "Ears directly command the neck" (D). No proposed interference with a living brain.
  Shape: flawed-model evaluation and evidence limit. Catch practice counts/records order, without a flawed-model judgment.
- **E101.** Reserved learned cue: triangle means food left; circle means food right. Dog sees triangle, it is hidden, dog chooses left. "Explain the choice after the card disappears using your model."
  Key: eyes first receive light information; brain processes remembered meaning and guides later choice. Wrong: "Remembered signals cannot guide action" (E); "Eyes command legs directly" (D).
  Shape: explain delayed action after vanished visual cue. Practice counts present sound-cue responses; no delayed cue.
- **E102.** Reserved rabbit sniffs hidden food and approaches; deer hears hidden rustle and retreats. "Revise your model to explain both animals' different information and actions."
  Key: nose receives odor, ears receive sound; each brain processes information and guides action. Brain processing is modeled, not directly observed here. Wrong: "Nose and ears command movement without brains" (D).
  Shape: two-input/action model revision. Practice annotates one animal's input at a time, without two-record revision.
- **E103.** Reserved trained animal approaches after bell, ignores new buzz. "Predict its response to a hidden but audible bell. Test your prediction against the new record."
  Reveal only after prediction: animal approaches hidden bell. Key: predict approach via hearing, brain and remembered meaning, then retain/revise with evidence. A justified uncertain prediction revised against evidence also meets. Wrong: "Remembered meaning cannot guide action" (E).
  Shape: hidden-input prediction then evidence test. Practice matches already-revealed actions to visible signal meanings; no prediction flap.
