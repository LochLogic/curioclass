- **s06-X99.** Invented for practice.  A hamster's whiskers brush a wall in the dark. It steps sideways. Draw how this information guides its movement.
  Key: touch information from whisker-associated sensing structures to brain processing to movement. No receptor details needed.
  Shape: full touch-to-brain-to-movement drawing

- **s06-X100.** Invented for practice. A dog hears a gate click, then turns its head. A model links ears directly to neck movement. Evaluate this model using the record and its limits.
  Key: hearing precedes turning, but order alone cannot distinguish direct command from brain processing. Include brain from taught model; record does not directly show brain. Other accurate evidence-limit accounts count.
  Shape: flawed-model evaluation and evidence limit. Catch practice counts/records order, without a flawed-model judgment.

- **s06-X101.** Invented for practice. learned cue: triangle means food left; circle means food right. Dog sees triangle, it is hidden, dog chooses left. Explain the choice after the card disappears using your model.
  Key: eyes first receive light information; brain processes remembered meaning and guides later choice.
  Shape: explain delayed action after vanished visual cue

- **s06-X102.** Invented for practice. rabbit sniffs hidden food and approaches; deer hears hidden rustle and retreats. Revise your model to explain both animals' different information and actions.
  Key: nose receives odor, ears receive sound; each brain processes information and guides action. Brain processing is modeled, not directly observed here.
  Shape: two-input/action model revision

- **s06-X103.** Invented for practice. A trained animal approaches after bell, but ignores a new buzz. Predict its response to a hidden, audible bell using your model.
  Key: Approach is a supported prediction: ears receive sound, brain processes remembered meaning and guides approach. A justified uncertain prediction is acceptable before evidence.
  Shape: one hidden-input prediction.

- **s06-X103-test.** New invented record: the animal approaches the hidden bell. Test your prediction against this evidence.
  Key: Confirm or revise with hearing, remembered meaning and guided approach. Preserve the initial prediction; one animal record does not establish an inevitable response.
  Shape: prediction tested against new record

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Retain dated system-model links. Structure/function is review; processing and remembered meaning revise the class role chart.

Day 100 ideal shows information before action without comparing ability. Soft ruler, mat and clear enclosure partly fit: count catches/misses, not reaction times or catch distances. Adult plus one consenting child, both wearing impact goggles, act with faces outside and hands below rim. Six groups have three minutes each, including goggle fitting, manufacturer-suitable wiping and drying. One handler per group, all thirty record; others sit outside boundary. Pretest containment/hygiene. If either fails or consent is absent, use Catch C, printed and said invented: six releases, four catches, two misses. Mark supplied, no ranking. Adult cleanup three minutes.

Day 101 uses action cards/tallies in six simultaneous groups. Five equal cue opportunities early and five later; step one matched group action. Gentle claps, seated pointing, no blindfolds. If records fail use Signal S, printed and said invented: early two of five, later four of five. Never replace valid non-improvement. No digital tool. No-kit room uses C/S and paper organ cards.
