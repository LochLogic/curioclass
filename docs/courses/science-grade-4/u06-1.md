Teacher distinguishes illuminating an object from viewing its image in a mirror. Eyes receive light; dull objects do not supply it. A dim yard still has light. No retina, cells or reflected colors. Draw direction arrows before naming geometric rays. Day 98 uses SL.4.5 to support a visual explanation.

- **E95.** "Jo says a bat's eyes send light toward cave walls. Name one box observation that would challenge Jo. Explain how your model changes."
  Key: open eyes cannot see the dull object with all sources off; lamp-on restores seeing. Add source-object-eye, remove eye-emission arrows. Other valid counterevidence counts. Wrong: "Eyes light the wall" (A); "No source is needed" (B).
  Shape: propose counterevidence and revise a claim. Box practice records conditions and visible/unseen, without flawed-claim repair.
- **E96.** Reserved enclosed lamp, paper star, mirror and viewing hole; lamp is the only source. Star is seen only through the mirror. "Draw the star's light path. Explain what changes when the card blocks the star-to-mirror route."
  Key: lamp-star-mirror-eye; the star disappears along that view. Wrong: "Eye-mirror-star" (A); "Light crosses the opaque card" (I).
  Shape: full image-viewing path plus interruption explanation. Practice manipulates single segments, without this drawing-and-blocking response or star case.
- **E97.** Reserved raccoon picture: eyes shine during a flash, then stop. "Explain the eye shine using your light model. Predict seeing when no light reaches the scene."
  Key: incident flash reflects toward viewer; zero illumination means no shine or visual seeing. Wrong: "Eyes make shine" (C); "It sees in zero light" (B).

- **E98.** Reserved spoon by the only lamp in a closed viewing enclosure. Covering the lamp hides it; uncovering restores it. "Draw and explain the spoon's light path using these observations."

  Shape: evidence-linked direct-view drawing. Practice is an oral audit of isolated labels, without an integrated drawn account.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: source, reflect, opaque, eye. Reflect means light bounces off. Frame: "The light comes from ___. It reaches ___." Anchor read aloud; independent labels/prompts at most 120 words daily. Read directions and evidence aloud without supplying links. Offer enlarged diagrams, tactile arrows, a scribe and home-language explanations. Keep an individual paper response; a recognition-only answer cannot replace an explanation. Printed sentences have at most 15 words within DEFAULTS.md caps. Public sharing is optional; no personal disclosures. Extend with another test of a single link.
