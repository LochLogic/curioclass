  Key: incident flash light reflects toward the viewer; removing illumination stops shine. In zero light neither reflected shine nor visual seeing occurs. Nonvisual sensing may still guide movement. Wrong: "The raccoon makes its own shine" (C); "Open eyes see without any light" (B).
  Shape: explanation of a changed shine plus a zero-light prediction. Teaching labels incident and returning light separately, then classifies box conditions; it never gives this combined account.
- **E98, s06-X98.** "A spoon sits beside the only lamp inside a viewing box. Covering the lamp hides the spoon; uncovering it restores the view. Draw and explain the spoon's light path using these observations."
  Key: lamp to spoon to eye; reflection carries illumination into the eye. Covering the lamp removes illumination despite an open view. Wrong: "The eye lights the spoon" (A); "An open view alone keeps it visible" (B).
  Shape: evidence-linked direct-view drawing. Practice attaches evidence to isolated wooden-block labels and explains one role orally, without a complete drawing or lamp-cover account.

Keep an individual paper response; a recognition-only answer cannot replace an explanation. Printed sentences have at most 15 words within DEFAULTS.md caps.
