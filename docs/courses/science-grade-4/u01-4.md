## 1. Standards and practices

**Share.** 4-PS3-1: "Use evidence to construct an explanation"; 4-PS3-3: "Ask questions and predict outcomes." Days 11 to 13 join these practices, PS3.A/B/C and energy and matter on new observations, then the anchor. Day 12 distinguishes force arrows on objects from energy destinations.

**Adult explanation.** Both motions change at contact; energy reaches receiving motion, sound and slight warming. Centimeters, sound words and force-arrow lengths are not measured energy. Keep object identity and contact conditions fixed across speeds. No speed calculation, stored-position energy or mass comparison. A second-receiver prediction depends on whether the first reaches it.

- **E11, day 11, 4-PS3-1; 4-PS3-3.** Invented wagon/carton record: repeated slower contacts move carton less than faster contacts; wagon slows. Print: "Predict a slower repeat using this record as evidence." Answer: expect wagon slowing and less carton motion than in faster contacts; controlled repeated effects support less starting motion energy available for transfer. Gather follow-up questions during Evidence notes. Wrong: equal starting energy (A); energy vanishes (C).
  Shape: one evidence-supported slower-repeat prediction. Practice fills live missing observations, without wagon/carton prediction records.

- **E12, day 12, 4-PS3-1; 4-PS3-3.** Invented record: bead slows but continues; card moves after contact. A supplied model loses the bead's energy and points its push arrow into air. Print: "Correct this model, showing what each arrow means." Answer: retain bead motion energy and transfer to card motion, sound and slight warming. Label force arrow on card as a contact push; energy arrows connect initial motion to destinations. Wrong: energy vanishes (C); force arrow into air (J).
  Shape: one corrected model with arrow meanings represented within it. Practice classifies correct truck/block overlays, never this faulty model.

- **E13, day 13, 4-PS3-1; 4-PS3-3.** Invented orange/bottle record: three trials per speed; faster always moves bottle farther and sounds louder. Picture adds a second bottle in the first bottle's path. Print: "Predict the two-bottle chain if contact occurs, using the record to support your model." Answer: first bottle can transfer energy into second-bottle motion if it reaches it, then slow, stop or rebound. Faster starting motion makes reaching the second more plausible, not certain. Retain sound, slight warming and energy in any continuing motion. Wrong: energy vanishes (C); all becomes sound (D).
  Shape: conditional chain prediction supported by a single-contact record. Practice predicts one contact, never the two-bottle chain.

**Item 1, day 14, 17 minutes.** Invented spool/flap table exactly: slower 4, 5, 4 centimeters; faster 11, 12, 11. Spool slows at contact. Print: "The same spool hits the same hanging flap. Explain how these repeated results connect speed and energy. Explain why flap movement supports your idea." Four look-fors: speed-energy relation, repeated evidence from both sets, transfer at contact, greater movement supporting more available transfer. Sample shown: "Faster rolls had more motion energy. Every faster swing was farther than every slower swing. Energy passed into flap motion. That repeated difference supports more energy available to transfer." Not yet seen: "Same spool, same energy" (A). Other wrong: centimeters are energy units (B).
  Shape: explain from two repeated numeric sets for a swinging receiver. Protect spool/flap and this explanation packet from all practice and model cards.

**Item 2, day 14, 15 minutes.** Add same-place quieter slower/louder faster reports. Fresh background card: invented illustration of a cylinder meeting a metal stop, not E5's folded target. Text: "At contact, some energy can pass into objects and the nearby air. This can warm them slightly. Our classroom equipment cannot detect this tiny change." This is a scientific account, not a measured temperature record; use U1-assessment-account. Print item 2a: "Ask a testable question about speed and energy transfer in this setup." Shape: generate one investigable question, five minutes. Print item 2b: "Model a slower repeat using the records and background card." Shape: evidence-supported before-and-after prediction, ten minutes. Administer as separate asks within the same fifteen minutes. Look-fors: speed change and observable outcome in question; both objects' motion-energy changes in prediction; sound/warming destinations. Sample shown: "Will a slower roll move the flap less? I predict less flap motion and usually quieter sound. The spool slows as energy transfers to the flap. Some energy reaches sound and slight warming." Drawing retains remaining spool motion energy if it still moves, and connects initial motion to each destination. Not yet seen: "All energy disappears as the spool slows" (C). Other wrongs: all sound (D); warmth must be felt (E); undefined best speed (F); force arrow into air (J). Accept equivalent questions and defensible sound uncertainty.
  Shape: compose a question and slower-repeat energy prediction/model from a fresh record. No practice assembles these on a swinging-receiver sheet.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

**Carried.** Individual observations day 11; correct arrow classification day 12; consensus evidence tags day 13, retaining unresolved questions. Review grade 3 second-color revision (science-grade-3 u01-2 day 8) and claim, evidence and reason (u06-4 day 113). Day 13 gives the student meaning of consensus: "A shared explanation supported by evidence." Earlier grades used consensus models; this section names that process explicitly. Remove class model day 14; return day 15.

**Day 11 apparatus.** One contained floor lane, cars, smooth block, existing room-wide impact eye protection, cleaning wipes and bags. Two handlers from rotating six, others behind boundary. Everyone present needs existing fitted impact eye protection, including the adult; otherwise use C. Six two-minute trials take twelve minutes, setup/protection three. Five cleanup minutes follow section 1.1: two to count and pack with protection on, then three cleaning all eye protection at six supplied places. Everyone records. Same car/target, visibly different approach speeds, not inferred solely from push lines. Failed readings remain unreadable; repeat once within the trial block if time permits, otherwise use invented Car record C from section 1.3, printed and said invented. No warming instrument.

**Instruments.** Ideal: live controlled observations and traceable board; these fit. No digital tool these days. No-kit C and retained marble R keep measured/invented records separate. Warming uses section 1.3's sourced account, not simulated readings.
