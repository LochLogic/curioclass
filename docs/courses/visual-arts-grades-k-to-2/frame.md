# Course frame: Visual Arts, grades K to 2

Course `visual-arts-grades-k-to-2` (catalogue id `visual-arts-k-2`), a band course for kindergarten, grade 1 and grade 2, run as three years one after another (year K, year 1, year 2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a looking and studio unit, an ideas and telling unit, a making
  and judging unit, and a showing and museum unit that ends the year with the class exhibit. Each unit joins two
  progression threads, one of creating and one of responding, presenting or connecting, so every unit makes,
  looks at another artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5).
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 5 minutes), the teaching and making, and clean-up and
  collecting work (about 7 minutes in a paint or clay session, 4 in a drawing or talk session); a readiness or
  retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days,
  its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the
  rows' load: the first unit of each year is longest in years K and 1 because it sets up the studio, its safe tool
  handling and its looking routine (four ids and two techniques); the exhibit unit is shortest because its making is
  mounting and labeling work already kept. These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md)
  spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time
  figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the visual arts educators' association's checklist for elementary visual arts asks for
  at least 50 minutes of art a week through the year, taught by a certified visual arts educator (2025 posting on
  arteducators.org, pages 3 and 4, read 2026-11-25). This course gives 80 minutes a week, 30 over that figure, and
  40 on the once-a-week path, 10 short of it; a school on that path can add time. The course is written so a
  classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a certified art teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look at a work that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one making day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into the
  openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path: years K and 1, units of 9, 8,
  6 and 5 sessions; year 2, units of 8, 8, 7 and 5; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond
  session. The check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (week 18 or 17, and week 26); the respond
  sessions fall one week later (weeks 19 or 18, and week 27), which are not check weeks. A merged day
  never drops a critique, a revision or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, visual arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year K claims only the 15 kindergarten ids (`.Ka`), year 1 only the 15 grade 1 ids
  (`.1a`), year 2 only the 15 grade 2 ids (`.2a`): 45 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and
  assessed in its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. No
  pre-kindergarten id is claimed. Every year touches all four artistic processes (creating, presenting, responding,
  connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed
  "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it
  studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the
  arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set
  adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py
  checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence still states its id
  count (15) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a child's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Visual arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied come
  from museum open-access collections (public domain works released as CC0 by the holding museum) and, for living or
  recent makers, from a description in our own words with a link to the maker's or museum's page, never a copy
  (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the
  item's own page, fails; a swapped work must keep the unit's ids met and the year's range. Each year's works
  include living makers and more than one region and period (APPROACH "Works and their settings"); the year's list
  is checked for range, since open images lean to older European and American works. Curio Class writes every step
  card, work card, problem card, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Drawing: the crayon grip and pressure (K), the line family and
  observational drawing, look, draw, look again (1), drawing a planned event scene (2). Paint: dab, wipe and stroke
  and mixing two colors (K), printing with found objects (1), tints and shades for mood (2). Form: pinch and roll
  clay or dough (K), a pinch pot made to use (1), paper sculpture that stands and building from cast-offs (2).
  Presenting: a paper border frame and a picture label (K), a mounted work with a dictated or written label (1),
  mounting, labels and frames told apart for a theme exhibit (2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills of a technique with feedback (a line drill, a
  grip check, a two-minute sketch, a mixing strip); in K to 2 it is mostly the five-minute opener and one or two
  fluency days a unit. Application: making with some choice; the second making day holds the unit's one critique
  (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: the self-check and the talk
  and table items of the unit's evidence line; the showing is scored from the kept work. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet". A
  unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, check,
  respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide
  (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept work after the session; a kept card or
  sheet made on the unit's last making days (a sort, a plan card), scored with the showing; and talk or table items
  on an unseen open-licensed work or a Curio Class original, at most two talk items a child. The end-of-unit
  assessment is a check day near the unit's end (two in units 3, 7 and 11, which own two products). Its 40 minutes:
  set-up 5; each child's self-check on the guide 5; six groups of five at the teacher's table for about four minutes
  each (24), where each child answers the talk items and, in units 1, 5 and 9, does the safe-supply or safe-handling
  item with one tool or card from a tray; clean-up 4. That is 38 minutes. The others mount or finish work with
  drawing tools, so a check day has no paint or clay, and the teacher watches one table, never a room and a table at
  once. The product's draft, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record sheet, and its
  revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each
  unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the
  first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every child has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One
  piece a unit goes into the portfolio folder with a one-line reflection, spoken to the teacher or a partner in K to
  2 (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Year K assumes no earlier art learning (pre-kindergarten ids are not taught; a
  kindergarten unit may use one as a readiness reference). Every year 1 and year 2 unit assumes the year before's
  row in the same thread, last used one grade back, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never
  a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from the band's order
  (K, then 1, then 2). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a child may meet year 2 before year 1: each
  retrieval task then carries its ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH "Readiness and the
  baseline look"). The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (K: 14 to 17 and 29 to 32; grade 1: 14 to 16, 19 and 29 to 32; grade 2: 14 to 16, 18 to
  20, 28, 29, 31 and 32). Mid-year: week 18 in years K and 1 (course days 35 and 36 of the year), week 17 in year 2
  (days 33 and 34). Year-end: week 26 in every year (days 51 and 52). Weeks 29 to 32 hold every core subject's
  year-end check in K to 2, so the year-end check comes before the exhibit unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and the
  exhibit unit's showing to families, in weeks 27 to 32, is its own end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 8 (the row asks children to act out museum jobs; the
  job-card sort keeps every id met without acting) and unit 12 (it studies two cultures' cut-paper traditions, one
  with a ceremonial use studied by looking only). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means
  the course page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, crayons, found material; nothing must be bought,
  and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One child at home: the parent is the
  audience and the critique partner, the partner task has a doubled version (parent and child), and the showing is
  to the family. The teacher needs no art training: each unit's teacher page says what to look for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as brushes and
  crayon sets counted once, printing of step and work cards), all optional beside the free path: year K $35, $15,
  $10, $10 ($70); year 1 $20, $5, $20, $5 ($50); year 2 $15, $10, $5, $10 ($40). Planning figures, not quotes; any
  ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work, a step or a recording; every day runs from the
  printed step card and work card without it. No owned id needs technology, so a paper-path student meets every id
  in full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  color-mixing practice tool, a work viewer) has a printed version that does the same job and needs no student
  account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards are picture-led with the words read aloud for K to 2; no child reads a card alone.
  English learners: each unit teaches its three to six words in depth, with a picture, a gesture and a frame ("I
  see ___ because ___."), a child may show the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the child made
  apart from what they said (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov) and California's environmental
  health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for schools, with its list of materials not to buy for
  grades K to 6 (oehha.ca.gov). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read
  checks them): only supplies labeled non-toxic and conforming to the art materials labeling standard; blades used
  by an adult only; no heat source; no powdered clay or dry pigment for children (premixed paint, moist clay or
  dough); hands washed after paint, clay and found objects; no small items in the mouth; food and dough allergies
  checked (wheat in flour dough); no sharing of face-contact items. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and
  controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 4 (the quilters
  of Gee's Bend, Alabama), unit 8 (ancient Egyptian burial objects; Oaxacan carvers), unit 12 (Chinese paper-cutting
  and Mexican papel picado). Every task that ties art to a child's own life (units 3, 7, 11) offers an invented or
  public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student
  tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, work
  cards, found and low-cost materials. Activities: technique taught in steps, works studied with their setting,
  making with a critique and a revision, a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band
  with reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per child on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the
  year's 15 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with children, and Curio Class makes no
  claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten opens the band and the subject: there is no course before and no pre-kindergarten course. The eight
kindergarten threads that start here with no earlier row are va-k-look, va-k-craft, va-k-ideas, va-k-revise,
va-k-design, va-k-judge, va-k-show and va-k-museum (their `assumes` cells are empty); each is first used in the unit
named in the table below.
