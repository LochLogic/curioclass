# The Curio Class approach to teaching social and emotional learning

**DRAFT for Cory's yes (2026-11-16).** Short by design. It fills the subject slots of the unit plan and the section plan
(docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md) for SEL. The standard, the grade sequence and the sensitive-ground rules are in
[STANDARD.md](STANDARD.md); the lens is docs/agents/lenses/sel.md.

## What a good SEL session does

- **Teaches one skill by name, with steps.** "Stop, breathe, name it" is a skill; "be kind" is not. The skill has a
  name the class keeps all year, two to four steps, and a reason it helps (explicit, in the SAFE sense).
- **Shows it, then has every student do it.** The teacher models the skill on an invented case, thinking aloud. Then
  students rehearse it: with a partner, in a short scene with invented characters, or as a body routine. Practice
  by doing is the core of the session, not an extra (bar item 5).
- **Uses invented cases.** A character in a short story, a picture, a scene card. Students may bring a moment of their
  own only if they choose, and may make one up. Nothing personal is asked for or scored (STANDARD section 5).
- **Carries it out of the session.** Each unit names a cue the teacher uses during the school day ("try your stop
  and breathe") and a family activity in the family note.
- **Comes back.** Each later unit brings earlier skills back in short practice (spaced practice, C7).

## Kinds of day in SEL (the GLOSSARY kinds)

| Kind | What it is in SEL |
|---|---|
| Understanding | Name the skill, see it modelled on an invented case, say why it helps and when to use it. |
| Fluency | Rehearse the skill until the steps come easily: partner rehearsal, a body routine, sentence frames for an I-statement. |
| Application | Use the skill in a new invented case, a scene card or a planned class moment (a group task where the skill is needed). |
| Check | The end-of-unit assessment: a rehearsal against a look-for list and scenario items (what would a good next step be, and why). |
| Respond | Return to the skill where the checks showed gaps: reteach the steps, then rehearse again. |

A section is usually two to four sessions at the minutes in STANDARD section 2: one Understanding, then Fluency and
Application, often in the same session in K to 2.

## Daily evidence

The day's evidence is **a rehearsal seen against a short look-for list** (did the student do the steps of the skill)
or **one scenario item** on an invented case (name the feeling, pick a fair next step, say why). It is recorded on a
paper class sheet as "seen / not yet", to plan the Respond day. It is never a rating of a child's feelings, character
or home life, never a survey, and never kept as student data (promise 2). A private journal page may close a
session; it stays with the student.

## What SEL adds to the unit and section plan fields

Proposed, to be tried on the first SEL unit plans (after the core subjects):

- Header: sessions by kind; sessions a week and minutes, as the frame states them (pass rule, smaller courses).
- Unit field 2: the CASEL sub-skill ids the unit teaches first (STANDARD section 3) with our own words; the skill
  names and steps the unit fixes for the year; what is carried (the class's skill names and cue words).
- Unit field 4 (readiness): one scenario item on the skills the unit builds on; the ten-minute reteach is the earlier
  skill modelled again.
- Unit field 5 (end-of-unit assessment): a rehearsal with look-fors and scenario items, each teacher-judged item with
  two sample answers. Nothing personal in any item.
- Unit field 6 (error register): the misreadings of the skill (for example "calming down means hiding the feeling",
  "an I-statement is a polite way to blame"), each with its source or "from memory".
- Unit field 8: scene cards, pictures, stories by title and record; no product or program names.
- Section field 1: before you teach: the skill as an adult uses it, and one thing to model (STANDARD section 4, E).
  The sensitive topics the section touches, named, and the school's reporting route if a child discloses harm.
- Section field 3: the day's evidence as above, with its look-fors.
- Section field 6: kind of day; how it opens (a calm start routine in K to 5); the invented case used.
- Section field 7: the talk routine (the same discussion norms as ELA that grade), the sentence frames, the rehearsal
  set-up, and the role-play rule (invented, low-stakes, no victim or perpetrator roles, no real classmates).
- Section field 9: supports: sentence frames, picture cues for feelings words, a quiet option to rehearse with the
  teacher or in writing; English learners get the frames in the class's languages where the family note has them.
- Section field 10: the carry-out cue for the school day, and the family activity line.

## Where the evidence is thin

Studies of school SEL programs as a group find gains in skills, behaviour and attainment (the meta-analyses in the
lens). They say nothing about these courses, which no study has tried. Short mindset lessons have mixed results.
Curio Class states this on every SEL course page and never calls these courses evidence-based.
