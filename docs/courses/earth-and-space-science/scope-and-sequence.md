# Scope and sequence: Earth and Space Science

Course `earth-and-space-science`, grade 12 elective (open to grades 10 to 12), draft.

The frame's nine units keep their order, owned rows and 149 days. Seven course-level check days and four course-level respond days bring the year to 160 days, five 50-minute lessons a week over 32 weeks. Course day and school day are the same number, so `school_day` stays blank. Each day's purpose, kind, skills, codes, assessment slot, tools, supplies and safety are in [days.csv](../../../data/plans/earth-and-space-science/days.csv); every base-text section is placed or cut in [source-map.csv](../../../data/plans/earth-and-space-science/source-map.csv), the days with no base lesson are its `ours` rows (one per unit and one per course-level check), and each adapted day names its sections in its note.

How every unit runs. Day 1 opens on the anchor, a question board and the class's initial model; named revision points follow, and the unit closes on a transfer task with a case the class has not seen. In units 3, 4, 5, 7 and 9 day 2 is the one-lesson reteach the frame counts for a missed readiness check, with an extension for students already ready. Each unit has about one Fluency day, never scored, and no score comes from the day a topic is first taught. Where a unit has a product (units 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9), it is drafted, gets written feedback, is revised on one revision lesson and is scored after revision. Every unit's end-of-unit sitting falls on its second-last day and its last day is the respond day: the response time, counted once, with a reteach per missed part and a fresh attempt. No unit runs over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Every end-of-unit sitting and course-level check asks a short prompt per part of each code it lists, timed in the day's note; a scored product names its parts in its note. Codes on a day are the course's own outcomes, CUR-ESS.1 to CUR-ESS.26; the core courses' expectations each unit deepens are reviewed, never claimed or checked here. The literacy codes each unit practices are named in its section and add no days.

## Unit 1. Reading a landscape

18 days, course days 1 to 18, weeks 1 to 4. Sections: 1.1, days 1 to 4, reading the map; 1.2, days 5 to 7, mapping in the field; 1.3, days 8 to 10, minerals; 1.4, days 11 to 18, rocks, the model revised, assessment and response.

The anchor asks why Monadnock stands alone. The map comes first because every later Earth unit reads one: contours and relief (day 2), then gradient and a profile (day 3), then practice (day 4). Field mapping follows while the weather is warm (days 5 to 7), and the draft map goes out for written feedback. Minerals before rocks, because a rock is named by the minerals in it (days 8 to 10), then igneous, sedimentary and metamorphic rocks (days 11 to 13), ending with the quartz-rich summit rock. Day 15 revises the Monadnock model with the bedrock map: the hard summit wears down slowest. Load: seven days on the two map outcomes (one of them field work), eight on the two rock outcomes, three on assessment and response.

Readiness: none (all retrieval). End-of-unit assessment: the revised field map, scored day 16 (CUR-ESS.2); the sitting on day 17, an unseen topographic map to profile and an unseen rock and mineral set (CUR-ESS.1, CUR-ESS.3, CUR-ESS.4), 40 minutes of prompts with the specimen items as a station rotation, one set per three students, and a supplied test record as its no-kit path. Respond: day 18. Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, RST.11-12.3, WHST.11-12.2, WHST.11-12.1.

Candidate cells: the practice element "develop, revise and use a model based on evidence" is kept on days 1 and 15; the crosscutting element "the significance of a phenomenon depends on the scale at which it occurs" is kept on days 2, 3 and 15. Both sit inside existing days; no day added. Sensitivity: day 1, Monadnock's name.

## Unit 2. The rock record

15 days, course days 19 to 33, weeks 4 to 7. Sections: 2.1, days 19 to 22, order from sections; 2.2, days 23 and 24, maps and correlation; 2.3, days 25 to 29, putting a number on time; 2.4, days 30 to 33, the record built, assessment and response.

Students meet the rock evidence before any number: Siccar Point (day 19), the principles in cut-out sections (day 20), unconformities, folds and faults (day 21). Maps and correlation come next (days 23 and 24) so a section can be built from a map. Day 25 follows Hutton's argument from rates alone; only then do logarithms solve the decay relation (day 26), bracket ages (day 27) and put a measured gap on the Siccar Point contact (day 29). Load: seven days on CUR-ESS.5 and CUR-ESS.6 (days 19 to 25), four on CUR-ESS.7 (days 26 to 29), which carries the new mathematics.

Readiness for unit 3 in the last ten minutes of day 33. End-of-unit assessment: day 32, an unseen section to order, a geologic map to section and an age bracket with its uncertainty (CUR-ESS.5 to CUR-ESS.7). Respond: day 33. Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, WHST.11-12.1.

Candidate cells: the nature of science element "natural laws operate today as they did in the past" is kept on days 25 and 29; the practice element "use mathematical representations of phenomena to describe explanations" is kept on days 26 to 28. No day added. Evidence meets belief (the age of Earth): days 19, 25, 29 and 30, and day 24 for the fossil succession. The evidence comes first, the days teach what it shows, plainly and respectfully, and students explain the evidence; no one is asked to declare a belief.

## Unit 3. Cascadia's hidden earthquake

18 days, course days 34 to 51, weeks 7 to 11. Sections: 3.1, days 34 and 35, anchor and reteach; 3.2, days 36 to 38, locating and sizing earthquakes; 3.3, days 39 and 40, plates measured; 3.4, days 41 to 45, the 1700 earthquake, the volcano and practice; 3.5, days 46 to 51, the town's hazard, assessment and response.

The ghost forest and the orphan tsunami raise the question; the tools to answer it come in the order the evidence needs them. Waves and location (days 36 to 38) and plate motion with a locked boundary (days 39 and 40) explain why a coast drops at once. Then the independent lines of evidence (days 41 and 42), written and oral records brought together on one date, a recurrence record turned into a chance (day 43), and Mount St. Helens on the same sinking plate (day 44). Day 45 practices location once more and reads a second Cascade volcano's record, so the volcano reading is met twice before the draft. The town argument is for a coastal town the class has not studied, on the Cascadia margin near another Cascade volcano, with its own evidence card; it is drafted on days 46 and 47 and revised after feedback on day 49. Load: three and a half days for CUR-ESS.8 (days 36 to 38 and half of day 45), two for CUR-ESS.9 (days 39 and 40), three for CUR-ESS.10 (days 41 to 43), one and a half for CUR-ESS.11 (day 44 and half of day 45), and four on the argument and the model for both (days 46 to 49), which join several lines of evidence on a new case.

Readiness: given on day 33; day 35 is the reteach. End-of-unit assessment: the town hazard argument, on a town and volcano not studied in class, scored after revision on day 49 (CUR-ESS.10, CUR-ESS.11); the sitting on day 50, unseen quake arrivals and an unseen station record (CUR-ESS.8, CUR-ESS.9). Respond: day 51. The argument is due in week 10, off ELA 12's process pieces (about days 14, 37, 101, 119 and 158). Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, RST.11-12.4, RST.11-12.8, RST.11-12.9, WHST.11-12.1, WHST.11-12.2.

Candidate cells: the nature of science element "arguments are strengthened by multiple lines of evidence" is kept on days 41, 42 and 49; the crosscutting element "change and rates of change can be quantified over very short or very long periods" is kept on days 40 and 43. No day added. Sensitivity: days 34, 42, 44 and 46 (the nations' published accounts, quoted and credited, never rewritten; quake and eruption worry; the 57 deaths at Mount St. Helens). The design practices from Biology and Chemistry return as retrieval on day 46.

## Unit 4. When the creek rises

17 days, course days 55 to 71, weeks 11 to 15. Sections: 4.1, days 55 and 56, anchor and reteach; 4.2, days 57 to 60, watersheds, discharge and hydrographs; 4.3, days 61 to 64, paving and flood chances; 4.4, days 65 to 71, groundwater, assessment and response.

The watershed is outlined first, on unit 1's contours (day 57), so discharge and the hydrograph have a place to belong (days 58 to 60). The stream table then tests paving against vegetation (day 61) before the gauge record turns floods into chances (day 62), and day 64 argues the anchor from the rainfall estimates, the county study and the gauge. Groundwater closes the unit because it needs the map and the idea of head (days 65 to 68), and the transfer on day 69 ties surface and ground water into one system. Load: ten days on the two flood outcomes, which carry field and lab work and probability; five on groundwater.

Readiness: given on day 54; day 56 is the reteach. End-of-unit assessment: day 70, an unseen gauge record, a watershed and well levels (CUR-ESS.12 to CUR-ESS.14), with the stream table's hydrograph reused unscored as one item's context. Respond: day 71, with unit 5's readiness check in its last ten minutes. Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, RST.11-12.3, WHST.11-12.2.

Candidate cells: the practice element "plan and conduct an investigation to produce data as evidence" is kept on days 61 and 65. The crosscutting element "systems can be designed to cause a desired effect" is dropped: designing stormwater fixes is Environmental Science unit 9's (CUR-ENV.20), this course owns no engineering outcome, and the unit explains why paving raises the peak without designing a fix. Sensitivity: days 55 and 64 (deaths in both floods).

Beside this course: the watershed is retrieval of the idea for a student who met it in Environmental Science unit 3 (weeks 7 to 11), with the outlining and the flow measurement new here (day 57); groundwater flow is taught here first (day 66).

## Unit 5. Lake-effect snow

17 days, course days 72 to 88, weeks 15 to 18. Sections: 5.1, days 72 and 73, anchor, weather log and reteach; 5.2, days 74 to 78, moisture, condensation and stability; 5.3, days 79 and 80, weather maps; 5.4, days 81 and 82, tropical cyclones; 5.5, days 83 to 88, the forecast, assessment and response.

Moisture and stability come before maps because the snow band is explained by them: dew point (day 74), the cloud bottle and the energy of condensing vapor (day 75), lapse rates from soundings (day 76), and day 78 explains the band. Maps and the jet stream (days 79 and 80) then let students forecast; the same condensation energy drives a hurricane (days 81 and 82). The forecast is drafted on day 83, scored against what happened on day 84 and revised after feedback on day 86. The class weather log runs from day 72 to day 87. Load: seven days on CUR-ESS.15 and CUR-ESS.16, six on CUR-ESS.17 with its forecast product and the transfer.

Readiness: given on day 71; day 73 is the reteach. End-of-unit assessment: the forecast discussion, scored on day 86 (CUR-ESS.17); the sitting on day 87, an unseen sounding and an unseen hurricane advisory (CUR-ESS.15, CUR-ESS.16). Respond: day 88. Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, WHST.11-12.2, WHST.11-12.1.

Candidate cells: the practice element "evaluate the claims, evidence and reasoning behind currently accepted explanations" is kept on days 82 and 84; the crosscutting element "changes of energy and matter as flows into, out of and within a system" is kept on days 75, 78 and 81. No day added. Sensitivity: days 72, 81 and 82 (deaths in the 2014 storm; hurricanes kill and displace people). Inversions and stability are taught here first; Environmental Science unit 8 meets them as retrieval (day 76).

## Unit 6. When the anchovies vanished

15 days, course days 89 to 103, weeks 18 to 21. Sections: 6.1, days 89 to 91, layers and water masses; 6.2, days 92 and 93, wind and upwelling; 6.3, days 94 to 96, El Niño and La Niña; 6.4, days 97 to 103, the collapse argued, assessment and response.

Density and water masses (days 90 and 91) come before wind-driven upwelling (day 92), because upwelling brings up the cold, dense water the profiles show. El Niño then reads as upwelling switched off by weaker winds and a feedback (day 94), seen in the buoy record (day 95) and in rain far away (day 96). The collapse is argued last, weighing warm water against heavy fishing (days 97 and 98), revised after feedback and left unscored as the frame sets (day 100). Load: four days on CUR-ESS.18, seven on CUR-ESS.19 and the argument, the rest on the anchor, transfer, assessment and response.

Readiness: none (all retrieval). End-of-unit assessment: day 102, an unseen profile set and an unseen year's buoy record (CUR-ESS.18, CUR-ESS.19). Respond: day 103. Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, RST.11-12.8, WHST.11-12.1, WHST.11-12.2.

Candidate cells: the crosscutting element "feedback can stabilize or destabilize a system" is kept on days 94 and 99; the practice element "construct, use and present an oral and written argument" is kept on days 97 and 98, the oral part in six groups of five. No day added. Sensitivity: days 89, 97 and 98 (livelihoods lost; the fishers described through Peruvian sources, never only as victims of the sea).

## Unit 7. Ice that remembers

End-of-unit assessment: the model report, scored after revision on day 121, week 25 (CUR-ESS.21), clear of ELA 12's mock opinion due about day 119 in week 24; the sitting on day 122, an unseen proxy record (CUR-ESS.20). ## Unit 8. Where did Mars's water go?

14 days, course days 124 to 137, weeks 25 to 28. Sections: 8.1, days 124 to 126, sunlight and effective temperature; 8.2, days 127 to 129, the layered greenhouse; 8.3, days 130 to 137, water on Mars, assessment and response.

The energy balance from unit 7 goes to three planets in steps: sunlight falling with distance (day 125), effective temperature (day 126), one layer that fits no planet well and fails Venus worst (day 127), then the number of layers fitted for each planet (day 128). The landforms from units 1 to 4 then become evidence elsewhere: craters and channels (day 130), a delta built in the tray (day 131), rover detections argued (day 132), and where the water went (day 133). The unit 9 sky log starts on day 124. Load: five days on CUR-ESS.22, six on CUR-ESS.23, two on assessment and response.

Readiness: none for unit 8; unit 9's in the last ten minutes of day 137. End-of-unit assessment: day 136, an unseen planet's balance and unseen images (CUR-ESS.22, CUR-ESS.23). Respond: day 137. Literacy practiced: RST.11-12.7, RST.11-12.8, WHST.11-12.1, WHST.11-12.2.

Candidate cells: the practice element "apply techniques of algebra and functions to represent and solve problems" is kept on days 126 to 128; the crosscutting element "the total amount of energy and matter in closed systems is conserved" is kept on day 128, read as: energy is conserved, and at steady state a planet radiates what it absorbs (a planet is not a closed system). No day added. Claims about water or life on Mars are kept to what the data show (days 124 and 132).

## Practice and crosscutting concept coverage
