# Scope and sequence: Library and Research Skills, grade 5

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-5`, grade 5, draft 2026-11-26. The days are in
data/plans/library-and-research-skills-grade-5/days.csv; this page holds the judgment.

## How the year sits

- **30 course days**, one session of about 35 minutes (about 29 working minutes) a week for 30 weeks, as the frame
  sets: 25 unit days (6, 6, 6, 7), two course-level check days and three respond days. Base text: none; every day is
  Curio Class's own, on paper, with no library access, device or account needed. No source-map.csv.
- **school_day = 5 x course day**, so course day n falls in week n ((school_day - 1) // 5 + 1 = n), the last day of
  that week. A school may move the session to any day of the week; the week is what counts.
- **Calendar.** Library is a special (CALENDAR.md rule 1, lead calendar pass 2026-11-26), so its checks do not count
  in the K to 5 stagger. The mid-year check (week 13) and the year-end check (week 28) are the weeks CALENDAR.md's
  table gives this course, and both miss every grade 5 core check week (ELA 17, 32; math 11, 22, 31; science 10, 21,
  30; social studies 16, 29). Week 28 also holds health grade 5's year-end check; health counts in the stagger and
  library does not, so this is no break.
- **Kinds** in library's words: Understanding is the show-me day, Fluency the practice day, Application the inquiry
  day, Check the assessment day, Respond the respond day. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the
  compelling question. Nothing is first taught on a practice day: unit 1's site-menu case, listed in the frame only
  under practice, is modeled on day 2 first.
- **Daily evidence:** an exit ticket doing the move once on a new case (about 5 minutes); working notes and the
  search log on inquiry days; one self-check per unit after the share (the card's steps rated, one thing learned,
  whether the answer or product showed its evidence, one thing to do differently).
- **Readiness and retrieval (rule 2).** Every row the course assumes is a library grade 4 row, the year before:

  | Assumed row | Last used | Treatment |
  |---|---|---|
  | g4-narrow-and-plan | library grade 4 | Retrieval, day 2 |
  | g4-search-more-kinds | library grade 4 | Retrieval, day 3 |
  | g4-own-question | library grade 4 | Retrieval inside day 3's sort |
  | g4-primary-secondary | library grade 4 | Retrieval, day 8 |
  | g4-evidence-notes | library grade 4 | Retrieval, day 16 |
  | g4-honest-use | library grade 4 | Retrieval, day 17 |
  | g4-class-survey; g4-asking-others | library grade 4 | Retrieval, day 22 |
  | g4-revise-from-advice | library grade 4 | Retrieval inside the revision day, day 25 |

  So no readiness check is placed. The name-and-photo rule of g3-safe-searching, last used in library grade 4 and assessed on day 11, is modeled on day 8 and retrieved on day 9. Rows used earlier this year come back as retrieval too (the `retrieves` column):
  the query change and search log in units 2 and 4, the lateral check and source choice in unit 3, notes and the
  bibliography in unit 4. A class that did not take grade 4 library gets each ten-minute reteach for every student.
- **Sources not loaded (later.csv L149).** The lateral-check days (8, 9, 10, 11 and the year-end check, day 28) lean
  on the method the online-reasoning lessons and web-literacy book teach; neither is loaded, so those days are
  placed from the frame alone. The Candidate days (2, 4, 7, 9, 15, 17, 18, 23, 26) come from the continuum's 3 to 5
  band file as the frame read it; that file is on the L149 load list too. The free grades 3 to 5 digital-citizenship
  presentation the frame names is for the sensitivity read on the search and lateral days (2 to 4, 8 to 10, 22); it
  was not opened.
- **Host tasks.** Each unit names a grade 5 host and a stand-alone question with a closed Curio Class source set,
  checked against data/plans/calendar.csv (below and in each unit's notes).

## Year 5 (the only year): units

### Unit 1. Research or guess (days 1 to 6, weeks 1 to 6)

*Which questions can research answer, and what do we do when a search is not working?* Owned: AASL.IV.A.1,
AASL.I.D.4. Carried: AASL.I.A.1 (grade 3), AASL.IV.B.1 (grade 4; practiced on days 2 to 4, not assessed). Three rows, six days.

Arc: day 1 models the three-way question sort and the rewrite; day 2 models the key-word list, the query change, the
source name, the ad, the site menu and the search log with its look-back, so day 3 only practices; day 4's inquiry
runs the cycle on the host question from judging it to the answer shared and the self-check; day 5 is the end-of-unit
sitting; day 6 is the respond day. Two show-me days because the unit carries two distinct moves (judging questions,
then searching and logging).

- Retrieval: day 2 (g4-narrow-and-plan, with a tentative answer on the plan line), day 3 (g4-search-more-kinds, and
  g4-own-question inside the sort).
- End-of-unit assessment: day 5, written sitting about 26 minutes (sort and rewrite 8, with "write one more question
  research could answer on the same topic" for ~AASL.I.A.1 inside it; two query changes with the menu case 10; a
  next-step line 8). Response time: day 6.
- Host: science grade 5 unit 1's matter questions (weeks 1 to 4 in calendar.csv), in step for days 1 to 4. ELA's
  e5-locate-sources comes later (weeks 9 to 16), so the unit teaches query changes itself. Stand-alone: "How do bats
  find their way in the dark?" The bat set says "most bats" echolocate, since many fruit bats find their way mainly
  by sight and smell.
- Candidates: gaps in what one knows and a manageable topic, **kept** as one line at day 4's judging step (about 2
  minutes); the tentative-answer cell, **kept** as the plan line in day 2's retrieval (about 1 minute).

### Unit 2. Choosing and checking sources (days 7 to 12, weeks 7 to 12)

*Which sources earn a place in our research, and who is behind them?* Owned: AASL.IV.A.3, AASL.IV.B.3. Carried:
AASL.IV.C.1 (begun, grade 6), AASL.VI.A.1 (grade 3). Two rows and the year's new method, six days.

Arc: day 7 models choosing three of six sources with reason lines and the group-written encyclopedia as a map; day 8
models the lateral check in full, including the statement checked against a second source (the accuracy half of
AASL.IV.B.3 the fix pass added); day 9 practices both, with a credible-looking page that carries a wrong statement;
day 10's inquiry chooses, checks and ends in the two-sentence sourced answer, shared with one source decision; day
11 is the sitting; day 12 the respond day.

- Retrieval: day 8 (g4-primary-secondary); day 9 brings back unit 1's query change and log, and the name-and-photo
  rule (g3-safe-searching) that day 8 models and day 11 asks.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 11, written sitting about 27 minutes (three reason lines 12, two lateral checks with one
  statement marked 15). Response time: day 12.
- Leans: social studies grade 5 unit 3 (ss5-sourcing-corroboration) runs weeks 6 to 9, before or beside; library
  borrows only the move, on sea turtle pages. ELA grade 5 unit 2 (e5-locate-sources) runs weeks 9 to 16, beside from
  day 9. Health grade 5 unit 16 (weeks 5 to 9) runs health's source check card (who made it, what they want, is there
  evidence, does an adult I trust agree) just before; day 8's note bridges it: the lateral check begins from "who
  made it, what do they want" and adds leaving the page and a second source.
- Host: ELA grade 5 unit 2's rainforest research, in step from week 9. Stand-alone: "How do sea turtles find their way
  back to the beach where they hatched?"
- Candidates: accuracy and comprehensiveness within a source, **kept**: accuracy is now the owned statement check, and
  comprehensiveness is a "does it cover my whole question?" tick on the reason line on days 7, 9 and 10 (about 1
  minute each); the verify-the-truth cells, **kept** as the lateral check's last line (no time beyond the frame's).

### Unit 3. Many sources, one answer (days 15 to 20, weeks 15 to 20)

*Where do our sources agree about redwood trees, where do they differ, and how do we credit every one?* No owned id.
Carried: AASL.I.B.1 and AASL.IV.B.4 (grade 4), AASL.II.B.2 (begun here, not assessed; grade 7), AASL.VI.B.2 (grade 2), AASL.VI.A.2
(grade 4). Two carried rows, six days.

Arc: day 15 models notes across three sources, the agree and differ marks and which layout fits which purpose; day 16
models the bibliography and the unnamed page; day 17 practices both with a layout choice and its reason; day 18's
inquiry ends in the brief answer (what the sources agree on, what still differs, which evidence backs it) with its
bibliography, shared; day 19 is the sitting; day 20 the respond day.

- Retrieval: day 16 (g4-evidence-notes), day 17 (g4-honest-use); day 18 brings back the lateral check and source
  choice.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 19, written sitting about 25 minutes (notes with marks and "say which plan your notes are
  sorted by and why" 13, three entries and the unnamed-page line 12). The agree and differ marks are facts only, so
  ~AASL.II.B.2 stays on teaching days 15, 17 and 18 and off the sitting. Response time: day 20.
- Leans and host: ELA grade 5 unit 2 (e5-research) runs weeks 9 to 16, so it ends in this unit's second week and its
  rainforest notes are in hand as a host; the unit still teaches notes across three sources and the bibliography
  model itself. The frame's second host, science grade 5 unit 5's water question, runs weeks 21 to 25, after this
  unit, so it is not in step (For the lead, item 2). Stand-alone: "How tall do redwood trees grow, and how old can
  they get?"
- Candidates: notetaking chosen for the purpose and an outline or mind map, **kept** (the fix pass put them in the
  row): modeled on day 15 (about 4 minutes), chosen with a reason on days 17 and 18 (about 2 minutes each); the
  credit-with-page-number cell, **dropped**, because the frame's Check set read places page numbers in a credit in
  grades 6 to 8.

### Unit 4. Researching together for a real audience (days 21 to 27, weeks 21 to 27)

*What can our group find out about how wetlands clean water, and who needs to hear it?* Owned: AASL.III.A.1,
AASL.I.C.4. Carried: AASL.V.C.2 (grade 4), AASL.I.B.3 (grade 3), AASL.I.C.3 (grade 4). Two owned rows with a group
product, its revision and the share, seven days.

Arc: day 21 models splitting, pooling, how the question grew and choosing an audience; days 22 to 24 are inquiry
days (research subquestions, pool and choose the form, draft with the bibliography); day 25 is an inquiry day, the
revision with advice (Application: it acts on advice, not on an assessment); day 26 is the share with the real audience, scored; day 27 is the
written sitting.

- Retrieval: day 22 (g4-class-survey, g4-asking-others), day 25 (g4-revise-from-advice); days 22 to 24 also bring
  back the year's search log, lateral check, notes and bibliography.
- End-of-unit assessment, in two parts on the unit's last days: day 26, the product on its rubric and each student's
  individual sharing result (groups of three, about 90 seconds each, about 20 minutes); day 27, the written sitting
  (what the group added and how the question grew 12, a form for a new audience 8). Day 27 reuses day 26's record and
  does not collect it again. Response time: day 29 alone (12 minutes of regrouped reteach).
- Leans: ELA grade 5 unit 3 (e5-presenting) runs weeks 17 to 25, before or beside; day 21 models choosing a form
  itself. Science grade 5 unit 6 (s5-protect-resources) runs weeks 25 to 30, so it meets only days 25 to 27; the
  research days use the stand-alone wetland question, the frame's "if later" line, with science unit 5 (weeks 21 to
  25) in step for water background.
- Host: protecting water by design, kept to how water is kept clean, never a local dispute. Stand-alone: "How do
  wetlands clean water, and who in our school should know?" Home route on days 22 and 26: the teaching adult
  researches a second subquestion, and the student presents their own sourced part to that adult.
- Sensitivity: groups mixed by the teacher, never by background; audience inside school on the classroom route;
  audience questions answered from the research, no student asked about home; honest use without shaming on day 24.
- Candidates: working with others and listening, **kept** as a say-back turn before pooling on day 23 (about 3
  minutes); presenting so evidence reaches the audience, **kept** as a line in day 26's rubric (no time).

## Shorter path

- **A shorter session (30 minutes, about 26 working minutes).** Teaching days cut the opening retrieval and the exit
  ticket to about 3 minutes each. Assessment days skip both. Day 11 runs its reason lines in 11 minutes (three lines,
  about 3.5 minutes each) and its lateral part in 15, for 26; the year-end check drops its 2 minutes of rereading;
  every other sitting (20 to 26 minutes) and the share (about 20) fit as written.
- **A year about 10 school days short (two weekly sessions lost).** Two fold-in days; nothing assessed and no owned
  part is cut:
  1. Day 12 (unit 2 respond), about 29 minutes saved: unit 2's regrouped reteach joins the mid-year respond day (day
     14), which already works from unit 2's class count, split 12 / 12 / 5 as on day 29.
  2. Day 30 (the year's reflect step) into day 29, about 29 minutes saved: day 29 becomes unit 4's regrouped reteach
     10 minutes, the year-end check's gaps 10, and the year's look-back 9 (the search logs reread, a written
     next-step line and the question carried to grade 6). The 5-minute fresh try is dropped, because the look-back
     line is itself a new written try at g5-search-log-look-back. Day 30 holds no assessment.
  Day 3, unit 1's only practice day, stays. Total about 58 minutes, two sessions, leaving 28 of the frame's 30 days.
- **No other reduced path is named in the frame.** A school meeting twice a week finishes in about 15 weeks; that is
  a faster pace, not a shorter path, and its checks keep their place after units 2 and 4.
