# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, grade 5

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-5` (catalog id `library-5`), grade 5. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the library pilot frame (kindergarten) beside the grade 3 and grade 4 frames; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one year of library and research skills, each unit one full inquiry cycle (ask, find, judge, use,
  share, reflect) on a small question, in the order a grade 5 class needs the moves: judging which questions research
  can answer and changing a search that is not working first, then choosing sources and leaving a page to check who is
  behind it, then notes across several sources and a bibliography, then a group inquiry shared with a real audience.
- **Days.** 30: 25 in units, 1 course-level check day after unit 2 and 1 after unit 4, and 3 respond days (1 after the
  mid-year check, 2 after the year-end check). One session of about 35 minutes, one session a week, for 30 weeks
  (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: the 3 to 5 default of 30 sessions of 35 minutes, about 17.5 hours a year, at the
  one-a-week end of the settled "one or two sessions a week"). The 35 minutes hold getting out notebooks and source
  sets, the session, putting books and cards back and collecting work; a lesson plan budgets about 3 minutes at each
  end for that, so about 29 working minutes, and every assessment form below is costed against those 29 minutes for a
  class of 30. One adult runs the room (the classroom teacher or a librarian). Nine grade 5 rows fit 25 unit days by
  load: unit 1 carries three rows (two owned) and gets 7 days; unit 2 two owned rows and the year's new method,
  lateral reading, and gets 6; unit 3 two carried rows and gets 6; unit 4 two owned rows and the share with a real
  audience, and gets 6. The year-end check sits in week 28 because weeks 29 to 32 hold grade 5's core checks, so two
  respond days follow it, the second the year's reflect step and the handoff to grades 6 to 8. Each unit's days
  include its end-of-unit assessment (no unit is over 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment); units 1 to 3 also hold
  their respond day, and unit 4's respond step is its revision day, with its regrouped reteach in the year-end check's
  first respond day. These are course days: with 30 days over 30 weeks, course day n falls in week n of
  docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, and the scope and sequence places them on school days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day):
  Understanding is the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through the move on an invented case, holding the unit's
  skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, varied tries of the same move on new cases, with feedback: sort six
  questions, fix three poor searches, check three invented pages laterally, build a bibliography for five sources);
  Application is the inquiry day (the class runs part of the cycle on its host question: question, search, judge,
  note, make, share). Fluency is modest in grade 5: one practice day in units 1 to 3, plus the five-minute opening
  retrieval on later days. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the compelling question.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words
  only, data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone).
  Ownership rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone.
  Grade 5 owns six ids (AASL.IV.A.1; AASL.I.D.4; AASL.IV.A.3; AASL.IV.B.3; AASL.III.A.1; AASL.I.C.4) and carries
  twelve as `~`, each named in its unit's What students learn with its owner. Skills: the nine progression rows, each
  in one unit. Check set: the Empire State Information Fluency Continuum, 3 to 5 band file, grade 5 column and its
  bold priority skills (read cell by cell for this frame, later.csv L103; result in the Check set read bullet); the
  "Check set adds" cells name what each unit plan should weigh from it. Values: K to 5 case sets stay neutral (no
  politics, elections, religion, identity groups or conflict; Cory, 2026-11-21).
- **Base text.** None. Every day is Curio Class's own. Curio Class writes: a short background passage and a source set
  for each unit's stand-alone question (passages at grade 5 level, a described video, a photo with caption, a data
  table invented for practice), invented results pages with source names and marked ads, invented web pages with
  "what others say" cards for the lateral check, a group-written encyclopedia article in an invented copy with its
  references, the question sort cards, the search log sheet, the bibliography model card, the subquestion and pooling
  sheet, one skill card a unit and the research notebook pages. No day needs a trade book, a paid source, a library
  visit or a database (no library access is assumed; Cory, 2026-11-11).
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from another grade 5 course, named by its row id for the
  teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question with a closed
  source set) so the unit runs when no core task is in step or at home (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence records
  both. A host is used for its question only when its topic keeps the K to 5 neutral rule; otherwise the unit borrows
  only the skill and runs on its stand-alone case (unit 2, below).
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question and closes with the share step and the
  self-check: each student rates their own use of the skill card's steps, names one step they did well and one thing
  to do differently next time (APPROACH.md, the form of daily evidence; the continuum's grade 5 reflect cells, on the
  product, the presentation and the process). The six steps on the cycle card (ask, find, judge, use, share, reflect)
  are each the focus of a unit's new move: ask (judging researchable questions) and reflect (the search log
  look-back) in unit 1, find and judge (choosing sources, the lateral check) in unit 2, use (notes across sources,
  the bibliography) in unit 3, share (a real audience) in unit 4.
- **Evidence.** Daily: an exit ticket that asks the student to do the unit's move once on a new case (about 5
  minutes, all at once), with error letters for the usual slips; on inquiry days, the working notes and search log in
  the research notebook (APPROACH.md). End-of-unit assessment: a written sitting on a case not seen before, all 30 at
  once, scored on a short teacher-judged rubric plus a few one-answer items checked on the page, in about 20 to 27
  minutes; unit 4 adds its shared product, scored on its rubric after the feedback and revision counted in its days.
  Each unit names its form and total below; no unit assessment or course-level check spills into its respond day. It
  is scored on a day after the move was practiced, never on a show-me day. The respond day's groups come from the class
  record sheet by the rubric step missed (APPROACH.md, unit field 7).
- **Reading level.** Source passages at about grade 5 level, 250 to 450 words, with a read-aloud copy on the teacher
  page and a shorter set for students who read below it (APPROACH.md, section field 9). Students write notes, logs,
  a bibliography and a group product.
- **Paper path.** The whole course runs on paper: printed source sets, invented results and web pages, the "what others
  say" cards, which stand in for a new tab, logs and notebooks. No day needs a device, an internet connection or a
  student account.
- **Devices.** None per student, and no owned or carried code needs technology. The lateral check is taught on the
  printed "what others say" cards; where a school has one shared device or a projector, the teacher may show a search
  of an invented maker's name on a teacher-checked page, under the stop rule, and the cards do the same job in full, so
  a paper-path student meets AASL.IV.B.3 completely. Unit 4's product is on paper or a display board.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond books, paper and pencils, no field work and no trip; unit 4's share
  with a younger class or families happens in school, so no laboratory or field guide applies. The screen-safety side
  is the stop rule (g3-safe-searching, kept on every search in units 1 and 2), checked by the sensitivity read
  (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, items 2 and 3).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned
  (Cory, 2026-11-14). No unit carries cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content: the case sets are
  bats, sea turtles, redwood trees and wetlands that clean water. What the agent studies first: APPROACH.md "Sensitive
  ground" items 1 (neutral K to 5 case sets; unit 2 does not borrow social studies unit 3's revolution sources, and
  unit 4 keeps science's water host to how water is kept clean, never a local dispute), 2 and 3 (the stop rule and
  privacy, units 1 and 2), 4 (honest use without shaming, unit 3's bibliography), 5 (whose voices a source set shows,
  C12) and 8 (unit 4's audience questions); and, for the search and lateral check days, the National Center for
  Missing and Exploited Children's free grades 3 to 5 presentation "Being a Good Digital Citizen: Grades 3-5", listed
  at https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/resources (listing read 2026-11-25; the kit was not opened, and a download
  may need a sign-in). No organization's name goes on a student, teacher or family page.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30: unit 1 about $8 (sort
  cards, invented results pages, log sheets), unit 2 about $10 (the six-source set and the invented pages with their
  cards, in pairs), unit 3 about $8 (three-source sets, bibliography sheets), unit 4 about $9 (group source sets,
  pooling sheets, display paper); about $35 a year.
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The 3 to 5 band file's grade 5 column and priority boxes, every indicator
  row (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-3-5\esifc-priority-3-5.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, nothing copied). Met by the rows: judging which questions facts can answer (g5-research-or-guess);
  using the menus and results of pre-selected sites (g5-search-revise, on invented pages); weighing print, video and
  online sources for relevance and for the credibility of their maker, and choosing one that fits (g5-choose-sources,
  g5-first-lateral-check); weighing information within sources against each other (g5-evidence-notes); note-making
  chosen for the purpose (g5-evidence-notes); presenting so the main points and evidence reach an audience, and
  revising from teacher and peer feedback (g5-share-real-audience); a bibliography to a model (g5-bibliography);
  reflecting on the process (g5-search-log-look-back); researching with others to a new understanding and listening
  to their ideas (g5-group-inquiry); fact-checking a source's credibility (g5-first-lateral-check). Differences, each a
  row change listed in For the lead item 1: a list of key words and synonyms before the search (g5-search-revise
  note); arranging notes in an outline or mind map with help (g5-evidence-notes note). Met without a row change: a
  tentative answer before research (unit 1's plan line, review of g4-narrow-and-plan's predicted answer); following
  personal interests in depth (g4-own-question, grade 4); own strengths and goals (the self-check). Left out: hypothesis
  testing (science) and forming one's own claim with evidence (ELA grade 5 opinion writing; STANDARD.md section 4);
  media elements and visuals against text (ELA grade 5, e5-visual-multimedia); several points of view on an issue and
  one's own assumptions about community issues (K to 5 case sets stay neutral, so grade 5 compares facts that differ;
  points of view are grade 6, g6-point-of-view, and sources that disagree grade 7, g7-sources-disagree); cyberbullying
  and online behavior (no student account or online space in Curio Class; health grade 5, g5-s-online-safety);
  copyright-free images and page numbers in a credit (grades 6 to 8 library); SEL dispositions and civic opinion
  (SEL, social studies); design thinking (science, STANDARD.md section 4). Beyond the band file: leaving the page to
  check its maker (lateral reading, owned here first as a method, STANDARD.md section 5) and skipping ads among results
  (click restraint). No row is two or more grades from its cell.
