## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.K.3, this section's share:** "narrate a single event or several loosely linked events, tell about the events in the order in which they occurred, and provide a reaction to what happened". Days 65 to 69 plan the story in a narrative-arc (character and setting, events, reaction); drafting is day 70 on (section 2.9).
- **RL.K.3:** "identify characters, settings, and major events", used on 02a as the model (days 65, 68).
- **L.K.1f, L.K.2a, L.K.2b:** "produce and expand complete sentences" (day 67, a setting sentence), first-word and I capitals and end marks seen in the teacher's printing of the dictated sentence.
- **SL.K.4, SL.K.5, SL.K.6:** day 66, describe the drawn character with detail and a voice that carries.
- **W.K.5, W.K.6:** "respond to questions and suggestions from peers and add details" (day 69, first teaching, unscored; scored on day 73 and 75); "explore a variety of digital tools" (day 69, a demonstration only; the child's own typing contribution is day 75).
- **Also owned here (unit plan owner column):** T1 (RL.K.1, RI.K.1, RI.K.2, SL.K.1a, SL.K.1b, SL.K.2, SL.K.3) and H1 print and sound components (RF.K.1a to c, RF.K.2a to c, RF.K.3c, L.K.1a formation), days 67 and 68, revision day 69, scored day 75 in section 2.10.
- **Foundations (RF.K.1 to RF.K.3, L.K.1a, L.K.2c):** cycle 9 lesson 50 and its check on day 65; cycle 10 (b, o, w; mapped words you and are) lessons 51 to 54 on days 66 to 69. Letters taught so far: a t h p n c m r v s g i d f l k y q u x, and b o w from day 66.
- **Limits that bite here.** Children plan by drawing, saying and pointing; nothing is written by a child beyond taught letters (their names, labels). The weather fact comes from 01 (a to e) or the class's observations, never from home. No thermometer numbers, no storm reenactment, no clothing inventory at home. The editor demonstration is the teacher typing a child's dictated words; it is not W.K.6 for the child.
- **Content notes.** gk-u2-02-a (the model): a spoiled picnic, a mild sad moment, an indoor ending; no danger or loss. T1 texts are two short invented cards (frost, a puppy and a bowl). No death, violence, religion or family inventory. Quiet alternative: pass, point or draw.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The narrative-arc: a beginning (character and place), a middle (what happens and what changes), an end (how the character feels). A character is a person acting; a reaction is a feeling shown, in a face, a word or an action. A "useful detail question" asks for something missing (where, what color, when) from the writer's own plan, not something else (AW). Capital and mark rules are the day 56 rules, carried.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 65: "What makes this a story, and which weather fact helps the writer?" Day 68: "What follows the first event, and which sentence in the weather reading matches the change?" Day 69: "What would you ask a writer to learn one more detail?"
- **Written task, day 67:** one dictated setting sentence printed with its capital and mark; the child may label the picture with a taught letter.
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** names where or when; the sentence is complete; the child points to the capital and the mark. Samples that meet: "The girl sits on the steps at dawn." "A boy waits by the gate at noon." Does not meet: "gate" (AE).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: plan, imagine, setting, reaction, question. Frames: "My story is about ___." "It happens at ___." "She feels ___."
- **Access.** Enlarged cards and boxes; a child who cannot draw points to cards or dictates; a child who cannot speak points or uses a communication card; large print for the poem.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each card twice; pointing, dictation and a quiet corner allowed; T1 in a pair if a group of five is too much.
- **Reading load.** No child reads alone; 02a (314 words) is read aloud; the student page holds at most 60 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second event to the plan.
