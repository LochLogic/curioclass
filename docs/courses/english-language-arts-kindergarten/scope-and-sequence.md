# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts, Kindergarten

Course `english-language-arts-kindergarten`, grade K, draft 2026-11-14.

The frame's four units, their order, their owned rows and their 34, 42, 39 and 39 days are unchanged. The 154 unit days plus four course-level check days and two course-level respond days total 160. ELA meets every school day, so course day is school day and `school_day` stays blank in days.csv. The day rows are the teaching map; this page records the decisions behind them. Each unit currently uses section `<unit>.1`; shorter sections are not designed here.

## How the two blocks work

Each unit day has 60 minutes of topic work and 60 minutes of foundations, divided into short segments. A typical topic block uses 15 minutes for reading, 10 for returning to the text in discussion, 20 for drawing, dictation or writing, 10 for language and vocabulary, and 5 for reflection. A typical foundations block uses 10 minutes for oral sound work, 10 for explicit sound-spelling instruction, 10 for formation, mapping or spelling, 25 for differentiated reading and practice, and 5 for checking and feedback. In units 1 and 2, oral language, letter work and shared print occupy the later decodable-reading time. Assessment days rearrange these minutes for individual conferences while classmates use taught routines. The omitted third hour supplies no required work.

Read-alouds carry complex language and knowledge; a child's own reading uses taught spellings. Every first reading marked whole stays whole. Later returns target a stated moment, fact, sentence or word. Turn and talk runs on the day's text most days, including at least twice in each five-day week. The teacher first models listening, turn taking, asking for help and continuing several exchanges; starters fade as children can sustain the routine. A partner interview, science talk and card-sharing discussion extend that routine, rather than substituting occasional formal discussion for daily talk.

Vocabulary is taught from the passages and then used in talk and writing. Unit 1 returns to attribute, preference and movement words; unit 2 adds weather, setting, event, reaction and word-part meanings; unit 3 uses need, depend, evidence, because and so; unit 4 returns to depend while adding community, point, reason, both and different. The unit plans select the actual sentences and preserve the text-dependent question target. No questions, items, keys or lesson scripts are written here.

There is no readiness check in this first grade of the subject: no kindergarten progression row assumes an earlier-grade row. The first foundations cycle observes entry knowledge without presuming letters or reading. Unit openings use short, unmarked retrieval tasks for the same-year work they need. Day 35 retrieves a toy-story detail, day 80 an informational topic and details, and day 119 tree needs and an opinion. These do not occupy readiness slots or displace the hooks.

### Foundations order and matching reading

The source-map.csv assigns all 130 foundations lessons to days, in lesson and cycle order. The daily notes name the exact lesson, focus, mapped words and concurrent topic reading. Letter names, formation and sound-to-letter spelling run together; each newly taught correspondence is added to sound-spelling-map. Large sound units precede phonemes. Within a cycle, explicit instruction precedes independent use. The cycle assessment stays in small-group rotations at the end of that cycle; the next foundations block starts with 10 minutes of response and a fresh retry. That response is counted once within its 60 minutes, including when the topic block has its own response.

High-frequency words are mapped from their sounds and letters, including the irregular part, and read both in a list and in text. The base cycle words are named in the daily notes. Added mapping supplies the frame's additional words without making an untaught word a decoding prerequisite. A keyword picture may recall a sound; it never supplies a guessed word. From day 85, each foundations block includes small-group and partner reading, rereading and a brief meaning response on a roughly 20 to 80 word decodable, with sound-based spelling alongside it. Groups needing letters keep explicit catch-up teaching as well as supported access to the topic read-aloud.

All 13 source reader documents and the digraph poem are mapped separately from the 130 lessons. Their titles and engagement texts do not establish that a child can decode them. Adapted child text must match the cycle's taught sound-spelling and mapped-word inventory, and uncleared pictures are replaced. The unit plan checks every word before use. A new cycle-appropriate text fills a gap; the adult may read a richer engagement text without assigning it as independent reading.

### Text placement and remaining text work

The days.csv note names the text id and title read that day, including returns during writing. These are the exact rows in data/plans/ela-texts.csv, not substituted trade books. A set-valued row is resolved into the following planned selections: unit 2's weather information has weather kinds, warmer/cooler, rain/snow, clouds and rainbow selections; its story set has two short weather stories. Unit 3's information set has living/nonliving and needs selections, and Tree Texts supplies at least two provided tree selections. Unit 4's story set has story A, the apartment-tree story, and a distinct story B; its informational set has why trees matter and how to plant one. The original texts remain to write, so these are placement specifications, not claims that finished texts exist.

Text demand rises through holding a single play-story event, holding the weather story across five days, connecting facts from several tree texts and then comparing familiar texts while distinguishing an author's reason from a child's preference. Demand rises through structure and task, not an invented numerical score. Unit 2's long snowstorm story is read whole in five consecutive sections on days 59 to 63. Its exact section breaks await the text. Unit 1's short riddles and class poem, and the later shared journals, remain purposeful lighter reading beside rich read-alouds.

The inventory lists 21 text-set rows: 8 anchors and 13 paired rows. Of the anchor rows, 4 are literary and 4 informational. Sets contain several texts, so this is a row count, not a claim about the final book or word balance. Eight rows are marked on file, twelve to write and the class-poem row to choose. No anchor has a completed measured text record in this inventory. Verse has no useful computed measure. Before use, each anchor needs its measure where meaningful, meaning/structure/language/knowledge analysis, reader-and-task reason for this position, matched supports and text read. Oral rehearsal, pictures beside already heard words and selective rereading support access to the complete text. Nothing here substitutes a simpler rewrite for the read-aloud.

## Unit 1. Toys and play

**34 days, course days 1 to 34, weeks 1 to 7.** The question is: What makes a toy fun, and how do we share it? Days 1 to 8 launch the read-aloud and discussion routines through the sharing story. Days 9 to 19 move from information and attribute words to main-idea-details, then modelled, shared and individual toy opinions. Days 20 to 34 read the child-plays-her-own-way story, revisit different text kinds, and turn a classroom interview into an informative drawing and sentence about a classmate's preference. The Magic Bow is read whole on day 23. The class poems return across the unit for shared print and rhyme.

Listening and book knowledge come first because later reading depends on them. The main-topic chart precedes informative writing. The opinion box begins with a named topic and preference, with an oral reason optional; it does not require a model from an earlier unit. An interview is modelled and rehearsed before anyone's contribution is observed. The foundations block uses 25 source lessons plus 9 added response/practice blocks, rather than dividing teaching time equally among the 14 owned rows.

There is **no readiness check**. The base first-unit assessment becomes the **mid-unit assessment on day 5**, on literary key-detail questions, illustration-to-word links and sustained partner talk, with topic response on day 8. The base second-unit assessment becomes the **second mid-unit assessment on days 17 and 18**, on topic-plus-preference writing and talk, with response on day 19. The unit's **end-of-unit assessment is observation on days 28 to 33**: interviews and multiple exchanges on days 28 and 29, topic-plus-information on day 30, main topic/details and informational picture links on day 31, book types/parts/creator roles, active group reading and all four word-relationship parts on day 32, then print/sound/letter/phonology evidence and the retained opinion product on day 33. The unit plans distribute the roster across these slots, gathering story and information questions, requests for clarification, both small- and larger-group conversation, and words used in context. The single closing topic response is day 34. The formation, sound, letter-name and onset-rime strands continue as the frame directs, with later completion evidence identified below.

**Candidate cell settled: kept.** The three base-unit assessments and the preference performance task are adapted to the named texts. Their cost is day 5, days 17 and 18, and observation/performance time on days 28 to 33, all within 34 days. The four foundations cycle checks stay in that block, on days 12, 20, 26 and 32, with response inside the next foundations block. No extra check day is added.

**Leans and sensitivity.** ELA teaches talk rules on day 1; social studies unit 1 begins school day 3, so ELA does not wait for it. Day 15 models the preference sentence locally if social studies' spoken opinion has not happened yet. The ten-minute toy-sorting bridge on day 11 precedes math unit 2, school days 19 to 44: sort by one attribute and compare by matching, without requiring counted totals. Every toy/interview day uses classroom objects, never a home inventory. Stories honor both children's feelings and do not replay a real child's conflict. Names stay in the classroom.

## Unit 2. Weather wonders

**42 days, course days 35 to 76, weeks 7 to 16.** The question is: How does the weather change what people wear and do? Days 35 to 48 use weather information for vocabulary, descriptions and informative journal work. Days 49 to 64 introduce story-map before retelling; teach word-parts for meaning, oral grammar and sentence expansion; and read the longer story whole on days 59 to 63. Days 65 to 76 move from a weather-story model and shared plan through a child's own event sequence and reaction, peer questions, added detail and publication.

Story elements precede retelling and planning a narrative. A model and shared language work precede the child's draft. Sentence expansion, first-word and I capitals, and recognized/named end marks are taught before the closing writing observation. Unknown-word work covers literary and informational questions, a new meaning applied accurately, and common inflections/affixes as meaning clues. It never replaces decoding. The 35 foundations lessons finish letter names and sounds while moving from larger oral units to three-phoneme operations, with 7 additional response/practice blocks. The new writing and language load earns this unit the year's longest allocation.

There is **no readiness check**; day 35 retrieves an earlier toy-story detail. The base first-unit assessment becomes the **mid-unit assessment on day 46**, on independent weather information and a detailed, clear description with a drawing; topic response is day 48. The base second-unit assessment becomes the **second mid-unit assessment on day 63**, on characters, setting and major events in the completed story; response is day 64. The **end-of-unit observation on days 74 to 76** joins the narrative and discussion with phoneme/word-meaning/oral-grammar evidence, complete and expanded sentences, capitals and named punctuation, and peer revision. The narrative shows event order and a reaction, not a grade 1 demand for elaborate closure. Day 75 records every child's contribution to a collaboratively typed class story on a shared device. Day 76 gathers remaining letter/sound/print evidence and supplies the one closing topic response, with a short fresh retry. The foundations response remains in its own 60-minute block.

**Candidate cell settled: kept.** Independent weather writing, weather-story elements, the closing small-group weather-story discussion and the narrative performance task cost the assessment and observation slots above, inside the 42 days. Cycle 5 to 11 checks cost small-group time on days 39, 45, 52, 58, 65, 70 and 75; their responses use the following foundations blocks. No separate day is charged twice.

**Leans and sensitivity.** Science weather unit 2 spans school days 23 to 44, and its chart runs from week 1. Day 35 uses that record where available; five minutes of ELA picture-journal observation supplies the frame's bridge where not. Temperature stays warmer/cooler in words and pictures. Weather stories end safely, use no disaster photographs, permit a quiet alternative and invite no private disaster account or home-clothing inventory. Describing a person offers a person you know rather than pressing for family details. The digital story uses no child account or external name/photo posting.

## Unit 3. Trees are alive

**39 days, course days 80 to 118, weeks 16 to 24.** The question is: What do trees need, and who needs trees? Days 80 to 88 use the living/nonliving and needs texts to introduce connections between two facts and the research-cycle: question, read/observe, record, share. Days 89 to 101 move through A Tree Is a Living Thing, Who Depends on a Tree?, Tree Texts and We Depend on Trees to a supported animal-needs piece. Days 102 to 118 run a second shared inquiry about a tree, move notes into information, and revise a tree collage before sharing it. The long-time tree story on day 84 supplies a literary view of change beside the information.

Needs facts come before because/so links that use them. Modelled note gathering precedes independent contributions to a shared notebook, and a class composition precedes the collage. These are two questions and two research products within the unit, following informal information gathering in the weather journal. Formal research ownership stays here. The 35 foundations lessons add digraphs, then one short vowel per cycle, decoding, spelling and meaningful decodable reading, with 4 added response/practice blocks. Four owned rows therefore receive substantial repeated time: decoding and research contain many more acts than a single topic label suggests.

There is **no readiness check**; day 80 retrieves topic and details. The base first-unit assessment becomes the **mid-unit observation on days 86 and 87**, on text connections and sharing evidence, with topic response on day 88. The base second-unit assessment becomes the **second mid-unit observation on days 97 to 99**, on shared research and writing about animals depending on trees, with response on day 101. The base third-unit science talks begin **end-of-unit observation on days 107, 109 and 110**. Days 111 and 116 to 117 finish decoding/spelling/decodable-understanding and research/connection evidence alongside the collage. W.K.7 requires the child's participation, W.K.8 the child's recall or gathering to answer the question; a group's attractive collage alone does not establish either. RI.K.3 requires a connection between two facts heard in the text. RF.K.3d requires identifying the changed letter's sound, and L.K.2d sound-based spelling. RF.K.4 includes meaning, not word calling alone. The one closing topic response is day 118; foundations response remains in that block. Long-vowel completion continues in unit 4.

**Candidate cell settled: kept.** The informational connection/picture task, animal-needs research/writing, science talks and collage retain the assessment/performance slots above inside 39 days. Cycle 12 to 18 checks occupy foundations rotations on days 84, 90, 95, 100, 106, 111 and 116; responses occupy the following foundations blocks. Informative output is a topic and some information, with drawing and dictation accepted. The source's more demanding focus-and-three-detail-sentence structure is not made a kindergarten scoring condition.

**Leans and sensitivity.** Science living-things unit 4 spans school days 73 to 98; its exact needs instruction is not guaranteed by ELA day 80. The ten-minute bridge reads the needed facts in ELA's own selection before research uses them. Social studies unit 4 now starts school day 81 and ends 99, rather than the frame's rough week-18 estimate; exact sequencing instruction may still follow day 82. Day 82 spends ten minutes modelling first/next/then and a two-fact because connection. The optional day 83 real-tree observation replaces a ten-minute picture look: adult supervision, checked pollen/nut allergies, touch only, nothing tasted. Photographs supply the observation when a visit is inaccessible or unsafe. Predation is stated without graphic pictures; phonetic spelling is accepted.

## Unit 4. Enjoying and appreciating trees

**39 days, course days 119 to 157, weeks 24 to 32.** The question is: Why do people care for trees? Days 119 to 128 read two whole original tree-care stories, each familiar before the characters' experiences are compared. Days 129 to 139 read the two informational selections and the true account of Wangari Maathai, distinguish an author's point and stated reasons from a child's own preference, and compare familiar informational texts. Days 140 to 157 use those readings to plan, draft, revise, illustrate, publish and explain a tree appreciation card.

Tree knowledge and the earlier opinion box are retrieved before an author's reasons are added to the model. A whole story is heard before the comparison chart; both informational selections are heard before their similarities and differences are requested. Reasons come from the author's words, while the child's reason remains optional under W.K.1. This unit owns two new rows but also carries the most independent process writing, 35 foundations lessons and the completion of reading and sound-spelling strands, with 4 added foundations practice/response blocks. Its 39 days are not 39 days devoted to comparison alone.

There is **no readiness check**. The base first-unit assessment becomes **mid-unit observation on days 126 and 127**, on both likeness and difference in familiar characters' adventures/experiences, with topic response on day 128. The base second-unit assessment retains **day 132** for an author's point and reasons, adding informational comparison, and **day 138** for the planting-place opinion; topic response is day 139. The base third-unit assessment begins **end-of-unit observation on days 142 and 143**, joined to the continuing card process and final evidence on **days 153, 154 and 156**. Both RL.K.9 and RI.K.9 are observed, alongside the author's stated reasons under RI.K.8. Day 155 records each child's contribution to collaborative audio publication, the second digital tool kind after day 75's typed story. Day 156 completes the five printed-vowel short/long associations and remaining taught-pattern reading evidence. Day 157 is the one closing topic response, with a fresh task, before the course-level check draws on earlier units.

**Candidate cell settled: kept.** The familiar-story comparison, author-point/opinion work, detailed drawing and card performance task are retained in these slots inside 39 days. Cycle 19 to 25 checks, including the last decoding check, occupy foundations rotations on days 123, 129, 134, 140, 145, 150 and 155; their responses use the next foundations blocks. Digital production is observed as part of the product, not added as a fifth kind of day.

**Sensitivity.** All planting choices use the school grounds or a class pot; no child needs a yard. Days reading or revisiting the Maathai account carry the sensitivity flag. Its author must use the frame's named movement, Kenyan, first-person and scholarly sources and show people acting with agency. Millions is said in words, never assigned as number work. That study, factual verification and the sensitivity read are pending. Card and audio sharing remain inside the class, without external child names or photographs. No child account is needed.

Base lesson order is kept. The material changes are the frame's replacements and boundaries: original texts replace trade books, the one long original weather story occupies the source's several trade-book return slots on days 59 to 63, quantitative temperature work becomes words and pictures, research writing stays within kindergarten's demand, and independent reading never inherits an untaught spelling from a source word list. The map records these as adaptations of kept lesson purposes. Required source credits belong in later day credits, using the frame's exact source-credit pattern; source names here identify provenance, not a required classroom product.

## Shorter path

**A shorter period.** A 100-minute meeting keeps all 60 foundations minutes. Its 40-minute topic block keeps 15 minutes of complete first reading or a purposeful return, 8 of text-based talk, 12 of drawing/dictation/writing and 5 of vocabulary/sentence work. Keep the sharing-story conflict and response, the toy attribute and movement passages, all five snowstorm sections, the needs/because passages, both stories' relevant experiences, and both informational texts' points, reasons and planting steps. Keep their vocabulary and text-dependent questions, including the child's own questions. Shorten repeated station play, display decoration, copying and celebration, rather than the reading, phonics or response. On process days, use brief rereading to release more of the same 40 minutes for writing. The 24-child conference budget above is 174 minutes across two 100-minute meetings, so it fits provisionally; actual text lengths and the unit plans must confirm it. This is a shorter-period design, not a claim that a half-day kindergarten course has been built. A smaller daily provision has not been demonstrated.

**A year about ten meetings short.** Fold the following unassessed days into the named neighbors. Keep the complete reading and the specified skill work; remove duplicated practice. Days.csv remains the 160-day reference, so assessment and handoff references do not silently change.

Ten folds yield 150 meetings. They do not by themselves solve the shorter whole-school calendar: these folds put the mid-year checks at compacted meetings 71/72 and the year-end checks at 148/149. That would put ELA in weeks 15 and 30 on a newly numbered five-day calendar, conflicting with the present social studies targets. A school using the shorter year must coordinate all four subjects' check windows, keep the five-school-day ELA/math separation and rerun the calendar check. The fixed 160-day plan makes no such calendar change. This adoption limit belongs to the lead, not to an unrecorded change in another course.

## For the lead

1. The frame's definition of ownership as teaching and checking a row to the standard conflicts with its foundations placements. Unit 1 owns all-26-letter recognition and every-consonant sounds but teaches only eight letters there; onset-rime continues later. Unit 2's L.K.2 parent needs unit 3 spelling; unit 3's RF.K.3b needs unit 4's long-vowel associations. Unit 2's W.K.6 variety of tools is completed by unit 4's audio publication. This draft preserves every owned row and explicitly stages evidence through days 76 and 156. Fix the frame's ownership/completion wording to permit documented distributed foundations evidence, or authorize a different ownership allocation. Do not treat a unit 1 sample as complete mastery of an all-letter standard.
2. The original texts, class poems and measured text records are not complete. This writing scope permits only the three sequence files, so text creation and its records/reads must be assigned before teaching. Day placement and text-demand judgments are provisional until those texts exist. The Maathai account and adapted decodables need their specified factual, rights and text/sensitivity checks.
3. The 150-meeting path needs a coordinated grade calendar before adoption. The 100-minute period has a provisional budget, not a classroom timing trial. Confirm actual text lengths and individual-conference load in the unit plans. No unit was resized to conceal these limits.

The least certain choices are actual read-aloud length, the word-level fit of each adapted reader, and one-adult conference timing with a different class size. The item-level decisions made here are the specific bridges, all four kept Candidate cells, the five-day long-story placement, two research inquiries, two digital tool kinds, distributed observation windows and the ten named folds.
