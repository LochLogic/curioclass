# Section 1.2: Planning and saying how friends play


## 1. Standards and practices


Children still do not read. They hear, point, draw, dictate and act; the teacher writes what they say. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

- **W.K.2, days 6 and 7.** "Use a combination of drawing, dictating, and writing to compose informative/explanatory texts ... name what they are writing about and supply some information about the topic." Day 6 is a class message composed together, then a drawn and dictated sentence; day 7 is a drawn plan with a label and one dictated sentence. A name alone is not yet information (error M).
- **SL.K.1, SL.K.1a, SL.K.1b and SL.K.3, days 6 and 8.** The talk rules and the help question from 1.1 are used in partner talk, two exchanges at least, and not taught again.
- **RL.K.10, RI.K.10 (day 7).** Shared reading of poem p02, Ask and Listen, pointing along large print.
- **RL.K.1, RL.K.7, SL.K.2 (day 8).** "With prompting and support, ask and answer questions about key details"; "describe the relationship between illustrations and the story". RI.K.1 and RI.K.7 have no information text yet, so the same routines run on the story; the information versions come on days 10 and 14.
- **Foundations, days 6 to 8.** Day 6 repairs cycle-1 evidence (t, print direction) and rereads I and my in the class message; day 7 teaches the letter a (name, sound, keyword, lines); day 8 repairs and retries. RF.K.1a to 1d, RF.K.3a, L.K.1a, L.K.2c, RF.K.2a, 2b and RF.K.3c are practiced; RF.K.2c (onset and rime) is not taught until cycle 5.
- **Limits that bite here.** Classroom toys and the shared story only; no home toys, no child as the example of a mistake; a child may pass. The class message uses invented friends, not named children.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the teacher writes while the children dictate (the child supplies the ideas, the teacher the letters). Why a plan names one toy and one action. That the sound /a/ is shown with a mouth shape and a keyword, never guessed from a picture.

## 2. Where it sits


Uses the notes from 1.1: A1 to A6 on the record sheet and the exit errors of days 1 to 4. Day 6 repairs the most missed item; its own results (E6, E7, the day 8 retry and the foundations notes) go to the record sheet and set the order of the groups for day 9.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Days 6 and 7 each have one exit; day 8 is a response day and its fresh retry is unscored.

**Exit questions.** Five minutes at the end of the topic block, read aloud once and again on request. All cards are invented for practice and say so. Item (a) is answered by every child on paper (circle the picture); item (b) is asked aloud of the day's rotating six, and a child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". One ask in each item.
- **E6 (day 6; W.K.2, SL.K.1).** Read: "Two friends stack cups. One holds the pile steady while the other adds a cup." (a) "Circle the picture that belongs in a class message about how these friends play." Three pictures: one child steadying a stack while another adds a cup; a child alone drinking from a cup; two children running. Answer: the steadying and adding picture. Wrong: the child drinking alone (A). (b) "Tell me one thing the message could say about these friends." Answer: "One holds the cups" or "They take turns." Wrong: "Cups" or "I like cups" (M for a name alone; A for an unrelated idea).
- **E7 (day 7; W.K.2).** Read: "Three children open a toy shop. One sorts the toys on a shelf." (a) "Circle the picture that goes in a drawing plan about this shop." Pictures: a child sorting toys on a shelf; one toy alone on a floor; a playground. Answer: the sorting picture. Wrong: the lone toy (A). (b) "What will you tell a reader about the shop?" Answer: names the shop and one action, "One child sorts the toys." Wrong: "I like it" (X).
- **Day 8 retry (unscored).** Read a new card: "Tal offers the drum. Bo asks for a turn. Tal passes it." (a) "Circle the picture that matches the last sentence" (Tal passing the drum; Tal holding it away; Bo walking off). Answer: passing. Wrong: holding away (B). (b) "How could a partner ask for help?" Answer: "Can you help me?" Wrong: "I cannot do it" (E).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The talk rules poster, the sound-spelling-map (t and, from day 7, a with the keyword apple), the story One Hoop, Two Friends, and the turn card. The class message is kept on the wall chart for day 8 and for 1.3, where "teach a reader how toys move" begins from it. Drawings go to the portfolio; day 16's opinion piece is the first revised piece.
- **Key moment, day 6: a class message heard back.** Ideal instrument: large chart paper at the rug, so every child sees the teacher's finger under each word. Spec: fits; paper and a pointer. No tool is used; no gaps or asks.
- **A room with nothing.** Torn paper for the plan, a hand-drawn chart for the message, two real toys; the poem copied large by hand.

## 6. Days

## Day 6


- **5 min. Repair.** The most missed of A1 to A6, from the record sheet: wrong example beside right, one fix.
- **6 min. Reread the story.** The teacher rereads One Hoop, Two Friends whole, pictures held up.
- **4 min. Talk.** "What do friends do to play together?" Pairs tell, two exchanges; the teacher hears three pairs.
- **10 min. Model the class message.** The teacher thinks aloud, writes "We take turns." on the chart, finger under each word left to right; the class says each word, finding I and my in the next line.
- **15 min. Compose the message together.** Each pair gives one fact from the story; the teacher takes six ideas and writes three sentences, rereads them pointing (the day's six decide the order).
- **15 min. Draw and dictate.** Each child draws one picture for the message (a friend passing, a friend waiting) and tells a partner one sentence; the partner says it back. The teacher takes dictation from the six at the table.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E6.
- **3 min. Opening.** Chant "t, t, /t/, top" and the name game.
- **9 min. Foundations response.** The most missed of N, O, P and print direction from day 5: wrong example beside right, then a fresh retry, each child writes t once.
- **5 min. Mapped words.** Reread I and my in the dictated sentences ("I take turns." "My turn.") as whole words pointed to, not decoded.
- **3 min. Closing.** "Where is the start of the line?" Children point.
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six, five 8-minute turns. Table: t and T, the sound /t/ and rhyme for the six; stations: draw and label, name-card match, big-book browse, finger t in the sand tray.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the chart; pairs hold the turn card. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dictating, circling. Evidence: E6; foundations notes for the six.

## Day 7


- **4 min. Repair.** Error A or M from E6, one wrong example beside a right one.
- **8 min. Shared reading, p02.** The teacher reads Ask and Listen in large print, pointing under each line, twice more with the class joining "We listen well."; ask "Which line tells us to wait?" (then it's your turn).
- **8 min. Model a plan.** The teacher draws a plan for the block corner, names one toy and one action, writes the label on request, and says one sentence: "We stack blocks."
- **10 min. Choose and plan.** Each child picks one classroom toy and one thing to do with a partner; pairs tell each other, two exchanges; the teacher hears three pairs. No home toys.
- **20 min. Draw the plan.** Children draw their plan; the teacher labels the toy word at the table for the day's six; the others label by sounding the first letter if they know t or a.
- **5 min. Share.** Six children tell their plan in one sentence; the class says "I heard ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7.
- **3 min. Opening.** Chant for t; "What's the word?" with a toy and an action.
- **9 min. Showing the sound a.** Say /a/ with a wide mouth and a bite of an imaginary apple; keyword apple; no letter story. Say the name "ay", then the sound (error N); add a and apple to the sound-spelling-map.
- **5 min. Lines for a.** Curve around, then straight down, in the air and on a partner's back, then on paper.
- **3 min. Closing.** Chant: "a, a, /a/, apple."
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six. Table: a, its sound and a toy's beats for the six; stations: finger a in the sand tray, draw and label, name-card match, big-book browse.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the plan model; the class holds the map. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dictating a label, tracing. Evidence: E7; foundations notes for the six.

## Day 8


- **15 min. Targeted reteach and retry.** 7 minutes: the two most common errors of days 1 to 7 (wrong example from a puppet, right example, class tries). 8 minutes: the fresh card, read once; every child circles (a); the day's six answer (b).
- **12 min. Reread pages 5 to 9.** Two questions: "Which words tell how Teo felt at the end?" and "What does the last picture show?" Children point back to words and picture.
- **13 min. Partner talk.** Each pair talks about the best way the friends shared, two exchanges, and asks one question to get a missing detail; the teacher hears three pairs.
- **10 min. Read, Point, Talk, Draw.** Draw one moment of passing, tell a partner which words fit.
- **10 min. Share.** Six children say one sentence about their drawing.
- **3 min. Opening.** Chant for t and a.
- **10 min. Foundations response.** The most missed of t, a, N, O, P and AA: wrong example beside right, then a fresh retry on a new strip: say the sound, pick the letter card, print it.
- **5 min. Mapped words.** Reread I and my in a dictated sentence ("I have my toy.") as whole words, pointed to.
- **2 min. Closing.** Chant for t and a.
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six. Table: t, a and rhyme for the six; stations as day 7.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the puppet and the retry card; pairs hold the turn card. Tool: None. By hand: circling, drawing, writing t and a. Evidence: the retry (unscored); foundations notes for the six.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 6, "What do friends do to play together?"; day 7, "Which line tells us to wait?"; day 8, "What does the last picture show?".
- Written task, day 7: draw the plan, with one toy label the teacher writes or the child sounds out, and one dictated sentence. Children do not write words.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) names one toy; (2) tells one thing a person does with it; (3) the drawing and sentence agree. Samples: shown, a drawing of blocks and "One stacks, one hands blocks"; shown with help, the toy named after a prompt; not yet seen, "I like it" (X) or a label with no action (M).

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 6 opens with the repair of the most missed item of day 5. Day 7 repairs E6; day 8 starts straight into the reteach (a repair block, no second warm-up).
- **Foundations frame, once.** Opening, whole-group work, closing, then five 8-minute rotations: the table (six children, one minute each, two minutes for moves) and four stations. Rhyme and a toy's beats run every day; letter prompts use t and, from day 7, a. Mapped words: I, my.
- **Fluency target.** None; no rate is scored.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: friend, plan, share, wait, pass, message. Frames: "I plan ___." "One ___, one ___." "We take turns." Home language, signs and pointing count as answers; the teacher says the English back.
- **Access.** Large print and a pointer; a tactile letter a; a child who cannot sit still may stand at the rug edge; a communication aid or pointed card is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit is read aloud; a child may point, circle, draw or answer privately; a scribe records without supplying ideas. A child who passes is "not yet heard", not missed.
- **Reading load.** None alone. The teacher reads the poem, story and cards aloud; the child sees a name card, the poem in large print and the letters t and a.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure adds a second fact to the dictated sentence.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Reread the story, ask the same two questions with pointing, and compose the message as a short list of two sentences together; the child draws a plan for one real toy and dictates one sentence. The teacher or parent is the partner and says the sentence back.
- **One projector.** Show the class message, the poem and the map on one screen; children answer on paper and point on the screen only in turn.
