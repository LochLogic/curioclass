**Exit questions.** Five minutes at the end of the topic block, read aloud once and again on request. All cards are invented for practice and say so. Item (a) is answered by every child on paper (circle the picture); item (b) is asked aloud of the day's rotating six, and a child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". One ask in each item.
- **E6 (day 6; W.K.2, SL.K.1).** Read: "Two friends stack cups. One holds the pile steady while the other adds a cup." (a) "Circle the picture that belongs in a class message about how these friends play." Three pictures: one child steadying a stack while another adds a cup; a child alone drinking from a cup; two children running. Answer: the steadying and adding picture. Wrong: the child drinking alone (A). (b) "Tell me one thing the message could say about these friends." Answer: "One holds the cups" or "They take turns." Wrong: "Cups" or "I like cups" (M for a name alone; A for an unrelated idea).
- **E7 (day 7; W.K.2).** Read: "Three children open a toy shop. One sorts the toys on a shelf." (a) "Circle the picture that goes in a drawing plan about this shop." Pictures: a child sorting toys on a shelf; one toy alone on a floor; a playground. Answer: the sorting picture. Wrong: the lone toy (A). (b) "What will you tell a reader about the shop?" Answer: names the shop and one action, "One child sorts the toys." Wrong: "I like it" (X).
- **Day 8 retry (unscored).** Read a new card: "Tal offers the drum. Bo asks for a turn. Tal passes it." (a) "Circle the picture that matches the last sentence" (Tal passing the drum; Tal holding it away; Bo walking off). Answer: passing. Wrong: holding away (B). (b) "How could a partner ask for help?" Answer: "Can you help me?" Wrong: "I cannot do it" (E).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: friend, plan, share, wait, pass, message. Frames: "I plan ___." "One ___, one ___." "We take turns." Home language, signs and pointing count as answers; the teacher says the English back.
- **Access.** Large print and a pointer; a tactile letter a; a child who cannot sit still may stand at the rug edge; a communication aid or pointed card is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit is read aloud; a child may point, circle, draw or answer privately; a scribe records without supplying ideas. A child who passes is "not yet heard", not missed.
- **Reading load.** None alone. The teacher reads the poem, story and cards aloud; the child sees a name card, the poem in large print and the letters t and a.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure adds a second fact to the dictated sentence.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Reread the story, ask the same two questions with pointing, and compose the message as a short list of two sentences together; the child draws a plan for one real toy and dictates one sentence. The teacher or parent is the partner and says the sentence back.
- **One projector.** Show the class message, the poem and the map on one screen; children answer on paper and point on the screen only in turn.
