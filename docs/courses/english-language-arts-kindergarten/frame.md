# Course frame: English Language Arts, Kindergarten

Course id ela-k12 (kindergarten year; slug english-language-arts-kindergarten), kindergarten. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text, in its order: toys and play, weather, trees as living things, people caring for trees. Each unit is one topic with its own set of texts, so knowledge and words pile up across it, and units 3 and 4 share one topic (trees) at two depths.
- **Days.** 160: 154 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 2, 2 after unit 4) and 2 respond days (one after each check). ELA is a daily, full-year subject, so the course uses the full yardstick. The time a day is about 120 minutes, five days a week, in two blocks: a topic block of about 60 minutes (the read-aloud and its questions, talk, writing and language work on the unit's texts) and a foundations block of about 60 minutes (phonological and phonemic awareness, letters and sounds, handwriting and spelling, decodable reading in small groups and with a partner), split into shorter segments of 10 to 20 minutes as kindergartners need. Kindergarten shares the day with math, science and social studies, so the course assumes two hours, not the base text's three: the base text's third hour (Labs, oral-language inquiry and play) is left out (source map). Why the total fits: the base text's four modules hold 139 topic lessons and its Skills Block 130 foundations lessons; 154 unit days give every topic lesson a day with 15 days over for finishing the unit's end task and rereading, and give the foundations block 24 days over for re-teaching after each cycle check and extra decodable reading. A full-day kindergarten is assumed (For the lead, item 1).
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for ELA, kindergarten (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Every kindergarten standard and lettered part is owned by one of the 29 rows and each row sits in one unit below; where a row's standard is completed by parts in two rows (RF.K.1, RF.K.2, RF.K.3, L.K.1, L.K.2, L.K.4), the rows name the split. RF standards live in the foundations block, the rest in the topic block. Check set: each unit plan compares the unit with the base text's unit assessments and checklists for its module and with the published K to 2 review criteria lines on phonics order and aligned decodables (named in the progression read, docs/reviews/progression-ela-codex-2026-11-13.md); the Units table's "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects. The bar is the lens (docs/agents/lenses/ela.md) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md; RUBRIC-ELA.md covers grades 3 to 12 and is used here only for its text, question and writing lines. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in order: higher court, backed sources, dignity and openness, grade and life experience.
- **Base text.** EL Education K-5 Language Arts, 2017 edition, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017, docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md): the four kindergarten modules (module lessons, assessments, performance tasks) and the 2017 K-2 Reading Foundations Skills Block (cycles 1 to 25, with its Learning Letters book). Every trade book is replaced by a Curio Class text written to the per-grade text spec (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12, course texts); the short texts the base text wrote itself ("Toy Riddles", "What's Alive and What's Not?" and the like) are adapted with the credit line; texts the base marks as written by others are replaced. The 2025 edition and the 2024 Skills Block (CC BY-NC) are never used. The source map closes this frame. The day credit follows BASE-TEXTS.md: "EL Education K-5 Language Arts Curriculum (2017), grade K, module M, unit U, lesson L", and for the foundations block the Skills Block cycle and lesson.
- **Where a row sits.** A row sits in the unit that teaches it to the kindergarten standard and checks it, which follows the base text's unit assessments. Before that unit the class meets the thread informally (a first "my favorite toy" sentence in unit 1 before opinion writing in unit 4); after it, the skill comes back as review on new texts. Foundations rows run in the base text's planned letter and pattern order across units: each sits in the unit where its first element is taught, and the units that carry it on say "continued" and name the cycles.
- **Texts and the reading level.** Kindergartners meet complex text through read-alouds two or three grade levels above what they can decode (the base text's read-alouds ran from about 250 to 820 on the common computed measure); they read decodable texts themselves. Each unit plan writes a text record for every anchor (measure, what makes it hard, the task, why it sits here), a question set and a word list (APPROACH-ELA, data/plans/<course>/ texts.csv, questions.csv, words.csv). Each unit mixes stories, poems and informational read-alouds, about half and half across the year. The year's long text read whole is unit 2's longer weather story, read across a week. Informational read-alouds may draw on recent US government pages for children (weather, trees), adapted for reading aloud and credited.
- **Decodable texts.** Curio Class writes its own (ek-emergent-reader): each uses only the letter-sounds and high-frequency words taught by that cycle, checked against the sound-spelling-map. No levelled predictable book, and no cue to guess a word from the picture or the first letter, anywhere in the course.
- **Writing calendar.** Unit 1: informative (a labelled drawing and sentence about a classmate's toy). Unit 2: narrative (a weather story, through the process) and the class weather journal. Unit 3: informative research (how an animal depends on a tree; the tree collage, through the process). Unit 4: opinion (where I would plant a tree) and the tree appreciation card (opinion, through the process). Three process pieces, one of each type across the year.
- **Talk.** A named discussion protocol on the unit's text most days, at least two a week (ek-discussion); routines are modelled first and sentence starters fade across the year.
- **Paper path and devices.** Every task has a paper route. No student accounts. W.K.6 is met on a teacher-held or shared classroom device on which the class makes one digital piece together (unit 2, again in unit 4); the paper route alone does not complete W.K.6. No child's name or photograph is posted outside the class. No timer is shown to a child; the teacher holds any watch.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 4 also needs a qualified reader for its true account of a tree-planting leader from Kenya (a reader from or expert on the place and people); who, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten is the first course of the subject. It assumes oral language and no letter or reading knowledge (data/progressions/parts/ela-k2-edges.md). Unit 1 starts every thread from the beginning: print concepts, letter names and sounds, rhyme, the read-aloud routine and talk rules.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 2 (2 days, mid-year).** One-to-one and small-group, spread over two days so every child is heard: all 26 letters named (upper and lower case); the sound for each consonant and short vowel; a rhyme made and syllables clapped; three spoken sounds blended and a three-sound word segmented; then a read-aloud not heard before, with key-detail questions and a retell in order using story-map picture cards. The teacher records what each child can do; no timer, no written test.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Small-group re-teaching of what the check shows (most often vowel sounds and segmenting), ending with a short fresh task to see that it took. Children still naming few letters get a named catch-up routine in the foundations block of unit 3.
- **After unit 4 (2 days, end of year).** A decodable text not seen before, using only taught patterns, read aloud to the teacher; ten taught high-frequency words; three CVC words spelled by sound; a cold informational read-aloud on a new topic with main-topic and key-detail questions and a drawn and written piece about it; an opinion sentence about a familiar book.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teaching of the gaps, in small groups; the record goes on to the grade 1 teacher with what the respond day changed, beside grade 1's own baseline.
