- **Section share, by standard.** RI.K.8 (identify the reasons an author gives to support points in a text) is taught on days 129 and 130 on Trees Help Our Community, a point and two stated reasons, and observed on day 132 (check C2) on an unseen card. RI.K.2 (identify the main topic and retell key details) is taught on day 129 only. RL.K.9 and RI.K.9 (compare two texts on one topic) are taught for informational text on day 131, on the reasons text and the planting text, and RI.K.9 is observed on day 132. RI.K.3 (describe the connection between two pieces of information) is first taught on day 131 with a planting step and its result, and returns in 4.4. Foundations: cycle 20's last lesson and its check on day 129, cycle 21 on days 130 to 132.
- **Limits that bite here.** The author's reasons are only the sentences the text states: children find them, never invent them, and a preference of their own is never an author reason. The point is a stated sentence, not a topic. A comparison names what the two texts say alike and what each adds; it is not a ranking. Planting is shown as a study of steps; no digging activity is planned. The reasons text is Trees Help Our Community; the planting text is Planting Together.
- **Connections.** RF.K.1, RF.K.3c and L.K.2d each day in the foundations block. RI.K.7 (pictures and words) in the day 131 questions.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between a topic (what a text is about), a point (what the writer wants us to believe) and a reason (why, stated in the text); why a "because" sentence helps; how to point to a stated sentence; that the check cards of day 132 stay unseen until then.

**Unit assessment items owned.** C2 on day 132 (RI.K.8, RI.K.9), in the table above (items are on the unit plan). Days 129 to 131 each run the exit below; day 132 has no separate exit.

**How C2 runs.** Six groups of five, all thirty children, 5 minutes each. Card A (read aloud once, then again): RI.K.8, 3 minutes: the five children place the point and the reasons on a mat with picture cards at once (1 minute, watched), then each child in turn says the writer's point and then one reason (about 10 seconds a stem, 100 seconds for five), with the 20-second reading it is 3 minutes. Card B: RI.K.9, 2 minutes: the teacher reads card B (15 seconds), each child says one way A and B are alike and one way they differ (about 20 seconds each). The teacher gives one prompt per child and never an answer. Marks: shown, shown with help (after the one prompt), not yet seen.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E129.** Card: "Keep our tree-watching notebook. Its pictures help us remember changes we saw." Ask 1: "Mark the picture of the writer's main point." Ask 2: "Say one detail that helps the point." Right: the notebook kept; "the pictures help us remember changes." Wrong: the picture of only the pictures in the notebook (B); "The point is its pictures help us remember" (B).
- **E130.** Card: "Use a tray for our tree drawings. It keeps pages together. We can carry them together to the table." Ask 1: "Mark the box-and-arrows picture that fits." Ask 2: "Say the box: the point and the two reasons." Right: point, use a tray; reasons, it keeps pages together and we can carry them together. Wrong: the picture whose box holds "keeps pages together" (B); "The reasons are I like trays and trays are blue" (C).
- **E131.** Card: "Text A: Our tree sketch shows three branches. Text B: Our tree sketch shows a split trunk and a hollow." Ask 1: "Mark the comparison picture that fits." Ask 2: "Say a Both ... but ... sentence about Text A and Text B." Right: both describe tree sketches, but A shows branches and B a split trunk and hollow. Wrong: the picture with two same sketches (E); "Both say three branches" (E).
