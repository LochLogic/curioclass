# Section 3.1: What makes a tree alive

<!-- skel -->
English Language Arts, Kindergarten. Unit 3, Trees are alive. *What do trees need, and who needs trees?* (days 80 to 118): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 80 to 84.** Days by kind: understanding 5 (80, 81, 82, 83, 84). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 9 in the unit; the unit plan holds its section fields as U3 to U11 (a one-section unit until the 2026-11-16 cuts).
- Day length (frames.csv): 120 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Children do not read in this section. Texts are read aloud; answers are spoken, pointed to, marked on picture cards, drawn or dictated. Each day is 120 minutes: 60 of topic work (the tree question) and 60 of foundations (sh, th and ch, spoken sound work, letter names). The section opens the class research question and begins the connection model the unit uses.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: living, need, root, leaf, because, first, next. Frame: "The tree needs ___ because ___." Accept a home language, gestures or drawing with dictation first, then the English line.
- **Access.** Enlarged, tactile or described pictures; a pointed card, a gesture or a communication aid is an answer. The tree visit is optional and has a photograph route. Dictation does not replace the sound-to-letter act of writing letters.
- **Check accommodations.** The prompt is read aloud twice; repeat without adding the relation. A child may mark, point, draw one mark or answer privately; a scribe writes what is said.
- **Reading load.** None for children; the teacher reads every text and caption aloud. Printed words children say: to, do and letter names only.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure explains a second connection from the same page ("What else does this fact join?").

## 10. Two rooms
