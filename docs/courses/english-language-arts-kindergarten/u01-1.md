Children do not read yet. Everything is heard, pointed to, drawn, acted out or marked on a picture card; the only print a child meets is a name card, a poem or big-book page read aloud and pointed to, and the letter t on day 5. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Records use one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

- **SL.K.1 and SL.K.3, this section's share.** "Participate in collaborative conversations ... follow agreed-upon rules ... continue a conversation through multiple exchanges" and "Ask and answer questions in order to seek help, get information, or clarify". Day 1 teaches the four talk rules (look, listen, wait, take a turn) and the question that asks for help; days 3 to 5 practise two-turn exchanges. Day 5 observes both (A3 to A5).
- **RL.K.10 and RI.K.10, "Actively engage in group reading activities".** Day 1 shared poem; days 2 to 5 the story heard whole and returned to. No child decodes any text.
- **RL.K.5, RL.K.6 and RI.K.5, RI.K.6 (day 2).** "Recognize common types of texts" (a poem on day 1, a storybook on day 2); "name the author and illustrator ... define the role of each"; "Identify the front cover, back cover, and title page". Day 9 repeats all of it on an information book.
- **RL.K.1, RL.K.7, SL.K.2 (days 2 to 5).** "With prompting and support, ask and answer questions about key details"; "describe the relationship between illustrations and the story". RI.K.1 and RI.K.7 have no information text before day 9: the same question and picture routines run on the story, and the information versions are first taught on days 10 and 11.
- **Foundations (cycle 1, days 1 to 5).** Words and names (RF.K.1b), print direction and spaces (RF.K.1a, 1c), straight and curved lines, then the letter t: name, sound, upper and lower case, printed (RF.K.1d, RF.K.3a, L.K.1a, L.K.2c). I and my are met as whole words (RF.K.3c). Rhyme and syllables are oral practice from day 1 (RF.K.2a, 2b).
- **Limits that bite here.** Classroom toys only: no child is asked what they own, shares about home, or stands as the example of a mistake; wrong examples are voiced by a puppet. A child may pass. Onset and rime (RF.K.2c) is taught from cycle 5, not here. Only t is a taught letter by day 5.
- **Connections.** SL.K.1 and SL.K.3 are used in social studies from school day 3 (one shared talk-rules poster). W.K.2 starts on day 6.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A question asks; a statement tells (why a statement given to a question is not wrong, only not yet a question). That the word we hear is the word we see, and every letter is a part of that word. That a keyword picture recalls a sound and is never used to guess a word. That "I heard" and "This shows" are sentence starters faded by day 24.

**Exit questions.** Five minutes at the end of the topic block. All cards are invented for practice and say so. Item (a) is answered by every child on paper (circle the dots or the picture); item (b) is asked aloud of the day's rotating six. A child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". Wrong: one dot (E); three dots (A). (b) "Dan tells Lu how to twist the lid. What does Lu do while Dan talks?" Answer: listens, waits, looks at Dan. Wrong: "I say my idea too" (C).

- **5 min. Hook and first words.** Two large classroom toys on the rug (no detachable parts). Name each with the children; show "toy" and "turn" with gestures.
- **12 min. Poem p01, Our Play, beside the toys.** Large print, teacher reads it once pointing under each line, then twice with the class joining the repeated line, holding up a toy at "roll it, spin it". Ask "What does the poem invite us to do?" (come and play) and "Which words tell whose turn comes next?" (you take a turn, then I take mine).
- **10 min. Model listening and taking turns.** The teacher and a puppet show listening and not listening (the puppet speaks one line at a time and talks over once). Post four rules: look, listen, wait, take a turn. Children do each move. One poster for ELA and social studies.
- **8 min. Ask for help.** The teacher cannot find where the toy cart lives and says it two ways: "I cannot find the place" and "Where does the cart go, please?" Children say the question back and ask a partner one question about a toy on the rug.
- **10 min. Turn and talk.** Pairs of neighbours, two exchanges: "Which toy would you like to play with here?", answer, swap, one thing said back. The teacher hears three pairs. No home toys.
- **10 min. Draw a toy from our room.** Each child draws one classroom toy; the teacher writes its word on request, and the rest are labelled in the foundations stations.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E1.
- **5 min. Word game: What's the word?** Show an object or an action (ball, cart, roll, push); the class says the one word.
- **8 min. Name game: the word we heard, the word we see.** Name cards face down; up to six volunteers say their name, the card is turned, "We heard it, now we see it, and it looks the same every time." Names stay in the room.
- **4 min. Words on paper.** The teacher writes two toy words and one action word the class said, left to right, finger under each.
- **3 min. Closing.** "Where can you find a written word in our room?"
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six, five 8-minute turns. Table: the six say a toy word, clap its beats, find the rhyme in "play, today"; stations: draw and label, name-card match, big-book copy browse, finger lines in the sand tray.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the rules and the question; each pair holds a turn card. Tool: None. By hand: speaking, drawing, circling dots. Evidence: E1; the record sheet notes (practice only).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: toy, turn, share, ask, answer, title. Frames: "I ask ___." "You answer ___." "My toy is the ___." Home language, signs and pointing count as answers; the teacher says the English back.
- **Access.** Large print and a pointer for every page; a tactile letter t; a child who cannot sit still may stand at the rug edge; a communication aid or pointed card is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit is read aloud; a child may point, circle, draw or answer privately; a scribe records without supplying ideas. A child who passes is "not yet heard", not missed.
- **Reading load.** None alone. The teacher reads the poem, story and cards aloud; the child sees a name card, the poem in large print and the letter t.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask a child who is secure for a second question about the same page.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the poem aloud, look at two toys and ask which one to play with; read the story pages aloud over days 2 to 4, ask one question and let the child answer by pointing. Day 5: read the short puppet story and ask the same three questions.
