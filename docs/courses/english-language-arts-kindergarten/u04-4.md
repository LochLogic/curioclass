- **Limits that bite here.** Children give an opinion only about a class pot or the school grounds, never a yard or garden. The true account is read aloud and its facts are used as the text states them: Kenya is located on a map, Wangari Maathai organized with women who shared concerns, the women set up nurseries and planted together. No word of dialogue, no feeling and no act is added; no child is told she acted alone; nothing ranks one place or culture over another. The number in the book's subtitle is said in words only and never worked with.
- **Connections.** SL.K.2 asking what a word means (day 134); RF.K.1c spaces each day.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A fact the text states against one it does not (V); an action and its result in the order the text gives (AE); how to say Kenya's place on the map without describing the country as poor or far away; that one page of the book says Wangari Maathai died in 2011 and the teacher says it plainly and briefly; that the sensitivity read named by the frame is to be done before the day is taught.

**Unit assessment items owned:** none (no check day in this section). The foundations cycle check F3 runs on day 134 inside the foundations block; its prompts are in s04 field 5.

**How the exits run.** As in section 4.1: the teacher reads the fresh card twice, it is invented for practice and said so; ask 1 is a mark on one of three pictures by every child; ask 2 is the spoken evidence from the day's rotating six at 30 seconds each; a mark on the near-miss picture is a screen, not a verdict.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E133.** Card: "The writer says a tree can shade the class bench. Two possible places are beside that bench and beside a storage shed." Ask 1: "Point to the place you would choose." Ask 2: "Say your plan: I would plant it ___ and I prefer ___." (a reason the writer gave may be added, labelled "the writer says"). Right: a named place with "I prefer ___", the bench shade labelled as the writer's reason; a child may choose the shed and give an own reason labelled as their own. Wrong: "The bench" with no preference (F); "The writer says I like the shed" (C).
- **E134.** Card: "Neighbors plant young trees together beside their school. A teacher holds the water can." Ask 1: "Mark the picture that shows the people and the place." Ask 2: "Ask me one question that would help us know more about their work." Right: neighbors planting beside a school; a question about how they planted together or who holds the can. Wrong: the picture of the neighbors beside a house (Y); "I do not know" with no question asked (W). The pictures are original and show no costume or stereotype.
- **E135.** Card: "Author A: Keep the tree because it marks our meeting spot. Author B: Keep the tree because its leaves feed the soil." Ask 1: "Mark the two-source picture that fits." Ask 2: "Say the reason each author gives." Right: A, the tree marks the meeting spot; B, its leaves feed the soil. Wrong: the picture of a heart on a tree (C); "A: I like trees" (C).
- **E136.** Card: "This nursery grows young trees. Neighbors take the young trees and plant them near their meeting place." Ask 1: "Mark the picture in the order it happened." Ask 2: "Say how the nursery and the planting are connected." Right: the nursery supplies the young trees that the neighbors plant. Wrong: the picture with planting first, nursery second (AE); "the planting makes the nursery" (AE); "the neighbors dig with a big machine" (V).
