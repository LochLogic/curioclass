# Section 1.4: Saying what we like, with a drawing


## 1. Standards and practices


Children still do not read. Opinions are drawn and dictated; the teacher writes. Each day is 120 minutes: topic block 60, foundations block 60. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Days 17 and 18 are check days and day 19 is a response day.

- **W.K.1, days 15, 17, 18, 19.** "Use a combination of drawing, dictating, and writing to compose opinion pieces in which they tell a reader the topic or the name of the book they are writing about and state an opinion or preference about the topic or book (e.g., My favorite book is . . .)." Day 15 models and jointly composes "My favorite classroom toy is the ___"; day 16 each child drafts one; day 17 revises it (O1); day 18 it is scored by the teacher after class (O4); day 19 is a fresh retry. A reason (because) is optional.
- **W.K.2, RI.K.2, day 16.** "Name what they are writing about and supply some information about the topic"; "identify the main topic and retell key details". Day 16 is a drawn, dictated fact about how a classroom toy moves, using Push, Pull, Roll and A Toy Can Turn.
- **SL.K.1a, SL.K.1b (day 17).** Observed in six groups of five: listening and taking turns (O2) and two connected exchanges (O3).
- **Foundations, days 15 to 19.** Cycle 2: poem letter search for h and p (day 15), articulatory gestures and rhyme (day 16), repair (day 17), the word I and feeling the beats of a toy name (day 18), repair (day 19). RF.K.1a to 1d, RF.K.3a, RF.K.2a, 2b, L.K.1a, L.K.2c, RF.K.3c (I, my, me) practiced; RF.K.2c (onset and rime) is not taught until cycle 5.
- **Limits that bite here.** Classroom toys only; a child never names a toy from home; a child may pass; a "no" to a toy is respected. No child is the example of a mistake; wrong examples come from a puppet. Check days teach nothing new and reteach nothing.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between telling what a toy is and saying what you prefer (a statement and an opinion), and that a reason is welcome but never required in kindergarten. That scoring uses topic and preference as two separate marks. That the opinion piece scored is the day 16 piece as revised on day 17.

## 2. Where it sits


Uses the 1.3 exits (E9 to E14) and the day 12 cycle notes already acted on in the day 13 response. Its own results (E15, E16, O1 to O4, the day 19 retry) go to the record sheet and the portfolio; O4 is retained for the day 33 close.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** O1, O2, O3 (day 17) and O4 (day 18), exactly as in the table above; day 33 retains O4 and adds no second score. Answers in short: O1 revises the day 16 drawing and dictated preference, shown by a named toy and a preference (a toy name alone is L); O2 shown by listening and taking a turn (interrupting is C); O3 shown by two connected back-and-forth turns (stopping after one answer is D); O4 scores topic and preference separately, shown by a drawing of a top with "My favorite toy is the top" (a bare "This is a top" is L). Reasons and spelling are not required.

**Exit questions (days 15 and 16 only).** Five minutes at the end of the topic block, read aloud once and again on request. All cards are invented for practice and say so. Item (a) is answered by every child on paper; item (b) is asked aloud of the day's rotating six, and a child outside the six is "not yet heard", never "missed". One ask in each item.
- **E15 (day 15; W.K.1).** Read: "The class shelf has a drum and a puzzle. The drum is loud. The puzzle has pieces." (a) "Circle the toy you choose for your opinion box." Pictures: the drum; the puzzle. Answer: either picture (the choice is the child's). (b) "Say your sentence about that toy." Answer: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___" naming the circled toy. Wrong: "The drum is loud" (L, the toy is named with no preference).
- **E16 (day 16; W.K.2).** Read: "The class ball bounces on the floor. It stops under the table." (a) "Circle the drawing that teaches a reader about this ball." Pictures: a ball bouncing, with a floor line; a ball with a happy face and no floor; a child waving. Answer: the bouncing ball. Wrong: the happy face with no movement (X). (b) "What will you tell a reader about the ball?" Answer: "It bounces." Wrong: "It is a ball" (M).
- **Day 19 retry (unscored).** Read a new menu: "The shelf has a doll, a puzzle and bells." (a) "Circle the toy for your opinion box." Answer: any picture. (b) "Say your sentence." Answer: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." Wrong: "This is a puzzle" (L). Not added to scores.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The opinion model chart ("My favorite classroom toy is the ___") with its optional reason arrow; the day 16 drawings, revised on day 17 and kept for scoring; the topic chart from 1.3; the talk rules poster and turn card; the sound-spelling-map (t, a, h, p). Drawings and O4 go to the portfolio and are retained, not re-scored, on day 33.
- **Key moment, day 15: telling what a toy is against saying what you prefer.** Ideal instrument: a puppet, a real toy and a large chart at the rug, so the two sentences are heard and seen together. Spec: fits; paper and a pointer. No tool is used; no gaps or asks.
- **A room with nothing.** A strip of torn paper for each child to hold the opinion sentence; chart paper from scrap; two real toys.

## 6. Days

## Day 15


- **4 min. Reopen and words.** Repair K or W if many missed E14. Words: favorite, choose, prefer; "because" is optional.
- **8 min. Reread Push, Pull, Roll, page 7.** The shelf picture and its questions: "Which ones do you push? Which ones roll?" Point to the toys.
- **10 min. Model the opinion.** A puppet says "This is a cart" and then "My favorite classroom toy is the cart." The class says which is which; the teacher draws a cart in an opinion box and writes the sentence, finger under each word left to right (error L). An optional arrow shows one reason, drawn.
- **10 min. Compose together.** The class chooses one toy by a show of hands; the teacher writes "Our favorite classroom toy is the ___" and the class reads it back pointing.
- **14 min. Pairs say it.** Each child picks a toy picture card and tells a partner "My favorite classroom toy is the ___", then asks "What is your favorite?" two exchanges; the teacher hears three pairs.
- **9 min. Draw.** Draw the chosen toy in an opinion box; the teacher labels it on request.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E15.
- **3 min. Opening.** ABC song and the chant for h, p, a and t.
- **10 min. Poem letter search.** In large-print p01 (Our Play), find h and p, lowercase and uppercase, name them and say the sounds (error S).
- **4 min. Printing.** Print h and p once each on handwriting paper (L.K.1a).
- **3 min. Closing.** The mapped word me, pointed to in a dictated sentence.
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six, five 8-minute turns. Table: name and sound of h and p, find them in a poem line, for the six; stations: finger h and p in the sand tray, draw and label, name-card match, big-book browse.

Who holds what is carried: the puppet and teacher hold the model; pairs hold the picture cards. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dictating. Evidence: E15; foundations notes for the six.

## Day 16


- **4 min. Reopen and words.** Repair L if many missed E15. Words: rock, move, fact.
- **8 min. Reread selected pages.** Push, Pull, Roll page 4 (a ball rolls) and A Toy Can Turn page 2 (a top spins). "How does the toy move? Which sentence says so?" Point back.
- **8 min. Model information.** The teacher draws the class rocking horse, labels it and says "The horse rocks." A name plus one fact (error M).
- **14 min. Draw and dictate a fact.** Each child takes a real classroom toy, draws it and tells a partner how it moves; the partner says it back. The teacher takes dictation from the day's six.
- **14 min. Draft the opinion.** On a second sheet, each child draws a toy and dictates "My favorite classroom toy is the ___"; this piece is kept for day 17.
- **7 min. Share.** Six children read their sentence back with a finger under each word as the teacher models; the class says "I heard ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E16.
- **3 min. Opening.** Chant for h, p, a and t.
- **10 min. Gestures and a poem.** Poem p04 (My Choice): the teacher shows the mouth for /h/ (a breath on the hand) and /p/ (a puff), the class uses them on h and p words in the lines.
- **4 min. Rhymes.** Find two rhyming words in p04 (ball, hall) and say another that rhymes (error Q).
- **3 min. Closing.** Chant "h, p, /h/, /p/."
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six. Table: a rhyme and the gestures for the six; stations as day 15.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the model; partners hold the say-it-back. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dictating. Evidence: E16; the opinion drafts are kept for day 17; foundations notes for the six.

## Day 17


- **4 min. Set-up.** Explain the six groups of five and what the others do; no repair, no reteach on a check day.
- **8 min. Revise the day 16 piece (O1).** Each child looks at their drawing and dictated preference with the teacher's written feedback attached and adds one thing by dictation or drawing; kept for day 18, not scored today.
- **30 min. Six groups of five, 5 minutes each (O2, O3).** O2, 2 minutes: each child shares a classroom toy choice following the listening and turn-taking rules. O3, 3 minutes: the group asks, answers and continues, two connected back-and-forth turns. The teacher records on the sheet: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. The other children draw one toy and one clue about it for a riddle (a picture strip, no words).
- **10 min. Riddles.** The teacher reads one riddle from Toy Riddles aloud; children show a picture and guess; partners tell which clue helped.
- **8 min. Share and tidy.** Pairs show the riddle strip; the revised drafts are collected.
- **3 min. Opening.** Chant for h, p, a and t.
- **10 min. Foundations response.** The most missed h or p item from days 14 to 16 (name, sound, print or letter written for a heard sound): wrong example beside right, then a fresh retry on a new strip.
- **4 min. Mapped words.** Read I, my and me in a line of p04, pointed to as whole words.
- **3 min. Closing.** Chant for h and p.
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six, familiar practice only; no reteaching beyond the response above. Table: the retry for the day's six.

Who holds what is carried: no one prompts during O2 and O3; the poster stays up. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dictating a revision. Evidence: O1, O2, O3 on the record sheet (O1 not scored today); retry notes for the six.

## Day 18


- **4 min. Reopen.** No repair on a check day. Look at the day 15 class opinion chart.
- **10 min. Reread Push, Pull, Roll, pages 4 to 7.** "Which toys do you push? Which do you pull?" Point back to the words and picture; no scoring.
- **20 min. Draw and confer.** Each child draws how one classroom toy moves for the portfolio while the teacher sits with the day's six at the table, one minute each, hearing each child say their preference sentence and checking it matches the drawing; this confirms the preference, nothing new is scored.
- **10 min. Partner share.** Pairs tell each other the toy and the way it moves, two exchanges.
- **8 min. Shared reading.** The class opinion chart is read together pointing under each word.
- **8 min. Portfolio and tidy.** Pieces are collected for the teacher; O4 is scored by the teacher in about 30 minutes after class, outside the children's day.
- **3 min. Opening.** ABC song and chant for h, p, a and t.
- **10 min. The word I.** Clues to the mystery word: it is a word, it is a letter, it is me; the teacher points to I in p01; the class says it; this maps the word I after its first meetings on days 3 and 5.
- **4 min. Feel the beats.** Say a toy name (puzzle, robot) and tap its beats on the hand: one, two; do not count sounds (error R).
- **3 min. Closing.** Chant for h and p.
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six, familiar practice only. Table: name and sound of h, p, a, t and the word I for the six.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher scores the day 16 piece after class; the class opinion chart stays up. Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: O4 (scored after class), the preference confirmed at the table; foundations notes.

## Day 19


- **15 min. Reteach and fresh retry.** Seven minutes: the most common error of days 15 to 18, nearly always L: the puppet says "This is a puzzle" and then "My favorite classroom toy is the puzzle"; the class names the difference and each child adds a preference to a named toy. Eight minutes: the day 19 card is read once, each child draws a chosen toy and dictates a sentence, the six at the table are heard.
- **8 min. Reread A Toy Can Turn, pages 2 to 5.** "What turns?" and "Which words tell how?" Point back to the words and picture.
- **12 min. Partner share.** Each child tells a partner a fact about their toy and their preference, two exchanges; the teacher hears three pairs.
- **15 min. Add a fact.** Each child adds one observable fact about their toy to the drawing by dictation (informative practice, W.K.2).
- **10 min. Share and keep.** Six children share; drawings go to the portfolio.
- **3 min. Opening.** Chant for h, p, a and t.
- **10 min. Foundations response.** The most missed of h, p, I, me and the beats: wrong example beside right, then a fresh retry on a new strip.
- **4 min. Map.** Find the letter or keyword on the map, both directions, for the day's response letters.
- **3 min. Closing.** Chant for h and p.
- **40 min. Rotations.** Five groups of six. Table: the retry for the day's six; stations: finger h and p in the sand tray, draw and label, name-card match, big-book browse.

Who holds what is carried: the puppet holds the wrong example. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, dictating. Evidence: the retry (unscored); foundations notes for the six.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 15, "Which toys do you push, pull, roll?"; day 16, "How does the toy move, and which sentence says so?"; day 19, "What turns, and which words tell how?".
- Written task, day 16: draw a classroom toy and dictate "My favorite classroom toy is the ___" (the piece revised on day 17 and scored on day 18). Children do not write words.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) a toy is named, in the drawing or the label; (2) a preference is stated ("My favorite ..."); (3) the sentence and drawing agree. Topic and preference are marked separately. Samples: shown, a drawing of a top with "My favorite toy is the top"; shown with help, the preference added after a prompt; not yet seen, "This is a top" (L).

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 15 and 16 open with the previous exit's repair (error letter, five minutes) when more than a third missed it. Days 17 and 18 have none; day 19 opens with its own reteach.
- **Foundations frame, once.** Opening, whole-group work, closing, then five 8-minute rotations: the table (six children, one minute each, two minutes for moves) and four stations. Letter prompts use t, a, h, p; rhyme and a toy's beats run every day. Mapped words: I, my, me.
- **Fluency target.** None; no rate is scored.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: favorite, prefer, choose, toy, bounce. Frames: "My favorite classroom toy is the ___." "It ___." "What is your favorite?" Home language, signs and pointing count as answers; the teacher says the English back.
- **Access.** Large print and a pointer; real toys to hold; a child who cannot draw may point to a picture card; a communication aid or pointed card is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Every prompt is read aloud; a child may point, draw or answer privately; a scribe records without supplying ideas. A child who passes is "not yet seen" and is asked again later in the week.
- **Reading load.** None alone. The teacher reads the books, riddles and cards aloud; the child sees large-print lines and the letters t, a, h, p.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure adds a because to the preference or a second fact about the toy.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the text aloud, model the preference sentence with a puppet or soft toy, and let the child choose between two real toys. The parent is the partner for the exchanges; keep the drawing for the portfolio. On check days use the same prompts and note the same marks in a shorter session.
- **One projector.** Show the opinion chart, the topic chart and the map on one screen; children answer on cards and point on the screen in turn.
