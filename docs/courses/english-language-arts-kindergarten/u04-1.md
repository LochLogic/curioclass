Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-kindergarten/4/s/4.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-kindergarten u04-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **Section share, by standard.** RL.K.1, RI.K.1 and SL.K.2 (with prompting and support, ask and answer questions about key details) are taught on days 119 to 122 on the two read-aloud stories, with RL.K.3 (characters, settings, major events; a story map, days 120 and 122) and RL.K.7 (the illustration and the story moment it shows, day 121). RL.K.9 (compare and contrast the adventures and experiences of characters) is taught on day 123 and practiced on day 124. W.K.1 is one drawn and dictated opinion on day 119. The foundations rows are cycle 19 (days 119 to 123) and the first lesson of cycle 20 (day 124).
- **Limits that bite here.** Children do not read the stories; the teacher reads them and the children answer by speaking, pointing, placing cards and drawing. The only print a child reads alone is the cycle reader (taught sounds and mapped words) and labels. Pages 3 and 9 of each story are kept for the day 126 and 127 checks: they are read straight through on days 120 and 122 and never discussed, compared or matched to a picture before then. Teaching comparisons use the rain and the noise only. Reader and verse words stay out of the cycle checks.
- **Connections.** SL.K.1, SL.K.1a, SL.K.1b and SL.K.3, day 123 (wait for a turn card; ask what a word means). RI.K.7, day 121, on one invented picture-and-caption pair. RF.K.1c (spaces) in the shared writing each day.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between an experience (what a character does and feels when something happens) and an appearance (clothes, hair); why two columns come before a "Both ... but ..." sentence; that a printed word is read from its letters with the picture covered; and how to hear a rotating six in the minutes given.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none (the first topic items are C1 on days 126 and 127, section 4.2). The foundations cycle check F1 runs on day 123 inside the foundations block; its prompts are in s04 field 5.

**How every exit runs (5 topic minutes).** The teacher reads the day's named page aloud twice (field 3); an ask that is not about reading stays on an invented card, said so. Ask 1: every child marks one of three paper pictures (the right one, a near miss that carries one named error, and an unrelated scene). Ask 2 is the evidence: one spoken ask, heard from the day's rotating six (30 seconds each, three minutes; the others wait with a picture book). A mark on the near-miss picture is a screen, not a verdict; the six heard are marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. If more than a third mark the near-miss picture, the next day's opening repair is shown to everyone.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E119.** On the text, not an invented card: 01a (A Tree Beside Our Door), page 1, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Why does Lina stop by her tree every morning?" Right: To say hello to her tree; she touches a leaf and whispers good morning. Wrong: says she stops to water the tree (V); says she likes the tree best (V). Ask 2 (still on its invented card: not a reading ask): "What is Nell's opinion? Say it so we hear what it is about and what she likes." Right: Nell likes watering the class plant. Wrong: says only the class plant (F); says She likes it (G).
- **E120.** On the text, not an invented card: 01a (A Tree Beside Our Door), page 2, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Tell the story map for this page: who, where and what happens." Right: Mr. Dube; beside the front door of the building, at the young tree; he planted it last fall, and now in spring it has pink blossoms. Wrong: says The tree has blossoms, with no one named (Z); says Mr. Dube planted it, with no place (AA); says Mr. Dube is by the tree, with nothing happening (AB).
- **E121.** On the text, not an invented card: 01a (A Tree Beside Our Door), page 8, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Which picture shows the last sentence?" Right: The picture of Lina looking up at the dripping leaves, smiling. Wrong: points to Lina pouring the can on dry soil (U); points to Lina at her window at night (U).
- **E122.** On the text, not an invented card: 01b (Reading Under the Tree), page 7, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Where does each picture card go: who, where or what happens?" Right: Tomas peeking around the trunk goes in who; the roots behind the tree go in where; the children reading on the roots go in what happens. Wrong: leaves the who box empty (Z); leaves the where box empty (AA); puts the roots card in the what-happens box (AB).
- **E123.** On the text, not an invented card: 01a page 10 and 01b page 10, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Say a Both ... but ... sentence about Lina and Ben on these pages." Right: Both end the story at their tree, glad, but Lina stands alone in the sunshine and Ben sits on the bench with two friends. Wrong: says Both ... but Lina has braids and Ben has glasses (A); says Both have backpacks, but Ben has a book (A).
- **E124.** On the text, not an invented card: 01a (A Tree Beside Our Door), page 2, first sentence, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Ask me one who question about that sentence." Right: A who question about the sentence, for example: Who looked after the building? Wrong: asks no question (X); asks What hummed from flower to flower? (Y). Ask 2: "Say a Both ... but ... sentence about how Lina and Ben feel." Right: Both feel good after a plan changes, but Lina feels warm all over and Ben feels calm. Wrong: says Both ... but Lina has boots and Ben has glasses (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The story map (unit 2): modeled on A Tree Beside Our Door on day 120 and on Reading Under the Tree on day 122, then returned to by every story day through day 150. The opinion box (unit 1, a topic and a preference): retrieved on day 119. The two-column chart with the frame "Both ... but ...": new on day 123, practiced day 124, returned to in 4.2.
- **Key moment 1, day 123: the two columns.** Ideal instrument: chart paper with movable picture cards, so the class can move an event from one story's column to the shared row. Spec: fits, as ordinary paper and cards; no Curio Class tool is used or asked for.
- **Key moment 2, day 121: the picture and the sentence.** Ideal instrument: the book's own page. Spec: fits, as print.
- **A room with nothing.** The printed manuscripts read aloud, chart paper, buttons for sound counters, hand-drawn picture cards.

## 6. Days

## Day 119


- **5 min. Vocabulary and hook.** Show the picture of the dry cracked soil by a young tree (page 4, no words). "What do you notice?" Words said and echoed with pictures: notice, care, soil.
- **15 min. Read-aloud, the opening.** A Tree Beside Our Door, pages 1 to 5 (about 280 words, with picture pauses). Then ask the unit's compelling question, "Why do people care for trees?", for spoken answers only.
- **5 min. Retrieval (unmarked).** The retained unit 3 collage and unit 1 opinion card: "Which detail shows a tree need?" then "Where does your opinion name its topic and preference?" A miss is re-modeled in the same minute.
- **15 min. Questions and talk.** "What does Lina notice?" and "Which words explain her plan to look after the tree?" (the teacher rereads page 5; children point to the picture, then say it to a partner). Any child may pass.
- **15 min. Draw and dictate an opinion.** 3 minutes model: "I like ___ in this story" with the story named. Then children draw their favourite part and tell a partner; the teacher takes dictation from the rotating six (about 2 minutes each, during the drawing). The others write the letters they know under the drawing.
- **5 min. Exit.** E119.

- **20 min. Awareness and sound-spelling (cycle 19, lesson 96: short vowels and digraphs).** Explicit pattern teaching from the source lesson, then joint reading of cycle-19 sentences. Model: segment hen with one counter for each sound, read it from its sounds with the picture covered, spell it, then change pin to pen by exchanging the middle counter and say the sound before and after. Modeled today, practiced tomorrow. Map of, have and from: mark the regular sounds and the part to remember, then read each in a sentence.
- **25 min. Small-group reading.** A Hen Can Nap (first use; the title is adult-read, the body uses only taught sounds and mapped words). Assignments 1 to 6 use the reader and the verse; 7 and 8 are the two isolated tasks (sounds without a text; one mapped word read and one taught word spelled). The teacher hears the day's rotating six for 10 minutes (about 100 seconds a child: one sentence read aloud, one meaning question, one isolated task) and names them in the record column; the other four groups of six partner-read and use assignment cards, with no timer shown.
- **10 min. Shared print and writing.** The teacher reads Tree Care Words, verse 1 (first heard today; adults read its rich language). Write one source sentence together on chart paper, a space between words, and each child writes the day's spelled word by its sounds.
- **5 min. Foundations check.** Say the middle sound of a new word (web), counters in hand; one ask, six children heard. Unscored; the record column notes shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the opinion box; the children hold the topic-plus-preference frame by speaking it. Tool: none. By hand: drawing and dictating, letter counters. Exit: E119. Out: E119 hand-backs (error F) to day 120's Vocabulary and return.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Preview with pictures and gestures: notice, care, soil, rain, root, bench, trunk. Frames: "Both ___. But ___." and "What does ___ mean?" Home-language rehearsal with a partner is welcome.
- **Access.** Enlarged picture cards, described pictures for a child who cannot see them, sound counters of any large size, dictation for a child who cannot write.
- **Check accommodations.** Ask 1 by pointing or by a held card; ask 2 by speaking or by placing cards in the story map; extra time is free and nothing is timed for a child.
- **Reading load.** Children read no story. They read, alone, only cycle-19 and cycle-20 reader words and the mapped words (a decodable reader of 20 to 80 words, one sentence a page). The teacher reads everything else.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a third Both ... but ... sentence about what each character does to care for the tree.
