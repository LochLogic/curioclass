# Section 3.2: Connections, checked and repaired


Children still read nothing but short decodable words. The topic block reuses the 3.1 texts; the foundations block turns to short a (cycle 13). Day 85 prepares a small science talk; days 86 and 87 are check days that sample only what 3.1 taught; day 88 responds. Each day is 120 minutes: 60 topic, 60 foundations.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **W.K.7 and W.K.8, days 85, 87, 88.** "Participate in shared research and writing projects" and "recall information from experiences or gather information from provided sources to answer a question." Day 85 revises the day 83 note; day 87 scores that revised note (R0, no sitting minutes); day 88 practices with a fresh text.
- **SL.K.1 (1a, 1b), days 85 and 87.** "Follow agreed-upon rules for discussions" and "Continue a conversation through multiple exchanges." Practiced on day 85; scored inside the connection talks on days 86 and 87.
- **SL.K.3, day 85.** "Ask and answer questions in order to seek help, get information, or clarify something that is not understood": partners ask "Where did you hear that?" in the talk preparation.
- **RI.K.3, days 86 to 88.** "describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text." Days 86 and 87 check it on a fresh card; day 88 repairs it. **RI.K.7, day 86.** "describe the relationship between illustrations and the text in which they appear": checked only, taught in unit 1.
- **Foundations, days 85 and 88.** RF.K.3b: "Associate the long and short sounds with common spellings (graphemes) for the five major vowels"; this section carries short a only. RF.K.3d: "Distinguish between similarly spelled words by identifying the sounds of the letters that differ" (mat and map). RF.K.4: "Read emergent-reader texts with purpose and understanding" (Ants at a Mat). L.K.2d: spell simple words phonetically. RF.K.2d and 2e: isolate and swap sounds in spoken words. RF.K.3c: at and has. Day 88 adds RF.K.1a to d, L.K.1a, RF.K.3a, L.K.2c and RF.K.2a to c as practice.
- **Limits that bite here.** A child reads only words made of taught letters, sh, th, ch, short a, the, a, in, to, do, at and has. No word in the reader or sentences may use an untaught pattern. Checks sample taught work only; days 86 and 87 give no new teaching. Two facts at a time.
- **Connections.** RL.K.1 and RL.K.10, day 85: the 07 return (T1). RF.K.1a to c, day 85: the print card (P1). RI.K.1 and RI.K.10: day 91.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to hear fifteen children on each check day in three ten-minute groups while the others work alone and quietly, and why no reteaching happens on a check day. Why short a is taught before any other vowel. How to chain a word (mat, map) by changing one letter and saying which sound changed.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** day 86 SL.K.1a and SL.K.1b (children 1 to 15), day 87 W.K.7, W.K.8 (R0), SL.K.1, SL.K.1a and SL.K.1b (children 16 to 30). Also given in these days, though counted under 3.1: card C1, page 3 of A Tree Is a Living Thing (03), for RI.K.3 (days 86 and 87) and RI.K.7 (day 86). Check days have no exit ticket; the items are the evidence. Scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Samples and look-fors:
- **C1, RI.K.3.** The teacher reads page 3 of A Tree Is a Living Thing (03) twice, its picture covered; the class first hears the chant on day 89. Spoken: "How does the trunk help the tree?" Look for the stated link. Meets: "The trunk carries the water up to the leaves." Wrong: "There is a trunk. There is water." (A).
- **C1, RI.K.7.** Three pictures on the C1 sheet: arrows up a trunk from the roots; rain beside a trunk with no arrows; an animal eating a leaf. "Which drawing shows what the words explain?" Meets: the arrows, "the water goes up the trunk." Wrong: the rain or the animal (B).
- **SL.K.1a.** Looks and waits; answers the partner's idea. Meets: "You said the trunk carries the water. I think the roots help too." Wrong: repeated interruption (G).
- **SL.K.1b.** Continues a second relevant turn about the trunk. Meets: answers the partner, then adds a detail. Wrong: stops after one comment (H).
- **R0, W.K.7 (day 87).** The day 83 note, revised on day 85 with an added fact from the needs chart. Meets: a relevant added fact. Wrong: no participation (F).
- **R0, W.K.8 (day 87).** The 01b fact the note supplies, located with help. Meets: the correct 01b sentence or picture. Wrong: a toy preference with no fact (E).

**Exit questions.** One teaching or response day each. Day 85's is a new invented case read aloud, "invented for practice"; day 88's is on page 2 of The Oak (05a), not yet worked in class.
- **E85.** Read: "Text A: A frog's skin dries out in hot, dry air. Text B: On a hot day, a frog moves into the shade." Mark: "Which card joins what Text A and Text B say?" Cards: the sun with an arrow to a frog in the shade; a frog on a sunny stone with an arrow to the sun; two frogs side by side. Spoken: "What do the two readings tell us together?" Answer: The sun with an arrow to the frog in the shade; A frog's skin dries out in hot air, so on a hot day it moves into the shade. Wrong: marks two frogs side by side (A); marks the frog on the sunny stone (B); says A frog moves, so it is alive (P); says I have a pet frog (E).
- **E88.** On the text, not an invented card: selection a (The Oak), page 2, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Which arrow picture shows how the facts on this page go together? (strip E88: an acorn in soil with an arrow to a small oak; an acorn with an arrow to a truck; a cloud with an arrow to an acorn)" Right: The acorn in soil with an arrow to the small oak. Wrong: marks the truck or the cloud picture (B). Ask 2: "What does the connection on this page explain?" Right: How a new oak grows: the acorn is the oak's seed, so it can grow into a new oak. Wrong: says An acorn. An oak. with no link (A).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: frog, camel, trunk, fact, link. Frame: "The ___ helps because ___." Home-language rehearsal with a partner first; then English.
- **Access.** Large or tactile C1 sheet and counters; a pointed card is an answer. A child who cannot write marks or says the changed sound; dictation does not replace the sound-to-letter act.
- **Check accommodations.** The card is read aloud twice; the child may mark, point or answer privately; a scribe records without adding ideas. Use an agreed access method rather than a default "not yet".
- **Reading load.** Words of taught letters only; the teacher reads every directions and every card.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure explains a second link from the same two readings.

## 10. Two rooms

- **One projector.** Show the C1 page and the reader on one screen; children mark and write on paper.
