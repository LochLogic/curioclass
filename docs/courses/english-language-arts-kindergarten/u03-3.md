# Section 3.3: Who needs trees: animals, notes and first writing


Children still read nothing but short decodable words. The topic block reads three adult-read texts: 03 (a tree is alive), 06 (who depends on a tree) and 05a (the oak, in Tree Texts). Days 89 and 90 teach the main topic and the link between an animal and what it needs; day 91 practices it; days 92 and 93 teach notes and a first piece of information writing; day 94 joins two notes. The foundations block finishes short a (cycle 13, days 89 and 90) and teaches short i (cycle 14, days 91 to 94). Each day is 120 minutes: 60 topic, 60 foundations. No check day falls here; the records (T1, T2, P1 to P5) are heard inside ordinary time.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.K.3, days 89, 90, 91, 94.** "describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text." Taught on days 89 and 90 (a repeated line that explains a need; an animal and its need); practiced on days 91 and 94.
- **RI.K.2, day 89.** "identify the main topic and retell key details of a text," with prompting and support. Taught once, on 03.
- **W.K.7 and W.K.8, days 89 to 94.** "Participate in shared research and writing projects" and "gather information from provided sources to answer a question." Taught on days 89, 90, 92 and 93; practiced on days 91 and 94. The class question is "Who needs trees?"
- **RL.K.1, RI.K.1 and SL.K.2, day 92.** "ask and answer questions about key details in a text" and "requesting clarification if something is not understood." Taught on 05a and 07; day 91 retrieves RI.K.1, RI.K.2, RI.K.10 and SL.K.2 inside the T2 reread of 06.
- **W.K.2, days 93 and 94.** "name what they are writing about and supply some information about the topic." Taught day 93, practiced day 94.
- **Foundations, days 89 to 94.** RF.K.3b: "Associate the long and short sounds with common spellings (graphemes) for the five major vowels"; short a to day 90, short i from day 91. RF.K.3d: "Distinguish between similarly spelled words by identifying the sounds of the letters that differ" (mat and sat; sat and sit). RF.K.4: "Read emergent-reader texts with purpose and understanding" (Ants at a Mat, then A Pig in a Pit). L.K.2d: spelling modeled day 89, practiced day 94. RF.K.2d and 2e: isolate and swap sounds. RF.K.3c: at and has (P5, day 90); is and it from day 91.
- **Limits that bite here.** A child reads only words made of taught letters, sh, th, ch, short a, short i from day 91, the, a, in, to, do, at, has, is and it. Texts 03, 06 and 05a are adult-read; children read no sentence of them. No reader or verse word may use an untaught pattern. Two facts at a time; one ask in each spoken stem.
- **Connections.** RI.K.10 and T1 return to 07 on days 89 and 90 (T1) and day 92 (RL.K.1). The person's need and a tree resource open section 3.4 on day 95.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to hear six children for a record (T1, T2, P4) inside whole-class work. Why a repeated line can explain a need. How to ask for clarification ("What does it mean?") and answer by rereading. Why short i follows short a, and how sat and sit contrast one letter.

**Exit questions.** One per teaching day: days 89, 90 and 94 reread a named page not yet worked; days 91 to 93 are new invented cases read aloud, "invented for practice". Each has a Mark and a Spoken part; the spoken answer is built, so a mark cannot pass it alone.
- **E89.** On the text, not an invented card: We Depend on Trees (04), page 4, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Which picture group shows the topic with one detail? (strip E89: a tree with an arrow to a family picking its apples; a family with a basket and a small tree far off; a sun with no tree)" Right: The tree with an arrow to the family picking its apples. Wrong: marks the family with the basket as the topic (X); marks the sun with no tree (B). Ask 2: "Who needs something the tree gives, and what is it?" Right: The family needs fruit; the tree gives apples. Wrong: says The tree gives a nest (D).
- **E90.** On the text, not an invented card: selection a (The Oak), page 3, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Which drawing shows the animal and what it gets from the tree? (strip E90: a squirrel holding an acorn on an oak branch; a squirrel running alone; an oak with no animal)" Right: The squirrel holding an acorn on the oak branch. Wrong: marks the squirrel running alone or the oak with no animal (B). Ask 2: "What does the squirrel get, and which words say so?" Right: Acorns, its food (the words: A squirrel eats acorns; acorns are food for animals). Wrong: says A squirrel. A tree. with no link (A); says The squirrel gets a nest (D).
- **E91.** Read: "An owl sleeps in a hollow tree during the day. The hollow tree is the owl's resting place." Mark: "Which drawing would add an accurate detail to the notebook?" Cards: an owl inside a hollow in a tree trunk; an owl on a bare branch at night; an owl flying at night. Spoken: "Which words in the reading support your drawing?" Answer: The owl inside a hollow in a tree trunk; sleeps in a hollow tree (or: the hollow tree is the owl's resting place). Wrong: marks the owl on the bare branch or the flying owl (B); says It hunts mice (D).
- **E92.** Read: "Our question is what a rabbit gets from a tree. Text A: A rabbit can eat tree leaves. Text B: A rabbit has soft fur and long ears." Mark: "Which picture note answers our question?" Cards: a rabbit eating a leaf, labeled A; a rabbit with long ears, labeled B; a rabbit hopping, with no label. Spoken: "Which text did your note come from?" Answer: The rabbit eating a leaf, labeled A; Text A. Wrong: marks the long-ears rabbit (E); marks the hopping rabbit (D); names Text B for the leaf note (AI).
- **E93.** Read: "This text is about a birch tree. Its bark peels in thin, papery pieces." Mark: "Which labelled drawing would teach this to a friend?" Cards: a birch tree with peeling bark, labeled birch; the same drawing with no label; a stack of paper, labeled paper. Spoken: "What fact will you dictate?" Answer: The birch tree with peeling bark, labeled birch; Birch bark peels in thin pieces like paper. Wrong: marks the drawing with no label (C); marks the stack of paper (B); says Birch bark is warm (D).
- **E94.** On the text, not an invented card: selection b (The Sugar Maple), page 3, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Which one picture shows both notes together? (strip E94: a squirrel holding a winged seed under a maple; a winged seed alone; a squirrel on bare ground)" Right: The squirrel holding a winged seed under the maple. Wrong: marks the seed alone or the squirrel on bare ground (B). Ask 2: "What sentence joins the two notes?" Right: The squirrel eats the seed that falls from the maple, so it depends on the maple for food. Wrong: says A seed. A squirrel. with no link (A); says It eats the leaves (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class question chart ("What does a tree need?" and, from day 89, "Who needs trees?"); the research cycle chart (ask, find, note, share); the animal, need and source boxes (day 90); each child's drawing notebook; the sound-spelling-map with short a, and short i from day 91; the records T1, T2 and P1 to P5. Charts stay on the wall through the unit; the notebook stays in the room.
- **Key moment 1, day 90: the three boxes.** Ideal instrument: a wall chart of three folded-paper boxes (animal, what it needs, where I heard it) with picture cards that slide in. Spec: fits; paper, tape and a marker. No tool is used, so there are no gaps or asks.
- **Key moment 2, day 92: the picture note and its label.** Ideal instrument: a note card with a picture area and a source sticker glued below. Spec: fits; card, a glue stick and a marker.
- **Key moment 3, day 93: writing from a note.** Ideal instrument: chart paper with the topic line above and the fact line below, written while children watch. Spec: fits; paper and a marker.
- **A room with nothing.** Chalk or a finger in a sand tray; a folded sheet for each box; pebbles for counters; the teacher's voice for the texts.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 89, "What do all the lines tell about?"; day 90, "What does this animal need from the tree?"; day 92, "Which sentence would answer our animal question?"
- Written task, day 93: a labeled drawing of the topic and one dictated fact from the day 92 note.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) the topic is named; (2) the fact comes from the heard text; (3) the child can point to the source sentence. Samples: "oak; An oak grows acorns; points to page 3" meets; "a tree; I like it" does not (C, D).

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 89: need and because on the chart; day 90: the arrow card; day 91: the three boxes; day 92: a fact and its source; day 93: the topic label; day 94: the day 93 page. Foundations: spoken sounds, the map, to, do, at, has, then is and it from day 91.
- **Fluency target.** None. Reading is untimed; partners check letters before pictures.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: need, depend, shelter, note, source. Frames: "The ___ needs ___ from the ___." and "I heard it in ___." Home-language rehearsal with a partner first; then English.
- **Access.** Enlarged, tactile or described cards; a pointed card is an answer. A child who cannot write marks or says the sound; dictation does not replace the sound-to-letter act.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit cards are read aloud twice; the child may mark, point or answer privately; a scribe records without adding ideas.
- **Reading load.** Words of taught letters only; the teacher reads every direction and every text.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure fills the boxes for a second animal and says where each fact was heard.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read 03 or 06 one page at a time; say the topic, then draw one animal, need and source box. For short a and short i, push counters, blend, and chain one letter. Hear the T1, T2 and P records in the same sitting once.
- **One projector.** Show the texts' pictures, the chart and the boxes on one screen; children draw and write on paper.
