# Section 3.4: People and animals depend on trees: the first animal piece

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-kindergarten/3/s/3.4), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-kindergarten u03-4.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Children still read nothing but short decodable words. Day 95 teaches how a person's need meets a tree resource, on 04. Day 96 plans and drafts the first animal piece (R1) from two read-aloud sources and three supplied cards, with no model of the answer. Days 97 to 99 are check days: ten topic minutes of revision, then thirty children heard in three groups of ten, one day each, with no reteaching. The foundations block closes short i on day 95 (the cycle 14 check and P5) and starts short u on day 96 (cycle 15). Each day is 120 minutes: 60 topic, 60 foundations.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.K.3, days 95 and 98.** "describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text." Taught on day 95 (a person's need and a tree resource); on day 98 the C1 card is reused, with no extra minutes, to fill a missing connection.
- **W.K.7 and W.K.8, days 95 to 99.** "Participate in shared research and writing projects" and "gather information from provided sources to answer a question." Taught on day 95; practiced on day 96; scored in the R1 groups on days 97 to 99.
- **W.K.2, days 96 to 99.** "name what they are writing about and supply some information about the topic." Practiced on day 96 and scored in the R1 groups. The base lesson's formal focus and three detail sentences are cut to a named animal and one accurate fact.
- **Foundations, days 95 to 99.** RF.K.3b: "Associate the long and short sounds with common spellings (graphemes) for the five major vowels"; short i closes on day 95, short u against a and i runs days 96 to 99. RF.K.3d: "Distinguish between similarly spelled words by identifying the sounds of the letters that differ" (hat, hit, hut). RF.K.4: "Read emergent-reader texts with purpose and understanding" (A Pig in a Pit, then Gus in a Tub). L.K.2d: spelling practiced. RF.K.2d and 2e: isolate and swap sounds. RF.K.3c: is and it checked on day 95 (P5); was and his mapped on day 96.
- **Limits that bite here.** A child reads only words made of taught letters, sh, th, ch, short a, short i, short u from day 96, the, a, in, to, do, at, has, is, it and, from day 96, was and his. Texts 04, 05a and 06 are adult-read; the R1 cards are adult-read. Two facts at a time; one scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen); one ask in each spoken stem.
- **Connections.** RI.K.3 returns on day 98 through C1. The animal piece is shared on day 100 (section 3.5). The collage prompt on day 102 reuses its habits: name, show a need, name a source.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to hear thirty children in three groups of ten across days 97 to 99 while the others write quietly. Why there is no reteaching and no model of the answer on a check day. How to write one line of feedback on each piece after day 96 and what a revision is. How a source card is attributed. How to chain hat, hit and hut.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** day 97 W.K.7, W.K.8 and W.K.2 (children 1 to 10); day 98 RI.K.3 (C1, every child), W.K.7, W.K.8 and W.K.2 (children 11 to 20); day 99 W.K.7, W.K.8 and W.K.2 (children 21 to 30). Check days carry no exit ticket; the groups are the evidence. Scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Each group of five takes ten minutes: two for contribution, three for source information, three for the earlier writing, two to move. Samples and look-fors:
- **R1, W.K.7.** Each child says what they added to the animal inquiry. Meets: "I found the deer eats an apple," with the piece and card C2a. Wrong: nothing added (F).
- **R1, W.K.8.** Each child shows which card answered the animal question. Meets: the matching card and its fact. Wrong: a fact from outside the cards, "I like deer" (E).
- **R1, W.K.2.** The piece after written feedback and revision: a drawn animal, its name, and one accurate fact (drawn, dictated or written). Meets: a deer and an apple, labeled "deer", "A deer eats an apple from the tree." Wrong: an unnamed animal (C); "A deer can jump over a fence" (D).
- **RI.K.3, day 98 (C1).** While the child explains the piece, record only a missing connection. Meets: "The tree gives the deer food, so it eats an apple." Wrong: "Deer. Apple. Tree." (A).

**Exit questions.** Teaching and application days only: day 95 rereads a named page not yet worked; day 96 is a new invented case read aloud, "invented for practice".
- **E95.** On the text, not an invented card: selection a (The Oak), page 6, read aloud again. Ask 1: "Which person-and-resource drawing fits? (strip E95: oak planks with an arrow to a chair and a table where a girl and her grandfather sit; a table alone; an oak tree alone)" Right: The oak planks with an arrow to the chair, the table and the people. Wrong: marks the table alone or the oak alone (B). Ask 2: "How do the words connect the people to the oak?" Right: People make chairs and tables from the oak's hard, strong wood. Wrong: says People. Chairs. Oak. (A); says The people eat the acorns (D).
- **E96.** Read: "Source A: A sloth hangs from a branch of a tree. Source B: A sloth eats leaves from the tree." Mark: "Which two-source picture plan explains how the sloth depends on the tree?" Cards: a sloth on a branch with a leaf, labeled A and B; a sloth asleep on the ground, labeled A; a branch of leaves with no sloth, labeled B. Spoken: "What will you take from each source?" Answer: The sloth on a branch with a leaf, labeled A and B; From A, it hangs from the branch; from B, it eats the tree's leaves. Wrong: marks the sloth on the ground or the branch of leaves with no sloth (B); says Sloths are slow (E); swaps the two sources (AI).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class chart of people and trees (day 95); the three boxes (animal, what it needs, where I heard it); the research cycle chart; the covered C2 source cards, opened on day 96; the covered C1 card, used on day 98; each child's R1 piece, kept with its feedback and revision; the R1 record sheet; the sound-spelling-map with short u from day 96. Charts stay on the wall; pieces and records stay in the room.
- **Key moment 1, day 96: the three source cards.** Ideal instrument: three large cards (C2a, C2b, C2c) read aloud once each, each with a source sticker a child can point to. Spec: fits; card, a marker and a glue stick. No tool is used, so there are no gaps or asks.
- **Key moment 2, days 97 to 99: the group record.** Ideal instrument: one sheet with a row for each child and a cell for each code, filled with shown, shown with help or not yet seen. Spec: fits; paper and a pencil.
- **A room with nothing.** Chalk or a finger in a sand tray; folded sheets for the boxes; pebbles for counters; the teacher's voice for the texts.

## 6. Days

## Day 95

- **5 min. What do people obtain?** Ask the class question; children point to a picture of a person's need.
- **10 min. Model the link (RI.K.3).** On the chart: name a person, the need, and the tree resource from one 04 page; say "The ___ needs ___, so the tree gives ___." Show how the repeated dependence line links the people and the trees.
- **15 min. Connect and note (W.K.7, W.K.8).** Pairs choose another person and need from 04, draw the person, the resource and an arrow, and say the connection. Six children not yet heard give the T2 record on 04: ask about a dependent, answer, name the topic, retell two details, and ask what "it" means.
- **10 min. Say the chant.** Speak the whole chant together, then each child draws one person and its tree resource.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E95.
- **5 min. Say it.** Blend /h/ /it/, /p/ /it/ and /s/ /it/ spoken, with counters.
- **8 min. Short i, again.** Read sit, hit and lip from tiles; chain pin, pit, pig, naming the changed sound each time.
- **10 min. A Pig in a Pit, reader 2.** Small groups reread and say what happened.
- **7 min. Spell and change.** All thirty write two dictated short i words and change one letter in a pair; this is the written half of the cycle 14 check, with no teaching.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 95, "How does the repeated line link people and trees?"; day 96, "Which facts answer our question?"; day 98, "Which card did your fact come from?"
- Written task, day 96: the first animal piece, an animal's name, one fact and the card that supplied it, revised on days 97 to 99.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 95: the arrow card and the three boxes; day 96: the day 94 sentence; days 97 to 99: the chart of what a good piece names. Foundations: spoken sounds, the map, to, do, at, has, is, it, and from day 96 was and his.
- **Fluency target.** None. Reading is untimed; partners check letters before pictures.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: source, detail, information, check, person. Frames: "The ___ needs ___ from the ___." and "I found it on card ___." Home-language rehearsal with a partner first; then English.
- **Access.** Enlarged, tactile or described cards; a pointed card is an answer. A child who cannot write dictates; dictation does not replace the sound-to-letter act on foundations tasks.
- **Check accommodations.** The R1 cards are read aloud twice; the child may point, draw or answer privately; a scribe records without adding ideas. Use an agreed access method rather than a default "not yet seen".
- **Reading load.** Words of taught letters only; the teacher reads every direction and every card.
- **Extension, same standard.** A child who is secure adds a second fact from a second card and shows both cards.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read 04 one page at a time, then draw a person, a need and the tree resource. On day 96 read the three cards, one at a time, and let the child choose; do not model an answer. Foundations: counters, tiles and the reader; the adult hears the records once.
- **One projector.** Show 04's pictures, the cards and the chart on one screen; children draw and write on paper.
