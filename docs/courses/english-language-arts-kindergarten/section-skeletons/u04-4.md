>
> **Text commissions.** These are specifications for original manuscripts, not reports of existing books. Write 01a as a whole short story about Lina, who enjoys a young tree beside her apartment building, sees dry soil, plans to water with an adult, and changes that plan when rain arrives. Neighbors enjoy the tree with her. Write 01b as a distinct whole story about Ben reading under a school tree, moving when nearby work grows noisy, and finding a quieter shaded place with classmates. Include two reserved scene pairs (scenes kept out of teaching): Lina invites a neighbor to smell blossoms, Ben invites a classmate to feel bark; in the endings Lina gives that neighbor a fallen leaf beside the apartment tree, Ben gives that classmate a drawing of the trunk beside the school tree. Read these within the whole stories, but never model, discuss or practise these comparisons or ending-picture matches before C1 or C3. Teaching comparisons use rain and noise only. Both characters experience pleasure and a changed plan; the differences concern experiences, not appearances. Use original characters and plots, not paraphrases of trade books. Commission 01a and 01b at roughly 400 to 700 words each, rich enough for read-aloud rereading, with pronoun references, time links and one implicit feeling. These are length targets, not measured complexity scores.
>
> Write 02a as a complete short explanation whose stated point is that trees help a community and whose two stated reasons are shaded resting places and shelter for birds. An author-reason task must use the actual sentences, never ask children to invent the author's reason. Write 02b as a distinct whole account of adults and children planting together, using an appropriate site, adult preparation, setting a young tree, filling and watering. Children study these steps, without an actual digging activity. Illustrations must convey information matched to the words. Write 03 about Maathai organizing with Kenyan women in response to their reported water, food and firewood concerns, and women growing seedlings and planting together. Field 10's movement account, paragraphs 2 and 3, supports these facts; do not invent a personal listening scene. Locate Kenya accurately. Do not invent dialogue, imply she acted alone or rank communities. Check the final wording against that account. Proposed lengths: 02a and 02b, 250 to 450 words each; 03, 400 to 600 words. No computed measures exist. Preview community and nursery with pictures, track actors through chronology and reread action-result links. The two information forms precede biography so students can distinguish reasons, steps and historical events.
>
> **Foundations.** Continue cycles 19 to 25, lessons 96 to 130 in their recorded order. Day 119 retrieves short vowels and digraphs; day 124 models an and am families; day 130 models short e and u contrast, with ank and ink heard only; day 135 teaches doubled final ll, zz, ss and ff; day 141 associates spoken long a and i with printed a and i; day 146 does the same for o and u; day 151 adds long e. On following days practise each with different words. R-controlled sounds are heard only in cycle 25. Independent reading never requires words with final silent e, vowel teams, clusters, printed ank or ink, or r-controlled spellings. A vowel letter naming a heard sound is not permission to decode a long-vowel pattern.
>
> Map these high-frequency words before independent use: of, have, from on day 119; there, they, where on day 124; we, all, one on day 130; but, not, no on day 135; what, when, then on day 141; you, said, were on day 146; she, her, like on day 151. Show regular sound-letter correspondences and the part to remember. Keep earlier mappings available. The entire mapped word is learned, without implying its spelling is a productive pattern. Practise RF.K.2d phoneme isolation and RF.K.2e additions or substitutions alongside RF.K.3d printed word changes; do not silently replace one with the other. Retrieve print direction, spoken-print matching, spaces, both letter cases, formation, consonant sounds, rhyme, syllables and onset-rime on days 128, 139 and 157.
>
> Commission seven whole original cycle readers, each about 20 to 80 words and one sentence a page: A Hen Can Nap, Sam and Pam, A Hen in a Tub, Moss on a Log, A Cat Can Sit, A Pup in a Hut, and A Hen Has an Egg. First use days 119, 124, 130, 135, 141, 146 and 151 respectively. Titles are adult-read until every spelling is taught. Body text uses only taught short vowels, digraphs, doubled finals and mapped words. Even the late-cycle readers do not introduce long-vowel decoding. Tree Care Words supplies seven complete short verses, first heard on those same dates, for shared print, rhyme and syllable work. Each verse needs a specific action sequence and meaningful description, not a string of target words. Adults read its rich language. Measures await prose manuscripts; a computed measure is not useful for verse or decodability. Readers supply purposeful independent meaning-making after unit 3; verses sustain richer shared language.
>
