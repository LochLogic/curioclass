- **Foundations** (RF.K.1, RF.K.2, RF.K.3a, RF.K.3c, L.K.1a, L.K.2c): cycle 5, lessons 26 to 29. The letters v and s (name, sound, form), the mapped words he and we, syllable beats (review from unit 1), and first, last and blended sounds in spoken three-sound words.
- **Limits that bite here.** Read-alouds are listening texts; no child reads a passage. Words children recognise are only the letters and mapped words taught so far. Weather is words and pictures: "temperature" is a word for how warm or cool, felt with hands, never a thermometer or a number. No child is asked what they wear or own. No storm scene is acted. The day skeletons list L.K.1c (plurals), L.K.1d (question words) and L.K.1e (prepositions) on days 36 and 38; the unit plan teaches them on days 39, 43 and 44, so here the teacher uses them in speech and no exit asks for them. L.K.4a and L.K.4b (a new meaning; affixes) are not taught here: "clear" for the sky is first met on day 36 and its second meaning is day 44; word parts start day 53.
- **Connections.** SL.K.5 with SL.K.4 on days 36 and 38 (a drawing added to a description). Unit 1's Magic Bow, day 35 (retrieval). Science weather (K-ESS2-1), day 35: its picture record is the bridge if it is on the wall.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At five, the main topic is what a whole text is mostly about, and a detail is one thing it says about the topic. A question about a key detail is asked about something the text said, not about the child's own home. Asking for clarification is a skill, so "Can you say that again?" is praised, never treated as a miss. "Weather observer" is a real job; children are invited to act as observers at the window and never asked to report anything from home. "Clear" has two senses (day 44 teaches the second); on day 36 only the sky sense is used.

Answer: names the thing and what it does ("Snow falls." "Snow falls on the snowman."). - **E38.** Two-picture strip S38: at sunrise a fox sits on a hill under a gray sky; at noon the same fox lies in bright sun. Spoken, item a: "Tell me what changed between the two pictures." Spoken, item b: the teacher says, "The fox went to the thing by the hill. What could you say to me?" Answer: "What thing?" or "Can you say that again?" Wrong: nothing said, or "OK" (AT).

- **4 min. Hook.** Show the first picture of 01a, a yard in sun and the same yard in rain. Read page 1 aloud, then post the compelling question: "How does the weather change what people wear and do?" Children point to what is different; no one is asked about their own clothes.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** R35 (field 3): partners tell one detail from The Magic Bow; the teacher listens at five pairs and says today's next step is "ask and answer about a detail".
- **5 min. Words.** With gestures and pictures: weather, observe, notice, detail. Sun and wind are named by gesture only.
- **14 min. Reading 01a.** First reading of 01a, Weather Changes, whole, about 4 minutes with its eight pictures and no stops except to show a picture. Then reread pages 1 to 4 with one question a page ("What do children wear on the rainy day?", "What do they do?"), children pointing to the picture detail and the teacher saying the words that match.
- **8 min. Model: ask and answer, and the main topic.** The teacher asks a detail question about page 3, points to the evidence, and asks for clarification once ("Can you say that again?"). Then the main-idea-details picture: topic card "weather" on top, two detail cards under it (sun hats; boots and umbrellas), and the topic named aloud as "what the whole page is mostly about".
- **8 min. Partners.** Pairs take turns: one asks about a detail on the page the teacher names (page 2 or 3), the other points and answers, then they swap. Discussion protocol review: one speaks, one listens, a token passes. The teacher listens at six pairs.
- **5 min. Science bridge.** If the science weather picture record is on the wall, read it together; otherwise look at page 1 again. Weather is words and pictures only.
- **6 min. Draw a detail.** Each child draws one detail from page 2 or 3 and tells a partner "I noticed ___."

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: weather, observe, notice, detail, warmer, cooler. Frames: "I noticed ___." "The ___ is ___." "What does ___ mean?" Children may answer in a home language with a picture, and the teacher records what was understood.
- **Access.** Raised-line or large cards; the real window or a seat near it; an adult points as the child directs; a communication aid or private exchange counts. A child who cannot hold a crayon dictates or chooses between picture cards.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token on a picture, drawing or dictation count for every exit; the spoken ask is repeated once on request. Weather content is recorded apart from speech.
- **Reading load.** None. Everything is read or told aloud; children read only the letters and mapped words already taught.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second detail for the same page, or a question about a detail that a partner has not noticed.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look out the window together and ask "What do you notice?" Read one page of the weather picture book aloud, point to the picture, and ask one detail question. Draw two times of the day in two boxes. No question about clothes or home.
