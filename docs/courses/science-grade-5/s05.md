## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do water, air, land and life shape each other, and how much of Earth's water can we use?"
Day 85 opens with the 1972 blue-Earth photograph, contrasting Cascades landscape records and a town beside salt water with a dry freshwater intake, labelled "invented for practice". Children notice, wonder and draw before receiving an explanation.
On day 97 children write the closing explanation: mountain and air interactions explain the wet-west and dry-east pattern; tray and watershed evidence show water shaping land; reservoir graphs explain why blue Earth does not promise accessible fresh water.
Model an evidence-labelled arrow on day 86 and equal-share graphing on day 93. Then ask which evidence warrants each revision; let pairs disagree before the consensus.

All 24 points require modeling or correcting and constructing graphs with an Earth-system idea on the unseen island case. Reserve five minutes for directions and checking, totaling 45; no teaching or response during the sitting. No earlier product is scored.

The atlas evidence tables use 1,000 equal shares, with every value and endpoint supplied, so children never convert or calculate percentages. Each square on its optional hundred-square organizer represents ten shares. Earth's estimated distribution remains the geological survey's table, rounded and rescaled; the new problem is diagnosing the display's claims and constructing defensible replacements. Keep the faulty display and correction sheets unseen until day 98.

Q2a evidence table, per 1,000 all-water shares: salt 975, fresh 25. Its note says salt includes ocean, salty groundwater and salty lakes; almost all salt water is ocean. The draft graph labels its 975 bar "ocean" and its 25 bar "fresh". Supply blank axes from 0 to 1,000 with endpoints 25 and 975; children draw corrected bars and labels. Q2b evidence table, per 1,000 fresh-water shares: ice caps, glaciers and permanent snow 687; fresh groundwater 301; surface and other fresh water 12. The draft plots these correctly but heads the graph "Shares of all Earth water". Supply blank matching axes with those endpoints for the corrected graph. A map key locates Greenland, Antarctica and mountain glaciers; children mark the combined ice category. The remainder includes several stores, not only lakes and rivers. Q2c evidence table: fresh lakes 2.6 and rivers 0.06 per 1,000 fresh-water shares. The draft uses a 0 to 2.6 lake axis and a 0 to 0.06 river axis; both bars fill their axes. Its captions say "Equal bars mean equal amounts" and "Nearby ocean guarantees a ready fresh supply". Supply blank identical inset axes from 0 to 3, with both endpoints marked, for corrected bars. Neither atmospheric amounts nor huge volumes appear.

Q1 scoring: water and land named as interacting parts, one point; arrow showing bank-to-stream transport, one; arrow showing downstream deposition, one; each observation used to explain surface change, two; explanatory annotation, one. Each comparison earns one point for the interaction: waves move land material, coastal water supplies habitat, and ocean water moderates nearby air temperatures. One final point connects a comparison to its specific observation while keeping the panels separate. Meets: "The stream carries grains off the bank and leaves them where the beach grows. My arrows connect the changing land to moving water. Waves move cliff grains; coastal water supports the young fish; ocean water moderates air temperature, helping explain the smaller coastal seasonal change." Does not: "Water, land, fish, air", with four disconnected labels (A).

Q2a scoring: two corrected bar endpoints, two points; all-water units, one; ocean label corrected to salt with dominance explained from the evidence note, one. Meets: "My bars reach 975 salt and 25 fresh all-water shares. Replace ocean with salt: salt dominates, but includes salty lakes and groundwater too." Does not: "Keep ocean because all salt water is ocean" (D).
Q2b scoring: three bar endpoints, three points; wrong heading replaced with fresh-only whole, one; ice and groundwater dominance explained with polar ice correctly located, one. Meets: "The heading must say fresh water, not all water. My bars reach 687, 301 and 12 fresh-water shares. Most is ice or groundwater; Greenland and Antarctic ice belong in the combined ice bar." Does not: "Keep all water: the ice bar holds 687 of every 1,000 all-water shares" (E).
Q2c scoring: two correct bar endpoints redrawn on the same scale, two points; fresh-water units, one; unequal draft scales diagnosed and lake exceeds river while both are small beside ice and groundwater, one; ocean guarantee rejected because salinity, frozen or underground stores do not promise ready freshwater access, one. Meets: "The draft bars use different scales, so equal lengths mislead. On my shared fresh-water axis, lake reaches 2.6 and river 0.06. Both are small beside ice and groundwater. Nearby salt water does not guarantee a ready fresh supply." Does not: "Keep equal bars: lakes and rivers hold equal amounts because the bars match" (H).

Other endpoint or copying errors receive descriptive feedback, not a misconception letter unless the child's explanation supports that letter. Read directions and labels aloud; allow enlarged axes, tactile bars or a neutral scribe placing bars exactly as directed. Keep graph construction and explanation with the child.

Invented for practice: a dune's rooted patch retains sand while its bare patch loses sand. Draw and explain a land-and-life model using these observations. Then explain whether equal-height bars showing 2 of 100 all-water shares and 2 of 100 fresh-water shares represent equal water amounts. Answer: roots hold land material; equal shares of different wholes need not be equal amounts, and the fresh-water whole is smaller. Wrong: disconnected labels (A), treating the fresh-water whole as all water (E). Give five minutes, unaided.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach watershed, groundwater and share with labelled drawings. Offer "This arrow shows ... because ..." and fade it before assessment. Read factual captions aloud; students need not research independently. Provide large, tactile and high-contrast maps and axes, or a neutral scribe. Extend by comparing what two valid scales reveal, within these expectations.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, one child predicts and observes one protected tray, then explains to an adult. One projector displays records while every child graphs on paper. Day 99 uses the same feedback and fresh-case sequence in either room.
