# Section 2.2: Boundaries and evidence limits

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 2, Where a tree comes from. "Where does a growing tree's matter come from, and where does it go when the tree dies?" Anchor: the tree-in-a-pot experiment Jan Baptist van Helmont ran in Brussels in the 1600s: a willow grew from 5 pounds to about 169 in five years while its soil lost less than a pound (days 18 to 41): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 23 to 26.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (23), fluency 1 (24), application 2 (25, 26). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

All following records are printed and announced "Invented for practice." Four-minute independent paper exits. One formative scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Initial jar predictions receive descriptive feedback; blanks remain missing evidence. Keys solved independently.

- **E23.** Record: "Two plant chambers begin at 820 g. The sealed chamber ends at 820 g, with inside droplets. The open chamber ends at 790 g. Both plants remain alive in light. Each weighing includes the same container and contents. The scale reads in 1 g steps." Ask: "Draw both boundary accounts. Explain what these records support and leave unresolved."
  Key: no detected sealed-total change; internal exchanges possible. The open chamber loses 30 g across its boundary. Droplets show water present, not every route. Light enters as energy. Totals do not identify substances lost or establish gas routes. Wrong: "Living plants exchange no gases" (J); "Survival proves water is their only input" (I).
  Shape: paired open/sealed boundary drawings with evidence limits. Practice uses one jar's labels/prediction and assembly, never paired chambers or numerical-loss inference.
- **E24.** Record: "A basil drawing shows a growing plant without visible solid food entering. The surrounding air and water are blank." Ask: "Revise the tiny-parts drawing to account for incoming matter."
  Key: unseen air matter enters leaves, water enters roots; dots are model parts too small to see, not named molecules. Sunlight remains energy. Wrong: "Only water has incoming matter" (B); "Light becomes new parts" (D).
  Shape: repair an incomplete tiny-parts plant drawing. Practice uses oral tracing/existing locators, never a flawed drawing or written revision.
- **E25.** Record: "A plant becomes taller after watering. No other conditions were recorded." Ask: "Mark whether this identifies all matter sources: supports the claim, or leaves it open."
  Key: leaves it open. Uncontrolled height change does not isolate sources. Recognition matches the Goal map; explanation is not required. Wrong: "Supports the claim that water supplied everything" (I).
  Shape: binary sufficiency judgment from uncontrolled watering. Practice generates missing measurements and locates evidence, never binary verdict cards or watering-height cases.
- **E26.** Record: "Pumpkin dry weight rises from 3 g to 33 g. Dry soil falls from 700 g to 698 g. Shed plant matter is collected separately. Matched air groups have equal water, minerals, light and temperature. Ordinary-air plants finish at 19 g. Plants receiving more useful air finish at 29 g." Ask: "Build a supported argument about the chief plant matter sources."
  Key: 30 g gain greatly exceeds 2 g soil loss, challenging mainly-soil. Matched air difference supports air contribution. With taught water uptake, argue chiefly air/water with small minerals. These records do not measure source fractions or show minerals unnecessary. Wrong: "Soil supplies most of the 30 g" (A); "Air cannot matter because water supplies everything" (B).
  Shape: full chief-source argument from combined dry-soil/air packet. Practice keeps comparisons and oral links separate, never this combined packet or pumpkin case.
