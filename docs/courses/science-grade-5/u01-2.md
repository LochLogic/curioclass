# Section 1.2: Where dissolved matter goes

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#science/science-grade-5/1/s/1.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py science-grade-5 u01-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Children read and write at this grade, but the evidence in these three days is what they observe, read in short records, draw and say. Every day runs 45 minutes with set-up and clean-up counted inside it. Drawings are practice and are kept; the scored model is unit assessment Q1 on day 16.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **5-PS1-1, this section's share.** "Make a model showing that matter is made of bits too tiny to see." Days 5 to 7 carry the dissolved half: matter that seems to vanish in water is still there, spread through it as parts too small to see. Children revise a model against evidence on day 5, test the claim "dissolved matter is gone" against dried residue on day 6, and on day 7 join the ball, the syringe and the salt water in one class model.
- **Limits that bite here.** Students never taste anything in the science room. No heating, no names for the tiny parts, and no account of how water leaves a dish: say only that the water dries away and the salt stays. A dot stands for a part too small to see, never a visible grain. Nothing is weighed in this section. Eye protection is on for every mixing day.
- **Connections.** RI.5.7 (drawing on several sources to answer a question), day 5: the teacher models answering one question from two short accounts; ELA grade 5 unit 2 starts later, so the bridge is modeled, not assumed. Scale (crosscutting) is joined on day 7: grains against parts too small to see.
- **Practices.** Developing and Using Models: day 5 revise the liquid model, day 6 use evidence to test a claim about the model, day 7 reach one consensus model. Crosscutting concepts: scale, and cause and effect (the clear liquid and the dried residue are effects of what the water holds).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** "Dissolved" here means spread through the water in parts too small to see; a clear liquid is not an empty one. Sweetness is given by a food-safe account (card S2), so no child tastes. The dried salt shows what stayed; it does not explain how water leaves, which this unit does not teach. The two cards S1 and S2 are original and invented for practice, not observations. The consensus model is the class's best account so far, and day 15 revises it.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1, the dissolved case (5-PS1-1), on day 16 (on the unit plan, s01 field 5). Nothing from days 5 to 7 is scored.

**Exit questions.** Tickets are named X5 to X7 here. Each takes four minutes, is unscored, uses a fresh case invented for practice, and is read aloud when needed. Letters refer to the unit's error register. If many (more than a third) miss one, the next teaching day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **X5.** Source P: "Water with added powder became clear; filtering left no visible powder on the filter." Source R: "Drying that filtered liquid left solid; drying plain water left none." Revise your drawing of the liquid using both records. Answer: dots spread through the liquid pass the filter and stay in it; the solid left after drying, and none from plain water, shows the powder was still there. Wrong: the powder is gone (C); each dot is a visible grain (B).
- **X6.** A clear solution in a closed jar forms crystals as it cools, and warming makes them disappear again without the jar being opened. Draw the clear stage, then use both observations to say whether the dissolved solid ever stopped existing. Answer: unseen dissolved parts stay in the clear liquid; crystals coming back and going from view again support that it is retained, not destroyed. No account of why is required. Wrong: it vanished when it disappeared (C).
- **X7.** A tire is squeezed without opening its valve. Clear mineral water leaves a solid when it dries. Explain both with one model that tells grains apart from parts too small to see. Answer: the tire's air is the same dots in less space; the mineral is dots spread through the water, left behind as a solid. Dots stand for parts much smaller than any grain. Wrong: air is nothing (A); dots are grains (B); the dissolved mineral is gone (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The particle model gains dissolved matter on days 5 and 6 and reaches class consensus on day 7 (consensus model 1, kept on the wall unchanged until day 15). Scale is joined on day 7. Children hold their own drawing and keep it beside the class model; the teacher holds the class model and the question board ("Where is dissolved material?"). A drawing is revised with a dated change line, never erased.
- **Key moment 1, day 5: clear water that still holds sugar.** Ideal instrument: a way to watch parts spread through water and stay. Spec: the Curio Class particle viewer partly fits, because it shows unseen parts but supplies no evidence, and it is spec only. Use paper dot panels until it is built. Gaps and asks: [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md).
- **Key moment 2, day 6: the dried dish.** Ideal instrument: matched dishes dried the same way, seen under a hand lens. Spec: fits; dishes, lens and a dated label. Gaps and asks: none.
- **A room with nothing.** Cups, spoons and water from a kitchen; the dried sample is two saucers set out beforehand. Without them, use N6 (salt water dries to white residue, plain water to none) and cards S1 and S2, labeled invented for practice, not observations, and dated residue photographs when they exist.

## 6. Days

## Day 5


- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **5 min. Notice dissolving.** Goggles on. Each group stirs one preportioned teaspoon of sugar into 50 g of water and watches until it clears. Nothing is tasted or brought near the eyes or mouth.
- **12 min. Investigate.** Groups of five compare the clear liquid with plain water, draw both, and write what they saw. The teacher reminds them sweetness is not tested here.
- **10 min. Two-source reading.** The teacher shows cards S1 (Appearance) and S2 (Sweetness), reads them aloud, and models answering "What can both accounts tell us about sugar that is no longer visible?" by citing S1's clear liquid and S2's reported sweetness. Students then say which line they would use.
- **10 min. Revise.** Students revise their liquid drawing so the sugar is still somewhere, adding the dated change line. Up to 5 of these minutes may use the particle viewer (key, show why, dissolving) if built; otherwise paper dot panels.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X5.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Spills wiped, cups and spoons rinsed, goggles sanitized, hands washed.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the revised drawing; the teacher holds the class model and the board. Tool: particle viewer, key, show why, up to 5 minutes, spec only (optional). By hand: stirring, drawing, citing a source. Evidence: X5. No-kit: observation records.

## Day 6


- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **5 min. Reopen.** Read the claim on the board: "Dissolved matter is gone." Repair error C first if many made it.
- **18 min. Residue investigation.** Goggles on. Groups compare the two dated dishes the teacher left to dry before day 5 (salt water and plain water), under a hand lens, and record what each leaves. They then mix a fresh teaspoon of salt in water, set it in a labeled dish to dry, and draw today's clear liquid. No heating; nothing is tasted.
- **14 min. Test the claim.** Groups use the residue to say whether the claim stands and draw the salt before, in the clear stage, and after drying. They revise the model; no explanation of how water leaves is asked.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X6.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Dishes capped and labeled, spills wiped, lenses collected, goggles sanitized, hands washed.

Who holds what is carried: groups hold the dated dishes; the teacher holds the board. Tool: None. By hand: using a lens, recording, drawing. Evidence: X6. If the dishes are still damp, use N6, labeled invented for practice.

## Day 7


- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **5 min. Reopen.** A gallery of the day 1, 3, 5 and 6 drawings; repair error B first if many made it.
- **20 min. Consensus model 1.** Each group draws one case (the ball's air, the squeezed syringe, the dissolved salt) with the same unseen parts, then the class agrees one model that covers all three. Planned question: "Which evidence does each case add?" Nothing new is taught; the day gives the model repeated, varied use.
- **14 min. Grains and parts.** For each case, children draw the visible grain or bubble and an enlarged circle showing dots far smaller, then write two sentences: what stays and how they know.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X7.
- **2 min. Put away.** Dishes, lenses and dot panels returned.

Who holds what is carried: the class holds consensus model 1; each child keeps a personal copy. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, writing. Evidence: X7.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 5, "What can both accounts tell us about sugar that is no longer visible?"; day 6, "What does the dried dish say about the claim that dissolved matter is gone?"; day 7, "Which evidence does each case add?"
- Written task, day 7: two sentences under each of the three cases: what stays and how you know.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) the unseen parts are drawn in the liquid or the air in every case; (2) a cited source, dish or reading supports each claim; (3) dots are described as far smaller than a grain.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 5 revisits the day 4 air drawing. Day 6 reopens the day 5 liquid drawing and the board claim. Day 7 reopens all earlier drawings together.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built without time pressure.** Day 7 gives the same model repeated, varied use on three cases, each explained in the child's own words; no timer and no race.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: dissolve, clear, residue, evidence, source, grain, model. Frames: "I changed ___ because ___ showed ___." and "Source ___ says ___; source ___ says ___; together they show ___." Accept home language, labels or drawing with dictation.
- **Access.** Read cups and dishes by touch or with a partner describing the residue; raised dots on panels; a lens on a stand. Splash goggles that fit over glasses.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each ticket and each source aloud; answer by drawing, pointing or dictation to a scribe who adds no ideas.
- **Reading load.** Cards S1, S2, P and R are two or three short sentences each; the teacher reads them aloud first. Children read captions alone.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan, without running it, a test that could tell "gone" from "spread out" with no tasting, and say what the result would show.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** An adult and one station: one cup, one spoon, goggles, a dated dish set out beforehand; read S1 and S2 together, then draw the clear liquid and the dried dish. If no dish is dry, read N6 together. Never require home equipment.
- **One projector.** Show S1 and S2, the dish photographs and the class model on one screen; children draw on paper.
