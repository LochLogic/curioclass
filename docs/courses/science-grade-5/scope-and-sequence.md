# Scope and sequence: Science Grade 5

Course `science-grade-5`, grade 5, draft, revised 2026-11-15.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-5/days.csv): 120 course days, 111 within the six frame units and nine at course level. Each meeting is 45 minutes. All school days are filled from the existing calendar pin, including course day 43 on school day 50. Weeks are five school days, not dates. No unit changes order, ownership or length. Section boundaries await the unit plans; `unit` therefore holds `<unit>.1` throughout this scope-stage file.

Each hook opens a question board and an initial explanation. Evidence changes the explanation at the revision points named below, followed by a consensus model and transfer to an unfamiliar case. Notebook evidence informs teaching each day. Assessment entries reserve purposes and performances, not items or answer keys. Every assessment asks every part of each code it lists, joining the practice, core idea and assigned crosscutting element; the coverage table specifies them. Course-level checks sample selected expectations and explicitly named parts of others, with no complete-code claim for the latter. Readiness samples prerequisites, not the grade 5 codes that its host day teaches or responds to. Each unit has exactly one named response day, counted inside its days. No unit exceeds 25 days, so none requires a mid-unit assessment.

There is no base text and no source-map.csv is needed. These are original investigation plans. Public data, historical records and images must retain their provenance when selected in the unit plans; reference-only curriculum supplies no wording, problems or images. The controlling dimensions are in [ngss-pe-dimensions.csv](../../../data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv); the state framework is a crosswalk, not an additional owner of these days.

## Unit 1. Matter too small to see

17 course days, days 1 to 17; school days 2 to 20, weeks 1 to 4.

The heavier pumped ball creates a need to explain matter that cannot be seen. Days 1 to 7 build the particle model from compressed air, dissolved sugar and recovered salt; drawings change on days 3 to 6 and reach a first consensus on day 7. Days 8 to 14 plan, measure and graph whole-system weights through heating, cooling and mixing, including open and balloon-topped systems and discrepant readings. Day 15 joins the evidence into a conservation model. This gives more time to planning and weighing than to particle vocabulary, and gives unit 2 an evidence-based account of air as matter. Neither atoms nor molecules are named, and evaporation has no particle-level explanation here.

Readiness for s2-pieces-new-object occupies the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook; day 2 provides one lesson of targeted reteaching or extension within the day. The end-of-unit assessment is day 16: an unseen change, a particle model and measured-weight graphs across heating, cooling and mixing, with an observed weighing performance. Day 17 is its sole response day, with unit 2 readiness in its final ten minutes. No mid-unit assessment is needed. Weights stay in whole grams on one scale, bridging math conversion taught on school days 110 to 118. Day 5 models answering one question from two supplied sources, bridging source synthesis before ELA unit 2 starts on school day 42.

Candidate settled: drop the perfume or orange-peel smell demonstration, zero days. The ball, syringe and dissolved-material evidence already establish unseen matter; a scent adds an avoidable inhalation or allergy concern without a missing owned part.

Day 2 begins drying soil, day 4 weighs the dried soil into tared pots and plants beans, and day 3 starts the shadow record, all within the first school week. Pots receive continuing teacher care without additional course days. Fizzing uses the frame's small quantities and a barely filled balloon; the complete apparatus is weighed both times. Lower readings prompt investigation of spills, escaping matter, changed apparatus or scale limits, rather than an automatic gas explanation.

## Unit 2. Where a tree comes from

24 course days, days 18 to 41; school days 21 to 48, weeks 5 to 10.

Days 18 to 27 ask where plant matter comes from, using historical tree-and-soil measurements, water-grown plants, air-comparison data and the particle model. The argument changes on days 21 and 22: the historical result challenged soil as the main source, but did not justify the experimenter's water-only conclusion. Days 23 and 28 to 33 open and revise the ecosystem model with closed jars, decomposers, matter routes, species' needs and the introduced-rabbit case. Days 34 to 36 then add sunlight-energy transfers to the food web already built. This order prevents a food-energy diagram from becoming an unexplained list of arrows. Day 36 also absorbs the former energy-model comparison and monthly shadow reading. Day 37 adds final class dry-soil evidence; day 38 joins the arguments and models.

Day 17's last ten minutes check s3-environment-traits and s3-habitat-survival, last taught in grade 3; targeted support or extension runs inside day 19 and informs the jar work on day 23. The hook retrieves s4-structures-function and this year's particle model; day 34 retrieves s4-energy-transfer. Day 39 assesses 5-LS1-1 with an argument from new plant data. Day 40 assesses 5-LS2-1 and 5-PS3-1 in one linked model of a new ecosystem: gases and wastes, decomposers, diversity and newcomers, separate matter and energy routes, and food's growth, repair, motion and warmth uses. Day 41 is its sole response day. No mid-unit assessment is needed. Readiness for unit 3 instead falls on course-level respond day 44, immediately before that unit.

Candidate settled: keep the fallen-log or leaf-litter comparison as enclosed leaf litter on day 30, costing one day within these 24. Dated schoolyard photographs and a teacher-collected sealed sample make decomposition in an open environment comparable to the closed jar; day 31 uses the evidence to revise the cycle, without adding another candidate day.

The class jar is set up on day 23 and observed on days 28, 31, 35 and 37. Only plants are enclosed; no animals are introduced or released. On day 35 the teacher separates and weighs plants and spreads soil to dry; day 37 weighs the re-dried soil after three school days of drying. Weather, plant growth and measurement error may prevent a neat result, so a sourced backup record supports comparison without replacing an unexpected class finding. Historical weights remain in pounds, class measurements in grams, with no conversion. Source charts and two-source teacher modeling bridge RI.5.7 and RI.5.9; a class flow-chart display on day 34 bridges SL.5.5 before ELA unit 3 on school days 84 to 123. Food supplies material as well as energy; sunlight supplies energy, not plant material. No photosynthesis chemistry or molecules.

## Unit 3. Mystery materials

17 course days, days 45 to 61; school days 53 to 74, weeks 11 to 15.

The four unknown powders launch the question board on day 45. Days 46 to 51 develop an identification model from multiple properties of powders, metals, minerals and liquids: color, hardness, shine, magnetism, dissolving and electrical and heat conduction. Measured quantities use standard units; density is excluded. Days 52 to 57 use that property evidence to plan and conduct controlled mixing comparisons with three trials, before-and-after records and checks on single suggestive signs. Day 57 absorbs the former day 58 model revision; day 58 consolidates the particle-and-change model. More time goes to collecting and comparing evidence than to guessing material names.

Readiness is the last ten minutes of day 44, on s2-material-properties, s2-best-material and s2-heat-cool-change. Day 46 carries one lesson of targeted property-and-purpose reteaching or extension; day 52 carries one lesson on reversible and irreversible changes or extension. The grade 4 fair-test routine and this year's particle model are retrieved inside day 52. Day 59 assesses 5-PS1-3 through observed identification with new samples, measurements and records across all named properties. Day 60 assesses only 5-PS1-4 through an observed collaborative three-trial fair comparison and new-substance conclusion. Each day covers its own listed code in full; six groups of five keep the observed work within the shared budget. Day 61 is the sole response day, with its last ten minutes reserved for unit 4 readiness. No mid-unit assessment is needed.

Candidate settled: keep the rock-and-mineral property-table addition on day 49, costing one day within these 17. A gentle fingernail-and-coin comparison on soft minerals supplies the hardness evidence; no hammer or sharp scratch tool is needed.

ELA unit 2 overlaps this unit rather than guaranteeing its research routines first. Days 46, 52 and 53 therefore retain the frame's source-lined note sheet and class investigation plus one teacher-chosen reading, bridging W.5.8 and W.5.7. Steel wool is teacher handled in closed displays; iron-and-sand magnetic separation is read from records instead of loose filings. Heat conduction uses teacher-handled warm water without flame. Mixing needs eye protection and no tasting; the kitchen story never permits putting lab samples into food. Evidence of reversibility alone does not decide whether a new substance formed.

## Unit 4. Earth and sky

20 course days, days 62 to 81; school days 75 to 100, weeks 15 to 20.

The falling-ball comparison here and in Australia, beside the accumulated shadow record, opens the unit. Days 62 to 66 revise down from a direction on the page to a pull toward Earth's center. Days 67 to 74 analyze hourly shadows, daylight and darkness and seasonal star visibility, then test and revise spin-and-orbit models against those data. Day 73 also locates moon orbit in the qualitative sky-pattern model. Days 75 to 77 use controlled equal-light evidence and relative star distances to argue why the sun looks brighter; day 77 absorbs the former day 78 argument comparison. Day 78 joins the models. Gravity establishes the round Earth before it spins; sky patterns establish the spatial model before star brightness uses it. The eight-day sky run carries three distinct data sets, not a single shadow graph.

Day 61's final ten minutes check s3-magnet-static, s1-sky-patterns and s1-daylight-seasons; day 63 provides targeted support or extension on all three inside the day. Their last uses are in the earlier-row table. Retrieve the force-arrow component taught in grade 4 on day 62 and s4-light-eye on day 75. Day 79 assesses 5-ESS1-2 with three new graph sets: hourly shadow lengths and directions, daylight/darkness and seasonal stars, with qualitative model links. Day 80 assesses 5-PS2-1 and 5-ESS1-1 as two short evidence arguments about center-directed gravity and the nearer sun; it requires no graph construction. Day 81 is the sole response day. No mid-unit assessment is needed.

Candidate settled: drop the optional home night-sky observation, zero days. Dated sky charts provide a common record without assuming safe outdoor access, equipment or a particular night's visibility.

The science argument frame on days 62 and 65 bridges W.5.1 while ELA unit 3 is only beginning. Bar graphs on days 68 and 70 bridge coordinate graphing, which math teaches on school days 135 to 149. Light travel times and a supplied grain-of-sand relative scale on day 77 bridge powers of ten, taught in math on school days 110 to 118. No number exceeds 1,000,000. The causes of seasons, numerical gravity and other causes of star brightness remain outside the assessment. Moon patterns provide core-idea context, not a new moon-phase mechanism requirement.

## Unit 5. Earth's systems and its water

15 course days, days 85 to 99; school days 105 to 123, weeks 21 to 25.

The blue planet beside water scarcity and the wet-west/dry-east Cascades opens the puzzle. Days 85 to 91 develop and revise a two-system interaction model, first from the mountain-air case, then ocean-coast, ocean-life, local climate and erosion evidence. Days 92 to 96 develop the distribution model from maps, two hundred-square grids, two overview bar graphs and a small-reservoir inset separating lakes and rivers. Day 97 joins the models. Interaction comes before distribution as the frame requires; distribution gets five days because changing the whole from all water to fresh water is a real reasoning demand.

Readiness for s3-climates is in day 84's final ten minutes; day 86 supplies targeted support or extension inside the lesson. Retrieve s4-weathering-erosion on day 85 and s4-earth-feature-maps with s2-water-where explicitly retaught in grade 4 day 78 on day 92. Day 98 assesses a new two-system interaction and description and graphing of all named reservoirs, including the changed whole and separate lake/river inset. Its 45 minutes reserve 15 for the model and short comparisons, 25 for graphs/descriptions and five for review and collection. Day 99 is the sole response day. No mid-unit assessment is needed.

Candidate settled: keep the local watershed map on day 90, costing one day within these 15. A public map and public utility source description trace runoff toward a community source; students do no home-address or family-water research.

The first graph uses about 97 salt-water parts (nearly all ocean) and 3 fresh parts of all water. The second uses about 69 ice caps, glaciers and permanent snow, 30 fresh groundwater and 1 surface and other fresh water, of fresh water alone. Polar ice is located within the combined ice category. The remainder includes lakes, rivers and swamps alongside ground ice, soil moisture, air and living things; it is not all lake/river/swamp water. Source: [USGS, Where is Earth's Water?](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/where-earths-water), Shiklomanov's 1993 table, checked 2026-11-15. Day 95 adds supplied freshwater shares: lakes 0.26, rivers 0.006 and swamps 0.03 parts per 100. Students draw the lake and river bars on a common prelabelled inset axis from 0 to 0.30, with marked endpoints; they compare quantities without calculating percentages. Math unit 6 decimal reading is already available. Atmospheric amounts and enormous absolute volumes stay outside assessment. The 69/30/1 overview is rounded; the inset retains the source's separate small stores. ELA's display teaching in unit 3 is available by day 91, and the science graphs are ready before ELA's water unit starts on school day 124. Unsafe or scarce water is discussed with dignity, never through questions about students' homes.

## Unit 6. Protecting water by design

18 course days, days 100 to 117; school days 125 to 147, weeks 25 to 30.

Storm runoff opens a protection model on day 100. Days 101 and 102 obtain and combine community information about watersheds, wetlands, runoff, water saving and air pollution, linking actions to the Earth systems they protect. Days 103 to 106 define the filter problem, generate and compare alternatives, plan controlled repeated tests and construct prototypes. Days 107 to 111 run and graph trials, locate failure points, choose one change, retest and compare against criteria and constraints. Days 112 and 113 join community evidence with bounded design claims and a consensus design-cycle model; day 113 absorbs the former presentation rehearsal. Day 114 critiques plans and keeps the monthly shadow record. Nine days for the design cycle before synthesis protect repeated testing from becoming a single demonstration.

Retrieve s4-energy-resources and unit 5 water on day 100. Day 103 retrieves s4-energy-device, s4-information-codes and s4-hazard-solutions, including criteria, constraints and comparing solutions. No readiness check is needed. Day 115 assesses 5-ESS3-1 through a new two-source community explanation, independently of device success. Day 116 assesses only 3-5-ETS1-3 through an unfamiliar runoff-catcher problem, not the class filter: an observed collaborative fair-test plan and three brief trials of each of two prepared prototypes, recorded failures, comparison against criteria/constraints and a proposed improvement. Day 117 is the sole response day. No mid-unit assessment is needed. The test uses quick small pours at one observed station, six groups of five, while others complete independent plan/failure explanations. The source task has its own day; the unit plan must verify the stated station budget.

Candidate settled: drop the younger-class talk, zero days. Explaining data to classmates inside day 113 already supplies communication practice without another audience or scheduling commitment; no action campaign or contact is planned.

W.5.8 is available from ELA unit 2; ELA's water unit runs beside this work. Math's line-plot unit spans school days 119 to 134 and may not have reached plots by day 105, so days 105 and 108 explicitly retrieve grade 4 plots in whole and half units. Flow measurements use those units; cloudiness remains a separate ordered rating, not a fractional measurement or an unsupported average. Clean classroom soil simulates runoff; no storm-drain samples are collected. Gloves, eye protection and hand washing apply, and filtered water is never drunk. Clearer is not safe to drink. Community accounts, backed evidence and limitations guide explanation; no cause is pushed or family access disclosed. Repeated trials teach why one result can mislead.
