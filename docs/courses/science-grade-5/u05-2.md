# Section 5.2: Flow routes and changing land

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 5, Earth's systems and its water. "How do water, air, land and life shape each other, and how much of Earth's water can we use?" Anchor: from space Earth looks blue, yet towns run short of water to drink; and one mountain range (the Cascades of Washington) has rain forest on its west side and dry land on its east (days 85 to 99): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 89 to 91.** Days by kind: fluency 1 (91), application 2 (89, 90). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 4 in the unit; the unit plan holds its section fields as U3 to U11 (a one-section unit until the 2026-11-16 cuts).
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.

- **E89, seven minutes.** Before/after record: "Water meets a gravel berm, then bends left. Marked grains start beside the berm." "Later those grains lie beyond the bend; the berm has a narrow notch." Ask: "Record the grain route. Revise arrows showing land and water affecting each other." Key: original grains beside berm, later beyond bend; land turns flow, water transports land material and cuts notch. Wrong: transport of loose grains called weathering (B); disconnected names as complete model (A).
  Shape: extract grain locations/flow and revise reciprocal arrows from an unfamiliar berm record. Practice observes real pours and predicts orally; no berm/notch cards or before/after arrow-revision sheet.
- **E90, seven minutes.** Map: ridge P at 90 height units, channel Q at 60, lake R at 30; outlet leads away. Source note: "Town K draws water from lake R. This note gives no testing results." Ask: "Trace runoff to the town's source. Explain what each record can establish." Key: downhill P-Q-R, stopping at source; map supports route, note identifies supply, neither proves safe drinking water. Wrong: labels alone instead of connected route (A); fresh water must be safe (G).
  Shape: trace a new height-key map and separate map/source evidence. Practice audits isolated symbols and single source claims, never traces a complete route-to-source with a limit paragraph. No Cedar River exit.
- **E91, seven minutes.** "A gust bends flexible stems. The stems rise after the gust stops." Ask: "Draw a two-system display and defend its arrow using these observations." Key: air acts on living stems; bending during gust/recovery afterward support interaction, not a growth claim. Wrong: disconnected names as full model (A); invisible air cannot act (C).
  Shape: independent new air-life display with evidence defense. Practice orally rehearses existing water-land displays, never drafts a new display or uses air/stem case.
