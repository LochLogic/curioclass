## 1. Standards and practices

Moon movement against background stars differs from its nightly apparent path. Do not imply every star disappears seasonally. No phases or seasonal-cause explanation.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Assessment contribution:** Q1c/d and Q1a/b links; final teaching ownership u04-4. All E cases invented for practice, withheld until independent paper. One ask, at most two parts; one formative scale.

- **E71.** "Invented for practice. Three winter evenings show C each evening and D on none. Three summer evenings show D each evening and C on none. Place, clock time and clear conditions match. Design a pictograph showing both groups' seasonal visibility." Key: One symbol per evening: winter C=3,D=0; summer C=0,D=3. Include key/group/season labels and explicit zeros. Cause not required; omissions alone are not D. Wrong: Daily Earth spin alone explains different seasonal night-star records. (error D).
  Shape: construct two-series seasonal pictograph. Practice tallies one group and checks a separate supplied symbol key, never constructs two series.
- **E72.** "Invented for practice. Far J is left; the sun is central; far K is right. Earth is right of the sun in February and left in August. A log says midnight faces J in February and K in August. Correct the log using an orbit model. Explain why daily spin alone cannot settle these month labels." Key: February faces outward right toward K; August faces outward left toward J. Log reversed. Orbit position changes outward night view; spin alone at fixed position cannot settle seasonal views. Wrong: Daily Earth spin alone explains different seasonal night-star records. (error D).
  Shape: reconcile conflicting log/locator through orbit reasoning. Practice has consistent views only, never month-label correction.
- **E73.** "Invented for practice. Vale moves during one night. Months later, Moss replaces Vale in a matched nighttime view. The moon returns near a background group after about a month. Connect each record to the motion your model needs." Key: Vale nightly path: Earth spin. Matched monthly replacement: Earth orbit. Moon background return: moon own orbit around Earth. Link each motion to its timescale, no phases. Wrong: Daily Earth spin alone explains different seasonal night-star records. (error D).
  Shape: explain three timescale links in one new set. Practice isolates each motion; no combined three-record matching card.
- **E74.** "Invented for practice. A northern display shows shadows turning and shortening toward noon. Complete-day strips show longer summer daylight. Target groups differ between winter and summer midnight views. Explain what these patterns contribute to your sky model." Key: Spin links daily shadow changes and daily light-dark cycling. Describe longer summer daylight without its mechanism. Earth orbit changes matched midnight outward view. Wrong: Every month has equal daylight duration. (error C).
  Shape: synthesize three evidence types with bounded causes. Fluency practice retrieves one graph set at a time, never three-panel synthesis. Do not add gravity/brightness.

Hand-backs: E71 graph issues/D to 72's five-minute repair; E72 D to 73's five; E73 D/H/I to 74's fifteen, five each; E74 C/D/G to 75, five each. Over ten of thirty means whole-class move; all get private notes. Never label a graph omission D.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: orbit, spin, star group, visible, background. Frame: "At this time, the night side faces __." Read charts aloud; at most 200 words independently, printed sentences fifteen words or fewer. Tactile fixed markers, raised symbols, audio description and scribe preserve child viewpoint decisions. Extension: explain why disk cannot predict every star at every latitude.
