# Section 5.1: Mountains, moving air and ocean interactions

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 5, Earth's systems and its water. "How do water, air, land and life shape each other, and how much of Earth's water can we use?" Anchor: from space Earth looks blue, yet towns run short of water to drink; and one mountain range (the Cascades of Washington) has rain forest on its west side and dry land on its east (days 85 to 99): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 85 to 88.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (85, 86, 88), application 1 (87). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 4 in the unit; the unit plan holds its section fields as U3 to U11 (a one-section unit until the 2026-11-16 cuts).
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

**Share:** 5-ESS2-1, "Use an example to model": evidence-backed interactions, two systems per panel. Developing and using models joins ESS2.A with systems and system models throughout. Day 88 extends mountain weather to ocean effects on land, life and air, introducing watershed and the fresh/safe distinction before later application. No pressure, particle or energy mechanism for rising air.
Teacher explains that a model connects parts through an action, not proximity. Land redirects moist air upward; clouds and precipitation accompany rising air. Air continues across the ridge with less moisture. Water gets its own trace, not a third system in an assessed panel. Ocean water provides habitat, transports land material and moderates nearby air temperatures. Connection: SL.5.5 on 88, pointing to evidence in a display; no ELA mastery assumed.

All exits take seven individual paper minutes. All unfamiliar records below are **invented for practice**, printed and said aloud, held unseen until the exit. Initial E85 ideas receive descriptive feedback. Later formative evidence uses only shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Keys were solved independently; accept scientifically supported alternatives.
- **E85.** Record: "The globe view shows wide oceans. A closer ridge view shows moving clouds and wet slopes." Ask: "Compare these views in an initial land-and-air panel. Mark what remains uncertain." Key: globe establishes widespread water, not this slope's rainfall cause; tentative ridge/air interaction links moving clouds with land, with uncertainty about mechanism. Do not require the full rain-shadow explanation yet. Wrong: air cannot interact because invisible (C); disconnected names presented as a complete model (A).
  Shape: initial wide/close comparison panel with uncertainty. Hook practice uses notices and questions only; no paired-view model or comparison panel.
- **E86.** New profile: "Moist air approaches from the north. The north slope is usually rainy; the south is usually dry." "Today the north slope is dry." Ask: "Annotate this profile to explain the usual pattern. Does today's observation overturn it?" Key: slope redirects incoming air upward; clouds/rain occur on the rising side; air crosses with less moisture. A dry day does not overturn a many-year pattern. Wrong: names alone form the model (A); invisible air excluded (C). Climate-only misses get descriptive feedback, not a false register letter.
  Shape: annotate a new oriented profile and separate timescales. Practice reads observations and orders separate action strips, never a completed profile or combined daily/usual response.
- **E87.** Record: "Moist air enters a gap from the east. Clouds form above its rising eastern path." "Air continues west; western slopes receive less rain." Draft: "The ridge stops all air." Ask: "Revise this explanation using the observations." Key: ridge redirects moist air upward, clouds/rain form on its rising side, and air continues with less moisture; continued travel contradicts the stopping claim. Wrong: replacement consists only of land/air names (A). Unsupported stopping claims receive descriptive feedback.
  Shape: revise a contradicted explanation from new gap records. Practice predicts missing stages orally from correct Cascades sequences; no flawed sentence or written explanatory revision.
- **E88.** Separate coastal panels: "A current moves pebbles along an inlet." "Shell animals remain underwater in a shallow coastal pool." "Coastal air changes less across seasons than inland air." Ask: "Explain the interaction in each separate panel." Key: water transports land material; water provides living habitat; ocean water moderates nearby air temperature. Keep each panel to two systems. Wrong: disconnected parts instead of explanations (A); air excluded because invisible (C).
  Shape: one explanatory action across three separate panels, as the Goal map requires. Practice sorts single observations and points to evidence, never a three-panel explanation package. Reserve Q1 island, cliff, fish and plants.
**Q1 Shape:** unseen island transport/deposition model plus three ocean explanations. Practice isolates components, never combines the assessment package. Section 5.3 reserves Q2 shapes.
