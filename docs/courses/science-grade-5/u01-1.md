# Section 1.1: Air as matter we cannot see

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 1, Matter too small to see. "What is everything made of, if we cannot see the pieces, and does any of it vanish when it changes?" Anchor: a flat ball weighed, pumped up with air, and weighed again: it is heavier, though all we added was air we cannot see (days 1 to 17): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 4.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (1, 2, 3), application 1 (4). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 4 in the unit; the unit plan holds its section fields as U3 to U11 (a one-section unit until the 2026-11-16 cuts).
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Children read and write at this grade, but the evidence in these four days is what they see, measure, draw and say. Every day runs 45 minutes with set-up and clean-up counted inside it. Every drawing in days 1 to 4 is practice and is kept; the scored model is unit assessment Q1 on day 16.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **5-PS1-1** (days 1, 2, 3, 4): Make a model showing that matter is made of bits too tiny to see.
- skill s5-particles (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 1: predict, then watch the teacher weigh a flat ball, pump it and weigh it again. Record both readings before explaining the increase. Ask the compelling question.
> Model recording evidence on days 2 and 8. Invite competing drawings, then introduce particles. Thereafter ask what evidence requires revision. Students choose fair comparisons on day 12.
>
> Question board: What entered the ball (days 1 to 4)? Where is dissolved material (5 to 7)? How could weighing test its presence (8 to 11)? Why might readings fall (12 to 14)? What explanation fits all records (15)? Move answers beside evidence. Each answer raises the next question. Three-dimensional goals: days 1 to 7 use explanatory models of persistent unseen matter to distinguish visible and much smaller scales. Days 8 to 12 measure and graph conserved totals across heating, cooling and mixing in standard grams. Days 13 to 15 join particle models and quantity comparisons to explain boundary crossings and investigate anomalies against repeated patterns. Transfer: unfamiliar protective packing with an air cushion, a wax temperature pack and a dissolving insert.
> Carried: cause-effect begins on day 1 with added air; day 4 adds compression evidence and day 13 adds gas crossing boundaries. The particle-model begins on day 1, gains closer spacing from the syringe on day 3, gains dissolved matter on days 5 and 6, and reaches consensus on day 7. Scale-proportion begins on day 1; day 7 distinguishes grains from unseen parts. Data-graph begins on day 8; days 9 to 11 add paired bars and day 14 adds apparatus boundaries and uncertainty. Day 15 joins all four in a consensus used on day 16.
<!-- /skel -->

- **5-PS1-1, this section's share.** "Make a model showing that matter is made of bits too tiny to see." Days 1 to 4 carry the gas half: air is matter, made of parts too small to see, drawn as dots that can sit closer together or farther apart. The dissolved-matter half is section 1.2 (days 5 to 7). Children draw a model, test it against a reading or an observation, and revise it.
- **Limits that bite here.** No names for the tiny parts (not atoms, not molecules), no definition of them, and no account of how evaporation works. A dot stands for a part too small to see, never for a visible grain. Weight is read in grams on one scale; mass and weight are not told apart. Density and buoyancy are not taught. Students never aim a syringe at a face, and never look at the sun.
- **Connections.** 3.MD.A.2 (grams on one scale), days 1 and 4: children read and record whole grams, nothing is converted. 5-PS1-2 is first scored from day 8; here a reading is a record, not yet a graph. Day 4 sets up the weighed bean pots that unit 2 reuses, and days 3 and 4 start the monthly shadow record that units 2 and 4 reuse.
- **Practices.** Developing and Using Models: day 1 first drawing, day 3 revision against the syringe, day 4 revision against the squeezed bottle. Crosscutting concepts: cause and effect (day 1: pumping caused the gain) and scale (day 1 onward: parts too small to see).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The ball gains a few grams because air has weight; a kitchen scale reads whole grams, so trial the ball first and pump enough to show a clear gain. Squeezed air keeps the same number of dots in less space; it does not lose any. A drawing is a claim to test, not a photograph. Dry soil, not wet soil, is what a later comparison needs, because water is not soil.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
- Day 1: assessment "readiness" (Understanding day).
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1, the gas case (5-PS1-1), on day 16 (on the unit plan, s01 field 5). Nothing from days 1 to 4 is scored.
**Exit questions.** Tickets are named X1 to X4 here and in items.csv (E1 and E2 there are unit assessment items). Each takes four minutes, is unscored, uses a fresh case invented for practice, and is read aloud when needed. Letters refer to the unit's error register. If many (more than a third) miss one, the next teaching day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **X1.** A bottle that looks empty is pushed mouth down into water. Little water goes in. Tilted, it lets out bubbles and fills. (a) Draw what filled the bottle before it was tilted. (b) Plan 1 weighs a ball, pumps air in and weighs it again. Plan 2 weighs a flat ball and a different pumped ball. Finish: Plan ___ is fair because ___. Answer: air filled it. Weigh the same ball before and after the pumping. Dots are not required yet. Wrong: nothing was in it (A); weigh two unlike balls (D).
- **X2.** (a) Six flat card pieces that made a gate are rebuilt as a shelf. Draw the shelf with all six pieces. Finish: Stiff card helps because ___. (b) A damp cloth weighs 64 g and dries to 52 g: water ___ g, cloth itself ___ g. Answer: all six pieces appear in the shelf, and stiff card keeps the shelf boards from bending under the books. 12 g of water; 52 g for the cloth itself, so water is not counted as the cloth. Wrong: a piece is left out (J); "stiff is good" with no link to the job (K); the damp weight, 64 g, written as the cloth itself (H).
- **X3.** A book flattens a pouch of air. Puffed, it has 9 dots; a classmate draws the flat pouch with 5. (a) Draw the flat pouch; finish: Flat, it has ___ dots. (b) A stick moved to shade: finish: A stick moved to shade is unfair because ___. Answer: 9 dots, closer together. Same stick, same place, same clock time. Wrong: air vanished and came back (I); a moved or different stick (L).
- **X4.** A capped plastic bottle with only air in it gives a little when squeezed; its air is drawn as 8 dots in a wide box. (a) Draw the air while the bottle is squeezed. (b) Jo zeroes an empty jar, adds a stone (58 g), a cutting (60 g), then water (86 g). Added: stone ___ g, cutting ___ g, water ___ g. Answer: the same 8 dots, closer together in less space. Stone 58 g, cutting 2 g, water 26 g, each line what was added, not the running reading. Wrong: no air in the bottle (A); the running reading written as the amount added (H).

## 6. Days

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding; assessment: readiness. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Weigh a ball before and after pumping air and draw an initial explanation.
- note: Hook and question board first. Last 10 minutes: s2-pieces-new-object readiness; shape day 2. No-kit: photographed scale readings.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **12 min. Hook.** Students predict on paper whether a flat ball will be heavier, lighter or the same after pumping, and why. The teacher weighs the flat ball in grams, pumps it alone (needle attached by the teacher only, below the rated pressure, ball held in a tray, eye protection on), removes the needle and weighs again. Record both readings before anyone explains. Post the compelling question and begin the question board.
- **17 min. Draw and compare.** Each child draws a first account of what entered the ball and what made it heavier. Pairs compare; the class sees two competing drawings. The teacher then offers dots as one way to draw parts too small to see, and children add dots to their drawing without a name for them.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X1, before the readiness check.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Ball, pump and needle go to the teacher; the tray is wiped.
- **10 min. Readiness check.** Alone, on paper: the six card pieces (s01 field 4, item 1). The teacher sorts the sheets afterward for a missing-piece gap and a property-use gap, recorded separately.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a first drawing; the teacher holds the readings and the board. Tool: None. By hand: predicting, drawing, reading grams. Evidence: X1, and the readiness sheet.

## Day 2

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Use small-piece constructions to address readiness results and prepare soil to dry.
- note: One-lesson targeted s2-pieces-new-object reteach or model extension. Spread soil on trays for two school days before day 4.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **10 min. Support or extension.** Chosen from the day 1 sheet. Missing-piece gap: four minutes matching and counting pieces before and after a rebuild. Property-use gap: four minutes testing stiffness as a support. Both end with two minutes explaining a fresh rebuild. Ready students explain why blocks cannot show the true size of unseen parts.
- **16 min. Rebuild.** Groups of five make one construction from six large interlocking blocks, rebuild it as another, and track every piece into the new whole, saying one way a block's shape helps its new job. The teacher links it: parts are conserved when a whole changes, and blocks show parts and wholes, not unseen sizes.
- **10 min. Spread soil.** With the teacher, groups spread a thin layer of potting soil on a labelled, dated drying tray. Allergy check first, dust avoided, nonlatex gloves on, teacher handles the soil if needed. It dries for two school days before day 4.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X2.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** Blocks counted back to six, trays set aside, tables wiped, hands washed.

Who holds what is carried: the group holds the blocks and its drawing; the teacher holds the soil trays. Tool: None. By hand: rebuilding, spreading soil. Evidence: X2.

## Day 3

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Compare squeezed air in a closed syringe with the first particle drawings.
- note: First shadow measurement within this day; monthly records at the same clock time. No-kit: syringe photo sequence and shadow data.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **5 min. Syringe safety.** The teacher shows a needle-free syringe with its tip closed, presses gently away from faces, and says what is never done. Eye protection is on for every squeeze.
- **23 min. Squeeze and compare.** Groups of five, one closed syringe each. They read the marked volume, press gently, read it again, and draw dots for both states beside their day 1 drawing. The first two minutes reopen the day 1 drawing and ball readings and run the J or K repair if many made it. Planned question: "What evidence says the syringe was not empty?" Up to 5 of these minutes may use the particle viewer (key, show why, gas squeezed) if it exists; otherwise paper dot panels.
- **10 min. First shadow reading.** Supervised and outdoors. Six fixed sticks in sand tubs, chalk mark, metre stick; record the length, place and clock time. Never look at the sun. The same place and clock time are used each month.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X3.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Syringes returned closed, chalk and ruler collected, hands washed.

Who holds what is carried: the group holds the volume readings; the teacher holds the class model and the shadow record. Tool: particle viewer, key, show why, up to 5 minutes, spec only (optional). By hand: pressing, reading volume, measuring a shadow. Evidence: X3. No-kit: syringe photo sequence and shadow data.

## Day 4

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Revise the air model and plant beans in dry-weighed soil.
- note: First-week pot set-up for unit 2: tare pots and weigh dry soil and seeds; log water added in grams. Never substitute wet-soil weight. No-kit: dated pot records.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **45 min day, in parts.**
- **5 min. Compare drawings.** Side by side, the day 1 and day 3 air drawings, plus the five-minute repair for any error many made.
- **13 min. Revise the air model.** The teacher squeezes a capped plastic bottle with only air in it ( kept away from faces). Students revise their drawing, keeping the dot count and changing the spacing, and write the caption "I changed ___ because ___ showed ___."
- **18 min. Plant beans in weighed soil.** The teacher models taring (zeroing the scale with the empty pot on it). Groups confirm the soil is dry (crumbly, pale and not sticking to a clean dry finger; if in doubt use the prepared dry sample), weigh dry soil, then two untreated beans, then water added in grams, logging each on its own line. Wet-soil weight is never used. Teacher maintains the plants; nonlatex gloves on for soil; hands washed; allergy checks and dust avoided.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X4.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** Soil swept into the bin, pots labelled and set in the window, scale wiped, hands washed.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a revised drawing; the group holds the pot record. Tool: None. By hand: revising, taring, logging grams. Evidence: X4. If the soil is not dry, use a prepared dry sample or N4, labelled invented for practice.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 1, "What went into the ball?"; day 3, "What evidence says the syringe was not empty?"; day 4, "What stays the same when air is squeezed, and what changes?"
- Written task, day 4: the caption "I changed ___ because ___ showed ___" under the revised drawing, two to three sentences.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) dots in both states with the same count; (2) a cited reading or observation (grams, volume, a bottle that gives); (3) dots described as parts too small to see, not grains.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: air, weight, gram, squeeze, space, dot, too small to see, dry. Frame: "I changed ___ because ___ showed ___." Accept home language, labels or drawing with dictation.
- **Access.** Read scale and syringe marks aloud or enlarge them. Dot panels can be felt (raised dots). A student who cannot hold a syringe directs a partner. A seat that avoids dust on days 2 and 4.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each ticket aloud; answer by drawing, pointing or dictation to a scribe who adds no ideas.
- **Reading load.** Captions only, a few words each. The teacher reads every question and record aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Predict and draw what the dots do when the syringe is pulled wider, and say what evidence would test it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** An adult and one station: weigh a ball on a kitchen scale before and after pumping, if one is at hand; otherwise read N1 together. Draw, compare with a squeezed bottle, and log a pot in grams. Never require home equipment. Day 2's soil needs a tray and two days.
- **One projector.** Show the scale readings and the syringe photos on one screen; children draw on paper. Dot panels stay on paper.
