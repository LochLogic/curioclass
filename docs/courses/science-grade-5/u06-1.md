# Section 6.1: Following runoff and combining evidence

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 6, Protecting water by design. "How can a community keep its water clean, and how do we know our filter works?" Anchor: after a storm, brown runoff pours from a parking lot into a stream that a town downstream drinks from (days 100 to 117): see [s06.md](s06.md). **Days 100 to 102.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (100, 101), application 1 (102). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **5-ESS3-1** (days 100, 101, 102): Gather and combine information about how communities put science to work protecting Earth's resources and environment.
- skill s5-protect-resources (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 100 opens with a public runoff photograph and an original parking-lot-to-stream drawing, labelled "invented for practice". The downstream town takes stream water for treatment. Children notice the brown plume, draw routes and ask the compelling question before explanations.
> Model source comparison on 101 and a controlled-test record on 105. Then step back: pairs justify choices, retain inconvenient trials and ask what evidence would change their claims.
>
> The question board follows the evidence: Where does runoff go (100)? How do communities protect connected systems (101 and 102)? What can our filter achieve (103 and 104)? How can we compare fairly (105 to 108)? What should change (109 to 111)? What protection claim survives both sources and trials (112 and 113)? Day 114 transfers the reasoning to unfamiliar plans. Assessments introduce Philadelphia street runoff and unfamiliar runoff catchers.
> Three-dimensional goals: combine trustworthy information with human-impact ideas to explain interacting system parts (100 to 102); collaboratively test controlled designs to locate failures and compare solutions within limits (103 to 111); join both explanations (112 to 114).
> Carried: keep system-model and cause-effect, redrawn on 100; revise on 102 with watershed, wetland, air and space evidence and on 112 with filter limits. Keep data-graph: initial trial table on 105, revisions from measured repeats on 108 and 111. Design-cycle begins on 103, adds alternative predictions on 104, failure evidence on 108, a proposed change on 109 and retests on 110. All reach consensus on 113 and support the transfer tasks on 115 and 116. Unit 5's scale-proportion model rests.
<!-- /skel -->

**Share.** 5-ESS3-1: "Obtain and combine" information, using ESS3.C human impacts and systems and system models together. On 100 trace runoff; on 101 evaluate accounts; on 102 connect protection actions to affected parts. Include farming, industry and daily life, and land, plants, streams, ocean, air and near-Earth space. No treatment recipe or advocacy.

Review grade 4 u02-4's "natural resource: material or energy supply found in nature" and "source (a card or page that tells you something)". Review grade 2 u01-1's "runoff (rain that runs over the ground)". Grade 5 u05-1 watershed/system panels and u05-3 water stores are planned only: directly explain drainage, interacting parts and fresh-water access. Teach wetland as water-covered or water-saturated land for part of the year; protection functions require evidence.

**Connections.** W.5.8 source locations on 101; 5-ESS2-1 interactions on 100/102. Teacher pages distinguish authority, observations, projections and values. Evidence can test an action's effects; it cannot choose a community's values. An action must address the particular harm route.
