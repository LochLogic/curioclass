## 3. Checks, written first


**Assessment contribution:** Q1a/b, final teaching ownership u04-4. All E records invented for practice and withheld until individual paper work; one formative scale only.

- **E67.** "Invented for practice. Another school's fixed-stick shadow is 43 cm westward at 10:00. At 14:00, it is 27 cm eastward. Explain what changed. Propose an initial sun-direction model that could explain it." Key: Sixteen centimeters shorter and changed west to east. Apparent sun direction changes relative to ground, opposite shadow. Tentative spin accepted, not demanded. Wrong: A fixed stick's shadow never changes direction through a day. (error B).
  Shape: infer both changes and propose a model from two readings. Practice records separate marks/predictions, never a combined comparison explanation.
- **E68.** "Invented for practice. Hourly lengths at 10:00, noon and 14:00 are 46, 22 and 39 cm. Directions are west, north and east. Comparable monthly noon lengths are 26 and 41 cm. Display the hourly pattern. Compare monthly lengths in a separate display." Key: Hourly bars 46,22,39 cm and keyed W,N,E arrows: decrease then increase, direction changes. Monthly bars 26,41 cm: second fifteen longer. Use zero baselines, labels and units. Wrong: A fixed stick's shadow never changes direction through a day. (error B).
  Shape: construct hourly length/direction display beside monthly comparison. Practice constructs hourly length bars only; direction/monthly evidence stay in raw tables, never a combined display.
- **E69.** "Invented for practice. A shadow is 16 cm eastward, then 30 cm northeastward. Darkness follows without a sun-cast shadow. Explain the whole sequence using an Earth-motion model." Key: Spin changes apparent sun direction/height, changing and lengthening shadow. Site then turns out of sunlight. Yearly orbit alone cannot explain this daily sequence. Wrong: A fixed stick's shadow never changes direction through a day. (error B).
  Shape: explain an observational sequence crossing from changing shadows into darkness. Practice tests one observer's illumination state and records shadow direction separately; no full shadow-to-darkness log.
- **E70.** "Invented for practice. March, June and September daylight lasts 12, 14 and 12 hours. Graph daylight and darkness for these months. Describe the seasonal comparison." Key: Paired bars: daylight 12,14,12; darkness 12,10,12 hours. June has two more light and two fewer dark hours than either other month. Spin explains daily cycling; seasonal cause not demanded. Wrong: Every month has equal daylight duration. (error C).
  Shape: derive complements, graph paired monthly bars and compare. Practice graphs daylight alone; dark-hour reasoning uses a separate single-day strip, never paired monthly graphs.

Return E67 B to 68; E68 B to 69; E69 B/G to 70; E70 C/G to 71. Five minutes per letter; whole class if over ten of thirty miss. All receive private notes.
