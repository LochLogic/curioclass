**Assessment contribution:** Q1a/b, final teaching ownership u04-4. All E records invented for practice and withheld until individual paper work; one formative scale only.

- **E67.** Another school's fixed-stick shadow is 43 cm westward at 10:00 and 27 cm eastward at 14:00. "Explain what changed. Propose an initial sun-direction model that could explain it." Key: 16 cm shorter and different direction; apparent sun direction changed relative to ground, opposite shadow. Accept tentative spin, not demanded final cause. Wrong: fixed-stick shadows never change direction (B).
  Shape: infer both changes and propose a model from two readings. Practice records separate marks/predictions, never a combined comparison explanation.
- **E68.** New lengths at 10:00/noon/14:00 are 46/22/39 cm, directions west/north/east. Two comparable monthly noon lengths are 26/41 cm. "Display the hourly pattern. Compare the two monthly lengths in a separate display." Key: labelled length bars and time-keyed W/N/E arrows; decrease then increase, direction changes. Monthly bars 26/41, second 15 cm longer. Wrong: direction unchanged all day (B). Graph mistakes alone get descriptive feedback.
  Shape: construct hourly length/direction display beside monthly comparison. Practice constructs hourly length bars only; direction/monthly evidence stay in raw tables, never a combined display.
- **E69.** A withheld log shows a 16 cm eastward shadow, later 30 cm northeastward. Darkness follows without a sun-cast shadow. "Explain the whole sequence using an Earth-motion model." Key: spin changes apparent sun direction/height, shifting and lengthening shadow before site turns out of sunlight. Wrong: yearly orbit alone produces this daily sequence (G); fixed shadows never turn (B).
  Shape: explain an observational sequence crossing from changing shadows into darkness. Practice tests one observer's illumination state and records shadow direction separately; no full shadow-to-darkness log.
- **E70.** A second site's March/June/September daylight hours are 12/14/12. "Graph daylight and darkness. Describe the seasonal comparison." Key: darkness 12/10/12, paired bars labelled hours; June has two more light hours and two fewer dark hours. Daily alternation reflects spin; no seasonal mechanism demanded. Wrong: equal daylight every month (C); yearly orbit causes each daily light-dark cycle (G).
  Shape: derive complements, graph paired monthly bars and compare. Practice graphs daylight alone; dark-hour reasoning uses a separate single-day strip, never paired monthly graphs.

Return E67 B to 68; E68 B to 69; E69 B/G to 70; E70 C/G to 71. Five minutes per letter; whole class if over ten of thirty miss. All receive private notes.
