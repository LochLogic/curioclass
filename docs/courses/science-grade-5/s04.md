## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do falling objects, shadows and stars tell us about Earth's shape and motion?"
Day 62: compare a soft-ball drop, an Australia fall diagram labelled "invented for practice", and accumulated shadow marks. Ask the compelling question.
Day 78: explain both drops through gravity, daily shadows through spin, the changing night view through orbit, and brightness through distance. Describe monthly shadow changes. Cite observations separately from diagrams.
Model evidence selection on day 64 and graphing on day 68. Thereafter ask which record challenges an explanation; let children revise before offering consensus.

Q1 records describe a different northern midlatitude site. Its fixed stick gives these readings in local solar time: 09:00, 54 cm toward northwest; 12:00, 18 cm north; 15:00, 54 cm northeast. The same site's rounded daylight and darkness hours are January 10 and 14, April 13 and 11, July 15 and 9, October 11 and 13. All pairs total 24. Four clear nights per listed month are observed at the same clock time and place: selected star group A is visible on 4, 4, 0 and 0 nights respectively; group B on 0, 0, 4 and 4. Visibility means above the unobstructed horizon at that observation time; zero does not mean a star ceased to exist. Separate moon records show a change of position relative to background stars across a week and a return near the first star group about a month later. The moon can also be visible in daytime.
