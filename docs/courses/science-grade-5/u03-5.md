- **R61-P, properties (A).** "Reference A is blue, bends and absorbs water. B is blue, stiff and repels water. C is blue, bends and repels water. Unknown N is blue, bends and repels water. All received 5 mL water for two minutes." Ask: "Identify N's matching reference. Explain using two distinguishing test results."
  Key: C, bend and repel; A differs in absorption, B in stiffness. Wrong: "All match because blue" (A).
  Shape: identify one new packing sample with two positive matches. Repair only locates one supplied component; no full identification response or these records.
- **R61-Q, quantities (B/D).** "A display shows 31 beside a new bead sample's weight. Its scale is marked g. A ruler shows the container's length as 7 cm." Ask: "Complete the labelled weight record. Explain whether 7 cm describes hardness."
  Key: bead sample 31 g; 7 cm describes container length, not hardness. Wrong: "31 needs no unit" (B); "Hardness is 7 cm" (D).
  Shape: complete a quantity record and distinguish its meaning. Repair only locates labels on other complete records, never fills this one.
- **R61-M, unseen material (F).** "A clear rinse moves from cup A into cup B. Nothing spills. A model leaves cup B empty." Ask: "Repair the model to show where the rinse material went."
  Key: material moves into B, including unseen dissolved material if recorded; clear does not mean empty. Do not add unsupported identities. Wrong: "It vanished when clear" (F).
  Shape: repair a transferred-liquid location model. Repair practice points to one correct material label, never completes a transfer diagram.
- **R61-I, causes (I).** "Three mixed-gel cups warm more than separate-gel controls. Mixed cups began warmer and contained larger portions. Starting properties were not recorded." Ask: "Explain whether this evidence isolates mixing as the cause. Name one needed repair."
  Key: no; match starting temperature or portions, and record starting properties before a controlled repeat. One named repair does not claim every flaw fixed. Wrong: "Several changes still isolate mixing" (I).
  Shape: judge a new gel comparison with one warranted repair. Repair locates isolated fixed conditions, never this confounded-record explanation. Other errors receive descriptive feedback unless they match a register letter.

- **R61-C, metal comparison (C).** "Reference metal A conducts electricity but is not pulled by a magnet. Reference metal B conducts electricity and is pulled. Sample Z conducts electricity but is not pulled." Ask: "Explain why Z cannot be rejected as metal from its magnet result."
  Key: the supplied nonmagnetic metal A is a counterexample; Z still needs further identification evidence. Wrong: "Every metal must be pulled" (C).
  Shape: explain a category counterexample using new recorded Z/A evidence. Repair only locates magnet-result components, never this full counterexample response.
- **R61-E, mixing claim (E).** "Two dry colored granule samples are mixed. Only a photograph of the combined pile is recorded." Ask: "Does mixing alone establish that a new substance formed? Explain what evidence is missing."
  Key: no; need starting/resulting properties and controlled comparisons, not the act of mixing alone. Wrong: "Every mixture makes new substance" (E).
  Shape: evaluate an unsupported granule-mixing claim. Repair locates one property component, never this claim-and-missing-evidence response.
- **R61-G, bubble claim (G).** "A pump pushes air into plain water. Bubbles stop when pumping stops. A note calls the water a newly formed substance." Ask: "Evaluate the note using the record."
  Key: supplied air explains bubbles; the record does not establish newly formed water or another substance. Wrong: "Any bubble proves a new substance" (G).
  Shape: evaluate a claim using an external-air source record. Repair locates observed labels only, never this pump-water explanation.
- **R61-H, method limit (H).** "A cloth does not separate two clear liquids. No starting or resulting property tests were recorded." Ask: "Judge whether the cloth's failure establishes new-substance formation."
  Key: no, the method may not separate existing materials; need controlled property evidence. Wrong: "Any failed separation proves formation" (H).
  Shape: judge the reach of one method on new liquid records. Repair points to one unsupported inference, never this full judgment.
