# Course frame: Science Grade 5

Course id science-k5 (grade 5 row), grade 5, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6: matter too small to see, where a tree's matter comes from and goes, mystery materials, Earth and
  sky, Earth's systems and water, then protecting water by design.
- **Days.** 120 teaching days: 111 in units and 9 at course level (6 for three course-level checks, 3 respond
  days). Elementary science shares the day, so the course assumes one science lesson of 45 minutes on four days a
  week (180 minutes a week) across 30 weeks (a school year of about 36 weeks, of which about 30 carry science
  lessons here; the rest hold breaks, the school's own testing and events). Science day n falls near school day 4n/3; the cross-subject calendar uses both. The year owns
  14 rows (14 performance expectations, 3-5-ETS1-3 among them). Several rows need investigations that run for days
  or weeks (a sealed jar ecosystem, a shadow record, weighed soil), and the last row is the band's full design
  cycle with repeated trials, so about 111 days in units fits; the full 160-day yardstick is not needed.
- **Standard.** The grade 5 performance expectations of data/standards/ngss-pes.txt, each taught in full, plus
  3-5-ETS1-3, completed here (grade 4 started it). The bar is docs/RUBRIC-SCIENCE.md. Practices and crosscutting
  concepts are named per unit in the C3 column (see "For the lead"). Plain facts (BASELINE rule 5, values rule
  b): how communities protect resources is taught from backed sources, with no cause pushed on students.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"): the elementary
  units in the source store are NonCommercial and reference only. Phenomena are chosen to be real, observable or
  measurable in class where possible, puzzling at grade 5 and explainable with the unit's core ideas; each unit
  names its anchor, and a teacher may swap in a local phenomenon that needs the same ideas (the class's own
  watershed in unit 6). Investigations are our own designs. Data and pictures come from open and public-domain
  material only: USGS water data and the water-distribution figures, NOAA and the National Weather Service for
  day length and climate, NASA images and star data, EPA pages on watersheds and runoff, public-domain historical
  accounts (the tree-in-a-pot experiment of the 1600s), and open simulations released before 2026-03-29. Curio
  Class's own online tools serve as simulations where a class cannot run the investigation (listed per unit,
  built later).
- **Materials.** Classroom-cheap: a kitchen scale reading to the gram, zip bags, balloons, plastic syringes without
  needles, a ball and pump, sugar, salt, baking soda, cornstarch, vinegar, steel wool, magnets, a battery-and-bulb
  tester, clear jars, soil, seeds, a stick and chalk for shadows, flashlights, sand, gravel, cloth and coffee
  filters. Every investigation names a household substitute where safe and a no-kit path where not (a class data
  set or a Curio Class simulation), said plainly to be weaker (PRODUCTION-PATH: substitutes, then no-kit). Every
  simulation has a paper path (the same data on a printed sheet) and needs no student account. Each unit plan
  states its cost.
- **Safety.** A course safety page for the year: eye protection for any mixing or fizzing; kitchen-safe substances
  only, never household cleaners; no tasting in the lab (the teacher does any taste demonstration with food-safe
  materials, or it is described); seal only with a balloon or loose cap; never look at the sun; filtered water is
  never drunk; gloves for muddy or runoff water; allergy checks for plants, soil and mold; hand washing. Every
  hands-on day carries its own Safety and set-up section.
- **Long investigations.** Three run in the background of other lessons. A shadow record, one measurement a month
  from the first month, read in unit 4, always at the same sun time: 1 pm while clocks are on daylight saving time,
  12 noon after they change back (or at the time the National Weather Service gives as solar noon for the town).
  Weighed pots, set up in the first week of the year: soil dried on trays for two days, weighed dry into each pot,
  fast-growing beans planted; at the end of unit 2 (about science day 41) the plant is lifted out and weighed, and
  the soil is dried and weighed again: the plant has gained many grams, the dry soil has lost about none. Sealed
  jar ecosystems, set up in unit 2 and watched for weeks.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor and builds a question board; each lesson set answers a question
  the last one raised. The particle model of unit 1 is the year's spine: it makes air a kind of matter (unit 2),
  explains what changes in a mixture (unit 3) and gives water a size scale (unit 5). Each unit has an initial
  model, named revision points and a consensus model; the end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has
  not met.
- **How science works.** Taught on purpose in unit 2 (the tree-in-a-pot experiment: a careful measurement
  overturned what people believed about soil, and later evidence corrected its own conclusion that the matter came
  from water alone) and unit 6 (repeated trials, because one result can mislead).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 6 (water that is unsafe or scarce,
  which some students' families live with) is named in its row. Every hands-on unit needs a science teacher's read
  of safety and materials before the course is called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3); who, and any pay, is Cory's.
- **Reading level.** Science texts are short (two to three pages), written at a grade 4 to 5 band with diagrams and
  data; words are taught when the class needs them.
