Four components, four points each.
  Shape: independently design a temperature/solid-amount experiment, interpret new clear-solution evidence, judge a confound and model with a limit. No practice uses clear-solution precipitation or that integrated package.

**Daily paper:** E56/E57 notebooks, E58 exit ticket; four minutes. Invented for practice, held unseen. Formative scale only: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

- **E56.** Invented for practice: Two clear rinses pass through identical filters into labeled dishes. The rinses supposedly contain equal amounts of the same dissolved material. After drying, dish A holds residue; dish B looks empty. Explain what these conflicting records establish. Name a check before revising the change model.
  Key: A supports recovery of dissolved material; B remains unresolved. Neither disappearance nor new formation follows from B. Check spills, retained liquid, incomplete drying or unequal starting portions; then repeat under matched conditions.
  Wrong: "Dissolved material vanished from dish B." (F); "No recovery once proves new material." (H)
  Shape: interpret conflicting clear-rinse separation repeats and choose a diagnostic check. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.

- **E57.** Invented for practice: Three damp scraps changed surface color; dry controls did not. Their material identities were not recorded. Another liquid bubbled briefly when shaken. Distinguish what each record supports about material change.
  Key: damp/dry contrast supports moisture-linked surface change, not specifically rust/new substance; brief shaking bubbles can be introduced air. Neither identifies a new substance. Child must distinguish evidential strength, not choose by elimination.
  Wrong: "Any bubble proves a new substance" (G)
  Shape: recognize evidential limits across unspecified scraps and shaking records. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.

- **E58.** Invented for practice: A white powder is named from color alone. A mixture is called new because a spoon cannot separate it. Revise both claims using property evidence and a controlled change model.
  Key: withhold powder identity pending distinguishing measured/reference properties; a spoon's failure cannot decide, so model starting/resulting material and seek controlled repeated property comparisons. More than one valid proposal.
  Wrong: "White proves its identity" (A); "Failure to spoon apart proves new substance" (H)
  Shape: repair one identification claim AND one separation claim through models. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.
