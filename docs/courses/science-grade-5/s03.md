## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we tell substances apart, and how do we know when mixing makes a new one?"
Day 45: inspect four coded powders beside a drawing of unlabelled kitchen jars. Appearance cannot settle identity. Ask the compelling question; laboratory samples never enter food.
Day 58: explain powder identities using property tables, then distinguish mixing from new-substance formation using controlled trials, separation and rusting evidence.
Model recording on day 46 and a controlled comparison on day 52. Thereafter ask what comparison challenges a claim; fade sentence frames.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


Question board: Which properties distinguish lookalikes (45 to 48)? Can the method identify minerals and explain warmth transfer (49 to 51)? Does changed appearance mean new material (52 to 53)? What do repeated mixing and separation establish (54 to 57)? What explains all records (58)? Post evidence beside answers; each uncertainty launches the next set. Transfer: an unfamiliar repair-kit inventory and solid formation from clear solutions.
Three-dimensional goals: observe and measure identifying properties in standard units (45 to 51); investigate new-substance claims through controlled repeated comparisons that separate causes (52 to 58).
Limits: no density, particle names, atomic mechanisms or distinction between mass and weight. No engineering expectation. Reversibility or one visible sign cannot settle identity. Acid clumps existing milk protein; curds alone do not prove new material.
Carried: data-graph and patterns start in day 45's property model; dissolving (47), magnetism and conduction (48 to 50) revise its tests; day 51 establishes consensus. Particle-model and cause-effect return on day 52: unseen material persists despite changed appearance. Fizzing (54), separation (56) and repeated rusting comparisons (57) revise before-and-after diagrams and causal claims without defining particles. Day 58 joins all four models; days 59 and 60 use them on new cases.

Q2 records, invented for practice: powder candidates salt and sugar are white. All powder tests use 8 g in 20 mL water at 22 degrees Celsius, stirred for two minutes and observed after five minutes. Sugar dissolves completely; salt leaves some solid. Powder P dissolves completely. Metal references: copper is reddish and nonmagnetic; aluminium is silver and nonmagnetic; steel is silver and magnetic. All three conduct electricity and transfer warmth. Metal M is shiny, silver, magnetic and conducts both. Pale gypsum is scratched by a fingernail; pale calcite is not. Pale specimen R is scratched. Hardness is an ordered comparison, not centimeters. Water mixes into more water; oil forms a separate layer. Liquid L is pale and clear in its sealed vial; its supplied water-mixing record shows two layers. Keys: P sugar, M steel, R gypsum, L oil. Require color, hardness, shine, magnetism, dissolving and supplied heat and electrical conduction in the completed property table. None requires density. Actual length and weight establish labeled measurements, not properties sufficient to identify a substance alone.

Q3 quantities: use 15 mL water per cup; each treatment also receives 1 g salt.

Q4 records, invented for practice, paper only: two white powders each dissolve completely when 1 g is stirred into 40 mL water, producing clear solutions X and Y. In three trials, mix 12 mL X with 12 mL Y. Controls mix 12 mL X with 12 mL water, or 12 mL Y with 12 mL water. All start at 22 degrees Celsius, receive 20 seconds of stirring and are observed after five minutes. Each treatment forms a lasting white solid; controls stay clear. Brief pouring bubbles occur in every cup. Filtering collects the treatment solid. Equal small portions of each starting powder and the collected solid are stirred separately into 10 mL fresh water for two minutes: both starting powders dissolve, but the collected solid remains. An extra trial doubles Y's volume and warms both liquids; more solid appears. These constructed records support reasoning, not empirical proof of a class result. Separate follow-up, paper only: new clear solutions U and V may form a solid when mixed. Plan an investigation of whether their starting temperature changes the amount of solid formed. No procedure or results are supplied; choose measurements, controls and repetitions.
Key: repeated controlled evidence and the different dissolving property support new-substance formation, without establishing the solid's identity. Filtering separates the resulting solid; it does not show that the solid existed before mixing. Pouring bubbles alone do not support reaction. The extra trial cannot isolate the effects of volume and warmth. Draw unseen dissolved material before mixing and resulting solid afterwards, without defining particles or naming atoms; material did not disappear.
Q4 planning key: compare U and V mixtures at two starting temperatures, fixing both solution amounts, stirring and observation interval; collect, dry identically and weigh solid in grams across repeated trials. Accept other fair measurable plans. Award one point each for varying only starting temperature, fixing relevant quantities and handling, measuring solid amount with units, and justifying repeated trials. For each other four-point component, give two for accuracy and two for evidence-linked explanation or model. Award partial credit component by component.

Each assessment day reserves 28 minutes for six groups of five: four observed minutes per group plus four transition minutes. Each child gets a labeled turn; peers inspect and record. Waiting groups independently organize the supplied records. The final 17 uninterrupted minutes are for Q2 or Q4, making 45 minutes total. Stage directions, portions and instruments beforehand; collection and wiping use two transition minutes, station movement the other two. No reteaching occurs. The day 59 observation checks measurement and identification; day 60 checks collaborative controlled trials, a different performance. Day 58 notebooks remain unscored teaching evidence and are retained without recollection. No earlier product contributes points. On each day 16 of 20 points require practice with a core idea on a new case, reaching the unit's 80 percent target. Q2 uses property evidence and standard quantities; Q4 uses controlled causes, changed properties and explanatory models.

Look-fors are the scored components above; spelling and drawing style do not earn points.
- Q1 meets: actual labeled gram and centimeter readings, obtained personally, plus a valid property observation. Does not: copied measurements with no personal measuring. Use actual values, not an expected class answer.
- Q2 meets: "P matches sugar at this amount; salt leaves solid. M is silver and magnetic, unlike copper or aluminium, and conduction supports the metal comparison. R is scratched, unlike calcite. L forms layers, matching oil despite being clear." Does not: "White means salt; shine identifies every metal." Full credit requires all four identities with discriminating comparisons.
- Q3 meets: "Only salt addition differs. We fix water, stirring and time and repeat three pairs so one odd result does not decide. I stirred trial two and recorded its before-and-after evidence." Does not: "My partner did everything; one clear cup proves reaction." Record the child's actual action and explanation.
- Q4 meets: "Only X with Y forms the solid across three trials. Unlike both starting powders, that solid does not dissolve in the matched test, supporting a new substance. Filtering removes the resulting solid; it does not undo its formation. The extra trial changes two conditions, so neither cause is isolated. My model keeps material present before and after; these records do not identify the solid." Does not: "Filtering proves it was only a mixture, and more Y alone caused more solid." Full credit also needs both diagrams and an independent U-and-V plan: for example, mix 6 mL of each at 20 or 30 degrees Celsius, stir for 30 seconds, wait seven minutes, filter and dry identically, then weigh each solid in grams; repeat three times to check consistency.
