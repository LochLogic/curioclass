# Section 1.3: Weigh the whole system

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 1, Matter too small to see. "What is everything made of, if we cannot see the pieces, and does any of it vanish when it changes?" Anchor: a flat ball weighed, pumped up with air, and weighed again: it is heavier, though all we added was air we cannot see (days 1 to 17): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 8 to 11.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (8), application 3 (9, 10, 11). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 3 of 4 in the unit; the unit plan holds its section fields as U3 to U11 (a one-section unit until the 2026-11-16 cuts).
- Day length (frames.csv): 45 minutes.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **5-PS1-2** (days 8, 9, 10, 11): Measure and graph to show that when substances are heated, cooled or mixed, the total weight stays the same whatever the change.
- skill s5-conserve-weight (grade 5)

- **Limits/connections.** Whole grams only; no conversions, particle names, microscopic state-change mechanism or mass/weight distinction. - **Teacher explanation.** Equal whole-gram readings mean no detected change at this instrument's step, not proof of exact equality. Compare complete apparatus, including initially separate ingredients. Never erase surprising results. Teach graph axes and component bars before testing.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** preparation for Q2 and Q3, heating, cooling and mixing; section 1.4 administers them on day 16. Q4 uses these measurement controls. Tickets are formative paper records for all thirty: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Use only this marking scale on that record. Absent practical evidence stays blank with its reason. Every supplied numerical case below is invented for practice, printed and said aloud as such. Keys are teacher only.

- **X8, day 8.** "A holder tips a soluble tablet into water. Plan a comparison using one whole-gram scale. Draw everything you would weigh twice, and explain your boundary."
  **Key:** cup, water, tablet and holder included before and after on the same scale. The boundary includes the initially separate tablet and retains every object. Accept an equivalent complete-tray arrangement. Wrong: omit holder afterward (D); omit tablet initially (D).
  **Shape:** generate a two-stage weighing-boundary drawing and justify it for an unseen tablet arrangement. Practice inventories physical objects and orders written action strips, never draws paired weighing plans. No tablet or release holder appears in teaching.
- **X9, day 9.** "A sealed butter pack melts on its tray. The complete tray and pack read 92 g before and after. Melted butter alone reads 80 g. Make a labeled evidence graph that tests whether melting lost matter."
  **Key:** equal before/after bars at 92 g; axis in grams from zero. Annotation includes tray and pack twice and no detected total change. Wrong: use 80 g after (D); invent a gain because warming creates matter (E); label 92 g on a bar ending at 82 g (F).
  **Shape:** select comparable totals from complete and contents-only records, then make an annotated graph. Practice graphs authentic complete-system readings without competing contents-only values. No butter/tray record is taught. This new total-weight case differs from grade 2's butter reversibility assessment.
- **X10, day 10.** "A pouch and sleeve weigh 110 g before freezing. Afterward, the frozen pouch weighs 100 g; its unchanged sleeve weighs 10 g. Graph comparable totals and judge whether cooling removed matter."
  **Key:** after total 100 + 10 = 110 g; equal labeled 110 g bars and no detected loss. Wrong: after total 100 g (D); freezing removed matter (E); plotted height contradicts its value (F).
  **Shape:** reconstruct the total from separated after-readings, graph and judge it. Practice never separates components, supplies a missing total or uses sleeves.
- **X11, day 11.** "A clear sugar mixture has two records. Sealed apparatus reads 98 g before stirring and 98 g after. Poured-out liquid alone reads 86 g. Make a graph explaining which record tests matter loss and where the sugar remains."
  **Key:** complete-apparatus record, equal 98 g bars, dissolved sugar retained unseen in the liquid. Wrong: sugar vanished (C); use 86 g after (D); endpoints contradict labels (F).
  **Shape:** evaluate conflicting boundaries and graph an explanation of invisible material. Practice uses one complete salt record, component graphing and oral evidence pointing, never rival sugar records.
