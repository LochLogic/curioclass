## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share.** 5-PS1-2: "Measure and graph" conserved total weight through heating, cooling and mixing. Mathematics joins PS1.A/PS1.B retained matter with scale, proportion and quantity: standard grams describe the same complete system twice. Day 11 also applies 5-PS1-1's dissolved-matter model.
- **Limits/connections.** Whole grams only; no conversions, particle names, microscopic state-change mechanism or mass/weight distinction. Review grade 2's bar graph: one bar for each design, bars start at the bottom line (science-grade-2 u02-2 day 19). Here labels become before/after and squares represent grams, taught day 8. 3.MD.A.2 supports readings; 5.MD.A.1 waits for mathematics unit 8.
- **Teacher explanation.** Equal whole-gram readings mean no detected change at this instrument's step, not proof of exact equality. Compare complete apparatus, including initially separate ingredients. Never erase surprising results. Teach graph axes and component bars before testing.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** preparation for Q2 and Q3, heating, cooling and mixing; section 1.4 administers them on day 16. Q4 uses these measurement controls. Tickets are formative paper records for all thirty: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Use only this marking scale on that record. Absent practical evidence stays blank with its reason. Every supplied numerical case below is invented for practice, printed and said aloud as such. Keys are teacher only.

- **X8, day 8.** "Invented for practice. A holder tips a soluble tablet into water. Plan a comparison using one whole-gram scale. Draw everything you would weigh twice, and explain your boundary."
  **Key:** cup, water, tablet and holder included before and after on the same scale. The boundary includes the initially separate tablet and retains every object. Accept an equivalent complete-tray arrangement. Wrong: omit holder afterward (D); omit tablet initially (D).
  **Shape:** generate a two-stage weighing-boundary drawing and justify it for an unseen tablet arrangement. Practice inventories physical objects and orders written action strips, never draws paired weighing plans. No tablet or release holder appears in teaching.
- **X9, day 9.** "Invented for practice. Solid pellets melt inside a sealed pack on a tray. Complete tray and pack: 92 g before; 92 g after. Melted contents alone: 80 g. Make a labeled evidence graph testing whether melting lost matter."
  **Key:** equal before/after bars at 92 g; axis in grams from zero. Annotation includes tray and pack twice and no detected total change. Wrong: use 80 g after (D); invent a gain because warming creates matter (E); label 92 g on a bar ending at 82 g (F).
  **Shape:** select comparable totals from complete and contents-only records, then make an annotated graph. Practice graphs authentic complete-system readings without competing contents-only values. No pellet-pack/tray record is taught. This avoids grade 2's held butter case.
- **X10, day 10.** "Invented for practice. A pouch and sleeve weigh 110 g before freezing. Afterward, the frozen pouch weighs 100 g. Its unchanged sleeve weighs 10 g. Graph comparable totals and judge whether cooling removed matter."
  **Key:** after total 100 + 10 = 110 g; equal labeled 110 g bars and no detected loss. Wrong: after total 100 g (D); freezing removed matter (E); plotted height contradicts its value (F).
  **Shape:** reconstruct the total from separated after-readings, graph and judge it. Practice never separates components, supplies a missing total or uses sleeves.
- **X11, day 11.** "Invented for practice. A clear sugar mixture has two records. Sealed apparatus: 98 g before stirring; 98 g after. Poured-out liquid alone: 86 g. Make an evidence graph testing matter loss and locating the sugar."
  **Key:** complete-apparatus record, equal 98 g bars, dissolved sugar retained unseen in the liquid. Wrong: sugar vanished (C); use 86 g after (D); endpoints contradict labels (F).
  **Shape:** evaluate conflicting boundaries and graph an explanation of invisible material. Practice uses one complete salt record, component graphing and oral evidence pointing, never rival sugar records.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Review system: the parts of one plant work together (science-grade-1 u02-1 day 11); extend linked parts to weighed objects without reusing the plant case. Children retain consensus model 1 beside raw records; teacher keeps the question board. Complete-system boundaries and paired gram bars extend data-graph on days 8 to 11. Review fair test: keep the same, change one thing, watch one thing (science-grade-3 u01-2 day 6).
- **Instrument.** Ideal: visible stable scale with a 1 g step. Household scale partly fits: it cannot resolve subgram changes. Copy each reading as displayed with date and apparatus. Rezero/check and retry once if unstable; retain both. Leak, spill, failure to melt/freeze or missing kit invokes N8, N9 or N10 from s01 U6, explicitly invented for practice. Handling remains unobserved. No digital or key tool.
- **Fallback.** N7 supplies a drawn inventory. Use original diagrams rather than promised photographs. Every child can reason and graph on paper.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **Language/access.** Words: total, gram, boundary, before, after. Frame: "We included ___ both times, so ___." Enlarge displays and axes; supply raised baseline and bars. A student directs a partner's handling; record only observed actions as practical evidence.
- **Checks.** Read records aloud without explaining the comparison. Accept dictated annotations, tactile bars or a scribe. Keep the reasoning goal. DEFAULTS.md holds page/type caps; printed sentences have at most 15 words.
- **Extension.** Explain why equal whole-gram readings cannot rule out a smaller change.
