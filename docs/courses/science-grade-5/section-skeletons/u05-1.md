> Day 89, each group of five: one shallow plastic tray, two cups clean damp sand, one cup water, pouring cup, towels, five splash goggles and five nonlatex glove pairs. Reuse kitchen trays and cups. The day before, the teacher wets sand, checks eyewash access, stabilizes trays and rehearses a small pour. Dust, splashes, ingestion and slippery spills are hazards: no blowing or tasting; goggles and gloves stay on through cleanup; contain pours and wipe spills. Stop for unsafe handling or missing protection. Only the teacher portions dry sand and disposes of sediment in solid waste. ### U3. Checks during teaching

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Notebook evidence appears in U6. The section exit is on day 97, separate from its rehearsed anchor explanation.
> Invented for practice: a dune's rooted patch retains sand while its bare patch loses sand. Draw and explain a land-and-life model using these observations. Then explain whether equal-height bars showing 2 of 100 all-water shares and 2 of 100 fresh-water shares represent equal water amounts. Answer: roots hold land material; equal shares of different wholes need not be equal amounts, and the fresh-water whole is smaller. Wrong: disconnected labels (A), treating the fresh-water whole as all water (E). Give five minutes, unaided.
