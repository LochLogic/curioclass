## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **5-PS1-4** (days 56, 57, 58): Investigate whether mixing two or more substances makes a new substance.
- **5-PS1-3** (days 58): Observe and measure properties to tell what a material is, such as a powder, metal, mineral or liquid.
- skill s5-new-substances (grade 5)
- skill s5-identify-materials (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 58: explain powder identities using property tables, then distinguish mixing from new-substance formation using controlled trials, separation and rusting evidence.
>
> Question board: Which properties distinguish lookalikes (45 to 48)? Can the method identify minerals and explain warmth transfer (49 to 51)? Does changed appearance mean new material (52 to 53)? What do repeated mixing and separation establish (54 to 57)? What explains all records (58)? Post evidence beside answers; each uncertainty launches the next set. Transfer: an unfamiliar repair-kit inventory and solid formation from clear solutions.
> Three-dimensional goals: observe and measure identifying properties in standard units (45 to 51); investigate new-substance claims through controlled repeated comparisons that separate causes (52 to 58).
> Carried: data-graph and patterns start in day 45's property model; dissolving (47), magnetism and conduction (48 to 50) revise its tests; day 51 establishes consensus. Particle-model and cause-effect return on day 52: unseen material persists despite changed appearance. Fizzing (54), separation (56) and repeated rusting comparisons (57) revise before-and-after diagrams and causal claims without defining particles. Day 58 joins all four models; days 59 and 60 use them on new cases.
