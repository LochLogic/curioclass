# Unit 1: Matter too small to see

Course `science-grade-5` (Science Grade 5). **Days 1 to 17** (17 course days; school days 2 to 20 (the course meets about 4 a week, so not every school day in the span), weeks 1 to 4). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 4); 1.2 (days 5 to 7); 1.3 (days 8 to 11); 1.4 (days 12 to 17). Days by kind: understanding 6, fluency 2, application 7, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Teaching groups: days 1 to 6, Finding unseen matter; days 7 to 12, Accounting for the whole; days 13 to 17, Testing and transferring the explanation.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What is everything made of, if we cannot see the pieces, and does any of it vanish when it changes?"
Day 1: predict, then watch the teacher weigh a flat ball, pump it and weigh it again. Record both readings before explaining the increase. Ask the compelling question.
Day 15: students write how added air explains the ball's increase and account for matter through changes, citing compression, recovered salt and complete-system graphs.
Model recording evidence on days 2 and 8. Invite competing drawings, then introduce particles. Thereafter ask what evidence requires revision. Students choose fair comparisons on day 12.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


Question board: What entered the ball (days 1 to 4)? Where is dissolved material (5 to 7)? How could weighing test its presence (8 to 11)? Why might readings fall (12 to 14)? What explanation fits all records (15)? Move answers beside evidence. Each answer raises the next question. Three-dimensional goals: days 1 to 7 use explanatory models of persistent unseen matter to distinguish visible and much smaller scales. Days 8 to 12 measure and graph conserved totals across heating, cooling and mixing in standard grams. Days 13 to 15 join particle models and quantity comparisons to explain boundary crossings and investigate anomalies against repeated patterns. Transfer: unfamiliar protective packing with an air cushion, a wax temperature pack and a dissolving insert.
Limits: no particle names, atomic reactions, density, mass versus weight, or microscopic evaporation mechanism. Dots represent unseen parts, not visible grains. Engineering design is not claimed.
Carried: cause-effect begins on day 1 with added air; day 4 adds compression evidence and day 13 adds gas crossing boundaries. The particle-model begins on day 1, gains closer spacing from the syringe on day 3, gains dissolved matter on days 5 and 6, and reaches consensus on day 7. Scale-proportion begins on day 1; day 7 distinguishes grains from unseen parts. Data-graph begins on day 8; days 9 to 11 add paired bars and day 14 adds apparatus boundaries and uncertainty. Day 15 joins all four in a consensus used on day 16.

Teacher look-fors and samples:
- Q1 meets: "Dots stand for parts smaller than grains. The cushion retains its parts in less space; the solid's parts spread through the water. Becoming clear does not prove disappearance." Does not: "Nothing remains inside either." Judge each case separately using its four components.
- Q2 meets: "I zeroed the scale. The whole dry pouch settled at 104 g on two readings, matching the earlier 104 g. No change was detected within the scale's whole-gram resolution; the readings support retained matter through warming." Does not: "I weighed only the water." The sample value is invented for practice; score actual readings and sound interpretation, not exact agreement.
- Q3 meets: three labelled equal-height pairs and "Heating gives 122 + 12 = 134 g and mixing gives 130 + 15 = 145 g afterward. The cooling bars must both reach 138 g: smaller wax volume does not lower the recorded total. Matter stays inside through all three changes." Does not: higher after-bars and "Warming adds matter." Judge each pair and its explanation separately.
- Q4 meets: "The check weight also fell 3 g, so the scale may have shifted. Zero it, verify the check weight and reweigh the whole pouch." Does not: "A lower number proves gas escaped."
