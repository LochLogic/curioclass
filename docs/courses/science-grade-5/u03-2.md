## 1. Standards and practices


**Share:** 5-PS1-3, "Observe and measure": compare metals/liquids, mineral scratch behavior and heat transfer, then consolidate identifying properties. Planning investigations joins PS1.A with standard units and patterns. Use vocabulary previewed on 46. No density or mass/weight distinction.

Adult explanation: magnetism is not a universal metal property. A failed circuit is not evidence of a nonconductor until a known conductor works. Scratch resistance is comparative, unlike measured length. Warmth moves from warmer water through a sample; dimensions and exposure affect comparisons. Mineral here means solid specimen, not unit 2's nutrient sense. No identification from size alone.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none; 3.4 owns teaching for Q1/Q2. E48 is a paper exit ticket; E49/E50/E51 are individual paper notebook evidence, four minutes each. One formative scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. All records below invented for practice, unseen until collection.

- **E48.** Invented for practice: Sample S is silver, nonmagnetic and conducts electricity. Liquid V is clear and pale. Explain what these observations establish. Describe a further comparison that could distinguish remaining identities.
  Key: S can be a nonmagnetic conductor; not every metal is magnetic. Neither sample has a unique identity from these observations. Compare S's further properties with candidate references; compare supplied V-water mixing records with references, never immerse the tester. Several defensible comparisons.
  Wrong: "S cannot be metal because it is nonmagnetic" (C); "Clear means water" (A)
  Shape: explain two samples' limited evidence AND propose comparisons. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.

- **E49.** Invented for practice: Pale mineral J scratches under a fingernail and measures 5 cm. Pale mineral K resists that scratch and measures 2 cm. A pale unknown scratches under a fingernail and measures 4 cm. Which reference matches the unknown's hardness? What does its 4 cm measurement describe?
  Key: J; 4 cm is length, not hardness.
  Wrong: "Its hardness is 4 cm" (D); "Both match because both are pale" (A)
  Shape: match a scratch reference AND interpret length. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.

- **E50.** Invented for practice: two equal display-rack bars contact a warm plate. Both start at 21 degrees Celsius. After equal contact times, their free ends read 29 and 23 degrees Celsius. Explain the warmth transfer shown. Name conditions that must match for a material comparison.
  Key: The first bar warmed more, consistent with greater warmth transfer from the plate under matched conditions. Match bar dimensions, starting temperatures, plate temperature, contact area and interval. The records alone do not identify either material.
  Wrong: "Different plate temperatures still isolate bar material." (I)
  Shape: infer transfer from two endpoints and specify comparison controls. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.

- **E51.** Invented for practice: A table names both white powders U and W as salt. Equal portions received equal water, stirring and waiting. U dissolved completely; W left grains. Revise the identification table to explain why color was insufficient.
  Key: retain shared white color, add differing dissolution results and withhold confirmed identities until reference matches under stated quantities exist.
  Wrong: "Keep both identities because white proves salt" (A)
  Shape: correct an identification table using conflicting properties. Practice uses separate components, never this held case or full response.
