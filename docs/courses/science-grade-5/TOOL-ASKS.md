# Science Grade 5 tool requests

## Unit 5: Water shares (light, spec only)

From [s05.md](s05.md), fields 8 and U5. This tool does not exist. Ideal: students can see one fresh subset become the new whole while equal shares remain equal. Verdict: partly fits as a plan; it cannot replace student graph construction.

1. Day 94 uses the nested-whole display for six minutes to show why. Children predict the fresh-only partition before opening it. Day 96 uses a separate comparison for six minutes to practise with feedback. Both uses replace six minutes of paper comparison, never extend the period.
2. Provide all-water and fresh-only hundred-square views with the whole always written out. The fresh selection expands to its own hundred equal squares, not to an equal amount of physical water. Keep both views visible.
3. Use the geological survey table cited in s05.md. All-water overview: about 97 salt and 3 fresh parts. State that the salt category includes salty groundwater and lakes as well as ocean water. Fresh-only overview: about 69 ice caps, glaciers and permanent snow, 30 fresh groundwater and 1 surface and other fresh water. Preserve the source's combined ice category and broader remainder.
4. The inset shows fresh lakes 0.26 and rivers 0.006 per 100 fresh-water parts on identical axes from 0 to 0.30. Label the whole on the inset too. Do not request percentage calculations, large water volumes or atmospheric quantities.
5. Pair every interaction with keyboard controls, text alternatives and an identical printed table and blank axes. Paper work includes two overview bar graphs and the inset. No automated score for explanations. No account, third-party media or network dependency.
6. Keep assessment's rescaled atlas and its unseen island case out of all feedback and practice.

## Unit 5: Mountain and air display (light, spec only)

From [s05.md](s05.md), field 8. The full-scale phenomenon cannot be recreated in class. This tool does not exist. Ideal: compare observations before interpreting a two-system profile. Verdict: partly fits as a planned evidence display, not a quantitative weather model.

1. Day 87, six minutes to show why: predict the wetter slope, reveal sourced west-and-east records, then revise an arrow on paper. Replace six minutes of printed comparison.
2. Use original diagrams based on the North Cascades source cited in s05.md. Show moist air rising over the barrier, cloud and precipitation observations on the windward side, and a drier leeward record. No pressure, energy equations, condensation-particle animation or claim that mountains stop all air.
3. Mark illustrative movement as a simplified model, not measured simulation output. Separate land-and-air from water tracing. Use no third-party photographs.
4. Supply the same sequence and source captions on paper. The classroom erosion tray remains the firsthand investigation; its no-kit backup consists of sequential raw observations and is weaker than collecting evidence.

## Unit 6: Filter test (light, spec only)

From [s06.md](s06.md), fields 8 and U5. This tool does not exist. Ideal: children can compare repeat trials, inspect failures and predict one controlled change before seeing results. Verdict: partly fits as a planned analysis tool; it cannot supply observed construction or conducting evidence.

1. Day 105 uses the comparison view for six minutes to show why, replacing six planning minutes. Children name the changed feature, fixed conditions, measurement and trial count before results appear. Day 110 uses six minutes to practise with feedback, after cleanup, replacing comparison and notebook minutes while retaining the paper response. The physical tests retain their time.
2. Offer separate initial and revised records. Keep every trial, including anomalies, visible. Require a prediction and one justified change before revealing its results. Retain original trials beside revised ones.
3. Distinguish collected volume from ordered cloudiness ratings. Use whole and half cups for line plots when the grade 5 math bridge is needed; never average cloudiness. Show measurement limits and explain that clearer water does not establish drinking safety.
4. Every invented trial set says "invented for practice". A record-based simulation is not a calibrated prediction of real filters. Label empirical records with their actual apparatus and source if any are later collected. No claim of microbial or chemical removal is allowed from appearance.
5. Provide keyboard operation, text alternatives and matching printed design cards and trial tables. Children graph, revise a system drawing and explain on paper; no automated judgment of open work, account or saved child record.
6. Preserve the no-kit limit: conducting and construction remain not yet observed. Keep Philadelphia's source cards and the assessment runoff catchers out of all examples and feedback. No third-party media.
