# Section 2.1: Testing plant matter sources

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science Grade 5. Unit 2, Where a tree comes from. "Where does a growing tree's matter come from, and where does it go when the tree dies?" Anchor: the tree-in-a-pot experiment Jan Baptist van Helmont ran in Brussels in the 1600s: a willow grew from 5 pounds to about 169 in five years while its soil lost less than a pound (days 18 to 41): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 18 to 22.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (18, 19, 21), application 2 (20, 22). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Keys solved independently.

- **E18.** Record: "A sealed chamber contains a seedling, air, water and soil. Its weight stays 940 g while the seedling grows. The scale reads in 1 g steps." Ask: "Draw a possible growth account. Explain whether equal chamber weights identify the plant's matter sources."
  Key: matter may move between chamber parts, with no detected total change. Equality does not identify inputs. Accept tentative incoming routes descriptively. Wrong expressed claim: "Equal weights prove soil supplied the growth" (I).
  Shape: chamber drawing plus an inference about equal totals. Practice avoids chambers, equal-total puzzles and source judgments from those puzzles.
- **E19.** Record: "A mint cutting grows new leaves without soil. Its water contains minerals. Air and light reach it." Ask: "Does this identify the main matter source? Name a weighing comparison testing whole-cutting gain."
  Key: no; stored cutting material could supply leaves. Compare initial/final dry whole-cutting weights, using matched samples because drying is destructive. Even gain does not separate air from water. No child dries plants here. Wrong: "New leaves prove water supplied everything" (I); "No soil means minerals are unnecessary" (C).
  Shape: critique new leaves and propose whole-cutting weighing. Practice avoids cutting-storage explanations and designing that weighing.
- **E20.** Record: "A seedling measures 7 cm, then 12 cm. Roots touch moist soil and leaves reach air. The ruler has 1 cm steps." Ask: "Show the height increase and two possible incoming matter routes in one model."
  Key: 5 cm; water through roots, part of air through leaves. Height does not establish sources. Wrong: "Sunlight enters as plant material" (D); "Five centimeters proves water supplied all growth" (I). Arithmetic slips get feedback, no forced science letter.
  Shape: supplied height difference integrated with two routes. Practice separates live height recording from oral part/job matches; no combined worksheet.
- **E21.** Record: "P changes air while water, minerals, light and temperature stay equal. R changes air and water. Both show greater growth with more useful air." Ask: "Explain which comparison challenges the water-only claim."
  Key: P isolates air while water stays equal; R confounds air and water. P supports air's contribution, without giving a fraction. Wrong: "Neither matters because water supplies everything" (B); "R proves air caused growth despite changed water" (I).
  Shape: choose between completed comparisons and justify. Practice interprets one matched study and individual controls, never competing complete designs.
- **E22.** Record: "A vine grows without soil in mineral water. Its whole-plant dry weight rises from 4 g to 23 g. Another comparison holds water, minerals, light and temperature equal. More useful air increases growth." Ask: "Build one supported account of incoming plant matter and sunlight's different role."
  Key: mainly air and water, small mineral input, light energy. Soil-free growth challenges soil as the necessary chief source; matched air supports air contribution. Fractions are unmeasured. Wrong: "Mostly soil" (A); "Light becomes material" (D); "Minerals are unnecessary" (C).
  Shape: supported account connecting two comparisons, inputs and light. Practice keeps single-source statements separate, never rehearsing that integrated package.
