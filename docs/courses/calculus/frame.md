# Course frame: Calculus

Course `calculus` (catalogue id `calculus`), grade 12, a core elective in math (data/courses.csv lists grade 12), draft frame, written for a class that took Precalculus first: Precalculus is the stated prerequisite (Cory, 2026-11-17). Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets the days later; this frame sets the units, their order and their allocations.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 11 units, in the order of the progression's calc- rows (data/progressions/math.csv), which follows what teaches best rather than the base text's chapter order (the Source map names each move and its reason).
- **Days.** 160 course days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks at five lessons a week, one lesson of about 50 minutes a day: a 5-minute opening of spaced, mixed practice on earlier work, then the day's lesson. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and one respond day after it; no unit is over 25 days (unit 2, the longest, has 22), so no unit holds a mid-unit assessment. Days follow the load each row's notes give ("Load: about N days"), and the eleven units add to 151 exactly. These are course days: docs/courses/CALENDAR.md places them on school days and weeks. Course days by unit: unit 1 days 1 to 14, unit 2 15 to 36, unit 3 37 to 50, unit 4 51 to 65, unit 5 66 to 81, check 1 82 to 84, unit 6 85 to 93, unit 7 94 to 102, unit 8 103 to 119, check 2 120 to 122, unit 9 123 to 132, unit 10 133 to 144, unit 11 145 to 157, check 3 158 to 160. Inside the period: the 50 minutes hold the opening and handing out and collecting papers and calculators (about 3 minutes at the end of any day that uses them); a readiness check takes ten minutes; no plan puts more than 50 minutes in a day, and a Shorter path fold-in never takes a feedback or revision day.
- **Day kinds.** Understanding is the first explicit teaching of anything: a definition, a theorem, a rule, a method or a model (the riemann-rectangles, the slope field); the first day of a rule is Understanding even when most of it is practice. Fluency practises only what an earlier day taught, and is real in this course: differentiation rules (unit 2), antiderivatives and substitution (unit 5) and choosing an integration method (unit 6), each after the Understanding day that taught the rule; a Fluency day never introduces a new rule and is never the source of a unit score. Application is using taught ideas on a new task: related rates, optimisation, a modeling task, a motion or accumulation problem in context, a convergence argument on a new series. Check and Respond days are named as such.
- **End-of-unit evidence.** Every unit's end-of-unit assessment is one sitting with unseen items on the unit's second-last day, before its respond day (unit 2's includes its untimed rules section), and an end day holds that one performance only. The modeling tasks of units 3, 4, 7 and 8 are scored on our rubric only after a draft, written feedback and a revision: the model is built and the report drafted on an Application day, the teacher's written feedback comes back, and the report is revised in part of a later day (never the end day or a day that first teaches a topic), all inside the unit's days. No score comes from a day a topic is first taught.
- **The route: Calculus follows Precalculus.** The row loads are written for a class that took Precalculus (by course order): every Precalculus skill Calculus uses (Seams, second table, seventeen pc- rows) opens as a retrieval task inside a Calculus day, and no day reteaches it. On the core route (Algebra I 9, Geometry 10, Algebra II 11) a student has one grade 12 year, so a student reaches Calculus through the accelerated path (data/plans/later.csv L15: Precalculus in grade 11, Calculus in grade 12), the route Cory set on 2026-11-17; the course page and data/courses.csv state Precalculus as the prerequisite. Which route the rows assume: the calc- rows' treatments are counted on the core grade numbers (Algebra I in grade 9, Geometry 10, Algebra II 11, Calculus 12), with Precalculus taught before Calculus by course order and its rows retrieved. On the parked accelerated path (L15: each core course one grade earlier, Precalculus in grade 11), Precalculus's days count as use in grade 11 for every row a pc- row assumes, and the count changes for the rows no pc- row builds on: alg2-polynomial-identities, alg1-piecewise-absolute-value and geo-arcs-sectors-radians become readiness for unit 1 (the last ten minutes of day 1), alg1-quadratic-forms-graphs for unit 3 (day 36), alg2-factors-zeros-graphs for unit 4 (day 50) and alg2-normal-distributions for unit 5 (day 65, unit 4's respond day); geo-solving-right-triangles turns from readiness to retrieval (pc-inverse-trig and pc-oblique-triangles use it in grade 11). The scope writer counts on the accelerated path, the route Cory set; the scope gate's count governs. A student without Precalculus in a class that took it: the course never assumes one student took an optional course, so that student gets the row's bridge (the bridge column of the second Seams table) as a printed catch-up pack and parallel support inside the retrieval task, worked in the unit's openings, with no added day; a whole class without Precalculus takes the alternative below (For the lead 8).
- **The alternative: straight from Algebra II, with a bridge and a cut.** A class straight from Algebra II needs about 20 bridge lessons of Precalculus content that the loads do not hold (sized row by row in the second Seams table): logarithm laws and ln as the inverse of e to the x, exact values at the special angles, the addition and double-angle identities, trigonometric equations, composition and inverse functions with the inverse trigonometric functions, asymptotes and adding rational expressions, conic standard forms, the informal limit, parametric equations, vectors and polar graphs, and sigma notation with the infinite geometric series. The named cut that pays for it: unit 9 (10 days, CUR-CA.25 and CUR-CA.26), which also removes its own 6 bridge lessons, and calc-power-series in unit 11 (7 days, CUR-CA.30), freeing 17 days for a bridge of about 14; the 3 days left go to the bridge's logarithm and trigonometric lessons. Series tests and Taylor polynomials with the Lagrange bound stay. It stays a named alternative (Cory, 2026-11-17), not the design of this frame: a school that opens it says so on its course page, with the cut outcomes named.
- **A tight course.** Calculus here carries both semesters' content of first-year college single-variable calculus in one school year: limits, derivatives and their uses, integrals and their uses, first differential equations, parametric, vector and polar calculus, and series. Every unit runs on its load with no spare day. A school that needs room trims in this order, each a compression and never a cut outcome: (1) improper integrals (unit 6) from 3 days to 1 and arc length (unit 8) from 2 days to 1, which the row notes mark as the lightest, saving 3 days; (2) the second slope field day in unit 7 folded into the Euler's method day, saving 1; (3) the review day inside calc-limits-meaning (unit 1) dropped, saving 1. A school that cannot fit both semesters' content teaches units 1 to 5 and 8 (first-semester content plus the measuring unit, its core integrals done with unit 5's substitution; differential equations, motion in the plane and series are left out) and says so on its course page: a scope decision for Cory, never made by a unit writer.
- **Standard.** Calculus goes past the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, so its standard is Curio Class's own outcome list, data/standards/curio-math-electives.txt, CUR-CA.1 to CUR-CA.30 (in our own words, numbered in teaching order), plus one Common Core (+) code deferred from Geometry, G-GMD.A.2 (Cavalieri's argument for the sphere), owned by calc-volume. Every code a row owns sits in one unit below; no outcome has lettered parts, so "in full" means every clause of the outcome's statement, and each unit's What students learn names them clause by clause. Contributions and revisits of codes other courses own are named in the prose, never as tilde codes. Math has no check set: the C3 column names the unit's practices, and "Check set adds" names, as candidates, the check and extensions.
- **Focus, coherence and rigor (L49 answer applied).** docs/RUBRIC-MATH-HS.md applies with focus 1a to 1c replaced by the method it sets once for Precalculus, Statistics and Calculus (The grade 12 courses; Cory, 2026-11-17, L49), applied here in the same words as the other two. (1) The owned-outcome account: every owned code is taught in full in the unit named below and assessed on that unit's end-of-unit assessment; the course-level checks sample codes again (Course-level checks) and never claim all of them. (2) The minutes line: the minutes spent on earlier courses' rows are counted and capped at 15 percent of unit minutes (151 unit days of 50 minutes, 7,550 minutes, so about 1,130). What counts: the review days and part-days in the rows' load notes, retrieval tasks outside them, readiness checks at ten minutes each, and the daily-opening minutes spent on earlier courses' rows; each minute counts once; check days, respond days and time on this course's own earlier units do not count. A unit reports no share: each unit plan lists its minutes on earlier courses' rows as a count, and the scope and sequence sums them and states the course share against the cap. (3) The modeling cycle stays whole: no step is cut to save time (Modeling). Planned here, for a class that took Precalculus: calc-limits-meaning's one review day (50 minutes), three readiness checks (30 minutes), the retrieval tasks of the seventeen Precalculus seams at about ten minutes each (170 minutes), and at most 75 of the 151 openings (375 minutes) on earlier courses' rows: at most about 625 minutes, about 8 percent. A bridge for a class straight from Algebra II (the named alternative) is shown separately. No day only reteaches an Algebra II or Precalculus row. Coherence 1d and 1e apply in full (Seams). Assessment 1f and 1g, rigor 2a to 2d with direct fluency, the practices 2e to 2l, supports 3a to 3u and the multilingual-learner criteria apply in full, as for Algebra II. Understanding comes first in every unit: the derivative is built from secant slopes before any rule, the integral from sums of rate times width before any antiderivative, convergence from partial sums before any test. Fluency follows (below), then application in context (rates, motion, accumulation, growth, optimisation).
- **Fluency without a timer.** The procedures this course expects to be fluent: differentiation rules, the power, constant multiple, sum, product and quotient rules and the derivatives of the trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions (CUR-CA.5, unit 2), the chain rule and inverse derivatives (CUR-CA.6, unit 2), implicit differentiation (CUR-CA.7, unit 2); antiderivative rules and substitution (CUR-CA.16, unit 5); and choosing and carrying out an integration method (CUR-CA.17, unit 6). Each is built in its unit and kept up in the daily 5-minute opening for the rest of the year, beside the rewriting skills these rules lean on: factoring, rational expressions and exponent rules from Algebra II, and the logarithm laws, special-angle values and trigonometric identities from Precalculus. The year-end check holds an untimed, independent fluency station that samples each named fluency directly. No countdown timer is ever shown to a student.

- **Technology and the paper path.** Graphing technology shows zooming in on a graph (local linearity), many-rectangle sums, slope fields, Euler's method with small steps, partial sums of a series and Taylor polynomials closing in on a graph, and evaluates the integrals (arc length, most volume checks) the rows say are done with technology. Each has a paper path: graphing by hand on grid paper from a table of values; slope fields drawn on printed grids of points; Riemann and trapezoidal sums and Euler steps worked in printed tables; partial sums in a table; polar grid paper printed from the course's own sheets (unit 9); parametric curves plotted from a printed table of t values; intersection points for area between curves found by a table of values and successive approximation, as in Algebra II (A-REI.D.11); a cooling or growth model fitted by taking logarithms of the given values (temperature minus room temperature) and fitting a line by eye on grid paper; and a basic scientific calculator for every number (a midpoint sum with ten slices stands in for an arc length integral). Any electronic tool is a free one that needs no student account, named in no student page.
- **Devices.** One teacher display every day; a class set of 30 basic scientific calculators (school-owned, kept in the room); on the days the scope marks for graphing (zooming in, many-rectangle sums, slope fields, partial sums, Taylor polynomials), one device for each pair of students (15 for a class of 30), with no student account. No owned code needs a device to be met in full: every value the rows leave to technology (arc length, intersection points, a volume check) has a calculator route, a ten-slice midpoint sum or a table of values, so a student on the paper path meets every code in full with the paper path above.
- **Data and facts.** Contexts are mostly given functions. Every data table is invented for practice and labelled so (the cooling drink, the fish or bacteria counts, the tank's inflow and outflow rates, the test cart's force-time table, the motion tables), except half-lives, which come from the national nuclear data pages (the National Nuclear Data Center, as of the date read). No constructed value is passed off as a measurement.
- **Extensions stay extensions (Cory, 2026-11-17, Desk card q-electives-frames-1).** The named extensions are never outcomes: the formal definition of a limit, logarithmic differentiation and curve-sketching asymptotes as owned work, Newton's method, 1 to the infinity, Simpson's rule, trigonometric substitution, mixing problems, volumes by shells, work, surface area, centres of mass, and the further series topics (the binomial series for powers that are not whole numbers, products of series, e to the i theta). On the accelerated route they are the first cut: a class short of time drops extensions before any step of A tight course's trim order. The owned series outcomes (calc-power-series, CUR-CA.30, unit 11) stay owned.
- **Cost ceiling.** About $400 a year for a class of 30 (Cory, 2026-11-17, Desk card q-electives-frames-1): printing (about $360) within it, and a class set of scientific calculators (about $300) bought once where the school has none. No unit's provisional cost passes $150; a unit plan that does names a cheaper route.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** The last weeks hold teaching: unit 11 runs to day 157, and check 3, the year-end check, takes days 158 to 160. If seniors leave early, check 3 moves up to the seniors' last days and the course folds the trim order of A tight course (improper integrals 3 days to 1, arc length 2 to 1, the second slope field day into the Euler day, unit 1's review day), up to 5 days, never a check, an end-of-unit assessment, a feedback or a revision day. A school that knows its seniors' last day plans these folds from the start; more than 5 days is For the lead 6.
- **Side standards.** None is claimed. The modeling reports and convergence arguments practise explanatory writing (WHST.11-12.2), practised, not owned.
- **Modeling.** Units 3, 4, 7 and 8 each carry one context taken through the whole cycle (assumptions, model, computation, interpretation, validation, revision, report), with our own rubric: a related rates situation (unit 3), a design to optimise (unit 4), a population or cooling model fitted to given data (unit 7) and a tank accumulation (unit 8). The unit plan names the task.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). No unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content that needs a named community study: contexts are motion, containers, shapes, cooling drinks, money in an account, populations of fish or bacteria and given decay data. Picks that keep it so: radioactive decay uses a tracer or dating context with given data, never a disaster; collision accumulation (unit 8) uses a test cart or helmet liner, with no injury figures; no context models medicine in a body or a student's own health, money or family. ### Where the calculus thread ends

Calculus is the last course of the math progression, and no calc- row is handed on. Its threads end here: limits (unit 1), the derivative and its uses (units 2 to 4), the integral and its uses (units 5, 6 and 8), differential equations (unit 7), motion in the plane and polar regions (unit 9) and series (units 10 and 11). What would follow is first-year college work past single-variable calculus (calculus in several variables, linear algebra, a full differential equations course), which no Curio Class course teaches. The year-end check record, with what its respond day changed, goes to the student and family as the picture of where each thread stands. Statistics sits beside this course in grade 12 and neither assumes the other; the area under a curve here (unit 5) and the normal curve in Statistics share the Algebra II start, alg2-normal-distributions.

A section is not a lesson: the days are ours, set by the scope and sequence, each adapting what it uses and crediting the base once a day in the credit line; a section may feed several days or part of one. Check and assessment days are always `ours`, never a kept section, and a kept section is never turned into a test. Goals and notes are in our words. Students read our adapted text, never the base, at most about 600 words a day (about ten minutes at the course's reading level), worked examples and figures beside it.

Every chapter and section of Contemporary Calculus is placed in one unit or left out with a reason. Decisions: keep (adapted), keep in part, ours (a day Curio Class writes), left out.
