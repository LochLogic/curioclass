# Course frame: Health, grades 6 to 8

Course `health-grades-6-to-8` (catalog id `health-6-8`), grades 6, 7 and 8, one band course with one part per grade year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 30 through the band: 10 in grade 6 (units 1 to 10), 11 in grade 7 (units 11 to 21), 9 in grade 8
  (units 22 to 30). Eight of them are sensitive units (9, 10, 19, 20, 21, 28, 29, 30), each removable. Each year is
  whole on its own: its own units, end-of-unit assessments, mid-year check and year-end check.
- **Days.** 144: 48 a year for three years. Each year has 44 days in units and 4 course-level days (a mid-year check
  and its respond day, a year-end check and its respond day). One or two lessons a week, three every two weeks,
  each about 45 minutes, over the whole 32-week school year, so the 45 minutes hold the opening scenario (five
  minutes at most), the work, collecting sheets and putting cards away. The dose is the health approach page's
  default (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, Pacing: 6 to 8 about 45 sessions a year of 45 minutes, kept by Cory
  2026-11-21, Desk q-prework-open-2). This frame sets 48, three over "about 45", for one reason: three lessons every
  two weeks is a rhythm a middle school can timetable for the whole year (an A week of two and a B week of one), and
  it puts the mid-year check on course day 25 in week 17 and the year-end check in week 32, both off every core
  check day. Days are weighed by load, not equal shares: a row that is one plain fact shares a day with its
  neighbor, a row that teaches or returns a step card gets a model day and a pair-practice day, a unit that ends in
  a product (an advocacy piece, a team project) counts its draft, written feedback and revision days, and every
  unit's last day is its end-of-unit assessment. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (no unit here
  runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment). These are course days: docs/courses/CALENDAR.md places
  them on school days. The national figure is higher: the 2007 National Health Education Standards (second
  edition, page 76) recommend at least 80 hours a year in grades 3 to 12; this course gives 36 hours a year. The
  course page names that figure and the gap so a school can add time (APPROACH.md, Pacing). A sensitive unit's days
  count as optional (STANDARD.md section 5 rule 6): a school that leaves one out uses its days to extend the
  non-sensitive unit named in its row. The planned route is 48 sessions across 32 weeks. A daily nine-week route needs a separately specified
  45-session short path that names three session reductions, keeps every owned indicator and check and respond
  cycle, and schedules the multiweek habit and advocacy evidence (units 26 and 27); it is not available until its
  session table exists, and a school that needs it brings the time limit to the lead.
- **Standard.** National Health Education Standards, 2024, SHAPE America: the 46 indicators of the 6-8 span
  (`NHES.S<n>.8.<n>`), each owned by one row (13 in grade 6, 17 in grade 7, 16 in grade 8) and placed in one unit
  here; ids and our own words only (data/standards/nhes-2024.txt), never the indicator text. Review tool: CDC's
  Health Education Curriculum Analysis Tool (HECAT), 2021 edition, keyed to the 2007 standards
  (docs/standards-notes/health-2007-to-2024.md); each unit names the healthy behavior outcomes it serves
  (`HECAT.<topic>.HBO<n>`), and every day ties to one (bar criterion 1). Every year touches all eight standards in
  its non-sensitive units, and every skill standard (2 to 8) is practiced in at least two topics in the band
  (STANDARD.md section 4), counted with the sensitive rows taken out so the claim holds in a school that leaves them
  out: standard 2 in personal health, food, physical activity, mental health and tobacco; 3 in personal health,
  safety and food; 4 in mental health, safety, alcohol and drugs, and violence prevention; 5 in food, tobacco,
  safety, physical activity, and alcohol and drugs; 6 in personal health (units 8, 17, 26) and physical activity
  (unit 8's activity goal, g6-pa-habits-supports-barriers); 7 in personal health and physical activity; 8 in
  safety, tobacco, food and physical activity. The C3 column of the Units table holds, for health, the skill
  standard the unit teaches or practices and its step card (the model). Check set: each unit planner reads the
  HECAT 2021 module of its topic, 6-8 knowledge and skill expectations, before planning (STANDARD.md section 3); the
  "Check set adds" column names what that read is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds,
  with the health lines of "What a health day never does" (APPROACH.md). Coverage map: the scope and sequence
  carries the 6 to 8 column of STANDARD.md section 4 with unit names in its cells; physical activity outcomes 3, 4
  and 6 are taught by moving in PE (Desk q-prework-health-pe-1), and this course names the PE rows instead.
- **Base text.** None. Health is Curio Class's own: scenarios, step cards, case cards, invented posts and ads,
  community profiles, source cards and family notes are written by Curio Class. Facts and figures come from public
  health agencies and national surveys (CDC, the national highway traffic safety agency, the national drug abuse
  institute, USDA's food guide pages, the national youth tobacco survey and a national survey of grade 8 students'
  alcohol and drug use), each with its source and year, checked by the health expert agent (below). No trade book,
  paid video or library visit.
- **The health unit shape.** Each unit names its behavior outcomes, three objectives (knowing, valuing, doing) and
  one skill with its steps (APPROACH.md). A skill is taught in the bar's five steps: why it matters, the steps, a
  model (the teacher says each step aloud on a scenario), practice on a realistic scenario, feedback. The teacher
  holds the model on the first fluency day, pairs hold the card on the second, the card is withdrawn on the first
  application day, and the end-of-unit assessment is taken without it. At three lessons every two weeks a skill
  that returns from an earlier unit or grade gets its card back for one day, then goes without. Norms and pressure
  (bar criterion 4): each year states one norm with a current national survey figure for the age group, on an
  Understanding day of a non-sensitive unit (APPROACH.md, Kinds of day): grade 6 unit 4, the share of middle school
  students who do not use e-cigarettes (the national youth tobacco survey, latest edition); grade 7 unit 12, the
  share of grade 8 students who have not had alcohol in the past month (the national survey of grade 8 students,
  latest edition, named for its age group); grade 8 unit 25, the same survey's grade 8 figures for alcohol and
  cannabis, set beside what students guess first. Each figure is entered with its source and year at the expert
  read, never from memory. Pressure is met in the influence units (4, 16, 23), the refusal units (12, 25) and the
  dare and group scenes (4, 12).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day"): Understanding (first teaching of a fact or idea, ending in a
  choice and a reason), Fluency (practice only: the scripted pair rehearsals of a step card, a refusal line, an
  I-statement, a boundary line, a help request), Application (the skill on a new scenario, the student's own goal
  or a real audience; drafting and revising an advocacy piece are Application), Check (the end-of-unit assessment)
  and Respond (the course-level respond days). Fluency in this course is short: a ten-minute pair rehearsal inside
  most days, and at most one whole Fluency day a unit, or the two a unit's row lists (units 3 and 12); first teaching
  is always an Understanding day.
- **End-of-unit evidence.** Every unit ends on a check day: a sitting with two or three unseen scenarios, read alone
  (a short scenario at most, APPROACH.md Supports) or read aloud on request, where each student writes what the
  character would say or do using the skill steps, scored on the steps with a guide, plus one facts-in-use item per
  fact the unit lists and one valuing item (what would be a good choice for this person and why, scored on whether
  the reason uses a unit fact, never on which choice; APPROACH.md). Units that end in a product (18, 26, 27) score
  the product on the steps after a counted draft day, written feedback and a revision day; no score comes from a day
  a topic is first taught. Twice a unit the student rates their own use of the steps and names one next step (bar
  criterion 8). The teacher marks met, partly or not yet. Where an owned indicator asks the student to do something
  (NHES.S7.8.3 and NHES.S6.8.5 in unit 26, NHES.S8.8.4 in unit 27), the evidence includes the student doing it at
  school: a teacher record of each student carrying out the team's habit and keeping its log, or delivering the
  advocacy piece to its audience, scored on the steps and the student's account, never on health, home habits or
  whether a goal worked. A character's log or plan shows understanding only and never stands alone for a doing
  indicator. Personal plans and self-checks stay with the student unless they choose to share. Communication and influence
  skills also get an individual performance record, kept by the teacher inside existing practice and application
  time and carried into the unit judgment and the course-level checks without rescoring: units 3 (receiving an
  unfamiliar message, asking a relevant question and saying back its meaning accurately), 11, 12, 14 and 24 (each
  student's own response to an unfamiliar prompt), and 23 (each student applying a taught strategy to an invented
  message in class). Speech, AAC or a written exchange with an adult or partner all count; a prewritten account of
  someone else's response never stands alone, and the written scenarios stay as added evidence. The unit plan sets
  spoken records as two or three short pair stations the teacher moves between. Where a mid-year check follows a
  unit (units 5, 15 and 25), the check is that unit's end-of-unit assessment and scores its indicators first; the
  unit's last day is Application with written feedback.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** The count starts from the grade of the year being taught; there are no routes. Every
  row grade 6 assumes is a grade 5 row of Health, grades 3 to 5, taught the year before, except two grade 4 rows;
  every row grade 7 assumes is a grade 6 row of this course; grade 8 assumes grade 7 rows, one grade 6 row and one
  grade 5 row. A row used the year before opens as a short retrieval task inside a day of the unit that needs it
  (sequence-write.md rule 2), named in the unit's row below. The two grade 4 rows were last used in grade 5
  (g4-meh-calm-stress in grade 5 units 15 and 18; g4-phw-prevent-illness-injury in grade 5 unit 17), so they open as
  retrieval tasks inside units 3 and 5 like any row used the year before. Rows last used two or more grades back get
  a readiness check of ten minutes, given before the unit opens: g5-fn-claims-that-hurt (grade 5) in the last ten
  minutes of unit 12's last day, before unit 13; g5-s-online-safety (grade 5) in the last ten minutes of unit 13's
  last day, before unit 14 (the two grade 5 handoffs settled by the expert read, For the lead 4);
  g5-phw-surroundings-community (grade 5, Health, grades 3 to 5) and g6-phw-adult-or-professional (grade 6, unit 2
  of this course) in the last ten minutes of unit 22's day 1, after the hook, since unit 22 opens the grade 8 year. The scope gate's count governs, and the
  scope fix pass updates this bullet.
- **Sensitive units and the leave-out rule.** STANDARD.md section 5: sensitive content lives only in its own units
  (unwanted touch and sexual harassment: unit 9; dating violence and sexual harassment: units 20, 28; self-harm and
  suicide: units 19, 29; sexual health: units 10, 21, 30); no skill lives only in a sensitive unit (each indicator a
  sensitive row touches is owned by a non-sensitive row of the band: S3.8.1 in unit 2, S4.8.1 in unit 3, S1.8.2 in
  unit 1, S4.8.3 in unit 11, S4.8.4 in unit 14, S4.8.7 in unit 26, S4.8.8, S4.8.9 and S4.8.10 in unit 24); getting
  help is taught in a non-sensitive unit every year (units 2, 11, 24, with unit 22's care services); no non-sensitive
  unit assumes a sensitive one; each sensitive unit has a leave-out note on its first page and a longer family note
  (English and Spanish) sent home two weeks before. Each year's sensitive units sit after its non-sensitive units
  and right before the year-end check, and no course-level check samples a sensitive unit, so leaving one out
  changes no check. In sensitive units rehearsal uses written scripts of the refusal, boundary or telling line only;
  the teacher or a card plays any pressure; no student plays a person who pressures, touches, harms or threatens
  (APPROACH.md). The self-harm and suicide units (19, 29) follow safe-messaging rules: no method detail, a help line
  (the 988 crisis line, named by purpose) and an adult plan on the first page, and a school counselor told ahead
  and on call for that lesson.
- **Reading level.** Students read at most a short scenario, case card or source card alone; longer text is read
  aloud or summarized on the card (APPROACH.md, Supports). Each unit teaches six to ten health words in depth with a
  sentence frame for each skill step ("I won't do that. I'm going to ..."; "I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___."). The
  right idea in another language, by pointing or with a frame is a language gap, not a content gap. No student must
  speak aloud: every rehearsal and check item can be done in writing, by pointing at a card, or with an adult.
- **Family notes.** Every unit sends a home task (a talk with a trusted adult the student chooses; a school adult
  counts), written in English and Spanish when the unit is finished. No task asks a student about their own drug
  use, sexual activity, diet, weight, mental health, abuse or family; scenarios are about characters, and personal
  goals stay with the student.
- **Devices.** None needed. No owned code needs technology: the source-check and false-message units (13, 23) use
  printed source cards and invented posts, and a teacher may show one agency page on one screen. Any Curio Class
  practice tool a unit plan adds (a scenario sort, a label reader, a source-check practice) runs on one teacher
  screen or a shared device, collects no health information and has a paper path with the same items, so a
  paper-path student meets every owned code in full.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper cards, case cards, logs and printed sources; any electronic tool has the
  same task on paper beside it, and no student account is ever made.
- **Safety.** Hands-on days and their controls are named in the last column of the Units table. Guides the safety
  read uses: CDC, "Clean Hands" handwashing pages (https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/); the national highway traffic
  safety agency's bicycle safety pages (https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/bicycle-safety) and its impaired driving
  pages (https://www.nhtsa.gov/risky-driving/drunk-driving); the school's own active-play, field and duty-of-care
  rules for the unit 26 walking group. Links named from memory, opened at the safety and expert reads. No real
  medicine, supplement, alcohol, cigarette, vape or lighter is ever brought to class; pictures and invented labels
  only. No food is tasted in this course.
- **Expert read (bar criterion 7).** The Curio Class health expert agent reads every unit plan and day for medical
  and scientific accuracy against current public health sources and records the date (STANDARD.md section 6),
  above all the survey figures, the sleep hours by age, the vaccine and infection facts, and the sexual health
  units. The course page may say figures were last checked against their sources on a date, and that a school's
  health staff should read it too.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14), and the safety and answer-key reads are agent reads too. Units
  that need the agent to study named sources first: units 9, 20 and 28 (unwanted touch, harassment, dating violence
  and assault: students who have been harmed and their families; the federal Child Welfare Information Gateway
  pages on preventing child sexual abuse, starting from https://www.childwelfare.gov/topics/preventing/, CDC's teen
  dating violence pages and a national child traumatic stress network's guidance for teachers); units 10, 21 and 30
  (sexual health: families of every structure, faith and culture, students of every gender identity and sexual
  orientation, students whose bodies change early or differently; the HECAT 2021 Sexual Health module 6-8
  expectations and CDC's adolescent sexual health, HIV and infection pages); units 19 and 29 (self-harm and suicide:
  students who live with these thoughts, who self-harm, or who have lost someone to suicide; a national suicide
  prevention resource center's safe-messaging guidance for schools and the 988 crisis line's pages); units 4, 5, 12,
  23 and 25 (tobacco, alcohol, drugs and impaired driving: students who live with someone who uses, families who
  have lost someone in a crash; CDC's youth tobacco pages, the national drug abuse institute's pages for teens and
  the national highway traffic safety agency's impaired driving pages); units 6 and 16 (food: students with too
  little food at home, cultural foods, and the risk of eating-disorder talk; USDA's food guide pages and a national
  eating disorders association's guidance for schools); unit 15 (health conditions: students living with asthma,
  diabetes, a disability or a long illness; CDC's pages on managing chronic conditions at school); unit 7
  (bullying: students who have been bullied or who bully; the federal stop-bullying pages); units 8, 17 and 26
  (goals and habits: bodies and weight; the same eating disorders guidance); unit 22 (care: families without
  insurance, transport or a regular doctor). Every unit with hands-on work (12, 26) also gets the safety agent's
  read against the guides above, and every check gets the answer-key agent's read. The unit planner names the exact
  page and its date for each named source before the unit plan's expert read; a page that refuses an automated read
  is browser check. Unit 7's source pack adds the federal bullying response page
  (https://www.stopbullying.gov/prevention/support-the-children-involved). Each sensitive unit's leave-in note says a
  school that keeps the unit may add one practice day before its check.
- **Course-level checks.** Placed off the core check days (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, grades 6 to 8 by school day,
  week 32 a finals week): grade 6's core checks fall in weeks 12, 15, 16, 18, 22 to 24 and 32, grade 7's in weeks
  9, 12 to 14, 22, 24 and 32, grade 8's in weeks 10, 14, 15, 18, 20, 24 to 26, 29, 30 and 32. Each year's mid-year
  check is course day 25, school day 84 in week 17 (no core check that week in any of the three grades), with its
  respond day in week 18; each year-end check is course day 47, school day 157 in week 32, the finals week, with its
  respond day on school day 160. Mid-year checks avoid core check days. Year-end checks share the permitted week 32
  finals period with core year-end checks.
