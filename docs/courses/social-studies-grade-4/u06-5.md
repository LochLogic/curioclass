## 1. Standards and practices

D2.Geo.8 and D2.Eco.4 carry "Explain"; MA.4.T4a.4 carries "Ask", "research", "analyze". Explain both settlement and movement, and both worker and business specialization/trade. Day 122 connects 4-ESS3-1 by naming water and its use, without assuming completed science instruction.

Review written grade 3 wording: natural resource (something from nature that people use); industry (all the work that gets or makes one kind of thing, such as fishing); specialize (a business that makes mostly one kind of thing); trade (goods move from where they are made to where they are sold); opportunity cost (the next-best thing given up; not the price, not every other choice). Extend specialization to a worker's learned skill. Review change the land and adapt on 121; goods and services on 124.

Teacher explains resources enable possibilities; skills, investment, transport and choices also matter. Content note: 123's Savannah history names slavery, without graphic detail. People forced into slavery are never willing trading partners. No personal financial disclosure.

  **Shape:** generate a researchable question and synthesize two records into an adaptation answer. Avoid question-plus-two-source answers in practice; annotate given question/detail links one record at a time.
- **E123, s06-X123.** Held C shows grain processing near homes; D shows forest products shipped away. Ask: "Compare resource links to settlement and movement with our Northeast case." Key: grain use supports nearby work/homes; wood becomes goods moved elsewhere. Compare these uses with Paterson's built waterpower system. Accept supported comparisons; no unshown route or extra limit required. Wrong: all Western residents cut trees (G); a resource list (U).
  **Shape:** compare two unseen resource/use relationships with the Northeast case. Avoid cross-case comparisons during practice; complete isolated within-source rows and identify missing evidence.
- **E124, s06-X124.** Held Trade Card supplies a fisher's boats/skills and bakery's ovens/skills. Ask: "Complete both actors' specialization and trade rows." Key: fisher sells fish, buys bread; bakery sells bread, buys fish for meals. Each reason uses its resources/skills. Money-mediated trade counts. Wrong: only bakery trades (H); specialists meet every need alone (V).
  **Shape:** generate both actors' reasons, sales and purchases in a table. Avoid complete two-actor tables and full oral equivalents in practice; audit one transaction or one actor at a time.
- **E125, s06-X125a/b.** Held Mechanic Card supplies repair tools/skills and a grain farm's land/skills. Ask: "Complete the mechanic and farm specialization, sales and purchases table." Then: "The mechanic chooses saving over a notebook. Name the opportunity cost." Key: mechanic sells repairs and buys grain for food; farm sells grain and buys repairs. Notebook is the next-best current choice given up. Wrong: each supplies everything alone (V); every unchosen option is the opportunity cost (Z).
  **Shape:** complete a new worker/business exchange table and identify one forgone alternative. Avoid complete actor tables and naming a next-best item during practice; trace single transactions and contrast two allocation diagrams.
