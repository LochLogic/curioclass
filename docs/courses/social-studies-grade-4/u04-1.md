## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 60): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.4.T3.1** (days 61): Explain how historians studying European voyages to the Americas use archaeology, maps, illustrations, and texts of the time, which are called primary sources.
- **D2.His.9.3-5** (days 61): Summarize how different kinds of sources are used to explain past events.
- **D2.His.11.3-5** (days 62, 63): Infer who a source was meant for and why it was made, using clues in the source itself.
- skill ss4-exploration (grade 4)
- skill ss4-primary-source (grade 4)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 60:** compare Columbus's claim of possession with the Taíno public account of their homeland. Could the same arrival mean different things? Who needs an accurate account?
> - **Co-write on day 60:** How do sources help us know (61 to 64)? Why sail (65)? What changed for people here (66 to 72)? What happened together (73 to 74)?
> - **Teaching:** model sourcing on 62, independence on 67 (modelled first), a cause and its effects on 68, scale on 73 and reasoning on 75. On 76 students work without a teacher model. Return to the question board after each supporting answer.
>
> - **Carried:** source-sourcing gains audience and purpose on 62; prompts fade on 76. Corroboration adds independence on 67 and returns on 75; the Norse case joins 75 and 77. Motive: one aim a voyage on the map, compared on 75. Timeline changes from supplied to student-constructed on 73; on 74 withdraw the example and add an equal-year grid. Revise cause-effect-chain on 68 to distinguish intended gains from harms. Perspective pairs each voyage with named peoples on 64 to 72. Keep unit 3's qualified claims and source log.
<!-- /skel -->

**Share and connections.** D1.1: "Explain why" the question matters to others, day 60. MA.4.T3.1: "Explain" four kinds' uses, day 61; D2.His.9: "Summarize" their contributions. D2.His.11: "Infer" audience and purpose from internal evidence, days 62 to 63. C3 verb phrases follow s04's paraphrases; the local file supplies identifiers only.

**Written review, ledger words:** supporting question (a smaller question that helps answer the big one), grade 3 u07-1 day 141; primary source and secondary source, for one question, grade 3 u04-1 day 73; source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose, grade 3 u06-1 day 126. Audience/purpose are review despite the frame. New work applies them to voyage evidence and distinguishes maker, later recorder and adapter.

**Limits and teacher explanation.** No conquest role play or imagined Native testimony. A primary source can be mistaken. A contemporary illustration need not be eyewitness evidence. Distinguish event, document and caption dates. Internal evidence supports a possible purpose. Teach archaeology through material evidence, separately from a historian's caption.
