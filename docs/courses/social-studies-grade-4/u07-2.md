Individual paper exits, collected from everyone. Practice matches labels or tests isolated details; it never composes a held exit response. Component scale only: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Equivalent supported wording accepted.

- **E142, paper exit.** "Biddy Mason was once enslaved. In Los Angeles, she owned property and supported community institutions. How does this change a Gold Rush account that lists miners alone?" Key: An African American woman also helped build community institutions, beyond mining. This caption concerns Mason, not everyone. Wrong: "Every Western resident was a miner" (I). **Shape:** Revise an incomplete account using a fresh personal caption.
  Shape exclusion: Avoid revising mining-only accounts and Mason; annotate source/date matches instead.
- **E143, paper exit.** "Japanese American families built flower businesses in Richmond, California. In 1942, the government forced them into confinement. Explain how force changed this community contribution." Key: Flower businesses provided livelihoods. Government removal and confinement disrupted these businesses and denied choice, unlike a freely chosen job move. Wrong: "They freely chose removal" (B); "Confinement protected their freedom" (K). **Shape:** Connect an unfamiliar livelihood with forced removal.
  Shape exclusion: Avoid livelihood/removal explanations and Richmond flowers; mark dated source evidence instead.
- **E144, paper exit.** "D: Duwamish people created a Longhouse and Cultural Center in Seattle. It shares their history and culture. W: Seattle's Wing Luke Museum uses a community-developed exhibit process. It presents Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander histories. Describe these contributions with one limit on what the cards show." Key: D shows Duwamish public history and cultural learning. W shows community participation in exhibits. Neither describes every resident's views or experience. W concerns several communities. Wrong: "Seattle has just one culture" (A). **Shape:** Synthesize two new community contributions with a shared limit.
  Shape exclusion: Avoid two-card contribution/limit synthesis and D/W cards; locate separate public-source details instead.
- **E145, paper exit.** "Made-up Pass Diagram. In 1850, a mountain pass connects towns; books and wool travel through. In 2020, its road carries medicine; arrivals join relatives. Explain continuity and change using land and community ties." Key: The pass still connects towns; transport and recorded goods differ. Terrain offers access; relatives help arrivals settle. Books carry ideas. The dates do not show when family networks began. Wrong: "Land alone determined every move" (D). **Shape:** Explain paired dates through land and social influences.
  Shape exclusion: Avoid paired-date land/social explanations and the pass diagram; classify separate dated fragments instead.
