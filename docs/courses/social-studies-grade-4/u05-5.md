- **E95, Purchase and Residents Captions.** Fresh summaries: "Russia and the United States agreed to Alaska's purchase in 1867." "Tlingit people lived there and protested the sale of their lands." Ask: "Explain what changed and whose agreement these captions do not establish." Key: Russia's claim passed to the United States by purchase; Tlingit agreement is not established, and their protest shows opposition. Do not infer every individual's position. Meets: "The national claim changed; Tlingit protest challenges the idea that residents agreed." Wrong: "Paying Russia proves Tlingit consent." (B); "It was gained through war with Russia." (T).
  Shape: political change/consent limit from paired captions. Practice separates transfer-party matching from community-detail annotation, never this explanation or reserved protest selection.
- **E96, Events and Status.** New captions separately identify the 1893 overthrow and 1898 annexation. Reserved Queen Liliuokalani protest summary challenges annexation. Ask: "Place the overthrow and annexation on this timeline." "Explain whether either event made Hawaii a state." Key: overthrow at 1893; annexation at 1898; neither was state admission. Meets: "The government was overthrown first; annexation followed, without statehood." Wrong: "Annexation immediately made Hawaii a state." (A); "Annexation proves everyone agreed." (B). Protest is evidence, not another demand.
  Shape: place two political events and explain statehood status. Practice attributes separate source details, never constructs a timeline/event-status bundle. Required dates recur as content; arrangement/details/response are new.
- **E97, Puerto Rico Transfer Card.** New locator crop and war/treaty summary; unused community caption: "The Instituto de Cultura Puertorriquena lists music programs today." Ask: "Explain how Puerto Rico's 1898 transfer differed from Alaska's purchase." "Give one present-day activity shown by the community card." Key: war with Spain followed by treaty cession, unlike purchase from Russia; Puerto Rican music programs continue. Meets: "War and treaty brought Puerto Rico under United States control; music programs continue today." Wrong: "Both were purchases from Russia." (T); "Puerto Ricans no longer live as a people." (M).
  Shape: compare acquisition methods, then extract living-community detail. Practice classifies individual methods and separately explores public activities; no combined answer or reserved music/map crop.
