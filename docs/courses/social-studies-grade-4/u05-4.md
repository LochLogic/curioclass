- **E91, Article VIII Card.** Reserved original summary: "Mexican residents could stay after the border changed. They could retain Mexican citizenship or acquire United States citizenship." Ask: "Explain how someone could gain United States citizenship without moving home." Key: governing borders changed around the home; the treaty allowed a citizenship choice. Meets: "The home stayed in place while the border changed around it." Wrong: "They must have moved north." (S).
  Shape: infer stationary residence with changed nationality from treaty terms. Practice separately labels boundaries or property-right promises, never this inference or Article VIII card.
- **E92, Arrival Cards.** Reserved summaries: "Hunger pushed many Irish people to leave. Some Germans fled punishment after political activity." Add: "Both cards describe people deciding to leave under pressure." Ask: "Explain how these departures differed from people being seized and transported." Key: hardship constrained choices, but departure was not capture and forced transport into enslavement. Meets: "Their choices were difficult; captives did not choose their journey." Wrong: "All were choosing jobs." (I).
  Shape: explain constrained departure versus seizure from paired records. Practice annotates individual chosen-migration motives only; no paired capture contrast or these selections.
- **E93, Captivity Caption.** Reserved original summary attributed to Equiano's narrative: "Equiano describes being kidnapped and enslaved. Later, he lived in freedom and argued against slavery." False label: "He came for a job." Ask: "Explain why this label misrepresents the account." Key: kidnapping and enslavement denied choice; employment language erases coercion. Meets: "His account describes being taken, rather than choosing employment." Wrong: "Forced work is a chosen job." (I); "Every Black person remained enslaved." (P). Family/resistance context is teacher information, not another exit ask. No birthplace inference.
  Shape: repair an employment label using a captivity account. Practice attributes individual details and identifies coercion, never corrects a job label or uses Equiano's reserved detail.
- **E94, Admission Map.** New schematic, explicitly not a historical boundary map: patterned states allowing/prohibiting slavery and a western territory seeking admission. Key names patterns without explaining political effects. Ask: "Explain how admitting this territory could deepen division over slavery." "Give the Civil War's beginning and ending years." Key: admitting a state could change slavery's reach and political power, worsening division; 1861 to 1865. Meets: "People disputed slavery's spread and power in new states; war lasted 1861 to 1865." Wrong: "The division had nothing to do with slavery." (J); "War caused the earlier admission dispute." (O).
  Shape: infer admission's political effect from a schematic, then give war span. Practice handles single factual labels or causal links; no admission-map explanation/date bundle.
