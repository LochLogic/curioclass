**Owned:** Q1. Keep A/B, their dates and pit diagram reserved through 57. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen, separate components, no points.

**Q1 Shape:** combine footprint/corridor findings, plot dates, compare route sequences and interpret a later pit. Practice never combines these, uses footprints or packet dates. Cold key: 21,000 to 23,000 is earlier than 15,000 and 12,600. The studied bottleneck's reported food conditions cannot explain that earlier presence. Coast remains possible, not demonstrated by footprints; Beringia can precede either southward route. Later pits defeat depth alone. Do not infer every corridor section was impossible before this study's date. Meets: "This corridor finding is later. Another route remains possible, but neither source traces a journey." Not yet: "Boats prove nobody crossed Beringia." Wrong: reversed years-ago order (A); presence means first arrival (B); land proves corridor (C); coast excludes Beringia (J); depth always dates (K).

  Shape: interpret a second site's dated caption for presence/route limits. Practice locates Nipéhe evidence or classifies supplied statements; never interprets a fresh dated caption independently. Friedkin stays withheld.
- **E47, Exit Nations Card, reserved.** Two secular factual lines credit Hopi public introduction for Hopitutskwa ties, and Andrea Hunter's Osage account for Ohio Valley origins/movement. "How should a museum label identify these accounts?" Key: identify Hopi Tribe and Osage Nation separately as tellers, neither as all nations or a belief test. Meets: "Name each nation beside its own account." Not yet: "Call both the Native story." Wrong: one account represents every nation (L).
  Shape: generate attribution rules for a joint label. Practice reads separate maker labels/self-descriptions, never makes joint labels or uses reserved geographic details.
- **E48, reserved Route Diagram and Dating Note, invented.** Coastal find: 10,000 years ago. Inland passage usable by 13,000; coastal travel also possible then. "Which route claim does this date support?" "What evidence would help choose between routes?" Key: presence on that coast by 10,000, no unique route; both possible. Dated route-linked finds, especially before inland usability, could help distinguish possibilities, not prove a whole journey. Accept relevant alternatives with reasons. Wrong: coastal find proves boat arrival (B); inland opening guarantees use (C); preference decides (H).

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: theory, migration, Beringia, corridor, origin account. Theory means an evidence-based explanation open to revision; migration means people moving between places. Frame: "This supports ___, but leaves ___ uncertain." Target 160 independent teaching words; all print within DEFAULTS. Neutral reading, tactile/large diagrams and child-directed dictation preserve reasoning. Extension critiques a further supplied reason.
