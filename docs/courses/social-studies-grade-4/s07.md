**M, constructed movement maps:** print two labelled diagrams, with the same river, rail line, farm area, port and town sites. In the nineteenth-century diagram, arrows show families moving toward river towns and grain traveling downstream; a newspaper travels along the rail line. **S, constructed settlement diagrams:** in an environmental case, river flooding makes lowland houses unusable; arrows lead to an uphill district and a neighboring city, whose temporary housing fills. In a technological case, a flood wall protecting a riverside district fails after a construction defect; arrows lead from those houses to another town. Key: the diagrams show displacement and connections, but not each person's reasons or eventual return. No invented counts are represented as historical data.

**P, constructed council record:** "Floodwater blocked the only shelter entrance. Residents asked the council for another route. Key: restricted access and costs are challenges; public input and more usable shelter access are opportunities. Do not claim step-free access serves only one kind of person.

**J, unseen New Zealand summary:** after earthquakes damaged central Christchurch, the city council asked residents what rebuilding should include, and a draft recovery plan followed. **Teacher notes:** teach slavery as forced labor and denied freedom, with resistance, skills and lives built. Never describe contributions as payment for rights or as making colonization beneficial. Native governments and contemporary people appear beside historical examples. Use communities' own names and public materials; omit sacred knowledge and craft imitation. Spanish missions include Native labor and coercion. Exclusion targeted Chinese labor immigration in 1882, with exceptions and restrictions on citizenship; it was not a ban on every Asian person. Japanese American incarceration denied freedom to people targeted for ancestry, including citizens; teach it with the later federal acknowledgment and apology. On 141, the African American soldiers were the Buffalo Soldiers: they built posts and towns and also fought in the army's wars against the Apache and other nations. Teach both. Do not debate whether a child or community deserved rights. No role play of exclusion, forced labor, removal or confinement, and no slurs from primary documents.
