"Explain how Texas changed between these dates, including one stated reason for independence." Key: declared separation from Mexico in 1836, joined United States in 1845; some settlers sought to preserve enslavement. Meets: "Independence came first, then annexation; preserving slavery was one reason for independence." Wrong: "Independence, annexation and war all happened in 1836." (F); "Enslavement was freely chosen work." (I). Shape: chronological political explanation plus stated motive. Teaching uses separate date/status matches and single-cause annotations, never this integrated explanation.
- **E89, War Account Labels.** Original factual summaries, no invented quotations: Polk, May 11, 1846 message to Congress, reports fighting on land he calls United States soil; Ramón A. Gutiérrez, later historical essay, locates clash between Nueces/Rio Grande, claimed by both countries. Before printing, verify essay's publication date; unavailable year becomes "later historian, not eyewitness", never guessed date. "Compare these accounts using their maker and date labels." "Explain one agreement and one claim needing a boundary map." Key: both describe fighting; exclusive United States soil assertion needs testing against disputed claims. Contemporary government argument differs from later scholarship. Wrong: "Every statement is equally established because both are accounts." (G). Shape: source-labeled agreement plus spatial claim requiring corroboration. Teaching matches makers/purposes separately, no paired agreement/map-needed response; summary selections differ from Q7's quotation.
- **E90, Two Cause Statements.** Fresh paraphrases of documented positions: Mexico began the fighting; United States troops entered land both countries claimed. New locator inset: clash between Nueces/Rio Grande, differently patterned claim lines. No evaluative answer in captions. "What does this record settle about the fighting's location?" "What question about the war's causes remains open from this record alone?" Key: clash lies in overlapping national claims; record alone cannot establish sole responsibility/all motives. Meets: "The clash was in disputed land; the map alone cannot settle sole responsibility." Wrong: "Polk's wording settles ownership and blame." (G); "Later treaty payment proves a freely chosen sale." (N). Shape: spatial finding plus causal limit from fresh pair/map. Practice tests individual causal assertions against separate details, never this finding-plus-limit response. Further evidence can test rival explanations; they are not automatically equally plausible.
