## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What can a map tell us, and what does it leave out?"
- **Hook, day 1:** compare Physical Map A and Political Map B of North America. Both are useful, but neither answers every question. Whom might an omission affect?
- **Co-write on day 1:** How do map parts help us locate features (days 2 to 4)? How far apart are places (6)? Which scale suits our question (8)? What do political boundaries mean (11)? How does the globe's grid locate places (13)?
- **Closing, day 14:** write one paragraph answering the compelling question with evidence from Physical Map A and Political Map B. Students may emphasize distance, boundaries or missing human detail.
- **Teaching:** model reading a source's maker and date before using it; audience and purpose come in unit 4. Model measuring on day 5 and a different claim before day 14's writing; keep the full paragraph frame. Students choose their own evidence and challenge a partner without a teacher model. Revise the question board after each supporting answer.

- **MA.4.T1.1:** Use a physical map of North America with directions, scale, key, and title to locate major rivers, lakes, oceans, mountains, deserts, and other physical features. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

- Q2 looks for correct locating, nested terms and a nation distinguished from a state. Meets: "Madison is a city in Dane County in Wisconsin. - Q3 looks for both cultural and environmental locations, two wider-scale locations, a justified scale choice and an omission. Meets: "E puts the library west of the park beside Lake Monona. **Teacher note, place names (as of November 2026):** since 2025, United States federal use names the Gulf of Mexico the "Gulf of America" and Denali "Mount McKinley" (Executive Order 14172); Denali National Park and Preserve keeps its name, and Mexico and most other countries keep "Gulf of Mexico". Label the gulf "Gulf of Mexico (Gulf of America in United States federal use since 2025)" and the peak "Denali (Mount McKinley in United States federal use since 2025)". State both as facts of naming; do not ask students which is right. Map-makers print both labels.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** Printed map sets, a ruler and movable paper bars fit the intended comparisons; no interactive spec or TOOL-ASKS.md is needed. Supply pencils, rulers, paper strips, a globe or labelled ball, physical and political maps with insets, source cards and a record sheet. Print every map, source and task in black and white; one projector can enlarge them. At home, the adult takes the partner's part. No student timer is shown.

Read map labels together and source cards aloud, at most 60 words each in grade 3 to 4 language. Teach scale, province, nation and evidence with examples. Frame: "Map __ places __ beside __, so __; it cannot tell __." Extension compares two defensible map choices for different readers.
