<!-- skel -->
Grade 4: North America, Its Land and Peoples. Unit 1, North America on the map (days 1 to 16): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 4.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (1, 2, 3), application 1 (4). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

> - **Co-write on day 1:** How do map parts help us locate features (days 2 to 4)? How far apart are places (5-6)? Which scale suits our question (7-8)? What do political boundaries mean (9-11)? How does the globe's grid locate places (12-13)?

Maps and labelled reference cards remain.
<!-- /skel -->

- **Shares.** D1.1: "Explain" importance to others; D1.2: "Identify" ideas open to different interpretations, day 1. MA.4.T1.1: "Use" map parts to "locate" water and land features, days 2 to 4. Measurement belongs to section 1.2.
- **Review, in ledger words.** Grade 3 u01-3 day 7: legend (the map key, the standard's word); title, north arrow, cardinal directions; bar length and whole bars. Its day 8: physical feature (a land or water place that nature made). Grade 3 u01-4 day 11: political map shows borders; physical map shows land and water. Grade 3 u01-2 day 4: hemisphere (a half of the globe).
- **Connections.** Day 1 reviews big question and supporting question from grade 3 u01-1 and u07-1. The question board returns by name from grade 3 u01-1 day 2. Day 3 reviews region from grade 3 u01-4 day 11, extending grouping beyond states before day 4 applies it.
- **Teacher explanation.** Key symbols, boundaries and feature shapes do different jobs. A missing label cannot establish absence. Present lands and ancestral homelands need distinct captions. Inuit are an Indigenous people; Nunavut is a territory, not a synonym for Inuit or a Native nation. No sacred content or identity role play.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 labels, key and direction, jointly with u01-2, on day 15 in s01 field 5.

**Records.** Every child submits paper evidence. Use one scale throughout the unit: shown, shown with help, not yet seen, one cell per component. Access supports do not count as content help. This replaces the unit plan's conflicting scales. Many means more than 10 of 30. Return each exit next day with one written cue; prioritize one gap per child. Six oral observations supplement, never replace, papers.

- **E1, Exit Visitor Case, invented.** "The student will walk to school. The traveler will compare countries. Write a supporting question about their different map needs. Explain why its answer matters to both." Teacher-judged: a relevant smaller question, both needs, and two defensible meanings of useful. Meets: "Which details does each reader need? Paths help the student; country locations help the traveler." Does not: "Which map is prettiest? Only my favorite map is useful." Wrong: only one use allowed (J); only personal preference offered (M), separate responses. Save E1 until day 14.
  Shape: independently generate a question and justify two readers' uses. Practice sorts existing questions; no practice pairs these readers or asks for this combined response.
- **E2.** "Mark Hudson Bay and Lake Winnipeg on Exit Water Map. Use its key to explain which feature is a lake." Key: both correctly located; Lake Winnipeg has the key's lake marking. Wrong: "The name alone proves it" (A); calling the bay a lake (Q).
  Shape: locate two named features, then explain a category using a key. Practice repairs symbol mismatches and traces single features; no paired locating-and-category explanation.
- **E3.** "Mark Sierra Madre Occidental and Mojave Desert on Exit Physical Map. Explain why missing nation labels cannot prove that land is empty." Key: correct range and desert positions; maps select information, so omission cannot show nobody lives there. Wrong: a boundary mistaken for the range symbol (A); "No label means nobody lives there" (K).
  Shape: two landforms plus an inference about omitted inhabitants. Practice uses source-caption corrections separately from landform matching; never this combined task or either exit feature.
- **E4.** "Mark the Canadian Shield and Amazon Basin on Exit Hemisphere Map. Name the continent containing each region." Key: Shield, North America; Basin, South America, with correct region placement. Wrong: naming a country as the continent (E).
  Shape: locate two outlined physical regions and assign continents. Practice assembles broad mountain-belt strips without a region-to-continent table. The new paired region-boundary case differs from grade 2's feature reminder.

**Q1 Shape:** name-bank labels, key explanation, rotated direction and measured distance on one reserved packet. No teaching sheet combines these operations. C and its five named features stay reserved; rotated practice uses arrow repair, never P-above-Q with north right. Key recomputed cold: north right makes page-up west. Measurement is u01-2's share.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Printed map sets, a ruler and movable paper bars fit the intended comparisons; no interactive spec or TOOL-ASKS.md is needed. Supply pencils, centimetre rulers, a globe or labelled ball, physical and political maps with insets, source cards and a record sheet. Print every map, source and task in black and white; one projector can enlarge them. At home, the adult takes the partner's part. No student timer is shown.
>
> Read map labels together and source cards aloud, at most 60 words each in grade 3 to 4 language. Teach scale, province, nation and evidence with examples. Frame: "Map __ places __ beside __, so __; it cannot tell __." Extension compares two defensible map choices for different readers.
<!-- /skel -->

- **Carried.** Return the four-part map card from grade 3 u01-3 day 7: title, legend, north arrow, scale bar. Keep it available; day 5 extends its scale. The teacher maintains the question board; students keep answers.
- **Ideal instrument, days 2 to 4.** Legible monochrome maps with distinct feature patterns and an index. Print partly fits until outlines and labels are checked against source data. A projection cannot show each child's locating. Tools: None; no spec.
- **Fallback.** Printed nation-authored paraphrases replace a live page, visitor or photograph. Use Navajo Nation History, Cherokee Nation About and Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami About Canadian Inuit, linked in s01. If a local page is unavailable, use these named sources. No nation boundary is guessed.
- **Without equipment.** Board outlines plus individual traced copies, retaining key and arrow. A ball replaces the globe.

## 6. Days

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Open "What can a map tell us, and what does it leave out?" and write the supporting questions together
- note: retrieval task on grade 3 ss3-map-skills (map parts, whole-bar scale)
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **7 min. Hook.** Compare A and B; show makers and dates. Pairs discuss one visible difference, two 45-second turns simultaneously. Teacher posts the compelling question.
- **6 min. Retrieval.** Each child marks the fresh trail map's four parts, dotted-line meaning and four 50-meter bars: 200 meters. North arrow left makes page-up east. Ungraded; note A, B or C for day 2.
- **10 min. Supporting questions.** Review the question board routine. Children sort question strips into needed information and unrelated information; class co-writes five board questions, without the exit's two-reader explanation.
- **12 min. Supported work.** Each child annotates one useful detail on A or B. Hear a rotating six, one 45-second ask each, within five minutes; record names. Everyone submits the annotation. Teacher posts answer-days 4, 6, 8, 11 and 13.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E1, alone on paper.

Teacher holds A, B and board; children hold four-part cards. Tool: None. By hand: retrieval, sorting, annotation, E1. Say invented people and places are made up.

## Day 2

- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Read a physical map of North America with title, key and directions: its rivers, lakes and oceans
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)

- **5 min. Return and repair.** One dominant E1 or retrieval gap; others read written cues. Fresh attempt collected.
- **10 min. Map parts.** Review the four-part card. Teacher repairs a swapped legend entry and north arrow on practice maps. Trace Mississippi River and Pacific Ocean singly on A, reading the index; review reminders, not checks.

## Day 3

- goal: Find North America's mountains and deserts; name the Native nations whose lands the map shows, in the present

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Dominant E2 gap; fresh symbol attempt.
- **10 min. Land and living peoples.** Trace Rocky Mountains and Sonoran Desert on A. Introduce a physical region as an area grouped by shared land features, extending grade 3's region. Read three credited present-day cards: Navajo Nation, Cherokee Nation and Inuit of Inuit Nunangat, including Nunavut. No sacred details or removal narrative.
- **15 min. Supported work.** Children correct a past-only caption using a taught nation's account. Separately match landform silhouettes to categories. Teacher meets one repair group for five minutes. Captions distinguish current lands from homelands; never make one equal the other.
- **5 min. Question board.** Pairs compare one caption correction, two 45-second turns. Collect every corrected caption.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E3, new map, alone.

Teacher holds source cards; children hold A and silhouettes. Tool: None. By hand: traces, tags, caption, E3. Facts await source rows, field 11.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E4, new region outlines, alone.

Teacher holds globe and hemisphere map; pairs hold strips. Tool: None. By hand: strip, annotation, E4. Nothing new on this application day.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 1: "Which detail could answer this board question?" Day 2: "What changes when the arrow turns?" Day 3: "What can this caption establish today?" Written task: day 3's caption correction. Look for a present-tense statement supported by the named source and no invented boundary or claim.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 1: grade 3 whole bars and four map parts. Day 2: the legend trace from grade 3 u01-3 day 7. Day 3: physical features and region from grade 3 u01-3 and u01-4. Day 4: hemisphere from grade 3 u01-2 and the named grade 2 mountain-belt reminder. No prescribed fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: legend, physical feature, region, hemisphere, omission. Frame: "The key marks ___ as ___." Read source cards aloud, at most 60 words each. Plan at most 180 student words daily besides labels, and 350 including labels. Use raised patterns, large labels and movable tags. A scribe records pointing or dictation without interpretation. Extension: explain why two keys can use different marks for one feature.

## 10. Two rooms

At home, the adult holds A and B and takes the partner turn; retain the paper exit. One projector enlarges a map while every child has a copy. No journey or personal home map.
