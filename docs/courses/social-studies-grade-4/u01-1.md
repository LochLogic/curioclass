# Section 1.1: Reading physical maps

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-4/1/s/1.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-4 u01-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Grade 4 social studies, [Unit 1: North America on the map](s01.md). A plan, not a lesson: timing and materials are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Shares.** D1.1: "Explain" importance to others; D1.2: "Identify" ideas open to different interpretations, day 1. MA.4.T1.1: "Use" map parts to "locate" water and land features, days 2 to 4. Measurement belongs to section 1.2.
- **Review, in ledger words.** Grade 3 u01-3 day 7: legend (the map key, the standard's word); title, north arrow, cardinal directions; bar length and whole bars. Its day 8: physical feature (a land or water place that nature made). Grade 3 u01-4 day 11: political map shows borders; physical map shows land and water. Grade 3 u01-2 day 4: hemisphere (a half of the globe).
- **Connections.** Day 1 reviews big question and supporting question from grade 3 u01-1 and u07-1. The importance and interpretation term rows are planned only. Teach those ideas independently through two map readers; never claim those terms were learned before. The question board returns by name from grade 3 u01-1 day 2: five supporting questions posted with their answer days. Day 3 reviews region from grade 3 u01-4 day 11, extending grouping beyond states before day 4 applies it.
- **Teacher explanation.** Key symbols, boundaries and feature shapes do different jobs. A missing label cannot establish absence. Present lands and ancestral homelands need distinct captions. Inuit are an Indigenous people; Nunavut is a territory, not a synonym for Inuit or a Native nation. No sacred content or identity role play.

## 2. Where it sits


No separate readiness check. Day 1 retrieves ss3-map-skills; day 2 holds its repair and E1 feedback. E4 goes to day 5's Return and repair in u01-2. No earlier unit or section exists in this course.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 labels, key and direction, jointly with u01-2, on day 15 in s01 field 5.

**Records.** Every child submits paper evidence. Use one scale throughout the unit: shown, shown with help, not yet seen, one cell per component. Access supports do not count as content help. This replaces the unit plan's conflicting scales. Many means more than 10 of 30. Return each exit next day with one written cue; prioritize one gap per child. Six oral observations supplement, never replace, papers.

- **E1, Exit Visitor Case, invented, s01-X1.** Context: "The student will walk to school. The traveler will compare countries." Ask: "Write a supporting question about their different map needs." Accept a relevant smaller question, such as "Which details does each reader need on a map?" Wrong: an unrelated question about pencil color (M).
  Shape: generate a supporting question for two new map users. Teaching sorts existing questions and annotates information; no model authors a two-reader question.
- **s01-P1-E, separate paper evidence.** "Explain why the answer matters to both readers." Meets: "Paths help the student find a walking route. Country locations help the traveler compare countries." Does not: "Only my favorite map is useful." Wrong: only one defensible use (J); preference without another reader's need (M), separate responses.
  Shape: explain two readers' different uses. Practice uses single-reader information matches, never this paired explanation. Save both papers for day 14.
- **s01-P2-E, held paper locator, day 2.** "Mark Hudson Bay and Lake Winnipeg on Exit Water Map." Key: broad inlet 1 and enclosed hatched water 2. Wrong: labels the inlet as the lake (Q).
  Shape: locate two named water features on an unseen map. Practice traces one taught feature at a time; neither exit feature is a practice case.
- **E2, s01-X2.** "Use its key to explain which feature is a lake." Key: Lake Winnipeg matches the enclosed, diagonally hatched lake symbol. Naming it from its name alone does not show key use. Wrong: appearance alone proves its kind (A).
  Shape: justify a category using the exit key. Practice corrects mismatched tags and traces features; it does not produce a key-based category explanation.
- **E3, s01-X3.** "Mark Sierra Madre Occidental and Mojave Desert on Exit Physical Map." Key: range at patch 1 in western Mexico; desert at patch 2 farther northwest. Wrong: marks a boundary instead of the mountain patch (A).
  Shape: locate two named landforms. Practice matches silhouettes and traces single features; reserve both exit landforms.
- **s01-P3-E, separate paper evidence.** "Explain why missing nation labels cannot prove that land is empty." Key: the map selects landform information and omits nation labels. Missing labels cannot establish whether people live there. Wrong: "No label means nobody lives there" (K).
  Shape: infer the limit of omitted labels. Practice corrects a past-tense caption using a named source, never reasons from omitted inhabitants. Collect s01-P3-2's nation names as practice, not another exit.
- **s01-P4-E, held paper locator, day 4.** "Mark the Canadian Shield and Amazon Basin on Exit Hemisphere Map." Key: Shield around the northern inland sea; Basin in northern South America. Wrong: treats a country outline as the region (E).
  Shape: locate two outlined physical regions. Practice assembles mountain-belt strips; reserve the two exit regions.
- **E4, s01-X4.** "Name the continent containing each region." Key: Canadian Shield, North America; Amazon Basin, South America. Wrong: names a country as the continent (E).
  Shape: supply continent names for two mapped regions. Practice compares belt shapes and never fills a region-to-continent record.

**Q1 component shapes, s01 field 5:** A1a labels five numbered features from an index; practice uses tracing/tag repair, never numbered feature labeling. A1b explains a line category from a key; practice repairs a supplied mismatch rather than writes that explanation. A1c gives a direction and arrow-based reason; practice corrects supplied arrow tags, never answers P-from-Q. Reserve Map C and its five-feature combination. North right makes page-up west. A1d belongs to u01-2.

**Q4 other-reader Shape:** A4c generates another reader's different question and needed map. Teaching matches supplied single-reader needs to visible details; never model that complete response. Accept any different, supported reader/question/map combination.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Return the four-part map card from grade 3 u01-3 day 7: title, legend, north arrow, scale bar. Keep it available; day 5 extends its scale. The teacher maintains the question board; students keep answers.
- **Ideal instrument, days 2 to 4.** Legible monochrome maps with distinct feature patterns and an index. Print partly fits until outlines and labels are checked against source data. A projection cannot show each child's locating. Tools: None; no spec.
- **Fallback.** Printed nation-authored paraphrases replace a live page, visitor or photograph. Use Navajo Nation History, Cherokee Nation About and Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami About Canadian Inuit, linked in s01. If a local page is unavailable, use these named sources. No nation boundary is guessed.
- **Without equipment.** Board outlines plus individual traced copies, retaining key and arrow. A ball replaces the globe.

## 6. Days

Each day block ends in individual paper evidence. Read printed stems once without solving them. Public sharing is optional. Pair turns happen simultaneously across 15 pairs; two 45-second turns take 90 seconds, not 45 minutes. A six-child hearing takes 4.5 minutes within a named five-minute slot. Collect the hand work from everyone else. Give repair groups one priority gap; all other children use written cues. A five-minute group includes a brief contrast and an individual fresh attempt, not four long oral turns. Source cards are paraphrases, credited to their row URLs; made-up people, places and pins are labeled and named aloud as made up.

## Day 1

- **7 min. Hook.** Compare A and B; show makers and dates. Pairs discuss one visible difference, two 45-second turns simultaneously. Teacher posts the compelling question. Explain importance and different meanings of useful without relying on planned-only terminology.
- **6 min. Retrieval.** Each child marks the fresh trail map's four parts, dotted-line meaning and four 50-meter bars: 200 meters. North arrow left makes page-up east. Ungraded; note A, B or C for day 2.
- **10 min. Supporting questions.** Review the question board routine. Children sort supplied question strips into needed and unrelated information. Teacher records the class's selected questions on the board, with answer days; no two-reader question is modeled.
- **12 min. Supported work.** Each child annotates one useful detail on A or B. Hear names 1 to 6, one 45-second ask each, within five minutes; record names. Everyone submits the annotation. Teacher posts answer-days 4, 6, 8, 11 and 13.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E1, two minutes; s01-P1-E, three minutes. Both alone on paper.

Teacher holds A, B and board; children hold four-part cards. Tool: None. By hand: retrieval, sorting, annotation, E1. Say invented people and places are made up.

Sources: 1A 1B 1C. Hand-back in: none. Out: E1 and P1-E to day 2; save copies for day 14. Materials/print: A/B, trail map, question strips, response sheet, individual paper for each child.

**Shape:** E1 generates a supporting question; P1-E explains two readers' needs. Practice sorts supplied question strips and annotates single-reader information; no model produces either two-reader response. Reserve the Visitor Case.

## Day 2

- **5 min. Return and repair.** One dominant E1 or retrieval gap; others read written cues. Fresh attempt collected.
- **10 min. Map parts.** Review the four-part card. Teacher repairs a swapped legend entry and north arrow on practice maps. Trace Mississippi River and Pacific Ocean singly on A, reading the index; review reminders, not checks.
- **15 min. Supported work.** Pairs repair mismatched feature tags, then trace one water feature each. At most three simultaneous pair exchanges, 90 seconds each. Reserve five minutes for a repair group of up to four, then five for oral observations of names 7 to 12, 45 seconds each. Remaining five: independent practice. Everyone submits paper; overlay follows the attempt.
- **5 min. Question board.** Each child writes one key-based observation. Collect all; hear at most two brief volunteers.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Held locator s01-P2-E, two minutes; E2 explanation, three minutes, alone.

Teacher holds A and overlay; pairs hold maps and pattern tags. Tool: None. By hand: tracing, repair, observation, E2.

Sources: 1A 1B 1C; Hudson Bay label source to check at data. Hand-back in: E1/retrieval cues. Out: E2 and locator to day 3. Materials/print: A, symbol/arrow slips, held Water Map, response sheet, individual paper for each child.

**Shape:** P2-E locates two named water features; E2 explains a lake category from the key. Practice traces single features and repairs supplied tags, without paired locating or a generated category justification. Reserve Hudson Bay and Lake Winnipeg.

## Day 3

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Dominant E2 gap; fresh symbol attempt.
- **10 min. Land and living peoples.** Trace Rocky Mountains and Sonoran Desert on A. Introduce a physical region as an area grouped by shared land features, extending grade 3's region. Read three credited present-day cards: Navajo Nation, Cherokee Nation and Inuit of Inuit Nunangat, including Nunavut. No sacred details or removal narrative.
- **15 min. Supported work.** Children correct a past-only caption using a taught nation's account. Separately match landform silhouettes to categories. Five minutes: one repair group. Five: hear names 13 to 18, one 45-second observation each. Five: independent matching; all submit paper. Captions distinguish current lands from homelands; never make one equal the other.
- **5 min. Question board.** Pairs compare one caption correction, two 45-second turns. Collect every corrected caption.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E3 locating, two minutes; s01-P3-E omission inference, three minutes, alone.

Teacher holds source cards; children hold A and silhouettes. Tool: None. By hand: traces, tags, caption, E3. Sources: 1A, 3G, 3C, 3I, 3N; exit 3D and the inspected 3G placement. See field 11 for the 3S timeout.

Sources: 1A 3G 3N 3C 3I; exit 3D/3G. Hand-back in: E2/locator cues. Out: E3, omission paper and practice caption/name record to day 4. Materials/print: A, credited cards, silhouettes, held Physical Map, response sheet, individual paper for each child.

**Shape:** E3 locates two named landforms; P3-E infers a limit from omitted nation labels. Practice matches silhouettes and corrects a sourced past-tense caption, without either exit form. Reserve the Sierra Madre Occidental and Mojave Desert.

## Day 4

- **5 min. Return and repair.** E3's dominant gap; fresh selective-map or category attempt.
- **8 min. Retrieve regions.** Return day 3's physical grouping and grade 3's hemisphere. Identify the Western Hemisphere on a globe. Revisit grade 2 u02-1 day 18's Rocky Mountains and Andes as a named reminder, never exit content.
- **17 min. Supported work.** Pairs assemble a north-to-south strip of western mountain belts using map outlines. Compare how mountain belts appear in the two Americas. Each pair has two 90-second exchanges. Teacher holds five minutes for one repair group.
- **5 min. Share evidence.** Hear names 19 to 24 for 45 seconds each. Collect everyone else's strip and one annotated difference; update the board.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** Held locator s01-P4-E, two minutes; E4 continent record, three minutes, alone.

Teacher holds globe and hemisphere map; pairs hold strips. Tool: None. By hand: strip, annotation, E4. Nothing new on this application day.

Sources: 1A 3G 3N; exit 4S 4B. Hand-back in: E3 and caption/name cues. Out: E4/locator to day 5. Materials/print: globe/outline, belt strips, held Hemisphere Map, response sheet, individual paper for each child.

**Shape:** P4-E locates two physical regions; E4 supplies their continents. Practice overlays and compares mountain-belt strips, without locating the two exit regions or completing a region-to-continent record. Reserve the Shield and Basin.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 1: "Which detail could answer this board question?" Day 2: "What changes when the arrow turns?" Day 3: "What can this caption establish today?" Written task: day 3's caption correction. Look for a present-tense statement supported by the named source and no invented boundary or claim.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 1: grade 3 whole bars and four map parts. Day 2: the legend trace from grade 3 u01-3 day 7. Day 3: physical features and region from grade 3 u01-3 and u01-4. Day 4: hemisphere from grade 3 u01-2 and the named grade 2 mountain-belt reminder. No prescribed fluency target.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: legend, physical feature, region, hemisphere, omission. Frame: "The key marks ___ as ___." Read source cards aloud, at most 60 words each. Plan at most 180 student words daily besides labels, and 350 including labels. Use raised patterns, large labels and movable tags. A scribe records pointing or dictation without interpretation. Extension: explain why two keys can use different marks for one feature.

## 10. Two rooms

At home, the adult holds A and B and takes the partner turn; retain the paper exit. One projector enlarges a map while every child has a copy. No journey or personal home map.
