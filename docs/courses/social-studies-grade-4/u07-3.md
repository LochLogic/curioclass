## 1. Standards and practices


Four regional codes carry "Explain" effects and both government/resident responses. Cover Southeast hurricanes/floods, Midwest tornadoes/floods/drought, Southwest hurricanes/drought, West drought/wildfire. Include Southwest hurricanes on 148 despite its shorter day label. D2.Geo.9 carries "Analyze" settlement/migration after environmental and technological catastrophes on 149/150. D2.Geo.12 connects receiving places on 150, continuing in 7.4.

Teacher distinguishes hazard, exposure and human decisions: disasters need not have one cause. No graphic losses, casualty totals, simulations or personal disaster accounts. Adults handle response/clearance; pupils analyze public records. A science lesson's planned date is not proof it happened.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Owned:** H1 to H4 and M2 settlement/migration. Cold H solutions follow the four invented records, not actual-event claims. M2 needs both events' housing loss, outgoing movement and receiving settlements; distant effects also belong to 7.4. One event cannot establish the whole indicator.

**Shape, H1/H2/H3/H4:** construct effect-plus-government-plus-resident explanations from unfamiliar records. Avoid complete three-link sentences or three-column response chains in practice; locate discrete evidence, compare durations and reject mismatched actions instead. **Shape, M2:** compare paired settlement diagrams for two catastrophe types, receiving places and limits. Avoid paired settlement diagrams, flood walls, defective water defenses, uphill housing and downstream water deliveries throughout practice. Investigate one event at a time with static inventories and separate testimony fragments.

All exits are individual paper, collected from thirty. One daily component scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Equivalent causal explanations accepted. All constructed records, towns and households are printed and said to be invented for practice.

- **E146, held paper exit.** "Made-up Southeast: A storm damaged drinking-water pipes. Officials supplied clean water. Residents delivered it where requested. Explain how both responses addressed the damage." Key: Broken pipes disrupted safe water. Officials supplied an alternative; residents helped it reach people needing water. **Shape:** Connect fresh damage with two actors' responses.
- **E147, held paper exit.** "Made-up Midwest: A dry growing season reduced harvests. Officials offered farm assistance. Residents organized food collections. Explain how both responses addressed the effect." Key: Water shortage reduced crops. Assistance addressed farm losses; food collections addressed resulting food needs. **Shape:** Explain slow hazard effects and two responses.
- **E148, held paper exit.** "Made-up Southwest: Hurricane flooding closed a highway. Officials marked a detour; residents staffed supplies. Explain how both responses addressed the effect." Key: Flooding blocked travel. Officials redirected travel; residents supported supply access during disruption. **Shape:** One fresh regional effect and two-response explanation. Second row: "Made-up West: Low reservoir water closed a boat ramp and reduced available water. Officials shared water with a neighboring town; residents used the shared supply. Explain what these responses addressed and what remained unresolved." Key: Sharing and using supplies addressed limited water. Nothing shows the ramp reopened or drought ended. **Shape:** Fresh supply response and unresolved-effect explanation.
- **E149, held paper exit.** "Made up: A storm hit a town. A bridge support had a construction defect. The bridge collapsed, closing the hospital road. Why does "only weather" leave out a cause?" Key: The built defect also contributed to failure. Storm and structural weakness both mattered. Road loss disrupted a connection; legal blame is not established. **Shape:** Critique a single-cause claim with fresh built-system evidence.
- **E150, held paper exit.** "Made-up Spill Diagram. Before: occupied district; two towns have available halls. After: spill closes district; arrows lead to both halls, now temporary housing. Explain settlement changes, including receiving places." Key: People left unusable district housing. Both receiving towns gained temporary residents. Permanence and return remain unknown. **Shape:** Infer origin and receiving changes from unfamiliar paired diagrams.
