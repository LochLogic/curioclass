**Owned:** H1 to H4 and M2 settlement/migration. Cold H solutions follow the four invented records, not actual-event claims. M2 needs both events' housing loss, outgoing movement and receiving settlements; distant effects also belong to 7.4. One event cannot establish the whole indicator.

**Shape, H1/H2/H3/H4:** construct effect-plus-government-plus-resident explanations from unfamiliar records. Avoid complete three-link sentences or three-column response chains in practice; locate discrete evidence, compare durations and reject mismatched actions instead. **Shape, M2:** compare paired settlement diagrams for two catastrophe types, receiving places and limits. Avoid paired settlement diagrams, flood walls, defective water defenses, uphill housing and downstream water deliveries throughout practice. All exits are individual paper, collected from thirty. One daily component scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Equivalent causal explanations accepted. All constructed records, towns and households are printed and said to be invented for practice.

- **E146, Water Record.** "In the Southeast, a storm damaged drinking-water pipes. Officials supplied clean water. Residents delivered it where requested. Explain how the responses addressed the storm's damage." Key: pipes failed, disrupting safe water; public provision and requested deliveries met that need. Meets: "Officials supplied water because pipes failed; residents carried supplies to people needing them." Wrong: "A storm happened" (S); "The county did everything alone" (O). **Shape:** connect damage and two actors' responses; avoid water pipes, water delivery and complete response accounts in practice.
- **E147, Maize Record.** "A dry Midwest growing season reduced harvests. Officials offered farm assistance. Residents organized food collections. Explain how both responses addressed the effect." Key: prolonged water shortage reduced crops; official help addressed farm losses and collections addressed food needs. Meets: "Less water reduced harvests; farm assistance and food collections addressed resulting losses and needs." Wrong: "A sudden windstorm caused the dry season" (M); "There was drought" (S). **Shape:** explain slow hazard, crop effect and both responses; avoid harvest loss, farm aid, food collections and whole response explanations in practice.
- **E148, Regional Records.** "Southwest hurricane flooding closed a highway. Officials marked a detour. Residents staffed a supply center." West: "A low reservoir closed a boat ramp and reduced available water. Town officials shared water with a neighboring town. Residents used the shared supply." Ask: "Explain the effect and responses in the Southwest case. Explain the effect and responses in the West case." Key: flooding blocked highway use; public detour and resident support addressed travel/supply needs. Low water limited access/use; sharing/using supplies addressed water needs, not rain or reopening the ramp. Meets: "Flooding closed the highway; officials diverted travel and residents staffed supplies. Low water closed boat access; officials shared water and residents used that supply." Wrong: "One national government did everything" (O); "Hurricane and reservoir are the full explanation" (S); "Sharing water creates rain" (T). **Shape:** two regional effect/actor explanations; avoid paired regional response tasks, highways/detours, boat ramps and shared-water cases. Clarify the West record's supply response without claiming it restores boat access.
- **E149, Bridge Record.** "A storm hit a town. Its bridge support had a construction defect. The bridge collapsed, closing the road to a hospital town. Why does 'only weather' leave out a cause?" Key: built weakness and storm both contributed; defect affected the crossing's failure and regional connection. Meets: "The support defect also helped cause collapse, interrupting the connection between towns." Wrong: "Human construction could not matter" (C). **Shape:** critique a single-cause claim in a new technological record; practice never critiques 'only weather', uses defective bridges or produces missing-cause explanations.
- **E150, Spill Diagram.** Before: occupied housing district; two nearby towns have available halls. After: spill closes district; arrows lead to both towns' halls, now temporary housing. Ask: "Explain how settlement and movement changed, including the places receiving households." Key: households leave unusable housing; origin loses residents and both receiving towns house displaced people. Permanence/return unknown. Meets: "People left the closed district; both nearby towns now provide temporary homes." Wrong: "Where people live did not change" (N); "Only the spill site was affected" (E). **Shape:** infer connected settlement changes from paired diagrams; practice uses no spill, paired settlement diagram or integrated origin/destination explanation.
