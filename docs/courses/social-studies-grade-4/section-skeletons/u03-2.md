| 58 | MA.4.T2.1 | Evaluate routes and dated evidence of early presence. | Q1: A reports White Sands footprints dated to about 21,000 to 23,000 years ago. B reports a study finding food-supporting conditions in an inland corridor by about 12,600 years ago. On the supplied timeline, place both findings beside the printed 15,000-year marker. Compare a Beringia crossing followed by that corridor with a Pacific coastal route. What fits, and what remains unknown? Then, in an invented diagram, a pit was dug through the layers after the footprints were made: does depth alone date them? Answer: the footprints are earlier than both the marker and B's corridor conditions. That corridor at that time cannot explain those earlier people; coastal travel remains a possibility, not a proven journey. Beringia could be part of either route, and B does not exclude later inland travel or every earlier possibility. No: a later pit mixes layers, so depth alone does not date them. Wrong: larger years-ago numbers are later (A); a date proves first arrival (B); a land bridge proves a usable southward corridor (C); boats exclude Beringia (J); the deepest object is always oldest (K). | section 3.2 |

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** No spec or TOOL-ASKS.md is needed. Supply printed packets, rulers, timeline strips, printed maps with a scale bar, pencils and record sheets. Every map, source and task has a printed version; a shared screen is optional. At home the adult is the discussion partner. No student timer is shown.
>
> Read captions aloud, at most 70 words each, in grade 3 to 4 language. Teach artifact, archaeologist, theory, migration, descendant and preserve. Frame: "Source __ shows __; this suggests __, but cannot establish __." Allow oral rehearsal and dictation. Extension compares two supported interpretations.
