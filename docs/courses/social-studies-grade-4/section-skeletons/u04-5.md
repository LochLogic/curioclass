## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 75, 76, 78): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 76, 78): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **MA.4.T3.1** (days 78): Explain how historians studying European voyages to the Americas use archaeology, maps, illustrations, and texts of the time, which are called primary sources.
- **MA.4.T3.2** (days 78): Explain who the Vikings were and describe evidence of their early encounters with Native Peoples on the North American Atlantic coast.
- **MA.4.T3.3** (days 78): Trace European explorations of North America and the Caribbean in the 1400s and 1500s, evaluate why nations sent them (empire, Asian trade routes, colonies, resources), and make a timeline of landings and conquests.
- **D2.His.1.3-5** (days 78): Make and use a time-ordered list of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time.
- **D2.His.9.3-5** (days 78): Summarize how different kinds of sources are used to explain past events.
- **D2.His.11.3-5** (days 78): Infer who a source was meant for and why it was made, using clues in the source itself.
- skill ss4-exploration (grade 4)
- skill ss4-primary-source (grade 4)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Closing, day 76:** answer the compelling question in two linked paragraphs using the Voyage Accounts and Living Peoples packets. Defend an emphasis on conquest, exchange or continuing Native agency with two named sources.
> - **Teaching:** model sourcing on 62, independence on 67 (modelled first), a cause and its effects on 68, scale on 73 and reasoning on 75. On 76 students work without a teacher model. Return to the question board after each supporting answer.
>
> - **Carried:** source-sourcing gains audience and purpose on 62; prompts fade on 76. Corroboration adds independence on 67 and returns on 75; the Norse case joins 75 and 77. Motive: one aim a voyage on the map, compared on 75. Timeline changes from supplied to student-constructed on 73; on 74 withdraw the example and add an equal-year grid. Revise cause-effect-chain on 68 to distinguish intended gains from harms. Perspective pairs each voyage with named peoples on 64 to 72. Keep unit 3's qualified claims and source log.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** No spec or TOOL-ASKS.md is needed. Supply printed packets, an accumulating voyage map, rulers, timeline strips, pencils and record sheets. Every map, source and task has a printed version; a projector is optional. At home the adult takes the discussion partner's role. No student timer is shown.

Frame: "Source __ suggests __ because __; it cannot establish __." Allow oral rehearsal and dictation. Extension compares two defensible source-based explanations.
