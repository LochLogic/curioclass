## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 128, 129, 131): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 129, 131): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **MA.4.T4a.1** (days 131): Locate the Northeast states on a political map of the United States.
- **MA.4.T4a.2** (days 131): Use atlases and maps to make a Northeast map showing cities, capitals, physical features, and a title, scale, compass, and key.
- **MA.4.T4a.3** (days 131): Explain the benefits of statehood over being a British colony in the 1700s, and build a class timeline of when each Northeast state joined the United States.
- **MA.4.T4a.4** (days 131): Ask questions, research, and analyze how people adapted to the Northeast environment and how its features and resources shaped settlement, urban growth, industry, and trade.
- **MA.4.T4b.1** (days 131): Locate the Southeast states, the national capital, and Caribbean territories on a political map, and add the states' admission dates to the timeline.
- **MA.4.T4b.4** (days 131): Describe Washington, D.C. as the national capital and give examples of its cultural and civic resources, such as the White House, the Capitol, and the Supreme Court.
- **MA.4.T4b.5** (days 131): Use atlases or state websites to map a Southeast state with physical features, a title, scale, compass, and key.
- **MA.4.T4c.1** (days 131): Locate the Midwest states on a political map and add their admission dates to the timeline.
- **MA.4.T4c.4** (days 131): Use atlases and sources to map a Midwest state with physical features, resources, and industries such as farming, plus a title, scale, compass, and key.
- **MA.4.T4d.1** (days 131): Locate the Southwest states on a political map and add their admission dates to the timeline.
- **MA.4.T4d.5** (days 131): Use atlases, historical sources or state websites to map a Southwest state showing physical features such as waterways and mountains, climate, settlements and movements of Native peoples (including current reservation lands), and European exploration and pioneer settlement of the 1600s to 1800s, with title, scale, compass, and key.
- **MA.4.T4e.1** (days 131): Locate the Western states and Pacific territories on a political map and add the states' admission dates to the timeline.
- **MA.4.T4e.5** (days 131): Use atlases or state websites to map a Western state with physical features, landmarks, national parks, and historic sites, plus title, scale, compass, and key.
- **D2.Geo.1.3-5** (days 131): Make maps and other graphic pictures of both familiar and unfamiliar places.
- **D2.Geo.8.3-5** (days 131): Explain how settlements and movements of people relate to where natural resources are and how they are used.
- **D2.Eco.4.3-5** (days 131): Explain why people and businesses specialize and trade.
- skill ss4-regions-resources (grade 4)
- skill ss4-national-capital (grade 4)
- skill ss4-states-and-statehood (grade 4)
- skill ss4-state-maps (grade 4)
