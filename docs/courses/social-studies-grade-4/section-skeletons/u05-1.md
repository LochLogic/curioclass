## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 80): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.4.T4.2** (days 81, 82): Give examples of how the United States added states and territories from 1791 to 1898: buying the Louisiana Territory from France, gaining Southwest land through war with Mexico, a treaty with Britain for the Oregon Territory, buying Alaska from Russia, annexing Hawaii, and adding territories such as Puerto Rico after a war with Spain.
- **MA.4.T4.1** (days 83): Describe how canals, roads, and railways built in the 1800s helped the United States expand westward.
- skill ss4-westward-growth (grade 4)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 80:** overlay United States claims on an Oregon homelands map beside the Cayuse, Umatilla and Walla Walla account. Did a larger country mean more land for everyone?
> - **Co-write on 80:** How did borders and travel change (81 to 84)? Why move, with what effects (85 to 86)? Whose lands did Mexico lose (87 to 91)? How did choice, force and slavery shape growth (92 to 94)? Whose consent was missing (82 and 95 to 99)?
> - **Teaching:** model mapping on 81, causation on 86, comparing accounts on 89, weighing accounts on 90 (claim matched to two details, frame on), cause and effect on 99, and a two-source claim on 100, withdrawing unit 4's frame for a checklist. Trail life returns in the 86 warm-up; the Gadsden strip on 91. D1.1.3-5 is practised, not owned. Revisit the question board daily.
>
> - **Carried:** retain source logs, independence checks and qualified claims from unit 4. Return map-scale on 81 and 98; read the timeline on 81 and construct it on 98. Revise cause-effect-chain on 86 for unequal effects; students build it on 99. Perspective returns on 89 to 91; withdraw completed comparisons on 90.
