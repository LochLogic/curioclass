## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 42): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.4.T2.3** (days 43): Explain how archaeologists research the past, through digs, artifacts, climate data, and teamwork, to build theories about migration, settlement, and culture in prehistory.
- **MA.4.T2.1** (days 44): Evaluate competing theories about how people first reached North America, such as a land bridge or a sea route, and the evidence dating early populations to about 15,000 years ago.
- skill ss4-first-peoples-evidence (grade 4)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 42:** compare a Mesa Verde wall photograph with the Pueblo Today card. What can each tell us about people? Who needs an accurate account?
> - **Co-write on day 42:** How does archaeology work (43)? What do dates and routes suggest (44 to 48)? What do regions and objects show (49 to 51)? Why preserve ancestral places (52 to 54)?
> - **Teaching:** model observation versus inference on 43, chronology on 44 and weighing evidence on 48. On 55 students select evidence without a teacher model. Return to the question board after each supporting answer.
>
> - **Carried:** return to map-scale on 49 and 52, with a ruler and bar; on 52 students measure from Mesa Verde to a named Pueblo community, no new exit. Revise claim-evidence-reasoning on 48 to include uncertainty; reduce its frame on 56. Introduce the given timeline on 44 and return on 48 and 57; students place dates but do not construct its scale. Keep unit 2's source log throughout; audience and independence deepen in unit 4.
