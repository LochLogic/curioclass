- **E137, held paper exit.** "Made up: A planner wants a center people can reach after flooding. Why might our unit question matter to this planner?" Key: Community needs and land conditions affect usable access. The planner needs evidence about both. **Shape:** Explain usefulness for a supplied person.
- **E138, held paper exit.** "In colonial New Amsterdam, enslaved Africans built houses and cleared shore areas for docks. Describe this contribution while distinguishing forced work from freedom." Key: They helped construct the town and prepare docks. Enslaved people were forced to work, without freedom to refuse. Free African people also lived in the colony, with restricted rights. **Shape:** Interpret a new construction caption through contribution and coercion.
- **E139, held paper exit.** "Black residents established Eatonville, a self-governing Florida town. What does this add to an account showing African Americans only as enslaved people?" Key: It adds Black town-building and self-government, beyond the incomplete account of enslavement. **Shape:** Expand an incomplete account from a new town caption.
- **E140, held paper exit.** "An Arab and Chaldean American council advised Michigan's government. What does this add to a Midwest account about European immigrants alone?" Key: Arab and Chaldean Americans also contributed civic knowledge and participation in public decisions. **Shape:** Expand a one-group account with new civic evidence.
- **E141, held paper exit.** "Some African American soldiers built roads in the Southwest after the Civil War. Describe this contribution without making it every African American person's occupation." Key: Some soldiers constructed roads connecting army posts. This describes particular soldiers, not every African American person or occupation. **Shape:** Write a qualified occupational description from an unfamiliar caption.
