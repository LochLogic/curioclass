## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q2a/Q2b (split records E2a to E2e), on s02 field 5. E9 is retained on day 35.

All exits are individual paper evidence. Dictation, labeled drawings and tables are accepted when they show the same idea. Record each component as shown, shown with help, or not yet seen. Access support is not content help. Many means more than a third. Label invented records and say aloud that their people, families and events are made up. A correct choice alone cannot pass a source-selection item: require its distinct evidential jobs and reasons. Accept supported alternatives only where the key allows them.

**E17 (s02-X17), withheld paper exit.** "A made-up family buys food delivered across a border. Explain why our unit question could matter to them."

Shape: Explain another family's need for the inquiry; practice generates questions and diagram claims.

Key: Understanding delivery connections helps explain food supply or interruptions. Look for: Gives a use for this family, not only class. Wrong: The teacher likes maps. (P).

**E18 (s02-X18), withheld paper exit.** "A question asks where a lake lies and how nearby residents use it. Name helpful source kinds with each kind's job."

Shape: Generate complementary source kinds and their jobs; practice sorts supplied source kinds.

Key: Map/atlas locates it; article/resident account describes uses. Accept alternatives with distinct relevant jobs. Look for: Location kind and experience/use kind; each job explained. Wrong: The decorated cover is best. (J); Only agreeing sources count. (A).

**E19 (s02-X19), withheld paper exit.** "Made-up ferry records: a clerk's 2026 trip table; a rider's 2026 crowding blog. A 1990 visitor guide gives old times. Choose two for current ferry use. Support your choice through makers, kinds, dates, and viewpoints."

Shape: Select complementary current records with maker, kind, date and viewpoint reasons; practice annotates metadata separately.

Key: Clerk table and rider blog: counts and one experience, both 2026. Guide is historical; neither current record describes all riders. Look for: Complementary records; maker/kind/date/viewpoint reasons. Wrong: An official table answers every question. (B); Only agreeing sources count. (A); The decorated cover is best. (J).

**E20 (s02-X20), withheld paper exit.** "A made-up school made a travel leaflet for school travelers in 1995. It describes that year's buses. Explain its usefulness for researching school travel then and now."

Shape: Explain historical use and a present limit using date and audience; practice labels setting.

Key: School advice for school travelers in 1995; current routes need a current source. Date and audience constrain use. Look for: Date and audience used; historical evidence distinguished from current proof. Wrong: Printed means current. (B).

**E21 (s02-X21), withheld paper exit.** "Made-up sign: This park opened in 1950. It has the friendliest ducks. Explain how you would judge the sign's opening-year claim."

Shape: Judge an opening-year claim through an independent check; practice repairs supplied evidence links.

Key: Check 1950 in an independent town opening record. Preference neither proves nor cancels the date. Look for: Relevant verification; preference kept separate. Wrong: A liking proves a fact. (C); One opinion makes all false. (K); Checkable means true. (L).

**E22 (s02-X22), withheld paper exit.** "Use the map and separately authored geography card. Compare their information about Lake Chapala's country, including one limit of agreement."

Shape: Compare a country finding across independent origins and state a limit; practice detects copies and conflicts.

Key: Both locate Lake Chapala in Mexico. Location agreement does not establish water quality or other unmeasured facts. Look for: Mexico compared in independent origins; limit stated. Wrong: This evidence proves an unrelated claim. (D); Two titles prove it. (N).

## 9. Supports and extension

Student words: atlas, a collection of maps; article, a written explanation; printed digital page, a paper copy of a screen page. Credible means believable for this question. Format means how information is arranged. Metadata means information about a source. Context means where, when and for whom it was made. Day 22 introduces critique through separate order, detail and accuracy examples. Frame: "This detail helps because ___." Read cards aloud; independent source text at most 160 words daily, all provided print within DEFAULTS. Use enlarged symbols, tactile arrows or dictation. Extension finds a missing perspective.
