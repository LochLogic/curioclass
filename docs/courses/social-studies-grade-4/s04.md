## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What did the voyages of the 1400s and 1500s mean for the people already here?"
- **Hook, day 60:** compare Columbus's claim of possession with the Taíno public account of their homeland. Could the same arrival mean different things? Who needs an accurate account?
- **Co-write on day 60:** How do sources help us know (61 to 64)? Why sail (65)? What changed for people here (66 to 72)? What happened together (73 to 74)?
- **Closing, day 76:** answer the compelling question in two linked paragraphs using the Voyage Accounts and Living Peoples packets. Defend an emphasis on conquest, exchange or continuing Native agency with two named sources.
- **Teaching:** model sourcing on 62, independence on 67, a cause and its effects on 68, scale on 73 and reasoning on 75. On 76 students work without a teacher model. Return to the question board after each supporting answer.

Begin with the four community sources above, then Nahua authorship and colonial mediation in the codex, Mexican scholarship in [Visión de los vencidos](https://historicas.unam.mx/publicaciones/publicadigital/libros/obras_leon_portilla/599.html), and [Indigenous journalists' guidance](https://indigenousjournalists.org/reporting-guides/). Study at least five sources, two community-owned. Check every voyage, especially 64 and 66 to 72, for community names, living continuity, contested affiliations, forced captivity and unequal power. Teacher notes must name enslavement and conquest plainly, show Native decisions and resistance, omit slurs and graphic imagery, and never ask children to represent a people.
