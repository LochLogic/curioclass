## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** End-of-unit assessment, from unseen sources: say what an archaeological find, a map, a drawing and a journal each add to the story of a voyage; source an unseen voyage source (who made it and when), inferring its audience and purpose from a clue inside it; describe the Norse and the evidence of their meetings with Native peoples; trace two voyages on a map and say why rulers paid for them; place landings and conquests on a to-scale timeline and compare two that happened at the same time; a claim with evidence from two sourced documents
- **Note:** every part of each listed code asked once; Data: half-sheet paper exits; before class print teacher-key.md separately. All public talk is optional; rotating six heard, all others reached through written evidence. Printed summaries/schematics are the fallback for live sources, visitors and photos. Assessment uses descriptions of real records instead of unchecked reproductions. Q1/Q2 gets ten minutes; Q6 gets eight, including collection. Timing remains untested.

**Skills:**

- ss4-primary-source (grade 4): taught or practised here
- ss4-exploration (grade 4): taught or practised here

**Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **MA.4.T3.1:** Explain how historians studying European voyages to the Americas use archaeology, maps, illustrations, and texts of the time, which are called primary sources.
- **MA.4.T3.2:** Explain who the Vikings were and describe evidence of their early encounters with Native Peoples on the North American Atlantic coast.
- **MA.4.T3.3:** Trace European explorations of North America and the Caribbean in the 1400s and 1500s, evaluate why nations sent them (empire, Asian trade routes, colonies, resources), and make a timeline of landings and conquests.
- **D2.His.1.3-5:** Make and use a time-ordered list of related events to compare developments that happened at the same time.
- **D2.His.9.3-5:** Summarize how different kinds of sources are used to explain past events.
- **D2.His.11.3-5:** Infer who a source was meant for and why it was made, using clues in the source itself.
- **D3.4.3-5:** Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D4.1.3-5:** Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
