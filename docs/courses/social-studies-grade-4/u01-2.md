Grade 4 social studies, [Unit 1: North America on the map](s01.md). A plan, not a lesson: timing and materials are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Shares.** MA.4.T1.1: "Use" scale to measure represented distance, days 5 to 6. D2.Geo.3: "Use" different scales to "describe" cultural and environmental locations, days 7 to 8.
- **Review.** Grade 3 u01-3 day 7: bar length and whole bars; paper strip laid end to end on a scale bar. Section u01-1's four-part card stays available. New: a measured ruler length, half bar and a supplied approximate conversion table. Teach kilometer and mile as distance units, not map lengths.
- **Connections.** The ruler bridge to 4.MD.A.2, day 5, is taught here without waiting for math unit 6. Claim and evidence review grade 3 u01-6 day 19; reasoning means how a detail supports a claim.
- **Teacher explanation.** Compare the printed bar and segment, not remembered page dimensions. Distinguish extent, detail and distance scale; avoid unexplained large/small-scale vocabulary. Review grade 1 u06-1 day 57 in ledger words: cultural feature (something people make, learn or do); environmental feature (land, water, weather). This section maps built places and land/water as examples. No memorized conversion factor.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Day 5 links the grade 3 strip to a centimeter ruler and a bar's half. Children measure prepared strips to check their labels; no student constructs a given ground distance before the day 16 retry. Withdraw tracing guides on day 6; keep ruler, scale and table. Day 7 carries three aligned map windows. The board records why scales serve different needs.
- **Ideal instruments.** Day 5: validated print masters and a ruler marked in centimeters. Partly fits until each bar and segment is measured after printing. If resized, reprint at actual size; a teacher-prepared full-size strip is the fallback. Never substitute an invented numeric reading for the child's measurement.
- **Day 7 ideal.** Three legible extents of one place; partly fits until source layers and invented pins are checked. Tools: None; no interactive spec.
- **Without equipment.** Hand-draw the validated bar and route at measured lengths; layer paper windows over board maps. Students still hold individual copies.

## 6. Days

Each day block ends in individual paper evidence. Read printed stems once without solving them. Public sharing is optional. Pair turns happen simultaneously across 15 pairs; two 45-second turns take 90 seconds, not 45 minutes. A six-child hearing takes 4.5 minutes within a named five-minute slot. Collect the hand work from everyone else. Give repair groups one priority gap; all other children use written cues. A five-minute group includes a brief contrast and an individual fresh attempt, not four long oral turns. Source cards are paraphrases, credited to their row URLs; made-up people, places and pins are labeled and named aloud as made up.

## Day 5

- **10 min. Build a scale.** Extend the whole-bar model: a 4-centimeter practice bar represents 112 kilometers, its half 56. Verify a prepared 12-centimeter strip labeled 336 kilometers: its three bars agree. Read ruler marks from zero before checking its labels. Model the supplied table, 112 kilometers about 70 miles; a half is 56 and about 35. These are approximate instructional equivalences.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 5: "How does this strip stand for a ground distance?" Day 7: "Which detail disappears beyond this window?" Day 8: "Which visible detail supports that sentence?" Written task: day 7's spatial-sentence repair. Look for a relationship rather than a list, with a map detail that supports it.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 5 reviews u01-1's region and grade 3's strip. Day 6 repeats day 5's given table with different whole-bar row comparisons. Day 7 reviews u01-1's selection and grade 3 reasoning. Day 8 returns to windows and legend. No prescribed fluency target; repeat short table sets with feedback, without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: centimeter, kilometer, mile, half bar, extent, cultural feature, environmental feature. Frame: "This bar stands for ___." Use tactile bars, raised windows and large ruler marks. Dictation/pointing preserve the spatial goal; the child directs a scribe's measurement if needed. Keep print within DEFAULTS; read directions and tables aloud without choosing an operation. Extension: compare two supplied scales for the same route without constructing a strip.

## 10. Two rooms

At home, the adult checks print size and takes the partner role. One projector shows windows or table feedback while children measure their own copies. Screen measurement never substitutes for a printed segment.
