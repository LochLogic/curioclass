# Section 1.2: Distance and map views




## 1. Standards and practices


- **Shares.** MA.4.T1.1: "Use" scale to measure represented distance, days 5 to 6. D2.Geo.3: "Use" different scales to "describe" cultural and environmental locations, days 7 to 8.
- **Review.** Grade 3 u01-3 day 7: bar length and whole bars; paper strip laid end to end on a scale bar. Section u01-1's four-part card stays available. New: a measured ruler length, half bar and a supplied approximate conversion table. Teach kilometer and mile as distance units, not map lengths.
- **Connections.** The ruler bridge to 4.MD.A.2, day 5, is taught here without waiting for math unit 6. Claim and evidence review grade 3 u01-6 day 19; reasoning means how a detail supports a claim.
- **Teacher explanation.** Compare the printed bar and segment, not remembered page dimensions. Distinguish extent, detail and distance scale; avoid unexplained large/small-scale vocabulary. Review grade 1 u06-1 day 57 in ledger words: cultural feature (something people make, learn or do); environmental feature (land, water, weather). This section maps built places and land/water as examples. No memorized conversion factor.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 distance and Q3, day 15, s01 field 5. E5 to E8 are individual paper exits. Use u01-1's component scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Many means more than a third. Return next day with one written cue, repairing one priority gap in the opening and one five-minute side group during Supported work.

- **E5.** "Exit Distance Map has a 2-centimeter bar for 80 kilometers. Measure the marked segment. Find its ground distance." The print master must give a 5-centimeter segment. Key: 2 whole bars and a half, 200 kilometers. Wrong: 5 kilometers (C); 80 kilometers for the entire segment (C), separate responses.
  Shape: measure an existing segment, then determine represented distance. Practice constructs a segment for a supplied ground distance; no forward measurement-and-distance question or exit route.
- **E6.** "One bar represents 60 kilometers. The table pairs 60 kilometers with about 40 miles. Find one-and-a-half bars in both units." Key: 90 kilometers, about 60 miles. Wrong: 60 kilometers for all the bars (C); 90 miles by copying the kilometer number (C).
  Shape: produce two unit distances from one-and-a-half bars. Practice repairs equivalence tables using whole bars and separate half-bar rows; never this combined computation or table.
- **E7.** "Describe the library's position relative to the river on Exit Denver Map. Use the wider maps to locate Denver within Colorado and North America." Key: library east of South Platte River; Denver in Colorado, in North America. Wrong: only lists library and river (O); all three maps contain streets (D). Treat the library pin as invented, not a claim about an actual building.
  Shape: produce a local relationship and a wider nested location. Practice aligns map windows and annotates visible details, without the combined local-to-continent chain.
- **E8.** "Choose a map for the walk, using a human feature and an environmental feature. Explain one limit of that map." Supply reserved Seattle city, Washington and continent maps, with two public endpoints and a completely mapped invented route. Key: city map; its streets and shoreline locate the route; it omits most countries. Accept another visible omission. Wrong: continent map gives all street detail (D); preference without evidence (H); city map shows everything (N).
  Shape: independently choose a scale for a walk, justify with two feature types and state a limit. Practice critiques provided choices; never this complete choice-and-limit response.

**Q1 Shape:** reserved C combines labels, rotated direction, ruler distance and conversion. Practice uses separate correction or construction tasks. Cold key: 3/2 bars times 800 = 1,200 kilometers; 500 + 250 = about 750 miles. Do not use C's table, segment, features or orientation case in teaching.

**Q3 Shape:** locate two features relationally, construct a three-scale chain, choose a scale for a statewide delivery and name an omission. Reserve Madison, Dane County, Wisconsin, Lake Monona and E/F/G. Practice uses map-window alignment and statement repairs, never the complete response. Key checked as a design requirement: library west of park, park beside lake; F is the state map. Cultural pins on these authored maps must be labeled invented.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Day 5 links the grade 3 strip to a centimeter ruler and a bar's half. Withdraw tracing guides on day 6; keep ruler, scale and table. Day 7 carries three aligned map windows. The board records why scales serve different needs.
- **Ideal instruments.** Day 5: validated print masters and a ruler marked in centimeters. Partly fits until each bar and segment is measured after printing. If resized, reprint at actual size; a teacher-prepared full-size strip is the fallback. Never substitute an invented numeric reading for the child's measurement.
- **Day 7 ideal.** Three legible extents of one place; partly fits until source layers and invented pins are checked. Tools: None; no interactive spec.
- **Without equipment.** Hand-draw the validated bar and route at measured lengths; layer paper windows over board maps. Students still hold individual copies.

## 6. Days

## Day 5

- **5 min. Return and repair.** E4's priority gap; fresh grouping response.
- **10 min. Build a scale.** Extend the whole-bar model: a 4-centimeter practice bar represents 112 kilometers, its half 56. Build a strip for a given 336 kilometers: three bars, 12 centimeters. Model the supplied table, 112 kilometers about 70 miles; a half is 56 and about 35. These are approximate instructional equivalences.
- **15 min. Supported work.** Children construct strips for 392 and 448 kilometers with that bar: 14 and 16 centimeters. Then repair two incorrect unit labels. Five minutes held for one repair group; pairs have at most three 90-second exchanges.
- **5 min. Compare constructions.** Hear six, one 45-second explanation each; collect all strips. Link ruler, bar and represented units explicitly.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5, independent measurement.

Pairs hold rulers and bars; teacher holds print-validation strip. Tool: None. By hand: measured construction, table repair, E5.

**Shared shape.** Practice: Make a strip for 392 kilometers using the practice bar. Make a strip for 448 kilometers using the practice bar. Exit ticket: Exit Distance Map has a 2-centimeter bar for 80 kilometers. Measure the marked segment. Find its ground distance. The named exit record stays covered until independent work.

## Day 6

- **5 min. Return and repair.** E5's C: one fresh unit-label repair and bar construction.
- **10 min. Table retrieval.** Use day 5's table. Match separate 112/70, 56/35 and 224/140 kilometer/mile pairs with whole, half and double bars. No new conversion method.
- **15 min. Supported work.** Correct two flawed whole-bar tables, then build a missing row by doubling. Answers: three bars, 336 kilometers, about 210 miles; four bars, 448 kilometers, about 280 miles. Teacher holds five minutes for one side group. Partners check after each attempt with the overlay.
- **5 min. Explain a correction.** Hear six for 45 seconds each; collect every corrected table. Accuracy before ease, no racing.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E6, fresh table, alone.

Students hold rulers without tracing guides and their table. Tool: None. By hand: matching, corrected rows, E6.

**Shared shape.** Practice: Match each pair to whole, half or double bars. Double the two-bar row to fill the missing four-bar row. Exit ticket: One bar represents 60 kilometers. The table pairs 60 kilometers with about 40 miles. Find one-and-a-half bars in both units. The named exit record stays covered until independent work.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 5: "How does this strip stand for a ground distance?" Day 7: "Which detail disappears beyond this window?" Day 8: "Which visible detail supports that sentence?" Written task: day 7's spatial-sentence repair. Look for a relationship rather than a list, with a map detail that supports it.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 5 reviews u01-1's region and grade 3's strip. Day 6 repeats day 5's given table with different whole-bar constructions. Day 7 reviews u01-1's selection and grade 3 reasoning. Day 8 returns to windows and legend. No prescribed fluency target; repeat short table sets with feedback, without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: centimeter, kilometer, mile, half bar, extent, cultural feature, environmental feature. Frame: "This bar stands for ___." Use tactile bars, raised windows and large ruler marks. Dictation/pointing preserve the spatial goal; the child directs a scribe's measurement if needed. Keep print within DEFAULTS; read directions and tables aloud without choosing an operation. Extension: construct a different scale for the same route.

## 10. Two rooms

At home, the adult checks print size and takes the partner role. One projector shows windows or table feedback while children measure their own copies. Screen measurement never substitutes for a printed segment.
