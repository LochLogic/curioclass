## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q5a/Q5b (E5a/E5b), on s02 field 5.

All exits are individual paper evidence. Dictation, labeled drawings and tables are accepted when they show the same idea. Record each component as shown, shown with help, or not yet seen. Access support is not content help. Many means more than a third. Label invented records and say aloud that their people, families and events are made up. No identity role play or personal disclosure.

A correct choice alone cannot pass a source-selection item: require its distinct evidential jobs and reasons. Accept supported alternatives only where the key allows them.

**E30 (s02-X30), withheld paper exit.** "Made-up case: one country specializes in glass jars. Another specializes in metal lids. Jar orders rise. Explain how rising jar orders increase reliance between these producers."

Key: Jar makers need more imported lids, and lid sellers need the increased purchases. Look for: Growing reciprocal reliance. Shape: explain dependence after an unused order increase. Practice labels inputs or diagnoses one break, never explains this growth case. Wrong: Glass jars and metal lids. (E); Each makes everything alone. (O).

**E31 (s02-X31), withheld paper exit.** "Use the made-up trade map. Explain what the oats arrow means for both countries."

Key: Canada exports oats; Mexico imports the same shipment. Look for: One flow interpreted from both ends. Shape: explain the two viewpoints on one held arrow. Practice labels separate imports/exports, never explains both ends of this oats arrow. Wrong: Oats. (E); Mexico exports oats to Canada. (D).

**E32 (s02-X32), withheld paper exit.** "Made-up case: a country buys more imported flour. Bakers get steady supplies. Local millers lose orders. Explain the different effects on bakers and local millers."

Key: Bakers gain reliable inputs; redirected purchases reduce local millers' orders. Look for: Both mechanisms within country. Wrong: Everyone wins. (F).

**E33 (s02-X33), withheld paper exit.** "Made-up case: cheaper imported parts lower repair costs. More customers choose repairs instead of replacement tools. Explain the effects on repair services and replacement-tool sellers."

Key: Repairers gain customers as costs fall; replacement sellers lose purchases through substitution. Look for: Both effects explained through changed purchases. Wrong: Everyone gains. (F); Imported parts; repairs; replacement tools. (E).

**s02-E5a.** Shape: explain three-country specialization, growth and disruption. Practice labels single inputs or diagnoses one interrupted link; reserve bicycles, tubes, wheels and the 4-to-7 orders.

**s02-E5b.** Shape: explain gains, losses and purchase mechanisms within three countries. Practice treats single-group links separately, never a completed effects matrix; reserve bicycle groups in D/E.

## 9. Supports and extension

Review specialize, import, export, relies on, buyer and seller in the ledger's words above. Input means something used to make a product. Interdependence means needing each other. A rival is another seller competing for purchases. Gloss national values as dollar amounts recorded then, without price adjustment; no computation target. Frame: "When ___ buys ___, ___ changes because ___." Independent source text at most 140 words; adult tables summarized aloud. All print within DEFAULTS. Thick arrows, movable labels, dictation without causal hints. Extension identifies supported uncertainty.
