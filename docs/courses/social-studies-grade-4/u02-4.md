**Unit assessment items owned:** Q5a/Q5b. Same component scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. All exits paper. Teacher-judged alternatives must trace the mechanism. All following cases are labeled invented, not actual national trade statistics. Do not infer actual crop origins, employment or trade amounts.

- **E30, Jar Case:** "One country specializes in glass jars. Another specializes in metal lids. Jar orders rise." "Explain why they exchange and how rising orders increase reliance." Key: complementary specialized parts; jar makers need more lids, lid sellers need those purchases. Meets: "Jar makers need more imported lids; lid makers need jar orders." Not yet: "Each makes everything alone." Wrong: goods list without reliance (E); self-sufficiency (O).
  Shape: complementary specialization plus increasing reliance. Practice labels inputs/buyers on a paper/printing chain and annotates one changed link, never a full why-trade/growth explanation, jars or lids.
- **E31, Map Case:** reserve illustrative arrows, oats Canada to Mexico, mangoes Mexico to Canada. "Read the oats arrow as export and import. Explain how larger deliveries could increase reliance." Key: Canada's export, Mexico's import; buyers need supplies and sellers need purchases as flow grows. Mangoes is not a second ask. Wrong: unrelated import/export lists (E); reversal against key (D).
  Shape: one flow from both viewpoints plus growth explanation. Practice annotates origins or destinations separately, never the complete two-view/growth response, oats or mangoes.
- **E32, Flour Case:** "A country buys more imported flour. Bakers get steady supplies. Local millers lose orders." "Explain a baker's gain and a local miller's loss." Key: steadier inputs support baking; redirected purchases reduce millers' orders. Meets: "Bakers keep baking; millers sell less because purchases moved abroad." Not yet: "Everyone wins." Wrong: uniform national effect (F).
  Shape: opposite effects within an importing country. Practice traces one group's purchase change at a time, never a paired gain/loss explanation or flour/baker/miller case.
- **E33, Repair Case:** "Cheaper imported parts lower repair costs. More customers choose repairs instead of buying replacement tools." "Explain the effects on repair services and replacement-tool sellers." Key: repair services gain customers; fewer replacements reduce sellers' orders. Meets: "Repairers gain work because repairs cost less. Replacement sellers lose purchases." Not yet: "All sellers gain from imports." Wrong: uniform benefits (F); list without mechanism (E).
  Shape: infer connected business effects through repair/replacement substitution. Practice diagnoses single supplied links in a growing-factory/farm case, never repair/replacement or a complete paired inference.

**Q5a Shape:** explain specialization, order growth and disruption across three countries. Cold key: Canadian assembly needs Mexican tubes and United States wheels; more orders increase reliance; late tubes interrupt assembly/delivery. Reserve bicycles, tubes, wheels and 4-to-7 orders. Practice labels components, never a complete growth/disruption explanation.
**Q5b Shape:** gains/losses and purchase mechanisms within all three countries, one table. Cold key: Canada workshop shifts/rival assembly losses; Mexico suppliers' orders/rivals' sales; United States buyers' prices/assemblers' orders. Practice uses separate groups, never a complete three-country effects matrix. All are invented case evidence.
