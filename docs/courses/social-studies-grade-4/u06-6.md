## 1. Standards and practices

MA.4.T4b.4 carries "Describe" the national capital and "give examples" of civic/cultural resources. Review written grade 3 u02-2 day 28: branch (one part of a government with its own job: makes laws, carries them out, or decides cases). Buildings/national office matches extend that review; grade 5 owns constitutional checks.

Teacher separates office, building, branch and government level. D.C.'s congressional limit is a precise legal fact, not an invitation to debate. D.C. has local government; lack of senators/final House vote never means no government or no voting. Day 127 connects sourcing to civic use. Content note: monuments commemorate people without requiring agreement or praise.

Judge these separately.
  **Shape:** infer a public use of an unseen commemorative resource and state a precise voting limit. Avoid composing public-use captions or reciting the complete district limit during practice; match questions to object/record evidence and distinguish committee action from final legislation.

**F6a Shape:** fill an unseen capital/worker/branch/building record. Avoid complete three-building records in teaching or review. **F6b held public-record task.** Give an original short factual adaptation of the National Archives' 1872 Yellowstone Act, section 2: "The Secretary of the Interior must make rules for the park's care." Identification: federal act, 1872, National Archives. Public-history question: "Who received authority to make the park's care rules?" Ask: "Explain how this archived record could help answer the question." Key: the act assigns that authority to the Secretary of the Interior, so the preserved law supplies evidence about who had that duty. A generic "it preserves records" is insufficient. Shape: connect an unused specific federal record to a public-history inquiry. Practice matches town questions to records, never sees this act, official or inquiry. The National Archives is the civic resource; do not print its general function as the answer. New source for the data owner: [1872 act](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/act-establishing-yellowstone-national-park), opened 2026-11-26; retain that actual date. No land-history or first-park claim is needed. **F6c Shape:** state the congressional limit. Avoid rehearsing a complete limit answer in practice. The teacher teaches the fact explicitly; the independent paper response is first composed at the check.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** "I can connect national workers with their buildings." One E125 exchange gap, fresh made-up shoemaker/fruit-stall account, or a next-best saving choice for Z. One priority per child within this block. Collect retry; others recall source detail.

## Day 127

**Shape:** practice matches questions to record/object evidence and distinguishes voting actions; E127 explains a new monument's public use and states a precise limit. Avoid complete use/limit answers in practice.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** E126/levels priority together on a fresh nested diagram, one block.
