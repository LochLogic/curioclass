# Course frame: Grade 4: North America, Its Land and Peoples

Course id ss-k5 (grade 4 row), grade 4, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7, in Massachusetts's own order (4.T1 to 4.T4e): the continent on the map, the neighbors, the
  first peoples, voyages and conquest, the country's growth, then the five regions today.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 150 in units and 10 at course level (6 for three course-level checks, 4 respond
  days). It assumes one social studies lesson of about 40 minutes every school day. The year owns 17 rows, fewer
  than grade 3 or 5, but they are wide (ss4-states-and-statehood alone holds six anchor codes, and five region
  sub-topics run through units 6 and 7), so the full yardstick fits; the progression review named the same.
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 grade 4 (4.T1 to 4.T4e) is the content anchor and every one of its standards
  is owned by a row placed below; C3 (3 to 5 band) carries the skills. The check set (California, Tennessee, New
  York) is read unit by unit at the unit plan; this frame names candidate additions with their source. The values
  rule governs every unit; the war with Mexico and conquest are taught from more than one account, with the record
  settling what it can (rule b).
- **Base text.** None (adoption bar section 1). Days are Curio Class's own, from open pieces adapted with credit:
  Library of Congress maps and journals, National Park Service pages for Mesa Verde, Cahokia and the national
  capital, USGS and Census maps, NOAA and FEMA public pages for hazards, state websites, public-domain images. Native
  nations' and the Canadian and Mexican governments' own pages are read and linked. NonCommercial programs are
  reference only.
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit runs the full arc. Grade 4's step up: students choose sources for a given
  question (unit 2), ask who made a source, when, for whom and why (unit 4), and compare two sources on one event
  with the check that they did not copy each other (unit 4).
- **Informed action.** Once, in unit 7: students choose one local hazard from their research, check what their
  town or state already advises, and make one product for the school community (a family preparedness guide, a
  map of safe routes, a talk to a younger class). Students choose; the course never picks a cause.
- **The own-place method.** Each region unit has a local thread: the student's own state found in its region, its
  admission date, its hazards. Worked examples rotate across the five regions.
- **Reading level.** Adapted and excerpted texts at a grade 3 to 4 band; primary sources are short (a journal
  entry of a few lines, a map, a drawing, an object) with a sourcing step before use.
