- **E128, Work Records.** Surveyor's map A: falls, mill beside falls, nearby homes. Separate employer notice B: "Mill workers wanted. Paid work starts Monday." "Give a tentative answer about work here, using both records and one limit." Key: location at falls and advertised jobs may help explain work/settlement; neither proves every resident's occupation or filled vacancies. Sample: "The falls and job notice suggest reasons to work here. They cannot show every job." Wrong: titles alone (J); liking mills as proof (X); everyone works at mill (G).
- **E129, Farm Records.** Land survey C marks fields beside a port; independent shipping clerk's log D records bean cargo leaving that port. "Write a claim using one detail from each record. Explain how both details support it." Key: fields provide production space; recorded cargo uses port access for trade, without determining every job. Sample: "Fields beside the port allow nearby farming. The bean shipment shows food moving to buyers." Stronger: "Land and transport can shape work; these records show fields and an actual food shipment." Wrong: everyone must farm (G); titles without evidence (J); two C details alone (Y); prettiest place as reason (X).
- **E130, Fresh Review Record.** Map places clay pit/pottery shop apart from grain farm. Caption: "This town sends pots away and receives grain." False claim: "Everyone has one job, so our region makes everything it needs." "Correct this claim using the records. Name one national branch and its building." Key: pottery/farming differ; trade supplies grain; any accurate branch/building match from 126. Sample: "Clay work and farming differ. Grain comes through trade. Congress works at the Capitol, legislative branch." Wrong: same job everywhere (G); no purchases needed (V); governor/White House (I).
