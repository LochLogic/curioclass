## 1. Standards and practices

- **D2.His.11.3-5** (days 67): Infer who a source was meant for and why it was made, using clues in the source itself.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1, Q2, Q5; Q1's synthesis is a separate component, not another task. Use u04-1's one marking scale.

- **E64, Camp Plan and Saga Note.** Reserved plan caption: "Recorded building foundations and ironworking remains support a Norse camp." Separately attributed note: "Later sagas describe encounters elsewhere in the region." Ask: "Explain what each contributes and whether they establish a meeting at this camp." Key: material presence and later regional encounter accounts; neither locates a particular meeting here or names a modern nation as participants. Sample: "The plan supports a camp; the saga reports encounters elsewhere, not this exact meeting." Wrong: "A camp proves a meeting there." (U); "The plan proves every saga episode." (B); "Norse were the first people here." (C). Shape: two-kind contribution plus combined location-limit judgment; teach camp identity and encounter geography separately, never the complete two-record explanation. Exact plan/saga selection: source to check. Fallback: labeled original reconstruction plus opened park's regional-encounter paraphrase, no fabricated saga words.
- **E65, Western Voyage.** "Give two reasons rulers funded western voyages. Explain what a mariner's astrolabe helps crews estimate." Key: any two distinct taught motives, Asian trade, resources, colonies or empire; latitude from sky-angle observations, not precise longitude. Sample: "Rulers sought trade and greater control; the astrolabe helped estimate latitude." Wrong: "Only harmless curiosity." (D). Shape: two motives plus an instrument contribution; practice classifies one motive or instrument at a time, never this composite. No replica or measuring activity required.
- **E66, Caribbean Route Inset.** New inset labels Spain, Canaries, Bahamas, without arrows; fresh attributed caption describes forced captivity by Columbus's men. Ask: "Trace the arrival route and explain one consequence for Taíno people from the caption." Key: Spain through Canaries west across Atlantic to Bahamas; captivity deprived people of freedom. Accept the actual sourced consequence, no unsupplied statistic. Wrong: "Empty islands." (C); "Possession proves agreement." (N). Shape: trace new configuration plus explain caption's consequence; practice orders detached route segments and classifies separate harms, never trace-plus-consequence. Exact caption/rights source to check; attributed factual paraphrase replaces an unavailable image, not imaginary testimony.
- **E67, Landing Reports.** Entirely made up. A: "Dock log: Three boats landed beside the red shed." B: "Visitor leaflet: Three boats landed beside the red shed. Families watched." Provenance: B copied A; the added sentence has no source. Ask: "Compare their agreement and difference. Explain whether they independently confirm the landing." Key: agree on three boats/red shed; B adds families; one underlying account, not two independent confirmations. Added detail unconfirmed. Wrong: "Two printings prove it twice." (K). Shape: compare agreement/difference/independence from fresh provenance; practice single metadata relationships and individual source claims, never this paired comparison. Explicit provenance repairs s04's inference from shared wording alone.

Q5 combines historical maker/date plus inside audience/purpose clues; no full historical-letter record in practice.
