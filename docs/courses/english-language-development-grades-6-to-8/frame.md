# Course frame: English Language Development, grades 6 to 8

Course `english-language-development-grades-6-to-8` (catalogue id `eld-6-8`), grades 6, 7 and 8, a band course of
three grade years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the shape of the pilot frame (English Language Development,
grades K to 2) and its fixes; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time. Each year's part of
this file (its units, its end-of-unit assessments, its two course-level checks) is that grade year's frame, which
meets STANDARD.md section 2 ("one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 26: 8 in grade 6 (units 1 to 8), 9 in grade 7 (units 9 to 17), 9 in grade 8 (units 18 to 26). One unit
  per content row of the progression (9 a year: LA Narrate, Inform and Argue; MA, SC and SS Explain and Argue),
  except that grade 6 pairs its two MA rows on one host (unit 8), because math grade 6 teaches equations and
  statistical questions in the year's last two months. The four SI rows of each year ride inside the content unit
  whose key language use they share. Each unit is built around one or two key language uses, in both modes. SI in
  every unit (bar item 1; STANDARD section 3): the unit's own SI row, or one SI use named as review in What students
  learn (units 4 to 8, 11, 14 to 17, 19, 22 and 24 to 26), each with its talk move on the host content and its
  look-for, chosen so that every key language use returns inside the year.
- **Days.** 480: 160 a year for three years. Each year: 154 days in units and 6 for two course-level checks (two
  check days and one respond day each). One lesson of 45 minutes a day, five a week, 32 weeks, the protected daily
  ELD period settled in STANDARD.md section 2 and APPROACH.md "Pacing" (120 hours a year). It is not ELA time and
  does not stand in for ELA (bar item 8). The 45 minutes hold the talk set-up, the roles of the year's talk routine,
  handing out and collecting cards and the exit ticket; there is no lab or field clean-up, since ELD runs no
  investigation. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, a mid-unit
  assessment): units 1 and 8 are over 25 days and each hold one mid-unit assessment day. Every unit's end-of-unit
  assessment is two days (a sitting with unseen items: an interpretive part on new content of the same kind, then a
  talk task heard by the teacher and a writing task, at each entry point), followed by one respond day inside the
  unit. Days follow the host windows first: each unit runs in the weeks its host is taught, and the two checks sit in
  fixed weeks, so a unit's length is mostly the gap between hosts. Within those windows the load sets the rest: an
  LA row (a host passage taken apart and an own piece built, in both modes) weighs most, a content row next, an SI
  row least, since it rides on the same unit's talk. Five units are tight for what they hold and say what they leave
  to the respond day: units 15, 16 and 17 (13 days each, grade 7's last three hosts all run in its last seven weeks)
  and units 25 and 26 (13 days each, grade 8's editorial and natural selection hosts both run in its last eight
  weeks). Each keeps its Fluency days to the forms it owns and leaves review forms to the respond day's groups; the
  scope and sequence may move days within a year, never across a check. Years run in school order: grade 6 is band
  days 1 to 160, grade 7 band days 161 to 320, grade 8 band days 321 to 480; days below are given within the year
  (grade 7 day 88 is band day 248). These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places
  them on school days and weeks, and at five a week a year's course day d falls on school day d, in week d divided
  by 5, rounded up.
- **Standard.** English language development standards (WIDA 2020, data/standards/wida-2020.txt), ids and our own
  words only: no Language Expectation, proficiency level descriptor or other framework wording appears in any page
  (STANDARD.md section 1); public pages say "English language development standards" with the id. The band's 39
  rows own 18 ids, all in grade 8, the owner year of cluster 6-8: LA Narrate, Inform and Argue and MA, SC and SS
  Explain and Argue, each in both modes (cluster 6-8 has no MA, SC or SS Narrate or Inform id). Grades 6 and 7 carry
  the same 18 ids, and all three years carry the SI 4-12 ids (owner year grade 4, in the ELD grades 3 to 5 course;
  the owner rule of STANDARD section 2): each unit that carries one names it in What students learn with its owner.
  Ids in full: every part of an owned id's expectations is taught in its unit, checked against the framework's own
  lists for the id (not the code alone) and said in our words (each grade 8 unit names its parts; For the lead,
  item 2). Check set: bar part 10 items 1 to 14 (ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS), the PRIME 2020 criteria in our words
  (docs/RUBRIC-ELD.md, a planning reference only; Curio Class claims no alignment) and the California criteria map
  rows of STANDARD section 4. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
- **Language through content.** Every unit names its host: the core unit of the same grade that the class studies
  that month (STANDARD section 3), cited with the host frame's own course days. The host column of the Units table
  (headed Host unit and key language uses, in place of C3) gives it. Where a row names two hosts, the unit uses the
  one that runs alongside it and retrieves the other where it was taught earlier; a host taught later in the year
  is not used, and the unit says so (units 1, 10, 11, 13 and 18). Some units run just after a host (unit 7 after
  science grade 6 unit 4, unit 8 after math grade 6 unit 6, unit 19 after math grade 8 unit 1) or a few days ahead
  (units 3, 5, 10 and 12, each with an "If later" line): these are the nearest fits that keep every row in host
  order with two checks a year, and the scope and sequence may move days within a year without changing units. Each
  host is checked against the host row's standard, not its title. Content is the grade's own, made reachable with
  help; it is never swapped for an easier text. For an LA unit the host is the ELA unit it pairs with (STANDARD
  section 5): ELD takes one passage of the ELA text apart and names its language, and the reading and writing
  lessons stay in ELA.
- **Key language uses as the spine.** Each year builds all four uses, and each returns with a harder text or task
  inside the year (bar item 13), through the unit's own row or its SI review use; what gets harder is named in that
  unit. Grade 6: narrate 1 to 4 (a first-week recount by class period grows to the week's weather told from the
  class's own data log, keeping one point of view), inform 1 to 5 and 7 (a routine in sections grows to a section
  with a heading and a definition, then to a hazard briefing), explain 3 to 4 and 8 (a cause chain of the Nile flood
  grows to a mechanism with an unseen step, then to a solving method a partner can use), argue 2 to 6, 7 and 8 (one
  claim with cited evidence grows to two interpretations weighed, then to a counterclaim from data and a
  counterexample). Grade 7: narrate 9 to 11 and 17 (a lab recount for a teammate grows to how the group solved a
  percent problem, then to a sequence of events at Rome's end), inform 10 to 15 (a classifying paragraph grows to a
  group's plan summed up for another group), explain 11 to 12, 16 and 17 (what each part of an equation means grows
  to a procedure with a reason for each step, then to two causes weighed), argue 13 to 14, 15 and 16 (a counterclaim
  answered grows to a research claim, then to claims from data and conditions). Grade 8: inform 18 to 19 and 26 (a
  formal section with varied transitions grows to a briefing on a proof's steps, then to a mechanism summary),
  argue 19 to 20, 21, 22 and 25 (a congruence claim grows to a counterclaim from a credible source, then to an
  editorial answered), explain 21 to 22, 24 and 26 (a group conflict and its solution grows to why a check on power
  works, then to a mechanism tied to evidence), narrate 23 to 25 (a scene with a flashback grows to a newsletter
  recount for readers who were not there).
- **Every unit, the same moves** (bar items 9 to 12; APPROACH, "Across the unit"): the key language use taught
  openly from a model taken apart, built with the teacher, then made alone; forms taught directly at the text, the
  sentence and the word (each form named with its job); structured talk every day with frames, before any writing;
  both modes (interpretive: listening, reading and viewing; expressive: speaking and writing, with drawing and
  labeled models as a step); one change of audience or purpose; one moment where students name a form and its job
  and judge their own language on a self-check page they keep; the host-teacher note (bar item 14: the content used
  and the language taught, two lines).
- **The talk routine.** One named routine a year, the same every week, each growing from the one before: grade 6
  "role circle" (chair, timekeeper, note taker and reporter, the roles turning each week), grade 7 "build or
  question" (each turn builds on or questions a named speaker's reason), grade 8 "student-led round" (a student
  opens, invites each view, challenges one reason respectfully and sums up). A grade 7 or 8 class that never had
  the earlier years learns the roles in the first week of its first unit (9 or 18).
- **Entry points.** Three, by proficiency (STANDARD section 2): levels 1 and 2, 3 and 4, 5 and 6. Every entry point
  does the same task on the same content at the same thinking demand; levels change the help, never the task (bar
  item 2). Help is withdrawn on the progression's default days: for 5 and 6 the frame comes off after the first
  application day; for 3 and 4 before the end-of-unit writing or talk task; 1 and 2 keep the word bank and lose the
  cloze before the check. Each year also drops one frame the year before kept for 3 and 4 (the rows' "is gone for 3
  and 4" lines). A student moves to the next entry point when the teacher's paper sheet shows the unit's look-fors
  "seen" in the end-of-unit assessment and in one course-level check, or on the school's own progress check (bar
  item 7). Placement and exit use the school's proficiency score; Curio Class gives no placement test and keeps no
  scores. Every unit has notes for newcomers, long-term English learners and English learners with disabilities,
  and a line on telling a language need from a learning need (APPROACH, "Sensitive ground": a gap in English is
  never read as a disability). Long-term English learners are common in middle school: many speak English easily
  and need the academic forms, so their note in each unit points to the written forms and the change of audience,
  never to beginner talk tasks, and treats them as the capable speakers they are.
- **Newcomer strand.** A 20-day on-ramp beside each year, outside its 160 days (STANDARD section 2; Desk
  q-fix-eld-1): every year works the SI 4-12 ids and WIDA.ELD-LA.6-8.Inform only, since cluster 6-8 has no MA, SC
  or SS Inform id. Its content is the school day itself (a schedule with several teachers, lockers and passing time,
  asking for help) and the current host unit's pictures and labeled models. A newcomer who needs early reading in
  English works the ELA K to 2 foundational rows beside it, at an age-fit pace and with age-fit texts. A newcomer
  arriving at any point takes it, then joins the class's current unit at entry points 1 and 2. The strand is written
  with the year's scope and sequence and gets its own sensitivity read before use.
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day in ELD": Understanding is
  the first teaching of a form, a text's structure or a model (the form shown in the host text, named, explained);
  Fluency practices forms already taught (talk rehearsal with the frame, sentence combining, word work and cognate
  sorts) and drafts nothing; Application uses the forms in talk and then in writing on the content (every day that
  drafts or revises is Application); Check is an end-of-unit assessment, mid-unit assessment or course-level check
  day; Respond reteaches the forms the sheet shows "not yet". Fluency is a smaller share than in K to 2 (about a
  fifth of teaching days), since grades 6 to 8 move more of the practice into writing.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Grade 6 picks up the grade 5 rows of the ELD grades 3 to 5 course. Every row grades
  7 and 8 assume was taught the year before in this band (grade 7 assumes grade 6, grade 8 assumes grade 7), and
  grade 6's assumes were taught in grade 5, so each opens as a retrieval task inside a day of the unit that first
  needs it (day 2, after day 1's hook), in the same words as sequence-write rule 2; no unit needs a readiness check.
  The count starts from the student's own grade year. A student who joins without the earlier year meets the
  retrieval task at entry points 1 and 2 with the earlier year's model on the card, and a newcomer takes the strand
  first. A host's earlier unit is retrieved the same way (named in each unit). A mixed-grade group uses the year of
  its youngest grade, and the teacher page says so (STANDARD section 2).
- **Base text.** None of its own (docs/reviews/eld-open-material-2026-11-16.md: no open ELD course exists). Texts are
  the host units' own: the ELA grades 6 to 8 courses' texts, credited through those courses, and the science,
  social studies and math units' Curio Class pages, data tables, records and labeled models. ELD adapts EL
  Education's Language Dive routine (a sentence from the text taken apart and rebuilt; CC BY 4.0), credited once a
  day where used. Curio Class writes the scaffolds (word banks, cause and claim cards, the frames per level, the
  model page each unit builds with the teacher) and each end-of-unit assessment's new content of the same kind,
  Curio Class's own and invented for practice, named per unit. No source map: ELD places no base units.
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs on paper, cards and talk. Any tool a unit names (a model-sentence replay tool:
  hear and replay the day's model sentence; a word deck) has a paper path beside it (the teacher reads the model
  aloud, the cards on the table) and needs no student account. No tool records a student's voice or writing, scores
  pronunciation or places a student at a level (APPROACH, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared display is optional for the model page, the host's data tables and the
  replay tool. No owned code needs technology: models are drawn and labeled on paper, so a paper-path class meets
  every id in full.
- **Costs.** Printing only: word banks, role and question cards and the four model sheets, reused across a year,
  about $4 a unit for a class of 30 (a planning figure, not a quote); no supplies are bought.
- **Home languages.** A help, never a ranking (bar item 6): planning and talk in the home language before English,
  cognates named (including hidden cognates, which share a root but look different), false friends flagged
  (asistir means attend, molestar means bother), and teacher notes on how Spanish does the same thing (other
  languages when a reader exists, APPROACH "Home languages"). No student translates for another, for the class or
  for a family. Family notes are in English and Spanish.
- **Safety.** ELD runs no investigation and no field work. Students talk and write from the host's own data,
  records and models; where a teacher repeats a moment from the host (a cart rolled down a ramp, a magnet brought
  near a clip), it is the host's own step under the host unit's hazards and controls (the host frame's safety sheet
  and its safety read), never a new test, and nothing is heated, tasted or mixed.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`, community: multilingual learners and immigrant families), and every Spanish note and family
  note gets a `spanish` read (APPROACH, "Sensitive ground", Cory 2026-11-21). Before reading, the agent studies the
  APPROACH page's Sensitive ground rules, the federal English learner toolkit (US Department of Education, Office of
  English Language Acquisition, free online, https://www.ncela.ed.gov/educator-support/toolkits/english-learner-toolkit),
  the National Academies 2017 report on children learning English (free to read online) and, for the units on
  floods, hazards, war and leaving home, the National Child Traumatic Stress Network guide for educators of children
  of war (free online, https://www.nctsn.org/sites/default/files/resources//children_of_war_guide_for_educators.pdf).
  Flagged units name more below: units 1 and 7 (a flood and a choice about moving; natural hazards), unit 2
  (punishments and slavery in an old law code), unit 9 (Karuk and Yurok river life), unit 12 (food and the body),
  unit 17 (invasion and the end of a state), unit 18 (loss in wartime and a parent living apart), units 20 and 24
  (slavery at the founding; Native nations and state power), unit 23 (a myth of seizure and a child shared between
  two homes), units 24 and 25 (civics and voting, never a question about status or papers), unit 26 (natural
  selection, beliefs respected). The newcomer strand and all family notes get the read before use. No outside human
  reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14); the course's checks get the answer-key agent's read.

**Weeks.** After-core checks do not take a core check day (docs/pipeline/briefs/frame-write.md, "After-core
courses"; for grades 6 to 8 the stagger is by school day, and week 32 is a finals week that may hold every
subject's year-end check). In the calendar the core frames give today (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md), the core
course-level check days before the finals week are: grade 6 ELA 59 and 60, 109 and 110; math 80 and 118; science
90; social studies 71 and 72, 114 and 115; grade 7 ELA 57 and 58, 109 and 110; math 45 and 119; science 67; social
studies 63 and 64, 116 and 117; grade 8 ELA 89 and 90, 125 and 126; math 70 and 116; science 75; social studies 48
and 49, 96 and 97, 145 and 146. So the ELD mid-year checks sit on grade 6 days 81 and 82 (week 17), grade 7 days
85 and 86 (weeks 17 and 18) and grade 8 days 98 and 99 (week 20), each on days no core check holds, and every
year-end check sits in the finals week (days 158 and 159). The mid-year checks are placed where each year's units
break most cleanly between host runs. The costs are stated plainly: each check carries the unit just before it
unscored (its end-of-unit assessment covers it), and each year's last unit (8, 17 and 26) is checked by its own
end-of-unit assessment only, with that record and the teacher's sheet passed on. In grade 8 every owned id but SC
Explain (unit 26) is sampled by a course-level check; SC Explain is checked by unit 26's end-of-unit assessment and
returns in grade 9. `frame.py calendar` lists these weeks beside other after-core checks; the weeks stay until the
lead's calendar pass across the after-core frames (For the lead, item 4).

**How a check runs in 45 minutes.** The interpretive parts (students read the new text, or the teacher reads it
twice for levels 1 and 2) and the writing task go to the whole class at once; the talk task is heard one student
at a time, about two minutes each, while the others write, over the two days. Each student is heard once in a
check. The respond day groups students by form and by entry point (at most four groups; the others rehearse in
pairs from the frame). No scores are stored; the teacher's paper sheet stays with the teacher (promise 2).
