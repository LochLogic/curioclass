# Scope and sequence: Psychology

Course `psychology`, grade 12 (open to grades 11 and 12), full year, 160 course days, draft (2026-11-23).

The days live in data/plans/psychology/days.csv and the base-text placement in data/plans/psychology/source-map.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them. The course meets every school day (50 minutes), so course day n is school day n, the `school_day` column is blank, and week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. The 16 units keep the frame's order, days and owned rows: 156 unit days and 4 course-level days (a check and a respond day after unit 8, days 81 and 82, and after unit 16, days 159 and 160).

**What holds in every unit.** Each unit opens on its compelling question with a hook on day 1 of the unit. Each closes with an end-of-unit assessment built on an unseen study or source (the full performance) and short items, with a prompt for each target the main task misses, listed in the day's note; the next day is the respond day, the unit's response time, counted once. No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Units 1 and 14 also score a written product after written feedback and revision (the research proposal; the informed action piece). Literacy codes (RST, WHST) and C3 indicators are practiced, not owned, and sit only in notes. Every adapted day carries the credit line once, "Adapted from OpenStax, Psychology, CC BY 4.0", from the retired CC BY edition; days that adapt no section are `ours` in the source map.

**Day kinds.** Most teaching days are Understanding, as the frame expects: almost every day first-teaches a target. Application days are the news-summary paragraph (day 10), the proposal draft and revision (days 11 and 12), the testing history source packet (day 75) and the two informed action days (days 137 and 138). The one Fluency day is unit 6's retrieval-practice set (day 60), practice only, nothing scored.

A grade 11 class (frame, course brief): the three US History I rows, the Algebra II rows and phys-wave-math are taught later the same year, so each day above carries its "if later" bridge in its note, inside that day; the three Algebra I rows take a readiness check in the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook (day 1's note), and bio-sources-of-variation joins the day 14 check.

**The writing thread** builds from short to long: a claim-evidence-reasoning paragraph on a study in the news (day 10); the research proposal, drafted, given written feedback and revised (days 11 and 12); a paragraph on a wrongful-conviction case record (day 58); a paragraph on a classic social psychology study and its critique (day 119); the informed action piece, the year's longest product, drafted and revised (days 137 and 138).

**Models carried.** research-design is introduced on day 5 and used in every later unit that reads a study; biopsychosocial is introduced on day 22 and used in units 4, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15 and 16; source-sourcing opens on day 2 and returns in the informed action (day 136); claim-evidence-reasoning on days 10, 58, 75, 119 and 137.

## Unit 1. The science of psychology (days 1 to 14, weeks 1 to 3)

Arc: what psychology studies, then telling scientific from non-scientific claims, the field's history and perspectives, basic and applied research and careers; then the methods and the research-design model, samples, validity and reliability, replication and the replication crisis (themes A and B), research ethics (theme G), and data analysis on pooled class data. A news-summary paragraph uses all of it, then the proposal is drafted and revised, and the sitting and respond day close the unit. Claims come before methods so the question "can we trust this?" is live before the tools to answer it; ethics comes after the methods so students know what a study would ask of people before judging it; data analysis comes last because it reads the results the methods produce.

- Readiness: none for a grade 12 class (every earlier row is grade 11, retrieved on days 2, 5, 6, 9 and 10).
- End-of-unit assessment, two parts: the research proposal, scored after revision on day 12 (SI.2, SI.3, theme G, with prompts for the parts the proposal misses), and the sitting on day 13 (SI.1, SI.4, themes A and B, an unseen worked claim and study graph). Respond day 14, which also holds unit 2's readiness check in its last ten minutes.
- Candidate: the field's history and major perspectives, kept, 1 day (day 3), inside SI.1.1 and SI.1.2.

## Unit 2. Brain and behavior (days 15 to 25, weeks 3 to 5)

Arc: the divisions of the nervous system, the neuron and synapse, the brain's structures, plasticity, the endocrine system and the tools that study the brain; then behavioral genetics and epigenetics, heredity and environment together with the biopsychosocial model and theme C, and evolutionary psychology. The nervous system comes first because every later biological explanation needs the neuron and synapse; heredity follows so twin studies are read with unit 1's methods and the brain days in hand (the base text puts genetics first; the source map gives the reason).

- Readiness: given on day 14 (four Biology rows); reteach stations of ten minutes on days 16 and 21, in practice time.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 24: an unseen brain or twin study with a prompt per target (BIO.1.1 to BIO.2.3, theme C). Respond day 25, which also holds unit 3's readiness check.
- Candidate: the brain-imaging tools compared, kept, 0 added days, inside BIO.1.6 on day 20.

## Unit 3. Sensation and perception (days 26 to 36, weeks 6 to 8)

Arc: transduction and receptors, psychophysics on a class task, vision and hearing (with the wave retrieval), the chemical and touch senses; then principles of perception and Gestalt grouping, depth and constancy, attention and its limits, and how experience and culture shape perception with theme E. Sensation before perception follows the signal from receptor to cortex; theme E closes the unit because it needs every earlier perception case.

- Readiness: given on day 25 (s8-senses-brain); reteach station on day 27.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 35: an unseen perception study and a prompt for each of the 14 targets. Respond day 36.
- Candidate: multitasking and phone use, kept, 0 added days, inside PER.2.5 on day 33.

## Unit 4. Consciousness and sleep (days 37 to 46, weeks 8 to 10)

Arc: states of consciousness and implicit processing, meditation and hypnosis, the circadian rhythm and the teenage shift, sleep stages, theories of sleep and dreams, sleep disorders, then drug categories and effects and drug action at the synapse with addiction through the biopsychosocial model. Sleep follows consciousness because it is the state students know best; drugs come last so they build on the synapse and the sleep-wake systems.

- Readiness: none (bio-homeostasis-feedback is retrieved on day 39). The family note for the drug days goes home on day 42.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 45: an unseen sleep record or sleep study, drug items on made-up people only. Respond day 46.
- Candidate: the teenage sleep shift and school start times, kept, 0 added days, inside CON.2.1 on day 39.

## Unit 5. Learning (days 47 to 54, weeks 10 to 11)

Arc: classical conditioning, its experimental and clinical uses with Little Albert read for method and ethics, operant conditioning, its uses sorted beside classical cases, observational learning with the Bobo doll studies and media violence weighed from two lines of evidence, and thinking in learning. Classical before operant follows the history and the simpler mechanism; the cognitive side comes last because it qualifies both.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 53: an unseen conditioning or modeling study and scenario items. Respond day 54.
- Candidate: delay of gratification with the later replication, kept, 0 added days, inside LRN.3.2 on day 52.

## Unit 6. Memory (days 55 to 62, weeks 11 to 13)

Arc: encoding, storage and retrieval on a neutral word-list task, the memory systems and their brain structures, study strategies with strong evidence, memory rebuilt at recall with eyewitness evidence, forgetting and memory disorders, and one retrieval-practice set using the unit's own strategies. Memory follows learning, as the frame sets; strategies come before the misinformation day so students use them on the rest of the unit.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 61: an unseen memory study. Respond day 62.
- Candidate: wrongful convictions and eyewitness procedures, kept, 0 added days, inside MEM.2.2 on day 58.

## Unit 7. Thinking and language (days 63 to 71, weeks 13 to 15)

Arc: concepts and schemas, problem solving and decisions, the biases, convergent and divergent thinking with framing and anchoring, then language structure with signed languages, language and thought with acquisition, and the brain's language areas. Thinking builds on unit 6's schemas; language closes the unit because it ties back to unit 2's brain areas.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 70: an unseen judgment study and decision and language items invented for practice. Respond day 71.
- Candidate: framing and anchoring in money decisions, kept, 0 added days, inside COG.2.2 on day 66 (Economics, in the spring, meets them from its side; prose seam only).

## Unit 8. Intelligence (days 72 to 80, weeks 15 to 16)

Arc: the construct and its theories, differences and everyday life in people-first terms, the history of testing from primary sources and its misuse analyzed on the source packet (Buck v. Bell with the dissent, Skinner v. Oklahoma, the apologies), then standardization and the normal curve, reliability and validity of scores, and the influences on scores with stereotype threat. The history comes before the statistics so students meet what scores were used to do before how they are built.

- Readiness: none (alg2-normal-distributions is retrieved on day 76).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 79: an unseen testing-history source or score report. It is the last sitting before the mid-year check; respond day 80 sits between them, since the frame counts the respond day inside the unit (the frame's "the day before the mid-year check" is read as the last sitting before it; see For the lead).
- Candidate: stereotype threat with its mixed record, kept, 0 added days, inside INT.3.2 on day 78.

## Course-level check 1, mid-year (days 81 and 82, week 17)

Day 81 samples units 1 to 8: an unseen study summary read through the research-design model, an unseen data graph and one or two short items per unit, with items on themes A, B, C and E. It follows unit 8's sitting, so its unit 8 item (validity and reliability) is set in units 1 to 7 content, a memory or perception measure. The day's codes are only those it samples. The proposal is not re-scored and its task form is not repeated. Day 82 is the respond day.

## Unit 9. Development across the life span (days 83 to 92, weeks 17 to 19)

Arc: biology and environment together with continuity and stability, the designs that study development, sensitive periods from the Romanian adoptee follow-up, then physical, cognitive and social development, adulthood with death and grief, and emerging adulthood. The methods come first so every later age is read as a study; the life span runs in time order from before birth to old age, and emerging adulthood comes last as the students' own stage.

- Readiness: none.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 91: an unseen longitudinal or cross-sectional study. Respond day 92.
- Candidate: emerging adulthood, kept, 1 day (day 90), inside DEV.2.3.

## Unit 10. Motivation and emotion (days 93 to 103, weeks 19 to 21)

Arc: influences on motivation, culture, achievement and affiliation, hunger and sexual motivation as biology and research, growth mindset on its evidence; then emotion's parts and theories, basic-emotion research from two studies the student weighs, facial feedback with its replications, culture and gender in expression, and negative and positive emotions. Motivation comes first because emotion's theories use arousal.

- Readiness: none. The family note for day 95 goes home before it.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 102: one unseen study with its replication record and a prompt for each of the 12 targets. Respond day 103.
- Candidate: growth mindset with its small and mixed effects, kept, 1 day (day 96), inside MOT.2.2.

## Unit 11. Personality (days 104 to 111, weeks 21 to 23)

Arc: biology and environment with the methods, social-cognitive approaches with delay of gratification revisited, traits and the Big Five with the psychodynamic history, self-concept with the humanistic history, then assessment with the Barnum effect and stability and change from longitudinal data. Approaches come before assessment because each technique measures a theory.

- Readiness: none (alg1-correlation-causation is retrieved on day 108).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 110: an unseen inventory's reliability and validity report. Respond day 111.
- Candidate: personality change across adulthood, kept, 0 added days, inside PSN.2.3 on day 109.

## Unit 12. Social psychology (days 112 to 121, weeks 23 to 25)

Arc: attribution, attitudes and dissonance, stereotypes and implicit bias with the debate on its measures (social cognition); then the presence of others and conformity, obedience with its variations and the prison simulation's critique, group relations and persuasion, attraction and aggression, and helping with the corrected bystander account (social influence). Cognition before influence lets unit 11's person side meet the situation; each classic study is read with its critique on the same day, so no telling stands alone.

- Readiness: none. The counselor is told before day 112.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 120: an unseen study with a published critique. Respond day 121.
- Candidate: the obedience variations and archival reanalyses, kept, 1 day (day 116), inside SOC.2.1.

## Unit 13. Culture, gender and identity (days 122 to 130, weeks 25 to 26)

Arc: culture defined, culture and identity, acculturation, power and privilege, measured effects with theme D, then sex, gender identity and orientation, and minority stress with the 1973 history. The culture rows come first and build on unit 12's prejudice; the gender rows follow so minority stress closes the unit and hands on to unit 14.

- Readiness: none. Counselor told and family note sent before day 122.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 129: an unseen study of culture or identity; no item asks about the student's identity. Respond day 130.
- Candidate: acculturation and bicultural identity, kept, 1 day (day 124), inside MCG.1.2.

## Unit 14. Stress, health and wellness (days 131 to 140, weeks 27 to 28)

Arc: stress as a response, its sources, its health results, coping, resilience with positive psychology's effect sizes, evidence-based wellness strategies with theme F and the choice of a peer question, then the informed action (draft, written feedback by the next lesson, revision and sharing), the sitting and the respond day. Stress and coping come before the action so students check a wellness claim against evidence they have read.

- Readiness: none (bio-homeostasis-feedback is retrieved on day 131).
- End-of-unit assessment, two parts: the informed action piece, scored after revision on day 138 (HLT.2.2, theme F), and the sitting on day 139 (HLT.1, HLT.2.1). Respond day 140. The look back is on day 160.
- The two action days are school days 137 and 138, in course week 28, clear of ELA 12's research milestone days (135, 145, 147, 150 to 156 and 158).
- Candidate: the positive-psychology claims set against their effect sizes, kept, 0 added days, inside HLT.2.1 on day 135.

## Unit 15. Psychological disorders (days 141 to 150, weeks 29 to 30)

Arc: what counts as a disorder and culture's part, the medical and biopsychosocial models, stigma and effects with dated prevalence, classification and why diagnosis is hard, then the main categories on four days (anxiety, obsessive-compulsive and trauma; mood; schizophrenia, dissociative and personality; eating and neurodevelopmental). Perspectives and classification come before categories so no symptom is met without the limits of a label.

- Readiness: none. Counselor told and family note sent before day 141.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 149: an unseen study of diagnosis or prevalence, items on made-up people, never a diagnosis. Respond day 150.
- Candidate: neurodevelopmental disorders and neurodiversity views, kept, 0 added days, inside DIS.2.3 on day 148.

## Unit 16. Treatment and getting help (days 151 to 158, weeks 31 to 32)

Arc: psychological treatments, biomedical treatments with the serotonin claim weighed, why many kinds, treatments for groups with the history of misuse and access to care, providers and their duties, and the help available, on the printed help list. The course ends on help-seeking, as the frame sets.

- Readiness: none. The help list is checked for the school's area before day 151.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 157: an unseen treatment trial summary and where a made-up person could get help. Respond day 158.
- Candidate: access to care, kept, 0 added days, inside THR.1.3 on day 154.

## Course-level check 2, year-end (days 159 and 160, week 32)

## Shorter path

**A shorter period.** On a 45-minute period every day keeps its teaching, its exit ticket and any assessment in full; the opening retrieval task shortens to three minutes, the pooled class-data step is done by the teacher between lessons, and a second worked case on a teaching day becomes at-home reading. On a 40-minute period the same, and the adapted reading (at most about 800 words) is cut to the passage the day's goal needs, read in class; an end-of-unit sitting keeps its full time by moving its short items to the respond day's first ten minutes, before the reteach.

**A year about 10 days short.** Ten named fold-in days, each saving one 50-minute period (500 minutes, 10 days): the course runs in 150 days against the frame's 160. Each fold merges the day's work into the neighbor named, nothing assessed moves, and no owned part is cut. No fold takes a readiness, draft, feedback, revision, end-of-unit or respond day, or any day of units 14 to 16 (frame, Senior calendar).

| Fold-in day | Into | What folds | Saves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 (history and perspectives) | days 1 and 2 | the perspectives become a one-page reference read at home; the history in five minutes of day 1 | 50 min |
| 32 (depth and constancy) | day 31 | the depth cues and constancies taught on one printed card after grouping | 50 min |
| 38 (meditation and hypnosis) | day 37 | one study each, read in the second half of day 37 | 50 min |
| 60 (retrieval-practice set) | warm-ups of days 61 to 71 | the set spread over the unit 7 warm-ups | 50 min |
| 69 (language and the brain) | day 68 | the aphasia cases read as day 68's second source | 50 min |
| 90 (emerging adulthood) | day 89 | the check-set reading merged into adulthood | 50 min |
| 96 (growth mindset) | day 94 | the mindset evidence card read with achievement motivation | 50 min |
| 107 (self-concept) | day 106 | self-concept taught after the traits, humanistic history in brief | 50 min |
| 116 (obedience variations) | day 115 | the original obedience study and one critique beside conformity; the variations become reading | 50 min |
| 124 (acculturation) | day 123 | acculturation read as part of identity development | 50 min |

A school that ends seniors up to a week early uses the last five (days 90, 96, 107, 116 and 124, one each from units 9 to 13), as the frame sets (unit 11's check-set addition already sits inside an owned day, so its fold is the self-concept day), and the year-end check moves into the seniors' last week, still followed by its respond day. These folds are untried on written days; the unit plans confirm them and say so if a unit cannot hold its coverage in the shorter time.

## Source map

data/plans/psychology/source-map.csv places every section of the retired CC BY edition: kept on one day, split over several, merged with another section on one day, or cut (the preface, industrial and organizational sections 13.2 to 13.4, which have no area in the 2022 standard, and the personal application questions as written). Sections that land in two units (7.4, 10.2, 10.3, 11.3) have one row per unit. Glossaries, review questions and solutions travel with their sections and are not lessons; no review question is a scored item without the answer-key agent's read. One `ours` row per unit lists its days with no adapted section (assessment, respond, writing and the frame's own-content days); the four course-level days are ours too. Where the course order differs from the book's (sensation before consciousness, memory before thinking, the nervous system before genetics, and the moved parts of chapters 10, 11 and 12), the row gives the reason, and none harms learning.
