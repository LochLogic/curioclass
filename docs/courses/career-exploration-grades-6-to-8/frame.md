# Course frame: Career Exploration, grades 6 to 8

Course `career-exploration-grades-6-to-8` (catalogue id `cte-explore`), one year, placed in grade 8 (open to grades 6 to 8 in the catalogue, typical grade 8), draft frame (2026-11-25); the scope and sequence sets the days later. This is the pilot frame for career education: Career Pathways (grade 10) copies its shape as the pattern notes of docs/reviews/frame-career-exploration-grades-6-to-8-fixes-2026-11-25.md say.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7 units: one opening unit on the self, the plan and the tools of the course; five cluster units holding the 14 national career clusters in the five groups of STANDARD.md section 3; and one closing unit on the move to high school and the job search.
- **Days.** 96 course days: 92 in units and 4 for the two course-level checks and their respond days. Three sessions a week of 45 minutes, 32 weeks (96 sessions, 72 hours a year), the default time of docs/courses/career-education/APPROACH.md ("Pacing", settled 2026-11-16 by Cory, two or three sessions a week; the session length and count set by the expert reader 2026-11-20, ours and untested). No national body publishes a time figure for this course that we found (APPROACH.md, searched 2026-11-20), so none is cited. Unit days: 16, 14, 15, 11, 10, 16 and 10. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment; no unit runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. The 45 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting the work: a field task day ends with five minutes to collect the piece and the plan page, and the paper production line in unit 3 names five minutes of clean-up (scissors counted back in). Readiness checks take ten minutes. These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them evenly over the 32 weeks, course day n on school day ceil(n x 160 / 96).
  **Why 96 fits.** Each cluster takes about three sessions (two explore days and one field task day); each unit ends with a plan day, an assessment day and a respond day (APPROACH.md, "The kinds of day"); the 57 practice and learner-goal rows are taught inside those days and in about 20 days of their own. Weighed by load, the four-cluster unit (6) and the opening unit (1, which introduces all six of the course's models) are the longest; the two-cluster units (4, 5) the shortest.
  **Shorter path.** A school at two sessions a week runs 64 sessions (48 hours, APPROACH.md): 60 in units (9, 9, 9, 7, 7, 11 and 8) and the same 4 course-level days. The 32 folds: each cluster's second explore day folds into its first (the three job cards are split across table groups and shared, 14 sessions); the two two-day field tasks (unit 2's stand, unit 3's production line) run in one session each, their planning half moved to the cluster's explore day (2); and the units' own learner-goal days fold into explore, packet, skill or field task days (16). Every cluster keeps one explore session and its field task session, and every unit keeps its plan day (the feedback and revision day), its assessment day and its respond day; no fold takes a feedback or revision day (lesson 21). The short path is the floor: counting the sessions that never fold (21 closing days, 16 field task sessions, 14 explore sessions, unit 7's two packet and two skill days) leaves exactly 5 for unit 1's own teaching, so it has no slack and its unit-by-unit folds are named in the table after these bullets ("Shorter path, unit by unit"). The scope and sequence sets each folded session by day; if its read finds a session overloaded, the fix is a change of breadth or dose, which is Cory's call ("For the lead").
- **Day kinds.** In the words of docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md: an **explore day** (a field, a job or a part of the self studied from a dated source) is Understanding; a **skill day** that first teaches a career task (the first resume line, the first interview answer, the first email to an employer, the first pay table read) is Understanding, because first teaching wins; a **field task day** or **plan day** is Application (every day that drafts or revises the piece or the plan); the assessment day is Check; the respond day is Respond. Fluency is small in this course: rereading a pay table or an outlook figure on a new field (the practice that returns, APPROACH section field 8) and a second interview answer, a few minutes inside other days, never a whole day.
- **Standard.** The 2024 national career clusters framework (14 clusters, 72 sub-clusters, 12 career ready practices) as content, and the 2024 National Career Development Guidelines as learner goals (STANDARD.md sections 1 to 3; ids in data/standards/cte-career-clusters-2024.txt and data/standards/ncdg-2024.txt, our own lines in ncdg-2024-words.txt). The course owns 85 rows: the 14 clusters, the 14 first-look sub-clusters fixed in the progression (one per cluster, settled by Cory 2026-11-21 and the expert reader), the 12 practices (each first taught in a task and judged with two or three look-fors on the piece, never on a list), and 66 learner-goal indicators on 45 rows. The other 58 sub-clusters are Career Pathways' (each cluster's map and job cards name them here; none is studied at depth). Cluster ids are names, not verbs, and no practice or indicator sentence is quoted (STANDARD.md section 1). The values rule: no route is the default; the plan is scored on its reasoning, never on the path chosen or the student's circumstances.
- **Base text.** None. Sources are public labor data, each figure with its source and year: the US Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook and wage tables (public domain, https://www.bls.gov/ooh/), the federal occupational database O*NET (CC BY 4.0, https://www.onetonline.org/, attribution in the credit line only) and a state labor office where the school adds one. Every data sheet is dated with the school year it was built for and carries the line "check these figures this school year"; each training route on a job card is checked against the handbook's typical entry level that year (STANDARD.md section 3). Figures shown are national unless marked local. Everything else (the cluster map, the 42 job cards, the 14 data sheets, the 14 field task sheets, the invented workers' accounts and every invented data set) Curio Class writes, each invented set marked "invented for practice".
- **The plan and the portfolio.** The career plan (introduced in unit 1) and the portfolio (from unit 1's plan day) are carried all year. Every unit's plan day returns written feedback on the unit's field task pieces and plan lines, and the student revises one piece of their choice (practice 12's portfolio choice with a reason) and writes the plan's changed line for each cluster studied (the cluster rows' "plan line the cluster changed"). The plan is private: it stays with the student, is read only by the teacher, and is never shown to the class.
- **Packet days.** Unit 7 holds the course's two packet days: each student runs a self-directed job study of one sub-cluster of their own choice (g8-all2-3-own-learning), from any of the 58 sub-clusters not studied at depth here, with the cluster's data sheet, the course's task template and a check-in line; the teacher meets each student briefly on the second packet day (the step done, one figure, the next step). Packet choices follow the school's choices on the sensitive sub-clusters (STANDARD.md section 5), with a same-cluster alternative offered; a packet touching one of the six sensitive sub-clusters carries its teacher note and the sensitivity read before use, and the packets on the topics STANDARD.md section 6 names carry health's disclosure steps. Every packet task is an age-appropriate paper task, with no clinical, emergency-response, weapons or hazardous equipment activity, and is exploration only, claiming none of Pathways' sub-cluster standards. This is the self-guided packet Career Pathways runs at larger size (STANDARD.md section 3, "Running it with one teacher"), tried here once.
- **Placement and grade.** Placed in grade 8, the typical grade (Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-career-1). A school may run it in grade 7 (STANDARD.md sections 3 and 7): it then swaps the grade 8 ELA leans for `e7-informative-writing`, `e7-revise-purpose-audience`, `e7-present-salient` and `e7-collab-discussion`, teaches the grade 7 math leans alongside rather than before (each field task then carries its own worked example of the percent or rate it needs), and treats every grade 8 lean (social studies, computer science, SEL, library) as a bridge the unit teaches. The counts in this frame start from grade 8.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** No career education course comes before this one, so no row assumes an earlier career row. Every unit gives the readiness items of APPROACH.md unit field 4 (one short item on reading a table or a percent, one on reading a dated figure; ten minutes, with a ten-minute reteach that models the same reading): unit 1's in the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook; unit 4's in the last ten minutes of the mid-year check's respond day (day 47); every other unit's in the last ten minutes of the previous unit's respond day. Day 1 of units 2 to 7 stays clean for the hook and the compelling question. The cross-subject leans count by the counted rule (sequence-write.md rule 2): the math 7 rows were last used in grade 7, the year before (math grade 7: `m7-unit-rate-fractions` in unit 4, days 47 to 68; `m7-percent-problems` in unit 4, days 47 to 68, again in units 5, 6 and 8 to day 153 and in the year-end check, days 158 to 160; `m7-compare-populations` in unit 8, days 139 to 157, and the year-end check), so each opens as a retrieval task inside the first day that uses it (units 1 to 6, named in the Units table). Computer science `g7-viz-choices` is an optional course's row (lesson 32), so it is a bridge, not an assumption: the field task sheet in units 1 and 2 shows one fair and one misleading chart of the same table, names the difference and has the student repair the misleading one, as retrieval for a student who took computer science grade 7 and first teaching for one who did not; library `g6-lateral-reading` is assumed from grade 6 and gets a readiness item in unit 1's slot, unless the library 6 to 8 course's scope shows a grade 7 use, in which case it is retrieval. The scope gate's count governs, and the scope fix pass updates this bullet.
- **Scored evidence.** Each unit's end-of-unit assessment is a product, never a quiz about clusters: the field task piece the student chose and revised, and the plan's changed lines, scored on a rubric with two or three look-fors per practice and two sample pieces at different levels, plus three to five unseen short items on reading data (one from each cluster's data sheet in the unit). **Practice evidence.** Portfolio choice never waives a practice: the scored set is the chosen revised piece, the revised plan lines, and a short evidence attachment for each practice first taught in the unit that the chosen piece does not show (an excerpt of the other piece or the teacher's record of it, with its two or three look-fors). The attachments by unit: unit 2, the stand's role card record (practice 4) and its budget line with the wage-against-costs sheet (practice 9); unit 3, the production line's timeline and layout (7), its failed fix, two weighed fixes and second try (3), the student's role, deadline and slip note (1), and the route's effects list (11); unit 4, the automated worker's next two steps (6); unit 5, the idea list, the two sketches and the defense (8); unit 7, the employer email and the record of the spoken answer (2). Units 1 and 6 need none (unit 1's one field task shows practices 5 and 10, the plan day practice 12; unit 6 first teaches no practice). Each attachment gets written feedback with the pieces and one revision on the plan day, so it is never scored on the day it was first taught. The piece is drafted on a field task day, gets written feedback after class, and is revised on the plan day before the assessment day scores it; no score comes from a day a topic is first taught. One full performance per assessment day.
- **Paper path.** Every task runs on paper: printed job cards, data sheets, the cluster map, the decision steps card and the plan page. Where a day offers a tool (a dated job profile, a pay table reader, a private plan template, a mock-interview question bank), the paper path does the same task in full, and no tool needs a student account or stores a plan, an interest or an answer off the device (APPROACH.md, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Devices.** None per student; one shared device for practice 5. One shared teacher screen or projector shows a profile or table on some explore days (the projector room of APPROACH section field 10). Practice 5 (digital tools and data) is the one owned code that needs technology: its practice includes operating a digital tool, not only reading a sorted table (the current practices as a state reproduces them, North Dakota Career and Technical Education, https://www.cte.nd.gov/sites/www/files/documents/CareerReady/New12ALL.pdf, opened 2026-11-25 by the second read). So in unit 1 each pair takes a turn of about three minutes at one shared device with a free offline spreadsheet (enter two job rows with source and year, sort by one column, read the decision the sort supports) while the class builds and sorts the same table on paper, and each student names one privacy step (no names or personal facts in the file, saved only on the school device). A school with a class set runs it in pairs at the devices. The paper path does the whole table, sort and decision by hand; a student who cannot use any device that year meets the digital operation part as begun, not taught, and Career Pathways' g10-prac-5-digital-data finishes it (the teacher record says so). Data Science and Artificial Intelligence describes automated tools and never uses one (settled by the expert reader, 2026-11-21).
- **Family note.** One per unit in English and Spanish (APPROACH.md unit field 9): what the unit explores, what a student may take home (the job card, the plan page), and one question about a person's own work, to ask a family member or instead any trusted adult, or to answer for an invented worker or from a public interview or profile, so no student is made to reveal a family's work status (Cory, 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-career-1); sharing is the student's choice.
- **What a school may leave out.** No owned row is on a sensitive sub-cluster (the six of STANDARD.md section 5 are named on the cluster map and in job cards only, a firefighter on the Public Service and Safety cards). A school may swap any section that touches Military and National Security, Behavioral and Mental Health, Emergency Response, Judicial Systems, Public Safety or Resource Extraction for a job card from another sub-cluster of the same cluster; every owned id is still claimed, and the course page says so.
- **Sensitive ground.** No task asks about a student's or family's income, immigration or work status, a criminal record or health (STANDARD.md section 6). Job models are invented workers from many groups; one worker from a group seldom shown in each field is met in every cluster, disabled workers doing the work among them across the year. A student's own resume line uses a school address or none. Unit 6 is the pay-gap unit (it holds g8-cd3-6-uncommon-jobs with g8-cd2-7-stereotypes and g8-cd3-5-open-jobs), so every one of its sections carries health's steps for when a student tells you something (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page, as does any section in units 4 or 6, and any unit 7 packet, that touches the sub-clusters above.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md, kind `sensitivity`); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Communities and sources per unit are in the last column of the Units table: disabled workers (every unit), the pay-gap unit (6), the military route (3), Resource Extraction and rural and tribal land work (4), family money (5), and any unit 7 packet a student takes on a sensitive sub-cluster (7).
- **Safety read.** Units 3 (paper production line with scissors), 4 (an optional seed tray) and 6 (the menu task, paper only unless a school adds cooking) get the safety agent's read before the course is called finished, against these guides by title, each free to read: Talking Safety, the young worker safety curriculum of the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/talkingsafety/); the young workers pages of the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (https://www.osha.gov/young-workers); and the position statement Safety and School Science Instruction (National Science Teaching Association, https://www.nsta.org/nstas-official-positions/safety-and-school-science-instruction) for hands-on hazards in general; and, for the optional seed tray, Food Safety for Your School Gardens, Classrooms, and Greenhouses (University of Maryland Extension, FS-1077, https://dev.extension.umd.edu/resource/food-safety-your-school-gardens-classrooms-and-greenhouses-fs-1077), for growing medium, dust and hand washing. Opened 2026-11-25 by the two frame reads: all four hold at these addresses; the safety agent opens each again before its read and replaces any that has moved, keeping the title. A student sign-off form is never cited as guidance. The last column of the Units table gives each hands-on unit's hazards and controls in a "Safety (days.csv words)" sentence.
- **Literacy and other side standards.** None claimed. Writing an email, a resume line or a short report to an owner, and presenting a five-minute lesson, are practiced here, not owned; ELA grade 8 owns the leans named in Cross-subject.
- **Reading level.** A job profile or worker account is at most 150 words at grade 8 level; longer sources are read aloud (APPROACH.md section field 9).
