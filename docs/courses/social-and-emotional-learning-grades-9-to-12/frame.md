# Course frame: Social and Emotional Learning, Grades 9 to 12

Course `social-and-emotional-learning-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `sel-9-12`), grades 9, 10, 11 and 12, one band course taught over four school years in the advisory period. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25, modeled on the K to 2 pilot frame and its reads; the scope and sequence sets the ordered days and school-day positions later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 24, six a year (STANDARD section 3): units 1 to 6 in grade 9, 7 to 12 in grade 10, 13 to 18 in grade 11, 19 to 24 in grade 12, numbered through the band so a seam or a check can name one unit without a year beside it. The Year column of the Units table names the year. Each year teaches all 40 sub-skills of the five competencies (self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making) at the 9 to 12 depth, a plan the student designs, with its trade-offs and a judgment of how well it worked (STANDARD section 3, depth ladder): grade 9 on school-day cases, grade 10 adding a second person's view, grade 11 adding a cost or a pressure, grade 12 (the owner year) adding a setting past school. Each year's units follow the order and the unit notes of the progression rows (data/progressions/sel.csv).
- **Days.** 256 sessions: 64 a year for four years, two sessions a week over 32 weeks, 40 minutes a session in grades 9 to 12 (STANDARD section 2: the advisory period), so 80 minutes a week. Each year is 60 sessions in six units and 4 for its mid-year and year-end course-level checks, each check with its respond session. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (the unit's Check session) and its Respond session; no unit runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. Unit sizes follow Desk q-fix-sel-1 exactly (STANDARD section 2, "How the sessions add up"): a three-group unit is 11 (3 groups of Understanding, Fluency, Application, then Check and Respond) and a two-group unit is 10 (2 groups of Understanding, two Fluency, Application, then Check and Respond), four of 11 and two of 10 every year. The course-level check after a unit is that unit's Check session and its respond is that unit's Respond session (frame-write, "After-core courses"): the 40-minute session holds the unit's unseen plan page and at most two sampled written items from the year's earlier units, so no unit loses a session to the checks and no size departs from q-fix-sel-1 (For the lead 1). In the Units table the unit before a check shows its teaching sessions only (an 11 shows 9, a 10 shows 8) and the check rows hold the other 2. Grades 9, 11 and 12 run 10, 11, 10, 11, 11, 11; grade 10 runs 10, 10, 11, 11, 11, 11 (its third unit has three groups). Course-level checks fall after the third and sixth unit of each year: the mid-year check at sessions 30 and 31 (weeks 15 and 16), the year-end check at sessions 63 and 64 (week 32, the finals week; frame-write, "Grades 6 to 12"). Short path, one session a week (32 a year, STANDARD C2): every unit and group kept, fewer minutes a unit, never fewer units. A three-group unit is 5 (3 sessions where each group's Understanding and Fluency share one, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond together); a two-group unit is 6 (2 shared, a second rehearsal per group, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond). Grades 9, 11 and 12: 6, 5, 6, 5, 5, 5; grade 10: 6, 6, 5, 5, 5, 5; 32 in every year. On the short path the shared Check and Respond session of the unit before a check is the course-level check and its respond. Readiness: every row assumes the same sub-skill one year before. Grade 9's rows assume the grade 8 rows (SEL 6 to 8, the year before); grades 10 to 12 assume this course's year before. So each unit opens with retrieval inside the session opening, never a readiness check. A "Given:" readiness item is used only where a school left out the earlier unit (Leave-out, below) or starts the course after grade 9 (a transfer student or a school that begins SEL in grade 10), never where the class used the earlier form the year before (then the opening is retrieval). The Given: item is the readiness evidence, marked on the class sheet; the response is kept apart from it: where it shows "not yet", the unit's first Understanding session teaches the card's one-step form in its first ten minutes.
- **Grade 12 seniors who leave early.** Where seniors finish school before week 32, unit 24 runs in its short-path form (5 sessions) with the year-end check in its shared Check and Respond session, so no sub-skill is dropped and no unit is cut; the school's last senior week is the check week.
- **Inside the 40 minutes.** Every session has one opening of about 5 minutes (a calm start from the coping plan from unit 2 onward, or, on each sub-skill group's Understanding session, one retrieval item with a short prompt for each earlier card the group assumes, never two openings), about 28 minutes of teaching and rehearsal, about 5 minutes for the exit item and the class sheet, and about 2 minutes to hand out and collect cards and plan pages. An Understanding session names and models at most three new sub-skills, each shown on an invented case and named as steps on its card; an eight-row unit splits 3, 3 and 2, a seven-row unit 3, 2 and 2. Units with a pilot or service task (2, 6, 12, 18, 21, 23) take set-up and clean-up from the 28 minutes, and their unit plans say how.
- **Standard.** CASEL 5 competencies, as written in data/standards/casel-5.txt (40 sub-skills, the five competency names and the codes CASEL.Competency.N). Grade 12 owns every code for the band; grades 9, 10 and 11 rows begin each one (~ in the rows), so their Owned standards cells are `None` and What students learn names each begun code with its owner unit in grade 12. The "Core link and routines" column holds, for SEL, the unit's one link to a core course of the same year (bar part 9 item 9): a lean the teacher may use, not a strand. Library: AASL.V.D.1 is taught with SM3 (owner unit 20), AASL.V.D.2 with SA8 (unit 20) and AASL.III.D.2 with RS4 (unit 21) in every SEL band (library STANDARD section 4, Desk q-fix-library-1; data/progressions/library-excluded.csv names them placed in SEL). The three owner rows name the codes in their standard cells and these units' Owned standards name them (scripts/progression.py accepts the three for SEL, owned once in each band's last year, 2026-11-26). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against APPROACH's kinds of day and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/sel.md) and the bar's part 9; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add.
- **Base text.** None. Every case, scene card, plan page and step card is Curio Class's own, with invented students, families, workplaces and community groups. Four settled court cases carry SO6, one a year, each taught from a court or museum source with a credit line, in our own words: Mendez v. Westminster (1947) in unit 5, Brown v. Board of Education (1954) in unit 9, Morgan v. Virginia (1946) in unit 18 and Heart of Atlanta Motel v. United States (1964) in unit 22. Sources for the sensitivity agent to read first, each with the community concerned (named in the unit's preparation note) and opened by the Codex read 2026-11-25 unless marked: Mendez (unit 5, Mexican American families; the Mendez family was of Mexican and Puerto Rican heritage): the U.S. Courts account with Sylvia Mendez's own words (https://www.uscourts.gov/data-news/judiciary-news/2024/09/26/mendez-v-westminster-how-a-young-girls-courage-transformed-schools). Brown (unit 9, Black American plaintiffs and families): the National Archives page (https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/brown-v-board-of-education) and Cheryl Brown Henderson's account at the Brown Foundation (https://www.brownvboard.org/brown-foundation-story); the page tells the 1954 ruling apart from the slower, resisted work of carrying it out. Morgan (unit 18, Black American interstate passengers): the Library of Virginia's account (https://www.lva.virginia.gov/collections/educator-resources/changemakers/items/show/44); any specific personal cost the unit teaches needs a separately named community account. Heart of Atlanta (unit 22): the Library of Congress Civil Rights Act exhibition section (https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/civil-rights-act/immediate-impact.html#obj204), whose linked testimony is the community-source route. The Oyez pages (https://www.oyez.org/cases/1940-1955/328us373, https://www.oyez.org/cases/1964/515) are browser check: the lead opens them in Claude in Chrome. Brown is also SEL 3 to 5's grade 5 case (unit 15): unit 9 returns to it on purpose at new depth (there the fact and three fairness questions; here how the norm looked to the people it held down and to those it favored, at the grade 10 second-person demand), and the 6 to 8 frame uses other cases. The invented partners from another place (units 3, 9, 16 and 22) take their one custom from a source the unit plan names, and the sensitivity read checks it.
- **Shared steps with health and PE.** The talk turn card, calm-down steps, I-statement frame, goal steps card and refusal steps use the one set of words in STANDARD section 7 ("Shared steps"), grown for 9 to 12 by the rows: the goal steps card becomes a designed plan (goal, dated steps, the trade-off accepted, a check date, a verdict), stop-think-choose gains a sixth step (look back), the problem size meter becomes a support map, the feelings scale adds what set the feeling off and what it pushes toward, the calm-down steps become a coping plan (five steps, a tool for each setting, a back-up, a review date), the talk turn card adds a check-back step and the I-statement frame keeps the 6 to 8 words, whose "I need ___" is already the request, and adds a check-back as a fifth part: "I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need ___. Does that work for you?". The refusal second push stays "say it again, move away, tell" (health's boundary steps). Where health or PE teaches a card first in a year, the SEL unit opens with it as retrieval where the school runs that course, and teaches it itself where it does not (Cross-subject, Health and PE).
- **Leave-out.** A school may leave out a unit (STANDARD section 5.6); the course page and the family note name the left-out sub-skill ids the course does not claim that year. Where a later unit uses a card the left-out unit made, that unit's first Understanding session teaches the card's one-step form itself in its first ten minutes, in place of the retrieval opening and part of that session's rehearsal, and claims none of the left-out unit's sub-skills. Bridges: the designed goal plan and the support map (unit 1; first needed by units 2 and 4, then every later unit that plans); the six-step stop-think-choose with its look-back step (unit 1, g9-soc-read-situation; first needed by unit 2, whose stressor pilot is judged with it, then units 3 and 5: unit 2's bridge models naming the problem, choosing a next step and checking what happened, in place of the retrieval opening and the first five minutes of rehearsal); the two-part feelings scale and the coping plan (unit 2; units 4, 6 and 8); the talk turn card's check-back step and the five-part I-statement frame (unit 4; units 6, 7 and 8); the refusal options, fairness questions, who-is-affected map and upstander options come from SEL 6 to 8, so a class without them gets the grade 8 one-step form in the first ten minutes of the unit that first uses each (units 4, 5, 5 and 6). In grades 10 to 12 the year before is the fallback: the left-out unit's rows rest on the same sub-skill's row a year earlier (or the nearest earlier year that was taught), checked at its one-step form only.
- **Kinds of day.** APPROACH "Kinds of day": Understanding (the skill shown on an invented case, then the steps named on the card, at most three new sub-skills), Fluency (the steps rehearsed in pairs or small groups on new cases until quick; in a two-group unit the first Fluency rehearses every sub-skill of the group and the second replays them all against a changed case, so the short year's fold-in of the second cuts no sub-skill's only rehearsal), Application (the student designs the plan on a new case or a real class task, names its trade-off and runs it), Check (an unseen written plan page and a watched rehearsal, against look-fors), Respond (a short reteach for the step not yet seen, and the rehearsal again). Every session rehearses; no session is posters and discussion alone. Each year is taught a new way, not the same lessons again: grade 9 designs and judges a plan on a school-day case, grade 10 plans with a partner who holds the other view, grade 11 plans under a cost or a pressure and judges what it cost, grade 12 plans for a setting past school and judges whether the plan would carry.
- **Evidence.** A class sheet per unit, the step marked "seen" or "not yet" on the day it was seen. It records a step, not a rating of the student; no behavior rating of any student is collected (STANDARD C). It is the teacher's working paper and is thrown away at the end of its unit (APPROACH, Daily evidence); a course-level check keeps its own sheet only until its respond session. Feelings in a check are a character's, never the student's own. Private pages (SA7, SA9) stay with the student, checked only that the steps were followed. End-of-unit evidence in every unit: an unseen plan page on a new invented case (the plan, its trade-off and a judgment of how it worked) taken by the whole class in the Check session; the watched rehearsal from an unseen card is marked across the unit's last Application session and the Check session, pairs not yet seen going first in the Check (no score from a session where a skill is first taught). On the short path the page sits in the shared Check and Respond session and the rehearsal is marked in the Application session. Any student may do a rehearsal with the teacher alone, not in front of the class, in writing or with a card (APPROACH, supports). Each student has their own look-for inside pair work. Each grade 12 owner unit's plan names which owned skills are judged in its Application sessions and which in its Check, so every owned skill has a named evidence point (unit 20 is the model).
- **Evidence base.** No study has tried this course; it is never called evidence-based or rated. The course page carries the logic model (C16) and this line (bar part 9 item 8; APPROACH, "Where the evidence is thin").
- **Carry it out and families.** Each unit names one cue the student uses outside the session ("Carry it out" in What students learn) and one family activity, sent in the unit's family note. Family notes are the only translated pages, in English and Spanish (STANDARD section 2); student pages are English only for now. A student 18 or over gets the family note and makes their own leave-out choice, with the school's own rules still applying (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-22, q-prog-sel-1; AGENTS.md).
- **Fidelity and before you teach.** A paper fidelity checklist per session (did it run as planned, which part was cut) and a "before you teach" note opening each unit's teacher page: the skill as an adult uses it and one thing to model, written for an advisory teacher of any subject, not an SEL specialist. Not training, and not claimed as such (bar part 9 items 11 and 13).
- **Equity.** Families of every kind appear in cases, LGBTQ families included; no student is asked about a family or culture by name, or asked to teach or explain their own culture. Home languages and culture are welcome as strengths when offered, never asked about (SA2). SA6 (snap judgments) is checking one's own guess on invented cases, never blame of a group. Student voice in class and school life (a class stressor in unit 2, a ninth-grade proposal in unit 6, a fairness fix in unit 12, a routine change in unit 18), with no cause picked by the course and the analysis of causes left to social studies (bar part 9 item 12). Grade 12 has its say through an invented graduate's case; real public action stays in the US Government and Politics course (Desk q-prog-sel-1).
- **Devices and paper path.** No device for students. Every session runs on the paper path: plan pages, scene cards, step cards, case logs and class charts, printed or written by hand. Invented apps, feeds and posts appear as printed mock pages with no product names. Any online tool a later unit plan adds has a paper path beside it and needs no student account.
- **Tools and costs.** Kept all band: the goal steps plan page, the six-step stop-think-choose card, the support map, the two-part feelings scale, the coping plan page (grade 9 units 1 and 2) and the talk turn card with its check-back step and the I-statement frame (grade 9 unit 4). Each unit names its tools, what Curio Class writes and a provisional cost for 30 students; printing runs about $10 a unit, about $60 a year and $240 for the band, plus small project supplies the class chooses in the pilot units (up to about $20 a pilot, gloves and bags for a cleanup, boxes for a drive). No supply ceiling is set for SEL (For the lead 7); these are planning figures, not quotes.
- **Safety.** Pilot and service tasks (units 2, 6, 12, 18, 21, 23) run inside school grounds by default; a book drive is the default community task, and an off-site park cleanup runs only under the school's field-trip and volunteer policy, with gloves and bags, no sharps or glass picked up (a seen needle or sharp is not touched; the student tells the adult), adults at the front and back, and the school's permission forms. Each such unit names its hazards and controls. Guides the safety read uses: the school's own field-trip and volunteer policy, plus Colorado's "Field Trips and Extracurricular Activities: School Nurse Checklist" (https://ed.cde.state.co.us/shs/fieldtrips, named by the Codex read 2026-11-25), whose accessibility, health-support, emergency-response and environmental points the activity plan carries; it does not replace the cleanup's own hazard check.
- **Disclosure.** Stress, help, friendship, refusal and upstander units can bring a disclosure. Every unit follows health's steps (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"): the school's reporting route and the counselor. Health owns substances, sexual health, dating and dating violence, and suicide and self-harm help (health 9 to 12 units 12, 19, 20 and 21, taken in grade 9); SEL never teaches them, and its teacher notes name the health unit where a case comes near.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Before units 5, 9, 18 and 22 (the settled court cases) the agent reads the sources named under Base text and checks the case is taught with dignity and as settled fact, the community concerned named and the ruling told apart from carrying it out; before units 3, 9, 16 and 22 (culture, identity and difference) it checks that no student is asked to explain their own culture and that each invented partner from another place is shown as one person's way, never a whole culture's; in units 2, 4, 8, 10, 14, 17, 20, 23 and 24 it checks each case stops short of health's topics and names the health unit. Units 2, 6, 12, 18, 21 and 23 also get the safety agent's read. The Spanish family notes get a translation agent's read (For the lead 6). The course-level checks get the answer-key agent's read.

### Handed on: no SEL course after

Grade 12 is the last SEL year; the grade 12 rows hand on to no row (their hands_on cells are empty). Each thread ends in its grade 12 owner unit, with the plan written for a setting past school (work, training, college, service or home), and the student keeps the band's plan pages: the one-year goal plan and support map (unit 24), the coping plan (unit 20) and the looking-ahead sketch (unit 19). The grade 12 year-end check is the band's last check.

## Course-level checks

Each year has a mid-year check and a year-end check. Each is the Check session of the unit it follows, widened: the unit's own unseen plan page and watched rehearsal (so the unit is checked there, not twice), plus at most two sampled written items from the year's earlier units on new invented cases, in one 40-minute session (the unit's page about 12 minutes, then the two sampled items about 4 minutes each, one prompt per part; no sampled rehearsal; each student has their own look-for in the pair rehearsal, and the unit plan's timed probe sets how many unfinished pairs may enter the Check); its Respond session is the unit's Respond session, reteaching the steps the sheet shows "not yet" across the unit and the sampled items. They sit after the third unit of each year (sessions 30 and 31, weeks 15 and 16) and after the sixth (sessions 63 and 64, week 32). Grades 9 to 12 have no after-core rule but the finals week (frame-write, "After-core courses"; CALENDAR.md stagger rules 3 and 4), so the year-end check sits in week 32, the finals week, and the mid-year check at the middle of the year; no week was moved to step around another after-core course's check (the lead's calendar pass, For the lead 10). Unit sheets are thrown away at the end of their unit (Evidence); a check's sheet is kept only until its respond session, then thrown away. Every unit is checked by its own end-of-unit assessment, and units 1, 2, 4 and 5 of each year are sampled once more in the year's checks; units 3 and 6 are assessed through those checks and return in the next year's retrieval openings. Grade 12's year-end check is the band's last. On the short path each check and its respond share one session, the shared Check and Respond of the unit before it (about 5 minutes opening, 15 for the page and rehearsals, 13 for reteach and the rehearsal again, 7 to close and collect); where seniors leave early, grade 12's year-end check runs in unit 24's short-path form in the school's last senior week.
