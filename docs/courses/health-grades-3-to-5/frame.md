# Course frame: Health, grades 3 to 5

Course `health-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `health-3-5`), grades 3, 4 and 5, one band course with one part per grade year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 21 through the band: 7 in grade 3 (units 1 to 7), 7 in grade 4 (units 8 to 14), 7 in grade 5 (units 15
  to 21). Six of them are sensitive units (6, 7, 13, 14, 20, 21), each removable. Each year is whole on its own: its
  own units, end-of-unit assessments, mid-year check and year-end check.
- **Days.** 90: 30 a year for three years. Grades 3 and 4 have 26 days in units and 4 course-level days (a mid-year
  check and its respond day, a year-end check and its respond day); grade 5 has 25 days in units and 5 course-level
  days (its year-end check has two respond days, below). One lesson a week, 30 minutes long, 30 weeks a year (weeks 1
  to 30 of the 32-week school year; weeks 31 and 32 hold core year-end checks in all three grades and no health), so
  the 30 minutes hold the opening scenario, the work, clean-up (active days name five minutes for it) and collecting
  sheets. The dose is the health approach page's default exactly (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, Pacing: K to 5
  about 30 sessions a year of 30 minutes, kept by Cory 2026-11-21, Desk q-prework-open-2). Days are weighed by load,
  not equal shares: a row that is one plain fact shares a day with its neighbor, a row that introduces a step card (a
  skill model) gets its own day, and every unit's last day is its end-of-unit assessment. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (no unit here runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment). At one lesson a week a
  unit of four days runs four school weeks, which is what lets the grade 5 habit log (unit 15) run for several weeks
  inside its unit. These are course days: docs/courses/CALENDAR.md places them on school weeks. The national figure is
  higher: the 2007 National Health Education Standards (second edition, page 76) recommend at least 80 hours a year in
  grades 3 to 12; this course gives 15 hours a year. The course page names that figure and the gap so a school can add
  time (APPROACH.md, Pacing). A sensitive unit's days count as optional (STANDARD.md section 5 rule 6): a school that
  leaves one out uses its days to extend the non-sensitive unit named in its row. A school that meets health twice a
  week for half the year keeps the same order and checks; the scope and sequence says so in its short path.
- **Standard.** National Health Education Standards, 2024, SHAPE America: the 39 indicators of the 3-5 span
  (`NHES.S<n>.5.<n>`), each owned by one row (11 in grade 3, 14 in grade 4, 14 in grade 5) and placed in one unit
  here; ids and our own words only (data/standards/nhes-2024.txt), never the indicator text. Review tool: CDC's
  Health Education Curriculum Analysis Tool (HECAT), 2021 edition, keyed to the 2007 standards
  (docs/standards-notes/health-2007-to-2024.md); each unit names the healthy behavior outcomes it serves
  (`HECAT.<topic>.HBO<n>`), every day ties to one (bar criterion 1), and the units together cover the 3 to 5 column
  of the STANDARD.md section 4 map. Every year touches all eight standards, and every skill standard (2 to 8) is
  practiced in at least two topics in the band (STANDARD.md section 4), counted with the sensitive rows taken out so
  the claim holds in a school that leaves them out: standard 2 in personal health, mental health and tobacco; 3 in
  safety, personal health, alcohol and other drugs, and food; 4 in mental health, violence prevention, tobacco,
  safety, and alcohol and other drugs; 5 in safety, food and mental health; 6 in personal health and physical
  activity (units 8 and 15, through two row tags added in this pass, For the lead 3); 7 in personal health,
  physical activity and mental health; 8 in physical activity, violence prevention, personal health, tobacco,
  safety and food. The C3 column of the Units table holds, for health, the skill standard the unit teaches or
  practices and its step card (the model). Check set: each unit planner reads the HECAT 2021 module of its topic,
  3-5 knowledge and skill expectations, before planning (STANDARD.md section 3); the "Check set adds" column names
  what that read is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds, with the health lines of "What a
  health day never does" (APPROACH.md).
- **Base text.** None. Health is Curio Class's own: scenarios, step cards, short character cases, invented labels and
  ads, and family notes are written by Curio Class. Facts come from public health agencies (CDC, the federal food and
  drug agency's label pages, USDA's food guide pages, the national highway safety agency, the national poison help
  line), each with its source and year, checked by the health expert agent (below). No trade book, paid video or
  library visit.
- **The health unit shape.** Each unit names its behavior outcomes, three objectives (knowing, valuing, doing) and
  one skill with its steps (APPROACH.md). A skill is taught in the bar's five steps: why it matters, the steps, a
  model (the teacher says each step aloud on a scenario), practice on a short scenario, feedback. Grade 3 to 5 step
  cards are four or five short written steps. The teacher holds the model on the first fluency day, pairs hold the
  card on the second, the card is withdrawn on the first application day, and the end-of-unit assessment is taken
  without it. At one lesson a week a skill that returns gets its card back for one day, then goes without. Norms and
  pressure (bar criterion 4): each year states one plain-word norm on an Understanding day of a non-sensitive unit,
  in words with no figure below grade 6 (APPROACH.md, Kinds of day): grade 3 unit 2, "most people your age do not
  smoke or vape"; grade 4 unit 12, "most children your age have never tried a cigarette or a vape"; grade 5 unit 17,
  "most students your age do not drink alcohol or use drugs" (each from CDC youth and adult use pages for the nearest
  age group, source and year at the expert read). Pressure is met in the drink and tobacco ads (unit 2), the offered
  vape, dare and snack (unit 12), and a group's offer and an online stranger's request (unit 17).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day"): Understanding (first teaching of a fact or idea, ending in a
  choice and a reason that uses a fact from the day), Fluency (practice only: the scripted pair rehearsals of a step
  card, a refusal line, an I-statement, an ask for help, a conflict script), Application (the skill on a new scenario,
  on a real class or school case, or on the student's own safe-list goal), Check (the end-of-unit assessment) and
  Respond (the course-level respond days). Fluency in this course is short: a five to eight minute pair rehearsal
  inside most days, and at most one whole Fluency day a unit.
- **End-of-unit evidence.** Every unit ends on a check day: a sitting with unseen short written scenarios (read aloud
  on request), on which each student writes or tells what the character would say or do using the steps, scored on
  the skill steps with a guide, plus one facts-in-use item per fact the unit lists and one valuing item (what would be
  a good choice for this character and why, scored on whether the reason uses a unit fact, never on which choice;
  APPROACH.md). In units that teach a spoken step (4, 10, 12, 18), the spoken step is heard from every student across
  the unit's last two days on a class record sheet, a third of the class on the second-to-last day and the rest on
  the check day while others write; the unit plan sets the record as two or three short pair stations the teacher
  moves between, not one student at a time to the teacher. Never a fact-recall test; no score on a day a topic is first taught. Twice a unit
  the student rates their own use of the steps on a self-check card and names one next step (bar criterion 8). The
  teacher marks met, partly or not yet. Where an owned indicator asks the student to do something, the evidence
  includes the student doing it at school: NHES.S4.5.2 in unit 4 (a listening record: each student attends to an
  unfamiliar short message, asks a question about it and says back its meaning, in two settings, by speech, text or
  AAC), NHES.S1.5.4 in unit 9 (each student explains, on an unseen character case, a taught routine and how an
  adult-supported condition plan helps the character; the routine shown step by step on a teacher record is added
  evidence), NHES.S6.5.5 and NHES.S7.5.2 in unit 15 (a habit log kept at school for the unit's four weeks,
  with a teacher record of each student carrying out the habit in ordinary school routines and one check-in with a
  helper), and NHES.S8.5.4 in unit 19 (a message delivered to a real audience). These are scored on the steps and
  the student's telling, never on health, home habits or whether a goal worked. A character's log or plan shows
  understanding only and never stands alone for a doing indicator. Unit 19 is a product unit: its draft day and its
  written feedback and revision day come before the scored delivery and are counted in its days.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** No readiness check in this course. Every row grade 3 assumes is a grade 2 row of
  Health, grades K to 2 taught the year before, every row grade 4 assumes is a grade 3 row of this course taught the
  year before, and every row grade 5 assumes is a grade 4 row taught the year before, so each opens as a short
  retrieval task inside a day of the unit that needs it (sequence-write.md rule 2), with the earlier unit named in the
  unit's row below. The count starts from the grade of the year being taught; there are no routes. A student new to
  Curio Class health shows any gap in the retrieval task, and the teacher reteaches that step on the card that day.
- **Sensitive units and the leave-out rule.** STANDARD.md section 5: sensitive content lives only in its own units
  (body safety: units 6, 13, 20; respect, growing up and consent: units 7, 14, 21); no skill lives only in a
  sensitive unit (each indicator a sensitive row touches is owned by a non-sensitive row); getting help is taught in a
  non-sensitive unit every year (units 3, 10, 16 and 17); no non-sensitive unit assumes a sensitive one; each
  sensitive unit has a leave-out note on its first page and a longer family note (English and Spanish) sent home two
  weeks before. Each year's sensitive units sit after its non-sensitive units and right before the year-end check, and
  no course-level check samples a sensitive unit, so leaving one out changes no check. In sensitive units rehearsal
  uses written scripts of the refusal or telling line only; the teacher or a card plays any pressure; no student plays
  a person who pressures, touches or threatens (APPROACH.md). Growing-up units (14, 21) name who is affected by leaving
  them out (children whose bodies change early) and the school offers a private route.
- **Reading level.** Students read at most a short scenario alone (a few sentences at the grade's level); longer
  cases, labels and source pages are read aloud or read together (APPROACH.md, Supports). Each unit teaches six to
  ten health words in depth with a sentence frame for each skill step ("I feel ___ when ___ because ___. I need
  ___."; "No thanks. I'm going to ___."). The right idea said in another language or shown by pointing is a language
  gap, not a content gap. No student must speak aloud: every rehearsal and check item can be done in writing, by
  pointing at a card or with an adult.
- **Family notes.** Every unit sends a home task (a talk with a trusted adult the student chooses; a school adult
  counts), written in English and Spanish when the unit is finished. No task asks a student about their own home,
  food, body, feelings, conditions or family; scenarios are about characters, and personal plans stay with the
  student unless they choose to share.
- **Devices.** None needed; no owned code needs technology. NHES.S1.5.5, S3.5.3 and S3.5.4 (finding and judging
  health information) are met on printed source pages, invented labels and flyers and a question to the school nurse;
  where a unit plan adds a teacher-chosen trusted website, the teacher shows it on one class screen and the same page
  is printed for the paper path. Any Curio Class practice tool a unit plan adds (a source-check sort, a step-card
  practice) runs on one teacher screen, collects no health information and has a paper path with the same items.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper scenario cards, step cards, printed labels and logs; any electronic tool has
  the same task on paper beside it, and no student account is ever made. Online safety (units 3 and 17) is taught on
  printed invented screens and messages, with no app or site named.
- **Safety.** Hands-on days and their controls are named in the last column of the Units table. Guides the safety
  read uses: CDC, "Clean Hands" handwashing pages (https://www.cdc.gov/clean-hands/); the national highway traffic
  safety agency's bicycle safety pages (https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/bicycle-safety); the federal Poison Help
  pages (https://poisonhelp.hrsa.gov/); CDC's school asthma and food allergy pages
  (https://www.cdc.gov/school-health-conditions/); the school's own food, allergy, emergency and active-play rules.
  Links named from memory, opened at the safety and expert reads. No real medicine, inhaler, injector, alcohol,
  cigarette, vape or lighter is ever brought to class; pictures and invented labels only. Drink labels are printed or
  on clean empty containers the teacher brings; no tasting.
- **Expert read (bar criterion 7).** The Curio Class health expert agent reads every unit plan and day for medical
  and scientific accuracy against current public health sources and records the date (STANDARD.md section 6). The
  course page may say figures were last checked against their sources on a date, and that a school's health staff
  should read it too.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14), and the safety and answer-key reads are agent reads too. Units
  that need the agent to study named sources first: units 6, 13 and 20 (body safety: children who have been harmed
  and their families; the federal Child Welfare Information Gateway pages on preventing child sexual abuse (the exact
  page named at the sensitivity read; the Prevention page, https://www.childwelfare.gov/topics/preventing/, is the way
  in), CDC's child abuse prevention pages and a national child traumatic stress network's guidance for teachers);
  units 7, 14 and 21 (respect, growing up and consent: families of every structure, faith and culture, including
  same-sex parents, kinship and foster families; children of every gender and who they like; children whose bodies
  change early; children living with HIV or with a family member who does; the HECAT 2021 Sexual Health module 3-5
  expectations, a national pediatrics association's puberty pages for families, and CDC's HIV basics pages); units 2,
  15 and 19 (food and drinks: children with too little food at home, cultural foods, and the risk of early body and
  weight talk; USDA's food guide pages for families and a national eating disorders association's guidance for
  schools); unit 9 (long-term conditions: children who live with asthma, an allergy or diabetes; CDC's school health
  conditions pages); units 3, 10 and 17 (safety, emergencies, medicine and alcohol: children who have lived through
  an injury or an emergency, or live with someone who smokes, drinks or uses drugs; no blame on a family member);
  units 4, 12 and 18 (bullying, limits and conflict: children who are bullied now). Every unit with hands-on or
  active work (1, 2, 8, 10, 15) also gets the safety agent's read against the guides above, and every check gets the
  answer-key agent's read. The unit planner names the exact page and its date for each named source before the unit
  plan's expert read; a page that refuses an automated read is browser check. Each sensitive unit's leave-in note says
  a school that keeps the unit may add one practice day before its check.
- **Course-level checks.** Placed off the core check weeks (`frame.py calendar`): grade 3's core checks fall in weeks
  9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28, 30, 31 and 32; grade 4's in 8, 10, 12, 16, 19, 21, 23, 27, 30, 31 and 32; grade 5's
  in 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29, 30, 31 and 32. Health checks fall in week 15 (grade 3 mid-year), week 14
  (grade 4 and grade 5 mid-year; grade 5's is pinned there, data/plans/course-list.csv `pins`, lead calendar pass
  2026-11-26, because SEL 3 to 5's grade 5 mid-year check is in week 15), week 29 (grade 3 and grade 4 year-end) and week 28 (grade 5 year-end, which takes two respond
  days in weeks 29 and 30 so the check stays out of week 29, a grade 5 social studies check week).
