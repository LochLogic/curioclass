## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

All three items are teacher judged. Score each component 0 for absent or mistaken, 1 for partly supported and 2 for precise and supported. Item 1 has contextual meaning and tonal effect; item 2 has two contextual senses and their effect; item 3 has two phrase explanations and the resulting speaker stance. Keep component results, not just a total. Accept other defensible readings.

Item 1 meets: "Array is the bird's fine plumage. Cheapens makes its show seem less impressive beside the flower, giving the comparison playful admiration." Does not: "The bird buys a cheaper flower" (A).

Item 2 meets: "Excuse means both a sufficient reason to exist and a defense against blame. Beauty answers the sages' charge of waste with calm confidence." Does not: "Excuse means only a defense against blame; it cannot also suggest a reason for existing" (B).

Item 3 meets: "Court gives the bird an admirer's action, so even its fine appearance yields to the flower. Rival of the rose makes the unnoticed shrub a worthy competitor. The speaker becomes its defender, moving from discovery to confident praise, while suppose leaves the final account tentative." Does not: "Court is personification and rival is comparison. The poem is nice" (C).
