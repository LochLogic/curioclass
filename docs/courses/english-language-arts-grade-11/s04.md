## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | An advocate's proposal proves its intended outcome occurred. | Confuses intention with result. Repair by pairing the proposal with a later outcome record. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | An archive's authority makes every inference from its holdings valid. | Confuses custody with evidentiary reach. Repair by naming what the document can establish. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Copied wording can be presented as independent expression. | Misses independent expression. Repair by closing the source, restating and checking borrowed phrasing. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | One source can replace a required multi-source synthesis. | Confuses multiple citations with distinct contributions. Repair by mapping each contribution before rewriting. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | A citation need not let a reader locate the evidence. | Misses traceability. Repair by adding author, title, edition and paragraph or section locator. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | Any attractive digital image strengthens reasoning. | Confuses decoration with evidence. Repair by stating which inference a visible feature clarifies. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| G | Delivery alone adapts a talk, so an unfamiliar term needs no explanation. | Misses audience knowledge. Repair by explaining a source-specific unfamiliar term. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| H | A related word keeps the same sentence role. | Misses derivational change. Repair by identifying suffix, meaning and grammatical role in a new sentence. | Anticipated, from memory. |
