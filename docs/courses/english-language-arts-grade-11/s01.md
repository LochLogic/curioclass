# Unit 1: Stories built on doubt, 1693 to 1890

Course `english-language-arts-grade-11` (English Language Arts Grade 11 (American literature)). **Days 1 to 16** (16 course days; school days 1 to 16, weeks 1 to 4). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 5); 1.2 (days 6 to 10); 1.3 (days 11 to 16). Days by kind: understanding 9, fluency 1, application 3, check 2, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Section 1.1, days 1 to 5: Salem, testimony and suspicion.
Section 1.2, days 6 to 10: Time, enclosure and unstable perception.
Section 1.3, days 11 to 16: Defending the limits of a reading.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do these stories refuse to settle, and how do we know?"
Day 1 opens with Faith's parting request in Young Goodman Brown. Students distinguish her words from Brown's interpretation before opening the compelling question.
Supporting questions: Why does Brown trust the traveller (day 2)? What does Mather assume (day 3)? How does Bierce reorder time (day 7)? How does Poe's poem prepare the collapse (day 10)? Which uncertainties survive comparison (day 12)?
The closing claim, drafted on day 10 and finished on day 13, explains how Usher and Owl Creek direct belief differently, with two passages from each and a competing reading.
Model evidence and reasoning on day 2, source limits on day 3 and a timeline on day 6. Step back during seminars on days 5 and 12; students reread difficult clauses before responding.


Keep all four shared texts on their listed days; field 10 fixes editions and boundaries. Reserve Poe's The Oval Portrait and an unread Mather selection for assessment. Open the independent shelf on day 1 with The Red Badge of Courage as the checked option; adjust targets within 20 to 30 minutes nightly, yielding to shared reading.
Quantitative measures are unavailable. Brown requires allegorical and historical distance: distinguish symbol from proof. Owl Creek separates sequence from duration: reconstruct both. Usher nests texts within unstable perception: parse clauses before comparing poem and ending. Mather compresses accusations: distinguish testimony from fact. Brief background, guided rereading and evidence charts support these short works before longer books; fade charts on day 12. Gothic is named as a mode in 15 minutes of day 12's evidence discussion, with no added day; the second Salem examination record is dropped (scope and sequence).
Vocabulary: week 1, Brown and Mather: melancholy, scruples, covenant, pious, communion, indicted, testimony, spectre. Week 2, Bierce: sentinel, deference, apprehension, imperious, perilous, sluggish, dictum, summarily. Weeks 2 and 3, Poe: insufferable, pervaded, importunate, sentience, pertinacity, cataleptical, vagaries, pallor. Introduce before reading on days 1, 3, 6 and 9; infer during reading; verify and reuse on days 4, 8 and 11. Day 16 recycles eight missed words. Covenant, communion, spectre, sentinel and cataleptical are Tier 3; others are Tier 2. Students add three self-selected words.
The sustained analytical response begins day 10: written feedback day 11, revision day 12, editing and sharing day 13, scoring day 15. Short writing includes daily notes and paragraphs for a peer, day 5, and the teacher, day 8.
Teach (RL) evidence and reading on days 1 to 2, structure on days 6 to 7; practice on days 8 and 12. Teach (RI) on day 3; practice on days 5 and 13. Teach (W) on day 2; practice on days 5 and 13. Teach (SL) on day 5; practice on days 8 and 12. Teach (L) syntax on days 2 and 7, word strategies on day 4; practice on day 11.
Band starts (~RL), (~RI), (~W), (~SL) and (~L) sample this unit's taught parts in field 5; band-end independence remains later work.
Carry claim-evidence-reasoning from Brown, day 2, through Mather, day 5, and later stories to day 16. Narrative-arc starts day 2; text-structure starts day 6; both return through Bierce and Poe to day 16. Sentence-combining returns from Brown, day 2, through Bierce, day 7, and Poe, day 11, to day 14. Context-clues and word-parts connect all four texts, days 4 to 16. Discussion-protocol returns on days 5, 8, 12 and 15. Each exchange uses written preparation, chosen roles, an agreed goal and deadline, evidence questions, invitations to dissent and a synthesis naming further research.

## 7. Response allocation

- **Respond days (days.csv):** day 16.

Group A and B repair inference and structure; C to F verify words on four cards, one repair each, taken by the letter missed; G revises syntax; H to N each receives that letter's specific field 6 repair on a separate task card, completed with a partner. At home, discuss missed items together.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

Tools: None. Supply printed texts, dictionary entries, notebooks, evidence charts and a record sheet, plus the shelf books, reading logs, the printed sentence guide, exit half-sheets and the day 16 repair task cards. Paper supports every task.

## 9. Family note

Students examine how American stories and a Salem account shape belief. Readings include execution, enslavement, premature burial and death; students analyze evidence without acting out harm or sharing private experiences.
