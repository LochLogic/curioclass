# Section 1.2: Time, enclosure and unstable perception

## 1. Standards and practices


Shares of the standards. Each is begun here and owned later. Nothing from days 6 to 10 is scored (s01 field 5).

- **~RL.11-12.5, "analyze".** Day 6: where Bierce begins and how his telling order differs from event order. Day 7: how the last paragraph recasts what came before. Day 8: test a different stopping point. Day 10: how a poem set inside a story prepares its ending. The part sampled is the choice "of where to begin or end"; comic against tragic resolution is not taught here.
- **~RL.11-12.1, "cite", with "where the text leaves matters uncertain".** Day 6: where the reveal sits in time. Day 9: a narrator's claims set against details that challenge them. Day 10: separate a gap in the text from missing background.
- **~L.11-12.3 and L.11-12.3a, "vary syntax for effect".** Day 7: recombine one long sentence and say what each order puts first.
- **~L.11-12.4 with 4b and ~L.11-12.6, "acquire and use accurately".** Day 8: the conceive family and the week 2 words (verify and reuse); resolution, ambiguity and inference used precisely.
- **~RL.11-12.10, "read and comprehend".** Day 9: Usher, first half, across class and home reading, with each support recorded; the literary log is begun.

**Limits that bite here.** Owl Creek is a hanging; Usher entombs a sister who may be alive. Content notes come before reading (days 6 and 9). Poe's gothic words (cataleptical, sentience) are taught as the period's vocabulary and not as a claim about illness; no illness is called a burden. The Oval Portrait and an unread Mather selection are held for assessment and never previewed.

**Connections.** SL.11-12.1 on day 8 (a teacher-led discussion, written feedback day 9, scored in 1.3). W.11-12.10 on day 8 (the paragraph for the teacher) and day 10 (the sustained response begins). L.11-12.4b with the conceive family, day 8.

**What the teacher page must explain.** In Owl Creek the actual time of the story is a few seconds on the bridge; the long middle is imagined, and the last paragraph returns to actual time. Retrospective placement: the scout's visit is told after the capture but happened before it. Usher's narrator reports what he "thought" he saw; the story never shows the sister's entombment from inside. The poem The Haunted Palace is an inset text that ruins an ordered palace in stages; it does not explain the house. Premature burial and catalepsy were a real cultural fear in the 1800s (from memory); the class does not diagnose Madeline. Resolution means how a plot's problem is settled; ambiguity means two supported readings remain.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Day 14 and 15 items belong to section 1.3. The day 8 discussion record and the day 8 paragraph are kept and scored only after written feedback and revision (s01 field 5).

**Exit questions.** Read after class by the teacher against the answer, about a minute each (ours, untested). If many (more than a third) miss one, the next day's warm-up or Return and repair part (5 minutes) opens with that error's wrong example. Each exit asks about a passage held aside from the day's teaching (rule 4) and is a new case (rule 5).

- **E6.** "In the last paragraph of part II, where does the event sit in actual time, and how does putting the scout's visit here direct what you believe?" Answer: before the execution; told afterward, it reframes the earlier calm as a trap. Wrong: the scout comes after the execution (B); the scout visits Farquhar (naming the event, no effect on belief) (B).
- **E7.** "How does Bierce's final paragraph change the meaning and the reader's experience? Then swap its two independent clauses and say what changes without losing a fact." Answer: the escape was imagined and the return to the hanging shocks; the swapped order puts the swinging body before the statement of death, delaying confirmation while keeping both facts. Wrong: it just tells us he died (B); drops a clause and says the meaning is the same (G).
- **E8.** "If Bierce had ended the story as Farquhar reaches his wife, what interpretation would that ending support? Use resolution and the correct form of conceive from the card." Answer: an apparent escape and a settled happy resolution, unlike the true ending's reversal; conception or conceive used in its own part of speech. Wrong: the story ends because he gets home, so the ending just tells what happened (B); "his conception he got home" (D).
- **E9.** "In the paragraph where the narrator meets the physician on the staircase, say what he sees and what "I thought" shows about how far he claims to know." Answer: he sees a look of cunning and perplexity; "I thought" marks a guess, so it suggests concealment and proves nothing. Wrong: the physician was hiding something (A); the narrator is lying (O).
- **E10.** "Compare The Haunted Palace's fourth stanza with Usher's final broken speech. How does the contrast prepare the ending while leaving the cause open?" Answer: an ordered poem of praise against a fractured speech before the fall, replacing harmony with terror; the parallel cannot show supernatural agency. Wrong: the poem proves the house is alive (A); the poem is only the house told as a song, and the ending destroys it (B).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The two-line time map (actual time and telling order), begun day 6, held by each student, redrawn on day 7 for part III; text-structure, begun day 6, returns on days 10 and 12 to day 16. The narrative arc strip from day 2 now holds a second story. CER card: used on days 6 to 10. Sentence recombining: day 7 (Bierce) to day 11 (Poe). The conceive family: day 8 and day 11. The discussion half-sheet roles from day 5: day 8 (teacher-led rings). The literary log: begun day 9, feedback day 10, revision day 12. The sustained response: begun day 10, feedback day 11.
- **Key moment 1, days 6 and 7: placing events in time.** Ideal instrument: a two-line timeline strip (event order above, telling order below) on paper or the board. It fits as paper; the teacher draws the class strip on the board and students keep their own.
- **Key moment 2, day 8: the discussion record.** Ideal instrument: a sheet of 30 names with columns for a cited passage, a probing question, a position invited and a synthesis, kept by the teacher during each ring. Paper fits; the teacher can mark 15 students per ring and each ring has 6 minutes, so tallies are single marks.
- **A room with nothing.** The teacher reads Owl Creek and Usher aloud in the allotted parts; the strip, the cards and the discussion sheet are all paper. No tools are named; nothing is spec only.

## 6. Days

## Day 6


- **9 min. Return and repair.** Day 5 exit repair (E5, letters A or L; one repair, 5 minutes), then each student reads the written feedback on the day 5 paragraph and marks one revision to make on day 8 (4 minutes). The paragraph returns with a half-sheet clipped on.
- **5 min. Content note and words.** The note: part I is a hanging, told plainly; anyone may step out. The eight week 2 words before reading: sentinel, deference, apprehension, imperious, perilous, sluggish, dictum, summarily, one sentence each.
- **12 min. Read parts I and II.** About 1,700 words (from memory); the teacher reads the opening aloud (3 min), then silent reading with a stop line marked at the end of part II. The last paragraph of part II is held aside. A student who has not reached the stop line finishes at home.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** e10-rl-structure-time and text-structure: put the first two parts in event order and find the narrated interruption. A miss: the teacher labels event time and telling order on two lines before new teaching.
- **9 min. Teaching first and worked example.** The two-line strip: event order above, telling order below. Model it on part I: where the story begins, where it breaks off, and the word "narrated interruption" for a stretch told out of order. Ask: "What do you believe at this point, and what moved you?"
- **6 min. Alone.** Complete the strip for part II; mark where the scout's visit sits and what the reader believed before it.
- **5 min. Exit.** E6, alone.

Carried: the strip, begun here, is held by each student; the wall arc strip gains Bierce. Tool: None. By hand: the strip, marked text, exit. Evidence: E6. Hand-backs in: E5 repair and the day 5 paragraph. Out: E6 to day 7's warm-up (error B). At home: independent book 20 to 30 minutes; reread part I and add two words to the log (10 minutes).

## Day 7


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return and repair for E6 (error B), one repair.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Narrative arc and text-structure: place Brown and parts I and II on the arc strip; name the turn in each.
- **14 min. Read part III.** About 2,100 words (from memory), read aloud by the teacher with students following (about 150 words a minute), then students mark where actual time returns. The final paragraph is read here and then held aside. Content note: the ending is a hanging.
- **9 min. Teaching first and worked example.** How a last line recasts earlier lines: the teacher models on Brown's closing, comparing it with an invented ending that leaves out the tombstone (a case for practice, not E7). Terms taught now and used on day 8: resolution (how the problem is settled), ambiguity (two supported readings remain), aesthetic impact (what the reader feels). The strip is redrawn for part III with actual time against imagined time.
- **6 min. Alone, then pairs.** Mark on the strip where the escape begins and where actual time returns; pairs compare.
- **7 min. Syntax.** e10-l-syntax-style and L.11-12.3a: one long sentence from the middle of part III, chosen by the teacher, is unpacked, recombined two ways, and each way's first fact named.
- **5 min. Exit.** E7, alone.

Carried: the strip and the term cards (resolution, ambiguity, aesthetic impact) held by each student. Tool: None. By hand: strip, syntax page, exit. Evidence: E7 (G on the syntax page, unscored). Hand-backs in: E6. Out: E7 to day 8's warm-up (B or G). At home: independent book 20 to 30 minutes; reread part III's escape and add one Q mark where the story leaves an uncertainty (10 minutes).

## Day 8


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Return and repair for E7 (B or G, the more common), one repair.
- **3 min. Word-family slot, week 2.** conceive, conception, conceivable (L.11-12.4b): choose the form that fits a claim about Farquhar's imagined escape, explain the changed meaning and part of speech, verify on the printed card, use it. A miss: the card, inside these 3 minutes.
- **4 min. Week 2 words.** Pairs verify the eight week 2 words against context and a card and write one reuse sentence each.
- **8 min. Revise the day 5 paragraph.** Alone, from the written feedback and the marked revision (not new drafting); handed in again.
- **14 min. Teacher-led discussion.** The class forms two rings of 15 and reads two stopping points the teacher chose beforehand (not the one in E8). Ring A discusses for 6 minutes in the middle while ring B listens with a sheet and writes one probing question; then they swap (2 minutes to move and reset). The teacher leads each ring and marks tallies for 15 names at a time. Everyone may pass. Evidence turns are recorded, not scored today.
- **11 min. Paragraph for the teacher.** Alone: one analytic paragraph on what stopping at one chosen point would change, using inference, ambiguity and resolution. Collected.
- **5 min. Exit.** E8, alone; the conceive family card is on the sheet.

Shared equipment: 15 names per ring, 6 minutes a ring: about 24 seconds a student on average, so the sheet records one mark for each of four moves (cited a passage, probing question, invited another position, synthesized), not a score. Carried: the paragraph and the discussion sheet go to the teacher; the term cards are used again. Tool: None. By hand: revised paragraph, new paragraph, listening sheet, exit. Evidence: E8. Hand-backs in: E7, the day 5 paragraph with feedback. Out: E8 to day 9's Return and repair; the day 8 paragraph and the discussion record with written feedback to day 9 (about 3 minutes a paper and 1 minute a record, ours, untested). At home: independent book 20 to 30 minutes and the log (10 minutes).

## Day 9


- **10 min. Return and repair.** Day 8 exit repair (E8, error B or D; one repair, 5 minutes), then each student reads written feedback on the day 8 paragraph and on the discussion record and marks one revision for day 11 (5 minutes).
- **5 min. Content note and words.** The note: Usher includes death and premature entombment and is read for what it shows and does not show. Words before reading: insufferable, pervaded, importunate, sentience, pertinacity, cataleptical, vagaries, pallor, one sentence each.
- **7 min. Teaching first and worked example.** How to read a narrator's claims against details that challenge them: two columns, "he says" and "the text shows", modeled on the narrator's first view of the house. A sentence like "I thought" is marked as a guess.
- **14 min. Read the first half.** About 1,800 words in class (from memory): the teacher reads aloud (4 min), then silent reading with the S and Q key; stop line marked in advance. The physician paragraph is held aside. The rest of the first half (about 1,800 words) is read at home.
- **5 min. Alone.** Fill the two columns for the passage read in class.
- **4 min. Set-up: the literary log.** A dated entry: passage, a one-sentence explanation, the support used (read aloud, background, glossary). Begun today; the teacher gives written feedback on day 10.
- **5 min. Exit.** E9, alone.

Carried: the log begins; the two columns are held by each student. Tool: None. By hand: two-column page, log, exit. Evidence: E9. Hand-backs in: E8, the day 8 paragraph and discussion record with feedback. Out: E9 to day 10's Return and repair; the log entry with feedback to day 10. At home (30 to 40 minutes): read the rest of the first half (15 minutes), then the independent book (20).

## Day 10


- **8 min. Return and repair.** Day 9 exit repair (E9, error A or O; one repair, 5 minutes), then read the written feedback on the day 9 log entry (3 minutes).
- **22 min. Read the second half.** About 3,500 words including The Haunted Palace (from memory): the teacher reads the poem and the closing scene aloud (about 10 min) and students read the middle silently with the S and Q key; stop line marked. Hand-backs in: E9, the log entry with feedback. Out: E10 to day 11's Return and repair (rule 1, section 1.3 must hold it); the draft with feedback to day 11; the log to day 12. ## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 7, "How does Bierce reorder time?"; day 8, "What would a different ending have told us?"; day 10, "How does Poe's poem prepare the collapse?"
- Written task, day 8: one analytic paragraph for the teacher (W.11-12.10, a single sitting), feedback day 9, revision day 11. Day 10: the first draft of the closing claim, feedback day 11, revision day 12, finished day 13. Both are scored only through later days (s01 field 5).
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors for each piece: (1) a claim that answers the question; (2) exact evidence from the named passage; (3) reasoning that uses structure (where the story begins, turns or ends); (4) the limit, what stays uncertain.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 7 and 8 repair the last exit (5 minutes, one repair); days 9 and 10 have a Return and repair part instead because feedback comes back. No timer.

Day 9: the S and Q key and the three-column chart (reported, implied, unproved). Day 10: the strip and arc from days 2 and 6, the claim, evidence, reasoning card.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standards set none. Day 8 is a fluency day: no new content is taught, and the discussion, word work and paragraph practice what days 5 to 7 taught.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sentinel, deference, perilous, summarily, pallor, entombment, resolution, ambiguity, structure. Frames: "At first the story __, but it __." and "He says __, but the text shows __."
- **Access.** Large print; Owl Creek part III and Usher's second half are read aloud; the strip may be drawn as a list; a student who cannot write long speaks the exit and paragraph to a scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits may be read aloud and answered aloud. The conceive family card is printed on the exit sheet. Record read-aloud help separately from independent reading evidence.
- **Reading load.** Parts I and II about 1,700 words; part III about 2,100; Usher about 7,000 in all (3,500 first half, with half at home; 3,500 second half). Bierce and Poe are read aloud in part. Measures not on file.
- **Extension, same standard.** Day 7: the effect of recombining a second sentence; day 10: draw how another story's inset text prepares its ending.
