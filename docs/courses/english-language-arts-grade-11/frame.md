# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 11 (American literature)

Course id `english-language-arts-grade-11`, grade 11, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9 units, each built on one named set of American texts, read beside US History I.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units and 12 for course-level checks and respond days (three checks, each followed by respond days). A full year of 32 weeks, five lessons a week of about 50 minutes, with home reading of about 20 to 30 minutes on school nights while a long text is open (The Scarlet Letter is about 63,000 words in four weeks; Douglass's Narrative about 40,000 in three). Days follow load, not row count: unit 1 starts eight year-long routines on short texts; units 3 and 4 hold a process argument and the research project; the two long texts (units 5 and 6) carry reading days; unit 8 is short because it is one source set on one question. Each unit's days include its readiness check, its end-of-unit assessment on a text not read in the unit, and its respond time.
- **Standard.** Common Core ELA, grades 11 to 12 band (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Grade 11 is the first year of a two-year band: every row here starts a band standard (codes marked ~) and grade 12 owns it. Each row names its grade 11 limit; the Units table says which unit teaches each lettered part, and a part left wholly to grade 12 is named there (the Shakespeare parts of RL.11-12.4 and RL.11-12.7, the legal part of RI.11-12.8). The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (the 6 to 12 criteria) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The "Check set adds" column lists candidate additions from the college-level English language and English literature course frameworks and New York's Next Generation 11 to 12 standards (see For the lead: ELA has no named check set yet in STANDARD form); each is confirmed or dropped in the unit plan. Check-set names never appear in student, teacher or family copy.
- **Base text.** None. ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Every anchor is public domain in the United States (published 1930 or earlier) or a US government work; each text's date is checked in its text record. CommonLit is not used. RL.11-12.9 and RI.11-12.9 name foundational American works and founding documents, so this year's anchors are older by the standard's own demand; under Cory's 2026-11-13 rule, the anchors the standards do not name are picked for being recognizable and as recent as the licence allows (Trifles, 1916; 'The War Prayer', printed 1916; Riis and his photographs, 1890), and short quoted passages of well-known recent American writers may sit inside Curio Class lessons beside an anchor (candidates named in units 3 and 5). No student is sent to a library or a paid copy: every anchor has a Curio Class print copy.
- **What this year anchors on.** The Scarlet Letter (1850) and Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass (1845), read whole; Trifles (1916) read whole as the band's American play; the founding documents short and whole. The independent long book comes from a shelf of recognizable American works, all public domain: The Red Badge of Courage (1895), The Awakening (1899), The Call of the Wild (1903), The House of Mirth (1905), Ethan Frome (1911), My Ántonia (1918), The Age of Innocence (1920), Little Women (1868), Up from Slavery (1901), with log and conference (e11-reading-band).
- **Reading level and supports.** Anchors sit in the 11-CCR band (about 1185 to 1385 Lexile, 2012 supplement to Appendix A), with scaffolding at the high end (the standard's grade 11 line). The Narrative (about 1080) is placed for its qualitative demand; poems and the play have no usable computed measure and rest on the qualitative read. Supports come before, during and after reading and never replace the text: background in under five minutes, a guided first read of the hardest passages, a reread for a named purpose, the long-sentence routine; audio sits beside the text, never in place of it on a check. Routine supports start to fade in the second half (units 6 to 9) as students show readiness; grade 12 completes the fade.
- **Anchor text records.** Each anchor's record (data/plans/<course>/texts.csv, fixed with the first ELA pilot plan) carries the computed measure taken from the full public-domain text, what makes it hard under the four headings (meaning, structure, language, what the reader needs to know), the task, and the reason for its place, which the Units table gives here in short. Shares on anchors, reported not scored: about half the reading days are on informational texts (founding documents, essays, speeches, the Narrative, Riis), half on literature; three whole long texts (two books, one play) and many short ones; writing across the year about 40 percent argument, 40 informative and 20 narrative.
- **Routines all year** (each starts in the unit that owns its row, then runs in every unit): a written exit question on the day's text and a weekly one-sitting analytic paragraph (e11-routine-writing); a seminar and a shorter text discussion at least twice a unit (e11-seminar); the unit word list of about 15 words from the texts and the student's own word log (e11-academic-words); a historical-dictionary check on old words (e11-word-strategies); the long-sentence routine on one anchor sentence a week (e11-syntax-effect); the independent book (e11-reading-band). No timer is shown to students; the teacher keeps time.
- **Process writing calendar.** Five process pieces: the informative essay on Transcendentalism (unit 2, due about week 7), the Thoreau argument (unit 3, about week 11), the research essay and talk (unit 4, about week 16, shared with US History I), the literary argument on The Scarlet Letter (unit 6, about week 24), the two-viewpoint narrative (unit 9, about week 31). None falls due in weeks 13 to 19 except the shared research piece, as US History I asks. Three one-sitting essays on cold passages, one per writing type, sit in the course-level checks. Each process piece has a model piece, the year's rubric for its type and one named feedback step.
- **Paper path and accounts.** Every task that publishes or uses a recording has a paper path: a posted copy and a class binder for "publish and update"; the teacher reads aloud or a printed transcript where audio is not available. No student account anywhere; research uses open archives and the school library's own access.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Units that also need a qualified or community reader: unit 3 (the Declaration's words on Native peoples: a Native reader), unit 5 (Douglass: a Black community reader and a reader for the sexual violence passages), unit 9 (domestic violence in Trifles and mental illness in 'The Yellow Wallpaper': a school counselor or like qualified reader). Who, and any pay, is Cory's.
