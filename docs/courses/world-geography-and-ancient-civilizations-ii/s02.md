## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How did ancient India's beliefs shape the way people lived and ruled?"
- **Hook, day 12.** Display a teacher paraphrase of Rock Edict XII asking people to respect other traditions. Ask whether a ruler can make people follow a belief. Post the compelling question before retrieval.
- **Supporting questions.** Write these together: How did Hindu traditions develop (days 12 and 13)? How did caste change, and for whom (days 14 and 15)? What did the Buddha teach (day 16)? What can rulers change (days 17 to 19)? What do texts and objects show about achievement (days 20 to 22)?
- **Closing claim, day 23.** Answer the compelling question from the Upanishad passage, Rock Edict XIII and the caste chart. Defend whether beliefs shaped everyday conduct or royal policy more clearly in this evidence; acknowledge another reading and one limit.
- **Teaching moves.** Teach content before interpretation. Model text attribution on day 13, continuity and change on day 15, and source usefulness and a claim on day 18. Pairs evaluate on day 19; withdraw writing frames on day 23. Gloss dharma, karma, rebirth, varna, jati, Dalit, edict, missionary, Sanskrit and stupa as encountered.

- **Limits.** No faith ranking, devotion, caste assignment or family disclosure. Study medicine historically, without treatment advice or practice. Teach migration evidence without equating ancestry, language and religion. Varna is a textual classification, not a complete account of jati. Article 17 abolished untouchability, not caste identities or every discriminatory practice. Sanchi spans centuries; Ajanta's later paintings had Vakataka patrons. Zero developed over time, not through one Gupta inventor.
