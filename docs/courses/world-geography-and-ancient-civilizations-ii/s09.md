## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Which Greek legacy matters most today?"
- **Hook, day 101.** Show Darius's claim to rule many peoples beside a map of Persia and Greece. Ask why a Greek account might tell another story. Ask how this connects to the compelling question.
- **Supporting questions.** Write together: Why did these wars happen, and what changed (101 to 103)? How do stories live in words (104)? Which accomplishment can we investigate, and which sources help (105 and 106)? How do historians' arguments compare (107)? How can an explanation improve (108 to 112)? What makes a legacy important (113)?
- **Closing claim.** On 113, defend a criterion for importance using two named research sources and a report from another area. Name a competing legacy and a limit. Different supported conclusions are welcome.
- **Teaching moves.** Model sourcing and causal arrows on 101, corroboration on 102, ranking on 103, allusion on 104 and credibility on 106. On 107 model two secondary arguments, guide another pair, then step back. Model structural critique before peer work on 110. Teach empire, alliance, conquest, consequence, myth, allusion, credibility, relevance and secondary argument in context.


**D2.His.15.6-8:** Evaluate how strongly different causes influenced past events. Parts: weigh causes; justify their relative influence.
**D3.2.6-8:** Evaluate a source's credibility through its relevance and intended use. Parts: determine relevance and intended use; justify credibility for this question.
**D2.His.17.6-8:** Compare the main arguments of secondary histories about related topics. Parts: identify arguments; explain similarities and differences.
**D4.5.6-8:** Critique how explanations are structured. Parts: identify an organizational strength or weakness; explain its effect. The local file holds codes only; indicators were checked in the official framework.
- **Limits.** Research one area per student, with all five represented in the class. Teach borrowing and transmission, not Greek invention of everything. No exhaustive battle catalog, complete epics, religious practice or conquest simulation. Myth is a narrative category, not a judgment about believers.
- **Carried.** Keep unit 8's map key and perspective prompts on 101 to 103. Return corroboration on 102, tie it to source notes on 108 and withdraw completed comparisons on 109. Return claim, evidence and reasoning on 103; revise it through the critique on 111; withdraw model paragraphs on 112 and 113. Keep blank source prompts. Market and clock models rest.
