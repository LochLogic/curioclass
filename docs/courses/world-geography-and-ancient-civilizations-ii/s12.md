## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did Rome fall, or did it change?"

- **Hook, day 145.** Place a map of western successor kingdoms beside the Ktisis floor mosaic, 500 to 550, shown in Brooks's essay on Byzantium. Ask how both could belong to a story called Rome's fall.

- **Supporting questions.** Write together: How did pressures reinforce each other (145 and 146)? What changed with Christianity (147)? Why do historians disagree (148 to 151)? How could ideas outlast governments (152 and 153)?

- **Closing claim, day 154.** Answer the compelling question using Ward-Perkins, Brooks, the religious-policy account and the ideas map. Weigh causes, distinguish regions and name the strongest counterclaim. Item 2 tests transfer on 155.

- **Teaching moves.** Teach causes before weighing them. Model sourcing on 145, interacting causes on 146 and distinguishing policy from belief on 147. Compare historians on 148; model a paragraph on 149 and a fair counterclaim on 150. Step back during drafting on 151. Model dated routes on 152: Buddhist teachings along trade networks, Greek artists moving to Rome, Greek geometry at Alexandria after conquest, and temple designs in Greek settlements in Sicily. Distinguish exchanging objects, moving people, military control and establishing settlements. On 153 add Roman law and Latin across Europe and compare Han and Rome through intermediaries; revise independently on 154. Teach corruption, civil war, inflation, invasion, transformation, diffusion, migration, conquest, colonization and counterclaim in context. RH.6-8.1 and WHST.6-8.1 connect evidence and writing on 151.

- **Limits and teacher background.** Explain all six named pressures, without treating third-century inflation as an uninterrupted fifth-century condition. Trade contraction was uneven, both cause and consequence. Distinguish the Western imperial office's end in 476 from Eastern survival to 1453, a timeline pointer only. Constantine supported Christianity; Nicene Christianity became the state religion in 380. Neither faith nor migration alone explains collapse. Teach beliefs as adherents' accounts; require no disclosure, devotion or conquest role play. Conquest involved violence and displacement. Medieval developments and a full counterclaim rebuttal belong later.

| 155 | MA.7.T4c.5 | Explain internal and external pressures and their effects. | **1, ten minutes.** "Using the factual strip, explain how corruption, civil wars, a weak economy and inflation could weaken government. Look for causation, multiple effects, sourced reasoning, regional qualification and a fair alternative. **Meets:** "The western state fell as resource problems and attacks reinforced each other. **Assessment sources, reserved from teaching.** Give a 180-word maximum packet with these original summaries and source labels, plus the factual strip below.

- **A, Eugippius, Life of Severinus 20, about 511, recalling fifth-century Noricum:** Public payments supported frontier soldiers. When payments stopped, units disappeared. Soldiers from Batavis seeking pay in Italy were killed. A Christian biography honors Severinus; it is neither an empire-wide survey nor a neutral military report.
- **B, original regional summary checked against Brooks and Cassiodorus, Variae I.27 (an official letter in King Theodoric's name, early sixth century):** Around 500, successor kingdoms governed former western territories. In Ostrogothic Italy, the king's letter sent a violent dispute in Rome to appointed judges and called Roman law supreme. Eastern Roman government continued at Constantinople. Christian institutions and Roman cultural traditions survived in changing forms.
- **Factual strip:** Civil wars diverted soldiers and resources. Corruption diverted public resources. Weak production and revenue constrained defense. Earlier inflation disrupted purchasing power. Invasions took territory. Insecurity restricted some trade; reduced exchange also weakened revenue.
