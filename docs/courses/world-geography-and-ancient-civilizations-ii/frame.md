# Course frame: World Geography and Ancient Civilizations II

Course id world-hist-7, grade 7, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12 units, in the anchor's topic order (7.T1 to 7.T4c): Central and South Asia, East Asia, Oceania,
  then Europe, ancient Greece and Rome, ending about 500 CE.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units, 12 for course-level checks and respond days (three checks of 2 days,
  each followed by 2 respond days). A full year, one period a day, five days a week; the scope and sequence states
  the minutes (the pick in "For the lead" is 45).
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 grade 7 (MA.7.T1 to MA.7.T4c) is the content anchor: every one of its 58 codes
  sits with one of the 29 grade 7 progression rows, so every code sits in one unit below. Parent codes carry their
  lettered parts where the rows cite only the parent: MA.7.T4b.3 carries 3a and 3b (unit 8), and MA.7.T4b.7
  carries 7a to 7e (unit 9: each student researches one area, as the standard asks, and the class covers all five).
  C3 6-8 indicators carry the skills (the C3 column). Each unit plan compares the unit with California, Tennessee
  and New York and records any addition with its source; the "Check set adds" column names the candidates this
  frame found, from the writer's reading, each to be confirmed in that unit's comparison table. Check-set names
  never appear in student, teacher or family copy. The values rule (a to d) governs every contested claim; faiths
  are taught from their own texts and practitioners, named as their account.
- **Base text.** None (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md: every day is Curio Class's own). Partial bases that may serve a
  unit, taken unit by unit and checked item by item for third-party material: Oak National Academy units (Open
  Government Licence v3.0, credit line only, written for England, the history ones for key stage 2, so reworded and
  reframed): Indus (unit 1), Shang (unit 3), key stage 3 Silk Road (unit 4), key stage 3 geography India (unit 1)
  and China (unit 3), Ancient Greece (units 8 and 9), Rome (units 10 to 12). Public domain and CC0 items (museum
  open-access images of seals, edicts, coins, inscriptions) are checked one by one. Massachusetts's Investigating
  History units and the New York toolkit are reference only. The frame maps no base lesson.
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit has a compelling question (the table) with supporting questions set by the unit
  plan, runs the C3 arc, and ends with an end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from an unseen source. Content
  runs in the anchor's order: by region, and in time order within each region. Two departures are the anchor's own:
  Buddhism's spread to Japan runs past 500 CE (unit 5), and time zones (unit 7) are a modern topic placed with
  Europe's geography.
- **The comparison thread.** Grade 6's complex-society traits and timeline return in unit 5, where India and China
  are set on parallel timelines beside Mesopotamia and Egypt; Greece and Rome are compared with them in words at the
  end-of-unit assessments of units 9 and 12.
- **Informed action.** Once this year, in unit 8 (ss7-athenian-democracy): one structured class deliberation under
  assembly rules on a school question, then the class chooses an action (a letter to the principal, a proposal to
  the student council), carries it out through the school and looks back. Action is the students' choice informed
  by evidence, never a cause the course picks.
- **Writing from sources.** Short sourced claims in units 1 to 7; a research report with credibility checks and two
  secondary works compared in unit 9; a multi-cause argument that names the strongest opposing claim in unit 12.
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own texts at grade 7 level; ancient sources as short translated excerpts (edicts,
  Analects sayings, Thucydides, Roman tombstones) with a glossary and a stated difficulty; objects and maps read as
  sources. Each source has a rights line.
- **Sensitive content.** Caste and discrimination against Dalits told plainly and fairly, with no student asked
  about family caste or faith; forced labor under Qin; slavery in Athens, Sparta and Rome named plainly with no role
  play; Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander and Maori peoples in the present tense, from their own materials, with
  care over images of people who have died and over sacred sites. Units 2, 6, 8 and 10 need the fresh-agent
  sensitivity read, and unit 6 a qualified person's read.
