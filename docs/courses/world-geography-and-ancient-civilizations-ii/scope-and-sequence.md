# Scope and sequence: World Geography and Ancient Civilizations II

Course `world-geography-and-ancient-civilizations-ii` (catalogue id `world-hist-7`), grade 7, draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/world-geography-and-ancient-civilizations-ii/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes; a timer only where it has a grounded purpose), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from an unseen source, and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Every unit's first day carries its sensitivity flag in the note column, and the days with Native, faith, caste or slavery content carry their own; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. Every end-of-unit assessment and course-level check asks about each part of each code it lists (a short prompt per part, no day added); where a product (a map, a report, a research portfolio, a comparison) stands for a code, the day's goal says so. A unit opens with a readiness check on an earlier grade's row last used two or more grades back; a row used the year before, or earlier this year, opens as a retrieval task inside a day. Fixed after the expert and Codex reads of 2026-11-14 (docs/reviews/sequence-6-8-fixes-2026-11-14.md).

One change from the frame's own day ranges, inside its rules: unit 8 holds one of its 15 days for the informed action look-back, which the frame places at the start of unit 10. Unit 8 therefore runs days 87 to 100, unit 9 days 101 to 115, course-level check 2 days 116 to 119, the look-back day 120, and unit 10 keeps days 121 to 133. Every unit keeps its day count; check 2 moves one day earlier inside its target week.

## Unit 1. South Asia: land and first cities (days 1 to 11, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** Central and South Asia on physical and political maps; the monsoon and farming; a large, growing population, land use and migration; the Indus cities; an unread script and what objects alone can show; inferring maker, date, use and audience; a short claim about one object.

Arc: four geography days (map, location, monsoon and settlement, population and movement), then five Indus days (the cities, the unread script, inferring from objects, corroborating with Mesopotamian records, a short claim), then assessment and respond. Geography first, as the anchor orders it, and the Indus days read the river map the first days build.

- Readiness: none. Day 1 opens with a retrieval task on grade 6's map tools and regional frame; day 6 retrieves grade 6's Kush.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 10: the maps with a location used in a text, settlement, population change and land use, and an unseen Indus object with its find context (maker, date, place of origin and audience inferred). Respond day 11.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 2. Faiths and empires of ancient India (days 12 to 25, weeks 3 to 5)

**Topics taught:** Hinduism's growth and central ideas from its own texts; how caste developed (MA.7.T1b.2e) and what continued and changed; the Buddha's teachings (2c); the Mauryan Empire, Ashoka and his edicts, his missionaries, and the Gupta Empire (2d); achievements in mathematics, science, art and architecture (2f); Hindus and Buddhists today.

Arc: four Hinduism and caste days, five on the Buddha and the empires (ending on the Gupta), two on achievements, a claim day, then assessment and respond. Time order: Vedic traditions before the Buddha, the Mauryans before the Gupta. Caste follows Hinduism because its varna ideal comes from the same texts; Ashoka's edicts follow the Buddha's teachings they claim to apply.

- Readiness: none; grade 6's rule for teaching a faith as history opens day 12 as a retrieval.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 24: each part 2b to 2f, and an unseen edict judged for what it shows of Ashoka's rule and of his subjects' lives, with a limit. Respond day 25.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 3. East Asia's land and early China (days 26 to 37, weeks 6 to 8)

**Topics taught:** East Asia on physical and political maps; how mountains, deserts and seas set China apart and how its rivers and loess fed farming; geography and settlement today; the Shang, oracle bones and bronze; the Zhou, the Mandate of Heaven and the dynastic cycle; support for learning; Confucius and Taoism (MA.7.T2b.2a, 2b).

Arc: four geography days, five history days (Shang, oracle bones sourced, Zhou and the cycle, learning, Confucius and Taoism), a claim day, then assessment and respond.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 36: the maps, geography and early China, settlement today, rule and learning under the Shang and Zhou (2a), Confucius and Taoism (2b), and an unseen saying or inscription sourced. Respond day 37.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 4. Qin unification and the Silk Roads (days 38 to 51, weeks 8 to 11)

**Topics taught:** the Qin's conquest, Legalism, standard weights, coins and script, and forced labor (2c); characters unlike an alphabet (2d); China's technologies on the timeline (2e); the Han, Zhang Qian and Buddhism's arrival (2f); the Silk Roads' geography (MA.7.T1b.2g); India's and Central Asia's links west (MA.7.T1b.1); the supply-and-demand graph and four applications (a good, labor, credit, currency exchange); barriers to trade.

Arc: four Qin days, three route days (the Han's side, the geography, India's links), four economics days (the graph, barriers, two days of applications), a claim day, then assessment and respond. The Qin comes first in time; the graph comes after the routes are mapped so prices have places.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 50: the Qin (2c), characters (2d), technologies (2e), China's Silk Roads and Buddhism's arrival (2f), the routes' geography (2g), India's links west, and short supply-and-demand prompts on competition, a good, labor, credit and a currency, with a trade barrier. Respond day 51.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame (the Han is inside MA.7.T2b.2f).

## Unit 5. Buddhism's journey east and comparing societies (days 52 to 62, weeks 11 to 13)

**Topics taught:** Buddhism's spread to China, Korea and Japan; Japan from the 6th to the 13th century (Shinto, the Heian court, The Tale of Genji); encounters among China, Korea and Japan; parallel timelines of India, China, Mesopotamia and Egypt; India's and China's developments judged with the traits and environment; how to compare fairly.

Arc: three days on Buddhism east and Japan (day 53 traces Buddhism's growth in Japan from its arrival through Korea to the Pure Land and Zen schools of the 13th century, beside Shinto and the Heian court), then six comparison days (parallel timelines, India judged, China judged, how to compare fairly, a draft comparison, the compelling question), then assessment and respond. The India and China days carry every lettered part of MA.7.T1b.2 (2a to 2g) and MA.7.T2b.2 (2a to 2f), as the frame draws them together here.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 61: Buddhism's spread and growth in Japan, Japan's contributions, East Asian encounters, and a comparison from unseen sources; the India and China comparisons of days 56 and 57 count for MA.7.T1b.2 and MA.7.T2b.2. Respond day 62.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 6. Oceania, past and present (days 67 to 77, weeks 14 to 16)

**Topics taught:** the Indian and Pacific Oceans, Australia, New Zealand, Antarctica, the Pacific Islands and Southeast Asia on maps; settlement in dry interiors, deltas and islands; two places compared by culture and environment; Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, their kinship, land and ancient sites; Polynesian voyaging and the Maori; the peoples today.

Arc: four geography days (ending on the two-place comparison, made and shared on day 70), four days on the peoples (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples today, rock art and oral tradition as evidence, Polynesian voyaging and Maori social organization, and early Maori archaeology such as Wairau Bar), a claim day, then assessment and respond. MA.7.T3b.1 asks for social features and for what evidence shows of early cultures, for both peoples, so the peoples get the fourth day.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 76: the maps, settlement, two places compared, both peoples' social features, and an unseen piece of evidence read for what it shows of early culture. Respond day 77.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 7. Europe's land and the world's clocks (days 78 to 86, weeks 16 to 18)

**Topics taught:** Europe on physical and political maps; how coastline, rivers and climate shaped settlement; a student-made keyed map; time zones, Greenwich, the date line, longitude by clock; times worked out from offsets.

Arc: four Europe days, three time-zone days, then assessment (which also answers the compelling question) and respond. Nine days hold no separate claim day; the compelling question is answered on the assessment.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 85: an unseen keyed map read, a political map used in a text, settlement explained, and time zones, longitude by clock, the date line and Greenwich explained with offsets worked out. Respond day 86.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Lean: math 7 adds and subtracts signed numbers at its days 69 to 77, before day 84. Met; day 84 carries the frame's bridge for a school that runs math later.

## Unit 8. Greek city-states and democracy (days 87 to 100, and day 120; weeks 18 to 20, and 24)

**Topics taught:** Greece and its colonies on a historical map; why mountains made city-states and the sea made traders; the polis (MA.7.T4b.3a), citizens and the vote (3b), assemblies and councils (3c), a written constitution (3d), the rule of law (3e); direct and representative government; one structured deliberation and the year's informed action; life in Athens and Sparta, women, enslaved people and helots; perspective.

Arc: two map days, three democracy days, three action days (three deliberative procedures compared before the class deliberation, for D2.Civ.9, choosing the action, carrying it out), three Athens and Sparta days, a claim day, then assessment and respond. The look-back is day 120, four weeks after the action, held as the frame sets. Democracy comes before the action because the deliberation uses the assembly's rules; Athens and Sparta come after so the comparison can test "was Athens a democracy?" against who was shut out.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 99: the map and Greece's reach, location and trade, the five democratic ideas, two deliberative procedures compared, Athens and Sparta compared, and an unseen source read for perspective. Respond day 100.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Lean: ELA 7 unit 4 (days 87 to 108) runs beside this unit, as both frames set; the democracy question stays here.

## Unit 9. Greek wars, myths and accomplishments (days 101 to 115, weeks 21 to 23)

**Topics taught:** the Persian Empire; causes and results of the Persian Wars, the Peloponnesian War and Alexander's conquests, with causes ranked; Greek gods, heroes and stories, Homer's epics among them, and words still used; a research report on one area of accomplishment (MA.7.T4b.7a to 7e, the class covering all five) with credibility checks, two secondary works compared and a peer critique.

Arc: three war days, one war each (the Persian Empire retrieved from grade 6 and the Persian Wars; the Peloponnesian War with Thucydides set against another source; Alexander and the ranking), one myth day, eight research days (the frame's "about 8"; days 107 and 110 open with a model before students compare arguments or critique an explanation), a claim day, then assessment and respond. Ranking causes needs the causes taught, so each war has its day.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 114: causes and results of all three wars with one war's causes ranked from an unseen source, and myths and mythological words (MA.7.T4b.5, D2.His.15, MA.7.T4b.6). The report and its research portfolio (credibility checks, the two works compared, the critique given) stand for MA.7.T4b.7, D3.2, D2.His.17 and D4.5, so the research is not tested twice in the week of ELA's and math's checks. Respond day 115.
- Candidates settled: the Persian Empire opener is placed by the frame (day 101). Homer's epics, kept in short retellings inside day 104 at no added day; ELA 7's allusion work comes after this unit, so the retellings serve it.
- Leans: ELA 7 teaches research steps and credibility in its unit 3 (days 61 to 86), before this unit. Met.

## Unit 10. The Roman Republic and its end (days 121 to 133, weeks 25 to 27)

**Topics taught:** Rome on a map, its growth to about 500 CE and how location supported its power; the Republic's offices, veto, Twelve Tables, separation of powers and civic duty, matched to the branches; Caesar and Augustus; the legion and decentralized rule (MA.7.T4c.3a); why the empire grew and lasted; Rome's classes and slavery from sources where enslaved and freed people's voices survive.

Arc: four Republic days, four days on the turn to empire (day 125 names the land question and the Gracchi, armies loyal to their generals and the civil wars before Caesar, so the compelling question has more than one defensible answer), two on classes and slavery, a claim day, then assessment and respond. Day 120, the look-back held from unit 8, opens the unit's week.

- Readiness check, in the last ten minutes of day 119 (course-level check 2's respond day), on grade 5's branches of government (ss5-constitution): last used in grade 5 and not in grade 6, so under the settled rule it gets a readiness check, not a retrieval task, given before the unit opens so its results shape day 121 (Cory, 2026-11-14). Day 120, unit 8's look-back held here, sits between.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 132: the map, growth and location, the Republic's government, Caesar and Augustus, the legion and provincial rule (3a) tied to growth and length, and classes and slavery from an unseen source. Respond day 133.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 11. What held Rome together (days 134 to 144, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** what Roman taxes paid for and who paid (MA.7.T4c.3b); one currency, roads and the Pax Romana (3c); customs duties; inflation from debased coins; who is hurt by inflation, deflation and unemployment, and savings kept as coins (the personal finance touch, no added day); roads, aqueducts, the arch and concrete; Latin, the Romance languages and word roots.

Arc: five economy days, two engineering days, two language days, then assessment (which also answers the compelling question) and respond. The economy leads because unit 12's internal causes need it.

- Readiness: none. Day 134 opens with a retrieval task and short reteach on a public budget (grade 3's row was used in grade 6 unit 6, so it is not idle two grades).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 143: taxes (3b), currency, roads and the Pax Romana (3c), a trade policy's benefits and costs, inflation, deflation and unemployment each with its own prompt, engineering, and Latin and word roots. Day 138's 33 CE case is a credit crisis with falling land prices, not a general fall in prices; a general deflation case is labeled illustrative. Respond day 144.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
- Leans: math 7's percent unit (days 47 to 68) and ELA 7's roots (days 61 to 86) come before. Met.

## Unit 12. Rome's fall and the spread of ideas (days 145 to 156, weeks 29 to 32)

**Topics taught:** internal and external causes of the Western empire's breakup; the Eastern empire's survival; Christianity's growth in the late empire as review; the historians' debate (fall or transformation); a multi-cause argument naming the strongest counterclaim; how ideas spread across Europe, Asia and Africa, with Han China and Rome compared.

Arc: three cause days, four argument days (the debate, the explanation, the counterclaim, the draft), two days on ideas, a day finishing the argument on the compelling question, then assessment and respond.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 155: a multi-cause argument with causes and effects and the counterclaim named, and the ideas map. Respond day 156.
- Candidate settled: the Han and Rome comparison, kept inside day 153 at no added day, on the closing map where it fits.
- Lean: ELA 7's argument unit (days 87 to 108) comes before. Met.

## Shorter path

The year is 160 days of 50 minutes. Two shorter routes keep every owned code, skill and assessment; the unit plans check them against real minutes, so neither is yet shown to fit.

- **45-minute periods, same 160 days.** Each teaching day keeps its source work, its product and its exit ticket, and trims the second worked example or the whole-class share; assessment and respond days stay whole. Unit 9's research trims peer sharing to one partner.
- **About 150 days.** These days fold into the day before or after, because each code or skill they carry is taught on another day too: 55 (parallel timelines, into 56), 97 (perspective, into 98), 122 (Rome's growth, into 121), 126 (Augustus, into 125), 140 (the arch and concrete, into 139), 146 (external causes, into 145), 147 (Christianity in the late empire, into 146), 153 (the ideas map, into 152), and the second respond day after checks 2 and 3 (119, 160). Check 1's two respond days stay.
