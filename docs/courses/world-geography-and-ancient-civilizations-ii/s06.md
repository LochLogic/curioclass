## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How have land and sea shaped life in Oceania, past and present?"
- **Hook, day 67.** Compare Pacific-centered and polar maps with a public account of living Torres Strait sea stewardship. Ask whether sea separates people or connects them; post the compelling question.
- **Supporting questions.** Write together: What changes when a map changes (67)? Where are countries and cities (68)? Why settle here (69)? How do environment and culture combine (70)? How do peoples describe responsibilities today (71 and 73)? What can early evidence establish (72 and 74)?
- **Closing claim, day 75.** Answer the compelling question using the climate map, Central Land Council account, Mirarr findings and Ngāi Tahu testimony. Connect past and present, explain two places and acknowledge a competing explanation and an evidence limit.
- **Teaching moves.** Model map tools on 67, causal explanation on 69 and observation versus inference on 72. Partners question sources on 74; withdraw completed examples on 75. Teach archipelago, atoll, Country, kinship, oral tradition and perspective through examples. Geography shapes possibilities; people make choices.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 76 (End-of-unit assessment): End-of-unit assessment: the region's physical and political maps, settlement and economies explained, two places compared, the social features of Aboriginal peoples and of the Maori, and an unseen piece of evidence (rock art or an archaeological find) read with care for what it shows of early culture


**Assessment pack and time.** Five minutes directions, forty unaided work, five checking for omissions. Items 1 to 5 receive seven, six, nine, seven and eleven minutes (5a five, 5b six). No student timer or self-marking. Reserve these map treatments, coordinate readings, comparisons, passages and prompts until day 76. Required place knowledge recurs; worked examples and exit tickets use other routes, numerical readings and source passages.

- **P, physical and thematic maps.** Give a new Pacific-centered world map with numbered leaders at the Indian Ocean, Pacific Ocean, Australia, New Zealand, Antarctica, Fiji and Papua New Guinea. Include Coral Sea, Bay of Bengal, South China Sea, Great Victoria Desert and Great Barrier Reef labels for matching to symbols. Add title, compass, key and a scale bar of 250 kilometers; a marked schematic segment is two bars long. Provide separate landform, climate and population panels. They show Australia's dry interior and coastal concentration, Southeast Asian deltas and small Pacific islands. Population panel: GHS-POP R2023A, 2020 epoch, 1-kilometer equal-area cells, estimated resident persons per cell, not national totals or a direct census count. Keep a consistent legend across regional insets. Credit Schiavina, Freire, Carioli and MacManus (2023), European Commission Joint Research Centre; identify the Curio Class crop and legend adaptation, retain the dataset citation and reuse terms linked in field 10. Do not treat schematic distances as voyage measurements.
- **Q, political map and text.** Show Australia, Fiji and New Zealand as countries, capital stars and a labeled Sydney city dot. Mark Canberra near 35° south, 149° east and Suva near 18° south, 178° east. Give this original sentence: "Canberra is a capital city in Australia." A regional inset places Fiji northeast of Australia and shows that the Coral Sea is off northeastern Australia.
- **R, new settlement dossier.** Provide climate, landform and resource maps with original factual captions: Australia spans tropical and temperate latitudes, with a large dry interior, populated coasts, mineral resources and ports. Vietnam lies in the northern tropics with wet tropical and monsoon environments, productive river deltas, rice farming, fishing and manufacturing linked to ports. Small Pacific islands have limited land and freshwater, marine resources and sea connections. Record 2024 population snapshots: Australia about 27.2 million at 30 June 2024 (official estimate released 12 December 2024); Vietnam about 101.1 million at 1 April 2024 (the 2024 mid-term census, a sample survey released January 2025). These figures have different measures and reference dates; they are not current counts. Geography helps explain where people live and how resources support population; migration, history and policy also affect total size. Do not claim all Vietnamese people live in deltas or all island economies are alike.
- **S, new comparison cards.** Pair a Torres Strait map and the authority's land and sea stewardship account with a southern New Zealand map and unused Ngāi Tahu testimony about exchanging seasonal foods. Describe tropical island seas and reefs beside cooler southern coasts and seasonal foods. Attribute stewardship and knowledge transmission to the named communities, not to every resident or an entire climate zone. These fresh selections differ from day 67°s introductory passage and day 73°s family-learning passage.
- **T, unfamiliar community cases.** Composite cases, invented for practice, based on the land council and Te Ara, not passages used in teaching: a Central Australian child learns which relatives must help care for particular places and whom to ask before visiting; a Māori extended family hosts relatives at its marae and works with related descent groups on community decisions. Both accounts concern living communities. Give only vocabulary glosses: kinship, Country, whānau, hapū, iwi and marae. Students infer social organization and responsibilities from actions, rather than copy named features. Do not invent named communities or restricted practices.
- **U, unseen Aboriginal source extracts.** Attribute to the Gundjeihmi Aboriginal Corporation's media release, dated 19 July 2017, page 1: Chris Clarkson reports "huge quantities of ground ochre"; Justin O'Brien says, "These findings reinforce the need for the highest level of conservation and protection for this site". Gloss ochre as mineral pigment and conservation as protecting a place. A separate original context note describes excavated pigment and possibly painted sandstone, without supplying the author's purpose. Students infer purpose from the attributed words. Instruction uses only the release's grinding tools and changing environments. No images or restricted meanings. Mirarr reproduction permission is not established: obtain permission for these exact extracts before issuing print copies; meanwhile read the designated paragraphs on the linked original, without republishing pages. The print packet remains to make until permission is recorded.
- **V, unseen Māori source extracts.** Attribute to Janet Davidson, Amy Findlater, Roger Fyfe, Judith MacDonald and Bruce Marshall, 2011, research report, pages 93 to 94: "The tool was made by working a cutting edge"; "there is no way of identifying a specific island source with confidence". Gloss cutting edge and confidence. Original context note: Wairau Bar shell tool E199.1245 uses a tropical shell species absent from New Zealand; similar tools occur in tropical East Polynesia. Provide the title and author affiliations, including Rangitāne, but no statement of purpose. Students infer the report's purpose from its evidence and caution. Reproduce these text extracts under the article's CC BY 4.0 licence, credit all authors, link the licence and identify omissions and added glosses. No burial material or images. Instruction uses only the separate site records in field 10, never this shell tool.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A projection or map edge is the shape or edge of the world. | Map conventions mistaken for physical reality. | From memory. Compare a globe sketch with the map key. |
| B | The largest city must be the capital. | Capital symbols are not secure. | From memory. Compare city size with the capital symbol. |
| C | Geography determines population and livelihoods completely. | A causal influence is treated as a sufficient cause. | From memory. Link each influence to evidence and a further cause. |
| D | Places sharing an ocean must have identical cultures. | Cultural and environmental characteristics are not analyzed together. | From memory. Pair a cultural practice with an environmental feature for each place. |
| E | All Indigenous communities have one identical culture. | Regional differences, continuity and contemporary authority are erased. | Community accounts in field 10. Contrast two regional systems. |
| F | An object reveals its maker's exact beliefs. | Material evidence is mistaken for direct access to beliefs. | From memory. Contrast an observable mark with an unknown belief. |
| G | Latitude and longitude are interchangeable. | Coordinate axes are confused. | From memory; anticipated. Trace a parallel and meridian to a fresh location. |
| H | Dry land is uninhabited. | Lower density is mistaken for no residents. | Regional geography; anticipated. Locate an interior community and its water source. |
| I | Indigenous peoples belong only to the past. | Contemporary communities and authority disappear. | Community accounts in field 10; anticipated. Pair early evidence with a present community statement. |
| J | Several scale bars represent only one bar's distance. | The unit length is not multiplied by the number of bars. | From memory; anticipated. Measure a fresh segment and multiply its bar lengths. |
| K | A large land area guarantees a larger population total. | Area is substituted for population evidence. | Population dossier; anticipated. Compare measured area and dated totals. |
| L | A labeled city must be a country. | Political map categories are confused. | From memory; anticipated. Sort country, capital and other city symbols. |
| M | Compass directions are reversed. | The map is not oriented before comparing positions. | From memory; anticipated. Use the compass to locate one place north or south of another. |
| N | One dated layer dates every associated image or tradition exactly. | A context date is extended beyond what it dates. | From memory. Separate the dated deposit from an undated image or account. |
| O | One activity proves every community member had that role. | Evidence of an activity becomes a universal social claim. | From memory. Contrast evidence of work with evidence about who performed it. |
| P | One imported object identifies its exact transport vessel. | Overseas connections are mistaken for a documented voyage. | From memory. Separate an imported object from an absent vessel record. |
