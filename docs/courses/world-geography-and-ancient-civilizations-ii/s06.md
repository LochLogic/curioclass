## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How have land and sea shaped life in Oceania, past and present?"
- **Hook, day 67.** Compare Pacific-centered and polar maps with a public account of living Torres Strait sea stewardship. Ask whether sea separates people or connects them; post the compelling question.
- **Supporting questions.** Write together: What changes when a map changes (67)? Where are countries and cities (68)? Why settle here (69)? How do environment and culture combine (70)? How do peoples describe responsibilities today (71 and 73)? What can early evidence establish (72 and 74)?
- **Closing claim, day 75.** Answer the compelling question using the climate map, Central Land Council account, Mirarr findings and Ngāi Tahu testimony. Connect past and present, explain two places and acknowledge a competing explanation and an evidence limit.
- **Teaching moves.** Model map tools on 67, causal explanation on 69 and observation versus inference on 72. Partners question sources on 74; withdraw completed examples on 75. Teach archipelago, atoll, Country, kinship, oral tradition and perspective through examples. Geography shapes possibilities; people make choices.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 76 (End-of-unit assessment): End-of-unit assessment: the region's physical and political maps, settlement and economies explained, two places compared, the social features of Aboriginal peoples and of the Maori, and an unseen piece of evidence (rock art or an archaeological find) read with care for what it shows of early culture


**Assessment pack and time.** Five minutes directions, forty unaided work, five short-item marking. Items 1 to 5 receive seven, six, nine, seven and eleven minutes (5a five, 5b six). No student timer. Reserve these map treatments, coordinate readings, comparisons, passages and prompts until day 76. Required place knowledge recurs; worked examples and exit tickets use other routes, numerical readings and source passages.

- **P, physical and thematic maps.** Give a new Pacific-centered world map with numbered leaders at the Indian Ocean, Pacific Ocean, Australia, New Zealand, Antarctica, Fiji and Papua New Guinea. Include Coral Sea, Bay of Bengal, South China Sea, Great Victoria Desert and Great Barrier Reef labels for matching to symbols. Add title, compass, key and a scale bar of 250 kilometers; a marked schematic segment is two bars long. Provide separate landform, climate and population panels. They show Australia's dry interior and coastal concentration, Southeast Asian deltas and small Pacific islands. Do not treat schematic distances as voyage measurements.
- **Q, political map and text.** Show Australia, Fiji and New Zealand as countries, capital stars and a labeled Sydney city dot. Mark Canberra near 35° south, 149° east and Suva near 18° south, 178° east. Give this original sentence: "Canberra is a capital city in Australia." A regional inset places Fiji northeast of Australia and shows that the Coral Sea is off northeastern Australia.
- **R, new settlement dossier.** Provide climate, landform and resource maps with original factual captions: Australia spans tropical and temperate latitudes, with a large dry interior, populated coasts, mineral resources and ports. Vietnam lies in the northern tropics with wet tropical and monsoon environments, productive river deltas, rice farming, fishing and manufacturing linked to ports. Small Pacific islands have limited land and freshwater, marine resources and sea connections. Record 2024 population snapshots: Australia about 27.2 million at 30 June 2024 (official estimate released 12 December 2024); Vietnam about 101.1 million at 1 April 2024 (the 2024 mid-term census, a sample survey released January 2025). These figures have different measures and reference dates; they are not current counts. Geography helps explain where people live and how resources support population; migration, history and policy also affect total size. Do not claim all Vietnamese people live in deltas or all island economies are alike.
- **S, new comparison cards.** Pair a Torres Strait map and the authority's land and sea stewardship account with a southern New Zealand map and unused Ngāi Tahu testimony about exchanging seasonal foods. Describe tropical island seas and reefs beside cooler southern coasts and seasonal foods. Attribute stewardship and knowledge transmission to the named communities, not to every resident or an entire climate zone. These fresh selections differ from day 67°s introductory passage and day 73°s family-learning passage.
- **T, social-feature reference strip.** Supply new factual summaries sourced to the land council and Te Ara: Central Australian kinship connects relationships with duties and rights concerning land; systems differ regionally. Country connects land and sea with law, family and knowledge. Māori whānau, hapū and iwi connect extended families and descent groups; marae sustain community meeting and relationships. Living organizations maintain and adapt these responsibilities. The strip supplies vocabulary, not the comparisons or explanation requested.
- **U, unseen Aboriginal evidence description.** Write an original, attributed account of the Mirarr research report's ochre-processing evidence: excavators found processed pigment in ancient occupation layers at Madjedbebe, with some ochre-coated sandstone fragments possibly once part of a painted wall. The site's long occupation does not date every painting. Identify the 2017 reporting partnership, its protection purpose and its distinction from the ancient material. No rock art image or restricted meaning is supplied. Instruction instead uses the report's grinding-tool and changing-environment findings.
- **V, unseen Māori evidence description.** Describe the Wairau Bar shell tool E199.1245 from Davidson and colleagues' 2011 research: its tip was deliberately ground into a cutting edge; its shell species does not live in New Zealand, and similar tools occur in tropical East Polynesia. Exact island origin and precise excavation context are uncertain. Identify Rangitāne's present authority separately from the researchers' material interpretation. Use text only; do not show the report's burial material. Instruction uses other public settlement evidence and later pā sites.
