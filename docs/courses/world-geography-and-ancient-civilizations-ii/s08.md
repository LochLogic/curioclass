## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was Athens a democracy?"
- **Hook, day 87.** Pair a map of separate Greek communities with Pericles' claim about government by the many, as Thucydides records it. Ask whom "the many" includes; post the compelling question without deciding it.
- **Supporting questions.** Write together: How did Greek communities connect (87 and 88)? Who could govern, and how (89 to 91)? How should we decide together (92 to 94)? Whose lives challenge the public ideals (95 and 96)? Why did people defend different arrangements (97)? What happened to our proposal (120)?
- **Closing claim.** On 98, answer using Thucydides, the constitutional account and the life comparison. Revise against unseen evidence on 99, distinguishing democracy among citizens from equal participation by all residents.
- **Teaching moves.** Model maps on 87, sourced claims on 89 and perspective on 90. Partners question evidence on 95 to 97; withdraw completed claims on 98. Teach polis, colony, citizen, assembly, council, constitution, rule of law, oligarchy, helot and perspective through examples. Explain constitutional changes over time, not one jointly authored document. Distinguish fifth-century assembly decisions from fourth-century lawmaking.


**D2.Civ.9.6-8:** Compare how varied groups deliberate in different settings. Parts: compare participation, discussion rules and decision procedures across groups and settings.
**D2.His.4.6-8:** Analyze several influences on people's perspectives in different historical eras. Parts: connect multiple contextual factors to perspectives; analyze across eras. The local file holds codes only; wording was checked in the official framework.
- **Limits.** Geography influences choices; it does not require democracy. Colonies occupied inhabited places and involved exchange and conflict. Greek influence was not one continuous empire. Wars, myths and accomplishments belong to unit 9.
