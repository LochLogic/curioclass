## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Could the Roman Republic have been saved?"
- **Hook, day 121.** Put Rome's river crossing on a Mediterranean map beside Augustus's claim to have restored public government. Ask how a small city gained such power, and whether keeping offices meant keeping a republic.
- **Supporting questions.** Write together: How did location support expansion (121 and 122)? Who governed, and who was excluded (123 and 124)? Why did institutions fail to contain powerful leaders (125 and 126)? How did armies and provincial government sustain conquest (127 and 128)? What could status and freedom mean (129 and 130)?
- **Closing claim, day 131.** Answer the compelling question using Polybius, Appian's land account and Augustus. Explain a plausible alternative, its obstacle and what the sources cannot establish. Accept supported disagreement; counterfactual certainty is impossible.
- **Teaching moves.** Model map inference on 121, source purpose on 123 and institutional comparison on 124. On 123 distinguish the traditional overthrow of kingship in 509 BCE from the Republic's gradual institutional growth and plebeian demands for protection and participation. Model a causal chain on 125; guide revision on 128 and withdraw completed examples on 131. Teach republic, consul, senate, tribune, veto, civic duty, dictator, legion, province, patrician, plebeian and manumission in context.

These are anticipated errors, not observations of children. On 122 compare changing boundaries with one timeless outline for A. On 124 contrast overlapping Roman functions with a modern branch chart for B, and public law with unequal rights for C. On 126 compare the Caesar-to-Augustus sequence with "one ruler" for D. On 128 contrast a mechanism with a label, and delegated authority with independence, for E. On 129 compare a wealthy plebeian with a poor free citizen for F. On 130 contrast one memorial's evidence with an unsupported universal claim for G. On 131 compare a qualified alternative with a guarantee for H. Each correction takes five to ten minutes, followed by a fresh case; use the whole class if more than a third missed, otherwise a small group.
