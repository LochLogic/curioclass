## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What can a city tell us when we cannot read its writing?"
- **Hook, day 1.** Show seal 49.40.2 without its catalogue caption. Ask what students see and what remains unknown. Post the question before retrieving maps; locate the object's region afterward.
- **Supporting questions.** Write these with the class: Where could people settle and work (days 1 to 3)? How does movement change land use (day 4)? What do cities and objects reveal (days 5 to 7)? Can another society's record help (day 8)? What remains uncertain (day 9)?
- **Closing claim, day 9.** Answer the compelling question using the seal, Mohenjo-daro's layout and the Meluhha record. Defend the inference the evidence best supports, and name one it cannot rule out.
- **Teaching moves.** Model map reading on day 1, causal explanation on day 3, observation versus inference on day 5, and sourcing and a short claim on day 6. Pairs source on day 7; model corroboration on day 8; withdraw sentence frames on day 9. Teach monsoon, land use, seal, context, audience and undeciphered. If grade 6 climate circulation is insecure, day 3 models uneven heating, winds and seasonal rain for five minutes within its explanation.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 10 (End-of-unit assessment): End-of-unit assessment: the region's physical and political maps with a location used to add to a text, settlement and economy explained, how population growth and movement change land use, and an unseen Indus object with its find context: a plausible maker, date, place of origin and audience inferred, and what it shows of Indus society

**Assessment pack.** Make new physical and political map views spanning Central and South Asia, with a compass, scale, degree grid and political legend. Include Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Maldives. Mark capitals distinctly from other cities. Use current dated boundaries, with disputed boundaries distinguished in the legend. No memorized country list is required. Hold all assessment views, locations, object cards and numerical cases out of teaching. Required feature names recur because they are the content; the map tasks and explanations are new.

**Minutes.** Five minutes directions, forty unaided work, five short-item marking. Items 1 to 6 receive five, six, eight, seven, seven and seven minutes. No student timer.

For items 5 and 6, provide three short cards in our own words, not copied translations or photographs:

- **Object X:** A broken stone stamp bears an incomplete elephant carving. Its back shows manufacturing marks and traces of a knob. It was recovered from a Kot Diji phase (Early Harappan) street deposit at Harappa. Maker, manufacture date and intended audience are not supplied. This is an original card based on the unfinished steatite elephant seal H2000-4474/8994-01 ([harappa.com, node 2339](https://www.harappa.com/node/2339)), named only in the teacher key.
- **Context Y:** Pottery associated with that layer belongs to the Early Harappan phase, about 2800 to 2600 BCE. A layer dates deposition, not necessarily manufacture. The unfinished surface has grinding marks. These observations come from Kenoyer and Meadow, PDF pages 10 and 11 (printed li and lii).
- **Comparison Z:** Other early Indus objects include stamp impressions in clay and standardized stone weights. Unfinished objects provide evidence of craft production. The source is the same excavation report, PDF page 7 (printed xlviii). This is corroboration within one report, not two independent authors.
