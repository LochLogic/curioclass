## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What can a city tell us when we cannot read its writing?"
- **Hook, day 1.** Show seal 49.40.2 without its catalogue caption. Ask what students see and what remains unknown. Post the question before retrieving maps; locate the object's region afterward.
- **Supporting questions.** Write these with the class: Where could people settle and work (days 1 to 3)? How does movement change land use (day 4)? What do cities and objects reveal (days 5 to 7)? Can another society's record help (day 8)? What remains uncertain (day 9)?
- **Closing claim, day 9.** Answer the compelling question using the seal, Mohenjo-daro's layout and the Meluhha record. Defend the inference the evidence best supports, and name one it cannot rule out.
- **Teaching moves.** Model map reading on day 1, causal explanation on day 3, observation versus inference on day 5, and sourcing and a short claim on day 6. Pairs source on day 7; model corroboration on day 8; withdraw sentence frames on day 9. Teach monsoon, land use, seal, context, audience and undeciphered. If grade 6 climate circulation is insecure, day 3 models uneven heating, winds and seasonal rain for five minutes within its explanation.


- **D2.Geo.12.6-8:** "Explain" how worldwide population redistribution changes land use in particular places. Parts: identify the distribution change, connect it to a place, explain land-use effects.
- **D2.His.11.6-8:** "Use" other historical evidence to infer a source's plausible maker, date, origin and intended audience when these are unclear. Parts: consult other sources; infer all four with reasons.


- **Limits.** No decipherment, known individual seal maker, settled Indus religion or modern territorial debate is claimed. Faiths and caste belong to unit 2; comparison across civilizations belongs to unit 5. Distinguish the broad 3000 to 1300 BCE span from the urban peak around 2600 to 1900 BCE.

- **Carried.** Keep all five models. Students read latitude and longitude on days 1 and 2, independently on day 10. The regional frame links environment to settlement on day 3, adding human choices on day 4. Source-sourcing separates origin from findspot on days 6 and 7. Corroboration compares independent evidence on day 8. Claim, evidence and reasoning returns from grade 6 on day 6, guides day 9 and is used unaided on day 10.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 10 (End-of-unit assessment): End-of-unit assessment: the region's physical and political maps with a location used to add to a text, settlement and economy explained, how population growth and movement change land use, and an unseen Indus object with its find context: a plausible maker, date, place of origin and audience inferred, and what it shows of Indus society

**Assessment pack.** Make new physical and political map views spanning Central and South Asia, with a compass, scale, degree grid and political legend. Include Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Afghanistan, Pakistan, India, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Maldives. Mark capitals distinctly from other cities. Use current dated boundaries, with disputed boundaries distinguished in the legend. No memorized country list is required. Hold all assessment views, locations, object cards and numerical cases out of teaching. Required feature names recur because they are the content; the map tasks and explanations are new.

**Minutes.** Five minutes directions, forty unaided work, five short-item marking. Items 1 to 6 receive five, six, eight, seven, seven and seven minutes. No student timer.

For items 5 and 6, give students X, Y and Z with the identified observation extracts and visual below. Quotation marks distinguish original words from Curio Class adaptations. Gloss stratum as layer, ceramic as pottery and striae as grooves; do not supply the inferences.

- **Object X:** A broken stone stamp bears an incomplete elephant carving. Its back shows manufacturing marks and traces of a knob. It was recovered from a sealed stratum attributed to the Kot Diji phase through associated pottery. Maker, manufacture date and intended audience are not supplied. This is an original card based on the unfinished steatite elephant seal H2000-4474/8994-01 ([harappa.com, node 2339](https://www.harappa.com/node/2339)), identified on the student source line; its catalogue description is not the student task.
- **Context Y:** Kenoyer and Meadow (2010), printed li to lii, record a "well-defined sealed stratum" dated through pottery associations and "Deep grinding striae" on the seal. These brief attributed observation extracts are the only quoted report wording; the copyrighted report and figures are not reproduced. Curio Class adaptation: the layer is Early Harappan, about 2800 to 2600 BCE; deposition need not date manufacture.
- **Comparison Z:** Supply the public-domain photograph of ancient seal 49.40.1 and its modern impression ([record and image](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/324062)), about 2600 to 1900 BCE. Label the impression modern, the seal ancient; ask students to compare their visible marks. Add Kenoyer and Meadow, printed l, observation extract: "soft clay used to seal a container". Curio Class adaptation: excavated impressions suggest marking containers; the later comparison does not prove X was used. Standardized weights are described on printed xlviii. The two excavation extracts share authors; the visual is a separate object record.
