# Section 2.1: Evidence of changing land

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-ESS1-1, this section's share (days 11 to 15): all of it.** "Use several sources as evidence that some Earth events happen fast, such as earthquakes, and some happen slowly, such as erosion." Children use two sources for each change, a picture pair and separate words, and sort the change as fast or slow. The "such as" list is examples, not parts.
- **2-ESS2-1, day 15 only.** The verb "Compare" is taught in section 2.2. Day 15 sets up what will be compared: wind and water reshape land, and a model shows that movement but not how long a real change takes.
- **Limits that bite here.** No years, no counts of time, no plate motion, no canyons, no erosion formulas. Erosion is never given one speed. No injured people in any picture, and no child is asked to tell what happened to them or their family.
- **Connections.** ELA RI.2.3, days 12 and 14: captions are read aloud and put in order. Unit 1's two-column source chart and water arrow, days 11 and 12.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A sudden event is shown by two sources that agree on words about when ("in one night"), not by the dates on a photograph. (2) A slow change is shown by an older view and a later view that differ, though no one watched a day of it; "looks the same from day to day, yet changes" is the stability-and-change idea. (3) Erosion is wind or water wearing land away and carrying it off; it can be fast or slow. (4) A tray shows how soil moves with water, not how long a real change took. (5) The teacher's own reading of the path (feet clear the plants, then rain carries the bare soil) is not told to children; they build it over the unit.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 23 (2-ESS1-1, six points; unit plan field 5). It is given on day 23, in section 2.3, as an observation on a new island packet; nothing here uses it.

**Evidence, every day.** A spoken exit of 3 minutes: picture cards "invented for practice", said aloud, one ask in a child's words; everyone points or draws on paper; the teacher hears a rotating six and the record sheet names who. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" opens with that letter's example (field 4).

**Exit questions.**
- **E11.** Cards: three pictures of the ground under the swings, "Monday", "Friday" and "one month later", only the last with a scuffed hollow. "Pip says the swing ground never changes, because it looked the same on Monday and on Friday. Point to the picture that shows Pip is wrong." Answer: the one-month-later picture. Wrong: Friday, or "Pip is right" (A).
- **E12.** Cards: two beach pictures, "before the storm" and "after", and the caption "Storm waves carried the sand away in one night." "Is this a fast change or a slow change? Point to the words that tell you." Answer: fast, "in one night". Wrong: slow, because erosion takes a long time (B); no change, it is still a beach (A).
- **E13.** Cards: two pictures of a sand pile at a building site and the caption "The wind blew the top off the pile one afternoon." "Fast or slow? Point to the words." Answer: fast. Wrong: slow, because wind and water wear things away slowly (B).
- **E14.** Cards: an older drawing of a flat ledge on a hillside terrace, a later drawing with a rounded slope, and the caption "Rain splashed small bits down, a little each year." "Nobody saw the ledge change on any day. Did it change? Point to yes or no." Answer: yes, the later drawing differs. Wrong: "No, nobody saw it change" (A).
- **E15a.** Cards: two records of Alder Creek bank: an older and a later view with the edge farther back and the caption "a little each season"; a picture and the caption "after the flood a big piece was cut away in one night". "Point to the record that shows the fast change." Answer: the flood record. Wrong: neither, because erosion is always slow (B).
- **E15b.** "The tray moved soil with water. What can the tray not tell us? Point to a card: which way the soil moves; how long a real change takes; where the water goes." Answer: how long a real change takes. Wrong answers here have no letter in the register (see field 11); the teacher notes them and repairs on day 16.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class change model starts on day 11 as each child's sketch of what made the path, with unit 1's water arrows and source labels kept. Day 12 adds fast-change evidence and a change arrow (a jagged arrow, labeled "fast" in print); day 14 adds slow-change evidence (a stepped arrow, labeled "slow") and revises the day 11 sketch; day 15 revises it again from both and adds a one-line model limit: a model shows how land moves, not how long. Pinned beside it is the unit 1 consensus runoff model, unchanged. The teacher holds the class chart; each child holds their sketch and two-column source chart.
- **Key moment 1, day 12: two sources agree.** Ideal instrument: ordered photograph cards with a separate caption card, read aloud. Spec: fits; printed cards, no digital tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 15: what a model shows and cannot show.** Ideal instrument: the unit 1 sand tray beside a fan over a sand tray. Spec: partly fits; this section has no wet supplies, so the model is the unit 1 pour record cards and one picture card of a fan tray, "invented for practice". The real fan tray is first run in section 2.3. Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-draw the picture pairs, read captions aloud, and use the class's own unit 1 pour drawings for day 15.

## 6. Days

## Day 11


- **5 min. Hook.** The path photographs (or the drawing, said aloud to be invented for practice) are shown beside the grass. Children point and say what they notice; the compelling question is read aloud, once.
- **3 min. Source practice (retrieval).** Two captioned animal cards: each child marks one fact from each source on the unit 1 two-column chart, with a source label. For a miss, the teacher models one labeled column.
- **5 min. Forecast practice (retrieval).** "A forecast warns of strong wind. Mark the picture of helpers securing loose objects before it arrives." For a miss, read the before and after captions together.
- **10 min. Sketch what made the path.** Each child draws the path before and now, adds water arrows from unit 1 and one label saying what they think made it. Nothing is marked right or wrong.
- **8 min. Steady, yet changed.** Three cards of the same path edge, out of order; the class orders them by the caption words and says the path looked the same on any one day. The words "steady" and "changed" are put on the chart.
- **6 min. Question board.** The class adds what it wants to find out: what changed, how quickly. Six rotating children say one question each (30 seconds each, 3 minutes), the rest follow on the chart.
- **3 min. Exit.** E11.
- Holds what is carried: each child their sketch; the teacher the question board. Tool: None. By hand: drawing and ordering cards. Evidence: E11. Sensitivity: calm landscapes, helpers only, no forced sharing.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the path before and now and orders path-edge cards; the exit gives three swing-ground pictures, two alike, and asks which one shows a claim that nothing changed is wrong, a new case and a new ask.

## Day 12


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E11 (A), at most 5 minutes (field 4).
- **2 min. Open the board.** The question "what changed, how quickly?" is read.
- **18 min. Four fast changes.** Four ordered card pairs, each with a separate caption read aloud: a volcano's new mound, shifted ground, a fallen slope, a flood-cut bank, 4 minutes each plus 2 for the first. For each, children order the pair, point to the words about when, and add a mark to the two-column chart (picture | words) with source labels. The federal photographs keep their credits.
- **5 min. The word erosion.** From the flood-cut bank: wind or water wearing land away and carrying it off. "Erosion can be fast or slow" is said once and written on the chart.
- **4 min. What the four share.** The class finds the "when" words ("in one night", "all at once") and writes the evidence check: ordered pictures, words that say when, a second source that agrees.
- **3 min. Say it.** Six rotating children say "I know it was fast because ___" (30 seconds each).
- **3 min. Exit.** E12.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class chart and fast column; each child their chart marks. Tool: None. By hand: ordering and pointing. Evidence: E12. Sensitivity: no injuries; no child retells an event.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice orders four fast-change pairs and points to the words that say when; the exit asks the same of one new beach pair, adding a fast-or-slow choice the practice never asks.

## Day 13


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E12 (A or B), at most 5 minutes.
- **8 min. Order and point again.** Pairs re-order the day 12 card pairs and point to the words about when. The teacher hears three pairs, six children, 1 minute each, while the rest work; the record sheet names who.
- **6 min. A second place, Marsh Hill (invented for practice, said aloud).** A slope after a sudden flood: two ordered pictures and a caption. The class runs the evidence check and says whether a fast explanation still fits, comparing with the day 12 flood-cut bank. This is the six-minute second-case comparison that replaces repeated partner turns.
- **8 min. Choose the evidence.** Pairs sort six source cards into "tells when it happened" and "only a story or a liking" (unit 1's evidence versus story).
- **5 min. Pip's claim.** Pip says, one line at a time: "I looked at one picture, so I am sure." Children test it against the chart: one source is not several. No child is the mistake.
- **5 min. Say it.** Six more rotating children say "I know ___ because ___".
- **3 min. Exit.** E13.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart with Marsh Hill added; each child their sort. Tool: None. By hand: sorting. Evidence: E13. Nothing new is taught; this is practice of days 11 and 12.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice orders the day 12 pairs, reads Marsh Hill and sorts source cards; the exit gives a new sand pile caption and asks fast or slow with the telling words, a choice the practice never makes.

## Day 14


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E13 (B), at most 5 minutes.
- **3 min. Hook.** A worn stone step (invented for practice): "Did anyone see it change in a day?"
- **14 min. Four slow changes.** Ordered older and later views, each with a separate caption read aloud: a worn step, a smoothed rock, a riverbank that has moved back, a sea cliff edge farther inland, about 3 minutes each, plus 2 to mark. Children order, point to the words ("a little each season"), and add to a slow column. The wide river bank is a different place from Alder Creek.
- **6 min. Fast beside slow.** The class compares the columns: "in one night" against "little by little", and says why a slow change can look steady on any one day.
- **6 min. Revise the model.** Each child adds slow arrows to the day 11 sketch and keeps the water arrows.
- **3 min. Say it.** Six rotating children say "I know it changed because ___" (30 seconds each).
- **3 min. Exit.** E14.
- Holds what is carried: each child their revised sketch; the teacher the two-column chart. Tool: None. By hand: ordering, drawing. Evidence: E14. No canyons and no time numbers.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice orders four slow-change pairs and sets their words beside the fast side; the exit gives a new ledge drawing pair and asks how the child knows it changed, with no speed word to point to.

## Day 15


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E14 (A), at most 5 minutes.
- **8 min. The path again.** Three invented path cards, older, later and latest, with captions ("children walked here all term"; "after a big rain this week"). The class sorts the evidence into fast and slow, two sources each, and does not yet say how to stop it.
- **9 min. What a model can show.** Unit 1's pour record cards and one picture card of a fan over a sand tray: the models show soil moving the way water or wind goes; they cannot show how long a real change took.
- **8 min. Revise the class model.** Fast arrows, slow arrows, the day 11 sketch and the model limit line are joined on one chart.
- **4 min. Exit.** E15a, then E15b.
- **3 min. Board.** What changed, how quickly: answered. Next: what protects the land? A bridge to day 16.
- **3 min. Say it.** Six rotating children say "I know ___ because ___" about the path.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class model; each child their final sketch. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, drawing. Evidence: E15a and E15b.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts path pictures and words and joins the class model. Part a picks the fast record from two new bank records; part b asks alone what a pan model cannot tell, which the practice asks only of the class.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 12, "How do you know it was fast?"; day 14, "How can we tell it changed if nobody saw it?"; day 15, "What can the tray tell us, and what can't it?"
- Written task, day 15: each child's revised sketch of the path with two printed labels, "fast" and "slow", and the frame "I know ___ because ___", scribed if wanted.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names one fast and one slow change; names the source for each (a picture or words); gives no number of years.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: fast, slow, change, erosion, steady, older, later. Frames: "I know it was fast because ___." "The later picture is different from the older picture."
- **Access.** Large photographs and tactile outlines; captions read aloud; pointing, speaking or a scribe instead of writing; a quiet task if a picture upsets a child.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are spoken with cards; a child may point, speak or draw.
- **Reading load.** Short printed labels only; every caption is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a third source for one change and say whether it agrees; no new standard.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use two pictures of a worn place at home or outdoors, an older and a later one, and ask "did it change?" Do days 12 and 14 with one pair each, reading the caption aloud; keep the chart on one sheet. Day 15 is the last day of this section: redraw the path sketch and say what is fast and what is slow.
- **One projector.** Show the ordered card pairs on days 12 and 14; everything else is on paper.
