# Section 2.3: Test wind protection and explain new places

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-ESS2-1, this section's share (days 21, 22, 24, 26).** "Compare several designed solutions that slow or stop wind or water from reshaping the land." Days 21 and 22 add wind to the water results of 2.2; day 24 compares several unfamiliar solutions for both; day 26 repairs and retries.
- **K-2-ETS1-3, this section's share (days 21, 22, 25, 26).** "Use test results from two objects built for the same problem to compare what each does well and poorly." Days 21 and 22 compare two wind barriers; day 25 observes it on an unfamiliar pair; day 26 retries.
- **2-ESS1-1, this section's share (days 23, 26).** "Use several sources as evidence that some Earth events happen fast, such as earthquakes, and some happen slowly, such as erosion." Taught in 2.1; here it is sampled on new evidence and repaired.
- **Limits that bite here.** Wind retreat in whole centimeters; the fan on low, run by the teacher; damp sand only. Limits are retreat at most 6 and path width at most 5 (day 22). A proposed route is an inference, never a measured fact. Conditions match within a test, not between wind and water. No numerical event times. Earlier products are unscored; no completed answer picture is shown; the day 24 hillside photographs cannot prove how fast the land changed.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.D.10, day 22 (wind results as bars); math 2.MD.A.1, day 21 (whole centimeters).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Scoring on one scale with points: each look-for is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and only "shown" counts a point; supported and independent evidence are recorded separately. (2) Why the teacher runs the wind trials: the fan and dust. (3) A difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same" and wind trials scatter more than water. (4) The assessment days: directions read neutrally, no conferences, hear six children during ordinary work on days 21 and 22 only. (5) Written feedback names one component and one next step, not a guessed misconception.

**Exit questions.**
- **E21.** Cards: a row of tall cardboard tubes; bars from matched fan tests for the tubes and a flat board; a close picture of the tubes. "Pip says the tall tubes will protect best because they are tallest. Which card decides? Point." Answer: the matched test bars. Wrong: the close picture of the tubes (C).
- **E22.** Cards: hedge H and fence F protecting the same dune strip, with bars: H lost 1 then 4 centimeters and uses 9 of path; F lost 3 then 3 and uses 2; limits are retreat at most 5 and path at most 6. "Which one meets both limits? Point." Answer: F. Wrong: H, because it lost the least land at first and so has no weakness (D).

**Day 26 retry (unscored; fresh packet from unit plan field 5, "invented for practice").** Only the missed component is asked.
- **Event.** A cliff loses a block suddenly in two records; two other records show a rocky surface smoothing gradually. Answer: both speeds, two sources each, no numbers. Wrong: the surface cannot be changing if it looks the same (A); erosion is always slow (B).
- **Solutions.** Equal water tests: R loses 7 then 8 and uses 9 of path; S loses 10 then 11 and uses 1; T loses 12 then 13 and uses 11. Limits: retreat at most 9, path at most 10. Answer: choose R (only R meets both); S saves path but fails retreat; T is worse than R on both. Wrong: choose S because it looks strongest (C); R has no weakness because it protects better (D).
- **Two barriers.** Wind trials: X loses 2 then 8, Y loses 5 then 5, limit at most 6 each run. Answer: X is better the first run but fails the second; Y loses more at first and meets the limit both times; choose Y and mark both runs against the limit. Wrong: Y has no weakness because it was chosen (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class change model and the design-cycle chart from 2.2 are revised from wind on day 21 (wind arrows added) and given bars on day 22 (strips become bars, wind added). On days 23 to 25 the revised conventions are used alone: the arrows, source labels, the dotted arrow for a route not seen (grade 2 unit 1) and the limit line. Day 26 joins the path evidence, the revised change and design models and the question board answer. The teacher holds the consensus chart; each child holds their own models.
- **Key moment 1, day 21: a teacher-run wind trial.** Ideal instrument: a tray under a guarded low-speed fan, with a paper strip marking the retreat and a fixed ruler. Spec: partly fits; the fan and damp sand are teacher-only and the class only watches and reads. Instrument step: whole centimeters; a difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same". Trial identical moisture and fan place the day before and keep a dated log. If dust lifts, the tray fails or the fan is not available, stop and use the matched photo series with measured data, said aloud to be invented (unit plan field 8). Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Counts.** Six runs of about 2.5 minutes: bare, wall and windbreak, twice each. After each run the teacher marks the retreat with a strip and one rotating reader a group reads it against a fixed ruler to the whole centimeter (about 1 minute). Children wear goggles, stay behind a line and do not touch the sand; clean-up is the teacher's damp-cloth sweep and wiped, bagged goggles (3 minutes).
- **Key moment 2, days 23 to 25: a model made alone on new evidence.** Ideal instrument: paper packets and blank model paper. Spec: fits; no tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Day 21 uses the photo series; days 22 to 26 are paper.

## 6. Days

## Day 21


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E20b (C or E), at most 5 minutes.
- **3 min. Set up and safety.** Goggles on; the line; the fan only for the teacher; "stop" means stop.
- **17 min. Wind trials.** Six runs (bare, wall and windbreak, twice each) with one reader a group reading the strip each time. Groups record the numbers and path width on their paper.
- **6 min. Compare.** Each group says one thing each barrier did well and poorly; the class chart is revised with wind arrows.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Board.** What protects against wind: answered. One question left open.
- **3 min. Exit.** E21.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart; each group its record. Tool: None. By hand: reading strips, recording. Evidence: E21. Hear six rotating children during the compare.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice runs six wind trials and records each retreat strip; the exit gives finished bars for new tubes and a flat board, and asks which lost less, with the numbers said aloud.

## Day 22


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E21 (C), at most 5 minutes.
- **6 min. Strips become bars.** Pairs turn the day 21 wind results into bars on squared paper, the water bars still pinned.
- **6 min. A second tray.** The teacher shows another soil tray with matched starting conditions (invented for practice): does the wind choice transfer? This is the six-minute second-case reasoning that replaces repeated data recitals.
- **12 min. Compare alone.** A field's windbreak (retreat 2 then 3, path 7) and wall (retreat 5 then 6, path 4), limits retreat at most 6 and path at most 5: each pair marks which meets which limit, draws the bars and says when each helps or fails. Answer: only the wall meets both; it protects less.
- **5 min. Pip's claim.** Pip says, one line at a time: "The one that lost least is always best." Children test it against the limit lines. No child is the mistake.
- **3 min. Say it.** Six rotating children say "It helps when ___ and fails when ___" (30 seconds each).
- **3 min. Exit.** E22.
- Holds what is carried: pairs their bars. Tool: None. By hand: bars, marking. Evidence: E22. Nothing is taught as new.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the class wind bars and tests written examples against two limits; the exit gives a new hedge and fence with two limits and asks which meets both, with the numbers that show it.

## Day 23


- **5 min. Directions.** The teacher reads the prompt aloud, neutrally, one step at a time with a pause; the island packet and blank model paper are handed out.
- **20 min. The packet.** Each child alone reads (listens to) the four records, marks the fast and the slow change, and revises their change model with source labels and arrows. No group conferences.
- **10 min. Revise the anchor.** Unscored. Each child revises the path design from their earlier results; the teacher teaches nothing.
- Holds what is carried: each child their model. Tool: None. Evidence: item 23 (six look-fors, one scale, "shown" counted as the point; the unit plan, field 5). Response on day 26 only.

## Day 24


- **5 min. Directions.** Same routine; the nursery-slope packet is handed out.
- **20 min. The packet.** Each child alone compares the water and the wind solutions, marks the records and draws arrows for soil and redirected flow.
- **10 min. The hillside pair.** Unscored. A pair of photographs of a protected hillside: "Which feature of our model fits what you see, and what can the photographs alone not tell us?" Capture dates are unstated; they cannot show a rate. Credit kept.
- Holds what is carried: each child their model and day 23 record. Tool: None. Evidence: item 24. Calm landscapes and helpers only.

## Day 25


- **5 min. Directions.** Same routine; the orchard packet with picture bars.
- **25 min. The packet.** Each child alone gives each screen's strength and weakness against the limit in both runs, chooses, and marks a revised barrier model with both retreat records against the limit.
- **5 min. Check and collect.** Names checked, papers collected.
- Holds what is carried: each child their model; days 23 and 24 records are kept, not collected again. Tool: None. Evidence: item 25. Response on day 26 only.

## Day 26


- **5 min. Return and read feedback.** Each child gets their records with one written next step for one component and reads or hears it; six rotating children say it back.
- **15 min. Repair by letter groups.** Group A with B (event reasoning), group C with D (design comparison), group E (matching conditions), each with the teacher for 4 minutes in turn while the others follow picture directions at their table; 3 minutes to move. Hand-backs: the day 22 exit slips (D) and the day 23 to 25 records. Paper repair moves: link each claim to two labeled source records; add evidence labels and change arrows to an incomplete model; complete both designs' strength-and-weakness boxes.
- **10 min. Retry.** The missed component only, on the fresh packet in field 3; unscored. Record supported and independent evidence separately.
- **5 min. Consensus.** The class joins the revised change and design models and answers the compelling question about the bare path and how to stop it, with unseen routes dotted.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the consensus chart; each child their retry. A child may follow one-to-one with an adult. Tool: None. Evidence: the retry in field 3.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice is feedback reading and repair by letter group; the exit is a fresh unscored retry of only the missed component, on new cards, answered alone one step at a time.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 21, "Which barrier did better against wind, and what did it do poorly?"; day 22, "When does each help and when does it fail?"; day 26, "Why did the path become a ditch, and how could we stop it?"
- Written task, day 26: the framed reason in the retry, "I choose ___ because the bars show ___, and it is poor at ___", scribed if wanted.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: uses the record, not how the design looks; names a strength and a weakness; marks anything not measured as an inference.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 21 returns: bar reading and strengths and weaknesses (2.2), the fair-test rule. Day 22 returns the water bars. Days 23 to 25 practice nothing; they sample days 11 to 22. Day 26 returns the letters from days 21 to 25.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: wind, windbreak, limit, strength, weakness, inference. Frames: "It is good at ___ but poor at ___." "I think ___, but we did not measure it."
- **Access.** Read every caption and prompt aloud neutrally; large pictures, tactile outlines, sentence frames and a scribe for dictated reasons. No spelling or drawing-quality points.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, large print, a scribe or an oral answer; everyone marks an individual picture record.
- **Reading load.** Labels only; every record and prompt is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Compare a third unfamiliar barrier on the same two limits.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 21: watch a fan on low (adult only) over a damp sand tray with and without a barrier, or use the photo series; read each strip. Days 23 to 25: three short sittings of the same packets. Day 26 is the last day of the unit: talk through the feedback, redo the weakest piece, then say why the bare path became a ditch and how to stop it.
- **One projector.** Show the day 26 consensus chart; everything else is on paper.
