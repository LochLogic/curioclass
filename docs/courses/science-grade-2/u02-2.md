# Section 2.2: Protect land from water

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 2. Unit 2, The land changes, fast and slow. *Why has the bare path down our slope become a ditch while the grass beside it has not, and how could we stop it?* Anchor: a bare, worn path on a schoolyard slope (or a photograph of one) beside grass that holds (days 11 to 26): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 16 to 20.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (16, 19), application 3 (17, 18, 20). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 35 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

- **2-ESS2-1, this section's share (days 16 to 20).** "Compare several designed solutions that slow or stop wind or water from reshaping the land." Water: four solutions are tested (card wall, rock barrier, fabric mat, plants) against a bare slope and compared. Wind: day 20 plans and sketches a stick windbreak and a low wall; the wind trials are section 2.3.
- **K-2-ETS1-3, this section's share (days 16 to 20).** "Use test results from two objects built for the same problem to compare what each does well and poorly." Day 17: wall and rocks; day 18: mat and plants; day 19: their results on one graph. Every comparison names a strength and a weakness of each object.
- **Limits that bite here.** Water only; retreat in whole centimeters; two criteria with two limits (retreat at most 6, path width used at most 4, day 19); fresh seedlings lack established roots; fabric stands in for a grass mat; no formulas, no amounts of water. A difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same" (ours, untested).
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1 (ruler zero and whole centimeters, days 16 to 18, taught locally); math 2.MD.D.10 (single-unit bar graph, day 19, retrieved before graphing); grade 1 design cycle and a two-bar chart (day 16 retrieval).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A fair test keeps the start equal (slope, wetness, cup, pour place), changes one thing and watches one thing. (2) The ruler is read to the whole centimeter; the sand edge is fuzzy, so two trays set up the same still differ a little, and the day 16 pair shows how much. (3) A limit is the most we allow; a design can win one criterion and lose the other. (4) A wall sends water around its ends into the land beside it. (5) Plants hold soil by established roots; new seedlings do not yet. (6) Adult set-up the day before, goggles, and the clean-up count (field 5).

- **4 min. Return and repair.** The E15b slips (a model cannot show how long) or letter B from E15a, whichever more children missed, 4 minutes.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** The grade 1 signal device: sketch how it met its purpose and show one test and one improvement; read a two-bar daylight chart and mark the longer bar. For a miss, model the step before trays.
- **3 min. The problem.** The path: stop the soil leaving. The cycle chart gets "compare" added after "test".
- **6 min. An equal start.** The teacher pours the same cup on two trays whose slopes differ (goggles on): the steeper tray loses more. The class concludes the slopes must match and sets them with the slope card.
- **9 min. Mark the retreat.** Each group pours once on two bare trays set equal, marks where the edge moved back from a fixed line with a paper strip, and reads the strip with a ruler to the whole centimeter. The teacher teaches ruler zero first. The two numbers show how much equal trays differ.
- **4 min. Two limits.** A barrier uses some of the path. "Retreat" and "path width used" are two things to watch, and a limit is the most we allow (the numbers come on day 19). Children sketch a barrier on the path.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Exit.** E16.
- Holds what is carried: each group its two strips; the teacher the cycle chart. Tool: None. By hand: pouring, ruler, sketch. Evidence: E16. Fallback: the invented bare-slope strips, said aloud.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: barrier, retreat, limit, wall, mat, roots, wind. Frames: "It does well at ___ and poorly at ___." "I chose ___ because the bar is ___."
- **Access.** Raised-line squared paper or tape bars; a partner pours and reads while the child records or points; goggles fitted over glasses; a scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are spoken with cards; a child may point, speak or draw.
- **Reading load.** Labels and numbers only; everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan a third barrier for the same slope and say which criterion it would win.
