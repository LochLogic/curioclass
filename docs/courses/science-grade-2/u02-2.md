# Section 2.2: Protect land from water

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-ESS2-1, this section's share (days 16 to 20).** "Compare several designed solutions that slow or stop wind or water from reshaping the land." Water: four solutions are tested (card wall, rock barrier, fabric mat, plants) against a bare slope and compared. Wind: day 20 plans and sketches a stick windbreak and a low wall; the wind trials are section 2.3.
- **K-2-ETS1-3, this section's share (days 16 to 20).** "Use test results from two objects built for the same problem to compare what each does well and poorly." Day 17: wall and rocks; day 18: mat and plants; day 19: their results on one graph. Every comparison names a strength and a weakness of each object.
- **Limits that bite here.** Water only; retreat in whole centimeters; two criteria with two limits (retreat at most 6, path width used at most 4, day 19); fresh seedlings lack established roots; fabric stands in for a grass mat; no formulas, no amounts of water. A difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same" (ours, untested).
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1 (ruler zero and whole centimeters, days 16 to 18, taught locally); math 2.MD.D.10 (single-unit bar graph, day 19, retrieved before graphing); grade 1 design cycle and a two-bar chart (day 16 retrieval).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A fair test keeps the start equal (slope, wetness, cup, pour place), changes one thing and watches one thing. (2) The ruler is read to the whole centimeter; the sand edge is fuzzy, so two trays set up the same still differ a little, and the day 16 pair shows how much. (3) A limit is the most we allow; a design can win one criterion and lose the other. (4) A wall sends water around its ends into the land beside it. (5) Plants hold soil by established roots; new seedlings do not yet. (6) Adult set-up the day before, goggles, and the clean-up count (field 5).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none; items 24 and 25 are owned by section 2.3.

**Evidence, every day.** A spoken exit of 3 minutes, picture cards "invented for practice", one ask, aloud; everyone points or draws; the teacher hears a rotating six and the record sheet names who. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" opens with that letter's example. Exit cases below use no taught or class numbers.

**Exit questions.**
- **E16.** Cards: a tray of dry sand and a tray of damp sand, same slope, same cup. "Pip poured the same cup on both and they lost different amounts. Does it matter that one was dry and one damp? Point to yes or no." Answer: yes, the start must match. Wrong: "No, the barrier is the only thing that matters" (E).
- **E17.** Cards: a tall wooden fence picture; tray result bars for the fence and two other barriers; a big picture of the fence. "Pip picks the fence because it looks strongest. Which card does Pip need before choosing? Point." Answer: the tray result bars. Wrong: the big picture of the fence (C).
- **E18.** Cards: a tray of wood chips spread across the whole path; a bar showing the chips lost the least sand; a tidy-looking picture. "The chips lost the least sand, and Pip says they have no weakness. Point to the card that shows something the chips might do poorly." Answer: the chips across the whole path (they use up the path). Wrong: the card that says the chips lost the least (D).
- **E19.** Cards: a two-bar picture graph, one square for each centimeter, with a limit line across the bars; one bar short, one past the line. "Which bar goes past the limit line? Point." Answer: the taller bar. Wrong answers here have no letter in the register (field 11); the teacher notes them.
- **E20a.** Cards: two pictures, a curved screen and a staggered row of posts protecting the same garden strip. "Sketch both shapes." Answer: both shapes drawn with an arrow showing how each turns the wind.
- **E20b.** Same cards. "How could we compare their protection fairly? Say it or point." Answer: same ground, same fan place, same conditions; measure the retreat. Wrong: pick the one that looks stronger (C); test them on different ground or with the fan at a different distance (E).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class change model and unit 1's runoff arrows and source labels stay pinned. The design-cycle chart from grade 1 is extended (define, sketch, build, test, compare); the sketch is made on day 16, tested on day 17, improved on day 18; strips become bars on day 19; wind is added in section 2.3. Each group holds its tray record; the teacher holds the class results chart and the cycle chart.
- **Key moment 1, day 17: a fair water test.** Ideal instrument: a tray, a slope card, a fixed cup, a paper strip from a fixed line and a ruler. Spec: fits; household trays, no digital tool. The ruler is read to the whole centimeter; a 1 centimeter difference is "about the same". If the day 16 pair of bare trays differ by more than 2 centimeters, reset and pour once more; if they still differ, the plan is the invented record (field 5 of the unit plan), said aloud to be invented. No trial photographs exist; keep dated logs.
- **Key moment 2, day 19: results become bars.** Ideal instrument: squared paper, one square a centimeter. Spec: fits. Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Counts.** Six groups of five, each with its own trays, so no tray is shared. Roles rotate (pourer, second pourer, marker, measurer, recorder); the teacher hears one child a group. Clean-up on days 16 to 18 and 20 is 5 minutes (4 on day 20): goggles wiped with damp cloths and bagged, hand washing in two waves at two sinks, an adult tips the sand into waste and never a drain, spills wiped at once. Stones and sticks stay out of mouths; seedlings are untreated and not eaten.
- **A room with nothing.** Day 18 has a measured tray-photo series; days 17 and 19 use the invented bar records, drawn on cards and said to be invented. The weaker evidence is named.

## 6. Days

## Day 16


- **4 min. Return and repair.** The E15b slips (a model cannot show how long) or letter B from E15a, whichever more children missed, 4 minutes.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** The grade 1 signal device: sketch how it met its purpose and show one test and one improvement; read a two-bar daylight chart and mark the longer bar. For a miss, model the step before trays.
- **3 min. The problem.** The path: stop the soil leaving. The cycle chart gets "compare" added after "test".
- **6 min. An equal start.** The teacher pours the same cup on two trays whose slopes differ (goggles on): the steeper tray loses more. The class concludes the slopes must match and sets them with the slope card.
- **9 min. Mark the retreat.** Each group pours once on two bare trays set equal, marks where the edge moved back from a fixed line with a paper strip, and reads the strip with a ruler to the whole centimeter. The teacher teaches ruler zero first. The two numbers show how much equal trays differ.
- **4 min. Two limits.** A barrier uses some of the path. "Retreat" and "path width used" are two things to watch, and a limit is the most we allow (the numbers come on day 19). Children sketch a barrier on the path.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Exit.** E16.
- Holds what is carried: each group its two strips; the teacher the cycle chart. Tool: None. By hand: pouring, ruler, sketch. Evidence: E16. Fallback: the invented bare-slope strips, said aloud.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice pours equal cups on tilted trays and marks the retreat on its own strip; the exit asks whether two different cup sizes matter on new trays, and reads the new strip's retreat in centimeters.

## Day 17


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E16 (E), at most 5 minutes.
- **3 min. Roles and plan.** Keep the same (slope, cup, pour place), change one thing (the barrier), watch one thing (retreat). Groups 1 to 3 build the card wall, groups 4 to 6 the rock barrier; each has a bare tray too.
- **4 min. Set up.** Slope card, barrier placed, cups filled.
- **8 min. Two runs.** Pour both trays, mark and read the strips, reset to the slope card, pour again. Both trays pour at once with two pourers.
- **3 min. Width.** Each group measures the width of the path its barrier uses with a strip.
- **4 min. Class chart.** Each group's two numbers go on the chart; the class names the middle of the three for each barrier.
- **5 min. Well and poorly.** For each barrier: one thing it did well and one it did poorly. Children see the wall sends water around its ends (redirect) and the rocks let more soil go.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **3 min. Exit.** E17.
- Holds what is carried: groups their trays; the teacher the class chart. Tool: None. By hand: pouring, strips, ruler. Evidence: E17. If the sand will not behave, use the invented strips.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice pours a bare and a barrier tray, marks strips and posts numbers; the exit gives a finished result card with two new bars and asks how many centimeters less the fence lost, a subtraction the practice only shows on the chart.

## Day 18


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E17 (C), at most 5 minutes.
- **3 min. Roots hold soil.** Original grass, shrub and tree drawings and a caption: established roots hold soil; a new seedling's do not yet.
- **4 min. Set up.** Each group sets a fabric mat tray and a plant tray, equal slope, same cup.
- **11 min. Two runs.** As day 17 with both trays; the class chart takes six numbers a design.
- **3 min. Record.** Retreat and path width used for each.
- **5 min. Improve.** Each group picks one change because the record says so (for example pegging the mat edge) and sketches it. It is not retested.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5; plants return to the adult.
- **3 min. Exit.** E18.
- Holds what is carried: groups their records; the teacher the class chart. Tool: None. By hand: pouring, sketch. Evidence: E18. Fallback: the supplied measured tray-photo series.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice pours two trays and chooses one change from the group's own record; the exit gives the record of a new design, a brush pile, and asks which one change the card supports.

## Day 19


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One letter from E18 (D), at most 5 minutes.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Single-unit bar reading from math: read a two-bar picture graph, one square a centimeter. For a miss, model one square.
- **8 min. The first two bars.** The teacher draws the bars for the bare slope and the wall from their strips on squared paper.
- **10 min. Graph the results.** Pairs draw five bars (bare, wall, rocks, mat, plants) from the class chart's middle second-run number and read which lost least.
- **7 min. Limits and the tradeoff.** A line at 6 shows the retreat limit; a second graph of path width used has a line at 4. The class finds which designs pass each and models the space tradeoff: a design that stops the most water may use the most path.
- **3 min. Exit.** E19.
- **2 min. Close.** The board: what protects against water?
- Holds what is carried: pairs their graphs; the teacher the limit lines. Tool: None. By hand: bars on squared paper. Evidence: E19. If class numbers are unusable, the invented record is graphed, said aloud.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws bars from class results and lays a limit line on them; the exit gives two new strip lengths on a blank grid and asks for the bars and a tick above the one past the limit.

## Day 20


- **5 min. Return and repair.** The E19 graph-reading slips (no letter), at most 5 minutes.
- **4 min. The wind problem.** Same problem, a different mover: air carries dry soil. The board: against wind.
- **6 min. Shape and purpose.** Original pictures of a low wall and a stick windbreak; children draw a shape-to-job arrow (grade 1) and a wind arrow showing how each turns the air and what the land behind it may do.
- **14 min. Build and sketch.** Groups build a card wall and a stick windbreak in soil trays (no fan), choose the stick spacing, and sketch both. The class agrees one spacing for the teacher's trials.
- **3 min. Fair wind plan.** Keep the same (tray, fan place, wetness), change the barrier, watch the retreat.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Hands washed in two waves; sticks counted back.
- **4 min. Exit.** E20a, then E20b.
- Holds what is carried: groups their sketches; the teacher the agreed spacing. Tool: None. By hand: building, sketching. Evidence: E20a, E20b. Sticks blunt, below faces; no fan today.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds a card wall and a stick row and sketches each; the exit gives two new shapes, a curved screen and staggered posts, to sketch with wind arrows, then asks how to compare them fairly.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 17, "What did each barrier do well and poorly?"; day 19, "Which design would you choose and what would you give up?"; day 20, "How does this shape change the wind?"
- Written task, day 19: the graph with a printed label for each bar and the frame "I chose ___ because ___ and it is poor at ___", scribed if wanted.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: reads a bar correctly against the limit line; names a strength and a weakness of the choice; uses the record, not how the design looks.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Each day opens with the one-letter repair from the day before. Day 16 retrieves grade 1 design work and a two-bar chart; day 19 retrieves single-unit bar reading.
- **Days 17 to 20:** the equal-start rule (day 16), the evidence check and source labels (section 2.1), and unit 1's water arrows are used again.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: barrier, retreat, limit, wall, mat, roots, wind. Frames: "It does well at ___ and poorly at ___." "I chose ___ because the bar is ___."
- **Access.** Raised-line squared paper or tape bars; a partner pours and reads while the child records or points; goggles fitted over glasses; a scribe.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are spoken with cards; a child may point, speak or draw.
- **Reading load.** Labels and numbers only; everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan a third barrier for the same slope and say which criterion it would win.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At a table: a baking tray of damp sand, a cup of water, a ruler and a strip. Do days 16 to 18 as one test of a bare slope against one barrier, with a second pour; day 19, draw the two bars; day 20, sketch a windbreak. This section's last day is day 20.
- **One projector.** Show the cycle chart and the day 19 bars; the trials are done by hand.
