# Section 3.2: Match properties to purposes

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 2. Unit 3, Materials and their best uses. *Why are rain boots made of rubber and mittens of wool, and not the other way round?* Design question: which material makes the best rain hat for the class's paper doll? Anchor: the class's paper doll, left by the window in a drizzle in a paper hat, comes back soggy, and the class tests rubber, wool, foil, cloth and plastic to find which hat keeps it dry (days 27 to 38): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 32 to 36.** Days by kind: fluency 1 (32), application 3 (33, 34, 35), check 1 (36). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 35 to 40 minutes.

- **2-PS1-2, this section's share (days 32 to 35).** "Use test results from different materials to decide which material's properties best fit a given job." Day 32 gives the hat winner a second job (a spill pad) from the day 30 records; day 33 tests bend for a bridge deck; day 34 tests hardness and texture for a cover and a grip; day 35 revises the failed hat.
- **2-PS1-1, this section's share (days 33 to 36).** "Investigate materials to describe them and sort them by properties you can observe, such as color, texture, hardness and bendiness." Days 33 and 34 add bend, press and texture comparisons to the sorting; day 36 is the unit's observation of this part, with sealed ice and lukewarm water.
- **Limits that bite here.** Bend under a fixed load is not breaking strength. Length only, whole centimeters; 1 centimeter is "about the same". Texture alone does not tell grip on another surface, so grip comes from pictured results of the same block on the same slope. No skin-friction tests. An adult places every load. No glass or sharp metal in student hands; nothing is tasted.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1, day 33 (whole centimeters). Writing W.2.7, day 34 (a framed observation page). Engineering and society, day 35: people choose materials by testing, and materials come from nature, some processed (wood from trees, then a block).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Direction of bend: less drop means stiffer, so a deck wants little bend and a strap may want much. (2) The same material can be best for one job and poor for another; the job comes first, then the property to test. (3) Hard means no dent under a gentle press; rough is a different property. (4) How day 36 is recorded: a live checklist for the proposal and the safe action, a paper record for the rest, one scale, one point only for "shown". (5) That a paper path cannot show a safe action.

## Day 33

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Test bridge-deck strength and flexibility and compare how far equally loaded decks bend.
- note: Only length is measured numerically. Equal large blocks supply loads without measuring mass. Retrieve two-design strengths and weaknesses.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error E from E32, at most 5 minutes: the class pairs two jobs with two materials from the records and says why one material is not best for both.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Strengths and weaknesses of two designs (grade 2 unit 2): "What did each do well, and poorly?" A miss: the teacher models one with a limit line.
- **4 min. Plan.** The job: a deck for a toy bridge that must stay level under a block. Fair test: same span between two low supports, same strip size, same block; change only the material; watch how far the middle drops. We compare how far it bends, not when it would break. Less drop means stiffer.
- **14 min. Test.** Six groups, two for each of card, plastic and fabric strips (new). The adult places the block over a catch tray; the measurer holds a ruler beside the middle and reads the drop to the whole centimeter; two trials a group. Goggles on, fingers and feet clear.
- **5 min. Compare.** Both groups' numbers for each material go on the class chart; a difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same". The class draws the three bars. Where two groups differ by more than 1 centimeter, the teacher asks where the block sat.
- **4 min. Clear blocks.** Blocks to the tray, strips returned, hands wiped.
- **3 min. Exit.** E33.
- **2 min. Board.** The class names a job that wants a strip to bend (the teacher offers a strap if none comes); the question stays open for day 35.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class chart and the choice model. Tool: None. By hand: placing, reading a ruler, bars. Evidence: E33. Hear six rotating children during the compare.

- **3 min. Directions.** Read aloud, neutrally, one step at a time. The order of the six groups is posted.

Observation windows.** Six windows of 4 minutes, one group at a time at one station: three unfamiliar samples (sanded pine, clear flexible plastic, new cotton, each 10 by 10 centimeters), a blunt stick, a flashlight, a sealed ice cup, a sealed lukewarm cup.
