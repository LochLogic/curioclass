# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 2

Course `science-grade-2`, grade 2. Draft, revised 2026-11-15.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-2/days.csv). Seven units keep their order, 12 owned skills, 12 expectation codes and 70 unit days. Two two-day course-level checks and their two respond days bring the total to 76. Section ids reserve groups of at most six days; they are not section plans. Every unit begins with a phenomenon, a question board and an initial model, then revises that model from evidence before an unfamiliar-case performance.

## Assessment method

No unit exceeds 25 days, so none requires a mid-unit assessment. Every code is taught before assessment. A closing window spreads full performances across the last lessons: unit 1 days 8/9, unit 2 days 23/24/25, unit 3 days 36/37, unit 4 days 45/46, unit 5 days 54/55, unit 6 days 63/64, unit 7 day 72. Each code's components are named below and in the CSV. Response occurs only on days 10, 26, 38, 47, 56, 65 and 73 for the respective units; course responses are 59 and 76.

For observed action, one teacher plans for 30 children: six groups of five, every child acting and an own paper record. Unit 3 uses six four-minute groups on day 36, six minutes set-up/transitions and five minutes anchor return. Units 4, 5 and 6 use three four-minute groups on each of two days, with three minutes transitions and 20 minutes for the named paper/teaching work. Day 45 adds three minutes of directions, 38 minutes in all, inside the course's 35 to 40. Other children independently use familiar change cards (unit 4), single-date graph/plan sheets (unit 5), or familiar sock/flower model labels (unit 6). Builds begin before the final day. Paper tasks with frames carry explanations; a spokesperson never stands for a group.

Collect once: keep each child's single observation record across its window. A course check within ten days carries it forward unchanged, with no new collection or re-scoring. Course checks list only newly sampled codes, use new cases and avoid task forms given in the preceding two weeks. Original unit records still establish all owned parts even when a later check samples fewer codes. Each response uses a fresh short retry only for a demonstrated gap, with supported and independent evidence distinguished.

## Unit 1. Land and water

Days 1 to 10, weeks 1 to 4. Runoff on the schoolyard drives the initial sketch, tray trial and model revisions on days 1 to 5. The school-grounds picture map tests a representation against a real place. Source choice on day 6 precedes combining location/state information on day 7. Local and contrasting land/water patterns are compared before independent transfer. No water cycle, amounts or numerical model scale.

Day 8 assesses 2-ESS2-3: independent useful-source choice and combined information from two caption/map/media sources about water in oceans, rivers, lakes, ponds, ice and snow, solid/liquid states and patterns. Day 9 assesses 2-ESS2-2 through each child's unfamiliar-area model product and explanation of landforms, water bodies and a route pattern, without scale. These are paper products, not 30 conferences. Day 10 responds for the whole day; unit 2 has no readiness check (day 11 retrieves sk-forecast-severe). Grade 1 source practice is retrieved on day 6. Candidate: **keep** the school-grounds picture map, one existing day, day 4. Adult-only seed-head collection/storage prepares unit 6. Watch runoff from a safe path or doorway, never drains or water.

## Unit 2. The land changes, fast and slow

Days 11 to 26, weeks 4 to 9. The worn path beside grass asks what changes land and what slows it. After the hook, day 10 evidence guides a brief severe-weather content reteach. Several-source fast/slow reasoning on days 12 to 15 establishes stability-change before tests of erosion solutions. Days 16 to 19 compare unprotected ground, walls, rocks and vegetation under equal water conditions and graph results. Days 20 to 22 compare windbreaks and walls, including a second site. Evidence precedes design choices; comparisons address every design's strengths and weaknesses, not only a winner. This largest unit carries three skills, multiple sources, two erosion agents and measured design comparisons.

Day 23 assesses 2-ESS1-1 through several-source evidence for fast AND slow changes, without measured event durations; anchor design revision follows. Day 24 assesses 2-ESS2-1 through comparison of several tested wind/water solutions, reshaping/slowing land and designed changes to surroundings. Day 25 assesses K-2-ETS1-3: matched results for two objects addressing the same problem, each strength AND weakness and fitness to the problem. Prior components are retained, not repeated. Day 26 responds and revisits the path. Grade 1 design/chart practices are retrieved on day 16. Candidate: **keep** protected-hillside before/after images on day 24, one existing day, with ten minutes after the independent comparison. Distinguish field observation from a controlled test. Calm disaster images show helpers and never require personal accounts. Fans are adult-operated on low; damp sand, shields, eye protection, safe pouring and handwashing stay in place.

## Unit 3. Materials and their best uses

Days 27 to 38, weeks 9 to 13. A soggy paper-doll hat requires property evidence. Days 27 to 31 develop agreed gentle tests, sorting, cooler/warmer solid/liquid observations, equal towel/hat trials and length graphs. Day 32 asks whether a different purpose changes the choice. Bridge-deck trials and protective cover/grip cases on days 33/34 develop strength, flexibility, hardness and texture as well as absorbency. Day 35 revisits the hat. Testing and graphing get more time than rehearsal.

Day 36 assesses 2-PS1-1: every child plans and conducts a safe test, describes/sorts observable properties, classifies cooler/warmer water as solid/liquid and identifies patterns. Day 37 assesses 2-PS1-2: new-purpose decisions from length-only results with cause-based evidence, plus the link from natural materials to designed objects. Do not re-collect day 36's action record. Day 38 responds. Retrieve grade 1 tests, charts and shape/function on day 27. Candidate: **drop** pencil dismantling, zero days; it adds sharp parts and composition rather than needed property evidence. No glass or sharp metal in student hands; new fabric/paper and adult-prepared lukewarm sealed samples only.

## Unit 4. Can we change it back?

Days 40/41 make observed same-pieces/different-whole accounts, including a second shape set. Water and chocolate changes on day 42 precede lasting heating/cooling cases on day 43: cooked egg, frozen/thawed leaf and heated-paper images. Paper explanations run alongside the remaining observed station. Day 47 responds. Retrieve grade 1 account/claim practices on day 39. Candidate: **drop** melted crayons, zero days; water/chocolate already provide reversible evidence. All heat is adult-only, eye protection at the bath, observers behind a boundary, no flame. Heated paper is photographs only; allergies, sealed displays and no eating apply.

## Unit 5. What plants need

Days 48 to 56, weeks 16 to 19. Pale grass under a board motivates two separate single-factor comparisons against light plus water. Eighteen pre-grown matched seedlings supply six groups' control/dark/dry conditions on day 49, school day 81. Dated observations on days 50 and 52, school days 83 and 86, lead to fairness and the second record on day 53: height alone can mislead when a tall plant is pale/weak. Final observation on day 55, school day 91, is ten school days and two weekends after setup. Adult care between meetings adds no student lesson. A single-date bar graph never becomes a connected time graph.

Days 54/55 assess 2-LS2-1 using the class seedling cups, trays, centimeter rulers and fitted splash goggles. Observe half the children each day, with every child's identified planning, measuring and dated data contribution from days 49/50/52/54/55 forming one conducting record. A fresh framed single-factor plan and interpretation check BOTH sunlight and water needs, each against the healthy control, using cause/effect. Generic class participation is insufficient. Day 56 responds. Retrieve grade 1 parts/light-test/chart teaching and plant needs on day 48. Candidate: **drop** the cooler fourth treatment, zero days; keep a child's temperature question without another study. Use untreated seedlings, wash hands, discard mould adult-only and restore treatment plants after observation.

## Unit 6. Seeds and pollinators

Days 60 to 65, weeks 23 to 25. Burrs on a sock and repeated flower visits raise two animal-job questions. Day 60's photographs show bees seeking nectar or pollen for food; pollen can move incidentally during those visits. The sock model on day 61 and smooth-seed comparison on 62 test attachment and limits. Day 63's pollinator has a visible pollen source and receiving patch on each flower, an animal body and food-seeking route. Students name what each part represents and distinguish pollen from seed. No detailed reproductive anatomy is required.

Days 63/64 assess 2-LS2-2 after the necessary teaching: each child starts an unfamiliar-animal design drawing/build on 63, finishes, demonstrates and revises it on 64. Half the groups are observed each day, with one record. Evidence links animal structure to plant/seed OR flower/source/receiver and pollen movement; a drawing communicates the proposed design. Both jobs are taught; every child models the jay's seed job, and pollen movement is checked on the day 63 retrieval card. Day 65 responds and explicitly answers BOTH burr and flower-visit anchors; unit 7 has no readiness check (day 66 retrieves sk-needs-places). Retrieve grade 1 structure/function on day 60. Candidate: **drop** a separate second-animal group model, zero extra days; independent transfer already serves that purpose. Stored seed heads or a labeled hook-and-loop stand-in, paper pollen and covered wire ends avoid seasonal and dust/sharp-part dependencies. No live bees.

## Unit 7. Habitats

Days 66 to 73, weeks 25 to 27. Two schoolyard spots open the question of kinds living in different places. After the hook, a five-minute retrieval of sk-needs-places covers places and their resources. Inventories, equal effort/area and one-picture-per-kind graphs precede pond/forest and desert/ocean-shore comparisons, including equal counts with different kinds. Survey days 66 and 70 are school days 124 and 129, a week apart. Day 71 compares repeated counts and revises the anchor model. Visibility and search effort can change a count without real habitat change. Observations establish patterns, never a wetter-is-richer rule or habitat ranking.

Day 72 assesses 2-LS4-1 through each child's unfamiliar several-habitat observations, plant AND animal inventories, counts of kinds rather than individuals, shared/different kinds and an equal-count pair. Scientific names are not assessed. This is an individual paper record, not an oral queue. Day 73 responds. Grade 1 parts/needs and earlier unit 5 content are retrieved on day 66. Candidate: **keep** repeated surveying on day 70, one existing day. Watch without collecting, stay on paths/in sight, check stinging insects and allergies; matched two-date photos replace an unsafe or frozen site.
