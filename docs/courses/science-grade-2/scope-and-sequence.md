# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 2

Course `science-grade-2`, grade 2. Draft, 2026-11-14.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-grade-2/days.csv). It preserves the frame's seven units, their order, all 12 owned skills and their 12 expectation codes: 70 unit days plus four course-level check days and two respond days, 76 altogether. Each unit opens with its anchor and a question board, develops an initial model, revises it from evidence and returns to its question before an individual unfamiliar-case performance. Section numbers in the CSV reserve groups of at most six days, not section plans. No items or lesson text are written here.

## Unit 1. Land and water

Days 1 to 10, weeks 1 to 4. The puzzle is where rainwater on the schoolyard goes. Days 1 to 4 move from an initial sketch through a tray trial and model revision to a school-grounds picture map. Days 5 to 8 test which patterns survive a contrasting area, choose a useful source and combine water-location information from two sources. The model comes before the map comparison; source choice comes before fact gathering. Frozen and liquid water connect the local model to water elsewhere without amounts, a water cycle or numerical scale.

Observation day 9 reserves an individual model of an unfamiliar area's land shapes and water bodies, plus independent source choice and a two-source water-location and state record. It covers 2-ESS2-2 and 2-ESS2-3 with modelling, information gathering and patterns. Day 10 responds and returns to the runoff anchor. Readiness: none; retrieval as above. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **keep** the school-grounds picture map on day 4, one day inside the ten. It tests the representation against a real place. Doorway observation or site photographs avoid unsafe runoff access. Teacher-only gathering and dry storage of seed heads on that day prepares unit 6 without adding a spring field dependency.

## Unit 2. The land changes, fast and slow

Days 11 to 26, weeks 5 to 11. The worn path beside grass asks why land changes and how its change can be slowed. Days 11 to 15 use several sources for fast events and slow wear, revising the stability-change model. Days 16 to 20 plan matched trials, compare unprotected ground, walls, rocks and vegetation under water, graph results and plan wind protection. Days 21 to 24 compare windbreaks and walls, weigh each design's strengths and weaknesses, improve a design and consider a real planted hillside. Evidence about change precedes attempts to slow it; data precede choosing a design. This unit has the most days because it carries three skills, several source types, two agents of erosion and a full two-design data comparison.

Day 25 reserves an individual several-source fast/slow explanation and comparison of new tested solutions, covering 2-ESS1-1, 2-ESS2-1 and K-2-ETS1-3 in full. It asks what each of two objects does well and poorly on the same problem, not only which wins. Day 26 responds and revises the path explanation. Readiness: none; retrieval on days 11 and 16. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **keep** the protected-hillside photograph pair on day 24, one day inside the sixteen. Students distinguish a real-world observation from a controlled comparison. Nature of science is explicit on day 15: observed natural changes help explain earlier changes. No numbered event time scales. Disaster images remain calm, show helpers and never solicit a child's experience. Fan operation is teacher-only on low; pouring and airborne material need eye protection and the controls in the CSV.

## Unit 3. Materials and their best uses

Days 27 to 38, weeks 12 to 16. A soggy paper-doll hat creates a need to investigate properties. Days 27 to 31 plan and carry out sorting tests, compare water at cooler and warmer conditions, then test towels and hat materials and graph lengths. Days 32 to 36 extend purpose-based evidence to bridge-deck strength and flexibility and to hardness and texture, then revise the hat choice. Sorting and naming properties precede testing for a purpose. These twelve days carry two investigations and a student-drawn bar graph, with unequal time for the more demanding tests.

Day 37 reserves an individual test plan and observed property comparison, classification of a material at two temperatures, and a new-purpose choice from length-only test results. It covers every part of 2-PS1-1 and 2-PS1-2 with property patterns and cause in simple tests. Day 38 responds. Readiness: none; retrieve grade 1 light-material tests on day 27 and design comparison from unit 2. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **drop** taking a pencil apart, zero days. It adds object composition rather than needed property evidence and introduces avoidable dismantling and sharp-part handling. Unit 4 addresses pieces and wholes with large safe blocks. New fabric and paper only; no glass or sharp metal in student hands; warm water prepared by an adult.

## Unit 4. Can we change it back?

Days 42 to 50, weeks 18 to 20. The three-part anchor compares a castle rebuilt as a boat, chocolate melting and hardening, and a cooked egg. Days 42 to 45 develop a same-pieces/different-whole account before comparing reversible heating and cooling observations. Days 46 to 48 contrast the cooked egg, frozen and thawed leaf and heated-paper images, argue from attempted returns and revise the anchor model. The visible parts account makes changing back discussable before the harder argument about lasting change. No particle account is introduced.

Day 49 reserves an unfamiliar reassembly demonstration and evidence-based account for 2-PS1-3, and an argument about both reversible and lasting changes caused by heating or cooling for 2-PS1-4. Cause and effect and pieces regrouping are used with the evidence. Day 50 responds. Readiness: none; retrieve grade 1 account/claim practices on day 42 and the water records from units 1 and 3. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **drop** the melted-crayon bag, zero days. Water and chocolate already supply reversible evidence; another heated material does not justify more heating and handling in nine days. All heating remains adult-only, with the frame's out-of-reach bath or hot plate and no flame near students. Heated paper is photographs only. Allergy checks, sealed displays and no eating apply throughout.

## Unit 5. What plants need

Days 51 to 59, weeks 21 to 23. Pale grass under a board raises a causal question. Day 51 plans two single-factor comparisons, both against light plus water. Matched seedlings are set up on day 52, school day 103; observations on days 53 and 55 lead to fairness discussion and a single-date graph on day 57. Final observations on day 58, school day 113, preserve two weeks including two weekends. Adult watering and care between meetings add no student lesson. A tall pale plant is not automatically healthy; appearance, leaves and growth all matter.

Day 58 also reserves an individual new-case single-factor plan and observed recording/interpretation performance, supported by the class's completed investigation. Both sunlight and water needs are assessed for 2-LS2-1 through fair investigation and cause and effect. Day 59 responds. Readiness: none; maintained kindergarten needs, grade 1 roots/leaves, investigation and chart skills are retrieved on day 51. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **drop** the cooler fourth set, zero days. The required two factors already fill the live-study window; an additional temperature comparison would increase care and analysis without establishing an owned part. Record a child's temperature question on the board without starting another treatment. Use pre-grown matched seedlings so germination delay does not consume the test window. Discard mould promptly, use untreated seeds, wash hands and restore treatment plants after final observations.

## Unit 6. Seeds and pollinators

Days 60 to 65, weeks 24 to 25. Burrs on a sock and visits between flowers raise two distinct animal-job questions. An initial system sketch leads to a sock seed-spread model on day 61, evidence and limitations on day 62, and a revised pipe-cleaner pollinator on day 63. Comparing the two prevents confusing pollen with seeds. Each model links a structure to its job and is revised after a trial; the drawing communicates the proposed model. This six-day unit has one owned skill and uses routines already developed in units 2, 3 and 5.

Observation day 64 reserves an individual working model from new animal evidence, with its design drawing, explanation and trial-driven revision, covering 2-LS2-2. Both dispersal and pollination have been taught; the expectation permits modelling either animal job in the new case. Day 65 responds. Readiness: none; retrieve grade 1 part/function modelling on day 60. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **drop** a separate group model of a second animal, zero additional days. The independent unfamiliar-animal performance on day 64 already transfers beyond the class sock model. Stored seed heads or a clearly labelled hook-and-loop stand-in avoid reliance on fresh spring burrs. No live bees; small chalk amounts only and paper pollen for allergies, with no blowing. Cover pipe-cleaner ends before use.

## Unit 7. Habitats

Days 66 to 73, weeks 26 to 27. Two schoolyard surveys open the question of what kinds live in each place. Days 66 to 69 develop a picture inventory, equal-effort comparisons, a one-picture-per-kind graph, then pond, forest, desert and ocean-shore comparisons. The same two spots are surveyed again on day 70, school day 131, a week after day 66, school day 126. Day 71 revises the model from repeated counts. Repeated observation raises why evidence varies; a count difference alone cannot distinguish visibility, effort and real habitat change. Equal counts can also contain different kinds. The sequence never equates diversity with the number of individual organisms or ranks habitats.

Observation day 72 reserves an individual unfamiliar plant-and-animal inventory comparison including equal counts with different kinds, covering 2-LS4-1 through observations and empirical patterns. Scientific names are not assessed. Day 73 responds. Readiness: none; maintained kindergarten needs/places and grade 1 parts reasoning are retrieved on day 66. Mid-unit assessment: not required.

Candidate: **keep** the repeated survey on day 70, one day inside the eight. This gives careful counting and repeated observations a purpose beyond graph making. Watch living things without collecting them, stay in sight, check stinging insects and allergies, and use matched two-date photos if the site is unsafe or frozen.

## Shorter path

For periods of about 25 to 30 minutes, keep the question, evidence gathering, notebook/model revision and individual evidence. Prepare trays and model pieces before class, use one shared demonstration instead of duplicated trials after the first student-planned trial, reduce copied prose to labelled drawings and a spoken sentence, and use already supplied matched observations where a full physical trial cannot fit. Do not shorten safety routines, replace a working model with a label sheet, omit either plant comparison or rush an individual assessment. Keep the two-part course-level checks as two days. For a 25-minute final-check period, prepare parts and source cards ahead and use three six-minute performance stations with seven minutes for directions and transitions. Unit plans must time those versions.

A year ten school days shorter still has 150 school days, beyond this map's finish at day 140. If ten science meetings themselves are lost, fold these ten unassessed days into their named neighbours, giving 66 meetings while retaining the 76 numbered curriculum references. ## For the lead

1. **Weekly time conflicts with the calendar.** The frame describes 76 days over about 34 weeks and an approximately 36-week school year, while frames.csv and CALENDAR.md require completion in 28 weeks within 32. Two meetings for 28 weeks plus eight third meetings yield only 64, twelve short of 76. This map uses ten two-meeting weeks, sixteen three-meeting weeks and two four-meeting weeks, 26 and 27. After the fixed week-17 check, 35 remaining days in eleven weeks cannot fit even three meetings every week. Keep the unit counts and check weeks pending a lead decision on weekly time or a revised calendar; the sequence cannot settle that scope/time choice. The first unit does finish in week 4 rather than the calendar snapshot's week 5.
2. **Spring availability and supplies need verification in unit planning.** The earlier calendar places seeds in weeks 24 to 25 and habitats in weeks 26 to 27. Stored burrs, indoor plants and matched photographs keep the order usable where flowers, unfrozen ground or safe habitats are unavailable. Exact local seasons, class inventory and unit costs have not been verified. The annual allowance is unchanged; no new purchase or budget decision is made here.
