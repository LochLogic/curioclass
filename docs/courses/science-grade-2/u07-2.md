# Section 7.2: Repeating the look and explaining what we found

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-LS4-1, this section's share (days 70 to 73).** "Observe plants and animals and compare how many kinds of living things are found in different habitats." Day 70 repeats the observation at the same two spots; day 71 compares two dated inventories and answers the anchor; day 72 observes and compares on three unseen scenes, alone; day 73 repairs and compares afresh.
- **Limits that bite here.** Observed variety only, without names; describing labels are provisional. A week apart, same area, time and labels reduce but do not remove differences caused by who looked and when. An inconclusive comparison is reported as inconclusive. No habitat ranking and no "wet means more" rule. Day 72 scenes are invented for practice, said aloud, not survey results.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.D.10 (picture graph, one picture per kind), days 70 to 72. Nature of science, "look for patterns and regular order": repeat careful counts, days 70 and 71. Claim, evidence and reason, "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___" (unit 5), day 71.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) One look can be luck, so we look again the same way. (2) A second date may differ because the place changed, the weather or time of day changed, or the looking changed; keeping area, time and labels the same lets us say the looking did not; it never lets us say all kinds were found. (3) "We cannot tell" is a finished answer. (4) Day 72 is unscored; its revised, retained version is scored once after day 73 on ten components, each marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen (the unit plan's ten points). Nothing taught reappears in an item.

**Exits** (spoken, picture cards, each labeled "invented for practice" and said aloud; every child touches or draws, six rotating children give a reason, 15 seconds each). Day 72 has no exit.
- **E70.** Pip drew a flower box for five minutes on Monday and saw four kinds; on Friday Pip looked for one minute and saw two. "Touch yes or no: can we say the flower box lost two kinds?" Answer: no, Friday's look was shorter. Wrong: yes (C).
- **E71.** Cards, invented for practice: a pond edge (reed, frog, dragonfly, snail, minnow) and a sandy lot (lizard, thistle). Pip says these two cards show that wet places always have more kinds. "Is Pip right? Say yes or no." Answer: no, two cards show only two places. Wrong: yes (D).
- **E73a.** The unit plan's park picture (three grass tufts, two ants), invented for practice. Pip counted five kinds. "Touch the right number: two, three or five." Answer: two. Wrong: five (A); three (A).
- **E73b.** The ditch picture (two reed clumps, three frogs). Pip says the park and the ditch show the same kinds because both have two. "Touch yes or no." Answer: no. Wrong: yes (B).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The habitat picture model and the question board from section 7.1, plus every child's day 66 to 69 inventory sheets and graphs. Day 70 adds a second dated row for each spot. Day 71 turns the two dates into a class claim with limits (what was seen both dates, what on one, what we cannot tell) and answers the anchor. Day 72 uses the blank model layout on new scenes; day 73 revises it once more. The teacher holds the class sheet; each child holds their dated sheets, the day 72 record and the revised version.
- **Key moment 1, day 70: the same look again.** Ideal instrument: the same patches at the same size and time of day. Spec: the day 66 rope loops, five minutes each by the wall clock (a pace cue), pencil and inventory sheet fit; the step is the whole minute. Same area, time, labels and, where the class can, time of day, are said aloud as "keep the same" (fair test).
- **Key moment 2, day 72: an unaided inventory.** Ideal instrument: living habitats. Spec: three printed scenes per child (meadow, stream edge, marsh) and the inventory and graph template fit; every kind is pictured whole and identical across scenes. The scenes stay withheld until day 72.
- **When a reading fails.** Unsafe or frozen site, or an allergy: the two-date schoolyard photographs, same framing both dates, said aloud to be weaker. If spots were mown or trampled, the record says so and the comparison is reported as inconclusive. An invented pair stands in only for the exit.
- **Counted equipment.** As day 66: twelve rope loops, thirty boards. Day 72: thirty printed sets, no sharing.
- **Clean-up.** Day 70 as on day 66: boards collected 1 minute, clothing check in pairs in the line, two sinks, about 4 minutes in all. Days 71 to 73: papers into folders, 2 minutes.
- **A room with nothing.** The photographs and paper. Day 72 needs only the printed scenes. No tool is used; the section has no key tool.

## 6. Days

## Day 70


- **3 min. Recall.** Day 66 sheets are held up. The class says what to keep the same: patch size, five minutes, the labels (review of fair test: keep, change, watch). Pip says "use a bigger loop this time" and the class tests the line.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** E69 (A, B): 2.5 minutes a letter with the teacher, the rest lay out their day 66 graphs.
- **4 min. Prepare and move.** Closed shoes, the four rules, the whole class walks together; an adult counts heads.
- **10 min. Survey.** The same rope loops, groups and spots, five minutes each. Each child draws every living thing on a new inventory sheet. Nothing is touched or collected.
- **9 min. Compare.** Each child circles repeats and draws one picture per kind on a second graph template, using day 67 labels (5 min). Groups lay it beside the day 66 graph and mark kinds on both dates and on one only (4 min).
- **3 min. Model.** The class sheet gets a dated second row for each spot, marked "seen again" or "seen this date only".
- **2 min. Exit.** E70.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class sheet, each child both dated sheets. Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: E70; both graphs, marked on the record sheet.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice repeats both spot surveys a week later and compares two graphs; the exit asks, for a new case of Pip's flower box on Monday and Friday, what Pip should keep the same, which the practice shows only to the class.

## Day 71


- **3 min. Return and repair.** E70 (C): the flap card; whole class if many, otherwise a small group.
- **5 min. Read the records.** Groups read their two dated graphs per spot aloud: counts, kinds on both dates, kinds on one date. Six rotating children say one thing the records show, 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **10 min. Compare.** Groups find shared kinds, kinds on one date only, and whether the counts match, and say a reason a kind may be missing: it was hiding, or the place changed, or we looked differently. If a pair of counts is equal, the groups touch whether the kinds match.
- **12 min. Anchor answer.** Opens with the wet and dry pair for D (3 min, whole class). Then the class dictates and the teacher writes the answer to the compelling question: which kinds were shared, which differed and what the repeated counts support, with "we cannot tell" where the records do not decide (5 min). Each child writes or draws "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___" (4 min).
- **6 min. Revise the model.** The class sheet and question board get the answer and its limits: what the two looks show, and not every kind.
- **2 min. Exit.** E71.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Sheets into folders.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class sheet, each child their strip. Tool: None. By hand: writing, drawing. Evidence: E71; the strip, marked on the record sheet.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice reads dated graphs and counts kinds on a wet and a dry pair with equal counts; the exit shows two new cards where the wet place has more, and asks whether two cards can show always.

## Day 72


- **3 min. Directions.** The teacher reads the prompts aloud without explaining. Every child has the meadow, stream-edge and marsh scenes and the inventory and graph template (a page under 40 words of labels). The scenes say "invented for practice". Papers are on the table; nothing is shared or discussed.
- **5 min. Item 1.** "Mark land and water features in these scenes. Show how you will look for and record both plant and animal kinds."
- **15 min. Item 2.** "Make each picture inventory and a graph with one picture per kind." Plant and animal inventories, each kind once, the graph.
- **10 min. Item 3.** "Compare all three counts, show a shared and a different kind, and explain the equal-count pair."
- **2 min. Item 4.** "Add one evidence-based comparison to your new habitat model and mark whether these pictures show every kind living there." Papers are collected.
- Holds what is carried: each child their record; the teacher the key and the unit plan's accept lists. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, labels, graph. Evidence: items 1 to 4, unscored today; written component feedback goes out after class (60 minutes of teacher time, ours, untested). Pointing and dictated answers are accepted; a scribe writes what the child says. Scientific names earn nothing.

## Day 73


- **8 min. Read feedback and revise.** Each child gets their day 72 record with written component feedback, read aloud or heard (3 min), revises one thing in pencil on the same page with the original kept attached (3 min); six rotating children say their change, 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **12 min. Repair by letter groups.** Hand-backs: the day 72 record (A, B, C) and D from the day 69 and 71 notebooks. A, B, C and D each meet the teacher for 3 minutes (2.5 plus a move) while the rest work with a partner from written directions. A: circle repeats on a card, count circles. B: two equal-count cards, touch the members that differ. C: a flap card, first view none, second view one. D: an invented equal-count wet and dry pair, a wet rain garden and a dry gravel lot, three kinds each. One child follows the same steps with an adult.
- **7 min. Fresh comparison.** Two cards, invented for practice: a hedge row (thorn bush, berry vine, finch, beetle) and a sand dune (beach plant, beetle, dune mouse). Each child draws both inventories and one graph (4 min); six rotating children say a shared and a different kind, 30 seconds each (3 min). Unscored.
- **3 min. Consensus.** The class revises the schoolyard model once more: count kinds once; equal counts can hide differences; a kind not seen may be hiding; places differ and wet is not always more.
- **3 min. Exit.** E73a and E73b.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Papers into folders; cards counted.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the consensus sheet, each child the revised record. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: E73 and the groups' repairs; after class the revised version is scored once (45 minutes of teacher time, ours, untested), the original attached so original performance and response gains stay separate.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice is feedback reading, repair by letter group and a fresh hedge and dune comparison drawn by the child; the exit gives new park and ditch pictures and asks the right number of kinds, then whether the places show the same kinds.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 70: "What are we keeping the same, and why?" Day 71: "What can our two looks tell us, and what can they not?" Day 73: "What changed when you looked again at your own record?"
- **Written task, day 71.** The strip: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___", with the class answer copied or drawn; no spelling counts.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors**, marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen: kinds counted once; a shared or different kind named from the records; a limit named ("we cannot tell", "other kinds may be hiding"); no ranking of places. Practice on day 71; the day 72 record is judged by the unit plan's ten components.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 70:** section 7.1 labels and graphs, fair test (keep the same). **Day 71:** claim, evidence and reason (unit 5), the picture graph and the E69 letters. **Day 72:** nothing is reviewed; it is the unaided record. **Day 73:** the day 72 letters, the equal-count and kinds-once ideas.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: kind, same, different, again, hiding, plant, animal, count. Frame: "Both days we saw ___. Only on ___ we saw ___." Pointing and dictated reasons are accepted.
- **Access.** Raised outlines, enlarged high-contrast scenes, pointing, a scribe, and an indoor photograph path for any child. The adult supplies access and never selects kinds or comparisons.
- **Check accommodations.** Prompts and feedback are read aloud without adding content; answers are drawn, pointed or dictated.
- **Reading load.** None alone: labels only, under 40 words a page, everything else read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Repeat a third look and say what it adds, or compare two sets from home photographs without a ranking.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 70: look again at the same two spots as on day 66, five minutes each, same labels, draw and compare with last week's sheet. Day 71: lay the sheets side by side and say what was seen both times, what once, and what you cannot tell. Day 72: give the three scenes, read each prompt aloud, do not explain, watch without hints. Day 73 is the last day of the unit: go over the note, redo the weakest part, then say together the answer to the question of what lives in each place and how they are alike and different.
- **One projector.** Show the cards on days 71 and 73. Day 72 is on paper at each seat; no screen. The surveys are outdoors.
