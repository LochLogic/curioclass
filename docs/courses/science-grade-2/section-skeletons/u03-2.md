| 36 | 2-PS1-1 | Investigate materials to describe them and sort them by properties you can observe, such as color, texture, hardness and bendiness. | 24 minutes of observation windows, four per group, six points. New problem: sort three unfamiliar safe samples for a classroom materials drawer. Read: "Agree how to compare and sort these samples. Each show a proposed test and what stays steady; do your test and record what the group finds. Use the cooler and warmer water evidence too." Each child proposes and performs one gentle test. Share colour, texture, press, bend and light observations, then individually mark a picture chart, sort by a chosen property and show a shared-property pattern. Adult-prepared sealed ice and lukewarm water give qualitative conditions and solid-liquid evidence. Answer depends on inspected samples: credit observations that match them, a consistent sorting rule, and ice as solid, warmer water as liquid. Wrong: rough means hard without pressing (A); one colour makes samples identical in all properties (B); ice is not water (C); change both lamp distance and sample to compare light passage (D). | section 3.2 |
| 37 | 2-PS1-2 | Use test results from different materials to decide which material's properties best fit a given job. | 25 minutes, fourteen points. New packet, "invented for practice": equal-sized card X, sheet Y and cloth Z were tested under matched conditions. Water climbed 3, 0 and 6 centimeters respectively; equally loaded strips bent 1, 5 and 2 centimeters; gentle pressing left no dent in X but dents in Y and Z. X and Z feel rough; Y feels smooth. On the same pictured sloping surface, with the same block and slope, X and Z linings held the block still; Y let it slip. Y and Z wrap a curve without cracking; X cracks. "Organize these records by tested property. Choose for each pictured job and show the evidence: a rigid display ledge, a curved parcel wrapper, a press-resistant stamping base, a rough non-sliding lining, and a mat that draws water farthest along a strip. Use our choice model to explain why changing purpose can change a choice." Answer: X for ledge and base, Y or Z for wrapper, X or Z for lining because both prevented slipping in the matched test, Z for mat; link each to the supplied comparison. Texture alone does not establish grip on another surface. A final tree-to-wood-to-block picture asks how material knowledge helps people design an object from natural materials. Accept testing wood before choosing a block that holds a display. Wrong: Y is best for every job because it is favourite (E); Y catches most water because none climbed (F); greatest bend means stiffest ledge (G). | section 3.3 |
