# Section 6.1: Models of animals that carry seeds and pollen

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-LS2-2, this section's share (days 60 to 65).** "Build a simple model that does what an animal does when it spreads seeds or pollinates plants." Both jobs are covered: carrying a seed (days 61 to 64) and carrying pollen (days 60, 62, 65). The seed model is built on days 61 and 63 to 64; the pollen job is shown and tried on day 62 and drawn on day 65.
- **Limits that bite here.** Observable movement and structure only: no flower parts beyond source and receiver, no fertilization, no life cycle. Pollen is not a seed. Neither job applies to every plant. A model shows carrying, not growing. Pieces are large paper, card, felt and hook-and-loop squares; no live animals, real pollen, chalk or dust. Distances are whole centimeters on one 30 centimeter ruler (its step is 1 centimeter), so the walk is at most 30 centimeters.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1 (ruler zero, whole centimeters), day 61. Design cycle (define, sketch, build, test, compare), days 61, 63, 64. Structure and function, days 61, 62, 64. System model, days 60, 62, 65.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Why hooks catch the loops of fuzzy cloth, and that hook-and-loop squares do the same job: said aloud to be a model, not a real burr. (2) Pollen is the yellow powder a flower makes; a bee collects it as food, some rubs onto its body and may be left on the next flower; it is not a seed and the bee does not mean to help. (3) A jay holds an acorn (a seed) in its beak, carries it and may leave it unburied: the beak is a holding structure, so hooks are not the only way a seed travels. (4) Grade 1 people copied a part of nature; here a model copies what the animal does. (5) The word "model" has a second sense here: a thing we make that does the animal's job (the earlier sense stands for a place). (6) Scoring: ten components on the retained day 64 work; only "shown" counts. (7) Pollen is first shown on day 62, a day the calendar calls fluency; it is never scored.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: days 57 to 59 (course checks); section 5.2 hands nothing forward to day 60, and the unit plans leave no plants growing into this unit, so none are used. Readiness: retrieval only, inside day 60 (grade 1 shape and job, the design cycle) and day 61 (the ruler). Its own results go to day 65's groups and, for the model record, to year-end day 75. After: day 66, which opens needs-and-places as retrieval.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** the day 63 and day 64 rows of unit plan field 5. Every child makes one individual record across the two days: drawing, working model and trial record, stored together. Day 63 is the first sitting, written feedback follows, day 64 is the revision; the retained day 64 version is scored once on day 65 before class on ten components (the ten of unit plan field 5), all on the jay case. Each is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen; only "shown" counts. Live observation corroborates and is not scored. Bat work is unscored practice. Days 60 to 62 are never scored. No item is added.

**Exits, days 60, 61, 62 and 65** (spoken, picture cards, each labeled "invented for practice" and said aloud; every child marks, six rotating children give a reason, 15 seconds each; a "heard" column on the record sheet). Days 63 and 64 have no exit.
- **E60a.** A fly leaves one flower with yellow powder on its legs and lands on a second flower. "Touch what the fly carried: the yellow powder or a seed." Answer: the powder. Wrong: the seed (A).
- **E60b.** "Draw an arrow for where the powder can go next." Answer: an arrow from the fly to the second flower. A missing or half arrow gets descriptive feedback; no letter.
- **E61a.** A card seed keeps slipping off a fuzzy mitten model. "Touch the idea that could help it hold: more fuzzy cloth where the seed touches, or a prettier face drawn on the mitten." Answer: more fuzzy cloth. Wrong: the prettier face (B).
- **E61b.** "Say or draw one thing this mitten model cannot show." Answer: whether the seed would grow, or a real animal's walk. An omission gets descriptive feedback; no letter.
- **E62a.** Records: a hooked seed falls from bare card and stays on fuzzy cloth; a smooth seed falls from both. "Touch the sentence the records support: every seed sticks to fuzzy cloth, or only the hooked seed stuck." Answer: only the hooked seed stuck. Wrong: every seed sticks (D).
- **E62b.** "Say or draw one thing these records cannot tell us." Answer: whether the seed would grow. An omission gets descriptive feedback; no letter.
- **E65a.** A model moth carries yellow dots from one flower but the second flower's patch never touches them. "Touch the part to move so the dots can be left there: the receiving patch, the moth's wings, or swap the dots for seeds." Answer: the receiving patch. Wrong: the wings (B); swap for seeds (A).
- **E65b.** "Why did the moth go to the flower? Touch food, or to help the plant." Answer: food. Wrong: to help (C).

Check items (days 63 and 64): the jay card and task are in unit plan field 5; nothing taught is reused. The sock and flower models appear on day 63 as an unscored independent explanation, and the flower model's pollen arrow on the retrieval card (s06-E11) is the one scored pollen component.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The plant-animal carry-arrow model: sketched on day 60 and kept as a class chart held by the teacher; each child holds their own drawings. Day 61 revises structure and function from the hooked trials (the hooks and the fuzz fit the job) and adds the design tests. Day 62 revises the seed claim with the smooth-seed failures and adds source-body-receiver arrows from the pollen carry. Days 63 and 64 are independent. Day 65 keeps both routes and their limits in one class explanation. The design-cycle chart (define, sketch, build, test, compare) is pinned from day 61. Review, in the ledger's words: structure and function (the shape and material fit the job), system (parts that are linked), claim and evidence (I think ___, because I saw ___), the question board.
- **Key moment 1, day 61: a distance at the ruler.** Ideal instrument: a centimeter ruler laid beside a taped zero line. Spec: fits; the 1 centimeter step is named beside each reading. A seed that never clings is recorded 0; one that rides the whole walk is recorded "rode the whole walk, 30".
- **Key moment 2, day 62: hooked and smooth seeds side by side.** Ideal instrument: the real burr under a lens. Spec: hook-and-loop model seeds and a hand lens on the stand-in fit; stored burrs are adult-handled only, in sealed bags.
- **Key moment 3, day 62: powder moving from source to body to receiver.** Ideal instrument: a manipulable model. Spec: card flowers, a felt body and large paper dots fit. The reading is the count of dots left on the receiver (whole numbers); no tool is used.
- **When a reading fails.** The adult trials every set the day before. If a seed will not cling, or the dots will not transfer, the adult swaps the set; if it still fails, the class reads an original measured trial photograph or tally card, said aloud to be invented, and no reading is scored. Scoring needs a working build.
- **Counted equipment.** One sock model and one flower set a group, five turns each inside the part (days 61, 62); thirty individual sets on days 63 and 64, so no waiting.
- **Clean-up.** Days 61 and 62: goggles wiped with one damp cloth a table and bagged (1 minute), pieces counted back into trays (2 minutes), tables wiped (1 minute); hands are washed at the next scheduled washing, said aloud (30 children at two sinks do not fit 4 minutes). Days 63 and 64: moves and the same wipe-down inside 3 minutes.
- **A room with nothing.** No hook-and-loop squares or sock: the photographs and drawn cards for days 61 and 62. The drawing and explanation components can be shown; the two carry-and-deliver components cannot, and the record says so. No tool is used and none is spec only.

## 6. Days

## Day 60


- **5 min. Hook.** The fuzzy sock with its enclosed seed head (or hook-and-loop stand-in, said aloud to be a model) beside two flower-visit photographs. Ask the compelling question and explain nothing. Children say what they notice; the teacher writes notices as questions on the question board.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Grade 1: three coverings on a card, "mark the one that could catch a hook". Then the five design-cycle cards laid in order, one marked at a time. Accept fuzzy fibers and the stated order. On a miss, model it with the grade 1 copied-part picture and the unit 2 chart. Say aloud: here a model copies what the animal does, not its parts.
- **8 min. Look, then explain.** The teacher looks aloud with the sock and a lens (2 min). Each group has one lens: five children, 30 seconds each, the rest look at the photographs (3 min). The teacher tells it: hooks catch fibers and the seed rides; the bee goes for food; yellow powder, pollen, rubs on and may be left on the next flower; seeds and pollen differ (3 min).
- **13 min. First model.** The teacher draws one carry arrow, plant to animal to new place, on the class chart (3 min). Each child sketches both routes on a two-box sheet, placing picture parts (plant, animal, seed, pollen) (6 min). Partners point and explain, the author redraws (2 min); the chart gets two arrows and the questions go on the board (2 min).
- **3 min. Exit.** E60a and E60b.
- **2 min. Clean up.** The adult bags the specimens; hands and tables wiped with damp wipes; hands washed at the next scheduled washing, said aloud.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart, each child their sketch. Tool: None. By hand: looking, sketching. Evidence: E60, with the "heard" column.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice looks at the sock through a lens and sketches the seed and pollen routes; the exit gives a new card, a fly on a flower, and asks what the powder is and where it goes next.

## Day 61


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Error A from E60: two route cards, seed path and powder path, touch which is which. If many, whole class; otherwise a small group while the rest start the ruler card.
- **3 min. Ruler retrieval.** Each child marks where zero starts and reads the end of a 9 centimeter strip on a card; a miss sees alignment shown at the ruler.
- **4 min. Define the job.** The class chart: carry a hooked card seed from the zero line to a new place and let it go; the limit is a walk of at most 30 centimeters; walk the design-cycle chart (problem, sketch, build, test, compare).
- **7 min. Build and sketch.** Each group builds one walking model, a fuzzy sock on a card-tube leg, with a taped zero line beside the ruler (3 min), goggles on. Each child sketches it and labels what stands for fur, hook and seed with "This stands for ___. It moves ___ because ___." (4 min).
- **8 min. Trials.** Five children, one walk each of about 90 seconds: touch the seed to the sock, walk it along the mat, let it go, mark the stop, read the ruler to the whole centimeter; the group recorder writes it.
- **3 min. Record and compare.** Each child writes their own distance, draws one change to try (more fuzzy contact) and one thing the model cannot show.
- **3 min. Exit.** E61a and E61b.
- **4 min. Clean up.** As in field 5.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart and the design cycle, each child their sketch and record. Tool: None. By hand: building, measuring, sketching. Evidence: E61 and the child's record.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds the sock model and walks it, recording stuck or fell; the exit gives a sliding seed on a new mitten to fix, a paper bird to name a model limit, and a ruler picture to read.

## Day 62


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Error B from E61: a drawn-on animal that cannot hold a seed beside a plain sock that does; touch the one that moved the seed. If many, whole class; otherwise a small group.
- **6 min. Smooth beside hooked.** Guess first: each child marks stick or not (1 min). Five hooked and five smooth card seeds, the same sock and touch on a tabletop tray: each child tests one of each, 45 seconds, and the group tallies stuck of 5 (5 min).
- **4 min. Revise.** Each child adds to their day 61 sketch "not every seed sticks" and the limit; the chart gets the hooks and fuzz (structure and function). If many guessed every seed sticks (D), rerun a smooth and hooked pair at the front instead of revising.
- **9 min. Pollen, shown.** The teacher shows the large card flowers, felt body and yellow paper dots to two rows of children: source, body, receiver, twice (3 min). Each child draws the source-body-receiver arrow (3 min). The teacher lays brown seed arrows beside yellow powder arrows, "pollen is not a seed" (1 min). Pip says "the bee goes to help the flower" (C); the class checks it against the photographs (2 min).
- **6 min. Pollen, tried.** In groups, each child one carry of about a minute with the card flowers and felt body while the others draw their own arrow; each child counts the dots left on the receiver and writes the whole number.
- **3 min. Exit.** E62a and E62b.
- **4 min. Clean up.** As in field 5; dots counted back.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart, each child their drawing. Tool: None. By hand: testing, tallying, carrying, drawing. Evidence: E62, the arrow drawing and the dot count (unscored).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice tests smooth and hooked seeds itself and carries card pollen; the exit gives finished stick-or-fall records and asks which sentence they support, then what they cannot tell.

## Day 63


- **5 min. Retrieve alone.** On a card each child labels source, body and receiver on a drawing of the class flower model and marks seed or pollen on two pieces. Directions read neutrally, no hints (unscored).
- **3 min. The new card.** The jay card, labeled "invented for practice": an acorn under an oak; the same acorn between a jay's beak halves; that acorn in leaf litter away from the oak. Captions give places and parts only; the teacher says the acorn is a seed. The task is read once: "Design and build something that does this animal's plant job. Show what your parts represent and try it. Use the result to improve your model and explain its limits."
- **2 min. Sketch.** Each child sketches a design on the record.
- **10 min. Build and try.** Each child alone, goggles on, with their own labeled set: folded-card beak halves, large card acorn, paper leaf litter, plant outline, folded-card handle. Try on the tray. No hints, no reteaching.
- **12 min. Observation windows.** The first three tables (15 children), three windows of 4 minutes. Each child, about 45 seconds, shows one carry and says what the parts stand for; the teacher marks the look-fors and the "heard" column. The other 15 independently label their day 61 and 62 sock and flower models and draw one limit (unscored).
- **3 min. Moves and clean-up.** Two moves of 30 seconds, goggles wiped, tables wiped; the adult labels and stores the sets.
- Holds what is carried: each child their record. Tool: None. By hand: sketching, building. Evidence: the day 63 row of unit plan field 5. After class, 60 minutes for one written note, 12 words or fewer, on each record.

## Day 64


- **20 min. Feedback, revise, try, record.** Each child gets their record with one written note and a picture; a partner reads it, and an adult reads it aloud to any child whose record says so (3 min). Revise the drawing and the build (9 min). Try the model again and record what happened, what changed and one limit (6 min). Name each part: "This stands for ___. It moves ___ because ___." (2 min). The teacher moves among tables and does not teach.
- **12 min. Observation windows.** The remaining three tables (15 children), three windows of 4 minutes as on day 63. The 15 seen on day 63 finish their labeled accounts alone.
- **3 min. Moves and clean-up.** As day 63. The adult bags each child's model with its drawing and record, to be scored together.
- Holds what is carried: each child their record. Tool: None. By hand: revising, building, writing or scribing. Evidence: the day 64 row of unit plan field 5. Score the retained versions before class on day 65 (60 minutes); no reteaching today.

## Day 65


- **5 min. Scored work returned.** Each child gets their model and record with one written next step; read or heard (3 min); six rotating children say theirs back, 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **12 min. Repair by letter groups.** A, B, C, D each with the teacher for 2.5 minutes in turn while the others work with a partner from picture directions; 2 minutes to move. Hand-backs: the day 63 and 64 records (A, B, C, D) and E62 (D). A: two route cards, label seed and powder apart, revise the labels. B: test whether the model moves a card between places, then repair it. C: trace a nectar visit and the powder left on the way. D: smooth and hooked on the same cloth, limit the every-seed claim. One child follows the same steps with an adult.
- **9 min. Fresh practice.** The bat card, labeled "invented for practice", with pollen, nectar and receiver named and no arrows. Each child draws the route and labels what moves and which part makes it possible; nothing is built (6 min). Six rotating children say it, 30 seconds each (3 min). Unscored.
- **4 min. Consensus.** The class dictates and the teacher writes, citing days 60 to 62 and 64: hooks catch fibers and carry seeds away; a jay carries a seed in its beak; bees visit for food and powder that sticks may be left on the next flower; pollen is not a seed; not every plant needs an animal for either job; models show carrying, not growing.
- **3 min. Exit.** E65a and E65b.
- **2 min. Clean up.** Pieces counted into bags, tables wiped, models stored.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the consensus chart, each child their next step. Tool: None. By hand: repairs, drawing. Evidence: E65 and the groups' repairs.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice is repair by letter group and a fresh bat card drawn by the child; the exit gives a new model, a moth carrying dots, and asks which part to move and why the moth went to the flower.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 60, "What do you think caught on the sock?" Day 61, "What caught, and what moved?" Day 62, "What does your model leave out?"
- **Written task, day 64.** Each child labels the drawing with "This stands for ___. It moves ___ because ___." and records the trial; a scribe or dictation is allowed.
- **Judged by the teacher with ten look-fors** (unit plan field 5), marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen: the animal part, the plant part, the carried material, the drawing tied to the model, a route that starts at the plant, a carry, a delivery, beak structure tied to holding and releasing, a revision or a reason to keep, a named limit.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 60:** grade 1 shape and job; the design cycle. **Day 61:** the ruler; the day 60 sketch. **Day 62:** the day 61 trial and sketch. **Day 63:** days 60 to 62 only, as retrieval. **Day 64:** the day 63 record. **Day 65:** the letters from days 61 to 64. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none. Day 62 repeats the same test on a new pair so ease comes from repetition, with no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with objects: seed, pollen, hook, loop, carry, drop, model, flower, beak. Frame: "This stands for ___. It moves ___ because ___." Pointing and a drawn label are accepted.
- **Access.** Large tactile parts, high-contrast pictures, pointing, a scribe, and a child directing an adult's handling while supplying the design and the revisions; the adult records the adaptation. No child needs a device.
- **Check accommodations.** All prompts and feedback are read aloud without adding content; answers are drawn, pointed or dictated.
- **Reading load.** None alone: labels only, a page under 150 words, a note of 12 words or fewer with a picture.
- **Extension, same standard.** Change one surface or one part of the model and predict what it will carry and what it will drop.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 60: look at the sock and photographs, sketch both routes. Day 61: build the sock model and measure one walk. Day 62: test a smooth and a hooked seed, then carry the powder from flower to flower. Days 63 and 64: build and try the jay model while you watch and listen, give no hint, then read the note and revise. Day 65 is the last day of the unit: go over the note, redo the weakest piece, then say together how the burrs got on the sock and why the bees visit flower after flower. At home, any day skippable, a grown-up asks the exit aloud. No supplies are needed.
- **One projector.** Show the photographs on day 60, the cards on days 62 and 65 and the chart on day 65; the sock, seeds and card flowers are on tables. Windows on days 63 and 64 run table by table.
