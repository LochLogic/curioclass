# Section 3.1: Describe and test materials

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-PS1-1, this section's share (days 27 to 30).** "Investigate materials to describe them and sort them by properties you can observe, such as color, texture, hardness and bendiness." Day 27 names the properties and plans the tests; day 28 tests color, texture, hardness, flexibility and whether light passes through, then sorts; day 29 observes water at two temperatures; day 30 adds water travel. The sorting is checked on day 36 (section 3.2).
- **2-PS1-2, this section's share (days 30, 31).** "Use test results from different materials to decide which material's properties best fit a given job." Day 30 tests two things a hat must do; day 31 makes the first choice from a graph.
- **Limits that bite here.** Observable properties only: no particles, density, heat insulation or chemical make-up. The only measurement is length, to the whole centimeter (the ruler's step); a difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same" (grade 2 unit 2). Water climbing a strip compares travel under matched conditions, not how much a material can hold. Warmer and cooler are words only; an adult prepares the sealed ice and lukewarm cups. Glass is shown in photographs only. Nothing is tasted.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.D.10, day 31 (a bar graph, one square one centimeter, review). Writing W.2.7, days 28 to 30 (one framed page of the class sorting book for each group; the teacher scribes).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A property is something we can see, feel or test; color tells color and nothing else. (2) Rough is how a surface feels; hard is whether it dents under a gentle press. (3) Ice is water in its solid form and the same water flows when warmer, so a property can depend on temperature. (4) Water climbing a strip and a doll staying dry are two questions; a good hat keeps water out. (5) What a 1-centimeter ruler can and cannot show. (6) Which six children are heard each day.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: unit 2 ends on day 26 and hands nothing forward, so day 27 opens clean. Its two retrieval tasks (day 27, five minutes; day 29, three minutes) are the unit's before-teaching evidence, and a miss is answered inside those parts. After: section 3.2 opens on day 32 with the day 30 and 31 records and the day 31 exit slips (error H); day 32 has a "Return and repair" part for them.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. The day 36 item belongs to section 3.2 and the day 37 item to 3.3 (unit plan field 5). Here the evidence is one exit on each teaching day.

**Evidence form.** Spoken, drawn or pointed to. Each exit is one ask, read aloud, after the teacher says "this is invented for practice". The teacher hears a rotating six, 20 seconds each, and the record sheet has a "heard" column. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" part opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E27.** Cards: a blue felt square and a blue foam square side by side. "Pip says: they are both blue, so they feel and bend the same. Is Pip right? Say yes or no." Answer: no; color tells only color, so we test the feel and the bend. Wrong: yes (B).
- **E28.** Cards: plan 1 keeps the flashlight on a stack of books and swaps two new sheets in front of it; plan 2 moves the flashlight closer for the second sheet. "We want to know which sheet lets more light through. Touch the fair plan." Answer: plan 1. Wrong: plan 2 (D).
- **E29.** Cards: a puddle in the morning with a thin skin of ice, and the same puddle at noon, flowing. "Pip says the ice in the puddle is not water. Is Pip right? Say yes or no." Answer: no; ice is water that is solid, and at noon the same water flows. Wrong: yes (C).
- **E30.** Cards: two new strips after a dip in a cup; on K no water climbed above the cup line, on L it climbed to the 9 line. "Touch the strip that took water in." Answer: L. Wrong: K (F).
- **E31a.** Blank labeled axes and a 1-centimeter grid. "Strip M and strip N both had water climb 4 centimeters. Draw the two bars." Answer: two bars of equal height at 4. Wrong: none listed; a slip in drawing gets a note on the drawing, not a letter.
- **E31b.** Cards: under hat M the doll stayed dry; under hat N it got wet. "Touch the hat to use." Answer: M, because the doll stayed dry although the water climbed the same. Wrong: N, "it takes water in, so it is best" (H); either, "the bars are the same" (H).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Day 27's first-choice drawing keeps unit 2's test-and-compare cycle (the design-cycle chart, review) and is held by each child until day 35. The pattern chart gains shared-property groups on day 28 and, on day 29, "a material's properties can depend on how warm it is." The question board starts with "What properties can we observe?" The choice model (job, the property we tested, what the test showed, our choice) is drawn on day 31 and revised from the dry and wet results. Structure and function: on day 31 the line "the shape fits the job" gains "and so does the material."
- **Key moment 1, day 28: a property shown by a test.** Ideal instrument: a sample kit with a blunt stick, a flashlight held still on a stack and a paper screen. Spec: none needed; paper and household items fit. Instrument step: dent or no dent, mark or none, bends or stays stiff, light on the screen or not; no numbers.
- **Key moment 2, day 30: water travel read to the whole centimeter.** Ideal instrument: equal strips standing in a shallow tray with ruler zero at the water line, plus a dry paper tab under each hat piece. Spec: none needed; fits. Instrument step: 1 centimeter, so 1 centimeter is "about the same"; a strip wet to the top reads "to the top". If no strip climbs by the reading, the class says so aloud and uses printed cards, said aloud to be invented (towel 9, wool 8, fabric 5, foil, plastic and rubber none; hat tabs: towel wet, wool and fabric damp, foil, plastic and rubber dry). A cold room or a failed pour is handled the same way.
- **Key moment 3, day 29: water at two temperatures.** Ideal instrument: sealed cups tilted by hand. Spec: none needed. Temperature is not measured. Keep spare sealed cups on a cold pack; if a seal leaks or the ice has melted, swap it. With nothing, use a photograph pair, said aloud to be a picture.
- **A room with nothing.** Photographs of the tests and matching observation sheets for each day. The path is weaker: it cannot show that children conducted a test. Record the gap; never credit an action from paper alone.

## 6. Days

## Day 27


- **7 min. Hook.** The tray from the window: a paper hat and a soggy doll, dampened by the teacher the day before. Children look, touch the hat with one finger and say what happened. Then the compelling question, asked aloud and left open: "Why are rain boots made of rubber and mittens of wool, and not the other way round?" No answer is corrected today.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** Read aloud, answered on one printed card by marking: which light-test picture keeps the lamp in one place; a sketch of a shape that sheds water; the longer bar on a daylight chart; one test-and-improve step from the unit 2 path work. A miss: the teacher shows the same comparison on a second picture before the next part.
- **10 min. Look, touch, name.** Each group has a tray of the six sample types (wood, dull metal, plastic, rubber, fabric, paper; blunt edges, new fabric and paper only, no glass or sharp metal). Children touch gently and sort by what they notice. The teacher names the idea: a property is something we can see or feel about a material, and a test can show more. Words with pictures: color, rough, smooth, hard, soft, bends, stiff. Pip says, one line: "These two are both blue, so they are the same all over." The class lays them side by side (error B). Pip says: "Rubber is my favourite, so make everything from rubber." The class says the job decides what to test (error E).
- **8 min. First choice.** Each child sketches a replacement hat for the doll and chooses a material, labeled by dictation. It is kept, unscored, for day 35.
- **4 min. Question board.** Groups add what they wonder; the first heading goes up: What properties can we observe? The class picks the tests for day 28: press, gentle scratch, bend, light through.
- **2 min. Pack up.** Trays closed; the doll tray wiped at once.
- **3 min. Exit.** E27.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the question board and the property-word chart; each child their first-choice drawing. Tool: None. By hand: touching, sorting, sketching. Evidence: E27. Hear six rotating children during the first-choice sketch.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice touches and sorts samples and draws a first hat; the exit shows a sponge and a board and asks which test card tells which is softer, a choice of test the practice never asks.

## Day 28


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error B from E27, at most 5 minutes: a blue felt square and a blue foam square (invented for practice), pressed and bent side by side.
- **4 min. Model the tests.** The teacher shows each test once, slowly, on one sample: press (blunt stick, gentle, below faces: dent or no dent), gentle scratch (a mark or none), bend (a gentle bend, never a stretch of rubber), light (the flashlight taped on a stack of books so it stays put, a paper screen behind the sample, never aimed at eyes). Rough is shown beside hard: a rough fabric dents, a smooth wooden block does not (error A). Light words are review: lets light through, lets some through, blocks light. Glass appears on a photograph card only.
- **12 min. Test.** Six groups of five, each with its own kit: six samples, one blunt stick, one flashlight on its stack, one chart. Two passes of 6 minutes. In pass one each child owns one property (color, texture, hardness, flexibility, light) for all six samples, about 1 minute a sample; in pass two each swaps to a different property. The child with the stick and the child with the flashlight work at the same time. Impact eye protection stays on.
- **4 min. Record.** Each child marks their tests on the group's sorting chart, one column for each property.
- **6 min. Sort.** Groups sort the six samples by one property they choose and find one property that two different materials share (a pattern). The teacher hears six rotating children, 30 seconds each: "These are alike in ___ but different in ___."
- **3 min. Book page.** Each group fills one framed page of the class sorting book: a picture, a label and one sentence the teacher scribes.
- **3 min. Pack up.** Samples counted back; sticks and flashlights returned.
- **3 min. Exit.** E28.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the pattern chart; each group its chart. Tool: None. By hand: press, scratch, bend, light, sort. Evidence: E28. Fair test is review (grade 1 unit 5, grade 2 unit 2).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice presses, scratches and bends six samples and sorts them; the exit asks the child to touch the fair plan for a new light test and sort two samples from a new chart.

## Day 29


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error D from E28, at most 5 minutes: the two light-test plans on cards (invented for practice); the class tapes the lamp position on a picture and changes only the sheet.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Unit 1 water states: mark solid water and liquid water in unfamiliar pond pictures and show what differs. A miss: the teacher compares a second picture pair.
- **4 min. Hook.** The teacher holds a sealed cup of ice and a sealed cup of lukewarm water (prepared by an adult before class). "Are these the same stuff?" Pip says: "The ice is not water." The class waits for evidence (error C).
- **12 min. Observe.** Each group has a tray with one sealed ice cup and one sealed lukewarm cup. Each child tilts each cup once and holds the ice cup for 20 seconds, goggles on, never opened or tasted: five children, two cups, about 3.5 minutes of handling, and the rest is looking and talking. The group draws on the observation chart: keeps its shape, or flows to fit the cup.
- **6 min. Classify.** Groups mark each cup solid or liquid. The class pattern chart gains: the same water has different properties when cooler and when warmer. Warmer and cooler are words only.
- **3 min. Shared record.** One sentence a group, scribed on the class record: "The ice ___ but the warmer water ___."
- **4 min. Clear trays.** Condensation wiped with the cloth, cups into the return tray, damp wipes for hands.
- **3 min. Exit.** E29.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the pattern chart; each group its observation chart. Tool: None. By hand: tilting, drawing. Evidence: E29. Hear six rotating children during the classify part.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice tilts sealed cups and marks each solid or liquid; the exit gives a similar claim on a new case, a puddle that is iced in the morning and flowing at noon, and asks yes or no.

## Day 30


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error C from E29, at most 5 minutes: one cup of water on a windowsill in three pictures (frozen, melting, flowing; invented for practice); the class says what stayed the same.
- **3 min. Plan.** Ruler zero and whole centimeters are review (grade 2 unit 2): zero sits on the water line. Equal start: same strip size, same water in the tray, same pour. We change only the material and watch two things, how far the water climbs and whether the paper tab under a hat piece stays dry. Climbing and keeping dry are different questions.
- **4 min. Set the strips.** Goggles on. Each group has one shallow tray filled to a marked line from one equal cup by an adult, and six equal strips (paper towel, fabric, wool or a substitute fabric, foil with dull edges, plastic, rubber) standing with their ends at the line.
- **11 min. Hat test.** While the strips climb. Each group tests three hat pieces on dry paper tabs (groups 1, 3 and 5: towel, fabric, foil; groups 2, 4 and 6: wool or its substitute, plastic, rubber). One child pours the group's cup slowly over each piece, three pours by three children; the other two lift the piece and mark the tab dry, damp or wet. Spills are wiped at once.
- **6 min. Read the strips.** After about 10 minutes of climbing, one reader a group reads each strip to the whole centimeter; a strip wet to the top is recorded "to the top". The teacher writes every group's numbers on the class record chart (W.2.7 shared test record).
- **3 min. Compare.** The class sees the two lists side by side: which strips took water in, and which hats kept the tab dry.
- **5 min. Dry tables and clean up.** Two waves of three groups: one wave wipes tables and its goggles (wiped and bagged) while the other washes hands at two sinks, 20 seconds a child; then they swap (review of grade 2 unit 1 clean-up).
- **3 min. Exit.** E30.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the record chart; each group its strips and tabs record. Tool: None. By hand: pouring, reading a ruler, marking. Evidence: E30. Hear six rotating children during the compare part. Wool irritation: any child may use the substitute fabric.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice pours over three pieces and reads its own strips; the exit gives two new strips with rulers and asks which took water in and how far the water climbed.

## Day 31


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error F from E30, at most 5 minutes: two pictured strips, one with water inside and one with water pooled outside (invented for practice); children trace where the water went.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Picture bar graphs from math: which bar is longer, and by how many squares (review of grade 2 unit 2 day 19). A miss: the teacher counts squares aloud on a second graph.
- **12 min. Graph together.** On squared paper, one square is one centimeter and the bottom line is ruler zero (review). The teacher draws the first bar from the class record chart; pairs draw the other five and label them. The hat results sit beside the bars as dry, damp or wet.
- **8 min. Choose.** Pairs fill the choice model drawn on the board: job (keep the doll dry), property we tested (water climb, dry tab), what the test showed, our choice. The class revises its structure-function line: the material, as well as the shape, fits the job. A hat that takes water in is not the same as a hat that keeps the doll dry (error H).
- **4 min. Question board.** The heading "Would a different job change our choice?" goes up; children name jobs.
- **3 min. Say it.** Six rotating children, 30 seconds each: "I choose ___ because ___."
- **3 min. Exit.** E31a, then E31b.
- **2 min. Hand in.** Graph, records and slips into the folder for day 32.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the choice model; each pair their graph. Tool: None. By hand: graphing, marking. Evidence: E31a, E31b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the class bars from its record and fills the choice model; the exit gives two equal climbs to draw on a blank grid, and new hat cards to choose from.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 27, "Why would a boot and a mitten want different things?" (open; ideas only). Day 28, "What is alike about two different materials?" Day 30, "The water climbed this strip. Did it keep the paper dry? Are those the same question?" Day 31, "Which material makes the hat, and what shows it?"
- **Written task, day 31.** A frame, drawn or scribed: "I choose ___ for the hat because the paper stayed ___ and the water climbed ___."
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** uses the dry or wet result, not liking; names the property tested; says water climbing alone does not decide.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 27 retrieves grade 1 light tests, sketching a shape, a daylight chart and unit 2's test-and-improve step. Day 28 returns the fair test. Day 29 returns unit 1's solid and liquid water. Day 30 returns ruler zero, equal start and whole centimeters. Day 31 returns the picture bar graph and strengths and weaknesses (unit 2).
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: rough, smooth, hard, soft, bends, stiff, dent, solid, liquid, climb, dry, wet. Frames: "It is ___ but that one is ___." "The water climbed ___ centimeters." "The ice ___ and the warm water ___." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** Tactile samples for a child who cannot see; a neutral adult description of the light patch; a helper presses while the child decides; a child sensitive to cold uses a mitt on the ice cup; a scribe; nobody is scored on spelling or drawing.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit is pointed or spoken; accept large print, a scribe or an oral answer.
- **Reading load.** None to read alone. Each student page stays under 150 words (labels, boxes, a few words); everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Sort the six samples by two properties at once on a grid and name a pattern.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a plastic lid, a cloth, a paper towel, a foil sheet with rounded edges and a rubber band or glove piece (clean, new). Press with the blunt end of a spoon, bend, shine a lamp through, then stand strips in a shallow dish of water and read a ruler. Use a cold cup and a sealed ice bag for day 29. Read each exit aloud and have the child point.
- **One projector.** Show the class record chart and the graph; the rest is on paper and the table.
