# Section 3.1: Describe and test materials

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 2. Unit 3, Materials and their best uses. *Why are rain boots made of rubber and mittens of wool, and not the other way round?* Design question: which material makes the best rain hat for the class's paper doll? Anchor: the class's paper doll, left by the window in a drizzle in a paper hat, comes back soggy, and the class tests rubber, wool, foil, cloth and plastic to find which hat keeps it dry (days 27 to 38): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 27 to 31.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (27, 29), application 3 (28, 30, 31). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 35 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->

- **2-PS1-2** (days 30, 31): Use test results from different materials to decide which material's properties best fit a given job.

- **2-PS1-1, this section's share (days 27 to 30).** "Investigate materials to describe them and sort them by properties you can observe, such as color, texture, hardness and bendiness." Day 27 names the properties and plans the tests; day 28 tests colour, texture, hardness, flexibility and whether light passes through, then sorts; day 29 observes water at two temperatures; day 30 adds water travel. The sorting is checked on day 36 (section 3.2).
- **2-PS1-2, this section's share (days 30, 31).** "Use test results from different materials to decide which material's properties best fit a given job." Day 30 tests two things a hat must do; day 31 makes the first choice from a graph.
- **Limits that bite here.** Observable properties only: no particles, density, heat insulation or chemical make-up. The only measurement is length, to the whole centimeter (the ruler's step); a difference of 1 centimeter is "about the same" (grade 2 unit 2). Water climbing a strip compares travel under matched conditions, not how much a material can hold. Warmer and cooler are words only; an adult prepares the sealed ice and lukewarm cups. Glass is shown in photographs only. Nothing is tasted.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.D.10, day 31 (a bar graph, one square one centimeter, review). Writing W.2.7, days 28 to 30 (one framed page of the class sorting book for each group; the teacher scribes).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A property is something we can see, feel or test; colour tells colour and nothing else. (2) Rough is how a surface feels; hard is whether it dents under a gentle press. (3) Ice is water in its solid form and the same water flows when warmer, so a property can depend on temperature. (4) Water climbing a strip and a doll staying dry are two questions; a good hat keeps water out. (5) What a 1-centimeter ruler can and cannot show. (6) Which six children are heard each day.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error B from E27, at most 5 minutes: a blue felt square and a blue foam square (invented for practice), pressed and bent side by side.
- **4 min. Model the tests.** The teacher shows each test once, slowly, on one sample: press (blunt stick, gentle, below faces: dent or no dent), gentle scratch (a mark or none), bend (a gentle bend, never a stretch of rubber), light (the flashlight taped on a stack of books so it stays put, a paper screen behind the sample, never aimed at eyes). ## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 27, "Why would a boot and a mitten want different things?" (open; ideas only). Day 28, "What is alike about two different materials?" Day 30, "The water climbed this strip. Did it keep the paper dry? Are those the same question?" Day 31, "Which material makes the hat, and what shows it?"
- **Written task, day 31.** A frame, drawn or scribed: "I choose ___ for the hat because the paper stayed ___ and the water climbed ___."
- **Judged by the teacher with three look-fors:** uses the dry or wet result, not liking; names the property tested; says water climbing alone does not decide.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 27 retrieves grade 1 light tests, sketching a shape, a daylight chart and unit 2's test-and-improve step. Day 28 returns the fair test. Day 29 returns unit 1's solid and liquid water. Day 30 returns ruler zero, equal start and whole centimeters. Day 31 returns the picture bar graph and strengths and weaknesses (unit 2).
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: rough, smooth, hard, soft, bends, stiff, dent, solid, liquid, climb, dry, wet. Frames: "It is ___ but that one is ___." "The water climbed ___ centimeters." "The ice ___ and the warm water ___." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** Tactile samples for a child who cannot see; a neutral adult description of the light patch; a helper presses while the child decides; a child sensitive to cold uses a mitt on the ice cup; a scribe; nobody is scored on spelling or drawing.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit is pointed or spoken; accept large print, a scribe or an oral answer.
- **Reading load.** None to read alone. Each student page stays under 150 words (labels, boxes, a few words); everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Sort the six samples by two properties at once on a grid and name a pattern.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a plastic lid, a cloth, a paper towel, a foil sheet with rounded edges and a rubber band or glove piece (clean, new). Press with the blunt end of a spoon, bend, shine a lamp through, then stand strips in a shallow dish of water and read a ruler. Use a cold cup and a sealed ice bag for day 29. Read each exit aloud and have the child point.
- **One projector.** Show the class record chart and the graph; the rest is on paper and the table.
