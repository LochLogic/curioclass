# Section 1.1: Where runoff travels

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-ESS2-2, this section's share.** "Make a model showing the landforms and bodies of water in a place." The place is the schoolyard. Children make a sketch of runoff (day 1), a sand-tray model (day 2) and a picture map (day 4), and revise the first from evidence (day 3). Landforms are hills, valleys and hollows (a hollow is a low place); bodies of water are puddles, a river, a lake and the ocean, the last three on picture pieces (day 3).
- **Limits that bite here.** No numerical scale. A model shows chosen features only. A water arrow follows a route the class saw, in a pour or a photograph, never a route guessed from the page. No water cycle, no amounts, no soaking in. Rivers run downhill to lakes or the ocean; not every river reaches the ocean.
- **Connections.** Social studies grade 1 landform words and map key (retrieval day 1, used days 2 and 4). The social studies grade 2 world-features unit (about week 6) uses this model, so the key stays plain.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Runoff is rain that does not soak in and runs over the ground. It follows the slope: water moves toward lower ground and collects in low places. "Down" on a model means lower, not the bottom of the page. A sand tray soaks in more water than pavement; say so, and keep the claim to routes. A model is useful because it leaves things out: ask what it shows and what it leaves out. The arrow here means "the water went this way". Earlier grades used arrows for cause and effect and for evidence, so say which kind each time.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: the start of the unit. No readiness check; the unit plan uses retrieval only. The day 1 retrieval of landform words and the map key is the only before-teaching evidence; the teacher notes who needed the symbol shown, and those children sit beside a partner on day 2. This section's exit results go to the next day's Return and repair; day 4's go to day 5 (section 1.2).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Days 1 to 4 are practice and nothing is scored; the day 8 and day 9 items belong to sections 1.2 and 1.3.

**Evidence form.** Each exit is asked aloud, one thing at a time, on a picture "invented for practice" (said aloud). Every child marks paper; a rotating six are heard for 30 seconds each during the day's work part, with a "heard" column naming who. One scale on every record: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. No exit is scored. Printed words on a child's page stay under 150.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. A slip with no letter is recorded in plain words.
- **E1 (day 1).** Two pictures of a rock slope with a dry hollow at its foot, then the same slope with the hollow full and wet streaks on the rock; no arrows. "Draw the way the water went from the slope to the hollow." Answer: a route drawn down the slope ending in the hollow, with the hollow shown holding water. Wrong: the route runs up the slope (E); only the full hollow is drawn, no slope (D).
- **E2 (day 2).** A tray picture with two hills, a valley pool and an arrow drawn from the pool up a hill, beside a pour record card showing the water ran from the hilltop down to the pool. "Draw this tray the way we draw ours, with the arrow fixed from the record." Answer: two hills, the valley pool, the arrow running downhill, and the key box filled. Wrong: the arrow still points uphill (E); the pool alone, hills left out (D); no key box (B).
- **E3 (day 3).** A model with two hills, a river and the ocean but no hollow, and a three-picture card: rain collects in a hollow between the hills, the water rises, then runs over the low edge into the river. "Change the model to match the new pictures." Answer: a hollow added between the hills holding water, and a route from the hollow into the river. Wrong: the new route points uphill (E); a water mark added with no land shape around it (D); the ocean is left off the redrawn model (G).
- **E4 (day 4).** A playground picture where water follows a low path into a dip, and a tray record where water collects between hills. "Draw this corner, and mark what its water does that the tray's water did." Answer: the path, the dip, water collected in the low ground, and the matching mark on the tray record. Wrong: the path runs up across the rise (E); the dip and water drawn with nothing around them (D); marks with no key (B).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** One class model of land and water, revised and never replaced (the old drawing stays beside the new one, dated). Day 1: a sketch with water arrows and a hollow, from photographs. Day 2: a keyed sand-tray model, checked by a pour. Day 3: revised from each group's own pour record. Day 4: placed on a picture map of the school and compared with the tray, differences named. The teacher holds the class model chart, the question board and the pour records; groups hold their trays and records; each child holds their own sketch. The key (arrow, hill, hollow, water) is supplied on day 2, written by children from day 3 and withdrawn before day 9.
- **Key moment 1, day 2: water running downhill and collecting in a low place in a landscape the children shaped.** Ideal instrument: a stream table with a tilted, clear-sided bed. Spec: partly fits. A household storage tray of damp play sand shows runs and pooling but not a long river, and the sand soaks some water in. No digital tool is needed. Gaps and asks: none for this section, so no TOOL-ASKS.md entry.
- **Key moment 2, day 4: a real place beside its model.** Ideal instrument: a walk over the grounds. Spec: partly fits. Photographs show the corner and its low path but not the feel of the slope. If the ground is dry and safe, the teacher may show the real corner from the doorway in the Look part; no walk is built into the minutes.
- **If a pour fails.** If the water soaks in with no route, dampen the sand and pour more slowly from lower; if it still fails, the group uses the supplied pour record card, said aloud to be invented for practice. No reading is measured in this section.
- **A room with nothing.** Day 1 and day 4 use teacher photographs or the Curio Class drawings; day 2 uses relief drawings and the supplied pour records. Revise from these weaker, supplied observations and say so aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 1


- **6 min. Hook and question board.** Teacher photographs of puddles beside moving curb water (or the doorway view from the dry threshold, never near runoff or drains). Children say what they notice; the teacher scribes each notice as a question on the paper board (review routine). The compelling question is read aloud.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Social studies grade 1 words and key. A picture map with a river, a hill and a key: "Mark the river and the hill. Use the key to find the water." For a miss, show the symbol beside the pictured feature, then let the child try another before modeling.
- **8 min. Worked example.** On one photograph the teacher traces a finger from high ground to low ground, draws one water arrow and a hollow, and says the words runoff, downhill, lower, hollow and collect. Pip draws only a puddle (error D); the children say what is missing; one line from Pip at a time.
- **10 min. Sketch the routes.** Each child sketches, from two photographs, where the water goes and where it collects, on model paper. Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes), with the "heard" column; the teacher scribes their words.
- **5 min. Partner share.** Partners take 1 minute each: one explains a sketch by pointing, the author redraws one thing. Then 3 minutes to pin three sketches as the first class model.
- **3 min. Exit E1.**
- **2 min. Pack up.** Collect sketches and photographs.
- Holds what is carried: each child their own sketch; the teacher the class model chart and the question board.
- Tool: None. By hand: all drawing. Evidence: E1. Safety: runoff is looked at from the doorway or a safe path; no child goes near water or drains. Photographs show no identifiable children.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sketches runoff routes on schoolyard pictures; the exit asks for the same kind of drawing on two new rock-slope pictures, with a hollow shown holding water, a case no practice part shows.

## Day 2


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 1 exit: one letter, E first (field 4).
- **5 min. Worked example: a model, its key and the tray.** The teacher shapes a demonstration tray with one hill and one low place, says the sand stands for land and the cup of water for rain, pours once and draws the arrow. The key box is drawn: arrow, hill, hollow, water. Pip leaves out the key (error B).
- **7 min. Build.** Splash goggles on for everyone (1 minute). Groups of five shape two hills and a valley in their tray, with three pebbles; each child has one job (shaper, pourer, pointer, drawer, wiper). Each group guesses the route with a finger first (guess, review).
- **6 min. Pour and watch.** Each group pours one cup slowly from a hilltop twice (2 minutes each, pouring by two different children), and the drawer marks the arrows seen. Six groups pour at once, one tray each, so no group waits.
- **6 min. Draw the pour record.** Each child draws the tray from above with the key box. Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **7 min. Clean-up.** Wipes and goggles first (1 minute, goggles wiped and bagged). Two waves of three groups wash at two sinks (about 2.5 minutes each, 15 children per wave) while the adult tips the other trays into a lined waste bin, never a drain. Waiting groups finish their drawing.
- **3 min. Exit E2.**
- **1 min. Question board.** One question for day 3, "Why did the water gather there".
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the key chart; each group its pour record; each child their drawing.
- Tool: None. By hand: shaping, pouring, drawing. Evidence: E2. Safety: damp clean play sand, below faces; never thrown or tasted; spills wiped at once; hands washed.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice shapes a sand tray, pours, and draws its own pour record; the exit hands over a drawn tray with an uphill arrow and a record card, and asks the child to fix the arrow, which no practice part asks.

## Day 3


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 2 exit: one letter, E first (field 4).
- **4 min. Show one revision.** The teacher redraws one group's day 2 model on the chart after reading its pour record, and says it with the frame "I changed ___ because I saw ___" (claim and evidence, review). Nothing new is taught.
- **12 min. Build with picture pieces.** Pairs place landscape picture pieces (hills, a hollow, a river, a lake in a hollow, the ocean) on model paper and draw the arrows and a key. Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **8 min. Revise from your own pour.** Each group takes its day 2 pour record and asks "what did our water do that our model does not show". Each pair makes one change and says it with the frame. Pip says the ocean is off the page (error G).
- **4 min. Gallery.** Pairs show a revision to another pair, "I saw, I think".
- **3 min. Exit E3.**
- **2 min. Put pieces away.** Pieces counted back into envelopes.
- Holds what is carried: pairs their model; the teacher the chart with the revision dated.
- Tool: None. By hand: arranging, drawing. Evidence: E3.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice places picture pieces and revises a model from the group's own pour; the exit gives a model with no hollow and three new pictures, and asks the child to add the hollow and its route.

## Day 4


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 3 exit: one letter, E first (field 4).
- **3 min. Look.** Three school-grounds photographs (or the real corner from the doorway, if dry and safe): an entrance walk, a field edge and a sloping path. "Which way is lower".
- **5 min. Show how to draw a picture map.** The teacher draws one corner from above on the chart with the same arrows and key box, said not to be to scale. Pip draws the map with no key (error B).
- **12 min. Draw the grounds map.** Groups of five, five map sheets each; every child draws one corner, with a route arrow, from the photographs. Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **6 min. Compare with the tray.** Each group lays its map beside its day 2 pour record and marks one thing the two routes share and one that differs, with the frame "the same is ___, different is ___".
- **3 min. Exit E4.**
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Pencils and sheets collected.
- **2 min. Question board.** "Does our map match the school" is answered and left open for day 5.
- Set-up for unit 6, teacher alone, during the draw part and not taking class minutes: the teacher dries seed heads in a bag; children do not collect, and burrs are checked and stored out of reach.
- Holds what is carried: groups their map; the teacher the chart.
- Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: E4. Safety: photographs mean no outdoor survey; any look from a path stays on dry paths in sight, nothing touched.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the grounds map from one picture with one route arrow; the exit gives a new playground corner and a tray record, and asks the child to mark one thing the corner's water did like the tray's.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 1, "Where did the water go, and where did it stay". Day 2, "Why did the water gather there". Day 4, "What does the school map show that the tray did not".
- Written task, day 3: the revision, with the frame "I changed ___ because I saw ___" (labels and one sentence, which the teacher scribes for any child who asks).
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names a change; names the evidence (pour record or picture); the changed route runs to lower ground.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 1: landform words and the map key (retrieval). Day 2: day 1's route words. Day 3: the key and day 2's pour record. Day 4: the key, the arrow and the day 2 and day 3 revisions. Each day opens with the exit's wrong example in Return and repair.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with picture cards: runoff, downhill, lower, hollow, collect, model, key. Frame: "The water goes ___ because ___ is lower."
- **Access.** A child who cannot see uses the sand tray by touch and raised-line outlines on the sketch. A child who cannot fold or write small points, uses stickers for marks or a scribe. A child who cannot hear sees every instruction on the chart.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, an oral answer to a scribe, or a large print card; the exit pictures are read aloud.
- **Reading load.** Labels only; the student page stays under 150 words. The teacher reads aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second hollow or a river with two branches to the model, and show the new routes with the key.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A baking dish with damp sand and a small cup of water, safety glasses on both of you; shape one hill and a dip, pour, and draw the arrow with a key box. Use a photograph of a street after rain for day 1. Wipe spills at once, put the sand in the bin, wash hands.
- **One projector.** Show the photograph, the class model chart and the key on the one screen; groups use paper copies. Trays are hands-on and need no screen.
