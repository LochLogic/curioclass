# Section 7.1: Observing and comparing kinds in different places

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-LS4-1, this section's share (days 66 to 69).** "Observe plants and animals and compare how many kinds of living things are found in different habitats." Observe and record: days 66 and 67, two schoolyard spots. Compare how many kinds: days 67 to 69, across the two spots and then a pond, a forest, a desert and an ocean shore. Count each kind once (day 67); equal counts can hide different kinds (day 69).
- **Limits that bite here.** Variety seen, never names: describing labels only, provisional, never scored. No food webs, adaptations or habitat rankings. A kind not seen is not a kind that is absent. Equal effort improves a comparison but cannot find every kind. Pond, forest, desert and shore cards are invented for practice, said aloud, and are not survey results.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.D.10 (picture graph, one picture per kind, a scale of one), day 67. Unit 1 land and water, days 68 and 69 (pond, shore). Fair test "keep the same" (kindergarten, grade 2 unit 2) as equal effort, day 66. Claim, evidence and reason, "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___" (unit 5), days 68 and 69.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A habitat here is only a place where living things find what they need; no ecology words. (2) A kind is a group that look alike; a label such as "tall green blade plant" is accepted if it tells two kinds apart; a young and an adult animal that look different may be two labels, said aloud, never scored. (3) Counting pictures of one kind is counting individuals; we count kinds. (4) People count as animals but are not inventoried. (5) Five minutes at one patch sees only some kinds; say so before the survey. (6) Watch only: nothing touched, moved or collected, even a stone.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none; items 1 to 4 sit on day 72 and belong to section 7.2. Days 66 to 69 are practice and are never scored. The record sheet has a "heard" column naming the rotating six; marks are shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

**Exits** (spoken, picture cards, each labeled "invented for practice" and said aloud; every child touches or draws, six rotating children give a reason, 15 seconds each). If many miss one, the next day's "Return and repair" part opens with that letter's wrong example.
- **E66.** Pip looked at a patch for one minute and drew no worms. "Touch what Pip can say: there are no worms here, or Pip did not see worms, and some may be hiding." Answer: did not see, some may be hiding. Wrong: no worms here (C).
- **E67.** One invented patch card shows four identical pill bugs, two identical moss clumps and one dandelion. "Touch how many different kinds: three, four or seven." Answer: three. Wrong: seven (A); four (A).
- **E68a.** Card X (garden bed) shows a tall flower, a worm and a sparrow; card Y (path edge) shows a worm and moss. "Touch the kind that is on both cards." Answer: the worm. Wrong: none, because X has three kinds and Y has two (B, nearest letter; see Plan points).
- **E68b.** Pip says every pond has five kinds because our pond card has five. A new pond card shows three. "Touch yes or no: is Pip right?" Answer: no. Wrong: yes (D, nearest letter; see Plan points).
- **E69.** The unit plan's set (field 7): a sheltered coastal eelgrass bed (eelgrass, crab, clam, snail) and a rocky tidepool (sea star, mussel, barnacle, hermit crab), invented for practice. "Compare the counts and show one different kind." Answer: four kinds each; eelgrass only in the first, barnacle only in the second. The eelgrass picture shows three blades and the tidepool two mussels. Wrong: the eelgrass bed has more kinds (A); the same kinds, since four each (B).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The habitat picture model: two schoolyard spots drawn on the class sheet, living things joined by links to the resources they use (day 66, joined to the unit 6 system model). Day 67 revises it with a kinds graph, one picture per kind. Day 68 adds a pond and two forests so one habitat type is not assumed alike everywhere. Day 69 adds shared kinds and equal-count contrasts. Children hold their own inventory sheets and graphs; the teacher holds the class sheet and the question board.
- **Key moment 1, day 66: two equal looks.** Ideal instrument: live patches at a fixed size and time. Spec: a rope loop about 1 meter across, five minutes each by the wall clock (a pace cue so every group keeps equal effort), pencil and inventory sheet fit. The step is the whole minute; no reading below it. A kind not drawn is recorded as "not seen", never "not there".
- **Key moment 2, day 67: one picture per kind.** Ideal instrument: the child's own sheet. Spec: the inventory picture cards and a picture graph template (one picture is one kind) fit.
- **When a reading fails.** A site unsafe or frozen, or a child with an allergy: the matched schoolyard photographs (two per group, same size) and the same five minutes each. The route is weaker because framing limits what can be seen, and the record says so. If a spot yields fewer than two kinds, count honestly and run the comparisons with the invented cards.
- **Counted equipment.** Twelve rope loops, six per spot, one pair a group; thirty boards, so no waiting.
- **Clean-up.** Outdoors, rope and boards are collected in 1 minute; clothing checked for insects in pairs while in line; hands washed at two sinks, 15 children each at 15 seconds, about 4 minutes in all. If the line runs over, the last children wash at the next scheduled washing, said aloud.
- **A room with nothing.** The photographs, pencils and folded paper. No tool is used; the section runs on print and has no key tool.

## 6. Days

## Day 66


- **4 min. Doorway look and predictions.** From the door or window the class looks at the two spots and hears the compelling question. Hands for same, some the same, or different (counted on the board); three of the rotating six give a reason, 20 seconds each (1 min).
- **2 min. Carry in from unit 6.** The day 65 consensus chart and plant-animal system model are read aloud; two children say one link each. Nothing is rescored.
- **8 min. Retrieval (unscored).** Needs and places (5): an unused picture mat labeled "invented for practice", with a child, bird, plant, air, puddle and soil. "Draw links from living things to the resources they use." Accept a child as an animal; do not imply all living things need soil. Miss: trace one link on the earlier picture model, then the child tries another. Then (3): on a new picture, "Mark the root that takes in water and the leaves that get light"; miss: use unit 5's needs-arrow drawing.
- **4 min. Prepare and move.** Closed shoes, the four rules (stay on the path, stay in sight, watch and touch nothing, tell an adult if unsure), then the whole class walks together; an adult counts heads.
- **10 min. Survey.** Six groups of five, a pair of rope loops each, one in each spot. Five minutes at spot 1, five at spot 2, the change called by the wall clock. Each child draws every living thing seen on their own inventory sheet; repeats may be drawn, they are sorted tomorrow. The teacher walks the groups; nothing is touched or collected.
- **5 min. Habitat picture model.** Each group sticks one drawing from each child on the class sheet (20 seconds a group, 2 min); six rotating children say what lives in one spot and which link it uses, 20 seconds each (2 min); the teacher reads the sheet aloud (1 min).
- **4 min. Clean-up.** As in field 5.
- **2 min. Exit.** E66.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class sheet and chart, each child their sheet. Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: E66; the record sheet "heard" column.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice surveys two schoolyard spots and draws what lives there; the exit gives a new patch card to draw on an inventory line, and a case of Pip seeing no worms, which no practice part asks the child to judge.

## Day 67


- **3 min. Return and repair.** E66 (C): the flap card, whole class if many, otherwise a small group while the rest lay out their sheets.
- **7 min. Agree labels.** Eight inventory picture cards copied by the teacher the day before from the class's own sheets are held up; the class agrees one describing label for each ("small black walker"), provisional. Hands vote; six rotating children say what tells two kinds apart, 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **14 min. Record and graph.** Each child circles repeats on their own sheet (5 min), then draws one picture per kind in the picture graph template, one row per spot, plants in the top half and animals in the bottom (9 min). A scale of one is recalled aloud: one picture is one kind.
- **8 min. Compare.** Groups lay two children's graphs side by side and find a shared kind and a different kind (5 min); each of six groups reports one, 30 seconds (3 min).
- **4 min. Revise the model.** The class sheet gets a kinds row for each spot.
- **2 min. Exit.** E67.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Cards and sheets into the group folder.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class sheet, each child their graph. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: E67; each graph, marked on the record sheet.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice agrees labels and graphs one picture a kind from eight cards; the exit gives one new patch card with repeats, four pill bugs, two moss clumps and a dandelion, to draw each kind once on an exit line.

## Day 68


- **3 min. Return and repair.** E67 (A): the circle-and-count card; whole class if many, otherwise a small group.
- **12 min. Observe and record.** Five paper sets a group, one a child: a pond card (lily-pad plant, tall edge plant, fish, dragonfly, water beetle; nine pictures), forest A (tree, fern, mushroom, squirrel, dragonfly; eight pictures) and forest B (tree, beetle; four pictures), all invented for practice. Four minutes a card: circle repeats, draw one picture per kind. Labels from day 67 are reused, new ones agreed aloud.
- **10 min. Compare.** Groups find the shared kind (dragonfly), a kind only the pond has, and the counts: five, four, two. Pairs answer "Why do forest A and forest B differ?" Six groups report one finding, 30 seconds each (3 min).
- **11 min. Revise the model.** The class sheet gets pond and two forests. The class states a pattern: places differ in kinds, and one forest is not another forest. Pip says "every forest has four kinds" and the class tests the line against the cards; six rotating children give "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___", 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **2 min. Exit.** E68a and E68b.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class sheet and pattern line. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, circling. Evidence: E68; graphs on the record sheet.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice records pond and forest cards one picture a kind and compares them in groups; the exit gives two new cards, a garden bed and a path edge, to touch the shared kind, and a claim about a new pond card.

## Day 69


- **5 min. Return and repair.** E68 (B, D): 2.5 minutes a letter with the teacher, the rest begin their sets.
- **10 min. Observe and record.** Desert card (cactus, lizard, beetle) and ocean-shore card (seaweed, gull, sand worm), invented for practice, five minutes each; circle repeats, draw one picture per kind.
- **12 min. Compare.** Four-habitat comparison (8 min): the groups' graphs are laid in four rows, pond five, forest A four, desert three, shore three; the class finds shared kinds and the largest count, and one wet place that is not the largest. Equal counts (4 min): desert and shore are three each; touch the kinds, none the same.
- **8 min. Revise the model.** Class sheet adds "equal counts can hide different kinds" and "wet is not always more". Each child writes or dictates "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___" on a strip; six rotating children read theirs, 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **3 min. Exit.** E69, shown on paper, labeled "invented for practice".
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the class sheet, each child a strip. Tool: None. By hand: all. Evidence: E69; the strip on the record sheet.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice records desert and shore cards and compares four habitat rows; the exit gives two new cards, an eelgrass bed and a tidepool with equal counts, and asks which shows more kinds and which kind only one shows.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 66: "What lives in each spot, and how will we know?" Day 67: "Is that the same kind, or a different kind? What do you see?" Day 69: "Both places have three. Are they the same kinds?"
- **Written task, day 69.** The strip: "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___", written, drawn or dictated; no spelling counts.
- **Judged by the teacher with four look-fors**, marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen: each kind drawn once; labels tell kinds apart; a shared or different kind named; a limit named ("I only looked here"). Practice only, never scored.

## 8. Practice that returns


- **Day 66:** needs and places (links to resources), plant parts and jobs, the unit 6 chart. **Day 67:** picture graph and the scale of one (unit 1 math graphs). **Day 68:** links and the day 67 labels. **Day 69:** claim, evidence and reason from unit 5. Each day also returns the letter from the day before.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words shown with the picture: kind, same, different, plant, animal, count, label, place. Frame: "Both have ___. Only ___ has ___." Pointing and drawing are accepted.
- **Access.** Raised outlines on the cards, high-contrast pictures, pointing, a scribe, and an indoor photograph path for any child or day. The adult supplies access and never selects kinds or comparisons.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are read aloud and answered by touch, a drawing or a spoken word.
- **Reading load.** None alone. The inventory sheet and graph template are labels only, under 40 words each, read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third spot or card: which two share the most kinds, and why that does not mean they are the same.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 66: pick two spots a few steps apart (a path edge, a planter), look five minutes at each from the path and draw what lives there, touching nothing. Day 67: circle repeats and draw one picture per kind. Days 68 and 69: use the cards or photographs the teacher prints to draw one picture per kind and say what differs.
- **One projector.** Show the doorway photographs on day 66, the label cards on day 67, the cards on days 68 and 69. Tables hold the paper sets; the survey is outdoors.
