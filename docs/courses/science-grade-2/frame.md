# Course frame: Science, Grade 2

Course id science-k5, grade 2. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7, each opening on an anchor phenomenon or design problem: land and water first (it is in step with
  social studies grade 2's world features), then how land changes fast and slow and how people slow it down,
  materials and their best uses, changes that can and cannot be undone, then three life science units in spring
  (plant needs, seeds and pollinators, habitats).
- **Days.** 76: 70 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 3, 2 after unit 7) and 2 respond days (one
  after each check). Science shares the grade 2 day with reading, math and social studies, so the course assumes
  two lessons a week of 35 to 40 minutes, with a third lesson in about 8 weeks (two of them in weeks 1 to 4, so
  unit 1's 10 days are done by week 4, ahead of social studies grade 2's world features; the rest where the erosion
  and plant tests need a follow-up look), across about 34 weeks (a school year of about 36 weeks, of which about 34
  carry science lessons here; about 80 minutes a week), not the full-year yardstick of 160.
  Unit days follow the rows' load: unit 2 carries the most (three rows, one a two-design comparison with
  measurement) and unit 3 two investigations with a bar graph; unit 6 (one model) the least.
- **Standard.** The 11 grade 2 performance expectations of the Next Generation Science Standards (2-PS1-1 to
  2-PS1-4, 2-LS2-1, 2-LS2-2, 2-LS4-1, 2-ESS1-1, 2-ESS2-1, 2-ESS2-2, 2-ESS2-3) and the band's third engineering
  expectation, K-2-ETS1-3, done in full here. Each is taught with every part its clarification names and inside
  its boundary. The C3 column of the Units table holds, for science, the practices and crosscutting concepts each
  unit develops. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the evidence statements of its expectations and
  the K-2 practice and crosscutting concept elements (Appendices F and G), and reads OpenSciEd elementary
  (reference only, never copied) for order and pacing; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects
  that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds; BASELINE rule 5: Earth's slow changes are taught as
  the evidence shows.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Phenomena are
  chosen so a grade 2 class meets them first hand at school (rainwater running across the schoolyard, a bare path
  worn into a ditch, burrs on a sock, a plant left in the dark); where it cannot (a volcano, a canyon, a desert or
  ocean shore), the stand-in is an open or public-domain photograph, map or video (USGS, National Park Service,
  NOAA, NASA), adapted with a credit line. The texts students gather facts from in units 1 and 2 are Curio Class's
  own short captioned pages beside those public sources. No trade book, paid video or library visit.
- **Materials: substitutes, then no-kit.** Every investigation has a household stand-in where safe and a no-kit
  data path otherwise (a class video or photo set with its measured data on a sheet, said plainly to be weaker).
  Expected kit, all classroom-cheap: a sand tray (a plastic storage box), sand, soil, pebbles, sticks, a small
  fan on low, paper towels of several kinds, fabric scraps, a sorting set of wood, metal, plastic, rubber and paper
  objects, building blocks, ice, butter or chocolate (not eaten), seeds, cups, potting soil, rulers, a sock or
  fuzzy glove, burrs and seed heads gathered in fall and kept dry (hook-and-loop squares as the stand-in), pipe
  cleaners, chalk dust. Our own online tools may later serve as simulations (a land-change
  time-lapse for slow events, a seed-spread model); each unit plan lists any it wants (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13),
  with a paper path beside it and no student account.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 2 students read short captioned texts; science words are taught as
  spoken and visual language (said aloud, shown with objects, labelled on pictures), and no student is asked to
  decode them alone. Students write claims and records with frames; the teacher reads every source text aloud
  with the class first.
- **The storyline in every unit.** The unit opens on its anchor; the class says what it notices and wonders and
  builds a question board; each lesson answers a question the last one raised; the unit closes by explaining the
  anchor with an argument or account from evidence (units 4, 5, 7), a working model (units 1, 6) or a design
  chosen from test data (units 2, 3). The class model is revised when new evidence comes in. Students return to
  explain the anchor with their revised model or evidence, and each unit also includes an individual end-of-unit
  task on a phenomenon or design problem students have not studied, using a practice with the unit's core idea and
  a crosscutting concept, inside the unit's days.
- **Practices across the year.** Asking questions (units 1, 2); developing and using models (units 1, 6);
  planning and carrying out investigations (units 3, 5, with one thing changed at a time); analysing and
  interpreting data, with bar graphs (units 2, 3, 5, 7); using measurement in whole centimeters (units 2, 5, 6);
  constructing explanations and designing solutions (units 2, 4); argument from evidence (units 3, 4); obtaining,
  evaluating and communicating information from several sources (units 1, 2). Crosscutting concepts: patterns
  (units 1, 3, 7), stability and change (introduced unit 2), cause and effect (units 3, 4, 5), structure and
  function (units 3, 6), energy and matter (unit 4), systems (units 4, 6, 7), scale, proportion and quantity
  (relative comparisons and whole-centimeter measurement, units 2, 5, 6). Nature of science: unit 2 (science assumes natural events happen today as they
  happened in the past, so the slow changes we see now explain the canyon), unit 5 (a fair test changes one thing
  at a time), unit 7 (scientists count and compare carefully, and different scientists find the same pattern).
- **Safety.** A one-page course safety sheet for the year (fan on low with fingers clear, spills wiped at once,
  hands washed after soil, all heating by an adult, nothing tasted, food and dust allergies checked, outdoor
  survey rules), and every hands-on day names its hazards and controls.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 2 (volcanoes, earthquakes,
  landslides, floods) needs a reader who knows young children after disasters (a school counsellor or similar);
  every hands-on unit, unit 4's heating above all, needs a science teacher's safety read before the course is
  called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3). Who, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 3 (2 days, mid-year).** Short talk, draw and write check on cases not seen before: a picture map of
  an unfamiliar place read for where the water runs; sorting a photograph set of events into fast and slow, with
  a reason for one; two new barrier designs' results (a short table) read for what each does well and poorly; a
  material chosen for a new purpose (a dog bed) from given test data, with the reason; a material shown cooler and
  warmer, each sample classified.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Re-teach what the check shows (often reading the data for strengths
  and weaknesses, and the fast or slow reason), in small groups, ending with a fresh short task.
- **After unit 7 (2 days, end of year).** Check on new cases: whether a new change can be undone (a candle's wax
  dripping and hardening; toast), argued from evidence; a new set of pieces made into a new object; a plant test
  someone else ran, read for whether it was fair and what it shows; a model of a new animal spreading seeds
  explained; an unfamiliar pair of habitat inventories (one pair with equal counts but different kinds) read for
  one way the places are alike and one way they differ.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teach the gaps, in small groups, before the record goes on to
  the grade 3 teacher with what the respond day changed.

Social studies grade 2 (ss2-continents-features) leans on 2-ESS2-2 from about week 6; this course teaches it in
unit 1, weeks 1 to 4 (two of the third-lesson weeks fall there), which honours it.

ELA grade 2 leans on 2-ESS1-1 at about week 8 (its fossils unit 2; unit 2 here runs about weeks 5 to 12, alongside)
and on 2-LS2-2 and 2-LS2-1 at about weeks 16 and 18 (its pollinators unit 3). Science teaches those two later, in
unit 6 (about week 27) and unit 5 (about week 23); ELA's own texts carry the facts, its bridge, and the class meets
the experiment and the model afterwards as evidence for what it read.

Weeks are counted at the course's pace (two lessons most weeks, three in about eight), with the mid-year check and
respond day included; the scope and sequence sets the exact dates.

## For the lead

1. **Time a week.** The frame assumes two lessons a week of 35 to 40 minutes, three in about eight weeks (76
   days). Pick: keep it. If schools give more time, unit 2 (land change and the design comparison) and unit 3
   (materials) take the extra days first.
2. **Seasonal order.** Unit 2's slope and unit 7's survey need unfrozen ground, unit 6 flowers and seeds. Schools
   in cold climates keep the order and use the no-kit path for unit 2's schoolyard anchor if snow has come. Pick:
   keep the order.
3. **Materials cost.** Pick: a stated ceiling of about $50 a class for the year beyond what a grade 2 room already
   has (seeds, soil and paper towels are the main costs), each unit plan giving its own cost. A money question, so
   Cory's to confirm.
