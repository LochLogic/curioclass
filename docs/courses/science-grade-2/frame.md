# Course frame: Science, Grade 2

Course `science-grade-2` (catalogue id `science-k5`), grade 2. Draft frame, revised 2026-11-15; the [scope and sequence](scope-and-sequence.md) and [day record](../../../data/plans/science-grade-2/days.csv) set the ordered days and school-day positions.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7, each opening on an anchor phenomenon or design problem: land and water first (it is in step with
  social studies grade 2's world features), then how land changes fast and slow and how people slow it down,
  materials and their best uses, changes that can and cannot be undone, then three life science units using indoor or stored evidence where seasons differ
  (plant needs, seeds and pollinators, habitats).
- **Days.** 76 course days: 70 in seven units, four course-level check days (two after unit 5 and two after unit 7),
  and two respond days, one after each check. Reserve three lessons a week of 35 to 40 minutes within 28 weeks;
  the day record uses no more than three meetings in any week and finishes on school day 140. Unit 1 finishes
  in week 4 before social studies needs it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: unit 2's three rows and measured
  design comparisons take most days; unit 6's one model takes least. Every unit includes an individual unfamiliar-case
  assessment window and response, counted once. A row last taught or assessed in grade g gets retrieval in g+1 and
  readiness in g+2 or later; passing retrieval or a maintenance label alone does not reset last use. Readiness takes
  the last ten minutes of the previous unit's final/respond day so it shapes the next hook; unit 1 alone would use
  its own day 1 after the hook. Here days 10 and 65 check the older forecast and needs/place content respectively,
  with short supported reteaching after the next hook when needed. Grade 1 practices and plant-needs content are
  retrieved. The mid-year check is week 20, days 57/58 on school days 96/98, with respond day 59 on 100, after unit 5.
  The lead settled week 20. After unit 4 would leave 26 meetings for 24 slots in weeks 21 to 28; after unit 5 leaves
  17 for those slots, keeping all units and counts. The requested shared calendar pin is course day 58 at school day 98,
  replacing the old day 40 at 84 in scripts/frame.py during the structure pass. Year-end check days 74/75 and respond
  76 are school days 136/138/140, week 28. Check weeks avoid social studies 14/31, ELA 15 to 16/32 and math 18 to 19/29.
- **Standard.** The 11 grade 2 performance expectations of the Next Generation Science Standards (2-PS1-1 to
  2-PS1-4, 2-LS2-1, 2-LS2-2, 2-LS4-1, 2-ESS1-1, 2-ESS2-1, 2-ESS2-2, 2-ESS2-3) and the band's third engineering
  expectation, K-2-ETS1-3, done in full here. Each is taught with every part its clarification names and inside
  its boundary. The C3 column of the Units table holds, for science, the practices and crosscutting concepts each
  unit develops. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the evidence statements of its expectations and
  the K-2 practice and crosscutting concept elements (Appendices F and G), and reads OpenSciEd elementary
  (reference only, never copied) for order and pacing; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects
  that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds; BASELINE rule 5: Earth's slow changes are taught as
  the evidence shows.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Phenomena are
  chosen so a grade 2 class meets them first hand at school (rainwater running across the schoolyard, a bare path
  worn into a ditch, burrs on a sock, pale grass under a board left on the lawn); where it cannot (a volcano, a canyon, a desert or
  ocean shore), the stand-in is an open or public-domain photograph, map or video (USGS, National Park Service,
  NOAA, NASA), adapted with a credit line. The texts students gather facts from in units 1 and 2 are Curio Class's
  own short captioned pages beside those public sources. No trade book, paid video or library visit.
- **Materials: substitutes, then no-kit.** Every investigation has a household stand-in where safe and a no-kit
  data path otherwise (a class video or photo set with its measured data on a sheet, said plainly to be weaker).
  The scope and sequence allocates the settled $50 additional-materials ceiling for 30 children: units 1 to 7 use $4, $10, $14, $5, $10, $6 and $1, counting reused purchases once. These are planning ceilings, not purchase quotes. Expected kit, with classroom equipment or a no-kit route: a sand tray (a plastic storage box), sand, soil, pebbles, sticks, a small
  fan on low, paper towels of several kinds, fabric scraps, a sorting set of wood, metal, plastic, rubber and paper
  objects, building blocks, ice, butter or chocolate (not eaten), seeds, cups, potting soil, rulers, a sock or
  fuzzy glove, burrs and seed heads gathered in fall and kept dry (hook-and-loop squares as the stand-in), pipe
  cleaners, chalk dust. Our own online tools may later serve as simulations (a land-change
  time-lapse for slow events, a seed-spread model); each unit plan lists any it wants (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13),
  with a paper path beside it and no student account.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 2 students read short captioned texts; science words are taught as
  spoken and visual language (said aloud, shown with objects, labelled on pictures), and no student is asked to
  decode them alone. Students write claims and records with frames; the teacher reads every source text aloud
  with the class first.
- **The storyline in every unit.** The unit opens on its anchor; the class says what it notices and wonders and
  builds a question board; each lesson answers a question the last one raised; the unit closes by explaining the
  anchor with an argument or account from evidence (units 4, 5, 7), a working model (units 1, 6) or a design
  chosen from test data (units 2, 3). The class model is revised when new evidence comes in. Students return to
  explain the anchor with their revised model or evidence, and each unit also includes an individual end-of-unit
  task on a phenomenon or design problem students have not studied, using a practice with the unit's core idea and
  a crosscutting concept, inside the unit's days.
- **Practices across the year.** Asking questions (units 1, 2); developing and using models (units 1, 6);
  planning and carrying out investigations (units 3, 5, with one thing changed at a time); analysing and
  interpreting data, with bar graphs (units 2, 3, 5, 7); using measurement in whole centimeters (units 2, 5, 6);
  constructing explanations and designing solutions (units 2, 4); argument from evidence (units 3, 4); obtaining,
  evaluating and communicating information from several sources (units 1, 2). Crosscutting concepts: patterns
  (units 1, 3, 7), stability and change (introduced unit 2), cause and effect (units 3, 4, 5), structure and
  function (units 3, 6), energy and matter (unit 4), systems (units 4, 6, 7), scale, proportion and quantity
  (relative comparisons and whole-centimeter measurement, units 2, 5, 6).
- **Nature of science (how science works).** Unit 2 (science assumes natural events happen today as they happened
  in the past, so the slow changes we see now explain a worn step or a sea cliff), unit 5 (a fair test changes one
  thing at a time), unit 7 (scientists count and compare carefully, and different scientists find the same
  pattern).
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs on paper and objects; any online tool a unit plan adds has a paper path beside
  it and needs no student account.
- **Safety.** A one-page course safety sheet for the year (fan on low with fingers clear, spills wiped at once,
  hands washed after soil, all heating by an adult, nothing tasted, food and dust allergies checked, outdoor
  survey rules), and every hands-on day names its hazards and controls.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 2 (volcanoes, earthquakes, landslides, floods): before it
  reads, the agent studies published guidance on young children and disasters (a national child traumatic stress
  network's guidance for parents and teachers, a pediatrics association's and a school psychologists'
  association's pages on talking with children after a disaster, and a geological survey's and emergency agency's
  hazard pages for children). Every unit here is hands-on, so every unit also gets the safety agent's read, against
  published school science safety guidance, unit 4's heating above all, before the course is called finished; the
  course's checks get the answer-key agent's read.

45/46, 54/55, 63/64 and 72. Builds start before the final day and finish there. Unit 3 day 36 uses six four-minute
groups, six minutes transitions/set-up and five minutes anchor return. Units 4, 5 and 6 use three four-minute groups
each day, three minutes transitions and 20 minutes paper/teaching work. Others independently finish familiar change
cards, single-date plant graphs/plans, or familiar sock/flower model labels respectively. Each child acts, not just a
spokesperson. Paper products provide the explanations; unit plans preserve every part named in the day notes.
Each response is counted once. Collect a child's evidence once in a window, and carry any record within ten days
unchanged into a later check; no repeated task form from the last two weeks, no re-scoring an artifact. Checks list
only newly sampled codes. Shorter-period variants and their reduced course-check samples are specified in the
scope and sequence; no unit-owned part is removed.
