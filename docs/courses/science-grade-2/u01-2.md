## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-ESS2-2, this section's share (day 5).** "Make a model showing the landforms and bodies of water in a place." Children revise the class model with a second, contrasting place, so the pattern is seen in two places and the shapes differ.
- **2-ESS2-3, this section's share (days 6 to 8).** "Gather information to find where water is on Earth and show that it can be frozen solid or a liquid." Gather: from the captions and keyed map of two supplied pages, read aloud. Where: oceans, rivers, lakes, ponds, ice and snow. Show frozen solid or liquid: each place is marked with its state and its source.
- **Limits that bite here.** No water cycle, no amounts, no numerical scale. Students do not search; every source is supplied and read aloud, and written words are labels. A "rule" that liquid lies low and solid lies high is not taught: it is true only of the packet in hand. Not every river reaches the ocean. The sealed cups are for seeing and holding, never opened or tasted, and no temperature is read.
- **Connections.** ELA W.2.8, gather information from provided sources to answer a question (days 6 to 8, two-column chart). Grade 1 science two-source practice on young animals (retrieval day 6). Social studies grade 1 map key (days 5 and 6).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A source for a science question reports what was seen, says where, and can be checked against a caption or a map key. A story or an opinion page may be pleasant and still cannot answer "where is water". "Source" here means a page or picture that gives information; it differs from "source of light" and "source of sound" in grade 1, so say so on first use. Snow and ice are water in solid form; a child who says "snow is not water" is excluding a solid, not misreading. A second source adds a place the first lacks; it does not repeat it. Ponds, lakes and rivers are told apart by size and movement only as the captions say, with no measure.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** the day 8 item (2-ESS2-3, six points, unit plan field 5). The day 9 item belongs to section 1.3.

**Evidence form.** As in section 1.1: each exit asked aloud, one thing at a time, on a page "invented for practice" (said aloud); every child marks paper; a rotating six heard for 30 seconds each in the work part; one scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen); no exit scored. Day 8 is the unit item and marks the six components on the same scale; only "shown" counts a point.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E5 (day 5).** One small page: Maple Hill, a hill sloping to a flat meadow with one hollow, rain falling on the hill. "Draw where the rain water runs and where it stops." Answer: an arrow down the slope to the hollow, water in the hollow with land round it. Wrong: the arrow up the hill (E); the water alone with no land round it (D); the water spread over the meadow (no letter; recorded in plain words).
- **E6 (day 6).** A captioned map with a dotted key puts a liquid pond beside a hill; a second page says "Ponds are lovely." "Which page tells where there is water? Mark the pond on it and where it is." Answer: the map page, with the pond dot marked beside the hill and "liquid" read from the caption. Wrong: the lovely page chosen because it sounds nicer (A); a dot marked without checking what the key says (B).
- **E7 (day 7).** Two pages: P shows a liquid ocean, river, lake and pond; Q shows solid ice and snow. Claim X: "Both pages show water." Claim Y: "Only P shows water." "Choose the claim both pages support, and show it with one mark from each page." Answer: X, a place on P marked liquid and a place on Q marked solid. Wrong: Y chosen (C); X chosen with marks from only one page (F).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class model of land and water. Day 5 revises it with a contrasting place: the pattern (water runs downhill and collects in low places) holds, the shapes differ, and a stream may end in a lake. From day 6 the model has captions, and each mark carries a source label. On day 7 solid-water places are added from a source (an open mark for solid, a filled mark for liquid, in the key). The two-column source chart grows: column 1 on day 6, column 2 on day 7. Symbols are withdrawn on day 8: no key is supplied and the child chooses the sources. The teacher holds the class model and the chart; each child holds their own copy.
- **Key moment 1, day 6: telling what a source can show.** Ideal instrument: real maps and captioned pictures from a named place. Spec: the two Curio Class pages, Water places and Frozen water places, are spec only: planned for version 1 and not yet made. Gaps and asks: none, since they are paper pages, not tools.
- **Key moment 2, day 7: liquid and solid water, side by side.** Ideal instrument: a sealed cup of ice and a sealed cup of liquid water, handled. Spec: fits. Sealed cups show solid and liquid without opening; the class does not read a temperature and does not time melting.
- **If a cup fails.** If a cup leaks or the ice has melted, the group uses the ice-water pictures, said aloud to be invented for practice, and the day goes on; no reading is lost.
- **A room with nothing.** Without cups, use the ice-water images on day 7. Without a printer, read the pages aloud and project one copy.

## 6. Days

## Day 5


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 4 exit: one letter, E first (field 4).
- **5 min. Look at a contrasting place.** Two picture views of Stone Bay (a coast) or Dry Ridge (an inland ridge with a lake and no ocean), whichever contrasts with the school. Read aloud, said to be invented for practice.
- **6 min. Compare.** On a two-column chart, "our schoolyard" against the contrasting place, the teacher points while children say what is the same (water runs downhill, collects in low places) and what differs (the shapes). Pip says "every river goes to the ocean"; children test it against the picture.
- **12 min. Revise your model.** Each child changes their own model once using the new place and writes the pattern frame: "Water runs ___ and collects ___; the shapes here are ___". Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **4 min. Share the pattern.** Pairs say their pattern to each other; the class chart is revised.
- **3 min. Exit E5.**
- **2 min. Question board.** "Where else is water" is added.
- **2 min. Pack up.**
- Holds what is carried: each child their revised model; the teacher the chart.
- Tool: None. By hand: drawing and the frame. Evidence: E5.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice revises the child's own model from Stone Bay or Dry Ridge with a frame; the exit draws where rain runs and stops on a new hillside, a case no practice picture shows.

## Day 6


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 5 exit: one letter, D first (field 4).
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Two new captioned chick pictures, read aloud: "Mark a detail from each page that helps explain how young survive." Accept calling and receiving food. For a miss, point to the source labels and show one fact in each column before the water task.
- **5 min. Choose a source.** The teacher reads two short pages aloud: Water places (a keyed map and captions on an ocean, a river and a lake) and a made-up river story. "Source" is named here, and its difference from a source of light or sound is said.
- **4 min. Talk.** Pairs say which page answers "where is water" and how they know, pointing to a caption or the key. Pip takes the story (error A).
- **8 min. Gather facts.** The teacher models the first caption into column 1 of the two-column chart; pairs find the rest, point to each dot, read its meaning from the key, and the teacher fills the chart.
- **8 min. Mark your page.** Each child circles each water place on their copy of the map and draws its label. Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **3 min. Exit E6.**
- **2 min. Question board.** "Where else is water, including ice" is added.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart; each child their marked page. Printed words stay under 150.
- Tool: None. By hand: circling and labels. Evidence: E6.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice gathers and circles every water place on one map from a source the class chose together; the exit asks the child to choose which of two pages tells where water is and mark the pond there.

## Day 7


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Day 6 exit: one letter, A first (field 4).
- **3 min. Recall.** Column 1 of the chart and its source label are reread.
- **5 min. Hold the cups.** Goggles on (1 minute). Each group of five has one sealed ice cup and one sealed liquid cup in a tray; each child holds each cup for 20 seconds (5 children times 2 cups, about 3.5 minutes), then wipes the condensation. Never opened, never tasted.
- **2 min. Talk.** Pip says "the ice cup has no water in it" (error C); children test it against what they held.
- **7 min. Second source.** The teacher reads Frozen water places aloud (ice and snow, with a map key and captions); pairs add its facts to column 2 with its source label.
- **8 min. Combine.** Pairs mark every place from both pages on one chart under the headings liquid and solid, with each mark's source label. Six rotating children are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Condensation wiped; goggles wiped and bagged; the adult empties the cups into a tray, not a drain.
- **3 min. Exit E7.** "Page P shows liquid brook, waterfall, spring and swamp. Page Q shows solid frost, hail and glacier. Claim X says that both pages show water. Claim Y says that only page P shows water. Which claim do both pages support? Show it with a mark on each page."
- **2 min. Question board.** The board is revisited: where water is, and what it can be.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the combined chart; each child their combined marks.
- Tool: None. By hand: marking and labels. Evidence: E7. Safety: sealed cups held briefly, never opened or tasted; spills wiped; goggles during handling.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice marks places from both pages on one chart under liquid and solid; the exit sets two claims against two new pages and asks which claim both support, with a mark on each page.

## Day 8


- **5 min. Directions.** The teacher reads the whole prompt aloud, neutrally, one step at a time, with a pause after each step (the prompt has several asks, so each is read and done before the next). Captions are read once, with no hint about which source to use.
- **25 min. The packet.** The unit plan's day 8 item (field 5): each child chooses the sources for the question, marks the six water places and states on a source-labeled chart and writes the pattern in the frame. Sentence frames and read-aloud support access. No key is supplied; no group conferences.
- **5 min. Check and collect.** Names checked, papers collected.
- Holds what is carried: each child their packet. Tool: None. Evidence: the six day 8 components, marked on one scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen), with "shown" counted as the point.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 5, "What is the same about the water in the two places, and what is different". Day 6, "Which page can tell us where water is, and how do you know". Day 7, "Is snow water, and how do you know".
- Written task, day 7: the combined chart, at least four marks, each with a source label and a state (open or filled mark, with its word).
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: each mark is on a place from the pages; each carries its source label; each shows liquid or solid correctly; the story page is not used as a source. Spelling and drawing quality are not judged.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 5: day 4's key and day 1 to 4's route words. Day 6: grade 1 two-source practice (retrieval); the key. Day 7: day 6's chart and source labels. Day 8: nothing new; it samples days 6 and 7.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: caption, source, map key, ocean, river, lake, pond, ice, snow, liquid, solid. Frames: "This page tells where water is because ___." and "___ is liquid (solid) because ___."
- **Access.** A child who cannot see gets raised-line copies of the map and cups to hold; a child who cannot write small points or marks with stickers; a child who cannot hear sees each prompt on the chart.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, large print, a scribe, an oral answer or pointing.
- **Reading load.** Labels only, under 150 words on a child's page; every page and caption is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a third place to the combined chart from a supplied caption and say which source gave it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a printed page and read the caption aloud. For day 7, two small sealed jars, one with ice and one with water, held briefly and never opened. Make the chart on one sheet of paper. Day 8 is a quiet paper packet you read aloud and then leave the child to do alone.
- **One projector.** Show one page at a time with its key and read it aloud; each pair has a paper copy to mark.
