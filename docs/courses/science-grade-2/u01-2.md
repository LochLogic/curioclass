- **2-ESS2-2, this section's share (day 5).** "Make a model showing the landforms and bodies of water in a place." Children revise the class model with a second, contrasting place, so the pattern is seen in two places and the shapes differ.
- **2-ESS2-3, this section's share (days 6 to 8).** "Gather information to find where water is on Earth and show that it can be frozen solid or a liquid." Gather: from the captions and keyed map of two supplied pages, read aloud. Where: oceans, rivers, lakes, ponds, ice and snow. Show frozen solid or liquid: each place is marked with its state and its source.
- **Limits that bite here.** No water cycle, no amounts, no numerical scale. Students do not search; every source is supplied and read aloud, and written words are labels. A "rule" that liquid lies low and solid lies high is not taught: it is true only of the packet in hand. Not every river reaches the ocean. The sealed cups are for seeing and holding, never opened or tasted, and no temperature is read.
- **Connections.** ELA W.2.8, gather information from provided sources to answer a question (days 6 to 8, two-column chart). Grade 1 science two-source practice on young animals (retrieval day 6). Social studies grade 1 map key (days 5 and 6).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A source for a science question reports what was seen, says where, and can be checked against a caption or a map key. A story or an opinion page may be pleasant and still cannot answer "where is water". "Source" here means a page or picture that gives information; it differs from "source of light" and "source of sound" in grade 1, so say so on first use. Snow and ice are water in solid form; a child who says "snow is not water" is excluding a solid, not misreading. A second source adds a place the first lacks; it does not repeat it. Ponds, lakes and rivers are told apart by size and movement only as the captions say, with no measure.

- **Carried.** The class model of land and water. Day 5 revises it with a contrasting place: the pattern (water runs downhill and collects in low places) holds, the shapes differ, and a stream may end in a lake. Spec: the two Curio Class pages, Water places and Frozen water places, are spec only: planned for version 1 and not yet made. Gaps and asks: none, since they are paper pages, not tools.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: caption, source, map key, ocean, river, lake, pond, ice, snow, liquid, solid. Frames: "This page tells where water is because ___." and "___ is liquid (solid) because ___."
- **Access.** A child who cannot see gets raised-line copies of the map and cups to hold; a child who cannot write small points or marks with stickers; a child who cannot hear sees each prompt on the chart.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, large print, a scribe, an oral answer or pointing.
- **Reading load.** Labels only, under 150 words on a child's page; every page and caption is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a third place to the combined chart from a supplied caption and say which source gave it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a printed page and read the caption aloud. For day 7, two small sealed jars, one with ice and one with water, held briefly and never opened. Make the chart on one sheet of paper. Day 8 is a quiet paper packet you read aloud and then leave the child to do alone.
- **One projector.** Show one page at a time with its key and read it aloud; each pair has a paper copy to mark.
