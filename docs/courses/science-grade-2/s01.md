## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "After a rainstorm, where does the water on our schoolyard go?"
Day 1 opens with puddles beside moving curb water, seen from the doorway or in teacher photographs. Children sketch routes.
On day 10, explain lower routes and hollows using tray, site and source evidence; mark unobserved routes uncertain.
Model keys on day 2 and captions on day 6; withdraw symbols and source choices before assessment.



Three dimensions (data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv):


Question board: Why move or collect (days 1 to 3)? Does our map match school (day 4)? What differs elsewhere (day 5)? Where else is water, including ice (days 6 and 7)? Answers raise the next question.
Limits: no water cycle, quantities, numerical scale or plate motion. Arrows show observed surface routes; not every river reaches the ocean.
Carried model: sketch day 1; revise routes day 3 from pours, placement day 4 from site evidence, patterns day 5 from contrasting landscapes. Add sourced solid-water locations day 7. Use conventions independently day 9; finalise consensus with uncertainties day 10.

Three-minute exit tickets use pictures withheld during teaching, labeled "invented for practice". Read aloud; every child marks paper. Hear six rotating speakers.

| Day | Evidence prompt |
|---|---|
| 1 | Before-and-after pictures: water leaves a rock slope and collects in a hollow; no arrows. "Sketch movement and collection." Accept the downhill route and filled hollow. |
| 2 | A tray shows hills, a valley pool and an uphill arrow; its pour record shows downhill flow. "Draw the land and water, correcting the arrow from the record." Accept hills, valley pool and downhill arrow. |
| 3 | A model omits a hollow; new evidence shows water filling it before spilling into a river. "Revise the land and water route using this evidence." Accept the added hollow, collected water and route into the river. |
| 4 | A playground picture shows water following a low path into a dip; a tray record shows collection between hills. "Draw this corner and mark what its route shares with the tray's." Accept path, dip, water and collection in low ground. |
| 5 | A new hillside drawing shows rain falling on a hill that slopes to a flat meadow with one hollow. "Draw where the rain water runs and where it stops." Accept an arrow down the slope and water in the hollow with land round it. |
| 6 | A captioned map places a liquid pond beside a hill, keyed by dots; another page says "Ponds are lovely." "Circle the source answering where water is, and mark its pond fact and location." Accept the map, liquid-water caption and dotted pond beside the hill. |
| 7 | P pictures liquid ocean, river, lake and pond; Q shows solid ice and snow. Claims: X, "Both pages show water"; Y, "Only P shows water." "Circle the supported claim and mark evidence from each page, labelling its state." Accept X, a P location marked liquid and a Q location marked solid. |

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 8 (End-of-unit assessment (observation)): Use two unfamiliar sources to locate frozen and liquid water and independently choose a source for the question.

| Day | Code | Part (own words) | The item, answer and wrong answers (error letter) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 2-ESS2-3 | Gather information to find where water is on Earth and show that it can be frozen solid or a liquid. | 25 minutes, six points. New packet, "invented for practice": A, a keyed map headed Pine Coast survey, shows liquid ocean along the coast, liquid river in a valley and solid ice on high ground; B, a picture log headed Pine Coast observations, shows liquid lake and pond in low hollows and solid snow on a ridge; C, a picture log headed Other Cove observations, shows liquid sea and solid shore ice. Prompt: "Where is water in Pine Coast, and in what states? Choose the records that answer this question and show why. Combine them to mark all six water forms or places, their states and a pattern." Children draw or mark a source-labeled chart. Answer: choose A and B for Pine Coast locations; C records another place. A supplies ocean, river and ice; B supplies lake, pond and snow. Both show liquid lower and solid higher in this case, not a universal rule. Wrong: C because it is my favourite place (A); snow is not water (C); B cannot add information (F). | section 1.2 |

Day 9 (End-of-unit assessment (observation)): Individually develop and use a model of an unfamiliar area to show patterns of land shapes and water bodies.

| Day | Code | Part (own words) | The item, answer and wrong answers (error letter) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 2-ESS2-2 | Make a model showing the landforms and bodies of water in a place. | 25 minutes, six points. New packet, "invented for practice": two views of the same unfamiliar bay show two rounded hills separated by a narrow valley, a bending river between them and its mouth in the ocean. A caption says only that rain falls on both hills, so the child finds the route from the model. "Make a picture model of this area with a key. Use it to explain a land-water pattern, then show how a map could represent a different area." Give blank drawing space, not placed features. Answer: both hill shapes, valley, bending river and bay are related as in the evidence; the river occupies the low space and joins the ocean. Different areas require their own shapes and placement. No scale required. Wrong: missing key (B); hills omitted as unimportant (D); arrow uphill across hills (E); ocean omitted as outside the area (G). | section 1.3 |

Each assessment day reserves five minutes for neutral directions and read-aloud, the item's 25 minutes, and five minutes to check names and collect work, totalling 35 minutes. Every child marks a picture record. Spoken explanations from a rotating six supplement teaching-day records; assessment pictures and frames provide evidence for all 30 without conferences.

Scoring: each component below earns one point when the child's record shows it accurately, zero otherwise. Do not score spelling, drawing quality or vocabulary recall alone. All 12 points require using a source or model with land-water ideas on new evidence, including observed patterns. Class models are references for conventions, not answer maps. Earlier practice is unscored. Day 9 retains day 8's source record unchanged and samples only modeling.

Day 10's unscored retry takes ten minutes within its response allocation. A new picture packet, "invented for practice", shows a rounded ridge, a hollow with a liquid pool and a lower stream, plus a second observer's close-up of solid ice on a small pond low beside the stream; an alternative caption gives only a favourite color. Read: "Use the observation sources to mark the water locations and states. Draw a keyed model showing the ridge, hollow and lower water route, and use it to explain the pattern." Every child marks paper and adds a framed reason; note only the components targeted for repair, keeping supported and independent evidence separate.

Day 8 look-fors, six points: chooses A and B because they record Pine Coast, excluding C's different site; uses A's map key; records ocean, river, lake and pond in their supplied locations across both sources; locates ice from A and snow from B; distinguishes those solids from pictured liquid water; states a location-and-state pattern supported by both sources. Example meeting these: "A and B show Pine Coast; C shows somewhere else. A's key locates liquid ocean and river and solid ice on high ground. B adds liquid lake and pond in hollows and solid snow on the ridge. Both show liquid lower and solid higher here." A record with all six placed correctly and that explanation earns six. Below expectation: "I chose C because I like it. Snow is not water." This supplies neither relevant source choice nor accurate snow evidence; correct components still earn their own points.

Day 9 look-fors, six points: represents both hills and the valley with recognisable shapes; represents the river and ocean with recognisable shapes; locates the river between hills and opening into the ocean; supplies a usable key; uses the model to explain the observed lower water route; explains that a map can represent another area using different shapes and locations. Meeting example, with the matching drawing: "These hills have the river between them. My key shows the ocean beyond the river mouth. My arrow follows the lower valley. I could map a different place with its own shapes." Below expectation: a correctly placed and keyed river and ocean, but water shown crossing hilltops: "Water goes uphill here." Credit the correct components; route reasoning is E. An omitted key is B, omitted hills are D, and an omitted ocean is G; none is inferred from unrelated wrong work.

Access: read every prompt and caption aloud neutrally; provide large pictures, tactile outlines, pointing, oral recording or a scribe without selecting evidence or placing features for the child.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A preferred place is a better source than recorded observations. | Preference substitutes for evidence. Repair by matching a captioned observation to the question. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | A symbol needs no key because everyone will know it. | The reader cannot identify the representation. Repair by having a partner interpret an unfamiliar symbol, then adding its meaning. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Snow is not water. | Solid water is excluded. Repair by tracing snow changing to liquid in paired photographs. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Land shapes can be omitted from a land-water model. | Only water is represented. Repair by placing hill and valley outlines around the water route. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | Surface runoff crosses a hill upward. | The route ignores relative height. Repair by tracing a low route in the supplied pour sequence. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | A second source cannot add information. | The first source is treated as complete. Repair by finding a detail present only in the second record. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| G | An ocean shown in the area need not appear in its model. | A relevant water body is excluded. Repair by matching each pictured water body to the model. | Anticipated, from memory. |

Each letter describes only its stated error. Other incomplete responses receive descriptive feedback rather than a guessed misconception.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

Allow $4 of the $50 annual class ceiling: $3 reusable pieces and $1 cards. Borrow remaining supplies or use paper; six groups of five. No digital tool is needed.

| Day | Materials, safety and set-up |
|---|---|
| 1 | Per child: photograph and sketch paper. Check the doorway route the day before. Stay behind the dry threshold, away from runoff and drains. No special eye protection; reserve two minutes to pack up. |
| 2 | Per group: storage tray, damp clean play sand, three smooth large pebbles, small cup of tap water and cloth. Adult checks sand, edges and worktops the day before. Everyone nearby wears splash goggles throughout. Keep material below faces, never throw or taste, wipe spills immediately and wash hands. Adult removes sand into waste, never drains. Allow five clean-up minutes. |
| 4 | Per group: site photographs, five map sheets and pencils. Adult checks paths, weather, insects and allergies the day before; use photographs if unsafe. Stay in sight on dry paths; touch no organisms. No special eye protection. Seed-head collection, burr inspection and dry storage are teacher-only. Allow three clean-up minutes. |
| 7 | Per group: two sealed plastic cups, one with ice and one with liquid water, tray, cloth and five source sheets. Adult checks seals the day before and prepares fresh ice before class. Handle briefly, never open or taste, wipe condensation. Use goggles during handling; adult empties cups. Allow four clean-up minutes. |

Stop if controls fail. Without materials, use relief drawings and pour records on day 2, site photographs on days 1 and 4, and ice-water images on day 7. Revise from these weaker, supplied observations.
