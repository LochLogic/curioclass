# Section 5.2: Compare growth evidence and revise explanations

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **2-LS2-1, this section's share (days 52 to 56).** "Investigate whether a plant needs light from the sun and water in order to grow." Children gather and compare the second week's dated records (day 52), read a second case (day 53), conduct a last observation and plan a fresh one (days 54 and 55), and repair and conclude (day 56).
- **Limits that bite here.** As section 5.1: one change at a time, each missing factor compared with the healthy plant only, observable growth, no mechanism. No averages and no connected time graph: day 54's graph is one date, one centimeter per square, a shared zero, three condition labels. Results are reported as they are: if the dry plant does not wilt or the dark plant does not pale, the class says it cannot tell yet.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1 (ruler at the soil) on days 52, 54 and 55; the math single-unit bar graph on day 54, retrieved first. Cause and effect and claim, evidence and reason (unit 4) on days 52 and 56.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Scoring: each look-for is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen, and only "shown" counts a point; the plan has eight planning components and two conducting components, and the interpretation is unscored. (2) Conducting evidence is what each child did, not group attendance. On day 52 the teacher cannot watch all 30 children in 17 minutes, so observed actions are noted for a rotating six and for any child whose record shows a procedure gap; the rest are shown by their own dated record, and the windows on days 54 and 55 watch every child once. (3) The check-day rules: directions read neutrally, no hints, no reteaching. (4) Why Q and P in the puppet problem differ in two things at once (light and water), so neither can be compared with the other. (5) The 45 minutes after day 55 for marking ten components per child. (6) Why day 53 has no live plants.

**Exits, days 52, 53 and 56** (spoken, picture cards, all labeled "invented for practice" and said aloud; every child marks, six rotating give a reason, 20 seconds each).
- **E52** (two items, each yes or "not yet"). Chive plants, matched, started May 2 at 6 centimeters with three green upright leaves. May 4: the watered dark plant and the unwatered sunlit plant are both still 6 centimeters with three green upright leaves. **E52a:** "Pip says the dark plant looks fine, so plants do not need light. Is Pip right yet?" Answer: not yet, keep recording. Wrong: yes (C). **E52b:** "Pip says the sunlit plant looks fine with no water, so sunlight is all it needs. Is Pip right yet?" Answer: not yet, keep recording. Wrong: yes (D).
- **E53.** A basil light test, equally watered. June 2: sunlit plant 7 centimeters, four green upright leaves; shaded plant 10 centimeters, two pale leaves, leaning stem. The shaded plant is moved into sunlight. June 9: sunlit plant 8 centimeters, five green leaves; moved plant still 10 centimeters, four green leaves, firmer stem. "Did the moved plant get healthier? Touch yes or no." Answer: yes; more green leaves and a firmer stem, though its height did not change. Wrong: no, because the height is the same (B).
- **E56.** Matched radish record, all started April 1 at 3 centimeters with two green leaves. April 15: watered sunlight 8 centimeters, four green leaves; watered darkness 11 centimeters, two pale leaves; unwatered sunlight 3 centimeters, two wilted leaves. "Touch the two pairs that test light and water fairly." Answer: watered sunlight with watered darkness, and watered sunlight with unwatered sunlight. Wrong: watered darkness with unwatered sunlight (A). Six then say what in each pair shows the need: pale leaves despite taller (light), wilted with no gain (water). Wrong: "the dark one is taller, so it is healthier" (B); "water is all a plant needs" (C); "sunlight is all a plant needs" (D).

**Day 56 retries** (unscored, fresh, "invented for practice"; only the missed component, two asks each, answered by touch or yes or no).
- **RA.** Two matched lettuce seedlings: L in a sunny window with water daily, M in a closet with no water. "Pip says L and M show light matters. Touch the two things that are different." Then: "Touch the plant we could add so each comparison changes one thing." Answer: light and water; a watered window plant. Wrong: "only light differs" (A).
- **RB.** A card of two plants: 17 centimeters, pale, leaning, two thin leaves; 12 centimeters, green, firm, four leaves. "Touch the healthier plant." Then: "Touch what tells you." Answer: the shorter; color, leaves and stem. Wrong: the taller (B).
- **RC.** A watered plant kept in a closet, green on Day 1 and pale and limp on Day 12. "Pip says water is all a plant needs. Touch the picture that tells." Answer: the Day 12 picture. Wrong: agreeing with Pip (C).
- **RD.** A plant in a sunny window with no water, limp, beside a watered one, green. "Pip says sun is all a plant needs. Touch the picture that tells." Answer: the limp plant. Wrong: agreeing with Pip (D).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The needs-arrow model and chart from section 5.1. Day 52 turns an arrow solid only where the dated records show a difference from the healthy plant; where they do not, it stays dotted and the class says so. Day 53 adds the tall, pale and weak case to the condition row. Days 54 and 55 add the final observations and a single-date graph. Day 56 joins light and water with evidence and uncertainty in one class explanation. The teacher holds the chart; each child holds their record. Unit 4's claim, evidence and reason is reviewed, not retaught.
- **Key moment 1, days 52, 54 and 55: a height at the soil.** Ideal instrument: a centimeter ruler. Spec: fits; its 1-centimeter step is named beside every reading, and a gain under 1 centimeter is recorded "same". Color, leaves and stem are recorded beside it.
- **Key moment 2, day 54: a single-date bar graph.** Ideal instrument: squared paper, one square a centimeter, at least 24 squares tall. Spec: squared graph paper fits.
- **Key moment 3, days 52 to 55: the dark plant.** The cover is lifted only for the measuring turn; its lifting time is the same on every date, and the result is reported with that said.
- **When a reading fails.** As section 5.1: a spare marked "replaced", or dated measured photographs from earlier plants, said aloud to be kept records. If most plants fail by day 54, the day 54 and 55 observations use those photographs and the conducting points are not offered.
- **A room with nothing.** The same photographs and cards; the eight planning components can still be shown, the two conducting components cannot, and the record says so.
- **Clean-up.** Days 52, 54 and 55: goggles wiped with one damp cloth a table and bagged (1 minute), tables and hands wiped with a damp cloth, spills wiped at once; hands are washed at the next scheduled washing, said aloud. Day 52 has 5 minutes of clean-up; days 54 and 55 have 3 minutes of moves that include it. No tool is used; there is no key tool.

## 6. Days

## Day 52


- **3 min. Read the feedback.** Each child gets their day 50 table line with one written next step and reads or hears it. Six rotating children say theirs back, 20 seconds each (2 min); the rest check their own (1 min).
- **6 min. Return and repair.** Errors A and B from E51, 3 minutes each: the pair M card beside the pair N card, then the day 51 cards side by side with every difference besides height touched. Small group otherwise.
- **17 min. Revise and gather.** Each child revises their day 50 entry from the feedback (3 min). Goggles on (1 min). Measuring in two rounds of 3 minutes (6 min): three children read a plant each, then two read the same plants again; the others sketch. Everyone enters the date, condition, height and appearance, and notes the difference from the healthy plant, never dark against dry (5 min); the teacher notes observed actions for a rotating six, 20 to 30 seconds each. The adult covers the dark plants (1 min).
- **6 min. Discuss the evidence and revise the model.** Table by table, "what is different from the healthy plant, and what is the same?" (3 min); no duplicate growth reports. Arrows turn solid only where the records show a difference; where they do not, the class says it cannot tell yet. Pip says "one week is enough to be sure about every plant"; the class checks the line against the records (3 min).
- **3 min. Exit.** E52 (two items).
- **5 min. Clean up.** As section 5.1.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart; each child their record. Tool: None. By hand: measuring, sketching, filling the table. Evidence: the day 52 conducting record (two look-fors, kept to day 55) and E52, with the "heard" column. Written feedback on the conducting record follows after class.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice revises the child's day 50 line and compares each treatment with its control; the exit gives dated cards for two new plants and asks yes or not yet about each match, with Pip's claim.

## Day 53


- **6 min. Return and repair.** Errors C and D from E52, 3 minutes each: the day 51 Day 1 beside Day 12 cards for C; a dry plant beside a watered plant for D. If many made one, it runs whole class and the other goes to a small group.
- **3 min. Retrieve the records.** Each child finds their day 52 line and the feedback on it.
- **7 min. Revise the day 52 records.** Alone, on paper, from the feedback (no live plants today); the teacher moves among the tables and notes the revisions. Six rotating children say what they changed, 30 seconds each, inside the 7.
- **12 min. Read a second case.** Printed cards of two matched oat seedlings, equally watered, Day 1, Day 7 and Day 14, all invented for practice. Day 1: both the same size. Day 7: the shaded one 18 centimeters, pale, bent, two thin leaves; the lit one 15 centimeters, green, upright, four leaves. Day 14: shaded 22 centimeters, falling over, two pale leaves; lit 20 centimeters, five green leaves, firm. Each table marks what changed, what stayed the same and what is watched (7 min, 1 minute a table from the teacher); the class says why the taller one may be less healthy (5 min).
- **9 min. Discuss and revise the model.** Pip says "the taller plant is the healthier plant" (B) and the class checks it against the oat cards (3 min). The condition row gets the oat case (2 min). Six rotating children say "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___", 30 seconds each (3 min); one minute to hand cards back.
- **3 min. Exit.** E53.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the chart. Tool: None. By hand: revising, marking cards, saying. Evidence: the revised day 52 records and E53, with the "heard" column.

- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice reads a second matched case, the oat cards, and marks what changed and stayed; the exit gives new basil cards and asks which card shows Pip is wrong, then whether moving the plant helped.

## Day 54


- **12 min. Observation windows.** The first three tables (15 children), three windows of 4 minutes. Each child puts on goggles, reads the assigned class plant at the soil, enters the date, condition and appearance, and says which comparison it belongs to; two rounds of 2 minutes, 5 children and 3 plants. The teacher watches each child about 30 seconds and notes the look-fors on the record sheet. A child with procedure feedback from day 52 reenacts the corrected steps, without coaching, and the teacher appends what was seen to the day 52 record. These observations are unscored practice. The other 15 children draw the unscored single-date graph from their day 52 records (5 min), then add color symbols from their records.
- **3 min. Moves and wipe-down.** Two moves of 30 seconds, then goggles wiped and bagged, hands wiped with a damp cloth and spills wiped; hands washed at the next scheduled washing, said aloud.
- **5 min. Graph.** The 15 who watched draw the single-date graph from their day 52 records (squared paper, one centimeter a square, shared zero, three condition labels). The 15 who graphed earlier check theirs against their records. Unscored.
- **3 min. Revisit the anchor.** The board-and-grass photographs: "What do we now think, and what can we not tell?" Six rotating children answer, 20 seconds each, and the teacher writes nothing as correct.
- **12 min. Draft the fresh plan.** All 30 children, alone, on paper, the framed sheet of eight boxes. The pea-seedling problem is read aloud one sentence at a time (about 1 min) and not repeated. No hint is given. Allow 45 minutes after class for written feedback on each draft.
- Holds what is carried: each child their record and draft; the chart is not pointed to. Tool: None. By hand: measuring, graphing, drawing and writing or scribing. Evidence: the day 54 row of field 3, marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen; no reteaching today.

## Day 56


- **5 min. Return and read the feedback.** Each child gets their record with one written next step for one component and reads or hears it. Six rotating children say theirs back, 30 seconds each (3 min).
- **12 min. Repair by letter groups.** Group A with E, group B, group C, group D, each with the teacher for 2.5 minutes in turn while the others work from picture directions at their table; 2 minutes to move (4 x 2.5 + 2). Hand-backs: E53 (B) and the day 54 and 55 records (A, B, C, D, E). Group A: the proposed watered sunlight plant and what differs for each pair; E, a modeled complete entry. Class explanation.** The class dictates and the teacher writes: missing light plausibly explains the pale grass; our seedlings show what happens without light or without water (days 50, 52 and 55); the board alone did not isolate a cause, and warmth is still a question. ## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 52:** the day 50 entry and procedure, and the day 51 cards. **Day 53:** the day 52 record, and the day 51 case. **Days 54, 55:** nothing is practiced; they sample days 48 to 53. **Day 56:** the letters from days 52 to 55. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none. Day 53 builds ease with "what changed, what stayed, what is watched" with no timer, by repeating it on one new case.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: healthy, pale, wilted, leaning, firm, control (the healthy plant), same, different, record. Frames: "The ___ plant is taller, but ___." "I cannot tell yet, because ___." Pointing is accepted.
- **Access.** As section 5.1: all captions read aloud, enlarged cards, tactile rulers, a scribe, and a child directing an adult's handling.
- **Check accommodations.** Directions and the problem are read aloud, answers are drawn, pointed or dictated.
- **Reading load.** None alone: labels only, under 150 words a page.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan a comparison for a plant the class has not grown, with the matched cups, soil and dates, and say what result would surprise you.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 52: read the table and compare each plant with the healthy one. Day 53: read the oat cards. Days 54 and 55: the child measures and records one plant and plans the fresh comparison while you watch and listen; give no hint. Day 56 is the last day of the unit: go over the feedback, redo the weakest piece, then say together what light and water did and what we cannot tell. At home, any day skippable, a grown-up asks the exit aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the chart on days 52 and 56 and the oat cards on day 53; days 54 and 55 are plants and paper, with the windows run table by table.
