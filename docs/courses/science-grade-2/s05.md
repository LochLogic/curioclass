## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why is the grass under the board yellow and pale when the grass around it is green?"
Day 48 opens with dated photographs of a board being lifted and the pale grass beside green uncovered grass. Ask the compelling question before offering causes.
Day 56's class explanation uses days 50, 52 and 55: missing light plausibly explains the pale grass; compare seedlings for evidence of light and water needs. Report inconclusive results honestly, using measured fallback records where needed. The board alone did not isolate a cause.
Model ruler use and recording on day 49. Let children choose comparisons, challenge fairness on day 51 and decide what later observations support.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A comparison is fair although a condition besides the intended factor also differs. | An extra changed condition prevents isolating the intended cause. Repair by matching all other conditions before comparing treatments. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | The tallest plant must be the healthiest. | Height substitutes for several growth observations. Repair by comparing height with leaves and stem condition in a tall pale plant record. | Anticipated, from memory; light reference in field 10 supports the counterexample. |
| C | Watered plants can grow healthily indefinitely without light. | Water is treated as sufficient. Repair with a dated watered light and watered dark comparison. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Sunlight replaces a plant's need for water. | Light is treated as sufficient. Repair with a dated watered and unwatered sunlight comparison. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | Height alone makes a complete starting record. | Baseline appearance is omitted. Repair on day 50 by completing leaf and stem boxes beside a height-only entry before measuring. | Anticipated, from memory. |

These are anticipated ideas, not measured frequencies. File omissions and recording mistakes descriptively.
