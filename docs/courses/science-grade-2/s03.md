## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why are rain boots made of rubber and mittens of wool, and not the other way round?"
Day 27 opens on a teacher-dampened paper hat and soggy doll in a tray. Ask the compelling question, then sketch replacements. Limits: keep the doll dry using equal-sized supplied pieces. Test on day 30; improve on day 35.
Day 38's class explanation uses days 30 and 31: materials differ in water passage, so the chosen hat keeps the doll dry under tested conditions.
Model safe tests on day 28 and recording on day 30, then release decisions to children.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


Group children's wet-hat questions on the question board: What properties can we observe (27 to 29)? Which keep water out (30 and 31)? Would a different job change our choice (32 to 34)? What explains the failed hat (35)? Transfer uses new sorting samples and packing and display problems on days 36 and 37.
Limits: describe observable properties, not particles, density, heat insulation or chemical composition. Measure only length. Water climbing a strip compares travel under matched conditions, not total absorption capacity; bending under a fixed load does not measure breaking strength. The boot-and-mitten question motivates different purposes; warmth remains an untested question.
Carried: day 27's initial choice drawing retains unit 2's test-and-compare cycle. Patterns gain shared-property groups on day 28 and temperature-dependent water states on 29. Day 31 revises the graph and structure-function account using water-travel and leakage evidence. Days 33 and 34 add load, bend, press and texture comparisons. Day 35's consensus links purpose, tested property and evidence; use this model on day 37 with new data. Retain these models for day 38.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 36 (End-of-unit assessment (observation)): Plan and carry out a safe test of unfamiliar materials and sort their observed properties, including cooler and warmer samples.

| Day | Code | Part (own words) | The item, answer and wrong answers (error letter) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 36 | 2-PS1-1 | Investigate materials to describe them and sort them by properties you can observe, such as color, texture, hardness and bendiness. | 24 minutes of observation windows, four per group, six points. New problem: sort three unfamiliar safe samples for a classroom materials drawer. Read: "Agree how to compare and sort these samples. Each show a proposed test and what stays steady; do your test and record what the group finds. Use the cooler and warmer water evidence too." Each child proposes and performs one gentle test. Share color, texture, press, bend and light observations, then individually mark a picture chart, sort by a chosen property and show a shared-property pattern. Adult-prepared sealed ice and lukewarm water give qualitative conditions and solid-liquid evidence. Answer depends on inspected samples: credit observations that match them, a consistent sorting rule, and ice as solid, warmer water as liquid. Wrong: rough means hard without pressing (A); one color makes samples identical in all properties (B); ice is not water (C); change both lamp distance and sample to compare light passage (D). | section 3.2 |

Day 37 (End-of-unit assessment): Choose materials for unfamiliar jobs using property-test data and explain how the tested properties support each choice.

| Day | Code | Part (own words) | The item, answer and wrong answers (error letter) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 37 | 2-PS1-2 | Use test results from different materials to decide which material's properties best fit a given job. | 25 minutes, fourteen points. New packet, "invented for practice": equal-sized card X, sheet Y and cloth Z were tested under matched conditions. Water climbed 3, 0 and 6 centimeters respectively; equally loaded strips bent 1, 5 and 3 centimeters; gentle pressing left no dent in X but dents in Y and Z. X and Z feel rough; Y feels smooth. On the same pictured sloping surface, with the same block and slope, X and Z linings held the block still; Y let it slip. Y and Z wrap a curve without cracking; X cracks. "Organize these records by tested property. Choose for each pictured job and show the evidence: a rigid display ledge, a curved parcel wrapper, a press-resistant stamping base, a rough non-sliding lining, and a mat that draws water farthest along a strip. Use our choice model to explain why changing purpose can change a choice." Answer: X for ledge and base, Y or Z for wrapper, X or Z for lining because both prevented slipping in the matched test, Z for mat; link each to the supplied comparison. Texture alone does not establish grip on another surface. A final picture of a bamboo plant, a bamboo pole and a bridge rail asks how material knowledge helps people design an object from natural materials. Accept testing the bamboo before choosing it, or naming the pole's property (straight, not bending) that the rail needs. Wrong: Y is best for every job because it is favourite (E); Y catches most water because none climbed (F); greatest bend means stiffest ledge (G). | section 3.3 |

Day 36 totals 35 minutes: six minutes for directions, transitions and clean-up, the six four-minute group windows, and five minutes independently revisiting the hat without feedback. Waiting children sort familiar pictures; after their window they finish their own new record. The last group completes its chart during the final five minutes alongside its hat drawing. Within each window, hear each child's brief proposal and observe one action; peers share the remaining tests. Record individual participation, not a group spokesperson. No earlier practice is scored. Day 37 uses five minutes for read-aloud directions, 25 for the item and five to collect, also 35 minutes. Carry day 36's record unchanged; do not rescore it or reteach on either day.

Day 36 look-fors, one point each: proposes a relevant test with a controlled condition; conducts it safely; records the five observed properties accurately; sorts consistently by an observed property; classifies cooler ice and warmer liquid water using shape or flow evidence; identifies a property shared across different samples. The teacher checks the named day 36 samples before class and makes an answer chart from their actual properties, including contrasts and a shared property. Meeting example: "I keep the lamp here and change the sample. These two block light, so they go together. Ice keeps its shape; warmer water moves inside the cup." With an accurate complete chart and observed action, this earns six. Below expectation: "These are both brown, so they bend the same" (B). Credit any separately accurate observations, never infer an action from a picture alone.

Day 37 scoring: organizing the length evidence and the qualitative evidence earns one point each. Each of five jobs earns one point for a supported selection and one for a relevant test comparison, ten points total. Explaining why purpose changes the choice earns one point; the natural-material design connection earns one. Thirteen of fourteen points use property investigation or analysis on new cases. Use the consensus picture model to connect each purpose, property and result. Meeting example: "X bends least under the same load, so it is the best tested choice when the job needs less bending. Z lets water climb 6 centimeters, so it draws water farthest along the mat. Y bends more and wraps without cracking, so it suits the wrapper." A complete organized record covering all jobs and both final explanations earns fourteen. Below expectation: "Y is my favourite, so use it for everything" (E). Award only independently correct components.

A three-minute section exit on day 31 uses new length data, blank labeled axes and leakage pictures, "invented for practice": strips M and N each have water rise 4 centimeters; M leaves the doll dry and N leaves it wet. Supply one-centimeter grid steps and M and N labels. Read: "Draw the two water-travel bars, then choose a rain hat and show why." Answer: equal bars at 4 for M and N; choose M because the doll stayed dry despite equal water travel. Give descriptive feedback on graph errors; choosing N because absorbency must make a better rain hat is H. Day 36's individual paper record also closes section 3.2. Day 38's fresh retry closes section 3.3: new strips, "invented for practice", show smooth flexible P and rough stiff Q. In matched water-entry tests, water climbs 1 centimeter along P and 7 along Q; separate matched hat tests leave the doll dry under P and wet under Q. "Sort the strips by bending, then choose a replacement rain hat using both water-entry and leakage results." Accept P as flexible and keeping the doll dry; Q draws water farther into its strip yet leaks, so greater water entry does not make it a better rain hat. Choosing by preference is E; other incomplete records get descriptive feedback. Everyone marks paper while prompts are read aloud; hear reasons from a rotating six each teaching day.

Day 38 retries target the missed component within the ten-minute allowance, not every component for every child. Additional new pictures, all labeled "invented for practice", show equally rough samples with a dent or no dent under matched gentle pressing for A, same-color strips bending differently for B, an unlabelled sequence of water cooling until it holds its shape in a sealed tray for C, and two light-test plans for D. Read the applicable question: "Which is hard, and what observation shows it?"; "Do these matching colours tell us how they bend?"; "Is the material at the end still water? What changed?"; or "Which plan keeps the light comparison fair?" Accept the undented sample as harder, bend differences, water remaining water while changing from flowing to holding its shape, and a fixed lamp position respectively. For G, compare new equal-load bend lengths of 2 and 4 centimeters and choose the stiffer ledge, 2 centimeters. E and H use the new hat case. For F, new equal-strip pictures show water climbing 0 and 4 centimeters: "Which shows water entering the strip?" Accept the 4-centimeter strip, without claiming its total absorption capacity. Record each corrected component separately. A paper retry cannot establish a missing safe-action point; leave that evidence unconfirmed.

Access: read all captions aloud; enlarge pictures, offer tactile samples, pointing and a scribe. Rehearse property words with gestures during teaching, not during assessment. Preserve the child's test decision and action; record physical assistance separately.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Rough materials must be hard. | Texture substitutes for pressing evidence. Repair by comparing rough soft fabric with smooth hard wood. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Materials of the same color share every property. | One observed feature substitutes for testing others. Repair by bending two same-color samples with different flexibility. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Ice is not water. | Solid water is excluded. Repair by matching ice and its melted liquid in a sealed-cup sequence. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Changing lamp distance along with the material gives a fair light test. | More than the tested material changes. Repair by fixing the lamp position before changing samples. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | A favourite material is best for every job. | Preference substitutes for fitness evidence. Repair by matching one material's result to two contrasting purposes. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | No water climbing a strip means it absorbs the most. | Water blocking is confused with absorption. Repair by tracing water remaining outside beside water entering a strip. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| G | Greater bending under the same load means a stiffer ledge. | The direction of the length comparison is reversed. Repair by aligning bend lengths beside the requirement to stay level. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| H | Absorbency makes a material best for a rain hat. | Water entering the material is confused with keeping the doll dry. Repair by comparing wetness beneath equally exposed hat samples. | Anticipated, from memory. |

Use each letter only for the stated error; record other omissions descriptively. These are anticipated errors, not observed frequencies.
