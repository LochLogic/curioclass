# Section 4.1: The same pieces, and changes that return

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 2. Unit 4, Can we change it back? *Our block castle became a boat, and melted chocolate hardened again, but a cooked egg never runs again: which changes can be undone?* Anchor: three changes shown side by side (a block build taken apart and rebuilt, chocolate melted in a warm-water bath and cooled, an egg cooked by the teacher) (days 39 to 47): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 39 to 42.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (39), fluency 1 (41), application 2 (40, 42). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 2 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 35 to 40 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

- **2-PS1-3, this section's share (days 39 to 41).** "Observe and explain, with evidence, how something built from a few kinds of pieces can be taken apart and rebuilt as something new." Day 39 starts the parts-and-whole sketch; day 40 is the first full record (a drawing before, the take-apart, a drawing after, the pieces matched); day 41 does it again on a second set. The unit's two reassembly items (days 45, 46) are run in section 4.2.
- **2-PS1-4, this section's share (days 39, 42).** "Argue, using evidence, that heating or cooling causes some changes that can be undone and some that cannot." Day 39 poses the question and takes only guesses. Day 42 gives the "can be undone" evidence (water, chocolate) with an attempted return. The "cannot" half is day 43, in section 4.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Observable changes only: no particles, no measured temperature ("cooler" and "warmer" are words, as in unit 3) and no weight. A change back need not restore the outline. Waiting is not an attempted return: the temperature is changed back and then looked at. All heating is done by an adult before class; children see photographs and sealed samples. Nothing is eaten.
- **Connections.** Writing W.2.8, day 40: a before-and-after drawing and one framed sentence, spoken first. Unit plan connection, day 42: scientists look for causes to explain what happens in nature. Crosscutting: systems (days 39, 41), cause and effect (day 42).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Matching is counting: a line from each old piece to its place in the new whole, with no number said. (2) A new arrangement is not new pieces (error A). (3) An attempted return is a third look after the temperature is changed back. (4) Why water and chocolate come before the egg: they are the clear returns, and the egg is the contrast. (5) Egg and milk allergies: the photograph path is always offered, and no child is asked what is eaten at home. (6) One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

- goal: Compare a block castle becoming a boat, chocolate rehardening and a cooked egg to ask what can change back.
- note: Hook and initial change model. Retrieve s1-see-with-light accounts and s1-like-parents evidence and reason, plus unit 3 water states. Food sensitivity: sealed displays or photographs.

<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Three cards read aloud, "invented for practice": a toy in a dark box seen only under a lamp (grade 1 review: we see it when light shines on it); a seedling with its parent's leaf shape but smaller (grade 1 review: alike, not exact copies); two water pictures from unit 3, one holding its shape and one flowing (review: solid water, liquid water). Children mark the observation on each card (1 min). Six rotating children say why, one card each, 20 seconds each (2 min). A miss: the teacher models observation-to-claim on a second picture (1 min). No heredity and no light mechanism.
- **6 min. The hook.** The teacher builds a castle of six large blocks, then in view takes it apart and builds a boat. The adult shows the sealed chocolate and sealed cooked egg at the front (adult-handled only, nothing opened), and the class sees the photographs of chocolate before and after melting and cooling and of the teacher's egg before cooking and after cooling. The compelling question is asked before any kind of change is named.
- **7 min. Take it apart.** Six groups of five, each with six blocks, paper and picture cards, all at once. Each group builds anything, takes it apart, builds something different, and matches old and new pieces by pointing one to one. The teacher circulates, about a minute a group. Blocks stay on the table.
- **5 min. Parts and whole.** Each child draws the group's two builds and the six pieces in boxes on the page. The teacher starts the class parts-and-whole sketch from three children's drawings.
- **8 min. Three changes.** Each group lays out three picture pairs (blocks, chocolate, egg) and makes a change arrow for each: before, arrow, after, and "I think ___ goes back or stays, because I saw ___." These are guesses and are kept as guesses. Six rotating children say theirs, 20 seconds each (2 min), inside this part. Pip says: "The egg will go back if we cool it." The class does not answer yet; the line goes on the board.
- **3 min. Question board.** The class groups its notices into five questions (castle pieces, another set, changing the temperature back, does that always work, what evidence settles a disagreement), each tagged with the day that answers it.
- **3 min. Exit.** E39.
- **2 min. Pack up.** Blocks to the tray.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the sketch, arrows and board; each group its blocks and cards. Tool: None. By hand: building, drawing, sorting. Evidence: E39 and the retrieval marks, with the "heard" column. Food: allergies are checked first, a child may use the photographs only, and no child is asked what is eaten at home.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: piece, whole, same, different, take apart, change, melt, harden, freeze, back. Frames: "It is made of the same ___." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing is accepted.
- **Access.** Read every caption aloud; offer enlarged or tactile pieces, pointing and a scribe, without supplying a decision. Keep the child's own decision and the physical help apart on the record.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are spoken; a child may touch, point or say yes or no.
- **Reading load.** Nothing alone beyond labels under 150 words a page; everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a third different whole from the same pieces and match them again.
