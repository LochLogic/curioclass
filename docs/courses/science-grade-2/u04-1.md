## Header


## 1. Standards and practices

> Group the day 39 question board in two strands, pieces (40, 41) and heating and cooling (42 to 44): Where did the castle pieces go (40)? Can another set make different objects (41)? Does changing temperature back restore things (42)? Does that always work (43)? What evidence settles a disagreement (44)? Each answer raises the next question. Transfer uses unfamiliar card rocket and bus builds and new butter, cookie, juice and cucumber records.

- **2-PS1-3, this section's share (days 39 to 41).** "Observe and explain, with evidence, how something built from a few kinds of pieces can be taken apart and rebuilt as something new." Day 39 starts the parts-and-whole sketch; day 40 is the first full record (a drawing before, the take-apart, a drawing after, the pieces matched); day 41 does it again on a second set. The unit's two reassembly items (days 45, 46) are run in section 4.2.
- **2-PS1-4, this section's share (days 39, 42).** "Argue, using evidence, that heating or cooling causes some changes that can be undone and some that cannot." Day 39 poses the question and takes only guesses. Day 42 gives the "can be undone" evidence (water, chocolate) with an attempted return. The "cannot" half is day 43, in section 4.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Observable changes only: no particles, no measured temperature ("cooler" and "warmer" are words, as in unit 3) and no weight. A change back need not restore the outline. Waiting is not an attempted return: the temperature is changed back and then looked at. All heating is done by an adult before class; children see photographs and sealed samples. Nothing is eaten.
- **Connections.** Writing W.2.8, day 40: a before-and-after drawing and one framed sentence, spoken first. Unit plan connection, day 42: scientists look for causes to explain what happens in nature. Crosscutting: systems (days 39, 41), cause and effect (day 42).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Matching is counting: a line from each old piece to its place in the new whole, with no number said. (2) A new arrangement is not new pieces (error A). (3) An attempted return is a third look after the temperature is changed back. (4) Why water and chocolate come before the egg: they are the clear returns, and the egg is the contrast. (5) Egg and milk allergies: the photograph path is always offered, and no child is asked what is eaten at home. (6) One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none run here. The two 2-PS1-3 rows printed above name section 4.2 as owner, because days 45 and 46 are in that section; section 4.1 taught the code and adds no item to them. This section's evidence is the record sheet of days 40 and 41 (before and after drawings, pieces matched, each look-for marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen) and one exit on each teaching day. Every exit is spoken, with picture cards, "invented for practice" said aloud, and answered by touching or by yes or no. Held back from teaching.

- **E39.** Cards: a toy train of five cars in a row, then the same cars joined in a curving line. "Pip says the curving line needed new cars. Is Pip right? Point to yes or no." Answer: no; the same five cars, a new arrangement. Wrong: yes, "it looks different" (A).
- **E40.** Cards: a round piece and a long flat piece from a block set. "Pip wants a ramp for a toy car. Touch the piece Pip should use." Answer: the long flat piece; the round one rolls away. Wrong: the round one, or "any piece does the same job" (E, nearest: a shape that suits the use, not stiffness).
- **E41.** Cards: a stiff card strip standing straight and a floppy paper strip drooping, each taped to a card gate on a toy fence. "Pip says the floppy strip holds the gate up as well as the stiff one. Is Pip right? Point to yes or no." Answer: no; the stiff strip keeps its shape. Wrong: yes, "they are the same size" (E).
- **E42.** Three cards laid left to right, first, then, last: a birdbath of liquid water, the birdbath frozen, the birdbath liquid again. "Touch the two pictures that show the water went back." Answer: the first and last (either liquid picture at either end). Wrong: the first two, "it froze, so it went back" (D). The unit plan's puddle sequence is changed to a birdbath, and its two asks are one; see Plan points in the report.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** From unit 3: the choice model (job, property, test, choice) and the solid and liquid water record. Built here: the parts-and-whole sketch (started day 39, revised day 41 with a second set so that shape and stiffness each suit a use); the change-arrow model (before, arrow, after, and the line "I think ___, because I saw ___", started day 39, with return arrows added day 42); the question board (grouped day 39, one question answered each day). The teacher holds all three on the wall; each child holds a record sheet.
- **Key moment 1, day 40: a recorded take-apart.** Ideal instrument: the child's own blocks and a printed before-and-after sheet. Spec: none needed; paper fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 42: the return.** Ideal instrument: the class watching a melt and a freeze. Spec: none; adult-prepared photographs plus sealed cups. The cups are looked at, not measured, so no instrument step applies. If the ice cup has not melted by the second look, the dated photograph strip supplies the melt and the teacher says so aloud.
- **A room with nothing.** Packaging card cut into rounded shapes or kitchen blocks; photographs only for water and chocolate; a zip bag in place of a sealed cup. Weaker for firsthand looking; never claim an observed action from a picture.

## 6. Days

## Day 39


- **5 min. Retrieval.** Three cards read aloud, "invented for practice": a toy in a dark box seen only under a lamp (grade 1 review: we see it when light shines on it); a seedling with its parent's leaf shape but smaller (grade 1 review: alike, not exact copies); two water pictures from unit 3, one holding its shape and one flowing (review: solid water, liquid water). Children mark the observation on each card (1 min). Six rotating children say why, one card each, 20 seconds each (2 min). A miss: the teacher models observation-to-claim on a second picture (1 min). No heredity and no light mechanism.
- **6 min. The hook.** The teacher builds a castle of six large blocks, then in view takes it apart and builds a boat. The adult shows the sealed chocolate and sealed cooked egg at the front (adult-handled only, nothing opened), and the class sees the photographs of chocolate before and after melting and cooling and of the teacher's egg before cooking and after cooling. The compelling question is asked before any kind of change is named.
- **7 min. Take it apart.** Six groups of five, each with six blocks, paper and picture cards, all at once. Each group builds anything, takes it apart, builds something different, and matches old and new pieces by pointing one to one. The teacher circulates, about a minute a group. Blocks stay on the table.
- **5 min. Parts and whole.** Each child draws the group's two builds and the six pieces in boxes on the page. The teacher starts the class parts-and-whole sketch from three children's drawings.
- **8 min. Three changes.** Each group lays out three picture pairs (blocks, chocolate, egg) and makes a change arrow for each: before, arrow, after, and "I think ___ goes back or stays, because I saw ___." These are guesses and are kept as guesses. Six rotating children say theirs, 20 seconds each (2 min), inside this part. Pip says: "The egg will go back if we cool it." The class does not answer yet; the line goes on the board.
- **3 min. Question board.** The class groups its notices into two strands of five questions: pieces (castle pieces, another set) and heating and cooling (changing the temperature back, does that always work, what evidence settles a disagreement), each tagged with the day that answers it.
- **3 min. Exit.** E39.
- **2 min. Pack up.** Blocks to the tray.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the sketch, arrows and board; each group its blocks and cards. Tool: None. By hand: building, drawing, sorting. Evidence: E39 and the retrieval marks, with the "heard" column. Food: allergies are checked first, a child may use the photographs only, and no child is asked what is eaten at home.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds, takes apart and circles whether each of three pictured changes goes back; the exit gives two new cases, toy train cars and toast, and asks how the child knows which can go back.

## Day 40


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error A from E39, at most 5 minutes: the castle and boat pictures; string from each block in the castle to its place in the boat.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** "What was the castle made of? What was the boat made of?" Pairs point at the class sketch and say it (1.5 min each child).
- **4 min. A modeled record.** The teacher, with six blocks, draws a before picture, takes it apart, builds a different whole, draws the after picture, draws matching lines, and says the sentence. The record is shown on the wall.
- **12 min. My own take-apart.** Each child alone, with their own six blocks and sheet, 30 children at once: draw before (2), take apart (1), build a different whole (3), draw after (2), match with lines (2), check with a neighbor (2). The teacher circulates and marks the look-fors on the record sheet as seen.
- **5 min. A piece and its use.** Pairs choose one piece in the new whole and say what its shape does (3 min; 15 pairs, 1.5 minutes each child). Six rotating children say it to the teacher, 20 seconds each (2 min).
- **4 min. One sentence.** Spoken to a partner first (1 min), then written or scribed (3 min): "My ___ became a ___. I used the same pieces." (W.2.8, spoken first.)
- **3 min. Pack up.** Blocks counted back, three minutes as in the unit plan.
- **3 min. Exit.** E40.
- **1 min. Board.** "Where did the castle pieces go?" is answered: into the boat.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the sketch and board; each child their record sheet. Tool: None. By hand: building, drawing, matching. Evidence: the record sheet and E40. Hear six rotating children in the piece-and-use part.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds a first whole, takes it apart, draws a different whole and joins old pieces to new; the exit gives a new bridge and gate to find a hidden piece, and asks which piece fits a roof.

## Day 41


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error E from E40, at most 5 minutes: a stiff and a floppy strip held up as a sign; the class says which stays standing.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** Partners say their day 40 sentence to each other, 45 seconds each way.
- **4 min. Stiff and floppy.** Each group has one stiff card strip and one floppy paper strip. Five children try both, 15 seconds each strip (2.5 min of the 4). Unit 3 words: stiff, bends easily.
- **10 min. The card set.** Each child alone, with six rounded card shapes: build a first whole and draw it (3), take apart, build a different whole (3), draw after (2), match with lines (2). The set differs from the blocks in what it can do: flat and light, not stacking high.
- **6 min. Second-set account (fold-in).** This replaces repeated block retellings. Pairs say the account, "same pieces, a different whole, this shape does ___, the stiff card ___", 1.5 minutes each (3 min). Six rotating children say it to the teacher, 30 seconds each (3 min).
- **4 min. Revise the parts model.** The card set goes beside the block set on the class sketch; the class names what each set can and cannot do (shape and stiffness each suit a use).
- **3 min. Pack up.** Cards counted back.
- **3 min. Exit.** E41.
- **1 min. Board.** "Can another set make different objects?" is answered: yes, and the set matters.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the sketch with two sets. Tool: None. By hand: building, drawing, trying strips. Evidence: the record sheet and E41. Nothing is taught as new.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds a second set from card shapes and tells the account; the exit gives two new bookends, one stiff and one floppy, with Pip's claim that they hold books alike, and asks yes or no.

## Day 42


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Error E from E41, at most 5 minutes: a stiff and a floppy strip held up as a sign; the class says which stays standing.
- **3 min. Retrieval.** New pond pictures, "invented for practice": mark the solid water and the liquid water and show what tells you (ice holds its shape, liquid flows). A miss: tilt an adult-prepared sealed cup.
- **7 min. Chocolate.** The adult's photograph strip (the sealed bag in a warm-water bath, liquid, then cooled hard in a flat sheet, a different outline) is laid out; the bath was cleared before children came in. Each group passes a sealed cooled sample, 5 children at 15 seconds each (1.25 min of the 7). The class marks: what the adult did to try to bring it back (cooled it), hard again, outline different (error B).
- **8 min. Water.** Each group has a tray with a sealed ice cup and a sealed liquid cup. Children draw both now (3 min); the ice cup stays on the tray. Dated photographs show the same water liquid, frozen in the adult's freezer (children never touch it) and liquid again: the return was made by warming.
- **6 min. Return arrows.** The class adds a return arrow to the change model for the water and the chocolate (3 min), and says what was done to change the temperature back. Six rotating children say "I think ___ went back, because I saw ___", 30 seconds each (3 min).
- **2 min. Look again.** Groups look at the sealed ice cup a second time: any liquid? Looked at, not measured. If it is unchanged, the photograph strip supplies the melt, said aloud.
- **3 min. Exit.** E42.
- **5 min. Clean up.** The adult disposes of the cups and the bag and clears spills at once; children wipe the tray and their hands with a damp cloth. Only sealed items were handled; hands are washed at the next scheduled washing, said aloud, because 30 children at two sinks cannot wash in this time. Adult splash goggles were worn for the warm-water bath before class.
- **1 min. Board.** "Does changing the temperature back restore things?" gets its first answer: sometimes.
- Holds what is carried: the teacher the change model and the dated strips. Tool: None. By hand: marking photographs, drawing, handling sealed cups. Evidence: E42. Food: nothing eaten, photograph path always offered.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice handles sealed samples, draws both water cups and adds return arrows; the exit gives three new birdbath cards and asks the child only to touch the two that show the water went back.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 39, "What stayed the same when the castle became a boat?" Day 40, "Which of your pieces is in both pictures?" Day 42, "What did we do to try to get it back, and what did we see?"
- **Written task, day 40.** The framed sentence, spoken first, then written or scribed: "My ___ became a ___. I used the same pieces."
- **Judged by the teacher with look-fors:** names both wholes; says the pieces are the same, shown by matching lines; names one piece's shape and a use. Each is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 39:** the grade 1 account of seeing and the alike-not-exact pattern; solid and liquid water (unit 3). **Day 40:** the day 39 sketch. **Day 41:** the day 40 sentence; stiff and bends easily (unit 3). **Day 42:** solid and liquid water from units 1 and 3. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: piece, whole, same, different, take apart, change, melt, harden, freeze, back. Frames: "It is made of the same ___." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing is accepted.
- **Access.** Read every caption aloud; offer enlarged or tactile pieces, pointing and a scribe, without supplying a decision. Keep the child's own decision and the physical help apart on the record.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are spoken; a child may touch, point or say yes or no.
- **Reading load.** Nothing alone beyond labels under 150 words a page; everything else is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a third different whole from the same pieces and match them again.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use six blocks or cut card. Day 39: build, take apart, build something different, match with a finger. Day 40: draw before and after and say the sentence. Day 41: do it again with card. Day 42: look at the photographs and a sealed cup of ice you froze the day before; any heating is done by you before the lesson.
- **One projector.** Show the sketch and the photograph strips; the take-apart stays on the table, one group at a time if blocks are few.
