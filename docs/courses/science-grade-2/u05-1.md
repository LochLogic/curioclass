# Section 5.1: Plan and begin fair plant tests

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 2. Unit 5, What plants need. *Why is the grass under the board yellow and pale when the grass around it is green?* Anchor: the yellow, pale grass found under a board or tarp left on the lawn for two weeks (or a photograph pair, where the ground is frozen or snow-covered); the dark-shelf plant is kindergarten unit 4's anchor, so it is not used here (days 48 to 56): see [s05.md](s05.md). **Days 48 to 51.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (48, 51), application 2 (49, 50). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 2 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 35 to 40 minutes.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **2-LS2-1** (days 48, 49, 50, 51): Investigate whether a plant needs light from the sun and water in order to grow.
- skill s2-plant-needs-test (grade 2)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Day 48 opens with dated photographs of a board being lifted and the pale grass beside green uncovered grass. Ask the compelling question before offering causes.
> Day 56's class explanation uses days 50, 52 and 55: missing light plausibly explains the pale grass; compare seedlings for evidence of light and water needs. Report inconclusive results honestly, using measured fallback records where needed. The board alone did not isolate a cause.
> Model ruler use and recording on day 49. Let children choose comparisons, challenge fairness on day 51 and decide what later observations support.
>
> Question board: What changed under the board (48)? How could we test light separately from water (49)? What changes can we record (50)? Is taller always healthier (51)? What do repeated observations show (52 and 53)? Which causes does our evidence support (56)? Each answer raises the next question. Transfer diagnoses a flawed pea comparison at a puppet stage and plans missing controlled comparisons.
> Carried: retain unit 4's claim, evidence and cause reasoning. Day 48 starts a needs-arrow model and comparison chart. Day 50 adds dated measurements. Day 51 revises the idea that taller means healthier using pale, weak stems. Day 52 revises causal claims from matched records; day 54 adds a single-date graph and day 55 final observations. Day 56's consensus joins light and water needs with evidence and uncertainty. Children use their revised models in transfer plans.
<!-- /skel -->

- **2-LS2-1, this section's share (days 48 to 51).** "Investigate whether a plant needs light from the sun and water in order to grow." Here the children plan the investigation (day 48), set it up and start the dated record (days 49 and 50), and test what counts as fair and as healthy (day 51). The conclusion about the needs is drawn in section 5.2; here they gather and judge evidence.
- **Limits that bite here.** Each test changes only one thing at a time: the dark plant and the dry plant are each compared with the healthy plant, never with each other. Observable growth only: no photosynthesis, soil nutrients or germination. Warmth stays on the question board as an untested question; no cooler plant is added. Heights are read to the whole centimeter, so a change under 1 centimeter reads "same" and colour, leaves and stem carry the rest.
- **Connections.** Math 2.MD.A.1 (ruler zero at the soil, whole centimeters), retrieved on day 49. Crosscutting cause and effect, days 48 and 51. Unit 4's spoken claim, "I think ___, because I saw ___, so ___", day 51.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A fair comparison here: the healthy plant has light and water, and each other plant lacks exactly one. "Control" is the teacher's word; children say "the healthy plant". (2) The board on the lawn changed light, rain and perhaps warmth together, so it cannot say which one mattered. (3) A plant grown in the dark often grows tall, pale and weak, so tall is not healthy; the cause is not taught. (4) The dark plant is covered except for the short measuring turn, said aloud to the children. (5) Scoring is marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. (6) Real readings and fallbacks: field 5.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: plant, light, water, dark, dry, pale, ruler, centimeter, same, different. Frames: "The ___ plant has ___ but not ___." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Pointing is accepted.
- **Access.** Read every caption and prompt aloud. Offer enlarged high-contrast cards, condition symbols, tactile rulers, a scribe and pointing. A child directs an adult's handling steps and supplies the observations and decisions; record this.
- **Check accommodations.** Exits are read aloud, answered by touch, drawing or voice.
- **Reading load.** None alone: labels only, under 150 words a page, 18 point.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan a fourth plant that would test only one more thing, and say what would change your mind.
