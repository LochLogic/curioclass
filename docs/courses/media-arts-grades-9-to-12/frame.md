# Course frame: Media Arts, grades 9 to 12

Course `media-arts-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `media-arts-9-12`), the high school band course in media arts, run as levels, not grades: Level 1 (Proficient), Level 2 (Accomplished), Level 3 (Advanced, at honors depth) and the optional Level 4 (Advanced, deepened, a series year the student leads), each a full year taken in turn (STANDARD.md 3.2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one level at a time, and each level's part of this file is that level's frame (data/courses.csv, "high school levels I to III, not grades").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 19: five in each of Levels 1 to 3 and four in Level 4. Levels 1 to 3 share one shape, so a thread
  comes back one level harder in the same place: an opening unit (the bridge in Level 1, a review and placement unit
  in Levels 2 and 3), then four units that each join two progression threads in the pairing the band below uses
  (media-arts-grades-k-to-2): reading media and its place in society; ideas and production; making and judging; and
  a showing unit that ends the level with a screening of a collection. Every unit makes, studies other makers' media
  work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Level 4 is one sustained, self-directed media series in four phases:
  proposal and studio, criticism, a piece for a real audience, and the series showing (APPROACH "High school levels
  and music"). Media arts here means pictures, sound and moving pictures framed, put in order, captured, joined and
  shown: photographs and photo sequences, sound pieces, animation, short film, interactive and branching pieces, and
  the screenings and channels that carry them. Every piece has a paper form (a printed photo sequence, a drawn
  animation strip or flip book, a sound score performed live, a branching paper piece), so a shared school device is
  one way in, never the only one.
- **Days.** 640: 160 a level for four levels (Level 4 optional). Each level: 154 days in its units, then course-level
  checks of 1 check day and 1 respond day at mid-year and 2 check days and 2 respond days at year-end, 6 in all. One lesson of 45 minutes a day, five a week,
  32 weeks (STANDARD.md 3.2 and 3.4: a level is a full 160-day year of 45 minutes, the 120 hours of the national
  Proficient descriptor; a plan never needs more than one period a day, bar criterion 9). The 45 minutes hold the
  set-up routine (about 3 minutes, devices and trays out where used), the teaching and making, and clean-up,
  hand-back and wiping of shared devices and collecting work (about 5 minutes; 7 in a cutting and gluing session); a
  readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and, in units
  over 25 days (every unit but the openers), one mid-unit assessment named in the unit's evidence line, and one
  respond day after the end-of-unit assessment. Unit days follow the rows' load: ideas and production units own five
  ids and the team production; making and judging units carry a finished piece with two critique stages; the showing
  units own five ids, a collection and two showings; the reading units own four ids, mostly sittings and one made
  piece. These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places them one a school day, so
  course day n of a level falls in that level's week n / 5, rounded up. National time figure: STANDARD.md 3.4 names
  weekly figures for K to 8 pages only; a high school level is set by the 120 hours of the national descriptor, which
  the full year meets (60 hours on the short path). The course is written so a teacher without media arts training
  can teach it; a school with a media arts teacher uses that teacher.
- **Short path.** Each level marks an 80-day path (a semester or a rotation) that keeps every unit and every id by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.2). On it: Level 1 units of 6 (the bridge), 15, 17, 18 and 20
  days; Levels 2 and 3 units of 3 (the review), 16, 19, 18 and 20 (units 4, 9 and 14 fold their reception drill into the
  written critique and give the day to the showing unit's dress run, scope and sequence fix of 2026-11-26); Level 4 units of 24, 14, 20 and 18; each level's
  mid-year check keeps 1 check day and 1 respond day (day 39 and 40 of the 80, week 8), and its year-end check 1 check
  day and 1 respond day (days 79 and 80, week 16, the half-year finals week). Each unit keeps, in this order and no
  fewer: the study of a work that carries the unit's response and its connecting step; the technique shown with its
  step card; the making days with the critique and the revision; the mid-unit checkpoint; the showing or the sitting
  (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into openers, the two rows' understanding days merge, and the piece
  is shorter (a 60-second piece in place of a 2- to 3-minute one; six frames of a sequence in place of twelve). A
  merged day never drops a critique, a revision, a mid-unit checkpoint or a respond day (lesson 21); the scope and
  sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, media arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The levels rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.2): Level 1 claims only the 18 Proficient ids (`.I`, `.Ia` and so on), Level 2 only the 18
  Accomplished ids (`.II`, `.IIa`), Level 3 only the 18 Advanced ids (`.III`, `.IIIa`): 54 in all, the count in
  STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its own level. Level 4 claims no new id: its four rows apply
  eleven Level 3 ids (the unlettered and `a` parts) again to new, self-directed work, and a student who ended Level 3
  at "partly" on one of them reaches it there. No grade 8 id is claimed (the bridge teaches grade 8 techniques and
  words as readiness only). Every level touches all four artistic processes (creating, producing, responding,
  connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit but the openers touches at least two. In the Units table the seventh
  column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and
  the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, and each
  id's action against the national standard's own text where a words line may have drifted, the nine criteria of the
  arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set
  adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py
  checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each level's scope and sequence still states its id
  count (18) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Levels, not grades.** The scope and sequence and every page name the level, never a grade; the `grade` column of
  data/progressions/arts.csv (9, 10, 11, 12) is a data key only. Entry: a student new to media arts starts at Level 1,
  in any grade, and Level 1 assumes no earlier media arts learning. Each later level requires the one before, or a
  placement: the student shows work scored against the previous level's year-end check and end-of-unit guides, and
  starts at the next level if it meets them; the review unit that opens Levels 2 and 3 is that placement. One level is
  a complete course and is enough for Proficient; a school need not offer all four. Advanced is claimed by assessed
  work, not by finishing Level 3.
- **Base text.** None. Media arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain films, photographs and posters from library and archive collections (the Library of Congress, the
  National Archives), films a public film agency streams free on its own pages, and, for living or recent makers, a
  description in our own words with a link to the maker's, museum's or distributor's own page, never a copy or an
  embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on
  the item's own page, fails; a swapped work must keep the unit's ids met and the level's range. Each level's works
  include living makers and more than one region and period; across the band they come from Africa (Senegal, Burkina
  Faso, South Africa), the Americas (Chemehuevi, Kanien'kehá:ka, Ho-Chunk and Pechanga, and Inuit makers, Gullah
  Geechee settings, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Brazil, Canada, the United States), Asia (China, Iran), Europe
  (France, the Soviet Union, Hungary, the United Kingdom) and Oceania (a Māori filmmaker of Aotearoa New Zealand).
  No work repeats an earlier media arts band, and each is studied once in the media arts line, K to 12; a maker who
  returns with a different work is named as a revisit with a harder question (Méliès in unit 1, Te Wei in unit 8,
  Skawennati in unit 10, Hopinka in unit 11), and Isuma (unit 15) is the company of the 6 to 8 frame's Zacharias
  Kunuk, a named link. The range is chosen on purpose, since open film and photograph collections lean to old European and American work (the visual arts pilot read
  flagged a lean on one tradition and no Native American work; each level here has a Native maker). Unseen work on a
  check is a public domain or openly licensed piece or a Curio Class original (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). Media
  remix uses only public domain or openly licensed sources, named on the piece. Curio Class writes every step card,
  work card, case card, brief card, record sheet, reception sheet and family note. Facts about works (dates, holders,
  whether a maker is living) are confirmed on the holder's own page when the unit plan is written, with an "as of"
  date where they can change.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Shot and sequence: the grade 8 shot set, framing and a six-shot
  sequence retaught (the bridge), a twelve-shot sequence with emphasis and tone (Level 1), continuity editing and
  juxtaposition cuts (Level 2), hybrid forms that join live action, animation and data or text (Level 3), a series
  with a consistent language of its own (Level 4). Sound: room tone and a sound bed under images (Level 1), a sound
  design that carries persuasion across senses (Level 2), sound used to bridge platforms and forms (Level 3).
  Animation and process: erase-and-redraw and cutout animation combining a usual and a new tool (Level 1), ink-wash
  or material tests repeated after failure (Level 2), rule-based and generative sequences on paper or screen (Level
  3). Interaction: a planned audience moment (Level 1), a branching piece (Level 2), a piece built to hold across
  platforms (Level 3). Presenting: a collection screened for two audiences with a printed program (Level 1), a
  curated collection with a virtual channel plan (Level 2), a promoted showing aimed at a stated effect, with results
  compared (Level 3), a self-run showing of the series (Level 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills with feedback (a shot-call drill, a cut-on-
  action drill, a three-minute storyboard sprint, a sound-level drill, a ten-second analysis of a clip played twice);
  in high school it is mostly the five-minute opener and two or three fluency days a unit. Application: making with
  choice that grows by level (Level 1 sets the problems, Level 2 lets the student pick among them and research,
  Level 3 has the student set the problem, Level 4 is the student's own proposal); the second making day of each
  piece holds the critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: a sitting
  with unseen items, a screening scored where it happens, or a kept product scored after the session. Respond: a
  second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-cut before a second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding,
  fluency, application, the mid-unit checkpoint, application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item, a mid-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide,
  named id by id in the unit's "End-of-unit evidence, id by id" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one
  form for the row, not one item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a sitting with
  unseen items (a public domain, openly licensed or Curio Class original piece played or shown twice, written
  answers); a look-for on the unit's guide (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept
  piece after the session; a kept log, plan or card made on the unit's making days (a planning portfolio, a production
  log, a revision log, a media-ethics check, a source log, a showing plan), scored with the piece; and look-fors marked
  at a screening. A sitting day's 45 minutes: set-up 3; the unseen piece shown twice and the written items 30; each
  student's self-check on the guide 5; collecting 2; 40 in all. A screening day: set-up 5; six teams of five screen
  in turn at the one screen, wall or table for about four minutes each with the change-over (24), the rest as the
  audience with a note card, while the teacher marks look-fors at the screening place; self-check 5; hand-back 5; 39
  in all. A sitting and a screening never share a day, and the teacher watches one place, never a room and a table at
  once. Tool-use evidence (MA:Pr5.1.Ic, IIc and IIIc) is never a check-day task: it is observed over the unit's last
  three production sessions, ten students a session for about 90 seconds each, on an individual record field, and
  screenings stay separate. All marks are in before the matching respond day. The piece's draft, its critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision come
  on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a
  baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two
  marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the portfolio (paper, or a file on the
  school's device) with a one-line reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Level 1's eight rows assume the grade 8 media arts rows (media-arts-grades-6-to-8,
  grade 8), last used one grade back for a student who takes Level 1 in grade 9 and two or more back, or never, for
  one who starts later or is new to media arts. The count starts from Level 1 taken in grade 9; because entry is open
  to every grade, the bridge unit treats every student as possibly new: a readiness check in the last ten minutes of
  the bridge's day 1, after the hook, on the grade 8 shot set, the storyboard and workflow, roles and the critique
  card, and the bridge then teaches those as readiness. Units 2 to 5 assume the bridge's teaching (the same year), so
  each opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener; day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. Levels 2
  and 3: each row assumes the level before's row in its thread, last used the year before when the levels are taken
  in turn, so units 7 to 10 and 12 to 15 open with a retrieval task inside day 2; a student who skipped a year or
  places in from outside takes the review unit, whose day 1 ends with the readiness check and whose placement task
  is scored against the level before's guides. Level 4's rows assume Level 3's, last used the year before, so units
  16 to 19 open with a retrieval task inside day 2. The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 9 to 12 have no stagger rule but the finals weeks (CALENDAR.md rule 4: week 32,
  and week 16 for a half-year course). In every level: mid-year check on day 77 (week 16) with its respond day on day
  78; year-end check on days 157 and 158 (week 32, the finals week) with respond days 159 and 160. On the short path:
  days 39 and 40 (week 8) and days 79 and 80 (week 16, the half-year finals week).
- **Grade 12 calendar.** Any level may be taken in grade 12, and Level 4 usually is. The last weeks hold teaching:
  the showing unit runs to day 156 and the year-end check sits in week 32. Where seniors leave before week 32, the
  days that fold first are the showing unit's days marked for merging on the short path (fluency and the second
  understanding day, never a critique, a revision or a respond day); the year-end check and its respond days move
  forward with them, so a senior sits the check in the last week they attend. A student of 18 or over gets the family
  note themselves and gives their own yes to be recorded or shown (AGENTS.md; the school's own rules still apply).
- **Recording and kept work.** No student is recorded, photographed or filmed without their own agreement (A student under 18 gives their own agreement, with family consent where required. A student aged 18 or over receives the family note and makes their own consent and leave-out choices.); a face or a voice is never required (hands, the work, a shadow, an object or
  the sound alone will do); a student who does not want to be recorded is observed live and the teacher's mark
  stands. Media made in or out of school points at things, places and work, and at people only with their yes; no
  student films strangers, films in a private place, or films while anyone drives. Kept work stays on paper or on
  the student's, family's or school's own device and goes nowhere else (APPROACH "Kept work"); no student posts the
  class's media or another student's media online, and a showing outside class needs each student's agreement under the same rule.
  A virtual channel is planned and mocked up on paper; it is published only in a space the school itself controls,
  by the teacher, with each yes.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. A student under 18 gives their own agreement, with family consent where required. A student aged 18 or over receives the family note and makes their own consent and leave-out choices. The school's own rules still apply. Units that a course page names: unit 2 (it studies early twentieth-century photographs of Native
  people, some showing dress or acts that may carry ceremonial meaning, studied by looking only, beside a Chemehuevi
  photographer's answer to them), unit 7 (wartime propaganda posters and a film on the disappeared of the Chilean
  dictatorship) and unit 11 (a film by a maker affiliated with the Ho-Chunk Nation and the Pechanga Band of Luiseño Indians, guided
  by a recording of his grandmother learning Pechanga from her mother). Swapping a
  work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's family note name the ids not
  claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, card from cereal boxes, charcoal or soft pencil,
  scissors, tape, found objects for sound, a wall or a sheet for a screen; nothing must be bought, and every unit
  names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience and
  the critique partner, every team task has a solo version (one student holds every role, named in turn), and the
  showing is to the family. The teacher needs no media arts training: each unit's teacher page says what to look and
  listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as six
  flashlights and six clip lamps counted once, printing of step cards, work cards, case cards, sheets and programs),
  all optional beside the free path: Level 1 $10, $15, $25, $15, $25 ($90); Level 2 $5, $15, $25, $15, $20 ($80);
  Level 3 $5, $15, $20, $15, $25 ($80); Level 4 $15, $5, $15, $25 ($60). No device is bought. Planning figures, not
  quotes; the ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** No device per student is required. Where a school has them, one shared school device per team of
  five (a camera, a sound recorder, or a computer with a free editor the school approves) is one way in; one teacher
  screen may show a work, a step or a playback. No student account is used, and no student signs up for any service.
  The owned codes that name capture, tools, interactivity, platforms or online channels (MA:Pr5.1.Ic, MA:Pr5.1.IIc,
  MA:Pr5.1.IIIc, MA:Pr4.1.III, MA:Pr6.1.IIa, MA:Cn11.1.Ib, MA:Cn11.1.IIb, MA:Cn10.1.Ib) are met in full on paper: in
  this course a media tool is anything that frames, captures, orders, plays or shows pictures and sound, so a
  paper-path student captures by drawing what a card viewfinder frames or by a photographed or traced shadow,
  animates by a drawn strip or flip book, scores sound on paper and performs it the same way twice, builds
  interactivity as a branching paper piece the audience steers, builds across platforms as one piece made for a wall,
  a printed booklet and a live reading, and curates a virtual channel as a printed channel page and release plan (the
  ids ask to design and curate for channels, not to publish on one); digital identity and online learning are studied
  through Curio Class case cards and printed pages. Shared devices are wiped and handed back by the device card's
  steps.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, card, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  frame-sequence viewer that plays drawn frames in order, a shot-list and storyboard builder, a branching-piece
  builder, a work viewer) has a printed version that does the same job (the flip book or strip, the storyboard sheet,
  the branching card deck) and needs no student account. A tool never judges a finished piece and never generates a
  student's work (APPROACH "Tools").
- **Reading load.** Case cards and work cards are at most one page at a high school reading level, read in class; a
  sitting's written items are short-answer and one paragraph, never a long essay, except the Level 4 criticism unit's
  essay, which has its own drafting days. English learners: each unit teaches its six words in depth, with a picture,
  a clip or a sound and a frame ("The shot ___ makes the viewer ___ because ___."), a student may show a word in a
  made piece instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student made apart from what they wrote (APPROACH
  "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against three free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov), the World Health Organization's
  "Make Listening Safe" pages on safe headphone volume (who.int), and the web accessibility guidelines' rule on
  flashing, success criterion 2.3.1 "Three Flashes or Below Threshold" (w3.org). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH
  "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): no filming, photographing or recording anyone without
  their yes (and the family's under 18); no flashing light in any piece, screening or shadow play, and no strobe, with
  any studied work that flashes described, not shown; screen breaks, with no student at a screen more than about 20
  minutes at a stretch; headphones, where used, at low volume, and no loud sound in anyone's ear; lamps and
  flashlights low-power, never shone in eyes, kept off paper and fabric, no laser pointers; device and lamp cords
  taped down or none; card cut with a blade only by an adult; filming only in the school's own spaces or where the
  school allows, in pairs, never on roads, roofs, water edges or while anyone drives; supplies labeled non-toxic;
  charcoal used with damp wipes and hands washed after. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the
  words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural, Native or hard-history setting name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 2
  (photographs of Native peoples made for a 1907 to 1930 survey; Chemehuevi photography; Brazilian gold miners), unit
  3 (South Africa), unit 4 (Argentina; rural Senegal, 1975), unit 5 (the National Museum of the American Indian's own teaching
  pages; a pan-African festival), unit 7 (United States wartime posters; Soviet civil war posters; the Chilean dictatorship's disappeared),
  unit 8 (China, 1988), unit 9 (the Soviet Union, 1905 and 1925; the Gullah Geechee people of the Sea Islands), unit
  10 (waste pickers of a Rio de Janeiro landfill, Brazil; Kanien'kehá:ka of Kahnawà:ke), unit 11 (Ho-Chunk and
  Pechanga), unit 12 (W. E. B. Du Bois and Black Georgians in 1900; Māori of Waikato and a 1937 to 1940 canoe build), unit 14 (Iran; Senegal and the migrants of Dakar),
  unit 15 (a data illustrator's published charts; Inuit media in Nunavut). Every task that ties media to a
  student's own life, identity or values (the Cn10 and Cn11 ids in units 2, 5, 7, 10, 12, 15 and 18) offers an
  invented, public-figure or class-shared option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). No task asks what a
  family watches, owns or posts, or for a student's own accounts, and a student's own media habits are never judged;
  digital identity is studied on an invented profile. Hard content in studied works (war, a massacre, a dictatorship's
  victims, racist caricature) is described and shown only in the parts the unit names, previewed by the teacher, never
  role-played. Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: one 45-minute lesson a day, step cards, work cards,
  case cards, paper media tools, found objects, an optional shared device per team. Activities: technique taught in
  steps, works studied with their setting, making in production roles with a critique and a revision, a showing or a
  sitting each unit, a screening that ends each level. Outputs: a portfolio of five pieces a level (four in Level 4,
  plus the series) with reflections, logs and plans; two marks a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome
  claimed: met marks on the level's 18 ids (Level 4: the eleven re-assessed ids on new work) and growth on the
  look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").

## Seams

### Picked up from Media Arts, grades 6 to 8 (grade 8)

The Media Arts, grades 6 to 8 frame hands on reading and society from unit 9, ideas and craft from unit 10, making
and judging from unit 11, and presenting and sources from unit 12. Each Level 1 row assumes its grade 8 row; unit 1
(the bridge) holds the readiness check and reteaches the needed grade 8 techniques, words and models as readiness,
claiming no grade 8 id.

### Handed on: where the threads end

Media Arts, grades 9 to 12 is the last media arts course; its threads end here. Level 3 hands every thread to Level 4
(ma-g11-ideas, ma-g11-make and ma-g11-craft to ma-g12-studio; ma-g11-present to ma-g12-exhibit; ma-g11-read and
ma-g11-judge to ma-g12-criticism; ma-g11-society and ma-g11-sources to ma-g12-purpose). The Level 4 rows hand on
nothing (their hands_on cells are empty). A student who stops after any level leaves with a complete course and a
portfolio: the level's pieces, logs and showing plans, which a later level's placement reads.
