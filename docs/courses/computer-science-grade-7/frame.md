# Course frame: Computer Science, Grade 7

Course `computer-science-grade-7` (catalog id `cs-k12`), grade 7, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the kindergarten pilot's shape and fixes; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6, one for each concept area at its grade 7 depth, with programming in two: read it, then design it
  (programming, with the algorithm design id that needs variables), build it as a team (programming: lists,
  reference pages, testing and code review), what the data shows and what it costs (data and analysis), algorithms
  that decide (algorithms and design, machine learning inside it), keeping the library's systems up, safe and open
  (systems and security), and computing changes things (computing and society, the year's closer).
- **Days.** 64 course days: 58 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 3, 2 after unit 6) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). Two lessons a week of about 45 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  grades 6 to 12 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), an elective, so course
  days 2k-1 and 2k fall in week k. The 45 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens with a
  retrieval trace or a predict task of three to ten minutes (APPROACH.md, "How a day opens"), and device sessions keep
  four minutes at the end to save or print work and shut devices down, so a session teaches for about 35 minutes.
  Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing), with the
  pilot's one shape for a unit that a course-level check follows directly: in units 3 and 6 the check stands for the
  unit's own end-of-unit assessment and the check's respond day is the unit's respond day. Each course-level check
  takes two lessons, since a 45-minute lesson holds one full performance (frame-write.md rule 19): the first scores
  the unit just ended in full, the second samples earlier units with short new items (frame-write.md rule 20).
  Units 1 to 6 teach on 8, 10, 8, 8, 9 and 7 days, 50 of the 64; units 1, 2, 4 and 5 add one assessment sitting and
  one respond day each. Days follow the load of the rows: unit 2 owns two ids and carries two used ones with a team
  product (draft, written code review, revision) and gets 12; unit 5 holds four systems rows and a closing brief and
  gets 11; unit 6's three society rows are case and claim sessions and get 7. The two second check lessons take one
  teaching day from unit 2, the largest unit (its revision and hand-in share session 20), and one from unit 5, whose
  load notes give each systems row one unplugged day: the library's own requirements join the closing brief
  (session 52), and g7-system-requirements, a used-again id, keeps its family or shop case (session 51). 64 sessions
  for 19 progression rows is about 3.4 sessions a row, counted with checks. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are
  no mid-unit assessments. Days outside the standards (bar criterion C1): the three list sessions of unit 2, 3 of
  its 50 teaching days (about 6 percent), under the 10 percent limit with about 2 days of room (Cory, 2026-11-21,
  Desk q-fix-cs-2).
- **Check weeks.** Grades 6 to 8 stagger by school day (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md rule 3). Grade 7's core check days
  are school days 45 (math), 57 and 58 (ELA), 63 and 64 (social studies), 67 (science), 109 and 110 (ELA), 116 and
  117 (social studies) and 119 (math), with every year-end check in week 32, the finals week. This course's mid-year
  check is course days 31 and 32 (school days 78 and 80, week 16), days and a week with no core check, and its
  year-end check is course days 62 and 63 (school days 155 and 158, weeks 31 and 32, week 32 the finals week), with
  respond days 33 (week 17) and 64 (week 32). The year-end lessons cannot share one week, since the respond day
  closes the year on day 64. Overlaps with other after-core subjects are the lead's calendar pass across the
  after-core frames (For the lead 1).
- **Course order.** Computer science in grades 6 to 12 is an elective, so this course never assumes the grade 6
  course was taken (frame-write.md rule 32). By course order, grade 7 follows grade 6: every row assumes a grade 6
  row, the year before, so for a student who took grade 6 each opens as a retrieval task inside the session the
  Seams name (frame-write.md rule 16). For a student who did not, the same items are given as a readiness check of
  ten minutes before the unit opens (unit 1: the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook; unit 2: the last ten
  minutes of unit 1's respond day, course day 10; unit 3: of unit 2's respond day, course day 22; unit 4: of the
  mid-year respond day, course day 33; unit 5: of unit 4's respond day, course day 43; unit 6: of unit 5's respond
  day, course day 54), and the unit's first understanding session carries a short worked example of the grade 6 idea
  for those students inside its own minutes, never an added day (unit 1: session 2, the first after day 1's check;
  unit 2: session 11; unit 3: session 23; unit 4: session 34; unit 5: session 44; unit 6: session 55). Day 33's check
  also covers a whole class whose school left out grade 6's unit 4 (Seams). Two owned ids are begun in grade 6
  (MS-DAT-IM-28 in g6-data-about-people, MS-SYS-SE-32 in g6-cia-practice): this course teaches both from the start
  (units 3 and 5), and grade 6's first look is a head start. No grade 7 row assumes a row two or more grades back.
- **Standard.** 15 MS ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, owned here by the progression
  (STANDARD.md "Ownership of MS and HS ids"): MS-ALG-PS-01, MS-ALG-PS-04, MS-ALG-ML-07, MS-ALG-IM-10, MS-DAT-DI-26,
  MS-DAT-IM-28, MS-PRO-RD-17, MS-PRO-PD-13, MS-PRO-TR-19, MS-SOC-HI-39, MS-SOC-ET-40, MS-SOC-CE-45, MS-SYS-NT-35,
  MS-SYS-SE-32, MS-SYS-IM-37. Five ids owned in grade 6 are used again in a new setting and named in the unit that
  uses them, never claimed here: MS-PRO-VD-16 (lists, unit 2), MS-PRO-PD-15 (code review, unit 2), MS-DAT-DC-23 (a
  larger table, unit 3), MS-SYS-HW-30 (requirements, unit 5), MS-SOC-HU-43 (who decides, unit 6). Each owned id is
  taught at the depth its owner row's notes and its boundary statement set, read in the source store
  (S:\CurioClass-sources\computer-science\csta-2026-11-21, 2026-11-25), said in our own words (STANDARD.md "Depth";
  boundary statements never copied or quoted), and "What students learn" says what is out. Plans cite ids and state
  each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md "Quoting"); the credit line "Standards: 2026 CSTA PK-12
  Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any day that leans on a statement. Check set: each unit plan
  checks itself against the computer science bar (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5, items 1 to 9) and the lens
  (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and reads the partial base unit it adapts for order; the "Check set adds"
  column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. No Specialty id,
  and no X + CS id, is claimed.
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's Practices and concept areas column (the C3 column of the core
  frames) names the concept area each unit teaches and the CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code and
  our label (STANDARD.md section 3), as bar items 1 and 2 ask; the codes follow the practices the standards pair with
  each owned id. Each unit plan names the practice's day and task. Across the year: CT6 define the problem (units 1,
  4, 5, 6), CT7 abstract and find patterns (unit 1), CT9 test, debug and refine (units 2, 4), IC3 explain and give
  feedback (units 1, 2, 3, 5), IC4 run a project (unit 2), IC5 be a reliable teammate (units 2, 3, 6, pair roles all
  year), HCD10 include different users (units 3, 4, 5, 6), HCD11 prototype and refine for use and access (units 2,
  3), HCD12 keep users informed and in control (unit 5), ESR1 weigh who gains and who is harmed (units 4, 6), ESR2
  respect people and protect data (unit 3). Dispositions are named in the unit plans, not here.
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, archived edition
  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived, key stage 3, resources dated 2023), which its publisher no longer maintains;
  England's current computing lessons are on Oak National Academy (STANDARD.md section 2). England's Year 8 is the
  grade 7 age. License: the scout read the Open Government Licence v3.0 "unless expressly stated otherwise" on the
  publisher's terms page; the unit pages opened carry no license line of their own, so each file's license line is
  read when the store folder is made. Adapted unit by unit and rephrased for an American audience (STANDARD.md
  section 2): US spelling, school years named as US grades, no money in pounds, no product or company name, our own
  browser editor in place of named apps. Its Year 8 Python unit feeds unit 1; two lessons of its Year 9 sequences
  unit feed unit 2's lists; one lesson of its Year 8 systems unit is read for order in unit 6; its other Year 8 units
  are left out (see Source map). Units 3, 4 and 5 and most of unit 6 are our own. The source's name appears only in a
  day's credit line, once a day. Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#teach-computing-curriculum): the source store
  holds no copy of this base, so the Source map rests on the public unit and lesson pages (lesson titles and lesson
  descriptions, opened 2026-11-25), and each adapted lesson's check for third-party images, text and tool screenshots
  waits on the store folder (For the lead 4).
- **Language.** Grade 7 writes new programs in Python text in our own browser editor, with blocks as a stand-in for a
  student who needs them (STANDARD.md "Blocks to text"); Python is named as a subject term on teacher pages.
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is an unplugged or trace session, the first teaching of a construct or idea (a printed program traced
  with a trace-table, a list drawn as numbered slots, a loop over a list traced from a worked example before it is
  changed in unit 2 session 13, a network map on cards, and unit 1 session 6, the first design session, taught from a
  worked design); a predict-and-watch session is Understanding too. Fluency is a modify session on a construct already taught:
  change a working program, list or test-table in small checked steps (unit 1 session 5, unit 2 session 16, and the
  retrieval traces that open sessions). Application is a make, design or society session: design and build a program
  against look-fors, improve a training set and measure again, or make a claim about a real case with two views and
  dated sources (unit 1 sessions 7 and 8, unit 2 sessions 17 to 20, unit 3 sessions 25, 29 and 30, unit 4 sessions 38
  and 41, unit 5 sessions 48, 51 and 52, unit 6 session 61). Check is an end-of-unit assessment or a course-level check;
  Respond acts on it by error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make, and no make day before
  a trace or modify day on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Trace and predict sessions end in an exit ticket with a short program and "what happens?", answered
  in writing, wrong answers by error letter; modify and make sessions are read from the program against two or three
  look-fors plus one line ("what I tested and what I changed"), the teacher reading a rotating third of the class each
  session; society sessions end in a short claim with two views and a dated source each (APPROACH.md, "The form of
  daily evidence"). Every end-of-unit assessment is a sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: read and
  trace items plus a short build, debug or design task (a society unit: a short claim task) with look-fors and two
  sample answers, one that meets them and one that does not, and a paper version (APPROACH.md, unit field 5). Each
  owned id has its own look-for: a "design" id (MS-ALG-PS-01) by the student's own design and trace, an "analyze" or
  "explain" id (MS-PRO-RD-17, MS-SYS-NT-35, MS-SYS-SE-32) by a written account of a new case, a "justify" or
  "evaluate" id (MS-ALG-PS-04, MS-SOC-ET-40, MS-SOC-CE-45) by a written verdict with reasons, a "create" id
  (MS-DAT-DI-26) by the student's own two charts, and MS-PRO-PD-13 by each student's own use of the reference page
  and error guide inside the unit 2 product (a call added from a supplied entry, a supplied error found and fixed,
  sessions 17 and 18). Unit 2 also scores its team product, whose draft (sessions 17 and 18) and written code review
  (session 19) come before its revision and hand-in (session 20), where it is scored. Units 3 and 6 are assessed in
  the course-level check that follows them (Days). No score comes from a session that first teaches a row.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day. Device days: unit 1 sessions 2, 5 and 7; unit 2
  sessions 12 to 14 and 16 to 20; unit 3 sessions 23 and 25; unit 4 sessions 36 to 38; unit 5 session 46: 17 of the
  50 teaching sessions, about a third, and every unit has at least one unplugged session (APPROACH.md, Pacing). Every
  device day is written four ways (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at stations
  with the other pairs on the paper version, one screen with a student at the device for each step, or no device. Two
  owned ids need a device to be met in full: MS-PRO-PD-13 (a reference page used while building, scored in the unit
  2 product) and MS-ALG-ML-07 (a model retrained and tested). On the no-device path the printed reference page with
  the call written by hand, and the sorting-card model, teach the idea, and the course record marks those two ids
  "part met without a device" (their owner rows' notes); day 21's hand-written reference task is supplementary
  evidence, never full credit on its own. Every other owned id is met in full on paper: MS-ALG-PS-01 asks for a
  design and trace, not running code; MS-PRO-RD-17 and MS-PRO-TR-19 are met by tracing and by a test-table run "be
  the computer"; MS-DAT-DI-26's boundary does not require a computational tool.
- **Paper path.** Every session has a paper version: printed programs with trace-tables and test-tables, list cards,
  printed tables and graph paper, sorting cards for the model, network maps and case cards. No tool asks for a
  student account, stores a student's name or sends anything off the device; work is saved on the device or printed
  (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety"). No live generated-text tool is used.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 7 readers. Code read is twenty lines at most (APPROACH.md, Supports), and
  error messages are plain words we write. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth, with the everyday
  words that have a computing meaning taught as two meanings (run, bug, list, server, feed, cloud), and the frames "I
  predict ___ because line ___ says ___" and "This choice helps ___ and hurts ___ because ___". A student may answer
  a trace in numbers, arrows or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. Unit 4 may be left out: MS-ALG-PS-04, MS-ALG-ML-07 and MS-ALG-IM-10 are then not claimed that year, the
  course still teaches data in unit 3 and society in unit 6 (bar part 5's fail line), unit 6 session 60 uses grade
  6's training-set as retrieval, and grade 8's g8-ml-evaluate, g8-modify-for-bias and g8-ai-tool-day then open with a
  readiness check (the family note names the ids). Units 3 and 6 may swap their case sets (the data sovereignty case,
  the automation jobs) for others on the same ids, not be left out, so the course always claims data and society
  ids. Units 1, 2 and 5 carry the programming and systems ids grade 8 builds on and are not swapped or left out.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Device cords stay behind the
  table, devices stay on the table, and unit 5's yarn network is laid on tables, never across a walkway. The scope and
  sequence copies these lines into each device day and into session 45.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $4 (printed anchor program,
  trace-tables, design sheets), unit 2 $5 (list cards, printed reference pages and error guide, review checklists,
  test-tables), unit 3 $5 (fifty-row tables, graph paper, profile cards), unit 4 $5 (sorting picture cards for the
  paper model, case cards), unit 5 $6 (network map cards, two balls of yarn, case and requirement cards), unit 6 $3
  (timeline strips, automation and task cards), about $28 in all.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 3 has Native content (a tribal nation's data sovereignty) and
  personal data: the agent first studies the Global Indigenous Data Alliance's CARE Principles
  (gida-global.org/care), the US Indigenous Data Sovereignty Network (usindigenousdata.org) and the Native Nations
  Institute's Indigenous data sovereignty pages (nni.arizona.edu), the first two community-owned, and checks the case
  is told from a nation's own published account. Unit 4 has race, dialect and disability bias cases: the agent studies
  the National Institute of Standards and Technology's report on demographic effects in face recognition (NISTIR
  8280, December 2019, nvlpubs.nist.gov), the open study of racial disparities in automated speech recognition
  (Koenecke and others, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, April 2020) and the NCDJ Disability Language
  Style Guide (ncdj.org/style-guide/). Unit 5 has disability, income, age and rural access: the agent studies the
  Autistic Self Advocacy Network's "Identity-First Language" page (autisticadvocacy.org/about-asan/identity-first-language/),
  the NCDJ guide and the NTIA Internet Use Survey (ntia.gov/data). Unit 6 has automation and families' jobs: the agent
  studies the Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook pages used (bls.gov/ooh, as of the day read).
  The agent widens each pack to the brief's five sources and records them. It checks the personal-data sessions of
  unit 3 and the security sessions of unit 5 carry health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground). The course's checks get the answer-key agent's
  read. No unit is hands-on in the laboratory sense, so no safety agent read is planned beyond the Safety lines above.
