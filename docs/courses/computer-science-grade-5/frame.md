# Course frame: Computer Science, Grade 5

Course `computer-science-grade-5` (catalogue id `cs-k12`), grade 5, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written on the shape of the kindergarten pilot (docs/courses/computer-science-kindergarten/frame.md and its fix report); the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, one for each concept area at its grade 5 depth, in the order the ideas build: how a message travels
  and stays safe (systems and security, so the four-part system-diagram, the network-diagram and the cia-triangle sit
  before any program stores or sends a value), flowcharts for a game (algorithms and design, the first flowchart with a
  variable, and who a design is for), programs that keep score (programming, turning unit 2's flowchart into block
  programs with variables, then debugging, remixing and building one in a team), data that trains a model (data and
  analysis, with machine learning on the class's own labeled drawings), and who computing includes, and who decides
  (computing and society, the year's closer).
- **Days.** 32 course days: 28 in units, 2 for course-level checks (1 after unit 3, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). One lesson a week of about 40 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  grades 3 to 5 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a specials rotation, so
  course day n falls in week n. The 40 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens with a
  three-minute retrieval trace from the second session on (APPROACH.md, Pacing), and device sessions keep four minutes
  at the end to save work to the device, close the tool and put devices away, so a session teaches for about 33
  minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing), with
  the pilot's one shape for a unit that a course-level check follows directly: in units 3 and 4 the check stands for
  the unit's own end-of-unit assessment and the check's respond day is the unit's respond day (pilot For the lead 2).
  So units 1 to 5 teach on 3, 3, 7, 5 and 4 days, 22 of the 32. Days follow the load of the rows: unit 3 owns four
  programming rows and five ids, each with a device version and a trace, modify and make order, and gets 7 teaching
  days; unit 4 owns four rows, two of them device-only ids (E5-DAT-DC-09, E5-ALG-ML-02) and the model training taught
  over two sessions, and gets 5; unit 5 owns three rows and five ids whose boundaries ask for different cases
  (inclusion and exclusion, culture and work, choosing to use a technology or not, professionals working together),
  and gets 4; units 1 and 2 own three and two rows that grade 4 has prepared (the system-diagram, the network-diagram,
  the written algorithm) and get 3 each, with unit 2's flowchart used again as unit 3's plan. 32 sessions for 16 rows
  and 19 ids is under the progression read's "about two sessions a row" (docs/reviews/progression-computer-science-2026-11-19.md),
  so the rows that share one id pair share one anchor (APPROACH.md, Pacing: two or three ids may share one anchor
  program), and the 40-minute session carries a trace and a short modify where grade 2's 30 minutes carried one. Each
  course-level check takes one check session and one respond session. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are no
  mid-unit assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The grade 5 core checks take weeks 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22 and 29 to 32 (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md,
  target weeks: ELA 17 and 32, math 11, 22 and 31, science 10, 21 and 30, social studies 16 and 29). This course's
  checks sit in week 18 (mid-year, after unit 3) and week 25 (year-end, after unit 4), with their respond days in
  weeks 19 and 26 (respond days are not check days). The mid-year check follows unit 3, not unit 2, because weeks 10
  and 11, where a check after unit 2 would fall, are core check weeks, and units 1 and 2 cannot be stretched without
  idle sessions; unit 2 ends in its own end-of-unit assessment (week 9) and respond day (week 10). Unit 3 runs across
  weeks 11 to 17, which include the core check weeks 11, 16 and 17, because it holds no course-level check. Unit 5
  runs in weeks 27 to 32 after the year-end check, as kindergarten's unit 5 does; its own end-of-unit assessment falls
  in week 31, a unit assessment, not a course-level check. Overlaps with other after-core subjects' check weeks are
  the lead's calendar pass (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** The 19 E5 ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, all taught here (STANDARD.md
  section 2): E5-ALG-PS-01, E5-ALG-ML-02, E5-ALG-IM-03, E5-PRO-PD-04, E5-PRO-PD-05, E5-PRO-VD-06, E5-PRO-RD-07,
  E5-PRO-TR-08, E5-DAT-DC-09, E5-DAT-DI-10, E5-DAT-IM-11, E5-SYS-HW-12, E5-SYS-SE-13, E5-SYS-NT-14, E5-SYS-IM-15,
  E5-SOC-HI-16, E5-SOC-ET-17, E5-SOC-HU-18, E5-SOC-CE-19. Each owner row's boundary was read in the source store's
  standards PDF for this frame (2026-11-25, S:\CurioClass-sources\computer-science\csta-2026-11-21, not copied or
  quoted), and each is taught at the depth its owner row's notes set, in our own words (STANDARD.md "Depth"), widened
  where the boundary asks for more than the row says (Row changes, For the lead 5); "What students learn" says what is
  out. Plans cite ids and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md "Quoting"); the credit line
  "Standards: 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any day that leans on a statement.
  Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the computer science bar (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5,
  items 1 to 9) and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and reads the partial base unit it adapts for
  order; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12)
  holds. No Specialty id, and no X + CS id, is claimed.
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's fifth column (the C3 column of the core frames) names, for
  computer science, the concept area each unit teaches and the CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code
  and our label (STANDARD.md section 3), as bar items 1 and 2 ask. Each unit plan names the practice's day and task.
  Across the year: CT6 define the problem (units 1, 2, 5), CT7 find patterns and make reusable parts (units 1, 2, 3,
  4), CT8 build and plan (units 2, 3, 4), CT9 test, debug and refine (units 3, 4), HCD10 learn from and include
  different users (units 2, 5), HCD11 prototype and test for use (unit 3), HCD12 keep users informed and in control
  (units 1, 5), IC3 explain and give feedback (units 1 to 5), IC4 run a project with shared tasks and documented work
  (unit 3), IC5 be a reliable teammate (unit 3, driver and navigator), ESR1 weigh who gains and who is harmed (units
  2, 4, 5), ESR2 credit others' work and protect information (units 1, 3, 4). Dispositions are named in the unit
  plans, not here.
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, archived edition
  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived, key stage 2, resources dated 2023), the same fixed edition the kindergarten
  frame maps (its For the lead 4). Year 6 is the grade 5 age (Year 1 is kindergarten in the pilot). License as the
  pilot states: the Open Government Licence v3.0 "unless expressly stated otherwise" on the publisher's terms page; the
  archived unit pages carry no license line of their own, so each file's license line is read when the store folder
  is made. Adapted unit by unit and rephrased for an American audience (STANDARD.md section 2, Cory 2026-11-16): US
  spelling, school years named as US grades, no money in pounds, no product or company name, our own browser editor
  or paper in place of the named block language, spreadsheet and microcontroller board. Four of its six Year 6 units
  feed units 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 in part (see Source map); its two creating-media units are left out, and the two
  internet-addressing lessons are offered to grade 6. The source's name appears only in a day's credit line, once a
  day. Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#teach-computing-curriculum): the source store holds no copy of this base, so
  the Source map below rests on the public unit pages (lesson titles and unit overviews, opened 2026-11-25), and each
  adapted lesson's check for third-party images, text and tool screenshots waits on the store folder (For the lead 2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is an unplugged or trace session, the first teaching of a construct or idea (students acting as the
  parts of a computer, a given flowchart traced on a trace-table, a given block program traced before it runs).
  Fluency is a modify session: change one value, one decision or one block of a working flowchart or program and
  check it; grade 5 has some of it (unit 3 sessions 2 and 4, unit 4 session 4, and the retrieval openings). Application
  is a make, design or society session: draw a flowchart for a new game (unit 2 session 2), remix a program or build
  a team component (unit 3 sessions 5 to 7), train and test a model (unit 4 session 3), or weigh a real case with a
  benefit-risk-chart (unit 2 session 3, unit 4 session 5, unit 5). Check is the end-of-unit assessment or a
  course-level check; Respond acts on it by error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make, and
  no make day before a trace or modify day on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Grade 5 answers trace and predict sessions on a written exit ticket with a short program or
  flowchart and the question "what happens?", wrong answers by error letter (APPROACH.md, "The form of daily
  evidence"); modify and make sessions are checked against two or three stated look-fors plus one line, "what I
  tested and what I changed", with the teacher reading the open part for a rotating third of the class each session.
  Every end-of-unit assessment is a sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: a written paper of about
  25 minutes (trace a new program or flowchart, label a new diagram, sort new cards, answer a new case) plus, in units
  3 and 4, one short build or debug task of about 10 minutes on the device or its paper version (APPROACH.md unit
  field 5). Each owned id has its own look-for: a "create" or "construct" id (E5-ALG-PS-01, E5-PRO-PD-04,
  E5-PRO-PD-05, E5-PRO-RD-07) is met by the student's own flowchart, remix, component or note; a "debug" id
  (E5-PRO-TR-08) by a found and fixed bug with the strategy named; a "train" id (E5-ALG-ML-02) by a retrained model
  and its test count; an "explain", "describe" or "distinguish" id (E5-SYS-HW-12, E5-SYS-SE-13, E5-SYS-NT-14) by a
  written answer on a new diagram or case; an "analyze", "examine" or "evaluate" id (E5-DAT-DI-10, E5-DAT-IM-11,
  E5-SYS-IM-15, E5-SOC-HI-16 to E5-SOC-CE-19, E5-ALG-IM-03) by a short claim with a reason on a new case, never by
  recognition of a supplied one. Units 3 and 4 are assessed in the course-level check that follows them (Days). No
  score comes from a session that first teaches a row. The respond day reteaches by error letter and repeats the
  missed item in a new form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per student. Device days: unit 1
  session 1 (part: a note typed and saved, traced through the system-diagram), unit 3 sessions 1 to 7 (the block
  editor with its text view and step-through trace), unit 4 sessions 1, 3 and 4 (a form and a table tool, the
  model-training tool) and session 2 in part (a sort of the dataset). That is about 12 of the 32 sessions, so well over
  half the year's time is away from a device, more than APPROACH.md's "about half" for grades 3 to 5 (ours, untested):
  the systems, algorithm and society rows are written unplugged, and the device days carry the parts that gain from
  a screen. Every device day is written four ways (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few
  devices at stations with the other pairs on the paper version of the same idea, one screen with a student at the
  device for each step, or no device. Two owned ids are met only on a device (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"):
  E5-DAT-DC-09 (collect and organize with a computational tool) and E5-ALG-ML-02 (train a model). On the no-device
  path their sessions teach the idea in full on paper (a paper form and a hand-sorted table; the class sorting cards
  as a pretend model, with a training set and a test set) and the course record marks those two ids "part met without
  a device"; it never says they were met. Every other E5 id is met in full on paper: printed programs, trace-tables,
  test-tables and cut-and-paste remixes meet E5-PRO-PD-04 to E5-PRO-TR-08, and drawn diagrams and acted parts meet the
  systems ids.
- **Paper path.** Every session has a paper version: index-card flowcharts, printed block programs with their text
  view, trace-tables and test-tables, a cut-and-paste remix, a paper form and hand tally, and card sorts for the
  pretend model. No tool asks for a student account, stores a student's name, uses the camera or microphone, or sends
  anything off the device (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety"); work is saved on the device or printed.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 5 students read grade-level text; code and flowcharts are read up to ten
  lines (APPROACH.md, Reading load), block programs always with their text view, and error messages are plain words
  we write. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth, with everyday words that have a computing meaning
  taught as two meanings (memory, network, model, train, remix, bug). The word variable returns from grade 4's named
  box; "score changes to score plus 1" is said as "set score to score plus 1", never "score equals score plus 1"
  (assignment is not equality, the lens's day error). Sentence frames: "First the program ___, then it ___" and "I
  predict ___ because line ___ says ___", and for society sessions "One benefit is ___; one risk is ___ for ___". A
  student may answer a trace in numbers, arrows or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. In this course every unit carries the only ids of its concept area, so units are swapped in part, not left
  out: unit 5's cases may be swapped for cases from the school's own community (the same five ids, one case set a
  session), and unit 4's data prompts and model drawings for other neutral sets, so the course always claims the data
  and society ids (the bar part 5 fails a course with nothing on data or society). Unit 2's given technology may be
  swapped for another familiar one. Units 1 and 3 carry the diagrams and the block-program and trace-table models the
  rest of the year and grade 6 use and are not swapped. A school that leaves out a unit anyway names in the course
  page and the unit's family note the ids not claimed that year.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Devices are carried with two
  hands and cords stay behind the table; unit 1's acted network uses yarn laid on the floor, not stretched at ankle
  height, and students walk. Unit 4's toy-car ramp is a book-and-board slope on the floor, cars are not thrown. The
  scope and sequence copies these lines into each device, acted-network and ramp day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $6 (printing role cards,
  system-diagram and network-diagram mats and cia-triangle threat cards; a ball of yarn), unit 2 $4 (index cards,
  printed flowcharts and trace-tables, benefit-risk-charts), unit 3 $7 (paper cups and labels for the named boxes, printed programs with text views,
  trace-tables, test-tables and the cut-and-paste remix sheets), unit 4 $9 (a classroom thermometer if the room has
  none, about $5; printing data tables, the pretend-model card set and case cards; toy cars and boards the class
  already has), unit 5 $4 (printing case cards, the timeline strip and benefit-risk-charts), about $30 in all.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 has online-safety content (passwords and personal
  information as the confidentiality side): the agent checks the section carries health's "If a student tells you
  something" steps word for word (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground) and that every
  account is a paper or pretend one. Unit 2's human-centered design session has disability content (a design that
  leaves out a user who cannot see the screen or use a mouse). Unit 5 has disability, language, naming and culture
  content (a form that does not fit every name, a voice tool that does not hear every accent, captions, a keyboard for
  a language made with its speakers) and work and automation content that can touch a family (APPROACH.md, Sensitive
  ground, "Work and automation"). Before it reads units 2 and 5, the agent studies a starting pack: the National
  Center on Disability and Journalism's Disability Language Style Guide (ncdj.org/style-guide/, free); the National
  Association of the Deaf's captioning pages (nad.org, its own account of captioning as access); the W3C
  Internationalization article "Personal names around the world" (w3.org/International/questions/qa-personal-names),
  for the name-form case; and, for the language keyboard case, the language community's own published account of the
  keyboard or font it made (the agent finds it; if none opens, the case is replaced by another inclusion case). The
  agent widens the pack to the brief's five sources, two of them owned by the communities the finished cards show,
  and records them. A Native language case gets the full read for Native content first. Unit 4's data and model
  sessions: the agent checks no prompt asks about a student's home, body, food, pets or family, and no drawing set
  shows a person. The course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on in the laboratory sense, so
  no safety agent read is planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity read confirms are on each day.
