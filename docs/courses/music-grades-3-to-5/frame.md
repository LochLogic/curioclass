# Course frame: Music, grades 3 to 5

Course `music-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `music-3-5`), a band course for grades 3, 4 and 5, run as three years one after another (year 3, year 4, year 5). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a listening and choosing unit (choosing and responding), an
  expression and improvising unit (inventing and expressing), a composing unit (writing down and revising) and a
  concert unit (reading and performing) that ends the year. Each unit joins two progression threads, so every unit
  sings or plays, listens to another artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). The shape differs from the
  music K to 2 frame, which reads in its first unit and revises in its concert unit: in grades 3 to 5 the reading
  row is the one that needs note values as parts of a whole bar (mu-g3-read, math:3.NF.A.1), so reading moves to the
  concert unit, after math teaches fractions, and the class reads the very piece it performs; revising then joins
  writing down, its natural pair in a composing unit.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes: found percussion or sticks out, folders
  handed out, the class in its rows or circle), the warm-up, the teaching and music making, and clean-up and
  collecting work (about 3 minutes: instruments back in their tubs, kept cards in the folder, hands washed after
  shared instruments); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit
  assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and one respond day
  after it, except that a unit followed at once by a course-level check (units 2, 3, 6, 7, 10 and 11) takes that check
  as its end-of-unit assessment (frame-write.md, after-core): its id-by-id evidence is taken on the check day, the
  check's respond day serves both, and the unit's own last two days are a rehearsal and revision day and a supported
  practice day. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the listening unit owns six ids (seven in year 5) but most are heard
  and written, so it runs 16 days; the improvising unit owns four ids in one technique and runs 12; the composing
  unit owns four ids but carries a product with a draft, a critique, a change list and a revision, so it runs 16; the
  concert unit owns six or seven ids, reading and a rehearsal arc, so it runs 16 with two check days. These are course
  days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in
  that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the music educators' association's
  National Opportunity to Learn Standards (2020, nafme.org, read 2026-11-21) ask for at least 90 minutes of general
  music a week in grades 3 to 5 at the basic level. This course gives 80 minutes a week, 10 short of that figure, and
  40 on the once-a-week path, 50 short; a school can add time. The course is written so a classroom teacher can
  teach it; a school with a music teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the listen to a work that carries the unit's response and its
  connecting sentence; one music-making or rehearsal day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond
  day. Fluency days fold into the warm-up openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on
  that path, every year: units of 8, 6, 7 and 7 sessions; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond
  session. The check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (weeks 15 and 24); the respond sessions fall
  one week later (weeks 16 and 25). Weeks 16 (grades 3 and 4) and 25 (grade 3) hold core checks; a respond session
  is not a check, so no rule breaks. The concert unit's two check days merge into one session there: the reading
  items at the teacher's spot during the group concert. A merged day never drops a critique, a revision or a respond
  day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, music performance standards (pre-kindergarten to grade 8 general
  music), by id only, each named with its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted
  (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 3 claims only the 21 grade 3 ids (`.3a` to
  `.3c`), year 4 only the 21 grade 4 ids, year 5 only the 21 grade 5 ids: 63 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2,
  every one taught and assessed in its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns,
  never as a claim. The connecting ids (`MU:Cn10.1`, `MU:Cn11.1`) carry one words line in every grade (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year meets them with its own music and words, and the unit says what changes. Every year touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at
  least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and
  anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  words-file lines of its ids and each id's national standard action (a words line can drift), the nine criteria of
  the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check
  set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence
  still states its id count (21) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met.
  The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Music is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain scores in a public domain or openly licensed recording (any recording's rights line is checked on its
  own page) and, for living or recent makers, a description in our own words with a link to the maker's, label's,
  museum's or archive's page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Songs the students sing are traditional
  songs or songs in the public domain, Curio Class originals or the students' own, never a copyrighted arrangement.
  Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails;
  a swapped work must keep the unit's ids met and the band's range. Range is checked across the band, and against the
  music K to 2, 6 to 8 and 9 to 12 frames' picks so no work is studied in two music bands (checked 2026-11-26; the
  9 to 12 copies of "Shenandoah", Dvořák's New World Symphony and Tate's Lowak Shoppala' were swapped there); a maker
  met again with a different work is named as a revisit (Beethoven: Symphony No. 5 in K to 2 unit 3, Symphony No. 9
  here in unit 9): Africa (Mali, Benin, South Africa), the Americas with Native makers
  (Blackfeet and Yakama singers, Tuscarora and other Native women singers, the Chickasaw Nation, the Kingdom of
  Hawaiʻi), Latin America and the Caribbean (Brazil, Mexico, Venezuela, Trinidad and Tobago), Asia (China, India, the
  Philippines), the Middle East (Iraq), Oceania (Aotearoa New Zealand) and Europe, with living makers each year.
  Curio Class writes every step card, notation card, listening-log page, form map, expression map, record sheet,
  Curio Class original tune and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Reading: rhythms of quarter notes, eighth pairs, half notes and
  quarter rests read by beat counts and syllables, then, after the fractions unit, as parts of a whole bar, and a
  short pentatonic tune on the staff in picture and standard notation (3); time signatures of two, three and four
  beats a bar, dotted half and whole notes, equal note values as equal parts of a bar, a tune read by letter names on
  the treble staff, form marked (4); standard notation as the default, a dotted quarter and eighth, mixed note values
  added within a bar, a melody with I and V chord symbols read (5). Voice and ensemble: unison singing in tune in
  the head voice and a two-part round (3); a drone or partner part held against the tune (4); two-part singing over a
  two-chord accompaniment sung or played (5). Making up: improvising on a five-note scale for a setting, and ideas
  inside a given tonality or meter the student names first (3); improvising over a drone, and two short ideas, one in
  a given major key in twos and one in its relative minor in threes (4); improvising over a I and V pattern, and two
  easy-chord accompaniments in those paired settings, tied to a setting (5). Expression: loudness and speed (3),
  then tone color (4), then articulation (5). Performing: an ensemble take judged on a class checklist and its weak
  spots rehearsed (3); solo or group takes judged on shared criteria, interpretation fitted to the genre (4); a
  rehearsal log over several weeks with kept takes that show growth (5).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied by listening with the unit's words (the artistic process
  "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills with feedback (an echo
  pattern, a four-beat reading card, a pitch-matching slide, a round sung twice, an expression-map run); in grades 3
  to 5 it is the five-minute warm-up opener of every teaching, practice and rehearsal day (a check day keeps its
  stated timetable, with any brief vocal or instrumental preparation inside the task time) and two or three fluency
  days a unit, more in the concert
  units, where rehearsal of a hard spot is fluency. Application: making up, composing, rehearsing or performing with
  some choice; the second application day holds the unit's one critique (describe what you hear, ask about a
  choice, suggest one change tied to a look-for), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: the
  self-check and the performance, written, talk and kept-card items of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second
  try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal and a second take. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency,
  application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in five kinds: a performance item at the teacher's
  spot (a pattern improvised, a phrase sung or played, a card read and played); a look-for on the showing's guide
  (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the live showing, or from a sound-only recording
  after the session; a kept card, form map, change list or listening-log page made on the unit's last making days, scored with
  the showing; a written item on an unseen public domain or openly licensed recording or a Curio Class original, one
  short card with a sentence frame (APPROACH "Reading load"); and talk items at the teacher's spot, at most two a
  student. The end-of-unit assessment is a check day near the unit's end. Its 40 minutes: set-up 3; the unseen
  recording played twice and its written card answered by every student, 6; each student's self-check on the
  guide, 3; six groups of five at the teacher's spot for about four minutes each (24), where each student does the
  performance items and answers the talk items; clean-up and collecting 3. That is 39 minutes. While the teacher
  hears one group, the others work on their listening logs with no instruments, so the room is quiet enough to hear
  one group, and the teacher hears one group, never a room and a group at once. In the composing units (3, 7 and 11)
  the check day is the composers' share in groups, and in the concert units (4, 8 and 12) the second check day is the
  class concert in groups: each group performs at the front, the class is the audience, and the teacher scores that
  one group on the guide and asks its talk items right after its turn. A unit owning two products (the concert
  units) has two check days. The product's draft, its critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record
  sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique is first
  taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH
  "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the
  portfolio folder (a kept card, a notated page, a listening-log page or a sound-only recording on the school's
  device) with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8). Every recording is optional: a student may show the
  work live, and the teacher's dated mark on the same guide is the evidence; growth over time may be three dated live
  marks with the student's change notes. Wherever a line below says "and its recording", read "or its recording or
  the dated live mark". A listening card for MU:Re7.2 has three labeled prompts, a feature of the form, a musical
  element and the setting, each tied to the response (described in grade 3, explained in grade 4, explained with
  evidence in grade 5). On a course check that is a unit's end-of-unit assessment, the unit's items are taken in its
  usual timetable, kept work from the last making days is scored after the session (once), at most two talk items a
  student, and the earlier unit is sampled by the check's one written card.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every year 3 unit assumes the grade 2 rows of music K to 2 in the same threads (its
  year 2, units 9 to 12, band days 129 to 192), last used one grade back, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day
  2's opener, never a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. Every year 4 and year 5
  unit assumes the year before's row in its thread, one grade back, with the same treatment. The count starts from
  grade 3 in the band's order (3, then 4, then 5). Music K to 2 is not a core course, so the seam is by course order
  (lesson 32): a student who never took it, or a mixed class running the years in turn where a student meets year 5
  before year 4, gets each retrieval task's ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH
  "Readiness and the baseline look"). The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (grade 3: 9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28 and 30 to 32; grade 4: 8, 10, 12, 16, 19, 21,
  23, 27 and 30 to 32; grade 5: 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22 and 29 to 32). Mid-year: week 15 in every year (course days 29
  and 30 of the year). Year-end: week 24 in every year (days 47 and 48). Weeks 29 to 32 hold year-end core checks in
  all three grades, so the year-end check comes before the concert unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and the concert
  unit's own two check days, in weeks 31 and 32, are its end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit can run alone through an entry route. One unit studies music with a ceremonial setting around it: unit 1 listens to powwow songs,
  social songs heard at a gathering that also holds ceremony; they are studied by listening only, never sung, drummed
  or danced by the class (one line on the course page, Cory 2026-11-21). A family whose faith limits music or singing
  may leave out a unit, or the singing in it (a student may take part by playing, conducting or listening); the
  course page and the unit's family note then name the ids not claimed that year. Units that study a named
  community's music, where a work may be swapped and the ids kept: units 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10 and 11. Swapping a
  work keeps the unit's ids met. Entry routes, inside the unit's own supported-practice time: when unit 2, 6 or 10
  was left out, the composing unit after it (3, 7 or 11) shows the least melody, drone or chord technique it needs and
  each student makes a fresh idea; when unit 1, 5 or 9 was left out, the concert unit (4, 8 or 12) uses a neutral
  Curio Class original piece, taught by ear. The left-out unit's ids stay unclaimed; entry work is support, not
  evidence for them.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 in rows or a cleared circle, the voice, hands and knees as the drum, found
  percussion (sealed shaker bottles, buckets, sticks), tuned plastic bottles of water as a pitched set, pencils,
  printed staff paper and cards; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4,
  APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, every group part
  has a doubled or solo version (parent and student sing it, or the student sings over the class recording), and the
  showing is to the family. The teacher needs no music training: each unit's teacher page says what to listen for,
  in plain words, and every model phrase is also on a Curio Class original recording the teacher can play.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (printing of step cards, notation cards, staff
  paper, form maps and listening-log pages; reusable items such as rhythm sticks counted once in the music K to 2
  frame and kept), all optional beside the free path: year 3 $10, $5, $5, $10 ($30, the last with concert programs);
  year 4 $5, $5, $5, $10 ($25); year 5 $5, $5, $5, $10 ($25). A school with no rhythm sticks adds $20 once. A pitched
  classroom set (bells, a xylophone) is optional and not counted: the voice, the tuned bottles and a free on-screen
  keyboard carry every pitch task. Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher device with a speaker plays the works and Curio Class's original tunes
  at a level the class can talk over. A free on-screen keyboard (a Curio Class tool) may be shared by pairs where the
  school has devices, for the harmony and chord tasks of years 4 and 5; it is never required. No owned id needs a
  device per student. A recording to keep an idea (MU:Cr2.1.3b, MU:Cr2.1.4b, MU:Cr2.1.5b) is always beside notation,
  which meets the id in full. The ids about recorded performers (MU:Re8.1.3a to MU:Re8.1.5a) need the one teacher
  device; a room with none hears the teacher (or a guest) perform two readings live, the work card describes the
  listening work, and a student meets every id in full. Chords with no keyboard are sung by three groups (the root,
  the third, the fifth) or played on the tuned bottles. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's
  device, sound only, never a face (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on the voice, the body, found instruments and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a
  unit plan adds (a rhythm reading tool, a notation editor, a keyboard, a listening-map player) has a printed version
  that does the same job (the reading card, staff paper, the sung chord, the printed listening map) and needs no
  student account.
- **Reading load.** A student reads at most one short card alone (APPROACH "Supports"); step cards are picture-led,
  the written card on a check day is one short card with a sentence frame, and the teacher reads any card aloud on
  request. English learners: each unit teaches its five or six words in depth, with a picture, a gesture and a
  sound and a frame ("The rhythm goes ___." "I hear ___ because ___." "It fits ___ because ___."); a student may show
  or perform the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student sang, played or showed apart from
  what they said or wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the federal
  deafness and communication institute's "It's a Noisy Planet. Protect Their Hearing." campaign pages
  (noisyplanet.nidcd.nih.gov), the National Center for Voice and Speech's voice care pages (ncvs.org), and, for any
  shaker or bottle set made in class, the federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide"
  (cpsc.gov). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): recordings
  at a set low level the class can talk over; headphones, where a school uses them with the keyboard tool, at a
  capped volume, each student's own or wiped between users, never shared earbuds; no drum or loud sound near a
  student's ear, hearing protection or a quiet seat allowed for any student, and a quiet rest after loud music;
  voice: no shouting, no singing beyond a comfortable range, voice rest and water, no student made to sing alone;
  moving to show a music idea in a cleared space, stepping in place, never running; found instruments with no small
  loose parts and no glass, shaker and tuned bottles plastic, filled and capped by an adult and taped shut; no mouth
  instrument (recorder, kazoo, whistle) shared; hands washed after shared instruments. Each unit's last column gives
  its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works and the songs with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14).
  Every traditional song is checked for its published history and verses before a unit plan keeps it, and a song with
  minstrel-show or other demeaning origins, or a sacred text, is dropped. Units with a community's music name the
  communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (Blackfeet and Yakama powwow singers; Mande griot
  families of Mali; Māori song, Aotearoa New Zealand), unit 2 (Chinese erhu music, Abing), unit 3 (Elizabeth Cotten,
  an African American musician of North Carolina; Brazilian music), unit 4 (Sweet Honey in the Rock, African American
  women's a cappella), unit 5 (Iraqi oud music; Beninese song; Dvořák's claims about African American and Native
  music), unit 6 (Ulali, Native women singers; Hindustani music of India), unit 9 (Miriam Makeba and apartheid South
  Africa; Germany in 1989), unit 10 (son and danzón of Mexico and Cuba; the steelpan of Trinidad and Tobago) and unit
  11 (the Chickasaw Nation; Liliʻuokalani and the Kingdom of Hawaiʻi). Every task that ties music to a student's own
  life (units 1, 5 and 9, the Cn10 ids) offers an invented or public option marked the same, and no student is asked
  to explain their family's music or culture (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Each section carries health's steps for
  "If a student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, notation
  cards, listening-log pages, found and low-cost instruments, one teacher device or none. Activities: technique taught
  in steps, works studied by listening with their setting, making up, composing and rehearsing with a critique and a
  revision, a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings
  (three of them concerts, three composers' shares), two marks a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome
  claimed: met marks on the year's 21 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with children, and
  Curio Class makes no claim that music study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").
