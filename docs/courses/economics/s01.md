## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who should decide what gets made: buyers, sellers, tradition or the government?"
- **Hook, day 1.** A fictional workshop has enough wood and labor for benches or tables, but not every request. Students propose who should choose and why. Keep their competing answers visible.
- **Supporting questions.** Write these together and accept revisions: What limits production (day 1)? What is forgone (day 2)? What can a curve show (day 3)? What changes choices (day 4)? Whose goals conflict (day 5)? Which institutions decide and protect choices (day 6)?
- **Closing claim.** On day 7, answer the compelling question conditionally, using the unseen frontier and workshop-policy sources below. Cite both, explain a tradeoff and answer a counterclaim. Different conclusions can earn full credit.
- **Teaching moves.** Model identifying author, date, purpose and limits before reading each source. Teach concepts directly; model a sourced explanation on day 5, then withdraw the writing frame on day 7. Day 6 compares country data without treating employment shares as proof of a whole system. Teach scarcity, capital, opportunity cost, incentive, equity, property rights and rule of law explicitly.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 7. Allow 40 minutes unaided, five minutes marking short parts, and five minutes recording uncertainties for day 8. Questions and sources below are ours, untested. Each numbered item is identified within the item cell. All belong to section 1.1.

**Unseen source A, fictional country production office.** One period, fixed resources and technology: efficient combinations of carts and sacks of grain are (0, 45), (3, 42), (6, 33), (9, 18), (12, 0), with carts horizontal. Intermediate combinations on straight segments are feasible. Actual output is (6, 18), with willing workers and usable land idle. A proposed tool raises the maximum grain at each cart quantity below 12, leaving the maximum carts unchanged. These are a teaching model, not observations about a real country.

**Unseen source B, fictional workshop council proposal and role statements.** A tax-funded payment rewards each cart delivered to remote residents. A workshop owner supports it because it raises expected receipts. A remote resident supports improved access. A taxpayer questions its cost. The proposal funds payments by reducing grain purchases for the public reserve. Workshop accounts say one more delivery costs 8 units and brings 11 units of extra receipts. An independent clerk verifies delivery. These statements express interests, not neutral predictions of all effects.

**Unseen source C, fictional institutional record.** A title register identifies who may use or transfer workshop equipment. Published contract rules apply to owners and officials alike; an independent court can enforce agreements. Some grain is sold privately, while the council buys a reserve with taxes. An older local custom allocates shared storage time by rotating turns. No country is represented by this invented record.
