## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What makes a spending choice a smart one, and who protects the buyer?"
- **Hook, day 59.** Two fictional job offers: higher pay with costly travel, or lower pay with benefits. Ask the compelling question before comparing budgets.
- **Supporting questions.** Which job fits (59)? Why do earnings change (60)? How do media and payment terms shape purchases (61)? What advice helps peers (62, 63)? How is information stolen (64)? What does insurance cover (65)?
- **Closing claim.** Day 65: answer the compelling question from the advertising study, protection advice and fictional quotes, with a tradeoff and a counterclaim. Revise on 67.
- **Teacher background, the federal consumer finance agency.** Read frame.md, "Teacher background, as of November 2026", unit 8, before teaching.
- **Teaching moves.** Model total compensation on 59, a labor-demand shift and net-income budget on 60, and sourcing and claim-evidence-reasoning on 61. Guide research on 62; withdraw examples on 63. Source every card (creator, date, audience, purpose, limits) and corroborate advice. Teach wage, salary, benefits, withholding, refund, balance due, premium, deductible, coverage and identity theft. Wages reflect institutions and bargaining as well as supply and demand; low income does not measure effort or worth. Day 61: advertising 12 minutes (the experiment, then an invented social-media purchase card), payments 20, protection 13, exit ticket 5. Day 65: insurance purposes 10, quotes 15, identity protection 10, closing claim 10, exit ticket 5 (two separate tasks). Untested estimates.


- **Limits.** Credit, compound interest and investing are unit 9's. Day 64 rehearses a wage-only federal return, not tax advice. Renting a first home is an extension (fictional deposit, moving costs, renters coverage), with no added day.
- **Carried.** Keep unit 7's claim-evidence-reasoning. Retrieve the public budget on 59 and turn it into a personal budget on 60; return to the labor-market graph on 60; students rebuild budget and reasoning alone on 66.

- **A, worker and budget.** Noor is 27, holds the certificates required by both offers, enjoys helping readers and prefers daytime work. Offer A is a nearby library assistant role: monthly gross pay 2,800 dollars, stated deductions 420, employer-paid health coverage, daytime hours and promotion training. Offer B is a distant evening support role: gross 3,100, deductions 465, no health coverage and limited advancement. Monthly living costs excluding travel and any separately purchased insurance are 2,100; travel costs A 50 and B 100. Coverage prices not supplied cannot be invented; identify that missing information before treating the cash remainder as total compensation.
- **B, unseen research evidence.** Original summary, about 100 words, of Blake, Nosko and Tadelis, "Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment", *Econometrica* 83, no. 1 (2015): a large online marketplace in the United States ran two tests. It stopped buying ads on searches for its own name on two search sites, keeping a third as the comparison; and it stopped its other paid search ads for 60 days in a randomly chosen 30% of United States geographic areas, keeping them in the rest, then compared purchases. Results to include: ads on the marketplace's own name changed almost nothing, as visits returned through ordinary results; other ads moved mainly new and infrequent buyers, not frequent ones; the authors estimate the ads returned less than they cost. Checked against the authors' free draft (BNT_ECMA_rev.pdf); the published version was not compared. Add one line marked invented: of 100 who saw a social-media post 15 bought, of 100 who did not 10 bought. Student copy names no company; it says "a large online marketplace". Preserve authorship and the authors' industry involvement in production records. This is a different study from the advertising experiment taught on day 61; use neither its findings nor its design during teaching.

Credit requires six payments of 50.50 dollars, including all interest and fees; no other charges if paid as agreed. Rent-to-own requires 24 payments of 18 dollars, with ownership only after the last payment; no other charges if completed. Do not generalize these fictional terms to all providers.
- **D, renters quotes.** Noor has 15,000 dollars of belongings, can afford at most 20 dollars monthly and can cover at most a 150-dollar deductible. **Teacher judgment.** Record each component as secure, partial or missing, including every listed factor, payment method and insurance purpose. Judge evidence and reasoning, not a preferred spending choice. Contrasting samples:

- **1.** Look for both remainders, all six job factors and the unpriced coverage. Meets: "Both accept Noor's certificates. A leaves 230 and B 435. A fits Noor's interests and location, has daytime hours, health benefits and training; B pays more, but its distant evening work and limited advancement matter. - **2.** Look for market interaction, two distinct shocks and hours-based income. Meets: "Supply and demand affect wages. Does not: "Pay cannot fall if the wage stays fixed."
- **3.** Look for the effect on purchases, who it differs for, the design and a limitation. Meets: "Switching ads off barely changed what frequent buyers bought, because they found the site anyway. Does not: "The study proves advertising never works."
- **4.** Look for three totals and a benefit and cost for every method. Meets: "Cash, debit and checks cost 275 and avoid interest, but cash can disappear and accounts and acceptance constrain the other two. - **5.** Look for both jurisdictions, source-based judgment and counterclaim. Meets: "State attorneys general investigate state-law deception; the federal agency enforces federal rules. I would compare total costs before buying because C's small payment hides a larger total and B shows that ads move some buyers more than others. Installments may solve an urgent need, but no agency guarantees a refund." Does not: "The government checks all ads, so any offer is safe."
- **6.** Look for six accurate purposes, all quote comparisons and a conditional recommendation. Meets: "Health covers medical costs; auto covers stated vehicle risks and liability; renters covers belongings and liability; homeowners adds the structure; life pays beneficiaries; disability replaces qualifying lost income. A costs 144 or 644, B 216 or 316, C 180 or 380 in the two scenarios. B alone meets Noor's deductible constraint and belongings need within the premium budget; flood remains excluded." Does not: "A is best because the monthly price is lowest and insurance covers everything."
- **7.** Look for harm, separate responses to all three channels and recovery. Meets: "Stolen identifiers could open accounts or claim a refund. Verify the shop independently, bypass the email link, and end the call without sharing the code. If information was shared, contact the institution and official recovery service." Does not: "Reply with the code if the caller knows your name."
