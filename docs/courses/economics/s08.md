## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What makes a spending choice a smart one, and who protects the buyer?"
- **Hook, day 59.** Show two fictional job offers: higher pay with expensive travel, or lower pay with benefits. Ask the compelling question before comparing their budgets.
- **Supporting questions.** Write together: Which job fits a qualified worker (59)? Why can earnings change (60)? How do media and payment terms shape purchases (61)? What advice can peers use (62 and 63)? How can information be stolen (64)? Which risks does insurance cover (65)?
- **Closing claim.** On day 65, answer the compelling question using the advertising study, public protection advice and fictional quotes below. Explain a tradeoff and answer a counterclaim. Revise after feedback on 67.
- **Teaching moves.** Section 8.1 owns every listed verb. Model comparing total compensation on 59, a labor-demand shift and net-income budget on 60, and sourcing and claim-evidence-reasoning on 61. Guide research on 62; withdraw completed examples on 63 and 65. Before each source, identify creator, date, audience, purpose and limits; corroborate advice. Teach wage, salary, benefits, withholding, premium, deductible, coverage and identity theft. Explain that wages reflect institutions and bargaining as well as supply and demand; low income does not measure effort or worth. Day 61 assigns 12 minutes to advertising, 20 to payment comparisons, 13 to protection and five to an exit ticket. Day 65 assigns 15 to insurance purposes, 20 to quotes, ten to identity protection and five to an exit ticket.

**Withheld pack.** All offers and figures are fictional except source B. Letters identify anonymous providers, not company names.

- **A, worker and budget.** Noor is 24, holds the certificates required by both offers, enjoys helping readers and prefers daytime work. Offer A is a nearby library assistant role: monthly gross pay 2,800 dollars, stated deductions 420, employer-paid health coverage, daytime hours and promotion training. Offer B is a distant evening support role: gross 3,100, deductions 465, no health coverage and limited advancement. Monthly living costs excluding travel and any separately purchased insurance are 2,100; travel costs A 50 and B 100. Coverage prices not supplied cannot be invented; identify that missing information before treating the cash remainder as total compensation.
- **B, unseen primary evidence.** The federal trade commission's October 2023 social-media fraud spotlight, January to June 2023 chart: online shopping is 44% of loss reports and 8% of reported dollars lost. Print the two labelled columns and the report's scope in an original summary. These are reported fraud losses, not all purchases. Use neither this chart nor these percentages during teaching.
- **C, desk offers.** Noor needs a desk priced at 275 dollars including tax. Cash, debit and accepted check have no added fee here and require sufficient available funds. Credit requires six payments of 50.50 dollars, including all interest and fees; no other charges if paid as agreed. Rent-to-own requires 24 payments of 18 dollars, with ownership only after the last payment; no other charges if completed. Do not generalize these fictional terms to all providers.
- **D, renters quotes.** Noor has 15,000 dollars of belongings, can afford at most 20 dollars monthly and can cover at most a 150-dollar deductible. Independent providers A, B and C quote monthly premiums of 12, 18 and 15 dollars; deductibles of 500, 100 and 200; belongings limits of 10,000, 20,000 and 15,000. Each has the same liability limit, covers the specified theft and excludes flood. One covered theft causes a 2,000-dollar belongings loss. There are no other claims or coinsurance; premiums plus the deductible give the stated claim-year total. Flood and losses beyond the limit remain uninsured.
- **E, patterns only.** An unfamiliar shop requests unnecessary identity details; an unexpected tax-refund email seeks a login through a link; a caller claiming to represent an account provider requests a one-time code. No live links, numbers, copied messages or usable personal identifiers appear.

**Teacher judgment.** Record each component as secure, partial or missing, including every listed factor, payment method and insurance purpose. Judge evidence and reasoning, not a preferred spending choice. Contrasting samples:

- **1.** Look for both remainders and all six job factors. Meets: "Both accept Noor's certificates. A leaves 230 and B 435. A fits Noor's interests and location, has daytime hours, health benefits and training; B pays more, but its distant evening work and limited advancement matter. I choose A pending comparison of coverage costs." Does not: "B is best because its salary is highest."
- **2.** Look for market interaction, two distinct shocks and hours-based income. Meets: "Supply and demand affect wages. Recession lowers hiring; automation can displace sorting jobs while increasing demand for other skills. Fewer hours reduce weekly pay from 840 to 630 even at 21 hourly." Does not: "Pay cannot fall if the wage stays fixed."
- **3.** Look for both denominators, a persuasion mechanism and a limitation. Meets: "Shopping is 44% of loss reports but 8% of dollars lost. Targeted ads and trusted-looking recommendations can lead people to order. Reports do not tell us the risk to every shopper." Does not: "The table proves ads cause 44% of shoppers to lose money."
- **4.** Look for three totals and a benefit and cost for every method. Meets: "Cash, debit and checks cost 275 and avoid interest, but cash can disappear and accounts and acceptance constrain the other two. Credit spreads 303 over six payments; rent-to-own starts with less but costs 432 and delays ownership." Does not: "Rent-to-own is cheapest because 18 is smallest."
- **5.** Look for both jurisdictions, source-based judgment and counterclaim. Meets: "State attorneys general investigate state-law deception; the federal agency enforces federal rules. I would compare total costs before buying because C's small payment hides a larger total and B shows reported shopping harm. Installments may solve an urgent need, but no agency guarantees repayment." Does not: "The government checks all ads, so any offer is safe."
- **6.** Look for six accurate purposes, all quote comparisons and a conditional recommendation. Meets: "Health covers medical costs; auto covers stated vehicle risks and liability; renters covers belongings and liability; homeowners adds the structure; life pays beneficiaries; disability replaces qualifying lost income. A costs 144 or 644, B 216 or 316, C 180 or 380 in the two scenarios. B alone meets Noor's deductible constraint and belongings need within the premium budget; flood remains excluded." Does not: "A is best because the monthly price is lowest and insurance covers everything."
- **7.** Look for harm, separate responses to all three channels and recovery. Meets: "Stolen identifiers could open accounts or claim a refund. Verify the shop independently, bypass the email link, and end the call without sharing the code. If information was shared, contact the institution and official recovery service." Does not: "Reply with the code if the caller knows your name."
