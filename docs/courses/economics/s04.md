## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When should government step into a market, and who pays?"
- **Hook, day 28.** A fictional district wants an outdoor warning siren. Everyone nearby hears it, including nonpayers. Students ask whether voluntary purchases would fund it.
- **Supporting questions.** What might markets leave unprovided (28)? When do competition and regulation help (29)? Can intervention disappoint (30)? Who funds which services (31)? How do taxes affect different people and public borrowing (32)?
- **Closing claim.** Day 32 reads the tax evidence and two community voices, one per side. The revision of day 30's explanation with that tax evidence moves to day 34's revised-claim block, so day 32 keeps its minutes for T4.7 and PFL.T1.3. Answer the compelling question, weigh a counterclaim and name uncertainty; day 34 transfers this to unseen sources. Day 32 uses T4.5 to classify two schedules; the 1913 amendment is read aloud in brief.
- **Teaching moves.** Teach public good, free rider, externality, antitrust, natural monopoly, revenue, progressive, proportional, regressive, marginal rate, deficit and debt. Model sourcing on 28, a private-cost and social-cost graph on 30, budget denominators on 31; withdraw worked answers on 32. Keep public provision apart from a technical public good, efficiency from distribution, and a tax transfer from a resource cost.
- **Teacher background, current law.** Read frame.md, "Teacher background, as of November 2026", unit 4, before teaching.

A proposed night-operation rule would reduce annual noise damage by an estimated 38 money units and use 46 in real compliance and enforcement resources. ## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

None. No tool ask. Printed source cards, tables, graph paper, rulers, calculators and an error record. Teach income share with labelled layers and "The share changes because ___." Check an oral explanation before treating unfamiliar English as a content error.

## 9. Family note

Students will use evidence to judge government policies and compare public budgets and taxes. All personal figures are supplied; nobody is asked about family income, benefits, debt or political views.
