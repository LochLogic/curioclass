## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When does a business get too big for the good of buyers and workers?"
- **Hook, day 19.** A fictional laundry merger promises cheaper service through better equipment but would leave one local employer. Students name evidence needed before judging the promise.
- **Supporting questions.** Who risks losses and controls decisions (19)? Who finances and represents people (20)? What constrains sellers (21, 22)? What constrains buyers (23)? Can productivity benefit everyone (24, 25)?
- **Closing claim.** Day 25 answers the compelling question using the historical accounts and productivity data below, with a counterclaim. Day 26 transfers that reasoning to unseen sources A and B, distinguishing buyers, suppliers and workers. Either conclusion can earn credit.
- **Teaching moves.** Teach liability, stock, union, nonprofit, monopoly, oligopoly, monopsony, marginal revenue and productivity. Model sourcing and cost calculations on 19 and 22; compare interested accounts on 23; withdraw worked examples on 25. Show how an extra sale changes revenue on all units when one price applies.

**Unseen source A, public procurement data.** The 2023 report by James M. MacDonald, Xiao Dong and Keith Fuglie for the US Department of Agriculture, Economic Research Service, table 6, reports the share processed by the four largest processors. For steers and heifers, the share was 36% in 1980 and 85% in 2019; for hogs, 34% and 67%. These are national shares counted by head of livestock, not shares of retail food sales or employment. The underlying records come from the agricultural marketing agency's annual reports. Define procurement as purchasing inputs. No individual firm is named.

**Unseen source B, research account.** An original summary of the same report's meatpacking discussion, written for this assessment: larger plants and reorganized production reduced processing costs, while concentration also raised concerns about bargaining and price competition. The report reviews research rather than treating concentration alone as proof of harm. Give the report's authors, publication date, research purpose and scope on both A and B. They share an origin and are not independent corroboration.

**Unseen source C, fictional container producer's weekly schedule.** The firm charges one price for every batch sold and has market power. For zero through five batches, prices are none, 42, 36, 30, 24 and 18 money units per batch; total costs are 15, 33, 48, 69, 102 and 150. Revenue is zero at zero output. Costs include opportunity costs. These are invented alternatives, not observations from A. Supply blank calculation columns, not completed differences.

**Unseen source D, dated productivity observation.** The US Bureau of Labor Statistics' February 15, 2024 release reports that US nonfarm business output per hour grew 1.2% in 2023, real output 2.6%, hours 1.3% and real hourly compensation 0.1%. Compensation includes wages and benefits; real means adjusted for price changes. Give its measurement purpose, sector and release date. This dated snapshot may be revised; it is not a description of today's economy or of A's industry. Reserve the 2023 observation for assessment, teaching with 2018 and 2019 instead.
