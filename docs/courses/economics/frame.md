# Course frame: Economics, with personal finance

Course `economics` (catalogue id `economics`), grades 11 to 12 (half year; rows sit at grade 12 in the data), draft frame (2026-11-13). The
scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9: seven of economics in the usual order (scarcity, markets, business, government, the whole economy,
  money and policy, trade), then two of personal finance.
- **Days.** 80 course days (a half year): 76 in units and 4 for two course-level checks, each an assessment day and a
  respond day. One lesson of about 50 minutes a day, five a week, across 16 weeks. 25 rows in 76 unit days is about 3 days a row, weighted by load, not shared equally: the graph-heavy
  market units and the five-row finance unit get the most. Each unit's days include its readiness check or retrieval task
  (a readiness check on ss8-public-budget in unit 1 only, covering the whole row; units 4, 8 and 9 open with a
  retrieval task on the part they use, and unit 1's results set who gets their targeted support), its end-of-unit
  assessment and one respond day (a unit over 25 days would also hold a mid-unit assessment; none is that long). If the scope and
  sequence finds a unit over, it brings a day-transfer proposal rather than cutting a standard, practice or a respond day.
- **Day budget, units 7 to 9 (provisional; the scope and sequence tests it day by day).** Unit 7, 7 days: readiness check
  on wh2-globalization inside day 1; comparative advantage worked (2); tariffs, quotas and the free-trade argument (1); balances of trade
  and payments and exchange rates (1); productivity and globalization's effects from data (1); end-of-unit assessment
  (1); respond day (1). Unit 8, 9 days: retrieval task and job choice, with targeted ss8-public-budget support that
  day (1); the labor market, income risk and the first-year budget, pay stub and withholding inside it (1); a
  first tax return, which opens identity protection (1); advertising evidence, ways to pay and consumer protection (1);
  insurance quotes, identity protection finished (1); the informed action (2); assessment (1); respond day (1). The tightest unit: if day 4 overruns,
  consumer protection is finished in the action days, which check public consumer-protection advice. Unit 9, 11 days:
  retrieval task, banks, simple and compound interest, real and nominal rates (1); saving policies investigated (1);
  credit as one running case on one made-up offer, its terms, report and disclosure (1); the results of not repaying
  investigated (1); the credit plan that totals its cost, its method carried to college aid offers (1: the offer and
  its payment table are set up once for all three days); financial markets, assets, prices and returns (1);
  diversification, asset types researched and the portfolio chosen (1), read from a printed series dated across years;
  the regulators judged from crises and enforcement records (1); the savings or investment plan with the institutions'
  services compared (1); assessment (1); respond day (1).
- **Check-set additions.** Additions ride inside an owned code's lesson where one fits; at most 4 days in the course are
  for additions with no home code. Riding inside: marginal thinking (T1.2), costs of production (T3.6, which needs
  them), externalities on the graph (T4.1), types of unemployment (T5.2), money creation through lending (T6.3), the pay
  stub and withholding (PFL.T1.1 and T1.2, the first-year budget). Paying for college rides inside the credit plan
  (PFL.T3.6) and the savings plan (PFL.T2.6). Taking days (1 of the 4): a first tax return (1, unit 8). Extensions named in the unit plan, not taught days: consumer and producer
  surplus, renting a first home.
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 ECON.T1 to ECON.T7 and Personal Financial Literacy PFL.T1 to PFL.T4 are the content
  anchor; every numbered code is owned by one row and every row sits in one unit. No NML code falls to this course (all
  of NML.T1 to T5 sits in US History I and Government). Skills cite C3: the course owns D2.Eco.1 to D2.Eco.14 and
  D2.Eco.15 and D2.Geo.11 at 9-12 (the last two with a depth line over grade 10). Each unit plan compares the unit with
  California, Tennessee and New York and with the college-level micro and macroeconomics course frameworks, with the
  economics content standards (CEE 2010) and the personal finance standards (NSPFE 2021) as references, and adds
  inside its days what a strong set treats as essential ("Check set adds"); check-set names never appear in student,
  teacher or family copy. The values rule governs: where economists' studies disagree (minimum wage effects, trade's
  winners and losers, the size of a stimulus), both lines of evidence are taught and the student judges.
- **Open to grade 11 or 12** (Cory, Desk card q-ss-progression-1). No row assumes US History I or US History II: the
  examples a row once took from US History I (the corporation, the trusts, the national bank fight, railroad booms) are
  retaught in brief from the source. Supply and demand and opportunity cost come from grades 7 to 10 and ss8-public-budget.
  US History II's unit 1 toolkit covers some of the same ground as a review; neither course assumes the other.
- **Personal finance rules.** Every offer, quote, portfolio and brand is made up; no real company or product is named
  or recommended. No student is asked about family income, debt or spending. Math is done on given figures.
- **Personal finance home.** This course is the main teaching of personal finance (units 8 and 9); there is no separate
  personal finance course (Cory, 2026-11-14). Other courses add only light touches inside days that already fit, with
  no added days: US History II's Great Depression unit reads bank runs and deposit insurance.
- **Base text.** None for the course (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, P2). Partial bases, adaptable with a one-line credit
  per day: the retired CC BY Principles of Economics 2e (store folder `openstax-retired-ccby\`) for units 1 (chapters
  1 and 2), 2 (chapters 3 to 5), 3 (chapters 7 to 10 and 14), 4 (chapters 11 to 13 and 18, with 30 for budgets), 5
  (chapters 19 to 22 and 24), 6 (chapters 27, 28 and 30 to 31), 7 (chapters 23, 29, 33 and 34) and 9 (chapter 17;
  chapter 16 serves unit 8's insurance); the public-domain youth financial education activities of the federal
  consumer financial protection agency (store folder `cfpb-youth-finance\`, its name and logo left out of pages) for
  units 8 (budgets, payment methods, consumer protection, insurance, identity theft) and 9 (saving, credit scores, card
  statements, avoiding debt, paying for college). The frame does not map them lesson by lesson: the unit plan names what
  it adapts.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md)
  before it ships; no outside human reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). The agent studies first, for: units 1 and 7,
  the countries' own statistical offices and development scholarship, so country cases are stated from data and never
  rank peoples; unit 7, labor and community accounts of towns that lost and gained jobs to trade; units 2 and 4, the
  published arguments on more than one side of the minimum wage, rent control and tax fairness; units 5 and 6, plain
  language guidance on teaching unemployment and inflation without asking about families; units 8 and 9, financial
  counsellors' and consumer advocates' published guidance on teaching money to teenagers without shame (debt,
  bankruptcy, repossession, scams) and on keeping every example free of a real company.
- **Paper path.** Every source, data set and task has a printed version; no day needs a device, an internet connection or
  a student account. Current indicators are printed with source and date; the tax return uses printed forms; the
  portfolio is tracked on paper; the information piece in unit 8 is printed and posted through the school.
- **Literacy standards (also met).** The unit work also meets these Common Core literacy standards for history and
  social studies (data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt; the rows' notes name them), with no added days: unit 2,
  RH.11-12.7; unit 5, RH.11-12.7; unit 6, RH.11-12.6. Across the half year: RH.11-12.2, RH.11-12.4 and RH.11-12.10
  (every unit reads and summarizes sources at the band's complexity) and WHST.11-12.10 (routine short and long
  writing). WHST.11-12.6 is not claimed: the paper path keeps every product off a device.
- **Review roles.** Review roles follow FOUNDATION section 7.3, revised 2026-11-14: the sensitivity, safety and answer-key agents do the content reads, each cold and after studying its sources; the cases and sources named here stay binding. The course-level checks get the answer-key agent's read before the course is called finished.
- **Inquiry arc and informed action.** Every unit runs on its compelling question, with a data or source task the
  student has not seen at its end. The year's informed action is in unit 8 (econ-spending-consumer): students choose a
  consumer question their peers face, check it against public consumer-protection advice, and make an information
  piece shared through the school. Never a cause the course picks.
- **Two graphs kept apart.** The market supply and demand graph (one good's price against its quantity, unit 2) and
  the aggregate supply and demand graph (the price level against real GDP, unit 5) are taught as different models with
  different axes.
- **The grade 12 timetable** (Cory, 2026-11-14: yes, as picked). The table is in docs/courses/us-history-ii/frame.md: suggested in the spring half for a
  grade 12 student taking all three courses, after US History II's unit 1 toolkit. A grade 11 student takes it beside
  US History I in either half. The `school_day` column of data/plans/economics/days.csv gives the spring placement; in a
  fall offering school day = course day, and the cross-subject timing (the Algebra II bridge, the ELA 12 milestones) is
  checked again for that half.
