# Course frame: Economics, with personal finance

Course id `economics`, grades 11 to 12 (half year; rows sit at grade 12 in the data), draft frame (2026-11-13). The
scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9: seven of economics in the anchor's order (scarcity, markets, business, government, the whole economy,
  money and policy, trade), then two of personal finance.
- **Days.** 80 teaching days (a half year): 76 in units and 4 for two course-level checks, each a check day and a
  respond day. 25 rows in 76 unit days is about 3 days a row, weighted by load, not shared equally: the graph-heavy
  market units and the five-row finance unit get the most. Each unit's days include its readiness check (and the
  one-lesson reteach of ss8-public-budget where it is dormant: units 1, 4, 8 and 9), its end-of-unit assessment and its
  respond day.
- **Day budget, units 7 to 9 (provisional; the scope and sequence tests it day by day).** Unit 7, 7 days: retrieval
  inside day 1; comparative advantage worked (2); tariffs, quotas and the free-trade argument (1); balances of trade
  and payments and exchange rates (1); productivity and globalization's effects from data (1); end-of-unit assessment
  (1); respond day (1). Unit 8, 9 days: readiness check and job choice, with the ss8-public-budget reteach as a station
  that day (1); the labor market, income risk and the first-year budget, pay stub and withholding inside it (1); a
  first tax return (1); advertising evidence, ways to pay and consumer protection (1); insurance quotes and identity
  protection (1); the informed action (2); assessment (1); respond day (1). The tightest unit: if day 4 overruns,
  consumer protection is finished in the action days, which check public consumer-protection advice. Unit 9, 11 days:
  readiness check, banks and simple and compound interest (1); real and nominal rates, saving policies and
  institutions' services compared (1); credit as one running case on one made-up offer, from its terms, report and
  disclosure to the plan that totals its cost (2: the offer and its payment table are set up once for both rows, which
  stay separate); paying for college (1); asset types researched, asset prices, returns and diversification (1); the
  portfolio chosen and the savings or investment plan (1), tracked at dated observations in the days after; the
  regulators judged from crises and enforcement records (1); plan finished (1); assessment (1); respond day (1).
- **Check-set additions.** Additions ride inside an owned code's lesson where one fits; at most 4 days in the course are
  for additions with no home code. Riding inside: marginal thinking (T1.2), costs of production (T3.6, which needs
  them), externalities on the graph (T4.1), types of unemployment (T5.2), money creation through lending (T6.3), the pay
  stub and withholding (PFL.T1.1 and T1.2, the first-year budget). Taking days (2 of the 4): a first tax return (1,
  unit 8) and paying for college (1, unit 9). Extensions named in the unit plan, not taught days: consumer and producer
  surplus, renting a first home.
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 ECON.T1 to ECON.T7 and Personal Financial Literacy PFL.T1 to PFL.T4 are the content
  anchor; every numbered code is owned by one row and every row sits in one unit. No NML code falls to this course (all
  of NML.T1 to T5 sits in US History I and Government). Skills cite C3: the course owns D2.Eco.1 to D2.Eco.14 and
  D2.Eco.15 and D2.Geo.11 at 9-12 (the last two with a depth line over grade 10). Each unit plan compares the unit with
  California, Tennessee and New York and with the college-level micro and macroeconomics course frameworks, with the
  economics content standards (CEE 2010) and the personal finance standards (NSPFE 2021) as references, and adds
  inside its days what a strong set treats as essential ("Check set adds"); check-set names never appear in student,
  teacher or family copy. The values rule governs: where economists' studies disagree (minimum wage effects, trade's
  winners and losers, the size of a stimulus), both lines of evidence are taught and the student judges.
- **Open to grade 11 or 12** (Cory, Desk card q-ss-progression-1). No row assumes US History I or US History II: the
  examples a row once took from US History I (the corporation, the trusts, the national bank fight, railroad booms) are
  retaught in brief from the source. Supply and demand and opportunity cost come from grades 7 to 10 and ss8-public-budget.
  US History II's unit 1 toolkit covers some of the same ground as a review; neither course assumes the other.
- **Personal finance rules.** Every offer, quote, portfolio and brand is made up; no real company or product is named
  or recommended. No student is asked about family income, debt or spending. Math is done on given figures.
- **Base text.** None for the course (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, P2). Partial bases, adaptable with a one-line credit
  per day: the retired CC BY Principles of Economics 2e (store folder `openstax-retired-ccby\`) for units 1 (chapters
  1 and 2), 2 (chapters 3 to 5), 3 (chapters 7 to 10 and 14), 4 (chapters 11 to 13 and 18, with 30 for budgets), 5
  (chapters 19 to 22 and 24), 6 (chapters 27, 28 and 30 to 31), 7 (chapters 23, 29, 33 and 34) and 9 (chapter 17;
  chapter 16 serves unit 8's insurance); the public-domain youth financial education activities of the federal
  consumer financial protection agency (store folder `cfpb-youth-finance\`, its name and logo left out of pages) for
  units 8 (budgets, payment methods, consumer protection, insurance, identity theft) and 9 (saving, credit scores, card
  statements, avoiding debt, paying for college). The frame does not map them lesson by lesson: the unit plan names what
  it adapts.
- **Inquiry arc and informed action.** Every unit runs on its compelling question, with a data or source task the
  student has not seen at its end. The year's informed action is in unit 8 (econ-spending-consumer): students choose a
  consumer question their peers face, check it against public consumer-protection advice, and make an information
  piece shared through the school. Never a cause the course picks.
- **Two graphs kept apart.** The market supply and demand graph (one good's price against its quantity, unit 2) and
  the aggregate supply and demand graph (the price level against real GDP, unit 5) are taught as different models with
  different axes.
- **The grade 12 timetable.** The table is in docs/courses/us-history-ii/frame.md: suggested in the spring half for a
  grade 12 student taking all three courses, after US History II's unit 1 toolkit. A grade 11 student takes it beside
  US History I in either half.
