- [[s03-W44]] (practice, judged by teacher): key: A claim weighing trade, faith and the sword, two explained details, an answered counterclaim and a named limit; the change from Chinggis to Khubilai and a Silk Roads consequence.; samples: The sword came first for the Mongols: Ibn al-Athir records the killing of women, men and children. Under Khubilai the Yuan ruled through government and a paper money that Polo says everyone had to take on pain of death, and that let people buy and sell across the realm. Under Mongol rule merchants, envoys and missionaries crossed the Silk Roads more safely (the day 44 card), but the conquests that made the roads safe had destroyed cities. In Mali, faith and trade worked together: Ibn Battuta says people kept the hours of prayer, and travellers traded salt and beads for food. Someone could say Mali's ruler also relied on force, since three hundred armed enslaved men walked behind him; but those guards show how he kept power at court, not how Mali's trade grew. Limit: Ibn Battuta was a visitor praising what matched his own training, and no source here is by the people of Mali. / Trade, because both were rich.
