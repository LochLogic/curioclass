## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did geography or human choice do more to decide which peoples met before 1000?"
- **Hook:** Show the Nabataean bowl beside a route map. What could explain related pottery appearing far apart? Separate observation from inference.
- **Supporting questions, drafted together on day 4:** What moved, and who benefited (days 2 and 3)? What happened simultaneously (day 5)? How did routes and choices change places (days 6 to 10)? Whose evidence survives (days 11 and 12)?
- **Closing claim, day 13:** Weigh geography against choices using the Periplus, the network map and the Indonesian Srivijaya account; answer a counterclaim and name an evidence limit.
- **Teaching:** Model classification on day 2, question revision on day 4 and sourcing on day 11. Partners explain on day 12; students select evidence independently on day 13. Teach interaction, monsoon, mortality, provenance and corroboration in context.

**Assessment packet, reserved for day 14:** The following are Curio Class paraphrases, not quotations. Supply maker, date, place, audience and purpose beside each source. Do not use these passages, stories, dates or model data in teaching examples or exit tickets.

- **U1:** Augustus's public account of his reign, prepared by 14 CE for display in Rome and imperial communities, describes his fleet reaching the northern ocean, his taking Egypt under Roman rule, his founding colonies of soldiers in several provinces, and embassies seeking Roman friendship. He presents expansion as achievement. The surviving copy at Ankara is an inscription; Shipley's 1924 translation supplies sections 26 to 31. This is a ruler's self-presentation, not the inhabitants' account.
- **U2:** Pliny's Natural History, about 77 CE, a Roman encyclopaedia dedicated to Titus, compiles information from earlier writers and travellers. In book 6, chapter 26, he describes ships sailing from the Red Sea toward India in a favourable season, an offshore anchorage near Muziris where small boats carried cargo, pepper brought from the interior to Barace, and the return season. He criticizes the expense of imports to Rome. His account does not record Indian dockworkers speaking for themselves.
- **U3:** Ashoka's Rock Edict XIII, third century BCE, was commissioned for public display in his realm. It reports conquest and suffering in Kalinga, expresses regret and presents spreading dhamma, moral conduct, as a preferable victory. It claims influence toward Greek rulers. A ruler's report establishes his message; it does not independently prove the response abroad. Use Hultzsch's 1925 translation, with the chosen inscription version identified in the finished packet.
- **M, invented model, not historical measurements:** A sheltered bay connects to an inland river through a mountain pass. A desert limits a second land route; grassland permits a third. Seasonal winds favour outward and return sea travel. Timber grows near the river. Traders build quays by clearing woodland. In town P, population begins at 10,000; births 400, deaths 600, arrivals 300 and departures 1,100. Town Q begins at 6,000; births 300, deaths 200, arrivals 1,600 and departures 200. Treat changes in population as possible influences on labour and demand, not proof of actual trade volumes.
- **Display data:** On an unfamiliar supplied eastern Mediterranean base map, distinguish Egyptian territory from a sea route and show an invasion arrow into Egypt. Supply locations and physical outlines rather than requiring memorized coordinates. Timeline data: Mauryan rule, about 322 to 185 BCE; Ptolemaic rule in Egypt, 305 to 30 BCE. The map and timeline are drawn on paper; no part of the sitting needs a device.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | All movement is conquest, or all exchange benefits everyone. | Categories overlap, but control, consent and unequal costs must be distinguished. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Geography determines all contact, and people never change land. | One-way environmental reasoning erases human decisions and feedback. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | A biased source is useless, or a primary source is automatically true. | Usefulness depends on the question and the maker's access and purpose. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | A route is a border; adjacent timeline lanes mean successive events; graph units can change silently. | Display conventions have replaced the underlying spatial or numerical relationship. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | A supporting question merely repeats the main question; reading cannot change an inquiry. | Questions are treated as a fixed list rather than tools for investigation. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | Two agreeing elite accounts represent everyone. | Selection ignores independence, relevance and absent perspectives. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| G | Bantu-speaking peoples were one uniform invading people, identifiable from any pottery. | A language family is confused with a single culture, race or migration event. | Schoenbrun, Moving Histories, introduction and conclusions. |

Section plans use these letters. They distinguish an unsupported first idea from evidence about an individual student.
