- **L, invented ledger, not a historical record:** Six investors each contribute 30 guilders to a voyage fund. A storm leaves 90 guilders to divide equally. Each receives 15 and loses 15. Explain shared capital and remaining risk; arithmetic is not scored. The separate Dutch wealth account names immigrant skills, shipbuilding, Baltic and overseas trade, urban finance, and wealthy art and instrument buyers. Label it a secondary account grounded in field 10.
- **S, science packet:** An original diagram shows Venus as a crescent and a broad illuminated disc beside Ptolemaic, Copernican and Tychonic arrangements. Both latter arrangements permit the observed phases. A second note, from Hooke's 1665 cork observation, describes repeated small compartments visible with magnification. Ask what checking by other observers adds. These observations challenge explanations without settling all cosmic motions. Supply short contextual notes on Galileo's telescopic work and Kepler's use of Tycho's observations to formulate elliptical paths. Students explain different contributions. A cargo-ship diagram shows a roomy hull and mixed sail arrangements; a note describes charts, a compass and latitude observations, with uncertain longitude.
- **U, unseen first-hand account:** Nieuhof's report of the 1655 to 1657 embassy, first published in Dutch in 1665, later translated and abridged, reproduces a letter attributed to the Qing emperor. Its reported terms permit a mission every eight years, at most one hundred men, with twenty proceeding to court. This is an original summary, not an authenticated imperial original. Supply author, dates, employment, European readership and mediation chain. Reserve the letter passage in Nieuhof, An Embassy from the East-India Company (London, 1669, trans.
