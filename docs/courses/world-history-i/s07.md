## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was the age of 1300 to 1600 a rebirth, or a story told about it later?"
- **Hook, day 94:** Compare Ortelius's 1570 map of the Ottoman Empire with Basawan's manuscript painting of an imagined visit by Alexander to Plato. Can a European label describe both? Open the compelling question; return to the painting on 96.
- **Supporting questions, drafted together:** How did rulers govern difference (97)? Who financed cultural change (98)? What was reborn, for whom (100)? Who decides significance (101)? How did religious reform redistribute power (106)?
- **Closing claim, day 107:** Answer the compelling question using Ortelius, the Mughal manuscript, the Venice evidence, Christine and Reformation sources. Explain all four strands and answer one counterclaim in one sentence.
- **Teaching:** Model comparison in the opening of 97, humanism on 99, significance in the last ten minutes of 100 and persuasion in the first ten of 105. Students question and corroborate independently on 101 and 105. Anabaptists: met on 103, again on 106. Teach sultan, devshirme, patron, humanism, indulgence, predestination and persecution in context.


- **D2.His.3.9-12:** Use questions about people and groups to judge how their actions acquire different significance over time and in different historical settings. Parts, by verb: use generated questions; assess changing significance; explain context.
- **Limits:** Safavid rivalry and Sikh origins stay within 95 and 96. Sikhism predates Mughal rule; it is a distinct tradition. Religious administration evolved; "millet system" does not mean one timeless arrangement. Trade continued after 1453. Rebirth language predates the nineteenth-century period label. The Taj Mahal and later wars are chronology references, not assessed content. Informed action stays in unit 10.
- **Carried:** Unit 6's models: comparison, sourcing, corroboration, cause-effect-chain, claim-evidence-reasoning, timeline. Return to region, map-key and map-scale on 94; perspective, source-sourcing and corroboration on 97 and 105. Revise supply-demand into trade and patronage links on 98. Use continuity-change on 99, cause-effect-chain on 106 and claim-evidence-reasoning on 107. Update timeline on 107. Withdraw completed comparison models on 97 and significance models on 101; retain blank organizers.

**Supplies:** Printed sources, maps, timeline strips, blank comparison organizers and record sheets. Day 108: fresh outline map, caption cards, S1, S2 and the key. Day 105: printed Protestant and Catholic prints. Every task has a paper path. Describe images aloud; cap independent extracts at 150 words and read dense passages aloud. Rehearse "Its significance changed because ___; this source cannot establish ___." Extension tests another interpretation. No student timers.

## 9. Family note

We will study empires, trade, art and religious reform through different accounts. Learners will explain changing historical significance and discuss coercion and persecution with dignity.
