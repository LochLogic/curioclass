## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Was a ruler's power in this age won mainly by trade, by faith or by the sword?"
- **Hook, day 31:** Contrast accounts of Musa's pilgrimage gifts and Chinggis Khan's conquests. Which shows power, and whose account is it? Introduce the question before readiness repair.
- **Supporting questions, drafted together:** How did African rulers connect resources and learning (day 34)? How do historians know about Musa (day 36)? How did conquest reshape exchange (day 40)? Whose power does a map show (day 43)?
- **Closing claim, day 44:** Weigh trade, faith and warfare from the section 3.4 packets (u03-4.md). Explain two details, answer a counterclaim, name a limit; revised on day 45 after written feedback.
- **Teaching:** Model overlapping power terms on day 31 and a source-to-interpretation link on day 35; withdraw the completed paragraph on day 36. Students inspect a further source, revise questions and answer a counterclaim. Reuse independently on day 40. Teach legitimacy, tribute, caravan, city-state, griot and interpretation through evidence.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Political terms are mutually exclusive labels. | Collapses distinct relationships. Apply overlapping terms. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Modern borders equal medieval states. | Projects current territory backward. Use a dated political map. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Routes carry goods in only one direction. | Misses relays and return exchange. Trace both directions. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Trade cancels conquest's harms. | Treats gains as compensation for violence. Record both separately. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | A source-maker could know every claim attributed to the source. | Ignores access and transmission. Check what the maker could know. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | A historian's conclusion is primary evidence. | Confuses interpretation with testimony. Trace the underlying source. | D2.His.9.9-12. |
| G | Naming another source counts as investigating it. | Omits inspection. Read a detail and revise the inquiry. | D2.His.12.9-12. |
| H | Different states and dynasties belong to one period. | Collapses chronology. Align separate dated bands. | Anticipated, from memory. |

## 7. Response allocation

- **Respond days (days.csv):** day 46.
- **Day length (data/plans/frames.csv):** 50 minutes.
Day 45: claim revision 5; assessment 40 (field 5); marking 5. All items are teacher-judged; in the last five minutes the A2a, A2b and A2d key is read aloud and students circle changes. Marking A2 and the sitting after class on day 45 takes about 3 hours for 30, untested.

Day 46: feedback 5, response 25, review 15, exit 5. Give group slips; marks stay private. Each group uses its move on a parallel prompt, then each student fixes their own answer. The review pairs a unit 1 or 2 timeline entry with a unit 3 one, for days 47 and 48. For one child, review missed items together.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

Tools: None. Print every source, map, survey option and task. Gather pencils, rulers and tracing paper. A projector is optional; an adult replaces partner discussion at home.

## 9. Family note

We will compare how African kingdoms and Mongol rulers gained power, using maps and accounts that sometimes disagree. Students will explain evidence while recognizing the lives and achievements of people beyond rulers.
