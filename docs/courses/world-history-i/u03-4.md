# Section 3.4: Mapping power and defending a claim

World History I, grade 9. Unit 3, Kingdoms of Africa and the Mongol world, 1000 to 1500 (days 31 to 46): see
[s03.md](s03.md). **Days 42 to 46.** Days by kind: application 3 (42, 43, 44), check 1 (45), respond 1 (46). A plan,
not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with students. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md. Social studies forms where
the shape is math-shaped: field 6 is one `## Day N` block a day (parts and minutes, sources, examples, hand-backs,
materials, print) instead of the days table; the sources and their rights are rows of
data/plans/world-history-i/sources.csv.

- **MA.WHI.T3.2, the standard's own task.** "Map" one kingdom or empire and "explain" its politics, economy and
  culture and its trade, alliances, warfare and exchange with other regions. Each student maps one option: a (Ghana,
  Mali and Songhai), b (the Swahili city-states with earlier Axum) or d (the Mongol Empire). Days 42 and 43 make the
  product; it comes back with written feedback, is revised on day 44 and scored as A2 from the folder that evening.
- **The options a, b and d.** Taught in sections 3.1 and 3.3; here they are surveyed on day 45 (A2a, A2b, A2d) for
  the options a student did not map.
- **MA.WHI.T3.1.** The words for power return in day 42's warm-up and day 44's discussion, and are checked in A1.
- **MA.WHI.T3.3, T3.4, D2.His.9 and D2.His.12.** Taught in sections 3.2 and 3.3; checked here on day 45 (A3, A4,
  A5a, A5b) on unseen sources.
- **MA.WHI.T1.5b** (owned by unit 1). The parallel timeline grows on day 43 and is reviewed on day 46.
- **Limits that bite here.** Options c and e to l are not offered (unit 4 and unit 7). No role play of rulers,
  conquerors, traders or enslaved people. The Mongol conquest's killing is named from Ibn al-Athir's chronicle,
  with no total the sources do not support. An alliance a source on the sheet does not record is reported as not
  recorded, never invented.
- **Connections.** D3.1.9-12 on day 44 (choosing evidence from several sources for the discussion); D4.1.9-12 in the
  closing claim (a counterclaim answered).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That the K2 chain runs inside the section: the map is practiced on days
  42 and 43, marked with written feedback, revised on day 44 and only then scored; the claim is written on day 44,
  marked with written feedback that evening and revised in day 45's first five minutes. That every quoted traveller
  was an outsider writing for readers at home, and Ibn Battuta dictated his book years later; the peoples of these
  places kept their own histories (griots' songs, the Tarikh al-Sudan, the Kilwa Chronicle, Ezana's inscriptions),
  named on the sheets from sources.csv 42-BG1 to 42-BG4, never quoted; the Secret History of the Mongols has a free
  translation (CC BY-NC-ND), and option d quotes one short excerpt unchanged as Source K (42K). That Kilwa's
  surviving buildings are coral stone, though Gibb's translation says wood.

## 2. Where it sits

Section 3.3 (days 37 to 41) ends with the words for power applied to Mali, the Mongols and the Yuan (day 41). This
section uses all of sections 3.1 to 3.3: students map what they learned and defend a claim on the unit's question.
Day 45 is the end-of-unit assessment; day 46 responds to it and reviews units 1 to 3 for course-level check 1
(days 47 and 48). Unit 4's readiness check is given on day 49.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** all of A1 to A5b (s03 field 5) sit on day 45; A2 is the map product of days 42 and
43, revised on day 44 and scored from the folder that evening; A2d's Chinggis-to-Khubilai and Silk Roads parts are scored from the closing claim of day 44,
revised on day 45.

**Exit questions.** Each is judged by the teacher against its look-fors (data/plans/world-history-i/items.csv,
`steps`). If many miss one (more than a third), the next day's warm-up and first five minutes repair it.

<!-- gen:exits (from data/plans/world-history-i; do not edit here) -->
- **E42.** Invented for practice, a traveller's note: "At the ford the king's men take one bar of salt in ten; the carriers say the salt comes from pans twenty days north." Sketch the connection from producer through carrier to collector. Then say one thing the note shows about the king's power, one about the economy, and one thing it cannot show. Answers: Salt pans to carriers to the king's men at the ford; power: the king takes a share of what crosses; economy: others dig the salt and carry it twenty days; the note cannot show what producers or carriers thought, or the king's whole income.. Wrong: The king was rich because the river was there. (I).
- **E43.** Invented for practice: state R lasts from 1180 to 1360 and state S from 1270 to 1420, and imported ceramics turn up at R's port. Plot both on a timeline, give the overlap, and explain what the ceramics show about R's links to another region. Answers: Overlap 1270 to 1360; the ceramics show exchange with another region, not foreign builders.. Wrong: R and S were one state, since their years overlap. (H); The ceramics show foreigners founded R. (K).
- **E44.** Invented for practice, a merchant's note: "The khan's riders guard the road. His officials take one bale of cloth in five. Traders pray at the shrine he built." Match each detail to the sword, trade or faith. Which mattered most for his power, and what can the note not show? Answers: Riders guarding the road: the sword; one bale in five: trade, taxed; the shrine: faith. Any one may be weighed most if the reason uses the note; the note cannot show what the traders thought or how the khan first won power.. Wrong: The market made the khan rich, so every trader gained. (P).
- **E46.** On the parallel timeline, choose two developments from different regions that you did not pair in the review. Give each its dated band, say whether they overlap, and name one link between them from a source, or say that no source here records one. Answers: Two developments from different regions dated from the timeline; overlap stated or ruled out; a link from a source, or its absence stated.. Wrong: Mali rose after the Mongol Empire fell. (H).

**The repair rule for this section.** A repair takes the warm-up and, where needed, a small group during the map
work. It never cuts the map or claim writing time. Days 44 and 45 have no warm-up of their own: the map and claim
feedback carry the repair.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The map-and-explain folder (option sheet, map, part 1, part 2, written feedback, revision) from day
  42 to day 44, then it stays with the teacher for scoring; the closing claim from day 44 to day 45; the class parallel timeline from day 5.
- **Key moment, days 42 and 43: the map.** Ideal instrument: a blank outline map of the student's region with a
  printed scale bar, and tracing paper for the route layer. No free outline map with a clear license was found for
  these three regions (later.csv L55); until ours are drawn, students draw on squared paper from the fact card's
  locator lines (for example, Timbuktu sits on the Niger's northern bend), and the teacher accepts a sketch map with a
  key and either a scale bar or a "not to scale" label. Journey times are not distances.
- **Key moment, day 44: the discussion.** Paper: the sources on the two source sheets, a three-column board
  (trade, faith, sword) the teacher fills as students speak.
- **A room with nothing.** Every source and fact card prints on the source sheets; the teacher reads each source
  aloud once.

## 6. Days

Every day is 50 minutes (frame). Every source is a row of sources.csv with its citation and rights line; its id is
the day and the letter on the source sheet (42C is day 42's Source C). Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton
reads them.

## Day 42

- **5 min. Warm-up.** [[s03-P42-1-v2]]: the three statements about a court source, then two words for power
  applied to Mansa Sulayman. Hand-back: day 41 exit, errors L and A.
- **8 min. Teaching first.** The task and its three options; what a dated map needs (years in the title, a key, a
  scale bar or a "not to scale" label, routes and centres); the option fact cards with their locator lines. A
  one-minute gist of Source A first; the other sources are read in the work block.
- **7 min. Worked example.** [[s03-K42-1-v2]], invented for practice: one label about 1300 and an arrow, with
  politics and economy; a second label is modeled from Source D.
- **25 min. Alone: the map, part 1.** [[s03-P42-2-v3]] on squared paper with the option's sources and fact card.
  The teacher confers with students who have not placed a date or a route.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X42-v2]], alone, on paper.

Sources: 42A to 42E, 42H (the poets), 42I (Mombasa), 42J (Mongke's letter) and 42K (the Secret History), the fact
cards (42-CARDS) and the insider-history lines (42-BG1 to 42-BG5). Example: s03-K42-1-v2. Hand-backs in: s03-X41. Out: s03-X42-v2 to day 43's warm-up. Materials:
option sheets, map-and-explain folder, squared paper. Print: student page, the three option sheets (sources-42a,
42b, 42d; kept to day 46), exit ticket.

## Day 43

- **5 min. Warm-up.** [[s03-P43-1]]. Hand-back: day 42 exit, error I.
- **5 min. Teaching first.** One culture model with its makers beyond the ruler, women among them (Source B's
  village women); for option b, the Swahili language and Kilwa's coral-stone mosque, with the Shona as an inland
  link. The four links (trade, alliance, war, exchange); "no source on my sheet records an alliance" is an answer.
- **22 min. Alone: the map, part 2.** [[s03-P43-2]].
- **8 min. Pairs: share.** Partners from different options swap maps; each writes one question on the partner's map
  about a link or a date. Ask: "Whose power does your map show, and whose does it leave out?"
- **5 min. Timeline.** [[s03-P43-3]]: Mali and the Yuan go on the parallel timeline as two bands, on side 2 of the
  student page.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X43-v2]], alone.

Folders are collected. That evening the teacher writes feedback on each map in short phrases, about 5 minutes a
map: dated geography, politics, economy, culture, every link, and the limit of a source used.

Sources: day 42's. Hand-backs in: s03-X42-v2. Out: the map product with written feedback and s03-X43-v2 to day 44
(errors H and K). Materials: option sheets, map-and-explain folder, parallel timeline. Print: student page, exit
ticket.

## Day 44

- **5 min. Read feedback.** [[s03-P44-1]]. Hand-back: the map product with written feedback, with s03-P43-1,
  s03-P43-3 and day 43's exit clipped to it.
- **8 min. Map revision.** [[s03-P44-2]], alone. A student with an H miss first redraws the two bands on the back of
  the day 43 ticket (3 minutes). The teacher walks the room and confers with H or K misses.
- **15 min. Discussion: trade, faith or the sword.** [[s03-P44-3]]. Sources F (Mongke Khan, through Rubruck) and G
  (Ibn Battuta on Mali) read aloud, then the option sheets. Round one: each speaker gives one sourced detail and a
  unit word for the kind of power; round two: answer a detail with a detail from another source. Ask: "Does this
  detail show power won, or power kept?" The teacher fills three columns on the board.
- **17 min. Closing claim.** [[s03-W44]], alone, from the board, the source sheets and the Chinggis-to-Khubilai card
  (44-CARD), with two sentence frames.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X44-v2]], alone.

Folders and claims are collected; folders stay with the teacher. That evening the teacher scores A2 from the folders
and writes six feedback lines on each claim: the weighing, two explained details, the counterclaim, the limit,
Chinggis to Khubilai, and the Silk Roads consequence with harms kept.

Sources: 44F, 44G and 44-CARD; day 42's. Hand-backs in: the map product, s03-P43-1, s03-P43-3, s03-X43-v2. Out: the
claim with written feedback to day 45; s03-P44-1 to s03-P44-3 kept for A2; s03-X44-v2 to day 46. Materials: option
sheets, day 44 source sheet, map-and-explain folder. Print: student page, day 44 source sheet, lined paper, exit
ticket.

## Day 45

- **5 min. Claim revision.** [[s03-W44]] comes back, then [[s03-P45-1]]: read the written feedback and revise the
  claim only; the revised claim is collected before the paper is handed out.
- **40 min. Assessment.** [[s03-A1-v2]] 5 minutes, [[s03-A2a]] 3, [[s03-A2b-v2]] 2, [[s03-A2d]] 5, [[s03-A3-v2]] 6,

## Day 46

- **5 min. Feedback.** The marked assessment comes back by component, with the packet and goods cards for

- **25 min. Response groups.** [[s03-P46-1-v2]]. Each group uses its move on a new case from a source already
  taught, then each student fixes or strengthens their own answer; others work in pairs from the written move.
- **15 min. Review: units 1 to 3.** [[s03-P46-2-v2]]: review units 1 and 2 entries beside unit 3 on the parallel

Sources: none new. Hand-backs in: the marked assessment, s03-X44-v2. Out: s03-X46-v2 to day 49. Materials: parallel
timeline, response slips. Print: student page, slips, exit ticket.

  does it leave out?"; day 44, "Was a ruler's power in this age won
  mainly by trade, by faith or by the sword?"
- Written task, day 44: the closing claim ([[s03-W44]]), revised on day 45 and scored in A2d.
- The map product (s03-P42-2-v3, s03-P43-2), revised on day 44, is scored as A2 that evening.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 42 and 43 repair the last exit; day 44 reads map feedback; day 45 revises the claim; day 46 reads
  the marked assessment. No timer; the warm-up ends when most have written.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standards set none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: dynasty, empire, tribute, caravan, city-state, sultan, khan, griot, deputy, alliance,
  interpretation. Frame: "This source shows __, but it cannot show __ because __ (who made it, when, or for whom)."
  Words for people: "enslaved people" in our words; the sources' own words stay inside the source, are glossed once
  and, read aloud, take one substitute each: "blacks" and "negroes", the people of Mali; "Negrolands", the lands of
  Mali; "slaves", enslaved people; "Tartars", the Mongols (outsiders' name for them).
- **Access.** Source sheets come in large print; the atlas image is described in words; a student who cannot write
  long dictates the exit and the claim. A student may draw the map on squared paper.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 45 may be read aloud and answered aloud with a scribe; the look-fors stay the same.
- **Reading load.** Sources of 60 to 180 words, glossed, read aloud before students work alone.
- **Extension, same standard.** Day 43: add a second link from another option's source sheet; day 46: compare Ibn
  Battuta's Kilwa with the stone mosque that survives there and say what the source got wrong.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The parent reads each source with the child and asks "who made it, when, for whom" first.
  The discussion on day 44 becomes a conversation: the parent takes the other side with a source.
- **One projector.** Show the source sheet; students keep their printed copy for marking.
