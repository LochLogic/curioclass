# Section 3.4: Mapping power and defending a claim

World History I, grade 9. Unit 3, Kingdoms of Africa and the Mongol world, 1000 to 1500 (days 31 to 46): see
[s03.md](s03.md). **Days 42 to 46.** Days by kind: application 3 (42, 43, 44), check 1 (45), respond 1 (46). A plan,
not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with students. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md. Social studies forms where
the shape is math-shaped: field 6 is one `## Day N` block a day (parts and minutes, sources, examples, hand-backs,
materials, print) instead of the days table; the sources and their rights are rows of
data/plans/world-history-i/sources.csv.

- **MA.WHI.T3.2, the standard's own task.** "Map" one kingdom or empire and "explain" its politics, economy and
  culture and its trade, alliances, warfare and exchange with other regions. Each student maps one option: a (Ghana,
  Mali and Songhai), b (the Swahili city-states with earlier Axum) or d (the Mongol Empire). Days 42 and 43 make the
  product; it comes back with written feedback, is revised on day 44 and scored as A2 on day 45.
- **The options a, b and d.** Taught in sections 3.1 and 3.3; here they are surveyed on day 45 (A2a, A2b, A2d) for
  the options a student did not map.
- **MA.WHI.T3.1.** The words for power return in day 42's warm-up and day 44's discussion, and are checked in A1.
- **MA.WHI.T3.3, T3.4, D2.His.9 and D2.His.12.** Taught in sections 3.2 and 3.3; checked here on day 45 (A3, A4,
  A5a, A5b) on unseen sources.
- **MA.WHI.T1.5b** (owned by unit 1). The parallel timeline grows on day 43 and is reviewed on day 46.
- **Limits that bite here.** Options c and e to l are not offered (unit 4 and unit 7). No role play of rulers,
  conquerors, traders or enslaved people. The Mongol conquest's killing is named from Ibn al-Athir's chronicle,
  with no total the sources do not support. An alliance a source on the sheet does not record is reported as not
  recorded, never invented.
- **Connections.** D3.1.9-12 on day 44 (choosing evidence from several sources for the discussion); D4.1.9-12 in the
  closing claim (a counterclaim answered).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That the K2 chain runs inside the section: the map is practised on days
  42 and 43, marked with written feedback, revised on day 44 and only then scored; the claim is written on day 44,
  marked with written feedback that evening and revised in day 45's first five minutes. That every quoted traveller
  was an outsider writing for readers at home, and Ibn Battuta dictated his book years later. That Kilwa's surviving
  buildings are coral stone, though Gibb's translation says wood.

## 2. Where it sits

Section 3.3 (days 37 to 41) ends with the words for power applied to Mali, the Mongols and the Yuan (day 41). This
section uses all of sections 3.1 to 3.3: students map what they learned and defend a claim on the unit's question.
Day 45 is the end-of-unit assessment; day 46 responds to it and reviews units 1 to 3 for course-level check 1
(days 47 and 48). Unit 4's readiness check is given on day 49.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** all of A1 to A5b (s03 field 5) sit on day 45; A2 is the map product of days 42 and
43, revised on day 44; A2d's Chinggis-to-Khubilai and Silk Roads parts are scored from the closing claim of day 44,
revised on day 45.

**Exit questions.** Each is judged by the teacher against its look-fors (data/plans/world-history-i/items.csv,
`steps`). If many miss one (more than a third), the next day's warm-up and first five minutes repair it.

- **E42.** Invented for practice: a ruler taxes a river crossing that inland farmers and miners supply. Mark the connection on the map, and explain what it meant for the ruler's power and for the economy. Answers: The crossing tied the ruler's revenue and authority to the producers' labour and to exchange.. Wrong: The ruler was rich because the river was there. (I).
- **E43.** Invented for practice: state R lasts from 1180 to 1360 and state S from 1270 to 1420, and imported ceramics turn up at R's port. Plot both on a timeline, give the overlap, and explain one link to another region without claiming foreign founders. Answers: Overlap 1270 to 1360; the ceramics show exchange with another region, not foreign builders.. Wrong: R and S were one state, since their years overlap. (H); The ceramics show foreigners founded R. (K).
- **E44.** Invented for practice: a trading town grows wealthy under its ruler while a local record lists forced labour. Which detail challenges the claim that trade benefited everyone, and how? Answers: The forced labour shows a cost carried by people who did not share the wealth.. Wrong: The town was rich, so everyone gained. (I).
- **E46.** Choose one assessment component marked 1 or 0 and correct it alone. Then put one development from this unit beside one from another region on the parallel timeline, and explain why they belong side by side. Answers: A missed component corrected with evidence; two developments placed in the same years and the overlap explained.. Wrong: Mali rose after the Mongol Empire fell. (H).

**The repair rule for this section.** A repair takes the warm-up and, where needed, a small group during the map
work. It never cuts the map or claim writing time. Days 44 and 45 have no warm-up of their own: the map and claim
feedback carry the repair.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The map-and-explain folder (option sheet, outline map, part 1, part 2, written feedback, revision)
  from day 42 to day 45; the closing claim from day 44 to day 45; the class parallel timeline from day 5.
- **Key moment, days 42 and 43: the map.** Ideal instrument: a blank outline map of the student's region with a
  printed scale bar, and tracing paper for the route layer. Our own outline maps (West Africa and the Sahara; East
  Africa and the Indian Ocean; Eurasia) are still to draw; until they are, students draw on squared paper from the
  fact card's coordinates and towns, and the teacher accepts a sketch map with a key and scale.
- **Key moment, day 44: the discussion.** Paper: the sources on the two source sheets, a three-column board
  (trade, faith, sword) the teacher fills as students speak.
- **A room with nothing.** Every source and fact card prints on the source sheets; the teacher reads each source
  aloud once.

## 6. Days

Every day is 50 minutes (frame). Every source is a row of sources.csv with its citation and rights line; its id is
the day and the letter on the source sheet (42C is day 42's Source C). Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton
reads them.

## Day 42

- **5 min. Warm-up.** [[s03-P42-1]]. Hand-back: day 41 exit, error L.
- **8 min. Teaching first.** The task and its three options; what a dated map needs (years in the title, a key, a
  scale, routes and centres); the option fact cards; Sources A to E, one read aloud for each option (42A, 42C, 42D).
  Students choose an option and write it on the folder.
- **7 min. Worked example.** [[s03-K42-1]], invented for practice: one label and two sentences, politics and economy.
- **25 min. Alone: the map, part 1.** [[s03-P42-2]] with the option's sources and fact card. The teacher confers
  with students who have not placed a date or a route.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X42]], alone, on paper.

Sources: 42A to 42E and the fact cards (42-CARDS). Example: s03-K42-1. Hand-backs in: s03-X41. Out: s03-X42 to day
43's warm-up. Materials: day 42 option sheets, map-and-explain folder, outline maps. Print: student page, the three day 42
option sheets (sources-42a, 42b, 42d; kept to day 44), outline maps, exit ticket.

## Day 43

- **5 min. Warm-up.** [[s03-P43-1]]. Hand-back: day 42 exit, error I.
- **5 min. Teaching first.** Culture with its makers beyond the ruler; the four links (trade, alliance, war,
  exchange) and where each option's sources record them; "no source on my sheet records an alliance" is an answer.
- **22 min. Alone: the map, part 2.** [[s03-P43-2]].
- **8 min. Pairs: share.** Partners from different options swap maps; each writes one question on the partner's map
  about a link or a date.
- **5 min. Timeline.** [[s03-P43-3]]: Mali and the Yuan go on the parallel timeline as two bands.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X43]], alone.

Folders are collected. That evening the teacher writes feedback on each map, one line each: dated geography,
politics, economy, culture, every link, and the limit of a source used.

Sources: 42A to 42E, fact cards. Hand-backs in: s03-X42. Out: the map product with written feedback and s03-X43 to day
44. Materials: day 42 option sheets, map-and-explain folder, outline maps, parallel timeline. Print: student page,
exit ticket.

## Day 44

- **5 min. Read feedback.** [[s03-P44-1]]. Hand-back: the map product with written feedback, day 43's exit clipped
  to it.
- **10 min. Map revision.** [[s03-P44-2]], alone. The first version stays in the folder.
- **15 min. Discussion: trade, faith or the sword.** [[s03-P44-3]]. Sources F (Mongke Khan, through Rubruck) and G
  (Ibn Battuta on Mali) read aloud, then the day 42 option sheets. Round one: each speaker gives one sourced detail; round
  two: answer a detail with a detail from another source. The teacher fills three columns on the board.
- **15 min. Closing claim.** [[s03-W44]], alone, from the board and the source sheets.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X44]], alone.

Claims are collected. That evening the teacher writes feedback on each claim, one line each: the weighing, the
Chinggis-to-Khubilai change, the Silk Roads consequence with harms kept, the counterclaim and the limit.

Sources: 44F and 44G; 42A to 42E. Hand-backs in: the map product with written feedback, s03-X43. Out: the revised
map to day 45; the claim with written feedback to day 45; s03-X44 to day 46. Materials: day 42 option sheets, day 44
source sheet, map-and-explain folder. Print: student page, day 44 source sheet, lined paper, exit ticket.

## Day 45

- **5 min. Claim revision.** [[s03-P45-1]]: read the written feedback and revise the claim; the revised claim and
  the revised map are collected for scoring before the paper is handed out.
- **40 min. Assessment.** [[s03-A1]] 6 minutes, [[s03-A2a]] 3, [[s03-A2b]] 2, [[s03-A2d]] 4, [[s03-A3]] 5,
  [[s03-A4]] 6, [[s03-A5a]] 6, [[s03-A5b]] 8. A student answers the survey items for the two options not mapped
  and may skip the one mapped (A2 already scores it). The day 45 source packet is unseen until now.
- **5 min. Mark.** Pens down; the key is read aloud for A2a, A2b and A2d and students circle what they would
  change; the teacher scores every item after class with the look-fors. About 40 minutes after class, an untested
  estimate.

Sources: 45-P1, 45-P2, 45-S, 45-F1, 45-F2 and the goods cards (45-GOODS), all reserved. Hand-backs in: the claim
with written feedback. Out: the marked assessment to day 46. Materials: day 45 source packet, map-and-explain folder,
outline maps (A2b and A3 insets). Print: the assessment paper, the day 45 source packet (F1 and F2 folded), lined paper.

- **15 min. Review: units 1 to 3.** [[s03-P46-2]] on the parallel timeline, for course-level check 1.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** [[s03-X46]], alone.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 42, "What has to be on a map before someone else can trust it?"; day 43, "Which link to
  another region does your source record, and which does it not?"; day 44, "Was a ruler's power in this age won
  mainly by trade, by faith or by the sword?"
- Written task, day 44: the closing claim ([[s03-W44]]), revised on day 45 and scored in A2d.
- The map product (s03-P42-2, s03-P43-2), revised on day 44, is scored as A2.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Days 42 and 43 repair the last exit; day 44 reads map feedback; day 45 revises the claim; day 46 reads
  the marked assessment. No timer; the warm-up ends when most have written.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standards set none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: dynasty, empire, tribute, caravan, city-state, sultan, khan, griot, deputy, alliance,
  interpretation. Frame: "This source shows __, but it cannot show __ because __ (who made it, when, or for whom)."
  Words for people: "enslaved people" in our words; the sources' own words (Gibb's 1929 "negroes", Browne's 1902
  "Tartars", "slaves") stay inside the source and are glossed once.
- **Access.** Source sheets come in large print; the atlas image is described in words; a student who cannot write
  long dictates the exit and the claim. A student may draw the map on squared paper.
- **Check accommodations.** Day 45 may be read aloud and answered aloud with a scribe; the look-fors stay the same.
- **Reading load.** Sources of 60 to 180 words, glossed, read aloud before students work alone.
- **Extension, same standard.** Day 43: add a second link from another option's source sheet; day 46: compare Ibn
  Battuta's Kilwa with the stone mosque that survives there and say what the source got wrong.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** The parent reads each source with the child and asks "who made it, when, for whom" first.
  The discussion on day 44 becomes a conversation: the parent takes the other side with a source.
- **One projector.** Show the source sheet; students keep their printed copy for marking.
