**Teacher background, as of November 2026 (fix of 2026-11-14; kept in field 5 for the word cap):**
- Day 52: the published standard's T3.2f also names "restoration of temples destroyed in war" (Massachusetts framework, 2018, printed page 142); the local standards file leaves it out. Teach it with Todai-ji: burned in 1180 in the Taira attack on Nara; the monk Chogen led rebuilding from 1181; the Great Buddha was reconsecrated in 1185 and the Great Buddha Hall completed about ten years later. The present hall is a later rebuilding. Sources: [Todai-ji history](https://www.todaiji.or.jp/en/history/), [Great Buddha Hall](https://www.todaiji.or.jp/en/information/daibutsuden/).
- Day 53: "feudalism" is a contested label. Historians such as Elizabeth Brown ("The Tyranny of a Construct," 1974) and Susan Reynolds (Fiefs and Vassals, 1994) argue it groups different relationships under one modern word. Use it as a convenient term, not a single system.
- Day 53: the "Mongol yoke" is a later Russian term. Historians such as Charles Halperin and Donald Ostrowski describe a more mixed relationship of tribute, borrowing and local rule.
- Day 58: excavations by Mexico's national institute of anthropology and history at the Huei Tzompantli, the skull rack of Tenochtitlan, began in 2015 and continued after 2020. By 2023 they had recorded more than 650 skulls, mostly of men but many of women and some of children. Sacrifice was large in scale; early Spanish totals are not reliable, so teach the scale from archaeology. Name the Triple Alliance (Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, Tlacopan).
- Days 58 to 61, mediation notes: the Codex Mendoza was painted in Mexico City about 1541 by Indigenous painters, with Spanish notes, for the Spanish crown; it records Mexica tribute as remembered after conquest. Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala, an Andean noble, finished his illustrated chronicle about 1615 and addressed it to King Philip III of Spain, to argue against colonial abuses.
- A8 source: the Ategawa petition (Kenji 1, 1275) is described in a Hiroshima University paper ([repository record](https://ir.lib.hiroshima-u.ac.jp/42814/files/3361)); the proprietor was the temple Jakurakuji, which passed its rights to Koyasan in 1277.
