Invasions failed in 1274 and 1281. Buddhist sculpture served devotion; Zen teachers used calligraphy to express and transmit teachings. Rebuilding Todai-ji after 1180 brought back worship and work for sculptors." Does not: separately: "Japan became a Mongol province" (P); "Every Buddhist sculpture was Zen" (R).
- **A3:** Look for two located cities, earlier chronology, temples and three traded goods. Meets: "Tikal and Copan were distinct Classic Maya cities. Temple-pyramids concentrated religious and royal activity, while obsidian, jade and cacao circulated across long distances. Their Classic florescence preceded the Mexica and Inca states." Does not: separately: "Classic Maya and the later empires flourished simultaneously" (O); "Shared evidence proves one American state" (S). Accept approximate Classic dates with an accurate earlier band.
- **A4:** Look for capital, conquered neighbours, extraction and monument interpretation. Meets: "Tenochtitlan drew tribute from conquered communities, reducing their resources and independence. The Sun Stone presents religious meanings and authority, but does not tell us whether tributaries approved." Does not: separately: "The sculpture proves every subject loved the ruler" (B); "All tribute was voluntary" (C).
- **A5:** Look for connected administration, coercion and distinct architecture. Meets: "Inca conquest brought communities under labour obligations. Roads moved supplies, and quipu specialists kept records. Cusco was the capital; monumental Machu Picchu served royal and ceremonial purposes." Does not: separately: "Machu Picchu was the capital" (H); "All required labour was voluntary" (C).
- **A6:** Look for five located regions, historical boundaries and one supported comparison. Meets: "The Rus lands were not one modern country, and Iberia held several kingdoms. European vassal ties and Japanese warrior rule shared military service, but their institutions differed." Does not: "Every country had the same pyramid of king, samurai and serf." Error E. Check the map separately from this sample.
- **A7:** Look for wealth, reform, feudal emergence, knighthood, chivalry, charter limits and early Parliament. Meets: "Estates and dues made Church offices wealthy. Reformers opposed buying Church offices, an internal struggle; royal-papal appointment disputes were another conflict. Weak royal protection encouraged local land-for-service ties; knights were mounted warriors, and chivalry set ideals. Magna Carta limited arbitrary royal demands; early Parliament represented selected groups rather than all adults." Does not: separately: "The Church had no internal disputes" (F); "Magna Carta made all people equal voters" (L).
- **A8:** Look for two textual details, maker and purpose, and a supported limit. Meets: "The villagers say the steward takes their work time and sends back those fetching timber. They appeal to their temple proprietor against the steward. It is one village trying to win relief, so it cannot show what all peasants thought; other estate records could test it." Does not: "All peasants hated the shogun because one village complained." Error B.

**Teacher background, as of November 2026:**
- Day 52: the published standard's T3.2f also names "restoration of temples destroyed in war" (Massachusetts framework, 2018, printed page 142); the local standards file leaves it out. Teach it with Todai-ji: burned in 1180 in the Taira attack on Nara; the monk Chogen led rebuilding from 1181; the Great Buddha was reconsecrated in 1185 and the Great Buddha Hall completed about ten years later. The present hall is a later rebuilding. Sources: [Todai-ji history](https://www.todaiji.or.jp/en/history/), [Great Buddha Hall](https://www.todaiji.or.jp/en/information/daibutsuden/).
- Day 53: Constance B. Bouchard, ["Feudalism" (2012), free Introduction and General Overviews extract](https://doi.org/10.1093/obo/9780195396584-0018), explains that the later label combines divergent institutions. These two sections were read; the restricted remainder was not. Attribute this caution to Bouchard, not to unread works by Brown or Reynolds. Use the term narrowly, comparing actual relationships.
- Day 53: Lawrence N. Langer, ["The Rus Principalities" (2023), chapter 19, pages 753 to 776, free Summary](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316337424.020), describes imposed tribute and Mongol confirmation of Rus princes. The summary was read, not the restricted chapter. Credit Langer's summary for the coexistence of coercion and princely government; do not attribute unread interpretations to Halperin or Ostrowski.
- Day 58: excavations by Mexico's national institute of anthropology and history at the Huei Tzompantli, the skull rack of Tenochtitlan, began in 2015 and continued after 2020. The [25 February 2023 report, bulletin 98](https://www.inah.gob.mx/images/boletines/2023/98/Boletin%2098.pdf) recorded 655 skulls: 60% male, 38% female and 2% infants. These describe excavated remains, not an empire-wide total. Sacrifice was large in scale; early Spanish totals are not reliable, so teach the scale from archaeology. Name the Triple Alliance (Tenochtitlan, Texcoco, Tlacopan).
- Days 58 to 61, mediation notes: the Codex Mendoza was painted in Mexico City about 1541 by Indigenous painters, with Spanish notes, for the Spanish crown; it records Mexica tribute as remembered after conquest. Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala, an Andean noble, finished his illustrated chronicle about 1615 and addressed it to King Philip III of Spain, to argue against colonial abuses.
- Days 53 to 54: model feudalism's emergence with northern France after Carolingian authority fragmented, about 900 to 1100. Local protection and military service strengthened landholding lords; this was uneven, not an instant European system. Day 56 practises the causal explanation; A7 checks it. Background: [medieval contracts, Fulbert of Chartres, 1020](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/fulbert1.asp).
