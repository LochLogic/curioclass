# Scope and sequence: World History I (about 500 to 1800)

Course `world-history-i` (catalogue id `world-hist-hs`), grade 9, full year (160 days), draft (2026-11-14), revised the same day from the expert and second reads (docs/reviews/sequence-9-11-fixes-2026-11-14.md). The days live in data/plans/world-history-i/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment built on unseen sources (bar item 7), and the next day is its respond day (the response time, counted once). Each assessment day's goal in days.csv is its blueprint: it names every part of every code it lists, and where a unit's extended product (a map-and-explain task, a report, the argument) carries a code, that product is scored beside the unseen task rather than redone in one period. No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Days with religious, Native, slavery or hard-history content carry a sensitivity flag in the note column; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. Personal finance is touched lightly inside existing days (supply and demand on day 71, shared investment and risk on day 127), with no added days.

The base text is partial: John Rankin and Constanze Weise, World History Since 1500 (ETSU, CC BY 4.0), chapters 1 to 5, serves units 7 to 10 only, with one exception: chapter 8's Russia passage (printed pages 99 to 102) is background on day 141. data/plans/world-history-i/source-map.csv records every chapter section, the day it lands on, and each cut with its reason; days that adapt it name the section in the note. Units 1 to 6 have no base and are written from primary sources and scholarship. Credit line on each adapted day: "Adapted from John Rankin and Constanze Weise, World History Since 1500, ETSU, CC BY 4.0." The book's images are not covered by that licence and are checked one by one.

Readiness checks open units 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8: every grade 5, 6 or 7 row the unit assumes that grade 8 did not use and this year has not yet met (the settled rule of 2026-11-14). ss7-rome-fall, used in grade 8 civics, opens as retrieval on day 1; grade 8 rows and rows already met this year open as retrieval tasks. The parallel timeline begun on day 5 is added to at the close of every unit.

The course is thin after the redraw (data/plans/later.csv L8), so each unit below names the topics it teaches in full; together they fill the year inside the frame's units and days.

## Unit 1. How societies meet (days 1 to 15, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** the seven ways societies interact (trade, ideas, migration, exploration, alliance, colonization, conquest), with who gained and who lost; Han China and Rome on the overland routes; Indian Ocean monsoon trade; the Bantu-speaking migrations from current scholarship; supporting questions and a source plan for an inquiry; the parallel timeline of empires to about 500; seas, deserts, steppe and monsoon winds as the shapers of contact; population charts (population, death rates, migration) and trade and land as a two-way system; maps of features, borders, routes and invasions; the Indian Ocean as one trade system and Srivijaya as a sea-trade state; a layered network map; the enduring issue a compelling question raises; the five source checks (origin, authority, structure, context, corroborative value) on texts, maps, objects and buildings, and a biased source as evidence of belief; a claim paragraph answering one counterclaim.

Arc: five days on the categories and the year's tools (the seven interactions, the first cases, the inquiry, the timeline), five on geography and its displays ending in a layered network map, then three on the five source checks and the year's first written claim. The tools come first because every later unit reuses them as review.

- Readiness: day 1, on grade 7's Europe maps (ss7-europe-geo, not used in grade 8). Grade 7's fall of Rome (used in grade 8 civics) and the comparison skill (kept in use in grade 8) open as retrieval tasks the same day.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 14: the seven kinds of interaction and the networks, geography and human factors, the five checks on two unseen sources, a map, a parallel timeline and a comparison chart item, the inquiry's enduring issue and supporting questions, and a short claim from selected evidence. Respond day 15.
- Day 10's layered map has a device route (an offline mapping tool page, supplied data, no account) and a paper route; only the device route shows the technology part of D2.Geo.1, and the record says which route a student took.
- Candidate: Srivijaya, kept, inside day 9, 0 added days. Candidate: the Indian Ocean as one trade system, kept, the rest of day 9, 0 added days. Candidate: Polynesian voyaging, dropped: no check set member names it and the unit has no free day.

## Unit 2. Belief and empire, about 300 to 1400 (days 16 to 30, weeks 4 to 6)

**Topics taught:** when and where six faiths began (timeline and map); the central beliefs of Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Confucianism, Christianity and Islam as their followers describe them; what the monotheistic faiths share and how they differ from polytheistic traditions; Indigenous religious practice in Africa (ancestors, a creator and lesser spirits, diviners and healers, oral tradition) from Yoruba, Vodun and Akan communities' own materials; how African practice survived in and shaped Ethiopia's church and West African Islam; Buddhism's spread to about 1400, the Tang and Buddhism to Korea and Japan; the spread of Christianity and Islam (decrees, missionaries, merchants, pilgrimage, sacred buildings, conquest) and the 1054 split; diffusion along one route and not another; the three empire options of 100 to 1000 (Gupta or Kushan, early Byzantine, Abbasid) surveyed, one studied in depth for the links among power, religion and culture; a policy (toleration, a law code, patronage of scholars) explained in its context.

Arc: five days on the six faiths in pairs, two on African practice, three on spread as a geography of diffusion, then three on one empire in depth that tie power back to belief. Faiths come before spread so students know what moved before they map how.

- Readiness: day 16, on grade 6's origins of Judaism, Christianity and Islam and grade 7's Hinduism, Shang and Zhou with Confucius, and Buddhism in East Asia (none used in grade 8), one item per faith; days 17, 18 and 23 use it.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 29: the six faiths and their timeline, the monotheisms compared, African practice and its survival, a diffusion map with the means of spread, and the chosen empire's power, religion and culture scored from days 27 and 28 beside a context question on an unseen source. Respond day 30.
- Day 24 works from prepared route maps so the day is the means of spread; day 25 keeps the diffusion reasoning.
- Candidate: the Tang and Buddhism's spread to Korea and Japan, kept, inside day 23, 0 added days. Candidate: the 1054 split, kept, part of day 24, 0 added days.

## Unit 3. Kingdoms of Africa and the Mongol world, 1000 to 1500 (days 31 to 46, weeks 7 to 10)

**Topics taught:** the words for power (hereditary rule, dynasty, kingdom, empire, feudal society); Ghana and Mali, gold and salt across the Sahara, and the trade in enslaved people beside gold; Mansa Musa's Mali, Timbuktu and its manuscripts, Songhai; Axum retrieved as the earlier Red Sea kingdom, then the Swahili city-states in Indian Ocean trade, with Great Zimbabwe's gold reaching the coast through Kilwa; two historians' accounts of Mansa Musa's pilgrimage traced to their Arabic sources, and a sourced claim paragraph on him; the Mongol conquests under Chinggis and Khubilai Khan with death tolls the sources support; goods on the protected Mongol roads; Yuan and early Ming China, contact with Europeans, Zheng He's voyages; travellers' accounts and maps (Ibn Battuta, Marco Polo, Rabban Sauma) sourced; whose view of a ruler's legitimacy a source records; the standard's map-and-explain task on one option.

Arc: four days on the African kingdoms, two on how a historian's account is built from them, five on the Mongol world read the same way, then the two-day map-and-explain task and a discussion of the compelling question. Africa first so the Mansa Musa case teaches interpretation before the travellers are read with it.

- Readiness: day 31, on grade 6's West Africa and Axum rows and grade 7's Silk Roads (none used in grade 8); day 37 uses the Silk Roads item.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 45: the words for power, the map-and-explain product of days 42 and 43 scored for politics, economy, culture and links, the Mongol roads and a traveller's account, and an unseen secondary claim traced to its primary sources with a further source it raises. ## Unit 4. Rulers and ruled in East Asia, Europe and the Americas, 1000 to 1500 (days 50 to 63, weeks 10 to 13)

**Topics taught:** Song China (examined scholar-officials, a commercial economy, paper money); Song landscape painting and calligraphy and the blending of Buddhist, Daoist and Confucian thought; Kamakura Japan (shogun and samurai, the failed Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281, Buddhist sculpture and Zen-influenced calligraphy and art); a brief survey of the kingdoms and feudal societies of Europe (England, France, Spain, Russia with Kievan Rus and the Mongol yoke, the Holy Roman Empire); the manor, knighthood and chivalry, the Church's wealth and its 11th century power struggles, within the Church and with kings over bishops; Magna Carta (1215) as a barons' charter whose meaning grew later, and early Parliament; feudal Europe, Kamakura Japan and Song China compared, with Russia as a fourth case; Classic Maya cities (Tikal, Copan), pyramid-temples and trade in obsidian, jade and cacao; the Mexica state (Tenochtitlan, conquests, tribute, the Sun Stone) from codices beside Spanish accounts; the Inca state (roads, quipu records, conquests and labour taken, Cusco and Machu Picchu); Mexica and Inca tribute compared from the peoples' own records.

Arc: three days on East Asia, four on Europe ending in the three-way comparison, four on the Americas ending in the tribute comparison, then the sourced comparison on the compelling question. The Americas close the unit so unit 8 starts from societies students know.

- Readiness: day 50, on grade 6's Mesoamerica and research report rows (idle grades 7 and 8), used on days 57 to 60. Grade 7's East Asia row opens as a retrieval task the same day.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 62: each option in full (Song, Kamakura, Europe's kingdoms, Maya, Mexica, Inca), the Church, feudalism, knighthood and rights in England, the feudal comparison, and an unseen source on how subjects saw a ruler. Respond day 63.
- Candidate: Kievan Rus and the Mongol yoke, kept, part of day 53, 0 added days. Candidate: Mississippian cities, dropped: the three owned American options fill the days; the unit plan's comparison table records it. Korea under Goryeo, dropped: the 14-day unit carries its owned comparisons and has no free day.

## Unit 5. Knowledge, crops and goods on the move, 800 to 1400 (days 64 to 74, weeks 13 to 15)

**Topics taught:** Islamic scholars in Baghdad and Cordoba translating and adding to Greek and Indian works, al-Khwarizmi and Ibn Sina by name; monasteries copying texts and the first universities (al-Qarawiyyin, Bologna, Paris, Oxford); what classical learning carried on, what its carriers changed, and how texts reached Europe through Arabic; gunpowder, the compass, printing and paper and their routes west; the medieval Agricultural Revolution in Africa, Europe and Asia (crops and large irrigation systems spreading through Egypt and North Africa into Spain, rice and sorghum in the inner Niger delta, Champa rice in Song China, and Europe's heavy plough and three-field rotation); India's cotton textile technologies as a production sequence with surviving textiles as evidence; supply and demand for Indian cloth, and a judgment of how farming and settlement changed the land and its culture in one place.

Arc: three days on learning (scholars, universities, continuity and change), two on Chinese inventions and one traced route, three on crops and cloth with supply and demand, then the short claim. Learning first because the cotton economy's demand question uses the trade routes the inventions travelled.

- Readiness: day 64, on grade 7's spread of ideas (ss7-ideas-spread, not used in grade 8).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 73: classical learning preserved and the universities, the Chinese inventions, the Agricultural Revolution in all three regions, cotton and its exports, a land and culture judgment, and a continuity and change task on unseen sources. Respond day 74.
- Candidate: al-Khwarizmi and Ibn Sina named as individuals, kept, inside day 64, 0 added days. Candidate: Champa rice, kept, part of day 69, 0 added days.

## Unit 6. Encounter, slavery and plague, about 750 to 1500 (days 75 to 89, weeks 15 to 18)

**Topics taught:** coexistence, tolerance and trade in Umayyad al-Andalus to the caliphate of Cordoba (756 to about 1000), with more than one historian's account of how tolerant it was, framed by the Reconquista; Norman Sicily (from 1061) and the causes of the Crusades; the course of the Crusades with the 1096 Rhineland massacres and the 1204 sack of Constantinople named plainly; Crusader, Byzantine, Muslim and Jewish accounts side by side; the results of the Crusades and the one-to-two page claim with a counterclaim; how common slavery was from the 400s to the 1700s across four continents, and its forms compared (household, military, agricultural; whether status passed to children; routes to freedom); the Black Death's spread 1347 to 1351 and death rates; its later outbreaks through the 1400s from dated evidence; its effects (wages, revolts, religious movements, the murder of Jewish communities blamed for it); a multi-cause, multi-effect chain; trade, politics and migration before and after.

Arc: six days on the Mediterranean encounter, ending in the year's middle writing step, two on the slavery comparison, four on the plague ending in the chain, then one day answering the compelling question (did the meetings of peoples around the Mediterranean and beyond bring more exchange or more harm?) from all three strands. The one-to-two page claim sits here because the Crusade accounts are the year's first extended four-perspective primary-source comparison; it is due day 80 (week 16), clear of ELA 9's argument in weeks 20 to 24. No role play or simulation anywhere in the unit.

- Readiness: day 75, on grade 7's Roman slavery and class (ss7-roman-slavery-class, not used in grade 8), used on day 81.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 88: al-Andalus, the Crusades' causes, course and results, how common slavery was, the plague's spread, death rates and repeated outbreaks, the chain and a before-and-after map, with an unseen account to source for perspective. Respond day 89, with review of units 4 to 6.
- Candidate: the Reconquista as the frame for al-Andalus, kept, part of day 75, 0 added days. Candidate: the Hundred Years' War and Joan of Arc, dropped: the plague carries the crisis of the 1300s and no day is free.

## Unit 7. New powers and new ideas, 1300 to 1600 (days 94 to 109, weeks 19 to 22)

**Topics taught:** the Ottoman rise from 1299 to the capture of Constantinople in 1453; Ottoman rule to Suleiman I (the millet system, the janissaries, the devshirme named plainly) and the Safavid rivalry; the early Mughal Empire (textiles, ships and firearms, illustrated manuscripts) and the founding of Sikhism; how Ottomans and Mughals ruled religiously mixed peoples; Venice, Florence and Genoa (banking, merchant republics, trade with the Byzantine and Ottoman empires, patrons, their part in the Black Sea slave trade); the Renaissance's roots and humanism; Leonardo, Michelangelo, Christine de Pizan, Erasmus and Durer; who is called significant now who was not then, and who named the Renaissance; the roots of the Protestant Reformation (Luther, the printing press, Luther's writings against Jews named plainly); Calvin and the Church of England; the Counter-Reformation (Trent, Ignatius Loyola, the Jesuits); pamphlets and woodcuts as persuasion; how the Reformations shifted power, brought persecution of religious minorities and fed wars.

Arc: four days on the Islamic empires, one on the city-states, three on the Renaissance ending in significance, five on the Reformations, then the sourced claim on the compelling question (was the age of 1300 to 1600 a rebirth, or a story told about it later?) with evidence from every strand. The Ottomans open because their hold on the eastern Mediterranean shapes Italian trade; city-state wealth explains the Renaissance, and print and humanism explain the Reformations.

- Readiness: none. Day 102 opens with a retrieval task on Christianity's origins (unit 2 and grade 6).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 108: the Ottomans and Mughals and their rule of mixed faiths, the city-states, the Renaissance, the Reformation's political and religious roots and effects, the Counter-Reformation, and power, persecution and wars, with a significance task on unseen sources. ## Unit 8. Oceans connected: Iberia, the Americas and the Atlantic slave trade, 1450 to 1700 (days 110 to 125, weeks 22 to 25)

**Topics taught:** why Europeans wanted a sea route to Asia; Spain and Portugal after 1492 (the expulsion of Jews and later Muslims, the Inquisition) from Sephardic and Morisco records; Portugal's rise through its wars with Muslim states, and Spain's expeditions to conquer and convert the Americas and the Philippines; the Columbian Exchange of crops, animals and diseases both ways, and land use on both sides of the ocean; the collapse of Native populations from epidemic, war and forced labour (encomienda, mita) and the impact of missionaries; Native resistance and adaptation, the Pueblo Revolt of 1680, Taino descendants and Native nations today; Potosi silver and the Manila galleons tying the Americas to China; the Atlantic slave trade mapped (routes, African states, European traders, about 12.5 million taken and about 10.7 million surviving the crossing); what traders' and owners' records can and cannot show, Afonso I of Kongo's letters, and a current interpretation of the trade's effect on West African states tested against them; the Middle Passage from survivors' own words (Equiano, read with care); plantation slavery in Brazil and the Caribbean, slavery made hereditary and tied to race; resistance, maroon communities and the lives and cultures people built; a table of who paid and who gained.

Arc: three days on Iberia and the voyages, four on the Americas ending in one global silver economy, five on the Atlantic slave trade ending in resistance and lives built, then two days answering the compelling question. The slave trade follows unit 6's comparison so its scale, heredity and race stand out. No role play or simulation of conquest, sale or the crossing.

- Readiness: day 110, on grade 6's Caribbean peoples and grade 5's slavery in the colonies (not used since).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 124: Iberia after 1492 with Portugal's part, the sea route, colonization's effects, the Columbian Exchange, missionaries and the slave trade, a land-use judgment, and an unseen trader's record set against day 118's interpretation. Respond day 125.
- Base: ETSU chapters 1 and 2 on days 110, 112, 114, 117, 118 and 121 (source map).
- Candidate: the Pueblo Revolt of 1680 (with maroon communities on day 121), kept, part of day 115, 0 added days. Candidate: Afonso I's letters, kept, the source of day 118, 0 added days. Candidate: Potosi silver and the Manila galleons, kept, day 116, 0 added days.

## Unit 9. Trade, science and empire in the 1600s (days 126 to 137, weeks 26 to 28)

**Topics taught:** the Dutch Republic's wealthy Protestant middle class, chartered companies and the joint-stock form; how shared investment spread risk, and how Dutch money paid for art and science (the Amsterdam exchange, Rembrandt, Vermeer, the microscope); the conquest of the Banda Islands in the 1620s and Dutch slave trading named plainly; the scientific method, the telescope and the microscope; the change from an earth-centred to a sun-centred universe from the evidence of the time, Islamic and Chinese astronomy before 1500 among it; two figures studied closely (from Copernicus, Galileo, Kepler, Newton, Maria Sibylla Merian); ships, maps and instruments that made the voyages possible; the 1600s Ottoman, Ming and Qing, Portuguese, Dutch, Spanish and British empires mapped on a partly completed base map; one first-hand account of travel, trade or diplomacy sourced for its writer's purpose, with Tokugawa and Qing limits on European trade among the accounts offered.

Arc: three days on Dutch trade, four on the Scientific Revolution as a change in evidence, then the empire map, a discussion of the compelling question and a short report on a first-hand account. Trade comes first so the Dutch money behind the microscope and the ships behind the maps can be seen.

- Readiness: none. Day 126 opens with a retrieval task on grade 7's Silk Road trade and the supply-demand model (unit 5); day 129 on grade 8 science's earth, sun and moon.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 136: Dutch trade and its support for art and science, the method, instruments, theory, two figures and the ships, and the 1600s empire map, with day 135's report scored and an unseen first-hand account to source. Respond day 137.

## Shorter path

The year is 160 days of 50 minutes. Two shorter routes keep every owned code, skill and assessment; the unit plans check them against real minutes, so neither is yet shown to fit.

- **45-minute periods, same 160 days.** Each teaching day keeps its source work, its product and its exit ticket, and trims the second worked example or the whole-class share; assessment and respond days stay whole.
