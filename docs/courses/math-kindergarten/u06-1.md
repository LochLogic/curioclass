## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.A.3** (days 104, 105, 106, 107): Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- **K.CC.B.5** (days 104, 105, 106, 107): Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- **K.CC.A.2** (days 106): Count forward beginning from a given number within the known sequence (instead of having to begin at 1).
- skill mk-count-to-20 (grade K)
- skill mk-how-many (grade K)
- skill mk-count-on-from (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does moving objects change how many, day 107? How do we hear ten in a teen name, day 110? How can a drawing and equation show the same parts, day 114?
> **Teaching moves.** Model touching once, tracking rows and marking a circle's start on day 104. Show count-out before day 105 rehearses it; teach numeral forms through twenty across the understanding days. Model ten ones before symbols; step back for independent composition on day 111. Keep every one visible.
<!-- /skel -->

K.CC.A.3, "Write"/"Represent": extend quantity labels toward twenty; K.CC.B.5, "Count"/"count out": track arranged sets through twenty, scatters through ten, stop at requested quantities; K.CC.A.2, "Count forward": supplied starts day 106. Connect K.CC.B.4b day 107 to unchanged cardinality. MP.6 day 105: one visit per object; MP.7 day 107: unchanged members preserve the whole. No standard algorithm/numeral comparisons above ten. Adult explanation: order/spacing changes neither membership nor quantity; a circle needs a stopping boundary. Twenty is a count, not a teen decomposition. Demonstrate intended u02-3 tracking/u02-4 quantity-label review afresh, planned only.
