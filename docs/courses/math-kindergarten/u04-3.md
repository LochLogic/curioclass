- **E73.** Read: "Rin has seven shell pictures and adds one. How many now?" Blank paper, optional pieces. "Show how your model answers this story." All retain paper model; hear six explain. Answer/look-for: Seven first, one joins, eight; parts tied to question. Meets: seven outlined, one added, "All eight shells." Does not meet: Unchanged seven after one joins (G). - **E74.** Read: "Rin has six toy ducks on a pond and moves two onto land. How many stay on the pond?" Pictures: six/two off; six/four off; six/two arriving. "Show which drawing tells this story." All mark a match and its starting/moved groups; hear six. Answer/look-for: First picture: six first, two off, four remain. Meets: six-start/two-off positively marked. Does not meet: Arrival picture chosen (C); four-off/two-left chosen because moved/remaining parts confused (E). Shape: Positive story-to-complete-model action match, not elimination alone; practice corrects one supplied drawing at a time, never chooses among complete candidate models or uses ducks. Selection alone does not establish method.

- **E75.** Read: "Rin has ten blocks on a shelf and moves six to a mat. How many stay on the shelf?" Ask: "Use marks to show the answer to this story." All mark blank shelf scene; hear six. Answer/look-for: Ten initial marks, six changed, four remain. Meets: ten dots, six crossed, four ringed. Does not meet: Moved six ringed as remaining (E). Shape: Full independent change-record on empty scene; practice adds a missing mark to supplied square drawings, never produces a whole empty-scene record or uses blocks/shelf.

- **E76.** Read two scenes: "Rin has three ribbons and adds seven. How many now?" Then "Rin has ten ribbons and puts seven away. How many remain?" Ask: "Compare what happens in these stories." All mark incoming/removing arrows; hear six comparisons. Answer/look-for: Both change by seven; join to ten, remove to three. Meets: "Seven come in here, seven go out there." Results support comparison, not extra asks. Does not meet: Treats removal as joining, seventeen (C). Shape: Independent comparison of opposite actions in two complete stories; practice sorts single action cues or finishes supplied contrast sentences, never compares a whole story pair or uses ribbons.
