# Course frame: Kindergarten Math

Course id math-k8, kindergarten. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8, Illustrative Mathematics Kindergarten's eight units in its own order, with two moves: the
  kindergarten measurement lessons gather into unit 3, and the ten-frame comes in unit 2 (both reasons in the Units
  table and the source map).
- **Days.** 160: 154 in units (each unit's days include one end-of-unit assessment day), 4 for course-level checks
  (2 after unit 4, 2 after unit 8) and 2 respond days (one after each check). Math is daily: one lesson of 60
  minutes a day, five days a week, 32 weeks. The 60 minutes is the base text's own design (every lesson fits a class
  period of at least 60 minutes, warm-up, activities, lesson synthesis and exit ticket, with the kindergarten centers
  inside that time). The base text's 137 lessons less one cut, ten Curio Class lessons and eight assessment days
  make the 154; the ten added lessons go where the rows weigh most (counting and writing to 10, sorting, length,
  teen numbers, story problems), not one per unit.
- **Major work.** Counted three ways, with the 75 percent line for K to 2 (docs/RUBRIC-MATH-K8.md): by days, 124 of
  160 (77.5 percent) sit outside units 3 and 7 and the two sorting days, which are the only additional and
  supporting work (K.MD, K.G), check and respond days counted with what they check; by units, 6 of 8; by base
  lessons, 121 of 136 kept lessons carry a major-cluster standard. The daily counting routine and the warm-ups in
  units 3 and 7 keep counting and number work going through the shape weeks.
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, kindergarten: every K standard and every lettered part
  is owned by a progression row and placed in a unit below (K.CC.B.4a, b and c in units 1 and 2; K.MD.A.1 and A.2
  together in unit 3). Skills: the eight Standards for Mathematical Practice, one or two named per unit (candidates
  in the Check set adds column; the unit plan settles them with the task where students do them). Check set: each
  unit plan checks its order, limits and models against the Progressions documents (CC BY 4.0) and the Eureka Math
  kindergarten reference (sequence and models only, never its problems or wording), and records any addition with
  its source.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Kindergarten (CC BY 4.0), adapted into Curio Class days
  with a credit line on each day; the source map at the end places every base unit and lesson. Only the free public
  pages are used: cool-downs, keys and assessments are behind a login and are not taken, so every exit ticket and
  assessment is Curio Class's own. The second edition (CC BY-NC) is never used.
- **The counting routine.** Every day opens with a short counting routine that grows through the year: to 10 and 20
  in units 1 and 2, counting on from a number from unit 4, by tens to 100 from unit 6, by ones to 100 by unit 8
  (mk-count-sequence, owned and checked in unit 8). Oral only: numerals are read and written to 20 (K.CC.A.3).
- **Fluency without a timer.** K.OA.A.5 (add and subtract within 5) is built with short spaced games (shake and
  spill, what is behind my back) and checked by watching and listening one to one: the teacher notes whether a child
  just knows, uses a quick strategy or counts all. No timer is ever shown, and no sheet is timed.
- **Concrete to written.** Objects, fingers and drawings come first, and each is tied to the numeral or equation in
  the same lesson where the rows ask for it (written numerals 0 to 20, expressions and equations within 10). The
  ten-frame is the one structured model of the year.
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs with objects, printed ten-frames and paper; any Curio Class tool is an extra, with
  no student account.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent read. No unit needs a community reader. Teacher notes in the
  last column cover counting children, family questions, food in lessons and the class's own data.

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten has no course before it in Curio Class. The course assumes no counting: unit 1 starts from recognizing
small groups and pairing one word with one object. A child who arrives already counting to 20 still does unit 1's
"how many" and "are there enough" work, which is about meaning, not reciting.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 4 (2 days, about week 17).** One to one or in small groups during centers, so every child is
  heard: count a collection to 10 and say how many without recounting; count out a number; write numerals 0 to 10;
  say which of two groups has more and which of two numbers is greater; solve two story problems with objects or a
  drawing and match one to an equation; name four flat shapes in unusual turns. The teacher records what each child
  can do on a class sheet.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Re-teach what the check shows (most often counting a scattered set and
  the equation that matches a story), in small groups, ending with a short fresh task to see that it took.
- **After unit 8 (2 days, end of year).** One to one and in small groups: count to 100 by tens and by ones; count up
  to 20 objects and write the numeral; show a teen number as ten and some more; break a number to 10 into two parts
  in two ways and give the partner of 10 for a number; add and subtract within 5 (watched in a game: just knows,
  quick strategy or counts all, never timed); compare two objects by length or weight; tell flat from solid and
  name a cube, sphere, cylinder and cone.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teach the gaps the check shows, then the record goes on to the
  grade 1 teacher with what the respond day changed.

## For the lead

1. **Time a day.** 60 minutes, five days a week, 160 days, the base text's own lesson length. Pick: keep it. A
   school with 45 minutes moves each day's center time into the next morning's warm-up; the frame does not plan a
   shorter day.
2. **Row link mk-one-to-one.** It hands on to no grade 1 row. Pick: hands_on m1-count-to-120 on mk-one-to-one, and
   assumes += mk-one-to-one on m1-count-to-120 (grade 1 counts large collections one by one before grouping in tens).
   Both rows edited by the fixer.
3. **data/modules.csv, math-k-sort-and-count, carries EK-ALG-ML-02** (a computer science standard). The math frame
   does not carry it (math is the Common Core). Pick: drop the tag from the math module; a computer science elective
   that sorts by a rule may point at mk-sort-count instead.
4. **The math lens gap line.** docs/agents/lenses/math.md says the K to 2 fluency and counting work "has no page on
   how it is checked without a timer". Replacement text: "The K to 2 math frames set how fluency is checked without
   a timer: watched in games and one to one, recorded as just knows, quick strategy or counts all (course brief and
   course-level checks of each frame)."
5. **The major work line** is met by 2.5 points on days (77.5 percent). Pick: keep unit 3's added length lessons
   (the standard and four science leans need them) rather than cut them to widen the margin.
