# Course frame: Kindergarten Math

Course `math-kindergarten` (catalogue id `math-k8`), kindergarten. Draft frame, updated 2026-11-15; scope-and-sequence.md and days.csv set the course days.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8, Illustrative Mathematics Kindergarten's eight units in its own order, with three moves: the
  kindergarten measurement lessons gather into unit 3, the position-word lesson opens unit 3, and the ten-frame comes
  in unit 2, with the base lessons that use or introduced it adapted (reasons in the Units table and the source map).
- **Days.** 160 course days: 153 in units (each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment day, except unit 8,
  whose assessment is folded into the year-end course-level check that covers its rows), 4 for course-level checks
  (2 after unit 4, 2 inside unit 8) and 3 respond days (one after the mid-year check, two after the year-end check).
  The year-end check falls in week 31, after unit 8's first 17 days, so unit 8's last 5 days follow it and its respond
  days (the Course-level checks table lists them as review days, so the gate counts 148 days in units and 12 at
  course level; the unit still has 22). Math is daily: one lesson of 60
  minutes a day, five days a week, 32 weeks. The 60 minutes is the base text's own design (every lesson fits a class
  period of at least 60 minutes, warm-up, activities, lesson synthesis and exit ticket, with the kindergarten centers
  inside that time). The base text's 137 lessons less one cut, ten Curio Class lessons and seven assessment days
  make the 153; the ten added lessons go where the rows weigh most (counting and writing to 10, sorting, measurement,
  teen numbers, story problems), not one per unit. The base text budgets two assessment days a unit; Curio Class
  keeps one, because its own end-of-unit assessment (the base text's is behind a login) is one period of short tasks,
  and the one-to-one evidence kindergarten needs (counting a collection, facts within 5) is gathered by watching
  during the unit's last center times. Each unit plan names what is gathered on the assessment day, what is gathered
  in the lessons before it, the observation minutes and how every child is reached, and that every owned standard is
  covered. If a unit's evidence cannot fit, the extra day goes to Cory before the scope and sequence fixes the year;
  no standard is cut to keep the total. After each end-of-unit assessment except unit 4, the respond work runs in the center time
  of the next two lessons, in small groups, on what the assessment showed. Unit 4 responds on day 83 itself; day 86 responds only to the course-level check. Unit 8 uses the course-level respond days 154 and 155 once. Unit 2, the one unit over 25 days, also
  gathers a mid-unit assessment by observation in center time near its middle (counting out and writing 0 to 10),
  inside its days.
- **Major work.** The scope and sequence states the single planning share by major-cluster minutes over instructional minutes, excluding assessment and respond days and embedded observation minutes. Unit plans verify those reservations from actual lesson parts. Daily counting continues through geometry and measurement; no unit or day estimate substitutes for the minute calculation.
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, kindergarten: every K standard and every lettered part
  is owned by a progression row and placed in a unit below (K.CC.B.4a, b and c in units 1 and 2; K.MD.A.1 and A.2
  together in unit 3). Math has no check set. The C3 column names the unit's practices (the eight Standards for
  Mathematical Practice, one or two per unit, settled by named tasks in the scope and sequence; the unit plan writes their evidence); the "Check set adds" column records the Progressions check for the unit and the extension choice settled in the scope and sequence.
  Check: each unit plan checks its order, limits and models against the Progressions documents (CC BY 4.0) and the Eureka Math
  kindergarten reference (sequence and models only, never its problems or wording), and records any addition with
  its source.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, Kindergarten (CC BY 4.0), adapted into Curio Class days
  with a credit line on each day; the source map at the end places every base unit and lesson. Only the free public
  pages are used: cool-downs, keys and assessments are behind a login and are not taken, so every exit ticket and
  assessment is Curio Class's own. The second edition (CC BY-NC) is never used.
- **The counting routine.** Every day opens with a short counting routine that grows through the year: to 10 and 20
  in units 1 and 2, counting on from a number from unit 4, by tens to 100 from unit 6, by ones to 100 by unit 8
  (mk-count-sequence, owned and checked in unit 8). Oral only: numerals are read and written to 20 (K.CC.A.3).
- **Fluency without a timer.** K.OA.A.5 (add and subtract within 5) is built with short spaced games (shake and
  spill, what is behind my back) and checked by watching and listening one to one: the teacher notes whether a child
  just knows, uses a quick strategy or counts all. No timer is ever shown, and no sheet is timed.
- **Concrete to written.** Objects, fingers and drawings come first, and each is tied to the numeral or equation in
  the same lesson where the rows ask for it (written numerals 0 to 20, expressions and equations within 10). The
  ten-frame is the one structured model of the year.
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs with objects, printed ten-frames and paper; any Curio Class tool is an extra, with
  no student account.
- **Readiness.** Kindergarten has no course before it, so no unit assumes an earlier grade's row and no unit opens
  with a readiness check; unit 1's counting-collections days are the teacher's first look at each child's counting,
  and units 2 to 8 build only on this course's own units.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content that needs a named community study. Unit
  3's pouring (water, sand, or beans and rice) and any play food get the safety agent's read. The course's checks and
  every keyed item get the answer-key agent's read (every item solved cold beside the script check, BASELINE rule
  27) before the course is called finished. Word problems use a range of names and settings; the unit plan lists
  the people and places its problems show. Teacher notes in the last column cover counting children, family
  questions, food in lessons and the class's own data.

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten has no course before it in Curio Class. The course assumes no counting: unit 1 starts from recognizing
small groups and pairing one word with one object. A child who arrives already counting to 20 still does unit 1's
"how many" and "are there enough" work, which is about meaning, not reciting.

## Models carried

| Model | Where it comes from | Introduced here | Used again here |
|---|---|---|---|
| ten-frame | New in kindergarten (mk-how-many, model_out) | Unit 2: the 5-frame for counts to 5 and two 5-frames side by side as a 10-frame for counts 6 to 10 | Unit 2 (mk-one-more: the next box filled is one more); unit 4 (mk-add-sub-meaning, mk-story-problems, mk-add-sub-expressions: counters on frames show joining and taking); unit 5 (mk-decompose-to-10, mk-make-ten: the full 10-frame, how many are missing to make 10); unit 6 (mk-count-to-20, mk-teen-ten-ones: two 10-frames show a teen number as a full ten and some more); unit 8 (mk-fluency-within-5: the 5-frame for facts within 5) |

The ten-frame goes on to grade 1 (m1-count-on, m1-add-sub-within-20 and others take it as model_in). Unit 1 meets
5-frames only as a tool to explore and to hold a small collection, before the model is taught in unit 2. The base
text introduces the 10-frame later (its lesson K.5.10) and uses none in its unit 4, so the source map adapts both:
unit 4's joining and taking activities add counters on a 10-frame, and K.5.10 becomes the full 10-frame and how many
are missing to make 10, not a second introduction.
