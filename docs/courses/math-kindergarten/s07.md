## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How are solid shapes different from flat ones?"
**Hook, day 120.** For eight minutes, offer a drawn square, two paper triangles and a cube. Ask children to make the square, then consider whether their flat pieces make something like the cube. Ask the compelling question; revisit it on day 126. Source: field 10.
**Supporting questions.** Can equal outlines use different numbers of pieces, day 121? How can pieces tell an equation story, day 123? Is a cube a square, day 127? Which features survive a turn, day 128?
**Closing claim.** "I can distinguish flat and solid shapes, name them after a turn, compare their parts and describe where they are." The comparison sheet and classroom-position record support this on day 134.
**Teaching moves.** Connect pieces to counts before equations. On day 126 model the distinction between an object and its drawing; on day 127 trace a cube's square face without calling the whole cube a square. Step back for feature-based sorting on day 128 and model choices on day 133.

- **K.G.B.5:** Model shapes in the world by building shapes from components (e.g., sticks and clay balls) and drawing shapes. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.G.B.6:** Compose simple shapes to form larger shapes. For example, “Can you join these two triangles with full sides touching to make a rectangle?” Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.CC.A.3:** Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.CC.B.5:** Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.CC.C.6:** Identify whether the number of objects in one group is greater than, less than, or equal to the number of objects in another group, e.g., by using matching and counting strategies. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.2:** Solve addition and subtraction word problems, and add and subtract within 10, e.g., by using objects or drawings to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.1:** Represent addition and subtraction with objects, fingers, mental images, drawings, sounds (e.g., claps), acting out situations, verbal explanations, expressions, or equations. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.3:** Decompose numbers less than or equal to 10 into pairs in more than one way, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each decomposition by a drawing or equation (e.g., 5 = 2 + 3 and 5 = 4 + 1). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.4:** For any number from 1 to 9, find the number that makes 10 when added to the given number, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record the answer with a drawing or equation. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.G.A.3:** Identify shapes as two-dimensional (lying in a plane, “flat”) or three-dimensional (“solid”). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.G.B.4:** Analyze and compare two- and three-dimensional shapes, in different sizes and orientations, using informal language to describe their similarities, differences, parts (e.g., number of sides and vertices/“corners”) and other attributes (e.g., having sides of equal length). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.G.A.1:** Describe objects in the environment using names of shapes, and describe the relative positions of these objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, and next to. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.G.A.2:** Correctly name shapes regardless of their orientations or overall size. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Calls a solid flat when it lies on its side. | Pose replaces dimension. | From memory. |
| B | Calls an entire cube a square. | A face is mistaken for the whole object. | From memory. |
| C | Rejects a familiar flat-shape name after a turn. | Orientation controls the name. | Geometry Progression, PDF page 6. |
| D | Uses color alone to justify a shape decision. | Color replaces geometric attributes. | From memory. |
| E | Calls behind in front of. | Depth relationships are reversed. | From memory. |
| F | Names a cone triangle from its outline. | The drawing's outline replaces the solid. | From memory. |
| G | Says the wider group must contain more objects. | Spacing is treated as quantity. | From memory. |
| H | Adds pieces when a story removes them. | The action is not represented. | From memory. |
| I | Counts only the added pieces as the total. | A part replaces the whole. | From memory. |
| J | Gives a partner that does not complete ten. | The whole of ten is not maintained. | From memory. |
| K | Leaves a gap between pieces meant to fill an outline. | Composition does not cover the target. | From memory. |
| L | Counts a single piece twice. | Tracking revisits an object. | From memory. |
| M | Calls a pictured solid flat because the drawing is on paper. | The representation replaces the represented object. | From memory. |
| N | Uses one piece-type count for both addends. | The two addends are not matched to their separate groups. | From memory. |
| O | Calls a solid flat because some of its faces are flat. | A part's flatness replaces the whole object's thickness. | From memory. |

**Wrong beside right, then repair.** Each repair is a second move, different from the day's teaching contrast. A, day 126: a sideways cylinder called flat beside the same cylinder called solid; stack paper circles into a thick pile beside one flat circle and feel which has thickness. B, day 127: cube called square beside cube with one square face traced; wrap the cube in paper and trace one face on the wrapping. C, day 128: tilted square rejected beside the same square upright; turn the card a quarter turn at a time, counting four equal sides and corners after each turn. D, day 128: red sphere grouped with red cube for a flat-face rule beside cube grouped with cylinder; sort the same objects twice, by color then by flat faces, and add one object to see which sort holds. E, day 130: hidden object called in front beside its correct behind position; place a toy behind a box, then in front, and have the child look from the front to see which toy hides. F, day 127: cone called triangle beside a cone and a triangle; roll the cone on the table and slide the paper triangle, then say which has a round flat base. G, day 121: four widely spaced pieces called more than six close pieces beside six matched to four with two extras; match one to one. H, day 124: six pieces losing two shown as eight beside four remaining; act out removal with paper. I, day 123: five plus one called one beside six altogether; enclose both parts before counting. J, day 125: eight and one claimed as ten beside eight and two; fill ten marked places and count the empties. K, day 120: a gap beside a fully covered outline; slide the full sides into contact. L, day 120: six pieces called seven after revisiting one beside six marked once; move each counted piece to a new place. M, day 126: a sphere called flat because its picture is on paper beside the real sphere and its picture both named as representing a solid; ask which of a ball and its photograph can roll, and say the picture stands for the ball. N, day 123: four squares and two triangles labeled 6 = 4 + 4 beside 6 = 4 + 2; circle squares in one color and triangles in another, then write each color's count under its own addend. O, day 128: a cylinder called flat for its flat ends beside the cylinder called solid; slide a hand under and over it to feel its thickness, then lay a flat paper circle beside it. Each letter has one repair. Day 135 uses this second move. An unrelated error gets a descriptive note.

If more than a third miss a component, use its repair in the next teaching day's first ten model and connected-work minutes; otherwise use a small center group. Preserve the day's new model. Never demonstrate repairs on day 134.

E: describe a cylinder newly placed behind a tray. F: name a cone pointing down as cone. M: mark a new pictured ball as representing a solid, distinguishing it from a flat circle drawing. N: connect the addends in 4 = 3 + 1 to a new picture of three squares and one triangle. O: sort a new tube with flat ends as solid and say what a flat shape lacks. ## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 121, "Why can the same outline use more pieces?" On day 127, "Is the whole cube a square? Show me." Day 133's written task labels a chosen solid and marks the feature connecting it to a classroom object; drawing quality is irrelevant.

## U8. Practice that returns

Count daily and mix joining and removal within five with immediate feedback. Note just knows, quick strategy or counts all separately from geometry. Shape composition links earlier stories to equations; day 125 retrieves ten's partners.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud. Teach flat, solid, face, side, corner and position words with gestures: "Both have ___; this one has ___." Accept home-language reasoning before restating English names. Use tactile outlines, large stable pieces and adult movement at the child's direction. Extend by proposing and testing two different feature-based sorts of the same objects.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, alternate builder and checker with the adult; keep the same paper evidence, written feedback and later revision. A thirty-five-minute path uses counting five, model five, building ten, discussion and recording ten, exit five; include within-five practice in recording. Spread assigned evidence across the same window. One shared device is optional.
