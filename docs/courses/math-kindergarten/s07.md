## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How are solid shapes different from flat ones?"
**Hook, day 120.** For eight minutes, offer a drawn square, two paper triangles and a cube. Ask children to make the square, then consider whether their flat pieces make something like the cube. Ask the compelling question; revisit it on day 126. Source: field 10.
**Supporting questions.** Can equal outlines use different numbers of pieces, day 121? How can pieces tell an equation story, day 123? Is a cube a square, day 127? Which features survive a turn, day 128?
**Closing claim.** "I can distinguish flat and solid shapes, name them after a turn, compare their parts and describe where they are." The comparison sheet and classroom-position record support this on day 134.
**Teaching moves.** Connect pieces to counts before equations. On day 126 model the distinction between an object and its drawing; on day 127 trace a cube's square face without calling the whole cube a square. Step back for feature-based sorting on day 128 and model choices on day 133.

- **K.G.B.5:** Model shapes in the world by building shapes from components (e.g., sticks and clay balls) and drawing shapes. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.G.B.6:** Compose simple shapes to form larger shapes. For example, “Can you join these two triangles with full sides touching to make a rectangle?” Parts, by verb: whole standard.

- **K.CC.B.5:** Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.CC.C.6:** Identify whether the number of objects in one group is greater than, less than, or equal to the number of objects in another group, e.g., by using matching and counting strategies. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.2:** Solve addition and subtraction word problems, and add and subtract within 10, e.g., by using objects or drawings to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.1:** Represent addition and subtraction with objects, fingers, mental images, drawings, sounds (e.g., claps), acting out situations, verbal explanations, expressions, or equations. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.3:** Decompose numbers less than or equal to 10 into pairs in more than one way, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each decomposition by a drawing or equation (e.g., 5 = 2 + 3 and 5 = 4 + 1). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.4:** For any number from 1 to 9, find the number that makes 10 when added to the given number, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record the answer with a drawing or equation. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

- **K.G.A.2:** Correctly name shapes regardless of their orientations or overall size. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 121, "Why can the same outline use more pieces?" On day 127, "Is the whole cube a square? Show me." Day 133's written task labels a chosen solid and marks the feature connecting it to a classroom object; drawing quality is irrelevant.

## U8. Practice that returns

Count daily and mix joining and removal within five with immediate feedback. Note just knows, quick strategy or counts all separately from geometry. Shape composition links earlier stories to equations; day 125 retrieves ten's partners.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud. Teach flat, solid, face, side, corner and position words with gestures: "Both have ___; this one has ___." Accept home-language reasoning before restating English names. Use tactile outlines, large stable pieces and adult movement at the child's direction. Extend by proposing and testing two different feature-based sorts of the same objects.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, alternate builder and checker with the adult; keep the same paper evidence, written feedback and later revision. A thirty-five-minute path uses counting five, model five, building ten, discussion and recording ten, exit five; include within-five practice in recording. Spread assigned evidence across the same window. One shared device is optional.
