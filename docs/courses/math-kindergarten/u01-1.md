## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.B.4a** (days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5): When counting objects, say the number names in the standard order, pairing each object with one and only one number name and each number name with one and only one object.
- skill mk-one-to-one (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Supporting questions.** How can a tool help us keep track, day 5? Is there one object for every place, day 11? Why does moving help, day 15? How can a drawing remember each object, day 16?
> - **Teaching moves.** Model slow word-and-move pairing after the hook. Compare moving with touching on day 5, match objects to drawn places on day 10, and model separating counted objects on day 12. Step back on day 13; ask children to show their route before offering a mat.
>
> - **Practices.** MP.5: day 5, choose touching, moving or a boxed mat for a collection of five blocks and explain the choice. MP.6: day 15, explain to a partner how each spoken word matches exactly one object. The assessment records both choices and precise pairing.
> - **Carried.** No formal model enters. Day 3 explores a 5-frame without teaching its structure. Days 9 and 16 connect objects to drawn marks. Withdraw teacher-directed moving on day 13; children retain chosen tracking supports. Numeral connections and structured frames belong to unit 2, not forced withdrawal here.
> - **Focus.** Major work occupies one of one units and all seventeen teaching lessons by primary goal. Minutes give the stricter share: days 1 to 5 each reserve thirty counting minutes (five oral counting, ten guided pairing, ten collection counting and five synthesis) and thirty exploration minutes; days 6 to 14 give sixty counting minutes each; days 15 to 17 give forty after observations. Thus 810 of 960 instructional minutes, 84.4 percent, are major work. Exploring shapes supplies objects to count, without claiming shape-standard coverage.

- **This section's share.** "say the number names in the standard order" and "pairing each object with one and only one number name": introduce coordinated words and actions while exploring tools.

- **Limits and strategies.** Begin with one and grow toward five. Formal shape names and frame structure are not goals. No algorithm is specified.

- **Practices.** MP.6, day 1, coordinate a word and cube move; MP.5, day 5, try touching, moving and a boxed mat with loose blocks.

- **Connections and adult explanation.** No other grade standard is claimed. Distinguish ordered words, object pairing and tracking from a total; a correct total can conceal an omission and a repeat.

Picture/material: Four large cut-paper buttons scattered above an empty tray outline.

Read aloud: "Move each button into the tray with one counting word for each move."

Answer and look-fors: Four separate moves, saying one, two, three, four. Dot each moved button; retain the paper tray and pieces.

Wrong examples: One button left above the tray (A); one button returned and counted again (B).

Shape: Moves buttons into a destination. No button pictures, empty-tray transfers or destination-filling practice; partners pass cubes between them.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Questions.** Day 1: "What could you make?" Day 3: "What happened to this counter when you said its word?" Day 5: "Which way helped you avoid going back?"

- **Paper practice.** Day 4, draw a construction and trace one counting touch to each drawn piece. Look for one trace per piece; judge ordered words only when heard.

- **Turns.** One question at a time; pairs answer simultaneously. Two public volunteers at most per share; children may pass. Every child returns paper.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 1 has no retrieval. Day 2 returns to partner turns; day 3 to moving tools once; day 4 to a touch per word; day 5 to choosing among taught supports.

- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Return to accurate coordination through spaced practice and feedback, without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **Language.** count, object, tool, partner, touch, move; "One word for this object."

- **Access and exits.** Enlarge and space pictures; offer tactile outlines or paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without giving counting words, recording help. Face children and use visual turn cues. A child unable to dot directs adult paper marking. No independent reading: read every stem and label aloud. Repeat directions without demonstrating the reserved solution.

- **Extension.** Change route or arrangement within ten, explaining how each object is reached once; no operations or numeral writing.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use 25 minutes: supported count 3, demonstrate 5, practice 8, return and repair 4, exit 5. Hear that child instead of rotating six. Shorten repeated center choices, keeping the demonstration and same new exit.

- **One projector.** Optional display only; all children use their own paper pieces. Draw on a board as the fallback. No device turns or online text is required.
