# Unit 3: Flat shapes and comparing

Course `math-kindergarten` (Kindergarten Math). **Days 45 to 63** (19 course days; school days 45 to 63, weeks 9 to 13). Sections: 3.1 (days 45 to 49); 3.2 (days 50 to 52); 3.3 (days 53 to 56); 3.4 (days 57 to 60); 3.5 (days 61 to 63). Days by kind: understanding 11, application 7, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Teach through three arcs: shapes and positions, days 45 to 51; building and direct comparison, days 52 to 56; drawing, composing and evidence, days 57 to 63.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What is the same and what is different about these shapes and things?"

**Hook, day 45.** In eight minutes, partners describe a circle card beside a classroom box so another pair can place a matching card. Ask the compelling question. Accept everyday language before modeling position words. Adapted exploration, field 10.
**Supporting questions.** Does turning change a shape, day 47? Which attribute are we comparing, day 53? Why align ends, day 54? Can different pieces make the same whole, day 61?
**Closing claim.** "I can name and locate shapes, build and draw them, join pieces, and compare objects fairly." The shape-language record, construction folder and comparison record support this claim on day 63.
**Teaching moves.** Model all six position words on day 45 with a fixed viewing direction. Contrast examples and nonexamples on day 50; days 51 and 52 line up ends as a plain routine, and day 54 teaches why ends must meet. On days 49, 52 and 57 the fifteen connected-work minutes open with partners telling where a piece or card goes, using two of the six position words. Step back during day 61 puzzles. Teacher pages explain that turning preserves shape and length, a square is a special rectangle, and capacity differs from height.


**Practices.** MP.6: day 54, align turned strips, name length and explain longer, shorter or equal. MP.7: day 61, fill one hexagon outline with different pieces and explain how their sides fit the boundary.
**Rigor.** Eleven understanding and seven application days give 61.1 and 38.9 percent of teaching days; zero fluency days. No owned standard sets arithmetic fluency. Daily counting rehearses known work without time pressure.
**Limits.** Name circle, triangle, square, rectangle and hexagon across sizes and turns. Flat comparisons begin K.G.B.4; unit 7 completes it and solid naming. No formal definitions, angle measures, length units, numerical differences or indirect comparison. Objects, never children, are compared.
**Carried.** Keep unit 2 tracking mats and optional ten-frames for counting only. Tie quantities to numerals in day 45 retrieval; withdraw supplied frames there after one try. Children retain paper shapes through composition. Tie built boundaries to drawings on day 57; withdraw supplied outlines for day 62 art, retaining access supports.
**Focus.** Zero of one units and zero of eighteen primary teaching lessons are major work. Eighteen five-minute counting routines supply 90 major-work minutes by the course method (scope and sequence); counting the twenty unit 2 response minutes on days 45 and 46 makes 110. Subtract five twenty-minute observation blocks from 1,080 teaching minutes: 110 of 980 instructional minutes, or 11.2 percent. Supporting composition practices major counting through piece counts and comparisons on days 59 and 61, inside those routines; no extra minutes are claimed.

Meets: "The circle is behind the box; the square is in front," with the other four relationships accurate. Does not meet: calls the hidden circle in front (A). F2 requires all five names across both size and turn. Meets: pairs both squares and says "square" for each. Does not meet: "The tilted one cannot be a square" (B). F3 requires both built and drawn models of real objects. Meets: four joined strips match the square coaster's boundary and corners on overlay, with a closed triangle drawing. Does not meet: a closed slanted rhombus whose corners do not match the coaster (N), even with a sound drawing. F4 requires full-side contact and the requested outer boundary. Meets: two triangles fill the rectangle and the shared edge is traced. Does not meet: the triangles leave a visible gap (E). F5 requires several measurable attributes of one object. Meets: "Its length, how tall it is, how heavy it is, and how much it holds," with corresponding marks. Does not meet: "It is big" (F). ## U7. Talk and writing

Day 47: "What changed when it turned?" Day 54: "Where must the ends meet?" Day 61: "How can different pieces keep the same outline?" Day 57's drawing is judged for a recognizable closed boundary that matches the construction, not neatness.

## U8. Practice that returns

Count orally to ten or twenty daily. Day 45 retrieves quantity and numeral. Days 49, 59 and 61 count and compare pieces during the routine; days 49, 52 and 57 reopen the position words in partner talk (field 2). No new arithmetic or timed fluency work.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all directions aloud; no independent reading. Pair position words, side, corner, longer, heavier and holds more with gestures. Rehearse "The ___ is ___ than the ___ because ___." Offer large high-contrast or tactile outlines, nonslip mats and directed adult movement. Extend by finding another fill of the same outline and explaining the fit, not introducing units of measure.

## U10. Two rooms

One child uses thirty-five minutes: count five, model eight, work twelve, discuss five, exit five. The adult takes partner roles and keeps dated products; day 63 reviews them, with response on days 64 and 65. A shared screen may show a scene; handwritten paper copies run the whole unit.
