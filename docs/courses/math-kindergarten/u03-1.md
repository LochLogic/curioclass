## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.G.A.1** (days 45, 46, 47, 48, 49): Describe objects in the environment using names of shapes, and describe the relative positions of these objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, and next to.
- **K.G.A.2** (days 45, 46, 47, 48, 49): Correctly name shapes regardless of their orientations or overall size.
- skill mk-shapes-positions (grade K)
- skill mk-how-many (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does turning change a shape, day 47? Which attribute are we comparing, day 53? Why align ends, day 54? Can different pieces make the same whole, day 61?
> **Teaching moves.** Model all six position words on day 45 with a fixed viewing direction. Contrast examples and nonexamples on day 50; days 51 and 52 line up ends as a plain routine, and day 54 teaches why ends must meet. On days 49, 52 and 57 the fifteen connected-work minutes open with partners telling where a piece or card goes, using two of the six position words. Step back during day 61 puzzles. Teacher pages explain that turning preserves shape and length, a square is a special rectangle, and capacity differs from height.
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 2 tracking mats and optional ten-frames for counting only. Tie quantities to numerals in day 45 retrieval; withdraw supplied frames there after one try. Children retain paper shapes through composition. Tie built boundaries to drawings on day 57; withdraw supplied outlines for day 62 art, retaining access supports.
> **Focus.** Zero of one units and zero of eighteen primary teaching lessons are major work. Eighteen five-minute counting routines supply 90 major-work minutes by the course method (scope and sequence); counting the twenty unit 2 response minutes on days 45 and 46 makes 110. Subtract five twenty-minute observation blocks from 1,080 teaching minutes: 110 of 980 instructional minutes, or 11.2 percent. Supporting composition practices major counting through piece counts and comparisons on days 59 and 61, inside those routines; no extra minutes are claimed.
<!-- /skel -->

K.G.A.1, "Describe" environmental objects and positions; K.G.A.2, "Correctly name" shapes across turns/sizes, begun here, completed in u03-2/u03-4. K.G.B.4 comparisons begin informally; unit 7 owns that standard. Use circle, triangle, square, rectangle and hexagon as spoken labels in context; no recited definitions, angle measures or solids naming. MP.6, day 45: fix the viewer before describing a placement. MP.7, day 48: compare boundary features. Teacher explanation: turning changes position, not shape; a straight boundary segment is a side, a corner joins sides, a circle has no straight sides/corners. No algorithm applies. K.CC.B.4b retrieval, day 45, ties counted objects to a numeral.

Answer/look-for: behind folder. Samples: "Behind the folder" with hidden position marked; "In front" (A). Data id: `s03-X45`.
- **E46, K.G.A.1.** Each child receives an unseen large flat outline inside a folded paper screen: three raised straight strips, a clear gap at one join. "What is this shape like?" Feel and sketch/mark before revealing; hear six descriptions. Answer/look-for: straight parts, points and opening, not a completed triangle. Samples: "Pointy and straight, with a space here"; "A closed triangle" despite gap (D). Shape: describe a concealed unfamiliar boundary with feature sketch; practice uses visible single-feature tracing and open/closed correction, never a concealed feel game or this outline. No formal name required. Data id: `s03-X46`.
- **E47, K.G.A.2.** Small tilted square target; three choices: larger upright square, long nonsquare rectangle, circle. "Which shape matches this one?" Join target to choice and mark all four equal sides and corresponding corners on both pictures; hear six. Answer: larger square. Samples: square with all four equal sides and corresponding corners marked; "None, this is a diamond" (B). Shape: target plus three choices and correspondence evidence; practice slides single pieces onto supplied outlines, never a choice display. Elimination alone leaves method unclassified. Data id: `s03-X47`.
- **E48, K.G.A.2.** Fresh tilted square and nonsquare rectangle with unequal adjacent sides. "How are these shapes alike and different?" All mark shared/different features on paired pictures; hear six. Answer: both four straight sides/four corners; square has four equal sides, other rectangle has two long/two short. Samples: "Both four corners; these square sides match" with contrasting sides marked; "This cannot be square because it is turned" (B). Shape: produce a paired comparison; practice corrects feature counts on single cards, never this pair or an alike/different sheet. Data id: `s03-X48`.
- **E49, K.G.A.2.** Fixed headings are picture exemplars of closed three-sided and four-sided figures; fresh target is a narrow sideways triangle. "Where does this card belong?" Draw card under three-sided heading and mark sides. Samples: correct group with three sides tracked; "It cannot go with triangles because it is too thin" (C). Shape: place one new card under fixed geometric headings; practice makes self-chosen groups/repairs a misplaced member, never fixed-heading placement. No color shortcut. Data id: `s03-X49`.

## 6. Days

Tools: None throughout. Children hold pieces and paper; teacher keeps dated originals, feedback and revisions. Fifteen pairs work concurrently, two 45-second turns plus handling per round. At most two public volunteers, with passing allowed. Rin is the sole actor in invented demonstrations, one scene at a time. Return and repair holds one five-minute action: whole class if more than ten of thirty share a letter, otherwise small group while others correct familiar paper. Queue additional letters across later slots. Never shorten repairs or run them during conferences. Everybody returns a corrected diagram. Eight-minute exits allow 2.5 minutes directions/paper, six 45-second turns, one minute collection. Other children return paper; extra help is recorded. Adult-cut large pieces and table trays avoid cutting/sink queues.
