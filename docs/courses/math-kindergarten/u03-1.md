Kindergarten Math, course `math-kindergarten`. Unit 3, Flat shapes and comparing: see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 45 to 49.** Days by kind: understanding 4, application 1, fluency 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children.

## 1. Standards and practices

K.G.A.1, "Describe" environmental objects and positions; K.G.A.2, "Correctly name" shapes across turns/sizes, begun here, completed in u03-2/u03-4. K.G.B.4 comparisons begin informally; unit 7 owns that standard. Informally model all five shape names with their shown features on day 45; formal circle/triangle contrasts follow day 50. Use circle, triangle, square, rectangle and hexagon as spoken labels in context; no recited definitions, angle measures or solids naming. MP.6, day 45: fix the viewer before describing a placement. MP.7, day 48: compare boundary features. Teacher explanation: turning changes position, not shape; a straight boundary segment is a side, a corner joins sides, a circle has no straight sides/corners. No algorithm applies. K.CC.B.4b retrieval, day 45, ties counted objects to a numeral.

## 3. Checks, written first

Read quoted stems aloud, one ask at a time. Every exit is teacher judged with paper from every child; hear six named children only. Mark each component shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Samples illustrate responses, not another scale. Never infer unheard speech: record its component as not yet seen, even when the paper component is shown. Reserve all check cases from teaching, repairs and centers throughout the unit. Original scenes are invented for practice, said aloud and printed on teacher masters. No answer captions or completed outlines cue an exit. The goal-map exit shapes stay fixed; change practice where necessary. Repeated classroom direction phrases are not repeated teaching or evidence cases.

## 6. Days

Tools: None throughout. Children hold pieces and paper; teacher keeps dated originals, feedback and revisions. Fifteen pairs work concurrently, two 45-second turns plus handling per round. At most two public volunteers, with passing allowed. Rin is the sole actor in invented demonstrations, one scene at a time. Return and repair holds one five-minute action: whole class if more than ten of thirty share a letter, otherwise small group while others correct familiar paper. Queue additional letters across later slots. Never shorten repairs or run them during conferences. Return papers for one minute, demonstrate one correction for one, all revise a fresh diagram for two, collect for one. Everybody returns paper; preserve original and supported revision. Give fifteen teacher minutes after each teaching day for thirty feedback notes, thirty seconds each. Assessment annotation uses the separately named time. Eight-minute exits allow one minute launch, two simultaneous paper minutes, six 45-second turns and half a minute collection. On days 45 to 49 hear children 1 to 6, 7 to 12, 13 to 18, 19 to 24, 25 to 30; restart the rotation on days 50 and 55, then hear groups 1 to 3 on days 60 to 62. The remaining groups carry to days 64/65, only on fresh goal-relevant cases. Other children return paper; extra help is recorded. Adult-cut large pieces and table trays avoid cutting/sink queues.

## 9. Supports and extension

Name with objects: shape, the outline of a flat figure; circle, a closed round outline; triangle, three joined straight sides; square, four equal sides and corners matching a paper corner; rectangle, four corners matching a paper corner, including squares; hexagon, six joined straight sides. Side is one straight boundary part; corner is where sides meet; straight does not bend; curved bends; open has a break; closed has none; flat lies on the page. Turn changes direction; size describes how large the figure is. Pointy is informal corner language. Model above/up from, below/down from, beside/next to at the side, in front of/nearer the viewer and behind/farther from the fixed viewer, using one relation at a time. Review boundary as an edge, extending u01-4's counted-space boundary to a shape boundary.

No independent reading. Read all print aloud. Teach words with objects and gestures; remove language frames from independent checks. Offer large high-contrast or tactile boundaries, nonslip mats, broad pencils, signing and usual communication aids. A scribe records words without supplying them. Adult movement follows the child's directions; record mathematical help separately. Direct comparisons cannot become picture guesses. Extend with another example or fit on the same standard, never units or new arithmetic. Frame: "The ___ is ___ the ___." Preserve concealment for E46; directed tactile exploration is available.
