## 1. Standards and practices


K.G.A.2, "Correctly name" circles/triangles/squares/rectangles across turn and size. K.G.A.1, "Describe" positions, day 52 review. K.MD.A.1, "Describe" side length; K.MD.A.2, "Directly compare" strips, both directions and equal. K.G.B.5, "Model" through joined flat boundaries; drawing follows day 57. MP.6, day 51, compare one attribute; MP.7, day 52, attend to connected sides. Teacher explanation: squares are special rectangles with four equal sides and square corners; four equal sides alone do not ensure square corners. No recited definitions, angle measures, units or algorithm. Count pieces within ten.

## 3. Checks, written first


Authored items control over copied U3 prompts. Read quoted stems aloud, one ask at a time. Every exit is teacher judged with paper from every child; hear six named children only. Mark each component shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Samples illustrate responses, not another scale. Never infer unheard speech. **Unit assessment items owned:** none; prepares F2/F3/F4 and F6 length.

- **E50, K.G.A.2.** Fresh sideways thin scalene triangle and small circle, unnamed. "Tell about these shapes." Children mark defining features; six describe names/features. Answer/look-for: triangle, three straight joined sides; circle, closed curved boundary without corners. Samples: "Triangle, three sides; circle, round" with marks; "That thin one is not a triangle" (C). Shape: independent naming/description of thin triangle and circle; practice repairs supplied labels/boundary traces, never blank naming of a pair. Paper marks do not prove unheard names. Data id: `s03-X50`.
- **E51, K.G.A.2.** New narrow rectangular card, unequal adjacent sides, turned diagonally; no choices or removable sides. "What shape is this?" All mark unequal adjacent sides; six name/describe it. Answer: rectangle, not square; four straight sides/square corners, two long/two short. Samples: "Rectangle; these sides are longer"; "A rectangle," after an adult supplies that name, with help recorded. Shape: classify one fresh long rectangle; practice compares supplied side strips and repairs given labels, never single-card classification. Unmatched rectangle-name misconceptions receive descriptive notes: the register has no honest matching letter, so none is invented. Data id: `s03-X51`.
- **E52, K.G.B.5.** Three reserved blunt strips, two equal and one shorter, pretested to close into a narrow triangle, with optional soft paper joins. "Build a triangle with these pieces." All trace their construction on paper. Answer/look-for: three connected straight sides, no gap; unequal sides allowed. Samples: closed narrow triangle matching trace; three strips with open join called complete (D). Shape: independent triangle from loose components; day 51/52 practice repairs partly built quadrilaterals and joins filled paper squares, never builds a complete triangle from loose strips. No numbered lengths. Data id: `s03-X52`.

## 6. Days

Tools: None throughout. Children hold pieces and paper; teacher keeps dated originals, feedback and revisions. Fifteen pairs work concurrently, two 45-second turns plus handling per round. At most two public volunteers, with passing allowed. Rin is the sole actor in invented demonstrations, one scene at a time. Return and repair holds one five-minute action: whole class if more than ten of thirty share a letter, otherwise small group while others correct familiar paper. Queue additional letters across later slots. Never shorten repairs or run them during conferences. Everybody returns a corrected diagram. Eight-minute exits allow 2.5 minutes directions/paper, six 45-second turns, one minute collection. Other children return paper; extra help is recorded. Adult-cut large pieces and table trays avoid cutting/sink queues.

## 9. Supports and extension

No independent reading. Read all print aloud. Teach words with objects and gestures; remove language frames from independent checks. Offer large high-contrast or tactile boundaries, nonslip mats, broad pencils, signing and usual communication aids. A scribe records words without supplying them. Adult movement follows the child's directions; record mathematical help separately. Direct comparisons cannot become picture guesses. Extend with another example or fit on the same standard, never units or new arithmetic. Words: triangle, circle, square, rectangle, closed, equal sides, longer, shorter, join. Frame: "These sides are ___." Teach equal length separately from planned u02-2 equal counts.
