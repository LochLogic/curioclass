## 3. Checks, written first

Read quoted stems aloud, one ask at a time. Every exit is teacher judged with paper from every child; hear six named children only. Mark each component shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Samples illustrate responses, not another scale. Never infer unheard speech: record its component as not yet seen, even when the paper component is shown. Reserve all check cases from teaching, repairs and centers throughout the unit. Original scenes are invented for practice, said aloud and printed on teacher masters. No answer captions or completed outlines cue an exit. The goal-map exit shapes stay fixed; change practice where necessary. Repeated classroom direction phrases are not repeated teaching or evidence cases.

**Unit assessment items owned:** none; prepares F2/F3/F4 and F6 length.

- **E50, K.G.A.2.** Fresh sideways thin scalene triangle and small circle, unnamed. "Tell about these shapes." Children mark defining features; six describe names/features. Answer/look-for: triangle, three straight joined sides; circle, closed curved boundary without corners. Samples: "Triangle, three sides; circle, round" with marks; "That thin one is not a triangle" (C). Shape: independent naming/description of thin triangle and circle; practice repairs supplied labels/boundary traces, never blank naming of a pair. Paper marks do not prove unheard names. Data id: `s03-X50`.
- **E51, K.G.A.2.** New narrow rectangular card, unequal adjacent sides, turned diagonally; no choices or removable sides. "What shape is this?" All mark unequal adjacent sides; six name/describe it. Answer: rectangle, not square; four straight sides/square corners, two long/two short. Samples: "Rectangle; these sides are longer"; "A rectangle," after an adult supplies that name, with help recorded. Shape: classify one fresh long rectangle; practice compares supplied side strips and repairs given labels, never single-card classification. Unmatched rectangle-name misconceptions receive descriptive notes: the register has no honest matching letter, so none is invented. Data id: `s03-X51`.
- **E52, K.G.B.5.** Three reserved blunt strips, two equal and one shorter, pretested to close into a narrow triangle, with optional soft paper joins. "Build a triangle with these pieces." All trace their construction on paper. Answer/look-for: three connected straight sides, no gap; unequal sides allowed. Samples: closed narrow triangle matching trace; three strips with open join called complete (D). Shape: independent triangle from loose components; day 51/52 practice repairs partly built quadrilaterals and joins filled paper squares, never builds a complete triangle from loose strips. No numbered lengths. Data id: `s03-X52`.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Paper shapes/position key continue. Day 51 ties side strips to boundary drawings and models quadrilateral joins; day 52 removes partly completed boundaries for E52, preserving motor supports. Ideal instrument: movable strips, soft paper joins and squared-paper corners. Paper strips replace blunt straws, folded connectors replace clay, paper squares replace blocks: fits, no digital spec/key tool. Choose paper joins to avoid clay cleanup/allergy concerns. Adult cuts all materials, no small connectors or mouth contact.

## 6. Days

Tools: None throughout. Children hold pieces and paper; teacher keeps dated originals, feedback and revisions. Fifteen pairs work concurrently, two 45-second turns plus handling per round. At most two public volunteers, with passing allowed. Rin is the sole actor in invented demonstrations, one scene at a time. Return and repair holds one five-minute action: whole class if more than ten of thirty share a letter, otherwise small group while others correct familiar paper. Queue additional letters across later slots. Never shorten repairs or run them during conferences. Return papers for one minute, demonstrate one correction for one, all revise a fresh diagram for two, collect for one. Everybody returns paper; preserve original and supported revision. Give fifteen teacher minutes after each teaching day for thirty feedback notes, thirty seconds each. Assessment annotation uses the separately named time. Eight-minute exits allow one minute launch, two simultaneous paper minutes, six 45-second turns and half a minute collection. On days 45 to 49 hear children 1 to 6, 7 to 12, 13 to 18, 19 to 24, 25 to 30; restart the rotation on days 50 and 55, then hear groups 1 to 3 on days 60 to 62. The remaining groups carry to days 64/65, only on fresh goal-relevant cases. Other children return paper; extra help is recorded. Adult-cut large pieces and table trays avoid cutting/sink queues.

## Day 51

**Shape:** Classify one fresh long rectangle without choices; practice compares supplied side strips or corrects labels, never single-card classification.

- **5 min. Warm-up: oral count.** Review u02-7 short/long, using written earlier-unit teaching; count within ten, supported oral continuation toward twenty.

- **5 min. Return and repair.** One five-minute C/B repair from preceding exit/queue; all return correction.

- **2 min. Tidy.** Return pieces to table trays concurrently; adult checks for damage.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 50: "What do the straight parts tell us?" Day 51: "Where do the ends meet?" Day 52: "What boundary did joining these squares make?" Separate turns. Day 51 written practice completes one missing edge on supplied rectangle diagram. Look for closure/corner match, not neatness; compare side strips directly before longer/shorter language.

The cited earlier-unit targets have written ledger evidence; retrieve them with fresh cases. Review Partner turns/Return and repair from u01-1. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none. ## 9. Supports and extension

Review circle, triangle, square and rectangle with u03-1 objects. Name join as bringing ends together; equal sides as sides whose lengths match; longer as extending farther from a common start; shorter as extending less. Teach full sides touching and overlap, one piece covering part of another, in day 51 construction. Review short/long from u02-7 and equal counts from u02-2 before modeling equal lengths. A square is also a rectangle; saying rectangle alone for a square is not an error.

No independent reading. Read all print aloud. Teach words with objects and gestures; remove language frames from independent checks. Offer large high-contrast or tactile boundaries, nonslip mats, broad pencils, signing and usual communication aids. A scribe records words without supplying them. Adult movement follows the child's directions; record mathematical help separately. Direct comparisons cannot become picture guesses. Extend with another example or fit on the same standard, never units or new arithmetic. Frame: "These sides are ___." Teach equal length separately from planned u02-2 equal counts.
