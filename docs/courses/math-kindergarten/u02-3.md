## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.B.4** (days 25, 27, 28): Understand the relationship between numbers and quantities; connect counting to cardinality.
- **K.CC.B.4b** (days 25, 27, 28): Understand that the last number name said tells the number of objects counted. The number of objects is the same regardless of their arrangement or the order in which they were counted.
- **K.CC.B.5** (days 25, 27, 28): Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- **K.CC.A.3** (days 25, 27, 28): Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- **K.CC.C.6** (days 26, 27, 28, 29): Identify whether the number of objects in one group is greater than, less than, or equal to the number of objects in another group, e.g., by using matching and counting strategies.
- skill mk-how-many (grade K)
- skill mk-compare-groups (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Practices.** MP.3: day 24, partners compare five blocks with eight, justify by matching and counting, and question a spacing-based claim. MP.7: day 28, circle the full five within eight on joined frames and use the remaining three to explain why eight exceeds five.

Sorting supports cardinality and comparison.

**E25, K.CC.B.5.** Eight original leaf drawings along an S-shaped open curve, no overlap. Say, "Mark each leaf as you count it." Key/look-fors: eight single marks and, for six heard, one through eight, eight final. Wrong: skips one leaf (K); visits one twice (L).

Shape: Counts a fixed curved leaf path, not movable objects or a taught route. No leaves, eight-picture curve, S layout or pretraced curve in teaching. Practice original square pictures in straight lines, arrays, circles and scatters, mainly five and ten.

**E26, K.CC.C.6.** Two unmatched rows of five bead drawings: one compact, the other wide, ends offset. Read, "Match the beads to compare the rows." Key/look-fors: five unique links, no extra bead. Six heard follow-ups: "What do your matches tell you?" Shown: "Same, every bead has a partner." Wrong: wider row called more because of spread (B).

Shape: Resolves misleading spacing in unseen bead rows. No beads, equal fives with offset ends or independent link-all row comparison in practice; verify supplied links on equal fours and correct a supplied wrong link.

**E27, K.CC.C.6.** Three unframed picture pairs: six/eight pencils, nine/nine pegs, ten/seven flags. Equal-length rows disguise unequal counts; equal pegs have different spacing. No links. Read, "Mark the pair with the same number by joining its partners." Key/look-fors: nine/nine pegs with nine unique links, no extras. A selection alone is insufficient, so elimination cannot replace verifying the pair. Heard reason: every peg has a partner. Wrong: unequal pair selected because rows cover equal space (B); a peg skipped during verification (K), if observed.

Shape: Recognizes and verifies one equal pair among three plausible pairs. No pencils/pegs/flags, 9/9 pair or three-pair board in practice; rehearse one physical frame comparison at a time.

**E28, K.CC.C.6; MP.7.** Six button drawings and nine star drawings in loose clusters, each above a blank two-row 10-frame. Read, "Use these frames to show which group has more." Key/look-fors: six dots as five and one, nine as five and four; nine/star frame marked as more. Heard follow-up: "How does five help you know?" Shown: "Both fill five; stars have four after it, buttons only one." Wrong: six called more because its source cluster spreads farther (B); nine explicitly called fewer despite its extras (I).

Shape: Independently represents two unseen amounts in blank frames to compare them. No buttons/stars, 6/9 pair or blank double-frame comparison sheet in teaching; inspect filled five/eight frames or repair one supplied representation at a time.

**E29, K.CC.C.6.** Three flowers beside an unlined empty garden panel with the familiar finish corner. Read, "Draw more flowers than this group." Key/look-fors: any four through ten flower marks; six is one shown sample. Multiple correct answers. Heard reason may use matching/extras. Wrong: two explicitly called more (I); three spread-out marks said to be more because of spread (B).

Shape: Creates an unscaffolded larger flower group for three. No flowers, three-object reference or independent larger-only blank drawing in practice; repair existing comparison drawings for five instead.

**Check reservations, solved cold.** M1 stays seven throughout. M2 stops at seven and picture totals are 6, 8, 10, 9. F2: three more than two; four equal to four.

Shape: M1 is an independent seven-tab count plus invariance predictions; M2 is seven-of-ten count-out plus four-layout picture routes; F2 compares two/three tabs and equal fours using both methods. No seven tabs, six-star line, eight-star array, ten-star circle, nine-star scatter, two/three tabs or equal-four check case in teaching. Use supported square-picture corrections and completed frames, never a complete check sequence.
