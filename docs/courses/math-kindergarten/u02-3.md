# Section 2.3: Track pictures and use five to compare

Kindergarten Math, course `math-kindergarten`. Unit 2, Numbers 1 to 10: see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 25 to 29.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 2. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children.

## 1. Standards and practices

**Share.** K.CC.B.5's "Count to answer" within ten in lines, arrays, circles and scatters, day 25; K.CC.B.4b's "Understand" that the total is unchanged regardless of counting order that day. K.CC.C.6's "Identify whether" groups are greater, less or equal, days 26 to 29. K.CC.B.4 connects totals throughout. K.CC.A.3's "Represent" is a later numeral connection, not assessed before day 30.

**Strategy and connection.** Review unit 1 tracking and u02-2 matching. Teach a circle start and row-by-row route. Day 26 teaches five occupied cells, then two five-cell rows as a 10-frame before day 27 rehearsal. MP.7, day 28: compare a full five plus remaining pieces, not outline sizes. No equation/algorithm. Teacher explanation distinguishes empty cells from objects; frames always have two equal five-cell rows.

## 2. Where it sits

Day 25 receives u02-2 comparison feedback and retrieves u01-4 tracking as review. No readiness check. Day 30 receives frame/comparison notes in Return and repair, then connects amounts to numerals, not yet numeral comparison.

## 3. Checks, written first

All exits are original, invented for practice, judged=teacher and path=paper. Read each single stem aloud. Every child marks or directs a mark on paper. Eight minutes holds directions 1, initial paper work 2, six named turns at 45 seconds 4.5, collection 0.5. The other twenty-four keep working during the six turns. Hear just the named follow-up, or observe the count already requested, without asking the child to repeat completed work. Record who was heard. Paper alone cannot establish unheard counting words or reasons.

Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen for each component. Each key/look-for is a shown sample; each listed observed wrong response is a not yet seen sample. Record any help. Assign an error letter only from the observed action or explanation. Reserve every field 3 case and Shape from teaching, practice, cards and repairs throughout the unit.

**Unit assessment ownership:** none new. Support M1 counting-order invariance, M2 picture arrangements and F2 comparisons without administering their cases.

**E25, K.CC.B.5.** Eight original leaf drawings along an S-shaped open curve, no overlap. Say, "Mark each leaf as you count it." Key/look-fors: eight single marks and, for six heard, one through eight, with each word paired to one leaf. Wrong: skips one leaf (K); visits one twice (L).

Shape: Counts a fixed curved leaf path, not movable objects or a taught route. No leaves, eight-picture curve, S layout or pretraced curve in teaching. Practice original square pictures in straight lines, arrays, circles and scatters, mainly five and ten.

**E26, K.CC.C.6.** Two unmatched rows of five bead drawings: one compact, the other wide, ends offset. Read, "Match the beads to compare the rows." Key/look-fors: five unique links, no extra bead. Six heard follow-ups: "What do your matches tell you?" Shown: "Same, every bead has a partner." Wrong: wider row called more because of spread (B).

Shape: Resolves misleading spacing in unseen bead rows. No beads, equal fives with offset ends or independent link-all row comparison in practice; verify supplied links on equal fours and correct a supplied wrong link.

**E27, K.CC.C.6.** Three unframed picture pairs: six/eight pencils, nine/nine pegs, ten/seven flags. Equal-length rows disguise unequal counts; equal pegs have different spacing. No links. Read, "Mark the pair with the same number by joining its partners." Key/look-fors: nine/nine pegs with nine unique links, no extras. A selection alone is insufficient, so elimination cannot replace verifying the pair. Heard follow-up: "What shows that these groups are the same?" Shown: "Every peg has a partner." Wrong: unequal pair selected because rows cover equal space (B); a peg skipped during verification (K), if observed.

Shape: Recognizes and verifies one equal pair among three plausible pairs. No pencils/pegs/flags, 9/9 pair or three-pair board in practice; rehearse one physical frame comparison at a time.

**E28, K.CC.C.6; MP.7.** Six button drawings and nine star drawings in loose clusters, each above a blank two-row 10-frame. Read, "Use these frames to show which group has more." Key/look-fors: six dots as five and one, nine as five and four; nine/star frame marked as more. Heard follow-up: "How does five help you know?" Shown: "Both fill five; stars have four after it, buttons only one." Wrong: six called more because its source cluster spreads farther (B); nine explicitly called fewer despite its extras (I).

Shape: Independently represents two unseen amounts in blank frames to compare them. No buttons/stars, 6/9 pair or blank double-frame comparison sheet in teaching; inspect filled five/eight frames or repair one supplied representation at a time.

**E29, K.CC.C.6.** Three flowers beside an unlined empty garden panel with the familiar finish corner. Read, "Draw more flowers than this group." Key/look-fors: any countable amount greater than three; four through ten are within the taught range, six is one shown sample. Accept a larger correct group without teaching beyond ten. Multiple correct answers. Heard follow-up: "How does your drawing show more?" Shown: "Each flower has a partner and mine has extras." Wrong: two explicitly called more (I); three spread-out marks said to be more because of spread (B).

Shape: Creates an unscaffolded larger flower group for three. No flowers, three-object reference or independent larger-only blank drawing in practice; repair existing comparison drawings for five instead.

**Check reservations, solved cold.** M1 stays seven throughout. M2 stops at seven and picture totals are 6, 8, 10, 9. F2: three more than two; four equal to four.

Shape: M1 is an independent seven-tab count plus invariance predictions; M2 is seven-of-ten count-out plus four-layout picture routes; F2 compares two/three tabs and equal fours using both methods. No seven tabs, six-star line, eight-star array, ten-star circle, nine-star scatter, two/three tabs or equal-four check case in teaching. Use supported square-picture corrections and completed frames, never a complete check sequence.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

One puppet demonstrates one practice mistake at a time, never a child's work. If more than ten of thirty share a letter, the next five-minute Return and repair models one correction, lets pairs retry simultaneously on a fresh practice case and collects everyone's corrected paper. Otherwise meet one group while others correct familiar work. Choose the earliest blocking action; queue other letters across later slots. Never stack five-minute repairs or use reserved art.

C, day 25: three from left called four from right versus three both ways. K, day 25: one of five squares omitted versus five unique visits. L, day 25: a square revisited versus five unique visits. B, day 26: four spread squares called more than four compact squares versus four matches. I, day 28: eight called fewer than five despite three extras versus eight more.

Repair C/K/L with a start and completed-visit marks; B with repaired links; I by pointing to extras and naming the word. Day 30 receives one unresolved five-minute action. Never combine the letters into a diagnosis without observing the particular action.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

**Carried.** Review Counted-uncounted boundary from u01-4. Day 25 adds Start and completed-visit marks for fixed pictures. The 5-frame material is review from u01-1, but its five-cell structure is explicitly taught on day 26: fill five, then join a second equal row and show eight as five and three. Day 27 rehearses; day 28 uses this structure to compare. Numeral ties wait for day 30; supplied frames withdraw on day 40 with access supports retained.

**Ideal instrument.** One visible piece per cell, two equal five-cell rows. Hand-drawn paired 5-frames fit; scrap paper and large scrap squares are the no-kit path. Empty cells are spaces, not counted objects. Adult-cut large pieces stay out of mouths; replace damaged pieces. No digital instrument or spec is needed.

## 6. Days

All digital tools: None; no key tool. Children hold pieces and paper; the teacher holds dated records. Fifteen pairs work simultaneously: one 45-second turn each takes 1.5 minutes per round. Use no more than two public volunteers; anyone may pass. Label printed stories "invented for practice" and say this aloud. Object-only scenes need no actors.

Review Partner turns, Counting centers and Paper count-dot record from u01-1. Each child returns a marked practice record and exit slip. Before the exit, teach or review Finish tick from u02-1: a corner tick means the child has finished, including an intentionally empty group; it never gives the answer.

Return and repair gives back the previous day's exit, heard-response note and practice papers with written feedback. Its five minutes hold return 1, one modeled correction 1, simultaneous fresh practice 2, collection 1. Retain original and revision. Repair one earliest obstruction; queue other letters in later slots. Hear further children during their later scheduled turns, keeping earlier unheard components not yet seen. After class allow fifteen teacher minutes for thirty paper sets at thirty seconds each; write one useful correction. No reserved case or Shape becomes repair practice.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 25: "Where did the count start?" Day 26: "Is an empty space a bead?" Day 28: "What is left after the full five?" Separate paired turns.

Day 28 paper practice corrects a supplied eight-square representation missing one dot. Look for five filled cells and three after it; no numeral required. E28 reserves the independent two-frame comparison.

## 8. Practice that returns

Warm-ups: day 25 retrieves u01-4 tracking and u02-2 matches; day 26 picture routes; day 27 the frame structure taught on day 26; day 28 extras; day 29 repaired comparison drawings. Review Partner turns, Counting centers, Paper count-dot record and Return and repair from u01-1; review Finish tick from u02-1. Count toward ten, with supported oral extension toward twenty.

No arithmetic-fluency target; the standards set none. Days 27 and 29 rehearse taught comparisons and drawing repairs with immediate partner feedback, no new notation and no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

**Language.** Row: things beside one another. Array: equal rows of things. Circle: a closed round arrangement. A 10-frame has two rows of five spaces. Say each while showing it. Review the named 5-frame material from u01-1; teach its structure explicitly on day 26. Review same/equal, more, fewer and extra from u02-2. Frame: "Both fill five; this has ___ after five." No how-many-more question.

**Access and exits.** Enlarge high-contrast pictures or use tactile pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without supplying counts or reasons and records help. A child unable to mark directs adult marking. Face children and show visual turn cues. Read all print aloud; independent reading is zero. Extend by rearranging quantities within ten while preserving comparison, with no new operation.

## 10. Two rooms

**Teaching one child.** Thirty minutes: count 3, model/rehearse 5, guided work 7, return and repair 5, exit 8, tidy 2. The adult alternates partner roles and hears this child. Keep original and corrected paper. A home session may be skipped under DEFAULTS.md.

**One projector.** Optional display only. Handwritten cards, board drawings and paper pieces give every child the full task without device turns.
