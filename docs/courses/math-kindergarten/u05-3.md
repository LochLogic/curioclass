## 1. Standards and practices

K.OA.A.4, "find" and "record": preserve a given part one through nine and complete ten. K.OA.A.3, "Decompose": relate occupied/empty parts to fixed ten on days 97/98. MP.7, day 97: five spaces and two more explain seven missing from three occupied; MP.8, day 98: one part grows as the other shrinks. Explain empty cells as places to fill, not extra objects already present. No make-ten arithmetic above ten, new algorithm or speed requirement. Day 98 ties the model to both equation orders before fluency rehearsal.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

| Letter | Wrong beside right; shown day | Receiving slot and move |
|---|---|---|
| C | Day 98: 10 = 4 + 6 rejected solely for order versus both sides built as ten. | Day 99 Return and repair, five minutes connect equal quantities. |
| D | Day 97: three given/three added makes six; right seven added makes ten. Day 98: four/four eight versus four/six ten. | E97 to day 98 Return and repair five minutes, preserve occupied cells and fill empty ones; E98 to day 99's second five. E99 to day 100. |
| E | Day 98: five empty places numbered 0,1,2,3,4 and reported four versus 1,2,3,4,5. | Day 100 Return and repair or day 101's five-minute slot: touch-count from one with fresh partial-record retry. |

Teach empty-space counting correctly day 97; day 98 contrast precedes fluency day 99. More than a third means many. Ten-minute slots hold at most two five-minute moves, small groups for fewer misses. ## 9. Supports and extension

Define occupied as a place with a piece, empty as a place without one, missing as the pieces still needed, and partner of ten as the other part that completes ten; frame: "___ are there; ___ more make ten." Distinguish partner number/classroom partner using pictures. Read all print aloud, including equation cards; independent reading is zero. Use large high-contrast pictures, pattern plus color, tactile outlines, nonslip mats and broad crayons. Children may sign, point, dictate or direct an adult to mark paper; record help without changing the goal. No required fingers, public speech or fine cutting. Adult cuts strips/pieces. Supervise large pieces; replace mouthed/damaged pieces with drawn marks. Checks retain quantities and one ask; never supply the missing part. Extend within ten by explaining another valid pair or repeat, never totals above ten.
