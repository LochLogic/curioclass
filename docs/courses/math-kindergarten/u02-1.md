## 1. Standards and practices

**Share and limits.** K.CC.B.4b: "Understand that the last number name said tells the number of objects counted" and that it is "the same regardless of their arrangement". Connect spoken requests to quantities on day 19; preserve a moved set on day 20. K.CC.B.5's "Count to answer" is sampled within ten; count-out and K.CC.A.3's "Write" belong later, not claimed here. Numeral cards and structured frames stay stored until teaching days.

**Strategy and connection.** Review K.CC.B.4a word-object pairing from u01-4. Touch or move once per word, enclose the finished set and name its total. No algorithm. MP.2, day 19: fingers or squares represent a spoken amount. MP.3, day 20: account for an unchanged set by noticing nothing entered or left. Teacher explanation distinguishes a number word, quantity and later numeral; recounting alone does not diagnose A.

## 3. Checks, written first

All exits are original, invented for practice, judged=teacher and path=paper. Read each single stem aloud. Every child marks or directs a mark on paper. Eight minutes holds directions 1, initial paper work 2, six named turns at 45 seconds 4.5, collection 0.5. The other twenty-four keep working during the six turns. Observe only the single exit action already requested, without asking the child to repeat completed work. Record who was heard. Paper alone cannot establish unheard counting words or reasons.

Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen for each component. Each key/look-for is a shown sample; each listed observed wrong response is a not yet seen sample. Record any help. Assign an error letter only from the observed action or explanation. Reserve every field 3 case and Shape from teaching, practice, cards and repairs throughout the unit.

**Unit assessment owned:** M1, s02 field 5, collected later. Days 19 and 20 collect no unit assessment.

Key/look-fors: seven distinct marks representing seven clips. Shown: all seven; observed omission of a clip is K. No cardinality reason is scored here; O19 records that separately.

Key/look-fors: all five moved, spread farther apart, none added or lost.

Shape: Transforms one compact bear set to a wider paper arrangement without losing or adding members; O20 independently records the invariance reason. ## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

One puppet demonstrates one practice mistake at a time, never a child's work. If more than ten of thirty share a letter, the next five-minute Return and repair models one correction, lets pairs retry simultaneously on a fresh practice case and collects everyone's corrected paper. Otherwise meet one group while others correct familiar work. Choose the earliest blocking action; queue other letters across later slots. Never stack five-minute repairs or use reserved art.

Wrong beside right: A, day 20, one through eight repeated for a total versus "eight altogether". B, day 20, eight squares called ten after moving versus eight both times. K, day 19 retrieval, one of five leaves untouched versus five single touches. C is reserved for M1 and taught on day 25, not inferred here.

Repair A by enclosing the counted group and naming its total; B by moving the same visible pieces and checking membership; K by moving each leaf to a counted area. Unit 1 response has its own ten minutes each day. Day 21 receives one unresolved A/B/K action in its five-minute Return and repair; letters remain separate, not a combined diagnosis.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

**Carried.** Review Counted-uncounted boundary from u01-4 and Adjacent object-to-mark links from u01-2. Children hold fingers or squares and paper; the teacher holds records. Day 19 connects supplied quantity pictures to adjacent marks; day 20 conserves a moved collection. Numerals wait for day 30, structured frames for day 26.

**Key moment, day 20.** Ideal instrument: every member stays visible while the set moves. Large paper squares fit; scrap-paper squares and hand-drawn mats are the no-kit path. Clips are drawings only; bears are large adult-cut paper pieces. Inspect pieces, keep them out of mouths and replace damaged pieces. No digital instrument or spec is needed.

## 6. Days

All digital tools: None; no key tool. Children hold pieces and paper; the teacher holds dated records. Fifteen pairs work simultaneously: one 45-second turn each takes 1.5 minutes per round. Use no more than two public volunteers; anyone may pass. Label printed stories "invented for practice" and say this aloud. Rin is the only actor in the clip scene; object-only scenes need no second actor.

Review Partner turns, Counting centers and Paper count-dot record from u01-1. Each child returns a marked practice record and exit slip. Day 19 teaches Finish tick: a corner tick means the child has finished, including an intentionally empty group; it never gives the answer.

Return and repair gives back the previous day's exit, heard-response note and practice papers with written feedback. Its five minutes hold return 1, one modeled correction 1, simultaneous fresh practice 2, collection 1. Retain original and revision. Repair one earliest obstruction; queue other letters in later slots. Hear further children during their later scheduled turns, keeping earlier unheard components not yet seen. After class allow fifteen teacher minutes for thirty paper sets at thirty seconds each; write one useful correction. No reserved case or Shape becomes repair practice.

## Day 19

**Shape:** Produces an unseen requested collection on a blank mat. No clip drawings, seven-object requests or blank-mat production in teaching; interpret displayed fingers and check an adult's representation instead.

- **8 min. Hook.** Show three squares. Interpret supplied fingers or choose a matching amount; fingers optional. Ask the unit question.

- **5 min. Oral count.** Supported count toward ten, no numeral test.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Unit 1 five-leaf pairing, unscored. Fifteen pairs together, one 45-second turn per partner; collect each child's marked leaf record.

- **7 min. Connect quantities.** Interpret displayed three, five, eight and ten fingers or squares. Model naming the whole amount; no seven-piece example.

- **5 min. Connected work.** Annotate an adult quantity picture with adjacent linked marks; children hold paper. Last five minutes are the separate O19 check, replacing repeated practice; the exit uses its own eight minutes and adds no reason ask.

- **20 min. Counting centers.** Ten minutes Unit 1 response: five return day 17 papers and records with feedback, five correct the earliest tracking need, reviewing Puppet omission in a five-square count from u01-4. Five separate minutes repair a new retrieval miss if needed; five familiar practice. Day 20 holds the fresh transfer. Others use supplied-picture tasks and return a record. Missing earlier observations stay on a list, never relabel response as assessment.

- **2 min. Tidy.** Return pieces concurrently at table trays.

- **8 min. Exit ticket.** E19; paper from everyone, six observations of the sole exit action.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 19: "Which mark stands for this finger?" Day 20: "What entered or left?" Paired turns, two public volunteers at most.

Day 19 paper practice annotates an adult's three-finger drawing with adjacent marks. Look for three distinct links, one per raised finger. This checks a supplied representation, not the blank exit mat.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 19 retrieves u01-4 word-object pairing with five paper leaves. Day 20 uses ten paper squares during Oral count and retrieval; eight leaves appear only in the reserved response transfer. Review Partner turns, Counting centers, Paper count-dot record, Return and repair and Try a tracking support from u01-1, plus Counted-uncounted boundary from u01-4. The written u01-4 last-word conversation is review; connect it explicitly to a whole-set total here.

No fluency target; the standard sets none. Count toward ten, with supported oral extension toward twenty as rehearsal only. No timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

**Language.** Total/altogether: how many in the whole group. Arrange: put the same pieces in places. Frame: "There are ___ altogether." Review count, object, number word, partner, touch, move and counter from u01-1; group/mark from u01-2; keep track from u01-4; represent from u01-5.

**Access and exits.** Enlarge high-contrast pictures or use tactile paper outlines. Fingers are optional; use squares instead. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without giving a quantity or reason, and records help. A child unable to mark directs adult marking. Face children and show visual turn cues. Read every stem and label aloud; independent reading is zero. Extend by changing arrangements within ten while preserving quantity, not by introducing operations.

## 10. Two rooms

**Teaching one child.** Thirty minutes: count 2, model 4, guided work/retrieval 4, Unit 1 response 10, exit 8, tidy 2. In the ten response minutes return earlier papers together, including E19 on day 20, repair one earliest need for five minutes and make a fresh transfer for five. Queue another need for the next session. The adult alternates partner roles and hears this child. Keep original and revised paper; a session may be skipped.

**One projector.** Optional display only. Handwritten cards, board drawings and paper pieces give every child the full task without device turns.
