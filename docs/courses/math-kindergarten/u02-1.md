# Section 2.1: Show quantities and keep the count


## 1. Standards and practices


**Share and limits.** K.CC.B.4b: "Understand that the last number name said tells the number of objects counted" and that it is "the same regardless of their arrangement". Connect spoken requests to quantities on day 19; preserve a moved set on day 20. K.CC.B.5's "Count to answer" is sampled within ten; count-out and K.CC.A.3's "Write" belong later, not claimed here. Numeral cards and structured frames stay stored until teaching days.

**Strategy and connection.** Review K.CC.B.4a word-object pairing from u01-4. Touch or move once per word, enclose the finished set and name its total. No algorithm. MP.2, day 19: fingers or squares represent a spoken amount. MP.3, day 20: account for an unchanged set by noticing nothing entered or left. Teacher explanation distinguishes a number word, quantity and later numeral; recounting alone does not diagnose A.

## 3. Checks, written first


Live exits below supersede older U3: teacher judged, path paper, invented for practice and read aloud. Eight minutes allows launch 1, concurrent paper 2, six named oral turns of 45 seconds 4.5, collection 0.5. Hear a count OR reason, not both in a turn. Everyone returns marked paper. Record names heard; paper does not prove unheard counting or reasoning. Use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen by component. Full keys below are shown samples; each first observed wrong action is a not yet seen sample. Record help. Diagnose letters from observed actions or heard reasons, never wrong marks alone. Follow-ups are separate single asks to six only.

Goal-map cases control over older U3. Reserve complete cases, layouts and response formats. Literal exclusion of every assessment integer is impossible with M3's 0 to 10; changing numbers alone does not make transfer. Never use reserved art in practice.

**Unit assessment owned:** M1, s02 field 5, collected later. Days 19 and 20 collect no unit assessment.

**E19, K.CC.B.4b.** Picture: Rin's empty mat, no dots, spaces, numeral or model. Say, "Draw seven paper clips on Rin's mat." Accept loops or strokes. Key/look-fors: seven distinct marks representing seven clips. Heard follow-up: "How many altogether?" Shown: "Seven." Wrong: repeats the sequence without a total when asked (A); leaves a requested clip unrepresented (K).

Shape: Produces an unseen requested collection on a blank mat. No clip drawings, seven-object requests or blank-mat production in teaching; interpret displayed fingers and check an adult's representation instead.

**E20, K.CC.B.4b.** Five large paper bear pieces begin close together on a small outlined patch, beside a larger blank patch. Teacher counts the starting pieces once with the class. Say, "Spread these bears out on the big patch." Children position and mark each piece on paper; temporary adhesive or outlines retain the arrangement. Key/look-fors: all five moved, spread farther apart, none added or lost. Heard follow-up: "What did spreading do to the number of bears?" Shown: "Only moved; none came or went." Wrong: "There are more now because it is wider" (B); a bear skipped during counting (K), only if observed.

Shape: Transforms a compact bear set to a wider paper arrangement and accounts for invariance. No bears, five-piece spreading or prediction-after-spreading in practice; reform eight square pieces into compact clusters or a bundle, not a widened row.

**M1 cold key and reservation.** Seven remains seven after spreading and after reversing the route. Whole sequence without a requested total is A; eight caused by spread is B; eight caused by a new start is C.

Shape: Independent seven-tab count, rearrangement prediction and reverse-route verification. No seven tabs, complete multi-stage conference or M1 sequence in practice; use partner-supported eight-square transformations. Counting-order invariance is explicitly taught on day 25 before M1.

**Later collection schedule, plan point.** M1 has four spoken asks (count, total, predict after moving, recount from another start); M2 adds a fifth. At about 45 seconds per ask plus handling/recording, allow four minutes per child, not two. A concrete allocation is five children for twenty minutes on each of days 34 to 39, thirty children total, closing the record on day 39. Give rolling feedback and response from earlier groups. This requires subsequent section/data alignment before use; the inherited days 34 to 36 window cannot hold all asks. Do not shorten or repeat interviews to hide the conflict.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


One puppet demonstrates one practice mistake at a time, never a child's work. If more than ten of thirty share a letter, the next five-minute Return and repair models one correction, lets pairs retry simultaneously on a fresh practice case and collects everyone's corrected paper. Otherwise meet one group while others correct familiar work. Choose the earliest blocking action; queue other letters across later slots. Never stack five-minute repairs or use reserved art.

Wrong beside right: A, day 20, one through eight repeated for a total versus "eight altogether". B, day 20, eight squares called ten after moving versus eight both times. K, day 19 retrieval, one of five leaves untouched versus five single touches. C is reserved for M1 and taught on day 25, not inferred here.

Repair A by enclosing the counted group and naming its total; B by moving the same visible pieces and checking membership; K by moving each leaf to a counted area. Unit 1 response has its own ten minutes each day. Day 21 receives one unresolved A/B/K action in its five-minute Return and repair; letters remain separate, not a combined diagnosis.

## 6. Days

All tools: None. Children hold paper and pieces; teacher keeps dated records and names heard. Fifteen pairs work concurrently: one 45-second turn per partner takes 1.5 minutes per round, leaving handling and feedback time inside the blocks. Two public volunteers at most; anyone may pass. Every child returns paper. Label all live cases invented for practice and say this aloud. No personal reporting. Teach a finish tick in the corner during the first practice record, before any exit uses it; it records intentional completion, never the answer.

Return and repair gives back the previous paper with feedback and holds one five-minute action from field 4. Collect a fresh correction and keep the original. No reserved exit/check art is used. Day 19 separately budgets unit 1 response and retrieval repair.

## Day 19

**Shared shape.** Produces an unseen requested collection on a blank mat. No clip drawings, seven-object requests or blank-mat production in teaching; interpret displayed fingers and check an adult's representation instead.


- **8 min. Hook.** Show three squares. Interpret supplied fingers or choose a matching amount; fingers optional. Ask the unit question.

- **5 min. Oral count.** Supported count toward ten, no numeral test.

- **5 min. Retrieval.** Unit 1 five-leaf pairing, unscored. Fifteen pairs together, one 45-second turn per partner.

- **7 min. Connect quantities.** Interpret displayed three, five, eight and ten fingers or squares. Model naming the whole amount; no seven-piece example.

- **5 min. Connected work.** Annotate an adult quantity picture with adjacent linked marks; children hold paper.

- **20 min. Counting centers.** Ten minutes Unit 1 response: five moving/touching repair plus five fresh transfer. Five separate minutes repair the retrieval miss if needed; five familiar practice. Others use supplied-picture tasks and return a record. Missing earlier observations stay on a list, never relabel response as assessment.

- **2 min. Tidy.** Return pieces concurrently at table trays.

- **8 min. Exit ticket.** E19; paper from everyone, six named follow-ups.

## 9. Supports and extension

Language: total, arrange; "There are ___ altogether." Review count, object, number word, partner, touch, move, counter, group, mark, keep track and represent as planned only, with demonstrations and gestures.

Enlarge and space pictures; use high contrast or tactile paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without supplying quantity or reason; record help. A scribe records reasons verbatim, never chooses a numeral for a writing check. Fingers are optional. Read all print aloud; no independent reading. Extend by changing arrangements within ten while preserving quantity, not by adding a new operation.
