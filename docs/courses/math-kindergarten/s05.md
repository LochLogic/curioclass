Course `math-kindergarten` (Kindergarten Math). **Days 87 to 103** (17 course days; school days 87 to 103, weeks 18 to 21). Sections: 5.1 (days 87 to 90); 5.2 (days 91 to 96); 5.3 (days 97 to 100); 5.4 (days 101 to 103). Days by kind: understanding 7, fluency 4, application 5, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How many ways can we make a number?"
**Hook, day 87.** Split six paper squares between two mats. Partners suggest another split without changing how many altogether. Ask the compelling question. Eight minutes; source in field 10.
**Supporting questions.** What stays the same when parts change, day 89? Can two different answers both fit a story, day 95? How does a full frame help find a missing part, day 97?
**Closing claim.** "I can record different pairs for a number, solve stories with two unknown parts, and find every partner of ten." The decomposition portfolio, partner sheet and story record support this on day 103.
**Teaching moves.** Model a split before recording it. Contrast different pairs without changing the total. Explain equals as the same quantity on both sides on day 91. Step back during story choices on day 95. Count shape pieces, never their unequal areas.

<!-- skel -->
- **K.OA.A.3:** Decompose numbers less than or equal to 10 into pairs in more than one way, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each decomposition by a drawing or equation (e.g., 5 = 2 + 3 and 5 = 4 + 1). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.1:** Represent addition and subtraction with objects, fingers, mental images, drawings, sounds (e.g., claps), acting out situations, verbal explanations, expressions, or equations. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.2:** Solve addition and subtraction word problems, and add and subtract within 10, e.g., by using objects or drawings to represent the problem. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.OA.A.4:** For any number from 1 to 9, find the number that makes 10 when added to the given number, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record the answer with a drawing or equation. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

**Look-fors and samples.** F1: both parts exhaust the whole, two different pairs are recorded for each positive total, and one equation has the total on the left. Meets: eight marks split into three and five, then two and six, labelled 8 = 3 + 5 and 8 = 2 + 6. Does not meet: the same three-and-five split drawn twice and described as a new pair (B). For one, accept zero and one as the one pair; for zero, require only zero and zero. Never record B for one or zero, and do not demand two different pairs where none exist.

F2: each given number is preserved, its added part completes ten, and a drawing or equation records it. Meets: seven occupied cells, three added marks, 7 + 3 = 10; "Five and two were there, three spaces were empty." Does not meet: seven given, seven added, fourteen total (D). F3: both story categories are labelled, both solutions total four, and they differ. Meets: three red and one blue, then two red and two blue; "Either could be the four stamps." Does not meet: three red and two blue called four; without a model, record an arithmetic error descriptively, with no letter. Judge each component separately; do not infer a reason from a correct numeral.

## U5. What is carried, and instruments

On days 91 and 98, movable squares beside handwritten equations fit the need to connect each part and whole. Children hold pieces and paper; the teacher holds records. No digital instrument is needed.

## U6. Days

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 89, "Why did the other part shrink?" On day 97, "Which spaces show the missing part?" Day 95 drawings need two labelled pairs, a fixed total and different category counts; judge these, not artistic detail.

## U8. Practice that returns

Daily counting retrieves cardinality. Within-five games mix joining and removal with immediate feedback; record just knows, quick strategy or counts all informally. Partner knowledge supports later fluency but is not itself a speed test.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud. Gesture part, whole and missing; offer "___ and ___ make ___." Provide tactile pieces, large pictures and directed adult movement. Extend by organizing more pairs for the same total and explaining why a pair repeats, within ten.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, keep sixty minutes through counting, model, paper work, partner talk with the adult and games; alternate adult roles. Spread the same portfolio across days and preserve feedback before scoring. One shared screen is optional; paper carries every task.

## U11. Not in this section, and still to make
