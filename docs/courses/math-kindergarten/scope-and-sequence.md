# Scope and sequence: Kindergarten Math

Course `math-kindergarten`, grade K, draft 2026-11-15.

The course has eight units and 160 daily course days. Course day and school day are the same, so `school_day` is blank in the CSV. The frame's unit allocations stay 18, 26, 19, 20, 17, 16, 15 and 22 days. The last allocation comprises 17 unit 8 days and five course-level review days. Thus the ledger has 148 unit days and 12 course-level days: four check days, three respond days and five review days. Sections are not divided at this stage; unit days use `<unit>.1`.

The CSV sets the teaching and assessment slots, without items or answer keys. The source map accounts for all 137 public first-edition base lessons: 136 retained and one cut, with ten added teaching days and seven unit assessment days. Course-level slots are separately identified as original assessment or response work. The five final base lessons occupy course-level review days. Login-gated assessments, answers and cool-downs are not used. Source links and licence information are provenance for the later day credits. All planned models are physical, with paper alternatives in `supplies`; no digital or separate drawing tool is required, so `tools` is empty. Math fills the optional `safety` column on loose-piece, pouring, shape, and paper and crayon days, and the pouring flag stays in the day note.

## Prerequisites, time and evidence

**Earlier-grade row inventory, before placing days:** none. Kindergarten has no preceding course or assumed progression row. There is no last-use grade to list and no readiness check. No prior counting is assumed. Each later unit instead has a retrieval task inside its opening day, on this year's earlier work. The day notes identify the row or model retrieved. The next grade extends counting to 120, moves from ten ones to one ten, extends fluency to ten, and develops unknowns and comparisons beyond kindergarten. This course stops written numerals at 20, comparisons of written numerals at 1 to 10, arithmetic at ten and required fluent arithmetic at five. It uses no hundred chart, number line or unknown-position equation instruction.

**Daily design:** 60 minutes including centers. Reserve five minutes for oral counting, ten for a shared investigation or explicit model, fifteen for connected tasks, twenty-five for centers and five for synthesis and daily evidence. These are planning budgets, not observed timings. From unit 4, count on from a supplied number within the known sequence. From unit 6, count by tens to 100, building ones to 100 by unit 8. Starting on day 71, five minutes within centers gives spaced addition and subtraction practice within five. Short oral tasks, counters and drawings stay connected to symbols; objects can be dropped as facts become known. There is no timed sheet or timer shown to a child.

**Observation windows:** for a planning class of thirty, reserve twenty center minutes on each named window day, with each child assigned a domain and a turn. Keep products from independent work for writing, drawing and construction; teacher time checks what the product cannot show. A closing assessment day reserves up to forty minutes for remaining children and interpretation. Response runs only in the named slots below; unit 4 uses its forty-minute closing block for ten minutes finishing evidence, ten interpreting and twenty responding, with no additional unit response on day 86. Except for unit 4, whose response is on its closing day, the next two named teaching days each use ten center minutes for response. This replaces center choices, adds no period and is counted once. Mid-unit 2 observes after instruction on days 34 to 36 and responds on days 37 and 38. Unit 2's full window has eighty center minutes before its closing day and forty minutes on it; other windows have 60 to 100 minutes before closing. Unit plans must allocate these minutes by domain and child, not ask every child to do every performance on the closing day.

Each observation or check code is a coverage obligation: retain a prompt or observed product for every part listed in that row's note. Check rows list only newly sampled codes; notes separately identify carried records. Every end day or course-level check holds at most one extended task, with short prompts or retained products for other parts. Opening retrieval is practice and is never assessment evidence. Parent K.CC.B.4 includes one word per object, cardinality and one more. Partial-range K.CC.A.3 and K.CC.B.5 work in unit 2 is explicitly completed in unit 6. Child-by-child evidence is gathered once per window. A nearby closing day or course-level check carries that record forward and gathers only missing evidence. A fresh response task checks a specific gap, not the whole battery again.

**Major-work share, one method:** minutes on major-cluster content divided by instructional minutes. Exclude Check and Respond days and rows closing mid-unit or observation assessments (days 36 and 151); also subtract the reserved observation minutes on other window days, so evidence time is not counted as instruction. Each remaining minute is counted once. The planning allocation retains thirty number minutes on each of the first five days and on sorting days 41 and 42; unit 3 retains five oral-counting minutes per teaching day; unit 7 retains forty number minutes on its first six teaching days and ten on later shape days; other teaching time is major work except the ten-minute geometry or measurement response slots on days 64, 65, 135 and 136. Those forty minutes stay in instructional time and are excluded from major work. Observation in sorting time does not consume its reserved number minutes. Embedded response on a teaching day stays instructional; whole course-level respond days are excluded.

## Unit 1. Math in our world

**Days 1 to 18, weeks 1 to 4.** Explore tools and partner routines before recognizing small groups, matching one object to a place, and tracking collections to ten. Tool choice comes before explaining how a count was kept accurate. The last collection lessons lead to the question of how we know the count, rather than rewarding a long recitation.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Days 15 to 17 gather one-to-one counting once, ten assigned children per day for two minutes each. Day 18 closes that record for K.CC.B.4a; respond on days 19 and 20 with moving or touching. MP.5 is choosing a tracking tool, and MP.6 is keeping words and objects paired; the closing record includes the child's explanation. Count objects and pictures, never classmates' bodies or features; larger pieces and boxed mats are available.

**Candidate settled:** retain the counting-and-cardinality Progressions comparison as teacher preparation, zero student days. Keep the optional collection to twenty as a five-minute choice on day 13 for children already secure, within its center time. It extends one-to-one tracking only and is neither a requirement nor an assessment of the unit 6 range.

## Unit 2. Numbers 1 to 10

**Days 19 to 44, weeks 4 to 9.** Retrieve one word per object on day 19. Establish last-number meaning and unchanged quantity under rearrangement, then compare by matching and counting. Teach the 5-frame structure, join two frames for six to ten, connect quantities to numerals and count out requested sets. The added day 35 consolidates writing and counting out, before one more, numeral comparisons and the two added sorting days 41 and 42. Five owned rows and the errors of spread-out collections, numeral meaning and ordered category counts justify the longest unit.

No readiness check. The **mid-unit assessment closes on day 36**, using observations on days 34 to 36 after day 34 instruction for count-out, last-number meaning, order and arrangement invariance, and independent writing products 0 to 10 including zero. Ten children daily receive two-minute counting turns, twenty center minutes for thirty children. Respond on days 37 and 38, ten center minutes each. The **end-of-unit window is days 40 to 44**, closing on day 44, for new evidence on K.CC.B.4c, K.CC.C.6, K.CC.C.7 and K.MD.B.3: one more, group comparisons by matching and by counting, numeral comparisons in words, and all three sorting steps. Carry forward days 34 to 36 for count-out, numeral-writing and K.CC.B.4b invariance; hear only flagged gaps again. K.CC.B.4a remains unit 1 evidence and is not a new end-window demand. Retain the day 41 sorting product; do not conference again on it. Respond on days 45 and 46. MP.3 comparison explanations and MP.7 five-and-some structure are visible in this evidence.

ELA unit 1 ends on school day 34 before sorting here; naming the categories and modelling the sort remains available within day 41. Science weather unit 2 occupies school days 23 to 44, overlapping math's work, so it keeps its own matching-and-teacher-counting bridge. Math may use weather picture groups of ten or fewer, without requiring larger monthly totals. Use classroom-object pictures and play food only, never categories about children's bodies, homes or families.

**Candidate settled:** retain the counting Progressions and frame-order comparison in teacher preparation, zero student days. Drop comparing groups to fifteen: the owned comparison limit is ten, and the two added sorting days need the available time.

The K.3.8 to K.3.12 and K.3.14 shape lessons follow on days 57 to 62 in base order; K.3.13 already opened the unit.

Keep built, drawn and composed products, plus environmental shape names, all six position words, varied turns and sizes, several attributes of one object and direct comparisons with descriptions of differences. Length and height, weight and capacity all have evidence. Respond on days 64 and 65. MP.6 precision and MP.7 shape structure are checked through explanations. Flat-shape comparisons start here, with K.G.B.4 completed and assessed with solids in unit 7.

Tie expressions and equations to quantities, with totals on either side. Both-addends-unknown work follows in unit 5 as the frame directs.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. **Days 79 to 83 gather the unit assessment**, closing on day 83: both operations within ten, joining and taking, putting together and taking apart, represented actions and spoken explanations, drawings, expressions and equations. The record includes two solved stories and an equation match for reuse at the nearby course-level check. Day 83 has the unit response; day 86 responds to the course-level check only. This is the sequence's item-level timing decision under rule 2's same-day response option, replacing the frame's general next-two-lessons convention for this unit because the course-level check intervenes. MP.4 modelling and MP.2 the meaning of quantities and symbols are assessed in explanations. K.OA.A.2 continues through the specified both-addends-unknown tasks of unit 5, checked on day 103, without changing its ownership.

Stories concern toys, animals and classroom objects, with play-food pictures if needed. No loss-of-home, hunger or family-separation story is used. Actions use objects, never children taken away from a group.

**Candidate settled:** retain the comparison against the addition-and-subtraction situation table as teacher preparation, zero student days. Drop totals to fifteen because the standard and frame limit this unit to ten.

## Shorter path

For a **55-minute period**, remove five minutes of unassessed center choice on ordinary days. On observation-window days keep the full twenty-five-minute center block and shorten independent task repetition by five minutes instead. Keep the count, explicit model, synthesis, daily evidence, all named performances and response slots. A **45-minute period plus a separate fifteen-minute same-day center block** preserves the frame's sixty-minute total; the shorter main block holds counting, model, connected work and synthesis, while the center and observation time is divided between the blocks. A schedule offering only forty-five minutes total needs repacing by the lead; this plan does not claim the full course fits it.

For a year **about ten math meetings short**, the following ten unassessed days can fold their work into the named neighbour's task and center choices. Their content remains; their separate meeting disappears. No observation-window day, check, respond day or new required part is removed.

These figures describe the sixty-minute shorter-year route; the 55-minute shorter-period route is a separate option, not an additional reduction to stack on it.

Use freed meetings to absorb interruptions before compressing assessment windows. These are baseline day references, not a replacement calendar. If the entire school year has 150 school days, the lead must coordinate all four subjects' check weeks and five-day gaps on that calendar; the stagger result below applies to the baseline 160-day plan.
