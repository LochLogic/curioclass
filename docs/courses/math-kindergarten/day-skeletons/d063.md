## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** Close the shape and measurement record and identify attribute or endpoint errors.
- **Note:** Window days 58 to 63: reuse built, drawn and composed products once; name five flat shapes across sizes and turns, describe environmental objects and all six positions. Describe several attributes of one object, then compare pairs by length, height, weight and capacity and describe differences. Respond in centers on days 64 and 65, 10 minutes each. MP.6 and MP.7: use precise comparison language and shape structure.

**Skills:**

- mk-shapes-positions (grade K): taught or practised here
- mk-build-compose-shapes (grade K): taught or practised here
- mk-measurable-attributes (grade K): taught or practised here

**Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **K.G.A.1:** Describe objects in the environment using names of shapes, and describe the relative positions of these objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, and next to.
- **K.G.A.2:** Correctly name shapes regardless of their orientations or overall size.
- **K.G.B.5:** Model shapes in the world by building shapes from components (e.g., sticks and clay balls) and drawing shapes.
- **K.G.B.6:** Compose simple shapes to form larger shapes. For example, “Can you join these two triangles with full sides touching to make a rectangle?”
- **K.MD.A.1:** Describe measurable attributes of objects, such as length or weight. Describe several measurable attributes of a single object.
- **K.MD.A.2:** Directly compare two objects with a measurable attribute in common, to see which object has “more of”/“less of” the attribute, and describe the difference. For example, directly compare the heights of two children and describe one child as taller/shorter.

> **Digital tools: None.** Paper cards, large pieces and drawings carry every task; no calculator is needed. Supply blank paper, pencils, tactile outlines and a viewing-point arrow.
