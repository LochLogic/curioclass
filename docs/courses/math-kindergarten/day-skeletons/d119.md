## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** Close the count-to-twenty and teen record and identify tracking or language errors.
- **Note:** Window days 115 to 119: write 0 to 20 and represent object counts including zero; count and count out 1 to 20, with lines, rectangular arrays and marked circles to twenty and scattered sets to ten. Start counting at a supplied number. Compose and decompose teens as ten ones and one to nine further ones, with a recorded drawing or equation. Use one product or observation per child per domain. Respond in centers on days 120 and 121, 10 minutes each. MP.6 and MP.7: connect each teen word and written form to ten ones and more. Data: authored section blocks control timing; exit: half. Read all print aloud. Everyone marks paper; six rotating 45-second oral samples, three per panel when two. Formation and unheard words recorded separately. Original masters separate from practice; familiar games untimed. Final window: 28 center minutes days 115 to 118; assigned roster eight/eight/eight/six, four 45-second asks each. Paper 16/16/8/0 minutes. Feedback after class; revise next day before judgment. Day 119 revision precedes ten-minute closeout.

**Skills:**

- mk-count-to-20 (grade K): taught or practised here
- mk-teen-ten-ones (grade K): taught or practised here
- mk-count-on-from (grade K): taught or practised here

**Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **K.CC.A.3:** Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- **K.CC.B.5:** Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- **K.NBT.A.1:** Compose and decompose numbers from 11 to 19 into ten ones and some further ones, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each composition or decomposition by a drawing or equation (e.g., 18 = 10 + 8); understand that these numbers are composed of ten ones and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones.
- **K.CC.A.2:** Count forward beginning from a given number within the known sequence (instead of having to begin at 1).
