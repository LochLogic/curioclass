## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** Describe solid shapes and positions in the classroom.
- **Note:** Base K.7.13. Classroom supplies are the default; any recycled packaging is optional and clean. No family contribution required. Daily oral count by tens to 100, with ones practice. Five-minute spaced addition and subtraction game within five, untimed; objects may be dropped as facts become known. Unit 7 final window: keep products and gather assigned feature-comparison observations once, 20 minutes in centers. MP.3 extension: a ten-minute partner solid-guessing center uses faces and other known features; its explanation can stand for this child observation. Data: authored sixty-minute budget; exit: half. Extra paper items on 125/130 are independent student-page Exit boxes. Read all print aloud. Shared counting/game rows reused. Six 45-second exit samples, one ask each; unheard language not inferred. E122 questions reach all thirty by day 127. Final-window paper 130/131, roster 6/8/8/8 conferences on 130 to 133; feedback and later revision before 134 closeout.

**Skills:**

- mk-analyze-shapes (grade K): taught or practised here

**Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **K.G.A.3:** Identify shapes as two-dimensional (lying in a plane, “flat”) or three-dimensional (“solid”).
- **K.G.B.4:** Analyze and compare two- and three-dimensional shapes, in different sizes and orientations, using informal language to describe their similarities, differences, parts (e.g., number of sides and vertices/“corners”) and other attributes (e.g., having sides of equal length).
- **K.G.A.1:** Describe objects in the environment using names of shapes, and describe the relative positions of these objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, and next to.

> **Digital tools: None.** Paper shapes, drawn outlines, pencils, frames and large paper counters cover all tasks. Inspect packaging for staples, sharp edges and residues; no glass or metal cans (general early-grade reference, field 10; the packaging and clay rules are ours, from memory). Check clay ingredients against known allergies or use paper joins. Supervise handling, stop mouthing or throwing, and keep builds low on tables. Paper drawings record builds; teacher models provide solid features when a drawing alone cannot.
