- **Note:** Base K.7.1. Retrieval task: unit 3 flat-shape composition and unit 4 equation meanings. Unit 6 response in centers. Daily oral count by tens to 100, with ones practice. **Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **K.G.B.5:** Model shapes in the world by building shapes from components (e.g., sticks and clay balls) and drawing shapes.
- **K.G.B.6:** Compose simple shapes to form larger shapes. For example, “Can you join these two triangles with full sides touching to make a rectangle?”
- **K.CC.A.3:** Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- **K.CC.B.5:** Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.

>
> **Digital tools: None.** Paper shapes, drawn outlines, pencils, frames and large paper counters cover all tasks. Provide clean boxes and tubes, paper balls and cones, blunt paper rods and soft paper joins; classroom supplies are the default. Inspect packaging for staples, sharp edges and residues; no glass or metal cans (general early-grade reference, field 10; the packaging and clay rules are ours, from memory). Check clay ingredients against known allergies or use paper joins. Supervise handling, stop mouthing or throwing, and keep builds low on tables. Paper drawings record builds; teacher models provide solid features when a drawing alone cannot.
