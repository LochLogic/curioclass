## 1. Standards and practices


- **This section's share.** "pairing each object with one and only one number name": verify noticed groups and connect an object to a recorded mark.

- **Limits and strategies.** Recognition uses small groups to four, without speed demands. A mark need not resemble its object. No numeral or invariance claim; no algorithm.

- **Connections and adult explanation.** No other grade standard is claimed. Distinguish ordered words, object pairing and tracking from a total; a correct total can conceal an omission and a repeat.

### E6, day 6

Picture/material: Three equal-sized cards show one, two and four leaves, all clearly separated, with order varied across copies.

Read aloud: "Mark the card with two leaves."

Answer and look-fors: Marks the two-leaf card. A spoken checking count is heard only from the rotating six.

Wrong examples: Touches only two of the four leaves and selects that card (A); touches the single leaf twice and selects it (B). An unexplained selection remains descriptive.

Shape: Selects a target quantity among unseen leaf cards. No leaf cards or target-card selection in practice; children name a briefly shown group then verify it with loose objects.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

### E7, day 7

Picture/material: An original art-corner picture shows three separate open trays: three paint pots, one large brush and five flat scraps. All items are visible; no count labels or answer rings.

Read aloud: "Find the tray with three things in this art corner."

Answer and look-fors: Rings the whole paint-pot tray and leaves a count-dot on each pot; a heard count is one, two, three. No other whole tray contains three; selecting three scraps from a five-scrap tray does not identify the whole tray asked for.

Wrong examples: Counts just three of the five scraps and ignores the rest (A); revisits the brush while counting it as three objects (B).

Shape: Finds and records a target group in an unseen art-corner scene. No art supplies or target-finding picture task in practice; the room hunt finds any group without a specified target quantity.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

### E8, day 8

Picture/material: A card shows four stars in a crooked column. Three button groups contain two, four and eight buttons in different arrangements; none matches the star layout.

Read aloud: "Join the star card to the button group that matches."

Answer and look-fors: One line joins the star card to the four-button group; quantity, not layout or object size, determines the match.

Wrong examples: Touches each of two buttons twice and chooses that group (B); skips four buttons in the eight-button group and chooses it (A). An unexplained wrong line remains descriptive.

Shape: Matches a symbol card to an object group on paper. No card-to-group matching, stars or buttons in practice; build a second loose-object collection beside a partner collection and check physical pairs.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

### E9, day 9

Picture/material: Three seashell pictures in an open triangle, with a blank record panel on the reverse.

Read aloud: "Draw one mark for each shell on the back of this paper."

Answer and look-fors: Three distinct marks, each corresponding to a different shell; artistic likeness is unnecessary. Children may flip the paper freely; this is not a memory task.

Wrong examples: Leaves one shell without a mark (A); records one shell a second time (B).

Shape: Creates a separate record without printed links. No shells or separate-panel records in practice; picture-book pages put adjacent marks and visible links beside drawings.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Retain touching/moving. Day 9 connects concrete objects to adjacent marks with visible links; numeral connections wait for unit 2. Keep records for day 16.

- **Key moment and verdict.** Day 9 needs visible object-to-mark correspondence. Ideal: loose pieces beside paper and linking strokes. Paper fits; no device, digital spec or key tool.

- **A room with nothing.** Large cut-paper squares replace counters; paper strips replace cubes; drawn/cut shapes replace pattern blocks; hand-drawn boxes replace mats and the 5-frame. Clean boxes, tubes or folded paper solids replace solid models. A folded-paper puppet replaces a toy. Exit objects are drawn or cut paper, never requested home supplies; a pencil replaces crayons for marks. Keep large pieces on trays, with no food, sharp parts or swallowable pieces.

## 6. Days

Digital tool: None on every day. Children hold objects and paper; teacher holds cues and record. Parts include distribution and transitions. Fifteen pairs work simultaneously: two 45-second turns per partner take three minutes, leaving the rest for thinking, handling and checking. Never hear thirty serial public turns. Everyone returns practice paper or a matching layout; teacher adds a short next-step mark after class, about fifteen minutes for thirty papers. Hand-backs go to the next Return and repair with a new practice case, never an exit as a worked example.

Return and repair holds one five-minute action chosen from field 4. If more than ten of thirty share it, model together; otherwise meet one small group while others revisit familiar loose-object tasks. Return all papers with feedback and name the next attempt. For several errors, address the earliest obstructing action first; carry unresolved letters to later repair slots rather than stacking five-minute repairs. Never take repair time from exits or observations.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Questions.** Day 6: "What did you notice before touching?" Day 8: "How could we check these groups?" Day 9: "Which object goes with this mark?"

- **Paper practice.** Day 9, make a picture-book page of five classroom blocks with one adjacent linked mark per block. Look for five distinct links; the partner checks each without scoring.

- **Turns.** One question at a time; pairs answer simultaneously. Two public volunteers at most per share; children may pass. Every child returns paper.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 6 returns to u01-1 tool choice; day 7 to noticing then checking; day 8 recalls any group from the hunt; day 9 returns to loose-object matches.

- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Return to accurate coordination through spaced practice and feedback, without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **Language.** group, match, mark; "This mark goes with this object."

- **Access and exits.** Enlarge and space pictures; offer tactile outlines or paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without giving counting words, recording help. Face children and use visual turn cues. A child unable to dot directs adult paper marking. No independent reading: read every stem and label aloud. Repeat directions without demonstrating the reserved solution.

- **Extension.** Change route or arrangement within ten, explaining how each object is reached once; no operations or numeral writing.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use 25 minutes: supported count 3, demonstrate 5, practice 8, return and repair 4, exit 5. Hear that child instead of rotating six. Shorten repeated center choices, keeping the demonstration and same new exit.

- **One projector.** Optional display only; all children use their own paper pieces. Draw on a board as the fallback. No device turns or online text is required.
