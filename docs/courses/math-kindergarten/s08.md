## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What can we count, add and make now?"
**Hook, day 135.** Offer mixed classroom counters and picture cards. Ask, "How could we organize these so someone else knows how many?" Gather suggestions for eight minutes, then ask the compelling question. Source: field 10.
**Supporting questions.** How does one extra object change a count, day 138? What makes a number book convincing, day 141? Can a picture tell our story, day 145? What repeats when we count, day 146?
**Closing claim.** "I can show counts, represent a school story, explain counting patterns and use known parts within five." Number books, days 144 and 145 story records, and separate counting and fact records support the day 151 reflection.
**Teaching moves.** Model tracking before comparing. Step back as children choose a classroom question; ask them to test whether their representation answers it. On day 146 explicitly model decade transitions. Fluency days rehearse established content.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 141, "Where are all ten ones?" On day 145, "What did you change so the picture tells the story?" Judge book pages by matching count, numeral and teen parts; judge stories by a model matching the action and question.

## U8. Practice that returns

Daily oral counting and mixed within-five games retrieve earlier units. Rotate starting numbers within twenty; practice ones and tens to one hundred. Fluency days consolidate familiar relationships, with counters optional and no speed ranking. Counting objects by group returns in the day 140 book pages and the day 143 choice of what to count; K.MD.B.3 has no later course day.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read all directions aloud; no independent reading is required. Teach more, fewer, equal, part, whole and ten ones with gestures: "I know ___ because ___." Accept home-language reasoning and usual communication methods. Offer large tactile pieces and adult movement at the child's direction. Seated picture trays replace a hunt; symbols replace color cues. Extend by finding two different representations for the same count or story, within the same limits.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, the adult is the partner and keeps the same paper records. For fifty-five minutes, remove five unassessed center-choice minutes on ordinary days. In observation windows, keep all twenty-five center minutes and remove five minutes of independent task repetition. Preserve counting, the model, exit evidence, observations and response. Shorter total schedules need lead repacing. Keep later-day feedback and revision. At most one shared device is optional.
