## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can ten help us count and show bigger numbers?"
**Hook, day 104.** First show the full frame and ask how many places are filled (field 4). Then show twelve paper squares in a line beside it, ask how the frame might help count the larger collection, then ask the compelling question. Eight minutes; source in field 10.
**Supporting questions.** Does moving objects change how many, day 107? How do we hear ten in a teen name, day 110? How can a drawing and equation show the same parts, day 114?
**Closing claim.** "I can count and write to twenty, start counting at a supplied number, and show teens as ten ones and more." The numeral sheet, counting record and teen record support this on day 119.
**Teaching moves.** Model touching once, tracking rows and marking a circle's start on day 104. Show count-out before day 105 rehearses it; teach numeral forms through twenty across the understanding days. Model ten ones before symbols; step back for independent composition on day 111. Keep every one visible.

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.A.3:** Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.CC.B.5:** Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.CC.A.2:** Count forward beginning from a given number within the known sequence (instead of having to begin at 1). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **K.NBT.A.1:** Compose and decompose numbers from 11 to 19 into ten ones and some further ones, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each composition or decomposition by a drawing or equation (e.g., 18 = 10 + 8); understand that these numbers are composed of ten ones and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 107, "What changed when the squares moved?" On day 110, "Where are ten ones in twelve?" Day 114's written task needs ten visible ones, the correct further part and an equation naming the same whole; artistic detail is irrelevant.

## U8. Practice that returns

Daily counting retrieves cardinality and supplied starts. Within-five games mix joining and removal with feedback; informally note just knows, quick strategy or counts all. Keep this arithmetic record separate from counting evidence.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud. Gesture ten ones and further ones; offer "___ is ten ones and ___ more ones." Allow home-language reasoning before restating English names. Provide large tactile pieces, spaced pictures and directed adult movement. Extend by explaining two tracking paths for the same arranged collection, within twenty.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, alternate adult and child counting, keep paper products and hear each domain once across the same window. Preserve written feedback and later revision before scoring. One shared screen is optional; paper carries every task.
