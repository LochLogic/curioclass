# Section 2.4: Connect quantities and numerals, including zero

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-kindergarten/2/s/2.4), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-kindergarten u02-4.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


**Share.** K.CC.A.3: "Write" and "Represent" quantities with numerals, 0 to 10 here, including zero. Day 30 explicitly connects all eleven forms to quantities with guided formation before independent labels. Day 31 keeps the numeral when objects move. K.CC.B.5's "given a number ... count out that many objects" is within ten, day 32; day 33 rehearses drawing spoken/printed requests. K.CC.B.4/B.4b connect totals and invariance.

**Strategy and connection.** Track pieces, retain the requested stop, label the result. No algorithm/operation symbols. Review u02-3 five-and-some; teach none/zero on day 30, not new on fluency day 33. MP.2, day 32: numeral to quantity. MP.7, day 31: amount/label across arrangements. Teacher explanation separates words, quantities and shapes; recognizable reversals receive formation feedback, not an automatic M label.

## 3. Checks, written first


Live exits below supersede older U3: teacher judged, path paper, invented for practice and read aloud. Eight minutes allows launch 1, concurrent paper 2, six named oral turns of 45 seconds 4.5, collection 0.5. Hear a count OR reason, not both in a turn. Everyone returns marked paper. Record names heard; paper does not prove unheard counting or reasoning. Use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen by component. Full keys below are shown samples; each first observed wrong action is a not yet seen sample. Record help. Diagnose letters from observed actions or heard reasons, never wrong marks alone. Follow-ups are separate single asks to six only.

Goal-map cases control over older U3. Reserve complete cases, layouts and response formats. Literal exclusion of every assessment integer is impossible with M3's 0 to 10; changing numbers alone does not make transfer. Never use reserved art in practice.

**Unit assessment owned:** M2 and M3, with u02-5 supplying later picture/numeral products. No mid-unit collection here. See u02-1 field 3 for the longer M1/M2 observation allocation requiring subsequent alignment.

**E30, K.CC.A.3; K.CC.B.4b.** Six button drawings scattered above all numeral cards 0 to 10 in mixed order and equal print size. Read, "Ring the numeral for this group." Key/look-fors: only 6 ringed; six counted. Heard follow-up: "How many buttons altogether?" Answer: six. Wrong: says six correctly but rings 3, a numeral-quantity link error (M). Other wrong choices are descriptive until an observed action supports a letter.

Shape: Recognizes the symbol for an unseen collection among all eleven forms. No buttons, six-button case or group-to-many-numerals sheet in practice; work the reverse direction, numeral to quantity picture or constructed group.

**E31, K.CC.B.4b; K.CC.A.3.** Four disk drawings in a row above a blank mat and blank number-label space. Read, "Draw this group another way with its number label." Key/look-fors: four disk marks in a visibly different arrangement, label 4. Heard follow-up: "What explains your number label?" Shown: "Still four; none came or went." Wrong: new arrangement called five because it is wider (B); correctly counts four but labels 2 (M).

Shape: Produces one rearranged quantity-and-label picture. No four disks or independent redraw-and-label sheet in teaching; physically move five/eight squares while retaining a supplied label, then check an adult diagram.

**E32, K.CC.B.5; K.CC.A.3.** Ten large button outlines available beside a blank order mat/label space; printed request 8 read aloud. The uncounted supply is not a counting task; the requested destination stays within ten. Read, "Make the eight-button order with its number label." Child marks eight selected outlines and places/draws corresponding large paper pieces on the mat, writing 8 in its label. Key/look-fors: eight selected, stop at eight, label 8. Heard follow-up: "Where did you stop?" Answer: eight. Wrong: passes eight and moves all ten (D); selects eight but labels 5 (M). Use large paper buttons only.

Shape: Independently produces a requested subset from surplus pieces with its label. No button orders, eight-from-ten task or independent full-order mat in practice; complete adult-started square groups under a supplied label instead.

**E33, K.CC.B.5; K.CC.A.3.** Original two-panel blank drawing order, numeral labels 5 and 0 read aloud as five and zero, none. Read, "Draw the groups for this order slip." Key/look-fors: five marks in the 5 panel, no group marks in the 0 panel; familiar finish corner marked for each, confirming intentional completion. No tracing guides or numeral-writing demand. Heard follow-up: "What does your empty group show?" Shown: "Zero; no pieces were asked for." Wrong: one mark in the zero group (O); continues beyond five after losing the stop (D).

Shape: Produces a paired positive/empty request record, one integrated drawing task. No five-and-zero paired order or blank order slip in practice; link individual numeral traces to supplied sketches, then repair one inaccurate sketch. The teacher reads labels, not two extra commands.

**M2 cold key and reservation.** Seven moved from ten, then stop; picture totals 6, 8, 10, 9. Moving all ten is D; omission is K; revisit is L.

Shape: Independent seven-of-ten tab count-out plus four-layout picture routes, six-star line, eight-star array in two rows of four, ten-star circle, nine-star scatter. No tab request, star cases or complete four-layout sheet in teaching; finish five/eight square groups with feedback.

**M3 cold key and reservation.** Collections zero through ten each take their matching numeral, including 0 for empty. Incorrect 1 for empty is E; a correctly counted eight labeled 3 is M.

Shape: Independent eleven-box count-and-write sheet; four star boxes also supply M2 and their minutes are not counted twice. No eleven-box mixed sheet, stars or independent all-range check in practice. Use isolated guided formation/matching. Separate physical formation support from numeral understanding. The inherited M2/M3 multi-ask scripts must be split into separately read prompts by the data/day writer without losing components.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas


One puppet demonstrates one practice mistake at a time, never a child's work. If more than ten of thirty share a letter, the next five-minute Return and repair models one correction, lets pairs retry simultaneously on a fresh practice case and collects everyone's corrected paper. Otherwise meet one group while others correct familiar work. Choose the earliest blocking action; queue other letters across later slots. Never stack five-minute repairs or use reserved art.

E, day 30: empty box labeled 1 versus 0. M, day 30: correctly counted five labeled 2 versus 5. B, day 31: moved eight called ten versus eight throughout. D, day 32: ten moved for five versus stop at five. O, day 33: one dot for zero versus intentional empty group. Fresh square practice cases only.

Repair E/O by clearing a group and connecting none/0; M by count-match-cover-write; B by checking unchanged membership; D by keeping the request visible and stopping there. Day 34 receives one five-minute repair, not five simultaneous repairs. No letter is diagnosed from a reversal alone.

## 6. Days

All tools: None. Children hold paper and pieces; teacher keeps dated records and names heard. Fifteen pairs work concurrently: one 45-second turn per partner takes 1.5 minutes per round, leaving handling and feedback time inside the blocks. Two public volunteers at most; anyone may pass. Every child returns paper. Label all live cases invented for practice and say this aloud. No personal reporting. Teach a finish tick in the corner during the first practice record, before any exit uses it; it records intentional completion, never the answer.

Return and repair gives back the previous paper with feedback and holds one five-minute action from field 4. Collect a fresh correction and keep the original. No reserved exit/check art is used. Day 19 separately budgets unit 1 response and retrieval repair.

## 9. Supports and extension

Language: numeral, zero, none; "This numeral names ___ pieces." Wide pencils, enlarged/tactile numerals and directed piece movement preserve access. Keep physical numeral-formation support separate from quantity understanding.

Enlarge and space pictures; use high contrast or tactile paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without supplying quantity or reason; record help. A scribe records reasons verbatim, never chooses a numeral for a writing check. Fingers are optional. Read all print aloud; no independent reading. Extend by changing arrangements within ten while preserving quantity, not by adding a new operation.
