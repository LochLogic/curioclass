# Section 2.4: Connect quantities and numerals, including zero

<!-- skel -->

Unit 2, Numbers 1 to 10. *How can we show how many, and which has more?* (days 19 to 44): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 30 to 33.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (30, 31, 32), fluency 1 (33). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.B.4** (days 30, 31, 33): Understand the relationship between numbers and quantities; connect counting to cardinality.
- **K.CC.B.4b** (days 30, 31, 32, 33): Understand that the last number name said tells the number of objects counted. The number of objects is the same regardless of their arrangement or the order in which they were counted.
- **K.CC.B.5** (days 30, 31, 32, 33): Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- **K.CC.A.3** (days 30, 31, 32, 33): Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- skill mk-how-many (grade K)

## 9. Supports and extension

Language: numeral, zero, none; "This numeral names ___ pieces." Wide pencils, enlarged/tactile numerals and directed piece movement preserve access. Keep physical numeral-formation support separate from quantity understanding.

Enlarge and space pictures; use high contrast or tactile paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without supplying quantity or reason; record help. A scribe records reasons verbatim, never chooses a numeral for a writing check. Fingers are optional. Read all print aloud; no independent reading. Extend by changing arrangements within ten while preserving quantity, not by adding a new operation.
