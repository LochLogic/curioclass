# Section 1.3: Match one object to every place

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-kindergarten/1/s/1.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-kindergarten u01-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Kindergarten Math, course `math-kindergarten`. Unit 1, Math in our world: see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 10 to 11.** Days by kind: understanding 1, application 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **K.CC.B.4a, this section's share.** "each number name with one and only one object": connect careful pairing to matching one item per place, retaining spoken object counts.

- **Limits and strategies.** Enough means a place has its partner; no formal more/fewer language or numerals. Model oral counting through ten by day 11. No algorithm.

- **Practices.** MP.6, day 10, check each object-place pair; MP.5, day 11, use a place mat to check fetching.

- **Connections and adult explanation.** No other grade standard is claimed. Distinguish ordered words, object pairing and tracking from a total; a correct total can conceal an omission and a repeat.

### E10, day 10: [[s01-X10]]

Picture/material: Four hat pictures spread widely across a sheet and three movable paper cups crowded at its bottom; all are visible.

Read aloud: "Show whether every hat can have a cup."

Answer and look-fors: Matches three cups to different hats and marks the remaining hat as needing a cup. This is one matched-picture response, not an added yes/no item; any hat may be left.

Wrong examples: Marks every hat supplied because the crowded cups look like many (E); counts the same cup twice while pairing (B).

Shape: Shows sufficiency through an unmatched location in an unseen hat-and-cup picture. No hats, cups or enough-choice pictures in practice; build complete counter-place pairs and restore a removed match.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

### E11, day 11: [[s01-X11]]

Picture/material: Four plate drawings in a curved row and eight large paper napkin pieces, without number labels.

Read aloud: "Get one napkin for each plate."

Answer and look-fors: Selects four napkins, places one beside each plate and dots each used napkin. Four unused pieces remain; no numeral writing.

Wrong examples: Takes all eight because the pile looks big enough (E); counts one plate twice and supplies two napkins there (B).

Shape: Selects just enough from an excess supply for a complete place setting. No napkins, plates or whole-set fetching from an excess pool in practice; carry one item per trip to separate floor places.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Separate objects and places use a link or adjacency to show each pair. Keep counting words audible; no equals sign or written number. Place mats remain optional on day 12.

- **Key moment and verdict.** Day 11 needs visible places and a supply that can be revisited. Ideal: paper places and large movable pieces. Paper fits without equipment or a digital spec; no key tool.

- **A room with nothing.** Large cut-paper squares replace counters; paper strips replace cubes; drawn/cut shapes replace pattern blocks; hand-drawn boxes replace mats and the 5-frame. Clean boxes, tubes or folded paper solids replace solid models. A folded-paper puppet replaces a toy. Exit objects are drawn or cut paper, never requested home supplies; a pencil replaces crayons for marks. Keep large pieces on trays, with no food, sharp parts or swallowable pieces.

## 6. Days

Digital tool: None every day; no key tool. Children hold pieces and paper; the teacher keeps cues and records. All quantities and pictures are invented for practice; print that label with any practice scene and say it aloud. One folded-paper puppet is the sole actor in each demonstration. Parts include materials, transitions and table-tray cleanup.

Use Partner turns: fifteen pairs work at once. Two 45-second turns each take three minutes per pair, never thirty serial turns. Hear only the named six at an exit; other public shares hear at most two volunteers, 90 seconds total. Children may pass. During guided pairing on days 2 to 5, hear up to six previously unheard children, one 45-second counting ask each (4.5 minutes within the ten), while other pairs continue. Day 6 Familiar centers provides the same 4.5-minute listening reserve if needed.

Collect every practice record and exit. Allow fifteen teacher minutes after class for short private feedback on thirty papers, circling the action to revisit and adding a teacher note; children need not read the note. Next day's Return and repair holds one minute to distribute marked papers and read the shared feedback direction aloud, one minute for a selected wrong/right model, two minutes for simultaneous fresh-case retry, and one minute to collect retries. If more than ten of thirty need it, model together; otherwise meet one small group while others retry familiar work. Choose the earliest obstructing action, log remaining errors for later slots, and do not stack repairs. Check retries after class in the same feedback allowance. Day 17 papers wait until day 19 Counting centers; day 18 classifies records, without reteaching.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Questions.** Day 10: "Which place still needs its partner?" Day 11: "How can you check that every place has one?"

- **Paper practice.** Day 10, draw five paired places and pieces with a link in each completed pair. Look for one piece per place, rather than group size in the picture.

- **Turns.** One question at a time; pairs answer simultaneously. Two public volunteers at most per share; children may pass. Every child returns paper.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 10 returns to u01-2 linked marks. Day 11 returns to matching, with choral number words supported through ten.

- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Return to accurate coordination through spaced practice and feedback, without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **Language.** Place: a spot for one thing. Enough: every place can have one thing; unused supply is allowed. Teach on day 10 with completed physical pairs. Review match from u01-2. Frame: "Every place has one."

- **Access and exits.** Enlarge and space pictures; offer tactile outlines or paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without giving counting words, recording help. Face children and use visual turn cues. A child unable to dot directs adult paper marking. No independent reading: read every stem and label aloud. Repeat directions without demonstrating the reserved solution.

- **Extension.** Change route or arrangement within ten, explaining how each object is reached once; no operations or numeral writing.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use 25 minutes: supported count 3, demonstrate 5, practice 8, return and repair 4, exit 5. Hear that child instead of rotating six. Shorten repeated center choices, keeping the demonstration and same new exit.

- **One projector.** Optional display only; all children use their own paper pieces. Draw on a board as the fallback. No device turns or online text is required.
