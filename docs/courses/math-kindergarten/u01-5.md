## 1. Standards and practices

- **K.CC.B.4a, this section's share.** "each object with one and only one number name and each number name with one and only one object": retain representations and verify ordered counting from observations.

- **Limits and strategies.** No new notation or algorithm. Marks do not prove a spoken sequence. Keep every sculpture piece visible. Last-number statements remain descriptive.

- **Practices.** MP.5, day 17, choose a support for sculpture pieces; MP.6, days 16 to 18, inspect observed word-object pairing.

- **Connections and adult explanation.** No other grade standard is claimed. Distinguish ordered words, object pairing and tracking from a total; a correct total can conceal an omission and a repeat.

### E16, day 16: [[s01-X16]]

Picture/material: Six toy-animal drawings in an uneven column beside an empty drawing panel, no outlines or correspondence lines.

Read aloud: "Draw one mark for each toy animal."

Answer and look-fors: Six distinct marks, one per animal. Judge correspondence, not likeness or a numeral; do not infer spoken sequence from marks.

Wrong examples: Leaves one animal unrepresented (A); records an animal a second time (B).

Shape: Records an unseen animal collection in a separate panel. No animals, six-object teaching sets or blank-panel records in practice; revisit adjacent ticks with visible links to loose objects.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

### E17, day 17: [[s01-X17]]

Picture/material: Eight large paper block pieces beside a blank vertical building area; actual cubes are optional, with paper pieces kept as the record.

Read aloud: "Build a stack with one counting word for each block you add."

Answer and look-fors: Eight pieces added once each, saying one through eight; dot each placed piece. A flat paper stack counts. Final word eight; no separate total question.

Wrong examples: Leaves one piece unused while counting (A); adds one piece while saying two words (D).

Shape: Builds a vertical stack by sequential counted placement. No stacks, eight-piece teaching sets or adding-count stack practice; count visible parts of a completed low, wide sculpture.

Meets: the complete correspondence described in the answer. Does not: the first observed wrong action above. Retain the paper and any heard sequence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Retain chosen tracking supports. Day 16 returns to day 9 object-to-mark links; day 17 to day 4 constructions. Keep initial performance, feedback and revision separate; close earlier records on day 18.

- **Key moment and verdict.** Day 17 needs all sculpture pieces visible. Ideal: a low construction or flat paper strips. Paper partly fits a solid sculpture because depth is absent, but fits counting pieces. No digital tool or key tool.

- **A room with nothing.** Large cut-paper squares replace counters; paper strips replace cubes; drawn/cut shapes replace pattern blocks; hand-drawn boxes replace mats and the 5-frame. Clean boxes, tubes or folded paper solids replace solid models. A folded-paper puppet replaces a toy. Exit objects are drawn or cut paper, never requested home supplies; a pencil replaces crayons for marks. Keep large pieces on trays, with no food, sharp parts or swallowable pieces.

## Day 18

**Check.** My counting record shows what I can do.

- **10 min. Familiar centers.** Children use already taught paper/object tasks. Teacher checks the missing-name list; no new teaching or reteaching.
- **40 min. Missing observations.** The s01-A1a to s01-A1c conference only for unheard children: at three minutes each, at most thirteen children plus a minute for materials. ## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 16 returns to u01-4 tracking and u01-2 linked marks; day 17 to u01-1 constructions and chosen routes. Day 18 offers familiar centers only, with no new teaching or repair.

- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Return to accurate coordination through spaced practice and feedback, without a timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **Language.** Represent: make a mark stand for something, a name for the day 9 action. Sculpture: a construction we make, recalling day 4. Stack: pieces arranged one above another; name a completed arrangement before E17 without demonstrating counted stacking. Review counted, uncounted and boundary from u01-4. Frame: "My mark stands for this object."

- **Access and exits.** Enlarge and space pictures; offer tactile outlines or paper pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without giving counting words, recording help. Face children and use visual turn cues. A child unable to dot directs adult paper marking. No independent reading: read every stem and label aloud. Repeat directions without demonstrating the reserved solution.

- **Extension.** Change route or arrangement within ten, explaining how each object is reached once; no operations or numeral writing.
