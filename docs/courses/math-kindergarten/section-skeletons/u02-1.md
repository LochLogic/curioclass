## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.B.4** (days 19, 20): Understand the relationship between numbers and quantities; connect counting to cardinality.
- **K.CC.B.4b** (days 19, 20): Understand that the last number name said tells the number of objects counted. The number of objects is the same regardless of their arrangement or the order in which they were counted.
- **K.CC.B.5** (days 19, 20): Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- **K.CC.A.3** (days 19, 20): Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- skill mk-how-many (grade K)
- skill mk-one-to-one (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does moving change how many, day 20? How can matching settle a comparison, day 24? How does five help, day 28? What does a numeral tell us, day 30? How can counts order categories, day 42?
> **Teaching moves.** Model moving without adding on day 20, matching on day 21 and counting another way on day 25. Use dot records and spoken totals before tying collections to numerals on day 30. Step back during day 39 comparisons. Model each sorting step on day 41. Teacher pages distinguish counting words, a quantity and its written numeral.
>
> - **~K.CC.B.5:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read everything aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair gestures with more, fewer, same and category. Rehearse "This has more because ___." Use large high-contrast pictures or tactile pieces, a counter mat instead of fingers, and directed adult movement. Keep numeral writing observable. Extend by finding two arrangements that mislead sight but preserve the comparison, within ten.
