## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.C.6** (days 21, 22, 23, 24): Identify whether the number of objects in one group is greater than, less than, or equal to the number of objects in another group, e.g., by using matching and counting strategies.
- skill mk-compare-groups (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does moving change how many, day 20? How can matching settle a comparison, day 24? How does five help, day 28? What does a numeral tell us, day 30? How can counts order categories, day 42?
> **Teaching moves.** Model moving without adding on day 20, matching on day 21 and counting another way on day 25. Use dot records and spoken totals before tying collections to numerals on day 30. Step back during day 39 comparisons. Model each sorting step on day 41. Teacher pages distinguish counting words, a quantity and its written numeral.
>
> **Practices.** MP.3: day 24, partners compare five blocks with eight, justify by matching and counting, and question a spacing-based claim. MP.7: day 28, circle the full five within eight on joined frames and use the remaining three to explain why eight exceeds five.
> **Rigor.** No owned standard requires arithmetic fluency. Keep fifteen understanding, six fluency and four application days: 60, 24 and 16 percent of teaching days. Fluency rehearses taught counting, drawing and comparison through untimed partner games, with explanation and feedback; no new content enters those days.
> - **Focus.** One of one units and 23 of 25 teaching lessons have primary major-work goals. Excluding days 36 and 44 and six twenty-minute observation blocks leaves 1,320 instructional minutes. On days 41 and 42, thirty minutes count and compare, ten classify, and twenty observe. Other instructional minutes count or compare: 1,300 of 1,320, or 98.5 percent, are major work. Sorting supports cardinality and comparison.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read everything aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair gestures with more, fewer, same and category. Rehearse "This has more because ___." Use large high-contrast pictures or tactile pieces, a counter mat instead of fingers, and directed adult movement. Keep numeral writing observable. Extend by finding two arrangements that mislead sight but preserve the comparison, within ten.
<!-- /skel -->
