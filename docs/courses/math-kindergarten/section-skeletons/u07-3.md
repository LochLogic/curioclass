## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.G.A.3** (days 126, 127, 128, 129): Identify shapes as two-dimensional (lying in a plane, “flat”) or three-dimensional (“solid”).
- **K.G.B.4** (days 126, 127, 128, 129): Analyze and compare two- and three-dimensional shapes, in different sizes and orientations, using informal language to describe their similarities, differences, parts (e.g., number of sides and vertices/“corners”) and other attributes (e.g., having sides of equal length).
- **K.G.A.1** (days 127): Describe objects in the environment using names of shapes, and describe the relative positions of these objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, and next to.
- **K.G.A.2** (days 127, 128): Correctly name shapes regardless of their orientations or overall size.
- skill mk-analyze-shapes (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Can equal outlines use different numbers of pieces, day 121? How can pieces tell an equation story, day 123? Is a cube a square, day 127? Which features survive a turn, day 128?
> **Teaching moves.** Connect pieces to counts before equations. On day 126 model the distinction between an object and its drawing; on day 127 trace a cube's square face without calling the whole cube a square. Step back for feature-based sorting on day 128 and model choices on day 133.
>
> **Practices.** MP.3: day 128, defend a sort by flat faces, then challenge a color-based rule. Day 130's optional guessing center reuses that explanation. MP.7: day 127, trace a cube face and connect its four equal sides to a square; day 131 uses faces to plan a stable build.
> **Carried.** Keep unit 6's optional frames for counting and partners of ten; day 120 links counted pieces to numerals, day 123 to equations, and day 125 to ten-part drawings. Withdraw supplied frames on day 126, retaining choice. Solids remain available for geometry because their features are the content.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read everything aloud. Teach flat, solid, face, side, corner and position words with gestures: "Both have ___; this one has ___." Accept home-language reasoning before restating English names. Use tactile outlines, large stable pieces and adult movement at the child's direction. Extend by proposing and testing two different feature-based sorts of the same objects.
