## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.CC.B.4** (days 30, 31, 33): Understand the relationship between numbers and quantities; connect counting to cardinality.
- **K.CC.B.4b** (days 30, 31, 32, 33): Understand that the last number name said tells the number of objects counted. The number of objects is the same regardless of their arrangement or the order in which they were counted.
- **K.CC.B.5** (days 30, 31, 32, 33): Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- **K.CC.A.3** (days 30, 31, 32, 33): Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- skill mk-how-many (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does moving change how many, day 20? How can matching settle a comparison, day 24? How does five help, day 28? What does a numeral tell us, day 30? How can counts order categories, day 42?
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 1 tracking mats. Teach the 5-frame structure on day 26 and join two that day, as five and some more, before day 27 rehearsal. Name one more in the oral count on days 38 and 40. Tie frames to written numerals on day 30; vary arrangements on day 31. Withdraw supplied frames for numeral comparisons on day 40, retaining optional supports and recording their use. Frames return in unit 4.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read everything aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair gestures with more, fewer, same and category. Rehearse "This has more because ___." Use large high-contrast pictures or tactile pieces, a counter mat instead of fingers, and directed adult movement. Keep numeral writing observable. Extend by finding two arrangements that mislead sight but preserve the comparison, within ten.
<!-- /skel -->
