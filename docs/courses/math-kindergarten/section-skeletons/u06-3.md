## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.NBT.A.1** (days 112, 113, 114): Compose and decompose numbers from 11 to 19 into ten ones and some further ones, e.g., by using objects or drawings, and record each composition or decomposition by a drawing or equation (e.g., 18 = 10 + 8); understand that these numbers are composed of ten ones and one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, or nine ones.
- **K.CC.A.3** (days 115): Write numbers from 0 to 20. Represent a number of objects with a written numeral 0-20 (with 0 representing a count of no objects).
- **K.CC.B.5** (days 115): Count to answer “how many?” questions about as many as 20 things arranged in a line, a rectangular array, or a circle, or as many as 10 things in a scattered configuration; given a number from 1–20, count out that many objects.
- skill mk-teen-ten-ones (grade K)
- skill mk-count-to-20 (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does moving objects change how many, day 107? How do we hear ten in a teen name, day 110? How can a drawing and equation show the same parts, day 114?
>
> **Carried.** Keep unit 5's optional frame. Day 108 introduces paired frames beside counted teens; day 109 ties them to numerals and separates ten ones from further ones; day 113 ties both parts to equations. On day 116 withdraw supplied frames and numeral references, retaining them by choice. Unit 8 receives the model.

### U9. Supports and extension

<!-- skel -->
> (shared across the unit) Read everything aloud. Gesture ten ones and further ones; offer "___ is ten ones and ___ more ones." Allow home-language reasoning before restating English names. Provide large tactile pieces, spaced pictures and directed adult movement. Extend by explaining two tracking paths for the same arranged collection, within twenty.
<!-- /skel -->
