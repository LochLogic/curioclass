# Section 5.1: Split one whole in different ways

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-kindergarten/5/s/5.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-kindergarten u05-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Kindergarten Math. A section plan, not a lesson; nothing here has been tried with children. Read with [s05.md](s05.md) and [DEFAULTS.md](DEFAULTS.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

K.OA.A.3, "Decompose" and "record": two exhaustive, disjoint parts, with more than one pair where possible. Totals zero to ten; zero has only 0 and 0, and one has only the unordered pair 0 and 1. Empty parts count. Reversing an unlabeled pair does not make a different pair. K.OA.A.1 connects objects/drawings day 87. MP.8, day 89: moving one makes one part grow and the other shrink. Teach this on day 87 so day 89 rehearses it. Teacher explanation: count pieces rather than area; a large shape is still one piece. No algorithm or speed threshold.

## 2. Where it sits

## 3. Checks, written first

Read each quoted stem aloud as one ask. Every child marks a paper picture; directing an adult to mark is available. Exits last eight minutes: one to launch, six for simultaneous independent paper work while six named children each have a 45-second listening turn, one to collect. Use five rotating groups of six, starting group one on day 87; record who was heard. Hear the same ask, without adding another question. Paper establishes its own component; an unheard explanation stays not yet seen. Teacher judgments use only shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record help separately. A bare wrong number does not establish an error letter.

Print and say "Invented for practice" for every story; Rin is its sole actor. Reserve each exit/check case and Shape across the unit, including repairs and cards. Practice completes or corrects supplied records; evidence requires independent production or a new correspondence. The full-range assessments necessarily revisit finite number pairs, but their cases, layouts and independent response formats are reserved. Timings and anticipated errors are ours, untested.

**Unit assessment items owned:** F1, s05 field 5, retained in days 99/100 and closed day 103. Use u05-3 field 3: second panels for every total two through ten, one panel each for zero and one.

- **E87, K.OA.A.3.** Paper shows two fresh five-dot crescents. "Show two different splits of five." Answer/look-for: each copy has two disjoint parts using every dot, with different unordered counts. Shown sample: 1 and 4, then 2 and 3. Wrong sample: 1 and 4 copied twice as a new pair (B). Shape: independently partition two unmarked crescent collections; avoid free partition recording in practice, models and cards. Practice completes one teacher-started six-square mat at a time.
- **E88, K.OA.A.3.** Two blank seven-slot design spaces, with a picture bank containing at least seven triangles and seven squares per design. Children draw or direct marks into the slots, choosing the piece type at each slot. "Show two ways to make a seven-piece design using triangles and squares." Answer/look-for: two designs each total seven with different type counts; e.g., 2 triangles/5 squares and 3 triangles/4 squares. Wrong sample: six assigned pieces and one unused (A). Counting a piece in both categories is F. Shape: produce new counted-shape decompositions; practice repairs supplied six-piece designs, never constructs a whole new design from blank paper. Reserve seven-piece design cards.
- **E89, K.OA.A.3; MP.8.** Fresh nine-cell cube-train picture; a nine-piece marked paper strip is the accessible manipulative. "Show where you would snap this train into two parts." Every child marks the picture; six describe their same split. Answer/look-for: any one boundary leaving two parts totaling nine, e.g., 4 and 5. Wrong sample: one cell outside both stated parts, 4 and 4 (A). Shape: choose an independent snap boundary and name its parts; practice moves a loose square between already separated six-piece groups, never chooses a boundary on an unmarked train.
- **E90, K.OA.A.3.** Four picture records for eight: 1/7, 2/6, a rearranged 1/7, 3/5. "Show why two records repeat the same split." Answer/look-for: connect first and third and match their one-piece and seven-piece parts with marks or spoken counts. Shown sample: links both one-piece parts and both seven-piece parts; rearrangement leaves the counts unchanged. A choice alone leaves the reason not yet seen. Wrong sample: calls rearranged 1/7 a new pair because positions changed (B). Shape: produce a count-based reason for repetition within an unordered multi-record list; practice completes one next six-piece partition, never selects matching records from a list.

## 9. Supports and extension

Day 87 oral definitions: a part is one group within the whole; the whole includes every piece; a pair is two parts; empty has no pieces. Split or decompose means break the whole into parts. Patterned has repeated marks. Snap means separate joined pieces at a joint. Day 90: repeat means the same counts again. Gesture each with pieces; children use the words while pointing. Frame: "___ and ___ make ___." Read all print aloud, including equation cards; independent reading is zero. Use large high-contrast pictures, pattern plus color, tactile outlines, nonslip mats and broad crayons. Children may sign, point, dictate or direct an adult to mark paper; record help without changing the goal. No required fingers, public speech or fine cutting. Adult cuts strips/pieces. Supervise large pieces; replace mouthed/damaged pieces with drawn marks. Checks retain quantities and one ask; never supply the missing part. Extend within ten by explaining another valid pair or repeat, never totals above ten.
