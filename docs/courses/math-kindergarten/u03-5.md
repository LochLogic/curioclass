## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K.G.B.5** (days 61, 62, 63): Model shapes in the world by building shapes from components (e.g., sticks and clay balls) and drawing shapes.
- **K.G.B.6** (days 61, 62, 63): Compose simple shapes to form larger shapes. For example, “Can you join these two triangles with full sides touching to make a rectangle?”
- **K.G.A.1** (days 63): Describe objects in the environment using names of shapes, and describe the relative positions of these objects using terms such as above, below, beside, in front of, behind, and next to.
- **K.G.A.2** (days 63): Correctly name shapes regardless of their orientations or overall size.
- **K.MD.A.1** (days 63): Describe measurable attributes of objects, such as length or weight. Describe several measurable attributes of a single object.
- **K.MD.A.2** (days 63): Directly compare two objects with a measurable attribute in common, to see which object has “more of”/“less of” the attribute, and describe the difference. For example, directly compare the heights of two children and describe one child as taller/shorter.
- skill mk-build-compose-shapes (grade K)
- skill mk-shapes-positions (grade K)
- skill mk-measurable-attributes (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Supporting questions.** Does turning change a shape, day 47? Which attribute are we comparing, day 53? Why align ends, day 54? Can different pieces make the same whole, day 61?
> **Teaching moves.** Model all six position words on day 45 with a fixed viewing direction. Contrast examples and nonexamples on day 50; days 51 and 52 line up ends as a plain routine, and day 54 teaches why ends must meet. On days 49, 52 and 57 the fifteen connected-work minutes open with partners telling where a piece or card goes, using two of the six position words. Step back during day 61 puzzles. Teacher pages explain that turning preserves shape and length, a square is a special rectangle, and capacity differs from height.
>
> **Practices.** MP.6: day 54, align turned strips, name length and explain longer, shorter or equal. MP.7: day 61, fill one hexagon outline with different pieces and explain how their sides fit the boundary.
> **Carried.** Keep unit 2 tracking mats and optional ten-frames for counting only. Tie quantities to numerals in day 45 retrieval; withdraw supplied frames there after one try. Children retain paper shapes through composition. Tie built boundaries to drawings on day 57; withdraw supplied outlines for day 62 art, retaining access supports.
> **Focus.** Zero of one units and zero of eighteen primary teaching lessons are major work. Eighteen five-minute counting routines supply 90 major-work minutes by the course method (scope and sequence); counting the twenty unit 2 response minutes on days 45 and 46 makes 110. Subtract five twenty-minute observation blocks from 1,080 teaching minutes: 110 of 980 instructional minutes, or 11.2 percent. Supporting composition practises major counting through piece counts and comparisons on days 59 and 61, inside those routines; no extra minutes are claimed.
