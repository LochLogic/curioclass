# Unit 2: Numbers 1 to 10

Course `math-kindergarten` (Kindergarten Math). **Days 19 to 44** (26 course days; school days 19 to 44, weeks 4 to 9). Sections: 2.1 (days 19 to 20); 2.2 (days 21 to 24); 2.3 (days 25 to 29); 2.4 (days 30 to 33); 2.5 (days 34 to 36); 2.6 (days 37 to 40); 2.7 (days 41 to 44). Days by kind: understanding 15, fluency 6, application 4, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Teach through five arcs: quantities and matching, days 19 to 24 (sections 2.1 and 2.2); picture comparisons, days 25 to 29 (section 2.3); quantities and numerals, days 30 to 36 (sections 2.4 and 2.5); number order, days 37 to 40 (section 2.6); sorting and application, days 41 to 44 (section 2.7).

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we show how many, and which has more?"

**Hook, day 19.** For eight minutes, show three counters. Children show that many with fingers or paper squares. Ask the compelling question. Accept different finger arrangements; compare quantities, never bodies. Adapted exploration, field 10.
**Supporting questions.** Does moving change how many, day 20? How can matching settle a comparison, day 24? How does five help, day 28? What does a numeral tell us, day 30? How can counts order categories, day 42?
**Closing claim.** "I can show how many, keep the count when objects move, and explain which has more." Counting records, comparison sheets and sorted topping pictures supply evidence closed on day 44.
**Teaching moves.** Model moving without adding on day 20, matching on day 21 and counting another way on day 25. Use dot records and spoken totals before tying collections to numerals on day 30. Step back during day 39 comparisons. Model each sorting step on day 41. Teacher pages distinguish counting words, a quantity and its written numeral.


**Practices.** MP.3: day 24, partners compare five blocks with eight, justify by matching and counting, and question a spacing-based claim. MP.7: day 28, circle the full five within eight on joined frames and use the remaining three to explain why eight exceeds five.
**Rigor.** No owned standard requires arithmetic fluency. Keep fifteen understanding, six fluency and four application days: 60, 24 and 16 percent of teaching days. Fluency rehearses taught counting, drawing and comparison through untimed partner games, with explanation and feedback; no new content enters those days.
**Limits.** K.CC.B.5 samples counting arranged and scattered sets and counting out requested sets within ten. K.CC.A.3 samples writing 0 to 10, including an empty set. Unit 6 finishes both to twenty. Comparisons use words, not inequality symbols or how many more. Category counts stay at most ten. Retain unit 1 evidence for K.CC.B.4a within the parent K.CC.B.4.
**Carried.** Keep unit 1 tracking mats. Teach the 5-frame structure on day 26 and join two that day, as five and some more, before day 27 rehearsal. Name one more in the oral count on days 38 and 40. Tie frames to written numerals on day 30; vary arrangements on day 31. Withdraw supplied frames for numeral comparisons on day 40, retaining optional supports and recording their use. Frames return in unit 4.
- **Focus.** One of one units and 23 of 25 teaching lessons have primary major-work goals. Excluding days 36 and 44 and six twenty-minute observation blocks leaves 1,320 instructional minutes. On days 41 and 42, thirty minutes count and compare, ten classify, and twenty observe. Other instructional minutes count or compare: 1,300 of 1,320, or 98.5 percent, are major work. Sorting supports cardinality and comparison.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 20, "What entered or left?" On day 24, "Where does each match end?" On day 42, "Why did the counts change when the sorting rule changed?" Day 39 writing links two drawn quantities with numerals and a comparison word; judge accurate amounts, labels and the comparison. Adults may scribe reasons.

## U8. Practice that returns

Daily oral counting and marking routes retrieve unit 1. Days 27 and 29 rehearse comparisons; days 33 and 35 mix zero, counting out and numerals; days 38 and 40 rehearse numeral order and comparisons, and their oral count names one more than a number given. Immediate feedback follows each partner turn. No timer or arithmetic-fluency claim.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud; no independent reading is required. Pair gestures with more, fewer, same and category. Rehearse "This has more because ___." Use large high-contrast pictures or tactile pieces, a counter mat instead of fingers, and directed adult movement. Keep numeral writing observable. Extend by finding two arrangements that mislead sight but preserve the comparison, within ten.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child, use a 35-minute path: count five, model eight, work twelve, discuss five, exit five. The adult alternates partner roles and retains one record. On day 44, review earlier records and finish gaps, with response on days 45 and 46. A shared screen may display pictures; every child works on paper without it.
