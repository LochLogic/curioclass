# Section 3.3: Choosing attributes and comparing fairly

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-kindergarten/3/s/3.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-kindergarten u03-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


K.MD.A.1, "Describe" several measurable attributes of one object; K.MD.A.2, "Directly compare" length, height, weight and capacity, describing both objects and the difference. MP.6, day 54: align endpoints/describe exposed part; day 56: distinguish capacity from height. No algorithm, measurement numerals, indirect comparison or comparisons of bodies. Teacher explanation: choose one attribute, keep conditions fixed, turn without stretching. Capacity is the maximum held at the rim, distinct from present contents. Hefting supports about the same weight, not exact measured equality. Actual pours support capacity evidence; dry paper scoops and picture sizes do not.

**Unit assessment items owned:** F5/F6, collected in u03-4/u03-5, closed day 63. Copied rows below are source records, not compound read-aloud scripts.

**F5. Shape:** three or more measurable attributes of one reserved clean storage tub, with corresponding marks/gestures. Practice corrects one named attribute at a time on a carton, never a several-attribute blank tub record. Spoken stem: "What things about this tub could we measure?" Look-for/sample: length/height/weight/how much fits, with gestures and marks; "Big" alone (F). Color alone is L. u03-4 budgets paper and heard description.

**F6. Shape:** reserved five-component record. Separate paper prompts: "Show the length comparison" for unequal strips; "Show the height comparison" for standing towers; "Show whether these strips have the same length" for equal strips initially turned. Each before/after sketch records common endpoints and protrusion, not just a guessed ring. Wave strip longer than dotted; ring tower taller than striped; equal strips stay equal after turning. Separate heard stems: "How do these tubs compare in weight?" then "Which cup holds more?" Child hefts/swaps hands, directs adult pour. Unequal-size tubs feel about the same; a full tall narrow cup poured into short wide cup leaves space, so short wide holds more. Practice corrects supplied diagrams/witnessed demonstrations, never this independent five-component record, equal-weight tubs, these markings or this pour direction. Look-for/sample: direct actions and accurate relationships, "About the same after changing hands" plus leftover-space mark; "The larger tub must be heavier" (H). Unaligned far-end judgment is G; turning said to lengthen is M; choosing tall cup from height is I. Pretest pairs and vessels; replace failures before use.

- **E53, K.MD.A.1.** New original large paper toy wagon, not a storage tub. "What could we compare about this toy?" All draw two attribute gestures beside outline and ring one choice; hear six descriptions. Answer: any two of length/height/weight/capacity with one choice. Samples: "How long, how heavy; I pick weight"; only "Big" (F), or only color (L). Shape: independent multiple-attribute proposal/choice for a new toy; practice fixes supplied one-attribute descriptions, never generates a multiple-attribute record or uses a toy wagon. No units/values. Data id: `s03-X53`.
- **E54, K.MD.A.2.** Fresh wavy-edged and straight-edged ribbons start separated/turned; straightened without stretching, wavy-edged is longer. "Show a fair length comparison." All align near ends on blank paper and mark exposed far part; hear six. Samples: common ends/exposed wavy end; "Straight-edged is longer" from initial far endpoint (G). "Turning made it longer" is M. Shape: produce alignment without printed baseline; practice corrects rigid-strip/height diagrams or uses supplied start lines, never two loose ribbons on blank paper. Not F6's equal pair. Data id: `s03-X54`.
- **E55, K.MD.A.2.** Per pair, reserved large ring envelope and small star envelope, safely sealed; adult pretests star noticeably heavier. "How do these objects compare in weight?" Both partners heft/change hands and independently mark heavier/lighter pictures; hear six. Answer: star heavier, ring lighter. Samples: "The small star one is heavier" after hefting; "Ring is bigger, so heavier" (H). Shape: direct fresh heft comparison with paper judgment; practice repairs supplied claims after witnessed adult heft, never independent pair weighing. Fifteen duplicate exit sets prevent queues; replace filling if pretest fails. Data id: `s03-X55`.
- **E56, K.MD.A.2.** Reserved low wide bowl holds more than tall narrow pot, pretested. "Which container holds more?" Children direct adult transfer from full bowl into empty pot, stopping at rim; water remains in bowl. All mark winner and leftover; hear six. Answer: bowl more, pot less. Samples: "Bowl; some water is still there"; "Pot because it is taller" (I). Shape: fresh capacity comparison with real transfer/leftover; practice follows supplied diagram of a different vessel filling another with spare space, never predicts/selects winner, uses bowl/pot or pours this direction. Adult hands preserve access; no scoop evidence. Data id: `s03-X56`.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


Children carry comparison pictures; objects move before drawings record them. Tie acts to marks days 54 to 56; remove supplied baselines for E54. Ideal instruments: movable lengths, safe sealed heft pairs and real water transfer. Household objects fit; no digital spec/key tool. Paper strips replace strings, standing folded cards replace books, folded wagon replaces toy, clean cartons/sealed envelopes replace tubs. For capacity, suitable unbreakable household cups, tray and towel remain necessary; no paper substitute supplies capacity evidence. Adult controls small amounts of room-temperature water, stops at rim, dries spills/floor immediately. No glass, food, tasting or child weights. Dry paper scoops rehearse handling only; an observed adult pour avoids wet hands.

## 6. Days

Tools: None throughout. Children hold pieces and paper; teacher keeps dated originals, feedback and revisions. Fifteen pairs work concurrently, two 45-second turns plus handling per round. At most two public volunteers, with passing allowed. Rin is the sole actor in invented demonstrations, one scene at a time. Return and repair holds one five-minute action: whole class if more than ten of thirty share a letter, otherwise small group while others correct familiar paper. Queue additional letters across later slots. Never shorten repairs or run them during conferences. Everybody returns a corrected diagram. Eight-minute exits allow 2.5 minutes directions/paper, six 45-second turns, one minute collection. Other children return paper; extra help is recorded. Adult-cut large pieces and table trays avoid cutting/sink queues.

## 9. Supports and extension

No independent reading. Read all print aloud. Teach words with objects and gestures; remove language frames from independent checks. Offer large high-contrast or tactile boundaries, nonslip mats, broad pencils, signing and usual communication aids. A scribe records words without supplying them. Adult movement follows the child's directions; record mathematical help separately. Direct comparisons cannot become picture guesses. Extend with another example or fit on the same standard, never units or new arithmetic. Words: length, height, weight, holds more/less, heavier/lighter, taller/shorter, longer, about the same. Frame: "The ___ has more ___ than the ___." Capacity can be teacher vocabulary; children may say how much fits.
