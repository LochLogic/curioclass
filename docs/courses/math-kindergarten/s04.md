## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What happens to how many when we put together or take away?"
**Hook, day 64.** Two paper mats hold two and three large squares. Partners bring them onto one mat and explain how many altogether. Ask the compelling question. Eight minutes; source in field 10.
**Supporting questions.** What changes when objects leave, day 68? How can a story become a model, day 71? Which marks show its action, day 75? What does each symbol mean, day 78?
**Closing claim.** "I can show and explain putting together and taking apart, solve stories within ten, and connect pictures with expressions and equations." The two-story record and equation match support it on day 83.
**Teaching moves.** Model actions before symbols. Ask what each object represents; compare clear and misleading drawings on day 74. Step back during day 77 story creation. Explain that equals means the same quantity on both sides.

## U5. What is carried, and instruments

The key moment on day 79 needs visible quantities linked to notation; movable paper squares and handwritten cards fit. Children hold pieces and paper; the teacher holds the record. No digital instrument is needed.

## U6. Days

Ordinary days use five oral-counting minutes, ten modelling minutes, fifteen connected-work minutes, twenty-five center minutes and five exit minutes. Oral counting includes starting from a supplied number within the known sequence; it does not require counting on to solve arithmetic. Children act, draw and explain; partners alternate doing and checking. Fluency days replace new modelling with rehearsal. All digital tools are None. From day 71, five center minutes give mixed, untimed within-five games with immediate feedback. Practice uses only 2 + 3, 1 + 3, 2 + 2, 3 + 3, 3 + 4, 4 + 4, their related removals 5 - 2, 4 - 1, 4 - 2, 6 - 3, 7 - 3 and 8 - 4, and 4 + 0, 4 + 1, 5 - 5, 9 + 1, 9 - 1 and 10 - 5. Do not use the reversed or other combinations reserved for evidence. Every invented classroom story is printed "Invented for practice". Credits belong on their day only.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask, "What does each counter stand for?" and "Which group answers the question?" Day 75 drawings show the initial quantity, change and result; judge mathematical marks, never artistic detail. Scribe spoken reasons when needed.

## U8. Practice that returns

Daily counting retrieves unit 2. Games from day 71 mix taught joining and removal facts within five with immediate feedback. Record just knows, quick strategy or counts all informally; this is preparation, not the unit 8 fluency assessment.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud. Gesture join, take away and remain; rehearse "These marks show ___." Offer tactile pieces, large high-contrast pictures and directed adult movement. Extend within ten by changing a story's action and explaining why its expression changes.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child: count five minutes, model eight, work twelve, discuss five, exit five. The adult alternates partner roles and retains the same records across days. One shared screen is optional; paper carries every task.
