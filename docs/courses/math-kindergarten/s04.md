Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-kindergarten/4/plan), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-kindergarten s04.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Course `math-kindergarten` (Kindergarten Math). **Days 64 to 83** (20 course days; school days 64 to 83, weeks 13 to 17). Sections: 4.1 (days 64 to 67); 4.2 (days 68 to 71); 4.3 (days 72 to 76); 4.4 (days 77 to 80); 4.5 (days 81 to 83). Days by kind: understanding 11, fluency 3, application 5, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Teach four arcs: actions, days 64 to 68; heard stories, days 69 to 73; drawings and notation, days 74 to 78; connecting representations and closing evidence, days 79 to 83.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What happens to how many when we put together or take away?"

**Hook, day 64.** Two paper mats hold two and three large squares. Partners bring them onto one mat and explain how many altogether. Ask the compelling question. Eight minutes; source in field 10.
**Supporting questions.** What changes when objects leave, day 68? How can a story become a model, day 71? Which marks show its action, day 75? What does each symbol mean, day 78?
**Closing claim.** "I can show and explain putting together and taking apart, solve stories within ten, and connect pictures with expressions and equations." The two-story record and equation match support it on day 83.
**Teaching moves.** Model actions before symbols. Ask what each object represents; compare clear and misleading drawings on day 74. Day 73 adds a heard put-together story. Step back during day 77 story creation. Explain that equals means the same quantity on both sides.


**Practices.** MP.4: day 72, choose objects or a drawing for a heard story and explain the match. MP.2: day 78, identify what both numbers and the operation in an expression mean in its story.
**Rigor and fluency.** Eleven understanding, three fluency and five application days are 57.9, 15.8 and 26.3 percent of teaching days. Owned standards set no fluency threshold; K.OA.A.5 belongs to unit 8. Day 66 adds one short modeled route through two scattered groups to its rehearsal; days 80 and 81 rehearse taught ideas. Zero and one are taught on day 67. Untimed games start on day 71.
**Limits.** Totals stay within ten. Both-addends-unknown stories continue in unit 5 and are checked on day 103. No comparison problems, unknown-position equations, number line or required counting-on strategy. Counting all remains valid.
**Carried.** Retain the optional unit 2 frames carried through unit 3. On day 64, match counters to quantity numerals; use frames beside objects for six to ten through day 76. Tie them to expressions and equations on day 79. Withdraw supplied frames on day 80; children may choose them afterward.

**Look-fors and samples.** F1 requires both actions, both expressions, equations with the total or remaining quantity on either side, and quantities explained in context. Meets: "Three were there, five joined; eight is the same as three plus five. Nine tiles, four bracketed and moved aside, so five remain; five is the same as nine minus four." Does not meet: "8 = 3 + 5 is backwards because the answer must follow equals" (D). F2 requires a faithful model for each story, a count or calculation, and an answer to its question. Meets: four squares and six triangles are grouped and labeled 10; eight bears have five crossed out and three labeled as remaining. Spoken explanation: "I counted all the pieces; I counted only bears still on the bench." Does not meet: crosses out five accurately but labels five as remaining (E). Judge components separately; a correct numeral without a model leaves modeling unclassified.

**Accommodations.** Read prompts aloud, repeat without changing quantities, enlarge or use tactile pictures, allow the usual communication method and adult movement directed by the child. Scribe reasons without supplying operations. Every child marks paper; counters replace fingers when preferred.

**Collect once.** Day 64 work is diagnostic, never scored. Addition, subtraction and drawing are taught before day 79; day 78 (U6 row) already taught the match of drawings and objects to equations, so day 79 is practice of a taught idea and F1 is scored only after its day 80 feedback and revision. Zero and one (F and G) are taught and rehearsed on days 67 and 81 and checked by exit 81 and the unscored day 83 transfers; they are not a scored end-of-unit part. Days 79 to 82 reserve twenty center minutes daily: eighty assessment minutes altogether. Day 79 uses this block for all children to make F2 in eight minutes and F1 in four, with eight minutes for completing marks and checking their own work without supplied answers. On days 80, 81 and 82, ten assigned children daily receive one two-minute turn each explaining their two stories and equation match from these same records. This is sixty teacher observation minutes for thirty children. Observe object actions within this turn, not a separate station. Retain paper and oral records without a second conference on day 83 or at the course-level check. If a turn cannot establish every component, leave the missing part pending rather than rush or infer it.

**Feedback, revision and scoring.** Allow fifteen teacher minutes after each window day to write feedback on that day's observations and paper. Give five minutes of the next day's connected work for revision, replacing practice; day 82 observations revise in day 83's finishing block. Keep originals, revisions and support notes. Score earlier-made work on day 83 only after its written feedback and revision opportunity. Work first made on day 83 receives feedback, stays pending, and is scored after revision on a later day; it never becomes same-period scored evidence.

**Day 83 budget.** Ten minutes finish assigned evidence or revisions, ten interpret existing records using F1's four and F2's six review minutes, twenty respond through independent application of earlier written feedback in two ten-minute groups, and twenty for familiar counting, independent work and synthesis. ## U7. Talk and writing

Ask, "What does each counter stand for?" and "Which group answers the question?" Day 75 drawings show the initial quantity, change and result; judge mathematical marks, never artistic detail. Scribe spoken reasons when needed.

## U8. Practice that returns

Daily counting retrieves unit 2. Games from day 71 mix taught joining and removal facts within five with immediate feedback. Record just knows, quick strategy or counts all informally; this is preparation, not the unit 8 fluency assessment.

## U9. Supports and extension

Read everything aloud. Gesture join, take away and remain; rehearse "These marks show ___." Offer tactile pieces, large high-contrast pictures and directed adult movement. Extend within ten by changing a story's action and explaining why its expression changes.

## U10. Two rooms

For one child: count five minutes, model eight, work twelve, discuss five, exit five. The adult alternates partner roles and retains the same records across days. One shared screen is optional; paper carries every task.
