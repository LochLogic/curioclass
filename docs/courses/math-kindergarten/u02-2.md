# Section 2.2: Compare groups by matching and counting

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#math/math-kindergarten/2/s/2.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py math-kindergarten u02-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Kindergarten Math, course `math-kindergarten`. Unit 2, Numbers 1 to 10: see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 21 to 24.** Days by kind: understanding 3, application 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children.

## 1. Standards and practices

**Share.** K.CC.C.6: "Identify whether" groups are "greater than, less than, or equal to" each other by matching and counting. Day 21 teaches more/fewer and constructing a contrasting group; day 22 teaches same/equal and close comparisons before day 23 application. Day 24 connects matches to counted totals.

**Limits, strategy and connection.** Within ten. Compare numbers of objects, not size, spread or value. No difference question, subtraction or inequality symbol. K.CC.B.4b supports counted totals on day 24. MP.3, day 24: critique a false comparison, then justify by complete matching/counting. Teacher explanation: unmatched members show more only after every object in the other group has one unique partner.

## 3. Checks, written first

All exits are original, invented for practice, judged=teacher and path=paper. Read each single stem aloud. Every child marks or directs a mark on paper. Eight minutes holds directions 1, initial paper work 2, six named turns at 45 seconds 4.5, collection 0.5. The other twenty-four keep working during the six turns. Hear just the named follow-up, or observe the count already requested, without asking the child to repeat completed work. Record who was heard. Paper alone cannot establish unheard counting words or reasons.

Use shown, shown with help, not yet seen for each component. Each key/look-for is a shown sample; each listed observed wrong response is a not yet seen sample. Record any help. Assign an error letter only from the observed action or explanation. Reserve every field 3 case and Shape from teaching, practice, cards and repairs throughout the unit.

**Unit assessment owned:** F2, the later retained matching/counting comparison record; no collection here.

**E21, K.CC.C.6.** Picture: three large drums in one irregular pile, ten small whistles in another, all fully visible. Read, "Show with matching which pile has more." Key/look-fors: three unique links, seven extra whistles, whistle pile marked. Heard follow-up: "How do your matches show that?" Shown: "Each drum has one; whistles are left." Wrong: drums more because their pictures are larger (N).

Shape: Independently proves a comparison of very different picture piles with drawn correspondence. No drums/whistles, 3/10 pair or independent picture-pile proof in practice; finish and repair adult-started physical pairings.

**E22, K.CC.C.6.** Picture: six crayons in one group, seven differently turned crayons in another. No matches or isolated extra clue. Read, "Mark the crayon that cannot have a partner." Child may draw links. Key/look-fors: six unique matches and exactly one of the seven marked as extra; any member of the seven may be left over. Heard follow-up: "Which group has fewer?" Answer: the six-crayon group. Wrong: calls the seven group fewer because of its extra (I); skips a crayon during matching/counting (K), only when observed.

Shape: Locates the unmatched member in close crayon groups. No crayons, 6/7 pair or independent single-extra worksheet in teaching; complete adult pairings with five/eight tiles, then equal fives.

**E23, K.CC.C.6.** Six stamp drawings above a blank panel with the familiar finish-mark corner. Read, "Make a paper group with fewer pieces than these stamps." Key/look-fors: any deliberate amount zero through five; five dots is a shown sample. Accept an empty group only with a finish mark and confirmation that none was intended, not an unfinished page. Heard follow-up: "How can you tell it has fewer?" Shown: "Some stamps have no partner." Wrong: eight pieces explicitly described as fewer (I).

Shape: Produces one smaller comparison group for six stamps without supplied pieces or answers. No stamps, six-piece reference or independent fewer-only blank panel in practice; choose and repair existing groups against more/fewer/same requests.

**E24, K.CC.C.6; MP.3.** Six paint pots and nine brushes in irregular differently spaced clusters, no matches. Read, "Use paper to explain which group has more." Key/look-fors: six unique matches and three extra brushes, or complete count marks with six/nine totals and a heard quantity-based reason. No numerals required. Heard follow-up: "What shows that in your paper?" Shown: "Every pot has a brush; three brushes have no pot." Wrong: nine brushes called fewer despite their extras (I); wider pot cluster called more because of spread (B).

Shape: Creates an independent comparison explanation for unseen groups. No paint pots/brushes, 6/9 pair or independent claim-proof sheet in teaching; critique supplied reasons and correct an incomplete five/eight diagram instead.

**F2 cold key.** Two/three tabs produce two pairs and one extra in three; four/four produce four pairs, none extra. Counting totals 2/3 and 4/4 agree. More: three, fewer: two; four equal to four. Wrong: equal fours judged by spread (B); unmatched extra group called fewer (I).

Shape: Two independent comparisons, unequal and equal, each justified by matching and counting. No two/three tabs, equal-four tab case or complete matching/counting interview in practice. Reserve both papers. Collection: nine simultaneous paper minutes on day 40, each matching, count or comparison ask read separately. Hear one F2 reason per child on days 40 to 42, ten children daily at one minute each: one 45-second ask and fifteen seconds to note it. The paper establishes matching/counts; heard reasons establish MP.3. Keep this separate from any F3 reason. See field 11 for the schedule decision.

**Day 23 comparison checks, K.CC.C.6.** During the last six Counting centers minutes, allow directions 1, independent paper 4 and collection 1. These are two separate single-ask items, not rehearsal for E23.

**Gm23, [[s02-Gm23]].** Five diamond pictures above a blank panel. Read, "Draw a group with more pieces than this group." Key/look-for: more than five distinct marks; six is a shown sample. Multiple correct answers. Accept a countable larger group even above ten; do not teach beyond ten. Wrong: three explicitly called more than five (I).

Shape: Independently draws a larger group for five diamonds in a blank panel. No diamonds, five-diamond reference or blank larger-group panel in teaching; change an already supplied square group instead.

**Ge23, [[s02-Ge23]].** Eight rectangle pictures above a separate blank panel. Read while pointing to the reference, "Draw a group with the same number of pieces." Key/look-for: eight distinct marks. Wrong: three reference rectangles omitted during observed matching to the drawing (K). An unexplained five-mark drawing does not establish a word reversal.

Shape: Independently copies the quantity of eight rectangles into a blank panel. No eight-rectangle reference or independent equal-group blank panel in teaching; repair supplied matched groups instead. Mark both checks after class within the fifteen-minute paper budget; return with E23 in day 24 Return and repair. Diagnose I only from an observed word reversal, not merely an incorrect drawing.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

One puppet demonstrates one practice mistake at a time, never a child's work. If more than ten of thirty share a letter, the next five-minute Return and repair models one correction, lets pairs retry simultaneously on a fresh practice case and collects everyone's corrected paper. Otherwise meet one group while others correct familiar work. Choose the earliest blocking action; queue other letters across later slots. Never stack five-minute repairs or use reserved art.

N, day 21: three large blocks called more than five small ones versus five with two extras. I, day 22: five called more than eight despite three extras versus five fewer. B, day 24: wide five called more than compact five versus five matches, same. K, day 22: one of eight tiles skipped versus one visit each.

Repair N/B by re-pairing despite size/spacing; I by pointing to extras and rehearsing the applicable word; K by marking each visit. Day 25 receives one unresolved five-minute action, never a hidden ten-minute warm-up.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

**Carried.** Review Counted-uncounted boundary from u01-4. Loose pieces become drawn links and dot records; complete one-to-one matching makes extras visible. Keep numeral cards stored until day 30 and frames until day 26.

**Key moment, day 24.** Ideal instrument: visible unique pairs with distinguishable extras. Paper tiles and drawn links fit; two scrap-paper sets with different drawn patterns are the no-kit path. Object differences identify groups, never children. Use large adult-cut pieces, inspect damage and keep them out of mouths. No digital instrument or spec is needed.

## 6. Days

All digital tools: None; no key tool. Children hold pieces and paper; the teacher holds dated records. Fifteen pairs work simultaneously: one 45-second turn each takes 1.5 minutes per round. Use no more than two public volunteers; anyone may pass. Label printed stories "invented for practice" and say this aloud. Object-only scenes need no actors.

Review Partner turns, Counting centers and Paper count-dot record from u01-1. Each child returns a marked practice record and exit slip. Before the exit, teach or review Finish tick from u02-1: a corner tick means the child has finished, including an intentionally empty group; it never gives the answer.

Return and repair gives back the previous day's exit, heard-response note and practice papers with written feedback. Its five minutes hold return 1, one modeled correction 1, simultaneous fresh practice 2, collection 1. Retain original and revision. Repair one earliest obstruction; queue other letters in later slots. Hear further children during their later scheduled turns, keeping earlier unheard components not yet seen. After class allow fifteen teacher minutes for thirty paper sets at thirty seconds each; write one useful correction. No reserved case or Shape becomes repair practice.

## 7. Talk and writing

Day 21: "Does a larger piece mean more pieces?" Day 24: "Where does each match end?" Then, separately, "What do the totals tell us?" Pairs answer one question at a time.

Day 24 paper practice corrects a missing link in an adult's five/eight tile diagram. Look for one partner per tile and three extras on eight. E24 independently creates a new explanation, rather than this correction.

## 8. Practice that returns

Warm-ups: day 21 retrieves unchanged quantities from u02-1; day 22 retrieves matching and totals; day 23 retrieves all three comparison words; day 24 retrieves extras and equal matches. Review Partner turns, Counting centers, Paper count-dot record and Return and repair from u01-1, and match/group from u01-2. Review Finish tick from u02-1. Count toward ten, with supported oral extension toward twenty.

No fluency target; the standard sets none. Repeat taught comparisons with immediate feedback and no timer.

## 9. Supports and extension

**Language.** More: a group has extras after complete matching. Fewer: some members of the other group have no partner here. Same/equal: every member has one partner, with none left. Extra: a member without a partner after matching. Introduce these with pieces before using them. Frame: "This group has ___ because ___." Review match and group from u01-2.

**Access and exits.** Enlarge high-contrast pictures or use tactile pieces. An adult follows pointed, signed or aided directions without giving a quantity or reason, recording help. A child unable to mark directs adult marking. Face children and show visual turn cues. Read every stem and label aloud; independent reading is zero. Extend by rearranging groups within ten to challenge a spacing claim. Never compare bodies, home resources or better groups.

## 10. Two rooms

**Teaching one child.** Thirty minutes: count 3, model/rehearse 5, guided work 7, return and repair 5, exit 8, tidy 2. The adult alternates partner roles and hears this child. Keep original and corrected paper. A home session may be skipped under DEFAULTS.md.

**One projector.** Optional display only. Handwritten cards, board drawings and paper pieces give every child the full task without device turns.
