# Unit 1: Math in our world

Course `math-kindergarten` (Kindergarten Math). **Days 1 to 18** (18 course days; school days 1 to 18, weeks 1 to 4). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 5); 1.2 (days 6 to 9); 1.3 (days 10 to 11); 1.4 (days 12 to 15); 1.5 (days 16 to 18). Days by kind: understanding 10, application 7, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Weight (Student Achievement Partners, data/standards/ccss-math-focus.csv): major (K.CC.B).

Teach through four arcs: explore tools, days 1 to 5; notice and represent groups, days 6 to 9; match and track objects, days 10 to 15; explain and record counting, days 16 to 18.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How many are there, and how do we know?"
- **Hook, day 1.** For eight minutes, partners explore three cubes, build something and show each piece. Ask the compelling question, accepting pointing before modeling one spoken word with each move.
- **Supporting questions.** How can a tool help us keep track, day 5? Is there one object for every place, day 11? Why does moving help, day 15? How can a drawing remember each object, day 16?
- **Closing claim.** "I can say one number for each object and show how I kept track." The line-and-scatter counting task on days 15 to 17 supplies the evidence closed on day 18.
- **Teaching moves.** Model slow word-and-move pairing after the hook. Compare moving with touching on day 5, match objects to drawn places on day 10, and model separating counted objects on day 12. Step back on day 13; ask children to show their route before offering a mat.

- **K.CC.B.4a:** When counting objects, say the number names in the standard order, pairing each object with one and only one number name and each number name with one and only one object. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

- **Practices.** MP.5: day 5, choose touching, moving or a boxed mat for a collection of five blocks and explain the choice. MP.6: day 15, explain to a partner how each spoken word matches exactly one object. The assessment records both choices and precise pairing.
- **Rigor.** K.CC.B.4a sets no fluency expectation. Retain ten understanding days, seven application days and no fluency days. Daily oral counting supports pairing without speed pressure. Matching supplies application; explanations supply understanding.
- **Limits.** Start within five; extend tracking to ten. Small-group recognition supports counting, not a speed test. Oral counting reaches ten by day 11 and goes to ten each day after (ours, untested). Day 14 begins last-number meaning by naming the last word only; no exit or record scores it. Unit 2 owns cardinality, invariance, comparisons and numerals. No numeral writing, operations or formal shape properties are assessed here.
- **Carried.** No formal model enters. Day 3 explores a 5-frame without teaching its structure. Days 9 and 16 connect objects to drawn marks. Withdraw teacher-directed moving on day 13; children retain chosen tracking supports. Numeral connections and structured frames belong to unit 2, not forced withdrawal here.
- **Focus.** Major work occupies one of one units and all seventeen teaching lessons by primary goal. Minutes give the stricter share: days 1 to 5 each reserve thirty counting minutes (five oral counting, ten guided pairing, ten collection counting and five synthesis) and thirty exploration minutes; days 6 to 14 give sixty counting minutes each; days 15 to 17 give forty after observations. Thus 810 of 960 instructional minutes, 84.4 percent, are major work. Exploring shapes supplies objects to count, without claiming shape-standard coverage.

## 4. Readiness check, whole

Start-of-year check: none needed; no earlier row is assumed, so there is no retrieval prerequisite either. Day 1 opens with the hook. During exploration, note each child's spontaneous object-to-word pairing; do not score it or require a counting sequence before teaching. A child who cannot pair begins with a teacher-modeled group of two on day 2; a child who already pairs works with a larger collection within ten. These observations change support, not placement.

Pair each word with exactly one object. | Reuse the line-and-scatter record from days 15 to 17, not a new interview. Three minutes per child total: three 45-second asks plus 45 seconds for written feedback and a missed-count retry. Show six cut paper kites in a line and nine scattered cut paper boats. Read: (a) "Count each kite aloud, showing which one goes with each word." (b) "Choose a way to keep track and count each boat aloud. Tell me how it helps." Answer: one through six, one kite per word; one through nine, one boat per word, with a demonstrated tracking choice. Skipping one boat and ending at eight is A. **Fresh assessment quantities.** Reserve six and nine for this task; worked examples, planned questions, tools and exit tickets use other quantities within ten. For the boats, cut nine large paper boats, one piece each, laid two apart near the top, four across the middle and three near the bottom, with no enclosing groups; cut the six kites as pieces too. The collections are original tasks, not a story case.

**One record per child.** Assign ten different children on each of days 15, 16 and 17, three center minutes each, ninety minutes for thirty children. Each turn includes the two counts, a short explanation, written feedback on the record and an opportunity to revise the same count. Written feedback is a short note beside the missed word on the record (for example "skipped one"); the revision is offered on the missed count only; a child with no miss gives only the explanation. Preserve the initial performance and the revision separately. Classify the record on day 18, after the earlier day's feedback and revision, never during its making. A revision after a strategy cue is labeled supported, not independent. Do not score picture-book pages, sculptures or first-day work.

**Look-fors.** Record the actual number sequence, the object touched at each word, omissions, repeat visits, words without an object, chosen support and explanation. Both arrangements must show ordered number names with exactly one word per object. A correct final total alone is insufficient.

**Meets:** the child touches the six kites once each, saying "one, two, three, four, five, six"; then moves each of nine boats once while saying one through nine. "I put the ones I counted over here" explains the choice. **Does not meet:** the child skips a boat, says one through eight, and leaves one untouched (A). A silent correct picture product supplies no evidence about ordered number names.

**Accommodations.** Read both prompts aloud; use large, widely spaced pictures, the child's usual communication method for number words, or an adult moving pieces exactly as the child directs, without supplying the count. Record any assistance. Offer unhurried completion within the day 18 reserve.

**Day 18 budget.** Up to forty minutes for children not yet heard, ten minutes for the teacher to close records and ten minutes for independent familiar centers: sixty total. Children already heard are not interviewed again. First observations made on day 18 remain descriptive, not scored that day; give written feedback and a revision opportunity during that observation, retain both performances, and classify this record on day 19 before the response slot. Reserve up to ten teacher minutes before class for this deferred marking. There is no reteaching on day 18.

## 7. Response allocation

- **Respond days (days.csv):** none in this unit.
- **Day length (data/plans/frames.csv):** 60 minutes.
- **Marking:** ten teacher minutes on day 18, using the initial and revised record to mark independent, supported or not yet demonstrated for sequence and pairing separately. Do not self-mark kindergarten oral performances.
- **Response:** ten center minutes on day 19 for the register repair, then ten on day 20 for one fresh transfer with eight paper leaves, counted by touching or moving. Record the day 20 transfer by sequence, pairing, tracking choice and error letter, preserving the earlier result. Give written feedback and a revision opportunity within the slot. Mark this retained work after class on day 21, allowing ten teacher minutes: independent performance updates that component to independent; cued success updates it to supported; a persistent error leaves it not yet demonstrated and names the next repair. These twenty response minutes replace center choices, counted once; deferred marking is outside class. If more than a third share an error, use the same ten minutes together; otherwise group by observed action: skipped object (A), revisit (B), sequence gap (C), two words on one touch (D) and judging by looks (E) each go to their own register repair, decided by the record's sequence and pairing columns. Records from days 15 to 17 are classified on day 18; records first made on day 18 are classified before class on day 19, which forms the day 19 groups. Secure children compare tracking choices.
- **One child:** an adult keeps one observation record, discusses its missed part and uses the same two response slots. A 35-minute home lesson uses five minutes counting, eight modeling, ten exploring, seven practicing and five for evidence.

## 9. Family note

We are learning to match one counting word to each object and explain how we kept track. Invite your child to touch or move large household objects such as spoons or socks as they count, keeping small items away from younger children. Praise careful matching rather than speed.
