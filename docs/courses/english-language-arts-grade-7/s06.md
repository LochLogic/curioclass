**Cold texts.** Withhold R, E and H until their check days. Curio Class, 2026, ours, invented for practice. Each concerns a different invented river and supplies no Klamath evidence.

**R, Alder River monitoring report.**

**Purpose.** Alder's small barrier was removed last autumn. A survey team wanted to learn whether fine sediment remained on the riverbed downstream. A clearer surface would not answer that question: particles might have settled rather than left the reach.

**Method.** The team marked six downstream stations and returned to the same locations in April and July. At each visit it measured the thickness of the fine layer. It did not sample tributaries, the estuary or fish populations. Keeping locations constant made visits comparable without separating every cause of change.

**Results.** The fine layer was thinner in July at five stations; at the sixth it was unchanged. Heavy rain occurred between visits. The map showed the unchanged station beside a sheltered bank. Shelter might explain a different pattern, but the survey did not test that explanation.

**Interpretation and limits.** The observations support a decrease at most sampled stations during this interval. They do not show that all sediment left the river or that every habitat recovered. Rain, shelter and removal could each affect movement. Another visit and additional upstream comparisons would help test explanations. This report offers a bounded finding and a reason to continue monitoring.

**E, Birch River editorial.**

The town should publish laboratory and field-kit results together when comparing readings. On the same morning, a technician split each of ten river samples between the kit and the laboratory. Every paired result differed by less than two units, within the study's stated tolerance of three units. The pairs support agreement within that tolerance for these samples. They do not prove the instruments give exactly identical results or identify which is closer to the true value.

The laboratory's results should also be discarded because its dated log has blank rows. The inspection sheet says every required row is filled. Nevertheless, the kit's log has a neatly ruled blue cover, which must mean the laboratory log is incomplete. That cover supplies no information about entries in another log. Judge the publication recommendation and the missing-data claim separately.

**H, Cedar River hearing excerpt.** Read twice without coaching, pausing for notes between readings.

Chair: Distinguish what our proposed channel can do from what the release totals show.

Engineer: First, the proposed channel can carry water toward the marsh under the surveyed conditions. Its inlet is above its outlet, and the survey traces a continuous downhill route. Its design capacity exceeds the planned release rate. These dimensions support the direction and capacity claim, although they do not establish protection against a larger flood.

Second, the upstream and downstream gates followed identical daily release schedules last week. Each gate released seventy units over seven days. The upstream gate released ten each day. The downstream gate released seventy on Monday and none on the other days. The totals are equal, so the daily schedules must be identical.

Chair: Both logs contain all seven days. Evaluate the two claims separately.
