## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

The Label Committee. Curio Class, 2026. Invented for practice.

1. A class preparing a smallpox exhibit had room for one introductory label. Its first draft praised one discovery and ended the story there. Another editor objected that the label had become a boast: it promised to explain a worldwide achievement while naming only one contributor. The editor wanted a bridge between an important discovery and the work that eventually carried protection to many communities.

2. A second draft was more reserved. It distinguished inoculation from vaccination and then explained that people had to organize the search for cases. The draft did not deny the importance of a vaccine. Instead, it asked what an account leaves unexplained when it stops at invention. Readers could learn that someone was immunized without learning how a campaign reached that person. Those are related questions, but they are not identical.

3. The committee placed two notes beside its label. One identified the source of a historical claim. The other explained why a medical term needed checking in a glossary as well as a general dictionary. A familiar word can become more precise in a specialist account. An unfamiliar ending, however, does not make every part of a word mysterious.

4. The students finally chose a label that left room for further inquiry. It named a discovery, described organized work, and acknowledged that its short explanation could not contain every contribution. No short label is a panacea for every question a visitor might bring. Their display offered a useful starting point for reading the sources, rather than a substitute for them. A visitor could challenge the emphasis by returning to those sources.

A. Counting What Stayed

1. Our trial exhibit explained smallpox eradication. Forty visitors read its labels. Afterwards, thirty could name 1980 as the year the world was declared free of smallpox. Only eight could explain surveillance, the systematic observation and reporting of disease cases. These results give the label editors reason for confidence: most visitors retained the exhibit's main date.

2. A short label cannot teach everything. Our evaluation instrument, a sheet of questions used to collect visitors' answers, asked both about the date and about methods. The date question concerned the ending of the story; the methods question concerned epidemiological work, the study of disease distribution and its causes in populations. We gave the date greater weight because a visitor needs an ending before investigating the details.

3. The date became the hinge of our explanation, connecting the opening question to a definite outcome. We propose the commanding heading "An Ending Worth Remembering" to give the result authority. We credit the editors' clear chronology for the strong date recall. The next trial can investigate methods more closely without discarding what these labels achieved.

B. Listening for an Explanation

1. The same trial left us with a different judgment. Thirty of forty visitors remembered 1980, but only eight explained surveillance. One visitor said, "I remember when the story ended, but I cannot explain how people found cases." Another asked for a diagram showing what a case report could do. These remarks expose a gap that the date count alone conceals.

2. We used the same evaluation instrument but gave explanation greater weight than recall. A cautious account distinguishes remembering a date from understanding a process; a reckless account calls any remembered detail full understanding. The visitors' difficulty was not evidence of carelessness. It was evidence that our labels needed to make their explanation easier to follow.

3. We credit the visitors' questions for identifying the next editorial task. We propose adding a report diagram and testing whether readers can explain its steps. This is a proposal, not a result already achieved. Our account treats the trial as an unfinished inquiry into what readers understand, rather than a completed success for the label editors.
