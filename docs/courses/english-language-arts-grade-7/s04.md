Fresh pair, Curio Class, 2026. Rights: ours. Historical statements draw on the primary selections in field 10. The museum and its proposed diagram are invented for practice. Keep this pair sealed until day 105.

**A. Could a Refusal Be Reviewed?**

1. A list of enrolled Athenian citizens records an outcome, not every decision that led to it. In section 42, Aristotle describes what happened if a man's local body, the deme, refused enrollment because it judged him not free born. He could appeal to the law-courts. The first refusal did not necessarily settle the matter.

2. The deme appointed five of its members to argue against the applicant in court. If he won, he could be enrolled without further question from the deme. If the court upheld the refusal, the state sold the applicant as a slave. An appeal therefore offered another hearing, not a promise of admission.

3. Some readers imagine that an appeal automatically reversed the first decision. Others assume that a local refusal could never be challenged. The account supports neither view. It distinguishes the initial judgment from a contested review. Explaining that distinction shows how a citizenship decision could be challenged without pretending that the challenge always succeeded.

**B. Draw Both Branches**

1. Our invented museum plans a diagram about this enrollment appeal. An editor suggests one arrow from "appeal" to "enrolled". That would be easy to follow. It would also turn one possible outcome into the only outcome.

2. Draw a decision point at the court, with one branch for admission and another for an upheld refusal. Show the deme's five accusers beside the hearing. That detail explains why an appeal was a contested proceeding rather than an automatic correction.

3. Another editor wants only the final citizen list, arguing that the decision process takes too much space. A list can show who was enrolled, but not how a refusal was challenged. Keep both branches even if the diagram needs more room. Visitors should see the choice the court made, not an arrow that silently makes it for them.
