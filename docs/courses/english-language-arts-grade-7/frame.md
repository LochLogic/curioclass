# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 7

Course id ela-k12 (grade 7 row), grade 7, draft frame, written 2026-11-14; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6, each one specific topic and its set of texts: a ranch family's drought year, the Dust Bowl, the
  fight against smallpox, who could speak in Athens, the marathon and the Olympic Games in poems and a play, and
  the Klamath River after its dams came down.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units and 12 at course level (6 for three course-level checks of 2 days, 6
  respond days, 2 after each check). It assumes one ELA lesson of about 50 minutes every school day, five a week,
  for 32 weeks, about 20 minutes of independent reading on four nights a week, logged, and 15 minutes of in-class
  independent reading twice a week, when the teacher confers with each student about once every two weeks. Choice
  books come from the Curio Class Reading Room shelf, printable, or any book the student already has. The course
  places 28 progression rows: a long text read whole, three pieces through the full writing process and six shorter
  ones, two research projects (taught and published with links in unit 3, applied again in unit 6), two findings
  talks (units 3 and 6) and a formal text discussion at least every two weeks; the units are weighed by that load in the table, not given
  equal shares.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 7 (RL, RI, W, SL and L). Every grade 7 code is
  owned by a row placed below, and every lettered part (W.7.1a to e, W.7.2a to f, W.7.3a to e, W.7.9a and b,
  SL.7.1a to d, L.7.1a to c, L.7.2a and b, L.7.3a, L.7.4a to d, L.7.5a to c) is named in the unit that teaches it.
  The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md and the approach is docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule governs every unit (rule
  b, facts and backed sources, for the disease, soil and river texts).
- **Base text.** None: ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Texts are chosen
  by BASELINE rule 29: anchors are original Curio Class texts written to the grade 7 text spec and read by a
  qualified ELA teacher before use, and recent US government nonfiction, public domain as federal works (17 U.S.C.
  105). An older public-domain text (published 1930 or earlier, or a recording fixed before 1923) appears only as a
  paired or optional read, never the anchor: in units 3 and 4 the standard is taught first on the original and
  federal anchors and then applied to short paired primary excerpts read with full support (the 1717, 1721 and
  1798 smallpox letters; Pericles; the 1912 recording). The grade 7 text spec gives each original literary text its
  own named narrator or speaker, voice and setting, and no two anchors in a unit share a voice. Each original is a
  commission to that spec; its text record (the measure where usable, the qualitative demands, reader and task, the
  placement reason) comes with the scope and sequence. Each unit's texts and their rights line are in the Units
  table's C3 column (ELA has no C3 framework; see "For the lead"). Images inside government pages are checked one by one.
- **Reading level.** The 2012 supplement's grades 6 to 8 band (925 to 1185 on its first measure). Working split,
  ours: grade 7 anchors centred in the middle of the band, more at its top with support (the dam-removal science reports, the
  smallpox articles; the paired Pericles excerpt and smallpox letters are read with full support). A poem or play rests on the qualitative judgment
  and the task. Anchors aim at about 55 percent informational, counted in the scope and sequence.
- **Knowledge threads.** Thread 1, citizens and government (e7-reading-range): grade 6 read the first law codes;
  grade 7 reads Athenian democracy and who counted as a citizen in unit 4 and the Greek games in unit 5, beside
  social studies grade 7's Greece units; words reused: law, code, justice, with citizen and democracy added.
  Thread 2, ecosystems, resources and human impact: grade 6 read Earth's hazards; grade 7 reads drought in unit 1,
  soil lost to the plough in unit 2 and a river's ecosystem returning in unit 6, beside science grade 7's
  ecosystems and resources units; words reused: system, data, evidence, with population, resource and impact added.
  Unit 3 (disease) builds a third word set, from the Greek and Latin roots of medicine.
- **Writing calendar.** Nine pieces: argument 3 (unit 4 process piece and a short on-demand piece first, unit 6
  on-demand), informative 3 (unit 2 process piece, unit 3 research article, unit 6 on-demand research write-up),
  narrative 3 (unit 1 process piece, unit 2 on-demand Black Sunday scene, unit 5 on-demand scene), a third each
  against the rubric's 35, 35 and 30, counted by pieces; the scope and sequence also reports them by days. Every
  reading day ends in an evidence paragraph or a written question (W.7.9, W.7.10). Typing: about two pages in one
  sitting by unit 2, three by unit 6.
- **Paper path and no accounts.** Every anchor has a print copy. Curio Class has no student accounts and hosts no
  student work. Unit 3's research is drafted with partner comments in a shared document and published in whatever
  the school already uses, with live links to sources, on a shared classroom device or in a lab session in the
  school day; a room without devices comments on printed drafts, writes by hand and lists the sources' addresses,
  and the plan says plainly that the paper path does not meet the linking and collaborating parts of W.7.6, which
  are recorded unfinished for that class.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read, and every original text gets the
  qualified ELA teacher's read (rule 29). Unit 3 (disease deaths, Onesimus, an enslaved man) is named for an
  African American history reader, unit 2 (farm families' losses) is flagged for care, and unit 6 is named for a
  Karuk and a Yurok reader, the Tribes who pressed for the Klamath dams to come down (who, and any pay, is Cory's).
