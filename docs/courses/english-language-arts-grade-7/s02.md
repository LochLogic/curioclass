## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why did the soil blow away in the 1930s, and how did people bring it back?"
Day 31 opens with the feature's first soil-loss scene. Students locate what disappeared and ask what held it before. Introduce the compelling question without supplying its answer.
Supporting questions: Which two ideas develop across the feature (day 32)? How does the history connect drought with public action (day 35)? What does fiction invent, and to what effect (day 38)? What do the film's selected details clarify (day 41)? Which historical explanation needs qualification beside drought definitions (day 52)?
The day 53 explanation answers the compelling question using the feature and both federal histories: land use increased vulnerability during drought; conservation required sustained practices and cooperation, rather than instantly replacing lost soil.
Model idea development on day 32, causal links with several citations on day 33, historical comparison on day 37 and media analysis on day 39. Step back during the corresponding application days. On days 36, 42 and 52, students prepare written citations, discuss with roles and a deadline, request elaboration, monitor progress and write how new evidence qualified their view. Record individual discussion moves.
