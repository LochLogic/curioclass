# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 7

Course `english-language-arts-grade-7`, grade 7, draft, 2026-11-15.

The 160 course days meet every school day: 148 unit days and 12 course-level days. School day equals course day, so `school_day` stays blank. The frame's six units, order, lengths and 28 owned progression rows stay fixed. `days.csv` is the day map; all sections remain `<unit>.1` until unit planning determines useful section boundaries. There is no base text and no source map to write.

## Decisions that govern the days

The brief's assessment and retrieval rules supersede the frame's older wording. Every unit has an end-of-unit assessment inside its days, including units followed by a course-level check. Each unit has one response block, on its last day; mid-unit response is a separate block responding only to the mid-unit evidence. Course-level response addresses the course check alone. No assessment items, answer keys or scoring guides are written here. Unit plans must provide those guides and collect evidence for every part of every code listed on an assessment row. A submitted product, revision trail, reading log or observed discussion may supply that evidence where the row explicitly says so. Short reading prompts replace additional full essays on reading-assessment days.

Earlier-grade foundations are listed below before their placement. All direct ELA assumptions in the frame/progression point to grade 6; none points directly to a grade 5 or older row. Grade 6 is therefore the provisional last-use grade for every listed `e6` row. Its current `data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-6/days.csv` is absent at this write, so these are progression-based placements, not verified day-level last uses. No forbidden earlier plan was consulted. This limitation needs the lead's attention before the sequence is final: a frame saying that a row was used is insufficient evidence under rule 2.

## Reading, writing and time

Each period has about 50 minutes. On two days each week the CSV reserves 15 minutes for independent reading and conferences, leaving about 5 minutes for word/sentence work, 25 for core work and 5 for the written response. These blocks normally occupy unassessed days. Week 22 has only one such day: its second block follows day 107's submission of the already-written argument product, with no new timed composition shortened. On other teaching days the core can take about 40 minutes. Partner rereading takes four minutes inside the last unassessed day's core work each week. Timed reading-assessment days have no independent-reading block. Four nights each week retain the frame's approximately 20 minutes of independent reading, logged; novella reading assignments are divided between class and this existing home-reading time, with a class catch-up route rather than an increased home requirement. Logs distinguish the common novella from the student's choice book. Students without access to home reading receive the print text and supported reading in class; the unit plan must check the manuscript against this budget before use.

The novella's ten provisional installments are successive tenths, approximately 3,000 to 3,500 words each, completed on days 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 and 19, divided over the adjacent reading days and home assignments. Day 15's installment must be read before the mid-unit assessment. Day 20 consolidates the whole book. Final chapter/page boundaries depend on the unwritten manuscript. Shorter accessible summaries never replace an anchor. Before reading, give the needed context and vocabulary; during reading, chunk difficult syntax and reread the original alongside explanations; after reading, return to the original for evidence. Supports follow the recorded difficulty rather than lowering the text demand.

Formal text discussions occur on days 9, 18, 26, 36, 42, 52, 59, 68, 77, 81, 86, 92, 96, 104, 111, 119, 126, 133, 141, 147 and 155: no gap exceeds ten school days. The eight-minute discussions embedded in editing, publication or response work use prepared text and the same four-part look-for; they are not an extra period. Daily partner talk supports these discussions. Students prepare evidence, track goals and deadlines, elicit elaboration, bring talk back on topic and acknowledge or change views. Frames fade as students demonstrate the routine. The first two weeks include private one-minute oral checks for flagged and new students; weekly rereading continues, without a public rate or a diagnostic claim.

Nine named writing pieces retain the frame's allocation: three argument, three informative and three narrative pieces, with full-process pieces in Units 1, 2 and 4. Dominant writing days for these nine pieces are argument 8 (96, 99 to 103, 107, 151), informative 11 (47 to 51, 53, 78 to 81, 149), narrative 9 (21 to 26, 28, 45, 129). That is about 29%, 39% and 32% of 28 dominant days, alongside the frame's equal piece counts. Model-study days, research reading, short daily responses and course-check writing are additional integrated writing time. These counts do not establish the whole year's 35/35/30 share: a W.7.9 response defending a textual claim counts as argument when the eventual task inventory is counted. The final inventory must report those pieces too, rather than claim balance from nine commissions alone. The three-page typing routine inherited from grade 6 is maintained on days 25, 79 and 102 through drafting/typing existing work in one sitting, rather than assigning three more new compositions; the two-page final explanations/arguments remain as the frame specifies.

The paper path retains every print anchor and written source trail. Unit 3's live linking, digital publication and reciprocal comments are scheduled during school on a shared device or in a lab, with no student account. If unavailable, record those W.7.6 parts unfinished. A chart alone does not demonstrate multimedia in SL.7.5; a transcript alone does not demonstrate listening or sound techniques. Teacher-read audio supplies a no-device listening route. Film stills and shot lists support visual reasoning, while unexperienced motion/sound techniques remain unfinished. Tribal pages stay linked rather than copied: a paper-only route to their full published voices needs a rights-respecting arrangement, still unresolved.

## Shorter path

For a shorter period, protect the anchor, its text-dependent response, assessment parts and one feedback action. Split a long passage between the existing home assignment and the next class reread; shorten duplicate practice and public sharing, retaining small-group performance and required media access. Independent reading remains twice weekly with rotating conferences. A 40-minute period with a 15-minute reading block leaves about 3 minutes for words, 18 for core work and 4 for response. The full novella manuscript and selected reports must be timed against that budget before this path can be called runnable; no easier replacement text or unrecorded extra home assignment is assumed.
