# Course frame: Media Arts, grades K to 2

Course `media-arts-grades-k-to-2` (catalog id `media-arts-k-2`), a band course for kindergarten, grade 1 and grade 2, run as three years one after another (year K, year 1, year 2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a looking-and-tools unit, an ideas-and-production unit, a
  making-and-judging unit, and a screening unit that ends the year with the class showing. Each unit joins two
  progression threads, and every unit makes, studies another maker's media work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5).
  Media arts here means pictures, sound and moving pictures framed, put in order, captured and shown; in K to 2 most
  of it is made with paper media tools (a card viewfinder, shadow puppets, picture cards in a paper stage, flip
  books, a paper film strip, a picture scroll), and a shared school device is one way in, never the only one.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 5 minutes, devices and trays out), the teaching and
  making, and clean-up, hand-back of devices and collecting work (about 5 minutes in a paper or device session, 7 in
  a cutting and gluing session); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and one
  respond day after it. Unit days follow the rows' load: the second unit of each year is longest (22 days) because it
  owns five ids, two techniques and the production roles; the first (18) sets up the tool rules and the looking and
  listening routine; units 3 and 4 of each year (10 each) make from the plans and tool skills already built, and the
  screening unit's making is joining work. These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md)
  spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time
  figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): no separate media arts weekly minimum was found in the visual and media arts
  association's elementary checklist or its general PreK to 12 program standards (the second-model read, at source,
  2026-11-25; the elementary 50-minute figure is for visual arts and is not borrowed). This course gives 80 minutes
  a week, 40 on the once-a-week path; no numerical comparison is claimed (For the lead item 2). The course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a media arts teacher
  uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look or listen at a work that carries the unit's response and its
  connecting sentence; one making session with the critique; one revision session (the kept cards finished, the
  rotating look-fors marked); the check; one respond session after every mark is in. Fluency days fold into the
  openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path, every year: units of 8, 9, 5
  and 6 sessions; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond session (32 in all). Weeks: the first
  unit in weeks 1 to 8, the second in weeks 9 to 17, the third unit's first three sessions in weeks 18 to 20, the
  mid-year check in week 21 and its respond session in week 22, the third unit's last two sessions in weeks 23 and
  24, the screening unit's six sessions in weeks 25 to 30, the year-end check in week 31 and its respond session
  in week 32. So the third unit pauses for the mid-year check; the year-end check comes after the screening unit, as
  on the full path, one week earlier because the once-a-week year has one session in week 32 (as in media arts 6 to 8).
  A five-session unit (3, 7, 11) runs demonstration, making with the critique, revision, check, respond. A six-session
  screening unit (4, 8, 12) runs demonstration, making with the critique, revision, the screening (check 1), the talk
  check (check 2), respond. The rotating look-fors of the last making days (Scored evidence) fall on the last two
  making sessions, fifteen children a session. No assessment is ever heard in a respond session. A merged day never
  drops a critique, a revision or a respond session; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, media arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year K claims only the 18 kindergarten ids (`.K`, `.Ka` and so on), year 1 only the 18
  grade 1 ids, year 2 only the 18 grade 2 ids: 54 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and
  assessed in its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. No
  pre-kindergarten id is claimed. Every year touches all four artistic processes (creating, producing, responding,
  connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed
  "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it
  studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, and each id's action
  against the national standard's own text where a words line may have drifted (For the lead item 1), the nine
  criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md);
  the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence
  still states its id count (18) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a child's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Media arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain films, photographs and posters from library and archive collections (the Library of Congress, film
  archives), and, for living or recent makers, a description in our own words with a link to the maker's, museum's
  or film board's own page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit
  plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work must keep the unit's ids
  met and the year's range. Each work is studied once in the media arts line, K to 12, and the later bands check
  against this one; a maker may return with a different work, named as such (unit 4's McLaren). Each year's works include living makers and more than one region and period (APPROACH
  "Works and their settings"); across the band they come from Africa (Mali, Nigeria), the Americas (Kiowa and
  Apsáalooke makers, Mexico, Canada, the United States), Asia (China, India, Korea) and Europe, since open film and
  photograph collections lean to old European and American work. Media remix uses only public domain or openly
  licensed sources, named on the piece. Curio Class writes every step card, work card, picture card set, record sheet
  and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Framing: holding a card viewfinder and choosing what is inside
  the frame (K), far, middle and close shots drawn or taken (1), placing the main part big or small, at the center or
  the edge, to draw the eye (2). Order and motion: shadow puppets on a stick and a three-box storyboard (K), a flip
  book of four to eight pages and a six-frame strip with a repeat (1), paper-cutout stop-motion (move a little,
  capture, move again) from a storyboard (2). Sound: a listening walk with a picture sound map (K), a found or voice
  sound made on cue at a page turn (1), a sound recorded and shaped (louder, softer, a longer pause) or written as a
  sound score and performed the same way twice (2). Presenting: a pull-through paper film strip screened with named
  roles (K), a picture-book tale shown with one task and the showing conditions (1), a narrated picture-scroll
  screening with conditions named and sources credited (2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills of a technique with feedback (a "far, middle,
  close" framing call with the viewfinder, a three-card order drill, a flip-book page drill, a name-that-sound
  listening game); in K to 2 it is mostly the five-minute opener and one or two fluency days a unit. Application:
  making with some choice; the second making day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the
  next day's revision is application too. Check: the self-check and the talk and table items of the unit's evidence
  line; a kept piece is scored after the session, and a screening is scored where it happens. Respond: a second try
  at the part marked "not yet". A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with
  the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept piece after the session; a kept card or sheet made on the
  unit's last making days (a storyboard, a screening-plan card, a source card), scored with the piece; talk items on
  an unseen public domain, openly licensed or Curio Class original piece, at most one talk item a child on any one
  day; rotating look-fors at the tray; and, in units 2, 4, 8 and 12, look-fors marked at a screening. Rotating
  look-fors: every safe-use, tool-use or role item (MA:Cn11.1.Kb, 1b and 2b; MA:Pr5.1.1c, 2a and 2c) is never an
  end-of-unit check day item; a course-level check may sample knowing the rule by a fresh paper task, and the use
  stays assessed in the unit. It is marked on a class list over the unit's last three making days, ten children a day at the tray for
  about a minute and a half each while the class makes, so the teacher sees the tool used, not described; where a
  unit's evidence has a second talk item, it is heard in the same rotation, so no child answers two talk items on one
  day. A talk check day's 40 minutes: set-up 5; each child's self-check on the guide 5; six groups of five at the
  teacher's table for about three and a half minutes each (21), one talk item a child; clean-up 4; 5 minutes of
  slack for a child who needs longer and for the turns between groups. That is 35 minutes planned. While a group is
  at the table, the others draw on a quiet drawing card (a picture prompt from the unit they already know) or finish
  and label work with paper and crayon; no device is out but the one on the teacher's table, which faces the room so
  every table is in sight. A second adult, where there is one, watches the room; the teacher watches one table, never
  a room and a table at once. A screening check day (units 2, 4, 8 and 12, the first of their two check
  days): set-up 5; six groups of five screen in turn at the one screen, stage or box for about four minutes each
  (24) while the rest are the audience and the teacher marks each child's look-fors at the screening place; each
  child's self-check 5; clean-up 4. No talk items are heard that day; they come on the unit's second check day. The
  piece's draft, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision come on
  earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a
  baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every child has two marks
  a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the portfolio folder (paper, or a file on the
  school's device) with a one-line reflection, spoken to the teacher or a partner in K to 2 (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Year K assumes no earlier media arts learning (pre-kindergarten ids are not taught; a
  kindergarten unit may use one as a readiness reference). Every year 1 and year 2 unit assumes the year before's
  rows in the same threads, last used one grade back, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never
  a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from the band's order
  (K, then 1, then 2). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a child may meet year 2 before year 1: each
  retrieval task then carries its ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH "Readiness and the
  baseline look"). The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Arts are specials in K to 2 and do not count against the classroom teacher's stagger
  (CALENDAR.md rule 1). Mid-year: week 21 in every year (course days 41 and 42 of the year). Year-end: week 32 in
  every year (days 63 and 64 of the year), after the screening unit of weeks 27 to 31, whose screening is its own
  end-of-unit assessment (lead structure pass 2026-11-26, the media arts group read's finding 1, option (a)).
- **Recording and kept work.** No child is recorded, photographed or filmed without the family's yes; a face or a
  voice is never required (hands, the work, a shadow or the sound alone will do); a child who does not want to be
  recorded is observed live and the teacher's mark stands. Media in the room points at things, places and work,
  never at a child who has not said yes. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device and goes
  nowhere else (APPROACH "Kept work"); no child posts or shares media of others online (the rows' teacher notes).
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 4 (the screening joins a class movement piece to the film
  strip; a found-sound track in place of the movement keeps every id met), unit 9 (the artist's photographs show
  Apsáalooke dress that may carry ceremonial meaning, studied by looking only) and unit 10 (ideas are acted out to
  improvise them; quick sketches keep every id met without acting). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving
  a unit out means the course page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, crayons, card from cereal boxes, found objects for
  sound; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One
  child at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, every partner or team task has a doubled
  version (parent and child), and the showing is to the family. The teacher needs no media arts training: each
  unit's teacher page says what to look and listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as six
  flashlights counted once, printing of step cards, work cards and picture cards), all optional beside the free
  path: year K $5, $15, $5, $10 ($35); year 1 $5, $10, $5, $10 ($30); year 2 $10, $10, $5, $15 ($40). Planning
  figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. Where a school has them, one shared school device (a camera, a sound recorder or a
  free drawing program) per group of five is one way in; one teacher screen may show a work, a step or a playback.
  Every day runs from the printed step card, work card and paper media tools without them, and no student account is
  used. The owned codes that name capture, recording or a tool (MA:Cr3.1.Ka, MA:Cr3.1.1a, MA:Pr5.1.Ka to Kc,
  MA:Pr5.1.1a to 1c, MA:Pr5.1.2a to 2c) are met in full on paper: in this course a media tool is anything that
  frames, captures, orders, plays or shows pictures and sound, so a paper-path child captures by drawing what the
  viewfinder frames or by a shadow held on the screen, records a sound as a picture-mark sound score performed the
  same way twice, and names and shows each method as a device user does (For the lead item 5). Shared devices are
  school-approved; each is wiped and handed back by the device card's steps.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, card, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  frame-sequence viewer that plays drawn frames in order, a work viewer) has a printed version that does the same job
  (the flip book, the paper film strip) and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards are picture-led with the words read aloud for K to 2; no child reads a card alone.
  English learners: each unit teaches its six words in depth, with a picture, a gesture or a sound and a frame ("I
  see ___ because ___." "It sounds ___."), a child may show the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what
  the child made apart from what they said (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against three free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov), the World Health Organization's
  "Make Listening Safe" pages on safe headphone volume (who.int), and the web accessibility guidelines' rule on
  flashing, success criterion 2.3.1 "Three Flashes or Below Threshold" (w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH
  "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): no filming, photographing or recording a child without
  the family's yes; no flashing light in any piece, screen or shadow play, and no strobe; screen breaks, with no
  child at a screen for more than about ten minutes at a stretch; headphones, where used, at low volume, and no loud
  sound in a child's ear; flashlights are low-power and never shone in eyes, and no laser pointers; device cords
  taped down or none; card cut by an adult with a blade, children use blunt-tip scissors; supplies labeled
  non-toxic; hands washed after glue and found objects; no small items in the mouth. A movement day (unit 4) uses a
  clear floor with room to move arms out, no jumping from heights and no partner contact. Each unit's last column
  gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (Bamako
  studio portraiture, Mali), unit 2 (the Arabian Nights setting of a 1926 German film), unit 3 (lianhuanhua picture-story books, China),
  unit 5 (Kiowa photographs, Oklahoma), unit 7 (Nigerian hairstyles photographed), unit 8 (two Mexican makers' books, one on
  Frida Kahlo), unit 9 (Apsáalooke photographs; 1930s United States posters), unit 10 (an Indian animated film) and
  unit 11 (a Korean-born American video artist) and unit 12 (patua scroll painters, West Bengal). Every task that ties media to a child's own life or day (units 1, 4,
  5, 8, 9 and 12) offers an invented, story-character or class-shared option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive
  ground"). No task asks what a family watches, owns or posts, and home media is never judged. Each section carries
  health's steps for "If a student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, work
  cards, paper media tools, found objects, an optional shared device. Activities: technique taught in steps, works
  studied with their setting, making with a critique and a revision, a showing or screening each unit. Outputs: a
  portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per child on the main
  look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 18 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried
  with children, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

Kindergarten opens the band and the subject: there is no course before and no pre-kindergarten course. The eight
kindergarten threads that start here with no earlier row are ma-k-ideas, ma-k-make, ma-k-craft, ma-k-present,
ma-k-read, ma-k-judge, ma-k-society and ma-k-sources (their `assumes` cells are empty); each is first used in the unit
named in the table below.

## Cross-subject

No media arts row of grades K to 2 carries a cross_subject lean (data/progressions/arts.csv), so this course waits
on no other course and no other course waits on it. The contacts below are practiced, not owned, each with its "if
later" line; all are core courses (lesson 32).

### Mathematics, kindergarten to grade 2 (same year)

| Used here | Contact | If math has not taught it yet |

### English language arts, kindergarten to grade 2 (same year)

Talk about media pieces and the critique practice collaborative conversation (SL.K.1, SL.1.1, SL.2.1), and telling a
picture-card tale practices retelling with key details: practiced, not owned. If ELA has not yet taught the
turn-taking rules, the critique card shows them in pictures (look, listen, one at a time) and the teacher models them
on the first critique day.

### Social studies, kindergarten to grade 2

Unit 9's "life of a time, a place and a people" (year 2) sits near social studies grade 2's unit 6, "Family stories
and culture". No lean either way: the art unit teaches the setting it uses itself, on work cards (bar criterion 7),
and never asks a child to explain their own family's culture.

### Computer science (not a core course)

STANDARD.md 6: media arts teaches the one idea of privacy or credit its unit needs in a short step and does not wait
for computer science. Here: asking before a picture and no faces (units 1, 5 and 9) and naming a source (units 9 and
12). Computer science goes deeper on attribution in grade 8 (computer-science:g8-attribution); its K to 2 courses
are not core, so nothing here assumes them.
