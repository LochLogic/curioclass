# Section 1.1: Where does the gas come from?

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 7. Unit 1, Chemical reactions and matter. How can we make something new that was not there before? Anchor: a bath bomb fizzing away in water while its mass goes down. (days 1 to 23): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 6.** Days by kind: understanding 4 (1, 2, 3, 6), application 2 (4, 5). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with students. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 4 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 50 minutes.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MS-PS1-2** (days 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6): Compare property data for substances from before and after they interact to decide whether a chemical reaction took place.
- skill s7-reactions-atoms (grade 7)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 1:** Watch a teacher add a plain bath-bomb blend to water. Record fizzing, disappearing solid and competing gas explanations. Introduce the compelling question in 15 minutes; safety takes 25 minutes, readiness ten.
>
> **Question board, day 4:** Mass records address "Did matter disappear?" on day 6; gas identity remains unresolved. Ingredient comparisons answer "Which combination?" on day 10. Gas records narrow candidates on day 12, raising "How could new particles form?" Heating and electrolysis comparisons address this on day 17. Atoms answer "What stays?" on day 20; odor records address receptors on day 21. Keep unanswered questions visible. Transfer: unfamiliar stone weathering, field 5.
> **Carried:** Keep the particle model. Individual drawings begin day 2, the class version day 4. Revise boundaries from mass records on day 6, inputs and outputs from properties on day 14, competing mechanisms on day 15, and atom arrangements from heating and electrolysis evidence on day 18. Day 19 adds repeating crystals; day 20 establishes the conserved-atom consensus used in transfer. Claim-evidence-reasoning begins day 4, adds qualified property claims on day 12, distinguishes evidence from historical authority on day 18, and supports the day 23 explanation. Neither model is withdrawn.
<!-- /skel -->

- **MS-PS1-2, this section's share.** Students "compare property data for substances from before and after they interact". Here they learn the habits that come first: separate what was seen from what is inferred, ask what a measurement could and could not show, and read a before-and-after record. The decision "whether a chemical reaction took place" is not made in days 1 to 6; the first full property comparison is day 13.
- **Limits that bite here.** Only density, melting and boiling points, how well it dissolves, whether it burns and smell are analysed for MS-PS1-2, and none is measured in this section. Mass before and after is a check on whether matter crossed a boundary, never a property that identifies a substance and never the evidence that decides a reaction. Days 2 and 4 use the particle side of the cards only; atom labels and the word "atom" wait for day 14 and day 18. No gas is named. No closed rigid vessel, no student mixing, no tasting or sniffing.
- **Connections.** MS-PS1-5 groundwork: mass across a boundary, days 5 and 6. Developing and using models: days 2 and 4. RST.6-8.7 (a table read as evidence): day 5. WHST.6-8.1 (a claim backed by evidence): day 4.
- **Practices.** Developing and using models (days 2, 4, 6); analysing and interpreting data (days 5, 6); argument from evidence (day 4 onward).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the section never names the gas: the class narrows candidates on day 12 and the unit leaves identity open. That gas has mass: in a closed flexible pouch the reading stays the same because the gas is still inside the boundary, and in an open cup it falls because gas leaves. That equal mass does not identify a gas and does not rule out trapped gas. That bubbles are not proof of a reaction. The three tiers of evidence used here, said aloud to students: our own reading, a supplied record, an invented record (weaker than collecting it).

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Items 1, 2 and 3 on the unit plan belong to section 1.4; the day 13 checkpoint belongs to section 1.3. Section exits for days 6 and 12 come from s01 field 5; the other exits here are ours (ours, untested).

**Exit questions.** If many (more than a third) miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. Each is answered alone on paper from fresh evidence, never the day's worked cases.
- **E1 (day 1, readiness).** Items 1 and 2 of s01 field 4, five minutes each, alone. Answers are on the unit plan. Wrong: "Powders that look alike are the same substance" (A); "Mixing two liquids always makes a new substance" (B). Also the group observation sheet, read after class: a note that sorts "what we saw" from "what we think is happening".
- **E2 (day 2).** Items.csv s01-X2. A classmate says chalk in vinegar "vanished"; drawing 1 shows no particles left in the open cup, drawing 2 shows some leaving through the top. (a) Which drawing agrees with her, and what does the other show? (b) Write a question a mass reading could answer, and what a lower reading shows. Answer: (a) drawing 1 agrees; drawing 2 shows the matter did not vanish, some of it left the cup. (b) for example "Did anything leave the cup?"; a lower reading means something left the cup. Wrong: "Nothing is left, so the matter is gone" (D); "The mass will not change because gas weighs nothing" (C).
- **E3 (day 3).** Items.csv s01-X3. A student drops a seltzer tablet in water and says, "The gas comes from the water, not the tablet." (a) Name one test that could count against her claim and the result that would count against it. (b) Before a teacher-only demonstration a classmate's long hair hangs loose beside the tray; say one thing to do. Answer: (a) plain water in a matching cup; if it does not bubble, that counts against her claim. (b) tie the hair back before anything is opened. Wrong: "Bubbles show a reaction, so her claim is proved" (E). A test with no result named gets a written comment.
- **E4 (day 4).** Items.csv s01-X4. A dish of water on a sunny windowsill is dry the next day; a student writes: Claim, the water stopped existing. Evidence, I cannot see it any more. (a) Which part does the evidence fail to support? (b) Name a second piece of evidence. Answer: not seeing it shows only that it is hidden or has moved; a second piece: a clear lid with drops collecting on it, or a sealed dish read before and after. Wrong: "The evidence is enough: it dried up, so the water is gone" (D). A second look is not a second kind of evidence and gets a written comment.
- **E5 (day 5).** Items.csv s01-X5. A puffed flexible pouch of yeast, sugar and warm water reads 61.2 g before and 61.2 g after. A student says the reading did not change, so gas weighs nothing; she opens it and lets the gas out, and it reads 60.8 g. Is her claim supported? How many grams left, and what does that show? Answer: not supported; the gas was inside the boundary; 0.4 g left, so gas has mass. This pouch is not one of the day 5 records (those use 87.6 g and 91.0 g). Wrong: "Equal readings show gas has no mass" (C); "A closed pouch that makes gas should read lower" (F).
- **E6 (day 6, from s01 field 5).** "A vessel sends gas by a leaky tube to a collector. Vessel: 132.8 g before, 132.0 g after. Collector: 44.5 g before, 45.1 g after. (a) How many grams left the vessel, reached the collector, and are in neither? Where could they be? (b) Can these readings tell you which gas it is? Write yes or no and why." Answer: 0.8 g left the vessel, 0.6 g reached the collector, and 0.2 g crossed a boundary into the air through the leak; the readings cannot identify the gas (the same case as s01 field 5). Wrong: "Gas has no mass" (C); "That mass names the gas" (J).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The particle model: each student's own drawing from day 2 stays in the notebook; the class version is built day 4 and its boundary is revised day 6. The question board starts day 4 and stays on the wall. Claim-evidence-reasoning is begun day 4 as a routine retrieved from grade 6, then revised on day 6. The notebook's two columns, "What we saw" and "What we think", carry from day 1. Nothing is withdrawn.
- **Key moment 1, days 1 and 5: mass before and after.** Ideal instrument: a balance reading to 0.01 g with a live display of gas leaving a boundary. Spec: does not exist; a scale to 0.1 g with a capacity of at least 300 g, one for the room, read aloud and shown on the screen. The closed-pouch case cannot be run live, so it is paper only. Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **Key moment 2, days 2, 4 and 6: the particle model with a boundary.** Ideal instrument: an on-screen model with movable boundary and a count of particles in and out. Spec: day 20's simulation (spec only, not used here) partly fits; this section uses printed cards and a paper mat. Gaps and asks: TOOL-ASKS.md.
- **A room with nothing.** Paper only: each record sheet prints on one page; the teacher reads the scale aloud, or uses the supplied card for day 1. This section runs on print and has no key tool.

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding; assessment: readiness. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Observe a fizzing bath bomb, record competing explanations and begin the year safety sheet.
- note: Base 7.1 lesson 1 (ideas only) / After the hook: 15 minutes initial observations, 25 minutes safety instruction, then readiness in the last 10 minutes on s5-identify-materials and s5-new-substances, both last used in grade 5. Targeted response inside days 7 and 10. Retrieve s6-particle-temperature and s6-state-change in day 2.
- credit: adapted from OSE 7.1 L1, Fizzing observations (split); ours, Year safety sheet (ours); ours, Unit 1 prerequisite readiness (ours)
<!-- /skel -->

- **15 min. Hook, demonstration and first observations.** Everyone wears splash goggles and sits at the set distance. 2 minutes: a goggle check (put on, strap snug, no gap). 3 minutes: the compelling question, already on the board, and the notebook's two columns. 4 minutes: the teacher alone mixes at one tray (a 250 mL cup, 50 mL water, at most one eighth teaspoon each of baking soda and citric acid in two paper twists). The cup and its contents sit on the scale, not the tray; the teacher reads the scale three times aloud and on the screen before mixing, waits one minute for the foam to settle, and reads again after the fizz stops, with the empty twists back beside the cup so only gas can leave. 6 minutes: each group of three fills one sheet, "What we saw" and "What we think is happening", and posts one explanation for the gas on a slip.
- **25 min. Year safety sheet.** Goggles, no tasting or sniffing, hair tied, spills wiped, hands washed, the teacher-only list and why; three readings of 3 minutes, a 3-minute write, 5 minutes for the group lines, and 3 minutes of clean-up run with the stop signal rehearsed during it (waste collected for permitted disposal, tray wiped, cup rinsed); 5 minutes are slack for the move to goggles off and the sink.
- **10 min. Readiness check.** Items 1 and 2, five minutes each, alone, on paper; results go to the teacher, never graded.

Real reading and fallback: the scale reads to 0.1 g with a capacity of at least 300 g (borrowed from the science or math department if needed); the expected drop is 0.1 to 0.3 g (from memory, ours, untested). If it shows no change, a rise, or reads whole grams, the teacher says so aloud, records the reading honestly, and says the supplied card "0.3 g lower" is invented and stands in for day 5. One scale, one trial, readings shared: no group waits for a turn.

Who holds it: groups hold the observation sheet; the teacher holds the explanation list for day 4. Tool: None. By hand: observation sheet, readiness paper. Evidence: E1.

## Day 5

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Compare before-and-after mass in open and flexible closed systems.
- note: Base 7.1 lesson 2 (ideas only) / No rigid sealed gas-generating vessel. Supplied flexible-system mass data are the default. Equal mass alone does not identify the gas or rule out trapped gas.
- credit: adapted from OSE 7.1 L2, Mass and gas evidence (split)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Repair of day 4 exit, error D: one supplied record, one sentence.
- **8 min. Words and boundary.** Open system, flexible closed system, boundary; how to read a before and after reading to 0.1 g. A short worked example on paper, not a live pouch: an open cup of vinegar and baking soda, 141.5 g before and 141.0 g after, then the same mix sealed in a flexible pouch, which puffs and reads 141.5 g (invented). (A real puffed pouch on a 0.1 g scale reads a little lower, because of the air it pushes aside, so the closed numbers are tidied and a live run would teach error C.)
- **22 min. Records, groups.** Each of ten groups gets four records: the class's day 1 reading (real, or the invented card), an invented open cup, 214.3 g to 213.9 g, an invented closed pouch, 87.6 g to 87.6 g and puffed, and an invented leaking pouch, 91.0 g to 90.7 g. For each: what is inside the boundary and where did the mass go? All closed readings are said aloud to be invented, because no closed trial is run.
- **10 min. Class.** What mass readings show and what they cannot: not the gas, and trapped gas is not ruled out. Update the board.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E5, alone.

Tool: None. By hand: record sheets, one set per group. Hand-backs in: E4. Evidence: E5.

## Day 6

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Use the mass evidence to revise what remains possible about the gas source.
- note: Base 7.1 lesson 2 (ideas only)
- credit: adapted from OSE 7.1 L2, Mass and gas evidence (split)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Repair of day 5 exit, errors C and F.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Follow mass across a boundary: draw one boundary round vessel and collector. Worked example, invented: vessel 98.6 g to 97.9 g, collector 30.2 g to 30.9 g, combined 128.8 g both times (the collector holds the gas, for example in an absorbent, so its reading rises).
- **15 min. Groups.** Card 1 for 3 minutes; each student draws the larger boundary in pencil on one of the group's three day 4 mats, 6 minutes; test each model against the mass evidence in the grid, 5 minutes, marking which stay possible (all three do: mass cannot tell the source); an Ask, 1 minute.
- **12 min. Class claim.** Return the day 4 claims with the teacher's comment (2 minutes to read it), then revise the day 4 CER: matter did not disappear; it crossed the boundary; the readings cannot name the gas.
- **3 min. Board.** "Did matter disappear?" is answered with its limit; gas identity stays open; new questions are posted.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E6, alone.

Tool: None. By hand: record cards, mat. Hand-backs in: E5; E6 goes to day 7's warm-up. Evidence: E6.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 2, "Which of our questions could a mass reading answer, and which could it not?"; day 4, "What would you need to see to change your mind about your model?"; day 6, "Where did the mass go, and how do you know it did not vanish?"
- Written task, day 4: one individual claim-evidence-reasoning, revised in day 6's class claim.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: the claim says what can be supported so far, not what is proved; the evidence is an observation or reading, not an opinion; the reasoning links them; one limit is named.

- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: observation, inference, boundary, evidence, claim, mass, dissolve. Frame: "I saw __, so I think __, but I cannot tell __."
- **Access.** Record sheets in large print; the scale reading read aloud; a student who cannot hold a pencil dictates the notebook; goggles go over glasses.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, oral answer or scribe; look-fors unchanged.
- **Reading load.** No reading over 120 words alone; all printed text is read aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Day 5: invent a fifth record that shows trapped gas, and say what reading would expose it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table the parent takes the teacher's part on day 1 with a spoonful of each powder, goggles on and no tasting, and a kitchen scale if there is one; if not, use the supplied card. The child keeps the two columns. Paper days run as written.
- **One projector.** Show the scale, the record sheets and the board; groups work from one printed set each.
