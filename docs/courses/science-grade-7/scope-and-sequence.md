# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 7

`science-grade-7`, grade 7, draft, 2026-11-14.

The six units retain the frame's order, 18 owned progression rows, 19 owned performance expectations and 156 unit days. Four course-level days make 160 days, five 50-minute meetings a week over 32 weeks. Course day equals school day, so `school_day` stays blank. The day record is `data/plans/science-grade-7/days.csv`; the lesson decisions are in `source-map.csv`. No assessment items or section plans are written here.

Each unit starts with its phenomenon, a question board and an initial model. Evidence changes that model at the named synthesis points before students transfer it to an unseen case. Sections are question-led runs of three to six days; their day ranges below establish section numbers without writing their plans. The base's lessons stay in order. The source map carries identifiers and ideas only, with original wording and tasks required later. Its 92 base-lesson rows include the two optional zero-day cuts, plus nine rows for our additions and course-level work. Shared transitions and model revisions use the longer periods; a shorter allocation is a merge, not permission to discard evidence.

## Unit 1. Chemical reactions and matter

**23 days: course days 1 to 23, weeks 1 to 5.** Sections 1.1 to 1.4: days 1 to 6, 7 to 12, 13 to 17, and 18 to 23. The question is how a fizzing bath bomb can make a substance that was not there before. Days 1 to 6 establish competing explanations and mass evidence. Days 7 to 13 compare ingredients and gas properties. The class then needs a particle explanation, so heating and electrolysis evidence precedes the atomic account on days 18 to 20. Day 21 connects molecular properties to odor receptors, a contribution that grade 8 completes. The final transfer concerns unfamiliar stone weathering.

This one progression row has a full development load: identifying substances, distinguishing physical and chemical change, and explaining conservation. Its days are not an equal share with a short completion row elsewhere. Day 1 retrieves grade 6 particle-temperature and state-change work. The year safety sheet takes about 50 minutes across days 1 and 3, after the hook and before student mixing. Grade 5 material identification and new-substance ideas are explicitly used on days 7 and 10, making them earlier-year retrieval when unit 3 needs them. Closed-system mass alone cannot identify the gas or eliminate the trapped-gas explanation. No settled atom mechanism is supplied before the evidence creates its need.

The base midpoint is retained on **day 13**: interpret a different gas-producing case using density, melting point, boiling point, solubility, odor and flammability evidence. Day 14's first 15 minutes respond, once. **Day 22 is the end-of-unit assessment**, covering every part of MS-PS1-2 and MS-PS1-5 with property comparisons and a student model that preserves each kind of atom. Inspect simple-molecule and extended-crystal models as contributing evidence for MS-PS1-1, completed in unit 3. **Day 23 responds**, once. No readiness check is required for unit 1's assumed grade 6 rows.

The heating and gas-test portion of base lesson 9 is condensed to day 16. The frame's flame, peroxide, heating and electrolysis work is teacher-only; this sequence chooses supplied observation and property records for those steps. Students still compare measurements, propose tests and critique limitations. No rigid sealed gas-generating vessel is used. Student mixing uses only the named bath-bomb ingredients with eye protection and no tasting. Extended structures are student models, not just pictures to recognize. Nature of science is explicit on day 18: model revision follows evidence.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero added days. Atomic masses, symbolic equation balancing and bonding forces remain outside this unit's boundary.

## Unit 2. Chemical reactions and energy

**18 days: course days 24 to 41, weeks 5 to 9.** Sections 2.1 to 2.4: days 24 to 28, 29 to 33, 34 to 38, and 39 to 41. A flameless meal heater raises the design problem. Initial criteria and an energy-transfer model precede controlled warming/cooling comparisons; amount trials then refine the criteria. Students build, test, compare, modify and retest a device. Partner feedback concerns whether written instructions reproduce the design, not presentation polish.

The two rows have different loads. The new thermal-process idea gets days 24 to 32; the completion of iterative model-based design is carried through days 26 and 31 to 40 using that same evidence. Day 25 retrieves grade 6 energy transfer and insulator design. Days 31 and 32 include a worked grade 6 ratio-table bridge because math's proportional-tables unit, days 13 to 33, is not yet complete. Independent proportionality judgments wait until after day 33.

There is no separate mid-unit assessment requirement for 18 days; the initial trial records and critique on days 35 and 36 guide redesign. **Day 41 is the end-of-unit assessment**. A tested device portfolio supplies direct evidence of designing, constructing, testing and modifying, including a student model that generates data through repeated changes. A new temperature-control case checks transfer across every part of MS-PS1-6 and MS-ETS1-4. Use the first 35 minutes for evidence and the **last 15 minutes for response**, counted once. No extra respond day is hidden outside the unit.

Small, teacher-portioned calcium chloride and ammonium chloride trials replace the base's chemical recipe. Do not assume that every warming or cooling observation proves a chemical reaction: supplied reaction evidence and the earlier property work support that distinction. The meal heater remains teacher-only in a ventilated room, with supplied observations the default here. Eye protection, gloves, no tasting and teacher quantity/temperature controls apply to student salt trials. The no-kit path retains planning, trial comparisons and revision but is explicitly weaker than physically testing a device; a data-table choice alone is not evidence that a student built one. Base lesson 9 takes days 39 and 40, retaining modification, retest and the shortened partner instruction trial.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero added days. Grade 8 owns the final expectations for systematic design comparison and combining features; their starts here add no new ownership.

## Unit 3. Metabolic reactions

**25 days: course days 42 to 66, weeks 9 to 14.** Sections 3.1 to 3.5: days 42 to 46, 47 to 52, 53 to 57, 58 to 62, and 63 to 66. A renamed student health case raises a question about interacting systems. Food-molecule size and absorption evidence come before digestive chemical changes. Scaled intestine images then make the structure-function problem visible. Only after digestion is modeled does the class ask where food matter goes, use combustion records, and model cellular respiration, growth and repair. Days 63 and 64 test the resulting model across systems and animals.

The molecular row completes earlier simple-molecule and extended-structure work; it is developed on days 43 and 47 to 54, with a comparison of large linked molecules, simple molecules and extended crystals. The body-systems completion spans the case and days 55 to 63. The new cellular-reaction row receives the sustained sequence on days 58 to 64. All six permitted systems matter: digestive, circulatory, respiratory, excretory, muscular and nervous, with cells forming tissues and organs. They are studied as interacting subsystems, not six isolated anatomy reports.

Day 42 retrieves grade 6 cells and systems and the material-property ideas already used in unit 1. Day 58 retrieves the matter-cycle model as a model reminder. **Day 54 retains the base digestive-model midpoint**: MS-PS1-1 is assessed in full, with digestive evidence used to guide the next teaching. It does not claim the six-system MS-LS1-3 performance before that work is taught. Day 55's first 15 minutes respond, once. **Day 65 is the end-of-unit assessment**, on an unseen animal case: every part of MS-PS1-1, MS-LS1-3 and MS-LS1-7 through molecular models, a six-system argument and a matter-and-energy model for growth, repair and respiration. **Day 66 responds**, once.

The frame's two three-to-two-day condensations are kept: base lesson 7 model work continues into day 55's opening, and lesson 12 model revision into day 63. Days 58 and 63 explicitly teach the claim-evidence-reasoning bridge, because ELA's grade 7 argument unit is later, days 87 to 108. Burning food is teacher-only, with eye protection and no nuts in the frame; use supplied gas and mass observations here. Indicator work uses no tasting or human body fluids. Every day flags the health sensitivity: no personal health, food, eating or weight disclosures, no body judgments. Mitochondria and food-growth contributions are named without claiming the whole-cell or growth-factor expectations that grade 8 completes.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero added days. Named intermediate respiration steps are excluded.

## Unit 4. Matter cycling and photosynthesis

**28 days: course days 69 to 96, weeks 14 to 20.** Sections 4.1 to 4.5: days 69 to 74, 75 to 80, 81 to 85, 86 to 90, and 91 to 96. Breakfast-label sources pose the question of where plant food originates. Students test possible inputs and revise a photosynthesis model from plant mass and gas evidence before explaining stored food, night respiration and germination. Processing plant products raises the natural-resource versus synthetic-material question. Decomposer evidence then closes a bounded ecosystem model in which matter cycles and energy enters and leaves.

Photosynthesis is the largest new load, developed through day 82 and applied in days 83 to 87. The short synthetic-material row uses days 88 to 90 and the **one added research day, day 94**. Resource, chemical change, material properties and function, source credibility, benefits and harms must all appear in its concise source record. It is driven by the earlier food-processing question. The ecosystem row takes days 91 to 95, using the plant and respiration evidence already developed, rather than reteaching those processes.

**Readiness is on day 68's last ten minutes**, on s5-food-energy-sun, whose use in grade 6 is not evidenced. Day 70 holds response inside the opening sequence. Day 69's hook remains clean. The plant-matter structure-function idea was carried through grade 6 cells; particle, cell, food-web and matter-cycle models have earlier-year or prior-year use and are retrieved at their named points. The grade 5 returning-row rule differs from the frame's blanket retrieval wording; see For the lead.

**Day 82 is the mid-unit assessment**, on every part of MS-LS1-6, including carbon dioxide and water inputs, sugar and oxygen outputs and energy from light. Day 83's first 15 minutes respond, once. **Day 95 is the end-of-unit assessment**, on an unseen ecosystem and source set, eliciting every part of MS-LS1-6, MS-PS1-3 and MS-LS2-3 through an explanation, information synthesis and a student model with a system boundary. **Day 96's first 40 minutes respond**, once; its last ten minutes hold unit 5 readiness, not additional unit 4 response.

ELA informative writing, days 31 to 56, is available. ELA research, days 61 to 86, is available by the source work, but the frame's guided-search bridge remains on day 89: a supplied source set with modeled credibility checks and no outside searching. Complete the short material record in week 19, after ELA's unit 3 and before social studies unit 9, days 101 to 115. It is not a second long report. Seed and leaf observations have explicit supplies, indicator eye protection, humane handling and no tasting; the default has no syrup tasting. No-kit gas/mass evidence and scaled micrographs preserve the reasoning with the stated limitation that students did not gather those measurements.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero candidate days. The frame's required synthetic-material addition is kept inside the 28 days, not counted as a candidate. Biochemical photosynthesis steps and chemical equations for ecosystem cycling are excluded.

## Unit 5. Ecosystem dynamics

**32 days: course days 97 to 128, weeks 20 to 26.** Sections 5.1 to 5.6: days 97 to 101, 102 to 107, 108 to 113, 114 to 119, 120 to 125, and 126 to 128. Rainforest land-cover and orangutan evidence opens a problem involving people and other organisms. Alternatives and local comparisons establish the design need before students define it precisely. Repeated resource/population trials precede cross-ecosystem interaction predictions and disruption arguments. Biodiversity and ecosystem services then become evidence for comparing land-use designs, not a prescribed solution students must repeat.

The five-row load is unequal. Resources and populations get days 102, 103 and 108 to 113; interactions get days 115 to 118; disruption draws on days 97 to 106 and 118 to 119. The short completion of problem definition uses days 104 and 107 and is reused in design. Biodiversity solutions get days 120 to 127, now that students can explain the effects they compare. Comparing individual growth/survival and population numbers, plentiful and scarce conditions, and living and nonliving changes is explicit.

**Day 96's last ten minutes hold readiness for three rows:** s1-parent-offspring, last used in grade 3; the disruption meaning of s3-environment-change-solution, last used in grade 4; and s5-protect-resources, last used in grade 5. **Day 100 reserves a full animal-behavior reteach period; day 101 reserves a full environmental-change period with resource-protection application.** Students already ready extend the anchor evidence. No third period is added: the resource-protection application uses the same environmental-change case. Day 97 retrieves the food web and matter cycling already used this year; day 107 retrieves grade 6 criteria and constraints. The hook has no readiness check.

**Day 113 is the mid-unit assessment**, with new resource/disruption data eliciting every part of MS-LS2-1 and MS-LS2-4, including judging a claim's evidence. **Day 114 responds**, once. **Day 127 is the end-of-unit assessment**, using an unfamiliar land-use case. Every part of all five owned expectations is elicited: individual/population data, predictions across ecosystems, a disruption argument and evidence judgment, comparison of biodiversity and services, and a precisely defined problem with measurable criteria, constraints and effects on people and nature. The revised design record supplies performance evidence. **Day 128 responds**, once.

ELA argument writing is available after day 108. Evaluating arguments is later, days 135 to 156; day 122 therefore models whether evidence supports a claim on the class data. Paper population cards and controlled trial tables preserve access when a simulation is unavailable. Repeated trials and model limitations remain required. Every day flags respectful treatment of Indonesian farmers and Indigenous communities, their varied needs and knowledge, and no blame on families' food choices. Optional base lessons 19 and 20 are cut from the counted path because the frame excludes them and every owned expectation is carried without them. There is no public campaign or outreach in this sequence.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero added days. The two readiness-response periods are frame reservations, not check-set additions.

## Unit 6. Earth's resources and human impact

**30 days: course days 129 to 158, weeks 26 to 32.** Sections 6.1 to 6.6: days 129 to 133, 134 to 138, 139 to 142, 143 to 148, 149 to 153, and 154 to 158. Flood/drought records pose a water-system question. Long-term data and an evaporation comparison come before atmospheric-gas mechanisms and historical records. Resource extraction and geologic distribution then explain carbon sources, and a revised carbon model links these to changing water systems. Students weigh responses and develop a method that both monitors and reduces a water impact, with a baseline, measured outcome, comparison, timing and a rule for revision.

Climate evidence is the longest new development, days 129 to 142 and 146 to 148. The required fossil-fuel formation/distribution extension stays inside base lesson 10 on **days 143 and 144**. **Day 145 is the added mineral-ore and groundwater day**, also considering soil, finite supply and an unfamiliar resource map through rock-cycle and plate evidence. Population and per-person consumption are treated together on days 139, 144 and 150 to 152. The human-impact design completes unit 5's start on days 152 to 157; it cannot be satisfied by a mitigation poster alone.

Day 129 retrieves grade 6 water-cycle and climate-circulation work; day 143 retrieves rock-cycle and plate models and resource ideas used in unit 4. Unit 5 used the older environmental-change and resource-protection rows, so they are now earlier-year retrieval on day 144. No additional readiness is required. Percent increase was taught in math on days 47 to 68, well before day 139; the frame's times-as-much and supplied-percent fallback remains for a school's different timing. Science teaches its own correlation, mechanism and natural-factor comparisons on days 139 to 148, so the reciprocal ELA lean never delays the science. ELA research is available; day 152 still supplies sources and a guided evidence frame.

**Day 148 retains the base lesson 12 assessment point as the mid-unit assessment**, eliciting every part of MS-ESS3-5 and MS-ESS3-4: evidence questions, human and natural factors, population and per-person use, Earth-system effects and changes that can reduce them. Day 149's first 15 minutes respond, once. **Day 157 is the end-of-unit assessment**, on an unfamiliar community: every part of MS-ESS3-1, MS-ESS3-5, MS-ESS3-4 and MS-ESS3-3, including resource geology and a student method for both monitoring and reducing impact. The earlier method and revision records supply design evidence. **Day 158 responds**, once, before the course-level check.

Base lesson 17 takes days 154 to 156, retaining method design, critique, revision and evaluation while shortening rehearsal. These dates overlap the span of ELA's river unit; the exact ELA research deadline is not in the frame/calendar, so the load conflict cannot yet be declared solved. Supplied profiles replace personal consumption disclosures. Every day flags water problems and climate worry: evidence and solutions, no blame. Human activities, chiefly fossil-fuel combustion, are taught as the main cause of recent global warming, with natural factors weighed. Science describes consequences; it does not decide social values. The lamp comparison has teacher set-up, hot-bulb and water/electricity controls, or a real-data no-kit path.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero candidate days. The required resource extension and added geology day are kept inside the frame's 30 days.

## Tools, supplies and access

The day rows name all planned simulations using the frame's tool names. They are planned tools, not a claim that they exist. Every use has matching paper models, cards or trial tables, requires no student account, and can proceed before a digital build. Physical supplies appear on hands-on days, with a hazard/control list or the single word `none` after weighing the paper/data path. Indicator identity and concentration, quantities, setup and disposal need the unit safety protocol before a live trial. Teacher-only high-risk work defaults to supplied evidence here.

Household stand-ins are identified only where used safely. Do not assume an ice-melt product has the required purity. No new purchase is authorized by this sequence. Exact consumable quantities, availability and local costs remain unit-plan work; printing costs are not represented as zero. A school without apparatus uses the named no-kit evidence. That preserves data interpretation, modeling and planning but is weaker for carrying out investigations and physically testing designs; the teacher must label that limit, especially for the device portfolio. Every unit retains its frame-required safety and sensitivity read before lesson use.

## Shorter path

For a shorter period, keep the phenomenon/question link, one necessary evidence comparison, an individual model/explanation and the closing question. Prepare source extracts and data tables in advance; collect a group trial once and pool results, retaining students' variable choices and uncertainty judgments. Use the no-kit comparison when setup cannot fit. Fold repeated recording and whole-class sharing into the next model revision. Never shorten safety instruction, skip a test/retest cycle, remove a listed expectation part, or take time from an assessment or its response. A very short period can distribute the same work across local meetings; 160 shorter meetings are not assumed to preserve 8,000 minutes automatically.

A year about ten course days short can fold these ten unassessed days into their named neighbors. Day numbers below are the full CSV's identifiers, not replacement numbering. Only repeated evidence organization or discussion is folded; the owned performances, data comparisons and individual reasoning remain.
