## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does food come from, and where does it go next?"
**Hook, day 69:** Compare unbranded ingredient labels for oats and maple syrup. Sugar comes from a tree that does not eat. Ask the compelling question and draw competing explanations.
**Closing, day 94:** Explain the breakfast ingredients through the forest model, using growth, gas, seed and decomposer evidence. Distinguish cycling atoms from flowing energy.
**Teaching:** Model controls. Students choose comparisons, challenge claims and revisit questions before investigations; fade frames.

<!-- skel -->
- **MS-LS1-6:** Explain from evidence how photosynthesis moves matter and energy into and out of living things. Emphasis: following where matter goes and how energy flows. Limit: Leave out the biochemical steps of photosynthesis.
- **MS-PS1-3:** Collect and make sense of information showing that human-made materials are made from natural resources and affect society. Emphasis: natural resources changed by a chemical process into the new material; examples include medicines, foods and alternative fuels. Limit: Information used is qualitative only.
- **MS-LS2-3:** Build a model of how matter cycles and energy flows among the living and the nonliving parts of an ecosystem. Emphasis: matter is conserved and energy flows into and out of ecosystems; students set the edges of the system. Limit: Leave out chemical reactions as a way to describe these processes.

**Item 1, invented for practice:** Pondweed grows in a soil-free tank supplied with water and mineral nutrients. Matched illuminated vessels with plants show carbon dioxide declining, oxygen increasing and sugar accumulating; vessels without plants show none of those changes. In darkness, matched plants show no net sugar accumulation. Explain where the added food matter and its stored energy came from, where matter leaves, and why the comparisons support the explanation. Use an annotated model from the unit, adapted to this organism. Do not infer that minerals are unnecessary or that a net measurement reveals each simultaneous process.

**Item 1 key and scoring:** Give two points each for: air and water as matter inputs, supported by the comparison; sugar formation with conserved atoms; oxygen output linked to the gas evidence; sunlight providing energy for rearrangement and storage; and a reasoned comparison with a limitation. Each component earns one for a correct unsupported assertion and two for evidence-linked reasoning. Meets: "The planted light vessel gains sugar and releases oxygen as carbon dioxide falls. Water and carbon dioxide supply atoms; light supplies the energy for making sugar, not carbon atoms. The controls strengthen this explanation, but do not measure each cell process separately." Does not meet: "Roots take food from soil" (A). Separately, "Sunlight turns into plant atoms" shows B.

**Day 93 unscored practice, invented for practice:** A salt marsh contains cordgrass, grass-eating snails, snail-eating crabs and microbes decomposing dead tissue. Supplied observations show sunlight entering, dissolved carbon dioxide entering cordgrass, snails feeding, and dead tissue declining where microbes are present. Microbes return carbon dioxide to air and dissolved nutrients to water and sediment. Organisms release heat; tides carry water and dissolved matter across the marsh edge. Develop a model that explains these observations. Choose and label its boundary, distinguish matter arrows from energy arrows, trace a returning atom route and an energy route, and state one limitation. Chemical equations are not required or used.

**Practice key:** Trace matter from cordgrass through feeding to snails and crabs, and from dead tissue through microbes and nonliving stores back to plants. Sunlight enters; food energy transfers to consumers and microbes; heat leaves. A boundary includes tidal exchanges; arrows omit rates. Give feedback without points.

**Item 2, day 94, invented for practice:** A new woodland contains alder trees, leaf-eating beetles, beetle-eating birds and fungi decomposing fallen leaves. Observations show sunlight entering, carbon dioxide entering leaves, beetles feeding and fallen leaves declining with fungi. Fungi return carbon dioxide to air and nutrients to soil water. Organisms release heat; a stream carries dissolved matter across the woodland edge. The woodland supplies plant sugar for packaging. Model this case independently. Choose and label a boundary, distinguish matter from energy arrows, trace a returning atom and an energy route, and explain how harvesting sugar moves matter and stored energy across the boundary. Name one limit; use no chemical equations.
