# Course frame: Science, Grade 7

Course id science-grade-7, grade 7, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6 units, one for each OpenSciEd grade 7 unit, in its order (7.1 to 7.6): reactions and matter,
  reactions and energy, metabolism, photosynthesis and matter cycling, ecosystems, Earth's resources and human
  impact.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 156 in units, 4 for course-level checks and respond days (two checks of 1 day, each
  followed by 1 respond day). One 50-minute lesson a day, five a week, 32 weeks. The base text plans its six grade
  7 units at 170 days of 45 minutes (7,650 minutes, leaving out unit 7.5's two optional lessons); 156 days of 50
  minutes is 7,800 minutes, so each unit gets its base time (base days times 0.9) plus about 3 days for what the
  rows add: the year safety sheet, two dormant readiness checks with their reteach lessons, retrieval openings on
  grade 5 skills and a model reactivation. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment, where the base unit
  puts its own.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards middle school performance expectations (never named in
  student, teacher or family copy). The course owns 19 codes on 18 progression rows. An expectation started here
  and completed in grade 8 (MS-LS1-2, MS-LS1-5, MS-LS1-8, MS-ETS1-2, MS-ETS1-3) is named in the unit that starts it,
  with the part taught; an expectation completed here (MS-PS1-1, MS-LS1-3, MS-ETS1-1, MS-ETS1-4, MS-ESS3-3) names the
  part done earlier. Practices and crosscutting concepts are counted by element in the unit plans (S11).
- **Base text.** OpenSciEd Middle School, 2022 standard units, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms;
  docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md). Each unit adapts one base unit whole, in the base's lesson order; the "## Source
  map" section places all 18 middle school units and their lessons, and gives the loss check's result (order kept,
  no loss brought to Cory). The "+CS" variant of 7.6 is CC BY-NC and never a base. One short credit line per
  adapted day. Never carried over: third-party photos and videos, the base's character names (the 7.3 student is
  renamed), brand names, kit links.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor phenomenon or design problem (named in the table), builds a
  question board, an initial class model and a consensus model, and revises the model as evidence arrives; each
  lesson answers a question the last one raised. The end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has not seen.
- **Engineering and the nature of science.** Design units: unit 2 (a flameless heater, MS-ETS1-4 completed),
  unit 5 (land use that serves wildlife and people, MS-ETS1-1 completed) and unit 6 (monitoring and reducing a
  human impact on water). The nature of science is taught on purpose in unit 6 (correlation is not cause; many
  lines of evidence; science describes consequences and does not decide what a society should do) and unit 1
  (Dalton's model of atoms revised by evidence, base lesson 11).
- **Materials.** Household stand-ins wherever safe, then a no-kit data path (a data sheet of real readings, said
  plainly to be weaker than collecting them), then a Curio Class simulation for what no class can run (large
  reactions, ecosystem models); simulations are listed per unit now and built later as tools (DESK-ANSWERS
  2026-11-13). Each unit plan states its cost. Every electronic tool has a paper path and needs no student account.
- **Safety.** This is the chemistry year: the year safety sheet is retaught in unit 1's first day with eye
  protection, no tasting, and the teacher-only list (flame, heating, the ration heater, burning food); every
  hands-on day has a Safety and set-up section. A science teacher reads safety and materials before the course is
  called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3; people are Cory's).
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own readings at grade 7 level, written from the base's readings in our words;
  data sets from public agencies (NOAA, NASA, USGS), each with its rights line.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. A school nurse or physician reads unit 3
  (a real digestive disease, weight loss, food); a reader with knowledge of Indonesia's farming communities reads
  unit 5.
