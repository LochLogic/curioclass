## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do things inside our bodies work together to make us feel the way we do?"
**Hook, day 42:** Read Ari's renamed case: stomach pain precedes tiredness and weight loss despite regular meals. Map symptoms without diagnosing; ask the compelling question before modeling.
**Closing, day 63:** Explain reduced absorption and effects on cells using membrane, digestion, lining and blood-gas evidence. Distinguish observations from inferences.
**Teaching:** Demonstrate boundaries and evidence reasoning. Students propose controlled comparisons and critique revisions. Revisit questions before investigations; gradually remove sentence frames.

<!-- skel -->
- **MS-LS1-3:** Argue from evidence that the body is made of subsystems that interact, each built from groups of cells. Emphasis: cells make tissues and tissues make organs suited to particular body jobs. Limit: Only six body systems (circulatory, excretory, digestive, respiratory, muscle and nerve), and not how one system works on its own apart from the others.
- **MS-PS1-1:** Build models showing which atoms make up simple molecules and large repeating structures. Emphasis: models of molecules of differing complexity (ammonia, methanol) and repeating structures (table salt, diamond); models may be drawings, ball and stick kits or computer images. Limit: Leave out valence electrons, bond energy, the ionic character of repeating units, and drawing every single atom of a large molecule or structure.
- **MS-LS1-7:** Build a model of how food is rearranged by chemical reactions into new molecules that help an organism grow or release energy. Emphasis: molecules are taken apart and rebuilt, and energy is released along the way. Limit: Leave out the detailed reactions of photosynthesis or respiration.

**Midpoint item 1, invented for practice:** A laboratory membrane passes small dissolved food molecules while a linked carbohydrate remains inside. Its structure cards show: hydrogen peroxide has two hydrogen atoms and two oxygen atoms in each separate molecule; a carbohydrate molecule contains 400 linked units, each containing carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, with six carbon atoms per unit; a diamond crystal has carbon atoms in a continuous repeating network, each joined to four others. These are supplied structures, not observations students must infer from the membrane alone. Develop and use three models to distinguish atomic composition, separate molecules and extended structure. Explain why a partial model can represent thousands of atoms and what the membrane evidence cannot establish about a living intestine. Do not draw every atom of the carbohydrate. The network drawing must continue beyond its displayed boundary.

**Item 1 scoring, 10 points:** Model the small molecule's kinds and numbers of atoms (2); model the linked carbohydrate with the correct atom kinds and a continuation key (2); represent a repeating connected carbon structure rather than isolated molecules (2); use the models to distinguish molecular size and an extended network at an unobservable scale (2); use the membrane result while naming a limitation of transferring it to a living lining (2).
