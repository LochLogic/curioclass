# Section 1.2: Which properties distinguish the substances?

<!-- skel -->
Science, Grade 7. Unit 1, Chemical reactions and matter. How can we make something new that was not there before? Anchor: a bath bomb fizzing away in water while its mass goes down. (days 1 to 23): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 7 to 12.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (11), application 5 (7, 8, 9, 10, 12). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with students. ## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MS-PS1-2** (days 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12): Compare property data for substances from before and after they interact to decide whether a chemical reaction took place.
- skill s7-reactions-atoms (grade 7)

> **Question board, day 4:** Mass records address "Did matter disappear?" on day 6; gas identity remains unresolved. Ingredient comparisons answer "Which combination?" on day 10. Gas records narrow candidates on day 12, raising "How could new particles form?" Heating and electrolysis comparisons address this on day 17. Atoms answer "What stays?" on day 20; odor records address receptors on day 21. Keep unanswered questions visible. Transfer: unfamiliar stone weathering, field 5.
> **Carried:** Keep the particle model. Individual drawings begin day 2, the class version day 4. Revise boundaries from mass records on day 6, inputs and outputs from properties on day 14, competing mechanisms on day 15, and atom arrangements from heating and electrolysis evidence on day 18. Day 19 adds repeating crystals; day 20 establishes the conserved-atom consensus used in transfer. Claim-evidence-reasoning begins day 4, adds qualified property claims on day 12, distinguishes evidence from historical authority on day 18, and supports the day 23 explanation. Neither model is withdrawn.
<!-- /skel -->

- **MS-PS1-2, this section's share.** Students "compare property data for substances" and learn which properties can tell one substance from another: density, melting and boiling points, solubility, flammability and odor. The "before and after" half comes next: day 10 compares a mixture with a substance whose properties differ, and day 13 compares six properties before and after. Deciding whether a reaction took place stays open until then.
- **Limits that bite here.** Only the six listed properties. Nothing is measured in class except what dissolves and what is seen in open cups; melting, boiling, density and flammability are read from supplied records, and gas flame tests are not run. No sniffing: odor is a supplied record. Student mixing is limited to the named bath-bomb ingredients, at most one eighth teaspoon each in 25 mL of water in an open cup. No atoms yet.
- **Connections.** RST.6-8.7 (a table read as evidence): days 7 and 11. WHST.6-8.1 (a qualified claim with evidence): day 12. MS-PS1-5 groundwork: none until day 14.
- **Practices.** Analysing and interpreting data (days 7, 10, 11); planning an investigation as a fair comparison (day 9); argument from evidence, adding qualified claims (day 12).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That a substance is identified by a profile of several properties, never one. That a property such as density or boiling point does not change with the amount, while the mass of a sample does. That a match in one property rules nothing in; it only fails to rule out. That a gas we cannot name is narrowed, never proved. That the invented gas candidates are coded and the real gas is not announced; the class ends day 12 with the question open.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** none; items 1 to 3 belong to section 1.4. The day 12 exit is from s01 field 5; the others are ours (ours, untested).

**Exit questions.** If many (more than a third) miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example. Each is answered alone, on paper, from fresh evidence.
- **E7 (day 7).** "Liquid M and liquid N sit in matching clear bottles. Each smells faintly sweet. A 50 mL sample of M has mass 40 g; a 50 mL sample of N has mass 50 g. Which property tells them apart? Would weighing 5 mL of M instead of 50 mL change your answer?" Answer: density differs, 0.8 against 1.0 grams per milliliter; smell matches so it cannot tell them apart; density does not depend on the amount. Wrong: "They smell the same, so they are the same substance" (H). A claim that 5 mL changes the density gets a written comment.
- **E8 (day 8).** "A student stirs a white powder into water. It vanishes from view and nothing fizzes. She writes, 'It made a new substance because it vanished.' Sort her note into what she saw and what she inferred, and say what could show whether the powder is still there." Answer: saw, vanished, no fizz; inferred, new substance; a test: let the water dry out and see if the solid returns, or compare the whole cup's reading before and after. Wrong: "It vanished, so it is a new substance" (B); "It vanished, so the matter is gone" (D).
- **E9 (day 9).** "A student plans to mix two clear liquids and says, 'If it bubbles, that proves they reacted.' Name two other trials she should run, and say what set of results would show the bubbles came from the mixing." Answer: each liquid alone, and stirring with nothing added; bubbles only in the combined trial. Wrong: "Bubbles in the mix prove a reaction" (E). A plan that changes two things at once gets a written comment (no register letter fits).
- **E10 (day 10).** "Liquid X alone does not fizz. Powder Y alone does not fizz. Together they fizz. A student concludes, 'A new substance was made.' What does the fizz alone establish, and what further evidence would you want?" Answer: gas appeared only with both, so the pairing matters; fizz alone cannot separate a new substance from a gas that was dissolved and came out, so compare the properties of what is left with each input. Wrong: "Fizz proves a new substance" (E); "Together they made a new substance because they mixed" (B).
- **E11 (day 11).** "Gas S is colorless and does not burn. The gas in a student's bag is colorless and does not burn. She writes, 'They are the same gas.' Is that supported? What would you compare next?" Answer: not supported; matching on two properties does not identify; compare density relative to the surrounding gas and repeat the flame record. Wrong: "They are both colorless, so they are the same" (A); "Neither burns, so they are the same gas" (H).
- **E12 (day 12, from s01 field 5).** "Unknown gas and candidates K and L all fail to burn; unknown and K are denser than the surrounding gas, while L is less dense. Which candidate remains supported, and what repeat measurement could challenge that identification?" Answer: K remains supported, not proved; repeat density under matching conditions. Wrong: "One matching property proves identity" (H).

## Day 7

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Compare bath-bomb ingredient properties and decide which tests could identify a substance.
- note: Base 7.1 lesson 3 (ideas only) / First 10 minutes respond to day 1 material-identification readiness; then compare supplied density, melting-point, boiling-point and odor records and safe solubility evidence; flammability through records only. This is the first checked return, not evidence of grade 6 use.
- credit: adapted from OSE 7.1 L3, Ingredient properties (split)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Repair of day 6 exit, errors C and J.
- **10 min. Return and repair.** The day 1 identification item: look-alikes told apart by a supplied solubility record, then a new pair. Whole class if many missed it; otherwise a small group at the teacher's table while the rest begin the property table below. This is the first of the two checked responses the unit plan names.
- **5 min. Property words.** Density (mass of one milliliter, in grams per milliliter), melting point, boiling point, solubility, flammability, odor: what each says and why amount of sample does not change it.
- **20 min. Property tables.** Each group of three compares the supplied records for baking soda stand-in and citric acid stand-in, with a third look-alike white powder. For each property: does it separate the pairs, and is a record needed or can we test it safely? Flammability and odor are records only. Each cell is marked checked or invented, and invented cells are said aloud to be invented. A cell that reads "not recorded" stays an open question.
- **5 min. Class.** Which tests could identify a substance; which could not (color, amount).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E7, alone.

Tool: None. By hand: record sheets, one set a group. Hand-backs in: E6 (errors C, J). Evidence: E7.

## Day 8

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Compare single ingredients in water and record which changes occur without combinations.
- note: Base 7.1 lesson 3 (ideas only)
- credit: adapted from OSE 7.1 L3, Ingredient properties (split)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Repair of day 7 exit, error H.
- **5 min. Safety brief and roles.** Goggles on for all mixing, hair tied, no tasting or sniffing, open cups on trays, stop for a spill or irritation; pourer, stirrer, recorder, rotated.
- **17 min. Trials.** Ten groups, each with its own tray, two 250 mL cups, two spoons, a cup marked at 25 mL, 100 mL water and two teacher-portioned powders (20 portions, made the day before). Trial 1: 25 mL water and at most one eighth teaspoon of baking soda stand-in. Trial 2: 25 mL water and at most one eighth teaspoon of citric acid stand-in. Record dissolves or not, bubbles, clear or cloudy, other changes. No combining.
- **5 min. Clean-up.** Wipe, collect waste for permitted disposal, rinse equipment; handwashing in turns, the rest of the group wiping while one washes.
- **13 min. Pairs and class.** Compare the two records with the day 7 tables: what each ingredient does alone; what "no change seen" means.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E8, alone, hands dry.

Real reading and fallback: students measure what they can see in their own cups. If a powder will not dissolve in two minutes of stirring, or nothing changes, they record "no change seen after 2 minutes"; that is a reading. If a cup is spilled or a portion is lost, the group uses the supplied observation card, said aloud to be invented and weaker than its own trial, and does not stop. Equipment counts: ten groups, each with its own kit; no group waits for a turn; at least two sinks or basins, or handwashing in turns runs into the next part.

Tool: None. By hand: trial record, supplied card. Hand-backs in: E7. Evidence: E8.

## Day 11

- note: Base 7.1 lesson 5 (ideas only)

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Repair of day 10 exit, errors B and E.

- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E11, alone.

Real reading and fallback: no gas is measured; records are supplied and invented, said aloud. If a record is missing, the group leaves the candidate open and says what it would ask for. Tool: None. By hand: candidate grids, one a group. Hand-backs in: E10. Evidence: E11.

## Day 12

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Use repeated gas-property evidence to challenge an identification claim.
- note: Base 7.1 lesson 5 (ideas only)
- credit: adapted from OSE 7.1 L5, Gas identification (split)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Repair of day 11 exit, errors A and H.
- **5 min. Qualified claims.** "Is supported", "is not ruled out"; why one run is not enough.
- **15 min. Groups.** Three supplied runs of the bag gas's relative density against one candidate; one run disagrees. Does the challenge stand? Decide, with conditions.
- **8 min. Individual CER.** Each student writes a qualified claim, two pieces of evidence and one measurement that could challenge it.
- **7 min. Board.** "What is the gas?" is narrowed, not answered; "How could new particles form?" is posted.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E12, alone.
- **5 min. Check and repair.** The key is shown; students mark their own and fix it; the teacher takes error H whole class with a pair of candidates sharing one property. The teacher reads the tickets after class.

Why the repair is on day 12: day 13 is a full 50-minute checkpoint with no slot, and day 14 repeats day 13's response. Tool: None. By hand: run records, CER sheet. Hand-backs in: E11. Evidence: E12.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 7, "Which test would you trust to tell two white powders apart, and why?"; day 10, "What does fizzing show, and what can it not show?"; day 12, "What could you measure again that would change your mind?"
- Written task, day 12: one qualified claim with two pieces of evidence and one challenge, individual.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: the claim says "supported" or "not ruled out", not "is"; the evidence names a property and its condition; no single property is treated as enough; a challenge is named.

- **Warm-up.** Days 7 to 12 each repair the last exit; day 7 returns the day 1 identification item and day 10 its new-substances item. Day 8 returns the day 7 property table; day 11 returns day 7's properties; day 12 returns day 11's narrowing. No timer.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: property, density, dissolve, evidence, candidate, qualified. Frame: "__ is supported, but not proved, because __."
- **Access.** Tables in large print; trial steps on one card; a student who cannot pour acts as recorder; goggles over glasses.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, oral answer or scribe; look-fors unchanged.
- **Reading load.** No reading alone over 120 words; tables are read aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Day 11: add a fifth candidate and say which record would separate it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** On days 8 and 10 the parent portions powders, the child wears goggles and no one tastes or sniffs; two cups, and a blank first. Paper days run as written; the parent asks "what else would you compare?"
- **One projector.** Show the record sheets and the board; groups keep printed copies.
