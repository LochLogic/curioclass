## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do changes in Earth's system impact our communities, and what can we do about it?"
**Hook, day 129:** Compare original location diagrams with accounts of Navajo Nation drought and repeated Ellicott City flooding. Ask the compelling question; these accounts alone establish neither a climate trend nor an event's cause.

**M1 record, invented for practice:** A fictional agency reports global temperature differences from one fixed reference average of -0.3, 0.0 and +0.9 degrees Celsius in 1920, 1970 and 2020. Carbon dioxide is 300, 325 and 415 parts per million; methane is 1,000, 1,400 and 1,850 parts per billion. Independent instruments find no sustained solar increase after 1970. Major eruptions produce brief cooling, not sustained warming. Fuel-use records show rising combustion. Laboratory measurements show carbon dioxide and methane absorb outgoing infrared energy. Print all records with units, method descriptions and coverage limits.
**M1 prompt:** Ask two questions that clarify evidence for the past century's warming, one testing human activity and one testing a natural explanation. For each, identify the needed measurement and explain what agreement or disagreement would support. Then use the supplied mechanisms to judge the main cause and explain how slow warming could upset a water system without proving the cause of one flood.
**M1 key and scoring:** Four points for two evidence-seeking questions with discriminating measurements; four for evidence-based answers weighing fossil-fuel greenhouse warming against solar and volcanic records; two for linking gradual energy change to evaporation, moisture transport or snow storage; two for explaining the record's event-attribution limit. **E1 record, invented for practice:** A town grows from 1,000 to 1,500 people; daily use rises from 100 to 120 liters per person. **Carbon-model evidence:** On days 146 and 149, use the rule: next atmospheric stock equals current stock plus carbon entering minus carbon leaving during the same interval. Every card carries the same carbon-mass unit and interval. Include photosynthesis, respiration, combustion and ocean exchange; do not equate carbon mass with carbon dioxide mass. Invented for practice: start with 20 stock cards, add six and remove four, then lower inputs to five with removal unchanged at four. Key: stock becomes 22, then 23 despite lower emissions. Next lower inputs to three: stock falls to 22. Explain why only inputs below removal reduce stock. The simulation must separate this bookkeeping from the energy-transfer model: rising greenhouse gases slow outgoing energy loss, not a universal direct proportionality between carbon stock and temperature.
