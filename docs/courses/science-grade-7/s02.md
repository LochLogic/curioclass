## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we use chemical reactions to design a solution to a problem?"
**Hook, day 24:** Read an observation sequence: adding water to a heater warms a separate meal without flame. Draw an unexplained energy source and pose a design problem. Never open a commercial heater.
**Closing explanation, day 40:** Explain energy transfer to a separate target and insulation using reaction evidence, temperatures and retests.
**Teaching:** Model boundaries and measurement first. Require predictions before results. Step back during critique; retain unsuccessful trials.

<!-- skel -->
- **MS-PS1-6:** Design, build, test and improve a device that gives off or takes in thermal energy through a chemical process. Emphasis: designing and changing the device, controlling energy transfer to the surroundings, using factors such as the kind and concentration of a substance (for example dissolving calcium chloride). Limit: Testing criteria are limited to the amount of substance, the time and the temperature.
- **MS-ETS1-4:** Build a model that produces data for repeated testing and changing of an object, tool or process until the design is as good as it can be.

**Assessment case, invented for practice:** A school transports wax models in a warm room. A removable cooling compartment must keep the target at or below 24 degrees Celsius at minute 4 and at or below 25 at minute 10, using at most 3 grams of substance. Starting temperature is 28 degrees Celsius. The table is a paper design model, not measured chemical performance. Each entry gives the predicted target temperature. Keep this case out of teaching examples.

| Amount in grams | Minute 4, bare container | Minute 10, bare container |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 | 27 |
| 2 | 23 | 26 |
| 3 | 22 | 24 |

The compartment dissolves ammonium chloride in water, absorbing energy. This is dissolution, not evidence of a new substance; no reaction product is claimed. A proposed sleeve leaves minute 4 unchanged and lowers minute 10's prediction by one degree. This rule is an invented local approximation, not a law of insulation. A new physical test of the 2-gram sleeved design gives 23 degrees at minute 4 and 25 degrees at minute 10; withhold this result until the student has recorded the prediction.

**Item 1 scoring, 8 points:** Construct an input-output model with amount and sleeve as inputs and time-specific target temperatures as outputs (2). Generate predictions for 1 gram bare, 2 grams bare, 2 grams sleeved and 3 grams bare (2). Use the results to select 2 grams sleeved as the least-material option meeting both temperature limits (2). Check its predictions against the withheld test and explain why another repeated test is needed before calling the design reliable (2). Key pairs are 25 and 27; 23 and 26; 23 and 25; 22 and 24. A prediction is not a measurement (D); a single success is not proof of reliability (E). Meets: "My boundary encloses the wax and cooling compartment. Inputs are grams and sleeve presence; outputs are wax temperatures at minutes 4 and 10. My table gives these temperature pairs in degrees Celsius: 1 gram bare, 25 and 27; 2 grams bare, 23 and 26; 2 grams sleeved, 23 and 25; 3 grams bare, 22 and 24. Only the last two meet both limits, so I choose 2 grams sleeved to use less material. Its predictions, 23 and 25, match the withheld measurements. Repeat with the same conditions to check reliability; one success does not establish it." Does not meet: "The one physical test succeeded, so this design will always work" (E).

**Item 2 scoring, 8 points:** Using item 1's model, specify ammonium-chloride dissolution, distinguish it from a new-substance reaction, and draw energy entering it from the wax and surroundings (2). Use amount, time and temperature evidence to explain why 2 grams bare needs modification (2). Draw the sleeve modification and justify reduced unwanted energy transfer from the warmer room, using the new case's results (2). Specify a controlled repeat: same target amount, starting temperature, compartment water, room and measurement times, changing only the sleeve in the comparison (2). Meets: "Dissolving ammonium chloride absorbs energy without establishing a new substance. Reactions form new substances and can release or absorb energy. Here energy enters the dissolving compartment: wax -> process; surroundings -> process. My drawing encloses the wax and compartment, with a sleeve around the container and a smaller room -> wax arrow. The model predicts 23 degrees at minute 4 and 26 at minute 10 for 2 grams bare, missing the 25-degree limit. The sleeve reduces unwanted transfer from the warmer room. The model predicts 23 and 25 degrees with it; the supplied physical test measures 23 and 25, meeting both limits within 3 grams. Repeat bare and sleeved tests with the same wax amount, starting temperature, compartment water, room conditions and measurement times; change only sleeve presence." Does not meet: "Cold flows from the compartment into the wax" (A). No mixing occurs during this assessment.

**Item 3, retained practical record, 4 points:** Day 35's first construction is formative. On day 39, each student rebuilds a named component during modification and physically retests. Score this later construction contribution (1), a measured reading with time and conditions (1), the changed feature with a model-based reason (1), and a numerical retest comparison against criteria (1). Day 35 supplies an unscored baseline. Day 39 work receives written feedback before day 40; its first ten minutes allow revision before the partner test. Score the revised record once on day 41. Day 40 reuses participation evidence; its new partner result is formative. No second conference. Meets, invented for practice: "On day 39 I rebuilt the loose sleeve to reduce energy loss to the room. With 2 grams, 30 milliliters of compartment water, a separate 30-milliliter target starting at 20 degrees Celsius and a 20-degree room, day 35's target reached 24 degrees at minute 6. With only sleeve fit changed, day 39's target reached 24.8 degrees at minute 6. Our criterion was at least 24.5 degrees at minute 6 using at most 3 grams: the baseline missed by 0.5 degree, and the modification passed by 0.3 degree. I revised my record after written feedback before scoring." Does not meet: "My partner built it; I copied their graph" (G). Actual records require observed readings and named contributions. Simulated records earn reasoning credit only; construction and physical testing remain not yet observed, with no complete MS-PS1-6 result.
