# Section 1.5: A classroom job, an explanation model, and a paper tool

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


**This section's share (verbs quoted).** **W.1.2**, "name a topic, supply some facts about the topic, and provide some sense of closure": day 21 assigns the product and names the parts, day 22 reads the sign model, days 23 and 24 plan and make, with the paragraph-frame as the carried model. Children say their facts; a word label is the most any child prints, and the teacher writes the sentences of the heard six. **SL.1.1, SL.1.1a to c, SL.1.3**, "build on others' talk ... through multiple exchanges", "ask questions to clear up any confusion": taught on day 21 in groups of three, practised on 23 and 24. **RF.1.3f** (plural -s on taught bases) and **L.1.5, L.1.5a to d, L.1.6** ("sort words into categories", "define words by category and by one or more key attributes", "distinguish shades of meaning among verbs", "conjunctions to signal simple relationships (e.g., because)"): taught on day 22, asked on days 29 and 30 in section 1.7. The other codes listed above are foundations and reading practice, run as the day lists show.

**Limits that bite.** No child reads a new text alone: every article and the sign model is teacher-read first, and children point or say. Children handle paper, tape strips the teacher pre-tears, and crayons; the teacher alone handles the real funnel and rice. The job menu is invented and school-only (no home or family work). Plural -s is used only on taught bases (cats, mats, pans, tubs, buns). Short o and e are not decodable yet. Day 22's word work must not use scoop, sieve, sand, flour or Mina, or the words thud, slip, stamp, chip, shut, chin, cap, tips: the day 27 and 29 items use them.

**Connections.** Day 21 links to the articles of day 2 (main-idea-details); day 22 to the paragraph-frame of day 7 and the shades of meaning of day 5. **The teacher page must explain** the difference between a tool and a material, why a design change needs a fact behind it, and that the hearing cohorts are a written note plus a retry the next day, never a grade.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned.** The L.1.5, L.1.5a to d and L.1.6 rows listed above: taught on day 22, asked on days 29 and 30 in section 1.7 (day 29 for the first three groups of five, day 30 for the second three).

**Exit questions.** One spoken stem each, one ask. All children point to or mark a picture on paper; the rotating six also give the reason aloud. Cards print "invented for practice" and reuse no worked example.
- **E21** (day 21). Card: "Our paper scraps scatter. A tray with low sides holds things in." Pictures: scraps staying together; a ball rolling away; books standing in a row. Stem: "Which job could a tray with low sides help with?" Answer: keeping the scraps together. Wrong: "It is a tray", no fact (AA); "The tray is blue", a picture fact (C); "It carries markers", not on the card (A).
- **E22** (day 22). Card, four sentences: "Our box holds chalk. Its sides keep pieces together. Its lid keeps dust inside. The box helps us tidy." Stem: "Point to the closing sentence and say it." Answer: "The box helps us tidy." Wrong: the lid sentence (AC); "Chalk is for writing" (A).
- **E23** (day 23). Card: "A tray's sides stop blocks rolling away. A paper handle helps lift the tray." Stem: "What does the handle do?" Answer: it helps lift the tray. Wrong: "It is good" (AA); "It stops the blocks rolling", the parts swapped (A); "The handle is brown" (C).
- **E24** (day 24). Card: "A paper pocket holds cards. Tape joins its sides. Its open top lets cards slide in." Stem: "How does the open top help the pocket?" Answer: cards slide in. Wrong: "The top is taped shut" (A); "The pocket is pink" (C); "It is a good pocket" (AA).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


**Carried.** The paragraph-frame (topic, fact, fact, closing) returns on day 21 with the funnel and on day 22 with the sign model, and stays on the table for 23 and 24; main-idea-details from the articles carries the facts; the discussion-protocol carries the group-of-three talk; the sound-spelling-map carries u, qu and plural -s. Nothing here is a tool beyond printed cards.

**Key moments.** (1) The funnel worked example (day 21): ideal instrument is a real funnel, a small-mouth jar and dry rice, handled by the teacher alone; a room with nothing draws the jar, funnel and spill and tells the first and second try. (2) The paper model (day 24): ideal is precut paper and pre-torn tape; a room with nothing folds paper and joins it with a glue stick. No blades, no sharp edges.

## 6. Days

## Day 21


- **5 min. Opening repair.** Day 20 leftovers: hear the teller of a new two-panel card (Pia tells; Mr. Cole speaks), invented for practice.
- **15 min. Hook and read.** The teacher alone pours dry rice into a small jar: first try with no funnel, it spills; second try with a funnel, it does not. Name it: a designed tool is made on purpose for a job. Read the funnel paragraph from "Tools in the Kitchen" and the wheelbarrow paragraph from "Builders at Work" aloud; show the picture only after the words.
- **10 min. Reread and question.** Reread Kofi and Gran pages 9 to 11 (the screwdriver that fits, the strap pulled tight). Question: "Which part of the tool does the work?" Point to the words, then the picture. Hear a rotating six.
- **10 min. Talk.** Groups of three; the teacher sits with two groups (six children heard). One speaks, one adds ("I add that ..."), one asks "Which part?".
- **15 min. Frame and choose.** Retrieve the paragraph-frame with the funnel (topic: funnel; fact: wide top; fact: narrow end; closing: it helps pour). Read the product assignment aloud: "Explain how your tool helps our classroom. Name it, give two facts, and finish." Invented school job menu, picture cards: pencils roll off desks (hold); carry pencils to the rug (handle); glue stick too high (long handle). Each child points to a job; the article link is the wheelbarrow handles, ladle bowl or hoe long handle.
- **5 min. Exit.** E21.
- **5 min. Oral sound work.** Spoken long and short u: cub, cube; tub, tube.
- **10 min. Map.** Add u (sun) and qu (queen) to the sound-spelling-map; plural -s with its /s/ and /z/ sounds on cats and pans; retrieve ek-unknown-words (an ending as a clue).
- **10 min. Letters and dictation.** T and t; dictation "Tim has mats."
- **30 min. Rotation.** Three groups of ten reread "Pats Map" (cycle 3, a reread), 10 minutes each, while the others read known texts.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Tidy; the teacher notes who chose which job.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the funnel; pairs hold the frame cards. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, drawing a job card. Evidence: E21.

## Day 22


- **5 min. Opening repair.** If many made A or C on E21, repair with one new card (field 4).
- **15 min. Shared reading of the sign model.** Read g1-u1-04 aloud whole: "We used tools to make our classroom sign. A crayon made the letters dark. Tape joined the paper pieces. The sign helps people find our room." Four strips: topic, fact, fact, closing.
- **10 min. Reread and question.** "Which sentence is the topic? Which tells what the crayon did?" Point to the line, then to the picture.
- **10 min. Word work.** Sort tool and material cards from the articles. Tools: hammer, ladle, hoe. Materials: wood, dirt, dough. Define by category and attribute: "A crayon is a tool that makes dark marks." School use: tape joining paper. Act out look, peek and glance with a door-gap picture; tiny and little reviewed from day 5. Because as a link, a review of kindergarten.
- **15 min. Say and arrange.** Pairs arrange the four strips in order, say each sentence, and say the whole model with "because" in the closing. The teacher hears a rotating six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E22.
- **5 min. Oral sound work.** Blend and segment spoken u words.
- **10 min. Map and read.** Map plural -s; decode "tub", "nuts", "buns".
- **10 min. Letters and dictation.** U and u; dictation "The tub has nuts."; spelling transfer with the untaught word "drum": every sound, plausible spelling.
- **30 min. Rotation.** First read of "Pats Lunch" (cycle 4) after its patterns and irregular words are mapped, three groups of ten.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Fill the word-sort chart.

Who holds what is carried: pairs hold the four strips. Tool: None. By hand: sorting cards, arranging strips. Evidence: E22.

## Day 23


- **5 min. Opening repair.** Repair from E22 if needed; otherwise reread yesterday's closing.
- **10 min. Reread.** The teacher rereads the articles and the sign model; each child points to one fact.
- **10 min. Reread and question.** Partners find the evidence before the teacher responds: "Which words say what the tool does?" Hear a rotating six.
- **10 min. Talk.** Pairs say the purpose of each material (paper, tape, crayon, blocks) in turn; the listener asks "Which part?".
- **20 min. Plan sheet.** Each child draws the job, the tool, two materials and one purpose each, with a word label per part.
- **5 min. Exit.** E23.
- **5 min. Oral sound work.** Spoken blending.
- **10 min. Map.** Plural -s on taught bases: tubs, buns.
- **10 min. Letters and dictation.** V and v; dictation "A van has a tub."
- **15 min. Hearing, cohort 1.** Ten children, 90 seconds each, read a short prose passage, two poem lines and a short information passage; the teacher gives a written note.
- **15 min. Matched reading.** Small groups reread the cycle 4 reader.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Collect plan sheets.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a plan sheet. Tool: None. By hand: drawing and labeling. Evidence: E23.

## Day 24


- **5 min. Opening repair.** Repair from E23 if needed.
- **10 min. Reread.** Reread the sign model's tape sentence; show how one part joins another.
- **25 min. Make the model.** Precut paper, tape strips the teacher pre-tore, no dispenser blades. Each child builds the tool from the plan; the teacher walks and asks "Which part helps with the job?"
- **15 min. Record.** Draw the finished model, label two parts, and say "The ___ helps because ___" to a partner. The teacher writes the sentences of the heard six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E24.
- **5 min. Oral sound work.** Blend and segment.
- **10 min. Map.** Decode and spell tubs, buns, nuts.
- **10 min. Letters and dictation.** W and w; dictation "Win has a wig."
- **15 min. Hearing, cohort 2.** Ten children, 90 seconds each, as on day 23.
- **15 min. Retry, cohort 1.** Cohort 1 rereads, using the written note.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Store models.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a model and a record sheet. Tool: None. By hand: folding and taping precut paper. Evidence: E24.

## 7. Talk and writing

Questions: day 21, "Which part of the tool does the work?"; day 23, "What is each material for?"; day 24, "How does that part help with the job?" Written task: the plan sheet (day 23) and the record sheet (day 24), a drawing with up to two word labels. Look-fors: a named tool; two parts with a purpose each; a closing that says what the job is; talk that waits and builds.

## 8. Practice that returns


Day 21 reviews ek-unknown-words; day 22 reviews because and the shades of meaning of day 5; day 23 reviews the sound-spelling-map and plural; day 24 reviews the paragraph-frame and the point-and-say of the bicycle story.

## 9. Supports and extension

English learners: tool, material, handle, closing; frame "The ___ helps because ___." Access: a picture for every word; a partner may point. Check accommodations: extra time, a scribe for a word label. Reading load: none beyond taught words. Extension: a second job for the same tool.

## 10. Two rooms

A parent: read the sign model to your child, then ask which sentence says what the tool is for. One projector: show the four strips and the plan sheet.
