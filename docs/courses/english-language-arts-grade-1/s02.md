## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What patterns can we see in the sky, and what stories do people tell about them?"

**Hook, day 34.** Read the complete *Sky Patterns* introduction. Match its sunrise and sunset sentences to a picture record, then open the compelling question.
**Inquiry.** Day 37: which repeated invitation changes the tale? Day 54: how does because connect the sun article's facts? Day 69: how can the sun poem imagine a speaker while preserving the information text's event order?
**Closing claim.** The day 71 poem shows an imagined sun witnessing at least two ordered events. Its sequence draws on *Sun Across the Sky*; its voice and sensory detail draw on *Sun's Day*. Children distinguish invention from a sourced sky pattern.
Model evidence on 34, message on 37, source notes on 50 and narrative structure on 65. Partners locate evidence on 46, 60 and 69; withdraw completed models during independent observations.

**T, Mira Waits for the Stars, day 47** (g1-u2-c47, courses/english-language-arts-grade-1/u02-4/cold-passage-day47.md, 108 words). Pictures show the bright sky with no stars, one star in the dark blue sky, and the sky full of stars. Lesson: waiting can bring good things.
