# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts, Grade 1

Course `english-language-arts-grade-1`, grade 1, draft 2026-11-14.

The day records are in `data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-1/days.csv`. They retain the frame's four units, all 31 owned progression rows, 154 unit days and six course-level days: 160 course days in 32 weeks. This course meets every school day, so course day equals school day and `school_day` stays blank. Section boundaries are deferred to unit planning; each unit currently uses its `.1` placeholder.

Each day has about 60 minutes for topic reading, discussion, writing and language, and 60 for foundations. Foundations continue during topic assessments and response days. The 146 base topic lessons and 130 foundations lessons occupy concurrent blocks, not 276 separate days. The source map retains all of them, splits one topic lesson across two days, and adds seven topic response days: 146 + 1 + 7 = 154. Foundations have 24 additional consolidation days, distributed 8, 7, 6 and 3 across the units. The third-hour Labs strand is cut. The source map records the source credit and every lesson decision; its source names are provenance, not course labels.

The first topic reading of a short story, poem or article is whole; a set's named member is read whole on its first day. Later encounters are purposeful rereads. A day's note identifies the catalogue text id, the base lesson and its foundations cycle. All 17 anchor and paired catalogue rows are placed. Grouped catalogue entries, such as the three tool articles, are distinguished in the day's goal. `g1-u1-04` reserves adapted short classroom-tool models; its exact source selections remain a unit-plan task.

Text records remain provisional because most texts are not written. Tools use a concrete problem, explicit event order and pictures that supplement words. Sky texts add connections across observations and distinguish informational explanations from two culturally located tales and sensory poems. Bird-body texts add searchable features, technical part-and-job vocabulary and two-source notes. Bird-care texts demand a plot held across five chapters, comparison between complete stories, and separate reasons on two sides of a question. Read-alouds remain richer than independent decoding; supported short grade 1 prose, poetry and information are also read by children. The unit plan must supply each final text's measure where meaningful, qualitative demands, reader/task judgment, matched supports and reason for this placement. No measured level is invented here. The numeric and writing-share targets in the grades 3 to 12 rubric are not applied to grade 1.

## Unit 1. Tools and work

**Calendar:** course days 1 to 33, weeks 1 to 7, 33 days.

The question is, *How do tools help people do their work?* Days 1 to 8 establish reading and listening routines through the building-site, farm and kitchen articles (`g1-u1-02`) and hammer poem (`g1-u1-03`). Questions and answers, picture-versus-word information and a modeled informative paragraph come before independent checks. Days 10 to 19 read and reread the bicycle story (`g1-u1-01`), moving from describing characters, setting and events to identifying the teller at different points. Days 21 to 33 move from a classroom job to a safe model tool and a topic/facts/closing piece, using the adapted short models (`g1-u1-04`). The paragraph-frame is introduced, not assumed. Talk precedes writing; facts come from the texts and model observations.

The load is deliberately unequal: eight introductory information days, ten story days, thirteen design-and-writing days and two response days. The design is a paper or safe craft model, with any real tools demonstrated by the teacher. Questions stay on workers in the texts, without asking about family employment. Class names and photographs stay in the room.

Foundations lessons 1 to 25, cycles 1 to 4, occupy days 1 to 25: kindergarten retrieval; short a and th; short i with ch and sh; short u and qu, then plural -s. Cycle 2 retains its ten lessons. Four- and five-phoneme oral blending, isolation and segmentation, including hearing long against short vowels, continue without requiring untaught printed long-vowel patterns. Days 26 to 33 consolidate, hear supported grade 1 reading and finish observations. All 52 letter forms are revisited, with independent samples across days 26 to 31. The early source cluster, -ng/-nk and ending examples are delayed to the frame's later slots, including in adapted readers. Short-vowel completion continues in unit 2; irregular words, spelling, phonemic awareness and fluency continue all year.

**Readiness and retrieval:** kindergarten is the preceding grade for every assumed ELA row. Day 1 retrieves print concepts, letters and sounds, CVC reading, high-frequency words, supported reading, story elements, picture/word information, details, informative writing, talk, sentences and word relationships. The frame also expressly requires a foundations baseline. Keep its ten-minute screening in the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook; confirm individual gaps in days 2 to 5 foundations groups. This is an explicit frame exception to the current retrieval rule, not evidence that these rows were last used two grades back. No earlier-grade row requires an additional readiness slot.

**Assessments and response:** day 8, the first mid-unit assessment, checks information questions and answers, listening details and picture/word information. Day 19, the second, checks story questions, descriptions using words and illustrations, and who tells at different points. Their dedicated response days are 9 and 20. The end-of-unit assessment is an observation task across days 31 to 33: foundations and supported prose/poetry/information reading on 31; story, informational reading and listening on 32; the designed tool and informative piece, discussion and word relationships on 33. Word relationships include categories, category-plus-attribute definitions, real-life uses, verb manner and adjective intensity. The final 20 minutes of day 33 are the single end-of-unit response allocation, including a fresh retry.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Adapt all three base-unit assessments and the designed-tool performance task, with foundations cycle checks restricted to the patterns taught. The topic cost is five days carrying assessment evidence, days 8, 19 and 31 to 33, plus dedicated response days 9 and 20. End response shares day 33. Cycle progress samples sit in foundations lessons 5, 15, 20 and 25 with feedback in that block; they add zero course days. All costs are inside the 33 days.

## Unit 2. The sun, moon and stars

**Calendar:** course days 34 to 75, weeks 7 to 15, 42 days.

The question is, *What patterns can we see in the sky, and what stories do people tell about them?* Days 34 and 35 open with the sky information (`g1-u2-01`) and its distinction from stories. Days 36 to 48 read the Efik and Ibibio tale (`g1-u2-03`), the Chinese tale (`g1-u2-04`) and night-sky poems (`g1-u2-02`), with repeated attention to events, a central message and feeling/sense words. Day 49 responds. Days 50 to 61 research sun, moon and star patterns using provided articles and class picture observations. Research notes precede complete-sentence science talks and drawings; day 62 responds. Days 63 to 75 use a jointly written moon poem to model narrative planning and peer feedback before independent sun poems, editing and collaborative digital publication. A poem must recount at least two events with details, time words and closure to serve W.1.3; a list of sky facts cannot stand for it.

This allocation follows the heavy writing and oral-language load: fifteen days for story reading, twelve for research and talk, thirteen for narrative writing and publication, plus two response days. Four simple and compound sentence kinds are explicitly developed across the reading and writing. Capitals for dates and names, end marks and commas in dates and word series are taught before the editing observation. Day 72 reserves a brief prompted sentence performance so every sentence kind is checked even when a child's poem does not happen to contain it.

Foundations lessons 26 to 60, cycles 5 to 11, occupy days 34 to 68: short o; two full cycles on short e; y as long i; wh and -ck; doubled finals; initial l/r clusters and final clusters, -ng and -nk. Endings move from -s and -ing through the three pronunciations of -ed, with explicit -es and comparative -er/-est on taught bases. No final-consonant doubling is required. Days 69 to 75 consolidate and collect end evidence. The source cycle 11 introduces ou, but the frame leaves that additional team to grade 2: omit that teaching and replace its untaught occurrences in the adapted reader. Silent-e endings and dropping e continue in unit 3.

**Retrieval:** day 34 retrieves the kindergarten rows for retelling, word relationships, books, connections, narrative, revision/publication, shared research, description, sentences, CVC decoding and word parts. These were all used last year, so there is no pre-unit readiness check and none on the unit opener. Earlier unit 1 routines return inside normal work. Science's 1-ESS1-1 unit is on school days 3 to 18, weeks 1 to 4, before ELA day 34. If science is delayed locally, day 34's information reading states the sun's rise/set pattern, moon motion and changing shape, and nighttime visibility of stars and records them in pictures. This bridge fits the reading block. No Earth-turning mechanism, clock time or measurement is required.

**Sensitivity:** both tales are named as living peoples' stories. The frame's tradition-specific sensitivity read, including the limits of the colonial-era collection and Chinese sources on the Qixi tradition, precedes use. Do not frame either as mistaken science. Night is described gently; home observations are optional and never graded. Choose the paired sky poems as Curio Class originals, still to be written and read. The digital book remains in class, with no student accounts or publicly posted names or photographs.

**Assessments and response:** days 47 and 48 retain the first base assessment's two-day allocation, adapted to retelling/message and feeling/sense words plus story/information distinctions. Day 49 is its response. Day 61 retains the second base assessment: an information connection, two-source research and a relevant-detail talk with a useful drawing and complete sentences; day 62 responds. The end-of-unit observation runs days 70 to 75: independent narrative on 70 and 71; feedback, sentence conventions and inflection reading on 72; recorded contribution to the shared typed book on 73; presentation on 74; short completion performances for reading and research on 75. The poem alone does not assess all the reading standards. The last 20 minutes of day 75 are the single end-of-unit response allocation.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Adapt all three base assessments, the sun-poem performance task and cycles 5 to 11 progress samples. Topic cost: nine days carrying assessment evidence, days 47, 48, 61 and 70 to 75; two dedicated response days, 49 and 62; end response within 75. Digital publication shares the existing performance days, with each child's contribution recorded. Cycle samples use foundations lessons 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55 and 60, with zero additional course days. All costs are inside 42 days.

## Unit 3. Birds' amazing bodies

**Calendar:** course days 79 to 119, weeks 16 to 24, 41 days.

The question is, *How do a bird's body parts help it survive?* Days 79 to 88 establish a main topic and supporting details through the beak, wing, feather and foot articles (`g1-u3-01`). Headings, table of contents, glossary and labels are introduced with a real search purpose; electronic menus and icons appear on a printed screen by day 85, before the first assessment. Read the window-watching story (`g1-u3-02`) whole on day 88 and the bird poems (`g1-u3-03`) whole on day 108, then revisit them. The paired poems are reserved as Curio Class originals, still to be written and read.

After response on day 89, days 90 to 106 move from joint feather research and writing to increasingly independent beak notes and a complete informative paragraph. Sharing on 106 preserves the product; day 107 is the dedicated response. Days 108 to 119 transfer research to an expert bird, verify clues against more than one provided source, and create and revise a riddle card with an accurate scientific drawing for the matching game. Main-topic work and features are needed for both research sequences, so they are taught first. The research block receives seventeen days, more than either the ten-day introduction or twelve-day riddle task, because it carries note-taking, factual selection, paragraph structure and gradual release.

Foundations lessons 61 to 95, cycles 12 to 18, occupy days 79 to 113: closed syllables, compounds, open syllables, then silent e with a, i, o, u and e. Count syllables by vowel sounds and decode two-syllable combinations. Cycle 18 explicitly teaches keeping e before -s and dropping e before -ing/-ed, on known bases without doubling. Days 114 to 119 consolidate. RF.1.3c continues through vowel teams in unit 4; the parent RF.1.3 receives its complete all-parts check there, after its last pattern. Unit 3 observations record the taught patterns without claiming that the whole parent standard is finished.

**Retrieval and leans:** day 79 retrieves kindergarten main-topic, book-handling, spoken syllable and CVC rows, plus the earlier paragraph-frame and research-cycle. No older prerequisite needs a readiness check. Science's parts unit, including 1-LS1-1, is on school days 20 to 43, weeks 4 to 9, well before day 79. This meets both the informative-writing and shared-research leans. If delayed locally, day 79's bird pages teach each body part and its job within the reading; writing and research use only those provided facts. Science's parent-and-young unit on school days 118 to 128, weeks 24 to 26, is a topic connection, not a prerequisite for either bird unit. Pictures or window watching replace outdoor work when needed; outdoor watching is supervised, with no touching birds, nests or eggs. Predators and prey are named without graphic detail.

Day 79 also starts the named catch-up routine from the mid-year check: ten minutes of map-blend-read-spell-reread in the foundations small-group allocation, replacing independent practice, not adding a third block. Regroup from fresh reading evidence rather than permanently assigning a reading level.

**Assessments and response:** day 86 checks main topic/details and a full feature search; day 89 responds. Days 104 and 105 retain the two-part second base assessment, adapted to complete research writing and its source evidence, with topic/detail retelling and a feature search. Day 107 responds. The end-of-unit observation is on days 115, 118 and 119: independent main-topic and feature use with the expert-bird research, foundations performances to the taught scope, and verification with a fresh bird page during the riddle/drawing share. A riddle without the source search cannot stand for RI.1.5. The final 20 minutes of day 119 are the single end-of-unit response allocation.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Retain the three adapted base assessments, research piece, riddle and drawing task, and cycles 12 to 18 progress samples. Topic cost: six days carrying assessment evidence, days 86, 104, 105, 115, 118 and 119; dedicated responses on 89 and 107; end response within 119. Samples occupy foundations lessons 65, 70, 75, 80, 85, 90 and 95 without additional days. No vowel-team mastery is claimed at this unit edge. All costs are inside 41 days.

## Unit 4. Caring for birds

**Calendar:** course days 120 to 157, weeks 24 to 32, 38 days.

The question is, *How can people help birds that need it?* Read the complete five-chapter story (`g1-u4-01`) across ten school days, 120 to 129, with first chapter readings on 120, 122, 124, 126 and 128 and intervening rereads. The shorter bird-help story (`g1-u4-02`) is read whole on 123. Early comparisons are provisional; the full comparison follows completion of both stories on 129. One source lesson is split across 127 and 128 to protect the final chapter reading. Day 130 responds. The story ends with the bird in adult care, without graphic descriptions; children call an adult and never pick up wild birds.

Days 131 to 140 move from bird-protection information (`g1-u4-03`) and the nesting-box account (`g1-u4-06`) to both sides of the adapted nest debate (`g1-u4-04`), then model, shared and independent opinion paragraphs. Author reasons are read before they are used in writing. Both positions remain available. Days 141 to 157 research bird-safe windows, read the adapted human-helpers text (`g1-u4-05`), then plan, draft, revise, edit and publish a window bird portrait and informative paragraph. This allocates ten days to complete-story work, one dedicated story response, ten to evidence and opinion including its response, and seventeen to research, word strategies, the portrait and closing observations. No unit days are added.

Word strategies begin with context in story sentences and transfer to informational text, including actual paired uses of a multiple-meaning word, affixes, known roots and a fresh word. Grammar moves through common/proper/possessive nouns, agreement, personal/possessive/indefinite pronouns, past/present/future verbs, adjectives, conjunctions, determiners and prepositions, with repeated text-based sentence work before the complete check on 156. The portrait's shared drawing or audio publication on 152 to 154 supplies a second kind of digital tool, following the typed book in unit 2. Each child's contribution is recorded; paper work alone does not complete W.1.6. Projects are class-based, require no yard or feeder, and keep names, voices and photographs private.

Foundations lessons 96 to 130, cycles 19 to 25, occupy days 120 to 154: ar/or, er/ir/ur, oa/ai/ea, ay/long-o ow, oo/ee/final-y long e, ie/igh, then two-syllable reading across all five taught syllable types. The oo pattern is retained as the frame requires; additional grade 2 teams are not introduced. Days 155 to 157 consolidate and complete the full foundations observation, including all RF.1.3 parts and the continued unit 1 and unit 2 rows. This protects the progression's grade 2 handoff.

**Retrieval:** day 120 retrieves kindergarten author reasons, opinion, comparison, word parts/meanings and oral grammar, plus earlier story-map, paragraph-frame and research-cycle work. All assumed rows were used last year or earlier this year. There is no pre-unit readiness check or readiness on the opener. Social studies teaches its written claim, reason and closing in unit 3, school days 33 to 51, weeks 7 to 11, using its own shared-writing bridge. That experience is a context link here; ELA still teaches and checks its own opinion standard.

**Assessments and response:** day 129 checks comparison of characters' experiences after both stories are complete; day 130 responds. Day 139 checks topic, opinion, one reason and closure in the independent nest paragraph; day 140 responds. The end-of-unit observation retains the third base assessment on day 147 for author reasons and word strategies, then completes foundations on 155, grammar, both kinds of comparison and an opinion transfer on 156, and reasons/word-strategy transfer and digital publication evidence on 157. The final 20 minutes of day 157 are the single end-of-unit response allocation. Every parent standard is completed with the earlier lettered parts, not just the parts first owned here.

**Candidate settled: kept.** Retain all three adapted assessments, the nest opinion, portrait and cycles 19 to 25 progress samples including the full end-year foundations check. Topic cost: six days carrying assessment evidence, days 129, 139, 147 and 155 to 157. Dedicated responses are 130 and 140; 140 is already the tenth base lesson. End response shares 157. Cycle samples occupy foundations lessons 100, 105, 110, 115, 120, 125 and 130, adding zero days. All costs are inside 38 days. The source's combined lesson files for 12/13, 14/15 and 16/17 still count as six distinct lessons; their flex allocations are retained.

## Assessment and daily teaching contract

The CSV reserves evidence, not items. Each observation, mid-unit assessment and course-level check must elicit every part of each code it lists. Its note names the product, performance or brief prompted completion that supplies that evidence. Parent-only codes also carry their lettered parts in the CSV where useful: phoneme moves, fluency, conversation and word relationships are not reduced to one convenient subskill. End tasks cannot be scored solely from the attractive product: text questions, feature searches, oral reading and sentence/word performances remain necessary.

Foundations checks at interim unit edges are limited to taught patterns. The complete RF.1.3 check is on 155, after cycle 25; the spelling and fluency rows opened in unit 1 and inflections opened in unit 2 also return there. L.1.2 on 72 includes spelling parts from unit 1 as well as capitals, punctuation and commas; L.1.1 on 156 includes printing and four sentence kinds from earlier units as well as grammar. This keeps ownership fixed while allowing the continued teaching required by the frame.

Every topic teaching day uses text vocabulary and short text-based sentence work. Turn and talk runs most days: follow rules, listen, build on a partner through several exchanges and ask for clarification. Research talks add a source or page reference. This guarantees at least two named discussion routines each full week rather than one discussion at a unit's end. Feedback frames fade as students become more independent. Vocabulary returns in rereads and writing; sentence models precede independent writing. Units 1, 2, 3 and 4 all include planning, drafting, revision and editing, while short independent responses and research notes provide writing in one sitting. Shared research is taught in unit 2 and reused in both bird units. Grade-level text demand grows through richer listening and supported reading without sending children to an untaught-pattern decodable.

No final text agent or sensitivity read is claimed here. The exact lesson items, scoring guidance, word lists, text records, source credit lines and matched supports belong to the next planning layers. Source-map rows identify original materials and exclusions so those writers can prepare them without silently restoring a trade book, picture or video.
