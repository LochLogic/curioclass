# Section 3.4: Research notes from two sources and a joint feather paragraph

## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Reading exits and end items read an unworked part of the unit's own texts (DEFAULTS.md exit line); every other card named below is a Curio Class original, invented for practice, and prints that label.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** The class begins to research and to write a joint explanation. A research question is asked of two provided sources (W.1.7, "Participate in shared research and writing projects"; W.1.8, "gather information from provided sources to answer a question") on 90 to 92, one feather paragraph is drafted jointly (W.1.2, "name a topic, supply some facts about the topic, and provide some sense of closure") on 93 and 94, and features are used to locate evidence (RI.1.5) on 91. The topic and details of the two new articles are named (RI.1.2) on 92 and 93. Foundations finish cycle 14 (open syllables) and start cycle 15 (silent e with a; RF.1.3c, RF.1.3f on 94).
- **Limits that bite.** All writing is dictated, drawn, printed in a word or a short sentence, or scribed by the teacher; the paragraph is joint. First-teaching work on 90 is practice and is not scored (unit plan field 5). Facts come only from the provided sources; the picture observation is kept in its own column. No outdoor watching; a class picture stands in.
- **Connections.** The research-cycle chart (ask, find, note, share), the note with its source named, the observation and article fact two columns, the paragraph-frame (topic, fact, fact, closing), the topic sentence and the closing sentence, and shared writing with the teacher as scribe, all reviewed from units 1 and 2; the annotated drawing of 87.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A research question is one a provided source can answer. (2) A source label names where a note came from; a note without one is not yet used. (3) The joint paragraph is practice; the scored product is later (104). (4) How a planted error in the 94 draft is found by pointing to a note. (5) The molt card on 91 teaches its own word; molt is not taught before the exit.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: question, source, note, gather, record, support, paragraph, ending. Frames: "My question is ___." "X says ___; Y says ___." "The ending is ___."
- **Access.** Content dictation; enlarged two-source pages; partner pointing; a printed class picture instead of any outdoor watching.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing or a spoken answer; stems read twice; a scribe for the printed sentence; no time pressure.
- **Reading load.** Both articles, the cards and the paragraph are read aloud; children read word labels, source labels and the cycle sentences.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second feather fact in a source and add it to the notes with its label.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Pick one body part and ask one question a page of the set can answer; write a note with its source named; say a topic sentence, two facts and an ending aloud and scribe them. For the planted error on 94, the parent reads a short draft with one wrong fact and the child points to the note that fixes it.
- **One projector.** Show the two source pages side by side and the joint paragraph as it grows; the child copies from the printed notes.
