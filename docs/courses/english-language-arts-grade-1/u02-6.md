- **Limits that bite here.** No child looks at the sun; the pictures and the class picture log stand in. No clock time and no numbers beyond counting; "about a month" is read aloud and not counted. Phase names are not taught (the text cut them). "Moves" is what we see: say "seems to move" aloud while the page keeps its words; the sun article gave Earth's turning as the reason (g1-u2-01, Sun Across the Sky, page 2) and page 5 of The Changing Moon gives the moon's travel. Night is described gently; home sky watching is optional. Dates are copied from the classroom calendar card, never worked out.
- **Connections.** Commas in a series and in a date (L.1.2c), day 59 (S, V); capitals and end marks carried from 2.5; the research-cycle chart (ask, find, note, share) and the connection arrow, both review from 2.1 and 2.5.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The moon has two patterns: it seems to move across the sky each night, and it changes shape over many days; the page names the first with "moves" and the second with "changes shape". (2) The moon makes no light of its own; the sun lights half of it, and we see more or less of that lit half (source: g1-u2-01, The Changing Moon, pages 4 and 5, from the text's checked sources). (3) Stars stay up in the day; the sun's light hides them (Stars in Daylight and Darkness, pages 3 and 4). (4) A note names its source; a story event never goes in the facts column. (5) How -ed sounds /d/ after a voiced sound, and why -ll, -ss, -ff, -zz double at the end of a short word.
