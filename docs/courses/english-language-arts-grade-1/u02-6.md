## 1. Standards and practices


- **RI.1.3, this section's share.** "Describe the connection between two individuals, events, ideas, or pieces of information in a text." Taught on 56 (the moon goes around Earth, so we see more or less of its lit half) and 58 (the sun's light fills the day sky, so we cannot see the stars, though they are still there). Practiced on 54; checked on 61.
- **W.1.7 and W.1.8, this section's share.** "Participate in shared research and writing projects" and "gather information from provided sources to answer a question": a source-named note on 56, a facts column on 57, a note joined to the picture log on 58 (all taught on 50 to 52; the class notebook from 2.5 carries them).
- **RL.1.5, day 57 (practice).** "Explain major differences between books that tell stories and books that give information": fact against story, retrieved from 2.1.
- **SL.1.4, SL.1.5, SL.1.6 and L.1.2c, day 59 (practice).** Describe a picture with a detail, add a drawing that helps, speak a complete sentence; commas in a word series and in a date.
- **Foundations, days 56 to 59:** cycle 9 lessons 48 to 50 (doubled finals -ll, -ss, -ff, -zz; s-blends; -ed said /d/) and cycle 10 lesson 51 (initial l- and r-blends, three-consonant clusters, -es, -er and -est on known bases): RF.1.1a, RF.1.2a to d, RF.1.3a, b, f, g, RF.1.4a to c, L.1.1a, L.1.2d, e. A cycle 9 progress sample is taken on 58.
- **Limits that bite here.** No child looks at the sun; the pictures and the class picture log stand in. No clock time and no numbers beyond counting; "about a month" is read aloud and not counted. Phase names are not taught (the text cut them). "Moves" is what we see: say "seems to move" aloud while the page keeps its words; the sun article gave Earth's turning as the reason (g1-u2-01, Sun Across the Sky, page 2) and page 5 of The Changing Moon gives the moon's travel. Night is described gently; home sky watching is optional. Dates are copied from the classroom calendar card, never worked out.
- **Connections.** Commas in a series and in a date (L.1.2c), day 59 (S, V); capitals and end marks carried from 2.5; the research-cycle chart (ask, find, note, share) and the connection arrow, both review from 2.1 and 2.5.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The moon has two patterns: it seems to move across the sky each night, and it changes shape over many days; the page names the first with "moves" and the second with "changes shape". (2) The moon makes no light of its own; the sun lights half of it, and we see more or less of that lit half (source: g1-u2-01, The Changing Moon, pages 4 and 5, from the text's checked sources). (3) Stars stay up in the day; the sun's light hides them (Stars in Daylight and Darkness, pages 3 and 4). (4) A note names its source; a story event never goes in the facts column. (5) How -ed sounds /d/ after a voiced sound, and why -ll, -ss, -ff, -zz double at the end of a short word.

## 6. Days

## Day 56


- **15 min. Read The Changing Moon.** Words before, with pictures: moon, night, change, shape, thin curve, half. Read g1-u2-01, The Changing Moon, whole, pausing after pages 2, 3 and 5 for the pictures. Nobody looks at the sun; the lit-half picture on page 4 is the record.
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E55, G, only if many missed it). Then reread pages 2 and 3: "Which page says the moon moves across the sky? Which says it changes shape?" Partners point.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "What are the moon's two patterns?" Pass allowed. Pip says one line at a time.
- **20 min. Two patterns, two notes.** The teacher models one source-named note (a small picture, the word "moves" or "changes", and the label "The Changing Moon") for each pattern, then page 5 with the arrow card: "The moon goes around Earth, so we see more or less of it." Each child draws two notes and one arrow; the label is the only writing.
- **5 min. Exit.** E56.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: the long vowel in a pair (rain, ran); blend /s/ /t/ /o/ /p/; first, vowel and last sound of spill; segment smell. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 9 lesson 48, explicit teaching first: -ll, -ss, -ff, -zz and s-blends on the sound-spelling-map. Word chain: hill, bell, kiss, miss, mess, puff, jazz; stop, step, skip, spin; -ed said /d/: yelled, spilled, smelled. Read the decodable passage matched to the groups (cycle 9 reader still to write, field 11).
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Print the letter pairs in dictation: "The bell is on the hill." (the, is mapped). Names with capitals.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the strips and arrow; partners hold their own note pages. Tool: None. By hand: two notes, one arrow. Evidence: E56; the heard-six note.

## Day 57


- **15 min. Reread a fact and a tale.** Reread The Changing Moon, pages 2 and 3 (g1-u2-01), then the first page of the tale (g1-u2-04). Say aloud, "This is a traditional Chinese tale, a story people tell." The tale is a story, not science; the weaver and the cowherd are apart and glad when they meet.
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E56, E). Then: "Which page told us a fact? Which told a story?" Partners point.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "How do we know it is a story?" Tale and information, two columns (from 2.1) laid on the floor; four picture cards sorted by pairs. No child is asked to be the example of a mistake.
- **20 min. Moon facts for the notebook.** Each child adds one labeled note to the shared notebook, from The Changing Moon only (the moon can be seen in the daytime; it changes shape; it makes no light of its own): a small picture and the label "The Changing Moon". Story events stay out of the facts column.
- **5 min. Exit.** E57.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: the long vowel in a pair (tape, tap); blend /s/ /k/ /i/ /p/; first, vowel and last sound of stiff; segment swim. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 9 lesson 49: read and write connected sentences with the cycle patterns. Reread the chain from 48; sentence reading: "Stan has a doll and a bell."
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation of two sentences from the cycle: "The doll is still." "Jess yelled and Stan smelled the mess." Capitals on names.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the two columns and the notebook. Tool: None. By hand: one labeled note. Evidence: E57; the heard-six note.

## Day 58


- **15 min. Read Stars in Daylight and Darkness.** Words before: star, bright, faint, daylight, dark. Read g1-u2-01, Stars in Daylight and Darkness, whole, pausing after pages 3 and 4. The day-and-night picture log (a barn view, drawn by the teacher before class, with no star marks by day) is shown beside it.
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E57, D). Reread page 4: "What does the sun's light do to the stars?" Partners point to the words.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "Where do the stars go in the day?" The class says, "They are still there; the bright sky hides them." Pass allowed.
- **20 min. The text and the log.** The teacher models the arrow: "The sun lights the sky, so we cannot see the stars." Each child draws two log pictures (day, night), an arrow between a text fact and its log picture, and the label "Stars in Daylight and Darkness".
- **5 min. Exit.** E58.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: the long vowel in a pair (mad, made); blend /s/ /n/ /a/ /p/; first, vowel and last sound of stuff; segment spill. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 9 lesson 50: the cycle progress sample (field 3) is taken on the rotation. Same-block regrouping follows the sample; teach only the cycle's correspondences and mapped words.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation: "Stan and Jess smell the mess." Letter pairs in names; stretch each word and mark every sound (repair for M).
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (the progress sample: read one reserved word, remove the card, write one dictated word; heard six by name); the other children reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the arrow and the log; partners hold their page. Tool: None. By hand: two log pictures, one arrow, one label. Evidence: E58; the progress sample; the heard-six note.

## Day 59


- **15 min. Reread the stars article.** Reread g1-u2-01, Stars in Daylight and Darkness, pages 1, 5 and 6, with the picture log. The class chooses one picture to describe.
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E58, E). Then: "What does the picture show that the words do not?" Partners point.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "What does our picture help a listener see?" Contrasts shown on the board: "Stars above" and "The stars are above the roof" (H); "sun moon stars" and "sun, moon, stars." (S); "May 2 2026" and "May 2, 2026." (V).
- **20 min. Describe it, draw it, write one line.** Each child draws the detail a listener needs, says one finished sentence to a partner, and writes the date from the calendar card on the page with its comma. The teacher scribes one sentence for any child who asks.
- **5 min. Exit.** E59a, E59b and the heard-six sentence.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: the long vowel in a pair (kit, kite); blend /f/ /l/ /a/ /g/; first, vowel and last sound of plum; segment brick. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 10 lesson 51 (explicit teaching first): initial l- and r-blends, then three-consonant clusters: flag, glad, plan, plum, club; brick, crab, drip, drum, trip; scrub, strap, split. Previous-cycle decodable until the cycle 10 words and mapped words are on the map.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation: "The crab is on the flat rock." (the, is mapped). Print each word letter by letter; names with capitals.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the notebook. Tool: None. By hand: one drawing, one dated line. Evidence: E59a, E59b; the heard-six sentence.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 56, "Which page says it moves? Which says it changes shape?"; day 57, "How do we know it is a story?"; day 58, "What does the sun's light do to the stars?"
- Written task, day 58: the text-and-log note (two pictures, one arrow, one label); day 59: a dated drawing with one finished sentence, said or dictated.
- Judged by the teacher: (1) the source is named; (2) two details are joined with the arrow; (3) nothing is added that the source lacks; (4) the drawing shows what a listener needs; (5) the sentence is finished. Marked shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** One repair of at most 5 minutes from field 4, inside each day's reread part.
- **Retrieved.** 56: research-cycle chart, source label. 57: tale and information columns from 2.1. 58: because and so from 2.5. 59: capitals for names and months from 2.5, and the four sentence kinds from 2.1.
- **Foundations.** Each day maps the cycle's pattern, spells with it, rereads the passage for meaning and expression, and returns to the earlier cycles' words in the chain.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: moon, night, change, shape, star, bright, dark. Frames: "The moon ___." "I see ___ in the picture." "The sun's light ___, so ___."
- **Access.** Large strips and cards; tactile arrows; a scribe for the one finished sentence; picture choices instead of pointing at small print.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a spoken answer or a scribe; stems read twice; no time pressure.
- **Reading load.** Children decode only taught patterns and mapped words; the articles and the tale are read aloud; the child reads word labels and the cycle passage.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second text fact to the arrow note and say which page it came from.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read one page of The Changing Moon at a time; draw the moon's two patterns; say, "The moon goes around Earth, so we see more or less of it."
- **One projector.** Show each page with its picture; cards and strips are paper, and every child points on paper.
