# Section 2.2: Feeling and sense words, and the Chinese tale

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-1/2/s/2.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-1 u02-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **RL.1.4, "Identify words and phrases in stories or poems that suggest feelings or appeal to the senses."** Day 39 teaches a feeling word (a face) apart from a sense word (an ear, a hand, an eye) in the tale; day 42 applies it to the ending of the second tale.
- **RL.1.2, "Retell stories, including key details, and demonstrate understanding of their central message or lesson."** Days 39 to 42: key details with feeling words (39), a complete retelling (40), first events of the second tale (41), a lesson from its ending (42).
- **RL.1.5, "Explain major differences between books that tell stories and books that give information".** Day 40 applies day 35's two columns to the tale.
- **Foundations, days 39 to 42 (cycle 6, lessons 31 to 34):** RF.1.1a, RF.1.2a to d, RF.1.3a, b, g, RF.1.4a to c, L.1.1a, L.1.2d, L.1.2e. Short e, first cycle; -ank, -ink, -unk and -onk are segmented orally first, then mapped as the lessons give.
- **Limits that bite here.** Children answer with a picture or a spoken phrase; at most a word label is written. The second tale is credited aloud as a traditional Chinese tale and named as a story people tell. No costumes, accent imitation or acting out; no child plays Zhinu or Niulang.
- **Connections.** Joined sentences (L.1.1j) with "and" on day 40 and "but" on day 42, said aloud. L.1.1a: the letter-form record continues, eight forms a day on 39 and four on 40 (O to Z), revisions the day after.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Feeling words say how someone feels; sense words say what is seen, heard, felt, smelled or tasted; "house" or "bark" names a thing, which is neither. (2) Content notes: the second tale has a family called apart and later glad to meet; the story says they are sad to be apart and glad to meet, and no one is asked about their own family. (3) Pronunciation of Zhinu and Niulang comes from the tradition-specific read (source to check). (4) The tale's last page states a lesson in the people's words; children first build it from the events, as on day 37.

## 6. Days

## Day 39


- **15 min. Reread for feeling and sense words.** Reread the tale (g1-u2-03) with the picture talk on how someone feels and what can be seen, heard or felt. The retelling's own words are marked when it is written; the public-domain source has "a broad smile" and "knee-deep" (source to check).
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E38). Then: "Which words tell how someone feels?" Partners point to a word on the picture page.
- **10 min. Talk.** "Which words help you feel what it was like?" Turn card; one turn each; pass allowed.
- **20 min. Feeling and sense words.** Chart with a face for feelings, an ear, a hand and an eye for senses, and a box for things. Sort cards read aloud (broad smile, knee-deep, house, huge, splash, fish). Each child draws one word and labels it, then says what it helps a listener picture.
- **5 min. Exit.** E39.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Spoken pairs: short e against short a; blend and segment a four-sound word.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Explicit teaching: short e on the sound-spelling-map (spell with bed, red, net, ten); segment bank, pink and junk orally, then map the pattern as the lesson gives; cycle 6 irregular words mapped; read the cycle 5 reader.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Eight letter forms (O to V, 4 of the 10 minutes); dictation with short o and short e words.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column and cycle record.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, one word label, letter forms. Evidence: E39; the heard-six note.

## Day 40


- **15 min. Read the tale whole.** Reread whole, then name the guiding question of the story half: what stories do people tell about the sky?
- **10 min. Retell with the map.** Partners retell with the first tale's story-map held by them; first, next, last.
- **10 min. Talk.** "Why is this a story?" Day 35's two columns: characters who talk and act, events in order, a lesson; against real facts. Join two sentences with "and" aloud.
- **20 min. Complete retelling.** Each child draws three panels (first, next, last), adds word labels and says the retelling to a partner. Hear the six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E40.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Isolate first, vowel and last sounds; blend a five-sound word with e.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Decode then spell taught short e words; read the cycle 6 reader (adapted, to write) only after its words are mapped.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Four letter forms (W to Z, 4 minutes) and the revision; dictation.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column and cycle record.

Who holds what is carried: partners hold the story-map. Tool: None. By hand: three-panel drawing, labels. Evidence: E40; the three-panel retelling.

## Day 41


- **15 min. Read the second tale whole.** Credit aloud: a traditional Chinese tale, told in China for a very long time. Words before: bird, meet, separate, gather, return, support, weaver, cowherd. Read *The Weaver Girl and the Cowherd* (g1-u2-04, about 460 words) with pauses after pages 3, 5 and 7. No costumes, accents or acting out.
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E40). Pages 1 to 3: "Who are the two characters? Where do they live?" Partners point.
- **10 min. Talk.** "What does each character care about?" Turn card. The pair are sad to be apart and glad to meet: said gently, nobody asked about their own family.
- **20 min. Opening story-map.** Fresh map with the teacher: characters (Zhinu, Niulang), places (sky, earth), the tale's origin named on the map, three first events (weaving; the cowherd and his ox; the meeting). Each child draws one event.
- **5 min. Exit.** E41.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Segment and blend words with ck-free short e; count sounds.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Decode and spell short e words; cycle 6 irregular words.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Eight letter forms revised; dictation.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column and cycle record.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher models; pairs hold the turn card. Tool: None. By hand: drawing events. Evidence: E41.

## Day 42


- **15 min. Reread pages 4 to 9.** The call home, the river of stars, the magpie bridge, the yearly meeting.
- **10 min. Reread and question, with opening repair.** Opening repair first (5 minutes, from E41). "What help comes near the ending? How does it change the two?" Partners point to the picture.
- **10 min. Talk.** "Which event shows a lesson?" Join two sentences with "but" aloud ("They are apart, but the magpies help.").
- **20 min. Lesson from the ending.** Chain on cards: event (the bridge), result (they meet), a lesson in the child's words. Each child draws the event, adds a feeling or sense word as a label, and says the lesson.
- **5 min. Exit.** E42.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Isolate the vowel in spoken words; segment and blend.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Decode then spell short e words; read the cycle 6 reader.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Four letter forms and the revision; dictation.
- **30 min. Rotation.** Five six-child groups, 6 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reading and spelling at the cycle's level); the other children reread, chain and spell at stations. Today's heard six go first.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column and cycle record.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher models; partners hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, one word label. Evidence: E42; the heard-six note.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 39, "Which words help you feel what it was like?"; day 41, "Where does the tale start, and who is there?"; day 42, "Which event shows a lesson?"
- Written task, day 40: three panels (first, next, last) with word labels, and a retelling said aloud.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) three events in their order; (2) the characters named; (3) a reason it is a story.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** One repair of at most 5 minutes from field 4, in the reread part each day.
- **Retrieved.** Story-map (39 to 42), the feeling and sense chart (40 on), the two columns (40), the turn card, the cycle readers.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: feel, hear, touch, bridge, apart. Frames: "The word ___ tells how ___ feels." "I can hear ___."
- **Access.** Large cards; tactile outlines for rough and smooth; a scribe for labels.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing; a spoken answer; a scribe.
- **Reading load.** Children decode only taught short e and short o words and mapped words; both tales are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second feeling word and a second sense word in one page.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read each tale in two sittings, sort a few word cards, draw the three panels.
- **One projector.** Show each tale's pictures page by page; the cards and strips are paper.
