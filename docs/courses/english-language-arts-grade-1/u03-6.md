# Section 3.6: The research paragraph: model, draft, revise and the mid-unit check


## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md), except day 119, which says its own split. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen (a unit plan "meets" is shown; "misses" is not yet seen). Reading exits and end items read an unworked part of the unit's own texts (DEFAULTS.md exit line); every other card named below is a Curio Class original, invented for practice, and prints that label; each bird fact on a card is checked in sources.csv (g1-u3-f28 onward).

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** Day 100 studies a model paragraph for topic, factual middle and closing (W.1.2, "name a topic, supply some facts about the topic, and provide some sense of closure"; RI.1.2, "Identify the main topic and retell key details of a text"). Days 101 to 103 draft, add to and revise one beak paragraph from two provided sources (W.1.2; W.1.8, "gather information from provided sources to answer a question"; W.1.7, "Participate in shared research and writing projects"). Day 103 uses text features to point to each note's place (RI.1.5, "locate key facts or information in a text"). Days 104 and 105 are the mid-unit check; the only new teaching is u_e and e_e in foundations on 104. Foundations run cycle 16 (i_e, o_e) to 103, then cycle 17 (u_e, e_e).
- **Limits that bite here.** The product assigned on 95 is "Explain how one bird's beak helps it get food. Name the topic, give two source-supported facts and finish your explanation." Facts come only from the provided pages. Writing is a printed or scribed sentence, a word label or a drawing. Days 104 and 105 give no feedback, no hint and no reteaching; the teacher scores out of class. Predators and prey are non-graphic; no outdoor watching is needed.
- **Connections.** The paragraph-frame and closing sentence (3.4, day 94); the connection sentence and the second-source check (3.5, days 98 and 99); features (3.1 and 3.2); the notes folder kept from 95.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A fact in the paragraph comes from a labelled note; a liking is not a fact (E). (2) The closing returns to the topic; it adds no new fact and does not trail off (F). (3) The model on 100 is composed live from Beaks Find Food and is a teaching chart, not a core text. (4) Children write about the heron or the duck, the two birds with a page of their own in g1-u3-01: the heron uses the 97 notes, the duck the 92 notes. (5) Days 104 and 105 are checks: a miss is recorded as not yet seen and answered on 106 or 107.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The research-cycle chart, the main-idea-details picture and the sound-spelling map stay on the wall; the map adds u_e and e_e on 104. The paragraph-frame is filled on 100 to 102 and faded on 103. Each child's notes folder holds the notes (95 to 99), the draft (101 and 102), its written feedback (102) and the revised paragraph (103). The folder is the evidence for the 104 and 105 rows and is not reopened for help on those days.

- **Key moment 2, day 103: the organizer.** Ideal instrument: a one-page organizer with the question, two labelled note boxes and a box for where each was found. Spec: fits; paper.

## 6. Days

## Day 100



- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest miss on E99, one component, a fresh pair of sources.
- **10 min. Words, then the model.** The teacher writes the model live from Beaks Find Food, reading each sentence aloud as it is written: "A sparrow's beak helps it get food. The sparrow has a thick, strong beak. The beak cracks hard seeds. So the beak helps the sparrow eat." (a teaching chart, not a core text). Words with pictures: topic sentence, fact, closing, source.
- **15 min. The three parts, taught.** The model is cut into four strips. Pairs slide each strip under topic, facts or closing and say what the sentence does. The teacher contrasts a liking ("I like sparrows. They are fun.") with the factual middle and points to the source line for each fact (repair for E). The closing returns to the topic and adds no new fact.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "How does each fact help explain the beak?" Six pairs heard; pass allowed.
- **15 min. Sort and draw.** Pairs sort the strips of the class's joint paragraph from 94 under the three labels. Each child then draws the heron's or the duck's beak with a word label; the teacher hears six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E100.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: a long and a short vowel in a pair (slid, slide); blend /s/ /m/ /o/ /k/; first, vowel and last sound of bike; segment pride. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 16 lesson 82: i_e and o_e. Contrast short and long vowels (hop, hole; pin, pine). Decode, then spell, taught words. The reader's irregular words are mapped first as parts to remember; the adapted cycle 16 reader is read only after its patterns are mapped, then reread for meaning and expression. A picture guess is set aside: map, blend, confirm in the sentence (I).
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation: "Mike has a bike." Print the pairs.
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reader and spelling; the adapted cycle 16 reader), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The rest reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the heron or duck beak drawing; the teacher holds the model strips. Tool: None. By hand: the drawing and its word label. Evidence: E100.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: topic, fact, closing, source, beak, first, last. Frames: "My topic is ___." "A fact is ___." "Source ___ says ___." "So the beak helps the bird ___."
- **Access.** Strips and cards in enlarged print; a scribe for content; partner pointing; a picture arrow for a connector.
- **Check accommodations (104 and 105).** Cards read twice; pointing or a spoken answer; a scribe for content, never for facts; no time pressure; the retell may be spoken or drawn.
- **Reading load.** The articles, cards and model are read aloud; children read word labels, source labels, their own sentences and the foundations passages.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a second paragraph about the other bird and say how its beak differs.
