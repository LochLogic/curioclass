# Section 4.4: An opinion about the city nest, from model to independent paragraph


## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Every exit card is a new Curio Class microtext, invented for practice, read once and kept out of teaching; the stories are read aloud by the teacher and children follow the pictures. Children write little: sentences are said, scribed or copied, and the paragraph on 139 is the first independent piece of the unit.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** W.1.1 ("Write opinion pieces in which they introduce the topic or name the book they are writing about, state an opinion, supply a reason for the opinion, and provide some sense of closure"): taught 136 on a model paragraph, practised 137 jointly and 138 on a first draft, checked 139, responded to 140. RL.1.10 and RI.1.10: *A City Nest: Two Views* is reread on 136 to 140 for its reasons. RI.1.8 is practised in choosing a reason that matches a position. L.1.1b to i and L.1.2d, L.1.2e: conjunctions (because), determiners and prepositions are retrieved in the writing; no new grammar is taught. RF.1.2, RF.1.3, RF.1.3c to g, L.1.1a and RF.1.4: cycle 22 (ay and long-o ow; lessons 112 to 115) and the first day of cycle 23 (oo, ee and final y as long e; lesson 116).
- **Limits that bite.** The opinion model on 136 takes the side the 139 key does not use and is not the key's paragraph; the 139 sitting gets no help, no hint and no reteaching; 140 is the only repair slot. The 138 draft is not scored in place of the 139 paragraph. No stance on the nest is required. Draft feedback is written by the teacher by the end of 138.
- **Connections.** Reviewed: paragraph-frame (unit 1 and unit 3, day 100), the closing sentence (units 1 and 3), the connection of a fact to its source, claim-evidence-reasoning (132), joint writing with the teacher as scribe, written feedback and peer feedback strips (unit 3), share in six groups of five (unit 3, day 106), the retry-with-a-fresh-card allocation.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The four parts: topic, opinion (I think), one reason (because) and closing. (2) How to give written feedback that asks a question rather than corrects (N). (3) What a reason is: it comes from the debate and explains the opinion. (4) How the 139 sitting is run and scored (0, 1 or 2 per part). (5) A scribe may take a child's sentences on 139 when printing is not yet fluent; then printing and spelling are not judged.

## 2. Where it sits


- No readiness check. The 135 exit and the 134 sample go to the 136 opening and the 138 catch-up. The 139 paragraph and the cycle 22 sample go to the 140 groups and the unit record; the sitting is not re-sat.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment rows run here (unit plan field 5).** Day 139, W.1.1: 20 minutes; a new independent paragraph on "Should the city nest stay? Name the topic, state your opinion, use a reason from the adapted debate and finish." Accept either position with a matching text reason. Meets: "The city nest should stay. The hawks need a place for their young. Leave the nest safe." Does not: "I like birds." (B). Score topic, position, matching text reason and closure, each 0 (absent), 1 (partial) or 2 (complete); no total replaces a missing part. The 138 practice draft is not scored instead. Response on 140 only.

**Exits.** The teacher reads the card once, then asks two or three single-ask stems, one after the other, each with three picture choices (a spoken answer for a "say" stem). Shown means every stem right unaided; shown with help means right after one reread; not yet seen otherwise. Six children give reasons, rotating. Each card is a new Curio Class microtext (2026, ours, invented for practice; facts source to check).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The point-and-reason strips (132 to 134) are the reason bank. The paragraph-frame (four parts) is used filled on 136 and 137, faded on 138 and removed on 139. The class's joint draft (137) hangs until 140. Each child's 138 draft with the teacher's written question is returned on 138 and revised the same day; the 139 paragraph goes in the record folder.
- **Key moment, day 139: the independent paragraph.** Ideal instrument: a printed response sheet with the question and lined space. Spec: fits; paper, one sheet each, no frame and no help.
- **A room with nothing.** The model, the reason strips and the response sheets are paper; the debate is read aloud.

## 6. Days

## Day 136


- **5 min. Opening.** Retrieve the four-part frame with picture cards: topic, I think, because, closing. The 135 exit's commonest miss gets one fresh card.
- **15 min. Read the model.** Read *A City Nest: Two Views* and an original opinion model (ours, in the day card; it takes the other side from the day 139 key and its reason is to check against the written debate). Children point to the topic, the opinion, the reason and the closing.
- **15 min. Talk.** "What position does the model take?" "How does its closing return to its topic?" "Which debate reason supports it?" Partners two turns, six heard.
- **12 min. Analyze the parts.** Each child colours or marks the four parts on a printed copy of the model and orders four strips (topic, opinion, reason, closing) into a paragraph.
- **8 min. Closing.** Choose which of three closings returns to the topic; say why. Two sentence forms (statement, exclamation) are named in the closing (L.1.1).
- **5 min. Exit.** E136.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** A long and a short vowel pair (tub, tube); blend /pl/ /ay/; first, middle and last sound of stay; segment snow.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 22 lesson 112: connect ay and ow to words and the matched reader; map one irregular word (named at build). Chain: stay, play, tray, away; window, pillow, shadow (two syllables).
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Print the topic strip as a copy. Dictation: "We stay away from the nest."
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the cycle record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (the adapted reader Pat's Birthday, first use, conditional on the map), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The rest reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record, the exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the marked model and four strips; the teacher holds the debate and the model. Tool: None. By hand: the marked model. Evidence: E136.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: opinion, reason, position, because, stay, leave. Frames: "I think ___ because ___." "The nest should ___ because ___." Home-language talk and pictures preview each word.
- **Access.** Enlarged cards and sheets; tactile strips; content dictation or a scribe; partner reading of the feedback strip; a sitting in a small group.
- **Check accommodations.** Stems read twice; a scribe for the sentences on 139; a spoken paragraph recorded by the teacher in the child's own words (the scribe cannot establish printing or spelling).
- **Reading load.** The debate and the model are read aloud; children read word labels and the cycle sentences.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second reason from the debate and say whether it strengthens the opinion.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the model and the debate aloud, mark the four parts together, and write the opinion with the parent as scribe. The 139 paragraph is written with no help on a printed sheet, scored 0 to 2 per part by the parent. Thirty-five minutes by the unit plan's split.
- **One projector.** Show the model and the joint draft; the sitting is on paper at desks.
