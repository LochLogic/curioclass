# Section 3.10: The expert-bird riddle, the drawing, the foundations observation and the unit response


## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md), except day 119, which says its own split. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen (a unit plan "meets" is shown; "misses" is not yet seen). Reading exits and end items read an unworked part of the unit's own texts (DEFAULTS.md exit line); every other card named below is a Curio Class original, invented for practice, and prints that label; each bird fact on a card is checked in sources.csv (g1-u3-f28 onward). Days 116 to 118 each take a ten-child foundations observation of 30 teacher minutes; day 119 says its own split (40, 20, 40, 20).

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** Day 116 drafts the expert-bird riddle from verified facts (W.1.2 and W.1.8 return through the notes; RI.1.2). Day 117 revises it after written peer feedback and edits taught spellings (L.1.2d, "conventional spelling for words with common spelling patterns"). Day 118 completes the scientific drawing (RI.1.6 and RI.1.7 return). Day 119 shares the riddle and drawing game and responds to the unit evidence. Days 116 to 118 carry the foundations observation: RF.1.3d ("every syllable must have a vowel sound"), RF.1.3e ("Decode two-syllable words ... by breaking the words into syllables") and RF.1.3f ("Read words with inflectional endings"), with RF.1.3c evidence and RF.1.3 completion left to the course record on 158 and 159.
- **Limits that bite here.** The riddle has a hidden name and two verified clues, printed or scribed; the drawing has labels. Observation cohorts are ten different children a day, 3 minutes each. The unit plan's "assessment: observe" tag on 116 to 118 names the foundations observation; the topic work is practice. Day 119 never repeats a completed record.
- **Connections.** The riddle card and verified clues (108 to 114); the peer-question idea (88 K repair); the annotated drawing and the scientific drawing card (87 to 89, 109); the unit's cycles 16 to 18.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Peer feedback is a question strip the partner picks (what, where, how), then written by the teacher for the child who cannot print it. (2) The ending list and the words of 119 are reserved and never taught. (3) On 119 observations finish before the midpoint; the response is the last 20 minutes.

## 6. Days

## Day 116



- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest miss on E114 (D), one component, a fresh page; day 115 carried no reteaching.
- **5 min. Riddle frame.** The teacher shows the riddle card: "I have ___. I ___. What am I?" with the name held back and the drawing under a flap.
- **10 min. Choose two clues.** Each child takes the verified clues and picks the two best, one for shape and one for use.
- **25 min. Draft the riddle independently.** Printed or scribed; the child writes both clues and sketches the bird. No help with the facts; the teacher circulates.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "How can the bird's name be held until the answer?" Six pairs heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E116.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: a long and a short vowel in a pair (hat, hate); blend /k/ /l/ /o/ /z/; first, vowel and last sound of mule; segment flute. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Consolidate the taught cycles in small groups: map, blend, read, spell and reread; a matched decodable and a short supported topic reading; no new pattern.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation: "Pete rides a bike." Print the pairs.
- **30 min. Observation cohort.** Cohort 1: ten children not yet recorded (children 1 to 10 on the class list), 3 minutes each at the teacher table (syllable count 0.5 minute, divide and decode 0.75, endings 1.75; field 3). The other twenty children retain explicit review and matched reading in pairs at stations; the teacher hears no one else.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Cohort record, heard-six column and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the riddle draft and the drawing sketch; the teacher holds the cohort list. Tool: None. By hand: the riddle draft and the sketch. Evidence: E116; the first cohort rows (field 3 of section 3.9).

## Day 119



- **40 min. Complete missing evidence.** The teacher hears each child whose 115 record or cohort entry is missing, 3 minutes each (up to 13 children) with a fresh bird page; no completed record is repeated. All observations finish before the midpoint (minute 60). The rest rehearse the riddle and drawing in pairs and set out the gallery.
- **20 min. Share the riddle and drawing game.** Six groups of five. Each child reads the riddle with the name held back (about 1 minute), the others guess and point to the drawing (1 minute) and the child names the source of one clue (1 minute); the teacher joins each group for about 3 minutes. This is a share, not a mark.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts on taught words, one per component. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Review map, blend, read, spell and reread on taught words.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation: "Mike has a kite." Print the pairs.
- **10 min. Rotation.** Four groups at the teacher table (matched reader), 2 minutes each, six heard; the rest reread in pairs.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column and the unit record.
- **10 min. Repair by gap.** Four groups at the teacher table, 2 to 3 minutes each, one repair per child from field 4: topic and evidence (A, B, C), features (D), writing and participation (E, F, G), foundations (H, I, J, K, L, M, N, O). If more than a third missed one component, the teacher models it to everyone first. The other groups read their riddle to a partner.
- **10 min. Fresh retry (the exit).** Each child retries only the missed component on the roadrunner card or its reserved words (field 3). The teacher hears every child in the completion group.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the missed component and the roadrunner card; the teacher holds the group list. Tool: None. By hand: the retry and the letter-form pairs. Evidence: the 119 retry, recorded as shown, shown with help or not yet seen against the 115 to 118 rows.

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 116: "How can the bird's name be held until the answer?" Day 117: a partner exchange after the peer strip (restate, then reply). Day 118: "How does the finished drawing support the riddle's clues?" Day 119: the riddle game guesses. A child may pass.
- **Written task, days 116 to 118.** An expert-bird riddle with a hidden name and two verified clues, a revision after a peer strip, and a scientific drawing with a label and a fact line.
- **Look-fors.** Each clue is a fact a page supports with its source label; the name is held back; the revision answers the strip's question; the drawing's labels name real parts and agree with the clues.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: riddle, clue, feedback, revise, edit, drawing, label. Frames: "I have ___. I ___. What am I?" "Your clue says ___. Where is it from?"
- **Access.** Enlarged cards and strips; a scribe for content; a pointer arrow; partner reading aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** No topic check falls here; the foundations observation is taken at the teacher table with the words read twice if asked; no time pressure beyond the 3 minutes.
- **Reading load.** The pages are read aloud; children read their own clues and the foundations words.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second riddle for another expert bird, with two verified clues.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Write and revise one riddle; take the observation words one at a time; play the game with a grown-up.
- **One projector.** Show the riddle card and the drawing; the gallery is printed.
