# Section 4.1: Ben and Nia, two children who call for help


## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Reading exits read an unworked part of the unit's own texts (DEFAULTS.md exit line); cards for the other exits are new Curio Class microtexts, invented for practice, read once and kept out of teaching; the stories are read aloud by the teacher and children follow the pictures.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** RL.1.9 ("Compare and contrast the adventures and experiences of characters in stories"): 120 to 123 build each child's experience on a story-map (Ben, then Nia); 124 teaches the comparison on the frame Both ___. But ___. RL.1.10 and RI.1.10 ("read prose ... with prompting and support"): *Ben and the Bird* chapters 1, 2 and 3 are read whole on 120, 122 and 124, *Nia Calls for Help* whole on 123. L.1.4 and L.1.4a ("Use sentence-level context as a clue to the meaning of a word or phrase"): the sentence clue for trained on 121 and the two meanings of fly on 124; RI.1.4 is the question "What does this word mean here?". L.1.4b and L.1.4c are taught on 126 and 131 (4.2, 4.3); here only the -ed on trained is shown as a part of a word (review of kindergarten). L.1.1b to e ("Use common, proper, and possessive nouns"; "singular and plural nouns with matching verbs"; "personal, possessive, and indefinite pronouns"): nouns 121, agreement 122, personal pronouns 123, possessive and indefinite pronouns 124. Tense, adjectives, conjunctions, determiners and prepositions are 4.2 and 4.3. RF.1.2, RF.1.3, RF.1.3c to g, L.1.1a, L.1.2d, L.1.2e and RF.1.4: cycle 19 (ar and or, lessons 96 to 100); all 52 letter forms are printed across 120 to 124 in dated sets.
- **Limits that bite.** Day 120 spends 10 of its 60 topic minutes on the retrieval task, and day 121 spends 10 on repair groups. The opening pair (Ben chapter 1 and Nia paragraph 1) is reserved for the day 129 check and is never compared in teaching; the worked comparisons on 124 use Ben chapters 2 and 3 against Nia paragraphs 2 and 3. The word kit is reserved for the 121 exit. The ending pair is reserved for 153.
- **Connections.** Reviewed from units 1 to 3: the story-map (who, where and when, what happens), the discussion-protocol with the turn card, opening repair, heard-six column, Return and repair, the wing facts of unit 3. Reviewed from kindergarten: Both ___. But ___. (kindergarten u04-1, day 123) and word parts -ed.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) The story's rule is said plainly each day: children never pick up a wild bird; they call a grown-up. (2) Which pair is reserved and why. (3) How the sentence clue is modeled by pointing, not by telling the meaning first. (4) Content note: a bird with a hanging wing and a dove stunned by a window; nothing graphic, both end in a trained helper's care. Nobody is asked about a yard, a pet or a window at home.

**Day 120 retrieval keys.** Two new cards, labeled invented for practice: "Jo sees a hurt bird. Jo calls Dad. Dad phones a bird helper. The bird needs trained care." "Al sees a hurt bird. Al calls Mum. Mum phones a bird helper. The bird needs trained care." Asks, one at a time: the same (both call an adult); different (Dad, Mum); the sentence that explains the call (the bird needs trained care); an opinion with that reason; what trained means and which words help (learned special care; bird helper); who receives the call, to expand "Jo calls" (Jo calls Dad); order topic, reason and closing cards; point to the problem on the story-map; put a source label beside a note. Misses: a preference with no reason (B); an unrelated fact as the reason (C); a word guessed without the sentence (E); a fragment (Q).

**Exits.** The teacher reads the card once, then asks two or three single-ask stems, one after the other, each with three picture choices (a spoken answer for a "say" stem). Shown means every stem right unaided; shown with help means right after one reread; not yet seen otherwise. Six children give reasons, rotating. Each card for a non-reading exit is a new Curio Class microtext (2026, ours, invented for practice; facts source to check). ## 6. Days

## Day 120


- **5 min. Opening.** Show the question card: "How can people help birds that need it?" Read page 1 of chapter 1 aloud, up to "It sits very still." Children notice and wonder in one turn each with a partner; five pairs are heard. Pictures preview the words notice and help.
- **10 min. Retrieval task.** The Jo and Al cards (field 3), eight asks, one at a time, each answered by partners in 20 seconds with two pairs heard: the same; the different; the sentence that explains the call; an opinion with that reason; what trained means; expand "Jo calls"; order the topic, reason and closing cards; point to the problem on the story-map and put a source label beside a note. The record names each miss by row.
- **15 min. Read chapter 1.** Read pages 1 and 2 up to "Then he stops." and stop: partners say what Ben could do (one turn). Read on to the end of page 3 (about 330 words, three minutes with the pictures). Reread page 2 slowly while children point to the hanging wing in the picture. Ben's call to Dad is the answer to the question card.
- **10 min. Talk.** Three spoken questions, one ask each, partners two turns with the turn card, six children heard: "What problem does Ben notice?" "Which action words show what Ben does?" "What do you know about wings that explains why the robin needs help?" (review of unit 3: wings help a bird fly, find food and get away).
- **15 min. Story-map.** Each child draws chapter 1 on a story-map in three boxes (who, where and when, what happens; review of units 1 and 2), nine minutes, and copies one word label under the problem (bird or wing) and the page number (2). Partners tell their map (six minutes).
- **5 min. Exit.** E120.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: a long and a short vowel in a pair (cap, cape); blend /j/ /ar/; first, vowel and last sound of cork; segment park. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 19 lesson 96: map ar (jar) and or (corn) on the sound-spelling-map and say that both letters make one sound. Chain: car, card, cart, part, park, dark; then for, fork, fort, short. Clap, chop, write each.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Print Aa, Bb, Cc, Dd, Ee (ten forms, the first dated set). Decode, then spell, two words; dictation: "A car is in the dark." Written feedback by tomorrow.
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the cycle record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (the adapted cycle 19 reader Looking for Mars, after the correspondence and irregular word are mapped (to check at build)), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The rest reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record, the printing set and the retrieval misses by row.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a story-map and a printing set; the teacher holds the cards and the story pages. Tool: None. By hand: the story-map and the dated set. Evidence: E120.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: bird, help, notice, wait, trained, helper. Frames: "Ben sees ___. He calls ___." "Both ___. But ___." Home-language talk and pictures preview each word.
- **Access.** Enlarged cards and print; tactile choices; content dictation; a picture route for any child upset by the hurt bird; partner pointing.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing or a spoken answer; stems read twice; a scribe for the labels; a small group instead of partners.
- **Reading load.** The stories are read aloud; children read word labels and the cycle sentences.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second likeness or difference between Ben and Nia and say which words show it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read a chapter aloud, draw the story-map together, then say "Both ___. But ___." for the child's two favourite pages. Four letter forms a day, a short word chain, 35 minutes (the unit plan's split). The parent says the rule: never pick up a wild bird.
- **One projector.** Show the pictures and the chart one page at a time; children work on paper in pairs.
