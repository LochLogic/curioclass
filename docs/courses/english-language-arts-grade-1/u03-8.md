# Section 3.8: The riddle launch, the scientific drawing card and the contents route


## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md), except day 119, which says its own split. Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen (a unit plan "meets" is shown; "misses" is not yet seen). Reading exits and end items read an unworked part of the unit's own texts (DEFAULTS.md exit line); every other card named below is a Curio Class original, invented for practice, and prints that label; each bird fact on a card is checked in sources.csv (g1-u3-f28 onward).

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** Day 108 launches the riddle task from a story, two poems and an article (RI.1.2, "Identify the main topic and retell key details of a text"; RL.1.10 and RI.1.10, read "with prompting and support"; SL.1.1, "Participate in collaborative conversations"). Day 109 analyzes a scientific drawing for accurate labels (RI.1.6 and RI.1.7 return here with the drawing). Day 110 chooses an expert bird and finds facts in two provided pages (W.1.7, W.1.8). Day 111 models the contents, heading and glossary route (RI.1.5, "locate key facts or information in a text"). Foundations: cycle 17 on 108, then cycle 18 on 109 to 111 (RF.1.3f, "Read words with inflectional endings").
- **Limits that bite here.** The riddle task assigned on 108 is: "Make a riddle about an expert bird: hide its name, give two true clues from the pages and draw the bird." Facts come from the provided pages. Writing is a label, a word, a short printed clue or a scribed one. Watching from a window or from pictures is welcome; nothing outdoors is needed.
- **Connections.** The story reread (g1-u3-02) and the poems (g1-u3-03) join the article set; the paragraph work of 100 to 107; the heading, glossary and menu routes (3.1 and 3.2); the riddle and clue terms of kindergarten (u01-3).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) A scientific drawing names only parts that are really there; a label is a word placed beside the part. (2) A line break puts a clue at a line end; it is a poem's choice, not a fact. (3) The four expert birds each have two or more pages in g1-u3-01 (sparrow, duck, heron, hawk; two pages each, made in the expert-bird page sets). (4) The unit plan's exit cases for 108 to 111 reuse taught birds (the sparrow beak, the duck feet), so new cards are used here (see the plan points). (5) The story of g1-u3-02 is Mina at the Window; the unit plan's exit names a different child, so Mina is used.

## 6. Days

## Day 108



- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest miss on E107 (A), one component, a fresh card.
- **5 min. Launch.** The teacher plays one riddle aloud (hidden name, two clues) and names the task: a riddle about an expert bird, two true clues and a drawing, shared on 119. The teacher says the riddle is invented.
- **12 min. Two poems.** The teacher reads Feather Coat and Wing Clues whole, twice (g1-u3-03, about 77 words in all). Children point to a clue word and to a line break; a line break is where a line ends and the next begins, and it puts a clue at the line end.
- **10 min. Story and article.** The teacher rereads one page of Mina at the Window (g1-u3-02) and the opening of Feathers Protect (g1-u3-01), under 120 words in all; children point to one body-part clue in each.
- **10 min. Discussion routine, reviewed.** The turn card and starters (review of grade 1 u01-3): listen, restate, reply. Pairs practice once on "Which clue was easiest to find?" The teacher hears six pairs.
- **13 min. Talk and draw.** Turn card: "What does the poem imagine that the article explains?" Then each child draws a bird part from the poem or the article with a word label; the teacher hears six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E108.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: a long and a short vowel in a pair (not, note); blend /k/ /l/ /u/ /z/; first, vowel and last sound of Pete; segment theme. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 17 lesson 90: the cycle progress sample (field 3) and same-block regrouping; read and spell only taught patterns and mapped irregular words. The cycle 17 reader is reread for expression.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Letter forms: print four pairs, I i to L l, dated and kept for 118. Dictation: "Luke has a flute." (ate mapped).
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (the cycle 17 sample, then the matched reader), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The rest reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations. Every child is sampled at the teacher table, one minute each across the day's groups.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a riddle card and a drawing; the teacher holds the poem pages. Tool: None. By hand: the drawing with a word label and the letter-form pairs. Evidence: E108; the cycle 17 sample (field 3).

## Day 109



- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest miss on E108 (A or K), one component, a fresh card.
- **10 min. Words, then the card.** Words with pictures: label, scientific drawing, accurate, correct, part. A scientific drawing names only parts that are really there. The teacher shows a card, invented for practice: a duck's foot drawn and labeled "feather", with the Duck at the Pond foot sentence beneath.
- **15 min. Analyze the card.** Pairs point to the wrong label, say the sentence that proves it (repair for C: a label is checked against the page, not the picture), then say the label that belongs. The teacher models the fix.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "What does the diagram show beyond the sentence?" Six pairs heard; pass allowed.
- **15 min. Draw and label.** Each child draws a duck's beak from the Duck at the Pond page with one word label and a source line; the teacher hears six.
- **5 min. Exit.** E109.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: a base and its ending (ride, rides; bake, baked); blend /b/ /a/ /k/ /t/; first, vowel and last sound of rides; segment baking. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 18 lesson 91: explicit teaching first. The endings -s, -ing and -ed on silent-e bases: the e is dropped before -ing and -ed (bake, baking, baked), and stays before -s (bakes). No consonant doubling. Taught words: bakes, baking, baked, rides, riding, used. The previous reader (cycle 17) is reread until the cycle 18 patterns are mapped.
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Letter forms: print four pairs, M m to P p, dated and kept for 118. Dictation: "Pete baked cakes." (baked mapped).
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (familiar words and the cycle 17 reader), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The rest reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds the drawing card; the teacher holds the model card. Tool: None. By hand: the beak drawing with a word label and the letter-form pairs. Evidence: E109.

## Day 110

- **10 min. Choose.** Each child points to one of four bird picture cards and takes that bird's two pages. A bird may be chosen by many children; no child is asked to report on home.
- **22 min. Find facts.** Each child finds two facts on the two pages, one from each, and writes or has scribed a note with its page label; the pointer arrow shows where on the page. The teacher hears six and repairs B at the page.
- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "Which two facts together make a clue only your bird has?" Six pairs heard.
- **5 min. Exit.** E110.
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: a base and its ending (ride, riding; use, using); blend /j/ /o/ /k/ /s/; first, vowel and last sound of rides; segment using. Spoken only.

## Day 111

- **10 min. Talk.** Turn card: "Why is this route stronger than guessing from a cover?" Six pairs heard.

- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Letter forms: print four pairs, U u to X x, dated and kept for 118. Dictation: "Mike baked a cake." (baked mapped).
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reader and spelling; the adapted cycle 18 reader), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. ## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 108: "What does the poem imagine that the article explains?" Day 109: "What does the diagram show beyond the sentence?" Day 110: "Which two facts together make a clue only your bird has?" Day 111: "Why is this route stronger than guessing from a cover?" Each is a turn-card exchange with linked turns; a child may pass.
- **Written task, days 108 to 111.** Short: a word label and a source line (109), two source-labeled notes (110), a numbered route (111). The riddle itself is drafted on 116.
- **Look-fors.** A label names a real part; a note has its page label; a route has all three steps; a reply restates the partner's point.

- **Check accommodations.** None; no check falls in this section.
- **Reading load.** The poems, story page and article pages are read aloud; children read word labels and the cycle words.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second expert bird, with two clues from its pages.
