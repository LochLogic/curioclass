# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 1

Course id ela-k12 (grade 1 year; slug english-language-arts-grade-1), grade 1. Draft frame, 2026-11-13, fixed 2026-11-14 after the two cold reads (docs/reviews/frame-ela-k-2-fixes-2026-11-14.md); the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text, in its order: tools and work, the sun, moon and stars, birds' bodies, caring for birds. Each unit is one topic with its own set of texts; units 3 and 4 share one topic (birds) at two depths.
- **Days.** 160: 154 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 2, 2 after unit 4) and 2 respond days (one after each check). The time a day is about 120 minutes, five days a week, in two blocks: a topic block of about 60 minutes (read-aloud and close reading, talk, writing and language on the unit's texts) and a foundations block of about 60 minutes (phonemic awareness, phonics, spelling and handwriting, fluency, decodable reading in small groups). Grade 1 shares the day with math, science and social studies, so the course assumes two hours, not the base text's three, and leaves out its Labs hour (source map). Why the total fits: the base text's four modules hold 146 topic lessons and its Skills Block 130 foundations lessons; 154 unit days give every topic lesson a day with 8 over for the end tasks and rereading, and the foundations block 24 days over for re-teaching and extra decodable and fluency reading. The 120-minute day, against grade 3's 90, is with Cory (For the lead, item 1).
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 1 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Every grade 1 standard and lettered part is owned by one of the 31 rows and each row sits in one unit below; where a standard is split across rows (RF.1.3, L.1.1, L.1.2, L.1.4), the rows name the split. Check set: each unit plan compares the unit with the base text's unit assessments for its module and the published K to 2 review criteria lines on phonics order, decodables and fluency; the Units table's "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects. The bar is the lens (docs/agents/lenses/ela.md) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md, with RUBRIC-ELA.md for its text, question and writing lines. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in order: higher court, backed sources, dignity and openness, grade and life experience.
- **Base text.** EL Education K-5 Language Arts, 2017 edition, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017, docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md): the four grade 1 modules and the 2017 K-2 Reading Foundations Skills Block, grade 1 (cycles 1 to 25). Every trade book is replaced by a Curio Class text written to the per-grade text spec; the short texts the base wrote itself (for example "What's Best? The Debate About Pale Male's Nest" and "Birds are Human Helpers" in module 4) are adapted with the credit line. The 2017 K-2 Skills Block is CC BY 4.0: EL's terms of use list "the EL Education K-2 Reading Foundations Skills Block (2017 Edition)" under CC BY 4.0 (store excerpt licence/eleducation-terms-of-use-excerpt-2026-11-12.txt, read 2026-11-12), and the held files, 2019 printings of the 2017 edition, print the CC BY 4.0 line and no NonCommercial line (the store's NOTES.md, "Skills Block answer"). Only the 2024 Skills Block is CC BY-NC; it is not held and never used, nor is the 2025 edition. Third-party items noted as such inside the Skills Block files, trade books, logos and the base text's videos are replaced, never adapted. Day credit: "EL Education K-5 Language Arts Curriculum (2017), grade 1, module M, unit U, lesson L", and the Skills Block cycle and lesson for the foundations block.
- **Where a row sits.** A row sits in the unit that teaches it to the grade 1 standard and checks it, following the base text's unit assessments. Before that unit the class works the thread at the kindergarten level; after it, the skill returns as review on new texts. Foundations rows run in the Skills Block's cycle order: each sits in the unit where its thread opens, every later unit that teaches more of it names it "continued" with the cycles, its full check comes in the unit where its last pattern is taught, and each unit's checks cover only the patterns taught by then.
- **Texts and the reading level.** Read-alouds sit two or three grade levels above what students decode; students read decodable texts bound to the patterns taught, and by unit 4 short grade 1 texts of their own. Each unit plan writes a text record (measure, what makes it hard, the task, why here), a question set and a word list. Each unit mixes stories, poems and informational texts. The year's long text read whole is unit 4's four-to-five-chapter Curio Class story of a child who cares for an injured bird, read across two weeks. The long story is read in module 4 unit 1's lessons in place of that unit's two trade-book stories of injured birds that people helped, so it adds no days; a second short Curio Class bird story beside it gives the RL.1.9 comparison. Unit 2 retells two pourquoi tales in Curio Class words, credited: an Efik and Ibibio tale of southern Nigeria, why the sun and moon live in the sky, from Elphinstone Dayrell's Folk Stories from Southern Nigeria (1910, public domain), and the Chinese tale of the weaver girl and the cowherd, two stars kept apart by the river of stars that meet once a year, retold from the traditional story (no single copyrighted telling followed).
- **Decodable texts.** Curio Class writes or adapts its own: the 2017 Skills Block decodable readers (24 readers and a poem across modules 1 to 4, CC BY 4.0) are adapted where their pictures are cleared and the gaps written new, each bound to the cycle's taught patterns and irregular words, checked against the sound-spelling-map. No guessing from the picture or the first letter.
- **Writing calendar.** The base text's tasks, kept. Unit 1: informative (a tool the child designs for the classroom and a piece on how it helps, the performance task), through the process. Unit 2: narrative poems about what the sun sees across its day, in order with time words, revised and edited (the performance task), and shared research notes on the sky. Unit 3: informative research (how a bird's body parts help it survive), then a bird riddle card and a scientific drawing for a matching game (the performance task), revised. Unit 4: opinion (whether a hawk's nest on a city building should come down, with a reason and a closing), through the process, then a window bird portrait with an informational paragraph on keeping birds safe (the performance task). The paragraph-frame starts in unit 1.
- **Talk.** A named discussion protocol on the unit's text most days, at least two a week (e1-discussion).
- **Paper path and devices.** Every task has a paper route. No student accounts. W.1.6 is met on a teacher-held or shared classroom device on which the class makes a digital piece together, with at least two kinds of tool across the year (a typed class book of the sun poems in unit 2, a drawing or audio tool for the bird portraits in unit 4), and the teacher records each child's part; the paper route alone does not complete it. No timer is shown to a child; fluency is heard by the teacher, who holds any watch.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 2's pourquoi tales from living traditions also need a qualified reader from or expert on each tradition (Efik and Ibibio; Chinese); who, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 2 (2 days, mid-year).** One-to-one and small-group: a decodable text not seen before read aloud to the teacher (accuracy and self-correction noted); words with short vowels, clusters, digraphs and endings read and spelled; ten grade 1 irregular words; a cold informational read-aloud with key-detail and connection questions; a short written retell of a story with its lesson. No timer shown; the teacher holds any watch.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Small-group re-teaching of what the check shows, ending with a short fresh task. Children reading below the decodable level get a named catch-up routine in unit 3's foundations block.
- **After unit 4 (2 days, end of year).** A grade 1 text not seen before read aloud for accuracy and expression; silent-e, vowel-team, r-controlled and two-syllable words read and spelled; a cold informational text read by the student with main-topic, text-feature and author-reason questions; an opinion paragraph about a familiar text.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teaching of the gaps; the record goes on to the grade 2 teacher with what the respond day changed.
