# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 1

Course id ela-k12 (grade 1 year; slug english-language-arts-grade-1), grade 1. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one for each module of the base text, in its order: tools and work, the sun, moon and stars, birds' bodies, caring for birds. Each unit is one topic with its own set of texts; units 3 and 4 share one topic (birds) at two depths.
- **Days.** 160: 154 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 2, 2 after unit 4) and 2 respond days (one after each check). The time a day is about 120 minutes, five days a week, in two blocks: a topic block of about 60 minutes (read-aloud and close reading, talk, writing and language on the unit's texts) and a foundations block of about 60 minutes (phonemic awareness, phonics, spelling and handwriting, fluency, decodable reading in small groups). Grade 1 shares the day with math, science and social studies, so the course assumes two hours, not the base text's three, and leaves out its Labs hour (source map). Why the total fits: the base text's four modules hold 146 topic lessons and its Skills Block 130 foundations lessons; 154 unit days give every topic lesson a day with 8 over for the end tasks and rereading, and the foundations block 24 days over for re-teaching and extra decodable and fluency reading (For the lead, item 1).
- **Standard.** Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 1 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Every grade 1 standard and lettered part is owned by one of the 31 rows and each row sits in one unit below; where a standard is split across rows (RF.1.3, L.1.1, L.1.2, L.1.4), the rows name the split. Check set: each unit plan compares the unit with the base text's unit assessments for its module and the published K to 2 review criteria lines on phonics order, decodables and fluency; the Units table's "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects. The bar is the lens (docs/agents/lenses/ela.md) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md, with RUBRIC-ELA.md for its text, question and writing lines. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in order: higher court, backed sources, dignity and openness, grade and life experience.
- **Base text.** EL Education K-5 Language Arts, 2017 edition, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017, docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md): the four grade 1 modules and the 2017 K-2 Reading Foundations Skills Block, grade 1 (cycles 1 to 25). Every trade book is replaced by a Curio Class text written to the per-grade text spec; the short texts the base wrote itself are adapted with the credit line. The 2025 edition and the 2024 Skills Block (CC BY-NC) are never used. Day credit: "EL Education K-5 Language Arts Curriculum (2017), grade 1, module M, unit U, lesson L", and the Skills Block cycle and lesson for the foundations block.
- **Where a row sits.** A row sits in the unit that teaches it to the grade 1 standard and checks it, following the base text's unit assessments. Before that unit the class works the thread at the kindergarten level; after it, the skill returns as review on new texts. Foundations rows run in the Skills Block's pattern order: each sits where its first element is taught, and later units name it as continued.
- **Texts and the reading level.** Read-alouds sit two or three grade levels above what students decode; students read decodable texts bound to the patterns taught, and by unit 4 short grade 1 texts of their own. Each unit plan writes a text record (measure, what makes it hard, the task, why here), a question set and a word list. Each unit mixes stories, poems and informational texts. The year's long text read whole is unit 4's four-to-five-chapter Curio Class story of a child who cares for an injured bird, read across two weeks. Unit 2 retells a West African pourquoi tale of why the sun and moon live in the sky in Curio Class words, from the public-domain 1910 telling by Elphinstone Dayrell, credited, alongside a Curio Class telling from a second tradition.
- **Decodable texts.** Curio Class writes its own, each bound to the cycle's taught patterns and irregular words, checked against the sound-spelling-map. No guessing from the picture or the first letter.
- **Writing calendar.** Unit 1: informative (how a tool helps a person do a job), through the process. Unit 2: narrative (a story about the sun, moon or stars, in order with time words) and a class research book. Unit 3: informative research (how a bird's body helps it survive), revised. Unit 4: opinion (the best way to help birds, with a reason and a closing), through the process. The paragraph-frame starts in unit 1.
- **Talk.** A named discussion protocol on the unit's text most days, at least two a week (e1-discussion).
- **Paper path and devices.** Every task has a paper route. No student accounts. W.1.6 is met on a teacher-held or shared classroom device on which the class makes one digital piece together (unit 2, again in unit 4); the paper route alone does not complete it. No timer is shown to a child; fluency is heard by the teacher, who holds any watch.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 2's pourquoi tales from living traditions also need a qualified reader from or expert on each tradition; who, and any pay, is Cory's.
