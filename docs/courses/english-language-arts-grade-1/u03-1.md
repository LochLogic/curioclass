# Section 3.1: Reading a bird article: topic, details and the page's features

## Header


Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label written. Each day is 120 minutes: a topic block of 60 and a foundations block of 60 (DEFAULTS.md). Marking is shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Reading exits read an unworked part of the unit's own texts (DEFAULTS.md exit line); cards for the other exits are Curio Class originals, invented for practice, and print that label.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **Share of the unit's standards.** This section opens the bird unit and teaches three things from nothing: finding the topic and key details of an information text (RI.1.2, "Identify the main topic and retell key details of a text"), using text features to find a fact (RI.1.5, "Know and use various text features ... to locate key facts or information in a text") and telling what a picture shows from what the words say (RI.1.6 and RI.1.7). Talk rules and linked turns are taught on 79 (SL.1.1, SL.1.3 and a to c). Foundations run cycle 12 (closed syllables in two-syllable words; RF.1.2, RF.1.3d, e, g, RF.1.4, L.1.1a, L.1.2d, e) in the order of the source map.
- **Limits that bite.** No child reads an article alone: the teacher reads each text aloud, and children decode only sentences written to the cycle's taught letters. Text features are taught on the printed pages of the set; no live screen is used here (the menu is 85). Facts come only from the set and from the cards, never from outside it. Predators and prey are named plainly and never pictured in a graphic way.
- **Connections.** Main topic and key details, the main-idea-details picture, the paragraph-frame, the research-cycle chart, the note with its source named, the turn card, the heard-six column and the opening repair all come from units 1 and 2 and are named as review, not taught again. Science 1-LS1-1 (bird body parts) is taught before day 79; if it was missed, the set's own pages teach each part and its job, with no outside facts.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** (1) Why a heading, a contents line and a glossary entry are different jobs: each leads somewhere, none is the fact itself. (2) What counts as a topic and what is only a detail, using the sorted cards. (3) Why a label names a part and the words say what it does. (4) How the daily catch-up group is chosen from the day 78 record. (5) The word "down" has two senses on 83: the glossary's soft feathers, and the direction word in "smooth its feathers down"; only the glossary sense is checked.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The main-idea-details picture (topic card on top, detail cards under it) is modeled on 79, held by partners on 80 and 81, and by each child on 82 and 83, with fewer prompts each day. The sound-spelling-map stays up for foundations. Syllable-types starts on 79 with the closed syllable (clap, then chop between the two middle consonants). The discussion-protocol (turn card and starters) is made explicit on 79 and returns daily. Heading, contents entry, label and glossary entry are carried as four printed feature cards on the wall, added one a day from 80 to 83.
- **Key moments and instruments.** (1) 81, finding a fact by a route: the ideal instrument is a printed contents page that the child can trace with a finger. Spec: fits; paper. (2) 83, a glossary and a label: the ideal is a printed glossary page beside a diagram. Spec: fits; paper. No tool in this section is a key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** The articles are read aloud from one printed copy; the diagram and the glossary are drawn on the board. Cards are paper.

## 6. Days

## Day 79


- **5 min. Hook and question.** Four bird-head pictures (the article's page 1, shown before the text) on the board: "What do you notice?" Pairs point and say; the class question goes up: "How do a bird's body parts help it survive?"
- **12 min. Words, then read Beaks Find Food.** Words with pictures first: bird, food, observe, survive, detail, topic, beak, bill. The teacher reads the whole article aloud (about 190 words, about 2 minutes), pausing at each heading for the picture; then a second read of page 1 with the children following by finger.
- **8 min. Retrieval.** Six minutes: name the topic and two details, then the title and author. Two minutes: order three blank cards (topic, facts, closing), and point to the source label on a unit 2 notebook page and say what question it answers. A miss: rebuild one step in the next writing group.
- **12 min. Topic and details, taught.** The teacher models the main-idea-details picture on "Strong Beaks for Seeds": topic card, then two detail cards. Pairs do "Flat Beaks for Mud" with the cards; the teacher moves between pairs and hears three pairs aloud.
- **8 min. Match and open the question.** Four feeding-sentence cards matched to four beak drawings in pairs; then the class question is reread and "What do we think?" is recorded on the chart. A child may pass.
- **10 min. Talk rules and linked turns.** The talk-rules card (listen with care, one speaker, ask if you are confused). The teacher and Pip show a turn: Pip speaks one line at a time. Pairs take two linked turns on "How does a beak help a bird?" and one clarifying question, "What do you mean?" Six minutes in pairs, four to hear three pairs.
- **5 min. Exit.** E79 (field 3).
- **5 min. Oral sounds.** Four spoken prompts: clap robin and split s-un, blend c-a-t and read cat (the retrieval, two minutes); a long and a short vowel in a pair (pin, pine); blend /s/ /l/ /e/ /d/; segment crab. Spoken only.
- **10 min. Map and spell.** Cycle 12 lesson 61, explicit teaching first: a closed syllable has one vowel sound; clap a two-syllable word, then chop between the two middle consonants (pic-nic, sun-set, nap-kin). Chain on cards: picnic, sunset, napkin, rabbit. The sound-spelling-map stays up. The cycle 11 reader is reread until the cycle's correspondences and irregular words are mapped (sample words; the lesson governs).
- **10 min. Letters, handwriting and dictation.** Dictation of two words and one sentence ("A rabbit is on the mat." a, is, the mapped). Print the letter pairs. The daily catch-up group begins today and replaces independent practice.
- **30 min. Rotation.** The ten-minute catch-up group first (the six children the record names; map, blend, read, spell, reread). Then four groups of six, 5 minutes each at the teacher table (matched reader and spelling; today the cycle 11 reader), heard one child at a time, 50 seconds each; the heard-six column names them. The rest reread, chain and spell in pairs at stations.
- **5 min. Organize and record.** Heard-six column, cycle record and the day's exit marks.

Who holds what is carried: each child holds a card set; the teacher holds the chart and the records. Tool: None.. By hand: the card sets and the match cards. Evidence: E79; the retrieval notes; the heard-six note.

## Day 80


- **5 min. Opening repair.** The commonest miss on E79, one component, with a fresh case (for example the topic of a different picture card). At most five minutes.
- **8 min. Reread page 2 of Beaks Find Food.** The sparrow paragraph and its diagram; the teacher reads, children point to the diagram label "beak". New words: label, diagram.
- **12 min. Picture facts and word facts.** Review of picture-only fact and words-only fact (unit 1) on a two-column sheet. The sparrow is brown (picture only); it cracks hard seeds (words). Contrast: wrong "brown is in the article" against right. Pairs sort four fresh pointers on the four-head picture of page 1 into picture-only and words-only.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: bird, beak, topic, detail, heading, contents, label, glossary. Frames: "The topic is ___." "A detail is ___." "I find it in the ___." Home-language talk to preview part names with pictures.
- **Access.** Enlarged print, tactile outlines of the four feature cards, content dictation, partner pointing for a child who cannot see small print; a window or picture route for a child with a bird fear or allergy.
- **Check accommodations.** Stems read twice; point or say the answer; a scribe writes the label; no time pressure.
- **Reading load.** All articles and cards are read aloud. Children read word labels and the cycle sentences written to taught patterns.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find the topic of a second paragraph on page 2 and say two details for it.
