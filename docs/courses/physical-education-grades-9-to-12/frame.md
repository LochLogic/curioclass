# Course frame: Physical Education, grades 9 to 12

Course `physical-education-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `pe-9-12`), grades 9, 10, 11 and 12, one band course in four
school years: grades 9 and 10 are the two required years, grades 11 and 12 the two elective years that deepen them
(Cory, 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21; STANDARD.md 3 rule 5). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the shape of the K to 2
pilot and its fix pass (docs/reviews/frame-physical-education-grades-k-to-2-fixes-2026-11-25.md); the scope and
sequence sets the days later, one year at a time. Each year's part of this file (its units and its two course-level
checks) is that year's frame, as the subject page's "one frame per grade year" asks. The Year column holds the grade
year (9, 10, 11, 12); grades 9 and 10 stand for the review tool's level 1 and grades 11 and 12 for its level 2
(STANDARD.md 3).

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 24: 6 in grade 9 (units 1 to 6), 6 in grade 10 (units 7 to 12), 6 in grade 11 (units 13 to 18), 6 in
  grade 12 (units 19 to 24). Each year is whole on its own: its own units, end-of-unit assessments and mid-year and
  year-end checks.
- **Days.** 320: 80 a year for four years. Each year: 74 days in units and 6 for course-level checks and respond days
  (two check days and one respond day at mid-year, the same at year-end); grade 9 74 + 6, grade 10 74 + 6, grade 11
  74 + 6, grade 12 74 + 6. One lesson of about 45 minutes, two or three lessons a week (2.5 on average) over the 32
  weeks of the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md): the PE dose APPROACH.md "Pacing" sets for 9 to 12 (80
  sessions of 45 minutes for each year taken, Cory 2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-pe-1). This frame keeps that default. A
  school that runs PE as a daily semester (five a week for 16 weeks) teaches the same 80 days in the same order. The
  45 minutes hold the instant activity, warm-up, focus, cool-down and closure, and the set-up, clean-up and
  collecting of equipment (about 4 minutes, done by students in their station groups); changing clothes follows the
  school's own policy and sits outside the 45 minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment. No unit is
  over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. The course says openly that 2 to 3 lessons a week (about 112
  minutes) is below the 225 minutes a week SHAPE America's guidelines set for middle and high school
  (shapeamerica.org/standards/guidelines); the course page, teacher pages and family notes offer short active breaks
  and home activity, and the grade 10 to 12 plans and programs are built to run outside class too. On the even spread
  the calendar uses, course day d of a year falls on about school day 2d of that year.
- **Standard.** SHAPE America National Physical Education Standards, 2024 (data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt): all 43
  span-12 learning indicators (SHAPE.S1.12.1 to S1.12.9, S2.12.1 to S2.12.17, S3.12.1 to S3.12.8, S4.12.1 to S4.12.9)
  are owned in grades 9 and 10, the required years, each by the one progression row that owns it
  (data/progressions/physical-education.csv, grades 9 to 12, 48 rows: 14 in grade 9, 14 in grade 10, 10 in grade 11,
  10 in grade 12). Grades 11 and 12 own none: their rows carry the indicators in new settings (`~` codes), named in
  What students learn with their owner unit. Grade 9's g9-choose-active is begun toward the S4 indicators owned in
  grade 10, unit 12. Ids and our own words only; no indicator sentence is quoted (STANDARD.md 2). Check set: CDC's
  PECAT 2019 9 to 12 tables (two levels; every outcome of the 2013 five standards expected at both levels), mapped
  through docs/standards-notes/pe-2014-to-2024.md and read for placement only. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12)
  and the appropriate practice points (STANDARD.md 4) hold in every unit.
- **Required and elective years.** A student who takes only grades 9 and 10 meets every indicator taught and checked
  in full; that is why grade 10 holds the whole personal plan in short form (unit 12). Grades 11 and 12 lean only on
  grades 9 and 10 (rule 32): grade 12 follows grade 11 by course order, with a fallback for a student who skips grade
  11 (Readiness and retrieval, below). A school may offer either elective year alone.
- **Grade outcomes.** Bar criterion 1 asks for an outcome for every standard in every grade (STANDARD.md 3 rule 2).
  In our words; the scope and sequence copies them and adds the indicators each year works on.
  - Grade 9. S1: uses correct form in endurance and strength training and does the key skills of two lifetime sports,
    of casual and backyard games, and of dance sequences, set and self-designed (S1.12.1, S1.12.2, S1.12.4, S1.12.5,
    S1.12.6; units 1 to 5). S2: rates effort and uses heart rate to train safely, uses force and motion to improve a
    skill, explains and uses tactics in lifetime sports and backyard games, joins dance forms, applies the exercise
    principles and plans practice toward a skill goal (S2.12.1, S2.12.2, S2.12.4, S2.12.6, S2.12.7, S2.12.9,
    S2.12.10, S2.12.13; units 1, 3 to 6). S3: shows awareness of others, courtesy and teamwork, encourages others and
    gives feedback unasked (S3.12.1 to S3.12.4; units 1, 2). S4: samples many kinds of activity, logs what each
    gave and, for two of them, says what each gave and why, begun toward S4.12.1 to S4.12.5 (unit 6, marked on the
    reason, never on what was chosen). Checked by skill tasks against cue sheets, short written or spoken
    knowledge tasks and conduct marks.
  - Grade 10. S1: does the key moves of outdoor activities, stretches and trains skill-related fitness with correct
    technique, and shows dry-land water safety (S1.12.3, S1.12.7, S1.12.8, S1.12.9; units 7, 10, 11). S2: plans
    outdoor tactics, makes and follows a mixed training plan with rest, judges a technology, names foods for
    performance and recovery, discusses quality of life and early adulthood, and how games and sport shaped societies,
    and shows water safety knowledge (S2.12.3, S2.12.5, S2.12.8, S2.12.11, S2.12.12, S2.12.14 to S2.12.17; units 8 to
    12). S3: examines how one activity is valued in two cultures, reasons through challenges, judges leadership and
    follows safe habits (S3.12.5 to S3.12.8; units 9, 10). S4: the personal plan in short form, every part of S4.12.1
    to S4.12.9 (unit 12, with a first-term activity choice made in unit 8). Checked the same way, with the plan and log products scored after feedback and revision.
  - Grade 11. S1: applies the skills of a chosen activity and correct training form in a program, used in new
    settings (S1.12.1 to S1.12.8; units 13, 14). S2: uses tactics in a chosen activity, trains by effort and heart
    rate with rest, and runs a term's program (S2.12.1 to S2.12.4, S2.12.6 to S2.12.10, S2.12.12, S2.12.13; units 13,
    14, 16). S3: sets its own safety checklist and leads a small group with feedback (S3.12.2 to S3.12.4, S3.12.6,
    S3.12.8; units 13, 15). S4: joins activities for expression, company, health and interests at a fitting challenge
    (S4.12.1 to S4.12.5; units 15, 17, 18). Checked the same way; all codes used in new settings.
  - Grade 12. S1: coaches a partner through a self-written cue sheet and a self-designed session (S1.12.1 to S1.12.8;
    unit 19). S2: designs and reviews a full program and plans an active adult week (S2.12.5 to S2.12.8, S2.12.10 to
    S2.12.12, S2.12.14 to S2.12.16; units 20, 22, 23). S3: organizes an activity event and evaluates its leadership,
    and examines an activity's value in a community (S3.12.1 to S3.12.5, S3.12.7, S3.12.8; unit 22). S4: builds goals
    with milestones, plans around barriers after school, uses ways to manage emotions and reflects on PE across the
    years (S4.12.6 to S4.12.9; units 20, 21, 23, 24). Checked the same way; all codes used in new settings.
- **Base text.** None. Physical education is Curio Class's own. Cue sheets, task cards, plan and log templates and
  knowledge cards are ours; the appropriate practice guidelines (2009) and PECAT are read into our words, never
  quoted. Each cultural game or dance is named only after its own source (the community's own organization or a
  public folklife archive) says it is open to be taught (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day": Understanding (a skill or
  concept shown and named in two or three cues, then tried at once, first try watched by the teacher); Fluency (many
  tries with feedback on one cue: form stations, partner drills, task cards, no lines); Application (the skill in a
  changing setting: a small-sided game, a dance, a training session, a route, a plan used and logged; drafting a plan
  or a reflection is Application); Check; Respond. In grades 9 and 10 Fluency is about a third of each unit's teaching
  days (form drills before any loaded or timed work); Application is the largest share. In grades 11 and 12 Fluency
  is small (form checks inside the student's own sessions) and most days are Application.
- **The Cues and settings column** holds, for PE, the unit's cues and the settings it moves through (alone, partner, station,
  small-sided game, session, route or event), as science's holds its practices.
- **Assessment.** Each unit's first skill day has a baseline watch of the unit's main cue in rotating groups (met,
  partly, not yet); its end-of-unit assessment repeats that cue as the skill task, adds a knowledge task (a short
  written task of unseen items, an exit-ticket length answer, or a scored product) and marks conduct once, against the
  S3 behavior the unit's Ends names (recorded during practice). Growth is the change against the student's own
  baseline, never a rank. Units of 10 days or fewer end with one check day; longer units with two, so the teacher sees
  every student (six groups of five, about five minutes a group while the others work at stations or write the
  knowledge task). A product unit (a plan, a log, a case, a reflection) counts its draft day, written feedback and
  revision before the score inside its own days and says so in its Ends; plans, logs and fitness records are scored on
  what the student planned, recorded and changed, never on fitness results. No score comes from a day a skill is first
  taught. A respond day follows each unit check; the next unit's instant activity also takes the "not yet" cue.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Each assumed row's grade and course of last use is named in Seams and in the unit that
  needs it. A row used the year before (grade 8 rows in grade 9; grade 9 in 10; grade 10 in 11; grade 11 in 12) opens
  as a retrieval task inside a day, in the instant activity of the first skill day that needs it. A row last used two
  or more grades back (grade 6 and 7 rows in grade 9; grade 7 and 8 rows in grade 10) gets a readiness check of ten
  minutes before the unit opens: the last ten minutes of the previous unit's respond day, or of the mid-year respond
  day between the two units; units 1 and 7 (first in their year) take the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook.
  Day 1 of every other unit stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from a student who took Physical
  Education, grades 6 to 8, and takes the years in order. A grade 11 elective taken in grade 12 meets its grade 10 rows
  two grades back: those retrieval tasks become readiness checks. A grade 12 student who did not take grade 11 gets a
  readiness check on the grade 10 row behind each grade 11 row the unit assumes (named in each grade 12 unit), and the
  unit's first Understanding day teaches the grade 11 step as a bridge (rule 32). The scope gate's count governs.
- **Space, weather and equipment.** Every unit runs in a gym, on a field or in a cleared room, with a low-equipment
  version (resistance bands or body weight for any weights, beach balls or balloons for trainer balls, tape lines and
  cones, paper maps) and a wet-weather version named in the unit plan (bar criterion 5). Class of thirty: stations in
  groups of four or five, one item a pair or better. Outdoor days carry a water break, shade and sun protection in warm
  weather, and the school's cold and heat rules; the unit plan names them (APPROACH.md, "Safe space"). Curio Class
  plans use body weight and resistance bands; no maximal lift is ever tested. A school that uses its weight room does
  so under its own trained staff and rules, with a trained spotter for any loaded lift (units 2, 14 and 19 point here).
- **Required access.** What the low-equipment path cannot replace: mats or another soft surface for floor strength
  work, stretching and landings (units 2, 7, 14, 19: mats from the school's stock, carpet or dry grass; on a hard floor
  with none, those tasks are done standing or wait for a dry outdoor day); real life jackets for unit 11's fastening
  task (borrowed or bought); a safe outdoor route on or next to school grounds for unit 10 (an indoor map course is the
  wet-weather and no-route version, and an off-site trip runs only where the school's trip policy allows it). Each
  unit's last column names what it needs, the safe substitute and the purchase if none is available.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30, counting reusable items once, if the school has
  no PE equipment at all: shown in each unit's last column. Counted from nothing: a high school building does not share the
  elementary closet. Across the band about $740 (grade 9 about $535, grade 10 about $185, grade 11 $0, grade 12 about
  $20), summed from the unit cells; unit 10's optional compasses add about $120. Almost all of it is reusable gear a PE
  closet usually holds, so a school with an ordinary PE closet spends little. The low-equipment path's listed buys and the Required access items are provisional,
  so the path is not promised at no cost (For Cory).
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared teacher device is optional: it shows cue pictures or a short skill video,
  runs an interval timer and plays music for dance and aerobic days (units 1, 5, 12, 17). One owned code needs
  technology: SHAPE.S2.12.15 (unit 8), where the student examines and uses a technology that supports an active life.
  No tool is required for it: a student with no device of their own, or who chooses not to use one, uses a non-screen
  technology (a stopwatch or wall clock with a paper interval chart, or a paper pedometer count kept by hand) for the
  same two weeks and judges what it showed against the same questions, so every student meets the code in full. A
  student's own device is used only with the student's choice, and no data leaves the student (APPROACH.md, "What a
  tool may and may not do").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs from printed cue sheets, task cards, plan and log templates and the teacher's record
  sheet; no tool is needed, no student account exists, and no tool judges movement, ranks students or collects fitness
  data. Recorded skills for analysis (units 3, 14, 19) are optional: a partner watching with the cue sheet is the paper
  path, and any recording stays on the school's device and is deleted after the lesson.
- **Reading load.** Cue sheets and task cards are picture-led with short lines at a grade 9 reading level; knowledge
  tasks are short. Each unit names its movement words (the rows' `words`), taught with a picture or demonstration and
  a sentence frame ("My knees track over my ___."). A student slow to say or write a cue may show it; the check marks
  movement and understanding apart, so a language gap is not marked as a skill gap.
- **Supports.** Every lesson, closure and check task has an adaptation for disabilities and a range of skill and
  fitness (equipment size and load, distance, speed, rules, roles). A student with a short-term injury or a medical
  condition follows the nurse's and family's guidance, with a moving role for the parts that are fine or a seated
  role (cue watcher, timer, coach with the cue sheet, keeper of their own log). Groups are made by the teacher, quickly
  and privately. No exercise as punishment, no elimination, no human targets, no public team picks, no activity split
  by gender. Every small-sided game offers a more and a less competitive court the student chooses (STANDARD.md 4,
  "Competition"); both teach and check the same cue. Extension is the same cue in a harder setting, never the next year's work.
- **Water safety.** Dry land only, for every school: unit 11 teaches fastening a life jacket, a reach rescue and a
  throw rescue to a target, cold-water and current risks and what to do in an emergency, and what a survival float
  and a stroke are, shown on the floor. In-water tasks belong to a school's own aquatics program with a qualified
  lifeguard on site and the school's swim policy; no student is made to enter water (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Swaps and leave-outs.** A school or family may swap a named cultural game or dance (units 5, 9, 22) for one whose
  source says it is open to be taught, or an outdoor activity that does not fit its place (unit 10) for another
  outdoor activity the place offers (STANDARD.md 5). A unit left out takes its owned codes off the course page's claim;
  no other unit claims them. A student 18 or over gets the family note and makes their own leave-out or consent choice
  (Cory, 2026-11-22).
- **Shorter path.** The 64-session path each year (APPROACH.md, "Pacing") drops 16 of the year's 80 days: practice days
  only (Fluency days and repeated small-game or session days that use a skill already taught): at most 4 in a unit of
  13 or more days, at most 1 in a unit of 8 or fewer, at most 3 otherwise, so the drops fall on the longer units and
  the short and product units keep their practice; never a unit's last Fluency day before its check, and never a check, respond, baseline, first-teaching, readiness,
  product draft, feedback or revision day; both course-level checks and every respond day stay. Days are dropped by
  practice load, not evenly. The scope and sequence names the 16 days a year in a per-unit table and confirms each unit keeps at least one
  Fluency or practice day and every owned outcome stays taught and checked.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** Grade 12 teaches to its last week, but its year-end check sits in week 28 (course days 68 and
  69, respond day 70), so a senior who leaves early has finished it. Unit 24 (the look back, weeks 29 to 32) follows.
  If seniors leave before week 31, unit 24 folds to its short form of four sessions: its first teaching day with the
  baseline watch; a draft day with written feedback; a revision day that ends in the scored reflection and the brief
  skill check; and its respond day. The other 6 days fold, and the reflection draws on the portfolio pieces kept
  through the year.
- **Logic model.** One page a year in the scope and sequence (inputs: 80 sessions of 45 minutes, the cue sheets, plan
  templates and the equipment list; activities: the units; outcomes: the grade outcomes per standard above); the
  course page says the course has not been tried with students and claims no effect on fitness or health (APPROACH.md,
  "Sources").
- **Sensitive ground and teacher notes.** Every PE teacher page carries health's disclosure steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"), and changing clothes follows the school's
  policy with no one changing in front of others (Cory, 2026-11-21). No weighing, no body measurements, no body weight,
  calorie or diet talk; fitness and heart-rate records are the student's own and private, never graded, posted or
  compared; a grade never rests on dress, attendance, effort alone or a fitness result. PE does not treat mental health
  conditions; the teacher page names the school counselor (units 12, 18, 21, 24).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside human reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14). Units with cultural content (5, 9, 22) name the communities
  whose games and dances they use and, before the read, the agent studies that community's own published sources on
  the activity and on whether it is open to be taught; each pick is a candidate until the agent's read confirms at the
  community's own source what it is, that it is open to be taught and that the adaptation used is fitting; only then
  does a unit plan name it. Each pick's spot-check status and source line, with its date, is in the unit's last
  column; "browser check" is kept only for a page that refused an automated read after another source was tried (For
  the lead 3). Picks are checked against every PE, dance, music and SEL frame of K to 12 so none repeats. Units with body, food, emotion or
  heart-rate content (1, 8, 10, 12, 18, 20, 21, 23, 24) and disability adaptations in every unit name their sources in
  the last column.
- **Safety read.** The safety agent reads, before they go live, every unit with resistance training, striking tools,
  plyometrics, mats or an outdoor activity (units 2, 3, 4, 7, 10, 14, 19, 20) and the water unit (11), against these
  free guides: the Nova Scotia Department of Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades Primary-12"
  (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf;
  opened 2026-11-25 by the K to 2 frame writer); SPHEReS ("School Physical Activity, Health and Education Resource for
  Safety", https://myspheres.ca/en/), the University of Alberta Injury Prevention Center's online guidelines for
  Alberta schools (home page opened 2026-11-25 by the K to 2 frame writer); and for water CDC's "Preventing Drowning"
  page (https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html, opened 2026-11-25, dated 2025-09-29). The safety agent
  reads the sections each unit needs (fitness and weight training, striking implements, gymnastics mats, outdoor
  pursuits such as orienteering, hiking and cycling, aquatics). No student sign-off form. Answer keys get the
  answer-key agent's read.

knowledge tasks with unseen items, and conduct watched again or carried unscored. A student is watched once in each
window. Across a year's two checks every standard (S1 to S4) is sampled at least once from units before the check.

check in week 32, the finals week; grade 12 holds it in week 28 (Grade 12 calendar). Mid-year checks fall in week 16
in every year. Clashes with other after-core courses wait on the lead's calendar pass across all after-core frames.
