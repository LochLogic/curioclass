# Course frame: Music, grades 9 to 12

Course `music-grades-9-to-12` (catalogue id `music-9-12`), a band course for high school run as levels, not grades: Level 1 (Proficient), Level 2 (Accomplished), Level 3 (Advanced, at honors depth) and an optional Level 4 (Advanced, deepened, a portfolio year the student leads), one level a school year (STANDARD.md 3.2). It is a composition and theory course on the national composition and theory strand (STANDARD.md 3.3). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one level at a time, and each level's part of this file is that level's frame.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 19: five in each of Levels 1 to 3 and four in Level 4. Levels 1 to 3 share one shape, so each thread
  comes back one level harder in the same place: an opening unit (the Level 1 bridge, or the Level 2 and 3 review
  that doubles as placement), then four units that each join two progression threads, one of creating and one of
  responding, performing or connecting: a models and ideas unit, a form and drafting unit, a meaning and revision
  unit, and a rehearsal and concert unit that ends the level with the class's showing. Level 4 is one portfolio
  year in four units with checkpoints (APPROACH "High school levels and music").
- **Days.** 640: 160 a level for four levels. Each level: 156 days in its units and 4 course-level days (a
  mid-year check and a year-end check, each followed by one respond day). One lesson of 45 minutes a day, five a
  week, 32 weeks a level (STANDARD.md 3.4: a high school level is 160 course days of 45 minutes, the 120 hours of
  the national level descriptor). The 45 minutes hold the opener (about 5 minutes), the teaching and making, and
  collecting work (about 3 minutes; 5 on a recording day, when takes are saved and devices put away); a readiness
  or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and, in units over 25
  days, its mid-unit assessment (one near the middle; two in units of 42 days or more). The bridge's 12 days are
  outside the course's standards (7.5 percent of the level, inside the bar's 10 percent limit); the review units'
  6 days are inside them. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the concert unit is longest because it owns eight
  ids, a rehearsal plan, a performance and a critique; the form unit is shortest in Level 1 because it owns three
  ids. One level is a complete course and is enough for Proficient; a school need not offer all four. These are
  course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places a level's day n on school day n. National
  time figure: Pending(docs/courses/arts/STANDARD.md#Music, high school): STANDARD.md 3.4 carries the music
  educators' association's K to 8 figure only; the high school part of the same 2020 opportunity-to-learn
  standards is read at source before this course page states a figure and our gap (225 minutes a week on the full
  path, about 112 when the short path is spread over a full year).
- **The short path.** Each level's scope and sequence marks an 80-day path (a semester or a rotation) that keeps
  every unit and every id by merging the marked days (STANDARD.md 3.2). Days on that path: Level 1, units of 6
  (the bridge), 17, 15, 17 and 21; Levels 2 and 3, units of 3 (the review), 18, 17, 18 and 20; Level 4, units of
  28, 10, 16 and 22; every level keeps its 2 check days and 2 respond days, the mid-year check on days 39 and 40
  (week 8) and the year-end check on days 79 and 80 (week 16, the half-year finals week). Each unit keeps, in this
  order and no fewer: the listening or score study that carries the unit's response to another composer's work
  and its connecting sentence; the technique taught with its step card; one drafting day with the critique; the
  revision; the showing or sitting (the check); and one respond day. Fluency days fold into the openers. A merged
  day never drops a critique, a revision or a respond day. The short path is 60 hours a level, half the
  descriptor's 120, and the course page says so.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, music, the composition and theory strand only, by id
  (`MU:Cr1.1.C.Ia` and so on), each named with its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt,
  never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). Level 1 claims the 21 `.Ia` to `.Ic` ids, Level 2 the 21 `.II` ids, Level 3 the
  21 `.III` ids: 63 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its level. Level 4
  claims no new id: it applies the Level 3 ids again to new, self-directed work, and Advanced is claimed by assessed
  work, not by finishing Level 3 (a student who ends Level 3 at "partly" has Level 4 to reach it). The ensemble,
  harmonizing instrument and music technology strands are not claimed (STANDARD.md 3.3). Every level touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each of its four
  thread units touches at least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works".
  Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the arts
  part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set
  adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file; each level's scope and sequence still states its id
  count (21) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Entry and placement.** A student new to music starts at Level 1, whatever their grade; the scope and sequence
  names the level, never a grade. Each level requires the one before, or a placement showing scored against the
  previous level's end-of-level work (the review unit of Levels 2 and 3 is that showing for a new student). Mixed
  grades in one level are expected.
- **No instrument needed.** Work is shared by notation, performance or technology, and performances may be live
  or recorded (STANDARD.md 3.3). Days use the voice (singing, solfege, sight-singing), body and found percussion,
  a free keyboard on any device or a Curio Class tool, and the student's own instrument whenever they have one, as
  one way in. A Curio Class tool may play back a score; it never judges a composition or a performance.
- **Ensemble companion page.** A page inside the course, with no days and no ids: how to find a school or
  community ensemble, a practice log and self-check, how a composition task can be written for the student's own
  instrument or section, and a plain note that ensemble standards are met and assessed by that ensemble.
- **Base text.** None. Music 9 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain scores and recordings where the holder's page says so, or, for living and recent composers, a
  description in our own words with a link to the composer's, publisher's or holding archive's page, never a copy
  (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the
  item's own page, fails; a swapped work keeps the unit's ids met and the level's range. Each level's works
  include living composers and more than one region and period (Africa, the Americas with Native American
  composers in the band, Asia, Europe); the list is checked for range across the band, since open recordings lean
  to older European and American music. Anything students perform is public domain, openly licensed, a Curio
  Class original or their own. Curio Class writes every step card, score card (a short reduction of a public
  domain passage), listening map, draft log, rehearsal plan form, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Notation and reading: treble and bass staves, simple meter to
  sixteenths, major keys to three sharps and flats, stepwise sight-singing in solfege (Level 1); minor keys, all
  key signatures, compound meter, interval quality, leaps in sight-singing (Level 2); clefs beyond treble and
  bass, mixed and changing meter, modes and scales from outside the major-minor system, graphic notation
  (Level 3). Harmony: major and minor triads and I, IV, V and I (Level 1); diatonic chords by Roman numeral and the
  four cadences (Level 2); seventh chords, secondary dominants and modulation (Level 3). Writing: motif,
  repetition and variation, one-part, cyclical and binary forms (Level 1); two-voice writing, text setting,
  ternary and rondo (Level 2); counterpoint, theme and variations, sonata form and through-composed form, writing
  for the sounds a class has (Level 3). Level 4 applies them all to the student's own portfolio.
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique
  shown in named steps with the step card, or a work studied by ear and from the score with the unit's words (the
  artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills with
  feedback, mostly the five-minute opener and two to four fluency days a unit (interval and chord naming,
  rhythm and melodic dictation, sight-singing a line, writing a cadence). Application: composing, drafting,
  rehearsing and revising with choice; the second drafting day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask,
  suggest), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: a sitting with unseen items, or the showing
  scored from the take and the kept score. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet". A unit runs
  roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id
  in the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row,
  never the whole line. Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the product's guide (three to five look-fors, met,
  partly or not yet), scored from the kept score, draft log, plan or take after the session; a written note made on
  the unit's last making days, scored with the product; and sitting items on an unseen public domain or openly
  licensed work or a Curio Class original, played twice from the teacher's device or performed from the score
  card. A check day's 45 minutes: set-up 3, the student's self-check of the product on the guide 5, the sitting 35
  (two unseen excerpts, each heard twice), collecting 2; one adult proctors one room. A showing day (the concert
  units) holds only the showing: groups perform live or play their takes, and the teacher scores from the live
  showing and the recordings before the respond day. The product's draft, its critique with the teacher's
  look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes
  from the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the
  first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit. One piece a unit goes into the
  portfolio (a folder of scores, logs and takes) with a one-line reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Level 1 is open to any grade and to students with no K to 8 music (STANDARD.md
  3.2). The count starts from grade 9 after Music, grades 6 to 8 in grade 8, which is one grade back and would be a
  retrieval task; because a Level 1 student may be in grade 10, 11 or 12 or may never have taken music 6 to 8, each
  Level 1 unit gives a readiness check on its grade 8 row instead, with the ten-minute reteach, and the bridge
  (unit 1) teaches the grade 8 techniques and words first. Unit 1's readiness look takes the last ten minutes of
  day 1, after the hook; units 2 to 5 take theirs in the last ten minutes of the previous unit's last day or of the
  mid-year respond day. Levels 2, 3 and 4 assume the level before's rows, used the year before, so each unit opens
  with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener; day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. A student placed
  into Level 2 or 3 by a placement showing meets the same retrieval tasks, each carrying its ten-minute reteach.
  The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 9 to 12 have no stagger rule but the finals weeks (CALENDAR.md rule 4). Every
  level: mid-year check on days 79 and 80 (week 16), year-end check on days 159 and 160 (week 32, the finals week).
  The concert unit's showing (weeks 30 and 31) is its own end-of-unit assessment, and the year-end check samples
  the units before it.
- **Grade 12.** A level taken in grade 12 teaches to its last week. A senior whose school ends before week 32
  takes the year-end check and its respond day on their last two days, and the days that fold are the concert
  unit's last fluency days (up to five), never its showing, critique, revision or respond day. A student of 18 or
  over gives their own yes to be recorded or shown outside class and gets the family note themselves; the school's
  own rules still apply (AGENTS.md).
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5);
  each unit stands alone. Units the course page names: unit 3 (it studies Javanese gamelan, a tradition with
  court and ceremonial uses, by listening only) and unit 12 (it studies a concert work written about a sacred
  space and its history). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and
  the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that level.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at desks, staff paper, pencils, the voice, body and found percussion;
  nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student
  at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, a group piece has a solo or doubled version (the
  student sings one line while a device or the parent plays or claps another), and the showing is to the family or
  a recorded take. The teacher needs no music degree: each unit's teacher page says what to listen for, in plain
  words, and gives worked answers to every sitting item.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per level for a class of 30, printing only (step cards, score cards,
  listening maps, staff paper, logs and plan forms), every item optional beside writing on plain paper: Level 1
  $35 (units of $10, $5, $5, $5 and $10), Level 2 $30 ($5, $5, $10, $5, $5), Level 3 $30 ($5, $5, $10, $5, $5),
  Level 4 $20 ($5 a unit). Planning figures, not quotes.
- **Devices.** One teacher device with speakers plays the works studied; with none, the teacher or the class
  performs the excerpt from the printed score card, and a work with no public domain score is met by its
  description and the score card's reduction. Students need no device: a shared device in pairs, where a school
  has one, may run a free keyboard or a Curio Class score tool for playback and for saving takes. No owned id needs
  technology: `MU:Cr3.2.C.Ia` and its later levels let a piece be shared by score or performance, and the
  `MU:Pr6.1` ids accept a live performance, so a paper-path student meets every id in full. Kept work stays on
  paper or on the student's, family's or school's own device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on staff paper, the voice, body percussion and printed cards; any Curio Class
  tool a unit plan adds (an interval and chord drill, a score playback tool) has a printed version that does the
  same job, and none needs a student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards and listening maps are one page; a day's reading alone is at most one page of our
  own text at the grade 9 level. English learners: each unit teaches its five or six words in depth, with a
  sound example and a frame ("The melody ___ because ___."), a student may sing, clap or play the word instead of
  saying it, and the check marks what the student composed or performed apart from what they wrote (APPROACH
  "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit uses voice and sound, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: "Protect
  Your Hearing Every Day: Information and Recommendations for Student Musicians" and "Protect Your Vocal Health",
  both from the National Association of Schools of Music with the Performing Arts Medicine Association
  (nasm.arts-accredit.org). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks
  them): headphones at a set low volume and never shared without a wipe; no loud sound at a student's ear; found
  percussion with no sharp edges and no glass; a voice warm-up before singing and voice rest after a long
  rehearsal; no forced singing alone. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv
  will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 3 (Javanese
  gamelan), unit 4 (the Chickasaw Nation, Jerod Impichchaachaaha' Tate's work), unit 7 (Inuit katajjaq; the
  Afro-Cuban chant behind Revueltas's Sensemayá), unit 9 (Egyptian song, Umm Kulthum), unit 12 (Raven Chacon, Diné,
  and the history his work addresses), unit 13 (Hindustani raga), unit 14 (Dvořák's claims about African American
  and Native American music; Ellington's portrait of Black American history). Every task that ties music to a
  student's own life (the `MU:Cn10.0` ids in units 2, 7, 12 and 18) offers an invented or public option marked the
  same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: a 45-minute lesson five days a week, step cards,
  score cards, listening maps, the voice and found sounds. Activities: theory taught in steps, works studied by ear
  and from the score with their setting, composing with a critique and a revision, a showing each level.
  Outputs: a portfolio of scores, draft logs and takes, one piece a unit, and a concert each level, with two marks a
  unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the level's 21 ids and growth on the
  look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that music study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").

### Where the threads end

Music 9 to 12 is the last music course. Level 3 ends the claimed ids; Level 4's four rows (mu-g12-studio,
mu-g12-perform, mu-g12-criticism, mu-g12-connect) hand on nothing (their `hands_on` cells are empty): the
threads end in the student's portfolio, program, reviews and capstone, which the student keeps. A student who
stops after any level has a complete course at that level. The ensemble companion page points to music after
school (community ensembles, further study).
