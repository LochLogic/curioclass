# Course frame: Social and Emotional Learning, Grades K to 2

Course `social-and-emotional-learning-grades-k-to-2` (catalogue id `sel-k-2`), kindergarten, grade 1 and grade 2, one band course taught over three school years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25, the pilot frame for social and emotional learning; the scope and sequence sets the ordered days and school-day positions later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 18, six a year (STANDARD section 3): units 1 to 6 in kindergarten, 7 to 12 in grade 1, 13 to 18 in grade 2, numbered through the band so a seam or a check can name one unit without a year beside it. The Year column of the Units table names the year. Each year teaches all 40 sub-skills of the five competencies (self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making), the kindergarten form first, grade 1 a step up, grade 2 the owner year. Each year's order follows the progression rows' unit notes (data/progressions/sel.csv): talk and helpers first, feelings and calm-down steps second, choosing and helping last.
- **Days.** 192 sessions: 64 a year, two sessions a week over 32 weeks, 20 minutes a session in K to 2 (STANDARD section 2). Each year is 60 sessions in six units and 4 for two course-level checks, each check with its respond session. Unit sizes, pilot choice (For the lead 1): Desk q-fix-sel-1 sets four units of 11 and two of 10, which fills the year and leaves no session for the course-level checks the frame brief requires. This frame keeps the three-group unit of 11 (3 groups of Understanding, Fluency, Application, then the unit's Check and Respond) and makes the two-group unit 8 (2 groups of Understanding, Fluency, Application, then Check and Respond), freeing four sessions a year for the checks. Grades 1 and 2: four three-group units of 11 and two two-group units of 8. Kindergarten follows the expert read (data/progressions/sel-edges.md, "For the kindergarten frame"): units 2, 5 and 6 take three groups and a twelfth session each (a second Fluency), and units 1, 3 and 4 take two groups and 8 sessions. Course-level checks fall after the second and fifth unit of each year (sessions 21 and 22, 51 and 52 in kindergarten; 17 and 18, 52 and 53 in grade 1; 20 and 21, 52 and 53 in grade 2), in weeks with no core course-level check (CALENDAR.md, weeks 14 to 20 and 28 to 32 avoided; For the lead 2). Short path, one session a week (32 a year, STANDARD C2): every unit and group kept, fewer minutes a unit, never fewer units. A three-group unit is 5 (3 sessions where each group's Understanding and Fluency share one, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond together); a two-group unit is 5 (2 shared sessions, 2 Application, 1 Check and Respond); each course-level check shares one session with its respond, 2. Kindergarten's short path is 4, 6, 4, 4, 6, 6 and 2. Readiness: kindergarten unit 1 opens with a start-of-year check watched inside the first game; every later unit's rows assume the year before's rows, which the course taught, so each unit opens with five minutes of retrieval, not a readiness check. A "Given:" readiness item is used only where a school left out the earlier unit (STANDARD section 5.6).
- **Standard.** CASEL 5 competencies, as written in data/standards/casel-5.txt (40 sub-skills, the five competency names and the codes CASEL.Competency.N). Grade 2 owns every code for the band; kindergarten and grade 1 rows begin each one (~ in the rows), so their Owned standards cells are empty and What students learn names each begun code with its owner year. The C3 column holds, for SEL, the unit's one link to a core course of the same year (bar part 9 item 9): a lean the teacher may use, not a strand. Library: AASL V.D.1 is taught with SM3, V.D.2 with SA8 and III.D.2 with RS4 in every SEL band (library STANDARD section 4); the rows do not carry these codes yet (For the lead 4). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against APPROACH's kinds of day and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/sel.md) and the bar's part 9; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add.
- **Base text.** None. Every story, scene card, step card and pictured page is Curio Class's own, with invented children and families. The two games from other places (Ampe in unit 5, jan-ken in unit 11) are written from a published cultural or education source with a credit line.
- **Kinds of day.** APPROACH "Kinds of day": Understanding (the skill shown on an invented case, then the steps named on a pictured step card), Fluency (the steps rehearsed in pairs or small groups until quick), Application (the steps used on a new scene card, or in a real class task), Check (a pictured page read aloud and a watched rehearsal from an unseen card, against look-fors), Respond (a short reteach for the step not yet seen, and the rehearsal again). Every session rehearses; no session is posters and discussion alone. Each year is taught a new way, not the same lessons again: kindergarten acts with puppets and pictures, grade 1 adds a partner and a second clue, grade 2 adds a cost, a second push or a second point of view.
- **Evidence.** A class sheet per unit, the step marked "seen" or "not yet" on the day it was seen. It records a step, not a rating of the child; no behavior rating of any child is collected (STANDARD C). Feelings in a check are a character's, never the child's own. Private pages (SA7, SA9) stay with the child, checked only that the steps were followed.
- **Carry it out and families.** Each unit names one cue the teacher uses outside the session ("Carry it out" in What students learn) and one family activity, sent in the unit's family note. Family notes are the only translated pages, in English and Spanish (STANDARD section 2); student pages are English only for now. A school may leave out a unit; its family note and the course page then name the left-out sub-skill ids the course does not claim that year (STANDARD section 5.6).
- **Fidelity and before you teach.** A paper fidelity checklist per session (did it run as planned, which part was cut) and a "before you teach" note opening each unit's teacher page: the skill as an adult uses it and one thing to model. Not training, and not claimed as such (bar part 9 items 11 and 13).
- **Equity.** Families of every kind appear in model pages, LGBTQ families included; no child is asked about a family or culture by name, or asked to teach or explain their own culture. SA6 (snap judgments) is checking one's own guess on invented cases, the wrong guess resting on a neutral trait, never blame of a group. Student voice in class life (a class choice in units 2 and 8, a class change in unit 14, a class helping task in units 12 and 18), with the analysis of causes left to social studies (bar part 9 item 12).
- **Devices and paper path.** No device for students. Every session runs on the paper path: step cards, scene cards, pictured pages, puppets and class charts, printed or drawn by hand. Any online tool a later unit plan adds has a paper path beside it and needs no student account.
- **Tools and costs.** Kept all band: the talk turn card, feelings faces chart, calm-down step card, stop-think-choose card, goal steps card (kindergarten), problem size meter and I-statement frame (grade 1). Each unit names its tools, what Curio Class writes and a provisional cost for 30 children; printing runs about $8 to $10 a unit, about $55 to $60 a year, plus about $15 once for two hand puppets (paper-bag puppets cost nothing), $6 once for six sand timers and small task supplies (tape, gloves, project supplies the class chooses). No supply ceiling is set for SEL yet (For the lead 7); these are planning figures, not quotes.
- **Safety.** Movement games (units 1, 5) on a cleared floor with walking feet; child-safe scissors (unit 7); a school walk inside the grounds with an adult at front and back (unit 10); helping tasks with gloves on checked ground, no glass, sharp or medical items, no cleaning chemicals (units 12, 18). Each such unit names its hazards and controls.
- **Disclosure.** Asking for help, calm-down, teasing and refusal units can bring a disclosure. Every unit follows health's steps (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"): the school's reporting route and the counselor. Health owns body safety, substances and mental health topics; SEL never teaches them.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Before units 1 and 11 (families) the agent studies an early childhood association's published guidance on families in the classroom; before units 5 and 11 it studies a published Ghanaian cultural or education source on Ampe and a Japanese one on jan-ken. Units 1, 5, 7, 10, 12 and 18 also get the safety agent's read. The Spanish family notes get a translation agent's read (For the lead 6). The course-level checks get the answer-key agent's read.

## Seams

### Picked up from: no course before

Kindergarten is the first SEL year; its rows assume nothing (STANDARD section 3). The start-of-year check in unit 1 watches what a child arrives with inside a game, and no unit assumes a skill taught outside this course.
