# Mathematics, grades 3 to 5: the progression around grade 4

**What this is.** Layer 4 of the Curio Class foundation: how the grade 4 mathematics course joins grade 3 behind it and grade 5 ahead of it. Unit plans take field 2 ("Builds on, leads to") and field 4 (the readiness check) from this page.

**Checked against.** The standards text in `data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt`, the official standards PDF for the footnotes that file leaves out, and the 2023 *Progressions for the Common Core State Standards for Mathematics*. See Sources for how much of each was read.

**Date.** 2026-11-11. **Status: draft, not yet read by the curriculum expert.**

**How to read the page pointers.** "Prog. p. 149 (143)" means page 149 of the Progressions PDF, printed page 143. PDF page is always printed page plus 6. "Std. p. 24" is a page of the official standards PDF. Words in quotation marks after a code are the standard's own words.

## 1. The strands across grades 3, 4, 5

### Multiplication and division

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Products and quotients as equal groups and arrays. "Fluently multiply and divide within 100"; "know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers". Properties used, not named (Std. p. 23, footnote 2). Two-step problems with a letter for the unknown. One-digit times multiples of 10 (10 to 90). | 3.OA.A.1 to A.4, B.5, B.6, C.7, D.8; 3.NBT.A.3 |
| Grade 4 does | "Interpret a multiplication equation as a comparison". Multiplies up to four digits by one digit and two digits by two digits, "using strategies based on place value and the properties of operations". Finds "whole-number quotients and remainders" with one-digit divisors. Multistep problems where "remainders must be interpreted". | 4.OA.A.1 to A.3; 4.NBT.B.5, B.6 |
| Grade 5 does next | "Fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm". Two-digit divisors. Multiplication as scaling. Parentheses and simple expressions. | 5.NBT.B.5, B.6; 5.NF.B.5; 5.OA.A.1, A.2 |

Progressions: Operations and Algebraic Thinking, grades 3 to 5, pp. 37 to 48 (31 to 42); Number and Operations in Base Ten, pp. 70 to 74 (64 to 68).

### Place value and the algorithms

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Rounds "to the nearest 10 or 100". "Fluently add and subtract within 1000"; "a range of algorithms may be used" (Std. p. 24, footnote 4). Grade 3 has no standard for reading, writing or comparing numbers; that is grade 2, to 1,000 (2.NBT.A.1 to A.4). | 3.NBT.A.1, A.2 |
| Grade 4 does | A digit "represents ten times what it represents in the place to its right". Reads, writes, compares, rounds "to any place". "Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm". All limited to numbers up to 1,000,000 (Std. p. 29, footnote 2). | 4.NBT.A.1 to A.3, B.4 |
| Grade 5 does next | Adds the other direction: 1/10 of the place to its left. Powers of 10 with exponents. Decimals to thousandths. | 5.NBT.A.1 to A.4 |

Progressions: Number and Operations in Base Ten, pp. 68 to 77 (62 to 71).

### Fractions

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | A fraction as a number built from unit fractions, placed on a number line. Equivalence "in special cases". Whole numbers written as fractions. Compares only "two fractions with the same numerator or the same denominator". Denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 only (Std. p. 24, footnote 5). | 3.NF.A.1 to A.3 |
| Grade 4 does | Explains why a/b equals (n × a)/(n × b). Compares any two fractions. Adds and subtracts with like denominators, mixed numbers included. Multiplies a fraction by a whole number. Denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 100 (Std. p. 30, footnote 3). | 4.NF.A.1, A.2, B.3, B.4 |
| Grade 5 does next | Adds and subtracts unlike denominators. Fraction as division. Fraction times fraction. Divides unit fractions and whole numbers by each other, not fraction by fraction (Std. p. 36, footnote 1). | 5.NF.A.1, A.2, B.3 to B.7 |

Progressions: Number and Operations, Fractions, pp. 139 to 161 (133 to 155).

### Decimals

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Nothing. No decimals, and no tenths or hundredths as fractions. | none |
| Grade 4 does | Writes tenths as hundredths and adds a tenths fraction to a hundredths fraction. "Use decimal notation for fractions with denominators 10 or 100". "Compare two decimals to hundredths by reasoning about their size". No decimal arithmetic. | 4.NF.C.5 to C.7 |
| Grade 5 does next | Reads, writes, compares to thousandths; rounds. "Add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals to hundredths". | 5.NBT.A.3, A.4, B.7 |

Progressions: Fractions, pp. 154 to 155 (148 to 149); Base Ten, pp. 69 to 70 (63 to 64) and 75 to 76 (69 to 70).

### Measurement and conversion

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Time "to the nearest minute", intervals in minutes. Liquid volume and mass in "grams (g), kilograms (kg), and liters (l)", one-step problems in the same unit. No conversions. No "times as much" problems (Std. p. 25, footnotes 6 and 7). Length units (inch, foot, cm, m) and money come from grade 2 (2.MD.A.1 to A.4, 2.MD.C.8). | 3.MD.A.1, A.2 |
| Grade 4 does | "Know relative sizes of measurement units within one system". Expresses "a larger unit in terms of a smaller unit" only. Two-column tables. Word problems with distance, time, liquid volume, mass and money. | 4.MD.A.1, A.2 |
| Grade 5 does next | "Convert among different-sized standard measurement units", both directions, decimals allowed. | 5.MD.A.1 |

Progressions: Geometric Measurement, pp. 108 to 110 (102 to 104) and 116 (110).

### Area, perimeter and volume

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Area by tiling with unit squares, then by multiplying side lengths. Area of a rectangle with sides a and b + c shows the distributive property. Rectilinear figures split into rectangles. Perimeter of polygons, including an unknown side. | 3.MD.C.5 to C.7, D.8 |
| Grade 4 does | "Apply the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real world and mathematical problems", including finding an unknown side. | 4.MD.A.3 |
| Grade 5 does next | Volume by packing unit cubes, then V = l × w × h and V = b × h, whole-number edges. Area of rectangles with fractional sides. | 5.MD.C.3 to C.5; 5.NF.B.4b |

Progressions: Geometric Measurement, pp. 106 to 108 (100 to 102), 111 to 112 (105 to 106), 116 to 117 (110 to 111).

### Angles and geometry

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Shapes in different categories "may share attributes"; rhombuses, rectangles, squares as quadrilaterals. Partitions shapes into equal areas. No angle measure, no parallel or perpendicular as taught ideas. | 3.G.A.1, A.2 |
| Grade 4 does | Angle as a turn through n degrees. Measures and sketches with a protractor. Angle measure is "additive". Draws and identifies lines, rays, angles, parallel and perpendicular lines. Classifies figures by them. "Recognize right triangles as a category". Lines of symmetry. | 4.MD.C.5 to C.7; 4.G.A.1 to A.3 |
| Grade 5 does next | Coordinate plane, first quadrant. Sorts two-dimensional figures into a hierarchy. | 5.G.A.1, A.2, B.3, B.4 |

Progressions: Geometric Measurement, pp. 113 to 115 (107 to 109); Geometry, pp. 130 to 135 (124 to 129).

### Data

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | Scaled picture and bar graphs. Line plots of lengths measured to "halves and fourths of an inch". | 3.MD.B.3, B.4 |
| Grade 4 does | Line plot of measurements "in fractions of a unit (1/2, 1/4, 1/8)". Solves addition and subtraction of fractions from the plot. | 4.MD.B.4 |

### Patterns and factors

| | What happens | Codes |
|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 ends with | "Identify arithmetic patterns (including patterns in the addition table or multiplication table), and explain them using properties of operations". | 3.OA.D.9 |
| Grade 4 does | "Find all factor pairs for a whole number in the range 1-100"; prime or composite. "Generate a number or shape pattern that follows a given rule" and notice features the rule did not state. | 4.OA.B.4, C.5 |

## 2. Stretch by stretch

### Stretch 1. Factors and Multiples (days 1 to 6; 4.OA.B.4)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** all one-digit products from memory and division within 100 (3.OA.C.7); division as an unknown-factor problem (3.OA.B.6); arrays and rectangles with whole-number sides (3.OA.A.3, 3.MD.C.7). Limit: products within 100.
- **Readiness: what to check.** (1) Recalls one-digit products and the matching quotients (3.OA.C.7). (2) Can turn 24 ÷ 3 into "3 times what is 24" (3.OA.B.6). (3) Can build or draw every rectangle with area 12 (3.MD.C.7).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** multiples and factors to find common denominators (5.NF.A.1); multiples language for powers of 10 (5.NBT.A.2; Prog. p. 74 (68)).
- **Limits in grade 4:** numbers 1 to 100. No greatest common factor or least common multiple (6.NS.B.4). No prime factorization.
- **What the Progressions say is hard:** finding all pairs needs a system, searching until the pairs reverse. Students "need not be fluent" in finding all factor pairs (Prog. p. 45 (39)).

### Stretch 2. Number Patterns (days 7 to 9; 4.OA.C.5)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** arithmetic patterns in the addition and multiplication tables, explained with properties (3.OA.D.9); skip counting from grade 2 (2.NBT.A.2).
- **Readiness.** (1) Continues a count by a one-digit number (3.OA.C.7). (2) Explains a table pattern, for example why 4 times a number is always even (3.OA.D.9). (3) Tells odd from even (2.OA.C.3).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** generating terms from a rule, to make two patterns and compare them (5.OA.B.3).
- **Limits:** the rule is given. The Progressions say the standards "do not require students to infer or guess the underlying rule" (Prog. p. 46 (40)). No ordered pairs, no graphing.
- **Hard here:** telling the rule apart from a feature the rule causes (Prog. pp. 45 to 46 (39 to 40)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 1, for patterns in multiples.

### Stretch 3. Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24; 4.NF.A.1, A.2)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** a/b as a parts of size 1/b (3.NF.A.1); fractions on a number line (3.NF.A.2); simple equivalents such as 1/2 = 2/4 and 4/6 = 2/3, whole numbers as fractions, comparing with the same numerator or same denominator (3.NF.A.3a to d). Limit: denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8.
- **Readiness.** (1) Says what the 3 and the 4 mean in 3/4 (3.NF.A.1). (2) Places 3/4 and 5/4 on a number line marked 0, 1, 2 (3.NF.A.2). (3) Says which is larger, 1/3 or 1/8, and why (3.NF.A.3d). (4) Knows the comparison only holds for the same whole (3.NF.A.3d). (5) Writes 3 as 3/1 and 1 as 4/4 (3.NF.A.3c).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** making equivalent fractions on purpose, to add unlike denominators (5.NF.A.1); benchmark comparing, to judge whether answers are reasonable (5.NF.A.2).
- **Limits:** grade 4 denominators only. No simplest-form requirement and no least common denominator (Prog. pp. 149 to 150 (143 to 144)).
- **Hard here:** numerator and denominator both grow while the pieces get smaller (Prog. p. 149 (143)). On number lines, students count tick marks, not lengths (Prog. p. 141 (135)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 1 (multiples).

### Stretch 4. Adding and Subtracting Fractions (days 25 to 38; 4.NF.B.3a to d)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** fractions as sums of unit fractions in all but name (3.NF.A.1); number line (3.NF.A.2); whole numbers as fractions, which is what mixed numbers rest on (3.NF.A.3c). Grade 3 does not add fractions.
- **Readiness.** (1) Counts by unit fractions past 1: 1/4, 2/4, ... 5/4 (3.NF.A.1, A.2). (2) Writes 2 as 8/4 (3.NF.A.3c). (3) Finds an equivalent fraction (4.NF.A.1, stretch 3). (4) Adds and subtracts within 20 without effort (2.OA.B.2).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** like-denominator adding and mixed numbers, as the last step of every unlike-denominator sum (5.NF.A.1, A.2).
- **Limits:** like denominators only. The one exception is tenths plus hundredths, in stretch 7 (4.NF.C.5). Std. p. 31, footnote 4: unlike denominators in general are "not a requirement at this grade".
- **Hard here:** adding tops and adding bottoms; seeing a fraction as a sum of unit fractions prevents it (Prog. p. 150 (144), margin). Subtracting from a mixed number needs a one broken into parts (Prog. p. 152 (146)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 3.

### Stretch 5. Line Plots (days 39 to 41; 4.MD.B.4)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** measuring to halves and fourths of an inch and plotting on a line plot (3.MD.B.4).
- **Readiness.** (1) Reads a ruler to the nearest quarter inch (3.MD.B.4). (2) Reads a line plot: how many, most common (3.MD.B.4). (3) Adds and subtracts like-denominator fractions (4.NF.B.3, stretch 4). (4) Renames halves and fourths as eighths (4.NF.A.1, stretch 3).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** the same plots for all fraction operations (5.MD.B.2).
- **Limits:** scale in halves, fourths, eighths. Adding and subtracting only. Where a problem mixes units, label the scale in eighths so the denominators match (Prog. p. 89 (83)).
- **Hard here:** the scale is a number line, so the tick-mark error from stretch 3 returns.
- **Earlier in this year:** stretches 3 and 4.

### Stretch 6. Multiplying a Fraction by a Whole Number (days 44 to 51; 4.NF.B.4a to c)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** 5 × 3 as five groups of three (3.OA.A.1); unit fractions (3.NF.A.1).
- **Readiness.** (1) Reads 4 × 6 as 4 groups of 6 (3.OA.A.1). (2) Writes 3/5 as 1/5 + 1/5 + 1/5 (4.NF.B.3b, stretch 4). (3) Changes 7/4 into a mixed number (4.NF.B.3c, stretch 4).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** fraction times whole number as equal groups, extended to fraction times fraction (5.NF.B.4) and to scaling (5.NF.B.5).
- **Limits:** whole number times fraction only. Multiplying by a fraction ("1/3 of 12") is grade 5 (Prog. p. 44 (38)).
- **Hard here:** multiplying both numerator and denominator by the whole number, a mix-up with stretch 3 (stated from memory; not found as a sentence in the pages read).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 4.

### Stretch 7. Decimals (days 52 to 61; 4.NF.C.5 to C.7)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** very little directly. Tenths and hundredths are outside grade 3's denominators. Number line skill (3.NF.A.2) and money (2.MD.C.8) carry over.
- **Readiness.** (1) Makes an equivalent fraction by multiplying top and bottom by 10 (4.NF.A.1, stretch 3). (2) Adds like-denominator fractions (4.NF.B.3, stretch 4). (3) Compares fractions with the same denominator (3.NF.A.3d). (4) Knows 10 dimes and 100 cents make a dollar (2.MD.C.8).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** decimals as fractions with denominator 10 or 100, to extend to thousandths (5.NBT.A.3) and to explain decimal arithmetic (5.NBT.B.7).
- **Limits:** tenths and hundredths. Comparing, not computing, in decimal notation. Adding is done as fractions (4.NF.C.5).
- **Hard here:** 0.2 against 0.09, read as "2 against 9"; rewrite as 20/100 and 9/100 (Prog. p. 155 (149)). Students expect a "oneths" place, as if the point were the centre (Prog. pp. 69 to 70 (63 to 64)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretches 3 and 4.

### Stretch 8. Place Value to a Million (days 62 to 71; 4.NBT.A.1 to A.3)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** rounding to the nearest 10 or 100 (3.NBT.A.1); one-digit times multiples of 10 (3.NBT.A.3). Reading, writing and comparing three-digit numbers is grade 2 (2.NBT.A.1 to A.4).
- **Readiness.** (1) Says 706 is 7 hundreds, 0 tens, 6 ones (2.NBT.A.1). (2) Compares two three-digit numbers with >, =, < (2.NBT.A.4). (3) Rounds 368 to the nearest 10 and 100 (3.NBT.A.1). (4) Knows 10 tens make a hundred and 10 hundreds a thousand (2.NBT.A.1a).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** "ten times the place to its right", to add "1/10 of the place to its left" and reach decimals (5.NBT.A.1, A.2).
- **Limits:** whole numbers to 1,000,000.
- **Hard here:** reading large numbers by their comma groups (Prog. p. 69 (63)). The Progressions add that rounding beyond the leftmost one or two places "need not be practiced" (same page).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 7, for the tenfold pattern.

### Stretch 9. Adding and Subtracting Multi-Digit Numbers (days 72 to 77; 4.NBT.B.4)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** fluent adding and subtracting within 1,000 by any sound method (3.NBT.A.2).
- **Readiness.** (1) Adds and subtracts three-digit numbers with regrouping, on paper, without drawings (3.NBT.A.2). (2) Subtracts across a zero, as in 402 − 178 (3.NBT.A.2). (3) Reads and writes six-digit numbers (4.NBT.A.2, stretch 8).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** the same algorithm for decimals (5.NBT.B.7).
- **Limits:** to 1,000,000. This is the one place grade 4 requires the standard algorithm.
- **Hard here:** not much is new. "This extension does not have to take a long time" (Prog. p. 70 (64)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 8.

### Stretch 10. Multiplicative Comparison (days 80 to 88; 4.OA.A.1, A.2, part of A.3)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** equal groups, arrays and measurement problems (3.OA.A.1, A.3); two-step problems with a letter (3.OA.D.8). Grade 3 has no "times as many" problems (Prog. p. 37 (31); Std. p. 25, footnote 7).
- **Readiness.** (1) Solves an equal-groups problem with the unknown in any position (3.OA.A.3, A.4). (2) Solves an additive comparison, "how many more" (2.OA.A.1). (3) Writes an equation with a letter for a two-step problem (3.OA.D.8).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** "times as much", to read multiplication as scaling (5.NF.B.5) and to write expressions such as "3 × (18932 + 921)" (5.OA.A.2).
- **Limits:** whole numbers. "One third as much" is grade 5 (Prog. p. 38 (32)).
- **Hard here:** the language. "Times as many" against "more than", and the misleading "times more than". Keep to "times as much" and "times as many" (Prog. p. 44 (38)).

### Stretch 11. Area and Perimeter Formulas, pilot (days 89 to 93; 4.MD.A.3)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** area as rows times columns, rectilinear figures (3.MD.C.7a to d); perimeter with an unknown side (3.MD.D.8); unknown-factor equations (3.OA.A.4).
- **Readiness.** (1) Finds the area of a 6 by 7 rectangle by multiplying, and says why that works (3.MD.C.7a). (2) Finds a perimeter from all four sides, not two (3.MD.D.8). (3) Solves 8 × ? = 48 (3.OA.A.4). (4) Tells apart rectangles with the same perimeter and different areas (3.MD.D.8).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** area of the base, for V = b × h (5.MD.C.5); fractional side lengths (5.NF.B.4b).
- **Limits:** rectangles. To "apply the formula" does not mean to plug into a memorized one (Prog. p. 112 (106)). See finding C1 for the numbers allowed on these days.
- **Hard here:** adding one length and one width for perimeter (Prog. pp. 106 (100) and 111 (105)).

### Stretch 12. Multi-Digit Multiplication (days 94 to 105; 4.NBT.B.5)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** facts (3.OA.C.7); the distributive property, used (3.OA.B.5); one-digit times multiples of 10 (3.NBT.A.3); area of a rectangle with a split side (3.MD.C.7c).
- **Readiness.** (1) Recalls all one-digit products (3.OA.C.7). (2) Finds 6 × 70 and explains it as 42 tens (3.NBT.A.3). (3) Splits 7 × 14 into 7 × 10 and 7 × 4 with an area drawing (3.MD.C.7c). (4) Writes 4,385 in expanded form (4.NBT.A.2, stretch 8).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** partial products understood well enough to shorten into the standard algorithm, with fluency (5.NBT.B.5); the same for decimals (5.NBT.B.7).
- **Limits:** up to four digits by one digit, and two digits by two digits. No fluency requirement.
- **Hard here:** two-digit by two-digit has four partial products and students drop two. Short methods hide what the digits mean (Prog. p. 71 (65)). Products like 5 × 60 carry an "extra" zero (same page).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretches 8, 9, 11.

### Stretch 13. Division with Remainders (days 106 to 117; 4.NBT.B.6)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** division as unknown factor (3.OA.B.6); sharing and grouping meanings (3.OA.A.2); facts (3.OA.C.7). Grade 3 has no remainders.
- **Readiness.** (1) Recalls division facts within 100 (3.OA.C.7). (2) Finds the largest multiple of 7 that is not more than 45 (3.OA.C.7; Prog. p. 72 (66)). (3) Computes 4 × 8 + 3 (3.OA.D.8). (4) Multiplies a one-digit number by a multiple of 10 or 100 (4.NBT.B.5, stretch 12).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** place-by-place division, for two-digit divisors (5.NBT.B.6); quotient with remainder, to read a/b as a ÷ b (5.NF.B.3).
- **Limits:** one-digit divisors, dividends to four digits. Fluency with the standard division algorithm is grade 6 (6.NS.B.2).
- **Hard here:** zeros in the dividend or the quotient, and remainders part way through (Prog. p. 72 (66)). Writing "84 ÷ 10 = 8 R 4" misuses the equal sign; write 84 = 8 × 10 + 4 (Prog. p. 45 (39)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 12.

### Stretch 14. Multistep Problems (days 120 to 123; 4.OA.A.3 in full)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** two-step problems, an equation with a letter, and checking by estimating (3.OA.D.8).
- **Readiness.** (1) Solves a two-step problem and writes its equation (3.OA.D.8). (2) Rounds to estimate a sum or product (3.NBT.A.1, 4.NBT.A.3). (3) Divides with a remainder (4.NBT.B.6, stretch 13). (4) Solves a "times as many" problem (4.OA.A.2, stretch 10).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** the same habits with fractions and decimals (5.NF.A.2, 5.NF.B.6, 5.NBT.B.7).
- **Limits:** whole numbers and whole-number answers. The Progressions cap these at three steps, with easy or medium addition and subtraction (Prog. p. 44 (38)).
- **Hard here:** deciding what a remainder means in the story: drop it, round up, or it is the answer (Prog. p. 45 (39)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretches 9, 10, 12, 13.

### Stretch 15. Measurement Conversions (days 124 to 131; 4.MD.A.1, A.2)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** time to the minute; grams, kilograms, liters; one-step problems (3.MD.A.1, A.2). From grade 2: inches, feet, centimeters, meters (2.MD.A.1 to A.4) and money (2.MD.C.8). New in grade 4: km, lb, oz, ml, seconds, and every conversion.
- **Readiness.** (1) Measures a length in centimeters and in inches (2.MD.A.1). (2) Has a feel for a gram, a kilogram, a liter (3.MD.A.2). (3) Finds elapsed time in minutes (3.MD.A.1). (4) Says "a meter is 100 times as long as a centimeter" (4.OA.A.1, stretch 10). (5) Multiplies by one-digit numbers and by 10, 100, 1,000 (4.NBT.B.5, stretch 12).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** unit sizes and the times-as-much link, to convert both ways with decimals (5.MD.A.1) and to take up cubic units (5.MD.C.3).
- **Limits:** larger unit to smaller unit only, within one system. 4.MD.A.2 allows "simple fractions or decimals"; see finding E1.
- **Hard here:** the smaller the unit, the larger the number. Two-column tables make this visible (Prog. p. 110 (104)).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretches 6, 7, 10, 12, 13.

### Stretch 16. Lines and Angles (days 132 to 143; 4.G.A.1; 4.MD.C.5 to C.7)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** shape attributes, with right angles as "square corners" (3.G.A.1); a fraction of a whole, for 1/360 of a turn (3.NF.A.1). Angle measure is new.
- **Readiness.** (1) Picks out right angles in a rectangle (3.G.A.1). (2) Names a quarter and a half of a circle (3.NF.A.1; 2.G.A.3). (3) Reads a ruler scale from a start other than zero (2.MD.A.4, 3.MD.B.4). (4) Adds and subtracts within 360 (3.NBT.A.2).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** perpendicular lines for the coordinate axes (5.G.A.1); angle size and parallel sides for sorting figures (5.G.B.3, B.4).
- **Limits:** whole-number degrees. Unknown angles only by adding and subtracting parts (4.MD.C.7). Supplementary and vertical angles are grade 7 (7.G.B.5).
- **Hard here:** judging an angle by the length of its arms; not seeing a right angle when it is tilted; reading the wrong protractor scale (Prog. pp. 113 to 114 (107 to 108)).

### Stretch 17. Shapes and Symmetry (days 144 to 147; 4.G.A.2, A.3)

- **Grade 3 hands it:** categories and subcategories of quadrilaterals by attributes (3.G.A.1).
- **Readiness.** (1) Sorts quadrilaterals and names an attribute they share (3.G.A.1). (2) Draws a quadrilateral that is not a rectangle, rhombus or square (3.G.A.1). (3) Identifies parallel and perpendicular sides and right, acute, obtuse angles (4.G.A.1, stretch 16).
- **Grade 5 needs from it:** classifying by properties, for the hierarchy (5.G.B.3, B.4).
- **Limits:** the standard names only right triangles as a category. The Progressions also discuss sorting triangles by sides and angles (Prog. pp. 132 to 133 (126 to 127)); treat that as optional, not assessed. No hierarchy statements yet.
- **Hard here:** symmetry of slanted shapes; a parallelogram has none though it looks as if it should (stated from memory).
- **Earlier in this year:** stretch 16.

### Stretch 18. The Year Together (day 148)

## 4. Fluency and facts across the three grades

| Grade | The standard says | Code |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | "Fluently add and subtract within 20"; know all one-digit sums from memory | 2.OA.B.2 |
| 3 | "Fluently multiply and divide within 100"; "know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers" | 3.OA.C.7 |
| 3 | "Fluently add and subtract within 1000", any sound algorithm | 3.NBT.A.2 |
| 4 | "Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm" | 4.NBT.B.4 |
| 5 | "Fluently multiply multi-digit whole numbers using the standard algorithm" | 5.NBT.B.5 |
| 6 | "Fluently divide multi-digit numbers using the standard algorithm" | 6.NS.B.2 |

Grade 4 has one fluency standard, 4.NBT.B.4. Factor pairs (4.OA.B.4), multi-digit multiplication (4.NBT.B.5) and division (4.NBT.B.6) carry no fluency requirement.

**What the grade 4 daily warm-up should carry:**

1. **Multiplication and division facts, all year.** Everything from stretch 1 to stretch 15 leans on them. Start with a check, not an assumption (finding D4).
2. **Adding and subtracting within 1,000, days 1 to 71**, by any method; **the standard algorithm with larger numbers from day 72** (4.NBT.B.4).
3. **One-digit times multiples of 10, 100, 1,000** from about day 62 (3.NBT.A.3, then 4.NBT.B.5). It feeds stretches 12, 13, 15.
4. **Fraction and decimal sense** after stretch 7: equivalents, comparing to 1/2 and 1, tenths and hundredths. This is upkeep, not a fluency standard.
5. **Multi-digit multiplication and division** after day 118, as practice. Do not time it; fluency is grades 5 and 6.

This matches the spine's fluency plan. The only additions are items 1 (check first) and 3.
