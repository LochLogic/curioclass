# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 10

Course `english-language-arts-grade-10` (catalogue id `ela-k12`), grade 10, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7 units, each built on one named set of texts.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 148 in units and 12 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks, five lessons a week of about 50 minutes. Days follow load: the three long works read whole (Frankenstein, Julius Caesar, All Quiet on the Western Front) and the sustained research project carry the most days; the unit that opens every year-long routine is long because it reads a whole novel while those routines start. Each unit opens with a readiness check on the grade 9 rows it leans on first and closes with its end-of-unit assessment inside its days.
- **Standard.** Common Core ELA, grades 9 to 10 band (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Grade 10 owns every band code: grade 9 started each one (marked ~ there), and this course finishes it with every lettered part, independent at the high end of the band by year's end. The Units table says which unit teaches each lettered part. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (grades 6 to 12, Gateway 2 and 3) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (higher court first, then facts and backed sources, dignity, age) governs every contested claim.
- **Base text.** None. ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Anchor texts are public domain in the United States (published 1930 or earlier, translations included, each checked for its own date), US government works, or original Curio Class texts written to the per-grade text spec; no commercial text collection is used. Grades 9 to 12 may anchor on older Reading Room texts (2026-11-12); every anchor not named by the standards or the rows is picked for being recognizable and as recent as the licence allows (2026-11-13). This year's recent anchor is All Quiet on the Western Front (1929 English translation, US public domain since 2025). Short quoted excerpts of well-known recent books may sit inside a Curio Class lesson as pointers, never as anchors. No student is sent to a library or a paid copy: every required anchor has a checked print copy in the Curio Class library before its unit is planned. Period texts that hold slurs are printed with each slur left out, an omission mark and a short editor's note saying what was left out and why; the edition is otherwise complete and every omission is disclosed (BASELINE rule 6, the grade 11 and US History I wording). Pending(data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-10/texts.csv): each anchor's text record, written with the scope and sequence before its unit is planned: the exact edition, translation and selection and where the copy is held, the quantitative reading measure where usable, the four qualitative headings (meaning, structure, language, what the reader needs to know), reader and task, the supports, and the reason for its place; unwritten original Curio Class texts are listed as such and read by a qualified ELA teacher before use.
- **Reading level.** Anchors reach the high end of the grades 9 to 10 band (a quantitative reading level of about 1185 to 1335, 2012 supplement to Appendix A, and the hardest older prose), read independently by year's end (RL.9-10.10 and RI.9-10.10, the end-of-grade-10 line). Routine scaffolds fade through the year; supported reading is kept apart from evidence of independent proficiency. Independence is shown at set points: units 4 to 7 each carry one independent reading checkpoint (a passage at the high end, no before-reading help), literary in units 4 and 5, literary nonfiction in units 6 and 7, and the year-end course-level check is the independent reading evidence for both codes (see Course-level checks). Grade 11 picks up in the 11-CCR band, stated in the same terms (a quantitative reading level of about 1185 to 1385). A poem or play has no usable measure and rests on the qualitative read. Audio sits beside a long text, never in place of reading on a check.
- **Routines all year.** The daily exit ticket and timed analytic writing (e10-w-text-analysis), student-led seminars at least twice a unit (e10-sl-seminar), about 8 to 10 words a week from the unit's texts, met before, during and after reading, plus the student's own word log from grade 9 (e10-l-word-strategies; the scope and sequence sets each list), and a long on-topic book each unit for independent reading with a log and a conference (e10-rl-range-independent). Each starts in unit 1 and returns in every later unit as review.
- **Writing calendar.** Five process pieces: the analytic essay (unit 1), a two-narrator narrative (unit 2), the literary argument (unit 4), the issue argument (unit 6) and the sourced explanatory research report (unit 7), plus two short research inquiries (unit 7) and timed analytic essays and short explanations every unit. Students set their own revision goal from the rubric and the teacher step is a conference. By count of process pieces: argument 3 (the analytic essay counted as argument from text, taught on W.9-10.9, defending a claim), explanatory 1 (the year's longest piece), narrative 1; across grades 9 and 10 the shares come to about 45, 36 and 18 percent, near the 40, 40, 20 planning guidance (ela.csv e10-w-argument). No ELA process piece falls due in World History II's shared research weeks except the shared project itself.
- **Style manual.** One for all four high school years: a short Curio Class style sheet that follows the MLA core-elements format for citations (the format, never the handbook's text), with the US Government Publishing Office Style Manual (a US government work) for spelling, capitals and numbers. Grade 9 started it; grade 10 owns L.9-10.3a in unit 4 and applies it in unit 7.
- **Devices.** Paper path for every task. W.9-10.6 (a shared or linked product, published and updated) is met on a shared device or in a lab session in the school day, using the school's own tools; a room without devices hand-writes and posts a linked source list on paper, which does not meet W.9-10.6, and the plan records the standard unfinished where no device route exists. SL.9-10.5 (digital media used strategically in the unit 7 talk) has its own digital element, made the same way on a shared device with no account and recorded apart; a paper visual keeps the talk whole and leaves the digital element recorded unfinished. Film, audio, staged recordings and the recorded hearing and debate are shown on one teacher screen; where no playback exists the teacher reads a transcript aloud for the listening tasks (SL.9-10.2, SL.9-10.3), which serves the listening judgment but is not digital-media work, and a two-medium comparison (RL.9-10.7, RI.9-10.7) uses a medium the room can show (the painting, a printed graph) or is recorded unfinished. No student account anywhere in Curio Class.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). What it studies first: unit 3 (colonial rule in India, the Philippines and South Africa), Indian and Bengali scholarship on Tagore, Filipino sources on Rizal (the Philippine national historical body's pages and Filipino scholars), South African scholarship on Plaatje, and Barolong and Tswana, and Ndebele, published histories of the Kunana attack and of Mzilikazi; unit 5 (war deaths, an amputation, suicidal distress), the published safe-messaging recommendations on suicide, a national school counsellors' association's guidance on teaching hard content and a veterans' trauma body's public guidance, checking the passages the unit's content note names before use; unit 6 (Chief Joseph's address and the Nez Perce removal), the Nez Perce Tribe's own published pages and history, the federal Nez Perce National Historical Park pages, scholarship by Native scholars, and a Native journalists' association guide. Original Curio Class texts (unit 2's essay, unit 7's news and opinion pieces) get the text agent's read (same brief, kind text) before use.
