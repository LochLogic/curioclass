# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 10

Course id `english-language-arts-grade-10`, grade 10, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 7 units, each built on one named set of texts.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units and 12 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks, five lessons a week of about 50 minutes. Days follow load: the three long works read whole (Frankenstein, Julius Caesar, All Quiet on the Western Front) and the sustained research project carry the most days; the unit that opens every year-long routine is long because it reads a whole novel while those routines start.
- **Standard.** Common Core ELA, grades 9 to 10 band (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Grade 10 owns every band code: grade 9 started each one (marked ~ there), and this course finishes it with every lettered part, independent at the high end of the band by year's end. The Units table says which unit teaches each lettered part. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (grades 6 to 12, Gateway 2 and 3) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (higher court first, then facts and backed sources, dignity, age) governs every contested claim.
- **Base text.** None. ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Anchor texts are public domain in the United States (published 1930 or earlier, translations included, each checked for its own date), US government works, or original Curio Class texts written to the per-grade text spec; CommonLit is not used. Grades 9 to 12 may anchor on older Reading Room texts (2026-11-12); every anchor not named by the standards or the rows is picked for being recognizable and as recent as the licence allows (2026-11-13). This year's recent anchor is All Quiet on the Western Front (1929 English translation, US public domain since 2025). Short quoted excerpts of well-known recent books may sit inside a Curio Class lesson as pointers, never as anchors. No student is sent to a library or a paid copy: every anchor has a print copy in the course. The text record (texts.csv) gives each anchor its measure, the four qualitative headings, the task and the reason for its place.
- **Reading level.** Anchors reach the high end of the grades 9 to 10 band (about 1185 to 1335 Lexile, 2012 supplement to Appendix A, and the hardest older prose), read independently by year's end (RL.9-10.10 and RI.9-10.10, the end-of-grade-10 line). Routine scaffolds fade through the year; supported reading is kept apart from evidence of independent proficiency. A poem or play has no usable measure and rests on the qualitative read. Audio sits beside a long text, never in place of reading on a check.
- **Routines all year.** The daily exit question and timed analytic writing (e10-w-text-analysis), student-led seminars at least twice a unit (e10-sl-seminar), a unit word list of 10 to 15 words plus each student's own word log (e10-l-word-strategies), and a long on-topic book each unit for independent reading with a log and a conference (e10-rl-range-independent). Each starts in unit 1 and returns in every later unit as review.
- **Writing calendar.** Five process pieces: the analytic essay (unit 1), a two-narrator narrative (unit 2), the literary argument (unit 4), the issue argument (unit 6) and the sourced explanatory research report (unit 7), plus timed analytic essays and short explanations every unit. Students set their own revision goal from the rubric and the teacher step is a conference. By count of process pieces: argument 3 (the analytic essay counted as argument from text), explanatory 1 (the year's longest piece), narrative 1; across grades 9 and 10 the shares come to about 45, 36 and 18 percent, near the 40, 40, 20 planning guidance (ela.csv e10-w-argument). No ELA process piece falls due in World History II's shared research weeks except the shared project itself.
- **Style manual.** One for all four high school years: a short Curio Class style sheet that follows the MLA core-elements format for citations (the format, never the handbook's text), with the US Government Publishing Office Style Manual (a US government work) for spelling, capitals and numbers. Grade 9 started it; grade 10 owns L.9-10.3a in unit 4 and applies it in unit 7.
- **Devices.** Paper path for every task. W.9-10.6 (a shared or linked product, published and updated) is met on a shared device or in a lab session in the school day, using the school's own tools; a room without devices hand-writes and posts a linked source list on paper, which does not meet W.9-10.6, and the plan records the standard unfinished where no device route exists. No student account anywhere in Curio Class.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent read. Units needing a community or qualified reader: unit 3 (readers from the South Asian, Filipino and Southern African communities whose colonial history the stories tell, and a Tswana reader for Mhudi), unit 5 (a reader with school counselling background for the war deaths), unit 6 (a Nez Perce reader for Chief Joseph's address). Who, and any pay, is Cory's.
