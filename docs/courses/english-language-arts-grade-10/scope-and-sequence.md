# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 10

`english-language-arts-grade-10`, grade 10, draft, 2026-11-15.

The course keeps the frame's seven units and 160 course days: 148 unit days and 12 course-level check and respond days. It meets every school day, so course day and school day coincide; `school_day` is blank. The authoritative day order, section identifiers, text placements, skills and codes are in `data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-10/days.csv`. Sections last one to six days. This document explains the choices rather than reproducing that table. No assessment items or lesson plans are written here.

## Reading, writing and evidence across the year

The text sets build knowledge of creation and life, colonial power, Roman political action, wartime persuasion, liberty and coastal flooding. The three whole long literary anchors remain whole: the 1818 Frankenstein, Julius Caesar and the 1929 Wheen translation of All Quiet on the Western Front. Frankenstein uses the original volume/chapter numbering, not a later edition's continuous numbering. Rizal's four chapters give a bounded comparison of colonial encounters; Mhudi's four chapters provide an extended opening, while summaries of its remaining chapters are labelled as summaries. Unit 5 supplies the world-literature row's whole-longer-work requirement. Four Freedoms is the complete named argument within an excerpt from a much longer annual message.

Anchor balance uses the shared catalogue's `use=anchor` rows only: ten informational and seven literary, 10/17 = 58.8 percent informational. Paired works, poems, short reads, independent-book offers and the new paired West African story do not enter this denominator. A bundle counted as one catalogue anchor stays one; a future anchor replacement requires recounting by this same method. This lies inside the lead's 45 to 65 percent interval. No count by unit days or uncatalogued planned anchors substitutes for this count. The older free-text shelf and excerpted non-European novels remain visible limits.

Reading demand rises through reduced routine help, not through an invented ascending numerical measure. Units 1 to 3 model long sentences, evidence choice and unfamiliar context, then fade those supports. Units 4 and 5 have independent literary checkpoints on days 82 and 110; units 6 and 7 have independent literary-nonfiction checkpoints on days 123 and 136. Days 156 and 157 supply the final independent high-end evidence for RL.9-10.10 and RI.9-10.10. No routine vocabulary preview, background lecture or audio substitute precedes those cold readings. Access accommodations are recorded separately. Targeted teaching continues after the first independent record is collected. Numerical measures and four-part qualitative records remain unconfirmed where the text catalogue says so; no planned checkpoint is called measured proficient merely by its slot.

The five process pieces are the unit 1 analytic argument from the novel, unit 2 two-narrator narrative, unit 4 literary argument, unit 6 issue argument and unit 7 explanatory research report. Their final due days are 25, 41, 83, 126 and 151, in weeks 5, 9, 17, 26 and 31. The narrative is gathered once on day 39 and published on day 41, without another story. Three pieces defend claims, one explains and one narrates: 60/20/20 by process-piece count; this count is reported separately from the longer report's writing time and the across-subject planning proportions. Timed analyses and short explanations recur alongside the daily text-based exit ticket. Models and sentence choices precede drafting, and each process piece includes an author-chosen revision goal, peer feedback, a teacher conference and revision evidence. In the play unit, short drafting blocks on days 66 to 80 carry the developing argument before day 81's sustained draft.

At least two student-led seminars occur in every unit: days 12/22, 32/38, 49/58, 65/75, 100/109, 120/124 and 131/144. Each includes an initial one-on-one evidence exchange, student decisions about rules, goals, deadlines and roles, questions connecting ideas, invitations to other voices and a closing qualified synthesis. Teacher-led hard-passage discussions remain alongside them. Daily partner talk and the exit ticket use the current text; the later unit plans must count text-dependent questions and tasks against the 80 percent target.

Weekly eight-word focus lists are in the CSV's week-start or nearest teaching-day notes. Each mixes anchor vocabulary and academic words for analysing it; unit planning checks the exact contextual examples. Each list is met before the relevant supported reading, during its reread and in later talk/writing; personal logs add independently found words. Lists never preview a cold assessment passage. Earlier lists return in new units. A long on-topic independent book, log and one conference per unit remain required; in-class reading and conference rotations share the existing reading/workshop blocks. The course text record and minute ledger below name free complete-book offers, completion time and the remaining source limitations; no required purchase or unannounced homework closes a budget gap.

At each end window, the tasks or named existing products cover every part of the listed codes. Lettered parts have their own CSV codes: for example, W.9-10.1a to e, W.9-10.2a to f, W.9-10.3a to e, SL.9-10.1a to d and all four L.9-10.4 strategies. No code is inferred solely from an attractive product title. A brief prompt or an existing product/observed performance supplies each part; unit plans write the prompts and scoring guidance. Response time is counted once per window. A conference is collected once, then reused wherever an assessment within ten course days needs that record. Digital publication/update and strategic digital media are distinct records; a paper substitute preserves the writing or talk but leaves the corresponding digital requirement unfinished. Shared devices are used during school, without student accounts or public uploads.

## Unit 1. Frankenstein

28 days, course days 1 to 28, weeks 1 to 6. The frame's question about Victor's responsibility drives a whole-novel reading from Walton's letters through the creature's account and the return to Walton. Evidence and character pairs come before the essay; frame transitions become visible before students judge several structural choices together. Theme analysis includes two themes in tension, traced through details, and a separate 200-word objective summary. The chapter reading is complete by day 22. Days 19 to 22 redistribute the ending: day 19 reads III.4 and III.5 through Elizabeth's May 18 letter; day 20 finishes III.5 and reads III.6; day 21 reads III.7 and Walton through September 5; day 22 finishes from September 7. Its roughly 3,000 words take 25 first-reading minutes, leaving five for partner preparation, 15 for seminar, three for synthesis and two for the exit. Conference rotations use days 18 to 21 and 24, within their independent work. The 1831 introduction and final essay stay on their existing days.

There is retrieval on days 1 to 5, no readiness check on grade 9 rows. Day 14's mid-unit assessment samples only RL.9-10.1 and RL.9-10.3: strong evidence for explicit meaning/inference and conflicting motives, interaction and plot/theme effects; day 15 contains its 15-minute response. Final evidence is distributed: day 23 nonfiction reasoning and word strategies, day 25 process/timed literary writing, day 26 summary and retained seminar/word-log audit, day 27 whole-novel reading plus a bounded cold transfer from The Last Man. This avoids demanding every full performance on one day. Day 28 is the single end-window response day. The substantial language and discussion load has explicit slots, rather than being squeezed behind the novel assessment.

Candidate decisions: keep the creature-story reread on day 16, one day already inside the 28; keep a reserved 350 to 450 word Last Man chapter 1 passage for ten minutes on day 27, within the existing end window; retain the whole-novel evidence and summary. Murder, wrongful execution, grief, isolation and appearance-based rejection are flagged on the relevant days; no personal or family disclosure is requested.

## Unit 2. What is life made of? From Huxley to cloning

14 days, course days 29 to 42, weeks 6 to 9. Huxley's whole essay is read in three parts with 25, 30 and 25 first-reading minutes on days 29 to 31, before students compare two authors' ordering of the same cloning process. Twenty independent-book minutes move from these days to day 39, replacing redundant packet verification; five writing minutes become first reading. Each day remains 50 minutes and no home reading is added. Sentence/paragraph function and the effect of moving or removing a passage precede that comparison. The original tissue essay brings the explanatory question into contemporary science. The two-narrator narrative transfers the preceding novel's nested telling into writing: opening/viewpoints, techniques, pacing/sequence, telling detail and reflective close are taught before final revision.

Day 29 retrieves the three grade 9 rows. There is no required mid-unit assessment in a 14-day unit. Days 39 to 41 spread the end window across the narrative/process record, a reserved unfamiliar science passage for evidence/central idea/development on day 40, the retained Huxley whole-essay summary and comparison of explanation order on day 41. The narrative is taken once, then reused while its publication finishes. Day 42 is the response day. Keep the two federal cloning explanations: their donor-cell nuclear-transfer passages supply comparable process material in different explanatory settings. The dated federal text editions and rights are recorded in unit 2 field 10; together they require about 5,400 words after duplicate displays are removed. The course records below replace the former 3,000-word pair cap. Pasteur and Tyndall are not course readings; their unused shared-catalogue rows remain a separate correction. No decision about the science follows from a student's religious or moral belief.

Candidate decisions: drop the third cloning account from a 1997 hearing, zero days, because two comparable explanations are needed before a third perspective and the narrative carries real drafting load; keep a 450 to 550 word unfamiliar science explanation on day 40, within its existing 50 minutes; reserve it from teaching. The new story does not consume another unit's day.

## Unit 4. Julius Caesar: Shakespeare and his source

25 days, course days 61 to 85, weeks 13 to 17. The whole play moves from public/private claims through the conspiracy, assassination, speeches, quarrel and defeat. North's corresponding Caesar and Brutus passages arrive beside the scenes they inform, before students judge what Shakespeare kept, cut or invented. The painting comparison occurs on day 68. The funeral speeches teach cumulative diction and parallelism; four complete poems then transfer those language judgments beyond the play. Counterclaims, strengths/limitations, audience, cohesion, formal style and conclusion are modelled before the full literary argument is completed.

Readiness was on day 60 for the grade 7 history row; results determine the short day 61 bridge. Grade 9 ELA rows are retrieved on days 61 to 66, with speech-presentation retrieval on day 69, before a brief III.1 excerpt performance, delivery-choice explanation and the separate play/painting claim. History unit 4, including interpretation critique, ends day 58, before the argument instruction; day 65 keeps the frame's two-reading bridge if history is later in a school's timetable. A 25-day unit has no required mid-unit assessment. Day 82 is the independent literary checkpoint, separate from supported play reading. Days 83 and 84 assess the existing argument/process/syntax/style record and source/medium/diction/figure comparisons. Day 85 is the response day. Both near-synonym nuance and the role of figures in context are assessed, not only a list of figures.

Candidate decisions: drop the third-medium staged recording, zero days, because the required text/painting comparison already meets the two-medium task and preserves drafting time; keep reserved sonnet 64 for ten minutes of day 84, alongside retained/source/medium evidence. Check 2 reuses day 84 diction, figure and transformation records; it scores independent cold-poem reading in a reconstruction form, then uses Paradise Lost with Frankenstein as unscored literary-source exploration. The independent checkpoint remains a distinct small slot.
