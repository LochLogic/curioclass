# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 10

`english-language-arts-grade-10`, grade 10, draft, 2026-11-15.

The course keeps the frame's seven units and 160 course days: 148 unit days and 12 course-level check and respond days. It meets every school day, so course day and school day coincide; `school_day` is blank. The authoritative day order, section identifiers, text placements, skills and codes are in `data/plans/english-language-arts-grade-10/days.csv`. Sections last one to six days. This document explains the choices rather than reproducing that table. No assessment items or lesson plans are written here.

## Reading, writing and evidence across the year

The text sets build knowledge of creation and life, colonial power, Roman political action, wartime persuasion, liberty and coastal flooding. The three whole long literary anchors remain whole: the 1818 Frankenstein, Julius Caesar and the 1929 Wheen translation of All Quiet on the Western Front. Frankenstein uses the original volume/chapter numbering, not a later edition's continuous numbering. Rizal's four chapters give a bounded comparison of colonial encounters; Mhudi's four chapters provide an extended opening, while summaries of its remaining chapters are labelled as summaries. Unit 5 supplies the world-literature row's whole-longer-work requirement. Four Freedoms is the complete named argument within an excerpt from a much longer annual message.

The catalogue's 17 `anchor` rows contain ten informational and seven literary rows: about 59 and 41 percent. Thirteen are whole-text rows, four excerpt rows; the sea-level report/graph row is a bundle, not a count of every eventual document. Adding the kept West African original as an anchor makes 18 planned anchor rows, ten informational and eight literary, about 56 and 44 percent, with fourteen whole-text rows. A fallback substitution in unit 2 requires recounting after its editions are settled. These are row counts, not words or reading minutes. Unit days remain 58 informational and 90 literary, about 39 and 61 percent. The roughly 70 percent informational comparison for high school is reported as a comparison, not a claim: this ELA course deliberately gives time to whole novels and a play, while informational reading is also taught in the other subjects. The older public-domain shelf and the predominance of excerpts among the non-European novels remain visible limits.

Reading demand rises through reduced routine help, not through an invented ascending numerical measure. Units 1 to 3 model long sentences, evidence choice and unfamiliar context, then fade those supports. Units 4 and 5 have independent literary checkpoints on days 82 and 110; units 6 and 7 have independent literary-nonfiction checkpoints on days 123 and 136. Days 156 and 157 supply the final independent high-end evidence for RL.9-10.10 and RI.9-10.10. No routine vocabulary preview, background lecture or audio substitute precedes those cold readings. Access accommodations are recorded separately. Targeted teaching continues after the first independent record is collected. Numerical measures and four-part qualitative records remain unconfirmed where the text catalogue says so; no planned checkpoint is called measured proficient merely by its slot.

The five process pieces are the unit 1 analytic argument from the novel, unit 2 two-narrator narrative, unit 4 literary argument, unit 6 issue argument and unit 7 explanatory research report. Their final due days are 25, 41, 83, 126 and 151, in weeks 5, 9, 17, 26 and 31. The narrative is gathered once on day 39 and published on day 41, without another story. Three pieces defend claims, one explains and one narrates: 60/20/20 by process-piece count; this count is reported separately from the longer report's writing time and the across-subject planning proportions. Timed analyses and short explanations recur alongside the daily text-based exit ticket. Models and sentence choices precede drafting, and each process piece includes an author-chosen revision goal, peer feedback, a teacher conference and revision evidence. In the play unit, short drafting blocks on days 66 to 80 carry the developing argument before day 81's sustained draft.

At least two student-led seminars occur in every unit: days 12/22, 32/38, 49/58, 65/75, 100/109, 120/124 and 131/144. Each includes an initial one-on-one evidence exchange, student decisions about rules, goals, deadlines and roles, questions connecting ideas, invitations to other voices and a closing qualified synthesis. Teacher-led hard-passage discussions remain alongside them. Daily partner talk and the exit ticket use the current text; the later unit plans must count text-dependent questions and tasks against the 80 percent target.

Weekly eight-word focus lists are in the CSV's week-start or nearest teaching-day notes. Each mixes anchor vocabulary and academic words for analysing it; unit planning checks the exact contextual examples. Each list is met before the relevant supported reading, during its reread and in later talk/writing; personal logs add independently found words. Lists never preview a cold assessment passage. Earlier lists return in new units. A long on-topic independent book, log and one conference per unit remain required; in-class reading and conference rotations share the existing reading/workshop blocks. Exact free editions and completion-minute budgets must be settled with the unit plans, rather than made required purchases or unannounced homework.

At each end window, the tasks or named existing products cover every part of the listed codes. Lettered parts have their own CSV codes: for example, W.9-10.1a to e, W.9-10.2a to f, W.9-10.3a to e, SL.9-10.1a to d and all four L.9-10.4 strategies. No code is inferred solely from an attractive product title. A brief prompt or an existing product/observed performance supplies each part; unit plans write the prompts and scoring guidance. Response time is counted once per window. A conference is collected once, then reused wherever an assessment within ten course days needs that record. Digital publication/update and strategic digital media are distinct records; a paper substitute preserves the writing or talk but leaves the corresponding digital requirement unfinished. Shared devices are used during school, without student accounts or public uploads.

## Unit 1. Frankenstein

28 days, course days 1 to 28, weeks 1 to 6. The frame's question about Victor's responsibility drives a whole-novel reading from Walton's letters through the creature's account and the return to Walton. Evidence and character pairs come before the essay; frame transitions become visible before students judge several structural choices together. Theme analysis includes two themes in tension, traced through details, and a separate 200-word objective summary. The chapter reading is complete by day 22, leaving time to judge the 1831 introduction as nonfiction and finish the essay without replacing the ending with a summary.

There is retrieval on days 1 to 5, no readiness check on grade 9 rows. Day 14's mid-unit assessment samples explicit/inferred evidence, theme development, character interaction and structure/time; day 15 contains its 15-minute response. Final evidence is distributed: day 23 nonfiction reasoning and word strategies, day 25 process/timed literary writing, day 26 summary, seminar and word-log evidence, day 27 whole-novel reading. This avoids demanding every full performance on one day. Day 28 is the single end-window response day. The substantial language and discussion load has explicit slots, rather than being squeezed behind the novel assessment.

Candidate decisions: keep the creature-story reread on day 16, one day already inside the 28; drop the cold Last Man chapter, zero added days, because the whole-novel assessment and the scheduled course-level cold readings already supply transfer without displacing completion of this novel. Murder, wrongful execution, grief, isolation and appearance-based rejection are flagged on the relevant days; no personal or family disclosure is requested.

## Unit 2. What is life made of? From Huxley to cloning

14 days, course days 29 to 42, weeks 6 to 9. Huxley's essay is read in three parts before students compare two authors' ordering of the same cloning process. Sentence/paragraph function and the effect of moving or removing a passage precede that comparison. The original tissue essay brings the explanatory question into contemporary science. The two-narrator narrative transfers the preceding novel's nested telling into writing: opening/viewpoints, techniques, pacing/sequence, telling detail and reflective close are taught before final revision.

Day 29 retrieves the three grade 9 rows. There is no required mid-unit assessment in a 14-day unit. Days 39 to 41 spread the end window across the narrative/process record, Huxley evidence/central idea/summary and comparison of explanation order. The narrative is taken once, then reused while its publication finishes. Day 42 is the response day. The cloning pair is provisional: if it does not order comparable material, the already-catalogued Pasteur and Tyndall selections replace days 33 and 34 one for one, not as extra readings. No decision about the science follows from a student's religious or moral belief.

Candidate decisions: drop the third cloning account from a 1997 hearing, zero days, because two comparable explanations are needed before a third perspective and the narrative carries real drafting load; drop the cold science essay at the unit end, zero days, reserving the cold science form for check 1 immediately afterward. Check 1 uses unit 1 content for the recently assessed unit 2 moves.

## Unit 3. Under colonial rule

15 days, course days 46 to 60, weeks 10 to 12. Complete Tagore stories and the freedom poem establish cultural comparison, then four Rizal chapters add a Philippine colonial setting. The original West African story broadens the range before Plaatje's historical background and the opening four chapters of Mhudi. Direct evidence is kept distinct from information supplied in background notes. Colon, semicolon and spelling instruction occurs in the actual comparison sentences, with a dedicated practice day because the row owns three distinct parts.

Day 46 retrieves the grade 9 world-story and punctuation rows; no mid-unit assessment is required. Days 58 and 59 assess regional cultural experiences through seminar and short comparison, plus all three punctuation parts. The whole-longer-work part is completed and assessed in unit 5, not falsely claimed for the Mhudi excerpt. Day 60 contains 40 minutes of response and the next unit's ten-minute readiness. The new story does not consume another unit's day.

Candidate decisions: keep one whole original West African colonial-era story on day 52, one day inside the unit, to address the progression's thin-region requirement; drop the additional cold Tagore story, zero days, because comparison of the taught set and punctuation already occupy the final window, with cold translated fiction reserved for check 3. The new story needs a specific place, perspective, text record and sensitivity read before use. The named texts' colonial clergy, period language and violence are flagged in the CSV. Mhudi's attack reading is on days 54 to 56, distinct from history's Armenian-genocide reading on day 48 and its assessment on day 57; these days were checked against history's available day map. The Lovedale edition's publisher alterations are disclosed. No cultural group is portrayed as inherently villainous.

## Unit 4. Julius Caesar: Shakespeare and his source

25 days, course days 61 to 85, weeks 13 to 17. The whole play moves from public/private claims through the conspiracy, assassination, speeches, quarrel and defeat. North's corresponding Caesar and Brutus passages arrive beside the scenes they inform, before students judge what Shakespeare kept, cut or invented. The painting comparison occurs on day 68. The funeral speeches teach cumulative diction and parallelism; four complete poems then transfer those language judgments beyond the play. Counterclaims, strengths/limitations, audience, cohesion, formal style and conclusion are modelled before the full literary argument is completed.

Readiness was on day 60 for the grade 7 history row; results determine the short day 61 bridge. Grade 9 ELA rows are retrieved on days 61 to 66, with speech-presentation retrieval on day 69. History unit 4, including interpretation critique, ends day 58, before the argument instruction; day 65 keeps the frame's two-reading bridge if history is later in a school's timetable. A 25-day unit has no required mid-unit assessment. Day 82 is the independent literary checkpoint, separate from supported play reading. Days 83 and 84 assess the existing argument/process/syntax/style record and source/medium/diction/figure comparisons. Day 85 is the response day. Both near-synonym nuance and the role of figures in context are assessed, not only a list of figures.

Candidate decisions: drop the third-medium staged recording, zero days, because the required text/painting comparison already meets the two-medium task and preserves drafting time; drop the cold sonnet or speech at the unit end, zero days, because check 2 immediately uses cold poetry and speech. Its transformation items draw on earlier unit 1 literary material instead of repeating this play's assessment. The independent checkpoint remains a distinct small slot. Act 5 stays on days 76 and 77 in week 16, the overlap the frame settled; suicides are read with the teacher, support contacts given and no extra method detail supplied. Assassination, mob killing and Portia's death are also named before reading.

Rhetoric is taught across interacting moves for an audience, not as device identification. The long battle and hospital chapters receive several reading blocks. Chapters 7, 9 and 10 fall in weeks 21 and 22; earlier amputation and death are still named and supported, not hidden by the frame's phrase 'hardest chapters'.

Day 90 retrieves grade 9 rhetoric; history propaganda is available from unit 5's end, day 72. The five-minute poster-based definition on day 93 remains the if-later bridge. No mid-unit assessment is required. Rhetoric evidence is assessed on day 108; the independent final chapter and whole-novel cultural comparison are assessed on day 110. The whole-longer-work portion of e10-rl-world-literature is expressly carried here. Day 111 has 40 minutes of end response and ten minutes of readiness for unit 6. No one rewrites the full rhetoric judgment on day 110.

Candidate decisions: drop the extra recorded 1941 wartime address, zero days, because its second-medium role is already supplied by the posters and unit 7 owns substantial listening work; drop the cold end speech, zero days, because whole-speech rhetoric is assessed here and unfamiliar speech reading already sits in check 2. ## Unit 6. Liberty, word by word

16 days, course days 112 to 127, weeks 23 to 26. The Declaration, Harper, Anthony, Chief Joseph and Four Freedoms build a related-theme comparison chronologically. The Anthony record and newspaper reports hold the event constant while register changes, so students can connect figurative, connotative and technical meaning to cumulative tone. The issue argument follows the whole set, after students have source-based language for their claims.

Readiness for the grade 8 constitution row was on day 111; day 112 uses the results and retrieves the grade 9 reading rows. No mid-unit assessment is required. Day 123 is an independent high-end literary-nonfiction checkpoint. Day 126 assesses the existing issue argument and its process record; day 127 assesses related themes and word choice, then includes ten minutes of response and the next unit's ten-minute scatter-plot readiness. The short end comparisons carry the reading codes; another full issue argument is not requested. The issue argument is due day 126, early week 26, separated from Chemistry's forecast endpoint day 130. This implements the frame's early/late-week choice; it does not create a clear week between due pieces.

Candidate decisions: drop the extra 1879-to-1941 citizenship/exclusion document, zero days, because the set already has five seminal voices and the trial/news pairing needs close attention; drop the cold founding-era end text, zero days, because the independent checkpoint and later course-level cold reading already supply unfamiliar-text evidence. ## Unit 7. Rising seas

28 days, course days 128 to 155, weeks 26 to 31. The source set's first four days distinguish accounts and integrate numeric, visual and spoken evidence. Two two-day inquiries teach question refinement, advanced search, usefulness judgments and cited synthesis before the sustained project. Four days then judge long print arguments and the hearing/debate speakers, including false claims, sufficiency, distorted evidence, correlation-as-cause and authority. The opinion is read cold before its guided analysis.

Day 127 readiness handles both grade 8 data leans; day 128 supplies interpretation as needed. Chemistry's forecast unit occupies days 113 to 130. The five-minute supplied forecast reading on days 128 to 130 bridges that overlap; from day 131 Chemistry is available, with the same fallback retained if the local timetable differs. ELA explains sources rather than asking students to run climate models or calculate advanced statistics. The grade 9 research row and earlier grade 10 argument/style work receive retrieval.

Day 140 holds the required mid-unit assessment, ten minutes of targeted response and project preparation: 25/10/15 minutes. It remains one of the frame's twelve sustained-project allocations, used for diagnosing transfer and preparatory work on the sea-level set. Thus no 29th day is added to fit the mid-unit requirement. The preceding source/inquiry/argument blocks remain four/four/four days. Sustained-project work occupies days 140 to 149 and 151 to 152, twelve allocations; talk instruction is day 150, panel assessment days 153 and 154, and end closure/response day 155. The mid-unit dossier covers the taught reading/research parts, not an untaught final report or talk.

In paired schools, history supplies its question/source set by the end of day 140, week 28. The shared project's question/source plan is set on day 141; five-source judged list due day 145; full draft day 148; revision and talk teaching by day 150; one report due day 151 and one talk per student across days 153 and 154. The shared 1,500 to 2,000 word report has ELA's scored explanation/figure and history's scored argued recommendation. A standalone ELA course writes the frame's 1,200 to 1,800 word explanation on the sea-level set. History's unit 11 opens day 145, within week 29, so ELA launches the common cycle before history's first meeting on it. Audience adaptation belongs to history in week 32. No second ELA process piece is due in these weeks.

Panels meet in parallel; half the class presents each day. For a 50-minute class of up to 30, use three panels of five per day: eight-minute talks plus about one minute each for questions, leaving five minutes for transitions. Choosing five is within the frame's five-or-six range; six ten-minute talks plus questions would not fit. Unit planning must assign the teacher's direct-observation rotation across every speaker over those two days; recording is optional, not a substitute for that plan. Larger classes need a new capacity plan within the existing days. The digital element and informal/formal adaptation have earlier practice evidence. Day 155 reuses the report, source dossier, short syntheses and written speaker judgments, filling only missing criterion evidence, then provides 15 minutes of response. It never asks for another full report, talk or individual conference. W.9-10.2a to f each has a named product feature: organization/headings/figure, development, transitions, precise/domain language, formal/objective style, implications in the close.

Candidate decisions: drop drought/water-use topic choice, zero days, because an additional complete source pathway would dilute the shared project's common preparation; drop the cold end source set, zero days, because the next three days are course-level cold reading and the end window can assess the actual research evidence. Unit 7 retains the frame's two short inquiries, sustained project and distinct hearing and debate. Original news/opinion texts and exact federal items remain to write/choose and check before unit use. ## Shorter path

For a 40-minute period, keep about five minutes for retrieval/word recurrence, twenty for the day's main reading or writing, ten for text-based practice/discussion and five for the exit ticket. Replace extended whole-class reporting with partner feedback; use the existing multi-day writing/end windows instead of demanding two full performances in one short period. On seminar and panel days, protect discussion/performance time by making preparation part of the preceding reading block. A panel of five or six can remain together across days 153 and 154, with two or three eight-minute talks plus questions each day. A class of 30 would need five panels of six running concurrently; that requires a different teacher observation rotation from the 50-minute plan and is a capacity question for the unit plan, not a proven fit here. Assess each student once. A parent/teacher listener serves the same talk functions at home. Do not shorten the anchor into a summary, remove a lettered part or make extra homework a condition of access. Unit plans must verify word counts, actual reading/writing minutes and panel sizes against the shorter period before claiming it fits.

A year ten days shorter folds the following unassessed work into named neighbours. The original day numbers remain reference labels; the local calendar renumbers meetings and brings the finals forward. All check, readiness, mid-unit and end-window evidence stays; these folds are not permission to delete a standard or a chapter.
