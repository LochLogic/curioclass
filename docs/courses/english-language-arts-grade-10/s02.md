## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What did scientists claim they could make, and how did each writer explain it to a general reader?"

**Hook, day 29:** Read Huxley's opening definition and surprising unity claim. What difference between appearances and underlying matter does he ask readers to explain?

**Supporting questions:** How does Huxley's water analogy support his claim (day 30)? Why does he deny being a materialist after using materialistic terms (day 31)? Why does the livestock primer place nuclear transfer after the farming problem (day 34)?

**Writing:** Assign a 1,500 to 2,000 word narrative on day 29: two fictional narrators disagree about announcing an unchecked laboratory result, labeled "invented for practice". Require two cloning-pair details and commentary on a Frankenstein structural borrowing. Plan on day 29; draft on days 32, 34 and 36; give written feedback after days 32 and 36; revise and edit on day 38; submit on day 39; publish on day 41. Distinguish claiming from establishing.
Model paragraph function on day 29 and narrative lines on day 32. Students lead comparison by day 34. Teach detail and closure on day 36; practice on day 38. The unit's two student-led seminars sit inside writing days: 32 (Shelley's frames beside Huxley) and 38 (how the cloning pair and tissue essay order ideas), each after partner preparation, with student-set rules and roles and a qualified close.


Read Huxley on days 29 to 31, the fact sheet on day 33, the primer on day 34 and the tissue essay on days 35 to 36. Omit unused fallbacks g10-u2-04 and g10-u2-05. Add Faraday for assessment; retain Frankenstein for modeling.

**Complexity:** Exact-selection measures are unavailable. Huxley's abstract, cumulative argument, older syntax and physiological assumptions need clause bracketing and a paragraph map, faded by day 31. The fact sheet needs a cell glossary before reading, a process outline during it and comparison afterwards. The primer's farming frame and shifts between process, risks and uses need matched-step annotation and removal analysis. The tissue essay must distinguish tissues from organisms: orient before reading, annotate mechanism and limits during it, then compare. Grade 10 readers transfer Frankenstein's inquiry to explanations; supported success does not certify high-band independence.

**Vocabulary:** Teach before reading, locate uses during it and reuse afterwards. Protoplasm, organism, tissue, organ, clone, narrator and pacing are Tier 3; others are Tier 2. Week 9 retrieves known words independently after collection.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A qualified claim is treated as an absolute. | Reread the limiting phrase and restate its scope. | From memory. |
| B | An objective summary substitutes personal judgment for the text's developed idea. | Remove the judgment and connect two refining details. | From memory. |
| C | An outline lists topics without explaining their connections. | Label what each move prepares for the next. | From memory. |
| D | Passage-function analysis only retells content. | Explain what the reader would lose if the passage were removed. | From memory. |
| E | The salt-column rise is attributed to dissolving the salt. | Reread the saturation condition and distinguish rising from dissolving. | From memory. |
| F | The reader cannot identify the speaker. | Name the speaker at the viewpoint change. | From memory. |

| H | Alternating narrators is mistaken for a connected event sequence. | Trace the shared event and revise one causal transition. | From memory. |
| I | General adjectives replace telling detail. | Choose a concrete observable detail that reveals the situation. | From memory. |
| J | The conclusion adds an unrelated moral. | Reflect on a consequence already developed in the story. | From memory. |
| K | A related word form is used with the wrong grammatical function. | Identify its word class, verify it and substitute it in context. | From memory. |
