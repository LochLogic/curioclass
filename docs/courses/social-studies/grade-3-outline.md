# Grade 3 social studies: Communities Then and Now (draft outline, pre-work P1d)

Written 2026-11-13. Draft for the grade 3 progression row and course frame; read in its turn by the expert (and a
person when there is one). Nothing here is a unit plan yet.

## Why this year exists

The anchor's grade 3 is "Massachusetts, Home to Many Different People". Cory decided (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) that
Curio Class does not teach one state's own content; it writes its own year in that place. Read closely, the
anchor's grade 3 carries two kinds of content:

1. **State-only content:** Massachusetts towns, maps, tribal territories, seaports, the Massachusetts
   Constitution. This is replaced: every child studies **their own** town and state, with worked examples from
   several states.
2. **The first pass at early American history** that grades 4 and 5 build on: first contact, a colony's founding
   and self-government, daily colonial life, slavery legal in every colony, trade by sea, the start of the
   Revolution, the Declaration and the idea of a constitution. The anchor teaches these through Massachusetts.
   This content is **kept**, taught through several places, so the K to 12 progression stays whole: grade 4
   (4.T3, exploration) and grade 5 (5.T1 and 5.T2, colonies and the Revolution) assume it.

**Threads through the year:** a biography in most units (people who made a difference, men and women from many
backgrounds, local and national); a timeline in every history unit, read in years, decades and centuries; and a
standing routine for telling a primary source from a secondary one.

Every unit runs the C3 inquiry arc at grade 3 depth (a question, sources, a claim with evidence, sharing it).

## The "your state" method

- Each unit has a **local thread**: a short guided task the class does about its own town or state (a map, a
  local government visit or video, the nation or nations whose land the school is on, a founding story).
- Each unit's teaching days use **worked examples from several states**, rotated across regions so no one region
  carries every example (rubric row C12).
- The teacher guide links each state's own standards page and its official state and tribal websites, so a
  school can add its state's required facts. Curio Class does not claim to meet any one state's grade 3 standards.

## What other states teach at grade 3

Read 2026-11-13 to fill the year, read only (cited by name; nothing copied): California (History-Social Science
Standards, grade 3), New York (Framework, grade 3), Texas (TEKS, grade 3), Colorado (2022 standards, grade 3) and
Florida (2023 standards, grade 3). Virginia, Michigan and Tennessee did not load for automated reading and were
not worked around. Tennessee was read later the same day in a normal browser: its 2024 standards (in classrooms
2027-28) make grade 3 a US history year, from Indigenous peoples and European exploration through the colonies,
independence and the Constitution to westward expansion. Our units 4 to 6 cover the first part of that in brief;
grade 5 carries the rest. What the five share and the anchor's grade 3 lacks or teaches thinly:

- **Community economics** in all five: producers and consumers, goods and services, scarcity, money, saving. Now
  unit 3.
- **Geography beyond the home state** in all five: continents and oceans, globe and map, grid, US regions, people
  changing the land. Now in unit 1.
- **Levels of government, paying for services, rights with responsibilities** in four of five. Now in unit 2.
- **Symbols, holidays and founding ideas** (Florida, Texas, California): now unit 6, which drops the Revolution's
  events because grade 5 teaches them in full.
- **Biographies and timelines** in four of five, and **primary versus secondary sources** in three: now threads.
- **Newcomers and comparing a world community** (New York, Colorado, California): in units 7 and 3.

## Values rule notes for this year

- Native nations are taught in the present tense as well as the past, from their own published materials where
  they exist; sacred or restricted knowledge is not taught.
- Slavery in the colonies is taught plainly at grade level: who was enslaved, that it was legal and cruel, and
  that enslaved people resisted and built lives.
- Harvest-feast stories are taught from the record: what the sources say, and what Wampanoag people say about it
  today.
