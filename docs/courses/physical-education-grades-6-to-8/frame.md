# Course frame: Physical Education, grades 6 to 8

Course `physical-education-grades-6-to-8` (catalog id `pe-6-8`), grades 6, 7 and 8, one band course in three
school years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the pattern of the fixed K to 2 pilot
(docs/reviews/frame-physical-education-grades-k-to-2-fixes-2026-11-25.md); the scope and sequence sets the days
later, one year at a time. Each year's part of this file (its units and its two course-level checks) is that year's
frame, as the subject page's "one frame per grade year" asks.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 22: 7 in grade 6 (units 1 to 7), 7 in grade 7 (units 8 to 14), 8 in grade 8 (units 15 to 22). Each
  year is whole on its own: its own units, end-of-unit assessments and mid-year and year-end checks.
- **Days.** 288: 96 a year for three years. Each year: 90 days in units and 6 for course-level checks and respond
  days (two check days and one respond day at mid-year, the same at year-end); grade 6 90 + 6, grade 7 90 + 6, grade
  8 90 + 6. One session of about 45 minutes, three sessions a week, over the 32 weeks of the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md): the PE dose APPROACH.md "Pacing" sets for 6 to 8 (96 sessions of 45 minutes, Cory
  2026-11-21, Desk q-fix-pe-1). This frame keeps that default. The 45 minutes are class time after changing (changing
  follows the school's own policy, outside the period); they hold the instant activity, warm-up, focus, cool-down
  and closure, and the set-up, clean-up and collecting of equipment (about 4 minutes, done by students in their
  station groups; about 6 on outdoor and orienteering days, named in units 14 and 20). Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment. No unit is over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. The course says openly that
  three 45-minute sessions (135 minutes a week) are below the 225 minutes a week SHAPE America's guidelines set for
  middle school (shapeamerica.org/standards/guidelines); the course page, teacher pages and family notes offer short
  active breaks and home activity. On the even spread the calendar uses, course day d of a year falls on about school
  day 5d/3 of that year.
- **Standard.** SHAPE America National Physical Education Standards, 2024 (data/standards/shape-pe-2024.txt): all
  61 span-8 learning indicators (SHAPE.S1.8.1 to S1.8.20, S2.8.1 to S2.8.22, S3.8.1 to S3.8.10, S4.8.1 to S4.8.9)
  are owned in this band, each by the one progression row that owns it (data/progressions/physical-education.csv,
  grades 6, 7 and 8, 58 rows). A row's `~` codes are begun or used in that unit and named in What students learn
  with their owner. Ids and our own words only; no indicator sentence is quoted (STANDARD.md 2). Check set: CDC's
  PECAT 2019 6 to 8 grade tables (every outcome of the five older standards expected in every grade), keyed to the
  2013 five standards and mapped through docs/standards-notes/pe-2014-to-2024.md, read for placement only (outcome
  stated, content teaches it, assessment checks it). Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) and the appropriate
  practice points (STANDARD.md 4) hold in every unit. Health's physical activity outcomes 3, 4 and 6 for grades 6 to 8
  (warm-up and cool-down, water during activity, staying safe while active) are taught here (Cory, 2026-11-20, Desk
  q-prework-health-pe-2): warm-up and cool-down in unit 2, water during activity in units 3 and 14, staying safe while
  active in units 1 and 20.
- **Grade outcomes.** Bar criterion 1 asks for an outcome for every standard in every grade (STANDARD.md 3 rule
  2). In our words; the scope and sequence copies them and adds the indicators each year works on.
  - Grade 6. S1: throws, rolls and catches with good form, passes, receives and dribbles on the move, takes a ready
    defensive stance, performs four-phrase dance sequences, and uses sound technique in health-related and
    skill-related fitness exercises (S1.8.2, S1.8.3, S1.8.4, S1.8.6, S1.8.8, S1.8.14, S1.8.15, S1.8.19; units 1 to
    6), and makes first tries at serves, strokes and tossed-ball striking (unit 7). S2: breaks a skill into its parts,
    names and sorts the fitness parts, builds a warm-up and cool-down, counts and explains heart rate, names movement
    ideas in changing settings, and lists ways to stay active (S2.8.1, S2.8.9, S2.8.14, S2.8.15, S2.8.16, S2.8.18;
    units 1, 2, 3, 5, 7). S3: welcomes differences and contributes to a team, follows rules and safe behavior
    (S3.8.1, S3.8.2, S3.8.6, S3.8.7; units 1, 4). S4: explains how self-expression affects involvement and takes part
    in many kinds of activity (S4.8.1, S4.8.3; unit 6). Checked by skill tasks, short written knowledge tasks and
    conduct marks.
  - Grade 7. S1: throws for accuracy, distance and power, swings a long tool with a mature pattern, strikes a tossed
    ball into space, serves with hand and tool, hits forehand and backhand strokes, and keeps form in circuits,
    routines and an outdoor walk (S1.8.5, S1.8.7, S1.8.9, S1.8.10, S1.8.11, S1.8.12; units 8, 11, 12, 14). S2:
    explains attack and defense in invasion games, changes dances with movement ideas, uses FITT and the training
    principles, uses skill-related fitness, links aerobic fitness and effort, plans rest, and links eating, exercise
    and well-being (S2.8.2, S2.8.3, S2.8.8, S2.8.11, S2.8.12, S2.8.13, S2.8.17, S2.8.20; units 9, 10, 13, 14). S3:
    talks over game plans, gives and takes feedback, settles disagreements and keeps the norms of good conduct
    (S3.8.3, S3.8.4, S3.8.8, S3.8.9; units 8, 11). S4: explains how others and choice affect involvement, examines
    challenges, and uses strategies to manage feelings (S4.8.2, S4.8.4, S4.8.5, S4.8.8; units 12, 13). Checked the
    same way.
  - Grade 8. S1: volleys, dribbles with a tool, shoots, gets open, keeps good form outdoors, and shows dry-land water
    safety skills (S1.8.1, S1.8.13, S1.8.16, S1.8.17, S1.8.18, S1.8.20; units 16, 17, 20, 21), and uses the band's
    skills in modified invasion, net and wall, and striking and fielding games and in a planned routine (units 16 to
    19). S2: picks shots in net games, explains striking and fielding tactics, uses problem-solving methods, tracks a
    chosen fitness test toward a goal, judges technology, and shows outdoor and water safety knowledge (S2.8.4,
    S2.8.5, S2.8.6, S2.8.7, S2.8.10, S2.8.19, S2.8.21, S2.8.22; units 15, 17, 19, 20, 21). S3: explains why an
    activity matters in a culture and links communication and courtesy to leading (S3.8.5, S3.8.10; units 18, 22).
    S4: sets participation goals, weighs chances and obstacles outside class, and reflects on what PE means to them
    (S4.8.6, S4.8.7, S4.8.9; units 15, 22). Checked the same way.
- **Base text.** None. Physical education is Curio Class's own. Cue words, task cards, knowledge cards and case
  cards are ours; the appropriate practice guidelines (2009) and PECAT are read into our words, never quoted. Each
  cultural game or dance is named only after its own source (the community's own organization, a national culture
  body or a public folklife archive) says it is open to be taught (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day": Understanding (a skill or
  concept shown and named in two or three cues, then tried at once, first try watched by the teacher; first teaching
  is always Understanding); Fluency (many tries with feedback on one cue: stations, partner tasks, task cards, no
  lines; Fluency only practices); Application (the skill used in a changing setting: a small-sided game of three to
  four a side, a dance, a circuit, a tactic in play, a planned week); Check; Respond. In grades 6 to 8 Fluency is
  about a third of each unit's teaching days and Application about a third: the span-8 indicators ask for skills in
  drills and in small-sided or modified games, so a unit moves from drill to game inside its days. Game play never
  comes before the skill is taught (lens).
- **The Cues and settings column** holds, for PE, the unit's cues and the settings it moves through (alone, partner,
  drill, small-sided game, routine, course), as the after-core brief heads it.
- **Assessment.** Each unit's first skill day has a baseline watch of the unit's main cue in rotating groups (met,
  partly, not yet); its end-of-unit assessment repeats that cue as the skill task, adds a knowledge task (a short
  written task on a half sheet, five minutes, or said to the teacher by a student who prefers; a student slow to
  write may draw or point) and marks conduct once, against the S3 behavior the unit's Ends names (recorded during
  practice). Growth is the change against the student's own baseline, never a rank. Units of 11 days or fewer end
  with one check day; longer units with two, except unit 15, whose one held check day is the retest, so the teacher sees every student in the skill rotation (six groups of
  five, about four minutes a group while the others work at stations). A one-day check holds one skill task watched
  in the rotation and the written knowledge task taken by all at once; where a unit names a second skill task, it is
  the closure watch of the unit's last Fluency day, carried as a mark. No score comes from a day a skill is first
  taught. Every unit has one respond day after its check (in its days). Each unit ends in its own end-of-unit
  assessment; no course-level check doubles as one.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Each assumed row gives the grade and course of its last use (Seams). A row last used
  the year before (every grade 5 row in grade 6; every grade 6 row in grade 7; every grade 7 row in grade 8) opens as
  a retrieval task inside a day, in the instant activity of the first skill day that needs it. A row last used two
  or more grades back gets a readiness check, in the words of sequence-write.md rule 2, given before the unit opens:
  unit 1's in the last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook (g3-stay-safe-own, g4-object-ideas); unit 14's at the end
  of unit 13 (g5-food-drink); unit 21's at the end of unit 20 (g5-water-skills); unit 22's at the end of unit 21
  (g4-world-activities). Last use counts by grade with one hop (sequence-write.md rule 2): a row a later row assumes
  is used where that later row is taught. So four rows that look two grades back by their own grade are retrieval,
  since the year before used them: g4-pulse-body (grade 5's FITT, technology and food rows; unit 3's first day),
  g4-reasons-strengths (grade 5's goal and review rows; unit 7), g5-conflict-solve (grade 6's welcoming row in unit 4;
  unit 11) and g5-review-experiences (grade 6's self-expression row in unit 6; unit 12) (scope expert read
  2026-11-26, which replaces the frame read of 2026-11-25 on g4-pulse-body). Day 1 of every other unit stays clean for
  the hook and the question. Two grade 6 rows are kept live in grade 7 by a named use, so grade 8 retrieves them:
  g6-catch-form (catching the partner's throw in unit 8 and fielding in unit 11) and g6-rules-safe (the outdoor rules
  in unit 14). The count starts from grade 6, the band's first year, on the 3 to 5 band's rows; a student who joins
  in grade 7 or 8 meets the same retrieval tasks, and the teacher re-teaches a cue on the spot where a student has
  never met it.
- **Space, weather and equipment.** Every unit runs in a gym, on a field or in a cleared room, with a low-equipment
  version (rolled socks or paper balls, milk-jug scoops, tape lines, rope nets between two chairs, pool noodles) and a
  wet-weather version named in the unit plan (bar criterion 5). Class of thirty: stations in groups of four or five,
  small-sided games of three or four a side on several small courts, one item a pair or better. Outdoor days carry a
  water break, shade and sun care; in hot weather the vigorous days move indoors or to the cooler part of the day (the
  unit plan names it; APPROACH.md, "Safe space").
- **Required access.** Three things the low-equipment path cannot replace: a dry, non-slip surface for agility and
  power-jump work (unit 2: a gym floor, carpet tiles or dry grass; with none, the power jump waits for a dry day); real
  life jackets in youth and adult sizes to fit the students for unit 21's fastening and fit task (borrowed or bought;
  pretend fastening is no substitute); and an outdoor route or school
  grounds the teacher has walked for units 14 and 20 (with none, the course runs indoors on a gym map, and the walk
  with pace moves to the hallways at a quiet time). Climbing in unit 20 is only on a wall or bar the school already has,
  with its trained staff; a school with none picks hiking, orienteering and paddling strokes on land. Each unit's last
  column names what it needs, the safe substitute and the purchase if none is available.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30, counting reusable items once, if the school
  has no PE equipment at all (a middle school building does not share the elementary closet): shown in each unit's
  last column. Across the band about $745 (grade 6 about $315, grade 7 about $65, grade 8 about $365). A school with an ordinary PE closet spends little beyond floor-hockey sticks,
  compasses and life jackets. Ceiling (decided, Cory 2026-11-26, Desk q-frames-money-1): about $745 across the band from nothing, as priced
  here. The low-equipment path's listed buys and the Required access items stay provisional until the unit plans
  price them, so the path is not promised at no cost.
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared teacher device is optional: it plays music for dance and circuit days
  (units 2, 6, 13, 14, 18, 22) and may show a skill clip for analysis (units 1, 8). No owned code needs technology:
  S2.8.18 (analyzing a skill) is met by watching a partner with the cue card, and S2.8.19 (judging technology) is met
  by rating printed tool cards that Curio Class writes (invented for practice, no product names), so a paper-path
  student meets every part in full. No student device or tracker is asked for, and no tool collects fitness data
  about a student.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs from printed cue cards, task cards, case and tool cards, the student's own record
  sheet and the teacher's record sheet; no tool is needed, no student account exists, and no tool judges movement,
  ranks students or collects fitness data (APPROACH.md, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Reading load.** Task cards are short and picture-led, at about a grade 5 reading level so PE does not test
  reading; the teacher reads each new card aloud once. Each unit names its movement words (the rows' `words`), taught
  with a picture or demonstration and a sentence frame ("I shift my weight from my ___ foot."). A student slow to say
  or write a cue may show it; the check marks movement and understanding apart, so a language gap is not marked as a
  skill gap.
- **Supports.** Every lesson, closure and check task has an adaptation for disabilities and a range of skill
  (equipment size, distance, speed, rules, roles). A student with a short-term injury or a medical condition (asthma,
  a heart or breathing condition, a neck or back concern, an allergy) follows the nurse's and family's guidance, with
  a moving role for the parts that are fine or a seated helping role (cue watcher, scorekeeper of their own count,
  official). Groups and teams are made by the teacher, quickly and privately. No exercise as punishment, no
  elimination, no human targets, no public team picks, no activity split by gender; competition is small-sided, and
  every small-sided game offers a more and a less competitive court the student chooses (STANDARD.md 4,
  "Competition").
- **Water safety.** Dry land only, for every school: unit 21 teaches the reach and throw rescue, fastening a life
  jacket, cold-water risks and what floating, treading and a stroke are, shown on the floor. In-water tasks belong to a
  school's own aquatics program with a qualified lifeguard on site and the school's swim policy; no student is made to
  enter water (Cory, 2026-11-20).
- **Swaps and leave-outs.** A school or family may swap a named cultural dance or activity (units 6, 13, 22) for one
  whose source says it is open to be taught, or an outdoor activity that does not fit its place (unit 20's teacher
  picks; STANDARD.md 5). A unit left out takes its owned codes off the course page's claim; no other unit claims them.
- **Shorter path.** The 64-session path each year (APPROACH.md, "Pacing": a school that meets twice a week) drops 32
  of the year's 96 days. A year has about 70 teaching days (90 unit days less each unit's check and respond days),
  about 47 of them Fluency and Application, so the path removes about two thirds of the practice days, and the course
  page says so in those words. It drops first the begun-toward days whose owner unit teaches the skill in full a year
  later (unit 7's first serves, strokes and swings; unit 9's first tries at the grade 8 skills; unit 14's walk toward
  S1.8.1), then Fluency and Application days by practice load: units whose rows are knowledge rows taught inside moving
  circuits give up fewer. Every unit keeps at least one Fluency and one Application day, its last Fluency day before
  its check and its last Application day, and practices its main checked cue on at least one kept day (a fold from a
  dropped day where needed); no check, respond or baseline day is dropped, no first-teaching day of a row the year
  owns is dropped (the begun-toward days above are the only Understanding days that go), and both course-level checks
  stay. The scope and sequence gives each year a per-unit table (Understanding, Fluency,
  Application, Check and Respond days; days dropped; days kept) that totals 32 and shows every owned outcome still
  taught and checked. Grade 8, with eight units, has only about 31 droppable days by this count, so its table is
  made first; if a year cannot reach 32, the frame's 72-session path applies to it (For the lead 1).
- **Logic model.** One page a year in the scope and sequence (inputs: 96 sessions of 45 minutes, the cue cards and
  the equipment list; activities: the units; outcomes: the grade outcomes per standard above); the course page says
  the course has not been tried with students and claims no effect on fitness or health (APPROACH.md, "Sources").
- **Sensitive ground and teacher notes.** Every PE teacher page carries health's disclosure steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"), and changing clothes follows the school's
  policy with no one changing in front of others (Cory, 2026-11-21). Middle school adds puberty: a student who needs a
  private moment (a period, a growth spurt that makes a skill feel new, sweat or smell worries) gets a quiet role
  change or a restroom break without a reason asked aloud. No weighing, no body measurements, no body composition, no
  calorie or diet talk; fitness words are habits, never scores; heart counts and fitness results are the student's
  own, never posted, graded or compared.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside human reader is planned (settled 2026-11-14). Units with cultural content (6, 13, 22) name the
  communities whose dances and activities they use and, before the read, the agent studies that community's own
  published sources on the activity and on whether it is open to be taught. Each pick is a candidate until the agent's
  read confirms at the community's own source what it is, that it is open to be taught and that the adaptation used is
  fitting (its spot-check status is in the unit's last column); only then does a unit plan name it. Picks are checked
  against every PE, dance, music and SEL frame of K to 12 so none repeats; the one shared pick, capoeira (dance 6 to 8
  unit 8 in grade 7, then PE unit 22), is named both ways and PE retrieves dance's study (For the lead 3).
  Units with emotion, body, fitness-test or food content (3, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 22) and disability adaptations in
  every unit name their sources in the last column.
- **Safety read.** The safety agent reads, before they go live, every unit with striking tools, sticks, power jumps,
  resistance exercise, outdoor trips or climbing (units 2, 3, 7, 9 for its stick station, 11, 12, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20) and the water unit (21),
  against these free guides, the same ones the K to 2 frame opened on 2026-11-25: the Nova Scotia Department of
  Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades Primary-12" (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf);
  SPHEReS ("School Physical Activity, Health and Education Resource for Safety", https://myspheres.ca/en/), the
  online guidelines for Alberta schools from the University of Alberta's Injury Prevention Centre; and for water CDC's
  "Preventing Drowning" page (https://www.cdc.gov/drowning/prevention/index.html, dated 2025-09-29), with the Palm Beach County Drowning Prevention Coalition's "Reach, Throw, Don't Go" page (https://discover.pbc.gov/drowningprevention/Pages/Reach-Throw-DontGo.aspx, opened 2026-11-26) for the
  reach and throw rescue steps. The safety agent
  reads the sections each unit needs (striking implements, floor hockey, fitness and resistance exercise, orienteering
  and hiking, climbing walls, aquatics). No student sign-off form. Answer keys get the answer-key agent's read.
