## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "In each story, what does the writer let the reader know, and when, and what does that do to us?"
Day 1 opens with Poe's first two paragraphs: why does a declared avenger keep smiling? Students mark the contrast before hearing the compelling question.
The daily evidence table moves from events to craft to comparison. Knowledge builds about concealed information in revenge, wish and pursuit stories.
Day 21's cold analysis explains one structural choice in Saki's "Sredni Vashtar" using explicit detail and inference. Day 18's Connell paragraph supplies the prepared writing record.
Model an evidence chain on day 2, assertion and inference on day 3, and withheld information on day 4. Hand passage selection to partners on days 8 and 13 and individuals on day 17.


Keep all five inventory texts; add only the reserved cold story. Read anchors on their listed days. The independent novel continues beyond day 19; completion is not required.
Measures are not on file; numerical band placement remains unconfirmed. Before reading, introduce setting briefly. During reading, mark Poe's older clauses, chart Jacobs's wishes, distinguish Saki's narrator from Vera's embedded inventions, and track knowledge in Connell. Afterward, reread for evidence. The novel needs chapter logs and recorded supports. Short texts establish routines before the epic; Connell extends stamina.
Mix Tier 2 words with the Tier 3 labels connoisseur, vault, foreshadowing and pacing. Italics mark analytical language rather than textual words. Teach the first twelve words deeply, the others briefly. Introduce Saki's and Connell's sets before reading (Poe's and Jacobs's words follow their reading), verify during, and reuse afterward in the daily task.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | An unsupported claim counts as textual analysis. | Evidence is not linked to the claim. Repair: attach two relevant details and explain each connection. | From memory. |
| B | A character's prediction is an established event. | Attribution has been lost. Repair: label the prediction, then check it against the ending. | From memory. |
| C | Naming a structural device explains its effect. | The causal explanation is missing. Repair: connect a specific ordering choice to a reader expectation. | From memory. |
| D | The first speaker may decide the group's rules alone. | Shared decision-making is missed. Repair: agree the procedure before using it. | From memory. |
| E | Repeating a personal claim propels discussion. | A contribution does not connect to another idea. Repair: pose one linked question and invite a response. | From memory. |
| F | A synthesis may erase disagreement. | Perspective is misrepresented. Repair: name the actual difference before qualifying a view. | From memory. |
| G | A familiar dictionary sense fits every occurrence. | Context is ignored. Repair: test two senses in the surrounding sentence. | From memory. |
| H | Word-family forms are grammatically interchangeable. | Part of speech is insecure. Repair: substitute noun and adjective in contrasting sentence positions. | From memory. |
| I | Any reference source supplies the needed entry. | Reference choice is unexamined. Repair: check the headword before selecting the source. | From memory. |
| J | A first guess needs no verification. | Meaning has not been checked. Repair: compare the guess with an entry and the original sentence. | From memory. |
| K | A new word may contradict its established meaning. | Productive knowledge is insecure. Repair: explain the word plainly before rewriting the text-based sentence. | From memory. |

These are anticipated errors, from memory, not observed prevalence. Each letter has one repair.
