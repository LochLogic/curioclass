# Scope and sequence: English Language Arts Grade 9

Course `english-language-arts-grade-9`, grade 9, draft, 2026-11-15.

The frame's eight units keep their order, owned rows and 148 days. Twelve course-level check and respond days make 160 course days in 32 weeks. This course meets every school day, so course day and school day are identical and `school_day` is blank in days.csv. The CSV is the day map; this page records the reasons for it. Section numbers group at most six days for later planning, not completed section plans. No assessment items, keys or lessons are written here.

The 9 to 10 band standards remain partial in grade 9, exactly as the frame assigns them. Grade 9 teaches every lettered part it starts: discussion 1a to 1d; word strategies 4a to 4d; narrative 3a to 3e; language 1a and 1b, 2a and 2c, 3a, 5a and 5b; argument 1a to 1e; explanation 2a to 2f; evidence writing 9a and 9b. Parent codes in the day table identify these band standards, not completion of their grade 10 extensions. Grade 10 completes cumulative diction/register analysis, multiple viewpoints/plot lines, multiple fully weighed counterclaims, punctuation 2b, and the fuller syntax/style, source-transformation and independent high-band demands. Grade 9's own high-band endpoint remains assessed with supports recorded.

## Reading, writing and recurring time

Short whole stories establish evidence and structural analysis before the supported epic; translated stories consolidate cultural reading between long works. Literary nonfiction then introduces the informational evidence and research cycle. Rhetoric precedes validity; science explanations precede explanatory writing. The whole play and poems finish the year at high-band demand with verse support. This is a rise in the independence and integration required, not a claimed numerical climb between unmeasured texts. Before reading, supply names, necessary context and a purpose; during reading, keep audio beside the actual words and model a difficult sentence; after reading, return to that passage for evidence, discussion and writing. Never replace an anchor with a simpler retelling.

In a 50-minute teaching period, budget about 3 minutes for word/sentence retrieval, 8 minutes for independent reading/log and rotating conferences, 32 minutes for the named reading/writing work including partner talk, and 7 minutes for text-based exit writing and feedback. On dense whole-book/scene days the independent-reading block may move to the adjoining practice day; anchor reading remains in school. On assessment days the assessment has the period, with only the explicitly reserved response time. Oral work runs in small panels, not a procession of whole-class talks that cannot fit. The teacher combines observation with outlines, visual artifacts and peer records; a live or local recorded sample can fill a specific evidence gap, never a second full performance from everyone. Do not claim the teacher heard every parallel talk in full.

Every teaching/respond day has partner talk and a written exit ticket on its named text. A discussion cell with SL.9-10.1a to 1d means prepared evidence, student-set norms/goals/roles, questions linked to larger ideas, drawing others in, and a synthesis of agreement/disagreement with a revised view. Whole-class discussion occurs at least twice per unit: days 6/11, 28/41, 54/58, 68/74, 87/91, 105/107, 121/127 and 137/141. Other named discussions add practice. Sentence work comes from the current text and student drafts. Research runs five days in unit 4 and three days in unit 7, where it feeds the same explanatory product. The four process pieces have a model, plan, complete draft, structured peer response, named teacher annotations, significant-change log and revision/editing. They are due on days 44, 116, 128 and 150, weeks 9, 24, 26 and 30.

Independent reading reserves a long on-topic book, a log and one conference window per unit, recorded on days 19, 46, 60, 80, 92, 112, 127 and 151. Day 19 uses the frame's named Christie offer. The other seven free book offers and their complexity/rights records remain a pre-unit dependency, not invented available copies. A short unit's completion pace cannot be established without those lengths. The later end/check days reuse the conference record; none requests the same child-by-child evidence again within ten days.

The frame has no adaptable base curriculum, so no source-map.csv is needed. All inventory anchors and paired texts have day placements in days.csv, including every scene of the play, all six paired poems/ballad, all four world stories, film/chart/talk, four separate fictional op-eds/letters, the survey and both island-bird prose sets. The inventory's `on file` status is not a checked edition, rights or complexity record: its rights fields and many measures remain blank. Original pieces are still to write and some media still to choose. The unit planner must clear those dependencies before writing its unit. Exact selection boundaries, word counts and film timestamps remain explicitly pending where the inventory has not supplied them; the day map does not certify their eventual reading load.

By anchor inventory rows, informational has 13/23, 56.5 percent, and literary 10/23, 43.5 percent. Fifteen rows are whole works, eight selections/excerpts. Two anchors are long: the epic in large parts and one whole play; the other inventory anchors are short. Paired works and independent books do not enter these counts. The informational comparison across the high school band is reported, not a reason to replace a frame unit; reading in the other subjects is outside this ELA denominator.

## Unit 1. Traps and bargains: four suspense stories

Days 1 to 22, weeks 1 to 5, 22 days. Sections 1.1 to 1.5 occupy days 1 to 6, 7 to 11, 12 to 14, 15 to 19 and 20 to 22. Poe establishes the evidence chart and paragraph; Jacobs makes foreshadowing visible; Saki sharpens the twist; Connell extends pacing over a longer story. Rereading and discussion precede independent transfer, so a structural label always explains a felt effect on the reader. The independent reading/log, word log and discussion norms begin here rather than being assumed.

Readiness: none, every assumed row was taught in grade 8; retrieval on days 1 to 6. Mid-unit assessment: none required for 22 days. End-of-unit assessment: day 20 samples all four discussion parts and all four word-strategy parts plus usable word knowledge; day 21 assesses thorough explicit/inferred evidence, a structural effect, supported band reading and an analytic paragraph on a cold story. Retain routine extended/short writing evidence for W.9-10.10 and the independent-reading record for RL.9-10.10. Day 22 is the single response block. The cold story is read before the paragraph within the period, not assigned unseen at home.

Candidate decisions: keep the hardest-passage reread in four existing day cells, days 5, 8, 13 and 17, about 15 to 20 minutes each alongside evidence/word work, zero added days. Keep Saki's Sredni Vashtar as day 21's one-day cold transfer, subject to its edition/band record. Its child death/cruelty gets a content note. Poe/Connell killings and Connell's racist slurs are flagged in the CSV; no hunter/hunted enactment and no spoken slur.

Washington introduces rhetorical situation and the three appeals before Jefferson and Lincoln provide practice. The Gettysburg Address then becomes the second closely read seminal document. Students compare the two documents' treatment of union after learning how a single move affects an audience. The unit stays short because one owned row carries two closely related reading standards, not a new full process essay.

## Shorter path

For a 35 to 40 minute period, keep the actual text, first teaching, feedback and assessments. Move independent reading/conferences to the designated adjoining practice windows, shorten repeated polishing and have partners prepare evidence before brief whole-class synthesis. Use parallel panels for talks and teacher written annotations for draft feedback. Break a long read across its adjacent scheduled reading days and carry the word/sentence practice with it. A shorter period does not turn an assessment into homework or remove the digital requirements. The exact extended epic selection lengths and original text lengths need a timed read before this can be called a tested fit.

For a year about ten meetings short, retain the canonical 160 day labels in this map and combine the following ten unassessed practice days into their neighbors. These are repeat practice, never first teaching or unique evidence. Move log/conference appointments and every named reread with the folded work; do not drop them. Each combined day replaces some repeated polishing, not an assessed part.
