# Course frame: English Language Arts Grade 9

Course id `english-language-arts-grade-9`, grade 9, draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets every day in order later; this frame names no single day.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8 units, each built on one named set of texts.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 148 in units and 12 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks, five lessons a week of about 50 minutes. Days follow load, not row count: the two long texts (the Odyssey in large parts, Romeo and Juliet read whole) and the unit that starts every year-long routine carry the most days; a unit with one or two rows is short only where its texts are short.
- **Standard.** Common Core ELA, grades 9 to 10 band (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt). Grade 9 is the first year of a two-year band: every row here practises a band standard in part (codes marked ~) and grade 10 owns it. Each row names its grade 9 limit and every lettered part it starts; the Units table says which unit teaches each part, and a part left wholly to grade 10 is named there and in Seams. The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (grades 6 to 12, Gateway 2 and 3) and docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (higher court first, then facts and backed sources, dignity, age) governs every contested claim.
- **Base text.** None. ELA 6 to 12 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Anchor texts are public domain in the United States (published 1930 or earlier, translations included, each checked for its own date), US government works, or original Curio Class texts written to the per-grade text spec; CommonLit is not used. Grades 9 to 12 may anchor on older Reading Room texts (2026-11-12); every anchor not named by the standards is picked for being recognizable and as recent as the licence allows (2026-11-13). Short quoted excerpts of well-known recent books may sit inside a Curio Class lesson as pointers, never as anchors. No student is sent to a library or a paid copy: every anchor has a print copy in the course. The text record (texts.csv) gives each anchor its measure, the four qualitative headings, the task and the reason for its place.
- **Reading level.** Anchors sit in the grades 9 to 10 band (about 1050 to 1335 Lexile, 2012 supplement to Appendix A), most in the lower two thirds, with scaffolding at the high end (RL.9-10.10 and RI.9-10.10, the end-of-grade-9 line). A poem or play has no usable measure and rests on the qualitative read. Supports come before, during and after reading and never replace the text; audio sits beside a long text, never in place of reading on a check.
- **Routines all year.** A written exit question on the day's text (e9-w-analysis-routine), partner talk daily and a whole-class text discussion at least twice a unit (e9-sl-discussion), a unit word list of 10 to 15 words met before, during and after reading (e9-l-word-strategies), and a long on-topic book each unit for independent reading with a log and a conference (e9-rl-range-scaffolded). Each starts in unit 1 and returns in every later unit as review.
- **Writing calendar.** Four process pieces (narrative, unit 2; issue argument, unit 6; explanatory, unit 7; literary argument, unit 8), two short research papers or talks (units 4 and 7), and on-demand analytic paragraphs and short essays every unit. Process pieces run a structured peer response, a named teacher feedback step and a revision log. By count of longer pieces: argument 2, explanatory 3 (with the research papers), narrative 1; across grades 9 and 10 the shares come to about 45, 36 and 18 percent, near the 40, 40, 20 planning guidance (ela.csv e10-w-argument).
- **Style manual.** One for all four high school years: a short Curio Class style sheet that follows the MLA core-elements format for citations (the format, never the handbook's text), with the US Government Publishing Office Style Manual (a US government work) for spelling, capitals and numbers. Grade 9 starts it in unit 4 (L.9-10.3a).
- **Devices.** Paper path for every task. W.9-10.6 (typed, published and updated) is met on a shared device or in a lab session in the school day; a class wall does not meet it, and the plan records the standard unfinished where no device route exists. No student account anywhere.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent read. Units needing a community or qualified reader: unit 4 (a Native reader for the boarding-school memoir), unit 8 (a reader with school counselling background for the suicides in Romeo and Juliet). Who, and any pay, is Cory's.
