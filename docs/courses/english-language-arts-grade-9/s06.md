**C115: Keep the early bell.** Curio Class, 2026, ours. Fictional, invented for practice. Number these paragraphs; keep the entire piece out of teaching and vocabulary practice.

1. A school considering a later bell should keep its existing schedule. Supporters promise better sleep every day of the week, but their survey concerns school nights. I oppose changing the bell. Their promise and the timetable decision need separate tests, however: showing that a promise exceeds the evidence does not prove that the policy has no value.
2. I asked five passengers on my bus whether they liked our morning route. All preferred it. That settles what every student in the school wants. After our route moved earlier last autumn, our team won its next tournament. The earlier bell must have produced that victory. Why risk undoing a winning arrangement?
3. There are only two choices: keep every part of the timetable exactly as it is, or cancel every afternoon activity. Any proposal between these extremes is just a disguise for cancellation. The student who proposed a trial is always untidy, so her timetable evidence deserves no attention.
4. The national student sleep survey assigned students randomly to different school start times. Its findings therefore show what a later bell will do at this school. The campaign leaflet also promises longer weekend sleep. Yet the survey question concerns an average school night, not weekends. Remove that weekend guarantee unless evidence about weekends supports it, even if officials eventually choose a later bell.
