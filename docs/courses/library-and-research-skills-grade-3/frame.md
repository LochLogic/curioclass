# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, grade 3

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-3` (catalog id `library-3`), grade 3. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the library pilot frame (kindergarten); the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one year of library and research skills, each unit one full inquiry cycle (ask, find, judge, use,
  share, reflect) on a small question, in the order a grade 3 class needs the moves: turning a topic into research
  questions first, then searching for them safely by key word, then telling kinds of sources apart and making notes
  in boxes, then researching as a team and making, crediting and giving feedback on a product.
- **Days.** 30: 26 in units, 2 course-level check days (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond days (1 after
  each check). One session of about 35 minutes, one session a week, for 30 weeks (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: the
  3 to 5 default of 30 sessions of 35 minutes, about 17.5 hours a year, at the one-a-week end of the settled "one or
  two sessions a week"). The 35 minutes hold getting out notebooks and source sets, the session, putting books and
  cards back and collecting work; a lesson plan budgets about 3 minutes at each end for that, so about 29 working
  minutes, and every assessment form below is costed against those 29 minutes for a class of 30. One adult runs the
  room (the classroom teacher or a librarian). Nine grade 3 rows fit 26 unit days by load: unit 1 carries one owned
  row and gets 5 days; unit 2 two rows (one owned, one bringing in the search plan model) and gets 7; unit 3 two
  carried rows done with help and gets 6; unit 4 four rows (three owner rows holding five owned ids) and gets 8, since it also holds the
  year's product, its draft, the gallery feedback and the revision. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit
  assessment (no unit is over 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment); units 1 to 3 also hold their respond day, and unit
  4's respond step is its revision day, with its regrouped reteach in the year-end check's respond day (split below). These are course days: with
  30 days over 30 weeks, course day n falls in week n of docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, and the scope and sequence places
  them on school days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day):
  Understanding is the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through the move on an invented case, holding the unit's
  skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, varied tries of the same move on new cases, with feedback: sort six
  questions, pull key words from three questions, sort a source set, fix two source lists); Application is the
  inquiry day (the class runs part of the cycle on its host question: question, search, judge, note, make, share).
  Fluency is modest in grade 3: one or two practice days in units 1 to 3, a practice half-day in unit 4, plus the five-minute
  opening retrieval on later days. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the compelling question.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words
  only, data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone).
  Ownership rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone.
  Grade 3 owns seven ids (AASL.I.A.1; AASL.VI.A.1; AASL.I.B.3; AASL.I.C.1 and AASL.I.C.2; AASL.III.D.1 and
  AASL.II.A.1) and carries seven as `~`, each named in its unit's What students learn with its owner. Skills: the
  nine progression rows, each in one unit. Check set: the Empire State Information Fluency Continuum, 3 to 5 band
  file, grade 3 column and its bold priority skills (read cell by cell for this frame, later.csv L103; result in the
  Check set read bullet); the "Check set adds" cells name what each unit plan should weigh from it. Values: K to 5
  case sets stay neutral (no politics, elections, religion, identity groups or conflict; Cory, 2026-11-21).
- **Base text.** None. Every day is Curio Class's own. Curio Class writes: a short background passage and a
  six-source set for each unit's stand-alone question (passages, a photo with caption, a log, a diagram), an invented
  printed catalog (title, author and key word pages) and an invented results page, a printed map of an invented
  library's main areas, the question sort cards, the scenario cards for safe searching, the note box sheet, the team
  sheet, the source list model card, the gallery note slips, one skill card a unit and the research notebook pages. No
  day needs a trade book, a paid source, a library visit or a database (no library access is assumed; Cory,
  2026-11-11).
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from another grade 3 course, named by its row id for the
  teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question with a closed
  source set of Curio Class passages and pictures) so the unit runs when no core task is in step or at home
  (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence records both. A host is used for its question only when its topic keeps the
  K to 5 neutral rule; otherwise the unit borrows only the skill and runs on its stand-alone case (unit 3, below).
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question and closes with the share step and the
  self-check: each student rates their own use of the skill card's steps on a three-face scale, names one step they
  did well and one thing to do differently next time (APPROACH.md, the form of daily evidence; this answers the
  continuum's grade 3 reflect skill, own strengths and a goal). The lens's six steps (ask, find, judge, use, share,
  reflect) are now named on the cycle card in grade 3: ask is the research question (unit 1), find the key-word
  search (unit 2), judge the source's kind, maker and a second-source check (unit 3), use the notes in boxes (unit 3)
  and the product (unit 4), share the gallery walk (unit 4).
- **Evidence.** Daily: an exit ticket that asks the student to do the unit's move once on a new case (about 5
  minutes, all at once), with error letters for the usual slips; on inquiry days, the working notes in the research
  notebook (APPROACH.md). End-of-unit assessment: a written sitting on a case not seen before, all 30 at once, scored
  on a short teacher-judged rubric plus a few one-answer items checked on the page, in about 20 to 25 minutes; the
  team skill in unit 4 is observed at team tables instead (about 3 minutes a team of four or five, about 21 minutes
  for seven teams, on its own day, after a practice meeting the day before). Each unit names its form and total below; no unit assessment or course-level
  check spills into its respond day. It is scored on a day after the move was practiced, never on a show-me day. The
  respond day's groups come from the class record sheet by the rubric step missed (APPROACH.md, unit field 7).
- **Reading level.** Source passages at about grade 3 level, 150 to 300 words, with a read-aloud copy on the teacher
  page and a shorter set for students who read below it (APPROACH.md, section field 9). Students write short notes,
  questions and lists; the product's writing load stays within ELA grade 3's informative writing.
- **Paper path.** The whole course runs on paper: printed source sets, the invented catalog and results pages,
  scenario cards, notebooks and the source list model. No day needs a device, an internet connection or a student
  account.
- **Devices.** None per student, and no owned or carried code needs technology. Where a school has one shared device
  or a projector, unit 2 may show the invented catalog on it and run the turn routine; the printed catalog and a
  pretend device card do the same job in full, so a paper-path student meets AASL.VI.A.1 (the stop rule, turns,
  never typing a name, school, place or photo, care of books and devices) completely on scenario cards.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond books, paper, cards and pencils, no field work and no trip, so no
  laboratory or field guide applies. The screen-safety side is the stop rule and the private-information rule,
  checked by the sensitivity read (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, items 2 and 3).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned
  (Cory, 2026-11-14). No unit carries cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content: the case sets are
  spiders, the moon, storms and weather records, and pond life. What the agent studies first: APPROACH.md "Sensitive
  ground" items 1 (neutral K to 5 case sets; unit 3 does not borrow social studies unit 4's Native nations content),
  2 (the stop rule, unit 2), 3 (privacy, unit 2), 4 (honest use without shaming, unit 4's source list) and 5 (whose
  voices a source set shows, C12); and, for unit 2, the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children's free
  grades 3 to 5 presentation "Being a Good Digital Citizen: Grades 3-5", listed at
  https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/resources (listing read 2026-11-25; the kit was not opened, and a download may
  need a sign-in). No organization's name goes on a student, teacher or family page.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30: unit 1 about $6
  (background passage, question sort cards, three exit tickets), unit 2 about $9 (the printed catalog in pairs,
  results page, library map, scenario cards reused all year), unit 3 about $8 (the source set in pairs, note box
  sheets), unit 4 about $10 (team source sets, team sheets, gallery slips, product paper); about $33 a year. A
  research notebook is any folded stack of paper.
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The 3 to 5 band file's grade 3 column and priority boxes, every indicator
  row (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-3-5\esifc-priority-3-5.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, nothing copied). Met by the rows: questions formed with guidance (g3-research-questions); one or two
  key words and a catalog search by author, title and key word with help (g3-key-words); simple note-making in a
  graphic organizer and the main idea with details (g3-notes-in-boxes); a simple author and title source list
  (g3-source-list, which adds year and kind, the grade 4 cell, one year early); choosing a product format and
  presenting main points clearly (g3-make-product); respect for and response to others' ideas (g3-gallery-feedback);
  contributing to group talk (g3-research-team); privacy and keeping to safe sites (g3-safe-searching). Differences,
  each recorded in its row note in data/progressions/library.csv: a background read from a teacher-given source before the questions
  (g3-research-questions note); the library's main areas (g3-key-words note); checking a fact stated as plain truth in
  a second source, since nonfiction can carry opinion (g3-source-kinds-and-makers note); saying whether the research
  answered the question (g3-make-product note); keeping a school password to oneself (g3-safe-searching note; how
  passwords work is computer science grade 4, g4-authn-authz), and telling an easily guessed invented password from
  a harder one on a scenario card (the same cell names strong passwords); telling a checkable statement from an
  opinion in a nonfiction account, with help (the grade 3 point-of-view cell; g3-source-kinds-and-makers note). Each
  is recorded in its row note in data/progressions/library.csv. Met without a row change: own strengths and a goal (the
  self-check). Left out: social and civic issue talk and own assumptions about community issues (K to 5 case sets stay
  neutral; social studies grade 3 owns civic talk); reading choice, story elements and illustrations (ELA, STANDARD.md
  section 4); media accuracy beyond the second-source check (unit 3's caption and photo items cover it on paper);
  mean messages online (health grade 3, g3-v-bullying, and grade 5, g5-s-online-safety); design thinking (science,
  STANDARD.md section 4); the SEL dispositions (SEL). Beyond the band file: primary and secondary sources with help
  (the grade 4 bold cell, one year early, done with help) and sorting questions a source cannot answer (the grade 5
  bold cell, begun with help in unit 1). No row is two or more grades from its cell, except the question sort, which
  is begun here with help and owned in grade 5 (g5-research-or-guess), as the row says.
