# Scope and sequence: Library and Research Skills, grade 3

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-3`, grade 3, draft 2026-11-26. The days are in
data/plans/library-and-research-skills-grade-3/days.csv; this page holds the judgment.

## How the year sits

- **30 course days**, one session of about 35 minutes (about 29 working minutes) a week for 30 weeks, as the frame
  sets: 26 unit days (5, 7, 6, 8), two course-level check days and two respond days. Base text: none; every day is
  Curio Class's own, on paper, with no library access, device or account needed.
- **school_day = 5 x course day**, so course day n falls in week n ((school_day - 1) // 5 + 1 = n), the last day of
  that week. A school may move the session to any day of the week; the week is what counts.
- **Calendar.** Library is a special (CALENDAR.md rule 1, lead calendar pass 2026-11-26), so its checks do not count
  in the K to 5 stagger. Even so, the mid-year check (week 13) and the year-end check (week 29) miss every grade 3
  core check week (ELA 16, 32; math 12, 23, 31; science 9, 20, 30; social studies 14, 25, 28). Week 29 also holds
  health grade 3's year-end check; health counts in the stagger and library does not, so this is no break.
- **Kinds** in library's words: Understanding is the show-me day, Fluency the practice day, Application the inquiry
  day, Check the assessment day, Respond the respond day. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the
  compelling question, and nothing else is first taught on a practice day: where the frame's practice day lists a
  move (the look-it-up sort in unit 1, the library map and the turn routine in unit 2, the checkable statement and
  opinion in unit 3), a show-me day before it models the move.
- **Daily evidence:** an exit ticket doing the move once on a new case (about 5 minutes); working notes on inquiry
  days; one self-check per unit after the share (three-face scale, one step done well, one thing to change).
- **Readiness and retrieval (rule 2).** Every row the course assumes is a library grade 2 row, the year before
  (g2-question-to-research, g2-catalog-lookup, g2-kinds-of-sources, g2-who-made-it, g2-two-books-differ,
  g2-fact-sort, g2-credit-line, g2-research-share, g2-group-research). Each opens as a retrieval task inside a day,
  never on a unit's day 1; no unit needs a readiness check, so none is placed. Rows used earlier this year come back
  as retrieval too (the `retrieves` column): the search plan and key words in units 3 and 4, notes in boxes in unit
  4, research questions in unit 3's inquiry.
- **Sources not loaded (later.csv L149).** Grade 3 leans on none of the online-reasoning lessons or the web-literacy
  book (6 to 12 partial bases). The check-set cells the Candidate days use (days 3, 7, 10, 16, 17, 18, 19, 24, 25)
  come from the continuum's 3 to 5 band file: the file is on disk and was read by the frame (2026-11-25); its store
  entry waits on L149. No day leans on the free grades 3 to 5 digital-citizenship presentation the frame names; it is
  a sensitivity-read source only (browser check: its download needs a sign-in). The password cards follow current
  federal guidance, which puts length first (a public guidance page opened by the expert read, 2026-11-26). No
  source-map.csv is written (waits on L149).
- **Host tasks.** Each unit names an in-step grade 3 host and a stand-alone question with a closed Curio Class source
  set (below and in each unit's notes).

## Year 3 (the only year): units

### Unit 1. Questions worth researching (days 1 to 5, weeks 1 to 5)

*What do we want to find out, and which questions can research answer?* Owned: AASL.I.A.1. Begun with help:
AASL.IV.A.1 (owned in grade 5). One owned row, so five days.

Arc: the show-me models the whole move on the teacher's own topic, including the three-way look-it-up sort, so the
practice day (2) only practices; the inquiry day (3) runs the cycle on the class topic from a background read to an
answer found and said back; the end-of-unit sitting (4) comes after practice and inquiry; day 5 is the respond day.
Day 3 adds the judge step the codex read asked for: a one-minute maker-and-purpose look at the background passage
(grade 2's move).

- Retrieval: day 2, one question-word question about a picture (g2-question-to-research). Day 3 also brings back
  g2-who-made-it.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 4, written sitting about 20 minutes, a new topic (how camels live in hot deserts; not birds' nests, which library grade 1 unit 3 asked): three research
  questions labeled thin or thick, one circled with its reason, four sort items. Response time: day 5 (pair shares
  about ten minutes, then the regrouped reteach).
- Host: social studies grade 3 unit 1 (ss3-inquiry-questions), the same weeks 1 to 5 in calendar.csv: in step. ELA's
  e3-short-research comes later (weeks 8 to 16), so unit 1 teaches research questions itself. Stand-alone: "How do
  spiders catch their food?" Spider pictures (days 1 and 3) show webs and silk, no prey being eaten.
- Candidates: the background cell, **kept** at day 3's opening (about 5 minutes, no day added); the predict cell,
  **kept** as one line on the question sheet on day 3 (about 2 minutes).

### Unit 2. Searching safely (days 6 to 12, weeks 6 to 12)

*How do we find a source for our question without getting lost or unsafe?* Owned: AASL.VI.A.1. Begun: AASL.IV.B.1
(owned in grade 4). Two rows, seven days: two show-me days and two practice days, one of each per row, so each
row's load gets a model and a practice before the inquiry.

Arc: safe searching comes first (day 6) so the stop rule is in place before any search; day 7 models key words, the
search plan (the new model) and the library map; day 8 practices the catalog search, day 9 the scenario cards and
turns; day 10's inquiry completes the cycle (search, find, read the found page, maker and purpose, a one-line answer
with its credit line, partner share, self-check); day 11 is the end-of-unit sitting; day 12 the respond day.

- Retrieval: day 7, find one book from a printed title entry and say its shelf label (g2-catalog-lookup). Day 10
  brings back g2-who-made-it and g2-credit-line.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 11, written sitting about 26 minutes: key words, a synonym and a filled search plan on
  a new catalog page whose "where to look" line names two places (the catalog and one other kind, the reference
  shelf on the map); one map item, "which area holds this book?"; four scenario cards, one each on the stop rule,
  private information, the two invented passwords, and turns and care of a shared device. Response time: day 12.
- Sensitivity: the stop rule and privacy on days 6, 9, 10 and 11 (teacher page: close it, the child tells, no blame;
  health's steps if a child tells something worrying); no personal details typed or written; no real password. The
  password pair (days 6, 9, 11): the harder invented password is a long string of four unrelated words, the easily
  guessed one a name or a short word; no symbol-swap example.
- Accuracy: the moon source set says the far side gets as much sunlight as the near side; it is the side always
  turned away from Earth, not a "dark side" (day 10).
- Host: ELA grade 3 unit 2's frogs research (e3-short-research, weeks 8 to 16) or science grade 3 unit 2's magnets
  question (weeks 5 to 9), both in step. Stand-alone: "What is on the far side of the moon?"
- Candidates: teacher-chosen lists over free searching, **kept** as the teacher page's reason for the closed list on
  day 10 (no time); nonfiction at a readable level, **kept** as a "can I read it?" tick beside "what came back" on
  days 7 and 10 (about 1 minute). Not a new line in the search plan: the model goes to grade 4 with its five lines.

### Unit 3. Sources and notes (days 15 to 20, weeks 15 to 20)

*Which sources tell us about stormy weather, and what do we keep from them?* No owned id. Carried with help:
AASL.IV.B.3 and AASL.IV.A.3 (grade 5), AASL.IV.B.4 (grade 4). Two carried rows, six days.

Arc: day 15 models source kinds, maker and purpose; day 16 models notes in boxes (about 10 minutes) and then, in a
think-aloud (about 7 minutes), a checkable statement beside an opinion and the second-source check, so day 17
practices them rather than teaching them; day 17 practices all of it (fact-sort retrieval 3, six-source sort with the
caption card 8, two accounts 6, second-source check 3, notes from one passage 7, the notes collected as the day's
exit evidence: about 27 minutes); day 18's inquiry chooses sources, takes notes with credit lines, runs a short
search plan as review and shares a brief answer; day 19 is the end-of-unit sitting; day 20 the respond day.

- Retrieval: day 16, the kind of source for two questions and the maker of one page (g2-kinds-of-sources,
  g2-who-made-it), and two books that differ as the second-source think-aloud's opening question
  (g2-two-books-differ); day 17, four fact cards into two boxes (g2-fact-sort). Day 18 brings back the search plan
  (g3-key-words) and research questions.
- End-of-unit assessment: day 19, written sitting about 27 minutes on a new pond-life set (no source shared with
  unit 4's team set): four clear-cut sources (a photo the maker took, a log, a letter, a book passage) labeled with
  maker and purpose, one claim checked in a second source, "circle the two sources you would use for this question
  and say why each earns its place", notes from one passage into two question boxes with one "why this box" line.
  The diagram and the captioned photo, which can fairly be read either way, stay in day 17's practice sort,
  discussed and not marked. Response time: day 20.
- Leans: social studies grade 3 unit 4 (ss3-primary-secondary) runs weeks 15 to 18, the same weeks; the unit
  borrows only the idea, on weather cases (K to 5 neutral rule), and either course second opens with retrieval.
  ELA's e3-main-idea is weeks 1 to 8, before; day 16 still names the main idea and key detail itself.
- Host, in step: science grade 3 unit 4, "Surviving where you live" (s3-habitat-survival, weeks 15 to 20), needs and
  habitats only, no hunt or predation picture. Later use: s3-climates (science unit 6, weeks 25 to 30). Stand-alone:
  "What happens in a thunderstorm?" Storms kept to how they form and staying safe indoors; storm safety follows the
  weather service's indoor rule ("when thunder roars, go indoors"), checked at the days (browser check).
- Candidates: evidence that answers the question with main ideas and details, **kept** on day 16 (a note goes in only
  if it answers its box's question, about 2 minutes); asking questions of the text, **kept** as one "question I still
  have" line under each box on day 18 (about 2 minutes, feeding unit 4's answered-or-not line); media accuracy,
  **kept** as a photo-and-caption card in day 17's sort and one item in day 19's sitting (about 2 minutes each).

### Unit 4. Research teams (days 21 to 28, weeks 21 to 28)

*What can our team find out about pond life, and how do we share it well?* Owned: AASL.III.D.1, AASL.II.A.1,
AASL.I.B.3, AASL.I.C.1, AASL.I.C.2. Review of AASL.VI.B.2 (owned in grade 2); begun: AASL.I.D.4 (grade 5). Four rows,
eight days, the year's heaviest unit.

Arc: day 21 models the team meeting; day 22 finds facts on subquestions and ends with an unscored practice meeting;
day 23 observes and scores the team skill; day 24 models a product from notes and the source list, with practice
fixing two lists; day 25 drafts the product; day 26 is the gallery walk (model, pair practice, independent notes on
one assigned product, self-check); day 27 revises from the notes; day 28 is the written sitting.

- Retrieval: day 22, what each of three sample team members added (g2-group-research); day 23, a credit line for one
  cover (g2-credit-line) and saying back one sample poster (g2-research-share). The frame puts the day 23 items on
  the observed-meeting day: they run as part one of the waiting teams' card while the teacher sits with one team
  (about 5 minutes); part two is "finish your subquestion notes from day 22 and star the one fact you will report,
  with its source" (about 10 minutes), which readies day 24's product, so each team has work for its roughly 18
  minutes of waiting. Day 22 also brings back notes in boxes and the search plan.
- Team turn: day 21's meeting opens with SEL's four talk turn steps in SEL's words (listen, say back, ask a
  question, add your idea); library adds "report one finding, name what a teammate added, the recorder writes it".
  Where the school runs SEL it is a reminder; where it does not, this day teaches it. No time added.
- End-of-unit assessment, in three parts inside the unit's days: day 23, the team skill observed (each student
  reports a finding and names what a teammate added, two results per student, about 21 minutes); the revised product
  handed in on day 27 and scored on its rubric; day 28, the written sitting, about 22 minutes (a source list for
  three covers, about 8 minutes; one one-answer item, "A classmate copied a sentence from a book into their poster.
  What should they do?", answered put it in their own words and credit it, about 2 minutes, so ~AASL.VI.B.2's
  "respect their rights" is asked; two gallery notes on a new product, about 12 minutes). Day 28 reuses the day 23 record and does not
  observe again. Response time: the year-end check's respond day (day 30), as the frame splits it; day 27 is the
  respond step on the gallery notes.
- Leans: ELA's e3-discussion and e3-writing-process are in ELA unit 1 (weeks 1 to 8), before; e3-speaking-reports is
  ELA unit 3 (weeks 17 to 24), beside. Days 21 and 26 model the team turn and the two notes themselves; the spoken
  report stays ELA's.
- Host: ELA grade 3 unit 3's speaking reports (weeks 17 to 24) or unit 4's freshwater research (weeks 24 to 32).
  Stand-alone: "What lives in a pond, and how do they depend on each other?"
- Sensitivity: honest use without shaming (day 25: a copied sentence is fixed privately); gallery notes kind and read
  by the teacher before day 27; teams mixed by the teacher, never by background; no predation pictures, and the
  stand-alone question's "depend on each other" is shown as shelter, plants and clean water, with no eating pictures
  (day 22).
- Candidates: main idea with details and the answered-or-not conclusion, **kept** in the product rubric's
  answered-or-not line (no time); presenting so main points are clear, **kept** as an unscored "is my main point easy
  to find?" line on day 25 (about 1 minute); what makes a good product, **kept** as the rubric read beside a sample on
  day 24 before drafting (about 4 minutes).

## Shorter path

- **A shorter session (30 minutes, about 26 working minutes with 2 minutes at each end).** Teaching days cut the
  opening retrieval and the exit ticket to about 3 minutes each (day 16: 3, 10, 7, 3; day 17, which has no separate
  exit ticket: 3, 7, 5, 3, 7 = 25). Day 26 runs 5, 4, 12, 5 (pair practice on one note pair; the self-check's
  written line drops, the faces stay). Day 30 runs 10, 11, 5. Assessment days skip both openers: days 4, 13 and 28
  (20 to 22 minutes) and the day 23 observation (21 minutes) fit as they stand; day 11 (about 26 minutes) and day 29
  hand out the catalog page before the session; day 19 and day 29 each drop one source label (about 25 minutes).
- **A year about 10 school days short.** For a once-a-week course that is about 2 lost sessions (the lead's count,
  DESK-ANSWERS "arts scope lead calls"). Two fold-ins cover them; nothing assessed and no owned part is cut:
  1. Day 2 (unit 1 practice) into day 3. The g2-question-to-research retrieval becomes the first of the student's
     three questions (written with a question word); the three questions are then sorted both ways on the same
     sheet (thin or thick, then know it, look it up or nobody can, about 3 minutes); the say-back stays. Day 3's
     budget: background 5, judge 1, predict 1, three questions 5, both sorts 3, pick and find 7, say-back 3,
     self-check 3 = 28 minutes. Day 1 is unchanged.
  2. Day 12 (unit 2 respond) into day 14: unit 2's regrouped reteach joins the mid-year respond day, whose groups
     already come by name from day 11's record sheet.
  3. Only if a third session is lost, day 9 (unit 2 scenario-card practice): the turn routine is practiced live on
     day 10, where pairs take turns at the closed catalog by the routine; day 8's exit ticket becomes one scenario
     card; day 6 keeps its own exit ticket.
  The check weeks move with the school's own calendar.
- **No other reduced path is named in the frame.** A school meeting twice a week finishes in about 15 weeks; that is
  a faster pace, not a shorter path, and its checks keep their place after units 2 and 4.
