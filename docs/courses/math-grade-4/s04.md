## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can a decimal and a fraction name the same number?"

**Hook, day 45:** Display equal squares showing one tenth and ten hundredths. Ask whether they cover equal amounts, then pose the compelling question. Use five activity minutes; field 10 names the adapted source.
**Supporting questions:** How can different-sized parts be joined (day 46)? What does a decimal name (day 49)? Why does another digit sometimes leave value unchanged (day 50)?
**Closing claim:** I can rename, locate and compare fractions and decimals using one whole. Grid Sums (day 46), Meter Points (day 51) and Mixed Names (day 53) prepare day 54's three items.
**Teaching:** Model subdivision on day 45 and sums, including totals beyond one, on day 46. Introduce decimal names through money on day 48, then grids on day 49. Model comparison through fractions on day 50. Step back for students' representations on days 51 and 53.


**Practices:** Day 51's Meter Points develops MP6: label zero, one meter, equal intervals and the shared point for 0.36 meter and 36/100 meter. Day 53's Mixed Names develops MP7: connect 0.5 and 0.50 to one point through five tenths and fifty hundredths.
**Fluency:** Owned standards set none. Days 47 and 52 consolidate taught sums and comparisons through untimed mixed practice, explanations and feedback.
**Balance:** Five understanding, two fluency and two application days give 300, 120 and 120 minutes. Major work receives 495 of 540 instructional minutes, 91.7 percent: nine lessons at 55 minutes. Exclude assessment and response. All nine instructional lessons and this unit center major work. Day 48's supporting money context practices fraction equivalence.
**Limits:** Tenths and hundredths only for new work. No decimal computation, general unlike-denominator arithmetic or required place-value comparison algorithm. Earlier fraction standards retain their unit 2 and 3 coverage; day 54 samples their use here.
**Carried:** Keep unit 3's number line; retire its strips, tapes and plot. Restore unit 2's area model on day 45, tied to equivalent fraction equations; tie the line to sums on day 46 and the chart to decimal names on day 49. Students explain each connection through day 51. Withdraw supplied grids on day 50, partitioned lines on day 52 and filled charts on day 53; blank paper and self-drawn models remain. Restore models for day 55 response.

**Scoring:** Score only after day 55 revision, on a later day than production. Keep separate component cells for each requested part, marked 0 for missing or incorrect, 1 for partial evidence and 2 for complete correct evidence. Item 1's extended explanation requires equal wholes, tenfold subdivision in both directions, preserved amount, joining and removal. Meets: "The grids use equal wholes. Each tenth becomes ten hundredths, so 9/10 = 90/100 without changing the shaded amount. Joining 90 and 26 hundredths gives 116/100 = 1 16/100. Regrouping ten hundredths per tenth preserves the amount: 60/100 = 6/10. Removing thirteen hundredths leaves 47/100; 60/100 - 13/100 = 47/100." Does not: "Change the denominator only" (A). Item 2 requires both notation directions, a length unit and a point consistent with the labeled unit interval. Meets: "1.43 meters is 143/100 meters, three hundredth intervals beyond 1.4 on the meter line. 17/10 meters is 1.7 meters, at the seventh tenth interval after one meter." Does not: "1.43 = 143/10" (E). Item 3 requires correct symbols, a brief model reason and rejection of an unsupported physical-amount comparison. Meets: "On equal-whole grids, 0.7 > 0.68 because 70 > 68 hundredths; 0.2 = 0.20 because both shade twenty hundredths; 0.46 < 0.49 because 46 < 49 hundredths. Unknown sheet areas prevent comparing the physical shaded amounts." Does not: "0.68 is larger because it has more digits" (G). Only item 1 needs an extended explanation; others accept labels and brief reasons.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Only the denominator changes when a fraction is renamed. | Equivalent names must preserve value. Repair: subdivide each tenth into ten equal cells and count the new parts. | From memory. |
| B | Denominators are added when fractions are joined. | Part size is treated as a count. Repair: rename tenths as hundredths before joining equal-sized parts. | From memory. |
| C | A completed whole disappears from a sum greater than one. | Regrouping loses value. Repair: enclose each full hundred-cell grid before counting remaining cells. | From memory. |
| D | A coin is counted as one cent regardless of its label. | Number of coins replaces coin value. Repair: exchange each labeled coin for its value in drawn pennies. | From memory. |
| E | A decimal names the wrong fractional unit. | Place labels are detached from part size. Repair: match each written place to a labeled tenth or hundredth on a grid. | From memory. |
| F | The starting tick is counted as the first interval. | Marks are counted instead of lengths. Repair: trace and count spaces from the labeled zero. | From memory. |
| G | A longer decimal is judged larger from its digit string. | Whole-number comparison overrides fractional size. Repair: rename both numbers in hundredths of the same whole. | From memory. |
| H | Decimal sizes alone determine physical amounts from unknown wholes. | The reference whole is ignored. Repair: shade 0.65 of a 100-cell rectangle and 0.55 of a 200-cell rectangle, using equal-sized cells: 65 < 110 shaded cells. Swap whole sizes: 130 > 55 shaded cells. Ask what information is missing when whole sizes are unknown. | From memory. |

Each letter has one repair. Use the described error, not merely a wrong total, to assign it; investigate other answers individually.
