## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we compare amounts by multiplying, and convert between units?"

**Hook, day 77:** Invented for practice: Show four counters beside twelve. Ask two ways to compare them, then pose the compelling question. Use five launch minutes to teach "times as many" beside "how many more," with matching tapes.
**Supporting questions:** What is unknown (day 79)? Why does a smaller unit need a larger count (day 83)? Which unit makes calculation possible (day 89)?
**Closing claim:** I can translate comparisons, solve each unknown and explain conversions with equations and scaled diagrams. Missing Amounts (day 79) and Measurement Records (days 86 to 91) prepare the checks on days 93 and 94.
**Teaching:** Model quantities beside equations on day 78 and conversions on days 83, 85, 87 and 88. Compare strategies on day 81; step back for independent applications on days 89 to 92.


**Practices:** Day 79's Missing Amounts develops MP4 through tapes and equations for three unknown positions. Day 86's Bottle Comparisons develops MP2: distinguish the multiplier from the measured amount and preserve units through conversion.
**Fluency:** Maintain 4.NBT.B.4 in five-minute warm-ups three meetings weekly, with grade 3 facts on two. Fractions or decimals share that time twice weekly. Use feedback and inverse checks without speed scores.
**Balance:** Keep eight understanding and nine application days, 480 and 540 minutes, with no dedicated fluency day. The 85 warm-up minutes are embedded. Excluding days 93 to 95, reserve 710 of 960 minutes for major work, 74.0 percent: 45 on day 77, 55 daily on days 78 to 82, 30 on day 83 and 40 daily on days 84 to 92. All sixteen included lessons contain major tasks; this is one mixed unit. Supporting measurement practises comparison, algorithms and fractions through those named tasks and exits.
**Limits:** Conversions run larger to smaller within one system. Whole-number comparison factors only; known facts, place value and shown break-apart methods precede unit 7 multiplication. Compute decimal prices in cents. Fractions keep grade 4 denominators and like-denominator addition. Begin two-step 4.OA.A.3 without remainders; unit 8 completes it and owns rectangle formulas. Other earlier codes maintain prior learning.
**Carried:** Keep unit 5's number line and unit 3's tape. Tie tapes to equations on day 78; replace counted marks with labelled amounts on day 80 and withdraw supplied tapes on day 81. ## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Interpret "times as many" as a fixed added amount. | The multiplier is treated as a difference. Repair: repeat the entire reference tape the stated number of times. | OA Progression, field 10. |
| B | Reverse the reference amount and the compared amount. | The larger quantity is treated as the repeated unit. Repair: label the single reference tape before drawing its copies. | From memory. |
| C | Give a measurement unit to the multiplier. | The number of copies is confused with the measured amount. Repair: label the repeated amounts with units and label only their count as the multiplier. | From memory. |
| D | Keep the numerical count unchanged when renaming a larger unit in smaller units. | Unit size is detached from the count. Repair: cover one larger unit with the correct number of smaller units, then repeat. | From memory. |
| E | Assume every unit conversion uses a factor of 100. | A metric relationship is overgeneralized. Repair: build one correct unit pair and use its actual factor. | From memory. |
