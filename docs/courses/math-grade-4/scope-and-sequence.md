# Scope and sequence: Math Grade 4

Course `math-grade-4`, grade 4, draft, 2026-11-15.

The nine units retain the frame's order, ownership and 145 days. Fifteen course-level days bring the year to 160 days of about 60 minutes. Math meets daily, so course day and school day are identical; `school_day` is blank in the day map. Weeks are five consecutive school days. The authoritative day rows are in `data/plans/math-grade-4/days.csv`; the 149 base lessons each have a disposition in `source-map.csv`, followed by 31 original teaching, assessment or response slots. Only the public first-edition material recorded in the base-text record is used. Assessment items, answer keys, lesson text and section plans are outside this draft.

The section numbers below divide the load into runs of at most six days. They reserve groups of days, not section plans. The default lesson budget is five minutes of mixed retrieval, 40 to 45 minutes of activity, five to ten minutes of synthesis and five minutes for an original exit ticket, totaling 60. A ten-minute readiness reminder or a saved assessment product replaces practice within that budget. It does not lengthen the day. The day map names drawn tools; no digital tool is required. Physical protractors, rulers, strips and their paper alternatives are supplies. Math safety cells remain blank as required by this brief.

## Unit 1: Factors and multiples

**8 days, course days 1 to 8, weeks 1 to 2.** Sections: 1.1, days 1 to 3, arrays and factor structure; 1.2, days 4 to 8, applications, assessment and response.

The rectangle question makes factors and multiples visible before numerical lists appear. Arrays lead to exhaustive factor pairs, then prime and composite status, then divisibility and the locker investigation. The six lesson days all serve the one owned row, m4-factors-multiples, and the full four demands of 4.OA.B.4. The special case of 1 is explicit. This supports the common multiples used for fraction equivalence without introducing greatest common factor, least common multiple or prime factorization.

Day 1 retrieves grade 3 facts with an untimed sample and immediate strategy feedback. Day 7 assesses factor pairs, each factor's multiple relationship, testing a one-digit divisor and prime/composite decisions, with a short justification. Day 8 supplies the sole planned unit response. No mid-unit assessment is needed. MP7 is the array-to-factor explanation; MP8 is the repeated locker reasoning, with explanation evidence retained in the day 7 task.

**Candidate settled:** keep the Progressions OA factor-language and no-fluency-demand guidance in days 2, 3 and 6, zero extra days. Drop the odd-number-of-factors extension to 50: the locker investigation already offers depth and a separate extension would crowd the eight-day unit. No owned expectation is dropped.

## Unit 2: Fraction equivalence and comparison

**17 days, course days 9 to 25, weeks 2 to 5.** Sections: 2.1, days 9 to 13, fraction size and location; 2.2, days 14 to 18, equivalence; 2.3, days 19 to 23, comparison; 2.4, days 24 to 25, assessment and response.

The first five days retrieve fractions, same-numerator/same-denominator comparisons and benchmarks while establishing one number line. The middle five carry the heavy explanation in m4-equivalent-fractions: cutting every part into the same number of pieces changes part count and size together. The next five use that understanding for m4-compare-fractions, including common numerators, common denominators, benchmarks, comparison symbols and the same whole. The source order is kept; only its first two grade 3 review lessons share day 9. Given denominators are 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 100; a common denominator generated while working may fall outside that list. Neither simplest form nor a least common denominator is required, and equivalence is not explained by multiplying by one.

Readiness is unnecessary because the assumed rows were used in grade 3. Retrieval occurs inside days 9, 10 and 13, with unit 1 multiples retrieved on day 14. Day 18 explicitly groups small parts into larger parts and divides both counts by a common factor, reversing the cutting argument without requiring simplest form. Day 19 includes distance from 1 on the shared line, without general unlike-denominator subtraction. Day 21 explicitly teaches common-numerator comparison before the claim task, replacing a repeated denominator example. These restore the earlier plan's routes within the existing activity time. Day 24 assesses every part of 4.NF.A.1 and 4.NF.A.2 through short generation, explanation and comparison tasks; day 25 responds once. No mid-unit assessment is needed. MP3 is a critique and defense of an equivalence or comparison explanation on days 16 and 21, carried into day 24; MP7 is the relation between part count and size.

ELA W.4.1 is owned in its unit 3, school days 81 to 119, well after these days. Day 19 therefore reserves five synthesis minutes for the frame's bridge: a spoken reason, then a claim/model sentence frame. Day 21 reuses it for the written application within its synthesis. **Candidate settled:** keep the Progressions NF cutting explanation and given-denominator limit within days 14 to 18, zero extra days. Days 26 and 33 retrieve grade 3 fraction representations and this year's equivalence; day 40 retrieves earlier line plots. No new readiness check precedes this unit. MP2 and MP4 are the quantity-to-tape-diagram-to-equation tasks on days 30, 31 and 39, carried into the final evidence.
