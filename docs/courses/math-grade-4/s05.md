## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How does each place help us read, compare and compute with big numbers?"

**Hook, day 58:** Show ten drawings labelled 1,000 each. Ask how one drawing could represent their total, then pose the compelling question. Use five activity minutes after the warm-up; field 10 names the adaptation.
**Supporting questions:** What changes when a digit changes place (day 61)? Which place decides a comparison (day 64)? When does rounding hide information (day 69)? What does an exchange record (day 71)?
**Closing claim:** I can read, compare, round and compute with whole numbers through one million, explaining place values. Digit Pairs (day 62), Rounding Choices (day 69) and Exchange Records (day 71) prepare day 75's four items.
**Teaching:** Model chart exchanges on day 58, three-digit grouping on day 59 and labelled intervals on day 63. On day 67, establish halfway rounding upward and model rounding into a new leading place; day 68 practises these taught cases. Model algorithms on days 70 and 71; step back for independent computation on days 72 to 74.


**Practices:** Day 66's Between Multiples develops MP6: name the rounding place, endpoints and equal intervals. Day 71's Exchange Records develops MP7: connect each written exchange to ten smaller units and compare it with exchanging one whole for fractional parts.
**Fluency:** 4.NBT.B.4 requires both standard algorithms. Days 72 and 73 consolidate taught methods, with inverse checks and immediate feedback, without speed scores. From day 71, five-minute warm-ups mix algorithms three meetings weekly, facts on two, and fractions or decimals on the second and fourth meetings.
**Balance:** Eleven understanding, four fluency and two application days give 660, 240 and 120 minutes. Major work receives 925 of 1,020 instructional minutes, 90.7 percent: 45 on day 58 and 55 on each remaining teaching day. Exclude assessment and response. This unit and all 17 lessons center major work; earlier-grade additional work earns no separate credit.
**Limits:** Whole numbers through 1,000,000 only. Decimal place relationships and decimal algorithms await grade 5. Mixed numbers maintain unit 3 learning; its story standard is not newly owned here. Rounding includes middle places and a new leading place, without an extra extension.
**Carried:** Keep unit 4's chart and number line; retire its grids. Tie blocks to chart equations on day 58, label any changed block unit explicitly on day 60, then withdraw blocks on day 64. Tie lines to numeral locations on day 63 and rounding on day 66; withdraw supplied partitions on day 68. Restore blocks for algorithm exchanges on days 70 and 71. Withdraw supplied drawings on day 72 and filled charts on day 74. Restore a drawing for a diagnosed error, then retry unaided.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A digit has the same value in every place. | Place is ignored. Repair: label two adjacent copies of a digit and exchange ten smaller units for one larger unit. | From memory. |
| B | An internal zero can be omitted from a numeral. | Empty places are not preserved. Repair: build the named number in a chart with every column present. | From memory. |
| C | Compare from the rightmost differing place. | Small places override larger ones. Repair: cover columns to the right of the first difference from the left and compare its units. | From memory. |
| D | Round to a place other than the requested one. | The target unit is lost. Repair: underline the requested place and name its adjacent multiples. | From memory. |
| E | Rounding always selects the lower multiple. | Distance is ignored. Repair: compare both endpoint distances on a labelled line. | From memory. |
| F | Rounding up loses the new leading place. | Composition stops at the original left edge. Repair: extend the chart one column before composing ten units. | From memory. |
| G | Omit a composed unit from an addition record. | The total loses an exchanged unit. Repair: circle ten smaller units and connect the new unit to its recorded digit. | From memory. |
| H | Decompose a unit without reducing the donor place. | The exchange changes the total. Repair: cross out and relabel both affected columns in a drawing. | From memory. |
| I | Subtract the smaller digit from the larger in each column regardless of order. | Digits replace the minuend quantity. Repair: model removal from the top quantity and exchange where needed. | From memory. |
| J | A mixed-number subtraction cannot exchange a whole for fractional parts. | A whole is detached from its equal parts. Repair: partition one whole on the line and rename it before subtracting. | From memory. |
| K | Equal numerical intervals may have unequal drawn lengths. | The scale is inconsistent. Repair: partition the line into equal spaces and label each interval value. | From memory. |

Assign a letter only when the written or spoken reasoning matches its description; investigate other wrong answers individually.
