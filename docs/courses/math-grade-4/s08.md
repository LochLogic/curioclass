## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we share or group large numbers, and what do we do with what is left?"

**Hook, day 113:** Invented for practice: Share 158 paper tiles among four trays. What changes if each tray must hold four tiles? Try a drawing for five minutes, then pose the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** How does an unknown side become division (day 115)? What does each partial quotient count (day 118)? What does the leftover mean here (day 120)?
**Closing claim:** I can explain a quotient, interpret leftovers and choose operations and rectangle formulas. Unknown Side (day 115), Leftovers (day 120) and Rectangle Decisions (day 123) prepare days 125 and 126.
**Teaching:** After the day 113 hook, teach a larger equal-group example. Model exchanges on day 116 and partial-quotient records on day 118, including four-digit division and remainder verification before day 119 practice. Step back for day 122 comparisons.


**Practices:** Unknown Side develops MP1 on day 115: identify the missing factor before choosing division. On day 125, Packing Decisions develops MP1 through independent solving and MP3 through a critique grounded in capacity.
**Fluency:** Maintain 4.NBT.B.4 three warm-ups weekly and grade 3 facts on two; fractions and decimals share two. Day 119 practices taught methods with feedback, without speed scores. Division-algorithm fluency belongs to grade 6.
**Balance:** Seven understanding, one fluency and five application days reserve 420, 60 and 300 minutes. This is one major-work unit. Excluding assessment and response, twelve major-work lessons supply 625/720 minutes, 86.8 percent: 45 each on days 113 and 115, 40 on day 123, 55 on the other nine. Supporting factors on day 114 practice quotient estimation; rectangle problems on days 115 and 123 practice division and lettered multistep solving. Count no formula-only minutes.
**Limits:** Complete 4.NBT.B.6, 4.OA.A.3 and 4.MD.A.3; other codes maintain earlier learning. Use at most four-digit dividends, nonzero one-digit divisors and three steps per problem. Answers remain whole numbers. Write dividend = divisor times quotient + remainder, with remainder smaller than divisor. No fractional quotients, fraction-by-fraction area or new grouping-symbol instruction.
**Carried:** Keep unit 7's unpartitioned area picture. Tie it to unknown-factor equations on day 115 and partial quotients on day 118; withdraw supplied partitions on day 119, retaining student drawings into grade 5. ## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A remainder at least as large as the divisor is accepted. | Equal groups have not been completed. Repair: remove another group and update the quotient. | NBT Progression, PDF page 16. |
| B | A zero place in the quotient is omitted. | Digit position has lost its value. Repair: label every place in an expanded quotient before shortening. | NBT Progression, PDF page 16. |
| C | Division stops at an empty higher place despite lower units remaining. | An empty place is mistaken for completion. Repair: exchange into the next place and continue. | NBT Progression, PDF page 16. |
| D | Only one length and one width are added for perimeter. | Half the boundary is counted. Repair: label and trace all four sides. | Geometric Measurement Progression, PDF page 16. |
| E | Area is used to answer a boundary question. | The measured attribute is confused. Repair: trace the boundary before choosing its equation. | From memory. |
| F | Half the perimeter is reported as the unknown side. | Half the boundary is mistaken for one side. Repair: subtract the known adjacent side from the half-perimeter. | From memory. |
| G | Leftovers are discarded when every object must fit in a container. | A complete-group count is mistaken for the needed capacity. Repair: draw an extra container for the remaining objects. | OA Progression, PDF page 30. |
| H | A known side is subtracted from area to find the other side. | The unknown-factor relation is missed. Repair: build equal rows and write the multiplication equation. | From memory. |
| I | Packet count and packet size are added to find their total. | Equal groups are treated as an additive join. Repair: draw one labeled group per packet and write its product. | From memory. |
| J | The unknown is never represented by a letter. | The calculation does not express the unknown quantity. Repair: name the quantity with a letter before writing its equation. | From memory. |
| K | Repeating the exact answer is offered as its reasonableness check. | No independent check is made. Repair: compare with rounded or compatible numbers. | From memory. |
| L | Fractional parts of lengths are omitted from perimeter. | The lengths are truncated to whole numbers. Repair: mark every side in eighths before combining. | From memory. |
| M | A partly filled packet is counted as complete. | The full-packet requirement is lost. Repair: circle only filled groups when counting complete packets. | From memory. |

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools:** None digital. Use pencils, plain and grid paper; hand-drawn grids are the no-kit path. Draw labeled base-ten units rather than requiring blocks. Partners alternate explaining and checking; at home the adult partners. Extend by proving that two decompositions give the same quotient. Any optional calculator check follows estimation.
