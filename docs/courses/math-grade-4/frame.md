# Course frame: Math Grade 4

Course `math-grade-4` (catalogue id `math-k8`), grade 4, draft frame, written 2026-11-13, synchronized with the scope and sequence 2026-11-15.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9, following the base text's units in its order: its unit 3 is split (fraction operations, then tenths
  and hundredths joined to its unit 4's decimals), its unit 6 is split (multiplication, then division), its units 7
  and 8 (angles, shapes) are joined, and its unit 9 (a year review) becomes course-level review days.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 145 in units and 15 at course level (3 course-level checks on 4 days, 4 respond
  days, 7 year-review days). Math is daily: one lesson of about 60 minutes every school day, five a week (the base
  text's lesson length: a warm-up of 5 to 10 minutes that mixes earlier work, the activities, a synthesis and our own
  exit question). 160 days is the full-year yardstick. The base text's year is 149 lessons (137 required) with 2
  assessment days a unit; the units here keep 122 lesson days (all required lessons through its unit 8, lessons
  in the angles unit merged twice and the two grade 3 review lessons that open unit 2 merged once, every optional
  lesson cut) and add 5 days of our own where the rows' load
  notes ask for them. Each unit's days are its lessons, one dedicated end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day; named earlier application products supply distributed evidence without another collection. An assumed earlier-grade row last taught or assessed in grade g gets retrieval in g+1 and readiness in g+2 or later. Grade 3 facts and fractions are retrieval. The four readiness slots are the last ten minutes of respond days 44, 57, 76 and 127, before the receiving unit opens; no first day holds a readiness check. There are nine unit respond days and four course-level respond days, plus the named in-day reminders.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, grade 4 (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt),
  taught in full, every lettered part placed (4.NF.B.3a to d, 4.NF.B.4a to c, 4.MD.C.5a and b). The 27 progression
  rows are the contract; each unit names the rows it owns. Math has no check set: the standard is used as written.
  The C3 column names the unit's practices; the "Check set adds" column names, as candidates, the Progressions check
  for the unit (the rows cite the Progressions documents by page) and one extension for students ready for more. Eureka Math is a reference for sequence and models only, never copied.
- **Major work.** Count once by minutes on major-cluster codes over instructional minutes, excluding assessment
  and respond days. The single figure and day-level task ledger are in the scope and sequence, Focus; older unit-
  and day-share estimates are retired. Unit 1 earns no credit simply for preparing equivalence. Supporting work
  counts only for an identified major-code task and exit on that day. Units 6 to 9 must report actual major-code
  minutes at unit-plan stage, and all units reconcile to that ledger. The current reservation is close to the
  required line; it is not a measured classroom result.
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, grade 4 (CC BY 4.0), adapted into our days with a
  credit line on each (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the store is S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\grade-4, and
  its scripted lesson list im-g4-lessons.csv). Its order is kept; the source map at the end places every lesson. Only
  the free public pages are used: cool-downs, answer keys, section checkpoints and end-of-unit assessments sit behind
  a login and are not taken, so every exit question and assessment is our own. The 2nd edition (CC BY-NC) is never
  used.
- **Fluency.** 4.NBT.B.4 (adding and subtracting multi-digit whole numbers with the standard algorithm) is the
  grade's required fluency, taught in unit 5 and kept up after it in the warm-ups: short, spaced, mixed practice
  with feedback, never a countdown timer shown to a student. Multiplying and dividing (4.NBT.B.5 and 6) are done
  with strategies and area models that may shorten toward the algorithm, every step explained; the multiplication
  algorithm is grade 5's fluency and the division algorithm grade 6's. The grade 3 facts are checked in unit 1 and
  again before unit 7. The warm-up rotation: times tables and adding and subtracting within 1,000 from the first
  day, each labeled on the page as grade 3 upkeep, not grade 4 teaching; the grade 4 algorithm the day after unit 5
  teaches it; fractions and decimals twice a week from the end of unit 4, so fraction work is not silent until the
  year review; multi-digit multiplying and dividing from the end of unit 8.
- **Fractions.** Denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 100 in tasks; fractions read in unit-fraction language
  ("three fifths", three pieces of size one fifth), never "three over five"; one number line model carried through
  units 2 to 4.
- **Practices.** Each unit names one or two Standards for Mathematical Practice in the C3 column (C3 is a social
  studies framework and does not apply); the unit plan names the task where students do them. All eight are placed:
  MP1 (unit 8), MP2 (units 3, 6), MP3 (units 2, 8), MP4 (units 3, 6), MP5 (unit 9), MP6 (units 4, 5, 9), MP7 (units
  1, 2, 4, 5, 7), MP8 (units 1, 7).
- **Readiness.** Four slots cover the earlier-grade rows not taught or assessed in grade 3: money on day 44
  for unit 4, three-digit place representations and comparison on day 57 for unit 5, unit size on day 76 for unit 6,
  and circle partitions on day 127 for unit 9. Each is at the end of a respond day immediately before its unit.
  Results shape the next opener and the named ten-minute earlier-model reminder. Grade 3 facts use the handoff
  and retrieval inside day 1, then a full facts-and-response day 100 before large multiplication. Grade 3 fraction
  work is retrieved inside unit 2 lessons. Money is explicitly taught and recorded again on day 48, so day 86
  retrieves that same-year work instead of giving another readiness check. The scope inventory lists every assumed
  earlier-grade row, its last-use grade and its one treatment.
- **Trapezoid.** The inclusive definition (a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides), the base
  text's own convention, stated when the word first appears in unit 9 and kept in grade 5.
- **Paper path.** Every manipulative has a paper form (fraction strips, 10 by 10 grids, base-ten drawings, paper
  protractors, dot paper), and every practice generator prints. No student account.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content that needs a named community study:
  contexts are measurement, food, school, building and art. The answer-key agent reads every item and key of the
  course's checks, solving each cold beside the script check (BASELINE rule 27), before the course is called
  finished. Word problems use a range of names and settings, and never ask about family money, body weight or home
  life; the unit plan lists the people and places its problems show.
- **Reading load.** Comparison language ("times as many", "times as much") is the hard part of unit 6 and is taught
  as precise words; every word problem may be read aloud.
