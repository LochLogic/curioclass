# Course frame: Math Grade 4

Course id math-grade-4, grade 4, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 9, following the base text's units in its order: its unit 3 is split (fraction operations, then tenths
  and hundredths joined to its unit 4's decimals), its unit 6 is split (multiplication, then division), its units 7
  and 8 (angles, shapes) are joined, and its unit 9 (a year review) becomes course-level review days.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 145 in units and 15 at course level (3 course-level checks on 4 days, 4 respond
  days, 7 year-review days). Math is daily: one lesson of about 60 minutes every school day, five a week (the base
  text's lesson length: a warm-up of 5 to 10 minutes that mixes earlier work, the activities, a synthesis and our own
  exit question). 160 days is the full-year yardstick. The base text's year is 149 lessons (137 required) with 2
  assessment days a unit; the units here keep 122 lesson days (all required lessons through its unit 8, lessons
  in the angles unit merged twice and the two grade 3 review lessons that open unit 2 merged once, every optional
  lesson cut) and add 5 days of our own where the rows' load
  notes ask for them. Each unit's days are its lessons, one end-of-unit assessment day and one respond day.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics, grade 4 (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt),
  taught in full, every lettered part placed (4.NF.B.3a to d, 4.NF.B.4a to c, 4.MD.C.5a and b). The 27 progression
  rows are the contract; each unit names the rows it owns. There is no check set: the standard is used as written.
  The unit plan checks each unit against the Progressions documents, which the rows cite by page; the "Check set
  adds" column names the candidate check. Eureka Math is a reference for sequence and models only, never copied.
- **Major work.** The major clusters (4.OA.A, 4.NBT.A and B, 4.NF.A to C) are the main work of 6 of 9 units
  (units 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, each predominantly major: unit 7 holds four base lessons on the additional patterns
  standard; 67 percent by units), and unit 6 opens on major work (multiplicative comparison) before its supporting
  measurement. By days it is counted two ways. Strictly (grade 4 major clusters only, by each lesson's main
  standard, the grade 3 review lessons that open unit 2 counted as review, assessment and respond days split in the
  same share, the year review all major, the checks at about three quarters): about 101 of 160, 63 percent. By the
  evidence guide's rule (indicator 1e), which counts supporting work that practises major work (units 1, 3, 6 and 8,
  named below): about 130 of 160, 81 percent. The line is 65 percent (RUBRIC-MATH-K8). The optional lessons are cut
  for the day budget, not for the line. By time: grade 3 facts are kept up in warm-ups from the start, and the grade
  4 addition and subtraction algorithm enters the warm-ups after unit 5; the scope and sequence checks the share in
  minutes, with the supporting-work links written down, and the day counts here are provisional until it does. The
  supporting work practises the major work:
  factors and multiples prepare equivalent fractions (unit 1), line plots in eighths practise adding fractions
  (unit 3), measurement conversion practises "times as many" (unit 6), area and perimeter formulas practise
  division (unit 8).
- **Base text.** Illustrative Mathematics K-12, 1st edition, grade 4 (CC BY 4.0), adapted into our days with a
  credit line on each (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the store is S:\CurioClass-sources\illustrative-math\grade-4, and
  its scripted lesson list im-g4-lessons.csv). Its order is kept; the source map at the end places every lesson. Only
  the free public pages are used: cool-downs, answer keys, section checkpoints and end-of-unit assessments sit behind
  a login and are not taken, so every exit question and assessment is our own. The 2nd edition (CC BY-NC) is never
  used.
- **Fluency.** 4.NBT.B.4 (adding and subtracting multi-digit whole numbers with the standard algorithm) is the
  grade's required fluency, taught in unit 5 and kept up after it in the warm-ups: short, spaced, mixed practice
  with feedback, never a countdown timer shown to a student. Multiplying and dividing (4.NBT.B.5 and 6) are done
  with strategies and area models that may shorten toward the algorithm, every step explained; the multiplication
  algorithm is grade 5's fluency and the division algorithm grade 6's. The grade 3 facts are checked in unit 1 and
  again before unit 7.
- **Fractions.** Denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 100 in tasks; fractions read in unit-fraction language
  ("three fifths", three pieces of size one fifth), never "three over five"; one number line model carried through
  units 2 to 4.
- **Practices.** Each unit names one or two Standards for Mathematical Practice in the C3 column (C3 is a social
  studies framework and does not apply); the unit plan names the task where students do them.
- **Readiness.** The rows mark four weak handoffs that skip grade 3; each opens the first unit that needs it with a
  readiness check and a short reteach with the earlier model: m2-three-digit-place-value and m2-compare-three-digit
  (unit 5), m2-money (unit 4, where cents are first written as hundredths of a dollar, and again in unit 6),
  m2-unit-size (unit 6), the circle half of m2-halves-thirds-fourths (unit 9). Unit 2's first base lessons revisit
  grade 3 fractions as its readiness check.
- **Trapezoid.** The inclusive definition (a quadrilateral with at least one pair of parallel sides), the base
  text's own convention, stated when the word first appears in unit 9 and kept in grade 5.
- **Paper path.** Every manipulative has a paper form (fraction strips, 10 by 10 grids, base-ten drawings, paper
  protractors, dot paper), and every practice generator prints. No student account.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. No unit needs a community sensitivity
  reader; contexts are measurement, food, school, building and art. A qualified math content reviewer reads every
  item and key of the course's checks before the course is called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3); who, and any pay, is
  Cory's. Word problems use a range of names and never ask
  about family money, body weight or home life.
- **Reading load.** Comparison language ("times as many", "times as much") is the hard part of unit 6 and is taught
  as precise words; every word problem may be read aloud.
