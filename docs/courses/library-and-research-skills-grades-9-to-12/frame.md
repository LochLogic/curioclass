# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, grades 9 to 12

Course `library-and-research-skills-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `library-9-12`), grades 9, 10, 11 and 12, one band course with a part for each school year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8, two a school year (units 1 and 2 in grade 9, 3 and 4 in grade 10, 5 and 6 in grade 11, 7 and 8 in
  grade 12), each unit one full inquiry cycle on a host task or its stand-alone question, with its product shared and
  the self-check. Each year is whole on its own: its two units, its end-of-unit assessments, a mid-year check and a
  year-end check. The order follows the band's growth: grade 9 stays with one question and judges and keeps sources,
  then uses them fairly and honestly; grade 10 sets its own topic, brings in missing views and puts sources together;
  grade 11 searches beyond articles and shows a reader how the work was done, then publishes; grade 12 builds a
  system for after school and ends with the capstone.
- **Days.** 72: 18 a year for four years. Each year: 14 days in units and 4 course-level days (a mid-year check and
  its respond day, a year-end check and its respond day); band total 56 in units and 16 for checks and respond days.
  One session of about 45 minutes, about one session every two weeks across the school year, 18 sessions a year over
  32 weeks (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: 18 sessions a year of 45 minutes, about 13.5 hours a year and about 54 hours
  across the band, as Cory kept them on 2026-11-21). Each session takes one period of a host class (most often ELA or
  history), led by the school librarian or the host class's teacher. The 45 minutes hold getting out the research
  notebook and the printed set, the session, and collecting work: a lesson plan budgets about 3 minutes at each end,
  so about 39 working minutes; a day's opening retrieval takes at most 5 of them. Load: each year carries five to seven
  rows; a unit gets about two teaching days a row, more where the row is a long product (the grade 9 research log, the
  grade 11 method note and interview, the grade 12 capstone) and fewer where it is a short move added to a product the
  unit already makes (labeling one's own work, a database's subject terms). Between sessions the host course carries
  the long research work; a class with no host in step runs the unit's stand-alone question on its closed source set
  inside the library sessions alone. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (no unit is over 25 days, so
  no mid-unit assessment); in units 1 to 7 the course-level check right after the unit is that unit's end-of-unit
  assessment, and unit 8 ends in its own (the capstone, day 70). These are course days: course day n of a year falls
  on about school day 9n of that year (week 2 for day 1, week 15 for day 8, week 31 for day 17, week 32 for day 18),
  and the scope and sequence places them on school days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day): Understanding is
  the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through a search, a lateral check, a license reading or a citation on an
  invented case, holding the unit's skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, varied tries of one move with
  feedback: three claims checked laterally, four sources cited, six works sorted by license); Application is the
  inquiry day (students run part of the cycle on the host task or the stand-alone question: question, search, judge,
  note, make, share), and drafting and revising a product are inquiry days. Fluency is small: one or two practice days a
  unit, plus the five-minute opening retrieval on most days. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day with the hook and the
  compelling question. The skill card is held by the teacher on the first show-me day, by pairs on the first practice
  day, and withdrawn on the first inquiry day each unit plan names; every check is taken without it.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words only,
  data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone). Ownership
  rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone. The band owns
  18 ids, each in the year whose row checks it alone (grade 9: I.D.2, IV.D.1, II.B.3, III.A.3, VI.C.1; grade 10:
  II.A.3, III.C.2, II.C.2, IV.D.2, VI.B.3; grade 11: IV.D.3, II.D.1, VI.C.2, VI.D.2; grade 12: VI.D.1, VI.D.3, I.D.3,
  II.D.3; each named with its phrase in its unit), and revisits the ids earlier grades own, each named in its unit with
  its owner. Skills: the 24 progression rows of grades 9 to 12, each in one unit. Check set: the Empire State
  Information Fluency Continuum, 9 to 12 band file, its 9-10 and 11-12 columns and bold priority skills (read cell by
  cell for this frame, later.csv L103; result in the Check set read bullet); the band file has two columns, so grades
  9 and 10 are checked against the 9-10 cell and 11 and 12 against the 11-12 cell, and the rows say how each year
  differs. Values: in 6 to 12, cases are invented or chosen evenly across views; hoax or hate pages are described or
  shown in an invented form, never reproduced (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, item 1).
- **Year differences.** Grade 9 researches inside a topic the teacher sets and runs the lateral routine with its card;
  grade 10 sets its own topic and question, and runs the lateral check without the card on machine-made text; grade 11
  is the time-pressure and show-your-method year on a short project; grade 12 puts the band to use on a real decision
  and on life after school. Grade 9 learns database operators and filters, grade 10 subject terms and following
  references, grade 11 reports and data sets, grade 12 saved searches in a personal system.
- **Base text.** None in the source store. Every day is Curio Class's own. The partial bases settled for 6 to 12
  (STANDARD.md section 3: the CC BY online-reasoning lessons and the open web-literacy book in
  docs/reviews/library-open-material-2026-11-16.md) are adapted item by item at the day, only items under an open
  license, each license re-read at source when the item is first adapted, credited in the day's credit, with every
  named platform replaced by a generic kind. Where they would fit, ours: the lateral reading lessons in units 1 and 4,
  news versus opinion, sponsored results and checking photos, video and data in units 4 and 5, the book's four moves
  (previous work, go upstream, read laterally, check your own feeling about a claim) as teacher background for units 1
  and 5. No source map is written now, since no item is in the source store yet (download approved, Decided by Cory, item 1). Curio Class writes, per
  unit, the invented source sets, invented search and database results pages, invented sites and their "other tabs"
  sheets, dated captures, the skill cards, the research notebook pages and every check.
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from a core course of the same grade, named by row id for
  the teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question with a
  closed source set) so the unit runs when no core task is in step, or at home (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence
  records both. Where a host runs a long research project in the same weeks (ELA grade 10 unit 7, ELA grade 11 unit
  4, the grade 12 projects), one question serves both courses where the teachers agree, and library never adds a
  second paper.
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question and closes with the share step and the
  self-check: the student rates their own use of the skill card's steps and names one thing to do differently next
  time (APPROACH.md, the form of daily evidence; bar part 11 item 6). All six steps (ask, find, judge, use, share,
  reflect; the continuum's connect, wonder, investigate, construct, express, reflect) run in every unit, and the Inquiry
  phases column names the phases each unit's rows teach.
- **Evidence.** Daily: show-me and practice days end with an exit ticket that asks for the move once on a new case;
  inquiry days keep the working notes (question, search terms tried, sources with a judgment line each) in the research
  notebook. End-of-unit assessment: a sitting of unseen items, each answered alone in writing in about 35 of the 39
  working minutes (a search, a judgment, a license reading or a citation actually done on a new invented case, scored
  on a short teacher-judged rubric, plus a few items with one answer checked on the page), and, where a row's check is
  a product (the grade 9 research log, the grade 10 seminar record, the grade 11 method note, the grade 12 guide and
  capstone), the product scored on the rubric after a draft day, written feedback and a revision counted in the unit's
  days. No score from a day a move is first taught. The respond day's groups come from the class record sheet by the
  rubric step missed (APPROACH.md, unit field 7). Results go to the student; no per-student record is passed on beyond
  what the school keeps for any course.
- **Reading level.** Source texts at the grade's level, short (one to two pages a source), with a shorter source set
  and read-aloud pages for students who read below it (APPROACH.md, section field 9). A practice day's reading load is
  about four short pages; an inquiry day's is the student's own sources.
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper: printed invented source sets, printed search and database results pages,
  printed "other tabs" sheets that show what other sources say about a site (so lateral moves are made on paper),
  dated captures of machine-made summaries and news, the research notebook, and printed license labels. No day needs
  a device, an internet connection or a student account, and no student types a name, school, place or photo into a
  search or a site (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, item 3).
- **Devices.** None required. On an inquiry day with open search a school may give pairs one shared device, under the
  teacher's guidance and the stop rule (close the page, tell the adult, no blame); the student page then says "your
  search results" and names no site. No owned or revisited id needs technology: database searching, lateral reading,
  publishing (VI.C.2: a one-page brief or an exhibit counts) and labeling one's work are met in full on the paper path.
  A tool may give practice on a closed invented set; it never uses the live web, records what a student types, or
  writes a student's notes (APPROACH.md, What a tool may and may not do).
- **Lateral reading time.** Grades 9 and 10 hold nine sessions whose main move is lateral reading, counted in working
  minutes, not periods: unit 1 day 3 (39: its retrieval is a lateral news check), day 4 (34: the operator string and
  the log's start move to the last five minutes), day 5 (34: each new source checked laterally before it enters the
  log), day 6 (34), day 7 (29: the findings note and self-check take the last ten); unit 3 day 23 (29: each
  missing-view source checked laterally before it goes in the log); unit 4 day 30 (39: its retrieval is the lateral
  routine), day 31 (34) and day 32 (34: the capture traced and the matrix's sources checked laterally; the matrix and
  map are drafted between sessions in the host course or the stand-alone notebook). That is about 306 working
  minutes, at least the six 50-minute lessons of the one high school field study and no claim beyond it (APPROACH.md,
  Pacing and dose: about 500 students, from the university's news release, the article not re-read); the study is
  background, not evidence that this course or its paper path gets its result. The scope and sequence records each of
  these sessions' lateral minutes, and a day plan that cuts one below its figure moves the other work, not the
  lateral time. No page claims the method raises scores (lens, Day errors). Grades 11 and 12 return to it in units 5 and 8.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** The last teaching day is day 70 (week 29), unit 8's capstone and its end-of-unit assessment.
  Weeks 31 and 32 hold only the year-end check (day 71) and its respond day (day 72), no new teaching. If seniors leave
  before week 31, days 71 and 72 fold: the capstone's score stands as unit 8's end-of-unit evidence, the year-end
  check's sampled items are not taken that year, and the respond day's feedback on the capstone goes to each student
  in writing. A student 18 or over gets the family note themselves and makes their own leave-out or consent choice
  (AGENTS.md), in every year of the band where it applies, the interview units above all.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond paper and, at most, a shared device; no field work and no trip, so no
  laboratory or field guide applies. Interviews outside school (unit 6) follow APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground" item 8:
  arranged through the school or a family, in a public or supervised setting, with consent. The screen-safety side is
  the stop rule on every open-search day, read by the sensitivity read.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned (Cory,
  2026-11-14). The license and fair use days (units 2 and 4, and unit 6's honesty review) get the kind `rights` read:
  general guidance, never legal advice, no claim that a use is lawful (APPROACH.md item 6); the agent studies first the
  US Copyright Office's free fair use index, https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/ (named here for the agent, not opened
  for this frame), and the license summaries of the open license used in each case. Machine-made text (units 4 and 6):
  no claim that any tool is reliable or unreliable (item 7). What the agent studies first for every unit: APPROACH.md
  "Sensitive ground" items 1 (even views, invented hoaxes), 2 (the stop rule), 3 (privacy), 4 (honest use without
  shaming), 5 (whose voices a source set shows, C12) and, for units 3, 6 and 8, item 8 (interviews). When a unit runs on
  a host task, the host unit's own sensitivity notes and named sources apply to that content (the host frame's last
  column); library's own case sets touch no cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content, and the
  units that ask about a student's own background or communities (3 and 8) keep every answer optional and private to
  the teacher. No organization's name goes on a student, teacher or family page.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Library is not a core course and students reach grade 9 from many middle schools, so no
  grade 9 student is assumed to have taken library 6 to 8 (lesson 32). The grade 8 rows in the Seams table are given
  as ten-minute readiness checks, unscored, in place of those days' opening retrievals: unit 1's rows
  (g8-test-assumptions with g8-research-product, g8-multiple-views-gather, g8-news-lateral, g8-compare-wikis) in the
  last ten minutes of day 1, after the hook; unit 2's (g8-multiple-views-gather on views for a decision,
  g8-fair-use-basics, g8-diverse-team) in the last ten minutes of day 9, check 1's respond day, since unit 1's day 7 is
  a lateral session. A class that shows a move missing gets a ten-minute reteach from the 6 to 8 show-me case at the
  start of the next session that needs it. Grades 10 to 12 keep their named five-minute retrievals: their rows are
  taught inside this band.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30, source sets printed
  once as a class set and reused by later sections, notebook pages and checks per student: unit 1 about $12, unit 2
  about $10, unit 3 about $10, unit 4 about $12, unit 5 about $10, unit 6 about $10, unit 7 about $8, unit 8 about $12;
  about $22 a year, about $84 across the band. No paid database, library card or library visit is assumed (Cory,
  2026-11-11).
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The 9 to 12 band file's 9-10 and 11-12 columns and priority boxes, every
  indicator row (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-9-12\esifc-priority-9-12.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, ids and meanings only, nothing copied). Grade 9 and 10 rows against the 9-10 cell, met: key words and
  synonyms kept in a search list and refining the question into a framework for the inquiry
  (g9-research-question-design, with the search plan); advanced search with operators and filters
  (g9-databases-operators); a source's authority judged and evidence chosen across sources (g9-source-log); civic online
  reasoning, verifying what is presented as true (g9-lateral-method); the effect of point of view and purpose on a
  source (g9-fair-sides, g10-own-lens); an authentic problem researched and a change proposed with others
  (g9-group-shapes-solution); a mind map of the big idea and its links, information organized independently, and a
  topic refined independently (g10-synthesis-map, grade 10's self-set topic); database features such as abstracts and
  subject headings (g10-database-deep); respect and empathy for diverse people, and confidence in questioning views
  unlike one's own (g10-others-views-in); misinformation judged by its maker's motives (g10-lateral-machine-text).
  Grade 11 and 12 rows against the 11-12 cell, met: publishing for an authentic audience and presenting to it
  (g11-publish-for-audience); copyright, fair use, open licenses, credit and permission respected, and one's product
  judged and revised from feedback (g11-honesty-review, g12-lateral-capstone); tolerance for different viewpoints
  (g11-learn-across-difference); fact-checking before sharing (g11-lateral-news, g12-personal-system); a line of
  argument that meets competing interpretations, a claim with evidence and reasoning, and problems with no single best
  answer (g12-capstone-decision); sources corroborated and challenged in notes (g12-lateral-capstone); a leadership

  laterally for context sits in the 11-12 bold, and library teaches it from grade 5 as its own method (g9-lateral-method);
  respect for copyright, open licenses and permission is an 11-12 bold, with copyright, fair use and permission plain

  (g11-lateral-news); contrasting primary and secondary sources and how a results page orders and marks sponsored
  results, 11-12 bolds (g11-advanced-search, the second a review of g5-search-revise); using information to look into
  next steps after school and setting goals from one's strengths, 11-12 bolds (g12-personal-system). Met without a row
  change: plans to test a hypothesis (g8-test-assumptions, revisited at g9-research-question-design); notes in a

### Where the threads end

Grade 12 is the subject's last year, and its rows hand on nothing. The research-cycle thread ends in the capstone
(g12-capstone-decision, unit 8), put to use on a real decision and published for the people it affects; the
search-plan and lateral-reading threads end in the personal system (g12-personal-system, unit 7), the student's own
way to find and check information after school; the lateral-reading thread's last classroom use is the capstone audit
(g12-lateral-capstone, unit 8); the citation thread ends in the capstone's method note and audit; honest use ends in
the student teaching it to someone younger (g12-help-others, unit 7). Beside library, ELA grade 12's senior research
project and US History II's researched argument use the same moves in their own courses.
