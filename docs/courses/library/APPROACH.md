# Library and research skills: approach (layer 3)

**Approved by Cory 2026-11-21 (Desk q-prework-open-2). Written 2026-11-16, read cold and fixed 2026-11-20.** Short by design: it fills the subject
slots of the unit and section plan (docs/courses/UNIT-PLAN.md) and leans on the shared approach (docs/APPROACH.md),
the lens (docs/agents/lenses/library.md) and the standard (STANDARD.md). Ours, untested. K to 5 courses meet one or two
sessions a week all year (Cory, 2026-11-15); the 6 to 8 and 9 to 12 courses about one session every two weeks
across their grades (Cory, 2026-11-16); the frame says which. Read cold 2026-11-20
(docs/reviews/prework-library-read-2026-11-20.md) and fixed.

## The kinds of day, in library's words

| Shared kind | In library | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding | Show-me day | A skill modelled on the host task: the teacher thinks aloud through a search, a lateral check or a citation |
| Fluency | Practice day | Short, varied tries of the skill with feedback: three claims checked laterally, five sources cited |
| Application | Inquiry day | Students run part of the cycle on the host task themselves: question, search, judge, note, make, share |
| Check | Assessment day | A search, judgement or citation actually done on a new case, scored on a short rubric, plus a few items |
| Respond | Respond day | The product or notes revised from feedback |

A unit runs at least one full inquiry cycle on its host task, with the product shared. Each unit names the kind of
host task and one example from the grade's core courses (by progression row id), for the teacher to schedule
against, and also carries a stand-alone host task of its own (an invented question with a closed source set), so the
unit can run when no core task is in step, or at home where a family uses no other Curio Class course. The scope and
sequence records both.

## The form of daily evidence

- **Show-me and practice days:** an exit ticket that asks the student to do the skill once on a new case (check a
  claim and say what other sources said; fix a citation), with error letters for the usual slips.
- **Inquiry days:** the working notes (question, search terms tried, sources with a judgement line each).
- **K to 2:** the teacher's observation of one named skill per child, on a class checklist kept by the teacher and
  thrown away at the end of the unit, plus a drawing or a spoken answer.

## What library adds to the plan fields

- Unit field 2: the host task and its course (and the stand-alone host task, above); the AASL ids the unit owns and
  the grade's priority skills, in our own words; the product and its audience; the day the skill card is withdrawn.
- Unit field 3 (where it sits): builds on (the earlier library row, or the ELA or social studies row it assumes, by
  id); leads to; comes back in.
- Unit field 4 (readiness): one short item on the skill the unit builds on (find the maker of a page; name a
  source's kind); the ten-minute reteach is the earlier unit's show-me case.
- Unit field 5 (end-of-unit assessment): a search, a judgement or a citation actually done on a new case, scored on
  a short rubric, plus a few items. Never a quiz on the names of the steps alone.
- Unit field 6 (error register): the usual slips (trusting the look of a page, staying on the page to judge it,
  copying instead of noting, a citation missing its date or maker).
- Unit field 7 (response allocation): the rubric is teacher-judged; items with one answer are checked on the page.
- Unit field 8 (tools and supplies): printed source sets, invented pages and search results, the research notebook;
  no product or service names.
- Unit field 9 (family note): in English and Spanish: the skill's name and steps and one thing to try together at
  home (checking who is behind a page you both use).
- Unit field 10 (sources): what was read and how much, what is from memory, no claim of effect.
- Section field 1: before you teach: the AASL ids and the priority skill, the skill as an adult uses it, and why the
  method beats a checklist of a page's looks.
- Section field 2 (where it sits): what the section builds on and leads to, in the unit.
- Section field 3: each day's evidence in the form above.
- Section field 4: expected slips beside a sound example.
- Section field 5 (what is carried and when it fades): the unit's skill card (the steps of the one search, check or
  citation move the unit teaches). The teacher holds it on the first show-me day, thinking aloud on an invented
  case; pairs hold it on the first practice day; it is withdrawn on the first inquiry day, which the section plan
  names. The unit check is taken without it. A skill returns in later units with the card for one day, then without
  it. K to 2 use the same steps as three or four pictured words said aloud.
- Section field 6: kind of day; the print path when no devices are free (printed source sets).
- Section field 7 (talk and writing): a search-aloud routine (one student types or points, one says what they see,
  one records), and sentence frames for a judgement ("I trust this less because...").
- Section field 8 (practice that returns): one earlier skill, named, tried once on a new case.
- Section field 9 (supports): English learners: the unit's few words taught with a picture and a frame; access: the
  print path and read-aloud pages; reading load: source texts at the grade's level or read aloud, with a shorter
  source set for students who read below it; extension: the same skill on a harder case, never the next grade's.
- Section field 10 (two rooms): a parent at a table with one device or none, using the printed set.
- Section field 11 (not in this section): what is left to another section, course or subject.

## Sites, search and real cases

Practice and check pages use invented sites, invented search results and described captures, with kinds named
generically ("an online encyclopedia", "a fact-checking site", "a video site"). Inquiry days may use the real web
under the teacher's guidance and the stop rule below; the student page then says "your search results" and names no
site. A teacher page may say "swap in a current case" without naming a product. No real site is named in any
page (Cory, 2026-11-21).

## What a tool may and may not do

A tool may give practice on an invented set of pages, searches and sources (a closed set, so every result is the
same for every student), check a citation against a model, sort claims into "checked" and "not yet checked", and keep
a private research notebook on the device. It may not use the live web or a real search service, record what a
student searches for or types, give a verdict on a real site, or write the student's notes or product.

## Sensitive ground, and the read each needs

Each read is a fresh-agent read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md, kind `sensitivity`), on the unit's case set.

1. **Cases about politics, health, elections, religion, identity groups or conflict.** Cases are invented or chosen
   evenly across views; hoax or hate pages are described or shown in an invented form, never reproduced. Read: every
   unit's case set, 6 to 12.
2. **Unsafe or upsetting search results** (adult content, violence, scams). Students use a closed set or a
   teacher-checked list; no child is sent into open search without the stop rule (close the page, tell the adult, no
   blame), and the teacher page says what to do if one appears. Read: the stop rule and the K to 5 plans.
3. **Privacy.** No student types a name, school, place or photo into a search or a site; a tool stores nothing off
   the device. Read: the tools reader's privacy check.
4. **Honest use taught without shaming.** A student who copied is helped to cite, never accused in front of the
   class. Read: part of the 9 to 12 read.
5. **Whose voices a source set shows** (C12): each unit records whose work and voices appear. Read: the same reads.
6. **Copyright and fair use** as general guidance, never legal advice; no claim that a use is lawful.
7. **Machine-made text and images as a source:** no claim that any tool is reliable or unreliable; the check is the
   same as for any source (who made it, what it cites). Read: the 6 to 12 plans.

## Pacing and dose

Settled (Cory, 2026-11-15 and 2026-11-16): K to 5 meet one or two sessions a week; 6 to 8 and 9 to 12 about one
session every two weeks across their grades. Default time, ours and untested: K to 2, 30 sessions a year of 25
minutes (about 12.5 hours); 3 to 5, 30 sessions of 35 minutes (about 17.5 hours); 6 to 8 and 9 to 12, 18 sessions a
year of 45 minutes (about 13.5 hours a year). Cory kept these (2026-11-21). A frame may change them with a reason. No national or
professional body publishes a time figure for library instruction that we found (the AASL scheduling position
statement, revised 2019, sets none; searched 2026-11-20). The one time figure behind a method here is the 2022
lateral reading field study (about 500 high school students, six 50-minute lessons, from the Stanford news release;
the article was not re-read), so the 9 to 12 course holds at least that much lateral reading and claims no more.
