## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** End-of-unit assessment, from two unseen sources and a map: how slavery began in the English colonies, that it was legal in every colony, and how common owning enslaved people was, early leaders among them; the Triangular Trade traced and the Middle Passage's conditions; enslaved and free lives compared: held as property, denied schooling, punished for escape, and the revolts these led to; skilled artisans hired out, the owner keeping the pay; the routes to freedom told apart (a completed indenture or term of service, an owner's grant, a freedom suit by Elizabeth Freeman or Quock Walker), with Prince Hall's petitions; gradual emancipation, Massachusetts by 1783, and free Black families, property and work; a claim with evidence from two sources
- **Note:** every part of each listed code asked once; sensitivity read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) with the unit plan: the unseen sources; paper path; exit: half; printed source summaries are the fallback; one repair priority only

**Skills:**

- ss5-slavery-colonies (grade 5): taught or practised here

**Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **MA.5.T1.5:** Describe the origins of slavery, its legal status in all the colonies through the 1700s, and how common slave ownership was, including by many of the country's early leaders such as Washington, Jefferson, Madison, and Mason.
- **MA.5.T1.6:** Describe the Triangular Trade and the harsh conditions of the Middle Passage for enslaved Africans.
- **MA.5.T1.7:** Compare living and working conditions of enslaved and free Africans in the 1700s colonies, and explain how some enslaved people sought freedom.
- **MA.5.T1.7a:** Describe how enslaved people were treated as property, denied education, abused, and punished for escape, and how these conditions led to rebellions.
- **MA.5.T1.7b:** Explain that many enslaved Africans became skilled artisans and could be hired out to work.
- **MA.5.T1.7c:** Explain how some Africans were freed after service or by grant, and how some in the North sued for freedom, such as Elizabeth Freeman and Quock Walker, or petitioned legislatures to end slavery, as Prince Hall did.
- **MA.5.T1.7d:** Explain that Northern states adopted gradual emancipation, with Massachusetts outlawing slavery in 1783, and that free African Americans could have families, property, and jobs.
- **D3.4.3-5:** Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D4.1.3-5:** Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
