# Section 7.2: Removal policy, resistance and surviving nations

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-5/7/s/7.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-5 u07-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


MA.5.T4.5b/c: "Describe" law and Mashpee resistance, 100/102; T4.5d: "Explain" removal's significance, 103; T4.5: "Explain" land/authority conflict, 101. D2.His.4/5: "Explain" differing views and links to circumstances, 104. T4.5a is assessed here only through the final check, taught in 7.1.

Review sovereignty and "forced removal: movement imposed against people's choice," written grade 4 u06-3 day 117. Teach authorization (legal permission), ruling (a court's decision), implementation (putting a decision into action), on 100/101. Review "consent (people agreeing to how decisions are made)," written grade 4 u05-5 day 95; extend it to freely given agreement about relocation. Connect D2.His.6 through author labels, 104.

Teacher distinguishes the 1830 law, the 1832 ruling, disputed treaty authority and federal coercion. Jackson signed the Act; his successor oversaw the Cherokee roundup. Nations' routes/dates differ; survivors govern today. Include enslaved Black people forced west by some Cherokee slaveholders and Cherokee Freedmen descendants, without making all Cherokee people enslavers. No role play, sacred content, slurs or personal comparisons. Explain coercion plainly; analysis never requires adopting Jackson's perspective.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1a, Q1c, Q1d, Q1e, Q2a, Q2b. Q1b remains 7.1 despite the copied ownership table. Use 7.1's single component scale. All exits are paper; more than a third on an idea triggers the next named repair.

**E100, X100, invented legal record.** A made-up government authorizes negotiated relocation. People protest; soldiers later force them away. Ask: "Name the president and 1830 removal law studied today." "Explain why this invented authorization does not prove consent."
Key: Andrew Jackson, Indian Removal Act; legal permission does not establish freely given agreement, especially amid protest/force. Wrong: the Court enacted removal (A); authorization proves voluntary movement (J). Meets: "Jackson signed the Indian Removal Act. Protest and force contradict freely chosen movement." Not yet: "A legal document proves everyone agreed."
Shape: retrieve signer/title and judge authority-versus-consent in a new hypothetical record. Practice labels authorizing actors and implementation events separately, never makes this judgment or uses this record.

**E101, X101.** Reserved original pairing: the Court's Georgia-jurisdiction conclusion, then a distinct Cherokee removal account. Ask: "Explain the difference between the Court's ruling and what the government later did."
Key: Georgia had no authority to impose its laws within Cherokee territory; federal policy nevertheless forced removal. Wrong: Court ordered removal (A); treaty proves voluntary movement (J). Meets: "The ruling restricted Georgia, but federal action still forced Cherokee people west." Not yet: "The Court forced them west."
Shape: explain ruling/action divergence from a new pairing. Practice orders isolated events and labels institutions; never pairs a ruling with an implementation conclusion.

**E102, X102.** Reserve Apess's second May resolution about wood/hay and permission. Read its original aloud; print a credited short rendering. Ask: "What governing right does this collective Mashpee decision assert? Explain using its resource detail."
Key: Mashpee people decide who may take resources from their lands; taking wood/hay requires their permission. Wrong: rule over every Native nation (B). Meets: "Their government decides who may take wood from their land." Not yet: "They decide for all nations."
Shape: infer collective governmental authority from a reserved resource-permission statement. Practice identifies speakers/actions in the first resolution and historical account; no resource-right inference, second resolution or June notice M.

**E103, X103.** Fresh original route key uses distinct segments: Choctaw 1831 onset; Muscogee 1836 to 1837; Cherokee 1838 to 1839. Separate source note names deaths in forced removal and living governments. Ask: "Explain a human cost shown by these records." "Explain why these labels cannot represent one march or vanished nations."
Key: forced homeland loss/deaths/disruption; different named nations and dates, with continuing governments. Wrong: voluntary trip (J); one shared march (K); vanished nations (C). Meets: "People lost homelands and died. Distinct dates show different removals, and governments continue." Not yet: "One march ended every nation."
Shape: infer human significance plus limits of route labels. Practice annotates dated general displacement arrows separately from present civic labels; never writes a cost-plus-survival account or uses X103 segments/layout.

**E104, X104.** Reserve Jackson's "Doubtless" sentence and Ross's "By the stipulations" sentence from s07. Give separate context about settler land demand and threats to Cherokee authority. Ask: "Explain why Jackson and Ross differed in this setting, using each source." "Does explaining Jackson's view make forced removal right? Explain."
Key: Jackson normalizes relocation by comparing settler movement; Ross defends threatened possessions and national rights. Different interests and circumstances help explain views; coercion remains wrong. Wrong: every group member agreed (D); explanation approves coercion (L); merely naming disagreement (R). Meets: "Jackson promoted settler growth; Ross defended threatened homes. Explaining Jackson's interest does not justify force." Not yet: "They differed because they had different opinions."
Shape: contextual comparison using two reserved passages plus analysis/endorsement distinction. Practice tags one person's interest at a time, never completes a paired why-they-differ explanation.

**Check Shapes, cold keys:**
- **Q1a Shape:** state Worcester's ruling and explain its later action gap using S. Reserve S and the full paired response. Key: Georgia lacked jurisdiction; removal still occurred; writing did not create sovereignty.
- **Q1c Shape:** identify signer, authorized negotiation and coercive consequence. Practice never combines these into the full three-component explanation. Key: Jackson; removal treaties; federal pressure/force, not universal consent.
- **Q1d Shape:** infer opposed control and chosen-officer action from June M. No June M or action-plus-grievance answer in practice. Key: Mashpee opposition to outside government/resource control and selection of officers.
- **Q1e Shape:** explain Cherokee losses and separate nations' removal histories/survival from an unseen key. Practice never gives the full five-nation significance account. Key: loss/deaths; distinct routes/dates, resistance, rebuilding and living governments.

## Day 101

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: authorization, consent, ruling, implementation, forced removal. Frame: "The writer emphasized __ because __." Read originals aloud; student print is a credited rendering, every sentence fifteen words or fewer. Keep all student print within 350 words daily and sources within 100 each. No full website printouts. Offer raised arrows, described keys, scribe or neutral read-aloud on checks. Extend by naming an unsupported group-wide inference. Private pause and non-graphic source detail preserve the goal if distress arises.
