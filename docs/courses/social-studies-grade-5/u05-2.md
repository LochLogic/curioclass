## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->

> **Teacher explanation:** Congress gained taxing power; states retained powers. The 1791 Bill of Rights constrained federal government; later law extended many protections against states. Branches divide national work; levels divide responsibilities geographically. Native nations govern themselves as sovereign nations within the United States today. On 63 the President's oath (Article II) shows officeholders' responsibilities. Background for the teacher: District of Columbia v. Heller (2008) held the Second Amendment protects an individual right; checked 2026-11-14. Equality was an ideal unevenly protected, not a general equality guarantee in the original text.
>
> **Carried:** keep unit 4's timeline, perspective, sourcing, corroboration and cause chain. Revise claim, evidence and reasoning on 70 to 72; remove the worked claim on 72. Build branches-of-government on 64; connect arrows to provisions on 65, then remove labels for independent cases on 70. Keep blank diagrams.

MA.5.T3.1 carries "Read" and "explain" six principles/responsibilities on 63. MA.5.T3.2 carries "Explain" duties, divided/shared power and checks on 64/65. MA.5.T3.3 carries "Describe" levels on 66. D2.Civ.1.3-5 carries "Distinguish" powers/responsibilities across levels, branches and times. Connect evidence links on 63 and change/continuity on 66.

Teacher: branches are jobs; levels concern jurisdictions, not human worth. School is an institution, not a fourth constitutional government level. Native nations govern through their own sovereign authority; Onondaga Nation governs today. Preamble purposes do not grant unlimited power. Teach general welfare, limited government, representative democracy and broader legal equality explicitly. Planned-only u04-1 rule of law receives independent instruction. Article II's presidential oath differs from Article VI's official oaths. Federal means national here; jurisdiction means authority over particular matters or places. General welfare concerns shared well-being; limited government means power has legal boundaries. Representative democracy uses chosen representatives. Explain habeas corpus as protection against unlawful detention. An oath or affirmation is a formal promise.
