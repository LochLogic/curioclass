**E30, paper.** Five new original notices, withheld: A, imported sugar/molasses customs duties and enforcement, 1764; B, required stamps on newspapers, 1765; C, import duties on glass/paint/paper/tea, 1767; D, special tea-selling terms with an existing tea duty, 1773; E, closed Boston port and restricted government, 1774. "Name each notice's Act. Explain why D could cause protest despite cheaper tea." Key: Sugar, Stamp, Townshend, Tea, Intolerable; retained tax and disputed consent, not price alone. Proclamation reviewed separately, assessed in Q3. Wrong: Tea began all colonial taxes (B). Shape: identify from five descriptions plus causal explanation; practice places already named Acts/dates, never decodes unnamed notices or uses these examples.

**E31, paper.** Reserve s03's Penn 1701 annual assembly/lawmaking selection, Boston 1768 committee vote and Virginia 1764 elected-consent petition. Use original short summaries, not long quotations. "Explain how these records show governing experience before independence. Explain why parliamentary taxes caused conflict." Key: annual elected assemblies made laws and town voters chose delegates before independence; Virginia argued taxes needed locally elected consent, not Parliament's sole decision. Rights were restricted, not universal. Wrong: government began in 1776 (G); demanded no taxes (I); everyone participated (P). Shape: integrated development-to-conflict explanation across three new records; practice annotates isolated powers and eligibility, never this combined explanation or these selections. Key: Stamp Act Congress coordinated formal petitions; Sons of Liberty organized opposition including intimidation; petition versus coercion. Wrong: every resistance action was peaceful (J). Shape: infer both groups and compare methods from fresh notes; practice labels single actions by method with actor names supplied, never paired group explanation.
