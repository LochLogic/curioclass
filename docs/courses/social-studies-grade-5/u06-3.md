**E87, MA.5.T4.3a; MA.5.T4.3.** X87 is a reserved Curio Class historian summary, not a witness account: British rules obstruct United States neutral shipping; officers take sailors into naval service by force; some United States expansionists seek western lands governed by Native nations. Source to check: s06 war overview plus a public secular Tecumseh/land-pressure source. Do not invent a Native participant.
Ask: "Explain how these pressures could combine to encourage war."
Key: challenged trading rights and livelihoods, coerced sailors and expansionist land demands created overlapping motives for conflict. Land claims did not establish Native consent. The case does not prove every person favored war. Wrong: naval service was voluntary (D); trade restrictions could not cause conflict (Z); land demands prove Native consent (W).
Shape: integrate maritime and land pressures into a causal explanation from new evidence. Practice sorts isolated cause labels, not a complete three-cause explanation or this summary.

**E88, MA.5.T4.3b; MA.5.T4.3.** X88 reserves new short historical captions: naval victories, 1812; burned Capitol and White House, 1814; signed treaty, December 1814; New Orleans victory, January 1815. They are factual inputs, not causal conclusions.
Ask: "Explain how victory could build pride despite wartime losses." Then: "Explain why New Orleans could not have caused the treaty's signing."
Key: celebrated victories could encourage confidence despite defeats; January's battle follows December's signing. It cannot cause an earlier signature. Wrong: all events were United States victories (M); the battle produced the signature (N); one celebration proves every group gained (T).
Shape: explain a mixed-outcome pride relationship and refute reversed causation using dated evidence. Practice marks single event identities or before/after labels; never writes a pride/chronology argument or uses X88 captions.
