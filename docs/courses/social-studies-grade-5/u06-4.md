Review written grade 4 u02-4: opportunity cost (the next-best thing given up; not the price, not every other choice); trade (goods move from where they are made to where they are sold). Review grade 4 u04-2: forced labor: work people could not refuse. This corrects the unit plan's new-term implication for opportunity cost.

Teacher explains productivity as output per worker-hour, human capital as learned skills and knowledge, equipment as a different investment, possible pay without guarantees, external effects as reaching nonparticipants. Paid work can be harsh without having enslavement's legal status. Enslaved people were forced; never describe their labor as a chosen opportunity cost. No machinery demonstration, invented historical numbers or personal financial questions.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q4a/Q4b/Q4c, Q5a/Q5b, Q6. All paper; component scale in 6.1.

**E89, MA.5.T4.4; D2.Geo.11.3-5.** X89 is a fresh invented regional schematic: an inland river has falls; a downstream channel reaches a harbor. Draw no candidate-site boxes or supplied industry placements.
Ask: "Mark a plausible mill cluster on this map." Then: "Explain one connection between that location and trade."
Key: mark the falls along the inland river; waterpower supports mills, with a river/port connection carrying inputs or goods. Several nearby marks can be defensible. Wrong: every mill must stand on the coast (O); a link alone proves historical change (E).
Shape: choose a spatial cluster and explain its trade relationship. Practice reads already located, named industry maps and tags separate resource symbols; never chooses a site or writes this combined explanation.

**E90, D2.Eco.6; D2.Eco.13.** X90, invented: three shops each make eighteen baskets. Old equipment/no training takes six worker-hours. Better equipment alone takes three. Training alone takes two.
Ask: "Explain the productivity differences among these shops." Then: "Explain what learning could mean for later pay, including any limit."
Key: three, six and nine baskets per worker-hour; equipment and training each raise productivity, training highest here. Skills can support higher pay; no guarantee follows. All calculations independently solved. Wrong: equal totals mean equal productivity (F); equipment is human capital (P); a gain guarantees pay (Q).
Shape: compare equal output under unequal work time, distinguishing investments and possible earnings. Practice uses single equal-hour output strips and separate investment labels, never three-shop ranking or a productivity-plus-pay paragraph. Do not use baskets or these values before E90.

**E91, D2.Geo.11.3-5.** X91 reserves two newly composed qualitative historical maps: earlier coastal exchange/household production, later inland cotton-mill clusters. A nearby port and distant Southern cotton connection appear, without explanatory arrows or a conclusion caption.
Ask: "Explain the location change using one nearby and one distant connection."
Key: cloth production increasingly clusters in river mill towns; nearby ports link them to distant cotton suppliers and markets. Explain how those links support concentrated production, not merely their existence. No claim household production vanished. Wrong: a route proves change without dated comparison (E); all production stayed coastal (O).
Shape: infer spatial change through local and distant mechanisms from a new map pair. Practice annotates one period's evidence at a time, never compares paired maps or produces this complete mechanism explanation.

**E92, D2.Eco.8.** X92, made up: expanding a metal shop crowds paid apprentices. Noncustomer households hear more noise. New evening lights also help passing noncustomer walkers. Keeping the shop unchanged adds none of these changes.
Ask: "Explain the effects on people outside the exchange." Then: "Explain which cost belongs inside the work."
Key: noise is an external cost; walkers' safer passage is an external benefit. Crowded working conditions concern participants. Contrast expansion with no added effects under unchanged size. Wrong: every cost is external (G). Meets: identifies both outsider effects and the participants' cost with their relationship to the exchange. Not yet: calls apprentice wages an outsider gain.
Shape: distinguish external benefits/costs from participant costs in a new decision case. Practice labels participants in one record and outsider effects in separate records; never classifies a complete three-effect case. Reserve noise, lights and crowding.

**E93, MA.5.T4.4; D2.Eco.8.** X93 reserves a new original source-grounded cotton-processing/demand summary. It gives faster cleaning, more mill purchases and expansion of enslavers' cotton production over decades, not evaluative labels.
Ask: "Explain an immediate benefit, naming who gained." Then: "Explain a lasting cost, naming who bore it."
Key: faster cleaning/cotton supply could benefit enslavers or mill owners; expanded enslavement imposed lasting coercion on enslaved people. Do not infer all workers gained, or that forced people chose the cost. Wrong: every gain is immediate (R); forced labor was a voluntary choice (S). Supported alternatives count, with correct person and time horizon.
Shape: infer contrasting time horizons and beneficiaries from a new cotton-demand account. Practice traces production dependencies and separately tags one time clue; never writes an immediate-benefit/lasting-cost pair or uses X93.

Q4a Shape: locate five named towns and identify associated industries on new M. Reserve M; no blank-map town/industry placement in practice. Correct key: Lowell/textiles, Springfield/machinery, New Bedford/whaling, Boston/shipping, Salem/China trade. Operational repair: word bank lists industries only, allows reuse and has no paired town answers; students supply locations without elimination.
Q4b Shape: four time-horizon cells using at least two industries and named affected groups. Practice uses one dated consequence at a time, never a complete four-cell chart. Key allows sourced alternatives; do not use whale depletion unless a specific opened source supports it.
Q4c Shape: explain changing production geography plus distant interaction from M2. Reserve M2. Key: inland river mill towns connected to cotton suppliers and ports, not all activity shifting inland.
Q5a Shape: compute separate equipment and training increments from W. Reserve spools and 8/12/15. Key: four then three more per equal worker-hour. Practice uses labeled single-stage representations, never a two-increment W table.
Q5b Shape: explain human capital's possible earnings effect with a limit. Reserve W's pay question. Practice tags isolated skill/output/pay statements, never a completed causal-and-limit response.
Q6 Shape: distinguish participant injury, outsider garden damage and free navigation benefit in one new record. Keep Q6 sealed. Practice never assembles these three categories in a single case.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: productivity, human capital, external, participant, immediate, lasting. Frame: "For __, this effect lasts __ because __." Independent sources at most 100 words, student print at most 350 daily. Use large labeled arrows, neutral read-aloud/scribe and manipulable output strips. Do not turn English grammar into an economics score. Extend by challenging an unsupported pay guarantee.
