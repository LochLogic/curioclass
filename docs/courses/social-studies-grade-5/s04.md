## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the Revolution keep the Declaration's promise?"
- **Hook, day 44:** set the Declaration's equality claim beside the 1777 freedom petition from Prince Hall and other Black petitioners. What promise were they asking lawmakers to keep?
- **Co-write questions, day 44:** What did independence promise (45 to 46)? Why did Britain lose (47 to 49)? Whose choices and leadership mattered (50 to 51)? Could the first government protect rights and meet needs (52 to 55)?
- **Closing claim, day 57:** answer the compelling question using the Declaration, Hall's petition and the Onondaga Nation's account of 1779. Attribute two details and explain their significance. Allow different judgments about how far the promise was kept, grounded in the record.
- **Teaching:** model the Declaration's argument on 45, perspective on 50 and a causal explanation on 53. Guide source comparison on 56; withdraw the worked argument on 57. Revisit questions daily. Teach natural rights, legal rights, consent, rule of law, Loyalist, neutral, confederation, inflation, deflation and unemployment.


- **D1.1.3-5:** Explain why the question matters to others. Part: explain importance.
- **D2.Eco.11.3-5:** Explain inflation, deflation and unemployment. Parts: explain each meaning.
- **D2.His.10.3-5:** Compare information across historical sources. Part: compare information.
- **D3.4.3-5:** Answer the compelling question with evidence. Parts: use evidence; develop claims.
- **D2.His.16.3-5:** Develop a historical claim from evidence. Parts: use evidence; develop a claim.
- **D4.1.3-5:** Build an argument from several sources. Parts: construct arguments; use claims and evidence.

**Teacher explanation:** natural rights are inherent; legal rights have protection in law. Independence did not mean equal participation. Congress adopted the Articles in 1777; ratification ended in 1781. Massachusetts debt collection and scarce coins fueled unrest, accelerating existing reform efforts.

**Limits:** end at the Convention's opening; constitutional design belongs to unit 5. No counterclaim, battle reenactment, slur, graphic image or family financial disclosure. Religious language is studied as a source's claim, without requiring belief.

**Carried:** retain unit 3's timeline, map scale, cause chain, sourcing, corroboration and argument. Extend the timeline on 47 to 55, marking earlier dates. On 46 and 50 distinguish nations, individuals and constrained choices. Keep blank organizers; withdraw source prompts on 51 and the writing frame on 57.
