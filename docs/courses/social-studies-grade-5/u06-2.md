**E83, MA.5.T4.1; MA.5.T4.2.** Reserved X83 combines a new river-access aim summary with a new claim-boundary sketch over Native homelands. Source: treaty and expedition background, source rows to check.
Ask: "Judge Jefferson's purchase against his river-trade aim, using X83." Then: "Identify a cost this growing claim could impose on other people."
Key: control claimed over New Orleans could serve trade access; the evidence does not prove every hoped-for result. Native peoples faced competing United States claims over homelands without consent established here. Other supported judgments count. Wrong: aim alone proves success (A); the mapped area was empty (B); France's signature proves Native consent (W).
Shape: evaluate aim/action and identify another group's cost from a new evidence pairing. Practice marks separate aim, place and sovereignty details, never writes this judgment/cost pair.

**E84, MA.5.T4.2.** X84 is a distinct Article I rendering with the two signing governments identified. A separate original homeland inset names residents, not signatories.
Ask: "Explain what this agreement changes." Then: "Explain whose consent these signatures cannot establish."
Key: France transfers its territorial claim to the United States; the signatures do not establish Native nations' agreement. No inference that all Native land rights disappeared. Wrong: a sale of empty land (B); outside signatures establish Native consent (W).
Shape: infer transferred authority and a consent-evidence limit from a treaty-party record. Practice reads separate homeland and political labels, never reconstructs a signatory/consent explanation. Withhold X84's rendering and inset.

Key: correct crossing; traveling around the eastward bend adds distance, even though the destination is north. Wrong: reversed coordinate axes (C); actual river travel is the straight connector (K). No distance computation required.
