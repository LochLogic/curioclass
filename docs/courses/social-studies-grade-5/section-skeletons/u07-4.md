## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D2.His.6.3-5** (days 108, 111): Describe how the views of the people who made them shaped historical sources.
- **MA.5.T5.3** (days 108, 111): Explain the ideas and roles of people who fought slavery and sought rights for African Americans, such as Harriet Tubman, Frederick Douglass, and Sojourner Truth.
- **D2.Civ.14.3-5** (days 109, 111): Show, with examples from past and present, how people change society.
- **D2.His.16.3-5** (days 110, 111): Use evidence to make a claim about the past.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 110, 111): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **MA.5.T4.5** (days 111): Explain 1800s conflicts between Native Peoples and governments over land and self-government.
- **MA.5.T4.5a** (days 111): Describe Tecumseh's 1810 call for Native Peoples to unite against loss of their land.
- **MA.5.T4.5b** (days 111): Describe Andrew Jackson and the Indian Removal Act of 1830, which forced Southeastern Native communities west of the Mississippi.
- **MA.5.T4.5c** (days 111): Describe the Mashpee Revolt of 1833, a Massachusetts dispute over self-government.
- **MA.5.T4.5d** (days 111): Explain the significance of the Trail of Tears in 1838 for the Cherokee and other Southeastern Native communities.
- **D2.His.4.3-5** (days 111): Explain why people living in the same period saw things differently.
- **D2.His.5.3-5** (days 111): Explain how the setting of a time connects to what people thought then.
- **MA.5.T5.1** (days 111): Trace, state by state, how Northern states abolished slavery while it expanded westward, and explain the 1808 ban on importing slaves and the continuing internal slave trade until the mid 1800s.
- skill ss5-abolition (grade 5)
- skill ss5-native-sovereignty-removal (grade 5)
- skill ss5-slavery-expansion (grade 5)
