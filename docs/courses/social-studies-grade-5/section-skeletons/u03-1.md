## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 27): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **D1.2.3-5** (days 27): Name the subject concepts tied to a big question that people can interpret in more than one way.
- **MA.5.T2.1** (days 28, 29): Explain the reasons for the French and Indian War, how its costs changed British imperial policy, and the key British policies and colonial responses.
- **MA.5.T2.1a** (days 29): Identify the main British policies: the Proclamation of 1763, the Sugar, Stamp, Townsend, and Tea Acts, and the Intolerable Acts.
- skill ss5-revolution-causes (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 27:** compare the Stamp Act Congress's claim that elected representatives must consent to taxes with Parliament's claim of authority in the Declaratory Act. Whose rule should colonists follow?
> - **Co-write questions, day 27:** Why did war change British policy (28 to 30)? Who could consent to taxes (31)? How did resistance grow (32, 36 to 37)? How can accounts disagree (33 to 35)? Who shaped events (38 to 39)?
> - **Teaching:** model causal links on 29, sourcing on 33 and comparison on 34. Guide evidence selection on 35; withdraw the worked argument on 41. Return to questions daily. Teach tax, representation, boycott, legislature, charter, audience, purpose and corroborate.
>
> **Carried:** retain unit 2's timeline and map scale. Build the cause chain on 29; revise it on 36 to 37. Replace prompted source cards with independent sourcing on 33. Build corroboration on 34 and claim, evidence and reasoning on 35. Remove prompts on 35 and the writing frame on 41; retain blank organizers.
