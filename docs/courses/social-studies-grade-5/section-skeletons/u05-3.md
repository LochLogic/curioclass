## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.5.T3.5** (days 67): Explain that the new Constitution did not extend voting and property rights to women.
- **MA.5.T3.6** (days 68, 69): Read the Bill of Rights, explain the freedoms it guarantees, and research and argue with evidence why one of the first ten Amendments was included in 1791.
- **D2.Civ.3.3-5** (days 69, 70): Examine where rules, laws and key parts of the U.S. Constitution came from and what they are for.
- skill ss5-constitution (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write questions, day 60:** Why strengthen government (61)? Whose power grew through compromise (62)? How was power limited and shared (63 to 66)? Who lacked equal rights (67)? How could the text change (68)? Why add rights protections (69 to 72)?
> - **Teaching:** model reasoning from a provision to its purpose on 63 and a First Amendment argument on 70; guide source comparison on 71. Remove the worked argument on 72; offer a writing frame because ELA W.5.1 comes later. Teach preamble, constitution, representative, branch, federal, compromise, ratify and amendment.
>
> **Limits:** no counterclaim, partisan policy debate, reenactment or personal disclosure. Study all ten amendments on 69 through short paired readings; do not promise unlimited freedoms. Detailed litigation waits.
> **Carried:** keep unit 4's timeline, perspective, sourcing, corroboration and cause chain. Revise claim, evidence and reasoning on 70 to 72; remove the worked claim on 72. Build branches-of-government on 64; connect arrows to provisions on 65, then remove labels for independent cases on 70. Keep blank diagrams.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

<!-- skel -->
Error register rows (unit plan field 6) that name one of this section's days:

| | The error or first idea | What it shows and how to respond | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A rule exists only because a leader says so. | Omits public purposes and origins. On 70 connect a rule to its problem and authority. | From memory; civics teaching. |
| E | The Constitution gave everyone equal rights. | Confuses ideals with legal participation. On 67 compare the text with documented exclusions. | From memory; rights history. |
| F | The President approves constitutional amendments. | Ordinary lawmaking is confused with constitutional change. On 68 label proposal and ratification separately; check a fresh incomplete process. | From memory; Article V. |
| G | All freedoms are unlimited. | Misses legal limits. On 69 distinguish a protected activity from an unsupported absolute claim. | From memory; Bill of Rights. |
| N | No woman could own property. | Erases property-law variation. On 67 contrast an unmarried woman's ownership with married women's restricted control under coverture. | From memory; women's property history. |
| O | Every amendment creates a new individual freedom. | Misses retained rights and reserved powers. On 69 sort the Ninth and Tenth beside rights safeguards. | From memory; Bill of Rights. |
| T | A simple majority of states can ratify an amendment. | Misses the constitutional threshold. On 68 contrast half with three-fourths on a state-count diagram. | From memory; Article V. |
| W | The Ninth gives Congress undelegated powers. | Misreads retained rights as a grant of power. On 69 connect the Ninth's words to rights people keep, then correct a fresh claim about congressional power. | From memory; Bill of Rights. |
| X | No woman could vote. | Erases voting-law variation. On 67 contrast qualifying women voting under New Jersey rules with women's exclusion elsewhere. | From memory; women's voting history. |
| Y | The Tenth lists all individual freedoms. | Misreads reserved powers as a complete list of freedoms. On 69 identify who retains undelegated powers, then explain a fresh reserved-power case. | From memory; Bill of Rights. |
| AA | A simple congressional majority can propose an amendment. | Confuses an ordinary majority with Article V's proposal threshold. On 68 label two-thirds in each house on a proposal diagram, then test a fresh vote. | From memory; Article V. |

A projector enlarges print; the adult partners at home. Read originals aloud beside grade 4 to 5 explanations, at most 120 words independently per source. Offer large print, oral answers, a scribe and bilingual vocabulary: "This provision limits __ by __." Check content orally before identifying a language gap. Extend by explaining a source's limits.
