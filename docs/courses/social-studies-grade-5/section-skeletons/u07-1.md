## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 97): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.5.T4.5** (days 98, 99): Explain 1800s conflicts between Native Peoples and governments over land and self-government.
- **MA.5.T4.5a** (days 98): Describe Tecumseh's 1810 call for Native Peoples to unite against loss of their land.
- skill ss5-native-sovereignty-removal (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 97:** place the Mashpee 1833 self-government resolutions beside the Tribe's present government page. Ask what people can do when others deny their right to govern. Each child writes why the question matters to others (D1.1).
> - **Co-write questions:** What did Native nations defend (98 to 104)? How could slavery end in some places and grow elsewhere (105 to 106)? How did abolitionists work for freedom and rights (107 to 109)?
>
> **Carried:** retain unit 6's timeline, sourcing, corroboration and claim reasoning. Reopen the timeline at 1810 on 98; add a parallel slavery strand on 105. Tie perspective to interests and circumstances on 100; remove filled comparisons on 104. Return to unprompted sourcing on 108; withdraw sentence frames on 110 while retaining vocabulary and access supports. Opportunity cost is not a model for forced removal or enslavement.
