## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D2.His.10.3-5** (days 56, 58): Compare what different historical sources say about the past.
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 56, 58): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D2.His.16.3-5** (days 57, 58): Use evidence to make a claim about the past.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 57, 58): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **MA.5.T2.4** (days 58): Read the Declaration of Independence and explain its main argument, its reasons for independence, and its key ideas on equality, natural and legal rights, and the rule of law.
- **MA.5.T2.5** (days 58): Describe the impact of later Revolutionary War events, locate them on a map, and explain why America won and Britain lost.
- **MA.5.T2.5a** (days 58): Describe the Battles of Trenton and Saratoga.
- **MA.5.T2.5b** (days 58): Describe the Continental Army's winter at Valley Forge.
- **MA.5.T2.5c** (days 58): Describe the Battle of Yorktown.
- **MA.5.T2.6** (days 58): Explain that many Americans stayed loyal to Britain or neutral, and that Native Peoples and free and enslaved Africans fought on both sides.
- **MA.5.T2.7** (days 58): Compare the impact of leaders such as John Adams, Franklin, King George III, Jefferson, Hamilton, Madison, Washington, and Lafayette during the Revolution and early Republic.
- **MA.5.T2.8** (days 58): Explain why the Articles of Confederation were adopted in 1781, their weaknesses as a plan of government, and why they failed.
- **MA.5.T2.9** (days 58): Analyze the causes of Shays' Rebellion and explain why it was a key event leading to the Constitutional Convention.
- **D2.Eco.11.3-5** (days 58): Explain what inflation, deflation and unemployment mean.
- skill ss5-independence-war (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Closing claim, day 57:** answer the compelling question using the Declaration, Hall's petition and the Onondaga Nation's account of 1779. Attribute two details and explain their significance. Allow different judgments about how far the promise was kept, grounded in the record.
> - **Teaching:** model the Declaration's argument on 45, perspective on 50 and a causal explanation on 53. Guide source comparison on 56; withdraw the worked argument on 57. Revisit questions daily. Teach natural rights, legal rights, consent, rule of law, Loyalist, neutral, confederation, inflation, deflation and unemployment.
