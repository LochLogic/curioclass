## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.5.T2.2** (days 39, 42): Use a historic map of 1770s Boston to locate key sites and analyze the roles of Massachusetts figures such as Samuel Adams, Crispus Attucks, Paul Revere, and Phillis Wheatley.
- **D2.His.16.3-5** (days 40, 41, 42): Use evidence to make a claim about the past.
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 40, 42): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 41, 42): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **D2.His.10.3-5** (days 42): Compare what different historical sources say about the past.
- **D2.His.11.3-5** (days 42): Infer who a source was meant for and why it was made, using clues in the source itself.
- **MA.5.T2.1** (days 42): Explain the reasons for the French and Indian War, how its costs changed British imperial policy, and the key British policies and colonial responses.
- **MA.5.T2.1a** (days 42): Identify the main British policies: the Proclamation of 1763, the Sugar, Stamp, Townsend, and Tea Acts, and the Intolerable Acts.
- **MA.5.T2.1b** (days 42): Explain the slogan "no taxation without representation."
- **MA.5.T2.1c** (days 42): Describe the roles of the Stamp Act Congress, the Sons of Liberty, the Boston Tea Party, the Suffolk Resolves, and the early battles at Lexington, Concord, and Bunker Hill, ending with the British leaving Boston.
- **MA.5.T2.1d** (days 42): Describe women's roles in boycotts, writing for liberty, running farms and businesses, raising funds, and supporting the Continental Army.
- **MA.5.T2.3** (days 42): Explain how colonial governments, such as legislatures, town meetings, and charters of rights, developed and helped lead to the Revolution.
- skill ss5-revolution-causes (grade 5)
- skill ss5-sourcing-corroboration (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write questions, day 27:** Why did war change British policy (28 to 30)? Who could consent to taxes (31)? How did resistance grow (32, 36 to 37)? How can accounts disagree (33 to 35)? Who shaped events (38 to 39)?
> - **Closing claim, day 41:** answer the compelling question about colonial resistance using the Stamp Act Congress resolutions, the Declaratory Act and George Hewes's Tea Party recollection (published 1834, decades later). Defend a condition for justified resistance through two attributed details and reasoning. Different conclusions are possible; revisit whose rights were denied using Wheatley's letter.
>
> **Carried:** retain unit 2's timeline and map scale. Build the cause chain on 29; revise it on 36 to 37. Replace prompted source cards with independent sourcing on 33. Build corroboration on 34 and claim, evidence and reasoning on 35. Remove prompts on 35 and the writing frame on 41; retain blank organizers.

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

<!-- skel -->
Error register rows (unit plan field 6) that name one of this section's days:

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| E | An argument needs no evidence-based reasoning. | Evidence is not connected to the question. On 35 and 40 contrast "resistance was right" with a conditional historical claim supported by an attributed detail and reasoning. Have students repair one link, then supply a new one. | From memory. |
| L | A map establishes a person's intentions. | Confuses location with motive. On 39 distinguish a mapped site from evidence about purpose. | From memory; map and profile records. |
| O | Attucks's death tells us his intentions. | Invents motive from an outcome. On 39 separate documented influence from uncertain intentions. | From memory; Attucks scholarship. |
| U | A historical question matters only to its asker. | Misses significance for others. On 43 connect a fresh question to another reader's historical decision. | From memory. |
| V | A question's date is an idea open to interpretation. | Confuses a factual label with an interpretive concept. On 43 contrast a date with two meanings of rightful authority. | From memory. |
