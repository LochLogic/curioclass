## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

Error register rows (unit plan field 6) that name one of this section's days:

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Personal interest alone establishes a question's importance to others. | Omits another audience's stakes. On 149 connect a question to people beyond the writer. | C3; predicted error. |
| B | A civic idea has only one defensible interpretation. | Treats interpretation as fixed recall. On 149 distinguish a fact from two meanings of winning a right. | C3; predicted error. |
| K | Any topic-related question helps answer the compelling question. | Omits the connection between questions. On 149 explain what each supporting answer would contribute. | C3; predicted error. |
| Q | A list of facts answers a request for interpretations. | Confuses evidence with an arguable idea. On 149 distinguish a date from two meanings of effective. | C3; predicted error. |
| R | A personal preference is a defensible historical interpretation. | Omits support. On 149 contrast a preference with two source-supported readings of winning. | C3; predicted error. |

Key: ask what decision-makers did with the petition; influence could mean changing public discussion or changing a policy. Errors B, K. | P3 and P5. |
