## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 127): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.5.T5.7** (days 128, 129, 130): Describe African American life after the Civil War under Jim Crow, including limited opportunity, segregation, white supremacist violence such as the Ku Klux Klan, and the unifying role of Black churches, groups, and newspapers.
- skill ss5-jim-crow (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 127:** put the 14th Amendment's equal protection beside a paraphrased school segregation law. Ask how both could exist. Explain why the question matters to people beyond our class.
> - **Co-write questions:** How were rights blocked, and how did communities respond (128 to 130)? Who gained voting protection (131 to 132)? How did organizing change law (133 to 135)? What did other movements learn and change (136 to 139)?
> - **Teaching:** source documents before using them. Model law against experience on 128 and causal reasoning on 134; guide comparisons on 136, then step back. Rehearse claim, evidence and reasoning before independent writing on 140. Teach segregation, discrimination, suffrage, enforcement, boycott, coalition and accessibility.
>
> **Carried:** keep unit 8's timeline, sourcing, corroboration and claim reasoning. Revise continuity and change on 128 with separate law and experience columns; students hold it on 140. Return to source-sourcing on 129 without prompts. Tie cause-effect-chain to organizing, decisions and enforcement on 134; revise across movements on 137. Withdraw filled charts on 140, retaining access supports.
