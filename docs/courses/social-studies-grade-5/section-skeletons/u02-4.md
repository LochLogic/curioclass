## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D3.4.3-5** (days 24, 25): Use evidence to build claims that answer a big inquiry question.
- **D4.1.3-5** (days 24, 25): Make arguments from claims and evidence drawn from several sources.
- **MA.5.T1.5** (days 25): Describe the origins of slavery, its legal status in all the colonies through the 1700s, and how common slave ownership was, including by many of the country's early leaders such as Washington, Jefferson, Madison, and Mason.
- **MA.5.T1.6** (days 25): Describe the Triangular Trade and the harsh conditions of the Middle Passage for enslaved Africans.
- **MA.5.T1.7** (days 25): Compare living and working conditions of enslaved and free Africans in the 1700s colonies, and explain how some enslaved people sought freedom.
- **MA.5.T1.7a** (days 25): Describe how enslaved people were treated as property, denied education, abused, and punished for escape, and how these conditions led to rebellions.
- **MA.5.T1.7b** (days 25): Explain that many enslaved Africans became skilled artisans and could be hired out to work.
- **MA.5.T1.7c** (days 25): Explain how some Africans were freed after service or by grant, and how some in the North sued for freedom, such as Elizabeth Freeman and Quock Walker, or petitioned legislatures to end slavery, as Prince Hall did.
- **MA.5.T1.7d** (days 25): Explain that Northern states adopted gradual emancipation, with Massachusetts outlawing slavery in 1783, and that free African Americans could have families, property, and jobs.
- skill ss5-slavery-colonies (grade 5)
