## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 113): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.5.T5.2** (days 114, 115): Identify the major causes of the Civil War, such as slavery, western competition, and the rise of the Republican Party, and its key outcomes, such as the end of slavery and Reconstruction.
- **MA.5.T5.5** (days 114): Describe Abraham Lincoln's role in building the Republican Party and his Civil War actions, including the Emancipation Proclamation and the 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments.
- **D2.His.14.3-5** (days 115, 116): Explain the likely causes and effects of events and developments.
- skill ss5-civil-war (grade 5)
- skill ss5-reconstruction-amendments (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 113:** compare original captions showing slavery's legal status in 1860 and the 1865 amendment. Ask what happened between them. Each child explains why the question matters to others.
> - **Co-write questions:** Why did states secede (114 to 116)? What changed through war (117 to 118)? How did people change laws and lives (119 to 122)?
> - **Teaching:** model weighing causes on 114, guide on 115, withdraw filled chains on 116. Model a history's central claim before guided practice on 123. Source every document first. Teach secession, Union, Confederacy, emancipation, ratification, citizenship, equal protection and Reconstruction.
>
> **Carried:** retain unit 7's timeline, sourcing, perspective, corroboration and claim reasoning. Revise the cause chain into connected, weighted causes on 114; students hold it on 116. Return to branches on 119, separating executive action from amendment-making. Tie continuity and change to law and lived experience on 120; revise on 122. Withdraw completed charts on 124, retaining vocabulary and access supports.
