## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 1): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.5.T1.1** (days 2, 3): Explain early relations between English settlers and Native Peoples, including disease, differing views of land ownership, and conflicts such as the Pequot War and King Philip's War.
- skill ss5-colonial-encounters (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 1:** compare an original New England homeland sketch with the Rhode Island charter's land grant. Who could promise land where other people already governed?
> - **Co-write questions on day 1:** How did disease change relationships (2)? What did agreements mean (3)? Why did wars happen (4 to 5)? Who lives and governs here today (6)? Why found colonies (7 to 8)? Why did English institutions persist (9)? How did climate, crops and labor connect (10 to 11)?
> - **Teaching:** model source identity and purpose on day 2, context on 4, and evidence and reasoning on 5. Students select evidence on 8 and 12. Revisit questions daily. Teach colony, charter, treaty, ownership, climate, labor and evidence with examples.
>
> **Carried:** read the century timeline on day 2, add agreements and wars on 3 to 5, revisit on 9. Read scale on 1 and 10; use it on 12. Build the map key and regions on 10, revise for labor on 11, apply on 12. Perspective connects land views on 2, war accounts on 4 to 5 and present voices on 6. Keep these models; withdraw the worked claim on 12.
