## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

<!-- skel -->
Error register rows (unit plan field 6) that name one of this section's days:

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 133 | X133, reserved original caption from the National Park Service's Little Rock Nine account: "In 1957, the Arkansas National Guard blocked Black students from entering Central High School. Federal troops later escorted them inside." After Brown outlawed school segregation in 1954, what does this evidence establish about protection and access? Key: the right existed, but officials blocked access; enforcement enabled entry. It does not establish equal experience in every school. Error A. | Q4 foundation; A. |
| 134 | X134, reserved original historian caption from the listed boycott document's editorial Details, not leaflet words: "After Rosa Parks's arrest, Jo Ann Robinson and the Women's Political Council circulated a boycott appeal. Religious and civic leaders met at a church; a committee including King and Abernathy revised the appeal, and volunteers distributed it." How does this show organizing beyond one person? Key: women organizers, church networks, leaders and volunteers shared different tasks. Error M. | Q4, Q5; M. |
| 135 | X135, reserved original caption from the listed Hamer and federal law histories: "Marchers demanded jobs and freedom. Hamer challenged exclusion of Black voters from political representation. The 1964 Act barred discrimination in public accommodations and employment; the 1965 Act strengthened voting protection." How did organizing seek change, and how did the laws differ? Key: public demands and representation challenges pressed for rights; distinguish accommodations and jobs from voting enforcement. Error A; dates alone as cause is F. | Q4 foundation; A, F. |
