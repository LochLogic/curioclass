## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.3-5** (days 44): Say why a big inquiry question matters to other people, such as classmates and adults.
- **MA.5.T2.4** (days 45, 46): Read the Declaration of Independence and explain its main argument, its reasons for independence, and its key ideas on equality, natural and legal rights, and the rule of law.
- skill ss5-independence-war (grade 5)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 44:** set the Declaration's equality claim beside the 1777 freedom petition from Prince Hall and other Black petitioners. What promise were they asking lawmakers to keep?
> - **Co-write questions, day 44:** What did independence promise (45 to 46)? Why did Britain lose (47 to 49)? Whose choices and leadership mattered (50 to 51)? Could the first government protect rights and meet needs (52 to 55)?
> - **Teaching:** model the Declaration's argument on 45, perspective on 50 and a causal explanation on 53. Guide source comparison on 56; withdraw the worked argument on 57. Revisit questions daily. Teach natural rights, legal rights, consent, rule of law, Loyalist, neutral, confederation, inflation, deflation and unemployment.
>
> **Carried:** retain unit 3's timeline, map scale, cause chain, sourcing, corroboration and argument. Extend the timeline on 47 to 55, marking earlier dates. On 46 and 50 distinguish nations, individuals and constrained choices. Keep blank organizers; withdraw source prompts on 51 and the writing frame on 57.
