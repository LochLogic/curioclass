Teacher separates permission in law, an individual record and a household proportion. Georgia's 1751 change completes legality across thirteen colonies, not universal ownership. Restrictions varied by time/place; do not claim every colony banned all schooling. Families existed despite legal threats. Explain Washington and Jefferson as enslavers through records. Never treat people as an inventory of goods.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1. Q4's restrictions/rebellion content is taught here although the skeleton assigns it to section 2.3. Use section 2.4's operative packet.

- **E18, paper.** Two expressly invented archival summaries: "A northern household record lists an enslaved person." "An early leader's private record lists someone he enslaves." Separate factual reference: Georgia permitted slavery from 1751. Ask: "Explain what these records establish, and cannot establish, about household ownership." "Was slavery then permitted in all thirteen colonies? Explain." Key: northern slavery and a leader's enslavement; no household proportion without counts/denominators; yes, Georgia's last ban had ended. Wrong: north excluded (C); every household enslaved someone (K). Shape: bounded inference from two individual records plus legality conclusion; teaching locates single records separately, never this pair/combined limitation. Replaces already-used Cambridge case.
- **E19, paper.** Made-up people: "Ruth is enslaved. She sews and has a child. Her enslaver can separate them through sale. He blocks reading lessons and punishes escape. Anne is a free Black seamstress. She keeps wages and lives with her child. She faces racial exclusion." Ask: "Compare their control over work and family life. Explain the restrictions Ruth faces." Key: both form families and sew; Ruth lacks labor control and family security, is denied learning and punished for escape; Anne has greater control without equality. Wrong: no enslaved families (D); Ruth cannot sew skillfully (Q); Anne has guaranteed equal rights (O). Shape: two-person comparison plus restriction explanation; teaching uses single rights fragments, never paired biographies or comparison paragraphs.
- **E20, paper.** Reserved original summaries of real sources. A: Unidentified colonial official, 1739, describes people marching south through Georgia toward Spanish Florida. B: George Cato's inherited family account, recorded about 1937, describes opposition to slavery and a plan to join the Spanish in Florida. Ask: "What freedom-seeking action do both records support? Explain how denied freedom helps explain it." Key: organized southward movement toward Florida to escape bondage; B is later family testimony, not eyewitness reporting. Wrong: ordinary employment (B); no resistance (E); only armed fighting counts (L). Shape: supported shared action and purpose across two accounts; teaching uses Fort Mose settlement history and separate action symbols, not paired Stono accounts or shared-action prose.

Q1 Shape: explain laws, retrieve year, describe prevalence and identify a leader through fresh panels. Practice keeps those decisions separate, never a combined list or option elimination. **R20, held paper check:** Invented record: a parent seeks permission to visit a child living elsewhere. "Explain what this request shows about family bonds despite separation." **R21, held paper check:** Invented enslaved adults ask officials for release, without an armed action. "Explain how this record challenges the claim that resistance meant only revolt."
