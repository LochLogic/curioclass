## 1. Standards and practices


MA.5.T1.5: "Describe" legality and uneven ownership, 18. MA.5.T1.7: "Compare" living/working conditions, 19. MA.5.T1.7a: "Describe" property status, blocked education, abuse, escape punishment and rebellion, 19/20. Connect prompted source work to D3.4 on 20.

Written review: forced labor: work people could not refuse, grade 4 u04-2 day 66; perspective: two sides of one event, u05-3 day 89; limit: what the evidence cannot show (this does not prove ___), u07-6 day 158; petition: a written request to officials, grade 3 u05-6 day 112. Teach resistance: action against denied freedom, including escape, requests and revolt.

Teacher separates permission in law, an individual record and a household proportion. Georgia's 1751 change completes legality across thirteen colonies, not universal ownership. Restrictions varied by time/place; do not claim every colony banned all schooling. Families existed despite legal threats. Explain Washington and Jefferson as enslavers through records. Never treat people as an inventory of goods.

- **E19, paper, exit: half.** `s02-X19a` (student-page Exit box): Made-up people: Ruth is enslaved. She sews and has a child. Her enslaver can sell them apart. He blocks reading lessons and punishes escape. Anne is a free Black seamstress. She keeps wages and lives with her child. She faces racial exclusion. Compare their control over work and family life. Key: Both sew and have families. Ruth lacks labor control and family security; Anne has greater control without equality. `s02-X19b` (half slip): Use Ruth's made-up record. Explain the restrictions she faces. Key: Sale threatens family security; her enslaver blocks learning and punishes escape. Shape: Compare two biographies; practice uses single rights fragments. Explain restrictions in held Ruth case, rather than annotate one clause. Never model these cases before the exit.
- **E20, paper, exit: half.** `s02-X20a` (half slip): A: A colonial official in 1739 describes people marching south toward Spanish Florida. B: George Cato retells inherited family testimony, recorded about 1937. It describes opposing slavery and trying to join the Spanish in Florida. Name the freedom-seeking action both records support. Key: Organized southward movement toward Florida to escape bondage. B is inherited testimony, not eyewitness reporting. `s02-X20b` (half slip): Use the two Stono summaries. Explain how denied freedom helps explain this action. Key: People sought escape from control of their lives; resistance included flight as well as revolt. Shape: Infer shared action from withheld accounts, unlike isolated action symbols. Explain motive from held accounts; no shared-action prose in practice. Never model these cases before the exit.
