Q6b Shape: identify amendment/protection from ten new cases. Reserve printer, musket, quartering, shop search, repeat prosecution, counsel/trial, civil jury, bail, unlisted right and library-power cases from practice. Cold key: I press; II keep/bear arms; III peacetime owner consent; IV unreasonable search/specific supported warrants; V double jeopardy; VI speedy/public criminal trial and counsel; VII qualifying civil jury; VIII excessive bail; IX retained rights; X reserved powers. Amend case 4 to an unsupported general warrant, not merely no warrant. Case 7 says federal common-law civil suit, not every suit. Case 10 explicitly states no delegated federal authority over this purely local library decision. No inference that all library-related federal laws are invalid. Preserve ten independent components.

Q7 Shape: compare withheld Mason A, Congress B and Fourth Amendment, then construct a two-source explanation of inclusion in 1791. A's broad-warrant objection differs from B's demands for limits/public confidence. Reserve these exact passages and the pair; practice isolated source links. Meets: "Mason condemned broad warrants; Congress reported demands against abuse. Those demands help explain explicit federal limits." Does not: "The amendment ended every search because both sources are old" (G). Neither source proves everyone's single motive.

Q8 Shape: construct dated four-event timeline and compare 1789 developments. Reserve inauguration/June proposal/September proposal/ratification cards. Cold order: April 30, June 8, September 25, 1789; December 15, 1791. Government operated during amendment proposals. Q1/Q2/Q3a/Q3b/Q4/Q9 shapes are in 5.1/5.2; Q3c/Q5/Q6a/Q7 origins in 5.3.

**E71, MA.5.T3.6; D2.His.10.3-5, paper.** X71 reserves Jefferson's December 20, 1787 "Let me add" sentence and Madison's June 8, 1789 "The applications for amendments" clause. Supply credited short renderings with maker/date/audience.
Ask: "Explain what each source establishes about reasons for a bill of rights."
Key: Jefferson asserts entitlement against government to Madison; Madison urges Congress to answer constituents' anxiety. Distinct contributions, not shared motives proved by matching words. Wrong: matching words prove same purpose (H); different details make comparison invalid (Q). Meets: "Jefferson asserts an entitlement; Madison reports public pressure." Does not: "Same topic means same motive."
Shape: produce complementary source findings with provenance. Practice marks separate audience clues, never paired findings or reserved sentences.

**E72, MA.5.T3.6; D4.1.3-5, paper.** Replace X72 Hall with New York ratifying convention's July 26, 1788 petition-right declaration. Pair with reserved Madison June 8, 1789 "But whatever may be" paragraph. Short renderings convey New York's declared petition right and Madison's explanation of rights declarations limiting power.
Ask: "Argue why petition protection belonged in the Bill of Rights ratified in 1791. Use both sources and explain each detail's link."
Key: explicit protection answers demands to safeguard requests and restrain power. New York documents the desired right; Madison explains the limiting purpose. No guaranteed relief or exclusive causal claim. Meets: "New York declared petition rights; Madison explained power limits. Protecting requests therefore helped restrain government." Does not: "Petitions are good. Two sources mention rights" (I).
Shape: new two-source argument with attributed details/reasoning. Practice uses a three-source banner with separate evidence labels/arrows, never this pair or complete two-source paragraphs.

**E72T, D2.His.1.3-5, paper.** New equal-interval 1754 to 1791 strip and dated captions: war starts 1754; independence 1776; Articles agreed 1777/in force 1781; Convention and Northwest Ordinance 1787; constitutional ratification 1788. Captions distinguish 1791 Bill of Rights from constitutional ratification.
Ask: "Place these events on the timeline. Explain one overlap during 1787."
Key: correct order and equal year spacing; Convention and Ordinance occurred while Articles operated. Wrong: later event instantly erases earlier government (P).
Shape: construct chronology and explain simultaneity. Practice inspects scale and labels isolated dates on a supplied timeline; no construction of this event set or overlap explanation.

**R75, one assigned fresh paper retry per child.** These are fully specified invented records, not original check cases.
- R1, power: "Congress passes a coinage bill. The President rejects it. Both houses reconsider." Ask: "Explain how Congress can pass this bill over the objection." Key: two-thirds of each house can override, limiting the President (K); President alone makes law is B.
- R2, guarantee: "An unmarried shop owner controls her property. A national document promises liberty without granting equal property rights." Ask: "Explain why this ownership does not establish equal national guarantees." Key: one actual owner's rights do not establish a universal guarantee (E); women cannot own is N.
- R3, process: "Congress proposes through two-thirds of each house. The Article V state-ratification threshold is missing." Ask: "Write the missing threshold and name who ratifies." Key: three-fourths of states; no presidential approval (T/F assigned only to actual error). For AA instead give state ratification completed and ask missing congressional threshold: two-thirds each house.
- R4, text: new attributed Ninth rendering, "Listing some rights does not cancel other rights people keep." Ask: "Explain what this provision protects." Key: retained unlisted rights, not congressional powers (W/O).
- R5, sources: "Archive notice A records a request for a rights clause. Notice B records officials fearing unchecked power." Ask: "Explain each notice's contribution to understanding the proposal." Key: A demand, B concern about power; complementary, not identical evidence (H/Q/I by actual miss).
- R6, time: "Project A runs from 1923 through 1928. Project B runs from 1926 through 1930." Ask: "Draw their dated bands and mark the overlap." Key: 1926 through 1928 (P).

- R4b, limits: new assembly caption, "A headline says peaceable-assembly protection also protects violent attacks." Ask: "Correct the headline using the provision's limit." Key: peaceable assembly does not protect violent attacks (G). For Y instead supply a fresh Tenth rendering reserving undelegated, nonprohibited powers; ask what is reserved. Key: powers, not a complete list of individual freedoms.
- R7, representation: new exhibit label, "Equal Senate seats created the Three-Fifths population count." Ask: "Correct this label by distinguishing the two settlements." Key: Senate equality concerns state representation; the population count affected House representation and electors (U/V according to actual error). D/R/M variants each replace that label with one claim: "The count granted partial votes"; "The count measured human worth"; or "Agreement proves the bargain was just." Ask only: "Correct the label and explain its mistake." Keys: no individual votes; apportionment not worth; agreement preserved slavery and was not proof of justice.
- R8, office: made-up town record, "Council appointed its health inspector. Voters now elect the inspector. Inspectors still check food stalls." Ask: "Explain the change and continuity." Key: selection changed; inspection duty remained (Z). For C ask only which government level the town council belongs to: local, not a branch.
- R9, schools: made-up record, "A state requires clear school exit paths. Town staff inspect school exits after a blockage." Ask: "Explain why school responsibilities belong to more than the town here." Key: state sets a requirement, local staff implement it (L).
- R10, audience: made-up reader, "A museum visitor compares powers under different governing documents." Ask: "Explain why our big question might help this reader." Key: it guides comparison of power limits and whose rights were protected, beyond liking history (S).
- R11, purpose: made-up charter, "Its introduction promises safety. Its body authorizes council inspections of public bridges only." Ask: "Explain whether the promise authorizes inspections everywhere." Key: no; a purpose does not erase the express power limit (J). For A instead give a new litter rule adopted after blocked drains; ask its origin and purpose. Key: blockage prompted a rule to keep drains clear, not simply a leader's wish.

Shape: R1 isolated override actor/limit; R2 bounded guarantee inference; R3 threshold completion; R4 clause meaning; R5 complementary archive notes; R6 two-band overlap. Response-group models exclude coinage, shop owner, missing-threshold format, Ninth rendering, archive notices and these project bands.
