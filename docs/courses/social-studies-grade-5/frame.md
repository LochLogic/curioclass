# Course frame: Grade 5: The United States to the Civil War, and the Fight for Civil Rights

Course id ss-k5 (grade 5 row), grade 5, draft frame, written 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10, in time order from the colonies to the twentieth century's civil rights movements, in
  Massachusetts's order (5.T1 to 5.T5).
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 152 in units and 8 at course level (4 for two course-level checks, 4 respond
  days). It assumes one social studies lesson of about 40 minutes every school day. Grade 5 carries the largest
  anchor load of the elementary years (21 rows holding about 80 anchor codes and parts, from the colonies to
  1990s disability law), so it takes the full yardstick and nothing less.
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 grade 5 (5.T1 to 5.T5, every lettered part) is the content anchor, and every
  code is owned by a row placed below; C3 (3 to 5 band) carries the skills. C3 places the counterclaim in grades 6
  to 8, so no grade 5 unit or check asks students to answer a counterclaim; they name two readings of an idea
  (D1.2) and weigh two accounts (D2.His.10). The check set (California, Tennessee, New York) is read unit by unit
  at the unit plan; this frame names candidate additions with their source. The values rule governs every unit:
  rule a for Worcester v. Georgia and the 2015 marriage ruling, rule b for the causes of the Civil War (the
  seceding states' own declarations).
- **Base text.** None (adoption bar section 1). Days are Curio Class's own, from open pieces adapted with credit:
  National Archives and Library of Congress documents (the Declaration, the Constitution and Bill of Rights, the
  seceding states' declarations, the Emancipation Proclamation, the amendments), Founders and Lewis and Clark
  journal excerpts in the public domain, Frederick Douglass's own writing (public domain), National Park Service
  pages, public-domain engravings and maps. Native nations' own pages are read and linked. NonCommercial programs
  (Massachusetts's Investigating History grade 5, the New York toolkit) are reference only.
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit runs the full arc. Grade 5's step up comes in unit 3: each source sourced
  unprompted (who, when, for whom, why), and two accounts of one event weighed by their sources, then a written
  claim with evidence from both. Before unit 3, tasks ask a claim from given sources at grade 4's depth (what two
  sources both confirm), never two accounts weighed by their makers. Unit 10's research writes its own supporting
  questions and finds its own sources.
- **Informed action.** Once, in unit 10, after the civil rights research: small groups name a school or local
  problem, compare two or three strategies from the year's history, predict what each would do, decide by an
  agreed process (grade 3's deliberation, retrieved) and carry one out. Students choose; the course never picks
  a cause.
- **Reading level.** Adapted and excerpted texts at a grade 4 to 5 band; founding documents and Douglass read in
  short excerpts with a glossary and a sourcing step; a short history written for young readers in unit 8.
