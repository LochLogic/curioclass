The 1791 protections constrained federal power; not every amendment created a new individual freedom. A warrantless search is not automatically unlawful. No personal disclosure, religious practice or weapon handling. Coverture generally placed a married woman's legal/property control under her husband. Amendment here means an alteration to the Constitution's wording. Retained means kept; reserved means set aside for those named.

**E67, MA.5.T3.5, paper.** X67, invented: "In 1791, two unmarried sisters own farms in different states. One qualifies to vote under her state's rules. The other's state excludes her."
Ask: "Explain what these cases show about both rights and the federal Constitution's limits."
Key: ownership was possible, voting varied, neither equal right was federally guaranteed. Wrong: equality achieved (E); none could own (N); none could vote (X).
Shape: reconcile two hypothetical profiles and both constitutional limits. Practice reads isolated restrictions, never sisters, combined profiles or a complete both-rights comparison.

**E68, MA.5.T3.6, paper.** X68, invented: "Two-thirds of each house propose a change. Only half the states ratify it."
Ask: "Explain whether this proposal became an amendment through this route."
Key: no; three-fourths of states must ratify; President has no approval role. A bare no does not show reasoning. Wrong: President can finish it (F); half suffices (T).
Shape: explain an incomplete process. Practice traces the real Bill of Rights and labels thresholds separately, never incomplete-proposal verdicts or a half-state card.

**E69, MA.5.T3.6; D2.Civ.3.3-5, paper.** X69's invented official says: "The Ninth gives Congress every undelegated power. The Tenth lists all individual freedoms."
Ask: "Correct each claim by explaining the provision and its purpose."
Key: Ninth prevents listed rights from denying unlisted rights retained by people; Tenth reserves undelegated, nonprohibited powers to states or people. Wrong: congressional power grant (W); complete individual-freedoms list (Y).
Shape: correct two provision/purpose claims. Practice annotates authority and restrictive wording, never false-claim correction or case-to-amendment matching.

**E70, D2.Civ.3.3-5, paper.** X70, invented class rule after interruptions and state smoke-alarm law after fatal fires. Replace Hall with Virginia's 1788 ratifying convention recommendation, item Fifteenth, requesting petition protection; pair a short credited rendering with First Amendment petition wording.
Ask: "Fill each row with its origin and purpose." Rows: class rule, state law, constitutional petition protection.
Key: interruptions/fair turns; fire deaths/fire safety; documented demands for petition protection/limits against obstructing requests for redress. Virginia documents a demand, not the sole cause. Wrong: leader's wish alone explains origin (A); disconnected details without relevance (I). A claim that every petition succeeds is an unlettered source-limit miss, not I.
Shape: infer origins/purposes across three institutional cases. Teaching keeps source identity, requested action and possible purpose separate, never this table or Virginia selection. Hall appears in no check.
