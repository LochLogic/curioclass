# Section 8.3: Rights, organizing and Reconstruction

## Header


DEFAULTS and 8.1's one evidence scale apply. Copied sensitivity notes name needed attention, not a completed review.

## 1. Standards and practices


T5.6 "Explain" citizenship/equal protection and race-based voting protections, 120 to 121; Civ.12 "Explain" people changing laws and laws changing society, 120 to 121; T5.2 "Identify" Reconstruction outcomes, 122. His.14 connects through cause/effect tags on 122.

Teacher distinguishes citizenship, due process, equal protection for persons and voting protection against race/color/previous-enslavement discrimination. Retain the citizenship clause's United States jurisdiction condition. The 15th did not prohibit exclusion by sex or begin all Black voting. Some Black men voted and held office under earlier Reconstruction laws. Native citizenship was not universal in 1868; Onondaga Nation retains sovereignty. No invented Native people or account of one nation speaking for all. Women's organizing and the 19th remain in unit 9.

Teach Black organizing and community school-building alongside federal aid. White supremacist violence and reduced federal protection undermined rights; 1877 did not repeal amendments or end organizing. No graphic detail, slurs, simulated exclusion or sacred content.

**E121, X121, original history captions, reference-based rather than invented records.** Louisiana 1868: "Black and white delegates helped write Louisiana's new constitution.
