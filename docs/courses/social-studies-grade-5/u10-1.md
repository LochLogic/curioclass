and are identified aloud as invented. Keys were solved cold. Alternative answers meeting the look-fors count.
Use one scale throughout: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record the help provided separately.
Teacher wrong answers do not print beside student items. No numeric or second scale.

independently assemble revised portfolio evidence. Preserve original, cue and dated revision.

**Unit assessment items owned:** P2, P3, P4, P5, assessed on 159.

- **E147 / X147.** "Made up: An exhibit credits one organizer. Volunteer records describe many contributors."
  "Who beyond our class needs accurate exhibit credits, and why?"
  Key/meets: "Volunteers need their contributions recognized so visitors understand who did the work."
  Visitors needing an accurate account also count. Wrong: "Only my grade matters" (A);
  "Only the named organizer contributed" (N).
  Shape: infer another audience's stake from conflicting exhibit credits. Practice chooses topics and
  labels actors separately; no exhibit, volunteer record or full audience-and-reason model.
- **E148 / X148.** "Made up: A researcher counts signatures on an equal-pay petition."
  "Write another question about its influence. Give two defensible meanings of influence."
  Key/meets: "How did officials respond? Influence could mean changing discussion or changing a rule."
  Accept another question about a possible effect, not a repeated count.
  Wrong: "How many signatures were there?" (K); "Influence can only mean changing a law" (B).
  Shape: complementary question plus two meanings of influence. No petition, signature count,
  paired influence meanings or complete question-and-interpretations response in practice.
- **E149 / X149.** "Made up: A study asks whether an employment rule was effective."
  "Name one needed fact. Give two defensible meanings of effective."
  Key/meets: "I need hiring outcomes. Effective could mean compliance or improved opportunity."
  Other relevant outcomes and distinct defensible criteria count.
  Wrong: "The date alone is an interpretation" (Q); "Effective has only one possible meaning" (B).
  Shape: needed evidence plus two success criteria for a fresh employment study. Practice labels
  fact, concept and one meaning separately; no employment-effectiveness case or integrated grid.

## 6. Days

All parts total forty minutes. Fifteen pairs work concurrently, 45 seconds each way, 90 seconds total.

4.5 minutes plus transitions. Rotate recorded names; everyone else hands back individual paper.

After class, return one criterion cue per practice record, about two minutes each, sixty minutes
for thirty children, plus fifteen minutes sorting exits, ours, untested. More than ten misses means
whole-class repair; otherwise a small group while others revise. A priority contrast and fresh component
retry share the protected Return and repair block; secondary needs remain recorded. No repair uses
reserved check cases. Tool: blank; no digital or key tool. Every product is made by hand.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: fact, interpretation (a meaning supported by clues), significance (why something matters).
Frame: "This clue supports __." Grade 5 u03-4 influence wording is planned only; explain the idea afresh. Before exits define
influence as effect on events and effective as achieving an intended result, without paired meanings. All daily student print, including credits, records and exits, stays within 350 words.
Each printed sentence has fifteen words or fewer. Use fresh daily extracts rather than whole-folder rereading.
Plan about 200 source words and 150 task words, with ten reading/rereading minutes inside work blocks,
ours, untested. Read difficult originals aloud; original credited renderings are non-graphic.
Large print, source descriptions, private dictation or a scribe retain the evidence demand.
Read check directions neutrally without supplying reasoning. Students may pass publicly and submit private paper.
Extension adds a justified limit to the same claim, not a new standard.
