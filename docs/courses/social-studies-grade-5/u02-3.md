Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-5/2/s/2.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-5 u02-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Use [DEFAULTS.md](DEFAULTS.md). Copied skeleton blocks are planning data, not print masters. Printed sentences have at most 15 words, with punctuation retained. Student print stays within 350 words daily; teacher prose within 600. Targets and timing are ours, untested.

Tools: None throughout; no digital or key tool. All thirty children submit paper, including dictation. Use one scale everywhere: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Record support separately; blank work does not diagnose an error. Many means more than ten of thirty. Fifteen pairs speak simultaneously, 45 seconds each way. Public talk hears six rotating children maximum, one 45-second observation each, within five minutes; record names and collect everyone's paper.

Return and repair protects ten minutes: five for one priority explanation, five for a fresh response. Teach together if more than ten share the gap; otherwise one adult group works while others use written cues. Retain unresolved gaps. Neutral summaries and a pause are available; no reenactment, graphic images, sacred practice or personal-history disclosures.

## 1. Standards and practices


MA.5.T1.7b: "Explain" artisans and hiring, 21. MA.5.T1.7c: "Explain" distinct legal routes, 22. MA.5.T1.7: "Compare" control of work/family, 23. MA.5.T1.7d: "Explain" gradual laws, Massachusetts courts and free lives, 23. Connect D3.4 through attributed evidence on 21.

Teach artisan: a skilled craftsperson; hired out: an enslaver sends someone to work for another; manumission: an enslaver legally grants freedom; emancipation: release from slavery; gradual emancipation: a law delays some people's freedom. Review written petition: a written request to officials, grade 3 u05-6 day 112. Indenture from section 1.4 is planned only; teach fixed-term bound service explicitly with independent wording.

Freeman's grade 3 u06-3 day 130 biography and Hall's grade 3 u06-4 day 133 petition are reminders, never held checks. Teach new procedures through Walker's cases and Flora's grant. Massachusetts decisions made slavery unenforceable by 1783; no gradual statute did this. Earning or keeping money alone proves neither freedom nor equality.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q3 to Q7. Q4 content belongs to section 2.2. Use section 2.4's operative packet, not placeholder ownership.

- **E21, paper, exit: half.** `s02-X21` (half slip): Made-up record: An enslaved carpenter builds doors for a merchant. The merchant pays the enslaver. The carpenter keeps a small share by permission. Explain what this shows about skilled work and legal freedom. Key: Carpentry is skilled; enslaver controls payment; keeping a permitted share does not grant freedom. Shape: Infer control/status from hiring with an earnings exception; practice separates details. Never model these cases before the exit.
- **E22, paper, exit: half.** `s02-X22a` (half slip): Four made-up records: Ada completed fixed-term service. Ben has a valid immediate freedom grant. A court rules Celia free. David has only a pending request to lawmakers. Identify the records that establish present freedom. Key: Ada, Ben and Celia; David has no decision granting freedom. `s02-X22b` (half slip): Explain why David's pending request differs from Celia's court judgment. Key: David asks lawmakers to act; Celia already has a judgment declaring freedom. `s02-X22c` (student-page Exit box): Explain whether Ada's completed term frees a newborn of an enslaved mother. Key: No. Ada has a fixed service endpoint; hereditary bondage passes through maternal status without that endpoint. Shape: Select free subset from four new records; practice follows single procedures. Distinguish request from judgment in held documents. Transfer endpoint distinction to newborn, independent of document selection. Never model these cases before the exit.
- **E23, paper, exit: half.** `s02-X23a` (student-page Exit box): Made-up cases: A Pennsylvania child is born to an enslaved mother in 1782. The 1780 act requires service until age 28. In Massachusetts in 1784, a free Black parent keeps wages and buys a home. The parent lives with a legally free child but faces discrimination. Compare who controls their labor. Key: The Pennsylvania child remains bound by delayed service; the Massachusetts parent controls paid work. `s02-X23b` (half slip): Use the two made-up cases. Explain what they establish, and leave unknown, about family rights. Key: The parent and legally free child have family security and property; discrimination persists. The records cannot establish every family experience or equality. Shape: Compare people under different rules; practice reads individual law clauses. Infer rights and limits from held cases, rather than tag a supplied right. Never model these cases before the exit.
