Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-5/5/s/5.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-5 u05-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


D1.1.3-5 carries "Explain" importance to others on 60. MA.5.T3.4 carries "Analyze" debates and "explain" union/slavery effects on 61/62. Connect sourcing on 61 and perspective on 62. Teach preamble, representative, House and Senate in context. Review written grade 4 u06-6 constitution/branch labels and grade 3 u07-5's "compromise (a plan that takes useful parts of other plans)."

Teacher: the two representation settlements differed. Counting enslaved populations increased enslavers' seats and presidential electors without giving enslaved people votes. Agreement did not make this fair. No reenactment, auction, human-fraction activity or debate over anyone's worth. People were forced into slavery, never goods or willing workers. Banneker's challenge establishes advocacy, not a successful remedy. Preamble means an opening statement of purposes; a representative is chosen to act for others. House and Senate are the two chambers of Congress.

**E60, D1.1.3-5, paper.** Invented card: "A person asks officials for a fair hearing." Ask: "Explain why our big question could matter to this person."
Key: knowing powers and rights helps the person seek fair treatment. Accept another supported public stake. Meets: "Rights limits may help this person challenge unfair treatment." Does not: "History is fun" (S).
Shape: infer another person's stake in fair treatment. Practice generates questions and source labels, never a fair-hearing relevance answer.

**E61, MA.5.T3.4, paper.** X61, invented: "Delegates want national taxes for debts. Others fear national control of state decisions. A populous state wants population-based seats. Two smaller states want equal state votes."
Ask: "Explain the power dispute. Explain how the House and Senate settlement addressed the representation dispute."
Key: effective national action competed with fear of concentrated power; population-based House representation and equal Senate representation answered different interests. Wrong: chambers reversed (V); Senate equality is the enslaved-count settlement (U).
Shape: explain two disputes using a new proposed-government case. Practice labels separate delegate concerns, never a paired power/settlement essay or X61 states.

**E62, MA.5.T3.4, paper.** Reserve Wilson's July 11 passage through the necessity of compromise. Pair an attributed short rendering with a new seats/electors diagram, excluding individual-voting arrows.
Ask: "Explain how this bargain helped agreement. Explain how it reinforced slavery."
Key: delegates accepted a bargain to hold states together; the count increased slaveholding states' representation/electors without votes or freedom for enslaved people. Wrong: partial votes (D); measured human worth (R); agreement proves fairness (M).
Shape: explain agreement and harm using withheld source/diagram evidence. Practice examines isolated claims and political-effect labels, never this paired explanation or diagram.

Cold Q4 key: House population, Senate equal state seats; enslavers gained power, enslaved people gained no rights. Cold Q9: practical stakes beyond personal interest. Allow supported explanations, not one preferred judgment.
