**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 and Q2 comparison. Q2 claim belongs to 3.5. Record each identity, date, audience, purpose and clue separately, using the same component scale. No nationality-based truth key.

**E33, paper.** Reserved Charleston agreement, July 22, 1769, original short rendering: "We sign this agreement and promise each other to follow these rules. We will encourage goods made in North America, especially our province." Caption gives signers, place, date and agreement form only. Ask: "Read this and make notes before using it as evidence." Key: Charleston subscribers, date; fellow subscribers or wider public with a clue; shared purchasing commitment, supported by promises and encouragement. Accept a wider audience if explained through circulation. Wrong: maker/date alone suffices (C). Shape: initiate a complete source note without checklist; models and practice isolate different components across different records, never a full four-component report or this agreement. Collect before offering missing prompts.

**E34, paper.** Reserve s03 Boston Port Act first provision and committee letter first paragraph. Print two original renderings: Parliament, March 31, 1774, law: "Beginning June 1, most cargo loading and unloading in Boston harbor is forbidden." Committee of Correspondence, May 13, 1774, circular letter: "Our port is closed. We face hardship and need support from other towns." Explain that these are summaries, not quotations. Ask: "What do both sources establish about shipping? Explain how one clue from each reveals audience or purpose." Key: port commerce restricted; legal prohibition directs compliance; committee's hardship/support appeal seeks sympathy or aid. Neither proves every effect or complete accuracy. Wrong: agreement proves every claim (D); only metadata matters (C); purpose makes evidence useless (M). Shape: shared fact with paired clue-to-purpose inferences; practice inventories single-source features, never a shared-fact-plus-two-inferences answer or this pair.

**E35, paper, multiple supported claims.** Reserve Franklin examination trade passage and Parliament repeal preamble. Original summaries: Franklin, colonial agent answering lawmakers, February 1766: "Without repeal, colonists will buy very few British-made goods." Parliament, March 1766, repeal explanation: "Continuing this law could harm our kingdoms' commercial interests." Ask: "Compare these sources and state a claim about repeal. Support it with one attributed detail from each and explain both links." Key sample: "Trade concerns supported repeal. Franklin warned of reduced purchases, so trade faced losses. Parliament cited commercial harm, showing lawmakers considered those losses. These passages do not prove the only cause." Not meeting: "Both were British, so both are right." Wrong: unexplained links (E); agreement proves sole cause (D); advocacy makes Franklin useless (M); nationality guarantees truth (R). Shape: integrated trade/repeal comparison and argument; practice uses separate evidence labels, origin notes and single reasoning arrows, never an integrated paragraph or this source pair.

Shape: Q1 requires spontaneous complete sourcing of two withheld Lexington accounts; no full two-source sourcing rehearsal, Parker/Barker selection or captions naming inferred audiences. Shape: Q2 compares agreement/conflict and weighs access, then makes a bounded outbreak claim; no taught first-shot debate, integrated comparison or Lexington packet. Historical chronology may recur; exact evidence and response format remain withheld.
