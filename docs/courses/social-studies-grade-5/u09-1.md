# Section 9.1: Rights in law and barriers in life

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-5/9/s/9.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-5 u09-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


D1.1: "Explain" why the compelling question matters on 127. T5.7: "Describe" forced
segregation, restricted opportunity, voting barriers, white supremacist violence and Black community
institutions on 128 to 130. D2.His.14 connects migration causes on 130.
Teacher distinguishes a constitutional rule, a discriminatory state law and an unlawful barrier.
Teach Jim Crow as laws and practices imposing racial separation and denying equal rights.
A poll tax is a tax required for voting; institutions organize community services.
Explain white supremacy as the belief that white people should dominate others.
Black people organize, learn, work and create community; harm is never their whole history.
Teach segregation as imposed racial separation and discrimination as unfair exclusion or treatment.
Name the Ku Klux Klan's white supremacist threats and killings without attack details.
Churches are civic institutions here, without religious instruction.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** Q1 and Q7. Q1 Shape: seven barrier/institution components
plus resistance reasoning from unseen A/B. Practice uses separate labels and migration maps,
never a full barrier/institution response. Q7 Shape: explain a library visitor's stake using
assessment evidence. Practice sorts evidence needs, never rehearses that visitor response.

**E127, paper.** Made up: a school council plans a display about rights in law and life.
"Write one supporting question for the display. Explain why answering it could help the council."
Key/look-fors: a smaller question about barriers or responses, with a relevant public use.
Meets: "How did newspapers help people act? The council could explain how people challenged barriers."
Wrong: "What is on our test? Only our class grade matters" (N).
Shape: compose a question and explain its use for a new audience. Practice sorts evidence needs
under supplied headings; no complete question-and-stake example.

**E128, X128, paper.** Original historian rendering: "Mississippi law required separate schools for Black and white children."
"Explain what this rule shows about equal protection and school experience."
Key/meets: "Equal protection existed, but this state rule imposed racial separation in schools."
Wrong: "The Constitution made every school equal" (A); "Children freely chose separation" (L).
Shape: infer a legal-promise/experience conflict from a new school rule. Practice annotates workplace
and transport clauses, never a school card or paired law/experience explanation. Mississippi
replaces Kentucky because the opened source supports Mississippi's ordinary-school law.

**E129, X129, paper.** Original historian caption: "Taxes and unfair tests kept Black citizens from voting.
White supremacist threats intimidated eligible citizens."
"Explain why legal voting protection did not ensure access in this record."
Key/meets: "Official barriers and violent threats prevented citizens from using their constitutional protection."
Wrong: "A protected voter could always vote safely" (A).
Shape: explain combined administrative/violent obstruction. Practice marks separate records for
actor and action, never this combined inference or caption. Never simulate a discriminatory test.

**E130, X130, paper.** Original history rendering: "During World War I, northern factories needed workers.
Black southerners faced oppression and few jobs. Newspapers shared migration news.
Northern churches and mutual aid groups helped communities confront racism. Housing discrimination continued."
"Explain a reason for moving and how an institution could help migrants."
Key/look-fors: work or safety linked to moving; news or practical/community support linked to help.
Meets: "People sought jobs. Newspapers shared opportunities, although moving did not end discrimination."
Wrong: "Black communities waited for outsiders to act" (B); "Moving ended every racial barrier" (A).
Shape: two-part migration-reason/institution-help explanation. Practice plots broad routes and
separately tags institution actions; no combined response, X130 or assessment clubs passage.

Every child submits paper. One scale per required component throughout: shown, shown with help,
not yet seen. No points or self-marking. Read exits after class, about one minute per child,
30 minutes for thirty, ours, untested. Return a private criterion cue in the next Return and repair.
More than ten missing means whole-class repair; otherwise meet a small group while others use
written moves. Each ten-minute block holds five minutes of component contrasts and five of a
fresh component retry. Related letters share one block; retain secondary gaps.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: segregation, discrimination, enforcement, migration, institution. Practice frame:
"The record names __ doing __." Withdraw filled frames for independent exits.
All student print, including credits, headings and prompts, stays within 350 words daily;
every sentence has fifteen words or fewer. Adult originals are reference; student renderings are
ours, credited. Read difficult text neutrally aloud. Use large print, described maps, dictation or
a scribe without supplying reasoning. Offer private pauses and equivalent non-graphic evidence.
No slurs, graphic harm, reenactment, simulation or personal disclosure. Extension adds a source
limit within the same goal. Independent captions normally stay below 100 words.

## 10. Two rooms

One child annotates the same sources with an adult, then answers reserved cases independently.
Replace public turns with one optional explanation. One projector shows instruction only; each
child has their own exit sheet. Credited captions replace absent scans or pictures. No visitor,
unopened photo, personal history or live page is needed.
