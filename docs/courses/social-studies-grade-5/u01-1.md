Keep these models; withdraw the worked claim on 12.

D1.1.3-5: "Explain why" the question matters to others on day 1. MA.5.T1.1: "Explain" epidemic effects, specifically attributed land practices, and agreements on days 2 and 3. Wars follow in section 1.2. Evidence selection retains prompts.

Teacher explains that a royal charter records an English claim, not universal consent. Aquinnah's communal practices do not describe every Native nation. Separate event and account dates. Modern tribal histories are sources about the past, not seventeenth-century eyewitness transcripts. No invented Native speaker, sacred account or reenactment.

Written review, ledger words: colony: a settlement far from the home country, which claims it (grade 4 u05-1 day 80); treaty: agreement between governments (u05-1 day 81); source-sourcing: maker, date, audience, purpose (u04-1 day 62); timeline: equal gaps for equal years and ten-year marks (u04-4 day 73). Review supporting question (a smaller question that helps answer the big one), u01-1 day 1. The frame's models are review. Review the word charter from kindergarten u01-2 day 5, a class agreement; explicitly teach its different historical sense here, an official grant. Introduce ownership as rights claimed over land and epidemic as disease spreading through a community.

**Unit assessment items owned:** Q7. The copied invented Native character conflicts with this job's sensitivity rule. Implement this replacement: "A made-up town plans to close a riverside path. A gardener uses it for deliveries. A walker uses it to reach the park. Why does the land decision matter to each person?" Key: delivery access and park access. Wrong: only the council cares (P). Two minutes, same standard, no invented Native person.
Shape: Q7 explains two affected people's stakes. Teaching uses maps and topic placement, never two-person access stories or rehearsed consequences.

**X1, paper, two parts.** Made-up town, no Native identity: "A town may replace its shared meadow with a storage yard." "Write two questions that would help investigate this land decision." "Explain why the decision matters to meadow users." Key example: Who uses the meadow? What would the yard change? Users could lose activity space. Accept other answerable supporting questions and supported stakes. Wrong: only the council matters (P); the yard is better without evidence (H).
Shape: generate supporting questions and explain importance from a new proposed land use. No practice question-writing about meadow conversion or a worked question pair. Practice sorts supplied topic words and connects answer days. This resolves the skeleton's question-writing/importance mismatch.

**X2, paper.** Reserved original Aquinnah card: "European diseases killed people in whole Wampanoag villages. English private land practices conflicted with Aquinnah communal practices." Attribution: Aquinnah public history, modern tribal account. "Explain one disease effect and one difference in land practices." Key: deaths harmed communities; communal/private arrangements differed without eliminating Native authority. Wrong: nobody remained (B); communal means nobody had rights (A). Accept two labeled boxes or a short paragraph.
Shape: two historical explanations from one fresh fact card. Practice uses separate date strips and rights diagrams, never this card or a disease-plus-land answer. Q1 later adds unseen grievance evidence; its packet stays sealed.

**X3, paper.** Reserved invented councils, not Native nations: "Cedar Council and Harbor Council agree to help repair a dock after storms." "Explain what this agreement promises and what it cannot prove about land consent." Key: shared storm repairs; no evidence of consent to later land transfers. Wrong: every later land claim is approved (A); the card proves an unrelated sale (H).
Shape: interpret a promise and its evidentiary boundary. Practice uses real dated relations, never dock agreements or promise/consent paired answers. The 1621 case remains retrieval because grade 3 already assessed it.

## 9. Supports and extension

Picture glossary: charter, communal, private, epidemic. Frame: "This source records __. Its date means __." Target 180 to 250 total student words daily, never over 350. Summarize adult texts; read difficult parts aloud. Offer neutral card reading, movable labels, tactile chronology and dictation without answers. Extend with a source limit.
