## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does it take to win a right?"
- **Hook, 147:** revisit Cone's account of disabled organizers seeking enforcement after a law passed. Ask whether a legal promise alone wins a right.
- **Co-write questions:** What counts as winning (148)? Whose evidence helps (150 to 152)? What changed, and how (153 to 154)? Which strategies could address our problem (155 to 156)? What happened (158)?
- **Closing claim, 158:** revise the day 153 argument about the chosen topic, using three sources from its field 10 set. Explain how organizing and legal change interacted; distinguish historical conclusions from the limited evidence of our own action.
- **Teaching:** model question revision on 148, usefulness on 151, argument on 153 and critique on 154; guide an attempt before independent work. Use the 504 case for models, other topics for independent evidence.

- **D4.4.3-5:** Critique arguments. Part: critique.

**Content parts:** MA.5.T5.8 researches one topic and analyzes its contribution to expanded rights.
**Limits:** research taught twentieth-century topics; no survey or required counterclaim. Rights are not decided by class vote. Name racism and exclusion; omit slurs, graphic images, simulations and personal disclosures. Explain disability access as a civil right, not charity.
