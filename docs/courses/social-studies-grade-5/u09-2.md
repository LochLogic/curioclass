<!-- /skel -->

T5.6a: "Describe" advocacy around the 14th/15th and its later link; 131 begins that link,
136 completes it. T5.6b: "Explain" national sex-based voting protection in 1920 without implying
every woman's first vote or safe access. T5.7's racial barriers connect on 132.
Teacher distinguishes citizenship, voting protection, earlier state access and continuing racism.
Citizenship means legal membership in a country; exclusion means keeping people from participation.
Black women sought race and sex equality together. No uniform women's viewpoint, racist quotation,
personal voting history or citizenship disclosure.

**E131, X131, paper.** Original position cards, not invented testimony:
"Advocate A accepts racial voting protection while continuing to seek women's votes.
Advocate B rejects an amendment that leaves women without sex-based voting protection."
Supply brief 14th/15th clause renderings, with no comparison answer.
"Explain these advocates' agreement and disagreement using the amendment evidence."
Key/look-fors: shared women's-suffrage aim, different support for the 15th; the 14th's citizenship
and 15th's race protection did not secure sex-based voting protection.
Meets: "Both sought women's votes. They differed over accepting the 15th without sex protection.
The 14th's citizenship provision had not secured women's voting rights."
Wrong: "Every advocate agreed" (C); "The 14th already secured women's votes" (K).
Shape: compare unseen strategic positions using legal context. Practice uses single-document
annotations, no paired advocate chart or complete comparison model.

**E132, X132, paper.** Made-up cases: "One woman citizen voted under state rules before 1920.
Another woman citizen faced racist intimidation after 1920."
"Explain how both cases fit the 19th Amendment."
Key/meets: "Some states allowed women's votes earlier. National sex protection did not remove racist intimidation."
Wrong: "No woman voted before 1920" (D); "Sex protection ended every racial barrier" (A).
Shape: reconcile two fresh cases with one rule. Practice marks dates and scopes separately,
never paired voter profiles or combined before-and-after access explanations.

## 9. Supports and extension

Words: suffrage, petition, amendment, citizenship, exclusion. Teach suffrage as the right to vote.
Practice frame: "This clause names __." Withdraw filled frames for exits.
All student print, including credits, headings and prompts, stays within 350 words daily;
every sentence has fifteen words or fewer. Adult originals are reference; student renderings are
ours, credited. Read difficult text neutrally aloud. Use large print, described maps, dictation or
a scribe without supplying reasoning. Offer private pauses and equivalent non-graphic evidence.
No slurs, graphic harm, reenactment, simulation or personal disclosure. Extension adds a source
limit within the same goal. Independent captions normally stay below 100 words.
