**E36, paper.** New dated notes, teacher summaries from the unit sources: December 1773, tea destroyed; 1774, Boston punished; September 1774, Suffolk delegates urged resistance; Congress endorsed their resolves. Notes are shuffled and each gives an action, not its causal link. "Connect the Tea Party, Suffolk Resolves and Congress in order. Explain the links between them." Key: tea destruction led to punitive measures; Suffolk responded by urging resistance/boycott/preparation; Congress endorsed the resolves and coordinated wider colonial action. Do not imply every later decision was inevitable. Wrong: all occurred at once (A); all were battles (Q). Shape: ordered, linked historical explanation from unseen notes; practice manipulates named dates and isolated action tags without multi-event explanations or these notes.

**E37, paper.** Reserve a new siege sketch and four short dated dispatch summaries, separate from teaching readings: Lexington militia encountered troops; at Concord armed resistance grew; British troops took Bunker Hill with heavy losses; Dorchester guns threatened Boston in March 1776. Later-war inset shows fighting elsewhere after March. "Explain what each location's events changed. Explain why leaving Boston did not end the war." Key: fighting began at Lexington, resistance spread at Concord; Britain gained the hill at high cost; guns/siege helped force evacuation; British forces left one city while war continued elsewhere. Wrong: all American victories (Q); Boston departure ended the war (S). Shape: integrate location-specific consequences and a geographic limit; practice uses dated single-event fact fragments and physical route tracing, never consequence labels on a siege map or this packet.

**E38, paper, invented case announced as made up.** Use s03's five invented household adults, no historical names: one refuses imported cloth and spins; another writes a public liberty argument; another manages farm/shop; another gathers donated coins; another prepares soldiers' linen. "Describe each woman's role. Explain two effects of their activities." Key: boycott/homespun; political writing; farm/business management; fundraising; army supply. Two supported effects: reduced import purchases, persuasion, continued production/trade, pooled resources or supplied clothing. Accept other supported effects, never a universal allegiance. Wrong: women only watched (F); all women supported independence (N). Shape: interpret five invented adults and connect two effects; practice inventories separate real source actions and dates, never five-role matching, invented household reconstruction or effect paragraphs.
