**Cold-solved teacher keys, never practice copy:** Q2: Massachusetts Bay enforced religious conformity; Rhode Island protected peaceful religious disagreement. A Penn preface note can report his aim to protect people from abuses of power. Card 6 complicates any universal-rights reading: law XXXIV restricted electors and officers, while XXXV protected worship for people acknowledging God. The research source is William Penn, Frame of Government, 1682, preface. Accept other accurately sourced notes, not unsupported biography (H). **X7, paper, two parts.** Reserved summaries: "Pennsylvania offered protection for certain religious beliefs." "Virginia's investors sought profit from a colony." Separate fact: "Massachusetts Bay banished Anne Hutchinson following a religious dispute." Sources: Penn laws, Virginia founding history, Hutchinson history. "Compare these founding aims. Explain what Hutchinson's banishment shows about religious freedom." Key: protection for beliefs versus investor profit; dissent could be punished. Wrong: identical aims (D); dissent was protected everywhere (I).
Shape: compare two fresh founding cards and interpret a separate dissent case. Practice sorts single aims, never this comparison or banishment conclusion. Teach Hutchinson questioning authority, reserving the banishment statement until X7.

**X8, paper, two parts.** Reserved Penn Frame II/III summaries: "Freemen chose council members. Yearly elections replaced one third of the council." Supply author, title, 1682 and section numbers, no suggested question. "Write a research question and a finding supported by these selections." Findings include source and supporting detail, taught on day 7. Key example: How were council members chosen? Penn's Frame II/III provides elections by freemen and yearly replacement of one third. Other supported questions/findings accepted. Wrong: everyone voted (H); unrelated unsupported biography (H). Gloss freemen as eligible voters named in the document, not everyone.
Shape: a student-generated question plus attributed finding from new passages. Practice annotates preface fragments and isolated source details; never a complete question/finding record, II/III, or the keyed question.

**X9, paper.** Reserved Massachusetts charter summary: "Officers took oaths. The governor could administer allegiance oaths to newcomers." Show its short English heading with a neutral modern-language gloss. "Explain how these practices could carry English language and government into later institutions." Key: repeated official English and allegiance to English authority could sustain institutions; earliest arrival is insufficient. Accept qualified explanations, not proof of every later institution. Wrong: England arrived first (E); English people were superior (L); everyone gained political rights (H).
Shape: infer continuity from a new oath provision. Practice sequences institutions and identifies languages without oaths or a complete continuity explanation.
