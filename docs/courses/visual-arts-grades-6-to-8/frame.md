# Course frame: Visual Arts, grades 6 to 8

Course `visual-arts-grades-6-to-8` (catalog id `visual-arts-6-8`), a band course for grades 6, 7 and 8, run as three years one after another (year 6, year 7, year 8). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape as the band below, so a technique and a
  thread come back one step harder a year later in the same place: a looking and studio unit, an ideas and revising
  unit, a designing and judging unit, and a showing and museum unit that ends the year with the class show. Each unit
  joins two progression threads, one of creating and one of responding, presenting or connecting, so every unit
  makes, studies other artists' work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5).
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 4 minutes), the teaching and making, and clean-up and
  collecting work (about 6 minutes in a paint, ink or print session, 3 in a drawing or writing session); a readiness
  or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25
  days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the
  rows' load, not equal shares: units 1 and 2 of year 6 are 16 days because the year's studio routine and its
  longest product (a planned inquiry carried to a revised collage) sit there; the first unit of years 7 and 8 is 18
  days because its main piece is worked over many sessions (a layered ink and wash piece in year 7, a two-color
  print edition in year 8); unit 11 is 16 days because it owns two products and a written argument and has two
  check days; unit 12 is 12 days because its show hangs work already kept. These are course days: the school
  calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week
  n / 2, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): Pending(docs/courses/arts/STANDARD.md#Visual arts 6 to
  8, and media arts): the visual arts educators' association's middle school checklist is read at source before a
  figure and a gap are stated here; this course gives 80 minutes a week, 40 on the once-a-week path. The course is
  written so a teacher without art training can teach it; a school with a certified art teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the look at a work that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one making day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into the
  openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path: year 6, units of 8, 8, 6 and
  6; year 7, units of 9, 7, 6 and 6; year 8, units of 9, 7, 7 and 5 (unit 11's two check days merge into one session,
  the written argument kept and the analysis item moved to the year-end sitting). Each year's mid-year check is
  session 17 (week 17, as on the full path) with its respond session in week 18; the year-end check is session 31
  (week 31, a week before the full path's) with its respond session in week 32. Neither check week is a core check
  week in grades 6, 7 or 8. A merged day never drops a critique, a revision or a respond day; the scope and sequence
  marks which days merge.
- **Rotations.** A middle school that teaches art as a daily rotation (a quarter wheel) runs the 64 course days of
  a year in order on consecutive school days; the units, checks and respond days keep their order and the school's
  own calendar places the check days. The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, visual arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 6 claims only the 15 grade 6 ids (`.6a`), year 7 only the 15 grade 7 ids (`.7a`),
  year 8 only the 15 grade 8 ids (`.8a`): 45 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in
  its own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches
  all four artistic processes (creating, presenting, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches
  at least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and
  anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Each id's action was checked against the national
  standard's own text as well as the words file (the pilot's pattern note); two rows' descriptions leave out part of
  an id and are listed under For the lead. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of
  its ids, the nine criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list
  (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule
  (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21);
  each year's scope and sequence still states its id count (15) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class (common in middle school electives)
  runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md 3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record
  shows which grade's standards they met. The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Visual arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied come
  from museum open-access collections (public domain works released by the holding museum) and, for living or recent
  makers, from a description in our own words with a link to the maker's or museum's page, never a copy (APPROACH
  "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own
  page, fails; a swapped work must keep the unit's ids met and the band's range. The works were range-checked across
  the band, not only within a year: Africa (Asante kente, the Benin plaques, Julie Mehretu, Cheri Samba), the
  Americas with Native makers in every year (Inuit snow goggles and the Seminole tribe's own museum in year 6, Jaune
  Quick-to-See Smith and a Plains ledger drawing in year 7, Robert Davidson's Haida pole and the national Native
  museum's treaty exhibition in year 8), the Caribbean (Wifredo Lam), Asia (Amrita Sher-Gil, the Sulawesi cave
  painting, Fan Kuan, the Qin terracotta army, Do Ho Suh), Europe (Lascaux, Matisse, Caillebotte, Minard, Mondrian,
  Rigaud, Leonardo) and the United States (Lawrence, Bearden, Bradford, Rockwell, LeWitt, Wiley, Baca, Corita Kent);
  living makers in every year. No Japanese woodblock print is picked (the band below leans on them), and no work of
  the K to 2 frame is repeated. Curio Class writes every step card, work card, context card, record sheet, reading
  and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Drawing: value and shading in graphite from a still life (6),
  one-point perspective (7), gesture and figure drawing from life (8). Paint and color: color schemes in painted
  collage papers (6), ink line over layered watercolor washes worked over many sessions (7), a two-color relief print
  edition with registration (8). Form and design: cardboard construction with tab and slot for a reworked object (6),
  information design with hierarchy, grouping, contrast and a scale (7), pictures and lettering arranged on a grid for
  a zine spread, and a joined panel mural made by a group (8). Presenting: mounting, labels and a drawn display plan
  (6), preparing work three ways and scoring the methods (7), jurying and hanging a themed group show with exhibition
  stories (8). Each builds on the band below's year 5 step (practice logged in a sketchbook, an artist statement, a
  curator's argument for mounting).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words and its setting (the artistic process
  "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills of a technique with feedback
  (a value strip, a wash drill, a one-minute gesture, a perspective box, a lettering line); in grades 6 to 8 it is
  the five-minute opener on most days and one or two fluency days a unit. Application: making with choice; the
  second making day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's revision is
  application too. Check: the self-check and the written items of the unit's evidence line; the product is scored
  from the kept work. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet". A unit runs roughly: understanding,
  fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line (where a row names "a short talk", the unit's evidence line
  governs). Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the product's guide (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not
  yet), scored from the kept work after the session; a kept card or sheet made on the unit's last making days (a
  plan, a log, a sort, a checklist score), scored with the product; and written items on an unseen open-licensed work
  or a Curio Class original, at most three a check day, answered in a sitting. A student may answer any written item
  aloud at the teacher's table instead (a support for an English learner or a student who writes with difficulty; the
  teacher writes the answer down). The end-of-unit assessment is a check day near the unit's end (two in unit 11,
  which owns two products and a written argument). Its 40 minutes: set-up and settling 3; each student's self-check
  on the guide 5; the written sitting 27; collecting work 3; and 2 to spare. A check day has no paint, ink, print or
  clay, and the teacher supervises one quiet room. The product's draft, its spoken critique with the teacher's
  look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from
  the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first
  practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a
  unit goes into the portfolio folder with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every year 6 unit assumes the grade 5 row in the same thread of Visual Arts, grades 3
  to 5 (last used one grade back, by the band's order), and every year 7 and year 8 unit assumes the year before's
  row (one grade back), so each unit opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never a readiness check, and
  day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from the band order (grade 5, then 6, 7 and 8).
  Many students reach grade 6 with little or no art in grades 3 to 5, and a mixed class may meet year 8 before year
  7: each retrieval task then carries its ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH "Readiness
  and the baseline look"), and a student new to the band takes the year's first unit as written. The Seams tables
  give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 6 to 8 after-core checks keep off the core check weeks of docs/courses/CALENDAR.md
  for the grade (grade 6: 12, 15, 16, 18, 22, 23, 24 and 32; grade 7: 9, 12, 13, 14, 22, 24 and 32; grade 8: 10,
  14, 15, 18, 20, 24, 25, 26, 29, 30 and 32), with week 32 a finals week that may hold every subject's year-end check
  (CALENDAR.md stagger rule 3, frame-write "Grades 6 to 12"). Mid-year: week 17 in every year (course days 33 and 34
  of the year), a week free of core checks in all three grades. Year-end: week 32, the finals week (days 63 and 64).
  So, unlike the band below, the show unit comes before the year-end check: it is assessed by its own end-of-unit
  assessment and its showing, and the year-end check samples the three units before it.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 5 (the Benin plaques include objects made for royal
  ancestral altars, studied by looking only), unit 8 (ledger drawings made by Plains men held as prisoners), unit 11
  (Wifredo Lam's painting draws on Afro-Cuban religious figures, studied by looking only) and unit 12 (a Haida crest
  pole, studied by looking only). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page
  and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, found material; nothing must be bought, and every
  unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience
  and the critique partner, a group task has a doubled version (parent and student, or the student and an invented
  group's brief), and the showing is to the family. The teacher needs no art training: each unit's teacher page says
  what to look for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as brushes,
  watercolor sets and brayers counted once, printing of step cards, work cards and readings), all optional beside the
  free path: year 6 $20, $15, $10, $15 ($60); year 7 $40, $10, $10, $10 ($70); year 8 $60, $10, $20, $10 ($100).
  Planning figures, not quotes; the ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work, a step or a slideshow; every day runs from the
  printed step card, work card and reading without it. Several ids speak of screens (VA:Pr4.1.6a digital art,
  VA:Cr2.2.7a sharing online, VA:Pr4.1.7a technology, VA:Re7.2.8a feeds), but none needs a device: digital work and
  feeds are studied from printed screen cards Curio Class makes, and online sharing is planned on paper and never
  done. So a paper-path student meets every id in full. No student account and no posting. Kept work stays on paper
  or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  value-scale or color-scheme practice tool, a perspective guide, a work viewer) has a printed version that does the
  same job and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Readings and work cards are Curio Class's own, at most one page a day, written at the grade's
  level, with the words in bold where they are taught. English learners: each unit teaches its five or six words in
  depth, with a picture, a gesture and a frame ("The ___ draws my eye first because ___." "I would judge it by
  ___."), a student may show the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student made apart from
  what they wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov, publication 5015) and California's
  environmental health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for schools, with its list of materials
  not to buy for grades K to 6, which binds year 6 and is followed in years 7 and 8 too (oehha.ca.gov). Rules for
  every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): only supplies labeled non-toxic
  and conforming to the art materials labeling standard; blades used by an adult only (students use scissors, and
  foam print plates are drawn into with a blunt pencil); no heat source in this course (APPROACH allows heat from
  grade 6; no unit needs it, so none is used); water-based paint, ink and block ink only, no solvents and no aerosol
  sprays; charcoal and chalk pastel dust wiped with a damp cloth, never blown, with pencil for any student with
  asthma; hands washed after paint, ink and found objects; no sharing of face-contact items. Each unit's last column
  gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural, Native, hard-history or disability topic name the communities and the sources the agent studies first:
  unit 1 (Black Americans in the Great Migration; Asante weavers of Ghana), unit 3 (Inuit makers; the disability
  rights movement), unit 4 (the Seminole Tribe of Florida), unit 5 (the Edo people and the Kingdom of Benin), unit 7
  (the Confederated Salish and Kootenai Nation), unit 8 (a Plains nation, named by the unit plan's pick), unit 11
  (Chicano and Los Angeles communities in the mural; Afro-Cuban religion in Lam's painting) and unit 12 (the Haida
  Nation; Native nations and treaties). Every task that ties art to a student's own life, interests, worries or
  group (units 2, 3, 7 and 11) offers an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"), and
  no task asks about a student's own body, looks or culture (unit 9 studies the image a person projects through
  portraits and invented personas). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards, work
  cards and readings, found and low-cost materials. Activities: technique taught in steps, works studied with their
  setting, making with a critique and a revision, a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over
  the band with written reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed:
  met marks on the year's 15 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio
  Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").
