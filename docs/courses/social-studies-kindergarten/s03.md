## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 20:** a puppet asks where the crayons are. Contrast pointing vaguely with locating them beside the paper. Post the compelling question and children's wonders.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** Which words help us locate things (20 to 21)? What can maps and globes show (22 to 23)? How can pictures stand for places (24 to 26)? How does an address help locate a school (27 to 28)?
- **Closing, day 29:** each child claims how to help someone locate things, citing Our Place Record, Two World Maps or School Garden. The teacher writes the child's words and named evidence. Words, models and maps can support different answers.
- **Teaching:** model position words on 20, comparison on 22, the key on 24, a picture graph on 25 and address nesting on 27. Step back on 26 and 29. Ask who made each source, when and why; compare a photograph with observation and a map, correcting mismatches. Teach position, map, globe, land, water, feature, symbol, key and address through objects and pictures. Explain that left and right need a shared viewpoint and maps select features.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 29 is marked `observe` in days.csv. The end-of-unit assessment combines that day's fresh source task with revised individual representations from days 26 to 28. First-teaching products are practice only. Day 29 hosts all seven items. Owned: 3.1 item 1; 3.2 item 2; 3.3 items 3 and 4; 3.4 items 5 to 7. This is an observation assessment within the unit, not an additional test day.

**Fresh source, withheld from teaching and exits:** School Garden is an original overhead map and matching illustrated view of an invented school garden. The school is at the left, a pond in the middle and the garden at the right. A bench is between the pond and garden; two trees are behind the bench in the illustrated view. A path curves around the pond from school to garden. The boxed key pairs a triangle with school, an oval with pond, a square with bench, a star with garden and a small circle with tree. Use outlines and patterns, not color alone. Keep the map facing the same way for everyone. Explain that the illustrator invented this place; it is not a photograph of our school. The key is on the page; the teacher reads a key word only when a child points to that key entry, and never says which map symbol matches it.

**Administration:** on days 25 and 26 note the rotating group during supported making; the initial products are practice records; only the later revised representations are scored. A place near school is met by each child choosing a place from the class list (playground, library, entrance, a visited place) to draw on 26; no home, no address; the school is the shared case. On 25, blue paper and cloth or clay stand for pond and hill; with a window view instead of a walk, count trees and rocks from Our Place Record photographs or room objects. Outdoor observation follows the school's own supervision policy. On 25 the teacher first models a two-category picture graph from counted features, one picture for each feature and a labeled place for zero, then children make their own. Save initial products as practice. Write and read aloud feedback on one feature, position or count to check. During day 26's existing supported making, children create revised model and graph records after day 25 practice; during day 28's ten work minutes they redraw their map after written feedback on day 26. Score only these later representations, each child directing the construction. Reserve four of those ten minutes for revisions and six for location practice. On day 28 every child privately marks the town and school on separate paper maps after the supported example is covered; record the help given. Introduce the task for two minutes, teach for six, allow ten for individual work and conversations, and use two for the exit ticket. Use the extension only after the supported example is done and the class has located town and school; it takes no example minutes and is dropped if time is short. Day 26 splits its ten supported minutes: four to revise the model and graph from written feedback, six for the first drawn map. Day 20 spends four minutes on the hook and wonder wall, six on modeling, eight supported and two on the exit.

On day 29 use two minutes to introduce School Garden, eight for individual picture responses to items 1, 2, 5 and 6, eight to confirm item 3 and hear any item 2 explanation still missing (the rest of the class quietly adds one picture to the class key card, unscored), and two to close the wonder wall. Closing claims (item 7) and other missing explanations move to day 30. Each response row has the source pictures or address strips and a clear place to point or mark; no partner answers. For item 5 the teacher touches and reads one strip at a time as each question is asked, rereads on request, keeps the strips in a fixed row, and the child marks the strip just read. Collect each response before showing an answer. Days 22 and 23 comparisons are practice. Write and read aloud a next step; hear revised comparisons only from day 26's rotating group, within its existing spoken minutes, before scoring the later record. Retain revised claims from 26 and confirm missing parts on 29. The spoken explanations for items 2 and 3 are heard from the rotating group on days 24 to 28, after feedback (item 3 on days 26 to 28), and only confirmed on 29; the eight hearing minutes hold that confirmation and any item 2 explanation still missing for at most six children (about a minute each, a record column naming who was heard); the rest move to day 30. Item 7 closing claims are heard on day 30. A child whose fresh map explanation supplies item 7 needs no second speech. These twenty-minute timings are ours, untested. Thirty-minute lessons add communication time. Record unheard explanations as not yet observed; use day 30's fresh-try allocation to finish them without rushing a communicator.
