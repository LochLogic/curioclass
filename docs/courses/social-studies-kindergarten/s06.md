## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 52:** watch the teacher prepare shared materials beside Work Around Us's picture of an adult cooking at home. What work helps someone? Post the compelling question and children's wonders.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** What does work require (52 to 53)? Why must people choose (54 to 55)? How do goods, services and paying help people get things (56 to 58)?
- **Closing, day 59:** the class returns to the question; scribe each child's claim, revised and recorded on 60, and named evidence from Work Around Us, Jobs Book or Our Store Record. Helping, caring and earning can support different answers; paid work is not the only work.
- **Teaching:** model a driver's knowledge, skill and tools on 53, a limited choice on 55 and a source-based because on 56. On 57 the teacher sells, a child buys, the class asks what was got and given. Step back on 58 to 59. Name each source's maker, time and purpose; compare a book description with an observed action. A photograph shows actions, not everything a worker knows. Teach work, skill, tool, need, want, choose, buy, sell, trade, goods, services, earn and pay with pictures.


The local C3 file lists codes only; meanings were checked in the publisher's framework.
- **D2.Eco.3.K-2:** Describe the knowledge and abilities needed to make goods or provide services. Parts: describe required knowledge; describe required skills. Tools support these descriptions but do not replace them.
- **D2.Eco.1.K-2:** Explain why not having enough for everything requires a choice. Parts: explain the connection between limited resources and deciding.
- **Anchor parts:** MA.K.T4.1 describes work inside and outside the home using experience and texts; MA.K.T4.2 asks questions, answers questions and explains choices among needs and wants; MA.K.T4.3 gives examples of paid goods and services linked to earned money.
- **Limits:** no coin values, change, wages, budgets or formal opportunity cost; these come later. No family income or employment questions. Tokens model exchange, not human worth; equal starting tokens are never earned through compliance. Trade is voluntary. Needs are not rewards. Home work may be paid or unpaid; not all money comes from work.

Restore unit 3's day 26 map and key on 57 and revise it after checking shelf locations on 58. Keep unit 5's perspective and spoken-reason routines for differing choices.
