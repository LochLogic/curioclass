- **D4.8.K-2** (days 14): Use listening, consensus and voting to decide on and take action in the classroom.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write supporting questions:** Who counts on a job (11 to 12)? How can we choose together (13 to 14)? What do story characters show us (15 to 16)? Did our decision help (17)?
> - **Teaching:** model finishing a job on 12, listening and seeking agreement on 13, and pointing to a story action on 15. On 14 children choose and act; on 17 they judge results. Teach job, responsible, respect, fair, caring, just (putting an unfair thing right), in charge, agree and vote with pictures. Name each source's maker, occasion and purpose; distinguish stories from records of our actions. Compare the dated chart with children's observations, including disagreement.
>
> - **Carried:** keep unit 1's perspective columns on 13 to 14, then revisit dissent on 17. Keep its spoken claim and because routine on 14 and 18 as review, not new ownership of D4.1 or D4.4. Withdraw supplied reasons on 18; retain picture and communication supports. Both models continue.
>
> On 14 offer two safe, accessible proposals from children's ideas. Seek a shared revision before voting, even when confirming agreement. Each child directs one identical block into a choice tower. Compare and count together. For a tie, revise and vote again; if still tied, agree to try both safely in turn. Record dissent without penalty.
<!-- /skel -->

- **D2.Civ.11.K-2, this section's share.** Children "explain how people can decide things together in the classroom". On day 13 they say what puppets can do before choosing (hear each reason, look for a way both can accept, vote if still apart). On day 14 they take part in a real choice, and item 2 asks them later to explain it from Our Decision Record.
- **D4.8.K-2, this section's share.** Day 14 only: children "use listening, consensus and voting to decide on and take action". Parts: listen, build agreement, vote, decide, act. The confirmation or revision of the decision belongs to 2.4, day 17.
- **Limits that bite here.** Classroom action only: no elections, campaigns or votes outside the room. Safety, rights and access are never put to a vote, and adults keep safety jobs. The class names the problem; the course picks no cause. The plant-name vote is a warm-up and never the action. The teacher checks both proposals are safe and accessible, and says so aloud.
- **Connections.** K.CC.B.5 (math), days 13 and 14: blocks in two towers compared side by side by height, then counted with the teacher (a bridge only; math owns counting). Unit 1's spoken "I think ..., because ...", day 14, as review (not new ownership of D4.1 or D4.4).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between agreement and a count: at five, first look for a change both sides can accept, then vote, and a vote does not make everyone want the same thing (error B). How to keep a loud child from deciding (error G): every child directs one identical block, and puppets give the quiet reasons. How to hold dissent without blame (error I). That choosing a plan is not carrying it out (error J). How to run a tie: revise, vote again, then try both in turn. Why the vote is public but no child is asked to defend a block.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> - **Tools:** None. The ideal instruments are actual jobs, movable choice cards and a dated picture record. Print fits; no tool request is needed.
> - **Supplies:** scrap paper, crayons, blocks or tokens, two paper puppets, job cards, source texts, record sheet, plant and jug. Hand-draw substitutes for all printed materials. No student timer. Use accessible jobs without lifting, climbing or blocking exits.
<!-- /skel -->

- **Carried.** Unit 1's perspective columns (two reasons side by side) on days 13 and 14; the spoken claim and "because" on day 14 as review. Our Decision Record, begun on day 13, holds dated proposals, reasons, the agreement attempt, the count, the action and dissent, and goes to 2.4. Dissent is revisited on day 17. Both models continue; nothing is withdrawn here.
- **Key moment 1, day 13: the problem and the proposals.** Must be seen: a problem is a place or a thing, not a person. Ideal instrument: picture cards of the room's own spots and two proposal cards. Spec: print fits; no tool. Gaps and asks: none (TOOL-ASKS.md has no entry).
- **Key moment 2, day 14: the vote.** Must be seen: each child hears both reasons and directs one block; the count is public; the result is acted on that day. Ideal instrument: two towers of identical blocks, side by side. Spec: objects fit; a room without blocks uses paper squares.
- **A room with nothing.** Hands or paper squares in two stacks, hand-drawn cards, and a record on any sheet.

## 6. Days

## Day 13

- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: A warm-up vote (naming the class plant), then the class names real classroom problems (crowding at the coat hooks, sharing the block corner)
- note: The plant vote is a warm-up only, never the action. Lean K.CC.B.5: math unit 2 (weeks 4 to 9); keep the vote small, blocks in two towers compared by height, then counted with the teacher
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Reopen.** Post and co-write "How can we choose together?" Recall unit 1's talk circle and perspective columns. If many missed errors A or H on days 11 to 12, repair here (a warm-up of up to 5 more minutes, day stays under 30). Job routine, as set on day 12, runs in its usual place.
- **5 min. Warm-up vote.** Naming the class plant, from two names the children offer. Each child directs one identical block to a tower; compare the towers side by side by height; count aloud with the teacher. The plant gets its name. Never the action.
- **7 min. Teaching first.** Two puppets disagree about where the drawing paper box goes (by the door, or by the table). Perspective columns hold each reason. Teach listen, agree and vote with pictures: ask "why", look for a place both can accept, vote only if still apart.
- **6 min. The class names problems.** Picture cards of two real spots (crowded coat hooks, the block corner) and any the children add. A problem is a place, never a child. Count hands aloud for which to fix first; a tie means the class agrees which goes first and the other goes on the wonder wall.
- **3 min. Ideas.** "What could we try?" The teacher draws two proposal cards from the children's ideas, checks both are safe and accessible, and reads each aloud.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E13.

After class the teacher starts Our Decision Record (date, problem, two proposals) and draws a "before" sketch of the place from a marked spot on the floor. About 4 minutes of teacher time, outside the day.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the perspective columns and the count; the puppets hold the reasons. Tool: None. By hand: two puppets, block tower, picture cards, record sheet. Evidence: E13.

## Day 14

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: The class decision: hear the choices, listen to reasons, try for agreement, vote with blocks or hands, count, plan the action and take its first step together
- note: The year's informed action. The problem is the class's own choice; the course picks no cause. The agreed arrangement runs in the class's normal routines from today to day 17. Lean K.CC.B.5 bridge as on day 13
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Reconnect.** The compelling question and Our Decision Record read aloud. If many missed error G on day 13, repair here (up to 5 more minutes). Job routine, as set on day 12, runs in its usual place.
- **4 min. Two proposals.** Each is heard with its reason, in the perspective columns; puppets voice them so no child is singled out. Each child offers "I want ___ because ___" through a puppet (review of the spoken claim).
- **3 min. Seek a shared revision.** Ask what change both could accept. Do this before voting, even if agreement looks near.
- **3 min. Vote and count.** Each child directs one identical block into a choice tower; compare and count together. For a tie, revise and vote again; if still tied, trying both safely in turn becomes the first action in the next block.
- **5 min. First action together.** The agreed arrangement starts now and runs in the class's routines until day 17.
- **1 min. Record.** Date, result, the reasons offered, dissent as a reason and not a person.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E14, a drawing; words written only for the rotating six.

Who holds it: the class holds the decision; the teacher holds the record and the safety check. Tool: None. By hand: block tower, proposal cards, record sheet. Evidence: E14; the observation of listening, vote and action feeds item 3.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 13, "What can we do before we choose?"; day 14, "What would you like us to try? Why?" and "What did you hear that made you think again?"
- Written task, day 14: draw or dictate the child's own part in carrying out the decision (the exit).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: agree, vote, choose, count, idea, problem. Frames: "I want ___ because ___." "I hear you want ___." "We can try ___."
- **Access.** Movable cards; an adult moves a block as the child directs; hand-raising is not required, and a communication aid or pointing counts. A tactile tower for children who cannot see the blocks.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token, drawing, dictation or a familiar aid; a private exchange for the spoken part. Civic understanding is recorded apart from speech or motor performance.
- **Reading load.** None. Everything is told or read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Offer a change both puppets could accept that neither has said, or say what would be heard first if the vote tied.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Choose a small problem in the learning space. Child and adult each give a reason, look for a change both accept, then vote with one block each. Say that two voters can tie, and try both ways in turn if so. Act that day.
- **One projector.** The perspective columns and proposal cards show on one screen, but the vote is physical: blocks or paper squares, one per child, not clicks.
