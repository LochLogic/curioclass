# Section 5.2: The flag, the eagle and the anthem: what symbols tell us

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-kindergarten/5/s/5.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-kindergarten u05-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.K.T1.4, this section's share (days 45 to 47).** All of it: "Ask and answer questions and use books to learn about national symbols, songs, and texts of the United States." The teacher models asking a page a question on 45; pairs ask one on 46 and the class finds the answer on the page; on 47 children answer from the page. Item 2, fresh on day 50.
- **MA.K.T1.4a (day 45).** "Explain why the United States flag has its colors, stars, and stripes." Stripes: thirteen, for the first thirteen states. Stars: one for each state, fifty today. Colors: the flag law gave no reason; people say what they mean. Item 4.
- **MA.K.T1.4b (day 46).** "Explain why the bald eagle is the national emblem of the United States." Leaders picked it for the Great Seal in 1782, a bird found only in North America. Item 3.
- **MA.K.T1.4c (day 47).** "Explain why 'The Star-Spangled Banner' is the national anthem." Key wrote it in 1814; a law made it the anthem in 1931. Item 5.
- **MA.K.T1.4d (days 45 to 47).** "Explain what the words of the 'Pledge of Allegiance' mean." One phrase group a day: 45, a promise of loyalty to the country the flag stands for; 46, one nation, together; 47, liberty and justice for all. Item 6.
- **Limits that bite here.** The class explains the Pledge and does not lead it. Nobody is asked to recite, stand, put a hand anywhere or sing; a child who stands quietly is respected, and the school's own practice goes on as the school directs. No date recall, no counting tiny stars, no state flags. No child is the example of a mistake: a puppet says it.
- **Connections.** MA.K.T3.3, review from unit 4: "long, long ago" for the old papers and "now" for the flag at school. Unit 3's symbol (a small picture that stands for a real thing) is the same idea here: a flag stands for the country. Unit 1's "I think ..., because ..." takes the page as its source.
- **What the teacher page must explain (every fact marked (S) is to be checked against the page the unit plan names before a piece is written).**
  - Flag: the 1777 resolution says thirteen stripes, red and white, and thirteen stars, white on blue; it gives no reason for the colors. The 1818 act keeps thirteen stripes and adds a star for each new state; fifty stars today (S: Postal Museum, govinfo). The colors were given meanings for the Great Seal in 1782 and people say red is courage, white purity, blue justice (S: Thomson's report, pages 4 and 7). Never say the law said red means bravery.
  - Eagle: the 1782 seal chose the bald eagle; it lives only in North America (S: Thomson's report, Fish and Wildlife page). A 2024 law also names it the national bird (S). "People say the eagle shows strength" is a later statement, sorted with the speech bubble.
  - Anthem: Key wrote the words in 1814 after seeing the flag still flying over Fort McHenry; a law in 1931 made the song the anthem (S: National Park Service).
  - Pledge: current text read in short phrases (S: Ben's Guide). Plain meanings: pledge allegiance is a promise of loyalty; the Republic is a government with chosen representatives; indivisible is one country together; "under God" is part of the text, not a belief asked of the listener; liberty and justice for all is freedom and fair treatment for everyone. Saying it is a choice (S: Barnette, 1943).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.**
  - The spoken sort (old paper, speech bubble): teacher holds it on 45; children place a statement with help on 46; pairs sort on 47; revisited on 48 and 49 and not withdrawn here.
  - Asking a page a question: modeled on 45, pairs ask on 46, children answer from the page on 47; "I think ..., because the page says ..." with the page as source.
  - The Pledge phrases: one group a day, kept on a wall strip of three pictures; not recited by the class.
  - The strip (unit 4): "long, long ago" and "now" on 45 to 47; kept for 5.3.
- **Key moment 1, day 45: stripes and stars on a large flag.** Ideal instrument: a large print of the flag with thirteen stripes and a few stars, labeled "first states" and "states now" by sticker. Spec: no tool; print fits; to make.
- **Key moment 2, day 46: the eagle on the seal on an old paper.** Ideal instrument: a dated source picture of the seal from a federal page. Spec: print fits; rights to check (S).
- **A room with nothing.** A flag drawn on chart paper with thirteen stripes; a traced eagle; two bins from scrap paper; folded-paper puppets; the page questions asked aloud.

## 6. Days

Every day is 25 minutes (frame 20 to 30); a 30-minute day adds its extra minutes to supported work. Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children. The read-aloud is the bound original "Symbols We Ask About" (ours; teaching pages, exit pages and assessment pages are separate, and no exit shows a teaching page's picture). On each page the teacher says the maker, the time and why it was made. The spoken sort has two picture bins, an old paper ("the record says") and a speech bubble ("people say it means"), at most two statements for each symbol; the teacher says the dates and no date or statement prints on a child's page. The marking scale is shown, shown with help, not yet seen; the rotating six are heard 40 to 60 seconds each and the record column names who was heard. Everything children say or mark here is practice; the teacher writes a source clue and a next step on each child's record after 45, 46 and 47 and reads it privately on days 48 and 49 (section 5.3). Every case is made up for practice and the teacher says so ("a pretend class").

## Day 45


- **2 min. Reopen.** Post the supporting question "What do symbols tell us, and how do we know?" Say symbol: a picture or thing that stands for something, as in unit 3's key.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** One repair: E44, letter H, only if many missed (whole class, 4 minutes: thank-you wall beside a quiet flag; no raised hands about family). If not needed, a small group while the rest look at the saved holiday cards.
- **7 min. Teaching first.** "Symbols We Ask About," the flag page (ours). Stripes and stars; the sort with two statements: "long, long ago, in 1777, a law said thirteen stripes, red and white" into the old paper bin; "people say red means being brave" into the speech bubble. Then the star for each state since 1818, fifty today. Model asking the page a question. Pledge begun: pledge is a promise of loyalty; the flag stands for the country; the class does not recite.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs place the two statements in the bins with tokens, then put a token on a star and on a stripe of a practice flag and say what each stands for. Hear the rotating six, 60 seconds each, about 6 minutes.
- **2 min. Exit.** E45: mark card; spoken ask to the six during the minute and the supported work.

Holds: the teacher holds the bins and the question. Tool: None. By hand: flag print, bins, tokens. Evidence: E45; practice for items 4 and 6.

## Day 46


- **5 min. Reopen and repair.** Retrieve day 45 (the star and the stripe, 1 minute). One repair: E45, letters C and J, if many missed (4 minutes).
- **8 min. Teaching first.** The eagle page: long, long ago, in 1782, leaders picked the bald eagle for the Great Seal, a bird found only in North America; the seal is on this old paper. Sort: "leaders picked it for the seal" into the old paper bin; "people say the eagle shows strength" into the speech bubble. Model asking a page "Who picked this bird?" and finding the answer. Holds: pairs hold the sort; children ask the question. Tool: None. By hand: eagle page, bins, tokens. Evidence: E46; practice for items 2, 3 and 6.

- **5 min. Reopen and repair.** Retrieve day 46 (the eagle's page, 1 minute). One repair: E46, letters B, D and I, if many missed (4 minutes; one repair, the one most missed).

Holds: the teacher holds the choice line. Tool: None. By hand: anthem page, puppets, pictures. Evidence: E47; practice for items 2, 5 and 6.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 45, "What does a star stand for? What does a stripe stand for?"; day 46, "Why this bird? What could we ask our book?"; day 47, "How did the song become the anthem? What does fair for everyone mean?"
- Written task, days 45 to 47: none by children. The teacher writes a source clue and a next step on each child's record.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: says what the part of the symbol stands for and why in a sentence, not only the word; separates "the record says" from "people say"; asks or answers from the page.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 45.** Holidays on the strip from 5.1; the because frame. **Day 46.** The flag's star and stripe. **Day 47.** The eagle page and the question routine; the choice line from 45.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: flag, stripe, star, symbol, eagle, anthem, pledge, promise, fair, the record says, people say. Frames: "The star stands for ___." "The record says ___; people say ___." "My question for the page is ___." Home-language words are welcome.
- **Access.** Raised-line flag and seal; a flag shown by pattern as well as color; a child who cannot see gets spoken descriptions. Pointing, tokens and communication symbols count as showing. A child may pass in talk; standing or sitting is a choice.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every page and prompt aloud; accept a point, token, home language or private exchange; extra time. Judge content apart from English, speech, vision and motor skill; never mark silence or refusing to recite as an error.
- **Reading load.** None; labels only, read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second question for the page and say which page answers it; say how the page differs from what people say.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Read the day's page, sort two statements into two bins, ask the page one question, and say it back. Never ask the child to recite the Pledge or sing; say the meaning in plain words. Do the work for each day in order.
- **One projector.** Show the page large and the bins on a screen; children mark picture cards on paper; the spoken part is heard at a table.
