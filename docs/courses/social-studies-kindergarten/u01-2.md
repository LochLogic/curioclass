Children do not read yet. Rules are shown as pictures and acted out. Each day runs 20 minutes; the 30-minute route adds time for communication and recording.

- **MA.K.T1.1, this section's share.** The whole standard, begun: "follow" a rule with little reminding (days 3 to 5, and every day after), and with help "ask and answer" why a rule exists (days 3 and 5, children echo the teacher's question and answer, then try their own). The scored occasions are on days 7 to 9; days 3 to 5 are practice.
- **D2.Civ.3.K-2, this section's share.** "Explain why rules are needed and what they accomplish," in both settings: school (day 3) and outside it (day 4). A purpose is named as safe, fair or helping everyone learn.
- **D2.Civ.12.K-2, this section's share.** "Identify" a public class or school rule and "explain how it works": who made it and what it does (day 4), and how our own class rule would work and might be changed (day 5).
- **Limits that bite here.** No rule-breaking scenario names or singles out a child, and no punishment is acted out; a consequence is the thing that happens to the shared room or garden, not to a person. No one reports on rules at home. Adults keep safety responsibilities, so a rule about water, roads or equipment is explained, not renegotiated. Preferences differ, but a pictured fact still limits a reason.
- **Connections.** W.K.1 and SL.K.1 by lean only. Day 5 hears "I think we need this rule, because ..." for the first time (claim and reason); day 8 is the second meeting and ELA teaches the drawn opinion in its own unit 1.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That a rule is a shared agreement with a reason, not an adult's command, and that the kindergarten word "fair" can mean several things; here it means everyone gets a turn or a share. That the class charter is real and made by the class, while the read-aloud and exit cards are invented and labelled so. That a rule can be changed, but through the people who share it, with an adult agreeing.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** items 2 (MA.K.T1.1, scored from days 7 to 9 records), 3 (D2.Civ.3.K-2) and 4 (D2.Civ.12.K-2). Nothing from days 3 to 5 is scored; the day 9 items use cases not seen here.

**Exit questions.** Each has a picture mark by every child and a spoken "why" heard from the day's rotating group of six; others are "not yet heard". Cases print "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E3.** Mark: circle the picture of the rule that helps. A new card shows a wet painting next to someone's clean clothes. Spoken: "What rule could help here, and why?" Answer: keep wet paintings on the drying rack so clothes stay clean; accept another supported proposal. Wrong: "Because the teacher says so" (B).
- **E4.** Mark: circle the person who made the rule. A new yard-gate card shows a closed gate between a schoolyard and a car lane. Spoken: "Why was this gate closed, and what does the rule do for children?" Answer: it keeps children out of the car lane. Key only the reason. Wrong: "Because grown-ups said so" (B); "Children can open it if they want" (H).
- **E5.** Mark: circle the picture of a fairer rule from two choices. A puppet rule says, "Only the puppet with the red hat chooses the song." Spoken: "Why is this not fair to the other puppets? What could we do instead?" Answer: the others never get a choice; take turns so all can choose. Wrong: "It is fine, it is a rule" (B); "I would just change it so I go first" (J); "It is fair because it is fair" (E).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

| B | "Rules are what grown-ups say," beside "The turn rule means both dolls get the glue." Days 3, 4 and 5. | Act the rule with and without it, with puppets, and name who benefits. Five minutes, whole class. |
| G | "I only follow it when the teacher reminds me," beside "I put the glue stick back because the next person needs it." Day 3. | Rehearse one easy rule with a picture cue, then fade the adult's reminder. Five minutes, small group. |
| H | "Each person can pick whether the gate rule counts," beside "The rule about pushing in chairs is for everyone who sits at the table." Day 4. | Contrast a private choice (which crayon) with a shared rule (the gate). Five minutes, whole class. |
| J | "I can change the rule without telling anyone," beside "We all agree to try a change." Day 5. | Model asking the class and the teacher first, then trying the change. Ten minutes, small group. |
| E | "The rule is good because it is good," beside "The rule is good because it keeps the blocks in the box so the next child can find them." Day 5. | Point to the pictured result and say "because" together. Five minutes, whole class. |

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The question "Why do we have this rule?" and its answer, modelled on days 3 and 5, echoed by children, and kept as a routine for the year. The idea of a rule and its consequence (what happens to the shared thing) comes from day 3 and holds to day 10. The spoken "I think we need this rule, because ..." is modelled on day 5 and tied to the charter. The charter itself is made on day 5 and kept for unit 2's retrieval. The teacher holds the charter and the questions; pairs hold the cards.
- **Key moment 1, day 3: rule and consequence.** Ideal instrument: movable picture cards (a rule, what it protects, who benefits). Spec: fits; paper cards. No tool is used.
- **Key moment 2, day 5: Our Class Charter.** Ideal instrument: chart paper with a picture symbol and the teacher's dictated words for each rule, with the reason written beside it. Spec: fits; print or hand-drawn. Gaps and asks: none.
- **A room with nothing.** Draw the rule cards on scrap paper. Two folded-paper dolls, a glue stick and a crosswalk drawn on the floor with tape stand in for the stories.

- **3 min. Reopen.** Hold up a maker card from day 4; ask who made it and why. Repair E4 in two minutes if needed (one picture, one sentence); a small group takes the rest in supported work.
- **8 min. The claim frame.** The teacher models "I think we need this rule, because ..." aloud with one sample rule (hang coats on the hooks) and a pictured reason, and a child-voice "Why do we have this rule?" with an answer. Children echo the frame.
- **14 min. Supported work.** The class proposes rules for its own room with a reason for each. The teacher prints three pictured candidate rules; each child drops a counter in a closed cup under one, or passes, the class counts the counters aloud, the two with the most stay, and the class gives a because for each, and the teacher writes the dictated words on chart paper and finishes the picture symbols after the lesson; this is Our Class Charter, and it is real, unlike the invented cards. The class sees one picture for "What if a rule does not work?" and agrees in one sentence how to try a change with the teacher.

Who holds what is carried: the class holds the charter; the teacher holds the frame. Tool: None. By hand: proposing, pointing, the exit mark. Evidence: E5; the charter proposals are practice for item 4.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 3, "Why do we have this rule?" (teacher models, children echo); day 4, "Who made it, and what is it for?"; day 5, "What if a rule does not work? What could we do?"
- Written task: none. Children do not write. The class charter is dictated to the teacher.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) the rule is named with a reason that uses the picture; (2) a consequence is shown on the shared thing, not a person; (3) a change is proposed to the people who share the rule. Record separately from speech and access.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 3 reopens the wonder wall and repairs E2. Day 4 repairs E3 with the matching error's wrong example. Day 5 repairs E4. No timer.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standards set none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: rule, fair, safe, turn, share, help. Frames: "The rule is ___ because ___." and "Why do we have this rule?" Home language and signs are accepted; the teacher restates in English.
- **Access.** Large and tactile picture cards; a pointed card or aid counts as an answer; acting a rule can replace speaking it.
- **Check accommodations.** Read the exit aloud; accept a mark, a point, a drawing with dictation or a private answer; a scribe records without adding ideas.
- **Reading load.** None.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask a secure child to name a second place where the same rule helps, or say which of safe, fair or helps everyone learn the rule serves.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: show the glue story with two toys, say "This rule helps because ...", ask "Why do we have it?", then look at a crosswalk or sign in the neighbourhood together. Make a small charter of two rules on paper, with a reason beside each.
- **One projector.** Show the cards and charter on a screen; children mark on paper.
