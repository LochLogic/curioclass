# Section 4.2: Old photographs, objects and what changed


## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.His.2.K-2, days 37 to 39.** "Compare life in the past with life today": a pictured then beside a pictured now, what changed and what stayed, for one place and one task. Never a whole era or people.
- **D2.His.10.K-2, days 37 to 40.** "Explain how sources can be used to study the past": a dated photograph or an object is a clue; ask who made it and when; say what it shows and what it cannot show.
- **MA.K.T3.3, MA.K.T3.3a, MA.K.T3.3b and D2.His.1.K-2, day 39.** Sat only: the time-order and time-word items taught in 4.1.
- **Limits that bite here.** No ranking of eras, cultures, households or equipment. Handwashing, gardening and sewing continue today. No family photographs, chore reports or appliance surveys; no child brings a photograph. The photographs show particular people, not a culture or an era, and no one is named or described by how they look. Unit 5 owns the holiday lesson.
- **Connections.** The unit 3 routine "who made it, when and why" (review, day 37) and the spoken "I think ..., because ..." (unit 1 to unit 3, review, days 37 to 39). The timeline from 4.1 (review): the dated photograph is placed at long ago on day 37.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A photograph is a picture taken at the time; a drawing is made later by someone; an object is a thing. An old source is shown old by its dated record, not by being gray. A photograph shows one place and moment, so it supports a claim about what it shows and cannot speak for everyone; say "the record does not say" when it does not (a maker's reason is unknown unless the record gives one). A tool changed but its purpose often stayed (both wash clothes, both write). The compelling question is answered by a bounded choice with a reason, never by calling a time better or worse; "people long ago were poor" is never a reason offered or accepted. The photographs show real people: treat them with care and read only the record's own words.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The timeline (4.1, review): the dated photograph is set at long ago on day 37. The continuity-change chart is new: two columns, then and now, with a row for what stayed, introduced on day 37 with a completed example (the washboard and washing machine pair from the day 34 hook, review) and revised on day 38 into blank charts with a shared-purpose sentence ("both help ___"); completed examples are withdrawn on day 38. The unit 3 source routine and the claim-and-because routine continue; the day 39 claim has no supplied reason. Perspective stays: different supported choices count. Day 36 logs are revised on day 38 and then scored for item 2. Supports stay: puppets, partner talk, the wonder wall.
- **Key moment 1, day 37: one old photograph with its record read aloud.** Ideal instrument: a large print of a federal photograph with its catalog caption (maker, place, date) read aloud, plus a ridged cardboard stand-in with no sharp edges. Spec: print fits; the prints are to make from public-domain records.
- **Key moment 2, day 39: each response made before an answer is shown.** Ideal instrument: paper response rows with the fresh then-and-now pairs, and the record sheet with a heard column. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn then and now pairs, a drawn chart of two columns, a teacher's own sketch of an old photograph with a written date.

## 6. Days

Every day is 20 to 30 minutes (frame); the parts below add to 20 (day 39 adds to 22), and a 30-minute day adds its extra minutes to supported work or hearing. Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 37, "What do you see? What do you think, because...? Who made this, and when?"; day 38, "What changed? What stayed the same?"; day 39, "Which change made the biggest difference to everyday work? Why do you think so?"
- Written task, day 39: none by children; a drawing of the chosen change, and the teacher scribes the claim, the named clue, the reason and what stayed.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors (shown, shown with help, not yet seen): one change chosen from a named photograph; a visible clue named; a reason that says what the work is then and now; one thing that stayed. A different supported choice also meets; "it is different" alone does not.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: photograph, object, clue, old, new, changed, stayed the same, because. Frames: "I see ___." "I think ___ because I see ___." "In this picture ___, but now ___; both ___." "___ changed. ___ stayed the same." Home-language words and pointing are welcome.
- **Access.** Large prints with a neutral spoken description of each picture, tactile outlines, an adult who points as the child directs.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every caption and prompt aloud; accept a dictated drawing, signs, an aid or a private exchange; judge history apart from speech or motor performance. Communicators get the time; day 40 finishes what is not yet observed.
- **Reading load.** None; all captions are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say what another source would need to show before a claim about everyone's work could be made.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the packet's prints, never your own family's: day 37, one old photograph with its record, the chart and the gray-drawing contrast; day 38, the four pairs on a blank chart; day 39, two new pairs and the claim, heard by you and scribed; day 40 (last day of the unit), re-teach the one missed part and try a new case.
- **One projector.** Show a print large with its caption read aloud; children mark paper copies. Hear the claims one at a time at a table; collect each response before showing any answer.
