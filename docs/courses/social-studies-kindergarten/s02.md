## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 11:** show Job Left Unfinished: a puppet waters a plant but leaves the shared jug across the walkway. What still needs doing? Post the compelling question before retrieving unit 1's rules.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** Who counts on a job (11 to 12)? How can we choose together (13 to 14)? What do story characters show us (15 to 16)? Did our decision help (17)?
- **Closing, day 18:** each child answers the compelling question using Our Jobs Chart, Our Decision Record or a story scene. The teacher writes the spoken claim verbatim beside its named evidence. Different jobs and reasons can support answers.
- **Teaching:** model finishing a job on 12, listening and seeking agreement on 13, and pointing to a story action on 15. On 14 children choose and act; on 17 they judge results. Teach job, responsible, respect, fair, caring, just (putting an unfair thing right), in charge, agree and vote with pictures. Name each source's maker, occasion and purpose; distinguish stories from records of our actions. Compare the dated chart with children's observations, including disagreement.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 18 is marked `observe` in days.csv. The end-of-unit assessment spans days 17 and 18, with dated job and decision observations from days 12 to 17. Section 2.1 owns every item.

**Fresh story, withheld from teaching and exits:** The Puppet Basket is an original six-scene picture text. Rae shares puppets so everyone can choose one. Mo sees that Sam was left out of the show line and makes room, so Sam gets an equal turn. Bo helps collect a classmate's dropped puppets. Jo, the basket helper, returns every puppet after the show. Teacher Lena organizes the show and checks the clear walkway. The class proposes and agrees to return one puppet before choosing another. Kim returns a puppet before choosing again. Read captions and describe the pictures neutrally; children locate the evidence. These are invented examples, not records of a real class.

**Administration:** dated observations supply items 1 and 3. Day 13 ends with two proposal cards drawn from children's ideas. Day 14's twenty minutes: two to reconnect with the question, four to hear the two proposals and reasons, three to seek a shared revision, three to vote and count, five for the first action, one to record the result and two for the exit. If agreement needs longer, take a reversible first trial and revisit its result on day 17. On day 17 use two minutes to reopen, six to compare before and after, ten for individual conversations and two for the exit ticket. On day 18 use two minutes to read the fresh story, six for individual picture responses to items 4 and 5, ten for conversations and two to close the wonder wall. For each of the seven identification prompts, read aloud and let every child mark a separate response row containing scene thumbnails. Pointing or placing a token also counts; record it before resetting. Children work independently, without a group answer or independent reading. Judge the selections after class; do not require six oral explanations from everyone. In conversations connect items 2 and 6, hearing fifteen children each day, about forty seconds each; earlier dated explanations reduce repeated questions. Others draw their contribution. The thirty-minute route adds communication time. These timings are ours, untested. Mark unheard parts not yet observed and complete them on day 19; never rush a communicator.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "I started, so my job is finished." | Starting is confused with completing a responsibility for others. | from memory |
| B | "Voting means everyone wants the same thing." | A fair shared decision is confused with unanimous preference. | from memory |
| C | "We chose it, so it worked." | Choosing replaces action or evidence of its effect. | from memory |
| D | "The biggest character is the responsible one." | Appearance or power replaces evidence of conduct. | from memory |
| E | "Following a rule means making it." | Agreement on a rule and acting under it are not distinguished. | from memory |
| F | "It helps because it helps." | A repeated claim replaces evidence and a reason. | from memory |

**Next-opening responses:** A, compare an unfinished job with a completed one, then finish a new shared job. B, let puppets hear reasons, find a point of agreement and cast one vote each; keep both preferences visible after the result. C, contrast the decision picture with the action picture and compare an observed before-and-after detail. D, point to an action, cover appearance cues and name what it shows. E, place agreeing and following scenes side by side, then identify each in a new story. F, point to a source detail and connect it to a claim with because. Model each contrast on days 12 to 16 before asking independently. If more than a third miss a look-for, use the whole class; otherwise a small group. Use five to ten minutes inside the next opening or day 19's response allocation, never extra days. These are anticipated errors from memory, not measured prevalence. Silence alone is not an error.
