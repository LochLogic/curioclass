## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 11:** show Job Left Unfinished: a puppet waters a plant but leaves the shared jug across the walkway. What still needs doing? Post the compelling question before retrieving unit 1's rules.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** Who counts on a job (11 to 12)? How can we choose together (13 to 14)? What do story characters show us (15 to 16)? Did our decision help (17)?
- **Closing, day 18:** each child answers the compelling question using Our Jobs Chart, Our Decision Record or a story scene. The teacher writes the spoken claim verbatim beside its named evidence. Different jobs and reasons can support answers.
- **Teaching:** model finishing a job on 12, listening and seeking agreement on 13, and pointing to a story action on 15. On 14 children choose and act; on 17 they judge results. Teach job, responsible, respect, fair, caring, just (putting an unfair thing right), in charge, agree and vote with pictures. Name each source's maker, occasion and purpose; distinguish stories from records of our actions. Compare the dated chart with children's observations, including disagreement.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 18 is marked `observe` in days.csv. The end-of-unit assessment spans days 17 and 18, with revised job and decision observations from days 13 to 17. First-teaching work is practice only. Owners: item 1 section 2.1 (with 2.4 observations); items 2 and 3 sections 2.2 and 2.4; items 4 and 5 section 2.3; item 6 section 2.4.

**Fresh story, withheld from teaching and exits:** The Puppet Basket is an original six-scene picture text, with situations unlike The Paper Workshop, exit 15 and exit 16. Rae invites a classmate who is standing alone into the show. The show-turn line moves up one place each show; Pat steps to the front again, and Mo asks that Sam go first today, as the turns go around, so turns stay equal. Bo holds the curtain steady for a classmate setting up the stage. Jo says she will fold the stage cloth and does. Teacher Lena decides where the stage is set up and that Pat sets it up. The class proposes and agrees to put the puppets back in the basket after the show. Kim puts them back. Read captions and describe the pictures neutrally; children locate the evidence. These are invented examples, not records of a real class.

**Administration:** revised observations supply items 1 and 3. After initial job practice on 12 and decision practice on 13 to 14, write a specific next step on each child's picture record and read it privately. Children retry jobs during existing routines on 13 to 17; revisit listening, agreement and action during day 17's supported work. Score only the later dated attempts, keeping the initial records as practice. Day 13 ends with two proposal cards drawn from children's ideas. Day 14's twenty minutes: two to reconnect with the question, four to hear the two proposals and reasons, three to seek a shared revision, three to vote and count, five for the first action, one to record the result and two for the exit. If agreement needs longer, take a reversible first trial and revisit its result on day 17. On day 17 use two minutes to reopen, three to compare before and after, three to confirm or revise the decision with equal votes (pairs then carry out its prepared next step inside the day's job routine), ten for individual conversations and two for the exit ticket. On day 18 use two minutes to read the fresh story, six for individual picture responses to items 4 and 5, ten for conversations, which also score each child's revised "did it help" judgment from day 17, and two to close the wonder wall. For each of the seven identification prompts, read aloud and let every child mark a separate response row containing scene thumbnails. Pointing or placing a token also counts; record it before resetting. Children work independently, without a group answer or independent reading. Judge the selections after class; do not require six oral explanations from everyone. In conversations connect items 2 and 6, hearing fifteen children each day, about forty seconds each; earlier dated explanations reduce repeated questions. Others draw their contribution. The thirty-minute route adds communication time. These timings are ours, untested. Mark unheard parts not yet observed and complete them on day 19; never rush a communicator.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "I started, so my job is finished." | Starting is confused with completing a responsibility for others. | from memory |
| B | "Voting means everyone wants the same thing." | A fair shared decision is confused with unanimous preference. | from memory |
| C | "We chose it, so it worked." | A decision replaces evidence of its effect. | from memory |
| D | "The biggest character is the responsible one." | Appearance or power replaces evidence of conduct. | from memory |
| E | "Following a rule means making it." | Agreement on a rule and acting under it are not distinguished. | from memory |
| F | "It helps because it helps." | A repeated claim replaces evidence and a reason. | from memory |
| G | "The loudest person can choose without hearing others." | Dominance replaces a cooperative process. | from memory |
| H | "Helping lets me take another person's materials without asking." | Helpful intent is confused with respect for others' choices. | from memory |
| I | "People who disagree with the decision are bad." | Dissent is treated as wrongdoing. | from memory |
| J | "Choosing a plan is the same as carrying it out." | Decision is confused with action. | from memory |

**Next-opening responses:** A, compare an unfinished job with a completed one, then finish a new shared job. B, let puppets hear reasons, find a point of agreement and cast one vote each; keep both preferences visible after the result. C, compare an observed before-and-after detail before judging success. D, point to an action, cover appearance cues and name what it shows. E, place agreeing and following scenes side by side, then identify each in a new story. F, point to a source detail and connect it to a claim with because. G, hear each puppet's reason before deciding. H, practise asking before moving shared materials. I, retain a dissenting reason beside the agreed plan without ranking people. J, match each chosen step with an actual action. Model each contrast on days 12 to 16 before asking independently. If more than a third miss a look-for, use the whole class; otherwise a small group. Use five to ten minutes inside the next opening or day 19's response allocation, never extra days. These are anticipated errors from memory, not measured prevalence. Silence alone is not an error.
