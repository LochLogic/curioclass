## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.K.T2.5** (days 24, 26): Use maps, photographs, or drawings to show and explain where important places are near home or school and how they relate.
- **D2.Geo.2.K-2** (days 24): Use maps, graphs, photographs and similar sources to describe places and the connections that shape them.
- **MA.K.T2.6** (days 25, 26): Make maps, drawings, and models that show the physical features of familiar places.
- **D2.Geo.1.K-2** (days 25, 26): Make maps, graphs and other pictures of familiar places.
- skill ssk-local-maps (grade K)
- skill ssk-position-words (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write supporting questions:** Which words help us locate things (20 to 21)? What can maps and globes show (22 to 23)? How can pictures stand for places (24 to 26)? How does an address help locate a school (27 to 28)?
> - **Teaching:** model position words on 20, comparison on 22, the key on 24, a picture graph on 25 and address nesting on 27. Step back on 26 and 29. Ask who made each source, when and why; compare a photograph with observation and a map, correcting mismatches. Teach position, map, globe, land, water, feature, symbol, key and address through objects and pictures. Explain that left and right need a shared viewpoint and maps select features.
>
> - **Carried:** tie map-key to observed features on 24, use it in models on 25, revise unclear symbols on 26, and withdraw supplied symbol meanings on 29. Keep the key and access supports. Retain unit 2's perspective routine for differing map choices on 23 and its claim-and-because routine on 26 and 29; supply no closing reason.
<!-- /skel -->

- **MA.K.T2.5, days 24 and 26.** "Use" maps, photographs or drawings "to show and explain where important places are near home or school and how they relate." The places are school places (playground, library, a nearby park or entrance) and a familiar place each child chooses; "how they relate" is next to, between, behind, near. The home part is met by a chosen familiar place, never a home.
- **MA.K.T2.6, days 25 and 26.** "Make" maps, drawings and models "that show the physical features of familiar places": hill, pond, trees, rocks, ground, and the placed rooms and objects of a classroom model.
- **D2.Geo.1.K-2, days 25 and 26.** "Make" a map, a simple picture graph and a model of a familiar place. **D2.Geo.2.K-2, day 24.** "Use" a photograph and a picture map to describe a place and what is next to it.
- **Limits that bite here.** A symbol's meaning is the maker's, shown in the key, never guessed. No scale, no compass directions, no neatness marks. Draw only what was seen: no invented pond. The graph has two groups, one picture for each counted thing and a labelled place for zero. No drawing of a child's home. First products are practice; only revised records are scored (days 26 and 28).
- **Connections.** MA.K.T2.1 position words, days 24 to 26: every map sentence uses them. Counting and sorting in math (from memory), day 25.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A photograph shows what one camera saw from one spot; a map is drawn from above and leaves things out, so every map is a selection by its maker. A symbol stands for a place and is not the place. A key is the maker's agreement about what each picture means. A block model is a small version made from what was seen. The feedback chain: a first product is practice, it gets written feedback on one feature, position or count, is revised, and only then scored.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** 3 (MA.K.T2.5 and D2.Geo.2.K-2) and 4 (MA.K.T2.6 and D2.Geo.1.K-2), on the unit plan. Item 4 is scored from the revised model, graph and redrawn map, never from the first products.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks a picture (point, circle, token or one mark) in the last two minutes; the teacher reads the prompt aloud. The spoken part is heard from the rotating group of six. Each card is new, printed "invented for practice", and shows the relation without the teacher's demonstration. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E24.** Mark: new courtyard map, a diamond means flower bed and a rectangle means shed, printed in the key, a diamond beside a rectangle; point to what is next to the shed (E24). Spoken, six (E24b): "Show me in the key what it is." Answer: the flower bed; point from the symbol to the key and back. Wrong: "the rectangle is a real shed, you can go in it" (C); "the diamond is a kite" with no look at the key (H).
- **E25.** Mark: new card, a window view beside a photograph of a block model of it; circle the block that stands for the tree. Second mark: new card of four cups and no spoons beside a two-row graph card with four cup marks and one spoon mark; put a finger on the row that is wrong. Answer: the spoon row, it has a mark for none (L). Spoken, rotating group: "How do you know that block is the tree?" Answer: it sits where the tree is, beside the window. The child's own model from supported making is the construction evidence. Wrong: "that block is a real tree" (C); "I picked the nicest block" (D); spoken "because it is" (G).
- **E26.** Mark: new drawn map with a key showing a swing and a fence; circle the key picture for the swing and point to the swing on the map. Spoken, rotating group: "How could someone find the swing?" Answer: the key's swing picture, then the matching picture beside the fence. The child's own drawn map is the map evidence. Wrong: "the triangle, because it looks like a swing" (H); "you find it because it is there" (G).

## Day 24

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Picture maps of places near the school (the playground, the library, a park) from photographs and drawings: where each is and what is next to it; the key, where a small picture stands for a real thing
- note: Map-key model introduced. Check-set additions kept inside this day and the next two: reading map symbols (California K.4.3) and locating familiar places (New York K.6)
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Reopen.** Hand-back: E23, errors B and I. Point to the supporting question "How can pictures stand for places?"
- **6 min. Teaching first.** A photograph of the playground from Our Place Record beside a picture map of the same place drawn from above. Ask who took the photograph, when and why; say what is the same and what the map leaves out. The key: a heart for the playground, a hexagon for the library and a flag for the park; trace from a symbol to the key and back (error H). Say it stands for: the heart stands for the playground, as the globe stands for Earth. Model one claim and because: "I know the hexagon is the library because the key says so, and it touches the heart, the playground."
- **10 min. Supported work.** Each pair has a picture map of places near the school with a boxed key and movable symbol cards. They place a card, trace to the key, and say what is next to it. The rotating group also says where a symbol card is, each using a position word not yet used that day. Hear the rotating group.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E24 on paper.

Holds: teacher holds the key and the claim. Tool: None. By hand: picture maps, symbol cards, key box. Evidence: E24, and the running position-word record (item 1).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: map, picture, symbol, key, feature, hill, pond, tree, rock, model, graph, zero. Frames: "The ___ is next to the ___." "The ___ means ___, because the key says so." "I counted ___."
- **Access.** Tactile string for paths and borders, raised stickers for symbols, a key box with matching raised shapes. Contrasting patterns, not color alone. A child who cannot draw small directs an adult to place pieces. Large print on key cards.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts aloud; accept a point, token, dictated drawing, symbols or a private conversation; extra time for communicators.
- **Reading load.** None; key cards are pictures.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second feature to the key and map, or tell which two places on a picture map are farthest and nearest, without measuring.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Make a map of the table top: a cup, a plate and a spoon from above, with a key box. Count the pieces of one kind and make a graph with one picture each and an empty labelled place for zero. For day 26 draw a familiar place such as a library, a park or a store. Do not draw the child's home.
- **One projector.** Show the photograph and the map together; pairs use paper copies. Show the key and the claim sentence; children trace on paper with a finger.
