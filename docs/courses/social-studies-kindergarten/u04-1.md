# Section 4.1: Putting events in order, and time words


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.K.T3.3, days 34 to 36.** "Put events from personal life, nature, and texts in time order": personal means the class's shared school day, nature means a seed growing and a caterpillar, texts means an original story and an original informational read-aloud. Never events from home.
- **MA.K.T3.3a, days 34 and 36.** "Use sequence words such as first, next, and last to order actions." Said, pointed to or placed on a label card.
- **MA.K.T3.3b, days 34 to 36.** "Use time words such as now, then, long ago, before, after, today, tomorrow, yesterday, morning, afternoon, night, last or next week, month, and year to place events in time." Day 34 gives morning, afternoon, night, yesterday, today and tomorrow; day 35 gives now, then, long ago, before, after, and last and next week, month and year; day 36 returns every word in a log. Before and after were taught in unit 2 as "earlier than the change" and "later than the change" (review, same words; here they place any two events on a strip).
- **D2.His.1.K-2, days 34 and 36.** "Put several events in time order": three or four events on a strip; day 36 names the strip a timeline.
- **Limits that bite here.** Three or four events, no date calculation, no equal spacing. Night is the school under a moon, never a bedroom. No child tells what happened at home or reports a birthday, trip or chore. Unit 5 owns the holiday lesson.
- **Connections.** RI.K.3 (English language arts unit 3, same weeks), day 34: pictures ordered before any text; day 36 texts are heard, never read by children. The wonder wall and puppets Moss and Wren (unit 1, review), days 34 to 36.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** To order is to say what came earlier and what came later. A time word means something only against a reference event: "next week" is after today's marker on the class calendar. A child may say "yesterday" for anything past, so anchor every word on the calendar. A bigger, brighter or favorite picture is not an earlier one. Left to right is the class's agreed way to read a strip, and some home languages run the other way; say so aloud. The day 34 hook only posts the compelling question and the children's wonders; nothing is answered until 4.2.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: day 33 (the course-level response), then unit 4 opens; unit 3 ended on day 30. After: section 4.2, day 37. No readiness items are used. Its own results go to the record sheet: the exits, the rotating group's time words on days 34 to 36, and the day 36 log, which is practice until revised on day 38.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1 (ordering four sets with first, next, last; MA.K.T3.3, MA.K.T3.3a, D2.His.1.K-2) and 2 (the full time vocabulary; MA.K.T3.3b), on the unit plan. Both are sat on day 39, which section 4.2 hosts; this section teaches and practices them. The day 36 log is practice; its revised copy (day 38) is what item 2 scores.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks a picture (circle, dot or token) in the last two minutes; the teacher reads the prompt aloud. The spoken part is heard from the rotating group of six during supported work, one ask at a time. Every card is new, invented for practice, and the teacher says "this is a pretend class". One marking scale on the class sheet: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E34a.** Mark: three fresh cards, a caterpillar, a chrysalis and a butterfly, shuffled. "Show first, next and last with dots." One dot, two dots, three dots in order. Answer: caterpillar 1, chrysalis 2, butterfly 3. Wrong: "the butterfly goes first because it is the prettiest" (A); butterfly 1, caterpillar 3 (H).
- **E34b.** Mark: a paper strip of yesterday, today and tomorrow squares and the card for one real class event set for tomorrow. "Where does tomorrow's card go?" Answer: the tomorrow square, after today. Wrong: the yesterday square, "because it is the next one to the left" (H).
- **E34c.** Spoken, rotating six: "Say the order as you point." Answer: "First the caterpillar, next the chrysalis, last the butterfly." Wrong: "the butterfly is first, it is nicest" (A).
- **E35a.** Mark: a fresh pretend dated record of a school gym with a calendar strip and today marker: a climbing rope hung long ago, a new floor mat coming next week. "Circle what happened long ago." Answer: the rope. Wrong: circles the mat, "long ago is the small picture" (B).
- **E35b.** Mark: the same record. "Put a token on what comes next week." Answer: the mat. Wrong: token on the rope, "next week is before today" (H).
- **E35c.** Spoken, rotating six: "How do you know the mat is next week?" Answer: "It is after today on the calendar." Wrong: "because it is new" (B).
- **E36a.** Mark: a fresh text read aloud, "Cooks wash the beans. Cooks boil the beans. Cooks put the beans in a bowl.", and its three cards, shuffled. "Put the cards in order on the strip." Answer: washing, boiling, the bowl, left to right. Wrong: the bowl first, "I like that one" (A); bowl, boiling, washing (H).
- **E36b.** Spoken, rotating six: "What happened first?" Answer: washing the beans. Wrong: "boiling, the pot is big" (A).
- **E36c.** Spoken, rotating six: "What happened last?" Answer: putting the beans in the bowl. Wrong: "washing, it is the last one on my strip" (H).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Ordered picture cards (day 34, teacher then pairs) become a strip with a today marker (day 35, set by the children) and are named the timeline on day 36, a picture strip read left to right; it returns in 4.2. The class calendar's yesterday, today and tomorrow squares (day 34) gain week, month and year rows (day 35). The day 36 log is kept for written feedback, read aloud to each child, and revised on day 38. First, next, last word cards come off the strip on day 36 and stay on the wall. Puppets Moss and Wren, the wonder wall and partner talk (unit 1, review) continue.
- **Key moment 1, day 34: the order seen as cards that move.** Ideal instrument: large movable picture cards and a class calendar with a today marker. Spec: no tool; print fits.
- **Key moment 2, day 36: the timeline and the log.** Ideal instrument: a blank strip, event cards and a dated picture log with label cards, one copy for each child. Spec: print fits; the log is to make.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn cards on scrap paper, a paper strip folded in thirds, a calendar drawn on the board.

## 6. Days

Every day is 20 to 30 minutes (frame); the parts below add to 20 (day 39 adds to 22), and a 30-minute day adds its extra minutes to supported work or hearing. Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children.

## Day 34


- **2 min. Hook and wonder wall.** Show two original drawings, a washboard and a washing machine (ours). Both help wash clothes. Ask once, "What might change about the work?" Post the compelling question and write the wonders the children offer; any child may pass; no one is asked about washing at home.
- **6 min. Teaching first.** Model first, next, last with the school day cards (morning, afternoon, night; the night card is the school under a moon) and three seed cards (seed, sprout, plant): 2 minutes each. Moss orders the seeds by the biggest picture last (error A); the class fixes it. Then 2 minutes at the class calendar: move the today marker and put one real class event on yesterday, today and tomorrow.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs order a new three-card set, putting on a coat (get the coat, put arms in, zip it), saying "First ..., next ..., last ...". Then each pair sets one real class card on a small paper strip of yesterday, today and tomorrow. The teacher hears the rotating six one at a time, about 80 seconds each (E34c).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E34a and E34b on paper.

Holds: the teacher holds the order, then pairs. Tool: None. By hand: cards, a paper calendar strip. Next day: more time words on the strip. Evidence: E34a to E34c.

## Day 35


- **2 min. Reopen and hand-back.** Hand-back: E34 (A, H). Moss orders the seed cards by size; the class fixes the order with a new set.
- **7 min. Teaching first.** On the class calendar and a strip with the today marker: now is the marker, then is any card behind it; before and after (review of unit 2, same words) place any two events; a real class event last week and one next week; the month page and the year strip with last and next month and year; long ago at the far end, a long time before this year. Cases are the class's own events, never a child's home. Wren shows error B.
- **9 min. Supported work.** Pairs set four cards on a blank strip with a today marker for Room 9, a pretend class: a puppet stage built long ago, a rehearsal last week, a puppet show next week, a new curtain next month. Each pair says one sentence, "The ___ is ___." The teacher hears the rotating six one at a time, about 80 seconds each (E35c).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E35a and E35b on paper.

Holds: pairs hold the strip. Tool: None. By hand: strip, cards, marker. Next day: events from a story on the strip, named the timeline. Evidence: E35a to E35c.

## Day 36


- **2 min. Reopen and hand-back.** Hand-back: E35 (B, H). Wren puts a "next week" card behind the marker; the class moves it.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Four minutes: an original story of Ria and her ball, four scenes with one actor each (Ria kicks the ball; the ball rolls under a bush; Tomas finds it; Tomas rolls it back); the teacher says the children are made up. The cards go on a strip left to right; name it a timeline, a picture strip read left to right. Four minutes: an original informational read-aloud, how a house is built (workers clear the ground; build the walls; put on the roof; paint inside), on a second strip, each card tied to the one before it (error A). Two minutes: say first, next, last, before and after for two pairs of cards, tying left to right to time words (error H).
- **6 min. Supported work.** The dated picture log, practice only: five sets of events (morning, afternoon and night of a class day; brushes washed the day before, drying that day, put away the day after; a new class shelf set up a week earlier and a visitor planned a week later; seeds planted a month earlier and a plant drawing planned a month later; a tree planted a year earlier and another a year later), invented, with dates relative to today. Each child circles the label card for each event on a paper log with a calendar page, about 13 circles. The teacher hears the rotating six one at a time, one minute each ("Which one is happening now?"), noting their time words (E36b and E36c). After class the teacher writes feedback on each log, one relation to revisit as a small picture pair, read aloud on day 38.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E36a on paper.

Holds: children hold the timeline. Tool: None. By hand: strips, the log. Next day: an old photograph as a clue (4.2). Evidence: E36a to E36c; the log is practice.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 34, "What happened first? What happened next? What happened last?"; day 35, "Is it before or after today? How do you know?"; day 36, "What happened just before this? Where does it go on the strip?"
- Written task, day 36: none by children; the log is marked by circling, placing and pointing, and an adult may move cards as the child directs.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors (shown, shown with help, not yet seen): earlier on the left and later on the right; a time word that fits its anchor (calendar or marker); before and after about the right event.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 34.** The wonder wall and "I wonder" (unit 1, review). **Day 35.** Ordering with first, next, last from day 34. **Day 36.** The strip from day 35 and every time word so far.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: first, next, last, yesterday, today, tomorrow, before, after, long ago, week, month, year. Frames: "First ___, next ___, last ___." "___ is before ___." "___ is last week." Home-language words are welcome; a child may point to the word card.
- **Access.** Cards with raised edges, large prints, a ridged today marker; an adult moves cards as the child directs; pointing, signs or eye gaze count.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; accept signs, pointing, an aid or a dictated drawing.
- **Reading load.** None; every label is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Order five events, or say what lies between two events on the strip.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the packet's cards and a printed calendar page: day 34, order the coat and seed cards and say yesterday, today and tomorrow on the page; day 35, lay a strip with today's marker; day 36, order the ball story and the house cards and say first and last. Use school or pretend events, nothing from your own home.
- **One projector.** Show the cards and the strip large; children mark paper rows or move paper cards. Hear the rotating six one at a time.
