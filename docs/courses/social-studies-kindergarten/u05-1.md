# Section 5.1: Days we remember, and whom they honor

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-kindergarten/5/s/5.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-kindergarten u05-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.K.T3.1, this section's share (days 41 to 44).** All of it, taught: "Describe how civic holidays honor important events or people in history." Each day names one holiday, the people or event it remembers, and how remembering is a way to honor them (honor: to show thanks and respect). Days 41 to 43 are on-the-day lessons; day 44 gathers them and adds four more holidays. Evidence is practice here; item 1 is heard revised on day 49 and fresh on day 50.
- **Limits that bite here.** No date recall: the teacher says "long, long ago, in 1865" aloud and no date prints on a child's page. No child is asked what their family does on a holiday, who in the family served, or what the family believes. No headdress, costume or acting out of Native people, of the harvest meal, or of separated people; the unfair-law pictures are looked at, never acted. A child may pass. Native nations are named by their own names, in the present tense. "A day off" is the error, not a fact to deny: many children have no school that day (A).
- **Connections.** Unit 1 and unit 2: "I think ..., because ..." and the sourcing question "who made it, and why?" as review (days 41 to 44). MA.K.T3.3, day 44 only: first, next and last on the school-year strip as review from unit 4; days 41 to 43 use the teacher's wall calendar, because unit 4 is not yet taught when they fall.
- **What the teacher page must explain (adult background; every fact marked (S) is to be checked against the unit plan's named page before a piece is written).**
  - Day 41: federal law names the day Columbus Day (5 U.S.C. 6103); many states and towns observe Indigenous Peoples' Day instead or as well (S). Use the name on the school's calendar and say "some places use another name". Indigenous Peoples' Day honors Native peoples, their histories and their living communities. Columbus Day recalls Columbus's 1492 voyage; it does not mark the discovery of an empty land; many Italian American families keep it as a day of pride in their heritage (S: Smithsonian Native Knowledge 360, Order Sons and Daughters of Italy page). Show a Native nation of our region from its own pages, today; where none is near, the Wampanoag nations (Aquinnah, Mashpee), who live in Massachusetts now (S).
  - Day 42: Veterans Day honors people who served in the armed forces (S: Veterans Affairs history page). The record: in 1621 Wampanoag people and English settlers shared a harvest meal; Paula Peters, a Wampanoag historian, explains why Wampanoag people came; Winslow's account is the English view (S, free archive). The meal does not show lasting fairness; settlers later took Native land (teacher knowledge, not said to children).
  - Day 43: King worked with many others, peacefully, to change laws that kept Black and white people apart (S: King Center biography). Name the unfair laws plainly and at grade level.
  - Day 44: the law names the day Washington's Birthday; many people call it Presidents' Day and remember Washington and Lincoln (S: 5 U.S.C. 6103, National Archives). Memorial Day remembers people who died while serving; Veterans Day thanks all who served (S). Independence Day remembers 1776 (S: National Archives Declaration page). Juneteenth line exactly as the unit plan gives it; it is read for sensitivity with day 43.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1 (MA.K.T3.1, on the unit plan). Item 8 is section 5.3's. Rule 4 holds: no card below shows a picture or case used in teaching on its day. Each day's exit has one picture-only mark (every child) and one spoken ask (the rotating six). The mark alone is never scored as an explanation; the spoken part is the evidence, marked shown, shown with help or not yet seen. If many miss a mark, the repair is in field 4.

**Exit questions.**
- **E41.** Mark: a new picture of a school bulletin board headed with the day's local name (the teacher reads the heading); circle the card the board is remembering. Cards for the Indigenous Peoples' Day set: people of a Native nation at a gathering today, a family cooking a large meal, a fireworks night. For the Columbus Day set: a sailing ship at sea, a family cooking a large meal, a fireworks night. Spoken: "Why do we remember these people on this day?" (frame: "I think ___ because ___"). Answer: to honor Native peoples, and nations who live here today; or to remember Columbus's voyage in 1492, with a clue from the page or picture. Wrong: "it is a day we do not go to school" (A); "it remembers them because they were the best" (G). Case note: the day cannot change between teaching and exit, so only the pictures, the bulletin board and the distractor cards are new.
- **E42.** Mark: new cards, a veteran visiting a school and a harvest display; circle the card that helps explain Veterans Day. Spoken 1: "Why do we have Veterans Day?" Answer: to thank and honor veterans, people who served in the armed forces. Wrong: "it is a day for a party and no school" (A); "it thanks them because they are the best" (G). Spoken 2 (same six): "Who shared the harvest meal in 1621?" Answer: Wampanoag people and English settlers, from the record card. No register letter fits a wrong answer to spoken 2 (plan point); the repair is the record card shown again.
- **E43.** Mark: three new drawings, people reading a book, people working together to change a rule, people playing; circle the picture that shows King's work. The mark never says "unfair laws". Spoken: "Why do we remember his work?" Answer: he worked with others to change unfair laws that kept Black and white people apart (the source detail, not "he was nice"). Wrong: "because he was the nicest man" (G); "so we get a day off" (A).
- **E44.** Mark: on a strip mat, put two new cards in order, a Presidents' Day card (a school door with two paper coins) and a Memorial Day card (a quiet flag at a stone), the one that comes first on the left. Spoken: "Pick one of your cards. Who or what does that day remember?" Answer: Presidents' Day, Washington and Lincoln, who were presidents; Memorial Day, people who died while serving, not all veterans. Wrong: "a day off from school" (A); "Memorial Day is for all the veterans" (H). Independence Day and Juneteenth have no exit; item 1 may use either.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.**
  - "I think ..., because ...": unit 1's spoken claim is review. The teacher models it with a source on 41; children use the frame on 42; on 44 no sentence is supplied. Picture and communication supports stay.
  - "Who made it, and why?" (unit 3's sourcing question): the teacher names each source's maker, time and purpose on 41 to 43; children say "who made it?" on 43.
  - Our Holiday Cards: one card for each holiday, saved on the wall and in the record (teacher holds; children add a drawing). It is carried to 5.2 and 5.3 and is the evidence for item 8.
  - First, next and last, and the strip (unit 4): review on 44; the teacher holds the strip and children place cards.
- **Key moment 1, days 41 to 43: a source seen with its maker.** Ideal instrument: large prints from the nation's own pages (41), our own drawings from the record (42) and from the King account (43), each with a maker and purpose line said aloud. Spec: no tool; print fits; the prints are to make (section 11).
- **Key moment 2, day 44: holidays in order on the school-year strip.** Ideal instrument: a long paper strip with season pictures and seven movable cards. Spec: no tool; paper fits.
- **A room with nothing.** A wall calendar page on scrap paper, a long folded sheet for the strip, holiday cards drawn on scraps, the nation's page read aloud with one sketch.

## 6. Days

Every day is 20 to 30 minutes (frame); the parts below are 25 to 29. Days 41 to 43 are on-the-day lessons taught weeks apart (about week 7, week 12 and week 19), so each opens by retrieving the saved holiday card, not by leaning on a neighbouring day. Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton reads them. A 30-minute day adds its extra minutes to supported work. No timer is shown to children. Marks are picture-only cards (nothing to read) and the spoken part is heard from the day's rotating six, 40 to 60 seconds each; the record column names who was heard and uses one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Every case card is made up for practice and the teacher says so ("this is a pretend class").

## Day 41


- **3 min. Hook and question.** The calendar card for the day, its local name read aloud, beside one picture from the Native nation's own pages. Ask "Who does this day help us remember?" and co-write "Whom do holidays honor, and why?" on chart paper. No hand-back: first day of the unit.
- **7 min. Teaching first.** Two short picture pages read aloud, from the nation's page and from our own calendar talk (S). Name each source's maker, time and purpose, then model one claim with a source: "I think this day remembers ___, because this page says ___." Teach holiday and honor by picture. The day's card goes on the wall as the first of Our Holiday Cards.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs match three practice cards (not the exit's) to the day's card and tell each other "I think ___ because ___"; a partner may speak for a pair. Hear the rotating six, 30 seconds each, about 3 minutes, while the rest draw one card.
- **5 min. Exit.** E41: mark card (1 minute); each child draws one picture on the saved card (4 minutes) while the rotating six are heard, 30 seconds each.

Holds: teacher holds the claim with a source and the maker question. Tool: None. By hand: calendar card, nation's page print, practice cards, chart paper, crayons. Evidence: E41 and item 1 practice record.

## Day 42


- **5 min. Reopen and repair.** Show the saved day 41 card; one retrieval, 1 minute ("Who did it help us remember? What helped you know?"). Repair for E41 (letters A and G): only if many missed and no other lesson has fallen between; otherwise it was made at that meeting's opening. Ours, untested: 4 minutes.
- **6 min. Teaching first: Veterans Day.** The picture sequence "A Thank-You Wall" (ours; a pretend class makes thank-you cards for people who served in the armed forces; one actor to a scene). Teach veteran and honor by picture. No child is asked whether anyone they know served.
- **6 min. Teaching first: the harvest meal and two voices.** Thanksgiving is a day coming soon. Say the record: in 1621 Wampanoag people and English settlers shared a harvest meal; the Wampanoag nations live in Massachusetts now. Say the record sentence (English settlers held a harvest celebration in 1621; Wampanoag people were there and brought food) and that Wampanoag people tell their own history on their own pages. The Winslow line and the Peters statement are cut: neither was reached on a free page (2026-11-19). No costume, no acting out.
- **9 min. Supported work.** Pairs match two question cards ("Who do we thank on Veterans Day?" "Who shared the 1621 meal?") to picture cards and say "I think ___ because ___". Hear the rotating six, 60 seconds each (both spoken asks), about 6 minutes.
- **2 min. Exit.** E42 mark card; the day's card goes on the wall.

Holds: children use the because frame; the teacher holds the two voices. Tool: None. By hand: sequence pages, record card, question cards. Evidence: E42 and item 1 practice record.

## Day 43


- **5 min. Reopen and repair.** Show the two saved cards; retrieve "Whom did each help us remember?" (1 minute). One repair from E42 only if many missed (4 minutes, whole class): the record card shown again; the repair for spoken 2 has no letter.
- **8 min. Teaching first.** Two picture pages (ours, from the King account (S); sensitivity read first). Page 1: a pretend town where a posted rule keeps some people apart from others, with "this is a pretend town; the real laws were like this: some laws kept Black and white people apart." Page 2: people working together, peacefully, to change a rule. Say: Martin Luther King Jr. worked with many others to change those laws. Name the maker, time and purpose. Looked at, never acted.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs sort three practice cards (a rule everyone shares, an unfair rule, people working to change a rule) and say "I think King's work was ___ because ___". Hear the rotating six, 50 seconds each, about 5 minutes.
- **2 min. Exit.** E43 mark card; the day's card goes on the wall.

Holds: children ask "who made it?"; the teacher holds the source detail. Tool: None. By hand: two pages, three practice cards. Evidence: E43 and item 1 practice record.

## Day 44


- **3 min. Reopen.** Post the unit's compelling question, "Why do we remember some days and some people?", on the wonder wall. Retrieve unit 4's review: children put the three saved holiday cards on the school-year strip, first, next, last (unmarked review; ours: if ordering needs support, rebuild the strip together in the next part).
- **4 min. Return and repair.** One repair only: E43 (letters A and G) if many missed, or the notes kept from 41 and 42. Whole class if many; otherwise a small group while the rest look at the saved cards.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** 4 minutes: "Two Presidents" (ours; one actor a scene): Washington was a president and the law names the day for his birthday; Lincoln was president long ago, in a hard time, and on January 1, 1863 issued the Emancipation Proclamation (National Archives); the day is for remembering presidents. The first-president line is cut (no checked page). 5 minutes: three picture cards, one sentence each, dates said aloud: Independence Day (1776), Memorial Day, and Juneteenth, "Juneteenth remembers the day in 1865 when enslaved people in Texas learned they were free."
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs put seven small cards on a strip mat with today marked: those before or on today on the left, those still to come on the right; each pair says whom two cards remember. Hear the rotating six, 60 seconds each, about 6 minutes.
- **3 min. Exit.** E44: strip-mat mark and spoken ask (six children, 20 seconds each, over the minutes); the question stays open on the wonder wall.

Holds: the teacher holds the strip; children place cards. Tool: None. By hand: strip, seven cards, wonder wall. Evidence: E44, item 1 practice record, and the saved card.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 41, "Who does this day help us remember?"; day 42, "Who do we thank on Veterans Day? Who shared the 1621 meal?"; day 43, "What did King work to change?"; day 44, "Whom does this holiday remember, and how do we know?"
- Written task, days 41 to 44: none by children beyond a drawing on the saved card. The teacher scribes the child's "I think ___ because ___" beside it.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names the person, people or event the day remembers; says what remembering does (thanks, respect, learning from their work), beyond a day off; gives one clue from a page or picture in their own words. No date is needed.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 41.** None; first day. **Day 42.** Day 41's card and "I think ___ because ___". **Day 43.** Both saved cards and the maker question. **Day 44.** The three saved cards in order (unit 4's first, next, last), then the whole run of cards.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: holiday, honor, remember, veteran, calendar, long ago, nation. Frames: "This day helps us remember ___." "I think ___ because the ___ shows ___." Home-language words are welcome; a name is said as its owners say it.
- **Access.** Large print, high contrast, pattern as well as color. A child who cannot see gets raised-line cards and a description of each picture. Pointing, tokens, signs and communication symbols count as showing. A child may pass in talk.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every card and prompt aloud; accept a point, a token, a home language or a private conversation; extra time. Judge the content apart from English, speech, vision and motor skill.
- **Reading load.** None for children; everything is read aloud, and printed words are labels only.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say what two different holidays remember (a person, a group, an event) and how each page helped.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the same picture pages and cards at a table: show the day's card, ask the day's question, find one clue in a picture, say "I think ___ because ___", draw it. Ask nothing about the child's home or family. Hold day 41 to 43 on or near the day itself if you can; day 44 needs the strip (a long folded sheet) and the saved cards.
- **One projector.** Show each page large; the strip on the wall or screen. Children mark picture cards on paper; the spoken part is heard at a table.
