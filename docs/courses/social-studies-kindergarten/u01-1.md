# Section 1.1: Wondering about our class

<!-- skel -->
Kindergarten: Living and Learning Together. Unit 1, Learning together. *What makes our class a good place to learn?* (days 1 to 10): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 2.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (1, 2). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 20 to 30 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Children do not read yet. Everything is heard, pointed to, drawn or marked on a picture card. Each day runs 20 minutes; the 30-minute route adds time for communication and recording.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **D1.1.K-2** (days 1, 2): Say why a big inquiry question matters to you personally.
- **MA.K.T1.1** (days 2): Follow rules and expectations with little reminding, and with help ask and answer questions about why rules exist.
- skill ssk-asking-questions (grade K)
- skill ssk-rules-why (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 1:** show Two Drawing Tables: one puppet holds all the crayons; at the other table both can reach them. Ask what each arrangement allows. Post the title's compelling question and record children's wonders without requiring public speech.
> - **Co-write supporting questions:** What do different classmates share (day 2)? Why have rules here and elsewhere (3 to 4)? How can we improve a rule (5)? How can people disagree and still listen (6 to 7)? What supports our answer (8 to 9)?
<!-- /skel -->

- **D1.1.K-2, this section's share.** "Explain why the compelling question matters personally." Days 1 and 2 only open the question and model a personal reason ("Finding this out matters to me because ..."). Children try the reason on both days, but day 1 and day 2 attempts are practice. The scored explanation is the one on day 9.
- **MA.K.T1.1, this section's share.** Day 2 prepares the second half of the standard: children "ask and answer" a question, with help, about one shared thing in the room. Following rules with little reminding is watched from day 3; no rule is taught yet.
- **Limits that bite here.** Spoken, drawn or pointed answers only. No child is asked to report on home, family or anything they own, to share about themselves in public, or to stand as the example of a mistake. A child may pass and show an answer privately. Wrong answers are voiced by a puppet or "someone might say", never by a named child. Preferences differ; the pictured facts still limit what counts as a reason.
- **Connections.** SL.K.3 (ask and answer questions): day 1, the teacher models the "I wonder" question; ELA unit 1 practises it in the same weeks. Day 2 is the New York kindergarten self-and-others addition from the unit plan, kept inside the day.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between a question and a statement ("I wonder why the bear is by the easel" against "That is a bear") and why the first is the one we model. That the day 1 check is an access observation, not a test, and that a child who points, signs or uses a communication aid has answered. That "matters to me" can be any personal use, small or large.

## 2. Where it sits

Section 1.1 opens the year, with no earlier social studies row. It uses the start-of-year check on day 1 (items 1 and 3 serve this section); item 1's bridge runs in day 4's supported work (section 1.2) and item 3's in day 2's supported work. Its results go on the record sheet and select picture cues and partners for section 1.2.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
- Day 1: assessment "readiness" (Understanding day).
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** item 1 (D1.1.K-2, personal importance), scored on day 9; the wonder wall look back is not scored (s01 field 5). Nothing from day 1 or 2 is scored.

**Exit questions.** If a child names home or family in an answer, thank them and bring the answer back to our room. Each has a picture mark by every child and a spoken "why" heard from the day's rotating group of six. A child outside the group is "not yet heard", never "missed". Fictional cases print "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E1.** Mark: circle the picture on a card of our room (the sink, the cubbies, the window, the rug) that you most wonder about. Spoken: "What do you wonder about our room? Why does our big question matter to you?" Answer: a question, asked or pointed to, and any relevant personal reason ("I want to know where things go so I can help"). Record the support used. Wrong: "Because you told us to" (A); "I have to find out because it is school" (A).
- **E2.** Mark: on a new card, circle what both classmates need. Two invented classmates like different things: one likes painting, the other likes clay. Spoken: "What do both need in their shared room? Why does that matter to you?" Answer: both need space and their materials; accept any relevant personal reason. Wrong: "They do not need the same things" (K); "The painter needs a room and the clay one does not" (K); "It matters because you said" (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The compelling question, posted on day 1 and kept on the wonder wall; it is reopened every day of the unit. The idea that Two Drawing Tables shows (the same room can work for everyone or for one) goes into day 2 as "what we share" and into section 1.2 as the reason for rules. The teacher holds both. Children hold only their own wonder card. Nothing is withdrawn here.
- **Key moment 1, day 1: the wonder wall.** Ideal instrument: a large paper wall where a child's spoken wonder is written by the teacher, beside a picture, and cards can be added later. Spec: fits; paper and tape. No tool is used, so there are no gaps or asks.
- **Key moment 2, day 1: the start-of-year check.** Ideal instrument: a teacher's record sheet, one row for each child, with a column for how the child prefers to answer. Spec: fits, printed or hand-ruled.
- **A room with nothing.** Draw the room card and the two tables on scrap paper. Two folded-paper puppets stand in for the hook. Chart paper or the back of a poster is the wall. A lid for an easel, a stuffed toy or drawn bear, torn paper puzzle pieces and the classroom's own blocks serve the day 1 check.

## 6. Days

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding; assessment: readiness. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Open the wonder wall: "I wonder" questions about our new class, and the compelling question posted; each child who wants to says why finding it out matters to them ("Finding this out matters to me because ...")
- note: Lean SL.K.3: ELA unit 1, the same weeks; social studies models the "I wonder" question itself
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **8 min. Hook and wonder wall.** Show Two Drawing Tables (original picture sequence with read-aloud captions): at one table a puppet holds every crayon; at the other both reach them. Name the maker and setting first: "We made this picture for our class; the puppets are pretend." Ask what each table allows. Post the compelling question. Model "I wonder ..." with the toy bear beside the easel; the teacher writes each volunteered wonder on the wall. No child must speak.
- **10 min. Start-of-year check.** Items 1, 2 and 3 of s01 field 4 as access observations: wonder about a picture (heard during the wall), a puppet pair reaching for one puzzle, a block-choice exchange in partner turns. Six children are watched; the rest are noted on days 2 and 3. No score.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E1.

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the question and the wall; children each own one wonder. Tool: None. By hand: speaking, pointing, circling, the exit mark. Evidence: E1 and the record sheet (practice only).

## Day 2

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: All about our class: each person is different, and the class still shares one room and one set of rules
- note: Check-set addition kept (New York kindergarten, self and others), inside this day. Picture cards; no child is pushed to share about themselves
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **2 min. Reopen.** Read the wonder wall. If many gave no personal reason on day 1 (error A), point to one of two classroom uses of an answer, two minutes; a small group takes the rest in supported work.
- **6 min. Everyone Has a Place.** Original picture read-aloud: two invented classmates, one who likes the reading corner and one who likes the water table. Name its maker and purpose: we made it to look at how people differ. Model "What do you need? What do I need?" and the teacher's own why-question: "Why do you think the room has a place for each?" Children echo the question once.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Picture cards of the real room (the rug, the sink, the shelves, the door). In pairs, children point to what many people use and to what one person uses. Item 3's bridge, where needed: a partner turn with picture cues, then fade the cue. No child tells about themselves; pairs describe the cards.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E2.

Who holds what is carried: the pair holds the card; the teacher holds the question. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, sorting cards, the exit mark. Evidence: E2; the day 2 notes complete the record sheet for children not seen on day 1.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 1, "What do you wonder about our room?"; day 2, "What do both classmates need in their room?" and "Why do you think the room has a place for each?"
- Written task: none. Children do not write. The teacher writes each volunteered wonder on the wall and each spoken reason on the record sheet, verbatim.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: (1) a question or a pointed choice, not only a description; (2) a reason that connects to the child's own use of the room; (3) the support used, recorded as information, not as a score.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 1 has none. Day 2 reopens the wonder wall and repairs E1: if many gave no personal reason, the teacher offers two uses of an answer (error A).
- **Fluency target.** None; the standards set none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: wonder, room, same, different, need. Frame: "I wonder ..." and "It matters to me because ...". Accept home language, signs or drawing with dictation; the teacher repeats the idea in English.
- **Access.** Large and tactile picture outlines. A communication aid or a pointed card is an answer. Eye contact and stillness are not look-fors.
- **Check accommodations.** The exit is read aloud. A child may mark, point, circle or draw one mark, or answer privately. A scribe records without supplying ideas.
- **Reading load.** None; the teacher reads every caption aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask a child who is secure to give a second reason the question might matter to someone else, using the picture.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: look at two pictures of the same room together, ask "What do you wonder?", write the wonder down, then ask "Why does that matter to you?" Day 2: show two drawn children who like different things and ask what both need.
- **One projector.** Show the picture sequence and the room cards on one screen. Children mark on printed cards; a screen alone never replaces a child's own mark.
