# Course frame: Kindergarten: Living and Learning Together

Course id ss-k5, kindergarten. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6, one year of social studies in Massachusetts 2018 kindergarten order of ideas (civics, geography,
  history, economics), with the class's own rules and talk first because every later unit runs on them.
- **Days.** 64: 58 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 3, 2 after unit 6) and 2 respond days (after
  the mid-year check). Social studies shares the kindergarten day with reading and math, so the course assumes
  about two lessons a week of 20 to 30 minutes (about 50 to 60 minutes a week) across 32 teaching weeks, not the
  full-year yardstick of 160. The 19 rows are short, talk-and-play skills; 58 unit days give each about three
  lessons, more where a row carries a made product (a map, a class store) or a sensitive topic.
- **Standard.** Content: Massachusetts 2018 kindergarten, MA.K.T1 to MA.K.T4, every standard owned by a row and
  placed in a unit below (the lettered parts of MA.K.T1.4 and MA.K.T3.3 are taught in full inside their rows).
  Skills: C3 K-2 indicators as owned by the rows (the K-2 band is shared with grades 1 and 2; each indicator sits
  in one grade, or deepens in a later one with a depth line in the row). Check set: each unit plan compares
  California, Tennessee and New York and records any addition with its source; the Units table's "Check set adds" column names
  what the writer expects them to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in order: higher court, backed
  sources, dignity and openness, grade and life experience.
- **Base text.** None (pre-work P2: no open social studies program fits). Every day is Curio Class's own. Sources
  are photographs, objects, simple maps and short read-aloud texts: public-domain and open pieces (Library of
  Congress, National Archives, National Park Service, public-domain images) adapted with a credit line, and Curio
  Class's own read-alouds. No day needs a trade picture book or a library visit.
- **Reading level.** Kindergarten students are pre-readers or early readers. Sources are read aloud or are
  pictures and objects; any student page uses pictures with a word or a short label. A source still gets its
  sourcing talk at this age (who made the photo, is it old or new).
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit opens with a compelling question on the class wonder wall (ssk-asking-questions,
  unit 1) and closes with a spoken "I think ..., because ..." answer (ssk-i-think-because, unit 1) drawn from what
  the class saw, read or did, then a look back at the wonder wall.
- **Informed action.** Once in the year, in unit 2: the class makes a real decision about its own classroom by
  listening, trying for agreement and voting, then acts on it (ssk-class-decision, D4.8.K-2). The choice is the
  class's; the course never picks the cause.
- **Own-state method.** Where the anchor names Massachusetts (MA.K.T2.3, the state in an address), the course
  teaches the student's own state, worked through two or three example states, and practises with the school's
  address, never a child's own.

listen to a short story and talk in a group with help, which unit 1 teaches as talk rules anyway.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 3 (2 days, mid-year).** A short one-to-one or small-group talk-and-point check, spread over two
  lessons so every child is heard: say why one class rule exists; give an "I think ..., because ..." answer to a
  class question; use position words to say where something is on a picture map not seen before; tell a map from
  a globe. The teacher records what each child can do; it is not a written test.
- **2 respond days after the mid-year check.** Re-teach what the check shows (most often position words and the
  "because" reason), in small groups.
- **After unit 6 (2 days, end of year).** Talk-and-sort check: order three or four picture cards in time; sort
  then-and-now photographs not seen before and say one thing that changed and one that stayed; sort needs and
  wants and goods and services; say who or what one holiday remembers. The record goes on to the grade 1 teacher.
