# Section 2.4: Did it help, and what we know


## 1. Standards and practices


- **D4.8.K-2, this section's share.** Day 17: children "use listening, consensus and voting to decide on and take action" again: each listens to a reason, seeks agreement, votes once, adds to the plan and carries out a step, confirming or revising the day 14 decision. Day 18 scores it from the later dated attempt (item 3).
- **D2.Civ.11.K-2, this section's share.** Children "explain how people can decide things together", from Our Decision Record (item 2, days 17 to 19).
- **MA.K.T1.2 and D2.Civ.7.K-2, day 18.** Item 1 is scored from the later dated job attempts of days 13 to 17, never the day 12 practice.
- **MA.K.T1.3, days 18 and 19.** Items 4 and 5 are given with a fresh story and repaired on day 19; they are owned by 2.3.
- **Limits that bite here.** No favorable verdict is required: "it did not help" with a seen reason meets. Nobody is blamed for a change that failed. First-teaching work is practice; only later dated attempts after written feedback are scored. Nothing new is taught on days 18 and 19. A communicator is never rushed; unheard parts are marked not yet observed and finished on day 19. Content is recorded apart from speech, English, handwriting or motor performance.
- **Connections.** Spoken "I think ..., because ...", day 18, with reasons no longer supplied (review; not new ownership of D4.1 or D4.4). RL.K.3 (ELA), day 18, in the fresh story.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The scoring chain: practice, written next step, a later dated attempt, then the score. How to compare before and after from one marked spot. How to judge a "did not help" finding. Why the day 17 exit sketch is made from another viewpoint and kept out of discussion. How the record sheet marks independent, content-prompted or not yet observed. The heard rotation: about fifteen children a day in conversation, the rest drawing their contribution.

## 2. Where it sits


Section 2.3 ends day 16 with the story questions; this section looks back at the decision and then checks the whole unit. After day 19 the unit ends and unit 3 opens on day 20. No readiness check. Its results go on the record sheet that goes forward with the class and drive day 19's groups.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 6, with items 1, 2 and 3 (shared with 2.1 and 2.2) on the unit plan. Items 4 and 5 are owned by 2.3 and given on day 18. Day 18 is the `observe` day (days.csv).

**Day's evidence.** Cases are "invented for practice" and never return in teaching or on day 18.
- **E17.** Mark: circle a clue in a reserved new sketch of the changed place, made after the change from a different viewpoint and kept out of the day's talk. Spoken: "Does this new sketch show that our change helped? What would you keep or change?" Answer: names an observed difference or a continuing problem and links it to the judgment; no favorable verdict is required. Wrong: "Yes, it worked because we picked it" (C); "It helped because it helps" (F).
- **F19.** Mark: circle the missed character or rule moment. Fresh try on a new story: a child returns borrowed rhythm sticks, a teacher organizes the music, and classmates agree to pass the box before taking one; another child follows it. Ask one question for each missed part, each followed by "Show me": "Who is in charge? Show me what they did." "Who did what they said they would do? Show me." "Where did the class agree on the rule? Where did someone follow it?" Wrong: "The teacher is in charge because she is biggest" (D); "The one who passed the box made the rule" (E). For a participation gap, the child carries out a fresh accessible shared job and decision.
- **Day 18** has no extra exit ticket; items 1 to 6 and the wonder wall supply the evidence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Our Jobs Chart and Our Decision Record, dated since days 12 and 13, are the class's own records and the evidence children cite. Perspective columns return on day 17 for dissent. The spoken claim and "because" are reviewed on day 18 with reasons no longer supplied; picture and communication supports stay. Both models continue to unit 3, unit 5 and unit 6.
- **Key moment 1, day 17: before and after.** Must be seen: the same place drawn from the same marked spot, side by side. Ideal instrument: the dated "before" sketch and a children's "after" sketch (drawn in day 17's 3-minute step), plus the reserved sketch for the exit, drawn by the teacher from the door, showing one clue the change helped and one thing still in the way. Spec: print fits; no tool. Gaps and asks: none (TOOL-ASKS.md has no entry).
- **Key moment 2, day 18: separate responses.** Must be seen: each child marks their own response row for each prompt (seven identification prompts), with scene thumbnails, without a group answer. Ideal instrument: printed rows of thumbnails with tokens. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn rows of scene sketches, paper tokens, and a record kept on any sheet.

## 6. Days

## Day 17


- **3 min. Reopen.** Read Our Decision Record aloud; the compelling question. If many missed error E on day 16, repair here with up to 5 spare minutes (the day stays inside its frame).
- **3 min. After sketch.** Children add the "after" drawing of the place from the marked spot. The teacher drew the reserved sketch beforehand from the door, with one clue it helped and one thing in the way.
- **4 min. Compare.** The "before" sketch and the children's new "after" sketch of the same place from the same marked spot, side by side.
- **5 min. Confirm or revise.** Listen to a reason, look for what both sides accept, one block each. Set any dissenting reason beside the plan in the perspective columns. Pairs then carry out the prepared next step inside the day's job routine. The teacher keeps a look-for checklist (listens, seeks agreement, votes, acts) for about six children a day, in rotation; the rest are read from Our Decision Record (item 3).
- **10 min. Conversations.** About half the class is heard, two of the four questions each: "How did we decide? Did it help? What shows that? What could we change?" The teacher writes key words against a look-for checklist. The others draw their first judgment on their before-and-after card. This judgment is practice; the teacher writes a next step on each card that evening.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E17, on the reserved sketch.

Who holds it: the class holds the decision, the teacher the record. Tool: None. By hand: both sketches, the reserved sketch, blocks, cards. Evidence: E17; the step and vote feed item 3.

## Day 18


- **3 min. Story.** Read The Puppet Basket (seven scenes, original, withheld from teaching and exits). Read captions and describe pictures neutrally.
- **8 min. Picture responses.** Items 4 and 5: each child works alone, marking separate rows of one response sheet (seven scene thumbnails a row, cards-response-rows), one row for each of the seven identification prompts. Pointing or placing a token counts; collect the sheets, then record. The teacher judges the marks after class.
- **3 min. Revise.** Each child adds or changes one mark on the day 17 card after hearing the written next step (read to the six; the rest look at their page with a partner).
- **11 min. Conversations.** The children not heard on day 17: items 1, 2 and 6, with Our Jobs Chart or Our Decision Record, and the claim "I think ..., because ..." to the compelling question, written down word for word beside its named evidence. The revised "did it help" judgment of every child is read from the day 17 card and its revision, and heard where possible. Others draw their contribution.
- **2 min. Wonder wall.** Close it: the class looks at the answers to the compelling question.

The thirty-minute route adds communication time. Unheard parts are marked not yet observed and finished on day 19; never rush a communicator. Who holds it: each child, alone. Tool: None. By hand: response rows, tokens, record sheet. Evidence: items 1 to 6.

## Day 19


- **2 min. Reconnect.** The compelling question; today fixes what the record showed.
- **12 min. Small groups.** At most two groups by the record sheet's letters A to J, 5 minutes each with the teacher; the others work in pairs from a picture card the teacher shows and tells aloud (no reading). Each group does its next-opening response (s02 field 6). Any aide or small-group time used is named on the record sheet. Children point to a completed action and name their next step.
- **13 min. Fresh try.** F19, a new story, then the missed question; unfinished observations are completed. A participation gap is met with a fresh accessible shared job and decision, not a talk about it.

Who holds it: the teacher moves among groups; each child holds their own next step. Tool: None. By hand: the record sheet, a fresh story, cards. Evidence: F19.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 17, "Did it help? What shows that? What could we change?"; day 18, "How can each of us help our class? Show something in a story or on our chart that supports your answer."
- Written task, day 17: a drawn and dictated first judgment on the before-and-after card, revised on day 18 after the teacher's written next step.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names an observed difference or a continuing problem; links it to the judgment, either way; names a change to keep or to try.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 17: day 14's decision and its steps; unit 1's perspective columns for dissent; the job routine step.
- Day 18: the spoken claim and "because"; day 15 and 16 character words, without picture help unless needed.
- Day 19: the error contrasts, only for groups that missed them.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: before, after, same, different, help, change. Frames: "Before, ___. After, ___. It helped (or did not help) because ___."
- **Access.** Raised-line before-and-after outlines; an adult moves a block or a token as the child directs; a communication aid or private exchange counts. Offer a quiet time slot for a child who cannot be heard in the circle.
- **Check accommodations.** Read everything aloud. Accept home language, signs, pointing with explanation, drawing, dictation, a familiar aid or a private exchange. No independent reading. Score civic understanding apart from speech, English, handwriting and movement.
- **Reading load.** None; captions are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second difference in the two sketches, or say what to try next if it did not help.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** After a week, look at the small change from the same spot, with a before and after drawing. Ask, "Did it help? What shows that? What could we change?" On the last day (day 19) repeat only what was missed with a fresh picture story and a fresh job or choice at the table. Ask one claim question: "How can each of us help our class?"
- **One projector.** Show each scene or sketch in turn; children mark their own response rows on paper, so the screen is never the answer sheet.
