## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.K.T1.3** (days 15, 16): With help, point to characters in stories and texts who show fairness, caring, justice, responsibility, or authority, or who show how rules are made and followed.
- skill ssk-story-characters (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write supporting questions:** Who counts on a job (11 to 12)? How can we choose together (13 to 14)? What do story characters show us (15 to 16)? Did our decision help (17)?
<!-- /skel -->

- **MA.K.T1.3, this section's share.** Children "point to characters in stories and texts who show fairness, caring, justice, responsibility, or authority, or who show how rules are made and followed", "with help". Day 15 teaches fair, caring and just (putting an unfair thing right), and a rule made and followed. Day 16 teaches responsible and in charge, and returns to the rule. Pointing counts as the answer; the help is picture cues and a replay of the scene.
- **Limits that bite here.** The stories are made up or very old; they are not records of our class, and the teacher says so (maker, occasion, purpose). A character is never judged by size, looks or clothes. The lion's threat in the fable is told calmly and is not legitimate authority; no one acts out the threat. The paw line is read word for word, slowly, with a pause after it, or retold as: the lion held her paw and the mouse begged. No moral is pressed on a child. Only Curio Class originals and one public-domain fable are used.
- **Connections.** RL.K.3 (ELA unit 2), days 15 and 16: the question "What did the character do?" is the bridge. Unit 1's rule reasons, day 15 (a rule has a reason). The spoken "because", days 15 and 16.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to read a picture story with pauses that ask "What did the character do? How do you know?" and cover the faces to keep to the action. That justice at five is putting an unfair thing right. That the fable is attributed to Aesop, from the 1919 text on read.gov (public domain in the United States; from memory, check the exact lines before the retelling is made), told with new drawings and a credit; its stated moral, "A kindness is never wasted", is read but the question stays on action. That being in charge means it is someone's job to decide for the group, like a teacher, which a lion's strength is not; making a rule together is a separate idea.

- **2 min. Reopen.** Job routine, as set on day 12, runs in its usual place (about six children watched and dated). Post and co-write "What do story characters show us?" Name the story's maker: Curio Class made it up for this class. It is not a record of what we did.
- **3 min. Words with pictures.** fair, caring, just (putting an unfair thing right), rule.
- **8 min. Read-aloud.** The Paper Workshop (six scenes, Curio Class original, to make), with a pause at each: "What did the character do? How do you know?" Pia shares paper, Nell collects scraps, Teo makes room so Isa gets an equal share, Teacher Ruiz organizes the work, the class agrees to clear the table and everyone does.
- **5 min. Teacher models pointing.** Cover the faces. Pia: caring. Teo: fair, and just (he puts the unfair thing right). The class agreement: a rule is made. Say "I know because she ..."
- **5 min. Pairs.** Each pair holds a scene card (twelve cards, two sets of six, or two pairs to a card); one points to an action and the other tells it with "The ___ did ___. I know because ___."
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E15.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the story and the question; pairs hold a scene card. Tool: None. By hand: two sets of six scene cards, word pictures. Evidence: E15.

- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Characters who are responsible or in charge in an open folktale of honesty or courage, beside our read-aloud, where the rule made and followed on day 15 is revisited

- **2 min. Warm-up.** Job routine, as set on day 12, runs in its usual place. Day 15's exit comes back: point to an action with faces covered (error D).
- **2 min. Words with pictures.** responsible (does what she said), promise. "In charge" is taught only with Teacher Ruiz below.
- **7 min. The Lion and the Mouse.** Aesop, 1919 text with new drawings and a credit. Say first that it is a very old story told by many people. The paw line is read word for word, slowly and calmly, with a pause after it (or retold: the lion held her paw, the mouse begged); no acting it out.
- **5 min. Responsible.** "What did the mouse say she would do? Did she do it?" Frame: "She said ___. She did ___." Name her courage.
- **7 min. In charge, and rules.** Back to The Paper Workshop scenes: Teacher Ruiz is in charge: organizing is her job, deciding for the group, like a teacher. Teach "in charge (someone whose job it is to decide for the group)" here. Ask of the fable one question, "Is the lion in charge? What shows that?" (strong is not in charge). Lay the scene where the class agrees to clear the table beside the scene where Nell clears it: one makes the rule, one follows it.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E16.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the two stories; children hold the words. Tool: None. By hand: the fable cards, the Paper Workshop scene cards. Evidence: E16.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 15, "What did the character do? How do you know?"; day 16, "What did she say she would do? Did she do it?" and "What makes someone in charge?"
- Written task, day 16: none beyond the exit. Each child may draw one character's action from either story, and the teacher writes "The ___ did ___."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names an action, not a look; ties it to one quality word (fair, caring, just, responsible, in charge); says how they know, by pointing or in words.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: fair, caring, just, responsible, in charge, rule, promise. Frames: "The ___ did ___. I know because ___." "She said ___. She did ___."
- **Access.** Raised-line scene cards; each scene described aloud for a child who cannot see it; an adult moves the card as the child directs; a communication aid or pointing counts.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token, dictation, a familiar aid or a private exchange. Civic understanding is recorded apart from speech.
- **Reading load.** None. Captions and the fable are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Point to a second character who shows a different quality, or find the quality in a scene the class has not discussed.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the six printed scenes or draw them, and ask on each, "What did the character do? How do you know?" Tell the fable from the credited text, then ask, "What did she say she would do?" Keep to actions.
- **One projector.** Show one scene at a time on the screen; the matching cards stay on the table for pointing, so the child points and does not click.
