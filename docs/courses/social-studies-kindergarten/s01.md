## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 1:** show Two Drawing Tables: one puppet holds all the crayons; at the other table both can reach them. Ask what each arrangement allows. Post the title's compelling question and record children's wonders without requiring public speech.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** What do different classmates share (day 2)? Why have rules here and elsewhere (3 to 4)? How can we improve a rule (5)? How can people disagree and still listen (6 to 7)? What supports our answer (8 to 9)?
- **Closing, day 9:** children answer the compelling question with a reason from Our Class Charter, Two Drawing Tables or the assessment cards. The teacher writes their spoken claims verbatim. Safety, fairness and listening can support different answers.
- **Teaching:** name each source's maker, setting and purpose before use. Distinguish a made-up illustration from our real charter. Model rule and consequence on day 3, claim and reason on 5, and listening on 6. On days 3 and 5 the teacher models a child asking "Why do we have this rule?" and an answer; children echo both. On day 8 the class reopens one charter rule and chooses with the talk rules (item 5). On 7 compare two views without ranking people; on 8 children choose reasons. Teach rule, fair, safe, reason and point of view through pictures and actions.


MA.K.T1.1 parts: follow expectations with little reminding; ask and answer why-questions with help. The C3 file lists codes only; indicator meanings were checked in the publisher's framework.
- **Limits:** spoken reasoning, not independent reading or writing. Preferences differ; pictured facts still constrain reasons. No punishment exercise, family disclosure or child labelled a rule-breaker. Unit 2 owns the year's informed action.
- **Carried:** perspective becomes two picture-and-reason columns on day 7, revisited on 9. Claim-evidence-reasoning is tied to the spoken because on 5 and 8. Withdraw teacher-supplied reasons on 9; keep picture references and communication supports. Retain both models for later units.

## 4. Readiness check, whole

**Given:** the start-of-year check, in the twelve minutes after the hook on day 1, once the compelling question is posted. No sitting comes before day 1.
Kindergarten assumes no earlier social studies row, so these are access observations, not a test. On day 1 the twelve minutes replace the day's supported work. Item 1 is heard during the hook's wonder wall. For item 2 the teacher reads the prompt aloud and every child points to or marks a picture choice; reasons are heard from six children. Item 3 runs as partner turns while the teacher watches six children. Children not yet seen are looked at in the supported work of days 2, 3 and 4 (item 3, item 2 and item 1, with that day's six and any child still unseen) and recorded on the record sheet by the end of day 4. Demonstrate the response choices and record each child's preferred way to communicate. No score bars entry. Results select picture cues, partners and the bridges below.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 9 is marked `observe` in days.csv: the end-of-unit assessment uses fresh tasks on day 9 and revised observations after teaching. First-teaching observations, including day 1, are practice only. Day 8 teaches. It is not a separate thirty-minute test. Owners: item 1 to section 1.1; items 2, 3 and 4 to 1.2; items 5 to 8 to 1.3.

**Fresh source packet, withheld from instruction and exits:** School Doorway shows a pretend school's notice, "Keep this doorway clear," beside a trolley blocking entry. Garden Path shows a pretend public garden's sign, "Stay on the path," beside crushed seedlings and an intact bed. These pictures are drawn for Curio Class and printed "invented for practice"; they are not records of actual events. Read the notices neutrally and describe visible details without supplying the reason. Two class-question cards ask whether the class playground balls live in a net bag by the door or in a bin in the hall; each shows balls within reach. Each child chooses freely. A peer may agree; comparison can identify either agreement or difference. Never assign a view to a child.

**Administration for thirty children:** Every day of the unit runs thirty minutes, with the time to move thirty children counted in. Day 1 uses twelve minutes for the hook and wonder wall, twelve for the readiness check and six for the exit. Days 2 to 7 each use three minutes to reopen, eight to model, fourteen for supported work while the teacher notes the rotating group, and five for the exit; a field 4 bridge replaces up to ten supported minutes, and only one runs on a day. On day 8 teach: four minutes to repair the day 7 exit and reopen one charter rule for a shared choice, seven minutes to model the claim and a why-question on a separate teaching card, fourteen for partner practice while the teacher notes talk rules (item 5) for the day's rotating group, five for the exit ticket, the six asking and answering in pairs. On day 9 use two minutes to introduce the task, twenty-five for the fresh-source items in five groups of six (five minutes a group and a minute to move between groups) and three to return to the wonder wall as a class look back, which is not scored. Within each group the minutes are: items 1 and 7 together two minutes (one sentence each in turn); items 3 and 4 together one minute, on School Doorway (Garden Path is heard on day 10); items 6 and 8 together two minutes (a pair exchange: each views, then the partner's why-question and the answer). Items 2 and 5 take no added day 9 minutes: each child's revised attempt is taken inside that child's own day 9 group (the item 6 partner talk gives a response to another's idea, the item 8 exchange gives the why-question and its answer), with the day 8 shared choice and dated rule-following occasions on days 7 to 9 as further records. Record separate look-fors even when one answer serves several. Other children sit at tables with paper, crayons and three pictured Our Class Charter rules laid out, and draw one picture of why one chosen charter rule helps. The teacher collects each child's dictated line in the look back and on day 10; a child who finishes draws a second rule or sorts the day 6 reply cards. The drawing is practice and is not scored. Score item 1 from the child's answer in the day 9 group, not from the whole-class look back. For items 2 and 5, the practice note comes from the one day among days 3 to 7 on which the child is in the rotating group of six (five days, thirty children; the record sheet's group-day column assigns each child to a day, and day 8 repeats the day 3 group): write the child one action and one next step on the picture record and read it privately that day or the next. The revised attempt is the child's day 9 group. Score only that later dated attempt; a child with no practice note by day 9 is recorded "not yet observed", not scored. Day 6 talk-rule observations are practice only; use days 7 to 9 for revised discussion evidence. This thirty-minute route is ours, untested. Do not treat an unheard response as a failure or rush a communicator: record it as not yet observed and finish it in day 10's fresh-try minutes. A part still unheard after day 10 stays "not yet observed" on the record; it is never inferred.
