## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 1:** show Two Drawing Tables: one puppet holds all the crayons; at the other table both can reach them. Ask what each arrangement allows. Post the title's compelling question and record children's wonders without requiring public speech.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** What do different classmates share (day 2)? Why have rules here and elsewhere (3 to 4)? How can we improve a rule (5)? How can people disagree and still listen (6 to 7)? What supports our answer (8 to 9)?
- **Closing, day 9:** children answer the compelling question with a reason from Our Class Charter, Two Drawing Tables or the assessment cards. The teacher writes their spoken claims verbatim. Safety, fairness and listening can support different answers.
- **Teaching:** name each source's maker, setting and purpose before use. Distinguish a made-up illustration from our real charter. Model rule and consequence on day 3, claim and reason on 5, and listening on 6. On 7 compare two views without ranking people; on 8 children choose reasons. Teach rule, fair, safe, reason and point of view through pictures and actions.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 9 is marked `observe` in days.csv: the end-of-unit assessment is observation over days 8 and 9, supported by dated notes during the unit. It is not a separate thirty-minute test. Section 1.1 owns every item below.

**Fresh source packet, withheld from instruction and exits:** School Doorway shows a pretend school's notice, "Keep this doorway clear," beside a trolley blocking entry. Garden Path shows a pretend public garden's sign, "Stay on the path," beside crushed seedlings and an intact bed. Curio Class's illustrator creates these explanatory cases, not records of actual events. Read the notices neutrally and describe visible details without supplying the reason. Two class-question cards ask whether to put picture books in baskets or on a low shelf; each shows books within reach. Each child chooses freely. A peer may agree; comparison can identify either agreement or difference. Never assign a view to a child.

**Administration for thirty children:** use five groups of three on each day, fifteen children per day. On day 8 use three minutes to model the claim routine on a separate teaching card, fifteen for observation and two for the exit ticket; on day 9 use two minutes to introduce the task, fifteen for observation and three to return to the wonder wall. Within each three-minute group, children connect their rule explanations, rule improvement, perspective comparison and closing claim, then exchange why-questions. Record separate look-fors even when one utterance serves several. Other children draw a reason from the teaching charter, independently. This twenty-minute route is ours, untested; the thirty-minute route adds ten minutes of communication and recording time. Dated earlier observations supply items 1, 2 and 5 where available, reducing repeated questioning. Do not treat an unheard response as a failure or rush a communicator: record it as not yet observed and finish during day 10's fresh-try allocation.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | "The question matters because the teacher says so." | Personal purpose is not yet expressed. | from memory |
| B | "A rule is only to stop us having fun." | A command is recognized without its shared purpose or consequence. | from memory |
| C | "Listening means sitting perfectly still." | Appearance is confused with attending and responding to meaning. | from memory |
| D | "We disagree, so one person is bad." | Different views are confused with different worth. | from memory |
| E | "It is best because it is best." | Repetition replaces a supporting reason. | from memory |
| F | A child names a fact but never asks or answers why. | Questioning and answering about a claim need teaching. Do not infer this from silence alone. | from memory |

**Next-opening response, inside existing minutes:** A, offer two possible classroom uses of an answer and invite a personal choice. B, enact a rule with and without it, naming who benefits. C, model a response to meaning while sitting or standing. D, place two reasons side by side and name agreement or difference without ranking people. E, point to a pictured consequence and connect it with because. F, model an asker and responder, then exchange roles on a fresh claim. Whole class if more than a third missed the look-for, otherwise a small group. These anticipated errors and responses are from memory, not measured prevalence.
