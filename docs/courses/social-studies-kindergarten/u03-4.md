# Section 3.4: Addresses, finding our school, and the check


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.K.T2.3, days 27 to 29.** "Name" the parts of an address: street number and name, city or town, state, and country, using the school's address and the class's own state; the standard's words "Massachusetts" and "United States" are the model, and the classroom state is used where it differs. All five parts are named.
- **MA.K.T2.4, days 28 and 29.** "With help, find" the school's town on a state map, then the school on a street map of that town. Help is recorded.
- **Day 29 observes the unit's earlier codes.** MA.K.T2.1, T2.2, T2.5, T2.6, D2.Geo.1.K-2 and D2.Geo.2.K-2 are taught in 3.1 to 3.3; this section runs the observation and the response on day 30.
- **Limits that bite here.** Never a child's own address. The practice addresses are invented and labelled so. No postal code, scale or compass directions. Locating our state on a United States map (day 28) is an unassessed extension, dropped if time is short. Unit 5 owns the holiday lesson.
- **Connections.** MA.K.T2.1 on day 29, School Garden. The claim and because routine, days 29 and 30.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** An address nests: a number on a street, in a town, in a state, in a country. A state map and a street map show one place at two sizes. Some children have no stable home address, or are uneasy about it, so the school is the example for everyone and no home address is asked, written or compared. The school is public. Day 29 sorts each record cell as independent, content-prompted or not yet observed; a child right only on the taught crop is content-prompted. Silence is not an error. A child who communicates with symbols, pointing or an aid is given the time and judged on geography alone.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: section 3.3, ending day 26 with a drawn map and key. After: the end of unit 3, then day 31, course-level check 1. No readiness items; none for unit 4 is reserved. Its own results go to day 30's groups, read from the record sheet.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 5 (MA.K.T2.3), 6 (MA.K.T2.4) and 7 (the spoken claim and because), on the unit plan. Day 29 hosts all seven items; items 1 to 4 are owned by sections 3.1 to 3.3. Day 29's evidence is the seven items below, so it has no extra exit ticket: items 1, 2, 5 and 6 are marked or pointed on paper by each child, and items 2, 3 and 7 are heard.

**Exit questions.** Each child marks a picture (point, circle, token or one mark) in the last two minutes; the teacher reads the prompt aloud. The spoken part is heard from the rotating group of six. Each card is new and printed "invented for practice". If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E27.** Mark: address strips read aloud, new fictional address 46 Cedar Road, Pine Town, Oregon, United States; circle the street, then the state. The teacher touches and reads one strip at a time as each question is asked, and rereads on request; strips sit in a fixed row, and the child marks the strip just read. Spoken: "Name the number, street, town, state and country." Answer: 46; Cedar Road; Pine Town; Oregon; United States. Wrong: "Oregon is the country" (E); "Pine Town is the street" (E).
- **E28.** Mark: fresh invented diagram of Willow State with Lake Town marked, and a separate Lake Town street map with a school on Birch Street; circle the town on the state map and the school on the street map. Spoken: "Find the town on the state map, then the school on the street map. What street helps you?" Answer: the Lake Town dot; the school on Birch Street; Birch Street. Wrong: points to the whole state as the school's place (F); "Birch Street is the town" (E).
- **E30.** Mark: fresh try, a new map and matching picture of a school beside an orchard, a bridge over a stream and a path between them; the key uses a crescent for school and a cross for bridge; circle the school and the bridge. Spoken: "Use the key to locate the school and bridge. How do people cross the stream?" Answer: school beside the orchard; the bridge over the stream; they use the bridge. Other missed parts are rechecked with the equivalent fresh task of day 30. Wrong: "the crescent is the bridge, it curves like one" (H); "people cross because they cross" (G).
- **E30, other parts (fresh, none used in teaching or on day 29).** Item 1: three new toys, a drum between a cup and a hat, page this way up; "Where is the drum? Where is the hat compared with the cup, from here?" Answer: between the cup and the hat; the hat is right of the cup (J, K). Item 2: a new globe and flat map pair, land patterned; "Show water on both; how are they alike?" Answer: both show Earth's land and water; the flat one is not a different Earth (B). Item 4: a new bounded patch of two cups and no spoons; make a two-row graph with a place for zero; answer: two cup marks, none for spoons (L). Item 5: "12 Aster Court, Fairview, Texas, United States", strips read one at a time; answer: 12, Aster Court, Fairview, Texas, United States (E). Item 6: a new Lake Town pair as in E28 with a school on Elm Street; answer: the town dot, then the school on Elm Street (F). Each is asked for the part missed only.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The address nesting model (country, state, town, street and number as rings): taught by the teacher on day 27, held by the children on 28; withdrawn on 29 when the strips are rearranged without the rings. The map-key: supplied meanings withdrawn on day 29; School Garden's key is on the page, and the teacher reads a key word only when a child points to that key entry, never saying which map symbol matches it. The claim and because routine: no supplied reason on days 29 and 30. Revised model, graph and map records are kept for scoring item 4.
- **Key moment 1, day 27: the nested rings.** Ideal instrument: floor hoops or tactile string rings, country largest, with address cards set into them. Spec: no tool; objects and print fit.
- **Key moment 2, day 28: the same place at two sizes.** Ideal instrument: a state map and a street map of the same town, printed large with the dot and the street matching. They must be original or public domain, with the school's location verified; no commercial screenshots. Spec: print fits; the maps are to make.
- **Key moment 3, day 29: each response collected before an answer is shown.** Ideal instrument: paper response rows with the source pictures and a clear place to mark, and the record sheet. Spec: print fits; the record sheet is a paper table.
- **A room with nothing.** Chalk or string rings, hand-lettered strips, a hand-drawn street map.

## 6. Days

Every day is 20 to 30 minutes (frame); the parts below add to 20, and a 30-minute day adds its extra minutes to the supported work. Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children.

## Day 27


- **2 min. Reopen.** Hand-back: E26 (H, G). Post "How does an address help locate a school?"
- **6 min. Teaching first.** The Massachusetts State House (24 Beacon Street, Boston, Massachusetts, United States) and the Washington State Legislative Building (416 Sid Snyder Avenue Southwest, Olympia, Washington, United States), each three minutes, strips read aloud and set into the rings (error E). Local facts are rechecked before printing.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs set address cards into the rings for the school's own address, then for the second building. The school is the shared case. Hear the rotating group, including item 3 for that group (a place near school, what is next to it, how people use the space).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E27 on paper.

Holds: teacher holds the nesting. Tool: None. By hand: rings, address cards. Never a child's own address. Evidence: E27.

## Day 28


- **2 min. Introduce.** Hand-back: E27 (E). Give each child the map feedback card written after day 26 and read it to them as they begin the redraw. Today's question: "How can a map help us find our school?"
- **6 min. Teaching first.** Model the State House: find the Boston dot on the state map, then Beacon Street on the Boston street map; model the Legislative Building the same way in Olympia (error F).
- **10 min. Supported work.** Four minutes: each child redraws the day 26 map from the written feedback; the redrawn map is scored (item 4). Six minutes: in table groups of four with one shared state and street map pair, each child in turn, with help, finds the school's town on the state map and the school on the town's street map; note the help for the rotating group only (every child's help is recorded on day 29, item 6). Hear item 3 from the rotating group. The United States map is an unassessed extension of a few minutes, used only after the supported example is done and dropped if time is short.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E28 on paper.

Holds: children hold the two sizes. Tool: None. By hand: redrawn map, state and street maps. Day 28 location work is practice. Evidence: E28; the redrawn map for item 4.

## Day 29


- **2 min. Introduce School Garden.** An invented place, not a photograph of our school. Read the labels aloud; give no symbol meanings.
- **8 min. Individual picture responses.** Items 1, 2, 5 and 6, each on its response row: position words from the key, the globe and flat map, the five address parts, and the fresh state and town map extracts. No partner answers; collect each response before showing an answer.
- **8 min. Hearing.** Item 3 was heard from the rotating group on days 26 to 28; today confirms it only, with the School Garden part ("Use the key to show two places") for each child, and hears any item 2 explanation still missing. The closing claim (item 7) and any missing explanation are heard on day 30. While the teacher hears, the rest of the class adds one picture to the class key card, quietly and unscored.
- **2 min. Close the wonder wall.** Read the wonders; mark the ones the unit answered.

Marking: about fifteen teacher minutes after day 29 combine dated products, selections and explanations by part (ours, untested). Holds: children hold the claim. Tool: None. Evidence: items 1 to 7.

## Day 30


- **2 min. Reconnect.** Hand back the day 29 record by part; each child sees one cell, privately.
- **8 min. Four tables, and pairs.** The record sheet's 0 and 1 cells set the tables, named by task and never as the children who missed: Words table, item 1 (A, J, K); Key table, items 2 and 3 (B, C, H, I); Map table, item 4 (D, L); Address table, items 5 and 6 (E, F). Pairs, changed each day, work on G. The teacher leads one table for 2 minutes in turn; the other tables use their written move with a partner, as one picture card each, on a new case from the contrasts in sections 3.1 to 3.4.
- **10 min. Fresh tries.** Each child tries a new case for the part missed (E30 for map and key), and the teacher hears the closing claim (item 7) with a named source, scribed beside it, and unfinished explanations from day 29. Each child names one corrected relation.

Holds: tables hold their move. Tool: None. Evidence: E30; children right only after help are content-prompted.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 27, "Which part is the number? the town? the state?"; day 28, "Which dot is the town? Where is the school on this street map?"; day 29, "How can we show someone where things are? Name something you saw that tells us."
- Written task, day 29: none by children. The teacher scribes each closing claim beside its named source.
- Judged with three look-fors: a claim; a named detail from a named source; a reason joined by "because". A supported argument for words, a model or another map also meets.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 27.** Position words with the address rings. **Day 28.** The key and the nesting from day 27. **Day 29.** All earlier practice, unsupplied. **Day 30.** The missed part only.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: address, number, street, town, state, country, school. Frames: "The street is ___." "The town is ___." "I know because ___." Home-language names for places are welcome.
- **Access.** Raised or string rings, large-print strips, a tactile dot for the town, an adult placing pieces as the child directs. Pointing with symbols counts as naming.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all text aloud; accept home language, signs, pointing with communication symbols, dictated drawing, an aid or a private conversation. Independent means no supplied content answer; label-reading help is kept apart from help that gives a location. Longer time for communicators; day 30 finishes any not yet observed.
- **Reading load.** None; strips are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** After the supported example, find the school's street on the street map without the label read aloud. Locating our state on a United States map is the unassessed extension.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the school's or a public library's address, or the capitol strips, never the home. Set the strips into rings on the table, then find the town on a state map and the building on a street map. On day 29 use a map you have not shown, and hear the closing claim. On the last day, re-teach the one missed part and try a new case.
- **One projector.** Show the strips and maps large; children mark paper rows. Collect each response before showing the answer. Hear the claims one at a time at a table.
