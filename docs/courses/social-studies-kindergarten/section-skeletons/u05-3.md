## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.K.T3.2** (days 48, 49, 50): Compare and contrast the traditions and celebrations of people from different cultural backgrounds.
- **MA.K.T3.1** (days 50, 51): Describe how civic holidays honor important events or people in history.
- **MA.K.T1.4** (days 50, 51): Ask and answer questions and use books to learn about national symbols, songs, and texts of the United States.
- **MA.K.T1.4a** (days 50): Explain why the United States flag has its colors, stars, and stripes.
- **MA.K.T1.4b** (days 50): Explain why the bald eagle is the national emblem of the United States.
- **MA.K.T1.4c** (days 50): Explain why "The Star-Spangled Banner" is the national anthem.
- **MA.K.T1.4d** (days 50): Explain what the words of the "Pledge of Allegiance" mean.

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Co-write supporting questions:** Whom do holidays honor, and why (41 to 44)? What do symbols tell us, and how do we know (45 to 47)? How are traditions alike and different (48 to 49)?
> - **Closing, day 50:** scribe each child's claim, named evidence and reason from the holiday record, Symbols We Ask About or Two Celebrations. Remembering struggles, honoring service and passing on traditions support different answers.
> - **Teaching:** model sourcing and a because on 41, distinguish record from interpretation on 45, and model comparison on 48. Name maker, time, audience and purpose; on 42 compare one short Winslow line with one Peters statement. Teach honor, veteran, symbol, anthem, pledge, tradition, alike and different through pictures.

> - **Carried:** return to unit 4's timeline on 44, separating the annual holiday strip from long-ago events on 45 to 47. Retain continuity-change for unit 6. Revisit unit 1's perspective on 48; revise it on 49: different practices have reasons, not one universal pattern. Withdraw supplied comparisons on 50; keep blank charts and the claim frame.
