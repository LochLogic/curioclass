## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.K.T1.2** (days 11, 12): Take on jobs at school and home, follow through on them, and be helpful and respectful to others.
- **D2.Civ.7.K-2** (days 11, 12): Practice good civic habits while taking part in school life.
- skill ssk-jobs-helping (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Hook, day 11:** show Job Left Unfinished: a puppet waters a plant but leaves the shared jug across the walkway. What still needs doing? Post the compelling question before retrieving unit 1's rules.
> - **Co-write supporting questions:** Who counts on a job (11 to 12)? How can we choose together (13 to 14)? What do story characters show us (15 to 16)? Did our decision help (17)?
> - **Teaching:** model finishing a job on 12, listening and seeking agreement on 13, and pointing to a story action on 15. On 14 children choose and act; on 17 they judge results. Teach job, responsible, respect, fair, caring, just (putting an unfair thing right), in charge, agree and vote with pictures. Name each source's maker, occasion and purpose; distinguish stories from records of our actions. Compare the dated chart with children's observations, including disagreement.
<!-- /skel -->

- **MA.K.T1.2, this section's share.** Children "take on jobs" and begin to "follow through on them" and be "helpful and respectful to others". Day 11 teaches what a job is and who counts on it. Day 12 is the first practice at finishing a job helpfully and respectfully. Children do the same jobs in the class's own routines on days 13 to 17, and those later dated attempts are what item 1 scores.
- **D2.Civ.7.K-2, this section's share.** Children "practice good civic habits" in school life: care for shared things, a fair turn at each job, and finishing what others count on.
- **Limits that bite here.** "At school and home": the home half is met with invented cases only (puppets, cards marked "invented for practice"). No child reports a chore, a family or a home. No sticker chart, ranking, reward or "best helper". Every job is accessible, with no lifting, climbing or blocking an exit, and jobs rotate with no job kept for boys or for girls. Adults keep safety jobs (the door in a drill, scissors).
- **Connections.** Unit 1's Our Class Charter, day 11 (a job is doing your part under the rules we made). The spoken "I think ..., because ..." (ssk-i-think-because), day 12, as review only.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** At five, a job is finished when the people who count on it can use what is left, so starting is not finishing (error A). "Respectful" includes asking before taking what another child holds (error H). Why home jobs are fictional: homes differ, and nothing about a family's care is inferred from chores. Why the exit cases are new (rule 4), and why the spoken "why" of each exit is heard from a rotating group of six, so each child is heard about once a week.

## 6. Days

## Day 11

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Class jobs: what each job is and who counts on it (line leader, plant waterer, door holder); the compelling question on the wonder wall
- note: Retrieval task: unit 1's rules (a job is doing your part under the rules we made). Home jobs are examples only; no child reports chores done at home
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **3 min. Hook.** Job Left Unfinished (picture read-aloud, to make): a puppet waters a plant and leaves the shared jug across the walkway. "What still needs doing?" Children point or say.
- **2 min. Wonder wall.** Post the compelling question, then co-write "Who counts on a job?" before anything is retrieved.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** R11 (field 3), in turn-and-tell pairs on a charter picture, the teacher listening at five pairs, with the charter on the wall.
- **6 min. Teaching first.** Three class jobs on picture cards: line leader, plant waterer, door holder. For each: what the job is, who counts on it, what finished looks like. Words with pictures: job, responsible. Name the story's maker and purpose: Curio Class made it for this class to think with, and it is not a record of what we did.
- **5 min. Pairs.** Each pair takes one job card; one points to who counts on the job and the other tells what finished looks like. The teacher listens at three pairs.
- **3 min. Back to the hook.** The puppet finishes: jug returned, walkway clear. Two pictures side by side, unfinished and finished.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E11, on paper.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the charter and the jobs; children hold the words with picture cards. Tool: None. By hand: picture cards, puppet, plant, jug, charter. Evidence: R11, E11.

## Day 12

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Take a class job and follow through; what helpful and respectful looks like and sounds like (a picture chart)
- note: Job practice receives written feedback; revised attempts during days 13 to 17 routines supply the later scored record.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **3 min. Warm-up.** Day 11's exit comes back as a finished or not finished pair of pictures (error A). Spoken claim as review: "I think this one is finished, because ..."
- **4 min. Worked example.** The teacher models the door holder: holds until the last child is through, asks before moving a stool someone sits on, and leaves the door as found. Say aloud what helpful looks like and respectful sounds like ("May I?", "It's ready for you").
- **5 min. Take a job.** Each child takes an accessible job card from a set with several copies and shared tasks (four paper passers, four shelf tidiers, table wipers, scrap pickers, one door holder, one plant waterer), and two children share one job where needed, so every child has a real job to finish and says who counts on it, with the frame "My job is ___. ___ counts on it."
- **6 min. Do the job through to the end.** The jobs happen in the room at once or in two rounds. The teacher watches for the finishing step and for asking before taking, and notes it on the picture page (practice; no score).
- **5 min. Our Jobs Chart.** Children and teacher draw what helpful looks like and what respectful sounds like; the teacher writes the words children say. This chart is our own record.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E12, a new partner job.

That evening the teacher writes one specific next step on each child's picture page (for example, "Put the jug back too"). It is read to the child privately during the class's routines on days 13 to 15, about 15 seconds each, outside the day's minutes. Children do the job again in routines on days 13 to 17; about six children are watched each day, in rotation, and dated.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the charter and the model; each child holds one job. Tool: None. By hand: job cards, picture pages, chart sheet, crayons, an envelope of sorting cards for each pair. Evidence: E12; the picture pages feed item 1.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 11, "Who counts on this job? What happens if it is not finished?"; day 12, "What does helpful look like? What does respectful sound like?"
- Written task, day 12: a drawn and dictated picture page. The child draws their job and says who counts on it; the teacher writes the words. Practice only; the written next step comes back.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names the job and who counts on it; shows or tells the finishing step; shows one respectful act (asking, waiting or leaving it ready).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: job, finish, ready, ask, help, respect. Frames: "My job is ___. ___ counts on it." "It is finished when ___." Home-language labels on cards where a family offers them.
- **Access.** Raised-line or tactile job cards; the real objects to hold; an adult moves an object as the child directs; a communication aid or private exchange counts. No job needs lifting or climbing.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token, drawing, dictation, a familiar aid or an adult who moves the card as directed. Civic content is recorded apart from speech or movement.
- **Reading load.** None. Everything is read or told aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Name a second person who counts on the job, or find a way to finish it so a classmate with different needs can use what is left.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Pick one real job in the learning space, such as watering a plant. Show it through to the end, then let the child do it. Ask, "Who counts on this? What makes it finished?" Use puppets or drawings for the unfinished job; ask nothing about chores at home.
- **One projector.** One screen shows the hook picture and the chart; the cards are drawn or printed once and passed; children act the jobs in the room, not on the screen.
