- **MA.K.T4.3 and MA.K.T4.2, days 57, 58, 60, 61.** Children "give examples of goods and services people pay for with money they earn": the store's drawings are goods, the paper-folding is a service, and tokens stand for money from work. They "ask and answer questions about buying, selling, and trading" and "explain how people choose among things they need and want" (the choice on 58). All parts, with help recorded.
- **D2.Eco.1.K-2, days 58, 60.** The limit is named aloud: five tokens cannot pay for both.
- **MA.K.T4.1 and D2.Eco.3.K-2, days 59, 60.** The closing claim describes work from a named source. When a claim names a worker, the teacher asks what that worker knows or can do.
- **Limits that bite.** No coin values, change, wages, budgets or opportunity cost. Prices are one to five identical tokens, each worth one (math K.CC.B.5, counting; already taught). Everyone starts with five tokens, never earned by good behavior or taken away. Trade is voluntary and a child may decline. Saving is allowed and never better. No question about what any family has or earns. Tokens model exchange, not worth.
- **Connections.** Unit 3's day 26 map and key (57, 58, review). Unit 5's hearing why people choose differently (58). Unit 1's "I think ..., because ..." (59, the claim frame). 6.1's know, do, use icons (59).
- **What the teacher page must explain.**
  - Every child has one roster turn on 58, 59 or 60 for the spoken parts (items 1, 4 and 7). A turn is a first try, one spoken next step, and a revised try; only the revised try is scored. Day 60's group also gives short reasons for the paper items. A spoken part not heard stays "not yet seen", never inferred.
  - The marking scale is one: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
  - Marks on paper are by every child; the teacher reads all prompts and judges reasoning apart from speech or handwriting.
  - A saved token is a choice, not a lesson; "not all money comes from work" is said aloud on 58.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: buy, sell, trade, token, save, choose, source. Frames: "I buy ___. I give ___ tokens." "I think ___, because ___." "I choose ___ because ___."
- **Access.** Raised tokens and price dots, large symbols, an adult placing pieces or handing tokens as the child directs. A child may decline any trade or buy.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; accept home language, signs, pointing, symbols, a private turn or longer time for communicators. Reasoning is judged apart from speech, English and handwriting.
- **Reading load.** None.
- **Extension, same standard.** Explain how one repair uses a good, such as a part, while the repair stays a service.
