- **MA.K.T2.2, this section's share (days 22, 23).** All of it, taught with help: "explain how maps and globes are alike and different, including maps of the world designed in different ways." Day 22 compares a globe with a flat map. Day 23 sets two world maps designed differently beside the globe. It is checked on day 29 (item 2, section 3.4).
- **Limits that bite here.** No projection words. No scale, distance or compass directions. The ocean names are said by the teacher; no child is asked to name continents or oceans. No map is called the right one or the wrong one. No child is asked to find where their family comes from.
- **Connections.** MA.K.T2.1 position words, days 22 and 23: "in the middle", "near the edge". Unit 2's perspective routine, day 23: a different maker made a different choice.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A globe is a small model of the round Earth and shows land and water as they sit, but only one side at a time. A flat map shows all of it at once, and that needs a choice: a round thing cannot be laid flat without cutting or stretching, so the maker decides where the cut and the middle go. Putting a different ocean in the middle changes the page and not the Earth. Say "the maker chose", never "the real map" or "the wrong map". Adult background only; children get the objects and the question "what does each help us see together?"

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 22, "How are the globe and the map alike? How are they different?"; day 23, "What is in the middle of this map? What does it help us see together?"
- Written task, day 23: none by children. The teacher scribes each child's one-sentence comparison, "Both ___, but ___."
- Judged with three look-fors: names one true likeness (land and water of Earth); names one true difference (round or flat, one side or all); says the maker chose the middle.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 22.** Position words from 3.1 on the tray ("What is next to the cup?"). **Day 23.** Land and water on the globe from day 22, and the viewpoint in "from here".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: globe, map, land, water, round, flat, middle, alike, different. Frames: "Both show ___." "The globe is ___, the map is ___." "This map has ___ in the middle." Home-language words are welcome.
- **Access.** Land is felt: raised or string outlines, rough paper for land and smooth for water. A child who cannot see traces with the teacher's hand over theirs. Large print, high contrast, pattern not color alone. Pointing, tokens and communication symbols count as showing water.
- **Check accommodations.** Read prompts aloud; accept a point, token, symbols, a home language or a private conversation; give extra time. Judge geography apart from English, speech, vision and motor skills.
- **Reading load.** None; labels are said aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a third thing that is the same on all three, or say which ocean is nearest to a land shape on each.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a ball (or a clean bowl) with paper land shapes beside a flat sheet drawing of the same shapes. Find water on both and say one way alike and one way different. For day 23, draw the shapes twice with a different middle and ask if the land moved. No map of the child's home or family land.
- **One projector.** Show the globe by camera or a picture of it, and the two maps side by side with the same land shape outlined. Children mark paper cards; the spoken part is heard at a table.
