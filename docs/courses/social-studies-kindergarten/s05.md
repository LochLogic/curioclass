## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

Day 50 is marked `observe` in days.csv. The end-of-unit assessment combines individual observations on days 45 to 49 with fresh tasks on 50. Section 5.1 owns all items. Record every lettered part separately; recognition or recitation alone cannot meet an explanation goal.

**Materials:** make a bound, original read-aloud, Symbols We Ask About, with dated source pictures, oral source notes and separate teaching, exit and assessment pages. Use the public-domain flag resolution and Thomson report, the anthem history and current Pledge text in field 10. Children ask questions and find answers in the book with adult reading support. Dates and full statements stay on teacher pages, not student pages. Printed picture cards have short labels. Do not reuse a taught question, scene or completed comparison for an exit or assessment. Required historical facts necessarily recur; change the source view and explanatory question rather than inventing new facts.

Reserve the photograph of Great Seal Hand Press, accession 2015.0028.01, for item 3. On 50 read its museum, object date and use aloud, without giving the eagle explanation. Its photograph is a source about an actual object; the original comparison drawings are secondary illustrations. Confirm the eagle is legible before finalizing the print.

For traditions, teach through food and gathering on 48: Hanukkah latkes and the Oshogatsu festival's rice-cake demonstration. On 49 compare singing and music using the Jewish customs account and the festival's koto performance, noting that music need not be a song. Describe greeting guests without claiming one universal greeting. Reserve two original assessment cards, with new compositions and oral source notes: Jewish families playing dreidel at Hanukkah, based on the Jewish customs account; and families watching calligraphy at the documented Oshogatsu festival. The adult reads what each scene depicts. Neither scene represents all Jewish or Japanese American families.

**Administration, ours, untested:** on 45 to 49 use two minutes to reconnect, six to read and model, ten for supported source work and individual observation, and two for the exit ticket. Note unaided responses before offering feedback; partner answers do not count. Rotate listening across the class and keep a checklist of missing parts. On 45 begin flag explanations; on 46 add eagle explanations; on 47 add anthem and Pledge explanations; on 48 to 49 gather missing symbol parts, holiday explanations, questions and source-based comparisons. Supported rehearsals remain teaching evidence, not independent passes.

On 50 use two minutes to introduce sources, fifteen for fresh-source tasks, missing observations and individual claims, and three to close the wonder wall. Other children mark source clues and rehearse privately while the teacher listens. The thirty-minute route adds ten minutes for individual communication. Record unheard parts as not yet observed; complete these during 51's fresh-try time before deciding a response. Do not mark silence or a voluntary refusal to recite as a content error.
