## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 41:** show an original calendar card and a picture of a Native nation of our own region today, from that nation's own pages (the Wampanoag nations where no nearby nation's pages exist). Ask "Who does this day help us remember?" as the day's question. The unit's compelling question is posted on day 44, with the saved holiday cards.
- **Co-write supporting questions:** Whom do holidays honor, and why (41 to 44)? What do symbols tell us, and how do we know (45 to 47)? How are traditions alike and different (48 to 49)?
- **Closing, day 50:** scribe each child's claim, named evidence and reason from the holiday record, Symbols We Ask About or Two Celebrations. Remembering struggles, honoring service and passing on traditions support different answers.
- **Teaching:** model sourcing and a because on 41, distinguish record from interpretation on 45, and model comparison on 48. On 44 add a Juneteenth picture card: "Juneteenth remembers the day in 1865 when enslaved people in Texas learned they were free." Step back on 49 to 50. Name maker, time, audience and purpose; on 42 compare one short Winslow line with one Peters statement. Teach honor, veteran, symbol, anthem, pledge, tradition, alike and different through pictures.

**Materials:** make a bound, original read-aloud, Symbols We Ask About, with dated source pictures, oral source notes and separate teaching, exit and assessment pages. Required historical facts necessarily recur; change the source view and explanatory question rather than inventing new facts.

Reserve the photograph of Great Seal Hand Press, for item 3 (accession number to confirm when the page is read).
