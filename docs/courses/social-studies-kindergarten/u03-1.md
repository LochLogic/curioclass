# Section 3.1: Words that say where

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-kindergarten/3/s/3.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-kindergarten u03-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.K.T2.1, this section's share (days 20, 21).** All of it is met for the first time: "Describe" where people, objects and places are, "using position words such as near, far, left, right, behind, and between." The words taught are near, far, left, right, behind, in front of, between and next to. Section 3.4 returns to it on days 29 and 30.
- **D2.Geo.2.K-2, day 21.** "Use" a simple classroom picture map to describe where things are. The children read a flat picture of a room. They do not make a map (section 3.3 does).
- **Limits that bite here.** Left and right are always said from a named viewpoint ("from here", or "with the page this way up"). Between always names two things. No compass directions, no measuring, no distances in steps or feet. Nobody is asked where a thing is at home.
- **Connections.** Math K.G.A.1 (position words), day 20: the course's math unit 3 runs the same weeks and reinforces; this unit teaches the words itself. Unit 2's talk routines, day 20: the wonder wall and the talk circle.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Left and right depend on whose side: one toy is left of a box for a child on this side and right of it for a child on the other, and neither child is wrong, so the class says the viewpoint every time. Between needs two reference things. Near and far compare two places and are not a measured distance. Many five-year-olds swap left and right; the words are practiced in use, not tested for speed (from memory). Wonders on the wall are scribed on cards without a name.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1 (MA.K.T2.1, on the unit plan). Item 1 is scored on day 29 from School Garden and from the running record of position words used in fresh arrangements on days 21 to 26. Day 20 work is practice; the next step is written and read aloud.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks a picture on paper in the last two minutes (point, circle, place a token or draw one mark); the teacher reads the prompt aloud. The spoken part is heard from the day's rotating group of six during supported work. A child outside the group is recorded "not yet heard" for the spoken part. Each card is printed "invented for practice", is new, and shows the relation without the teacher's demonstration. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E20.** Mark: new card of two toy children facing the viewer, one between a drum and an easel and one beside the drum only; circle the child between the drum and the easel. Spoken: "Which child is between the drum and the easel? Say where that child is." Answer: between the drum and the easel (both named). Wrong: circles the child beside the drum only and says "she is between, she is next to the drum" (J); says "next to the easel" for the child in the middle (J).
- **E21.** Mark: new playground drawing, gate at the page's left, slide in the middle, sandbox at the page's right; circle what is between the gate and the sandbox. Spoken, page this way up: "Where is the gate compared with the slide?" Answer: circles the slide; the gate is left of the slide. Wrong: "the gate is right of the slide" (K); turns the card and says "left is the way my hand points" (A).

The wrong examples are always spoken by a puppet or shown on a teacher-made card, never taken from a child's answer.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The viewpoint ("from here"; "the page this way up"): the teacher holds it on day 20 and names it in every sentence; on day 21 the children say it first and the teacher says it only when it is missing; it is not withdrawn here and returns on day 29 when School Garden faces everyone the same way. The eight position words: the teacher models them on 20, the children choose them on 21.
- **Key moment 1, day 20: the relation must be seen to change.** Ideal instrument: real objects and toy figures a hand can move, with a second object added so between can be seen. Spec: no tool; objects and print fit.
- **Key moment 2, day 21: the page has one fixed way up.** Ideal instrument: a printed picture map of the classroom drawn from Our Place Record photographs, one copy for each pair, held the same way up for all. Spec: no tool; print fits. Tool gaps and asks: none (the course TOOL-ASKS.md has no entry for this unit).
- **A room with nothing.** Two lunch boxes, a toy and a lid; chalk lines on the floor; a hand-drawn room picture.

## 6. Days

Every day is 20 to 30 minutes (frame); the parts below add to 20, and a 30-minute day adds its extra minutes to the supported work. Parts are the lines in bold: the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children.

## Day 20


- **4 min. Hook and wonder wall.** A puppet asks where the crayons are. The teacher first points and says "over there", then locates them with words, beside the paper. Post the compelling question "How can we show someone where things are?" and scribe three wonders on cards. Co-write the first supporting question, "Which words help us locate things?"
- **6 min. Teaching first.** Model near, far, left, right and between with a toy car, a box, a basket and a toy child. Move one thing at a time. Turn the viewer and name the new relation (error A); add a second object to the toy child and ask what is between (error J).
- **8 min. Supported work.** Each pair has a tray of small objects. One child places a toy and says where it is; the partner places a different one. The teacher names behind, in front of and next to as children place toys, and hears the rotating group of six.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E20 on paper.

Holds: teacher holds the viewpoint. Tool: None. By hand: tray of objects, toy figures, wonder cards. Evidence: E20.

## Day 21


- **2 min. Reopen.** A game with the day 20 words and a tray of objects. Hand-back: E20, error J. The written position-word next step is read to each pair.
- **6 min. Model on a real place.** The teacher describes where things are on the playground, from the Our Place Record photograph on the wall, or from the playground itself if the day's routine already has the class outside (no extra walk inside these minutes). Then the same on the classroom picture map: turn the card the same way up for everyone, name left and right on the page, then the place (error K).
- **10 min. Supported work.** Each pair places a toy person on the classroom picture map and says where two things are. The teacher scribes one dictated sentence under each child's map, "The ___ is ___ the ___, from here." Hear the rotating group.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E21 on paper.

Holds: children say the viewpoint first. Tool: None. By hand: classroom picture map, toy person, dictation line. Evidence: E21, and the running position-word record (item 1).

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 20, "Where is the toy now? What else is near it?" and "What is between them?"; day 21, "Which side is that, from here?"
- Written task, day 21: none written by children. The teacher scribes each child's sentence "The ___ is ___ the ___, from here" under the child's picture map.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the word fits the picture; a second thing is named; the viewpoint is named or the page is kept the same way up. Left and right slips are noted, not marked down.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: near, far, left, right, behind, in front of, between, next to. Frames: "The ___ is ___ the ___, from here." "The ___ is between the ___ and the ___." Home-language words and gestures are welcome and the teacher adds the English word beside them.
- **Access.** Objects can be felt: a child who cannot see touches each object and the hand-placed relation. A child who cannot hear gets gestures, signs and picture cards for the words. A child who cannot write small or place small pieces points, uses tokens or directs an adult to place pieces. Large print and high contrast on cards.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all prompts aloud. Accept a point, token, communication symbols, a home language or a private conversation. Give communicators extra time; never infer understanding from speech or movement.
- **Reading load.** None; no child reads. Labels are pictures.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say two relations about one object at once (for example behind one thing and next to another), or direct a partner to place an object using only position words.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a tray of small things at the table: a toy and a cup, a spoon and a lid. Move one and ask where it is, then add a second and ask what is between. Say "from here" each time. For the exit, draw a picture of three things on paper and have the child circle the one in the middle. Never ask about the child's own room or home.
- **One projector.** Show each picture while the children mark their own paper cards. The rotating group's spoken part is heard at a table while the others finish their cards.
