## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Which measurements are enough to build a shape?"
**Hook, day 121:** Match turn diagrams with different ray lengths but equal openings. Which measurements describe the shape? Sort before naming relationships; defer angle addition to day 122. Field 10 names the source structure.
**Questions:** Does adjacency fix a sum, day 123? When can three lengths close, day 127? Does changing orientation change a triangle, day 128? Which faces count, day 134?
**Closing claim:** Conditions can determine one shape, several or none; angle facts, decomposition and layers determine missing measurements. Construction record C1, slice practice and composite record P1 support this claim; day 137 checks it.
**Teaching:** Model angle equations on day 124, compass intersections on day 127, plane slices on day 131 and layers on day 132. Step back for construction comparisons and applications. Explain why adjacency alone gives no fixed sum and why prism height is perpendicular to its base.

<!-- skel -->
- **7.G.B.5:** Use facts about supplementary, complementary, vertical, and adjacent angles in a multi-step problem to write and solve simple equations for an unknown angle in a figure. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.EE.B.4a:** Solve word problems leading to equations of the form px + q = r and p(x + q) = r, where p, q, and r are specific rational numbers. Solve equations of these forms fluently. Compare an algebraic solution to an arithmetic solution, identifying the sequence of the operations used in each approach. For example, the perimeter of a rectangle is 54 cm. Its length is 6 cm. What is its width? Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.G.A.2:** Draw (freehand, with ruler and protractor, and with technology) geometric shapes with given conditions. Focus on constructing triangles from three measures of angles or sides, noticing when the conditions determine a unique triangle, more than one triangle, or no triangle. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.G.A.3:** Describe the two-dimensional figures that result from slicing three-dimensional figures, as in plane sections of right rectangular prisms and right rectangular pyramids. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.G.B.6:** Solve real-world and mathematical problems involving area, volume and surface area of two- and three-dimensional objects composed of triangles, quadrilaterals, polygons, cubes, and right prisms. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask why adjacency alone fails on day 123 and what an overlay establishes on day 128. P1 requires a written decomposition with units; field 5 supplies its look-fors and samples.

## U8. Practice that returns

Field 4 supplies spaced signed arithmetic, decimal expansions and both two-step equation forms. Students attempt before feedback and compare arithmetic and algebraic routes without time pressure.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach adjacent, vertical, unique, plane section and perpendicular with labeled examples. Offer "These conditions allow ___ because ___." Read prompts aloud; rehearse reasons before writing. Provide large-print or tactile outlines, explicit labels without color dependence and a scribe following directions. Extend by finding two nonidentical triangles with three shared measures, keeping the same standard.

## U10. Two rooms

With one child, alternate the adult's counterexample and the child's independent construction. One projector supports paper tasks; student digital turns remain required for complete construction evidence. Day 138 names unfinished geometry for the handoff.

## U11. Still to make

Field 10 lists remaining materials. Field 8 gives device preparation for student-controlled construction.
