## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How do we find a fair price after a change?"
**Hook, day 47:** Compare two invented geometric banners with fractional dimensions and shaded portions. Can a larger banner have a smaller shaded percentage? Connect unit rates and the reference whole to the compelling question about a changed price. Field 10 names the source structure.
**Questions:** Which quantity is one whole, day 48? Which division gives the requested rate, day 49? What is 100 percent after a change, day 54? Which base does each charge use, day 61? What makes an error percentage meaningful, day 64?
**Closing claim:** A justified price comparison names every percent base and checks the result. Day 61's price audit, day 62's fixed-principal comparison and day 66's resource claims support this claim; day 67 checks it alongside fractional rates.
**Teaching:** Model labeled division on day 49, equivalent expressions on day 52 and the accepted-value denominator on day 63. Step back for day 61's plan, estimate and revision and day 66's interpretation of incomplete claims. Teacher pages explain why changing a base changes the question.

- **7.RP.A.1:** Compute unit rates associated with ratios of fractions, including ratios of lengths, areas and other quantities measured in like or different units. For example, if a person walks 1/2 mile in each 1/4 hour, compute the unit rate as the complex fraction 1/2/1/4 miles per hour, equivalently 2 miles per hour. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.RP.A.3:** Use proportional relationships to solve multistep ratio and percent problems. Examples: simple interest, tax, markups and markdowns, gratuities and commissions, fees, percent increase and decrease, percent error. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.RP.A.2b:** Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs, equations, diagrams, and verbal descriptions of proportional relationships. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.NS.A.2d:** Convert a rational number to a decimal using long division; know that the decimal form of a rational number terminates in 0s or eventually repeats. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.EE.A.2:** Understand that rewriting an expression in different forms in a problem context can shed light on the problem and how the quantities in it are related. For example, a + 0.05a = 1.05a means that “increase by 5%” is the same as “multiply by 1.05.” Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.RP.A.2c:** Represent proportional relationships by equations. For example, if total cost t is proportional to the number n of items purchased at a constant price p, the relationship between the total cost and the number of items can be expressed as t = pn. Parts, by verb: whole standard.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask which quantity means one on day 49, why reversing a discount needs a different operation on day 54, and what a resource total cannot reveal on day 66. Day 61's written audit must name each base, order the changes and check the result against an estimate.

## U8. Practice that returns

Use field 4's retrieval. Other warm-ups alternate taught scale factors, rate tables, equations and percent forms. Give feedback after an independent attempt. Days 48 and 56 mix familiar representations and numbers without a countdown or speed comparison.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach whole, per, principal, commission and accepted value through labeled examples. Offer "The base is ___ because ___." Read short contexts aloud; students rehearse explanations before writing. Provide large-print lines, tactile partitions and a scribe following directions. Keep labels readable without color. Extend by designing two offers with equal final costs but different charges, then proving equality.

## U10. Two rooms

With one child, the adult supplies the second comparison or challenges a stated base; the child keeps individual work. On day 68, review a miss before the ten-minute readiness check. With one projector, display one statement while every student draws and calculates on paper. No device is required.
