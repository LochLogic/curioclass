## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can you tell if two quantities grow together at the same rate?"

**Hook, day 13:** Display paper mixture cards, invented for practice: 4 scoops with 10 cups, 8 with 20, and 8 with 24. Which preserves the recipe, and what numbers establish that? Begin with the compelling question; embed retrieval in these first five minutes. Field 10 names the source structure.

**Questions:** What does one unit mean, day 14? Why test every row, day 20? Why must the graph include the origin, day 26? When is a model defensible, day 31?

**Closing claim:** Constant ratios, equations and lines through the origin express the same relationship. The representation records made on days 28 to 30, revised on day 31 and scored on day 32, establish this; day 31's water comparison supplies application.

**Teaching:** Model unit labels on day 15 and table-to-equation translation on day 17. Compare student strategies before summarizing. Step back for missing-information requests on day 24; explicitly connect points with equations on day 26.


**Practices:** MP2 develops on day 14 when students explain each quotient as output units per input unit. MP4 develops on day 24 through selecting engineering measurements and testing a heater model, then on day 31 through water-use assumptions.

**Rigor:** Retain nine understanding, two fluency and six application days. No owned standard sets expected fluency. Days 14 and 25 practice established methods with feedback, without time pressure or new content.

**Focus:** All 17 teaching days develop major work. Start with the recorded 765 major minutes in 850 teaching minutes. Restoring retrieval on days 25 and 27 puts G's ten geometry minutes in the cores; day 25's geometry exit takes three more. Named grade 7 retrieval on days 18, 23 and 28 adds 15 major minutes: 767 before assessment exclusions. Remove 78 major assessment-production and revision minutes and 10 geometry assessment minutes: 689 of 762 instructional minutes, 90.4 percent. Assessment and respond days are excluded. Scale work practices constants on day 25; there is no supporting cluster here.

**Limits:** Section 2.1 completes 7.RP.A.2a to d; 7.G.A.1 is retained practice from unit 1. Use positive rational constants and nonnegative quantities. Fractional unit-rate computations and percentages belong to unit 4; formal slope, functions and general linear equations wait for grade 8. No prescribed algorithm applies. The teacher page explains constant ratios versus constant differences, the origin without dividing zero by zero, and discrete points versus continuous quantities.

**Carried:** Keep unit 1's ratio table and double number line; use its area model only to repair scale-area errors. Tie both ratio models to multiplication statements on day 13 and equations on day 17. Fade completed tables on day 18; withdraw supplied tables and number lines on day 19. ## U7. Talk and writing

Ask why unit labels determine the quotient on day 14, why a compatible table is not proof on day 20, and what happens at zero input on day 26. Day 31's written comparison needs matched units, correct equations and explicit assumptions; judge the mathematical case without judging family routines.

## U8. Practice that returns

Use field 4's retrieval. Other openings mix earlier scale and already taught proportional work. Days 14 and 25 alternate independent attempts with feedback and explanations. No expected fluency target applies; accuracy and understood methods guide practice.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach quantity, per, constant, origin and reciprocal with labeled pairs. Offer "For each ___, there are ___, so ___." Read short prompts aloud and rehearse explanations with a partner. Use large-print grids, tactile axes and a scribe following student directions. Preserve the same mathematical goal. Extend by making a nonproportional table that agrees with three proportional pairs before failing at a fourth.

## U10. Two rooms

With one child, the adult holds the missing-information cards and asks for justified requests. Use 40 minutes by trimming ten minutes of parallel practice, preserving saved work, retrieval, bridges and exits. On assessment days shorten unscored work to 30 minutes. Keep the final change explanation. With one projector, use the full 50 minutes; display one representation while every student constructs the others on paper.
