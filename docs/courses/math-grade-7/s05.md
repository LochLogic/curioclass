## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does it mean to add, subtract, multiply and divide signed numbers?"
**Hook, day 69:** A supplied thermometer record starts at -6 degrees Celsius. What change returns it to zero? Students locate the reading before connecting the return journey to the compelling question. The record is a Curio Class original, invented for practice.
**Questions:** How does an addend determine a move, day 70? Why can subtraction become addition, day 73? Why must two negative factors give a positive product, day 77? Why must rational decimals end or repeat, day 83? How can an estimate expose a mistaken calculation, day 84?
**Closing claim:** Signed operations preserve number relationships and operation properties. Directed moves, day 73's missing-addend argument, day 77's distribution and day 89's value record support it. Days 78 and 90 check the reasoning.
**Teaching:** Model directed addition on day 70, inverse subtraction on day 73 and distribution on day 77. Step back for contextual choices on days 75 and 89. Explain why a negative time labels an earlier instant, not a negative duration; properties justify product signs.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask why reversing subtraction changes its sign on day 74, what distribution requires on day 77 and what a repeated remainder predicts on day 83. S1's written explanation must identify the percent base, connect operations to the record and compare the result with an estimate.

## U8. Practice that returns

Use field 4's retrieval. Later openings alternate percent multipliers, signed operations and, after day 83, long division. Feedback follows an independent attempt. Fluent work means accurate, flexible calculation with a sensible check, without a countdown or speed comparison.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach opposite, difference, distance, rate and remainder with labeled examples. Offer "The move is ___ units toward ___ because ___." Read contexts aloud; students rehearse explanations before writing. Use large-print or tactile lines, explicit signs without color dependence and a scribe following directions. Extend by choosing rational positions with equal distances but opposite differences and proving the relationship.

## U10. Two rooms

With one child, the adult supplies a contrasting claim and hears the explanation while the child keeps individual work. Day 91 ends with the repaired model ready for equations. With one projector, display one prompt while each student draws and calculates on paper. No device is required.

## U11. Still to make

Field 10 lists the print materials. No digital specification is needed. Full two-step equations and arithmetic-algebra comparisons remain in unit 6; repeating decimals back to fractions remain in grade 8.
