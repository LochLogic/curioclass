## Unit 1. Scale drawings

**12 days, course days 1 to 12, weeks 1 to 3.** Recognizing faithful copies leads to a common length factor, then to reciprocal factors and the separate behavior of area. Interpreting a supplied drawing comes before making one, and making one comes before changing its scale. Equivalent scales with and without different unit labels close the teaching arc. This answers how a small map can preserve measurable relationships to a larger place.

The grade 6 ratio work is retrieved, not checked for readiness. The older angle and protractor check is on day 1, with rebuilding on day 2. The merged day 3 keeps drawing, multiplicative reasoning and measured corresponding angles; the merged day 10 keeps equivalent-scale reasoning and unit conversion. Their 50-minute budgets and omitted activities are in both CSVs. The end-of-unit assessment is day 11, covering actual lengths, actual areas and reproduction at another scale; day 12 supplies the sole response time. No mid-unit assessment is needed.

**Candidate settled:** keep the ratio and geometry Progressions comparison as a planning constraint, costing zero student days. Drop the optional full-room drawing and four-day route-mapping extensions: days 8 to 11 already require creating, rescaling and applying drawings, and another project would displace that evidence. MP2 is visible in day 7's unit interpretation; MP6 is visible in day 3's measured correspondence argument. Use school or public park drawings, never home plans.

## Unit 2. Introducing proportional relationships

**21 days, course days 13 to 33, weeks 3 to 7.** Informal paper comparisons become constant-ratio table tests. Equations follow the tables, including reciprocal constants, before nonexamples expose the difference between a common ratio and a common difference. Graphs come after that numerical foundation. Interpreting an arbitrary point, the origin and the unit-rate point precedes comparison across stories, tables, equations and graphs.

All prerequisites were taught in grade 6 or earlier this year, so opening retrieval replaces the frame's older readiness wording. Its extra readiness-day allocation remains inside this unit as day 14's retrieval-linked practice. Day 21's mid-unit assessment covers table tests, constants and equations; day 22 responds. Day 32's end-of-unit assessment includes every part of 7.RP.A.2a to d, including both table and graph tests and constants in diagrams and words; day 33 responds. Tables and equations receive more time than any one graph feature because they establish the concept and expose additive reasoning.

**Candidate settled:** keep the Progressions comparison, zero student days. Keep the different-unit table-versus-graph extension for 10 minutes inside day 30. MP2 appears in day 14's interpretation of a rate; MP4 appears in day 24's engineering-model decision and day 31's water-use assumptions. ## Unit 3. Measuring circles

**11 days, course days 34 to 44, weeks 7 to 9.** Measurement and circle vocabulary prepare the circumference-to-diameter investigation. Circumference applications precede area estimation, which precedes the sector rearrangement and area formula. The contrast between proportional circumference and nonproportional area makes the unit 2 connection explicit.

Grade 6 polygon area and earlier proportional reasoning receive retrieval. There are no readiness or mid-unit assessments. Day 43 assesses knowing and using both formulas and an informal derivation linking circumference to area; day 44 responds. MP5 is the measurement-tool choice on day 36. Absolute measurement error comes before percent error and possible-error intervals. The final interpretation task joins these meanings in supplied claims.

## Unit 5. Rational number arithmetic

**23 days, course days 69 to 91, weeks 14 to 19.** Directed moves and zero pairs build signed addition before subtraction becomes adding the opposite. Distance is distinguished from signed difference before contextual combinations. Multiplication then grows from repeated signed changes and distribution; division follows as its inverse. The explicit finite-remainder day finishes rational decimal expansions before multistep applications mix number forms, estimates and operations. The addition/subtraction strand and the multiplication/division strand each have several days because their explanations differ.

Readiness is on day 68 for the two grade 5 fraction rows. Day 69 uses the indicated ten-minute refresher while grade 6 signed positions and absolute value are retrieved. Day 78's mid-unit assessment checks all signed-addition/subtraction parts and negative-product reasoning; day 79 responds. Day 90 checks every part of 7.NS.A.1a to d and 7.NS.A.2a to d with short prompts, using day 89's saved multistep application for 7.NS.A.3 and 7.EE.B.3. Day 91 supplies the only end-of-unit response time. Day 83 includes negative rational numbers, repeating notation, terminating zeros, long division and the finite set of possible remainders.

**Candidate settled:** keep the number-system Progressions comparison, zero student days. Drop conversion of a repeating decimal back to a fraction: grade 8 owns that direction. ## Unit 7. Angles, triangles and prisms

Layer-based volume, decomposed bases and nets precede decisions about volume and exposed surface area in composite applications. Cylinders, cones, spheres and formal congruence criteria wait for later grades.

## Unit 8. Probability and sampling

**19 days, course days 139 to 157, weeks 28 to 32.** Informal likelihood becomes numerical probability before repeated trials distinguish a model from observed frequencies. Uniform and data-based models precede compound simulations, organized sample spaces and independently designed trials. Comparing complete distributions introduces center-relative-to-spread reasoning before sampling. Representative selection, repeated equal-size random samples and variability of estimates prepare comparison of populations through both mean/MAD and median/IQR.

Grade 6 percent and data rows receive retrieval. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed. The end-of-unit assessment spans two existing days: day 155 uses the retained final base comparison lesson for sample-based inference, and day 156 covers the probability and compound-event parts, reusing day 148's simulation design and trial record. Day 154's independent sample estimate and variation explanation are reused on day 155. Day 157 is the one response day for both parts. This changes no unit day count and collects no second individual performance. Multiple samples are actually generated on days 152 to 154, rather than merely described.

**Candidate settled:** keep the statistics-and-probability Progressions comparison, zero student days. Drop the extra information-gap population comparison: its full exchange would exceed the final comparison day's budget. That base lesson is nonoptional, so the source map records the cut explicitly and preserves both comparison methods on day 155. MP4 is the designed simulation on day 148 and MP3 the spread-relative argument on day 149. The unit plans must retain these activities and timings before the planned share can be treated as implemented.
