## Unit 1. Scale drawings

**12 days, course days 1 to 12, weeks 1 to 3.** Recognizing faithful copies leads to a common length factor, then to reciprocal factors and the separate behavior of area. Interpreting a supplied drawing comes before making one, and making one comes before changing its scale. Equivalent scales with and without different unit labels close the teaching arc. This answers how a small map can preserve measurable relationships to a larger place.

The grade 6 ratio work is retrieved, not checked for readiness. The older angle and protractor check is on day 1, with rebuilding on day 2. The merged day 3 keeps drawing, multiplicative reasoning and measured corresponding angles; the merged day 10 keeps equivalent-scale reasoning and unit conversion. Their 50-minute budgets and omitted activities are in both CSVs. The end-of-unit assessment is day 11, covering actual lengths, actual areas and reproduction at another scale; day 12 supplies the sole response time. No mid-unit assessment is needed.

**Candidate settled:** keep the ratio and geometry Progressions comparison as a planning constraint, costing zero student days. Drop the optional full-room drawing and four-day route-mapping extensions: days 8 to 11 already require creating, rescaling and applying drawings, and another project would displace that evidence. MP2 is visible in day 7's unit interpretation; MP6 is visible in day 3's measured correspondence argument. Use school or public park drawings, never home plans.

## Unit 2. Introducing proportional relationships

**21 days, course days 13 to 33, weeks 3 to 7.** Informal paper comparisons become constant-ratio table tests. Equations follow the tables, including reciprocal constants, before nonexamples expose the difference between a common ratio and a common difference. Graphs come after that numerical foundation. Interpreting an arbitrary point, the origin and the unit-rate point precedes comparison across stories, tables, equations and graphs.

All prerequisites were taught in grade 6 or earlier this year, so they receive opening retrieval. Day 14 is retrieval-linked rate practice, keeping the frame's fixed allocation. Day 32's end-of-unit assessment includes every part of 7.RP.A.2a to d, including both table and graph tests and constants in diagrams and words; day 33 responds. Tables and equations receive more time than any one graph feature because they establish the concept and expose additive reasoning.

## Unit 5. Rational number arithmetic

Distance is distinguished from signed difference before contextual combinations. Multiplication then grows from repeated signed changes and distribution; division follows as its inverse. The addition/subtraction strand and the multiplication/division strand each have several days because their explanations differ.

## Unit 8. Probability and sampling

**19 days, course days 139 to 157, weeks 28 to 32.** Informal likelihood becomes numerical probability before repeated trials distinguish a model from observed frequencies. Uniform and data-based models precede compound simulations, organized sample spaces and independently designed trials. Comparing complete distributions introduces center-relative-to-spread reasoning before sampling. Representative selection, repeated equal-size random samples and variability of estimates prepare comparison of populations through both mean/MAD and median/IQR.

Grade 6 percent and data rows receive retrieval. No readiness or mid-unit assessment is needed. Day 146 merges the sample-space lessons, retaining lists, tables, trees and favorable-outcome counting. The next seven teaching days shift one day earlier: the simulation is day 147, complete-distribution comparison day 148, sampling foundations days 149 to 151, repeated quantitative samples day 152, with five minutes of categorical inference practice inside its sampling block, and independent categorical population estimates day 153. Day 153 retains written feedback and revision; the revised categorical record is scored after class on day 154. Day 154 explicitly teaches comparative inference from two samples through mean/MAD and median/IQR, including box-plot overlap and the center gap relative to spread. Thus every assessed comparison follows instruction.

The end-of-unit assessment spans two days: day 155 is an original changed-context comparative inference, one extended task using both methods, plus short selection and variability prompts; day 156 covers probability and compound events with short prompts. Day 155 reads the day 153 independent categorical population estimate and variation score; day 156 reads day 147's independent simulation and trial score. Neither collects or scores those products a second time. Day 157 is the one response day for both parts. Multiple equal-size samples are generated on days 151 to 153, rather than merely described.

## Focus, practice and evidence limits

Focus uses one count: minutes on named major-cluster activities divided by instructional minutes, excluding all assessment and respond days and the ten-minute readiness slot within day 1. The denominator is 128 instructional days of 50 minutes minus that slot, **6,390 minutes**. The daily notes count each minute once. Unit numerators are 180, 765, 120, 810, 882, 1,120, 115 and 185 minutes: **4,177 of 6,390, or 65.4 percent**. Assessment and response contribute no numerator. Day 75 excludes its three-minute ratio-parts retrieval, leaving 47 major-work minutes. Earlier-grade retrieval and rebuilding do not qualify; grade 7 mixed retrieval does where its activity is named. Day 132's one-step missing height and day 135's one-step missing dimension do not count as 7.EE.B.4a. Statistics earns credit only for the specified multistep rational predictions and population scaling, not simple fraction-to-percent conversion. These are planned allocations; unit plans must preserve the named work and time.

The five kinds in the CSV preserve understanding before fluency, then real applications; assessment and response are separate allocations. The daily five-minute opening spaces previously taught work. Models are tied to symbols: scaled lengths to ratio tables, signed moves to arithmetic, tape/balance structure to equation steps, nets to surface calculations and trees to event fractions. All eight practices have named tasks above; the unit plans must also make them visible in their evidence. Assessment accommodations, family notes, error-specific responses, original items and answer keys belong to that next layer. Neutral supplied contexts and paper stand-ins are named in the rows; no account or web search is required for ordinary work. The digital construction exception remains explicit.
