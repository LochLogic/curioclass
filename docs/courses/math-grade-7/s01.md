## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can a map or a model be true to something much bigger?"
**Hook, day 1:** Compare an invented park sketch with an enlargement and a stretched version. Ask which preserves the paths' shapes. Field 10 names the source structure.

**Questions:** What corresponds, day 2? Why multiply rather than add, day 3? Why does area need two factors, day 5? How can two scales describe one space, day 10?

**Closing claim:** A faithful drawing preserves angles and multiplies every distance consistently. The park-map work, day 7, and rescaling work, day 9, supply revised length, area and reproduction evidence scored on day 11.

**Teaching:** Model correspondence on day 2 and connect tiles to products on day 5. Then compare students' methods before summarizing. Step back for scale choices on day 8.

<!-- skel -->
- **7.G.A.1:** Solve problems involving scale drawings of geometric figures, including computing actual lengths and areas from a scale drawing and reproducing a scale drawing at a different scale. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **7.RP.A.2b:** Identify the constant of proportionality (unit rate) in tables, graphs, equations, diagrams, and verbal descriptions of proportional relationships. Begun here through scale factors in tables, diagrams and words; unit 2 completes and assesses all representations.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask why equal angles alone cannot establish scaling on day 3, why area changes differently on day 5, and why the new drawing shrinks on day 9. Day 9's written comparison needs two valid routes, consistent units and the same dimensions; written feedback precedes revision within day 10's synthesis and day 11 scoring.

## U8. Practice that returns

Use field 4's retrieval prompts. Days 4 and 9 alternate independent attempts with partner explanations and feedback on established methods. Accuracy and reasoning guide practice; the standards set no fluency target here.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach corresponding, scale factor and actual length with labeled pairs. Offer “Each ___ represents ___, so I ___.” Rehearse explanations with a partner before writing. Read short prompts aloud; require no independent extended reading. Provide large-print grids, high-contrast or tactile angle rays, large protractors and a scribe who follows the student's drawing directions. Preserve the mathematical decisions. Extend by finding two fitting scales and comparing drawing areas.

## U10. Two rooms

With one child, the adult presents contrasting copies and hears explanations instead of arranging partner talk. Keep individual exits and day 12's change record. A 40-minute home path trims ten minutes of parallel practice, preserving checks. With one projector, display one example; every student draws and measures on paper.
