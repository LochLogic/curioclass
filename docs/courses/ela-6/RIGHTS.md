# Rights list: grade 6 ELA, stretch 2 (Heroes and Their Trials)

Second draft, 2026-11-11 (Sara's list, piece 2), revised once after the curriculum expert's cold read
([../../reviews/rights-ela-6-s2-2026-11-11.md](../../reviews/rights-ela-6-s2-2026-11-11.md)). The texts are those
named for stretch 2 in [../ela-6-spine.md](../ela-6-spine.md), "The texts, stretch by stretch".

**This file gathers. It does not rule.** Every row is marked **found, not yet second-read**. No text here may be
taught from until a second reader has checked its licence line and its source (FOUNDATION, section 7). Nothing
below is a legal judgment. Every licence line is copied from the publisher's own page or file, opened on
2026-11-11, with the address beside it. The expert opened every address again the same day and matched every
quoted line by script.

**What "exists" means here.** I opened the catalogue page and the plain-text file, and found the named part in
the file. Nobody has compared any file with a printed copy.

**What "level" means here.** Rough figures, none a text record: (a) the score the host's own catalogue page
shows; (b) a Flesch-Kincaid grade from my quick script on the plain-text file; (c) the expert's Flesch-Kincaid
grade, measured separately. The band for grades 6 to 8 is 6.51 to 10.34; for grades 4 to 5 it is 4.51 to 7.73.
The spine's review gave 8.7 for Colum. Neither of us could reproduce that figure, so it is not used here.

**Pictures.** Not gathered for any row. Rows 1 and 6 are illustrated books; this list covers their text only.

## What the second reader should look at first

These are things we saw and cannot settle. Each is a question, not an answer.

1. **Outside the United States.** Every public domain line above is about the United States, and each host says
   so. Colum died in 1972, Werner in 1954 and Pogany in 1955 (dates from the catalogue pages). Curio Class is a
   public site that anyone anywhere can open. Is hosting these texts for readers in other countries a problem?
   Does it matter where Curio Class is served from, and would a notice on the page be enough?
2. **The words "Project Gutenberg".** Paragraph 1.C lets the work be reused "as long as all references to
   Project Gutenberg are removed"; other paragraphs set terms for copies that keep the name. The permission page
   describes the name as a registered trademark. The Reading Room credits its sources. Does a source line or a
   link that uses the name bring those terms in, or need anything else?
3. **The printing of Colum.** The file seems to follow a 1927 reprint of a 1918 book. Did the text change
   between printings? The file's credit line says it was produced by a volunteer group in Europe. Does where a
   file was made bear on anything?
4. **Church.** With no publisher or date in the file, which printing does the text follow, and does it matter?
5. **The pictures in rows 1 and 6.** Who made them, when was each first published, and does the status of the
   text say anything about them?
6. **Row 5** has several translators and an editor, and the file spells one translator's name differently from
   the catalogue. Which translation is each part, and when was each first published?
7. **The Core Knowledge readers carry pictures that belong to others.** The last pages credit picture agencies
   by name. Their terms page (https://www.coreknowledge.org/terms-of-use/) says the open licence applies "where
   expressly identified as covered by that license" and that "certain stock images may be licensed from
   Shutterstock or other providers" and "are not automatically covered by the Creative Commons license." Do text
   and pictures need different answers?
8. **Core Knowledge credit wording.** The credit asked for in the PDFs differs from the wording on the terms
   page. Which applies to a 2018 or a 2021 file?
9. **Core Knowledge, page 4.** Each PDF carries both the open licence and "All Rights Reserved", and lists
   trademarks. How do these sit together, and may the reader's or the publisher's name appear on a Curio Class
   page?
10. **ShareAlike.** If one chapter is used without its pictures, with word help added, is that altering it, and
    what must the page then carry? Does ShareAlike reach the Curio Class questions and teacher note printed
    beside it, or the tool that shows it?
11. **NonCommercial.** docs/LIBRARY-PLAN.md says a non-commercial licence fits because Curio Class is free. That
    is our plan's reading, not the licensor's. Does a donation page change it? Does a printed copy sold at cost
    by a school or a print shop count? Does a funder's grant?
12. **The audio, credit.** One reader's name is on the anchor recording, and several on the other. What, if
    anything, do the LibriVox pages ask about credit? (The two passages we found are quoted in row 7.)
13. **The audio, host and cuts.** The LibriVox pages speak of the United States, and the files are served from
    another host. Whose terms cover the file itself, and do cover art and summaries need their own check? If
    Curio Class cuts three sections out of a recording, or joins them, does anything follow?
14. **A Curio Class retelling** made from one named old telling (row 11, the row 4 retelling and the
    background explainer): how close may it stay to that telling's wording?
15. **Which copy is being checked?** The Reading Room copies (library/...) are not the same text as the host
    files: they have already been cut and reshaped. Is the second reader checking those or the host files?

## What we found that is not about rights

Settled by the expert unless marked otherwise. Items for the spine are for the main session; this session does
not edit the spine.

- **The pilot passage sits below the grade 6 to 8 band by measure.** Two separate measures agree: 5.9 and 6.0
  for the passage, 7.0 and 7.1 for the book. The text record must say so plainly: below the band by measure,
  placed in grade 6 on a person's judgment of its old language and the knowledge it assumes, and the easiest
  anchor passage of the year, placed first on purpose. The pilot stays on Colum. It tests the teaching of
  RL.6.2, not the level of the text. **For the spine:** drop "8.7", "23.5" and the sentence that the year opens
  on some of its hardest prose.
- **What makes the passage hard is old language, not long sentences.** Old forms (thee, thou, hast and the like)
  come about 90 times in 5,700 words. The middle sentence is 17 words and the longest is 48. My earlier "up to
  88 words" was wrong: my script ran two sentences together. So the pilot's supports are a sheet on the old
  forms, a name chart and a note on who is speaking to whom, not the long-sentence routine. **For the spine:**
  the scaffold table.
- **The passage holds content the spine's teacher-note list does not name:** the Cyclops kills and eats two
  men, six more are taken by Scylla, and in chapter V Odysseus weighs throwing himself into the sea. **For the
  spine:** add both to the Colum line. A fresh sensitivity read of the passage is still owed.
- **The pilot passage was misnamed.** "Cyclops through Circe" would end with chapter V, mid-tale. The theme
  needs chapter VI, where the Cyclops's curse is paid out. **For the spine:** the new name in the text table,
  the reading load and "The pilot".
- **The way-in story is harder by measure than the anchor passage** (8.0 against 5.9). **For the spine:** call
  the order "shorter and complete first", not "easier to harder"; the king's death at the end needs a line in
  the teacher note; say "the Reading Room's set II".
- **The reading load for stretch 2** should count the way-in story (about 3,200 words) and the Monkey King cut
  (about 2,000 words). **For the spine.**
- **Both background readers are history, not myth.** Homer is named once. The same publisher may have a grade 6
  unit of retold Greek stories; neither of us looked for it, because the spine did not name it.
