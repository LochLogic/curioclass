# Rights list: grade 6 ELA, stretch 2 (Heroes and Their Trials)

Draft, 2026-11-11 (Sara's list, piece 2). The texts are those named for stretch 2 in
[../ela-6-spine.md](../ela-6-spine.md), "The texts, stretch by stretch".

**This file gathers. It does not rule.** Every row is marked **found, not yet second-read**. No text here may be
taught from until a second reader has checked its licence line and its source (FOUNDATION, section 7). Nothing
below is a legal judgment. Every licence line is copied from the publisher's own page or file, opened on
2026-11-11, with the address beside it.

**What "exists" means here.** I opened the catalogue page and the plain-text file, and found the named part in
the file. I did not compare the file with a printed copy.

**What "level" means here.** Three rough figures, none a text record: (a) the score the host's own catalogue page
shows; (b) the Flesch-Kincaid grade the spine's review measured; (c) a Flesch-Kincaid grade from a quick script
of mine on the plain-text file. They disagree, as rough measures do. The band for grades 6 to 8 is 6.51 to 10.34.

## What the second reader should look at first

These are things I saw and cannot settle. Each is a question, not an answer.

1. **Outside the United States.** Every public domain line above is about the United States, and each host says
   so. Colum died in 1972, Werner in 1954 and Pogany in 1955 (dates from the catalogue pages). Curio Class is a
   public site that anyone anywhere can open. Whether hosting these texts for readers in other countries is a
   problem is a question for the second reader and then for Cory.
2. **The words "Project Gutenberg".** Paragraph 1.C lets the work be reused "as long as all references to
   Project Gutenberg are removed"; other paragraphs set terms for copies that keep the name. The Reading Room
   credits its sources. Whether our credit line counts as a reference that brings those terms in is for the
   second reader.
3. **The 1927 printing of Colum.** The file follows a 1927 reprint of a 1918 book. I did not check whether the
   text changed between printings.
4. **Two dates rest on our own sources file,** not on the book file: Baldwin 1895 and Church 1906.
5. **The Core Knowledge readers carry pictures that belong to others.** The last pages credit picture agencies
   by name. Their terms page (https://www.coreknowledge.org/terms-of-use/) says the open licence applies "where
   expressly identified as covered by that license" and that "certain stock images may be licensed from
   Shutterstock or other providers" and "are not automatically covered by the Creative Commons license." So text
   and pictures may need different answers.
6. **NonCommercial and ShareAlike.** docs/LIBRARY-PLAN.md says a non-commercial licence fits because Curio Class
   is free. Whether a donation page changes that, and what ShareAlike asks of a page that shortens or adds word
   help to a chapter, are for the second reader.
7. **The audio.** One reader's name is on the anchor recording. LibriVox asks for no credit; whether we give one
   is a courtesy choice, not a rights one.

## What I found that is not about rights

These go to the curriculum expert or to Cory, not to the second reader. None changes which texts are taught.

- **The pilot passage may sit below the band by a computed measure.** My rough figure for Colum's Cyclops through
  Circe is grade 6.0, under the band floor of 6.51, and 7.1 for the whole book, where the spine's review had 8.7.
  The catalogue's own score also says sixth grade. Rough measures run about a grade either way, and what makes
  this text hard (long sentences, up to 88 words; old words; many names) is the kind of thing a person judges.
  The text record must settle it with a proper measure. Until then the spine's "about grade 7" and "8.7" should
  not be leaned on.
- **Neither background reader is about the myths or Homer.** Both are history: city-states, Athens, Sparta. The
  two short background texts would have to be chosen from chapters such as "The Ancient Greek City-States". The
  same publisher has a grade 6 unit of retold Greek stories; I did not open it, because the spine did not name
  it.
- **The spine calls the Baldwin text "set II: the heroes" and the shelf's finder word is "Hercules".** The book
  has no sets and no Hercules story. The way-in story has to be picked from the five hero stories in row 2.
- **The Monkey King chapter is about 12,900 words,** twice the pilot passage. It opens with the author's comment on the source, not with the story; I did not read
  it through, so how much of it is told as a story is not known. The spine has not yet counted it in the reading load.
