# Course frame: Theater, grades 3 to 5

Course `theater-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `theater-3-5`), a band course for grade 3, grade 4 and grade 5, run as three years one after another (year 3, year 4, year 5). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: an inventing-together unit (improvising and group rehearsal), an
  actor's tools unit (body and voice exercises and the watching eye), a design and community unit (sets, costumes,
  lights and sound, and the drama's ties to community and culture), and a story and judging unit that ends the year
  with a play for families. Each unit joins two progression threads, one of creating or performing and one of
  responding or connecting, so every unit makes, performs, studies another artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4
  and 5). Grades 3 to 5 theater has eight rows a year; judging becomes its own thread in grade 3 and joins the story
  unit.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes: chairs and bags to the walls, the clear
  floor checked, the group agreements card shown), the warm-up (5), the teaching, improvising and rehearsing, and the
  close (about 4: chairs back, props to the class box, the day's evidence collected); a making session with card,
  tape and fabric takes 6 for clean-up. A readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (two check days: a showing day and a written day; no unit is over 25 days, so none has a
  mid-unit assessment) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the design and community unit
  owns seven ids and two products (a design and a community scene), so it is longest in year 3, where design sketches
  that suit a setting and tech compared across works are first met; the actor's tools unit is longest in year 5,
  where responses are written and defended for the first time and feeling is traced to breath and pitch; the story
  and judging unit is shortest (five ids) in years 3 and 5 and two days longer in year 4, which compares two
  cultures and two eras besides rewriting an ending. These are course days: the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2,
  rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the Educational Theatre Association's 2016 opportunity-to-learn
  standards were checked at source in the second-model read of 2026-11-25: they address grades 6 to 12 and give no
  elementary weekly-time figure, so none is stated here. This course gives 80 minutes a week, and 40 on the
  once-a-week path. The course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a drama teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the study of the
  works and the demonstration with a step card, opened by the look or listen at a work that carries the unit's
  response and its connecting sentence; rehearsal with the critique and the revision; the showing check; a separate
  written check session; and a respond session that starts from marks already made, so no first-time evidence is
  collected in a respond session (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). A five-session story unit runs: 1 study and
  demonstration, 2 rehearsal, critique and revision, 3 showing check, 4 written check, 5 respond. Fluency days fold
  into the openers. On this path the year keeps its year-end course-level check (1 check session in week 26, off
  every core check week, and 1 respond session in week 27) and drops the mid-year one: every id is already assessed
  id by id in its own unit, the mid-year check samples only 4 or 5 ids, and the two freed sessions go to the design
  and community unit, the heaviest of the year (expert read of the scope and sequence, 2026-11-26, finding 1).
  Sessions on that path: year 3, units of 8, 8, 9 and 5; year 4, 8, 8, 9 and 5; year 5, 8, 9, 8 and 5 (30 unit
  sessions a year, plus the year-end check and its respond session). The design unit starts in week 17 (week 18 in
  year 5). The story unit's first session holds the study and teaching only; its baseline run and the judging
  teaching open the second session, before its critique and revision. On this path the story unit's showing check is the family showing
  (with an in-class version for any student without a family yes); no separate family performance follows the
  respond session. A merged day never drops a critique, a revision or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks
  which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, theater performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule binds
  (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 3 claims only the 24 grade 3 ids (`.3a`, `.3b`, `.3c`), year 4 only the 24 grade 4 ids,
  year 5 only the 24 grade 5 ids: 72 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its own
  year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at
  least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and
  anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  words-file lines of its ids, the national standard's own action for each id (a words line can drift), the nine
  criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md);
  the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence
  still states its id count (24) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met.
  The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Theater is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open theater recordings). Works studied are of four
  kinds: tales and plays whose source texts are public domain, retold or cut by Curio Class in its own words with the
  source credited (scripts the class performs are these retellings, Curio Class originals or the students' own,
  APPROACH "Rights"); theater forms and makers, living and past, described in our own words and linked to the
  maker's, a museum's or a cultural body's own page, never embedded or copied; one silent film public domain in the
  United States (Chaplin's The Gold Rush in its 1925 release, unit 6; a restored copy or added score carries its own
  rights line), shown from an archive's copy or described; and,
  for unseen check work, a short scene performed by the teacher or read from a Curio Class original (APPROACH "Scoring
  creative work"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's
  own page, fails; a swapped work keeps the unit's ids met and the year's range. Range across the band (checked
  across the three years, not only within one; checked against theater K to 2 on 2026-11-26: no K to 2 work or form
  repeats; Kamiriithu, once in unit 11, moved to theater 6 to 8 unit 8 only, so each band studies it at most once):
  Africa (Guinea and the Mande griots, South Africa, Botswana), the Americas (Sicangu Lakota and Zuni Pueblo makers and stories, Mexican
  American farmworker theater, Appalachian community theater, Trinidad and the United States), Asia (Japan, Indonesia,
  Korea, China), the Middle East (Türkiye) and Europe (Italy, Germany, Russia, Switzerland, ancient Greece, England,
  Denmark, France), with living makers each year. Curio Class writes every step card, retelling, scene card, fact
  card, audience card, response card, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Improvising: characters, a place and a story invented in a group
  inside a time limit (3), ideas steered by given circumstances and found by questioning characters (4), a scene
  shaped from a character's inner life and the group's questions (5). Rehearsal: rehearse then reflect, alone and
  together (3), named jobs and a repeat-and-review cycle with a critique the students run (4), a set job each and a
  self-review run (5). The actor's body and voice: sets of body, voice and thinking exercises (3), a character's walk
  and posture and a chosen ensemble exercise (4), physical actions that carry meaning and warm-ups matched to the
  piece (5). Design: costume, prop and set sketches that suit the setting, tech named (3), sets, lights and sound
  that serve the given circumstances and a central idea, snags fixed as a group (4), a light or sound cue worked on a
  cue sheet, fixes invented, tech that carries a theme (5). Story: a written story reshaped with a beginning, a
  problem and an end (3), an ending rewritten (4), the hidden thought behind a line written (5).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown in
  named steps with the step card (a freeze, a walk, a cross, a cue), or a work watched, heard or described and
  studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day).
  Fluency: the warm-up routine and a scene beat run several times with feedback (a mirror exercise, a walk changed
  three ways, a line said with three hidden thoughts); in grades 3 to 5 it is the five-minute warm-up and two fluency
  days a unit. Application: improvising, rehearsing, sketching and making with some choice of role, line, job or
  design; the second rehearsal day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's
  revision is application too. Check: the showing day and the written day of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a
  second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second showing. A unit runs roughly:
  understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, two check days, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide
  (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live as each group performs; a kept card or sheet (an
  idea sheet, a rehearsal-log page, a design sketch, a cue sheet, a story map, a criteria card) made on the unit's
  last rehearsal or making days, scored after the session; and written items on one response card about the unseen
  scene or the unit's works, at most two written items a student. The end-of-unit assessment is two check days near
  the unit's end, so the teacher never marks a performance and reads or hears written items at once. The showing
  day's 40 minutes: set-up 3; warm-up 5; six groups of five perform for about 4 minutes each (24) while the others
  are the audience and fill their audience-journal card where the unit uses one; each student's self-check on the
  guide 3; close 4. That is 39 minutes. The written day: set-up 3; the teacher performs the unseen Curio Class scene
  twice, or reads it with two students (6); each student writes the written items on one response card (15), while
  the teacher hears at the table any student who answers aloud instead (an English learner's or an access choice,
  marked the same); kept cards are collected and each student adds the one-line portfolio reflection (5); close 4.
  That is 33 minutes, with 7 spare. A group the teacher cannot watch live is recorded on the school's device that
  day, hands, props and sound only where the family has said no to faces, and marked before the respond day
  (APPROACH "Kept work"). The showing's rehearsals, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the
  record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique
  is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days
  (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes
  into the portfolio folder (an audience-journal card, a design sketch, a cue sheet, a story map or a photo of a
  prop) with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Year 3 assumes the grade 2 rows of Theater, grades K to 2 (last used in grade 2, one
  grade back), by course order: theater is not a core course, so a school may not have taught it (lesson 32). Each
  year 3 unit therefore opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener that carries its ten-minute reteach of the
  grade 2 technique as written, so a student new to theater meets it there; day 1 stays clean for the hook and the
  question. Every year 4 and year 5 unit assumes the year before's row in the same thread (one grade back) and opens
  the same way, with its retrieval task inside day 2's opener. The count starts from the band's order (3, then 4,
  then 5). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a student may meet year 5 before year 4: each retrieval task
  then uses its ten-minute reteach (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline look"). The Seams tables give each row's last
  use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a core
  check for the grade (grade 3: 9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28, 30 to 32; grade 4: 8 to 10, 12, 16, 19, 21, 23, 27,
  30 to 32; grade 5: 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29 to 32). Weeks chosen by the units' load, not to dodge other
  after-core courses (the lead's calendar pass does that): mid-year in week 17 in years 3 and 4 (course days 33 and
  34 of the year) and week 18 in year 5 (days 35 and 36); year-end in week 27 in years 3 and 5 (days 53 and 54) and
  week 26 in year 4 (days 51 and 52). Weeks 30 to 32 hold core year-end checks in all three grades, so the year-end
  check comes before the story and judging unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and that unit's showing to families, in
  weeks 28 to 32 (27 to 32 in year 4), is its own end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Every unit of this course uses play-acting, which STANDARD.md 5 names as a reason a community may
  leave a unit out; a school that leaves out play-acting leaves out the course, and the course page says so plainly.
  Units that study a cultural or ceremonial tradition, named on the course page: unit 2 (Japanese kyogen), unit 4
  (the Mande griots and the Sosso-Bala of Guinea, studied by hearing and looking only), unit 5 (Indonesian wayang,
  studied by looking only), unit 7 (a Sicangu Lakota playwright's work on Native characters), unit 8 (ancient Greek
  theater, made for a festival of a god; the Turkish meddah), unit 10 (Korean pansori), unit 11 (Mexican American
  farmworker theater; a Botswana village theater campaign) and unit 12 (a Zuni story, studied by reading and comparing only).
  Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's family note name
  the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 with a clear floor (desks pushed back or a hall), chairs, card, tape, paper,
  fabric scraps, found objects, pencils and the voice; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in
  (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience and the critique partner, a
  group scene has a doubled version (parent and student, or the student playing two characters with two objects), and
  the showing is to the family or a recorded take. The teacher needs no drama training: each unit's teacher page says
  what to look and listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items counted once,
  printing of step cards, scene cards, response cards and audience cards), all optional beside the free path: year 3
  $5, $5, $20 (card, tape and fabric scraps for costume pieces and set models), $5 ($35); year 4 $5, $5, $25 (six
  small LED flashlights, reusable, and colored film), $5 ($40); year 5 $5, $5, $15 (card for cue sheets and set
  models; the flashlights reused), $5 ($30). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work's page, a still or a public domain film clip;
  every day runs from the printed step card, scene card and description without it. A school device may record a
  group the teacher cannot watch live (Scored evidence). No owned id needs technology: TH:Re9.1.3b, TH:Re9.1.4b and
  TH:Re9.1.5b study tech elements in works, which a paper-path student meets through the work card's description and
  still pictures in full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on the floor, the voice, paper, card, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class
  tool a unit plan adds (a stage-directions practice tool, a cue-sheet builder, a sound-effects board) has a printed
  version that does the same job and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** A student reads at most one short card alone (APPROACH "Supports"): a scene card, a fact card or
  a response card of a few lines; retellings and scripts are read aloud by the teacher or in parts, and a student's
  lines are learned by ear and by the card. English learners: each unit teaches its six to ten words in depth, with a
  picture, a gesture or a sound and a frame ("My character wants ___ because ___." "The light changes when ___."),
  a student may show or act the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student performed apart from
  what they wrote or said.
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on (movement on a shared floor; prop, costume-piece, set-model and light
  work), so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against free
  guides, named here and opened by the agent: the federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety
  Guide" (cpsc.gov) and California's environmental health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for
  schools, with its list of materials not to buy for grades K to 6 (oehha.ca.gov), for every making day; and the same
  federal agency's button battery safety pages (cpsc.gov), for the flashlights. Rules for every unit, from APPROACH
  "Safe use" and marked "from memory" until that read checks them: a clear floor with room for arms out, chairs and
  bags to the walls; bare feet or soft soles on a clean floor; no lifts, no falls rehearsed, no contact stage fights
  and no real or toy weapons (a sword is mimed or is a rolled paper tube held still); partner contact only by
  agreement and never required; voice rest and no forced loud sound; props checked by the teacher before use (no
  sharp edges, nothing small enough to swallow, no cords long enough to wrap a neck); no standing on chairs or
  tables; flashlights LED only, with screw-closed battery doors and no coin or button batteries; no light shone into
  eyes and no flashing or strobe; supplies labeled non-toxic; blades used by an adult only; no heat source; hands
  washed after glue and found objects. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv
  will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (commedia
  dell'arte of Italy), unit 2 (kyogen of Japan), unit 3 (Geoffrey Holder of Trinidad and The Wiz; Roadside Theater
  of Appalachian Kentucky; the class's own community), unit 4 (the Mande griots and the Sosso-Bala of Guinea), unit 5
  (wayang of Indonesia), unit 6 (Deaf West Theatre and the Deaf community), unit 7 (Larissa FastHorse, Sicangu Lakota,
  and the Native characters of Peter Pan), unit 8 (the meddah and Nasreddin Hodja tales of Türkiye; Gcina Mhlophe of
  South Africa; ancient Greek theater), unit 10 (pansori of Korea), unit 11 (El Teatro Campesino and the farmworkers'
  strike; Laedza Batanani of Botswana) and unit 12 (Ye Xian of China; the Zuni story of the Poor Turkey Girl).
  No student plays a character by an accent, a costume of another culture or a stereotype; no student is cast against
  their wish; no role play puts a student in a victim's or a harmer's place, and a villain or a boss is played by the
  teacher, a puppet or an object (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). Every task that ties drama to a student's own
  life or community (units 3, 7 and 11) offers an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive
  ground"). The experience items of TH:Re8.1.3a, TH:Re8.1.4a and TH:Re8.1.5a ask for at least one experience from
  this year's theater class (a class scene, a partner showing), which every student has, since the standard asks for the
  student's own; an invented one may be the second. TH:Re8.1.5b (cultural views shaping the student's response) keeps
  its invented-viewer option as a deliberate sensitivity trade (expert read of the scope and sequence, 2026-11-26,
  finding 5). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Content notes to families.** A studied work gets a content note to families (to the student at 18 or over) when
  its subject includes violence against a person, a killing, sexual violence or drug use, even when only described;
  other hard history is named plainly with no note (group read of the theater scopes, 2026-11-26, finding 6; the lead
  states the same line once in docs/courses/arts/APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground"). The grape strike (unit 11) is hard
  history named plainly with no note; no work studied in this band meets that line.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, a clear floor, step
  cards, scene cards, response cards, found and low-cost materials. Activities: the body, voice and design taught in
  steps, plays, forms and makers studied with their setting, improvising and rehearsal with a critique and a revision,
  a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks
  a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 24 ids and growth on the
  look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with children, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").
