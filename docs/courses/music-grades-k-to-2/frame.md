# Course frame: Music, grades K to 2

Course `music-grades-k-to-2` (catalog id `music-k-2`), a band course for kindergarten, grade 1 and grade 2, run as three years one after another (year K, year 1, year 2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a beat and listening unit (reading and responding), an
  expression and choosing unit, an inventing and writing-down unit, and a concert unit (revising and performing)
  that ends the year. Each unit joins two progression threads, so every unit sings or plays, listens to another
  artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5).
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes: found percussion or sticks out, the class in
  its circle or rows), the warm-up, teaching and music making, and clean-up and collecting work (about 3 minutes:
  instruments back in their tubs, kept cards in the folder, hands washed after shared instruments); a readiness or
  retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days,
  its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and one respond day after it, except that a unit followed
  at once by a course-level check (units 2, 3, 6, 7, 10 and 11) takes that check as its end-of-unit assessment
  (frame-write.md, after-core): its id-by-id evidence is taken on the check day, the check's respond day serves both,
  and the unit's own last two days are a rehearsal and revision day and a supported practice day. Unit counts follow the
  rows' load: the first unit of year K is longest because it sets up the steady beat, the singing voice, the
  listening routine and the sound rules for the first time; the inventing unit of year K is shortest (three ids, one
  technique); in years 1 and 2 the units owning two to five listening and reading ids run 16 days and the inventing
  and concert units 14. These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a
  week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md
  3.4): the music educators' association's National Opportunity to Learn Standards (2020, nafme.org, read
  2026-11-21) ask for at least 90 minutes of general music a week in K to 2 at the basic level. This course gives 80
  minutes a week, 10 short of that figure, and 40 on the once-a-week path, 50 short; a school can add time. The
  course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a music teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the listen to a work that carries the unit's response and its
  connecting sentence; one music-making or rehearsal day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond
  day. Fluency days fold into the warm-up openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on
  that path: year K, units of 9, 9, 4 and 6 sessions; years 1 and 2, units of 8, 8, 6 and 6; each year's two checks
  keep 1 check session and 1 respond session. The check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (week 19
  in year K and week 17 in years 1 and 2, and week 25 in every year); the respond sessions fall one week later
  (week 20 or 18, and week 26). Week 18 holds grade 2 math's mid-year check; a respond session is not a check, so no
  rule breaks. A merged day never drops a critique, a revision or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which
  days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, music performance standards (pre-kindergarten to grade 8 general
  music), by id only, each named with its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted
  (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year K claims only the 19 kindergarten ids (`.Ka`,
  `.Kb`), year 1 only the 20 grade 1 ids, year 2 only the 20 grade 2 ids: 59 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2,
  every one taught and assessed in its own year (music K is uneven: 19 against 20, so no year is counted by an even
  spread). A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. No pre-kindergarten id
  is claimed. The connecting ids (`MU:Cn10.1`, `MU:Cn11.1`) carry one words line in every grade (STANDARD.md 3.1);
  each year meets them with its own music and words, and the unit says what changes. Every year touches all four
  artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at least
  two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor
  standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  words-file lines of its ids and each id's national standard action (a words line can drift), the nine criteria of
  the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check
  set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21); each year's scope and sequence
  still states its id count (19, 20, 20) against STANDARD.md 3.2 (MU:Cr2.1.Kb added to the id file 2026-11-26).
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a child's record shows which grade's standards they met. The
  scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Music is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied are
  public domain scores in a public domain or openly licensed recording (a recording by the United States Marine Band,
  a work of the federal government, is public domain; any other recording's rights line is checked on its own page)
  and, for living or recent makers, a description in our own words with a link to the maker's, label's or archive's
  page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Songs the children sing are traditional songs in the public
  domain or Curio Class originals, never a copyrighted arrangement. Each work named below is a pick the unit plan
  keeps unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work must keep the unit's ids met
  and the band's range. Range is checked across the band (Africa, the Americas with Native American makers, Asia,
  Europe; living makers each year), since open recordings lean to older European works. Curio Class writes every
  step card, picture-notation card, listening-log page, record sheet, Curio Class original tune and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Beat and reading: the steady beat patted and walked (K), picture
  notation read and clapped, beats as hearts with "ta" and "ti-ti" (1), rhythm and three-note melody patterns read
  and played, with a rest, from picture or standard notation and a two-line or three-line staff (2). Voice: the
  singing voice found by a soft siren slide, singing soft and loud without shouting (K), one song sung two ways for a
  purpose (1), planned loud, soft, fast, slow, smooth and short parts on an expression map (2). Making up: a motif
  from a movement (K), a reply phrase for a purpose, in twos and threes, major and minor (1), improvised patterns and an
  A-B piece inside a given tonality and meter (2). Major and minor are taught by short reference patterns sung against
  a tonal home and compared; "happy", "sad", "bright" and "dark" may be a listener's own words for a response, never
  the key a tonality item is scored by. Performing: echo practice and audience manners (K), a named
  rehearsal strategy on a hard spot (1), a chosen strategy and a set checklist for judging a take (2).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied by listening with the unit's words (the artistic process
  "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated drills with feedback (an echo
  pattern, a beat-keeping round, a pitch-matching slide, a four-beat reading card); in K to 2 it is mostly the
  five-minute warm-up opener and one or two fluency days a unit. Application: making up, rehearsing or performing
  with some choice; the second application day holds the unit's one critique (describe what you hear, ask about a
  choice, suggest one change tied to a look-for), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: the
  self-check and the performance, talk and table items of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second try at the
  part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency,
  application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a performance item at the teacher's
  spot (a short pattern clapped, a phrase sung or played, a beat patted to an unseen piece); a look-for on the
  showing's guide (three to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet); a kept card or listening-log page made on the
  unit's last making days, scored with the showing; and talk or table items on an unseen public domain or openly
  licensed recording or a Curio Class original, at most two talk items a child. The end-of-unit assessment is a
  check day near the unit's end. Its 40 minutes: set-up 3; each child's self-check on the picture guide 5; six groups
  of five at the teacher's spot for about four minutes each (24), where each child does the performance items and
  answers the talk items; clean-up 4. That is 36 minutes. The others draw in their listening logs with no
  instruments, so the room is quiet enough to hear one group, and the teacher hears one group, never a room and a
  group at once. In the concert units (4, 8 and 12) the check day is the class concert in groups: each group
  performs at the front for the class, the class is the audience, and the teacher scores that one group on the guide
  and asks its talk items right after its turn. The product's draft, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for
  marks on the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a
  technique is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice
  days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every child has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes
  into the portfolio folder (a kept card, a listening-log page, or a sound-only recording on the school's device)
  with a one-line reflection, spoken to the teacher or a partner in K to 2 (bar criterion 8). Every recording is
  optional: a child may show an idea live, and the teacher's dated mark on the same guide is the evidence. On a course
  check that is a unit's end-of-unit assessment, the unit's showing is the group's turn at the teacher's spot, kept
  cards and log pages made on the last making days are scored from kept work after the session (once), at most two
  talk items a child, and the earlier unit is sampled by one performance item only.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Year K assumes no earlier music learning (pre-kindergarten ids are not taught; a
  kindergarten unit may use one as a readiness reference). Every year 1 and year 2 unit assumes the year before's
  rows in the same threads, last used one grade back, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never
  a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. The count starts from the band's order
  (K, then 1, then 2). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a child may meet year 2 before year 1: each
  retrieval task then carries its ten-minute reteach of the earlier technique as written (APPROACH "Readiness and the
  baseline look"). The Seams table gives each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (K: 14 to 17 and 29 to 32; grade 1: 14 to 16, 19 and 29 to 32; grade 2: 14 to 16, 18 to
  20, 28, 29, 31 and 32). Mid-year: week 19 in year K (course days 37 and 38 of the year), week 17 in years 1 and 2
  (days 33 and 34). Year-end: week 25 in every year (days 49 and 50). Weeks 29 to 32 hold every core subject's
  year-end check in K to 2, so the year-end check comes before the concert unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and the
  concert unit's own check day, in week 31 or 32, is its end-of-unit assessment.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit can run alone through an entry route. No unit studies a sacred or ceremonial work. A family whose faith limits music or singing may
  leave out a unit, or the singing in it (a child may take part by playing or listening); the course page and the
  unit's family note then name the ids not claimed that year. Units that study a named community's music, where a
  work may be swapped and the ids kept: units 1, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 and 11. Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met.
  Entry routes, inside the unit's own application time: when unit 3, 7 or 11 was left out, the concert unit after it
  (4, 8 or 12) opens with the minimum technique retaught and each child making a new short motif, reply phrase or
  two-pattern piece before revising it; the left-out unit's ids stay unclaimed, and the entry work is support, not
  evidence for them. Every unit that builds on an earlier unit of its year reteaches the least technique it needs when
  that unit was left out.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 in a cleared circle or at tables, the voice, hands and knees as the drum,
  found percussion (sealed shaker bottles, buckets, sticks), paper and crayons; nothing must be bought, and every
  unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One child at home: the parent is the audience
  and the critique partner, every group part has a doubled version (parent and child sing it), and the showing is to
  the family. The teacher needs no music training: each unit's teacher page says what to listen for, in plain words,
  and every model phrase a teacher sings is also on the class recording or a Curio Class original the teacher can
  play.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as rhythm
  sticks and a hand drum counted once, printing of step cards, picture-notation cards and listening-log pages), all
  optional beside the free path: year K $40, $5, $5, $5 ($55; 15 pairs of rhythm sticks shared in turns and one hand
  drum in unit 1); year 1 $5, $5, $5, $5 ($20); year 2 $5, $5, $5, $5 ($20). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling
  is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher device with a speaker plays the works and Curio Class's original tunes,
  at a level the class can talk over; a free on-screen keyboard (a Curio Class tool) may play the major and minor
  versions of a tune in year 1. No owned id needs technology. A recording to keep an idea (MU:Cr2.1.Kb, MU:Cr2.1.1b,
  MU:Cr2.1.2b) is always beside picture notation, which meets the id in full. A room with no device: the teacher sings
  the unseen tune or plays it on a found instrument, the work card describes the listening work, and a child meets
  every id in full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device, sound only, never a face
  (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on the voice, the body, found instruments and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a
  unit plan adds (a beat practice tool, a picture-notation reader, a keyboard) has a printed version that does the
  same job (the beat strip of hearts, the notation card, the sung version) and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards and notation cards are picture-led with the words read aloud for K to 2; no child
  reads a card alone. English learners: each unit teaches its five or six words in depth, with a picture, a gesture
  and a sound and a frame ("The beat goes ___." "I hear ___ because ___."); a child may show the word instead of
  saying it, and the check marks what the child sang, played or showed apart from what they said (APPROACH
  "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the federal
  deafness and communication institute's "It's a Noisy Planet. Protect Their Hearing." campaign pages
  (noisyplanet.nidcd.nih.gov), the National Center for Voice and Speech's voice care pages (ncvs.org), and, for any
  shaker made in class, the federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov). Rules
  for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): recordings at a set low level
  the class can talk over, no headphones for K to 2, no drum or loud sound near a child's ear, hearing protection or a
  quiet seat allowed for any child, and a quiet rest after loud music; voice: no shouting, no singing beyond a
  comfortable range, voice rest and water, no child made to sing alone; moving to the beat in a cleared space with arms
  out, walking or stepping in place, never running; found instruments with no small loose parts and no glass, shaker
  bottles filled and taped shut by an adult; no mouth instrument (whistle, kazoo, recorder) shared; hands washed after
  shared instruments. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No
  student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works and the songs with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14).
  Every traditional song is checked for its published history before a unit plan keeps it, and a song with
  minstrel-show or other demeaning origins is dropped. Units with a community's music name the communities and the
  sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (Yoruba drummers, Nigeria; a Ghanaian game song), unit 2 (Japanese American
  taiko players), unit 5 (Navajo and Ute flute music; Japanese song), unit 6 (Zulu isicathamiya singers, South
  Africa), unit 8 (mariachi, Mexico and Mexican American communities; a Chilean children's song), unit 9 (Korean and Eastern European Jewish
  folk song; ragtime), unit 10 (the Oneida Nation, Haudenosaunee), unit 11 (Yoruba and Nigerian drumming), unit 12
  (the juba of enslaved African Americans, as Florence Price used it). Every task that ties music to a child's own
  life (units 2, 6, 10) offers an invented or public option marked the same, and no child is asked to explain their
  family's music or culture (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student
  tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, step cards,
  picture-notation cards, listening-log pages, found and low-cost instruments, one teacher device or none.
  Activities: technique taught in steps, works studied by listening with their setting, making up and rehearsing
  with a critique and a revision, a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with
  reflections, 12 showings (three of them concerts), two marks a unit per child on the main look-for. Outcome
  claimed: met marks on the year's ids (19, 20, 20) and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with
  children, and Curio Class makes no claim that music study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

## Seams

### Picked up from the course before

Kindergarten opens the band and the subject: there is no course before and no pre-kindergarten course. The eight
kindergarten threads that start here with no earlier row are mu-k-invent, mu-k-document, mu-k-revise, mu-k-read,
mu-k-express, mu-k-perform, mu-k-choose and mu-k-respond (their `assumes` cells are empty); each is first used in the
unit named in the table below.
