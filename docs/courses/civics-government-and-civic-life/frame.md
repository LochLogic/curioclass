# Course frame: Civics: Government and Civic Life

Course `civics-government-and-civic-life` (catalogue id `us-hist-8`, kept from the old list), grade 8, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the
days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10 units, in the anchor's topic order (8.T1 to 8.T7) with two moves named below, ending with the
  year's civic action.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 148 in units, 12 for course-level checks and respond days (three course-level checks
  of 2 days, each followed by 2 respond days). A full year: one lesson of about 50 minutes a day, five a week, across
  32 weeks. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and one respond day (a unit over 25 days also holds a
  mid-unit assessment; none here is over 20); a unit that assumes an earlier grade's row last used two or more grades
  back opens with a readiness check on its first day (units 2 and 6, on grade 5 rows unused in grades 6 and 7), and a
  row used the year before (or earlier this year) opens as a retrieval task inside a day (units 1, 3 and 7).
- **Standard.** Massachusetts 2018 grade 8 (MA.8.T1 to MA.8.T7) is the content anchor: every one of its 56 codes
  sits with one of the 27 grade 8 progression rows, so every code sits in one unit below. Parent codes carry their
  lettered parts where the rows cite only the parent: MA.8.T5.6 carries MA.8.T5.6a to MA.8.T5.6c (unit 6: each
  student researches one area, as the standard asks, and the class covers all three), and MA.8.T6.8 carries
  MA.8.T6.8a to MA.8.T6.8c (unit 7, taught for the student's own state). C3 6-8 civics indicators carry the skills; D2.Civ.9.6-8 is owned in grade 7 (one
  procedure) and here (ss8-civic-action compares procedures), each with its depth line. The check set: California,
  Tennessee and New York teach civics as a high school course, so each unit plan reads their government courses
  at grade 8 depth and records any addition with its source; the "Check set adds" column names the candidates this
  frame found, from the writer's reading, each to be confirmed in that unit's comparison table. Check-set names
  never appear in student, teacher or family copy. The values rule governs: (a) where a higher court has ruled, the
  course sides with it (the later ruling stands where the Court overruled itself); (b) backed sources decide claims;
  (c) dignity and openness, LGBTQ people where history includes them (the 2015 marriage ruling in unit 6); (d) the
  age of the student.
- **"Your state" (bar item 13).** The anchor's Massachusetts government topic (8.T6) is taught as a method for the
  student's own state: its constitution, declaration of rights, government, local bodies and budget, with worked
  examples from several states (Massachusetts one of them, never the subject) and a link to each state's own pages.
  Unit 7 carries it; units 8 and 9 use the state constitution's press and petition protections the same way.
- **Base text.** None (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md: every day is Curio Class's own). Partial bases that may serve a
  unit, checked item by item: the retired OpenStax American Government 3e (CC BY, college level, so rewritten for
  grade 8) for background in units 3, 4, 7 and 8; Washington OSPI's Since Time Immemorial middle school unit 1C
  (the Medicine Creek Treaty of 1854), on the terms in the next bullet, as the worked example of a Native nation's
  government and treaty rights in unit 7 (and background in unit 1); National Archives (DocsTeach,
  CC0 activities) and the founding documents themselves (public domain) in units 2, 3 and 6; court opinions (public
  domain) in units 3 and 6. The frame maps no base lesson.
- **Since Time Immemorial.** Where a unit adapts Since Time Immemorial (Washington OSPI's tribally developed
  curriculum, CC BY 4.0), the content is kept as written (the facts, the tribes' own words and framing); the lesson
  shape and voice follow Curio Class; each adapted day carries one credit line saying it is adapted from Since Time
  Immemorial and that the content is the tribes' (Cory, 2026-11-14). Here: unit 7's worked example (middle school
  unit 1C, the Medicine Creek Treaty), with background in unit 1.
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit has a compelling question (the table) with supporting questions set by the unit
  plan, runs the C3 arc, and ends with an end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from an unseen source.
  Content is taught directly: foundations, founding, structure, then participation, rights, the levels of
  government, change and the press.
- **Founding documents lens.** The Declaration, the Constitution and the Bill of Rights are read seven times across
  Curio Class (ELA 8, this course, ELA 9, ELA 11, US History I, ELA 12, Government). Here the lens is what each
  document does: its claims, its structure and powers, and what it left out; rhetoric belongs to ELA.
- **Informed action.** Unit 10, the year's last: the class deliberates on a school or community problem, compares
  deliberative procedures, chooses an action itself and presents to an audience beyond the class. Action is the
  student's choice informed by evidence, never a cause the course picks. The teacher books the audience by the end
  of unit 8 (about day 130); the end-of-unit assessment and its respond day come before the build, so the
  presentation falls on about day 158 (day 157 where the booked audience needs it); the proposal is one shared class
  product (a short evidence brief and presentation, no second research essay, since ELA 8's unit 5 holds its own talk); the last day is the look-back, which records
  the answer received or what is still pending and what the class would do next.
- **Writing from sources.** A claim weighed against counterclaims in unit 1; sourced short pieces in units 2 to 5;
  the year's longest research argument in unit 6 (court opinions); an editorial analysis in unit 9; a public
  proposal in unit 10.
- **Party and family neutrality.** Examples come from several parties and eras; students never say whom they or
  their families support. Classes include non-citizens and mixed-status families: no question asks about a
  student's or family's status.
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own texts at grade 8 level; founding documents and opinions as short excerpts
  with plain-word glosses, and a stated difficulty; each source has a rights line.
- **Sensitive content.** How the Constitution protected slavery, and slavery as the central cause of secession,
  named plainly; Dred Scott's language summarized, not printed; Jehovah's Witness families' persecution named
  plainly; Native nations in the present tense with sacred or restricted knowledge left out; civil rights and the
  2015 marriage ruling taught from the record.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md)
  at its unit plan and again at its days; no outside human reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14; FOUNDATION section 7
  item 3). The agent studies first, at least five sources with two of the community's own: unit 1 (the Haudenosaunee
  Confederacy: the Confederacy's and the Onondaga Nation's own pages and Haudenosaunee scholars, and historians on
  both sides of the influence debate); unit 7 (the Medicine Creek Treaty nations, the Nisqually, Puyallup and
  Squaxin Island tribes: each tribe's own government and education pages and Since Time Immemorial unit 1C); units 2
  and 6 (slavery and its law: Black historians' scholarship and a national museum of African American history's
  teaching pages); unit 6 (Jehovah's Witnesses' own account of the flag salute cases; disability rights in the
  disability community's own published voice and a disability language guide; LGBTQ people in a media reference guide
  written by an LGBTQ organization); unit 5 (immigrant and mixed-status families: an immigration journalism style
  guide).
- **Paper path.** Every source, map, data set and task has a printed version; no day needs a device, an internet
  connection or a student account. Lateral reading in unit 9 is taught on printed pages of several sites the teacher
  prepares, so it runs on paper too; unit 10's presentation needs no device.
- **Literacy standards (also met).** The unit work also meets these Common Core literacy standards for history and
  social studies (data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt; the rows' notes name them), with no added days: unit 1:
  RH.6-8.1, RH.6-8.8; unit 2: RH.6-8.1, RH.6-8.6; unit 3: RH.6-8.3, RH.6-8.4, RH.6-8.5; unit 4: RH.6-8.7; unit 6:
  WHST.6-8.1, WHST.6-8.7, WHST.6-8.8, WHST.6-8.9; unit 7: RH.6-8.7; unit 9: RH.6-8.6, RH.6-8.8; unit 10: WHST.6-8.4.
  Across the year: RH.6-8.2, RH.6-8.4 and RH.6-8.10 (every unit reads and summarizes sources and their words at the
  band's complexity) and WHST.6-8.10 (short and longer writing all year). WHST.6-8.6 is not claimed: the paper path
  keeps every product off a device.
