## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Do elections give citizens real power?"
**Hook:** Day 52 opens with an invented caption, "The national popular-vote winner always becomes President," beside the Archives electoral-process account. Ask how votes become authority before judging the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Write with students: Who selects each official (days 52 to 54)? What do parties do (55)? Who can influence a decision, and who can make it (57)? What does election information establish, and what remains unknown (60)?
**Closing claim:** On day 61, answer the compelling question using the Constitution, one dated party statement and the Census turnout evidence. Include a counterclaim about unequal participation or indirect selection and explain a limit on the conclusion.
Model selection arrows on day 52, sourcing on day 55 and evidence versus inference on day 58. Pairs investigate on days 56 to 60. Model a qualified claim on day 59 using different evidence; step back for independent writing on day 61.

**Reserved sources for item 5:** S, F, A and N are original fictional documents authored by Curio Class, not actual election records. T is a real public-domain survey table. Keep these names, amounts, stories and data out of teaching and exit tickets. Label every card with author, date, audience and purpose; label the four fictional cards explicitly.
- **S, candidate statement:** Mira Chen, candidate for a fictional state legislature, October 3, election year X, to district voters: "I propose state grants for evening buses. The legislature must approve funding." This establishes her stated position, not feasibility or fulfillment.
- **F, finance filing:** Chen committee treasurer, October 10, year X, disclosure for the public, covering September: individual receipts $3,600; party committee receipts $900; total receipts $4,500; spending $2,800. These are complete category totals for this reporting period, not the entire campaign. The larger source is individuals, 80 percent. The record shows reported receipts and spending, not bought votes or an election outcome.
- **A, advertisement:** Friends of Evening Transit, October 12, year X, to district voters: "Chen will deliver buses immediately. Vote Chen." Footer: "Paid for by Friends of Evening Transit. Not authorized by any candidate." Its purpose is persuasion by an outside group; compare its certainty with S's conditional proposal. Payment and independence do not establish truth.
- **N, news brief:** Staff reporter, District Bulletin, October 14, year X, to district readers: "Our election feature gives three paragraphs to Chen's bus plan and one sentence to her opponent's library plan. Both remain proposals requiring legislative approval." Space and topic selection can shape attention; the text gives no evidence that coverage changed votes.
- **T, turnout table:** Census Bureau, Table 2, national survey data for November 2018, public statistical reporting. Audience: the public and researchers. Purpose: to report survey estimates. Print the release date copied from the Census page; the survey month is not the release date. Supply headers, units and these two age rows from the linked original: ages 18 to 24, citizen population 26,950, reported voted 8,726; ages 65 to 74, citizen population 29,534, reported voted 20,126. All counts are estimates in thousands. Published rates are 32.4 and 68.1 percent, a 35.7 percentage-point gap. Students calculate from rounded counts with a calculator and round to one decimal place. This table concerns national 2018 estimates, not Chen's fictional district or election. It cannot establish individual motives or statistical significance without uncertainty information.

Use field 2. Blank selection charts fit the idea without a digital tool. ## U6. Days and work by hand

Teaching days use five minutes to open, ten to model, twenty-five to investigate, five to discuss and five for an exit ticket. Repairs replace investigation time. Day 60 begins with a two-minute denominator retrieval inside its opening: "What population is counted when 9 of 15 surveyed adults respond yes?" Answer: 15 sampled adults, 60 percent; this is not automatically the whole population. A miss triggers ten minutes of ratio and sample repair during investigation. Day 61 replaces modelling and investigation with thirty-five minutes of independent writing; exit 61's errors are repaired on day 63. Assessment and response follow fields 5 and 7. No timer is shown to students.

## U7. Talk and writing

Use field 2's questions. Ask, "Does showing who paid settle whether a claim is true?" Day 61's argument needs a qualified conclusion, accurate evidence from three named sources, reasoning and an answered counterclaim. Judge evidence and limits, not a preferred party or answer.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve branch limits on day 54 and source purpose on days 55 and 58. Day 60 builds accurate table reading through repeated explanations, without a speed target. Revisit electoral routes in the closing argument.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach primary, nominee, confirm, term, platform, interest group, receipts, turnout and percentage point in context. Frame: "This source supports ___ because ___; it cannot show ___." Read difficult excerpts aloud with glosses; keep new independent source reading under 300 words daily. Accept oral rehearsal, labeled arrows and a scribe without reducing reasoning. Extend by testing whether a changed denominator reverses a comparison.

## U10. Two rooms

One child discusses sources with an adult, then answers independently without hints; the adult marks and responds on the stated days. A projector can display the same printed packet. No student account or device is needed.
