## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What is the best way to change a law?"

**Hook:** Day 120 opens with Kitty Cone's account of the 1977 disability rights sit-in beside the First Amendment. Why did people protest after a law already existed?
**Supporting questions:** Write together: How can protest influence officials (121)? What changes across eras (122)? Whose interests enter debate (123)? What do institutions do economically (124)? What did the policy aim to do and how was it carried out (125)? Does enactment deliver results (126)? Can two causes be compared fairly (127)? Which method fits which problem (128)?
**Closing claim:** On day 128, answer the compelling question using Cone, Mayerson, the disability law, California's Proposition 13 (1978) and Tea Party Patriots' stated goals and organizing methods. Defend a conditional choice, answer a counterclaim and name an evidence limit.
Model sourcing on 120, comparison on 121 and evidence-based writing on 123. Ask who made each source, when, for whom and why; corroborate before inferring effects. Withdraw completed examples on 127.


- **D2.Civ.14.6-8:** Compare earlier and contemporary approaches to changing society and advancing the common good. Parts: compare methods; compare their common-good purposes.
- **D2.Civ.6.6-8:** Describe how political, civil and economic organizations affect lives. Parts: describe each category's roles.
- **D2.Eco.9.6-8:** Describe corporations, nonprofits and labor unions within a market economy. Parts: describe each institution's economic roles.
- **D2.Civ.13.6-8:** Analyze why policies exist, how they operate and their effects in several settings. Parts: analyze purposes, implementation and consequences across settings.

**Limits:** Detailed campaign finance and litigation wait for Government. Peaceful assembly and petition are protected; civil disobedience can involve legal penalties. No protest reenactment, recruitment or family political disclosures. Explain disability exclusion plainly; interrupt stereotypes without inviting diagnoses. Rights are not put to a class vote.
**Carried:** Return unit 6's continuity and change chart on 120; revise on 122 to separate methods, authority and effects. Keep unit 7's blank authority and cost organizers; park its supply-demand graph. Use sourcing and perspective throughout. Carry blank charts and corrected claims onward on 130.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask, "Whose evidence could challenge this claim?" on 123 and "What changed besides the policy?" on 126. Judge day 128's claim, which is practice, for named evidence, an answered counterclaim and a stated limit; comments return on 130.

## U8. Practice that returns

Return rights and authority on 121, sourcing on 123, economic roles on 126 and continuity on 127; label political, civil and economic organizations on 126 and 127. No speed target applies.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach protest, petition, lobbying, institution, implementation and consequence. Frame: "The source establishes ___, but cannot establish ___." Allow home-language notes. Keep independent reading near 250 words daily; read harder passages aloud. Provide large print and linear charts. Extend by changing a setting or testing a rival explanation.
