**Limits:** Citizenship is a legal category, not a measure of worth. No personal status questions, eligibility advice or immigration simulations. Extended rights research belongs to unit 6, tribal sovereignty to unit 7 and external action to unit 10.
**Teacher background:** Distinguish citizenship at birth, qualifying automatic acquisition after birth and application-based naturalization. Teach the usual adult application, residence, character, language and civics requirements, interview and oath, with exceptions and accommodations, without judging anyone's eligibility. As of November 2026 (checked 2026-11-14): Executive Order 14160 (January 20, 2025) sought to deny citizenship to some children born here to non-citizen parents; in Trump v. Barbara (June 30, 2026) the Supreme Court held it unconstitutional under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause (judgment 6 to 3; five justices joined the constitutional reasoning). Teach birth in the United States, subject to its jurisdiction, as the rule the Court upheld. No question asks about any student's or family's status. Applicants who file on or after October 20, 2025 take the 2025 civics test (up to 20 questions from a 128-question bank, 12 correct to pass); earlier filers take the 2008 test (up to 10 of 100, 6 correct). ## U9. Supports and extension

Teach citizen, naturalization, duty, due process, sovereignty, delegate and trustee in context. Frame: "This action serves ___ but risks ___; source ___ supports ___." Read difficult originals aloud with glosses; independent new source reading stays under 300 words daily. Offer oral rehearsal, large print, accessible tables and a scribe while preserving reasoning. Extend by changing one interest in a case and testing whether the conclusion changes.
