## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does a good citizen owe, and to whom?"

**Hook:** Day 64 opens with an invented claim, "Only voters can help govern," beside the First Amendment petition protection. Ask who can contribute before answering the compelling question.
**Supporting questions:** Write together: How does citizenship begin (64)? Which rights and duties are shared (66)? How do values conflict (69 and 70)? What makes leadership responsible (71 to 73)?
**Closing claim:** Day 74 answers the compelling question from the Constitution, Tinker majority and dissent, and Smith's speech. Require evidence, reasoning, an answered counterclaim and a source limitation; judge reasoning, not political agreement.
Model citizenship routes on day 64, sourcing and perspective on day 70, and leadership criteria on day 71. Pairs investigate before independent writing on day 74.


- **D2.Civ.2.6-8:** Explain specific citizen roles. Parts: explain voting, jury service, taxpaying, military service, petitioning, protesting and holding office (C3, printed page 31).
- **D2.Civ.10.6-8:** Explain why personal interests, perspectives, civic virtues and democratic principles matter when addressing problems in government and civil society. Parts: explain each factor in both settings (C3, printed page 32).

**Limits:** Citizenship is a legal category, not a measure of worth. No personal status questions, eligibility advice or immigration simulations. Extended rights research belongs to unit 6, tribal sovereignty to unit 7 and external action to unit 10.
**Teacher background:** Distinguish citizenship at birth, qualifying automatic acquisition after birth and application-based naturalization. Teach the usual adult application, residence, character, language and civics requirements, interview and oath, with exceptions and accommodations, without judging anyone's eligibility. As of November 2026 (checked 2026-11-14): Executive Order 14160 (January 20, 2025) sought to deny citizenship to some children born here to non-citizen parents; in Trump v. Barbara (June 30, 2026) the Supreme Court held it unconstitutional under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause (judgment 6 to 3; five justices joined the constitutional reasoning). Teach birth in the United States, subject to its jurisdiction, as the rule the Court upheld. No question asks about any student's or family's status. Applicants who file on or after October 20, 2025 take the 2025 civics test (up to 20 questions from a 128-question bank, 12 correct to pass); earlier filers take the 2008 test (up to 10 of 100, 6 correct). State graduation civics tests may still use an older bank; use your state's, found on its education department's civics test page. The sources for these dated facts (the Supreme Court opinion in Trump v. Barbara at supremecourt.gov, the Federal Register notice for Executive Order 14160 and the agency page for the 2025 test) were not read for this plan: not confirmed from the source.
**Carried:** Retain unit 4's blank sourcing and claim organizers. Return grade 7's perspective chart on day 70; add interest, principle and evidence columns. Withdraw completed examples on day 74; retain blank organizers.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach citizen, naturalization, duty, due process, sovereignty, delegate and trustee in context. Frame: "This action serves ___ but risks ___; source ___ supports ___." Read difficult originals aloud with glosses; independent new source reading stays under 300 words daily. Offer oral rehearsal, large print, accessible tables and a scribe while preserving reasoning. Extend by changing one interest in a case and testing whether the conclusion changes.
