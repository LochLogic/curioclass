## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How have Americans widened the rights the Constitution promises?"
**Hook:** Day 77 juxtaposes the Constitution's fugitive-slave clause with the Thirteenth Amendment. Ask what changed, who demanded change and what a legal text cannot prove about daily life.
**Supporting questions:** Write together: Why did slavery become a constitutional crisis (78)? What did amendments change (79 to 81)? How did movements change laws and perspectives (82 to 84)? How did courts change rights, and with what limits (85 to 93)?
**Closing claim:** Day 93 answers the compelling question using the Fourteenth Amendment, a civil rights law, movement evidence and the chosen court pair. Require an answered counterclaim and a limit; judge evidence and reasoning, never personal agreement.
Model sourcing on day 78, continuity and change on day 84, and opinion comparison on day 85. Confer during research, then withdraw completed examples on day 90.

**Limits and teacher background:** Slavery was the central cause. Constitutional disputes concerned its protection and expansion. Dred Scott predates the Fourteenth Amendment; summarize its racism without printing slurs. Incorporation applies most Bill of Rights protections to states through later decisions, not automatically in 1868. Distinguish procedural fairness, substantive liberty and equal protection. Amendments precede statutes to distinguish legal mechanisms; the timeline preserves chronology. No role play, personal disclosures or debate over classmates' dignity. Action belongs to unit 10. MA.8.T5.4 in the 2018 framework names congressional protections concerning race, gender and disability; the local wording above drops them, so day 82 teaches Title II (public accommodations) and Title VII (employment, including sex discrimination) of the 1964 Act, and item 3 checks both. Holder (2013) ended the preclearance coverage formula and Louisiana v. Callais (April 29, 2026) narrowed section 2 vote-dilution claims. ## U7. Talk and writing

Ask on day 80, "Why does the level of government matter?" On day 84, "Whose perspective changed, and what explains it?" Judge the report and closing claim through field 5's evidence, reasoning, counterclaim and limitation criteria.

## U8. Practice that returns

Retrieve unit 3's courts on days 80 and 87, unit 5's rights and values on days 79 and 83, and unit 1's counterclaim on day 89. No fluency target is set.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach incorporation, precedent, dissent and enforcement in context. Frame: "Source ___ supports ___ because ___, but cannot establish ___." Allow home-language planning and oral rehearsal; distinguish language difficulty from a mistaken legal idea. Limit new independent source reading to 300 words daily; read difficult originals aloud in chunks. Provide large print, accessible linear charts and a scribe. Explain Hazelwood's privacy dispute without intimate details. Extend by testing whether changed facts alter a holding.

## U10. Two rooms

One child researches one area and compares adult-provided summaries of the others. The adult reads opinions aloud before independent analysis and uses day 95 for correction and a fresh attempt. One projector shows the same printed sources; pairs annotate paper, so no student device or account is needed.

## U11. Not in this section, and still to make

Detailed Civil War campaigns belong to later history; contemporary legal advice is outside this course. Make the packet and daily materials listed in field 10, including safe excerpt boundaries and samples for each research area. No tool specification is assumed.
