## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How have Americans widened the rights the Constitution promises?"

**Hook:** Day 77 juxtaposes the Constitution's fugitive-slave clause with the Thirteenth Amendment. Ask what changed, who demanded change and what a legal text cannot prove about daily life.
**Supporting questions:** Write together: Why did slavery become a constitutional crisis (78)? What did amendments change (79 to 81)? How did movements change laws and perspectives (82 to 84)? How did courts change rights, and with what limits (85 to 93)?
**Closing claim:** Day 93 answers the compelling question, in a short claim, using the Fourteenth Amendment, a civil rights law, movement evidence and the chosen court pair. Require an answered counterclaim and a limit; judge evidence and reasoning, never personal agreement.
Model sourcing on day 78, continuity and change on day 84, and opinion comparison on day 85. Confer during research, then withdraw completed examples on day 90.


- **D2.His.5.6-8:** Explain how and why people's perspectives changed over time. Parts: explain the change; explain its causes (C3, printed page 47).
- **D4.1.6-8:** Build arguments from several sources and acknowledge strengths and limitations. Parts: construct a supported claim; acknowledge strengths; acknowledge limits (C3, printed page 60).
- **Parts traced, by verb:** T5.3 analyze (a) the issues behind the war (77 to 78), (b) how they led to greater federal power and rights (79 to 80). T5.2 explain context and significance of (a) the 14th (79 to 80), (b) the 19th, (c) the 26th (81). T5.4 explain laws on (a) race and (b) sex (82), (c) disability, with section 504 of 1973 and the 1990 Act (83), and (d) how views of human rights evolved (83 to 84).

**Limits and teacher background:** Slavery was the central cause. Constitutional disputes concerned its protection and expansion. Dred Scott predates the Fourteenth Amendment; summarize its racism without printing slurs. Incorporation applies most Bill of Rights protections to states through later decisions, not automatically in 1868. Distinguish procedural fairness, substantive liberty and equal protection. Amendments precede statutes to distinguish legal mechanisms; the timeline preserves chronology. No role play, personal disclosures or debate over classmates' dignity. Action belongs to unit 10. MA.8.T5.4 in the 2018 framework names congressional protections concerning race, gender and disability; the local wording above drops them, so day 82 teaches Title II (public accommodations) and Title VII (employment, including sex discrimination) of the 1964 Act, and item 3 checks both. Plessy v. Ferguson's racist language is summarized, not printed, as for Dred Scott. As of November 2026 (checked 2026-11-14): the 1965 Act's reach today differs from 1965, because Shelby County v. Holder (2013) ended the preclearance coverage formula and Louisiana v. Callais (April 29, 2026; six to three) held that the Act did not require an additional majority-minority district, so the map was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. Obergefell stands; on November 10, 2025 the Court denied a petition asking it to revisit the ruling (Davis v. Ermold), which is not a new ruling on marriage; confirm its date on supremecourt.gov before day 83. Trump v. Barbara (June 30, 2026; five Justices joined Roberts, three dissented) applied the Citizenship Clause: children born here to parents unlawfully or temporarily present are citizens. Both checked at supremecourt.gov. State these as fact; students weigh evidence, not parties.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach incorporation, precedent, dissent and enforcement in context. Frame: "Source ___ supports ___ because ___, but cannot establish ___." Allow home-language planning and oral rehearsal; distinguish language difficulty from a mistaken legal idea. Limit new independent source reading to 300 words daily; read difficult originals aloud in chunks. Provide large print, accessible linear charts and a scribe. Explain Hazelwood's privacy dispute without intimate details. Extend by testing whether changed facts alter a holding.

## U10. Two rooms

One child researches one area and compares adult-provided summaries of the others. The adult reads opinions aloud before independent analysis and uses day 95 for correction and a fresh attempt. One projector shows the same printed sources; pairs annotate paper, so no student device or account is needed.
