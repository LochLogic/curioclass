## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Who should decide: the nation, the state or the town?"

**Hook:** Day 100 opens with a school bus crossing a state line. Who decides school rules and road maintenance? Then retrieve earlier knowledge.
**Supporting questions:** Write together: What authorizes government (102)? How do tribal governments differ (104)? What state protections are added (107)? Which procedure fits (111)? Who gains and pays (117)?
**Closing claim:** Day 111 drafts an answer to the compelling question from constitutional provisions, tribal sources and a local procedure, with evidence, reasoning and a counterclaim answered. Finish it in the day 117 revision.
Model sourcing on 101, writing on 105 and graphs on 113; step back on 107 and 117.


- **D2.Civ.5.6-8:** Explain origins, functions and structure through federal and state constitutions and another governmental system. Parts: explain origins, functions and structure.
- **D2.Civ.11.6-8:** Distinguish decision procedures across classroom, school, civil society and three government levels. Parts: differentiate procedures by intended civic purposes.
- **D2.Eco.8.6-8:** Explain how benefits and costs outside transactions change market outcomes. Parts: explain external benefits; explain external costs.
- **D2.Eco.10.6-8:** Explain interest-rate changes' effects on borrowing and investing. Parts: explain both effects.
- **D2.Eco.11.6-8:** Use data to judge employment, unemployment, inflation, output, income and growth. Parts: use appropriate data; evaluate all six.
- **D2.Eco.2.6-8:** Evaluate economic alternatives. Parts: compare costs and benefits for different groups and society.

**Teacher background:** The Tenth Amendment reserves powers to states or people, within federal limits. Supremacy concerns valid federal law. State constitutions constrain broad powers differently from enumerated federal powers. Local authority derives from state law; tribal sovereignty predates the United States. As of November 2026 (checked 2026-11-14): a March 20, 2025 executive order directed steps toward closing the federal Department of Education, and on July 14, 2025 the Supreme Court, in McMahon v. New York, let its layoffs proceed (6 to 3) while the case continues, without deciding their legality. The July 4, 2025 budget law (Public Law 119-21) raises states' share of food-aid (SNAP) administrative costs from half to three quarters from October 2026 and adds a state share of benefits tied to payment error rates from October 2027. Verify each against its official source before building (field 10). State these as facts; students contrast responsibilities at each level, not parties. **Limits:** No financial disclosures or reenactment. Detailed jurisdictional litigation waits for Government.
**Carried:** Keep blank organizers; park unit 6's timeline and continuity chart for unit 8. Revise branches on 108 to 110. Return budget and opportunity cost on 112, tied to allocations on 114. Revise supply and demand on 113. Withdraw examples on 117.
