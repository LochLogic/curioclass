## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What can we do about a problem in our school or community?"
**Hook:** Day 149 opens with students naming two school problems and the dated Bellevue school food-share case. Which concerns need evidence before action? The case models inquiry; students choose their own problem.
**Supporting questions:** Write together: Which procedure fits (150)? Whose concerns matter (151)? How does this problem appear elsewhere and over time (152)? What can we influence (153)? What response changes our next step (160)?
**Closing claim:** On 156, the shared brief answers the question with a recommendation, two cited sources, reasoning, a counterclaim and uncertainty. Use the chosen problem's local record, community account and regional or global comparison. On 160, revise in light of the audience response or pending status.
Model sourcing on 149, procedures on 150 and analysis through civics and economics on 152. Compare evidence before choosing action on 153. Withdraw completed examples on 153; retain blank organizers.

**Day 154 packet, unseen and reserved:** All cards below are original fiction, and each card and the day 155 transfer records print the line "invented for practice". They are not evidence about a real town.

**A, Brook Town maintenance record, 2025:** "The public footpath to the community center closed on four rainy days in 2020 and twelve in 2024. Inspectors found blocked drains and more paved ground uphill. The town controls this path. Clearing drains is within the maintenance budget; raising the path needs council funding. Inspectors cannot yet separate the causes of the increased closures."

**B, regional watershed office explanation, 2025:** "Water crosses town boundaries. Upstream paving can increase runoff downstream. Three towns now share drainage maps, but their different budgets slow joint work. Rainfall and drainage maintenance also affect flooding."

**C, international public-works research summary, 2025:** "Studies of paths in several countries between 2000 and 2024 describe closures associated with heavier rain, paving and neglected drainage. Shared mapping and maintenance sometimes help. Local conditions differ; these accounts do not prove what caused Brook Town's increase."

**D, Brook Town community center users' minutes, 2025:** "Long detours make visits harder. We ask for reliable access. We disagree about spending on a raised path before drain clearing is tried."

**E, procedure cards:** "A neighborhood assembly hears timed turns, accepts amendments and decides by majority vote. A volunteer planning group uses a consensus round: everyone may speak or pass, objections prompt revisions, and unresolved objections postpone agreement. ## U3. Exit questions

Use the final five minutes for each exit. These original cases are reserved from worked examples and assessment. Print each one "invented for practice". Each answer needs its reason. Record the error letter; ask a follow-up before treating an incomplete answer as a misconception.

- **149, D4.6.6-8:** "A community garden reports fewer volunteers than last year. What record and comparison would help explain the problem, and what is one norm that would keep a discussion of it fair?" Answer: dated participation records and another period or place, with questions about causes rather than assumed blame; a norm such as equal turns or a hearing for a different view (C).
- **150, D2.Civ.9.6-8:** "A sports club has to choose equipment today; a neighborhood group is developing a shared design over several meetings. Compare a majority decision after debate with revising toward consensus." Answer: the vote can decide promptly while recording dissent; consensus can improve a shared design but may take longer (G).
- **151, D2.Civ.7.6-8:** "Your suggestion lost a vote, but its access concern was not answered. What can you do while respecting the decision and others' rights?" Answer: record the unresolved concern and propose an evidence-based revision without silencing others (B). Actual participation is assessed in item 1.
- **152, D4.6.6-8:** "A school reports overflowing bicycle racks. Name a civics question, an economics question and evidence needed before claiming a regional change." Answer: who controls space; what an expansion would displace or cost; comparable dated records from other schools (C).
- **153, D4.7.6-8:** "A park's sign is unreadable. Name one request the parks office could grant and one it could not, with the reason for each." Answer: repairing or replacing the sign is within the office's work; rewriting the city budget or promising a date is not, and a group can present evidence and ask without guaranteeing the decision (H, D).
- **155, D4.6.6-8; D4.7.6-8:** "A town's waiting list for study rooms grew after opening hours shortened; nearby towns share bookings. What causal uncertainty and collective opportunity remain?" Answer: demand may also have changed; compare dated records and request coordinated scheduling through the responsible bodies (C, D).
- **156, D4.3.6-8:** "A youth group's noise report is going to residents and a budget committee. What adaptation helps each audience while preserving evidence?" Answer: explain effects and terms for residents, foreground costs and authority for the committee, and retain source dates in both (E).
- **157, D4.3.6-8:** "A rehearsal slide says 'every neighbor agrees,' based on an open comment box. Revise it for a public audience." Answer: report only participating commenters' views and name the sampling limit (A, E).
- **158, D4.3.6-8; D4.8.6-8:** "An audience asks a cost question that the sources cannot answer. What should presenters say and record?" Answer: state the limit, record the question and identify a suitable source or decision-maker to consult (K).
- **159, D4.8.6-8:** "A group received an acknowledgement but no decision. How should it describe the result and decide its next action?" Answer: receipt is confirmed, decision pending; hear alternatives and use its agreed procedure to choose a next step (F, J).
- **160, D4.7.6-8; D4.8.6-8:** "A community proposal was declined because its requested authority was wrong. What can its authors learn without claiming the problem is solved?" Answer: identify the proper body, reassess capacity and revise through a fair decision; record the unresolved outcome (H, F).
