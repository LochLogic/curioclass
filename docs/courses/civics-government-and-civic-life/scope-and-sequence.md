# Scope and sequence: Civics, Government and Civic Life

Course `civics-government-and-civic-life` (catalogue id `us-hist-8`), grade 8, draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/civics-government-and-civic-life/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them. The course has no base text, so it has no source-map.csv.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from an unseen source, then its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Every unit's first day carries its sensitivity flag in the note column, and the days with Native, slavery, faith, disability, LGBTQ or immigration content carry their own; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. Personal finance is a light touch (a pay stub's withholding in unit 7, interest in unit 7) with no added day.

later.csv L8 names this course as thin after the redraw. Each unit below names its topics in full, so the year is a full civics year inside the frame's ten units and 160 days: every Massachusetts grade 8 topic (T1 to T7) and its C3 partners are placed, and nothing is added outside the frame.

## Unit 1. Roots of self-government (days 1 to 15, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** Athens: voting, juries, citizenship and the rule of law (MA.8.T1.1); the Roman Republic: separated powers, representation and civic duty (T1.2); where the founders chose representation over direct democracy; Locke on natural rights and consent, Montesquieu on separated powers (T1.3); the Magna Carta, habeas corpus, Parliament and the English Bill of Rights (T1.4); colonial legislatures and town meetings; Blackstone and the common law; a cause-and-effect chain from these ideas to the founders' plan; the Haudenosaunee Confederacy and the Great Law of Peace from its own materials, in the present tense (T1.5); the evidence for and against its influence (Franklin's Albany Plan remarks, the 1988 congressional resolution) and judging whether evidence is enough (D3.4.6-8).

Arc: three Greek and Roman days, five English and Enlightenment days, four Haudenosaunee and evidence days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. Time order runs Athens to the colonies; the influence question comes last because it needs the other traditions in hand to weigh.

- Readiness check, day 1, on naming the strongest counterclaim, as the frame sets (see For the lead).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 14. Respond day 15.
- Candidate cell settled: Blackstone kept as one short glossed excerpt inside day 7, no added day.

## Unit 2. The founding documents (days 16 to 30, weeks 4 to 6)

**Topics taught:** the causes of independence and the Declaration read closely: its argument, claims and grievances (MA.8.T2.1); equality, natural rights, consent and limited, representative government in it (D2.Civ.8.6-8); who its words left out in 1776 and how later movements used them, with Jefferson's cut passage on the slave trade; why the Articles of Confederation failed, and Shays' Rebellion (T2.2); the Convention's leaders and debates and the Great Compromise (T2.3); slavery in the Constitution's text (the Three-Fifths Compromise, the slave-trade clause, the fugitive slave clause); Federalists and Anti-Federalists, with Federalist No. 51 and Brutus No. 1 (T2.4); the Bill of Rights as the bargain that won ratification; the Convention seen by a small state, a large state and an Anti-Federalist.

Arc: four Declaration days, four Articles and Convention days, four ratification days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond.

- Readiness check, day 16, on grade 5's causes of independence and the Constitution (idle in grades 6 and 7).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 29. Respond day 30.
- Candidate cell: Federalist No. 51 and Brutus No. 1 are placed by the frame (day 25).

## Unit 3. The Constitution and the three branches (days 31 to 47, weeks 7 to 10)

**Topics taught:** the Preamble and the Constitution's purposes; Articles I to VII in plain words, and Article V's amendment process; the Bill of Rights in plain words (MA.8.T2.5); a constitution compared with a treaty and a charter (D2.Civ.3.6-8); the three branches and the executive agencies told apart (MA.8.T3.1, T3.3); Congress's powers and limits, a bill traced through both houses, and Congress's leaders (T3.2); the President, the Cabinet, the agencies and the armed services; checks and balances in action (veto, override, confirmation, impeachment) and a parliamentary system compared (D2.Civ.4.6-8); the necessary and proper clause and McCulloch v. Maryland (T5.1); judicial review from Marbury v. Madison, and how a case reaches and is decided by the Supreme Court, with a majority and a dissent (T5.5).

Arc: five text days, six branch days, three court days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond.

- Readiness check, day 31, on grade 5's branches model, as the frame sets.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 46. Respond day 47.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 4. Elections and parties (days 52 to 63, weeks 11 to 13)

**Topics taught:** how members of Congress and the President are elected, primaries and general elections, the Electoral College, terms compared and the 22nd Amendment (MA.8.T3.4); how judges and executive officials are nominated and confirmed, and life tenure; the roles of parties (T3.5); how citizens take part through elections, parties, interest groups and the media (T4.5, D2.Civ.1.6-8); candidates' positions, ads and campaign finance records sourced (T4.6); voter turnout data read as tables and percents.

Arc: four days on how officials reach office, two on participation, three on election information, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. Examples from several parties and past elections; no student says whom anyone supports.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 62. Respond day 63.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 5. Citizens and civic values (days 64 to 76, weeks 13 to 16)

**Topics taught:** birth and naturalization (MA.8.T4.1); the naturalization civics test's public questions as review; citizens' rights and duties compared with non-citizens' (T4.2, D2.Civ.2.6-8); civic, political and private life (T4.3); core civic values (T4.4); tensions between values (T4.10) and one real issue heard from more than one view (D2.Civ.10.6-8); judging leaders by evidence (T4.7); working with elected leaders and public service careers (T4.8, T4.9); the delegate and trustee views, and political courage from more than one party and era (T4.11).

Arc: four citizenship days, three values days, three leadership days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. No question asks about any student's status.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 75. Respond day 76.
- Candidate cell settled: the naturalization civics test practice kept at about half of day 65, inside the unit's 13 days.

## Unit 6. Rights over time (days 77 to 95, weeks 16 to 19)

**Topics taught:** constitutional issues before the Civil War and the secession declarations, with slavery named as the central cause (MA.8.T5.3); the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments and federal power; due process, equal protection and incorporation (T5.2); the 19th and 26th Amendments; the 1964 Civil Rights Act, the 1965 Voting Rights Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act and court rulings that extended rights to new groups, such as marriage in 2015 (T5.4); a continuity and change chart on rights (D2.His.5.6-8); a nine-day research argument on one area where Supreme Court decisions changed lives, the class covering all three: First Amendment freedoms (T5.6a), due process and equal protection (T5.6b), rights against community interests (T5.6c), with the claim, counterclaim and limits built in (D4.1.6-8).

Arc: five amendment days, three civil rights days, nine research days, then assessment and respond. The heavy research writing falls in weeks 18 and 19, after ELA 8 has taught counterclaims (its days 31 to 62) and research steps (by its day 76).

- Readiness check, day 77, on grade 5's Civil War, Reconstruction amendments and civil rights movements (idle two grades; see For the lead).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 94; the research report counts with it. Respond day 95.
- Candidate cell settled: incorporation kept, named plainly inside day 80, no added day.

## Unit 7. Federalism and your state (days 100 to 119, weeks 20 to 24)

**Topics taught:** what state and national governments each do (MA.8.T6.1); the provisions that define and divide powers, enumerated and implied powers (T6.2, T6.3); the Tenth Amendment (T6.5, D2.Civ.5.6-8); tribal nations as governments today, and the Medicine Creek Treaty of 1854 as the worked example (Since Time Immemorial middle school unit 1C, content unchanged, one credit line); the student's state constitution and declaration of rights beside the Bill of Rights (T6.4, T6.6); federal, state and local duties contrasted (T6.7); the state's governor, agencies, legislature and courts (T6.8, 8a to 8c); local government (T6.10); whether each decision procedure does its job (D2.Civ.11.6-8); tax-supported services, public goods and spillovers on a supply-demand graph, with a pay stub's withholding (T6.9, D2.Eco.8.6-8); a real budget's trade-offs; bonds and interest (D2.Eco.10.6-8); a public-data panel on the economy (D2.Eco.11.6-8); weighing two solutions to a local budget question (D2.Eco.2.6-8).

Arc: five federalism days (including the tribal nations and treaty days), three state rights days, five state and local government days, five budget days, then assessment and respond. The split is 5, 3, 5, 5 against the frame's about 5, 3, 6, 6; the compelling question shares day 117 with the budget weighing so the unit fits its 20 days. Taught for the student's own state, with Massachusetts one worked example among several.

- Readiness check, day 100, on grade 5's federal system, as the frame sets (see For the lead).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 118. Respond day 119.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 8. How change happens (days 120 to 130, weeks 24 to 26)

**Topics taught:** protest in a democracy and the First Amendment (MA.8.T4.12); past and present ways of seeking change, and what continued and changed (D2.Civ.14.6-8); interest groups, policy research organizations, unions, businesses and nonprofits in public debate (T4.13, D2.Civ.6.6-8); corporations, nonprofits and unions in the economy (D2.Eco.9.6-8); one policy traced from problem to law to results, and who gained and who paid (D2.Civ.13.6-8); two cases from different sides tested with the same questions.

Arc: three protest days, two group days, two policy days, a comparison day, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. The teacher books unit 10's audience by day 130.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 129. Respond day 130.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 9. A free press and the news (days 131 to 144, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** press freedom in the First Amendment and state constitutions (MA.8.T7.1); a free press as competing information (T7.2); news articles, editorials, cartoons and op-eds told apart (T7.3); digital news and social media (T7.4); checking who is behind a claim, lateral reading on printed pages, and spotting misinformation (T7.5); point of view in editorial pieces, pieces from more than one side judged, credibility critiqued and an editorial analysis written (T7.6, D4.4.6-8).

Arc: two press freedom days, five news literacy days, four editorial days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. No outlet is named as trusted or untrusted in student copy.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 143. Respond day 144.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 10. Civic action (days 149 to 160, weeks 30 to 32)

**Topics taught:** naming school or community problems and setting discussion norms (D4.6.6-8); deliberative procedures compared (D2.Civ.9.6-8); deliberating with civic virtues (D2.Civ.7.6-8); a problem's causes at local, regional and global levels; levers, outcomes and the choice of action (D4.7.6-8); a proposal built from sources and rehearsed for an audience beyond the class (D4.3.6-8); presenting it, following up and the look-back (D4.8.6-8).

Arc: seven building days, the end-of-unit assessment on day 156, the presentation on day 157 (the frame's "by about day 157"), the respond day 158, follow-up 159 and the look-back on day 160, the year's last day. The class chooses the problem and the action; the course never picks a side.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 156. Respond day 158 (the presentation sits between, as the frame dates it).
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.
