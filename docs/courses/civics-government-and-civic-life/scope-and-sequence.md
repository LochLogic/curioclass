# Scope and sequence: Civics, Government and Civic Life

Course `civics-government-and-civic-life` (catalogue id `us-hist-8`), grade 8, draft (2026-11-14). The days live in data/plans/civics-government-and-civic-life/days.csv; this page gives the judgment behind them. The course has no base text, so it has no source-map.csv.

The course meets every school day (50 minutes, no timers shown to students), so course day n is school day n and the `school_day` column is blank. Week = (day - 1) // 5 + 1. Every unit closes with a one-day end-of-unit assessment that includes a task from an unseen source, then its respond day (the response time, counted once). No unit is over 25 days, so none holds a mid-unit assessment. Every unit's first day carries its sensitivity flag in the note column, and the days with Native, slavery, faith, disability, LGBTQ or immigration content carry their own; the sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) comes with each unit plan. Literacy codes (RH, WHST) are also-met and sit only in notes. Every end-of-unit assessment and course-level check asks about each part of each code it lists (a short prompt per part, no day added); where a product or an observed performance (the court research report, the deliberation, the presentation) stands for a code, the day's goal or note says so. A unit opens with a readiness check on an earlier grade's row last used two or more grades back; a row used the year before, or earlier this year, opens as a retrieval task inside a day. Fixed after the expert and Codex reads of 2026-11-14 (docs/reviews/sequence-6-8-fixes-2026-11-14.md). Personal finance is a light touch (a pay stub's withholding in unit 7, interest in unit 7) with no added day.

later.csv L8 names this course as thin after the redraw. Each unit below names its topics in full, so the year is a full civics year inside the frame's ten units and 160 days: every Massachusetts grade 8 topic (T1 to T7) and its C3 partners are placed, and nothing is added outside the frame.

## Unit 1. Roots of self-government (days 1 to 15, weeks 1 to 3)

**Topics taught:** Athens: voting, juries, citizenship and the rule of law (MA.8.T1.1); the Roman Republic: separated powers, representation and civic duty (T1.2); where the founders chose representation over direct democracy; Locke on natural rights and consent, Montesquieu on separated powers (T1.3); the Magna Carta, habeas corpus, Parliament and the English Bill of Rights (T1.4); colonial legislatures and town meetings; Blackstone and the common law; a cause-and-effect chain from these ideas to the founders' plan; the Haudenosaunee Confederacy and the Great Law of Peace from its own materials, in the present tense (T1.5); the evidence for and against its influence (Franklin's Albany Plan remarks, the 1988 congressional resolution) and judging whether evidence is enough (D3.4.6-8).

Arc: three Greek and Roman days, five English and Enlightenment days, four Haudenosaunee and evidence days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. The English and Enlightenment examples are grouped thematically; day 8 places them in chronological order before students weigh influence. The influence question comes last because it needs the other traditions in hand to weigh.

- Retrieval task, day 1, on naming the strongest counterclaim (ss7-rome-fall, used in grade 7, the year before).
- Day 12 opens with a worked model of answering a counterclaim in full (concede what its evidence shows, then answer with stronger evidence) before students write the year's first argument.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 14: a short prompt per tradition traced to US government, and an unseen source on influence with a claim and counterclaim each weighed for strengths and limitations. Respond day 15.
- Candidate cell settled: Blackstone kept as one short glossed excerpt inside day 7, no added day.

## Unit 2. The founding documents (days 16 to 30, weeks 4 to 6)

**Topics taught:** the causes of independence and the Declaration read closely: its argument, claims and grievances (MA.8.T2.1); equality, natural rights, consent, limited and representative government, the rule of law, the common good and the purpose of government in it (D2.Civ.8.6-8); who its words left out in 1776 and how later movements used them, with Jefferson's cut passage on the slave trade; why the Articles of Confederation failed, and Shays' Rebellion (T2.2); the Convention's leaders and debates and the Great Compromise (T2.3); slavery in the Constitution's text (the Three-Fifths Compromise, the slave-trade clause, the fugitive slave clause); Federalists and Anti-Federalists, with Federalist No. 51 and Brutus No. 1 (T2.4); the Bill of Rights as the bargain that won ratification; the Convention seen by a small state, a large state and an Anti-Federalist.

Arc: four Declaration days, four Articles and Convention days, four ratification days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond.

- Readiness check, day 16, on grade 5's causes of independence and the war (ss5-revolution-causes, ss5-independence-war: not used in grades 6 or 7). ss5-constitution, used in grade 7, returns as a retrieval task on day 20.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 29: the causes and key ideas of the Declaration, what it left out and how its principles shaped later claims, the Articles and the road to the Convention, the Convention's leaders and settlements, and contrasting unseen Federalist and Anti-Federalist excerpts compared on the issue they disagree about. Respond day 30.
- Candidate cell: Federalist No. 51 and Brutus No. 1 are placed by the frame (day 25).

## Unit 3. The Constitution and the three branches (days 31 to 47, weeks 7 to 10)

**Topics taught:** the Preamble and the Constitution's purposes; Articles I to VII in plain words, and Article V's amendment process; the Bill of Rights in plain words (MA.8.T2.5); a constitution compared with an ordinary law, a treaty, an international agreement and a charter by origins, purposes and impact (D2.Civ.3.6-8); the three branches and the executive agencies told apart (MA.8.T3.1, T3.3); Congress's powers and limits, a bill traced through both houses, and Congress's leaders (T3.2); the President, the Cabinet, the agencies and the armed services; checks and balances in action (veto, override, confirmation, impeachment) and a parliamentary system compared (D2.Civ.4.6-8); the necessary and proper clause and McCulloch v. Maryland (T5.1); judicial review from Marbury v. Madison, and how a case reaches and is decided by the Supreme Court, with a majority and a dissent (T5.5).

Arc: five text days, six branch days, three court days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond.

- Retrieval task, day 31, on grade 5's branches model (used in grade 7 and in unit 2).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 46: the text and the Bill of Rights, two founding agreements compared (day 35's work counts), the branches and their roles, checks and balances applied to an unseen case with a prompt each on Congress's powers and leaders, a bill's path (day 37's trace counts) and the executive's build, implied powers and judicial review. Respond day 47.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Course-level check 1 (days 48 to 51, weeks 10 to 11)

Check days 48 and 49 (week 10, CALENDAR.md's target), respond days 50 and 51. Unit 3's assessment falls on day 46, so the check does not repeat it: day 49 applies the branches and their checks to a founding-era case from units 1 or 2 (the Articles' missing executive and national courts, or Montesquieu's separated powers).

## Unit 4. Elections and parties (days 52 to 63, weeks 11 to 13)

**Topics taught:** how members of Congress and the President are elected, primaries and general elections, the Electoral College, terms compared and the 22nd Amendment (MA.8.T3.4); how judges and executive officials are nominated and confirmed, and life tenure; the roles of parties (T3.5); how citizens take part through elections, parties, interest groups and the media (T4.5, D2.Civ.1.6-8); candidates' positions, ads and campaign finance records sourced (T4.6); voter turnout data read as tables and percents.

Arc: four days on how officials reach office, two on participation, three on election information, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. Examples from several parties and past elections; no student says whom anyone supports.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 62: how officials reach office with terms and the two-term limit, parties' roles, ways citizens take part, the separate duties of citizens, parties, groups and the media, and unseen election information (a statement, a finance record, an ad, a news item's influence, a turnout table) each sourced and judged. Respond day 63.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 5. Citizens and civic values (days 64 to 76, weeks 13 to 16)

**Topics taught:** birth and naturalization (MA.8.T4.1); the naturalization civics test's public questions as review; citizens' rights and duties compared with non-citizens' (T4.2, D2.Civ.2.6-8); civic, political and private life (T4.3); core civic values (T4.4); tensions between values (T4.10) and one real issue heard from more than one view (D2.Civ.10.6-8); judging leaders by evidence (T4.7); working with elected leaders and public service careers (T4.8, T4.9); the delegate and trustee views, and political courage from more than one party and era (T4.11).

Arc: four citizenship days, three values days, three leadership days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. No question asks about any student's status.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 75: becoming a citizen, rights and duties compared, a short scenario set on civic, political and private life, a value tension from more than one view, a leader judged from an unseen source, why cooperation with elected leaders and public service matter, and the delegate and trustee views applied to that leader. Respond day 76.
- Candidate cell settled: the naturalization civics test practice kept at about half of day 65, inside the unit's 13 days.

## Unit 6. Rights over time (days 77 to 95, weeks 16 to 19)

**Topics taught:** constitutional issues before the Civil War and the secession declarations, with slavery named as the central cause (MA.8.T5.3); the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments and federal power; due process, equal protection and incorporation (T5.2); the 19th and 26th Amendments; the 1964 Civil Rights Act, the 1965 Voting Rights Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act and court rulings that extended rights to new groups, such as marriage in 2015 (T5.4); a continuity and change chart on rights (D2.His.5.6-8); a nine-day research argument on one area where Supreme Court decisions changed lives over time, each question comparing at least two decisions (Tinker with a later school-expression case; Dred Scott as background before later 14th Amendment cases; Gobitis and Barnette), the class covering all three: First Amendment freedoms (T5.6a), due process and equal protection (T5.6b), rights against community interests (T5.6c), with the claim, counterclaim and limits built in (D4.1.6-8).

Arc: five amendment days, three civil rights days, nine research days, then assessment and respond. The heavy research writing falls in weeks 18 and 19, after ELA 8 has taught counterclaims (its days 31 to 62) and research steps (by its day 76).

- Readiness check, day 77, on grade 5's Civil War, Reconstruction amendments and civil rights movements (not used in grades 6 or 7).
- Research days 85, 87 and 93 ask what changed between two decisions, for whom, and what continued, as MA.8.T5.6 asks.
- End-of-unit assessment, day 94: the war's constitutional issues, a constitutional change from an unseen document, a civil rights law's context and significance, how views of rights changed, and a short argument from an unseen opinion excerpt with strengths and limitations; the research report counts with it. Respond day 95.
- Candidate cell settled: incorporation kept, named plainly inside day 80, no added day.

## Course-level check 2 (days 96 to 99, week 20)

Check days 96 and 97 (week 20, CALENDAR.md's target), respond days 98 and 99. Unit 6's assessment falls on day 94, so the check does not repeat it: day 97's constitutional change is the 22nd Amendment (unit 4), and its argument works from an opinion met in unit 3 (McCulloch or Marbury).

## Unit 7. Federalism and your state (days 100 to 119, weeks 20 to 24)

**Topics taught:** what state and national governments each do (MA.8.T6.1); the provisions that define and divide powers, enumerated and implied powers (T6.2, T6.3); the Tenth Amendment (T6.5, D2.Civ.5.6-8); tribal nations as governments today, and the Medicine Creek Treaty of 1854 as the worked example (Since Time Immemorial middle school unit 1C, content unchanged, one credit line); the student's state constitution and declaration of rights beside the Bill of Rights (T6.4, T6.6); federal, state and local duties contrasted (T6.7); the state's governor, agencies, legislature and courts (T6.8, 8a to 8c); local government (T6.10); whether each decision procedure does its job (D2.Civ.11.6-8); tax-supported services, public goods and spillovers on a supply-demand graph, with a pay stub's withholding (T6.9, D2.Eco.8.6-8); a real budget's trade-offs; bonds and interest (D2.Eco.10.6-8); a public-data panel on the economy (D2.Eco.11.6-8); weighing two solutions to a local budget question (D2.Eco.2.6-8).

Arc: five federalism days (including the tribal nations and treaty days; day 100 pairs the state and national functions with the federal, state and local duties, MA.8.T6.7), three state rights days, four state and local government days ending on the procedure day (111), where the compelling question is answered from the levels and procedures in hand, six budget days (services and the pay stub, public goods and spillovers on the graph, a real budget, bonds and interest, the data panel, two solutions weighed), then assessment and respond. The split is about 5/3/4/6, inside the unit's 20 days; it gives spillovers their own day and the compelling question its evidence day. Taught for the student's own state, with Massachusetts one worked example among several.

- Retrieval task, day 100, on grade 5's federal system (ss5-constitution, used in grade 7 and in units 2 and 3).
- End-of-unit assessment, day 118, composite: powers and duties applied to an unseen problem, rights compared, the branches and a local body, a procedure judged, services named, and a budget trade-off from unseen data with spillovers, interest and the data panel each prompted; the spillover graph (113), the borrowing and investing work (115) and the data-panel reading (116) count with it. Respond day 119.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 8. How change happens (days 120 to 130, weeks 24 to 26)

**Topics taught:** protest in a democracy and the First Amendment (MA.8.T4.12); past and present ways of seeking change, and what continued and changed (D2.Civ.14.6-8); interest groups, policy research organizations, unions, businesses and nonprofits in public debate (T4.13, D2.Civ.6.6-8); corporations, nonprofits and unions in the economy (D2.Eco.9.6-8); one policy traced from problem to law to results, and who gained and who paid (D2.Civ.13.6-8); two cases from different sides tested with the same questions.

Arc: three protest days, two group days, two policy days, a comparison day, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. The teacher books unit 10's audience by day 130.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 129. Respond day 130.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 9. A free press and the news (days 131 to 144, weeks 27 to 29)

**Topics taught:** press freedom in the First Amendment and state constitutions (MA.8.T7.1); a free press as competing information (T7.2); news articles, editorials, cartoons and op-eds told apart (T7.3); digital news and social media (T7.4); checking who is behind a claim, lateral reading on printed pages, and spotting misinformation (T7.5); point of view in editorial pieces, pieces from more than one side judged, credibility critiqued and an editorial analysis written (T7.6, D4.4.6-8).

Arc: two press freedom days, five news literacy days, four editorial days, the compelling question, then assessment and respond. No outlet is named as trusted or untrusted in student copy.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 143: press freedom and competing views, the four news forms' purposes told apart, digital news's and social media's benefits and challenges, an unseen claim read laterally, and an unseen editorial analysed. Respond day 144.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Unit 10. Civic action (days 149 to 160, weeks 30 to 32)

**Topics taught:** naming school or community problems and setting discussion norms (D4.6.6-8); deliberative procedures compared (D2.Civ.9.6-8); deliberating with civic virtues (D2.Civ.7.6-8); a problem's causes at local, regional and global levels, through two disciplines and over time, with the obstacles and opportunities facing those addressing it; levers, outcomes and the choice of action (D4.7.6-8); a proposal built from sources and rehearsed for an audience beyond the class (D4.3.6-8); presenting it, following up and the look-back (D4.8.6-8).

Arc: five days to the choice of action (149 to 153), the end-of-unit assessment on day 154, its respond day 155, building the class's shared proposal on 156, rehearsal on 157, the presentation on 158, follow-up 159 and the look-back on day 160, the year's last day. Assessing before the build lets the respond day improve the one product it can, and puts rehearsal next to the presentation. Where the booked audience needs day 157, build and rehearse share day 156 and the presentation moves to 157. The class chooses the problem and the action; the course never picks a side.

- End-of-unit assessment, day 154: an unseen local problem analysed at three levels, through two disciplines and over time, its levers weighed and two procedures compared. The teacher's record of the day 151 deliberation counts for D2.Civ.7, and the presentation and follow-up (158, 159) for D4.3 and D4.8. Respond day 155.
- Candidate cells: none in the frame.

## Shorter path

The year is 160 days of 50 minutes. Two shorter routes keep every owned code, skill and assessment; the unit plans check them against real minutes, so neither is yet shown to fit.

- **45-minute periods, same 160 days.** Each teaching day keeps its source work, its product and its exit ticket, and trims the second worked example or the whole-class share; assessment and respond days stay whole. Unit 6's research trims peer sharing to one partner.
- **About 150 days.** These days fold into the day before, because each code or skill they carry is taught on another day too: 28 (the unit 2 question, into 27), 38 (Congress's leaders, into 37), 57 (the sorted cases, into 56), 65 (the civics test review, into 64), 70 (one issue from more than one view, into 69), 122 (change between eras, into 121), 127 (two cases tested, into 126), 142 (the unit 9 question, into 141), and the second respond day after checks 2 and 3 (99, 148). Check 1's two respond days stay. The research days, unit 7's six budget days, every assessment and every first respond day are protected.
