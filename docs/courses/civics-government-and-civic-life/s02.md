## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the founders' documents live up to their own words?"
**Hook:** Compare the Declaration's equality claim with Black petitioners' demand for freedom in 1777. What could the words accomplish?
**Supporting questions:** Write with students: What justified independence (day 18)? Whose freedom remained denied (day 19)? What did the Convention change and protect (day 23)? What persuaded opponents of ratification (day 26)?
**Closing claim:** Day 28 answers the compelling question using the Declaration, slavery clauses, petition and ratification essays. Weigh achievement against exclusion and answer a counterclaim.
Model sourcing on day 17, principle-to-grievance reasoning on day 18, causation on day 21 and perspective on day 24. Model a fair counterclaim on day 26; withdraw the worked paragraph on day 28.

**Limits:** Read documents for governmental claims, not literary rhetoric. Detailed articles, branch procedures and amendments belong to unit 3; later rights struggles receive fuller treatment in unit 6. The Declaration's last grievance accuses the king of inciting attacks and names Native peoples with a slur. Student pages summarize it as "a grievance that blamed the king for Native nations' attacks and described Native peoples with an insult"; the teacher names it as a slur and does not have it read aloud. On day 19 it is evidence of how the document excluded Native nations, set beside the Oneida Nation's own account of its Revolutionary alliance.
