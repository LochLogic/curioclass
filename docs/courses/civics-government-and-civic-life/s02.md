## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Did the founders' documents live up to their own words?"
**Hook:** Compare the Declaration's equality claim with Black petitioners' demand for freedom in 1777. What could the words accomplish?
**Supporting questions:** Write with students: What justified independence (day 18)? Whose freedom remained denied (day 19)? What did the Convention change and protect (day 23)? What persuaded opponents of ratification (day 26)?
**Closing claim:** Day 28 answers the compelling question using the Declaration, slavery clauses, petition and ratification essays. Weigh achievement against exclusion and answer a counterclaim.
Model sourcing on day 17, principle-to-grievance reasoning on day 18, causation on day 21 and perspective on day 24. Model a fair counterclaim on day 26; withdraw the worked paragraph on day 28.

**Limits:** Read documents for governmental claims, not literary rhetoric. Detailed articles, branch procedures and amendments belong to unit 3; later rights struggles receive fuller treatment in unit 6. The Declaration's last grievance accuses the king of inciting attacks and names Native peoples with a slur. Student pages summarize it as "a grievance that blamed the king for Native nations' attacks and described Native peoples with an insult"; the teacher names it as a slur and does not have it read aloud. On day 19 it is evidence of how the document excluded Native nations, set beside the Oneida Nation's own account of its Revolutionary alliance.
**Carried:** Keep unit 1's blank claim organizer and causal chain. Tie sourcing to annotated text on day 17; revisit on day 25. Build the perspective chart on day 22, revise on day 27, and withdraw completed charts on day 28. Retain blank organizers.

| 29 | D2.Civ.8.6-8 | Analyze principles and explain social and political influence. | **2.** Read the fresh assessment passage P below. Explain how its principle relates to the Declaration. | 29 | MA.8.T2.2 | Analyze weaknesses and the road to the Convention. | **3.** A new museum caption says, "The Articles created a king, and Shays' Rebellion alone invented the idea of constitutional reform." Identify each side and compare its claim and reason, using one detail from each. Then explain each side's position on federal and state powers, checks and balances, limited government and a bill of rights. Why did promises of amendments help ratification without making all opponents agree? **Look-fors:** accurate opposing arguments; all four issues; text-specific reasoning and qualified historical outcome. **Meets:** "F is Federalist: many interests make an oppressive majority harder to form. R is Anti-Federalist: distant representatives may not know or express people's views. Use the supporting questions. Judge day 28's writing by an answer, accurate evidence from at least three sources, an explained contradiction and a fair counterclaim response. Different qualified conclusions can succeed.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach grievance, consent, apportionment and ratification with examples. Frame: "This provision protected ___ by ___; it denied ___." Limit independent reading to 300 words daily; read harder passages aloud. Offer large print, oral descriptions, oral answers and a scribe. Extend by testing a claim against another provision.
