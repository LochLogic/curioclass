## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Which traditions most shaped the founders' plan, and where does the evidence of influence fall short?"

**Hook:** Compare the Athenian assembly with the elected House. Why did self-government take different forms?
**Supporting questions:** Write with students: What was kept from Athens and Rome (day 3)? How did rights reach colonists (day 8)? What establishes influence (day 11)?
**Closing claim:** Day 13 ranks traditions using Locke, Montesquieu, English documents, Franklin and the Confederacy's account. Defend a qualified answer with evidence and a counterclaim; rankings may differ.
Model sourcing on day 1 and causal inference on day 8. Model corroboration on day 10 and evidence sufficiency on day 11. Day 12 models conceding a counterclaim's valid evidence before answering it; day 13 removes the worked paragraph.


- **D3.4.6-8:** Construct claims and counterclaims; explain the strengths and limits of each. The local file lists codes only; wording checked against the [C3 indicator, printed page 55](https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.pdf).

**Content parts:** T1.1 explains the founders' Athenian reference and traces voting, juries and law. T1.2 describes republican offices and explains divided power and civic duty. T1.3 explains both thinkers' influence on revolution and governmental design. T1.4 explains English legal protections and colonial institutional practice. T1.5 analyzes evidence for Native governmental influence. Field 5 checks each part.
**Limits:** Athens is an important surviving democratic precedent, not the beginning of all self-government. Teach exclusions, including women and enslaved people, plainly. Detailed constitutional powers belong to unit 3; full Enlightenment history belongs to grade 9.
**Carried:** Revise the branches diagram on days 2 and 3 so Roman offices are not identical to United States branches. Tie timeline entries to causal arrows on day 8; dates alone prove no influence. Build the corroboration chart on day 10, question its independence on day 11, and tie it to claim, evidence and reasoning on day 12. Withdraw completed models on day 13; retain blank organizers.

## U7. Talk and writing

Use the three supporting questions. On day 13, judge the written claim by relevant evidence, reasoning, fair opposition and both sides' limits. Students underline their strongest link and mark one uncertainty.

## U8. Practice that returns

U6 names retrieval. Return to participation on day 9, legal limits on day 12 and source independence on day 13. No fluency target is set.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach consent, sovereignty, corroboration and counterclaim with examples. Frame: "This supports ___ because ___; it cannot show ___." Read difficult excerpts aloud; independent reading stays below 300 words daily. Offer large print, verbal descriptions, oral answers and a scribe. Ask for oral explanation to distinguish language from reasoning gaps. Extend by finding an additional evidentiary limit.

## U10. Two rooms

One child explains both sides to an adult, then writes independently. Use the same checks and response. One projector displays the printed packet; students need no device.
