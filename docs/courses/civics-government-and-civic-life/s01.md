## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Which traditions most shaped the founders' plan, and where does the evidence of influence fall short?"
**Hook:** Compare the Athenian assembly with the elected House. Why did self-government take different forms?
**Supporting questions:** Write with students: What was kept from Athens and Rome (day 3)? How did rights reach colonists (day 8)? What establishes influence (day 11)?
**Closing claim:** Day 13 ranks traditions using Locke, Montesquieu, English documents, Franklin and the Confederacy's account. Defend a qualified answer with evidence and a counterclaim; rankings may differ.
Model sourcing on day 1 and causal inference on day 8. Model corroboration on day 10 and evidence sufficiency on day 11. Day 12 models conceding a counterclaim's valid evidence before answering it; day 13 removes the worked paragraph.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach consent, sovereignty, corroboration and counterclaim with examples. Frame: "This supports ___ because ___; it cannot show ___." Read difficult excerpts aloud; independent reading stays below 300 words daily. Offer large print, verbal descriptions, oral answers and a scribe. Ask for oral explanation to distinguish language from reasoning gaps. Extend by finding an additional evidentiary limit.
