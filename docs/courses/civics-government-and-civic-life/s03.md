## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Does the Constitution keep any one part of government from growing too strong?"
**Hook:** A fictional President announces a new national rule without Congress. Compare that claim with the Preamble: who gives government its authority?
**Supporting questions:** Write with students: What does each constitutional part do (day 34)? How do agreements differ (day 35)? Who can block whom (day 40)? How do courts limit government (day 44)?
**Closing claim:** Day 45 answers the compelling question from Articles I to III, Marbury and the Clinton majority and dissent, with a counterclaim and a limit on the conclusion.
Model text structure on day 32, comparisons on day 35 and institutional arrows on day 40. Model opinion reasoning on day 43; pairs compare on day 44; withdraw completed models on day 45.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach article, ratify, enumerated, implied, jurisdiction, agency and dissent in context. Frame: "___ may ___ under ___, but ___ limits that power." Explain arrows orally before writing; accurate reasoning in developing English counts as content knowledge. Read legal excerpts aloud; limit new source text read independently to 300 words daily. Read assessment directions aloud. Use large print, labeled arrows, oral descriptions and a scribe. Extend by testing a check against another provision on the same standard.
