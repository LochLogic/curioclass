# Unit 1.2: Multiples, prime and composite

Grade 4 math. Stretch 1, Factors and Multiples (days 1 to 6): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 4 to 6.** Days by
kind: understanding 2, fluency 0, use 0, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with
children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.OA.B.4, this unit's share.** Parts (b), (c) and (d): students **recognize** that a whole number is a
  multiple of each of its factors, **determine** whether a number is a multiple of a given one-digit number,
  and **determine** whether a number is prime or composite.
- **Limits that bite here.** "A multiple of" is tested against one-digit numbers only. A leftover only
  means "not a multiple"; reading a remainder is stretch 13. No divisibility rules taught as rules.
- **Connections.** None from grade 4 yet.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** None set. Taught: test a multiple by building from a product you know (ten
  times, then on); test for prime by searching for a factor pair in order.
- **Practices.** MP.7: day 4, 3 × 7 = 21 is read as two factors and one multiple. MP.8: day 5, every even
  number after 2 has a pair with 2 (Progressions).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Factor and multiple are one fact read two ways. Why the last digit
  settles a multiple of 2, 5 or 10 and not of 4 or 6: 10 is a multiple of 2 and 5, not of 4 or 6. Why 1 is
  neither: prime means exactly two factors. Why testing 2, 3, 5 and 7 is enough: 10 × 10 is 100, so a
  composite number under 100 has a factor below 10.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The rectangle, as a sketch only. Day 4: a multiple of 6 makes rows of 6 with none left, tied
  that day to the equation and its two sentences ("72 = 12 × 6, so 72 is a multiple of 6 and 6 is a factor
  of 72"). Day 5: a prime's only rectangle is one long row; the written form is the word with its proof
  pair. Withdrawn at each exit.
- **Key moment 1, day 4: a multiple is where counting by a number lands; a leftover means not a multiple.**
  Ideal: something that packs a number into equal groups one after another, with a running count, and
  shows the last group full or short, for many numbers fast. **Egg Carton Factory's Fill Line fits** (key
  tool). Gap: no prediction before the run, so students write yes or no on paper first ([TOOL-ASKS](TOOL-ASKS.md), Egg
  Carton Factory, ask 9). Rush shift 3 fits for practice but is not placed: the day could not hold two tool parts.
- **Part (b), day 4: one equation read both ways.** Ideal: the student's own sentences. Rush shift 5 fits,
  but it is spec only; until it is built, paper.
- **Key moment 2, day 5: prime is what a search proves, not how a number looks.** Ideal: a chart marked by
  hand, a proof pair under each composite; then sorting where "composite" must be proved. Rush shift 2 and
  card 4c fit for practice. Card 2c is left out: it holds 77 and 93, which the checks use ([TOOL-ASKS](TOOL-ASKS.md), Egg
  Carton Factory, ask 1).
- **A room with nothing.** Jumps on a drawn number line; a chart ruled by hand; paper sort cards.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: multiple, prime, composite, each with its picture. "Multiple" and "multiply"
  are said side by side. Frames: "__ is a multiple of __, because __ × __ = __." "__ is composite, because
  __ × __ = __."
- **Access.** A large-print chart; a multiplication table to 9 × 9 for a student below the facts mark.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud; answers may be said aloud or scribed.
- **Reading load.** Low. The written task may be dictated.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Can a multiple of a prime be prime?" In day 6's respond half, for
  students with nothing to fix: the sieve on a 1 to 100 chart. Cross out the multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7,
  but not those four. What is left, and why were the multiples of 4, 6, 8 and 9 not needed? It waits until
  after the end check because it gives away every prime.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use beans. "Can 30 beans go into rows of 4 with none left? Rows of 6?" Say both
  sentences for each yes. "Take 13 beans. Make a rectangle that is not one long row." It cannot be done;
  that is prime. On the last day give the six items, mark them together, then give stretch 2's readiness
  items.
- **One projector.** Day 4: every student writes yes or no before the teacher runs the line (draws the
  jumps while the tool is not live). Day 5: a student who says "composite" must name the proof pair.
