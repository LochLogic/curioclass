# Section 4.4: Fraction word problems

Grade 4 math. Unit 4, Adding and Subtracting Fractions (days 25 to 38): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 36 to 38.**
Days by kind: understanding 0, fluency 0, application 2, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.B.3d, this section's share.** "Solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions
  referring to the same whole and having like denominators, e.g., by using visual fraction models and equations
  to represent the problem" (part g), now with mixed numbers, in both operations, with the unknown in each
  place (result, change, start) and in comparing stories. Section 4.2 did the first stories under and just past 1.
- **Limits that bite here.** Like denominators; one or two steps; every story's parts come from one whole,
  except the one planned story on day 37 where they do not and cannot be joined (progression page: "include
  one").
- **Connections.** Measurement in context (length, mass, liquid volume, 3.MD.A.2 and 3.MD.B.4), days 36 and 37,
  as units; unit 5's line plots use these stories next.
- **Practices.** MP.1: day 36, each story read twice and a tape drawn before any number is written; day 37, a
  two-step story kept going after the first step. MP.2: day 37, the answer said with its unit and checked
  against the story.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The story's action (join, separate, compare) sets the equation, not
  its key words: "more" can mean subtract. A tape with a box for the unknown makes the action visible. Any true
  equation for the story is right; the add-subtract relation lets a student write a missing-addend equation
  and solve it by subtracting.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** 4.4a and 4.4b (s04 field 5). Day 38 gives the whole assessment.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's opening shows that error's wrong example.

- **E36.** "Jada's trail is 3 1/10 km long. She has walked 1 4/10 km. How much farther is it to the end? Draw a
  tape, write an equation with a box for the unknown, and solve." Answer: 1 4/10 + □ = 3 1/10 (or 3 1/10 −
  1 4/10 = □); 1 7/10 km (or 17/10). Wrong: 2 3/10 (F); 4 5/10 (H).
- **E37.** "Rae cut 1 3/5 m off a rope. She had 2 1/5 m left. How long was the rope at first? Draw a tape and
  write an equation." Answer: □ − 1 3/5 = 2 1/5 (or 1 3/5 + 2 1/5 = □); 3 4/5 m (or 19/5). Wrong: 3/5 (H: "cut
  off" read as subtract); 3 4/10 (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The tape diagram, drawn by the student, with a box for the unknown. Not withdrawn (the standard
  names a visual model); by day 37 a student may draw it roughly.
- **Key moment 1, day 36: the action, not a key word, sets the equation.** Ideal: a story the student draws,
  writes and solves, then sees the tape shaded to check. Recipe Scaler (spec only), kitchen stories: fits as a
  light tool. It takes any true equation, and its answer is never a number on the card. All eight stories are
  in one kitchen, so the paper stories carry other contexts.
- **Key moment 2, day 37: before the assessment, a check of each part, unaided.** Recipe Scaler, "on your own":
  partly fits. Ten items, two per code, scored by code, but it covers the spec's five codes and not this unit's
  seven parts, so the teacher reads it beside E36 and E37. Asks: TOOL-ASKS.md, Recipe Scaler.
- **A room with nothing.** Tapes drawn by hand; the paper copy of the "on your own" items, marked by the
  teacher.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "The story says 'more'. Does that mean we add? How do you know?" (day 36). "Could Ana and
  Ben have eaten 5/6 of a sandwich? What would have to be true?" (day 37).
- Written task, day 37: "Write a story that 2 5/8 − 1 6/8 = □ could answer, and solve it." Look-fors: one whole
  in the story; separating or comparing; the answer 7/8 with a unit.
- Judged by the teacher with those three look-fors; the plan-data job writes the sample answers.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 36: mixed numbers renamed both ways (section 4.3). Day 37: one sum and one difference with a
  break (section 4.3). Day 38: none; the assessment opens the day.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: farther, left, in all, at first, longer than, each with a tape. Frame: "The
  whole is __. The part I know is __. The box is __." Stories are read aloud first.
- **Access.** Tapes pre-drawn as blank bars; stories in large print; an equation may be said for a helper to
  write.
- **Check accommodations.** As in DEFAULTS.md; every story on the assessment is read aloud once.
- **Reading load.** The heaviest of the unit: two to three sentences a story, about eight stories a day. Each
  is read aloud once; the student page keeps sentences under 15 words.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write one story for each place of the unknown with the same three numbers,
  2 1/4, 1 3/4 and 4.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Make stories from the kitchen or a tape measure; draw the tape together; ask the
  planned questions. On day 38, give the items, then go over the missed ones together (DEFAULTS.md).
- **One projector.** Draw each tape on the board after students draw theirs.
