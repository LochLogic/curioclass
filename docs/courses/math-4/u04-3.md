# Section 4.3: Mixed numbers

Grade 4 math. Unit 4, Adding and Subtracting Fractions (days 25 to 38): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 32 to 35.**
Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 2, application 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.B.3c, this section's share.** All of it: "add and subtract mixed numbers with like denominators, e.g.,
  by replacing each mixed number with an equivalent fraction, and/or by using properties of operations and the
  relationship between addition and subtraction" (parts e and f). Both ways are taught (day 33), both
  operations on every day from 33, and every difference is checked by adding.
- **Limits that bite here.** Like denominators; wholes to 6 in the given numbers. No simplest form; an answer
  not regrouped (5 15/12) is right and noted. No "times the bottom, add the top" rule: renaming is a sum,
  2 3/4 = 1 + 1 + 3/4 = 4/4 + 4/4 + 3/4 (progression page).
- **Connections.** Section 4.1's sums with wholes (day 27), day 32. 2.OA.B.2 and the regrouping of unit 9
  (later in the year; the same idea, a one broken into parts), named on day 34's teacher page.
- **Strategies.** The standard names two: (1) rename each mixed number as a fraction, add or subtract the
  counts, rename back; (2) wholes and parts apart, by the properties: make a one from parts past 1, or break a
  one into parts when the parts are short; check a difference by adding.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 32, "2 and 3/4" read as "2 plus 3/4".
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A mixed number is a sum, a whole number plus a fraction less than 1,
  and so adding two of them is adding four numbers in any order (commutative and associative properties). When
  the parts pass 1, b/b of them make a new whole; when the parts are too few, one whole is broken into b/b.
  Both are the same fact as 1 = b/b from section 4.1.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** 4.3a, 4.3b and 4.3c (s04 field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's opening shows that error's wrong example.

- **E32.** "(a) Write 1 7/10 as tenths: 1 7/10 = 1 + 7/10 = __/10. (b) Write 14/5 as a mixed number." Answers:
  (a) 17 (b) 2 4/5. Wrong: (a) 8 (C); 7: the whole dropped; (b) 4/5: the wholes dropped; 1 9/5: one whole taken
  out only.
- **E33.** "Find 1 4/6 + 2 5/6. Show one way." Answer: 4 3/6 (or 27/6, or 4 1/2); 3 9/6 is right and noted
  "not regrouped". Wrong: 3 9/12 (A); 3 3/6 (G).
- **E34.** "Find 3 1/6 − 1 5/6. Check by adding." Answer: 1 2/6 (or 8/6); 1 2/6 + 1 5/6 = 3 1/6. Wrong: 2 4/6
  (F); 2 4/0 (A).
- **E35.** "(a) 4 − 2 1/10 = __ (b) 2 5/12 + 3 11/12 = __" Answers: (a) 1 9/10 (or 19/10) (b) 6 4/12 (or
  76/12, or 6 1/3). Wrong: (a) 2 1/10: the whole not broken (F); (b) 5 16/24 (A);
  5 4/12 (G).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line, now from 0 to 5, and whole strips with their parts. Day 32 ties a mixed
  number to a point (wholes counted first, then parts). Day 33 ties the hops past a whole to the written new
  whole (G); day 34 ties a broken whole strip to the written renaming (F). Withdrawn on day 35: a sketch only.
- **Key moment 1, day 33: two ways give one answer.** Ideal: the same sum done both ways side by side, with
  the student's answer checked after it is written. Recipe Scaler (spec only), the double batch and the test
  bake: fits for adding, since the child types each doubled amount before the pour regroups it. It shows one
  way at a time, the child's choice; the side-by-side is on paper.
- **Key moment 2, day 35: many sums and differences, each checked.** Recipe Scaler, the pantry rounds: fits.
  Answers are typed before the pour; every take-away is checked by adding back. Asks for both parts:
  TOOL-ASKS.md, Recipe Scaler.
- **A room with nothing.** Whole strips cut from paper, folded into the day's parts; lines ruled to 5.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "What does the 2 in 2 3/4 count, and what does the 3?" (day 32). "Both ways gave 4 2/5.
  Why must they?" (day 33). "When do you need to break a whole, and how do you know before you start?" (day 34).
- Written task, day 34: "Explain how you found 4 1/3 − 2 2/3 and how you know it is right." Look-fors: a whole broken into 3/3 (or both
  renamed as thirds); the answer 1 2/3; a check by adding.
- Judged by the teacher with those three look-fors; the plan-data job writes the sample answers.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Sections 4.1 and 4.2: a fraction as a sum, like-fraction sums and differences.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built with no timer.** Days 34 and 35: short mixed sets, each answer checked the other way
  or by adding; the tool's pantry rounds give feedback on each try.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: mixed number, whole, break apart, regroup, check. Frame: "__ wholes and __ parts
  of size __ is __ parts of size __." Read "two and three fourths".
- **Access.** Whole strips pre-cut and pre-folded; a line to 5 in large print; a student may say each step for
  a helper to write.
- **Check accommodations.** As in DEFAULTS.md.
- **Reading load.** Low: exits are numbers and a short instruction.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find two mixed numbers in eighths whose sum is 5 and whose difference is 1 6/8.
  (The extension's answers, 3 3/8 and 1 5/8, are not used by any check.)

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use paper strips for wholes and a ruler marked in eighths for parts. Ask the planned
  questions and let the child choose a way, then check it the other way.
- **One projector.** Do each example both ways in two columns on the board; students write the answer first.
