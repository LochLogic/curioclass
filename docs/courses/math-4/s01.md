# Stretch 1: Factors and Multiples

Grade 4 math, supporting work. **Days 1 to 6.** Units: [1.1 Rectangles and factor pairs](u01-1.md) (days 1 to
3); [1.2 Multiples, prime and composite](u01-2.md) (days 4 to 6). Days by kind, summed from the units:
understanding 4, fluency 1, use 0, check 1 (the spine's first cut has 3, 2, 0, 1; see field 10). A plan, not a
lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **4.OA.B.4.** Parts, by verb: (a) **find** all factor pairs for a whole number from 1 to 100; (b)
  **recognize** that a whole number is a multiple of each of its factors; (c) **determine** whether a number
  from 1 to 100 is a multiple of a given one-digit number; (d) **determine** whether a number from 1 to 100 is
  prime or composite.
- **Limits.** Whole numbers 1 to 100 only. "A multiple of" is tested against one-digit numbers. No greatest
  common factor, no least common multiple, no prime factorization (grade 6). Students "need not be fluent" in
  finding all pairs (Progressions).
- **Carried.** The rectangle of unit squares: in hand on days 1 and 2, tied to its equation on day 1 and
  to the ordered list on day 2, withdrawn on day 3. Unit 1.2 uses a sketch only.

- **Item 2, an answer with no 1 or no 52.** This is not error A. It shows a different idea: the student
  does not yet see that 1 is a factor of every number, or that a number is a factor and a multiple of
  itself. Read 1 × 52 = 52 both ways with the student.
- **Item 5.** Look-fors: says Ana is not right; gives a factor pair other than 1 × 99; says a prime has only
  1 and itself. Meets (ours, untested): "Ana is wrong. 9 × 11 = 99, so 99 has another factor pair. A prime

- **Item 6.** Look-fors: says Maya is right; says 7 × 5 is 5 × 7 turned; says later pairs repeat. Meets:
  "Yes. 7 goes with 5 and she already has 5 × 7. After that the pairs just turn around." Does not: "Yes,

## 6. Error register

"IM": the open course in field 10, unit 1.

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Lists some factor pairs and stops; often leaves out 1 × n | There is no order to the search | IM lesson 2; Progressions (search in order) |
| B | Finds only pairs from the times tables: no 3 × 14, no 4 × 17 | A factor is "a fact I know", not "divides with none left" | From memory |
| C | Counts a turned pair as new, or keeps searching past the turn | 4 × 9 and 9 × 4 are not seen as one rectangle | From memory; the Progressions describe the turn |
| D | Swaps factor and multiple: "the multiples of 6 are 1, 2, 3, 6" | The two words are not yet tied to one equation | From memory |
| E | Judges "a multiple of" by the last digit or by even and odd, or stops at ten times | A rule that works for 2, 5 and 10 is stretched; the tables are taken for all the multiples | From memory |
| F | Odd, or "not in my tables", means prime | Prime is a look, not the result of a search | From memory; IM lesson 3 notes only that its primes are odd except 2 |
| G | Calls 1 prime; calls 2 composite because it is even | Prime is "few factors", not exactly two | From memory; IM lesson 3 records the question "Is 1 a prime number?" |
