## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **4.MD.A.3:** "Apply the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real world and mathematical
  problems." Parts, all **apply**: (a) the area formula, a side unknown; (b) the perimeter formula, a side
  unknown; (c) perimeter with multi-digit sides; (d) perimeter with fraction sides; (e) choosing the measure
  in a real problem; (f) the perimeter formula in a mathematical problem.
- **This unit's share.** All of it, with small numbers. To apply is to write the equation for this
  rectangle, with its numbers and one unknown, and solve it; not to recall a formula and fill it in
  (Progressions).
- **Limits, and the numbers rule.** Rectangles only. Areas are products within 100, or a one-digit number
  times a multiple of 10. An unknown side from an area is a factor-pair problem. Perimeter may use
  multi-digit addition and like-denominator fractions. The spine adds (decided 2026-11-11): with
  multi-digit sides, 2 × (l + w) is found by adding the sum to itself; an unknown side from a perimeter uses
  an even perimeter of 100 or less.
- **Connections.** Unknown factors and factor pairs (4.OA.B.4): day 90. Multi-digit addition (4.NBT.B.4) and
  like-denominator fractions (4.NF.B.3): day 91. "Times as long" (4.OA.A.1): day 92.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 90, writing the equation for each note. MP.8: day 89, four sides added again and
  again become 2 × (l + w). MP.4 is used on day 92 and in item 5.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** 2 × l + 2 × w = 2 × (l + w) is the distributive property; why an
  unknown side from an area is an unknown factor.
- **Carried.** The rectangle with its unit squares. Grid paper and tiles are in hand on day 89 and tied to
  the letters that day: A = l × w, and P = l + w + l + w = 2 × (l + w). The grid is withdrawn on day 91: a
  rectangle is then a sketch with labelled sides.

## 3. Where it sits

- **Builds on (strong handoff).** 3.MD.C.7a to d (area as rows times columns), 3.MD.D.8 (perimeter, an unknown
  side; same perimeter with different areas), 3.OA.A.4 (unknown factor).
- **Leads to.** Stretch 12. Grade 5: volume (5.MD.C.5), fractional sides (5.NF.B.4b).

- **Item 5.** Look-fors: finds the length, 20; uses area for the cover, in square feet; uses perimeter for the
  border, in feet. Meets (ours, untested): "Length 4 × 5 = 20. Cover 5 × 20 = 100 square feet. Border 20 + 5 +
  20 + 5 = 50 feet." Does not: "5 + 4 = 9. Cover 45 square feet, border 28 feet" (right measures, wrong
  length).
- **Item 6.** Look-fors: sides that add to 19 and are not 11 and 8; says length plus width, or half the
  perimeter, stays the same. Meets: "12 by 7, because 11 + 8 = 19 and 12 + 7 = 19, and the perimeter is double
  that." Does not: "10 by 9. I added 10 + 9 + 10 + 9 and got 38."
- **Accommodations.** Items read aloud; large print; a scribe, or an oral answer for items 5 and 6.

## U7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Is 2 × (l + w) always the same as l + w + l + w? How do you know?" (day 89). "Two
  rectangles have the same perimeter. Must they have the same area?" (day 92).
- Written task, day 92: "Which pen did you choose, and why? Use the words area and perimeter."
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: names the sides; shows the perimeter is 36; compares areas; gives
  a reason.

## U8. Practice that returns

Warm-up: multi-digit adding and subtracting; times tables, with the second facts check on days 89 and 90;
fractions twice. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none.

## U9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: area, perimeter, length, width, square unit, formula, each on a labelled
  rectangle. "Covers" and "goes around" are cue words at first; later problems use neither. Frame: "The
  missing side is __ because __ × __ = __."
- **Access.** Large-square grid paper; tiles and string for touch.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud; a sketch may be drawn for the student from their
  words.
- **Reading load.** The heaviest of the three trial units: days 92 and 93 are word problems of two to four
  sentences. Read each aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Every pen with area 36 and whole-number sides, with its perimeter. Then
  every rectangle with area 60. A rectangle has area 60 and perimeter 32: find its sides.

## U10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Measure a real table or rug to the nearest foot and find both measures. Then: "This
  rug is 24 square feet and 4 feet wide. How long?" Build it from paper squares. On the last day, give the
  six items, then go over the missed ones together.
- **One projector.** Day 90: the teacher runs the tool; for each note the class writes the missing side and
  the equation on paper before the squares are laid.
