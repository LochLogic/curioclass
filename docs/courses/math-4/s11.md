## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **4.MD.A.3:** "Apply the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real world and mathematical
  problems." Its parts:

| 4.MD.A.3 | (d) | apply | perimeter with fraction sides |
| 4.MD.A.3 | (e) | apply | choosing the measure in a real problem |
| 4.MD.A.3 | (f) | apply | the perimeter formula as 2 × (l + w): length plus width is half the perimeter |
| 4.MD.A.3 | (g) | apply | the area formula, both sides known |
<!-- /gen -->

- **This unit's share.** All of it, with small numbers. To apply is to write the equation for this
  rectangle, with its numbers and one unknown, and solve it; not to recall a formula and fill it in
  (Progressions).
- **Limits, and the numbers rule.** Rectangles only. Areas are products within 100, or a one-digit number
  times a multiple of 10. An unknown side from an area is a factor-pair problem. Perimeter may use
  multi-digit addition and like-denominator fractions. The spine adds (decided 2026-11-11): with
  multi-digit sides, 2 × (l + w) is found by adding the sum to itself; an unknown side from a perimeter uses
  an even perimeter of 100 or less.
- **Connections.** Unknown factors and factor pairs (4.OA.B.4): day 90. Multi-digit addition (4.NBT.B.4) and
  like-denominator fractions (4.NF.B.3): day 91. "Times as long" (4.OA.A.1): day 92.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 90, writing the equation for each note. MP.8: day 89, four sides added again and
  again become 2 × (l + w). MP.4 is used on day 92 and in item 5.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** 2 × l + 2 × w = 2 × (l + w) is the distributive property; why an
  unknown side from an area is an unknown factor.
- **Carried.** The rectangle with its unit squares. Grid paper and tiles are in hand on day 89 and tied to
  the letters that day: A = l × w, and P = l + w + l + w = 2 × (l + w). The grid is withdrawn on day 91: a
  rectangle is then a sketch with labelled sides.

- **Item 5.** Three look-fors, each ticked alone: (1) finds the length, 20; (2) uses area for the sheet and
  perimeter for the border; (3) writes square feet and feet. Part (e) is look-for 2. Meets (ours, untested):
  "Length 4 × 5 = 20. Sheet 5 × 20 = 100 square feet. Border 20 + 5 + 20 + 5 = 50 feet." Does not: "Length 20.
  Sheet 20 + 5 + 20 + 5 = 50 feet. Border 20 × 5 = 100 feet." Partly: "5 + 4 = 9. Sheet 45 square feet, border
  28 feet": look-for 2 is met; the length goes to stretch 10's help. An answer of "20 feet by 5 feet" for (a)
  is asked again on day 94: "how much plastic is that?"
- **Item 6.** Look-fors: (1) two sides that add to 19 and are not 11 and 8 (fractions count, and so does 9 1/2
  by 9 1/2); (2) a reason that rests on length plus width, on half the perimeter, or on "one side 1 longer, the

- **A room with nothing.** Hand-ruled grids; paper squares.

**The generator sheet (day 91).** Twelve problems, no pictures, in mixed order: 2 area with both sides known
(one within 100, one a one-digit number times a multiple of 10); 2 area with a side unknown (products within
100); 2 perimeter with a side unknown (even perimeter, 100 or less); 3 perimeter with multi-digit sides (two-
and three-digit, sum under 1,000); 2 perimeter with like-denominator fraction sides (denominators 2, 3, 4, 5,
6, 8, 10 or 12; mixed numbers under 10); and 1 "times as long" area. Then 5 wrong answers to fix, one for each
error A to E. Half bare rectangles, half one-sentence stories. The generator refuses any pair of numbers that
appears in items.csv or in the tool's notes. The answer key prints the equation, not only the number. Counts
are ours, untested.

## U7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Is 2 × (l + w) always the same as l + w + l + w? How do you know?" (day 89). "Every
  pen used 36 feet of fence. What is the same about the length plus the width of each? Why?" then "Two
  rectangles have the same perimeter. Must they have the same area?" (day 92).
- Written task, day 92, on one of the six problems: "Did this problem need area or perimeter? How did you
  know?"
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: says what is measured (the inside or the edge); names the
  measure; the unit matches; the reason comes from the situation, not from one word.

## U8. Practice that returns

Warm-up: multi-digit adding and subtracting; times tables, with the second facts check on days 89 and 90;
fractions twice. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none.

## U9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: area, perimeter, length, width, square unit, formula, each on a labelled
  rectangle. "Covers" and "goes around" are cue words at first; later problems use neither. Frame: "The
  missing side is __ because __ × __ = __."
- **Access.** Large-square grid paper; tiles and string for touch.
- **Reading load.** The heaviest of the three trial units: days 92 and 93 are word problems of two to four
  sentences. Read each aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Every pen with area 36 and whole-number sides, with its perimeter. Then
  every rectangle with area 60. A rectangle has area 60 and perimeter 32: find its sides.

## U10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Measure a real table or rug to the nearest foot and find both measures. Then: "This
  rug is 24 square feet and 4 feet wide. How long?" Build it from paper squares.
- **One projector.** As in DEFAULTS.md; the class writes the equation too.
