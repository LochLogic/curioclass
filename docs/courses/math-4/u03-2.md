# Section 3.2: Why multiplying top and bottom works, in both directions

Grade 4 math. Unit 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 13
to 16.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 2, application 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built
or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.1, this section's share.** The rule. Students **explain why** a/b equals (n × a)/(n × b) by matching
  each number in the rule to a cut on the number line, then "use this principle to **recognize** and
  **generate** equivalent fractions" with no drawing.
- **Limits that bite here.** Denominators from the grade's list; hundredths enter on day 15. No simplest
  form. Never "multiplying by 1".
- **Both directions. Decided, Cory, 2026-11-11 (always cover the whole standard).** Day 14 teaches the
  rule read backwards: parts are put in equal groups, so both numbers are divided by the same number (8/12 =
  2/3). The section's title now says so. The Progressions read each diagram both ways (PDF page 149 (143)), and
  both open courses read give it lessons. Without it a student cannot see that 2/8 and 3/12 are equal.
- **Connections.** Multiples and factors (4.OA.B.4, unit 1): days 13 and 14.
- **Practices.** MP.7, use structure: day 13, each number in the rule is matched to the cut. MP.8, use what
  repeats: day 15, a table of names for one fraction.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why cutting each part into n, or grouping parts by n, changes the
  count and the size of the parts and never the point. Why "multiply by n/n" is not used: it needs a
  fraction times a fraction, grade 5 (Progressions, PDF page 150 (144)).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line. On day 13 the rule is written under the line and each number is matched to
  the cut: the day the drawing is tied to the written rule. The old name sits above the line, the new one
  below (federal fractions guide, roadblock 2.3). Still printed on day 14. Withdrawn on day 15: none is
  given, and a student may sketch one to check or explain. Written form: 2/5 = (2 × 2)/(2 × 5) = 4/10.
- **Key moment 1, days 13 and 14: each number in the rule is a count of parts.** Ideal: a printed line the
  student cuts or groups, with the equation under it. Paper fits. The splitter named on the unit plan (pick
  n, write the new name, then see the cut, on a line from 0 to 2) would serve as a light tool. It does not
  exist, and waits until unit 7 is planned (decided 2026-11-11).
- **Key moment 2, days 15 and 16: many cases with quick feedback.** Ideal: a light practice set that marks
  each missing number, shows the cut line after a miss and counts right first tries, with no clock. It does
  not exist. Fraction Wall Duel (spec only) does not fit: its equivalence work is six blank-card rounds and
  three steals inside a 36-round comparing game, on a wall that stops at 1. Paper with a key. The section runs
  on print, so it has no key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Lines ruled by hand; the key read aloud.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "We multiplied both numbers by 2. Is 4/10 twice as much as 2/5?" (day 13). "8/12 has a
  name with smaller numbers. Does 3/8? How can you tell?" (day 14). "What would you show Lena?" (day 16).
- Written task, day 16: "Mo wrote 5/3 = (2 × 5)/(2 × 3) = 10/6. What does each 2 mean on the number line?"
- Judged by the teacher: its look-fors (the plan's) and samples (ours) are row `s03-W16` in items.csv, so the key
  prints them.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Grade 3 maintenance (adding and subtracting within 1,000, times tables); multiples and factor
  pairs from unit 1 on days 13 and 14.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Days 15 and 16 use short sets, marked at once, with a
  count of right first tries. No clock.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: multiply, divide, group, equal, each on a cut line at first use. Frame, to
  take or leave: "Each part was cut into __, so I multiply both numbers by __."
- **Access.** Large-print lines; cuts may be ruled for the student from their words.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud; lines printed large; answers may be said aloud.
- **Reading load.** Low: numbers and short sentences. Lena's and Jo's claims are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Find every fraction equal to 3/2 with a denominator from our list." "3/4 as
  hundredths: what is n?"

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Rule a line, mark fifths, cut each in two and write the equation under it
  together. A ruler does day 14: 4/8 inch is 2/4 inch is 1/2 inch, one mark. Then ten missing-number items a
  day, marked at once.
- **One projector.** One line on the screen; students write the new name and the equation on paper before
  the cut is drawn.
