# Unit 9.1: The standard algorithm for adding

Grade 4 math. Stretch 9, Adding and Subtracting Multi-Digit Numbers (days 72 to 77). **Days 72 to 74.** Days by kind: understanding 1, fluency 2, use 0. A plan,
not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NBT.B.4** (major work; the grade's one fluency standard): "Fluently add and subtract multi-digit whole
  numbers using the standard algorithm."
- **This unit's share.** Students **add**. Subtracting is unit 9.2. "Fluently" is begun here and carried by
  the warm-up to day 150.
- **Limits.** Whole numbers; sums to 1,000,000.
- **Strategies or algorithm:** the standard algorithm, by name. Fluent means accurate and reasonably fast,
  with a method that is understood, and never with blocks.
- **Practices.** MP.6, be precise: day 72, saying what each small 1 is worth. MP.8, use what repeats: day 73,
  ten of any unit make one of the next, in every column.

## 2. Where it sits

- **Builds on (strong handoff).** 3.NBT.A.2, fluent adding and subtracting within 1,000 by any sound method.
  This year: place value to a million (stretch 8) and rounding to estimate a sum (day 71). Source:
  math-3-5-progression.md, stretch 9, which says little is new and the step need not take long.
- **Leads to.** Unit 9.2; stretches 10, 12, 13, 14. Grade 5: the same algorithm for decimals (5.NBT.B.7).
- **Comes back.** The daily warm-up to day 150; perimeter with large sides (stretch 11); multistep problems
  (stretch 14); measurement (stretch 15); Checks 2 to 4.

## 4. Expected errors

All are teaching notes written from memory, not from a source. Every sum was checked by hand.

| | The error | What it shows | Wrong example beside a right one; day | If many made it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Drops a carry | Each column is added as its own small sum | 3,746 + 2,538 = 5,274. Day 72 | One sum in expanded form beside the algorithm; say each carry by its value |
| B | Lines numbers up from the left | Digits are matched, not places | 6,285 + 947 = 15,755, beside an estimate. Day 73 | Place names over the columns; say both numbers aloud first |
| C | Calls every carry "one" | The steps work but the place value is gone | Asked aloud on day 72: "one what?" | Students name each carry aloud for a day |
| D | Writes a two-digit sum in one column | A place is not seen as holding one digit | 3,746 + 2,538 = "512714". Day 72 | The same sum in expanded form |
| E | Loses the last carry into a new place | Stops at the last digit of the longer number | 64,859 + 47,463 = 12,322 (it is 112,322). Day 73 | Estimate first: the sum must pass 100,000 |

## 6. Model and instruments

- **Carried.** Expanded form written beside the standard algorithm, under place names. On day 72 the teacher
  shows both and students do two sums both ways: the day the model is tied to the written method. Expanded
  form is withdrawn on day 73, and the place names on day 74. No blocks.
- **Key moment 1, day 72: a carry is ten of one unit written as one of the next.** Ideal: paper, two methods
  side by side. Road Trip Logbook's odometer (spec) shows a wheel rolling past 9, which is counting on by
  ones, not adding in columns. It does not fit this moment.
- **Key moment 2, days 73 and 74: many sums with quick feedback, then none.** Ideal: a light column workspace
  that marks each column at first, then only whole answers, and counts how many were right on the first try,
  with no clock. Road Trip Logbook partly fits: its Trip ledger and its unaided items do this job, but every
  part of the tool mixes in subtracting across zeros (unit 9.2), and its trip sums are grade 3 size. It fits
  the stretch on day 77, not this unit. Asked for: an adding-only setting of the ledger, or a shared practice
  template. The unit runs on print without either, so it has no key tool.
- **Supplies.** Grid paper, or lined paper turned sideways; generator sheets with keys. A room with nothing:
  sums copied from the board.

## 8. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Where are the 14 ones in the standard algorithm?" (day 72). "Without adding, how do you
  know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" (day 73). "Why do expanded form and the standard algorithm give the same
  sum?" (day 73).
- Written task, day 74: "Tell a third grader, in two sentences, what the small 1 means."
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: names the unit (a ten, a hundred); says ten of one unit were
  traded for one of the next. This field is thin for a procedure unit. One short task is honest.

## 9. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 72: adding and subtracting within 1,000, as since day 1, and times tables. Day 73: four- and
  five-digit sums, and one-digit numbers times multiples of 10. Day 74: sums to six digits, and fractions and
  decimals. Subtracting stays within 1,000 until unit 9.2 has taught it.
- **Fluency target** (ours, untested): by day 77, at least 4 of 5 sums of four to six digits right on the
  first try, unaided. No time target is set.
- **How fluency is built with no timer.** Short sets every day, mixed with older work, marked at once. A
  student records how many were right on the first try and compares only with their own last count. A teacher
  or parent may run a short paper sprint on work already taught, never graded or posted. No tool shows a clock.

## 10. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sum, regroup, estimate, and the place names, each on the worked sum at first
  use. Families may say "carry"; both words are welcome. Frame: "__ ones is __ ten and __ ones."
- **Access.** Grid paper with large squares; numbers read aloud; a keypad or typed columns for a student who
  cannot write small digits.
- **Extension, same standard.** Three addends. Sums with a missing digit in each addend. "Find two five-digit
  numbers whose sum needs exactly four carries."

## 11. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Do day 72 at the table: one sum, two ways, side by side. Have the child say each
  carry by its value. Estimate together before each sum. Six sums a day are enough if they are right; then
  stop explaining and keep the practice short.
- **One projector.** One sum on the screen; the class works on paper a column at a time and holds up answers.
- **Family note.** "Your child is adding numbers up to a million the way you probably learned. Ask what the
  little 1 at the top is worth."
