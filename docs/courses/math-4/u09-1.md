# Unit 9.1: The standard algorithm for adding

**Decided, Cory, 2026-11-11: unit 9.1 on days 72 to 73 and unit 9.2 on days 74 to 77. The spine says the same.**

Grade 4 math. Stretch 9, Adding and Subtracting Multi-Digit Numbers (days 72 to 77): see [s09.md](s09.md).
**Days 72 to 73.** Days by kind: understanding 1, fluency 1, use 0, check 0. A plan, not a
lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md), a second
draft whose fields wait for Cory's yes.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NBT.B.4, this unit's share.** Students **add**. Subtracting is unit 9.2. "Fluently" is begun here and
  carried by the warm-up to day 150.
- **Limits that bite here.** Whole numbers; sums under 1,000,000.
- **Connections.** Rounding to estimate a sum (4.NBT.A.3): day 73, before every sum. Reading numbers by their
  comma groups (4.NBT.A.2): day 73, saying both numbers before lining them up.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** The standard algorithm, by name. Fluent means accurate and reasonably fast,
  with a method that is understood, and never with blocks.
- **Practices.** MP.6, be precise: day 72, saying what each small 1 is worth. MP.8, use what repeats: day 73,
  ten of any unit make one of the next, in every column.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why the standard algorithm is expanded form written short: each
  column is one place, and ten of a unit are written as one of the next unit, in the next column.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Expanded form written beside the standard algorithm, under place names. On day 72 the teacher
  shows both and students do two sums both ways: the day the model is tied to the written method. Expanded
  form is withdrawn at the start of day 73, and the place names before E73. No blocks.
- **Key moment 1, day 72: a carry is ten of one unit written as one of the next.** Ideal: paper, two methods
  side by side.
- **Key moment 2, day 73: many sums with quick feedback, then none.** Ideal: a light column workspace that
  marks each column at first, then only whole answers, and counts how many were right on the first try, with
  no clock. **Road Trip Logbook (spec only) does not fit:** every trip sum is grade 3 size (386 + 457) and
  every evening includes subtracting from 2,000, which is unit 9.2's work. Asked for, in place of an
  adding-only tool: one shared column-practice template with settings (add, subtract or both; number of
  digits; marks by column or by answer; a right-first-try count). It would also carry the warm-up to day 150.
  Until then, paper with a key. The unit runs on print, so it has no key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Sums copied from the board; lined paper turned sideways.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Where are the 14 ones in the standard algorithm?" (day 72). "Without adding, how do you
  know 6,285 + 947 is not 15,755?" (day 73). "Why do expanded form and the standard algorithm give the same
  sum?" (day 73).
- Written task, day 73: "Tell a third grader, in two sentences, what the small 1 means."
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: names the unit (a ten, a hundred); says ten of one unit were
  traded for one of the next. This field is thin for a procedure unit. One short task is honest.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 72: adding and subtracting within 1,000, as since day 1, and times tables. Day 73: four- and
  five-digit sums, and one-digit numbers times multiples of 10. Subtracting stays within 1,000 until unit 9.2
  has taught it.
- **Fluency target** (ours, untested): by day 77, at least 4 of 5 sums of four to six digits right on the
  first try, unaided. No time target is set.
- **How fluency is built with no timer.** Short sets every day, mixed with older work, marked at once. A
  student records how many were right on the first try and compares only with their own last count. A teacher
  or parent may run a short paper sprint on work already taught, never graded or posted. No tool shows a clock.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sum, regroup, estimate, and the place names, each on the worked sum at first
  use. Families may say "carry"; both words are welcome. Frame: "__ ones is __ ten and __ ones."
- **Access.** Grid paper with large squares; numbers read aloud; a keypad or typed columns for a student who
  cannot write small digits.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions on large-square grid paper; numbers read aloud; a scribe or typed
  columns; the "worth" answer may be said aloud.
- **Reading load.** Very low: numbers and one short sentence. The written task is two sentences and may be
  dictated.
- **Extension, same standard.** Three addends. Sums with a missing digit in each addend. "Find two five-digit
  numbers whose sum needs exactly four carries."

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Do day 72 at the table: one sum, two ways, side by side. Have the child say each
  carry by its value. Estimate together before each sum. Six sums a day are enough if they are right; then
  stop explaining and keep the practice short.
- **One projector.** One sum on the screen; the class works on paper a column at a time and holds up answers.
