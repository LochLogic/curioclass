# Unit 11.1: Area and perimeter formulas (the pilot unit)

Grade 4 math. Stretch 11, which is this one unit. **Days 89 to 93.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 1,
use 2. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.MD.A.3** (supporting work): "Apply the area and perimeter formulas for rectangles in real world and
  mathematical problems. For example, find the width of a rectangular room given the area of the flooring and
  the length, by viewing the area formula as a multiplication equation with an unknown factor."
- **This unit's share.** All of it, with small numbers. To **apply** is to use a formula with meaning.
- **The major work it practises.** Unknown factors and factor pairs (4.OA.B.4), multi-digit addition
  (4.NBT.B.4), like-denominator fractions (4.NF.B.3), "times as long" (4.OA.A.1).
- **Limits, and the numbers rule.** Rectangles only. Areas are products within 100, or a one-digit number
  times a multiple of 10. An unknown side from an area is a factor-pair problem. Perimeter may use multi-digit
  addition and like-denominator fractions. This plan adds: with multi-digit sides, 2 × (l + w) is found by
  adding the sum to itself; an unknown side from a perimeter uses a perimeter of 100 or less.
- **Practices.** MP.4, model a real situation: day 92, the dog pen. MP.1, make sense: day 90, the notes.

## 2. Where it sits

- **Builds on (strong handoff).** 3.MD.C.7a to d (area as rows times columns), 3.MD.D.8 (perimeter, an unknown
  side), 3.OA.A.4 (unknown factor).
- **Leads to.** Grade 5: volume (5.MD.C.5), fractional sides (5.NF.B.4b).
- **Comes back, and must.** Area with larger sides returns in stretch 12 (a 24 by 36 floor as four
  partial products), stretch 13 (area 1,248, width 6: how long?), stretch 14, and Checks 3 and 4.
  Without that return the standard is half taught.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E89.** "A rectangle is 8 m long and 3 m wide. Find the area and the perimeter, with units." 24 square m;
  22 m. Wrong: 11 (B); swapped (A); units swapped (E).
- **E90.** With devices: the tool's four items done alone, shown by score code. On paper: "A poster's area
  is 56 square inches. It is 7 inches wide. How long is it? Write the equation." "A frame's perimeter is 30
  inches. It is 9 inches long. How wide?" 7 × ? = 56, 8 inches; 6 inches. Wrong: 49 (C); 21 or 12 (D).
- **E91.** "(a) A hall is 40 feet long and 6 feet wide. Area? (b) A park is 235 yards long and 148 yards wide.
  Perimeter?" 240 square feet; 766 yards. Wrong: 24 or 2,400 (a zero slip); 92 (A); 383 (B).
- **E92.** "A patio is 4 yards wide. Its length is 3 times its width. How many yards of edging go around it?
  How many square yards of tile cover it?" 32 yards; 48 square yards. Wrong: length 7 ("3 more", stretch 10);
  swapped (A).

## 4. Expected errors

(P): from the progression page. The rest: teaching notes, from memory or the tool spec.

| | The error | What it shows | Wrong example beside a right one; day | If many made it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Uses one formula for the other measure | The words have no pictures yet | An 8 by 3 rectangle with "area 22". Day 89 | Sort ten situations: "covers" or "goes around" |
| B | Adds one length and one width (P) | The edge is not seen going all the way round | "9 and 4, so the perimeter is 13". Day 89 | Trace and name four sides; write l + w + l + w |
| C | Subtracts or adds to find a side from an area | The formula is not seen as a multiplication | "48 square feet, 6 wide, so 42 long". Day 90 | Rows of 6 on grid paper until the squares are used |
| D | From a perimeter, takes off one side, or does not halve | One pair of sides is counted | "40 feet of fence, 12 long, so 28 wide". Day 90 | Fill in 12 + __ + 12 + __ = 40 |
| E | Wrong unit: "40 feet" for an area | Squares and lengths are not told apart | A label to fix. Day 91 | Say "square" with every area |

## 5. Readiness

Day 89, ten minutes, all written; items from the progression page.

1. 3.MD.C.7a: "A rectangle is 6 units by 7 units. Find its area. Write the multiplication." 6 × 7 = 42.
2. 3.MD.D.8: a rectangle with two sides labelled 9 cm and 4 cm. "Find the perimeter." 26 cm.
3. 3.OA.A.4: "8 × ? = 48". 6.
4. 3.MD.D.8: "On grid paper, draw two rectangles with perimeter 12 and different areas." 1 by 5 and 2 by 4.

- **Gap on 1.** Ten minutes: draw the rows on grid paper and count them. Tile the Floor (grade 3, spec only).
- **Gap on 2 or 4.** Ten minutes: trace and name all four sides. Fence Builder (grade 3, spec only).
- **Gap on 3.** Fact help in warm-ups only (the spine's rule).

## 6. Model and instruments

- **Carried.** The rectangle with its unit squares. Grid paper and tiles are in hand on day 89 and tied to the
  letters that day: A = l × w, and P = l + w + l + w = 2 × (l + w). The grid is withdrawn on day 91: a
  rectangle is then a sketch with labelled sides.
- **Key moment 1, day 89: each formula is a count.** Ideal: grid paper, tiles, a finger on the edge.
- **Key moment 2, day 90: one number is fixed and a side is missing.** Ideal: each student builds the
  rectangle from the fixed number (48 squares in rows of 6), writes the equation, then works with the pieces
  gone. A class set of tiles is too much to ask, so a tool is right. **Community Plot Planner (spec) fits, and is the key tool.** Gaps in its spec: no worked example
  comes first; its 30 by 20 lot breaks the numbers rule if a student must find its area; every note names its
  measure, so the student never chooses; whole feet and real-world notes only. Paper carries the rest.
- **Key moment 3, day 92: same fence, different room.** Ideal: a 36-unit string loop on grid paper.
- **Supplies.** Per student: grid paper, about 20 tiles or paper squares, a string loop. With nothing:
  hand-ruled grids.

## 8. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Is 2 × (l + w) always the same as l + w + l + w? How do you know?" (day 89). "Two
  rectangles have the same perimeter. Must they have the same area?" (day 92).
- Written task, day 92: "Which pen did you choose, and why? Use the words area and perimeter."
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: names the sides; shows the perimeter is 36; compares areas; gives
  a reason.

## 9. Practice that returns

Warm-up: multi-digit adding and subtracting; times tables, with the second facts
check on days 89 and 90; fractions twice. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none.

## 10. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: area, perimeter, length, width, square unit, formula, each on a labelled
  rectangle. "Covers" and "goes around" are cue words at first; later problems use neither. Frame: "The
  missing side is __ because __ × __ = __."
- **Access.** Large-square grid paper; tiles and string for touch; problems read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Every rectangle with area 60 and whole-number sides, with its perimeter.

## 11. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Measure a real table or rug in whole feet and find both measures. Then: "This rug
  is 24 square feet and 4 feet wide. How long?" Build it from paper squares.
- **One projector.** Day 90: the teacher runs the tool; for each note the class writes the missing side and
  the equation on paper before the squares are laid.
- **Family note.** "Area covers. Perimeter goes around. Ask your child for both measures of your table."

## 12. Not in this unit

Areas with larger sides (stretches 12 to 14). L-shaped figures (grade 3). Fraction sides in area (grade 5).
Still to make: the generator sheet, the class record sheet, sample answers. The key tool is a spec, not built. No working teacher has read this unit.
