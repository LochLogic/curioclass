# Unit 3.3: Comparing with a common denominator or numerator

Grade 4 math. Stretch 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 17
to 20.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1, use 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built
or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.2, this unit's share.** Students **compare** "two fractions with different numerators and
  different denominators" by "creating common denominators or numerators", and **record** "with symbols >,
  =, or <". Benchmarks and the same whole belong to unit 3.4. Every day here still asks "how do you know?".
- **Limits that bite here.** Fractions given have denominators from the grade's list. A common denominator
  found while working may fall outside it (15, 20, 24, 30, 40), and so may a renamed fraction (6/9). No
  least common denominator is asked (Progressions, PDF page 150 (144)).
- **Connections.** 4.NF.A.1: every day. Multiples (4.OA.B.4): day 18.
- **Practices.** MP.7, use structure: day 18, the product of the two denominators always works. MP.3, build
  an argument: day 19, "which parts are bigger, and how do you know?"
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why parts of one size are compared by count, and equal counts by
  the size of the part. Why any common multiple works, and the least is not needed.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check item owned:** 3.3a, given on day 24 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E17.** "Rename one fraction in each pair so the parts are the same size. Then write >, = or <. (a) 5/12
  __ 1/2 (b) 3/2 __ 11/8." Answer (a) 5/12 < 6/12, so <; (b) 12/8 > 11/8, so >. Wrong: (a) > or (b) < (D);
  1/2 = 1/12 (A); right renaming, sign turned round (G).
- **E18.** "Compare 3/4 and 7/10. Rename both so the parts are the same size. Write the comparison with >, =
  or <." Answer 15/20 > 14/20, so 3/4 > 7/10; fortieths are also right. Wrong: 3/4 < 7/10 (D); 3/20 and
  7/20 (A); sign turned round (G).
- **E19.** "(a) 5/12 __ 5/8. (b) Make the numerators the same, then compare: 2/3 __ 6/10. Which parts are
  bigger, and how do you know?" Answer (a) <; (b) 2/3 = 6/9, ninths are bigger than tenths because the whole
  is cut into fewer parts, so 2/3 > 6/10. Wrong: (a) > or (b) < (D); 2/3 = 6/3 (A).
- **E20.** No line given. "Write >, = or <. Use any way, and show the fractions you renamed. (a) 2/3 __ 5/8
  (b) 6/12 __ 3/5 (c) 4/10 __ 40/100." Answer (a) 16/24 > 15/24, so >; (b) 6/12 < 6/10, so <; (c) =. Wrong:
  (a) <, (b) > or (c) < (D); signs turned round (G). Results go to unit 3.4.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line, now as the proof. On day 17 both fractions are marked on one printed line
  and the sign is written beside it: the day the drawing is tied to the written comparison. Printed on days
  18 and 19. Withdrawn on day 20: none is given, and a student may sketch one to justify. Written form:
  2/3 = 8/12 and 8/12 > 7/12, so 2/3 > 7/12.
- **Key moment 1, days 17 and 18: counts can be compared only when the parts are the same size.** Ideal: one
  printed line for each pair; the student cuts, marks both points and writes the sign. Paper fits. Fraction
  Wall Duel (spec only) does not fit: it has a wall and no number line; the wall stops at 1, and day 17
  compares 5/4 with 9/8; its "Same pieces" step takes the renamed fractions first and shows the split
  after, which is practice, not showing why.
- **Key moment 2, day 20: choosing between the two ways, with feedback on each try.** Ideal: a light
  practice set that gives a pair up to 2, takes the sign, the way and the renamed fractions, then shows both
  on a line. The Duel's compare round is this loop, so it partly fits, and it is not used: every deck deals
  Half line rounds and traps or claims, which are unit 3.4's work, and one listed pair uses sevenths (5/8
  and 3/7). With a setting for the reasons in play, deck 1 would serve day 20 as a light tool. Until then,
  paper with a key. The unit has no key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Lines ruled by hand; pairs copied from the board; the key read aloud.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Why can't we just compare the 2 and the 7 in 2/3 and 7/12?" (day 17). "Is 15 the only
  number of parts that works for thirds and fifths?" (day 18). "Both have 4 parts. Which parts are longer,
  tenths or twelfths?" (day 19).
- Written task, day 20: "Compare 1/4 and 2/10 two ways. Write the sign. Which way was quicker, and why?"
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: each renaming is right (5/20 and 4/20; 2/8 and 2/10); both
  ways give 1/4 > 2/10; a reason for the choice.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Grade 3 maintenance; common multiples (stretch 1) on days 17 and 18; missing-number equal
  fractions (unit 3.2) on days 19 and 20.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none. Day 20 uses short sets marked at once. No clock.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: greater than, less than, common denominator, common numerator, each beside a
  marked line. The signs are read as words every time. Frame, to take or leave: "__ and __ are both __ths
  now, and __ is more than __, so __ > __."
- **Access.** Large-print lines; sign cards to place instead of writing; a comparison may be said aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud, signs included; a scribe for the renamed fractions.
- **Reading load.** Low: fractions and short sentences. The claims in field 4 are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Find a fraction between 2/3 and 3/4. Prove it."

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A ruler: which is more, 3/4 inch or 5/8 inch? Find both marks, then rename and
  check. Six pairs a day are enough if the child says the reason each time.
- **One projector.** One pair on the screen; each student writes the renamed fractions and holds up a sign
  card before the line is shown.
