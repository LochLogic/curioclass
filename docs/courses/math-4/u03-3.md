# Unit 3.3: Comparing to one half and one; the same whole

Grade 4 math. Stretch 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 17
to 18.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 0, use 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built
or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

Decided, Cory, 2026-11-11: benchmarks and the same whole are taught before common denominators. This unit was
3.4 (days 21 to 24) and is now 3.3; its choose-a-way day (23) and the check day (24) went to unit 3.4.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.2, this unit's share.** Students **compare** "by comparing to a benchmark fraction such as 1/2",
  and to 1; **recognize** "that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole";
  and **justify** "the conclusions, e.g., by using a visual fraction model".
- **Limits that bite here.** Denominators from the grade's list. Benchmarks 1/2 and 1 only. From day 17
  every story says whether the whole is the same, or leaves it out on purpose.
- **What this unit may not assume.** No common denominator or common numerator has been taught. A fraction
  is set against 1/2 by a name for 1/2 in its own parts (unit 3.2: 1/2 = 4/8, so 3/8 is less), by half of
  its denominator (half of 3 thirds is one and a half thirds, so 2/3 is more), or on the printed line. Two
  fractions on the same side of 1/2 are not compared here, except two that are each one part short of 1.
- **Connections.** 4.NF.A.1: day 17, names for 1/2. 3.NF.A.3c: day 18, 1 as 6/6.
- **Practices.** MP.3, build an argument: day 17, "does that show 1/4 > 1/3?". MP.7, use structure: day 18,
  a fraction one part short of 1 is short by a unit fraction.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why fractions of different wholes cannot be compared: on the line
  the whole is the length from 0 to 1, and it is fixed. Why "one part short of 1" is settled by the size of
  the missing part.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check items owned:** 3.3a and 3.3b, given on day 24 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E17.** "(a) Sam drank 1/3 of a small bottle of water. Tia drank 1/4 of a large bottle. Tia drank more
  water. Does that show 1/4 > 1/3? Explain in one sentence. (b) Write >, = or <: 3/8 __ 1/2. 2/3 __ 1/2. So
  3/8 __ 2/3." Answer (a) no: the bottles are different wholes; (b) <, >, <. Wrong: (a) "yes" (H); (b) 3/8
  > 1/2 or 3/8 > 2/3 (D).
- **E18.** "(a) 9/10 __ 7/6. How do you know without renaming? (b) 7/8 and 11/12 are each one part short of
  1. Which is closer to 1, and why? So 7/8 __ 11/12." Answer (a) <: 9/10 is less than 1 and 7/6 is more;
  (b) 11/12, because a twelfth is smaller than an eighth; <. Wrong: (a) > (D); (b) = (I); > "because eighths
  are bigger" (J: looks at the parts kept, not the part missing). Results go to unit 3.4.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line from 0 to 2, with 1/2 and 1 marked. On day 17 "more than half" becomes "to
  the right of 1/2", and the comparison is written under the line: the day the benchmark is tied to the
  written form (2/5 < 1/2 and 1/2 < 5/8, so 2/5 < 5/8). Printed on day 18. It is withdrawn in unit 3.4, on
  day 22: benchmark comparisons come back in the warm-ups of days 19 to 23, with the printed line through
  day 21 and with none from day 22. A sketched line is always one way to justify.
- **Key moment 1, day 17: a fraction is of a whole.** Ideal: two real wholes of different size that the
  student halves and lays together. Two paper strips fit. No tool is needed.
- **Key moment 2, days 17 and 18: a benchmark is a point.** Ideal: a printed line from 0 to 2 with 1/2 and 1
  labelled by several names. Paper fits. The unit runs on print, so it has no key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Two strips torn from scrap paper; a line ruled by hand with 1/2 and 1 marked.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Half of this strip and half of that one: are they the same length? Are they both one
  half?" (day 17). "5/6 and 7/8 are each one part short of 1. Are they equal?" (day 18).
- Written task, day 18 (new 2026-11-11; the old task needed common denominators and went to unit 3.4): "Dee
  says 3/4 and 5/6 are the same distance from 1, because each is one part short. Is Dee right? Mark both on
  the line and explain."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: names the missing parts, 1/4 and 1/6; says a sixth is smaller
  than a fourth, so 5/6 is closer to 1; writes 3/4 < 5/6.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Grade 3 maintenance; one missing-number equal fraction (unit 3.2) each day, a name for 1/2
  among them on day 17.
- **Returns later.** One benchmark comparison in the warm-ups of days 19 to 23 (unit 3.4).
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: benchmark, the same whole, closer to, each on the marked line or the two
  strips. Frame, to take or leave: "__ is less than 1/2 and __ is more than 1/2, so __ < __."
- **Access.** Wide strips; a large-print line.
- **Check accommodations.** E17 and E18 and items 3.3a and 3.3b read aloud; a scribe or an oral answer for
  any explanation.
- **Reading load.** Middling: E17's story is three sentences, read aloud once. The claims in field 4 are
  read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Put 5/12, 9/8 and 3/5 in order and say which benchmark you used."

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Fold a small and a large sheet of paper in half: which half is bigger, and are
  both one half? Then a ruler: is 3/8 inch more or less than half an inch? Ask for the reason each time.
- **One projector.** The two strips under the camera, then one line on the screen; each student writes
  "less than 1/2", "more than 1/2" or the sign on paper before the point is marked.
