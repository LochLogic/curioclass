# Unit 10.1: Times as many

Grade 4 math. Stretch 10, Multiplicative Comparison (days 80 to 88): see [s10.md](s10.md). **Days 80 to 82.**
Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 1, use 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.OA.A.1, all of it.** Students **interpret** a multiplication equation as a comparison ("35 is 5 times as
  many as 7 and 7 times as many as 5") and **represent** "verbal statements of multiplicative comparisons as
  multiplication equations".
- **Limits that bite here.** The stretch's numbers rule (proposed, waiting for Cory's yes). "Times as many"
  for things counted; "times as much", "as long", "as tall" for measures. Never "times more than". No word
  problems to solve yet.
- **Connections.** Equal groups (3.OA.A.1): day 80. The commutative property (3.OA.B.5): day 81. Ten times
  (4.NBT.A.1, stretch 8): day 82.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** Neither: this is a meaning. Facts are recalled.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 81, saying what each number in 12 = 3 × 4 counts in the strip picture. MP.4: day
  82, writing the equation for a sentence.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How a comparison differs from grade 3's equal groups: two amounts,
  one measured in copies of the other. Why one equation reads two ways. Why "times more than" is avoided
  (Progressions).

## 2. Where it sits

After Check 2 and its respond day (78 to 79). It uses readiness item 1. Unit 10.2 follows on day 83 and needs
what E82 shows: a student who cannot write the equation for a sentence cannot yet write one for a story.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check items owned:** 1 and 2, given on day 88 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E80.** "Draw a strip for 2 cubes. Under it, draw a strip that shows 5 times as many cubes. How many cubes
  is that? Finish: __ is __ times as many as __." Answer: five copies of the 2-strip; 10; "10 is 5 times as
  many as 2". Wrong: 7 (A); 12, or six copies drawn (D); "2 is 5 times as many as 10" (C).
- **E81.** "12 = 3 × 4. Write the two comparisons this equation shows. Draw a strip picture for one of them."
  Answer: 12 is 3 times as many as 4; 12 is 4 times as many as 3; three copies of a 4-strip, or four of a
  3-strip. Wrong: "12 is 3 more than 4" (A); one sentence only; a picture of 3 and 4 with no copies (A).
- **E82.** "Write a multiplication equation for each. (1) 14 is 2 times as many as 7. (2) 10 times as many as
  6 is 60. (3) Kai's rope is 16 feet long. That is 2 times as long as Lu's 8-foot rope." Answers: 14 = 2 × 7;
  10 × 6 = 60; 16 = 2 × 8. Wrong: 2 + 7 = 9, or 14 = 2 + 7 (A); 7 = 2 × 14 (C); 16 + 8 or 16 × 2 in (3) (B).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The strip picture. Day 80: cubes snapped into a strip, then drawn; cubes are put away that
  day. Day 81: the strip is tied to the equation. Day 82: drawn only when a sentence is hard.
- **Key moment 1, day 80: "4 times as many" is four copies of the other amount, not four added.** Ideal:
  cubes in the student's hands, then drawn. No tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 81: one equation, two comparisons.** Ideal: paper. An array of 12 squares cut into 3
  strips of 4, then 4 strips of 3.
- **Key moment 3, days 81 and 82: many sentences and equations, marked at once.** Ideal: a light card set.
  **Growth Spurt (key, spec only), part Read it both ways: partly fits** day 81, as practice with feedback.
  Gaps: it has three cards, too few for day 82, which runs on paper; the part must open by itself; it needs
  cards that go from sentence to equation, with 10 times and with measures.
- **A room with nothing.** Squares drawn on paper in place of cubes.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Where is the 4 in your cubes? Where is the 3?" (day 80). "Why does 12 = 3 × 4 tell us
  two things?" (day 81). "A ten is how many times as much as a one?" (day 82).
- Written task, day 82: "Jo says 15 is 5 times as many as 3. Draw a picture and write an equation to show
  whether Jo is right."
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: five equal copies of 3; the equation 15 = 5 × 3.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Subtracting and adding with the standard algorithm; times tables every day; fractions or
  decimals twice a week; one-digit numbers times multiples of 10.
- **Fluency target.** The standard sets none. Ours, untested: by day 82, at least 5 of 6 sentences turned
  into right equations on the first try. No time target.
- **How fluency is built with no timer.** Short mixed sets on day 82 and in the warm-ups of days 83 to 86,
  marked at once; a student compares only with their own last count.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** This unit is mostly language. The frame "__ is __ times as many as __" stays on the
  wall all stretch, over a strip picture. "Times" also means occasions ("three times a day"); say so on day
  80. A student may say the comparison in a home language first.
- **Access.** Cubes that snap; grid paper; sentences read aloud; frames to fill, not copy.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud; E80 may be built with cubes; answers may be said aloud.
- **Reading load.** Low but exact: one phrase carries the meaning.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Write every comparison that 24 can show." Sentences with 10 times a
  two-digit number.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use beans, coins or bricks. You set out 3; the child sets out 4 times as many, then
  4 more, and says which is which. Swap roles. Ten sentences on day 82 are enough if they are right.
- **One projector.** The teacher builds under a camera or draws strips; the class draws and holds up
  equations. The day 81 cards are read aloud and answered on paper.
