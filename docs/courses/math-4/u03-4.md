# Unit 3.4: Comparing to one half and one; the same whole

Grade 4 math. Stretch 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 21
to 24.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 0, use 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built
or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.2, this unit's share.** Students **compare** "by comparing to a benchmark fraction such as 1/2",
  and to 1; **recognize** "that comparisons are valid only when the two fractions refer to the same whole";
  and **justify** "the conclusions, e.g., by using a visual fraction model".
- **Limits that bite here.** Denominators from the grade's list. Benchmarks 1/2 and 1 only. From day 21
  every story says whether the whole is the same, or leaves it out on purpose.
- **Connections.** 4.NF.A.1: day 21, names for 1/2. 3.NF.A.3c: day 22, 1 as 6/6. Unit 3.3's two ways: day 23.
- **Practices.** MP.3, build an argument: day 23. MP.7, use structure: day 22, a fraction one part short of
  1 is short by a unit fraction.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why fractions of different wholes cannot be compared: on the line
  the whole is the length from 0 to 1, and it is fixed. Why "one part short of 1" is settled by the size of
  the missing part.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check items owned:** 3.4a and 3.4b, given on day 24 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E21.** "(a) Sam drank 1/3 of a small bottle of water. Tia drank 1/4 of a large bottle. Tia drank more
  water. Does that show 1/4 > 1/3? Explain in one sentence. (b) Write >, = or <: 3/8 __ 1/2. 2/3 __ 1/2. So
  3/8 __ 2/3." Answer (a) no: the bottles are different wholes; (b) <, >, <. Wrong: (a) "yes" (H); (b) 3/8
  > 1/2 or 3/8 > 2/3 (D).
- **E22.** "(a) 9/10 __ 7/6. How do you know without renaming? (b) 7/8 and 11/12 are each one part short of
  1. Which is closer to 1, and why? So 7/8 __ 11/12." Answer (a) <: 9/10 is less than 1 and 7/6 is more;
  (b) 11/12, because a twelfth is smaller than an eighth; <. Wrong: (a) > (D); (b) = (I); > "because eighths
  are bigger" (J: looks at the parts kept, not the part missing).
- **E23.** No line given. "Ana and Ben walk the same trail. Ana has walked 2/5 of it. Ben has walked 3/8 of
  it. Who has walked farther? Write the comparison with >, = or <, and show or tell how you know." Answer
  Ana: 2/5 > 3/8 (16/40 > 15/40, or 6/15 > 6/16). Wrong: Ben (D); "cannot tell" (takes the same-whole rule
  too far: the trail is one whole); sign turned round (G).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line from 0 to 2, with 1/2 and 1 marked. On day 21 "more than half" becomes "to
  the right of 1/2", and the comparison is written under the line: the day the benchmark is tied to the
  written form (2/5 < 1/2 and 1/2 < 5/8, so 2/5 < 5/8). Printed on day 22. Withdrawn on day 23: none is
  given, and a sketched line is one way to justify.
- **Key moment 1, day 21: a fraction is of a whole.** Ideal: two real wholes of different size that the
  student halves and lays together. Two paper strips fit. No tool is needed.
- **Key moment 2, days 21 and 22: a benchmark is a point.** Ideal: a printed line from 0 to 2 with 1/2 and 1
  labelled by several names. Paper fits.
- **Key moment 3, day 23: choosing a way and proving it, try after try.** Ideal: a tool that hides the sizes
  until the student has given a sign and a reason, turns away a reason that does not fit the pair, has the
  student carry it out, then shows both fractions on a line from 0 to 2; in some rounds the wholes differ.
  Print cannot answer the choice of a reason on each try. **Fraction Wall Duel (spec only) partly fits, and
  is the key tool for this one day.** Its gaps are the eight asks on the stretch sheet (field 8). Its 36
  four-step rounds in "20-30" minutes are untested, so this plan takes one deck in 25 (ours, untested). If
  the asks stay open, day 23 runs on its paper path and the tool is a light tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Day 23: pairs on cards, a partner holds the key; two trap cards read aloud.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Half of this strip and half of that one: are they the same length? Are they both one
  half?" (day 21). "5/6 and 7/8 are each one part short of 1. Are they equal?" (day 22). "Which way did you
  pick for this pair, and why not another?" (day 23).
- Written task, day 23: "Which is greater, 5/6 or 9/10? Prove it two ways. One way may be a number line."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: 5/6 < 9/10, with the sign; each way is complete; says, or
  shows on one line, that both are of the same whole.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Grade 3 maintenance; one missing-number equal fraction and one pair to compare (units 3.2 and
  3.3) on days 21 to 23.
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: benchmark, the same whole, closer to, each on the marked line or the two
  strips. Frame, to take or leave: "__ is less than 1/2 and __ is more than 1/2, so __ < __."
- **Access.** Wide strips; a large-print line; the tool's own access (tap or keyboard, Read on every card).
- **Check accommodations.** E21 to E23 and items 3.4a and 3.4b read aloud; a scribe or an oral answer for
  any explanation.
- **Reading load.** The heaviest of the stretch: day 23's stories and the tool's cards. Stories are two or
  three sentences, read aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Put 5/12, 9/8 and 3/5 in order and say which benchmark you used." Also the
  respond half's task for a student who missed nothing.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Fold a small and a large sheet of paper in half: which half is bigger, and are
  both one half? On day 23 the child plays the deck; sit beside them for the reasons. On the last day give
  the seven items, go over the missed ones together, and give stretch 4's readiness check the next morning.
- **One projector.** Day 23: the teacher plays one deck on the screen. Every student writes the sign and the
  reason on paper before the bars fill, and holds up "cannot compare" for a trap.
