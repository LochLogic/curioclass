# Section 3.4: Comparing with a common denominator or numerator

Grade 4 math. Unit 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 19
to 24.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1, application 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built
or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

has six days, one more than the scope and sequence's usual five, because the assessment day closes the unit.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.2, this section's share.** Students **compare** "two fractions with different numerators and
  different denominators" by "creating common denominators or numerators", **record** "with symbols >, =, or
  <", and on day 23 choose a way and **justify** it. Benchmarks and the same whole were section 3.3. Every day
  here still asks "how do you know?".
- **Limits that bite here.** Fractions given have denominators from the grade's list. A common denominator
  found while working may fall outside it (15, 20, 24, 30, 40), and so may a renamed fraction (6/9). No
  least common denominator is asked (Progressions, PDF page 150 (144)).
- **What this section may assume.** Section 3.3's benchmarks, 1/2 and 1, and the same whole. They are used as a
  check on a renamed comparison (days 20 and 22: "one is under a half and one is over, so the sign is
  right") and as one of the ways to choose from on day 23. Every story on days 19 to 22 says the whole is the same; day 23 mixes in stories where
  it is not, so students must check.
- **Connections.** 4.NF.A.1: every day. Multiples (4.OA.B.4): day 20.
- **Practices.** MP.7, use structure: day 20, the product of the two denominators always works. MP.3, build
  an argument: day 21, "which parts are bigger, and how do you know?", and day 23.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why parts of one size are compared by count, and equal counts by
  the size of the part. Why any common multiple works, and the least is not needed.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line, now as the proof. On day 19 both fractions are marked on one printed line
  and the sign is written beside it: the day the drawing is tied to the written comparison. Printed on days
  20 and 21. Withdrawn on day 22: none is given, there or on day 23, and a student may sketch one to
  justify. Section 3.3's line from 0 to 2 with 1/2 and 1 marked is withdrawn on day 22 as well. Written form:
  2/3 = 8/12 and 8/12 > 7/12, so 2/3 > 7/12.
- **Key moment 1, days 19 and 20: counts can be compared only when the parts are the same size.** Ideal: one
  printed line for each pair; the student cuts, marks both points and writes the sign. Paper fits. Fraction
  Wall Duel (spec only) does not fit: it has a wall and no number line; the wall stops at 1, and day 19
  compares 5/4 with 9/8; its "Same pieces" step shows the split after the renamed fractions are given.
- **Key moment 2, day 22: choosing between the two ways, with feedback on each try.** Ideal: a light
  practice set that gives a pair up to 2, takes the sign, the way and the renamed fractions, then shows both
  on a line. It does not exist. Paper with a key.
- **Key moment 3, day 23: choosing among all the ways and proving it, try after try.** Ideal: a tool that
  hides the sizes until the student has given a sign and a reason, turns away a reason that does not fit the
  pair, has the student carry it out, then shows both fractions on a line from 0 to 2; in some rounds the
  wholes differ. Print cannot answer the choice of a reason on each try. **Fraction Wall Duel (spec only) is
  a light tool for now (decided, Cory, 2026-11-11; follow up after more progress), and day 23 runs on
  paper.** Its gaps are in [TOOL-ASKS.md](TOOL-ASKS.md). The section has no key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Lines ruled by hand; pairs copied from the board; the key read aloud. Day 23:
  pairs on cards, a partner holds the key; two trap cards read aloud.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: greater than, less than, common denominator, common numerator, each beside a
  marked line. The signs are read as words every time. Frame, to take or leave: "__ and __ are both __ths
  now, and __ is more than __, so __ > __."
- **Access.** Large-print lines; sign cards to place instead of writing; a comparison may be said aloud.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions and item 3.4a read aloud, signs included; a scribe for the
  renamed fractions, and a scribe or an oral answer for any explanation.
- **Reading load.** Low on days 19 to 22: fractions and short sentences; the claims in field 4 are read
  aloud. Day 23 is the heaviest of the unit: its stories are two or three sentences, read aloud once.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Find a fraction between 2/3 and 3/4. Prove it." Also the respond half's
  task for a student who missed nothing.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** A ruler: which is more, 3/4 inch or 5/8 inch? Find both marks, then rename and
  check. Six pairs a day are enough if the child says the reason each time. On day 23 the child takes the
  cards and you hold the key. On the last day give the seven items, go over the missed ones together, and
  give unit 4's readiness check the next morning.
- **One projector.** One pair on the screen; each student writes the renamed fractions and holds up a sign
  card before the line is shown. Day 23: one card at a time on the screen; every student writes the sign
  and the reason on paper, and holds up "cannot compare" for a trap.
