# Unit 10.2: Comparison word problems: times as many or more than?

Grade 4 math. Stretch 10, Multiplicative Comparison (days 80 to 88): see [s10.md](s10.md). **Days 83 to 86.**
Days by kind: understanding 1, fluency 0, use 3, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.OA.A.2, all of it.** Students **multiply or divide to solve** "word problems involving multiplicative
  comparison", "by using drawings and equations with a symbol for the unknown number", **distinguishing**
  "multiplicative comparison from additive comparison".
- **Limits that bite here.** One step. The stretch's numbers rule (proposed, waiting for Cory's yes):
  dividing stays inside the one-digit facts. "Times as many" and "times as much" only.
- **Connections.** Unknown-factor equations (3.OA.A.4): day 84, 4 × b = 32 and b = 32 ÷ 4 say the same thing.
  "How many more" problems (2.OA.A.1): day 83.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** Neither. One routine: read, draw two strips, put the letter on the unknown
  part, write the equation, solve, answer in a sentence, reread the story with the answer in it.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 83, saying what the 2 means in "2 more" and in "2 times as many". MP.4: day 84,
  writing the equation from the strip picture.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The three places the unknown can sit (the standards' glossary,
  Table 2): the larger amount (multiply), the smaller amount (divide), the number of times (divide). Why the
  word "times" does not tell you to multiply. How the two strip pictures differ.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check items owned:** 3, 4, 5 and 6, given on day 88 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E83.** "Ty has 4 toy cars. Mo has 2 more toy cars than Ty. Di has 2 times as many toy cars as Ty. Draw a
  strip picture for Mo and one for Di. How many cars does each have?" Answer: Mo 6 (a 4-strip and a 2-piece);
  Di 8 (two 4-strips). Wrong: both 6 or both 8, or swapped (A); Di 12 (D).
- **E84.** "A big tank holds 21 fish. That is 3 times as many as the small tank holds. How many fish does the
  small tank hold? Draw strips, write an equation with a letter, and solve." Answer: 3 × s = 21, or
  s = 21 ÷ 3; 7 fish. Wrong: 63 (B); 18 (A); 24 (A, added).
- **E85.** "Pat read 20 pages. Jon read 5 pages. Pat read how many times as many pages as Jon? Write an
  equation with a letter, and solve." Answer: n × 5 = 20, or n = 20 ÷ 5; 4 times. Wrong: 15 (A); 100 (B);
  "Jon read 4 times as many" (C).
- **E86.** "A pumpkin weighs 24 pounds. That is 4 times as much as a melon weighs. A squash weighs 4 pounds
  more than the melon. How much does the melon weigh? How much does the squash weigh?" Answer: melon 6
  pounds, squash 10 pounds. Wrong: melon 96 (B) or 20 (A); squash 24 (A, the other way round); melon only.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The strip picture, with a letter on the unknown part; tied to the letter equation on day 84.
  From day 86 only a student who is stuck draws.
- **Key moment 1, day 83: the same number means different things in "2 more" and "2 times as many", and the
  gap grows as the first amount grows.** Ideal: two amounts the student can stretch, with a switch between
  the two kinds of comparison, so many cases are seen in minutes. Print cannot do that. **Growth Spurt (key,
  spec only), part Growing: fits.** Job: show why. Gap: the part must open by itself.
- **Key moment 2, days 84 and 85: the unknown moves.** Ideal: paper. One story three times, the unknown in
  each of the three places, drawn one under another.
- **Key moment 3, day 86: telling which kind a problem is.** Ideal: a strip builder that marks each step.
  **Growth Spurt, part Which one?: partly fits.** Job: practise with feedback. Eight problems, three of them
  additive, all inside the numbers rule; the child builds every strip. Gaps: it asks for the kind before the
  strip, and the plan draws first; only one problem has the number of times unknown.
- **Not used:** Tray jobs, Orders and On your own (unit 10.3, field 5; stretch field 8).
- **A room with nothing.** Day 83: a table on the board, starting amounts 1, 2, 5 and 10, a column for "3
  more" and one for "3 times as many". Day 86: sort cards.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "In which story does the 2 tell you how many copies?" (day 83). "If Bo had 10 stamps,
  which gives Cy more: 3 more, or 3 times as many? What if Bo had 1?" (day 83). "The problem says 'times'. Do
  we multiply? How do you know?" (day 84). "Who has more?" (every day).
- Discussion, day 85: two strip pictures for one problem, one with every copy drawn and one long strip
  marked "5 times"; the class connects each to the equation.
- Written task, day 86: "Write one story where the answer is 2 × 9 and one where the answer is 2 + 9. Use the
  same two children in both."
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: the first story says "times as many" or "times as much"; the
  second says "more than".

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Sentences and equations from unit 10.1; times tables and their division facts; subtracting
  and adding with the standard algorithm; on days 85 and 86, one grade 3 two-step problem (for unit 10.3).
- **Fluency target.** None: the standard sets none and this is a use unit, so nothing here is timed. The
  facts it leans on are in the warm-up; the second facts check is on days 89 and 90.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** The hard part is the sentence. Words: as many as, as much as, more than, each on a
  strip picture. Short sentences, one fact in each, no idioms. "That is 3 times as many as..." is taught as a
  pattern on day 84. Students retell each problem, in a home language if they wish, before drawing.
- **Access.** Problems read aloud; paper strips to move; a routine card.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud, twice if asked; strips may be built from paper; the
  answer may be said aloud.
- **Reading load.** The highest in the stretch: two to four sentences a problem, with short names and plain
  contexts.
- **Extension, same standard.** Three amounts in one story. Comparisons with 10 times and with a one-digit
  number times a multiple of 10. Writing a problem for a given strip picture.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Tell stories about the child's own things: "You have 4 cars. I have 2 more. Now I
  have 2 times as many." Draw both. On days 84 and 85 keep one story and hide a different number each time.
  Ask "who has more?" before any working.
- **One projector.** Day 83: the teacher drags the plant; the class predicts the other height on paper before
  each move. Day 86: one problem on the screen; the class draws on paper and one student places the letter.
