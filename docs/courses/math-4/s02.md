## 2. Standards, limits, practices and what is carried

<!-- gen:parts (from data/plans/math-4; do not edit here) -->
| Code | Part | Verb | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.OA.C.5 | (a) | generate | a number pattern that follows a given rule |
| 4.OA.C.5 | (b) | generate | a shape pattern that follows a given rule |
| 4.OA.C.5 | (c) | identify | features of the pattern that were not in the rule itself |
| 4.OA.C.5 | (d) | explain | informally why a feature will keep going (the standard's own example: "explain informally why the numbers will continue to alternate") |
<!-- /gen -->

- **4.OA.C.5, all of it.** Every part has an item on the end-of-unit assessment.
- **Limits.** The rule is always given: the standards "do not require students to infer or guess the
  underlying rule" (Progressions). No rule written with a variable. Reasons are informal: words, dots or a
  drawing. No "100th shape" questions, which need remainders (unit 13). No two patterns compared, no
  graph (grade 5). Ours, untested: rules add or multiply by a whole number, terms stay under 1,000, and the
  farthest step asked for is the 10th. One exception: a repeating shape pattern may ask for a step that is
  a multiple of its length, such as the 30th shape of a pattern of three, which needs no remainder.
- **Connections.** 4.OA.B.4 on days 7 and 8: the features students notice are multiples, even and odd.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** The standard sets none.
- **Practices.** MP.8, use what repeats: day 7, the same jump every time is why a feature repeats. MP.3,
  build and judge a reason: day 8's written task and unit assessment item 3. MP.7 is used on day 8 (a step seen
  as "the first shape and so many jumps").
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why odd plus even is odd and odd plus odd is even: an odd number
  is pairs and one left over. Why adding 5 makes the ones digits take turns: two jumps of 5 are one jump of
  10. Why three more examples are not a reason. Why grade 4 never asks students to find the rule.
- **Carried.** The pattern as a numbered list, the rule drawn as a jump between neighbours. On day 7, dot
  pairs show even and odd, and are tied that day to the sentence "odd plus even is odd". On day 8, squares
  and sticks are in hand, and each shape is tied that day to its count in the list. All of it is withdrawn
  on day 9.

## U7. Talk and writing

## U9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: rule, pattern, term, feature, odd, even, take turns. "Feature" is hard:
  say "something true about the numbers". Frames: "I notice __." "It will keep going because __."
- **Access.** Large counters; a student who cannot draw directs a partner.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions read aloud; reasons may be said aloud or given to a scribe.
- **Reading load.** Medium on days 7 and 8: reasons are written. They may be dictated.
- **Extension, same standard.** "Start at any number and add 6. When are all the numbers even? All odd?
  Can they ever take turns? Why?" Then: "Make a rule whose numbers all end in 3 or 8."

## U10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Say a rule: "start at 3, add 4". The child writes eight numbers. Ask "what do you
  notice that I did not say?" and wait. Then "will it keep going? How do you know?" If the child gives more
  numbers, pair up coins to show why. Next day build the stick squares with toothpicks. On the last day
  give the six items, mark them together, then give unit 3's readiness items.
- **One projector.** One tape on the screen; every student writes the next three terms and one feature
  before the teacher cranks.
