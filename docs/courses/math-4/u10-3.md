# Unit 10.3: Two-step problems: a letter for the unknown, an estimate to check

Grade 4 math. Stretch 10, Multiplicative Comparison (days 80 to 88): see [s10.md](s10.md). **Days 87 to 88.**
Days by kind: understanding 0, fluency 0, use 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.OA.A.3, this unit's share.** Students **solve** word problems of two steps "posed with whole numbers and
  having whole-number answers", **represent** them "using equations with a letter standing for the unknown
  quantity", and **assess** "the reasonableness of answers using mental computation and estimation strategies
  including rounding". The rest of the standard (more steps, all four operations with larger numbers,
  "problems in which remainders must be interpreted") is unit 13.3 and stretch 14.
- **Limits that bite here.** Two steps; no remainders (spine). The stretch's numbers rule (proposed, waiting
  for Cory's yes): the multiplying or dividing step is a one-digit fact or a one-digit number times a multiple
  of 10; the adding or subtracting step stays within 1,000. **Also proposed, waiting for Cory's yes:**
  problems are chosen so that one equation needs no parentheses (multiply or divide first, then add or
  subtract). A student may always write two equations. The progression page puts working with grouping
  symbols in grade 5.
- **Connections.** Comparison problems (4.OA.A.2): half of day 87's problems have a "times as many" step.
  Rounding (4.NBT.A.3): the estimate. Order of operations with no parentheses (grade 3): the wrong example
  for error H.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** Neither. The routine of unit 10.2 with two additions: name what the letter
  stands for before writing the equation, and write an estimate beside the answer.
- **Practices.** MP.4: day 87, one equation for a two-step story. MP.2: day 87, the estimate, and saying
  what each part of the equation stands for in the story.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why 10 + 3 × 3 is 19 and not 39. Two right ways to write a
  two-step problem: one equation, or two short ones with the letter in the second. What a good-enough
  estimate is: close enough to catch an answer that is double or ten times too big, not a second exact
  answer.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check items owned:** 7 and 8, given on day 88 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss E87, day 89's warm-up opens with error F's wrong example.

- **E87.** "Pencils come in boxes of 30. A school buys 3 boxes and hands out 52 pencils. How many pencils are
  left? Write one equation with a letter. Solve it. Then write an estimate that shows your answer makes
  sense." Answer: p = 3 × 30 − 52; 38 pencils; about 90 − 50 = 40. Wrong: 90 (F); 142 (adds the 52); 38 with
  no letter, or a letter never named (G); no estimate, or "38 is about 40" with no working from the story.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The strip picture, when a step is a comparison; otherwise a quick sketch of the story. The
  letter is written on the picture before the equation. Nothing is withdrawn: stretch 14 picks this up.
- **Key moment 1, day 87: one equation can hold both steps, and the letter stands for the thing asked.**
  Ideal: paper. Two short equations written first, then joined into one, for the same story.
- **Key moment 2, day 87: an estimate catches a wrong answer.** Ideal: paper and talk. Three finished
  answers, one far too big, judged by estimating before anyone computes.
- **Growth Spurt (key, spec only), part Orders: does not fit.** It has the right shape (an estimate box
  first, then an equation with a letter), but its four orders use 3 × 14, 4 × 18 and 96 ÷ 6, which are outside
  the numbers rule before day 94, and one has a remainder. Rewritten inside the rule it would do day 87's
  practice with feedback. Its Tray jobs part is remainder work and belongs to stretches 13 and 14. The unit
  runs on print.
- **A room with nothing.** Problems on the board, one at a time; lined paper.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "What does your letter stand for?" "Which part of your equation is Omar's money?" "Is 80
  the answer to the question that was asked?" "Could the answer be more than 1,000? How do you know without
  working it out?" (all day 87).
- Written task, day 87: "Mia says the answer to a problem is 12. Her estimate was 120. What should Mia do
  next, and why?"
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: says the two do not agree, so one is wrong; says to check both
  the working and the estimate, not just pick one.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 87: two comparison sentences turned into equations, times tables, one difference with the
  standard algorithm. Day 88: none; the check starts at once. Two-step problems return in the warm-up twice a
  week until stretch 14.
- **Fluency target.** None: the standard sets none.
- **How fluency is built with no timer.** Not for this unit.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: in all, left, each, estimate, about. "Left" and "in all" are shown on a
  sketch, not taught as signals for an operation. Students retell the story and the question before writing.
- **Access.** Problems read aloud; two equations allowed; a routine card.
- **Check accommodations.** E87 read aloud, twice if asked; the estimate and the letter's meaning may be said
  aloud; a scribe.
- **Reading load.** High for one period: three or four sentences a problem. Six problems, not twelve.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a two-step story for n = 6 × 20 − 45. A problem with one fact that is
  not needed.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Three problems are enough. For each: the child tells the story back, says the
  question, says what the letter will stand for, and guesses "about how many" before working. Compare the
  guess with the answer every time.
- **One projector.** One problem on the screen. The class writes the equation on paper and holds it up; two
  different right equations are put side by side and the class says how each matches the story.
