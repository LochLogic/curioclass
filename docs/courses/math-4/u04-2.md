## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.B.3a and 3d, this section's share.** Students "understand addition and subtraction of fractions as
  joining and separating parts referring to the same whole" (part b), both directions and the same-whole
  condition; and "solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions referring to the same
  whole and having like denominators" (part g), with fractions under and past 1 and no mixed-number work yet.
- **Limits that bite here.** Like denominators only. Sums past 1 are left as one fraction or written as 1 and
  a part, either right. No mixed-number subtraction (section 4.3).
- **Connections.** 3.NF.A.2 (the number line), day 29. 2.OA.B.2 (facts within 20): the numerators. 4.NF.A.2
  (unit 3, the same whole), day 30. Day 28's missing parts are day 30's counting up.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 29, "five eighths and two eighths" said before it is written. MP.7: day 30, 1 seen as
  12/12 before 1 is taken from 17/12. MP.1: day 31, a story read twice, the tape drawn before any equation.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Adding like fractions joins counts of one part size, so the
  numerators add and the part size stays; subtracting takes a count away, or counts up from the part taken to
  the whole amount (the same difference, found two ways). Both only make sense when the parts come from one
  whole, which is why 1/2 of a small cup and 1/2 of a big cup are not "one cup". Adding and subtracting undo
  each other, so a difference is checked by adding.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line. Day 29 ties a hop of one part size to each count in the equation (the day the
  line is tied to adding); day 30 hops back, and counts up, for subtracting. The tape diagram enters on day 31
  for stories. From day 29 each day's student page prints its own lines in the day's part size: section 4.1's
  line pages have no eighths and their lines are already marked, so they stay in the folder as a record.
- **Key moment 1, days 29 and 30: joining keeps the part size; separating takes a count away.** Ideal: a line
  in the chosen part size where the student draws the hops, then writes. Paper does it. No tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 30: parts of different wholes.** Ideal: two strips of different length, each in
  fourths, laid together. Each student makes them from the envelope: one blank whole strip, and a second folded in
  half and cut on the fold; both halves go back in the envelope for days 31 and 37 and day 38's group E. Recipe
  Scaler (spec only), the mug and pan rounds: fits as a light tool, since the unequal parts are shown side by
  side after the student commits and finishes a sentence. Two rounds only (rows s04-T30-1 and 2), so the strips
  carry the rest. Asks: TOOL-ASKS.md, Recipe Scaler.
- **A room with nothing.** Lines ruled by hand; two paper strips of different length.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "When we join two eighths and three eighths, why do we not get sixteenths?" (day 29).
  "You hopped back and you counted up. Why did both give the same answer?" and "Can half a small cup and half a
  big cup make one cup? What would have to be true?" (day 30). "How do you know whether to add or subtract
  before you write a number?" (day 31).
- Written task, day 30: "Explain why 7/10 − 2/10 is not 5/0." Look-fors: the part size is tenths and stays
  tenths; seven parts take away two parts leave five; 5/10. Handed back on day 31, 3 minutes.
- Judged by the teacher with those look-fors; the sample answers are in the row's `samples` cell (items.csv).
- E30 (b): the key holds yes or no; the why is read with one look-for, "names two different wholes".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: join, separate, difference, count up, the same whole. Frame: "__ parts of size __
  and __ parts of size __ make __ parts of size __." Stories on day 31 are read aloud first.
- **Access.** Lines in large print with wide gaps; hops may be counted aloud while a helper draws.
- **Check accommodations.** As in DEFAULTS.md. E30 (b) and E31 are read aloud.
- **Reading load.** Moderate on day 31: six stories of two sentences each; the teacher reads each once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a story whose answer is 4/6 by subtracting, and one by adding.
