# Section 4.2: Joining and separating like parts

Grade 4 math. Unit 4, Adding and Subtracting Fractions (days 25 to 38): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 29 to 31.**
Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 0, application 1, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.B.3a and 3d, this section's share.** Students "understand addition and subtraction of fractions as
  joining and separating parts referring to the same whole" (part b), both directions and the same-whole
  condition; and "solve word problems involving addition and subtraction of fractions referring to the same
  whole and having like denominators" (part g), with fractions under and past 1 and no mixed numbers yet.
- **Limits that bite here.** Like denominators only. Sums past 1 are left as one fraction or written as 1 and
  a part, either right. No mixed-number subtraction (section 4.3).
- **Connections.** 3.NF.A.2 (the number line), day 29. 2.OA.B.2 (facts within 20): the numerators. 4.NF.A.2
  (unit 3, the same whole), day 30.
- **Practices.** MP.2: day 29, "five eighths and two eighths" said before it is written. MP.1, make sense and
  keep going: day 31, a story read twice, the tape drawn before any equation.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Adding like fractions joins counts of one part size, so the
  numerators add and the part size stays; subtracting takes a count away. Both only make sense when the
  parts come from one whole, which is why 1/2 of a small cup and 1/2 of a big cup are not "one cup". Adding and
  subtracting undo each other, so a difference is checked by adding.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line. Day 29 ties a hop of one part size to each count in the equation (the day the
  line is tied to adding); day 30 hops back for subtracting. The tape diagram enters on day 31 for stories.
  Pieces stay withdrawn; the line is drawn, not printed, from day 31.
- **Key moment 1, days 29 and 30: joining keeps the part size; separating takes a count away.** Ideal: a line
  in the chosen part size where the student draws the hops, then writes. Paper does it. No tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 30: parts of different wholes.** Ideal: two strips of different length, each in
  fourths, laid together. Recipe Scaler (spec only), the mug and pan rounds: fits as a light tool, since
  the unequal parts are shown side by side after the student commits and finishes a sentence. Two rounds only,
  so the strips carry the rest. Asks: TOOL-ASKS.md, Recipe Scaler.
- **A room with nothing.** Lines ruled by hand; two paper strips of different length.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "When we join two eighths and three eighths, why do we not get sixteenths?" (day 29).
  "Can half a small cup and half a big cup make one cup? What would have to be true?" (day 30).
- Written task, day 30: "Explain why 7/10 − 2/10 is not 5/0." Look-fors: the part size is tenths and stays
  tenths; seven parts take away two parts leave five; 5/10.
- Judged by the teacher with those look-fors; the sample answers are in the row's `samples` cell (items.csv).

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Facts within 20 (days 29 and 30); day 31, a fraction as a sum (section 4.1).
- **Fluency target.** None; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: join, separate, difference, the same whole. Frame: "__ parts of size __ and __
  parts of size __ make __ parts of size __." Stories on day 31 are read aloud first.
- **Access.** Lines in large print with wide gaps; hops may be counted aloud while a helper draws.
- **Check accommodations.** As in DEFAULTS.md. E30 (b) and E31 are read aloud.
- **Reading load.** Moderate on day 31: six stories of two sentences each; the teacher reads each once.
- **Extension, same standard.** Write a story whose answer is 4/6 by subtracting, and one by adding.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a ruler marked in eighths as the line: hop 3/8 and then 2/8 more. Measure two
  cups of water to show half a small cup and half a big cup.
- **One projector.** Hops drawn on the board line; students write each sum before the hops are shown.
