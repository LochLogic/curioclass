# Unit 9.2: The standard algorithm for subtracting

**Decided, Cory, 2026-11-11: unit 9.1 on days 72 to 73 and unit 9.2 on days 74 to 77. The spine says the same.**

Grade 4 math. Stretch 9, Adding and Subtracting Multi-Digit Numbers (days 72 to 77): see [s09.md](s09.md).
**Days 74 to 77.** Days by kind: understanding 2, fluency 1, use 0, check 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing
here is built or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NBT.B.4, this unit's share.** Students **subtract**, "using the standard algorithm". "Fluently" is
  begun here and carried by the warm-up from day 78 to day 150.
- **Limits that bite here.** Whole numbers to 1,000,000; the answer is never below zero.
- **Connections.** Adding (unit 9.1): from day 75 every difference is checked by adding it to the number
  taken away. Rounding to estimate (4.NBT.A.3): day 75, before each difference. Expanded form (4.NBT.A.2):
  days 74 and 76.
- **Strategies or algorithm.** The standard algorithm, by name. A student may ungroup everything first and
  then subtract, or ungroup column by column; both are the standard algorithm (Progressions). Fluent means
  accurate and reasonably fast, with a method that is understood, and never with blocks.
- **Practices.** MP.6, be precise: day 74, saying what each ungrouped unit became. MP.8, use what repeats:
  day 76, one of any unit is ten of the next, even when the next place holds a zero.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Ungrouping is carrying run backwards: one of a unit is rewritten as
  ten of the next smaller unit, and the top number keeps its value. Why 4 hundreds, 0 tens, 2 ones is the
  same number as 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 12 ones. Why adding the answer to the number taken away is a full check.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Expanded form beside the standard algorithm, under place names. Tied on day 74: two
  differences done both ways. Withdrawn at the start of day 75; back for ten minutes on day 76, for zeros
  only. Place names come down before E75. No blocks.
- **Key moment 1, day 74: ungrouping rewrites the top number and does not change it.** Ideal: paper, two
  methods side by side.
- **Key moment 2, day 76: ungrouping across zeros.** Ideal: paper. The top number is renamed in one line
  before any column is subtracted.
- **Key moment 3, days 75 and 77: many differences with quick feedback, then none.** Ideal: the shared
  column-practice template asked for in unit 9.1, set to subtract or both. Asked for, not specced; until
  then, paper with a key.
- **Road Trip Logbook (light, spec only): partly fits, on day 77 only.** Its Trip ledger part (twelve mixed
  sums and differences to six digits, no clock, a right-first-try count) fits the mixed ten minutes: practise
  with feedback. It does not fit days 74 to 76: every trip problem is grade 3 size. Gaps: the ledger cannot
  be opened without the five-day trip; it marks whole answers only; twelve problems are too many for ten
  minutes. The unit runs on print, so it has no key tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Differences copied from the board; lined paper turned sideways.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Where did the 12 ones come from, and what happened to the 7 tens?" (day 74). "Without
  subtracting, how do you know 45,206 − 3,874 is not 6,466?" (day 75). "Why do the two zeros in 6,005 turn into
  9s and not 10s?" (day 76).
- Written task, day 76: "Tell a friend, in two sentences, why the zeros in 6,005 become 9s."
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: says one thousand was ungrouped; says each place passed one on
  and kept nine. Thin, as in unit 9.1: one short task is honest for a procedure unit.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Warm-up.** Day 74: sums of four to six digits, and times tables. Day 75: differences with one ungrouping.
  Day 76: differences with several ungroupings, and one-digit numbers times multiples of 10. Day 77: the mixed
  set is the warm-up. Subtracting with the standard algorithm stays in the warm-up from day 78 to day 150.
- **Fluency target** (ours, untested): by day 77, at least 4 of 5 differences of four to six digits right on
  the first try, unaided, one of them across zeros. No time target is set.
- **How fluency is built with no timer.** As in unit 9.1: short sets every day, mixed with older work, marked
  at once; a student compares a right-first-try count only with their own last count. No tool shows a clock.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: difference, ungroup, check, and the place names, on the worked example at
  first use. Families may say "borrow" or "regroup"; all are welcome. Frame: "1 __ is 10 __."
- **Access.** Large-square grid paper; numbers read aloud; room above the top number; typed columns for a
  student who cannot write small digits.
- **Check accommodations.** The same, plus a scribe; the "what did they become" answer may be said aloud.
- **Reading load.** Very low. The written task may be dictated.
- **Extension, same standard.** Differences with a missing digit in each number. "Subtract from 1,000,000 by
  subtracting from 999,999 and adding 1. Why does that work?"

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Do day 74 at the table: one difference, two ways. Ask what each crossed-out digit
  became. On day 76 say the renamed number together before writing. Six differences a day are enough if they
  are right, each checked by adding.
- **One projector.** One difference on the screen; the class works on paper a column at a time and holds up
  answers.
