# Unit 3.1: Same amount, different parts

Grade 4 math. Stretch 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24). **Days 10 to 12.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 0, use 0. A plan, not a lesson:
nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.1** (major work): "Explain why a fraction a/b is equivalent to a fraction (n × a)/(n × b) by using
  visual fraction models, with attention to how the number and size of the parts differ even though the two
  fractions themselves are the same size. Use this principle to recognize and generate equivalent fractions."
- **This unit's share.** The visual half. Students **explain** with strips and number lines why two names are
  the same amount, and **recognize** and **generate** equal fractions by cutting parts. The rule
  (n × a)/(n × b), and generating without a drawing, belong to unit 3.2.
- **Limits.** Fractions given in tasks have denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12; hundredths wait for unit
  3.2. No simplest form. Equivalence is explained by cutting each part into n smaller parts, never by
  "multiplying by 1".
- **Practices.** MP.3, build an argument: day 12, the written task. MP.7, use structure: day 11, every part
  is cut the same way.

## 2. Where it sits

- **Builds on (strong handoff).** 3.NF.A.1, 3.NF.A.2, 3.NF.A.3a to d: a/b as a parts of size 1/b, fractions on
  a number line, simple equivalents such as 1/2 = 2/4, with denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, 8. Fifths, tenths and
  twelfths are new. This year: multiples (stretch 1).
- **Leads to.** Unit 3.2 (the rule), units 3.3 and 3.4 (comparing), stretches 4 to 7. Grade 5: equal fractions
  made on purpose to add unlike denominators (5.NF.A.1).
- **Comes back.** Warm-ups twice a week from day 62; quarter and half turns in stretch 16; Checks 1 to 4.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E10.** "Shade 2/3 of the strip. Cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the new name: 2/3 = __/__." Answer
  4/6. Wrong: 2/6 or 4/3 (A); 4/5 (B).
- **E11.** "The line from 0 to 1 is marked in fourths. Put a point at 3/4. Cut each fourth into 2 equal parts.
  What is the point's new name? Did the point move?" Answer 6/8; no. Wrong: 5/8 or 7/8 (C); 3/8 (A); "yes, it
  moved" (a new name is taken for a new number).
- **E12.** No strips. "Is 3/4 the same amount as 5/6? Draw a number line to show. Then write one fraction that
  is the same amount as 3/4." Answer: no; 6/8 or 9/12. Wrong: "yes", or writes 4/5 (B). Results go to unit 3.2.

Not owned here: items on the rule or on comparing, and cumulative Check 1.

## 5. Readiness

Day 10, ten minutes; items from the progression page (W written, T the teacher listens).

1. W, 3.NF.A.2: "Place 3/4 and 5/4 on the number line." The line is marked 0, 1, 2.
2. W, 3.NF.A.3d: "Circle the larger: 1/3 or 1/8. Shade a strip for each."
3. T, 3.NF.A.3d: two pizzas of different sizes are drawn. "Is half of one equal to half of the other? Why?"
4. W, 3.NF.A.3c: "Write 3 as a fraction. Write 1 as fourths." 3/1 and 4/4.

- **Gap on 1 or 4.** Ten minutes before day 11: mark off a one-fourth strip from 0, counting "one fourth, two
  fourths" to "five fourths"; four fourths lands on 1. Earlier tool: Fraction Trail (grade 3, spec only).
- **Items 2 and 3** serve comparing. Their results go on the class record sheet for unit 3.3; no reteach here.
- No item tests 3.NF.A.3b (1/2 = 2/4). The teacher watches for it at day 10's first fold.

## 6. Model and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line, with paper strips. Strips are in hand on day 10. On day 11 the strip is laid
  on the line and its end is the point: the day the object is tied to the drawing. Strips are withdrawn on day
  12, except to check; E12 has none. The written form is the equation with its sentence: "each part was cut
  into 2, so 2/3 = 4/6." The drawn line stays into unit 3.2, which must name the day it is dropped.
- **Key moment 1, days 10 and 11: the same length gets a new name when every part is cut.** Ideal: paper
  strips the student folds, then a printed number line.
- **Key moment 2, day 12: cuts that cannot be folded (into 3, 5 or 10) and fractions past 1.** Ideal: a strip
  above a number line where the student picks n, writes the new name first, then watches every part split
  while the point stays. No spec does this. Fraction Wall Duel (spec) partly fits: its blank-card rounds do
  this job, but inside a comparing game (units 3.3 and 3.4), on a wall that stops at 1, with no number line.
  The unit runs on print, so it has no key tool.
- **Supplies.** Per student: a strip sheet (equal strips, halves to twelfths), scissors, an envelope, two
  pages of blank number lines. A room with nothing: strips cut from scrap paper, lines ruled by hand.

## 8. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" (day
  11). "Jo says 3/4 = 5/6 because she added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" (day 12). "Can
  1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" (day 12).
- Written task, day 12: "Explain why 3/4 and 6/8 are the same amount. You may draw."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: says how each part was cut; says there are more parts and each
  is smaller; says the length or the point is the same. Sample answers are still to be written.

## 9. Practice that returns

Warm-up: adding and subtracting within 1,000 and the times tables (grade 3 maintenance), with multiples from
stretch 1 on day 12, since unit 3.2 leans on them. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none.

## 10. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: equivalent, numerator, denominator, each on a labelled strip. Fractions are
  read "three fourths", not "three over four". Frame, to take or leave: "Each part
  was cut into __, so there are __ times as many parts and each part is smaller. The length is the same, so
  __ = __."
- **Access.** Pre-cut, wide strips; large-print number lines; cuts may be ruled, not folded.
- **Extension, same standard.** Every name for 1/2 with the grade's denominators. "Can 2/3 be written as
  tenths? Why not?"

## 11. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Cut strips from one sheet of paper. A ruler marked in eighths is a real number line:
  find 1/2 inch, 2/4 and 4/8 at one mark. Ask the three questions in field 8 and wait for the reason. If you
  know the multiplying rule, hold it back; unit 3.2 gets there.
- **One projector.** Strips under the camera; students write each new name before the cut is shown.
- **Family note.** "This week one amount gets many names. Ask your child to fold a paper strip and show you
  why 1/2 and 2/4 are the same."

## 12. Not in this unit

The rule, and generating without a drawing (3.2). Comparing (3.3, 3.4). Simplest form (not required in grade
4). Hundredths. Still to make: the strip sheet, number line pages, class record sheet, sample
answers. The splitter has no spec.
