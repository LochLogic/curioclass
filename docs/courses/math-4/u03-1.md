# Unit 3.1: Same amount, different parts

Grade 4 math. Stretch 3, Equivalent Fractions and Comparing (days 10 to 24): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 10
to 12.** Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 0, use 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built
or tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md), a second draft whose fields wait for
Cory's yes.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.A.1, this unit's share.** The visual half. Students **explain** with strips and number lines why two
  names are the same amount, and **recognize** and **generate** equal fractions by cutting parts. The rule
  (n × a)/(n × b), and generating without a drawing, belong to unit 3.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Denominators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12; hundredths wait for unit 3.2. No simplest
  form. Never "multiplying by 1".
- **Connections.** Multiples (4.OA.B.4, stretch 1): the day 12 warm-up, since unit 3.2 leans on them.
- **Practices.** MP.3, build an argument: day 12, the written task. MP.7, use structure: day 11, every part
  is cut the same way.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Why cutting each part into n multiplies both numbers by n: there
  are n times as many parts in the whole and n times as many in the shaded piece, each one nth the size, so
  the length does not change.

## 3. Checks, written first

**End-check items owned:** 3.1a and 3.1b, given on day 24 (stretch field 5).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.

- **E10.** "Shade 2/3 of the strip. Cut each third into 2 equal parts. Write the new name: 2/3 = __/__." Answer
  4/6. Wrong: 2/6 or 4/3 (A); 4/5 (B).
- **E11.** "The line from 0 to 1 is marked in fourths. Put a point at 3/4. Cut each fourth into 2 equal parts.
  What is the point's new name? Did the point move?" Answer 6/8; no. Wrong: 5/8 or 7/8 (C); 3/8 (A); "yes, it
  moved" (a new name is taken for a new number).
- **E12.** No strips. "The line from 0 to 1 is marked in twelfths. Mark 2/3 and 4/5 as well as you can. Are
  they the same amount? Then write another name for 2/3." Answer: no; 2/3 is at 8/12 and 4/5 falls between
  9/12 and 10/12; 4/6 or 8/12. Wrong: "yes" (B). Results go to unit 3.2.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The number line, with paper strips. Strips are in hand on day 10. On day 11 the strip is laid
  on the line and its end is the point: the day the object is tied to the drawing. Strips are withdrawn on day
  12, except to check; E12 has none. The written form is the equation with its sentence: "each part was cut
  into 2, so 2/3 = 4/6."
- **Key moment 1, days 10 and 11: the same length gets a new name when every part is cut.** Ideal: paper
  strips the student folds, then a printed number line. Fractions past 1 (5/4, 3/2) are done here, on the
  line.
- **Key moment 2, day 12: cuts that cannot be folded (into 3, 5 or 10).** Ideal: printed number lines the
  student marks by hand. Fraction Wall Duel (spec only) partly fits: its blank-card rounds do the splitting,
  but inside a comparing game, on a wall that stops at 1, with no number line. A separate splitter tool is
  not asked for. Worth having later, as a mode: the wall's split tool set over a number line from 0 to 2,
  where the student picks n, writes the new name, then sees the cut. The unit runs on print, so it has no key
  tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Strips cut from scrap paper, lines ruled by hand.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "When we cut each fourth into two parts, what changed and what stayed the same?" (day
  11). "Jo says 3/4 = 5/6 because she added 2 to the top and the bottom. Do the lines agree?" (day 12). "Can
  1/2 be written as tenths? As thirds? How do you know?" (day 12).
- Written task, day 12: "Explain why 3/4 and 6/8 are the same amount. You may draw."
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: says how each part was cut; says there are more parts and each
  is smaller; says the length or the point is the same.

## 8. Practice that returns

Warm-up: adding and subtracting within 1,000 and the times tables (grade 3 maintenance), with multiples from
stretch 1 on day 12. Fluency target: none; the standard sets none.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: equivalent, numerator, denominator, each on a labelled strip. Fractions are
  read "three fourths", not "three over four". Frame, to take or leave: "Each part was cut into __, so there
  are __ times as many parts and each part is smaller. The length is the same, so __ = __."
- **Access.** Pre-cut, wide strips; large-print number lines; cuts may be ruled, not folded.
- **Check accommodations.** Exit questions may be read aloud; E12's line is printed large; an answer may be
  given aloud while pointing.
- **Reading load.** Low. Exit questions are one to three short sentences. The day 12 claims (Jo, Kai) are
  read aloud by the teacher.
- **Extension, same standard.** Every name for 1/2 with the grade's denominators. "Can 2/3 be written as
  tenths? Why not?"

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Cut strips from one sheet of paper. A ruler marked in eighths is a real number line:
  find 1/2 inch, 2/4 and 4/8 at one mark. Ask the three questions in field 7 and wait for the reason. If you
  know the multiplying rule, hold it back; unit 3.2 gets there.
- **One projector.** Strips under the camera; students write each new name before the cut is shown.
