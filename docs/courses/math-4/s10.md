# Stretch 10: Multiplicative Comparison

Grade 4 math, major work. **Days 80 to 88.** Units: [10.1 Times as many](u10-1.md) (80 to 82); [10.2
Comparison word problems: times as many or more than?](u10-2.md) (83 to 86); [10.3 Two-step problems: a letter
for the unknown, an estimate to check](u10-3.md) (87 to 88). Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1, use 4,
check 1, as the spine has them. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields
follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **4.OA.A.1.** Parts, by verb: (a) **interpret** a multiplication equation as a comparison; (b) **represent**
  a comparison statement as a multiplication equation.
- **4.OA.A.2.** (c) **multiply** to solve a comparison word problem; (d) **divide** to solve one, where the
  smaller amount is unknown (d1) or the number of times is unknown (d2); (e) **distinguish** "times as many"
  from "more than".
- **4.OA.A.3, this stretch's share.** (f) **solve** two-step word problems; (g) **represent** them with an
  equation with a letter; (h) **assess** whether an answer is reasonable, by estimating. Remainders, and
  problems of more than two steps, wait for stretches 13 and 14.
- **Limits.** Whole numbers and whole-number answers; two steps; no remainders (spine). "Times as many" and
  "times as much" only. "Times more than" is talked about once, on day 83, and is never in a check
  (Progressions). "One third as much" is grade 5.
- **The numbers rule. Decided, Cory, 2026-11-11, and now in the spine** (the spine was silent, and multi-digit multiplying
  starts on day 94). Comparing uses only: products of two one-digit numbers and the matching divisions; a
  one-digit number times a multiple of 10 up to 90, multiplying only; and 10 times a one- or two-digit number,
  multiplying only. The adding or subtracting step in a two-step problem stays within 1,000. Money is in whole
  dollars or whole cents; measures are whole units with no conversion.
- **Carried.** The strip picture (tape diagram): one strip for the smaller amount, and a row of equal copies
  of it for the larger. Cubes or counters on day 80 only. The strip is tied to the equation on day 81 and to
  the letter on day 84. It is not withdrawn in this stretch: from day 86 a student draws it only when stuck,
  and may draw it in the end check.

## 3. Where it sits

- **Builds on (weak handoff).** 3.OA.A.1 and 3.OA.A.3 (equal groups and arrays), 3.OA.D.8 (two-step problems
  with a letter). Grade 3 has no "times as many" problems; the nearest base is grade 2 "how many more"
  (2.OA.A.1). So the readiness check matters here (progression page).
- **Leads to.** Stretches 12 to 15. Grade 5: multiplying as scaling (5.NF.B.5), writing expressions (5.OA.A.2).
- **Comes back.** Word problems in stretches 12 and 13; "times as long" on day 92; unit conversions in
  stretch 15; 4.OA.A.3 in full in stretch 14; Checks 3 and 4.

- **Item 7.** Look-fors: one equation, or two joined steps, with a letter that stands for all the beads; both
  steps done; 100. Meets (ours, untested): "b = 20 + 4 × 20. 4 × 20 = 80. 20 + 80 = 100. b = 100 beads." Also
  meets: "4 × 20 = 80, 80 + 20 = b, b = 100." Does not: "4 × 20 = 80 beads."
- **Item 8.** Look-fors: rounds or uses easy numbers; compares the estimate with 175; finds 67. Meets: "7
  times 7 is almost 50. 50 and 20 is 70. 175 is more than double that. s = 67." Does not: "Ravi is wrong, it
  is 67." If many gave no estimate: an estimate is written before every warm-up problem for a week.
- **Accommodations.** Items read aloud, more than once if asked; large print; a scribe; items 1, 7 and 8 may
  be answered aloud; cubes are not offered, a drawn strip is.

## 6. Error register

"IM": the open course in Sources, unit 5, section A. "Prog.": the Progressions, PDF page (printed page).

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Reads "times as many" as "more than": 5 times as many as 3 is 8 | Comparing still means adding on, as in grade 2 | IM lessons 1 and 2; Prog. p. 44 (38) |
| B | Multiplies because the problem says "times", when the smaller amount or the number of times is unknown | A key word is followed in place of the situation | IM lesson 3 |
| C | Swaps the roles: says the smaller amount is so many times as many as the larger | Lost track of which number is the multiplier | IM lesson 3 |
| D | Draws the first amount and then that many more copies, or counts both amounts together | "Times as many" taken as copies added on top | Partly IM lesson 2; the rest from memory |
| E | First idea, not an error: "3 times more than 5" could mean 15 or 20 | The phrase is unclear in everyday English | Prog. p. 44 (38) |
| F | Stops after one step | Solves the part that was easy to see | IM lesson 5 |
| G | Gives an answer with no equation, or a letter that stands for nothing named | The equation is seen as extra, not as the plan | From memory |

## 9. Family note

"Your child is learning what '3 times as many' means, and how it differs from '3 more'. Put out 2 spoons. Ask
for 3 times as many, then for 3 more. Ask which is bigger, and why."
