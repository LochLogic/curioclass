# Section 4.1: A fraction as a sum; taking it apart more than one way

Grade 4 math. Unit 4, Adding and Subtracting Fractions (days 25 to 38): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 25 to 28.**
Days by kind: understanding 3, fluency 1, application 0, check 0. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or
tried with children. Fields follow [UNIT-PLAN.md](../UNIT-PLAN.md).

## 1. Standards and practices

- **4.NF.B.3 and 3b, this section's share.** Students "understand a fraction a/b with a > 1 as a sum of
  fractions 1/b" (part a), and "decompose a fraction into a sum of fractions with the same denominator in more
  than one way, recording each decomposition by an equation" and "justify decompositions" (parts c and d).
  Joining unit fractions is the first adding (3a), named as joining but taught as such in section 4.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** Denominators from the list; fractions past 1 from day 25; wholes written as
  n/n from day 27 (the standard's own example form, 2 1/8 = 8/8 + 8/8 + 1/8). The word "mixed number" waits
  for section 4.3.
- **Connections.** 3.NF.A.3c (a whole as n/n), day 27. 4.NF.A.1 (unit 3), day 28's warm-up: an equal name
  is one number.
- **Practices.** MP.2, reason with what numbers stand for: day 25, "six fifths" said as six parts of size one
  fifth. MP.3, build an argument: day 27, the written task on Tam's and Ana's kind of claim, with new numbers.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The denominator names the size of the part and the numerator
  counts the parts, so a/b is a copies of 1/b, and any grouping of those copies is a sum that makes a/b.
  This is why the bottom number does not change when like parts are joined, and why 1 = b/b lets a whole be
  split into the same parts.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Unit-fraction pieces. On day 25 each piece laid down is one addend written: the day the
  object is tied to the sum. On day 26 the pieces are ringed into groups; on day 27 whole strips are laid
  under the pieces to show 1 = 10/10. Withdrawn on day 28: a sketch only; E28 has none.
- **Key moment 1, day 25: six fifths is six pieces, and it runs past 1.** Ideal: pieces the student lays on a
  line from 0 to 2. Paper does it. No tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 26: one amount, grouped more than one way.** Ideal: pieces that can be ringed, with a
  check that each ring's sum and the total agree after the student writes them. Recipe Scaler (spec only),
  building rounds: fits. The student writes each equation into empty boxes before the cup confirms it, and
  every second way must differ (its "Can it be beaten?" table). The cup is not the line; day 26 lays the
  pieces on the line by hand after the tool. Asks: TOOL-ASKS.md, Recipe Scaler.
- **A room with nothing.** Pieces cut from scrap strips; rings drawn round sketched pieces.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: "Six fifths: how many pieces, and how big is each one?" (day 25). "Is 3/8 + 2/8 a new
  way if you already wrote 2/8 + 3/8?" (day 26). "Why can we write 1 as 6/6 but not as 6/5?" (day 27).
- Written task, day 27: "Lee says 1 2/4 = 1/4 + 2/4. Is Lee right? Show with a drawing." Look-fors: says
  no; shows the whole as four fourths; gives 4/4 + 2/4 or 6/4.
- Judged by the teacher with those three look-fors; the sample answers are in the row's `samples` cell (items.csv).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: unit fraction, sum, equation, whole, each on a labelled piece. Read "six fifths",
  never "six over five". Frame, to take or leave: "__ is __ pieces of size __, so __ = __ + __."
- **Access.** Wide pre-cut pieces with raised edges; a line page in large print; rings may be drawn by a helper
  as the student points.
- **Check accommodations.** As in DEFAULTS.md. E26's strip is printed.
- **Reading load.** Low: each exit is one or two sentences; Lee's claim is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Every way to write 6/8 as a sum of two eighths; then of three. How many ways
  are there, and how do you know you have them all?

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Cut a paper strip into fifths and lay the pieces along a ruler; count past one whole.
  Ask the three planned questions and wait for the reason. Hold back any adding rule; section 4.2 gets there.
- **One projector.** Pieces under the camera; students write each sum before the pieces are shown.
