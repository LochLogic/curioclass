# Course frame: Precalculus

Course `precalculus` (catalogue id `precalculus`), grade 12 (data/courses.csv lists grades 11 to 12, typical 12; grade 11 is the accelerated path, data/plans/later.csv L15, on which Calculus follows in grade 12), draft frame. Its scope and sequence (PRODUCTION-PATH step 3) sets the days later; this frame sets the course allocations.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 10 units, in the order that teaches best: functions, then the algebraic families, then trigonometry, then the plane (triangles, vectors, matrices, polar and parametric curves, conics), and last the road to calculus. The base text's order is kept inside each unit; where the course order departs from it, the Source map says why.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 151 in units and 9 for course-level checks and respond days. A full year of 32 weeks: one lesson of about 50 minutes a day, five a week, 32 weeks, each lesson a 5-minute opening of spaced, mixed practice on earlier work and then the day's work. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days, the longest is unit 6 at 19), and one respond day after the assessment. Each unit's days are the sum of its rows' loads in data/progressions/math.csv (the rows' "Load: about N days", read as including the assessment and respond day), so days follow load: unit 6 (19 days) carries three rows and 14 codes, unit 8 (18) carries three rows with the course's most new representations, unit 1 (16) carries three rows of which one is mostly a common language for families already met, and unit 9 (11 days, conics) carries one row with two derivations and a construction. Unit 10 (14) is the last unit and has no readiness day. 160 fits the yardstick for a full year exactly: the rows' 151 days of teaching, assessment and respond, plus three course-level checks of two check days and one respond day each. Count by grade number (sequence-write.md rule 2): a row last taught or assessed in grade g gets retrieval in grade g+1 and readiness in grade g+2 or later. Inside the period: the 50 minutes hold the opening, handing out and collecting papers and calculators (about 3 minutes at the end of any day that uses them) and putting away compasses or string on construction days; a readiness check takes ten minutes; no plan puts more than 50 minutes in a day, and a Shorter path fold-in never takes a feedback or revision day.
- **Day kinds.** Understanding is the first explicit teaching of anything: a new function family, formula, derivation or proof, or a new representation (the sign chart, the polar grid, a matrix as a transformation). Fluency practices only what an earlier day taught: exact trigonometric values, rewriting with identities already proved, the logarithm rules, rewriting rational expressions, converting between polar and rectangular form; a Fluency day never introduces a new identity or rule and is never the source of a unit score. Application is using taught ideas on a new task: a modeling task, a context problem, a proof of a new identity built from proved ones, a mixed set across rows. Fluency is a minority here, about one day in twelve, mostly in units 3, 4 and 5; most days are Understanding or Application. Check and Respond days are named as such.
- **End-of-unit evidence.** Every unit's end-of-unit assessment is one sitting with unseen items on the unit's second-last day, before its respond day, and an end day holds that one performance only. Unit 6, which owns 14 codes, sits twice: a triangle sitting at the close of pc-oblique-triangles' days and the vector sitting at the unit's end, both inside its 19 days. The modeling tasks of units 3, 6, 7 and 8 are scored on our rubric only after a draft, written feedback and a revision: the model is built and the report drafted on an Application day, the teacher's written feedback comes back, and the report is revised in part of a later day (never the end day or a day that first teaches a topic), all inside the unit's days. No score comes from a day a topic is first taught.
- **Standard.** Two lists, both owned in full. The Common Core State Standards for Mathematics (data/standards/ccss-math-standards.txt): 42 codes, the 37 (+) standards the core courses deferred and 5 lettered parts (data/progressions/math-excluded.csv now holds none), each owned by one pc- row. In this course the (+) codes are required, never optional. Curio Class's own outcome list (data/standards/curio-math-electives.txt): CUR-PC.1 to CUR-PC.16, numbered in teaching order, for what the course teaches beyond the (+) codes (function analysis, every zero of a polynomial, sign charts, logarithm rules and models, the six trigonometric functions and their graphs, identities and equations, the dot product, polar and parametric curves, conics in standard form, infinite series, rates and informal limits). Every code a row owns sits in one unit below, and each unit names every lettered part it teaches (N-VM.B.4a to c, N-VM.B.5a and b, F-BF.B.4b to d). The Owned standards cell holds only the codes its rows own, semicolon-separated; revisits of core codes are named in What students learn, with their owner. Math has no check set: the C3 column names the unit's practices, and "Check set adds" names, as candidates, a Progressions check (the high school progressions of the Common Core Standards Writing Team, where one covers the unit) and extensions.
- **Focus, coherence and rigor (L49 applied).** docs/RUBRIC-MATH-HS.md is written for the standards without a (+) mark, so its focus test (1a to 1c: every non-(+) standard in full, more than half the time on the prerequisite-cluster list) has nothing to measure here, and no focus percentage line is set. In its place stands the method docs/RUBRIC-MATH-HS.md sets once for Precalculus, Statistics and Calculus (The grade 12 courses; Cory, 2026-11-17, L49), applied here in the same words as the other two. (1) The owned-outcome account: every owned code is taught in full in the unit named below and assessed on that unit's end-of-unit assessment; the course-level checks sample codes again (Course-level checks) and never claim all of them. (2) The minutes line: the minutes spent on earlier courses' rows are counted and capped at 15 percent of unit minutes (151 unit days of 50 minutes, 7,550 minutes, so about 1,130). What counts: the review days and part-days in the rows' load notes, retrieval tasks outside them, readiness checks at ten minutes each, and the daily-opening minutes spent on earlier courses' rows; each minute counts once; check days, respond days and time on this course's own earlier units do not count. A unit reports no share: each unit plan lists its minutes on earlier courses' rows as a count, and the scope and sequence sums them and states the course share against the cap. (3) The modeling cycle stays whole: no step is cut to save time. Planned here: 14.5 review days in the load notes (725 minutes: 1.5 in pc-function-analysis, 2 each in pc-polynomial-complex-zeros, pc-rational-functions-inequalities and pc-trig-graphs, 1 each in seven more rows), with retrieval tasks inside those days or the openings; three readiness checks (30 minutes); and at most 75 of the 151 openings (375 minutes) on earlier courses' rows, the rest on this course's own earlier units: at most 1,130 minutes, 15 percent. Each review day reviews only what that row's new work uses that week (the rubric's gap 7, read for this course). Everything else in the rubric applies in full: coherence (1d, 1e) through the Seams below; assessment (1f, 1g); rigor (2a to 2d) with direct fluency; the eight practices (2e to 2l); supports (3a to 3u) and the multilingual-learner criteria (a language goal beside the content goal in each section, carried into the scope and sequence). Coherence inside the course: inverses (unit 1) give logarithms (unit 3) and the inverse trigonometric functions (unit 4); the unit circle (unit 4) gives identities (unit 5), polar coordinates and the polar form of complex numbers (unit 8); the law of cosines (unit 6) gives vector magnitudes; vectors give matrix transformations (unit 7) and the geometry of complex multiplication (unit 8); asymptotes and holes (unit 2), e and infinite sums all return as limits in unit 10. Rigor: each unit builds understanding (a derivation or proof: the laws of sines and cosines, the addition formulas, the conic equations from their foci), fluent procedure and application, each shown by the student alone on its end-of-unit assessment.
- **Fluency without a timer.** The procedures this course expects to be fluent: exact trigonometric values of the special angles and their multiples (F-TF.A.3, unit 4); rewriting with trigonometric identities, the Pythagorean, addition and double-angle forms (F-TF.C.9, CUR-PC.7, unit 5); the logarithm rules and change of base (CUR-PC.4, unit 3). Each is built in its unit and kept up in the daily opening for the rest of the year, beside rewriting rational expressions (A-APR.D.7, unit 2) and completing the square (needed again for conics in unit 9). No countdown timer is ever shown to a student. The year-end check holds an untimed, independent fluency station that samples each named fluency directly; its timing is a plan, never a speed score.
- **Practices.** All eight are placed (C3 column): MP1 (units 2, 6, 10), MP2 (units 3, 4, 6, 7, 8), MP3 (units 2, 4, 5, 9), MP4 (units 3, 4, 6, 7, 8), MP5 (units 3, 6, 8, 9), MP6 (units 1, 5, 10), MP7 (units 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 9), MP8 (units 2, 7, 8, 10).
- **Modeling.** No base modeling prompts come with this course, so Curio Class writes one modeling task a unit in units 3, 6, 7 and 8, each taken through the full cycle (assumptions, model, computation, interpretation, validation, revision, report) inside the unit's days, with our own rubric: a growth model fitted to a real data set (unit 3), a crossing plan for a boat in a current (unit 6), a ranking of a small bus network's connections by matrix powers (unit 7), and a thrown ball's path written parametrically and checked against a table of positions (unit 8). F-TF.B.7, the course's one starred modeling code, is met in unit 4 through the tide task taken through a compact cycle (assumptions, model, solve with an inverse function, interpret, check against the tide table) inside pc-inverse-trig's days.
- **Base text.** OpenStax Precalculus, 1st edition (2014, revised 2021-12-08, the archive version recorded as ebc5beb; CC BY 4.0; retired at its publisher and still readable there), placed section by section in the Source map at the end. Credit line on adapted days: "Adapted from OpenStax, Precalculus (1st edition), CC BY 4.0." (a day that adapts one section names it after the title, "Precalculus (1st edition), section 7.2", in the same line). The 2nd edition is CC BY-NC-SA and is never used, not even as reference for wording. Figures are carried only where their own credit line says CC BY or public domain. The copy is in the source store (S:\CurioClass-sources\openstax-precalculus-1e\: the publisher's 1st edition PDF, revision PR-2015-002(03/17), with NOTES.md and MANIFEST.csv; CC BY 4.0 read from its copyright page and the publisher's book record on 2026-11-17) and recorded in docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md. This frame's Source map was built from the web edition's table of contents; each placement was checked against the loaded PDF (scope, 2026-11-24). What the book lacks Curio Class writes (marked "ours"): rational expressions as a system, sign charts, the vector projection, matrices as transformations, the tangent construction, and every exit ticket, end-of-unit assessment, key and course-level check. Every goal and note is written fresh in Curio Class words.
- **Readiness after a gap.** The rows' re-entry lines follow the count by grade number (sequence-write.md rule 2, frame-write lesson 16): a row counts as used in grade 11 where an Algebra II day teaches it or teaches a row that assumes it (one hop). So every alg2- row (grade 11), and alg1-domain-range, alg1-function-notation, alg1-inverse-functions, alg1-completing-square, alg1-graph-features-rates, geo-right-triangle-trig, geo-transformations-coordinates and geo-circle-parabola-equations (each used by an Algebra II row that assumes it), opens as a retrieval task inside a day. Readiness checks go only to rows last used in grade 10 or earlier: geo-solving-right-triangles (unit 4), geo-rigid-motion-definitions, geo-dilations and alg1-linear-systems (unit 7), and geo-circle-angle-relationships (unit 9). Each is given before its unit opens, in the last ten minutes of the day before: day 49 (check 1's respond day) for unit 4, day 101 (check 2's respond day) for unit 7, and day 132 (unit 8's respond day) for unit 9. Unit 6's geo-solving-right-triangles is retrieved, since unit 4 uses it earlier the same year. Start-of-year check: none needed; retrieval only (unit 1's rows were all used in grade 11). Day 1 of every unit stays clean for the hook and the compelling question. The last use of each assumed row (grade, core course, unit and day) is in the Seams table, read from the core courses' scopes (data/plans/algebra-i, geometry and algebra-ii days.csv, 2026-11-16; unit days only, and a day that teaches a row which assumes it counts as one hop). The scope gate's rule 2 count governs: where it differs, the scope fix pass updates this frame. Grades: the count starts from grade 12 with Algebra II in grade 11. On the parked accelerated path (L15) every core course and this one sit one grade earlier, so the gaps and every treatment stay the same. Results target reminders with the earlier model and parallel support, never a replacement for the next hook.
- **Technology and the paper path.** Graphing, curve fitting, 3 by 3 matrix inverses and polar plots use technology. Each has a paper path: grid paper, polar grid paper and semi-log paper (printed from the course's own sheets), printed tables of values, a protractor, compass and straightedge, and a basic scientific calculator; a 3 by 3 inverse on the paper path is given and checked by multiplying back. A logistic fit on paper: estimate the carrying capacity L from where the counts level off, plot ln(y / (L - y)) against time on grid paper, fit a line by eye, and read the growth rate from its slope and the midpoint year from where the line crosses zero; residuals are checked in a printed table. Any electronic tool is a free one that needs no student account, named in no student page.
- **Devices.** One teacher display every day; a class set of 30 basic scientific calculators (school-owned, kept in the room); on the days the scope marks for graphing or a matrix tool, one device for each pair of students (15 for a class of 30), with no student account. Codes that need technology: F-TF.B.7 and CUR-PC.8 (inverse trigonometric values evaluated with technology: the scientific calculator meets both in full on the paper path) and A-REI.C.9 (the inverse of a 3 by 3 or larger matrix found with technology). A student on the paper path meets A-REI.C.9 on a shared device or in a scheduled device session inside the school day, entering the matrix and reading the inverse; where no device route exists the plan records that part unfinished, never met by a given inverse alone. Every other use of technology (graphing, curve fitting, polar plots) has the full paper path above.
- **Cost ceiling.** About $400 a year for a class of 30 (Cory, 2026-11-17, Desk card q-electives-frames-1): printing (about $360 at $0.05 a page, 45 pages a day) within it, and the reusables (a class set of scientific calculators, about $300, protractors and compasses, about $60) bought once where the school has none. No unit's provisional cost passes $150; a unit plan that does names a cheaper route.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** The last weeks hold teaching: unit 10 runs to day 157, and check 3, the year-end check, takes days 158 to 160, its respond day writing the record for the Calculus teacher. If seniors leave early, check 3 moves up to the seniors' last days and the course folds review time, never a check, an end-of-unit assessment, a feedback or a revision day, one day each in this order: unit 1's retrieval of transformations and domain (1.5 days to 0.5), unit 2's retrieval of complex arithmetic and division (2 to 1), unit 4's retrieval of sine and cosine graphs (2 to 1), unit 9's retrieval of the circle and parabola equations (into the openings), unit 10's retrieval of finite geometric series (into the openings): up to 5 days. A school that knows its seniors' last day plans these folds from the start; more than 5 days is For the lead 6.
- **Side standards.** None is claimed. The modeling reports practice explanatory writing (WHST.11-12.2), practiced, not owned.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md). No unit has cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content that needs a named community study: growth models use a reintroduced wildlife population and the spread of a new technology, not illness; half-life uses carbon dating; forces and navigation use boats, planes in a crosswind and hanging signs, with no military context; matrix "payoffs" are points in a game or a league table, never betting. If a unit plan swaps in a health context (a drug dose decaying, for example), that unit becomes health content and the agent first studies a national public health body's plain-language pages on medicines and a disability and illness language guide. The answer-key agent reads every check, assessment, modeling rubric and key, solving each item cold beside the script check (BASELINE rule 27), before the course is called finished. Word problems use a range of names and settings; the unit plan lists the people and places its problems show.
- **Who takes it.** Grade 12, after Algebra II on the core route (Algebra I in grade 9, Geometry in 10, Algebra II in 11). Statistics stands beside it and assumes neither; Calculus picks up from it by course order (Seams). The route to both (Cory, 2026-11-17): the accelerated path (data/plans/later.csv L15) puts Precalculus in grade 11 and Calculus in grade 12, and Precalculus is Calculus's stated prerequisite; Calculus straight after Algebra II, with a bridge of about 20 lessons, stays the named alternative. A student who takes only Precalculus in grade 12 meets nothing missing.

## Course-level checks

Each unit's own end-of-unit assessment and its respond day sit inside its unit days; these are extra. Each check is Curio Class's own and mixes concept, procedure and application in two task forms: an audit-and-repair set on the first check day (worked solutions with errors to find, explain and correct, and "which method and why" items, on fresh cases) and one extended task on the second, so each day holds one performance and neither repeats the unseen-item form of the unit sitting two days before. The year-end fluency station is direct, with no supplied work, beside the first day's set. Each check day names the part it samples beside each multi-part code. Every item names its standard and what to do next for a student who misses it. ## Source map

Base text: OpenStax Precalculus (1st edition), CC BY 4.0, archive version ebc5beb (revised 2021-12-08), read from its table of contents; the copy is in the source store (Base text bullet). Every chapter and section is placed in one unit or left out with a reason. Decisions: keep (adapted), merge (folded into the unit's retrieval or another day), split (across two units), cut (left out, reason given), ours (a day Curio Class writes with no base section). A section is not a lesson: the days are ours, set by the scope and sequence, each adapting what it uses and crediting the base once a day in the credit line; a section may feed several days or part of one. Check and assessment days are always `ours`, never a kept section, and a kept section is never turned into a test. Goals and notes are in our words. Students read our adapted text, never the base, at most about 600 words a day (about ten minutes at the course's reading level), worked examples and figures beside it. Each chapter's Introduction opens its unit's hook material; its Chapter Review and Review Exercises feed the unit's practice; its Practice Test is not used as an assessment (Curio Class writes its own end-of-unit assessments). The course order follows the book except where noted: vectors (8.8) move up beside the laws of sines and cosines, because the vector-arrow model is needed before the complex plane, and matrices (chapter 9) come before polar coordinates (8.3 to 8.7) so that rotation by a matrix and rotation by multiplying complex numbers can be compared in unit 8; the average rate of change in 1.3 waits for unit 10.
