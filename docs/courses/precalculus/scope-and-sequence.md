# Scope and sequence: Precalculus

Course `precalculus`, grade 12, draft, 2026-11-24; reads applied 2026-11-24 (docs/reviews/sequence-precalculus-fixes-2026-11-24.md).

Ten units keep the frame's order, owned rows and lengths: 16, 16, 14, 16, 14, 19, 13, 18, 11 and 14 days, 151 in all, plus 9 course-level check and respond days, 160 course days. The course meets every school day for 32 weeks, so course day n is school day n and `school_day` is blank. The days live in data/plans/precalculus/days.csv and the base-text placements in data/plans/precalculus/source-map.csv (OpenStax Precalculus, 1st edition, section numbers checked against the PDF in the source store; they match the frame's Source map); this page gives the judgment behind them. Every unit ends with its end-of-unit assessment on its second-last day and a respond day on its last; unit 6 sits twice, as the frame sets. No unit is over 25 days, so no unit holds a mid-unit assessment. Every day opens with five minutes of spaced, mixed practice; exact values (from day 53), the logarithm rules (from day 37), rewriting rational expressions (from day 26), the identities (from day 76) and completing the square (from day 20) stay in that opening for the rest of the year, untimed.

**Kinds.** 96 Understanding, 22 Application, 12 Fluency, 17 Check and 13 Respond days. The kind follows what each day does: a review day that first carries a code in this course is Understanding, and every Fluency day practices only what an earlier day of this course taught. Fluency is 12 of 151 unit days (about one in 13), six of them in units 3 to 5; the frame's Day kinds bullet now reads "about one day in twelve".

**Devices.** A day whose `tools` cell names a graphing tool, a spreadsheet or the matrix tool uses one device per pair (15 for 30), no student account, with the frame's paper path: grid, semi-log and polar grid paper, printed tables and the scientific calculator. A-REI.C.9 (days 111 to 113) is met on a device only: a paper-path student uses a shared device or a scheduled device session.

A retrieval task is practice and never check evidence; a student who misses it gets a brief reminder with the earlier model before the new work. On a review day it is the day's first part; where a row has no review day in its load, or is needed before its review day (days 6, 10, 20, 34, 62, 66, 86, 115, 122, 126, 147, 149, 152), it is that day's five-minute opening. Unit 1 has no readiness rows, so day 1 holds no check, and the first day of every unit stays clean for the hook and the compelling question.

**Minutes on earlier courses' rows** (RUBRIC-MATH-HS, the grade 12 courses): the frame's 14.5 review days (725 minutes: days 2, 17, 18, 27, 33, 39, 50, 55, 56, 80, 110, 133 and 144 whole, and half of days 1, 24 and 28), three readiness checks (30) and at most 70 openings on earlier courses' rows (350), the 13 opening retrieval tasks among them: at most 1,105 of 7,550 unit minutes, 14.6 percent, under the 15 percent cap (about 1,130). Each review day reviews only what that row's new work uses that week. Unit plans list their own counts.

**Language goals.** Each section carries a language goal beside its content goal; the unit sections below name them. Every one asks for a spoken or written explanation with the unit's words. They grow toward independence: units 1 to 3 give sentence frames and a word bank on every goal; units 4 to 7 give frames for new forms only (proof links, the choice of law, what a matrix means); units 8 to 10 drop frames on the written goals and keep them for spoken ones. **Practices.** Each unit section names the days where each of its frame practices (the frame's C3 column) is the task's demand; unit plans build those tasks around the practice, not in passing.

## Unit 1. Functions as one toolkit

Course days 1 to 16, weeks 1 to 4. Sections: 1.1 analysis (days 1 to 5), 1.2 composition (6 to 9), 1.3 inverses (10 to 16). Day 1 hooks with a card sort of the families met so far and brings back transformations; day 2 brings back domain and range and adds interval notation, the unit's one new tool, with piecewise pieces; days 3 and 4 add rising and falling, turning points, symmetry and end behavior, and day 5 applies the whole set of questions to unfamiliar functions. Composition follows (formulas and tables, graphs and domains, a context, then splitting into inner and outer), and inverses last, one lettered part a day (4b, 4c, 4d), because each needs composition. Day 14 practices the unit.

Assessment: day 15 on CUR-PC.1, F-BF.A.1c and F-BF.B.4b to 4d, each part its own prompt; response on day 16. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Functions, building functions and inverses) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days. Extensions kept, no day added: the function, its inverse and y = x on one grid (day 11) and a chain of three functions, shown once (day 8, 10 minutes). Language goals: describe a function's behavior with interval words (1.1), say which function acts first (1.2), explain why a restriction makes an inverse (1.3). Sensitivity: day 8's standard atmosphere table is said to be a model. Practices: MP6 day 2 (interval notation written exactly), MP7 day 9 (seeing a function's inner and outer structure).

## Unit 2. Polynomial and rational functions, all their zeros

Course days 17 to 32, weeks 4 to 7. Sections: 2.1 complex numbers and division (17 to 20), 2.2 every zero (21 to 23), 2.3 rational expressions (24 to 26), 2.4 rational functions and sign charts (27 to 32). The two review days come first (complex arithmetic with division by the conjugate added; division and the Remainder Theorem with synthetic division added), then complex factoring and the fundamental theorem of algebra, so that day 21's rational candidates and day 22's conjugate pairs can find every zero. Rational expressions come as a system before rational functions are read from factored form, because reading the form uses them; sign charts come last because they use both the zeros and the excluded values.

Assessment: day 31 on N-CN.A.3, N-CN.C.8, N-CN.C.9, CUR-PC.2, A-APR.D.7 and CUR-PC.3; response on day 32. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Algebra, polynomials and rational expressions; Number, complex numbers) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days. Extensions kept, no day added: a degree 5 polynomial from its graph (day 23, 15 minutes, optional) and the cost-per-item inequality (day 30). Language goals: explain each step of a division (2.1), argue that every zero is found (2.2), compare rational expressions with fractions (2.3), justify a sign on each interval (2.4). Practices: MP1 day 22 (every zero of a quartic, kept at until all are found), MP3 days 20 and 25 (the theorem shown for every quadratic; the closure argument), MP7 day 28 (reading a function from its factored form), MP8 day 18 (synthetic division from repeated long division). For its complex number part (N-CN), which no Progressions document is known to cover, the unit plan records that and the check ends there.

## Unit 3. Logarithms as inverses, and growth models

Course days 33 to 46, weeks 7 to 10. Sections: 3.1 the logarithm as an inverse (33 to 36), 3.2 equations and models (37 to 40), 3.3 data and the modeling task (41 to 46). The logarithm enters as unit 1's inverse (day 33 review, day 34 composition and the reflection, ln as the inverse of e to the x), then the properties and change of base, practice, and exact equations. Models follow: half-life and doubling with continuous growth retrieved, logarithmic scales, straightening data (semi-log and log-log), and the logistic model read and fitted on paper, so that day 43's wolf-count task can use every tool. The report is drafted on day 43 and revised on day 46 after written feedback.

Assessment: day 45 on F-BF.B.5, CUR-PC.4 and CUR-PC.5; response on day 46, whose last 25 minutes are the report revision; the report is scored on our rubric after it. Unit 3 splits its respond day where units 6 to 8 do not because no other day fits: day 44 is a Fluency day (no writing revised there) and day 45 the end day, and feedback on day 43's draft needs two evenings, so the first 25 minutes of day 46 go to the most-missed items of day 45, reteach by group and a parallel retry, and the unit plan keeps that response to those items. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Functions, exponential and logarithmic models; Modeling) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days. Extensions kept, no day added: one more year of counts in the revision (day 46) and the planets' log-log plot as the log-log case (day 41). Language goals: say what a logarithm asks (3.1), explain why a candidate is rejected (3.2), argue how far a model can be trusted (3.3). Sensitivity: carbon dating (day 39) and wildlife counts (days 42, 43, 46); no illness context. Biology's carrying capacity is retrieved on day 42, with the frame's bridge. Practices: MP2 day 40 (what a step of one means on each scale), MP4 day 43 (the wolf-count cycle), MP5 day 41 (choosing semi-log or log-log).

## Unit 4. Trigonometric functions, exact values, graphs and inverses

Course days 50 to 65, weeks 10 to 13. Sections: 4.1 exact values and symmetry (50 to 53), 4.2 the six graphs (54 to 59), 4.3 inverses and the tide (60 to 65). Exact values come first from the special triangles, then reference angles, then symmetry and periodicity, then practice; the six values from the unit circle open the graph section, sine and cosine with phase shift are brought back over two review days, then tangent and cotangent, then secant and cosecant, then practice. Inverses come last because they need unit 1's restriction and the graphs that show where each function always rises or falls; the tide task closes the arc with a compact modeling cycle (assumptions, model, solve with an inverse, interpret, check against the table).

Assessment: day 64 on F-TF.A.3, F-TF.A.4, CUR-PC.6, F-TF.B.6 and F-TF.B.7; response on day 65. Readiness: geo-solving-right-triangles on day 49. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Functions, trigonometric functions) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days. Extension kept: inverse cosine on the calculator against the unit circle's two answers (day 61). Extension dropped: the sum of two sines, because the frame puts sums of sinusoids out of the unit and three rows fill its 16 days. Language goals: explain an exact value from a triangle (4.1), describe a graph's four changes (4.2), say what an inverse's answer means in context (4.3). Sensitivity: tide predictions said to be predictions (day 63). Physics' wave math is retrieved on day 56, with the frame's bridge. Practices: MP2 day 62 (what each answer means in context), MP3 day 52 (arguing oddness and periodicity from the circle), MP4 day 63 (the tide cycle), MP7 day 51 (the symmetries as structure for any real x).

## Unit 5. Identities and equations

Course days 66 to 79, weeks 14 to 16. Sections: 5.1 proof chains and addition formulas (66 to 69), 5.2 double and half angles (70 to 72), 5.3 equations (73 to 79). Day 66 sets the rule for proof (a chain of equal expressions, never an operation on both sides), then the cosine addition formula is proved from the unit circle and the sine and tangent formulas from it, then practice. Double-angle, power-reducing and half-angle formulas follow in that order, each from the one before, and day 72 proves new identities from the proved ones. Equations follow the identities because many need one first: special angles, then factoring or an identity, then technology, then practice and the daylight and sound models.

Assessment: day 78 on F-TF.C.9, CUR-PC.7 and CUR-PC.8; response on day 79. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Functions, trigonometric identities) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days. Extension kept: the product-to-sum formulas derived (day 72, 15 minutes, optional). Extension dropped: tan(x/2) in sine and cosine, because the half-angle formulas already carry CUR-PC.7 and no later row uses it. Language goals: state the reason for each link of a proof (5.1, 5.2), explain why an equation has more than one solution (5.3). Practices: MP3 days 66 and 72 (proof by a chain of equal expressions), MP6 day 73 (every solution in a stated interval and the general solution), MP7 day 70 (the double-angle formulas seen inside the addition formulas).

## Unit 6. Any triangle, and vectors

The area formula, the law of sines, the ambiguous case and the law of cosines come in the base text's order, then the surveying day and the triangle sitting (day 85). Vectors follow because the law of cosines is how a resultant's magnitude is found (day 89): notation, components, sums and why a sum is usually shorter, magnitude and direction of a sum, and subtraction, which completes N-VM.B.4. Scalar multiples, forces and the boat task come before the dot product and projection, which day 96's revision and mixed set then join.

Assessment: two sittings, as the frame sets: day 85 on G-SRT.D.9 to D.11 and day 97 on the N-VM codes and CUR-PC.9, each lettered part its own prompt; one response on day 98 for both, counted once. The boat report is drafted on day 93 and revised on day 96. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Geometry, trigonometry of general triangles) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days; for the vector codes, which no Progressions document is known to cover, the unit plan records that and the check ends there. Language goals: justify the choice of law (6.1), describe a vector by magnitude and direction (6.2), explain what a dot product of zero means (6.3). Sensitivity: boats, planes and signs, no military context (days 92, 93, 96). Physics' net force is retrieved on day 92, with the frame's bridge. Practices: MP1 day 84 (the survey, choosing and checking a law), MP2 day 92 (forces and velocities as quantities with direction), MP4 day 93 (the boat cycle), MP5 day 82 (compass and ruler against the law of sines for the ambiguous case).

## Unit 7. Matrices

## Unit 10. Infinite sums, rates and limits, the road to calculus

Course days 144 to 157, weeks 29 to 32. Sections: 10.1 series (144 to 146), 10.2 the binomial theorem (147 and 148), 10.3 rates (149 to 151), 10.4 limits (152 to 157). Sigma notation and the finite sum come back first, then the infinite geometric series and why other ratios fail, then the harmonic table beside the sum of 1/n squared; the binomial theorem follows with combinations as a bridge. Rates come before limits because the secant slopes give limits their first case; limits then gather unit 2's holes and asymptotes, unit 3's e and this unit's infinite sums, and continuity closes the content.

Assessment: day 156 on A-APR.C.5, CUR-PC.14, CUR-PC.15 and CUR-PC.16; response on day 157. Candidate settled: the Progressions check (Algebra, the binomial theorem; Functions, rate of change) is kept as a unit-planning check, zero student days. Extensions kept, no day added: 0.999... as a geometric series (day 145, 10 minutes) and the cooling curve as day 151's task. Language goals: explain why a sum can be finite (10.1), say where each coefficient comes from (10.2), describe a rate at an instant (10.3), describe what a function approaches (10.4). Practices: MP1 day 155 (the road-to-calculus set), MP6 day 154 (naming each break in continuity exactly), MP8 days 146 and 150 (partial sums and secant slopes repeated toward a value).

## Shorter path

**A shorter period (45 minutes).** The opening drops from five minutes to three on teaching days, and each teaching day's practice set is cut by its last few items (the exit ticket stays); review days keep their retrieval, readiness checks keep their ten minutes, and assessment sittings and check days keep their items in the full period. Modeling days keep every step of the cycle and move practice to the next opening.

**A year about 10 days short.** Ten fold-in days, each a review or practice day whose work goes into a neighbor's day and the openings, nothing assessed and no owned part cut, and never a feedback, revision, check or respond day. The first five follow the frame's Grade 12 calendar order, so they also serve seniors who leave early.
