# Course frame: Library and Research Skills, grade 2

Course `library-and-research-skills-grade-2` (catalog id `library-2`), grade 2. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the kindergarten pilot's shape and fixes, beside the grade 1 frame; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, one year of library and research skills, each unit one full inquiry cycle (ask, find, share,
  reflect, and from unit 3 check) on a small question, in the order a grade 2 class needs the moves: turning a
  wonder into a question a source could answer and naming the kind of source to try, then finding the book by
  catalog and shelf order and crediting it, then sorting and checking facts from two books and their makers, then
  the year's product, a pair poster built from books and from asking a person.
- **Days.** 29: 24 in units, 2 course-level check days (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 3 respond days (1 after
  the mid-year check, 2 after the year-end check). One session of about 25 minutes, one session a week, for 29
  weeks (APPROACH.md, Pacing and dose: the K to 2 default of 30 sessions of 25 minutes, about 12.5 hours a year, at
  the one-a-week end of the settled "one or two sessions a week"). The course sets 29, one fewer than the default,
  because of the grade 2 calendar: the year-end check must sit by week 27 (core checks fill weeks 28, 29, 31 and 32),
  so the units end in week 26 and only respond days may follow; a third respond day would only fill a week (the
  kindergarten read's finding 5), and week 30 is left to the school (For the lead, item 3). The 25 minutes hold
  getting to the carpet or the book corner, the session, putting books back and collecting sheets; a lesson plan
  budgets about 3 minutes at each end for that, so about 19 working minutes, and every assessment form below is
  costed against those 19 minutes for a class of 30. One adult runs the room (the classroom teacher or a librarian);
  while the adult checks a small group, the rest of the class works on a book-corner task it has already practiced.
  Nine grade 2 rows fit 24 unit days, 6 a unit: units 1, 2 and 4 carry two rows each; unit 3 carries three "with
  help" rows that share one case set and one find step (sorting found facts, the two facts that differ, and who made
  each source), each a short sorting or circling move. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and its
  respond day (no unit is over 25 days, so no mid-unit assessment). These are course days: with 29 days over 29
  weeks, course day n falls in week n of docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, and the scope and sequence places them on school
  days.
- **Day kinds.** In library's words (APPROACH.md, The kinds of day; docs/GLOSSARY.md, teaching day):
  Understanding is the show-me day (the teacher thinks aloud through the move on an invented or read-aloud case,
  holding the pictured skill card); Fluency is the practice day (short, repeated tries of the same move on new
  cases, with feedback: match three questions to source kinds, put a mixed-up shelf in order, write credit lines for
  three covers, sort fact cards into boxes); Application is the inquiry day (the class or a pair runs part of the
  cycle on its host question and makes its drawing, page or poster). Fluency is small in grade 2: one practice day
  a unit in units 1 to 3, pair tries with the card at the end of day 3 in units 1 and 2 (the move shown that day is
  tried before the inquiry day withdraws the card), plus the five-minute opening retrieval on later days. Every unit's day 1 is a show-me day
  with the hook and the compelling question, and nothing else.
- **Standard.** AASL National School Library Standards, second edition, learner framework (ids and our own words
  only, data/standards/aasl-words.txt; never the competency text, and no claim of alignment approved by anyone).
  Ownership rule (STANDARD.md section 3): an id is owned once, in the school year where it is first checked alone.
  Grade 2 owns two ids, AASL.IV.A.2 (g2-kinds-of-sources, unit 1) and AASL.VI.B.2 (g2-credit-line, unit 2), and
  carries twelve as "with help" contributions, each named in its unit's What students learn with its owner
  (AASL.I.A.2, owned in grade 1, comes back as review). Skills: the nine progression rows. Check set: the Empire
  State Information Fluency Continuum, PK to 2 band file, grade 2 column and its bold priority skills (read cell by
  cell for this frame, later.csv L103; result in the Check set read bullet); the "Check set adds" cells name what
  each unit plan should weigh from it. Values: K to 5 case sets stay neutral (no politics, elections, religion,
  identity groups or conflict; Cory, 2026-11-21).
- **Base text.** None. Every day is Curio Class's own. Curio Class writes: four short information read-alouds (one a
  unit) and paired short passages that disagree on one fact for unit 3, source-kind picture cards, a printed
  catalog card set and call labels with a mixed-up shelf set of spine cards, cover cards for credit lines, fact
  cards and picture-box mats, invented web pages on paper (a museum's page, a store's page, a writer's page, no real
  site named), the five-picture cycle card, one pictured skill card a unit, the interview question frame and the
  poster template, and the invented "pretend class" posters and pages used in checks. Any information books the
  room already has may be added. No day needs a trade book, a paid source, a library visit or a database (no
  library access is assumed; Cory, 2026-11-11).
- **Host tasks.** Every unit runs its cycle on a host task from another grade 2 course, named by its row id for the
  teacher to schedule against, and carries a stand-alone host question of its own (an invented question with a
  closed source set of Curio Class read-alouds, passages and pictures) so the unit runs when no core task is in step
  or at home (APPROACH.md). The scope and sequence records both.
- **The inquiry arc.** Each unit opens with its compelling question, a wonder from the class board, and closes with
  the share step and the self-check: the child points at the pictured steps, says which step went best and one to
  do better next time (the spoken look-back that g2-research-share checks in unit 4), and adds one new "I wonder" to
  the board (the continuum's grade 2 reflect cells: one's own strengths and a goal, and "what do I wonder about
  now?"). Grade 2 keeps the four kindergarten and grade 1 pictures and words (ask, find, share, reflect) and in unit
  3 adds a fifth, "check", between find and share: the lens's judge step, met at grade 2 depth as spotting two facts
  that differ, deciding to check a third source and asking who made each source and why (g2-two-books-differ,
  g2-who-made-it). Use is the found fact sorted into its box and put on the poster at share.
- **Evidence.** Daily: the teacher's observation of the one named skill per child on a class checklist kept by the
  teacher and thrown away at the end of the unit, plus the child's sheet, drawing or spoken answer (APPROACH.md,
  K to 2). End-of-unit assessment: a task on a case not seen before, in the grade 1 forms whose time a unit plan
  shows: a whisper walk (the teacher walks round and each child whispers the answer, about 10 to 20 seconds a child,
  so no child repeats a neighbor); a station of three to five children (about 1.3 to 2.5 minutes a group) while the
  others do a practiced book-corner task; a scanned pointing item (about 2 minutes); and a sheet item the whole class
  answers at once on paper, the teacher reading every word aloud, by circling, sorting, copying or writing a short
  line (about 3 to 8 minutes), marked on the checklist from the page afterwards. Each unit names its forms and total
  below; no unit assessment or course-level check spills into its respond day. It is scored on a day after the move
  was practiced, never on a show-me day. Sheets go home after marking, so no per-child record outlives its unit. The
  respond day's groups come from the checklist by the step missed.
- **Reading level.** Grade 2 children read short texts and write short lines. Sources are Curio Class texts at a
  grade 2 level, read aloud as well for any child who needs it; every check item is read aloud. What a child writes
  alone: a question-word question, a short "because" line, a credit line (title and author), a call label copied, a
  word or phrase on a fact card, and two facts on a poster.
- **Paper path.** The whole course is the paper path: printed read-alouds and passages, picture cards, the printed
  catalog cards and spine cards, cover cards, fact cards and box mats, invented web pages on paper, poster paper,
  the class book corner and crayons. No day needs a device, an internet connection or a student account.
- **Devices.** None per student, and no owned code needs technology. A school that has a catalog on a screen may
  show it on one shared device on unit 2's show-me day, with the stop rule (g2-catalog-lookup); the printed catalog
  cards do the same job in full, and the carried AASL.IV.B.1 and AASL.VI.A.1 are met in full on paper. Unit 3's
  invented web pages are printed, so who made a page is asked without open search.
- **Safety read.** No hands-on materials beyond books, paper, cards and crayons, no field work and no trip, so no
  laboratory or field guide applies. Unit 4's interview is in school, with a staff member visiting the class or a
  classmate, by APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground" item 8; a family interview at home is optional. The screen-safety
  side is the stop rule on any device day, checked by the sensitivity read (APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground, item 2).
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`) on its case set at the unit plan and again at the days; no outside human reader is planned
  (Cory, 2026-11-14). No unit carries cultural, Native, hard-history, health or disability content: the case sets
  are frogs in winter and maps, fossils, turtles and plant needs, and how a school's plants are cared for. What the
  agent studies first: APPROACH.md "Sensitive ground" items 1 (neutral K to 5 case sets, above all unit 3's two
  books that differ, which differ on animal and plant facts only), 2 (the stop rule on a device day), 3 (privacy:
  no name typed, and a person's answers reported without a full name), 4 (honest use without shaming, units 2 and
  4), 5 (whose voices a source set shows, C12) and 8 (unit 4's interview: neutral class topic, questions checked
  first, no question about home or family, any staff member standing in); and, for any device day, the National
  Center for Missing and Exploited Children's free K to 2 online-safety presentation "Be Safer Online with NetSmartz:
  Grades K-2", listed at https://www.missingkids.org/netsmartz/resources (listing read 2026-11-25 for the
  kindergarten frame, not re-read; its K to 2 rule, tell a trusted adult when something online makes you feel sad,
  scared or confused, matches the stop rule). No organization's name goes on a student, teacher or family page.
- **Supplies and cost.** Printing at about 5 cents a page (ours, provisional) for a class of 30: unit 1 about $5
  (source-kind cards, sheets, the frog read-aloud), unit 2 about $7 (catalog cards, call labels, spine cards, cover
  cards), unit 3 about $6 (fact cards, box mats, paired passages, invented pages), unit 4 about $9 (question frames,
  poster templates, and 15 sheets of poster paper at about 25 cents); about $27 a year. Spine cards and box mats are
  reused all year.
- **Check set read (L103, 2026-11-25).** The PK to 2 band file's grade 2 column and priority boxes, every indicator
  row (S:\CurioClass-sources\library\aasl-2026-11-21\esifc-pk-2\esifc-priority-pk-2.docx, read as text with
  `python -I`, ids and meanings only, nothing copied). Met by the rows: seeing gaps in what one knows and knowing
  that questions can be answered by finding information (g2-question-to-research); fiction and nonfiction sources
  told apart and sources chosen with guidance, dictionaries and maps among them (g2-kinds-of-sources); the fiction
  shelf arrangement, the library area for a topic and the catalog by author or title (g2-catalog-lookup); naming the
  maker of a website, with help on invented pages (g2-who-made-it); a list of sources with title and author, and
  crediting sources by author and title (g2-credit-line, owned); seeing when information answers the research
  question and organizing it into categories with a teacher's tool (g2-fact-sort); not all information is true, and
  drawing a conclusion with guidance (g2-two-books-differ); a product explaining main ideas, a first sense of
  audience, presenting in more than one way and comparing new ideas with what was known (g2-research-share); shared
  research, and acknowledging others' ideas and asking others (g2-group-research). Met without a row change: one's
  own strengths and a goal, and "what do I wonder about now?" (the self-check, The inquiry arc); revising from the
  teacher's feedback (the respond days); following a modeled inquiry process each session (the cycle run each unit).
  Differences, each recorded in its row note in data/progressions/library.csv: "I wonder" questions made
  independently, a bold skill (each child first writes their own, g2-question-to-research); periodicals and globes,
  and designated online encyclopedias and databases (a children's magazine and a globe join the kinds; a page the
  teacher chose stands in for the online ones, since no device or database is assumed, g2-kinds-of-sources); the
  bold skills on library rules and returning resources on time, and on the school's rules for computer use (kept in
  the shelf and device routine, g2-catalog-lookup); the bold skill on notes as words, phrases and drawings, not
  copied sentences (g2-fact-sort; key-word notes planned by the student stay in grade 3). Left out: main ideas and
  key details, story points of view, predictions, opinions with evidence, folktales and multicultural texts, text
  features as reading (index and glossary), and media against print (ELA grade 2: e2-main-topic-paragraphs,
  e2-points-of-view, e2-opinion, e2-fables-folktales, e2-text-features; STANDARD.md section 4; C12's voices record
  covers who appears in the source sets); one or two key words and the website against the database (grade 3,
  g3-key-words, one grade on; no database assumed); cyberbullying (computer science grade 2, g2-online-consequences,
  and health grade 3, g3-v-bullying); technology tools for products (no device is assumed); design thinking and the
  social and emotional cells (science, computer science and SEL). No row is two or more grades from its cell.
