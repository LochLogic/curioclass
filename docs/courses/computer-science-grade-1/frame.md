# Course frame: Computer Science, Grade 1

Course `computer-science-grade-1` (catalog id `cs-k12`), grade 1, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written to the kindergarten pilot's pattern; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, one for each concept area at its grade 1 depth, in the order the ideas build: using our tools well
  (systems and security, so the device rules and the parts' names exist before the first device day of the year),
  plans in parts (algorithms, with the first look at systems that find patterns and at what people and computers each
  do well), code that waits for a tap (programming, built from unit 2's step-card plans), counting, looking and asking
  (data), and computers in our town, then and now (computing and society, the year's closer).
- **Days.** 32 course days: 28 in units, 2 for course-level checks (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). One lesson a week of about 30 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  kindergarten to grade 2 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a specials
  rotation, so course day n falls in week n. The 30 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens
  with a three-minute retrieval task from the second session on (APPROACH.md, Pacing), and floor-grid and device
  sessions keep four minutes at the end to put cards, tape and devices away, so a session teaches for about 23
  minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing); a
  course-level check that directly follows a unit is that unit's end-of-unit assessment (frame-write.md, After-core
  courses), so in units 2 and 4 the check scores the unit in full and the check's respond day is the unit's respond
  day. Units 1 to 5 teach on 4, 6, 6, 4 and 2 sessions, 22 of the 32. Days follow the load of the rows: unit 2 owns
  four unplugged rows, three of them new ideas at this grade (splitting a task into parts, systems that find patterns,
  people and computers compared), and unit 3 owns four programming rows with device versions, but three of them carry
  kindergarten constructs forward one step (an event, the fix itself), so both get six teaching sessions; unit 5's two
  society rows are single unplugged picture sessions. 32 sessions for 15 rows is the kindergarten count (32 for 14),
  22 of them teaching. Each course-level check takes one check session and one respond session, since a 30-minute
  session holds a whole-class paper task and one spoken answer a child heard in five groups (Evidence gives the
  minutes). No unit runs over 25 days, so there
  are no mid-unit assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The grade 1 core checks take weeks 14, 15, 16 and 19 (mid-year) and 29 to 32 (year-end)
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, target weeks: social studies 14 and 31, science 15 and 29, ELA 16 and 32, math 19 and
  30). This course's checks sit in week 13 (mid-year, after unit 2) and week 27 (year-end, after unit 4), with their
  respond days in weeks 14 and 28 (respond days are not check days). Unit 3 runs in weeks 15 to 22, across the core
  check weeks 15, 16 and 19, because it holds no course-level check; its end-of-unit assessment falls in week 21.
  Unit 5 runs in weeks 29 to 32 after the year-end check, as the kindergarten pilot's unit 5 does; its own
  end-of-unit assessment (week 31) checks its two rows. Overlaps with other after-core subjects' check weeks are the
  lead's calendar pass (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** The 16 E1 ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, all taught here (STANDARD.md section
  2): E1-ALG-PS-01, E1-ALG-ML-02, E1-ALG-IM-03, E1-PRO-PD-04, E1-PRO-VD-05, E1-PRO-RD-06, E1-PRO-TR-07,
  E1-DAT-DC-08, E1-DAT-DI-09, E1-DAT-IM-10, E1-SYS-HW-11, E1-SYS-SE-12, E1-SYS-IM-13, E1-SOC-HI-14, E1-SOC-HU-15,
  E1-SOC-CE-16. Each is taught at the depth its owner row's notes set, in our own words, and each owned id's boundary
  statement was read in the source store's standards PDF (S:\CurioClass-sources\computer-science\csta-2026-11-21,
  read 2026-11-25, never copied or quoted; STANDARD.md "Depth"); "What students learn" says what is out. Plans cite
  ids and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md "Quoting"); the credit line "Standards: 2026
  CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any day that leans on a statement. Check set: each
  unit plan checks itself against the computer science bar (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5, items 1 to 9) and
  the lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and reads the partial base unit it adapts for order; the "Check
  set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. No
  Specialty id, and no X + CS id, is claimed.
- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's Practices and concept areas column names the concept area each unit teaches and the
  CSTA practices it teaches inside the content, by code and our label (STANDARD.md section 3), as bar items 1 and 2
  ask; the pairs follow the practices the standards PDF pairs with each owned id. Each unit plan names the practice's
  day and task. Across the year: CT6 define the problem (units 2, 3, 4), CT7 find patterns (units 2, 3, 4), CT8 build
  and plan (units 2, 3), CT9 test, debug and refine (unit 3), IC3 explain and give feedback (every unit), IC5 be a
  reliable teammate (units 1, 3, pair roles), HCD10 learn from and include different users (units 1, 2, 4), ESR1
  weigh who gains (units 1, 2, 4, 5), ESR2 respect people and protect information (unit 1). Dispositions are named in
  the unit plans, not here.
- **Base text.** Partial: England's open computing curriculum, the Teach Computing Curriculum, archived edition
  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived, key stage 1, resources dated 2023), which its publisher no longer maintains,
  mapped as a fixed text as the kindergarten pilot does. Year 2 is the grade 1 age
  (docs/reviews/computer-science-open-material-2026-11-16.md). License: the scout read the Open Government Licence
  v3.0 "unless expressly stated otherwise" on the publisher's terms page; the archive pages carry no license line of
  their own, so each file's license line is read when the store folder is made. Adapted unit by unit and rephrased for
  an American audience (STANDARD.md section 2, Cory 2026-11-16): US spelling, school years named as US grades, no
  money in pounds, no product or company name, our own browser tool or paper in place of named apps and floor robots.
  Four of its six Year 2 units feed units 1 to 5; its two creating-media units are left out (see Source map). The
  source's name appears only in a day's credit line, once a day.
  Pending(docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md#teach-computing-curriculum): the source store holds no copy of this base, so the
  Source map below rests on the public unit pages (lesson titles, opened 2026-11-25), and each adapted lesson's check
  for third-party images, text and tool screenshots waits on the store folder (kindergarten frame, For the lead 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is the first teaching of a row, construct or method, here an unplugged or trace session (a task cut
  into parts on cards, a picture sort, a block program predicted before it runs, a worked program traced, the first
  debugging session). Fluency is practice of what earlier days taught and teaches nothing new; here it is a modify
  session: change one step of a working plan or one block of a working program and check it; grade 1 has a little of
  it (unit 2 session 3, unit 3 session 5, and the retrieval openings). Application is a make session: split a new
  task into parts and step-cards a partner carries out (unit 2 session 4), turn a given plan into a block program,
  run it, find and fix what goes wrong (unit 3 session 6), or collect counts and descriptions in more than one way to
  answer a class question (unit 4 session 2). Check is the end-of-unit assessment or a course-level check; Respond
  acts on it by error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make, and no make day before a trace
  or modify day on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Grade 1 answers by pointing, sorting, ordering, drawing or saying aloud to a partner; the teacher notes
  by exception on a class checklist (APPROACH.md, "The form of daily evidence"). Every end-of-unit assessment is a
  sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: a whole-class paper task of about 15 minutes (cut a new job
  into its parts and order the steps, sort new picture cards, circle the first wrong block and draw the fix, count and
  describe two new block towers) collected from every child, while the teacher hears one spoken answer a child in
  five groups of six (about three minutes a group: about 20 seconds a child, plus the group's change-over and the
  checklist mark). Early readers need cards read aloud, and a teacher reading to the class cannot hear groups at once,
  so on a check sitting the read-aloud items are done together first, each card read twice, and the picture-only
  items are done alone while the groups are heard (the scope gives each check's minute plan). Where a unit has a
  second spoken look-for, it is heard earlier on an unseen item, during the
  partner work of the unit's last teaching sessions, after the row was first taught: half the class in each of two
  sessions, about six minutes of teacher time a session; the check's cell names where. Partner talk alone is never a
  child's scored record. Each owned id has its own look-for: a "create" or "debug" id
  (E1-PRO-PD-04, E1-PRO-TR-07) is met by a child laying out or reordering block cards, a "use methods" id
  (E1-DAT-DC-08) by the child collecting a count and a description and using both to answer a question, a "break
  down" id (E1-ALG-PS-01) by the child marking the parts of a whole job before ordering the steps, an "explain",
  "describe" or "examine" id (E1-ALG-IM-03, E1-PRO-RD-06,
  E1-SYS-HW-11, E1-SYS-IM-13, E1-SYS-SE-12, E1-DAT-IM-10, E1-SOC-CE-16) by a spoken answer, and a "recognize",
  "identify", "compare" or "differentiate" id by a sort or a choice on paper with one spoken reason where the id asks
  for one. Units 2 and 4 are assessed in the course-level check that follows them (Days). No score comes from a
  session that first teaches a row. The respond day reteaches by error letter and repeats the missed item in a new
  form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per child: 15 devices for a class of
  30, with the few-device and one-screen versions below for rooms with fewer. Device sessions: 5 of the 32, unit 1
  session 3 (the parts of a real device and their jobs, and the teacher signing out) and unit 3 sessions 2, 3, 5 and
  6 (the picture-block tool, with an event block). At about 10 minutes at the device in unit 1 session 3 and about 18
  in each unit 3 session, that is about 80 minutes of device time, so about 40 minutes per child at the keys over the
  year with pair turns. 27 of the 32 sessions, about five sixths, are away from a device, more than APPROACH.md's
  "about two thirds" for kindergarten to grade 2 (ours, untested): the E1 rows are written unplugged first, the
  unplugged form teaches them as well, and the device days carry the parts that gain from a screen. That sits below
  APPROACH.md's device share; For the lead 6 proposes the line that states it. Grade 2 keeps these five device
  sessions' worth and adds one (6 of 32, its repeat programs on the editor), so the share does not fall from grade
  to grade. Every device day is written four ways (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at
  stations with the other pairs on the paper version of the same idea, one screen with a child at the device for each
  step, or no device. No E1 id needs a device to be met in full: the boundary statements ask for no computational
  tool, block cards with a start card on the floor grid meet E1-PRO-PD-04, E1-PRO-RD-06 and E1-PRO-TR-07 in full, and
  the picture cards of device parts meet E1-SYS-HW-11, so the course record never marks a grade 1 id part met for lack
  of a device.
- **Paper path.** Every session runs on paper, cards, tape and objects; the picture-block tool of unit 3 has the
  block-card program beside it (picture block cards with a "when tapped" start card, laid on the same floor grid and
  "run" by a partner when the start card is tapped). No tool asks for a student account, stores a child's name or
  sends anything off the device (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety"); unit 1's login is a paper pretend login with a
  given pretend password.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 1 children are early readers. No code or word is read alone: blocks and
  cards are icons with a short label (start, when tapped), every word is said aloud and shown with a picture or an
  object, and the teacher writes. Each unit names five to eight words taught in depth from its rows' word lists, with
  the everyday words that have a computing meaning taught as two meanings (bug, run, mouse, pattern). Sentence frames:
  "First it ___, then it ___", "I think it will ___ because ___" and "When I tap, it ___". A child may answer with
  arrows, pointing, a drawing or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. Unit 5 may be left out (homes, places and family life are its ground): E1-SOC-CE-16 and E1-SOC-HI-14 are
  then not claimed that year, and the course still teaches computing and society through E1-SOC-HU-15 in unit 2.
  Unit 4 may be swapped for a different set of neutral class questions and things to measure, not left out, so the
  course always claims E1-DAT-DC-08, E1-DAT-DI-09 and E1-DAT-IM-10 (the bar part 5 fails a course with nothing on
  data). Units 1 to 3 carry the device rules and the models the rest of the year uses and are not swapped or left out.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Floor-grid sessions run in a
  cleared space with tape that lies flat, children walk the grid (no running, no blindfolds), and device cords stay
  behind the table; devices are carried with two hands, food and drink stay away from them, and headphones, where a
  school uses them, are set to a low volume by the teacher first. The scope and sequence copies these lines into each
  floor-grid and device day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $3 (printing part-and-job cards,
  rule story cards and the paper pretend logins), unit 2 $4 (task part cards, step-card sets, attribute-card sets and
  the people-or-computer activity cards), unit 3 $8 (two rolls of masking tape for a floor grid, printing the block
  cards with start cards and the value picture cards), unit 4 $5 (sticky dots and chart paper for the picture graph,
  record cards, question cards; the linking cubes and objects to measure are classroom items), unit 5 $2 (printing
  the place pictures and the before-and-after activity strips), about $22 in all.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 has online-safety content (passwords, someone asking for
  one) and disability content (adapted parts shown beside the usual ones: a large-button mouse, a switch, headphones,
  a screen magnifier). Before it reads, the agent studies the pilot's starting pack: the Autistic Self Advocacy
  Network's "Identity-First Language" page (autisticadvocacy.org/about-asan/identity-first-language/), one community's
  own account of language and identity, not taken as every community's; the National Center on Disability and
  Journalism's Disability Language Style Guide (ncdj.org/style-guide/, free, English and Spanish); and, as background
  on accessible hardware only, Section508.gov's "Hardware ICT Overview" (section508.gov/test/ict-hardware-overview/).
  The agent widens the pack to the brief's five sources, two of them owned by the communities whose devices the
  finished cards show, and records them. It then checks the keep-safe section carries health's "If a student tells
  you something" steps word for word (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground). Unit 2's
  people-or-computer sort touches feelings (comfort a friend, notice a mood): the agent checks no card asks a child
  about their own feelings or a classmate's, and no card says a computer feels or thinks. Units 4 and 5 touch homes,
  families and the town (class data, community needs, devices at home, talking to someone far away): the agent checks
  no question asks about a child's home, food, pets, devices or a family member's work or whereabouts. The course's
  checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on in the laboratory sense, so no safety agent read is
  planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity read confirms are on each day.
