# Course frame: Dance, grades 9 to 12

Course `dance-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `dance-9-12`), the high school band of dance, run as levels, not grades: Level 1 (Proficient), Level 2 (Accomplished), Level 3 (Advanced, at honors depth) and the optional Level 4 (Advanced, deepened: a year the student leads), one full year each (docs/courses/arts/STANDARD.md 3.2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the shape of the visual arts K to 2 pilot and its fix pass (docs/reviews/frame-visual-arts-grades-k-to-2-fixes-2026-11-25.md); the scope and sequence sets the days later, one level at a time, and each level's part of this file is that level's frame. The Year column holds the level. In data/progressions/arts.csv the grade key 9, 10, 11 and 12 stands for Levels 1 to 4; it is a data key, never a student's grade, and no page names a grade for a level.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 19: five in each of Levels 1 to 3, four in Level 4. Levels 1 to 3 share one shape, so a thread comes
  back one level harder in the same slot a year later: an opening unit (the Level 1 bridge, or the Level 2 and 3
  review that doubles as placement; no owned id), a body, rhythm and rehearsal unit (elements and technique), a
  making unit (ideas and form), a watching and changing unit (revision and response) and a production and research
  unit (staging and connecting) that ends the level with the class concert. Each owning unit joins two progression
  threads, one of creating or performing and one of performing, responding or connecting, so every unit makes or
  dances, studies another artist's dance and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Level 4 is one sustained project in four
  phases (a series of dances from a proposal, critique, a led showing, a capstone), with its checkpoints as check
  days and critique on its respond days (APPROACH "High school levels and music").
- **Days.** 640: 160 a level for four levels (480 for the three levels a school must offer to reach Advanced; one
  level is a complete course, STANDARD.md 3.2). Each level: 156 days in its units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One lesson of about 45 minutes a day, five a
  week, 32 weeks: 160 lessons of 45 minutes is 120 hours, the time the national Proficient descriptor names for one
  high school arts course (STANDARD.md 3.2 and 3.4). The 45 minutes hold the set-up (chairs and desks back, the floor
  checked, spots marked: about 3 minutes; students dance in their own comfortable clothes, and no change of clothes
  is assumed), a warm-up (about 7), the teaching, dancing or writing, a cool-down with the closing line or log entry
  (about 4) and putting the room back (about 3); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes of its own on day 2,
  after the warm-up (day 2: set-up 3, warm-up 7, retrieval 10, teaching 18, cool-down and closing line 4, room back
  3: 45 minutes); the retrieval combines the unit's assumed threads, and any reteach replaces part of the teaching,
  never adds time; the short path keeps the same block in the unit's second session. Each unit's days
  include its end-of-unit assessment and, since every owning unit is over 25 days, its mid-unit assessment. Unit
  counts follow the rows' load: the body and rehearsal unit and the watching and changing unit each own six ids (in
  the first, three performed and three planned or analyzed; in the second, four written response ids beside a
  revision and a notation task), so they are longest (38 days in Level 1, 40 in Levels 2 and 3); the making unit
  owns four ids (34 days); the production unit owns five ids and holds the concert's rehearsals (34 days in Level 1,
  36 in Levels 2 and 3, where a whole concert and then concerts in more than one place are staged). The Level 1
  bridge is 12 days, the Level 2 and 3 reviews 6. In Level 4 the studio phase is longest (70 days: proposal, a series
  of dances and its revisions), then the led showing (40), the critique set (24) and the capstone (22). These are
  course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places one a school day, so course day n of a level
  falls in that level's week n / 5, rounded up. National time figure: STANDARD.md 3.4 carries the dance educators'
  association's K to 8 figures only; its high school lines are not read at source, so this frame states none (For
  the lead 2). The course gives 225 minutes a week, 120 hours a level, and 60 hours on the short path. The course is
  written so a teacher without dance training can teach it; a school with a dance specialist uses that teacher.
- **The short path.** A school that cannot give dance every day (a semester, a rotation or alternate days) keeps every
  unit and every id of Levels 1 to 3 in 80 days by merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.2). Days on that
  path: Level 1, units of 6, 18, 16, 19 and 17 days; Levels 2 and 3, units of 3, 20, 17, 20 and 16 days; each level's
  two checks keep 1 check day and 1 respond day (76 in units and 4 for checks: 80). The bridge's 6 days are 7.5
  percent of 80, inside the bar's 10 percent limit on days outside the course's standards (STANDARD.md 3.2). Fluency
  days fold into the warm-ups, and each row's understanding days merge into one; a merged day never drops a
  critique, a revision, a rehearsal before a showing, a mid-unit or end-of-unit assessment or a respond day. Level 4
  has no short path: STANDARD.md 3.2 sets the 80-day path for the three levels of each discipline only. The scope and
  sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, dance performance standards, by id only, each named with its line
  in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The level rule binds
  (STANDARD.md 3.2): Level 1 claims only the 21 Proficient ids (`.Ia`, `.Ib`, `.Ic`), Level 2 only the 21 Accomplished
  ids (`.II`), Level 3 only the 21 Advanced ids (`.III`): 63 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and
  assessed in its own level. Level 4 owns no id: it assesses the Level 3 `.IIIa` ids again on new, self-directed work
  (the rows' `~` codes, owned in Level 3). Advanced is claimed by assessed work, not by finishing Level 3. Level 4 reassesses the 11 Advanced
  ids listed in its units; an unmet Advanced id is completed only when new evidence shows its full action, and taking
  or finishing Level 4 does not establish it (the scope of that promise is a question for Cory, For the lead 10). No grade 8 id is claimed (the bridge teaches grade 8
  techniques and words as readiness only). Every level touches all four artistic processes (creating, performing,
  responding, connecting; bar criterion 1). In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works":
  the processes and anchor standards the unit works on, and the dances it studies. Each id's action was checked
  against its words-file line and the rows; the 2026-11-26 independent read checked them against the national text and its
  additions are in units 2, 5, 7, 8, 12, 14 and 15; each unit plan still checks its ids' actions against the national
  standard's own text before it is written, as the pilot's fix pass found a words line can drift (For the lead 3 names the two ids where the course's rules shape
  how an id is met). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine
  criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md);
  the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file; each level's scope and sequence still states its id count
  (21) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Entry and placement.** A student new to dance starts at Level 1, whatever their grade; Level 1's first unit
  assumes no earlier arts learning. Each level requires the one before or a placement: the Level 2 and 3 review units
  double as placement, where a student new to the school shows work scored against the previous level's year-end
  check and its last unit's guide, and starts at the next level if it meets them (STANDARD.md 3.2). The scope and
  sequence names the level, never a grade.
- **More than one level in one room.** Many schools teach dance levels together. Because Levels 1 to 3 share the
  same five slots, a mixed room runs the same slot at once (body and rehearsal, making, watching and changing,
  production), each student on their own level's rows, ids, guide and checks; the warm-up, the works watched and the
  concert are shared. The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Dance here, motor skill in physical education.** Dance in this course is the art: making, performing for an
  audience, responding to and connecting dance. Movement sequences and dance forms as movement skill are physical
  education's (STANDARD.md 6; physical education grade 9 unit 5, "Dance by design"). Dance teaches the movement
  technique its own artistic performance standards need (balances, alignment, safe landings, rolls and slides) and
  assumes no earlier physical education instruction; physical education develops movement competence, fitness and
  teamwork in its own contexts. Physical education designs a sequence as a movement problem; dance owns
  choreography with stated intent, the motif and response. Units 2 and 3 name that unit in their connections lines
  (Cross-subject, below).
- **Base text.** None. Dance is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open dance recordings). Dances studied are shown from
  public domain films (early films in the Library of Congress's collections) and, for living or recent makers and
  for every tradition, from a description in our own words with a link to the maker's, the company's or an archive's
  own page, never a copy or an embed (APPROACH "Rights"). Each dance named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless
  its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; the unit plan confirms each title, year and maker on that
  page and records whether a maker is living, as of its date. A swapped dance must keep the unit's ids met and the
  band's range. The band's dances come from Africa (South Africa, Senegal through the École des Sables, Sierra
  Leone through Asadata Dafora), the Americas (the United States including the Yup'ik people through Emily Johnson,
  Brazil), Asia (Taiwan, Korea, Japan, and Bangladesh and India through Akram Khan's story), Europe (Denmark,
  Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, the United Kingdom, France and Russia through The Rite of Spring) and the
  Pacific (Samoa and New Zealand), from 1836 to today, with living makers in every level (APPROACH "Works and their
  settings"). No dance named in the Dance K to 2, 3 to 5 or 6 to 8 frames is shown again as the same work, with
  named returns: the serpentine dance comes back as Loie Fuller's own design in unit 10 (the K to 2 band saw
  Annabelle Whitford Moore's film); Swan Lake comes back as Dada Masilo's version (unit 13; K to 2 saw Ivanov's
  Little Swans); Germaine Acogny's École des Sables, met in 3 to 5 unit 1, comes back as the school that made the
  2021 Rite of Spring staging (unit 9); and a few makers come back with a different work (For the lead 9). The unseen dance in every check is a short Curio Class original
  danced live by the teacher from a step card, or a written description, never an embed (APPROACH "Scoring creative
  work"). Curio Class writes every step card, work card, prompt card, notation sheet, record sheet, process log page,
  family note and every unseen dance.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Body and rhythm: low partner balances at contrasting levels,
  syncopation and accents, energy through the whole body (Level 1); purposeful space in ground and aerial sequences,
  unpredictable time from an inner rhythm, phrases started with energy and linked (Level 2); the fullest range of
  space toward an audience, layered rhythms, dynamics across a broad range (Level 3). Making: many sources tested for
  one piece and an artist's statement (Level 1), invented and established movement blended across styles and a
  statement of aesthetic (Level 2), risk, a personal voice and a statement of cultural and artistic viewpoint (Level
  3), a self-directed series from a proposal (Level 4). Revising and recording: devices and structure reworked and
  two notation methods compared on one section (Level 1), revision against criteria and a plan to record a whole
  dance (Level 2), every revision logged and defended and a complete dance notated (Level 3). Rehearsing and staging:
  a rehearsal planned with peers and analyzed, a production design option chosen (Level 1); solo and group
  rehearsals planned, a whole concert staged from a production plan (Level 2); rehearsals led, concerts staged in
  more than one place (Level 3); the student's own showing led (Level 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a phrase, an element
  or a device shown in named steps with the step card, or a dance watched with the unit's words (the artistic process
  "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated practice with feedback (the warm-up,
  a phrase run three times for recall, a syncopation drill, a notation drill of eight counts); in high school it is
  the seven-minute warm-up and two or three fluency days a unit. Application: making, rehearsing, drafting a
  statement or analysis, and revising; the second application day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask,
  suggest), and the next day's revision is application too. Check: a showing scored live on the guide, or a written
  sitting with unseen items. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second
  showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, the mid-unit assessment and its respond day,
  then understanding, fluency, application with the critique, revision, the end-of-unit assessment, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit or mid-unit item or a look-for, named id by id in the unit's
  "Evidence, id by id" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one item for each
  id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on a showing's guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live as each group dances; a written item in a sitting on an unseen
  Curio Class dance or description; a kept piece (a process log entry, an artist's statement, a notation sheet, a
  rehearsal or production plan) drafted, given written feedback and revised on earlier counted days, then scored;
  and a spoken item in a peer discussion, marked live. Two kinds of check day, never mixed on one day. A showing day:
  set-up 3, warm-up 7, six groups of five dance for about 4 minutes each (24) while the rest are the audience,
  cool-down and the self-check on the guide 5, room back 3: 42 minutes; a group the teacher wants to see again may be
  recorded on the school's device that day (with the family's yes, or the student's own at 18 or over) and the
  recording stays there (APPROACH "Kept work"). A written sitting: set-up 2, the teacher dances the unseen phrase twice
  or reads the description (about 4), written items alone (about 33), collecting 3: 42 minutes. Either way the
  teacher watches one thing at a time. An end day holds at most one full performance. No score comes from the day a
  phrase, an element or a device is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during
  the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change.
  One piece a unit goes into the portfolio (a log entry, a statement, a notation sheet, or a recording kept on the
  school's device) with a one-line reflection (bar criterion 8); the portfolio is the evidence Levels 3 and 4 build
  their growth reviews from.
- **Taking part without dancing alone.** No student must dance alone for the class: every showing is in a group, a
  solo task has a duet or trio version marked on the same look-fors, and a student may show at the teacher's spot.
  A student who cannot move freely dances a seated or one-limb version, or takes a choreographer's, rehearsal
  director's or production role for a task where the id allows it. Each unit plan maps the student's movement
  version to the look-fors it shows; a director's, production or counting role counts only for what it shows, and
  where an owned movement action stays unseen, the teacher sees a short movement version with the student's usual
  supports and records the support. A student who does not want to be recorded is marked live.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Level 1 assumes no earlier dance learning: Dance, grades 6 to 8 is not a core course,
  so no Level 1 unit assumes it was taken (lesson 32). Each Level 1 row assumes its grade 8 row (da-g8-ideas to
  da-g8-connect, dance 6 to 8, grade 8 year), which for a student straight from that course is one grade back and for
  most is never met; the bridge (unit 1) teaches each one's techniques and words to every student as readiness, so
  each later Level 1 unit opens with a ten-minute retrieval task on the bridge's version on day 2, after the warm-up, and day 1 stays
  clean for the hook and the question. Each Level 2 row assumes its Level 1 row and each Level 3 row its Level 2 row:
  for a student taking the levels in consecutive years that is the year before, so each unit opens with a ten-minute retrieval
  task on day 2, after the warm-up; for a student returning after a gap or new to the school, the review unit (units 6 and 11)
  is the readiness check and its placement showing, given before the year's first owning unit opens, and each
  retrieval task carries one reteach of its threads together, inside the teaching time (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline look"). Level 4's
  rows assume the Level 3 rows of the year before: the same day 2 retrieval block in units 16 to 19. The Seams tables give
  each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 9 to 12 have no stagger rule but the finals week (CALENDAR.md rule 4). Levels 1 to
  3: mid-year check in week 17 (Level 1 course days 85 and 86 of the level, its respond day in week 18; Levels 2 and
  3 days 81 and 82), year-end check in week 32, the finals week (days 159 and 160). Level 4, usually taken in grade 12:
  checkpoint 1 in week 15 (days 71 and 72), checkpoint 2 in week 28 (days 137 and 138), so a senior who leaves early
  has finished it. The production unit's concert (Levels 1 to 3) is its own end-of-unit assessment, in weeks 31 and
  32, before the year-end sitting.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** A senior may be in any level. Levels 1 to 3 teach to their last week; the year-end check sits
  in week 32. If seniors leave before week 32, a senior sits the same year-end check in the school's senior exam window
  and its respond day folds into a written note on the marked items; if that window falls before the concert, the
  senior's concert mark comes from the dress rehearsal showing, scored on the same guide; the production unit's
  critique, revision and rehearsal days are never the ones that fold. Level 4's last unit (the capstone, weeks 28 to
  32) is assessed by the capstone talk, which a senior leaving early gives in week 30, folding the two last talk
  rehearsal days into one. A student of 18 or over gets the family note and gives their own yes to be shown or
  recorded (AGENTS.md, Cory 2026-11-22).
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a dance for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5). A
  student aged 18 or older receives the family note directly and makes their own leave-out and consent choices; the
  school's rules still apply. A unit that works on an earlier unit's dance carries a fallback for a class that left
  that unit out: unit 4 revises an original Curio Class practice dance with its process record; unit 10's concert
  uses the unit 8 dance after a short counted revision and rehearsal sequence; units 17 to 19 use an existing
  student series or a supplied original practice series. Missing words and routines are retrieved or taught before
  use. Supplied work serves the later unit's own ids and never recovers an omitted unit's creating ids. Units that a course page names: unit 5 (Asadata Dafora's Kykunkor stages a West African
  ritual story, studied by watching and reading only; La Sylphide keeps DA:Cn11.1.Ia met beside a unit 3 work) and
  unit 9 (The Rite of Spring stages an invented sacrificial rite; Ahn Eun-me's work keeps every response id met). Swapping a dance keeps
  the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed
  that level.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 in a cleared classroom, hall, gym or stage with room for every student to
  stretch both arms out without touching another, floor tape or chalk for spots and zones, a speaker or the voice
  and clapping for the beat; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4,
  APPROACH "Supplies"). Production design uses what the room has (house lights on or off, a window, a doorway, plain
  clothing in one color, sound from one device or live percussion). One student at home: the parent is the audience
  and the critique partner, a group piece has a solo or parent-and-student version, and the showing is to the family
  or a recorded take on the family's device. The teacher needs no dance training: each unit's teacher page says what
  to look for, in plain words, and every unseen dance is on a step card the teacher can learn in a few minutes.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as floor tape,
  a hand drum and fabric counted once, printing of step cards, notation sheets, log pages and programs), all optional
  beside the free path: Level 1 $10, $5, $5, $10, $25 ($55); Level 2 $5, $5, $5, $10, $30 ($55); Level 3 $5, $5, $5,
  $10, $35 ($60); Level 4 $10, $5, $30, $5 ($50). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student, and no student account. One teacher device may play music and show a linked dance;
  every day runs without it, from the printed work card (a description and a link the teacher opens), the teacher
  showing the phrase, and the voice, claps or a drum for the beat. One school device for recording rehearsals, shared
  by groups in turn, where the school has one. Owned ids that name technology: DA:Pr5.1.Ic names video as one
  analysis strategy among others, and a paper-path class meets it in full with a peer observer's sheet (what the
  observer saw, counted against the rehearsal goal); DA:Cr3.1.IIb asks for notation or media, and notation meets it in
  full; production design (DA:Pr6.1.Ib, DA:Pr6.1.IIb, DA:Pr6.1.IIIb) is met with what the room has, designed on paper.
  A paper-path student meets every id in full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device
  (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on printed cards, the floor and the body; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  beat keeper, a notation sheet maker, a rehearsal planner) has a printed version that does the same job and needs no
  student account.
- **Reading load.** High school students read step cards, work cards and the process log themselves; a work card is
  at most one page in our own words. English learners: each unit teaches its five to six words in depth, with a
  picture, a movement and a frame ("The pattern repeats to show ___." "My revision changed ___ because ___."), a
  student may show the word in movement instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student danced apart from
  what they wrote or said (APPROACH "Supports"). Written analysis practices argument with evidence (Cross-subject).
- **Safety read.** Every unit is a moving unit, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the Nova
  Scotia Department of Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades Primary-12" (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf, its dance section on printed page 43), its sections on dance and on the activity space, and the dance educators' association's
  opportunity-to-learn standards (2017 revision, https://nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/NDEO%20Opportunity%20to%20Learn%20Standards%202017%20rev.pdf), its lines on the dance space and floor. Floor rule:
  jumps, leaps, rolls, slides use a level, resilient surface the school confirms suitable; a wooden finish over concrete, tile or a
  loose mat on a hard floor is not enough by itself. On an unsuitable floor the class does those moves low and
  without impact, and their look-for is seen in a suitable school space before it is marked met, never from a
  written stand-in. Where the room cannot hold every group moving at once, groups rotate through marked traveling lanes and the
  waiting groups sit outside the movement area.
  Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): a clear floor with room
  to move arms out, checked for spills and objects before each session; bare feet or soft-soled shoes on a clean
  floor, never socks on a smooth floor; a warm-up before and a cool-down after every dancing session; no lifts (no
  student's whole weight on another); balances low to the floor, by agreement, with a solo version always offered;
  no jumping from heights or any raised surface (aerial work is jumps and leaps from the floor, with landings taught
  first: bent knees, heel down); no inverted moves (no head spins, headstands, handstands or flips); partner contact
  only by agreement and never required; water and rest offered; pain is a stop signal, never danced through.
  Production: no student on a ladder or a raised platform edge, cables taped down, no hot lamp touched, no haze and no
  strobe or flashing light; an off-site venue follows the school's own field trip rules. Each unit's last column gives
  its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the dances with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 2 (Vincent
  Mantsoe, South Africa; tap's African American history), unit 3 (Akram Khan's story of the India and Bangladesh
  border), unit 4 (Emily Johnson and the Yup'ik people; Xhosa history in Gregory Maqoma's work), unit 5 (Asadata
  Dafora, Sierra Leone and West African dance in 1930s New York), unit 7 (Brazil), unit 9 (Ahn Eun-me and Korea; The
  Rite of Spring and its 2021 cast from African countries), unit 12 (Lin Hwai-min and tai chi, Taiwan; Ronald K.
  Brown and West African dance), unit 13 (Dada Masilo and Tswana dance, South Africa), unit 14 (Dance Theatre of
  Harlem and free Creoles of color; butoh and Kazuo Ohno, Japan; Lemi Ponifasio and Samoa). The health-habit ids (units 2, 7 and 12) study food
  choices and eating plans through an invented dancer or dancers in general; no task asks about a student's own diet,
  weight or body, and a student's own habits come in only as warm-up, rest and safe practice, by the student's choice
  (APPROACH "Sensitive ground", Cory 2026-11-21). Every task that ties dance to a student's own life (units 4, 9, 13,
  15 and 19) offers an invented or public option marked the same; a student names only a heritage they choose to
  name and no one is asked to represent a group (da-g11-form). A dance from another culture is watched, and learned
  only where the community's own teaching says it is open to be taught; no costume, no accent, no imitation of a
  people, and no sacred or ceremonial dance as an exercise, warm-up, costume or imitation (the rows' teacher note;
  Cory, 2026-11-20). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: five 45-minute lessons a week, a cleared floor, step
  cards, work cards, notation sheets and the process log. Activities: phrases and elements taught in steps, dances
  watched with their setting, making and rehearsing with a critique and a revision, written analysis, a showing each
  unit and a concert each level. Outputs: a portfolio over the levels (one piece a unit, with reflections), a concert
  a level, two marks a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the level's 21 ids
  (Levels 1 to 3), the Level 3 ids met on new work (Level 4), and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried
  with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

### Where the threads end

This is the last dance course. The Level 3 rows (da-g11-ideas, da-g11-form and da-g11-revise to da-g12-studio;
da-g11-elements, da-g11-technique and da-g11-stage to da-g12-perform; da-g11-respond to da-g12-criticism;
da-g11-connect to da-g12-connect) hand on to Level 4, and the four Level 4 rows hand on nothing (their `hands_on`
cells are empty). Every thread ends at Advanced: owned and assessed in Level 3, and assessed again on new work in
Level 4 for a student who takes it. Beyond the course, the portfolio and the growth review are what a student carries
to college dance study or a community company.

## Cross-subject

One dance row of the band carries a cross_subject lean: da-g9-technique (unit 2, Level 1) names physical education's
g9-dance-design and health's g9-phw-evaluate-habits. A Level 1 student may be in any grade from 9 to 12, so each
lean is same-year only for a student in grade 9; the unit teaches what it needs itself either way, and every lean
names a course a school must offer (physical education's two required years, health's semester; lesson 32).

### English language arts, grades 9 to 12

Written analysis, comparison and critique (units 4, 9, 14 and 17) practice argument writing with evidence
(W.9-10.1, W.11-12.1): practiced, not owned. If ELA has not yet taught the claim-and-evidence form that year, the
dance unit's analysis frame gives it (a claim, a time-stamped or described moment as evidence, a reason) and the
teacher models it on the first analysis day.

### Social studies

Units 4, 5, 9, 14 and 15 teach their works with time and place using social studies' sourcing habits (STANDARD.md 6;
who made it, when, where, for what): practiced, not owned. Each unit teaches the setting it uses on the work card
itself (bar criterion 7). No math beyond counts of eight and beats is used (lesson 13).
