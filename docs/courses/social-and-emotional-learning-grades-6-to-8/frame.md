# Course frame: Social and Emotional Learning, Grades 6 to 8

Course `social-and-emotional-learning-grades-6-to-8` (catalog id `sel-6-8`), grade 6, grade 7 and grade 8, one band course taught over three school years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 on the shape of the pilot (docs/courses/social-and-emotional-learning-grades-k-to-2/frame.md) and its two reads; the scope and sequence sets the ordered days and school-day positions later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 18, six a year (STANDARD section 3): units 1 to 6 in grade 6 ("New school, new me"), 7 to 12 in grade 7 ("Pressure and belonging"), 13 to 18 in grade 8 ("Choices that count"), numbered through the band so a seam or a check can name one unit without a year beside it. The Year column of the Units table names the year. Each year teaches all 40 sub-skills of the five competencies (self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making): grade 6 chooses among options, grade 7 takes in another person's view, grade 8, the owner year, chooses when every option costs something. Each year's order and unit titles follow STANDARD section 3 and the progression rows' unit notes (data/progressions/sel.csv). A year is whole on its own: it holds its own mid-year and year-end checks, and no year waits on another course.
- **Days.** 192 sessions: 64 a year, two sessions a week over 32 weeks, 30 minutes a session in grades 6 to 8 (STANDARD section 2), so 60 minutes a week. Unit sizes follow Desk q-fix-sel-1 exactly (For the lead 1): each year has four three-group units of 11 sessions (3 groups of Understanding, Fluency, Application, then the unit's Check and Respond) and two two-group units of 10 (2 groups of Understanding, two Fluency sessions, Application, then Check and Respond), 4 x 11 + 2 x 10 = 64. The course-level checks are the Check and Respond sessions of each year's third and sixth unit (frame-write, after-core courses: a course-level check right after a unit is that unit's end-of-unit assessment when it covers the unit), so those units show 9 (three-group) or 8 (two-group) days in the Units table and the check and its respond carry the other 2: each year is 60 unit days and 4 check days. No unit runs over 25 days, so none has a mid-unit assessment. Sizes by year: grade 6 runs 10, 11, 8 + 2, 11, 11, 9 + 2; grade 7 runs 11, 10, 8 + 2, 11, 11, 9 + 2; grade 8 runs 10, 11, 9 + 2, 11, 10, 9 + 2 (grade 8 puts its second two-group unit fifth so its mid-year check lands on school day 78, clear of the grade 8 science check on day 75). Short path, one session a week (32 a year, STANDARD C2, Desk L43: fewer minutes, never fewer units): a three-group unit is 5 (3 sessions where each group's Understanding and Fluency share one, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond together); a two-group unit is 6 (2 groups of 2 shared sessions, 1 Application, 1 Check and Respond), 4 x 5 + 2 x 6 = 32. In units 3 and 6 of a year the shared Check and Respond session is the course-level check, check and respond in one session. Readiness: no readiness check. Grade 6 rows assume the grade 5 rows SEL 3 to 5 taught the year before, so grade 6 unit 1 opens with retrieval like every later unit (a row last used the year before is a retrieval, never a readiness check; after-core scope lead calls, DESK 2026-11-26); a class that did not take SEL 3 to 5 gets the grade 5 card forms (goal steps, stop-think-choose, the problem size meter) in session 1's first ten minutes as the bridge, claiming nothing, as SEL 3 to 5's first session does for a class without K to 2. Every later unit's rows assume the year before's rows, which the course taught, so each unit opens with retrieval inside the session opening, not a readiness check. A "Given:" readiness item is used only where a school left out the earlier unit (STANDARD section 5.6).
- **Inside the 30 minutes.** Every session has one opening of about 5 minutes (one retrieval item or returning skill with its card, never two openings), about 20 minutes of teaching and rehearsal, and about 5 minutes to mark the class sheet, write the one-line reason the session asks for and collect cards. An Understanding session names and models at most three new sub-skills, each with its card step and one invented case every student works once; every group in this course holds two or three sub-skills, so no unit needs a second Understanding session. Units with a class project (6, 12, 18) take its planning and carrying out from the 20 minutes of their Application sessions, and their unit plans say how.
- **Standard.** CASEL 5 competencies, as written in data/standards/casel-5.txt (40 sub-skills, the five competency names and the codes CASEL.Competency.N). Grade 8 owns every code for the band; grade 6 and grade 7 rows begin each one (~ in the rows), so their Owned standards cells are empty and What students learn names each begun code with its grade 8 owner unit. Each grade 8 owner row teaches the whole of its sub-skill, the one step its code names, at the grade 8 demand (a cost on every option). The seventh column, Core link and routines, holds for SEL the unit's one link to a core course of the same year (bar part 9 item 9): a lean the teacher may use, not a strand. Library: AASL.V.D.1 is taught with SM3 (owner unit 15), AASL.V.D.2 with SA8 (unit 15) and AASL.III.D.2 with RS4 (unit 18) in every SEL band (library STANDARD section 4, Desk q-fix-library-1; data/progressions/library-excluded.csv names them placed in SEL). The three owner rows name the codes in their standard cells and these units' Owned standards name them (scripts/progression.py accepts the three for SEL, owned once in each band's last year, 2026-11-26). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against APPROACH's kinds of day, the lens (docs/agents/lenses/sel.md) and the bar's part 9; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to add.
- **Base text.** None. Every case, scene card, message thread, data page, step card and written page is Curio Class's own, with invented students, families, apps and towns; no real app, platform or brand is named. Two settled changes from history, one a year, so no case repeats in the band: grade 7 unit 9 takes the Nineteenth Amendment (1920: the vote could no longer be denied on account of sex), and grade 8 unit 16 takes a disability-access law (the Americans with Disabilities Act, signed July 26, 1990; Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, 1973, only if the unit plan first opens a federal page for it). Each is taught from federal pages, opened 2026-11-26: for the amendment, the National Archives' 19th Amendment milestone page (https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/19th-amendment, ratified August 18, 1920); for the ADA, ADA.gov's introduction to the ADA (https://www.ada.gov/topics/intro-to-ada/, the law's reach) and the National Archives' ADA research page (https://www.archives.gov/research/americans-with-disabilities, "Signed on July 26, 1990"). The National Archives milestone list ends in 1965 and has no 504 or ADA page, so 504 needs its own federal page, opened at the unit plan (browser check if it blocks). Each is also taught and from one published account by the community concerned (women's suffrage history from a women's history museum or association for unit 9; a disability-led organization's history of the 504 protests or the ADA for unit 16), named at the unit plan (FOUNDATION section 7). Each page tells the law apart from the slower work of carrying it out; for unit 9, after 1920 many women of color were still kept from the polls, Black women by discrimination and intimidation, and Native American women, who were not counted as US citizens until 1924 (National Park Service, "Who was excluded?", https://www.nps.gov/articles/who-was-excluded-women-s-suffrage.htm, opened 2026-11-26). Brown v. Board of Education stays in SEL 3 to 5 (grade 5 unit 15) and returns in SEL 9 to 12 only as a stated return at new depth (For the lead 11). The clashes of ways of working in units 6, 7 and 16 are invented students' own ways; in each, one way draws on a cited source and is shown as that one student's way, never a whole culture's: a cultural or education source on work or feedback customs in units 6 and 7, picked at the unit plan apart from SEL 3 to 5's and PE's picks, and in unit 16 a Deaf and hearing team drawing on the Deaf-Hearing Communication Centre's communication tips (https://www.dhcc.org/resources/communication-tips, named by the Codex read 2026-11-25).
- **Shared steps with health and PE.** The talk turn card, calm-down steps, I-statement frame, goal steps card and refusal options use the one set of words in STANDARD section 7 ("Shared steps"), so a student meets one card across SEL, health and PE. Where health or PE teaches a card first in a year, the SEL unit opens with it as retrieval where the school runs that course, and teaches it itself where it does not (Cross-subject, Health and PE).
- **Leave-out.** A school may leave out a unit (STANDARD section 5.6). Where a later unit uses a card the left-out unit made or grew, that unit's first Understanding session teaches the card's one-step form itself in its first ten minutes, in place of the retrieval opening and part of that session's rehearsal, and claims none of the left-out unit's sub-skills: the feelings scale with the exact word (unit 2; first needed by unit 3, then 4, 10, 15: unit 3's bridge models naming a character's feeling from a clue and telling mild from strong, in place of the retrieval opening and the first five minutes of rehearsal); the think-feel-do chain with other possible thoughts (unit 2; units 10, 13); the five-step calm-down steps and the five-step stop-think-choose (unit 3; units 4, 5, 6, then 8, 10, 15); the talk turn card's first step, choose how to talk, the refusal options and the I-statement (unit 4; units 6, 8, 11, 14, 17); the fairness questions and the who-is-affected map (unit 5; units 9, 12, 13, 16); the upstander options (unit 6; units 11, 18). Grade 6 units 1 and 2 use the grade 5 forms of the cards, which SEL 3 to 5 taught; session 1's ten-minute bridge (the grade 5 card forms) covers a class that did not take it.
- **Grade 6 Applications.** The scope and unit plans name the pressure each grade 6 Application works under (onlookers, an online pull, a deadline), which the rows already hold: a choice among options with no pressure named is not the grade 6 depth (STANDARD section 3 depth ladder; 3 to 12 expert read J2).
- **Kinds of day.** APPROACH "Kinds of day": Understanding (the skill shown on an invented case, then the steps named on a step card), Fluency (the steps rehearsed in pairs or small groups on new cards until quick), Application (the steps used on a new case, a message thread or a real class task, with the choice and its reason written), Check (a written scenario page from unseen cases and a watched rehearsal from an unseen card, against look-fors), Respond (a short reteach for the step not yet seen, and the rehearsal again). Every session rehearses; no session is posters and discussion alone. Each year is taught a new way, not the same lessons again: grade 6 lays options side by side and picks one, grade 7 runs each case from a second person's side, grade 8 puts a price on every option and judges the choice afterward.
- **Evidence.** A class sheet per unit, the step marked "seen" or "not yet" on the day it was seen. It records a step, not a rating of the student; no behavior rating of any student is collected (STANDARD C). It is the teacher's working paper and is thrown away at the end of its unit (APPROACH, Daily evidence); a course-level check keeps its own sheet only until its respond session, then it is thrown away too. Feelings in a check are a character's, never the student's own. Private pages (SA7, SA9) stay with the student, checked only that the steps were followed; SA3, SA4, SA7 and SA9 are checked on invented cases or private pages, and the course never records a student's own answer to SA7 or SA9 (STANDARD section 3). End-of-unit evidence in every unit: the written scenario page is taken by the whole class in the Check session; the watched rehearsal from an unseen card is marked across the unit's last Application session and the Check session, pairs not yet seen going first in the Check (no score from a session where a skill is first taught). In units 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 and 18 the course-level check is that Check session. On the short path the page sits in the shared Check and Respond session and the rehearsal is marked in the Application session. Once a week, the Application session marks the unit's watched rehearsal; the codes judged on Application days in the full path move to the shared Check page as one written part each (the page then holds the unit's own items, up to six), and in units 6, 12 and 18 the class carries out one part of its proposal or improvement, chosen at the unit plan. Any student may do a rehearsal with the teacher alone or with a card, not in front of the class (APPROACH, supports). Each grade 8 owner unit's plan names which owned skills are judged in its Application sessions and which in its Check, so every owned skill has a named evidence point (unit 18 is the model).
- **Evidence base.** No study has tried this course; it is never called evidence-based or rated. The course page carries the logic model (C16) and this line (bar part 9 item 8; APPROACH, "Where the evidence is thin").
- **Carry it out and families.** Each unit names one cue the teacher uses outside the session ("Carry it out" in What students learn) and one family activity, sent in the unit's family note. Family notes are the only translated pages, in English and Spanish (STANDARD section 2); student pages are English only for now. A school may leave out a unit; its family note and the course page then name the left-out sub-skill ids the course does not claim that year (STANDARD section 5.6).
- **Fidelity and before you teach.** A paper fidelity checklist per session (did it run as planned, which part was cut) and a "before you teach" note opening each unit's teacher page: the skill as an adult uses it and one thing to model. Not training, and not claimed as such (bar part 9 items 11 and 13).
- **Equity.** Families of every kind appear in model pages, LGBTQ families included; no student is asked about a family, culture, faith or home language by name, or asked to teach or explain their own culture. SA6 (snap judgments) is checking one's own guess on invented cases, the wrong guess resting on a neutral trait (new to the team, a clothing style, a quiet manner), never blame of a group. SO6 uses settled history and invented school norms only. Student voice in school life (a class proposal in units 6 and 12, a school improvement carried out in unit 18), with no cause picked by the course and the analysis of causes left to social studies (bar part 9 item 12).
- **Devices and paper path.** No device for students. Every session runs on the paper path: step cards, scene cards, printed message threads and invented feeds, data pages, planners and class charts, printed or drawn by hand. Online life is taught on paper copies of invented screens; no student opens an account, an app or a real feed. Any online tool a later unit plan adds has a paper path beside it and needs no student account.
- **Tools and costs.** Kept all band: the talk turn card, feelings scale (the grade 5 feelings faces with their 1 to 5 strength), calm-down step card and tool menu, stop-think-choose card, goal steps card, problem size meter and I-statement frame (all from SEL 3 to 5, grown here in units 2, 3 and 4), plus the refusal options, fairness questions, who-is-affected map and upstander options, all grown from SEL 3 to 5's cards in units 4, 5 and 6. Each unit names its tools, what Curio Class writes and a provisional cost for 30 students; printing runs about $8 to $10 a unit, about $55 to $60 a year, plus about $5 to $10 of project supplies in units 6, 12 and 18 and $4 once for a two-minute sand timer per group (unit 13). No supply ceiling is set for SEL (For the lead 10); these are planning figures, not quotes.
- **Safety.** No movement game or tool hazard in this course. The project units (6, 12, 18) stay inside the school day and grounds, with an adult present; any sign-up sheet, poster or survey goes up only where the school allows. Online cases are paper only, so no student is put in contact with anyone. Each project unit names its controls; the safety agent reads units 6, 12 and 18 against the school's own grounds and activity policy.
- **Disclosure.** Asking for help, stress, pressure, friendship, conflict and upstander units can bring a disclosure, and middle school cases (a worry that does not go away, a friend's secret, a mocked classmate) make one more likely. Every unit follows health's steps (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md, "If a student tells you something"): the school's reporting route and the counselor. Health owns substances, body safety, sleep and mental health topics, self-harm and suicide; SEL never teaches them, and its cases never use them.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Cultural picks: a place already used for a game or custom in any SEL band (and in that grade's PE and arts) is not used again; the unit plan lists the places used so far. Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Before units 9 and 16 the agent reads the archive page and the community's own account named under Base text and checks each change is taught as settled history, with dignity, the law told apart from carrying it out; in units 6, 7 and 16 it reads the cited source and checks each way of working is shown as that one invented student's, never a whole culture's or group's, and that unit 16 asks no student to simulate hearing loss or speak for another identity; in units 2, 7 and 14 it checks identity, home language and values work stays on invented characters, the student's own page optional and private; in units 5, 8 and 13 it checks no real platform, trend or person is named. Units 6, 12 and 18 also get the safety agent's read. The Spanish family notes get a translation agent's read (For the lead 9). The course-level checks get the answer-key agent's read.

## Course-level checks

Each year holds a mid-year and a year-end check, and each check is the end-of-unit Check of the unit before it (units 3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18), with its own Respond session after it. An owned code not sampled on a course-level check is assessed by its own unit's end-of-unit assessment, which is rule 3's named reason; the two-sample cap stays. Each check takes a written scenario page on unseen invented cases (every sampled item a character's choice, never the student's own) and one watched rehearsal per pair from an unseen card, marked on the check's own sheet, which is kept only until its respond session. The page holds the unit's own items and at most two sampled items from earlier units, written only, five or six items in all; a sampled step is never a second rehearsal, and earlier skills not sampled return in later retrieval openings. Each student has their own look-for inside the pair rehearsal; pairs not yet seen in the unit's last Application session are watched first, and the unit plan's timed probe sets how many unfinished pairs may enter the Check. On the short path the shared Check and Respond session runs about 5 minutes opening, 12 minutes page and rehearsals, 8 minutes reteach and rehearsal again, and 5 minutes to close.
