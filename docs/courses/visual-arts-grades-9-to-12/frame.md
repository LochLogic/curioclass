# Course frame: Visual Arts, grades 9 to 12

Course `visual-arts-grades-9-to-12` (catalog id `visual-arts-9-12`), a band course for high school, run as four levels one after another (Level 1, Level 2, Level 3 and the optional Level 4), each a full year, named by level and never by grade (STANDARD.md 3.2). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one level at a time, and each level's part of this file is that level's frame.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 19: five in each of Levels 1, 2 and 3, four in Level 4. Levels 1 to 3 share one shape, so a thread
  comes back one level harder in the same place: an opening unit (Level 1's bridge, the review unit in Levels 2 and
  3), then a looking and materials unit, an ideas and revision unit, a design and judging unit, and a curating and
  museum unit that ends the year with the level's show. Each of the four paired units joins two progression threads,
  one of creating and one of responding, presenting or connecting, so every unit makes, studies other artists' work
  and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Level 4 is one sustained body of work in four units, one for each Level 4 row:
  the proposal and series, the critical essay, the project with a purpose, and the exhibit (APPROACH "High school
  levels and music").
- **Days.** 640: 160 a level for four levels (Level 4 optional; a school that offers three levels uses 480). Each
  level: 156 days in its units and 4 for course-level checks and respond days (a mid-year check and a year-end check,
  each with one respond day). One lesson of 45 minutes a day, five a week, 32 weeks a level (STANDARD.md 3.4: a high
  school level is 160 course days of 45 minutes, the about 120 hours the national Proficient descriptor names; the
  80-day short path is 60 hours). The 45 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes), the teaching and making,
  and clean-up and collecting work (about 7 minutes in an ink, paint, clay or printing session, 3 in a drawing,
  writing or talk session); a retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment
  and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessment, each with a respond day. Unit counts follow the rows' load:
  the design and judging unit is longest in Levels 1 to 3 because it holds a team or solo build with a written
  rationale and a written judgment; the bridge and review units are short because they own no id. These are course
  days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) runs them one a school day, so course day n of a level falls
  in that level's week n / 5, rounded up. National time figure: the visual arts educators' association's high school
  opportunity-to-learn checklist (2025 posting, read at source in the Codex read of 2026-11-25) asks for regular
  instruction on a par with other academic subjects, allows semester and other schedules, and gives no weekly minute
  minimum, so no gap is stated; this course gives 225 minutes a week, about 120 hours a level on the full path and 60
  on the short path (For the lead item 5).
- **The 80-day short path (a semester or a rotation).** Each level's scope and sequence marks an 80-day path that
  keeps every unit and every id by merging the marked days (STANDARD.md 3.2). Days on that path: Level 1, units of
  6 (the bridge), 15, 16, 20 and 19; Levels 2 and 3, units of 3 (review), 16, 17, 20 and 20; Level 4, units of 25,
  14, 19 and 18; each level's two checks keep 1 check day and 1 respond day. Checks fall in week 8 (mid-course) and
  week 16, the half-year finals week; Level 4's first two units fill 39 days, so it checks on day 40 (week 8) and
  responds on day 41 (week 9). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the demonstration with a step card,
  opened by the look at a work that carries the unit's response and its connecting line; the making days with the
  one critique and the revision; the mid-unit and end-of-unit assessments; the respond days. Fluency days fold into
  the openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge where the scope marks them. A merged day never drops a
  critique, a revision or a respond day. The bridge's 6 short-path days are 7.5 percent of 80, inside the bar's 10
  percent limit on days outside the course's standards (12 of 160 on the full path, the same share).
- **Levels, entry and placement.** STANDARD.md 3.2 binds. Level 1 is Proficient (`.Ia` ids), Level 2 Accomplished
  (`.IIa`), Level 3 Advanced at honors depth (`.IIIa`), Level 4 an optional year the student leads, assessed again
  on `.IIIa` with new, self-directed work. A student new to visual arts starts at Level 1 in any grade. Each level
  needs the one before, or a placement showing: the student's work scored against the previous level's year-end
  check, met at the review unit (units 6 and 11). The sitting alone never places a student: the review unit
  also fills a placement checklist of the level before's creating, presenting, responding and connecting needs, from
  work the student brings (with a short spoken or written account) or, where a need has no evidence, a short making,
  handling or showing task, each scored on the level before's guides. Advanced is claimed by assessed work, not by finishing Level 3; a
  student who ends Level 3 at "partly" has Level 4 to reach it. One level is a complete course. Choice grows by level
  (APPROACH): Level 1 sets the problems, Level 2 lets the student pick among them and research, Level 3 has the
  student set the problem, Level 4 is the student's own proposal.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, visual arts performance standards, by id only, each named with
  its line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2); each id's action
  was checked against the national standard's own text for this frame. Level 1 claims the 15 `.Ia` ids, Level 2 the
  15 `.IIa` ids, Level 3 the 15 `.IIIa` ids: 45 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in
  its own level. Level 4 claims no new id: its rows contribute to the `.IIIa` ids, which it assesses again on new work.
  No grade 8 id is claimed (the bridge teaches grade 8 techniques and words as readiness). Every level touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, presenting, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at
  least two. In the Units table the seventh column is headed "Processes, anchors and works". Check set: each unit
  plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the arts part of
  docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column
  names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds: on political and social works,
  facts and backed sources decide claims, people are taught with dignity, and a student's own view is never scored,
  only the evidence and reasoning behind it. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file; each level's
  scope and sequence still states its id count (15) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Base text.** None. Visual arts is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found material to study, not plans to follow). Works studied come
  from museum open-access collections and public domain government collections (public domain works released by the
  holding museum or library) and, for living or recent makers, from a description in our own words with a link to
  the maker's or museum's page, never a copy (APPROACH "Rights"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps
  unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work keeps the unit's ids met and the
  level's range. Across the band the works come from Africa, the Americas (with six Native makers or Native-led
  shows, in units 5, 9, 13, 18 and 19), Asia, Europe and Oceania, from about 420 BCE to now, with living
  makers in every level (APPROACH "Works and their settings"); open images lean to older European and American works,
  so the list was built for range. No work studied in another visual arts band is studied again as new work: a
  return is named as one with its new task (Lawrence's Migration Series, met in grade 3, is Level 1's entry work for a
  student new to the band), and a maker met before comes back with a different work (El-Salahi, van Gogh, Rivera);
  the 2026-11-26 fix pass swapped the works shared with the 3 to 5 and 6 to 8 frames. Curio Class writes every step card, work card, hazard card, reading, case card,
  record sheet, guide and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Drawing: value scales, contour and gesture, one-point
  perspective (the bridge, as grade 8 readiness), then thumbnails and observational studies (Level 1), sustained
  studies over a term (Level 2), a planned series (Level 3), a self-set series (Level 4). Wet media: ink and
  watercolor worked without a plan (Level 1), mixed media weighed old and new (Level 2), a series in one chosen
  medium (Level 3). Form and space: a scale model of a school space in card (Level 1), a redesigned object or place
  model (Level 2), a work about material culture (Level 3). Printmaking: foam-plate and collagraph prints, no blade
  cutting (Levels 1 and 2). Presenting: a small show and a kept collection (Level 1), a sited portfolio show with
  hanging and lighting chosen (Level 2), a curated event show with justified choices and a conservation plan (Level
  3), a public exhibit with labels and a talk (Level 4).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown
  in named steps with the step card, or a work studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is
  taught here, never on a respond day); first teaching is always understanding. Fluency: short repeated drills with
  feedback (a two-minute gesture set, a value strip, a thumbnail page, a mixing chart, a looking routine on a new
  image); in high school it is the five-minute opener on most days and five or six fluency days a unit. Application:
  making and writing with some choice, drafting and editing included; the second making week holds the unit's one
  critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the revision days after it are application too. Check: the mid-unit and
  end-of-unit assessments and the course-level checks. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet". The
  shortest paired unit (32 days) runs: 8 understanding, 5 fluency, 14 application with the critique and the
  revision, 1 mid-unit check and 1 respond, 2 end check days, 1 respond; longer units add understanding, fluency and
  application days by their making and research load.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in
  the unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one
  item for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the product's guide (three
  to five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), scored from the kept work after the session; a kept page made on the
  unit's making days (a planning portfolio entry, a hazard card, an idea log, a research page), scored with the
  product; a written item in a sitting on an unseen open-licensed work or a Curio Class original; and the artist
  statement or written rationale, drafted, given written feedback and revised before it is scored. Each paired unit
  has two end check days: day one is a sitting of written items on unseen works (set-up 3 minutes, 38 minutes of
  writing, collecting 4); day two is the showing, where each student self-scores on the guide and the class gives
  the showing's audience notes, and the product is scored from the kept work afterwards. A check day has no paint,
  ink or clay, and one adult watches one room. The product's draft, its critique with the teacher's written marks on
  the record sheet, and its revision come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique
  or a work is first taught. The mid-unit assessment (one check day and one respond day inside every paired unit)
  scores the unit's planning or materials item while the product is still in progress. Each unit opens with a
  baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two
  marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the portfolio with a one-line written
  reflection (bar criterion 8); the portfolio is the Level 2 portfolio show's source and Level 4's starting point.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Level 1 assumes no earlier arts learning (STANDARD.md 3.2). Its unit 1, the bridge,
  teaches the grade 8 techniques and words Level 1 needs to every student, as readiness, and claims no grade 8 id; it
  stands in for a readiness check, since the grade 8 rows were last used one grade back for a student who took Visual
  Arts, grades 6 to 8 straight through, and two or more grades back (or never) for a student who starts later. Units
  2 to 5 then open with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener on the bridge's technique and the grade 8 row in the
  same thread (taught the same year in the bridge), and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. Levels 2, 3
  and 4 assume the level before, taken the year before (the count starts from the band's order: a student who takes
  one level a year). Their units open with a retrieval task inside day 2 on the level before's row in the same thread.
  A student who placed in, or skipped a year between levels, meets the level before's techniques again in the review
  unit (units 6 and 11), whose placement sitting stands as the readiness check; Level 4 has no review unit: a
  returning student's readiness check is ten minutes on unit 16's day 2, on the Level 3 guide. The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 9 to 12 have one rule: week 32 is the finals week (CALENDAR.md stagger rule 4).
  Each level's mid-year check is in week 16, at the semester's end (course days 79 and 80 in Level 1, 77 and 78 in
  Levels 2 and 3, 79 and 80 in Level 4); its year-end check is in week 32, the finals week (days 159 and 160). The
  curating unit runs in weeks 24 or 25 to 32 (days 119 or 121 to 158), its show by day 155 and its respond days in
  week 32 beside the year-end check; the show is its own end-of-unit assessment, and the year-end check samples the
  units before it.
- **Grade 12 calendar.** Any level may be taken in grade 12 (Levels 3 and 4 most often). Its last weeks hold
  teaching: the curating or exhibit unit runs into week 32 beside the year-end check. In a school whose seniors leave
  before week 32, a senior sits the year-end check on the school's senior exam day, and the curating unit's last
  fluency and making days merge: a finish by day 155 (week 31) needs five merged days, a finish by day 150 (week 30)
  needs ten. The scope numbers the senior calendar day by day; the show, its critique, revision, checks and respond
  days never fold. A Level 4 senior's exhibit opens by week 30 for the same
  reason. A student of 18 or over gets the family note themselves and gives their own yes to be shown (AGENTS.md;
  va-g12-exhibit); the school's own rules still apply.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units this course names: unit 15 (Siqueiros's América Tropical, a political mural with a
  crucified figure), unit 14 (war and colonial imagery: Guernica and the Dufour wallpaper) and unit 5 (a museum show built
  around objects of slavery). Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and
  the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that level. A student aged 18 or older gets the family note
  directly and makes their own leave-out and consent choices; the school's rules still apply.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 at tables, paper, pencils, charcoal, found material; nothing must be bought,
  and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent or a
  sibling is the audience and the critique partner, a team task lets the student make alone, but where an id asks for
  collaboration (VA:Cr2.3.Ia, unit 4) the planning is shared with a partner or the home adult, and the showing is to
  the family. The teacher needs no art training: each unit's teacher page says what to look for, in
  plain words; a school with a certified art teacher uses that teacher.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as brushes,
  ink sets and printing brayers counted once, printing of cards and readings), all optional beside the free path:
  Level 1 $15, $45, $15, $30, $15 ($120); Level 2 $5, $40, $35, $25, $20 ($125); Level 3 $5, $35, $40, $25, $20
  ($125); Level 4 $45, $5, $35, $25 ($110). Planning figures, not quotes; the ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work, a step or an exhibition's own pages; every day
  runs from the printed step card, work card and reading without it. Units 3 and 7 offer a photo walk or photo log on
  a phone or a shared school camera, by the student's choice; the paper path does the same with viewfinder thumbnail
  drawings. No owned id needs technology, so a paper-path student meets every id in full. Kept work stays on paper or
  on the student's, family's or school's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on paper, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  color-mixing practice tool, a work viewer, an exhibit floor-plan tool) has a printed version that does the same job
  and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Readings are Curio Class's own, at grade 9 to 10 text level in Level 1 and grade 11 to 12 in
  Levels 3 and 4, about 600 to 900 words a reading day; a museum's or artist's own page is read in excerpts the unit
  plan names. English learners: each unit teaches its five or six words in depth, with an image and a frame ("The
  evidence in the work is ___, which suggests ___."), a student may show a technique instead of naming it, and the
  check marks what the student made apart from what they wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the
  federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov) and California's environmental
  health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for schools, with its precautions for grades 7 to 12
  (oehha.ca.gov). Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): only
  supplies labeled non-toxic and conforming to the art materials labeling standard; a hazard card (our own words,
  from the maker's safety data sheet) read before each new material; blades used by an adult only, so prints are
  foam-plate or collagraph, never cut lino; water-based media only, no solvents, no aerosol sprays or fixatives in
  the room; no powdered clay or dry pigment mixing (premixed paint, moist air-dry clay); charcoal and pastel dust
  wiped with a damp cloth, never blown; each material's label and safety data sheet
  followed, and where they ask for ventilation, fresh air with the used air carried away from users (a fan that only
  stirs the room's air is not enough; a room that cannot give it uses the unit's pencil or dry path); hands washed after every wet session;
  no heat source, kiln or hot glue in the plans; no food or drink at the work tables; students do not climb to hang
  work, an adult hangs above head height. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words
  days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural, Native, hard-history or political subject name the communities and the sources the agent studies first,
  in the last column: units 2, 5, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 18 and 19. Every task that ties art to the student's own
  life (the Cn10 ids in units 4, 9 and 14, and the Level 4 project) offers an invented or public option marked the
  same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"); a social or political aim is the student's choice, from a list the teacher
  checks, and its side is never scored. Rows that ask it (va-g11-design, va-g11-museum) have a second adult read a
  work on a sensitive subject before it is shown. Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you
  something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: a 45-minute lesson a day, step cards, work cards,
  readings, found and low-cost materials. Activities: technique taught in steps, works and exhibitions studied with
  their settings, making with a critique and a revision, a show each level. Outputs: a portfolio of four to five
  pieces a level with reflections and artist statements, a show a level, two marks a unit per student on the main
  look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the level's 15 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried
  with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

### Where the threads end

This is the last visual arts course. The four Level 4 rows (va-g12-studio, va-g12-purpose, va-g12-exhibit,
va-g12-criticism) hand on nothing: their `hands_on` cells are empty, and the threads end in the Level 4 portfolio,
the project, the exhibit and the essay. For a student who stops after Level 3, the threads end in unit 15's show and
its year-end check; for a student who stops after Level 1, in the Proficient level, which is a complete course.
