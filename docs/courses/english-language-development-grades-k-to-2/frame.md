# Course frame: English Language Development, grades K to 2

Course `english-language-development-grades-k-to-2` (catalogue id `eld-k-2`), grades K, 1 and 2, a band course of three
grade years. Draft frame, written 2026-11-25 as the pilot frame for English language development; the scope and
sequence sets the days later, one year at a time. Each year's part of this file (its units, its end-of-unit
assessments, its two course-level checks) is that grade year's frame, which meets STANDARD.md section 2 ("one frame
per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 20: 6 in kindergarten (units 1 to 6), 7 in grade 1 (units 7 to 13), 7 in grade 2 (units 14 to 20).
  One unit per content row of the progression (K 6, grade 1 7, grade 2 8, where grade 2 pairs its science Explain
  and Argue rows on one host, unit 17). The four SI rows of each year ride inside the content unit whose key
  language use they share. Each unit is built around one or two key language uses, in both modes. SI in every unit
  (bar item 1; STANDARD section 3): the unit's own SI row, or one SI use named as review in What students learn
  (units 3, 6, 11, 12, 13, 16, 17 and 18; unit 4 adds a narrate return to its own SI row), each with its talk move on the host content and its look-for.
- **Days.** 480: 160 a year for three years. Each year: 154 days in units and 6 for two course-level checks (two
  check days and one respond day each). One lesson of 30 minutes a day, five a week, 32 weeks, the protected daily
  ELD period settled in STANDARD.md section 2 and APPROACH.md "Pacing" (80 hours a year). It is not ELA time and does
  not stand in for ELA (bar item 8). The 30 minutes hold the talk set-up, handing out and collecting cards and the
  exit ticket; there is no lab or field clean-up, since ELD runs no investigation. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments): units 1, 2 and 19 are over 25
  days and each hold one mid-unit assessment day. Every unit's end-of-unit assessment is two days (a sitting with
  unseen items: an interpretive part on new content of the same kind, then a talk task heard by the teacher and a
  drawing or writing task, at each entry point), followed by one respond day inside the unit. Days follow the host
  windows first: each unit runs in the weeks its host is taught, and the two checks sit in fixed weeks, so a unit's
  length is mostly the gap between them. Within those windows the load sets the rest: an LA row (a host text taken
  apart and an own piece built, in both modes) weighs most, a content row next, an SI row least, since it rides on
  the same unit's talk. Three units are tight for what they hold, and say what they leave to the respond day: unit
  10 (19 days, two rows, its host window ends at the mid-year check), unit 14 (19 days, two rows and the year's new
  talk routine, before ELA's paragraph unit opens) and unit 17 (22 days, two content rows on one host, before the
  mid-year check). Each keeps its Fluency days to the forms it owns and leaves review forms to the respond day's
  groups; the scope and sequence may move days within a year, never across a check. Years run in school
  order: kindergarten is band days 1 to 160, grade 1 band days 161 to 320, grade 2 band days 321 to 480; days below
  are given within the year (grade 1 day 86 is band day 246). These are course days: the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) places them on school days and weeks, and at five a week a year's course day d falls on
  school day d, in week d divided by 5, rounded up.
- **Standard.** English language development standards (WIDA 2020, data/standards/wida-2020.txt), ids and our own
  words only: no Language Expectation, proficiency level descriptor or other framework wording appears in any page
  (STANDARD.md section 1); public pages say "English language development standards" with the id. The band's 33 rows
  own 26 ids: kindergarten owns the 12 ids of cluster K (LA, MA, SC and SS, each key language use in both modes);
  grade 1 owns the 14 ids of cluster 1. The SI K-3 ids (all three years) and grade 2's cluster 2-3 ids are carried
  (owner year grade 3, in the ELD grades 3 to 5 course; the owner rule of STANDARD section 2): each unit that carries
  one names it in What students learn with its owner. Ids in full: every part of an owned id's expectations is
  taught in its unit, checked against the framework's own lists for the id (not the code alone) and said in our
  words (units 4, 9 and 10 name theirs). Check set: bar part 10 items 1 to 14 (ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS),
  the PRIME 2020 criteria in our words (docs/RUBRIC-ELD.md, a planning reference only; Curio Class claims no
  alignment) and the California criteria map rows of STANDARD section 4. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
- **Language through content.** Every unit names its host: the core unit of the same grade that the class studies
  that month (STANDARD section 3), cited with the host frame's own course days. The host column of the Units table
  (headed Host unit and key language uses, in place of C3) gives it. Where a row names two hosts, the unit uses the
  one that runs alongside it and retrieves the other where it was taught earlier; a host taught later in the year is
  not used, and the unit says so. Some units run just after their host (unit 6 after math grade K unit 7, unit 11
  after social studies grade 1 unit 5, unit 18 after math grade 2 unit 5, unit 19's last four weeks after social
  studies grade 2 unit 5) or a week or two ahead (units 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 16, each with an "If later" line): these are
  the nearest fits that keep every row in host order with two checks a year, and the scope and sequence may move
  days within a year without changing units. Each host is checked against the host row's standard, not its title.
  Content is the grade's own, made reachable with help; it is never swapped for an
  easier text. For an LA unit the host is the ELA unit it pairs with (STANDARD section 5): ELD takes one page or
  passage of the ELA text apart and names its language, and the reading and writing lessons stay in ELA.
- **Key language uses as the spine.** Each year builds all the uses its ids name, and each returns with a harder
  text or task (bar item 13): kindergarten inform (units 1, 3, 5, 6), narrate (2), explain (4), argue (5, SI only,
  since ELD-LA and ELD-SS have no K Argue id); grade 1 inform (7, 11, 12, 13), narrate (8), argue (9), explain (10);
  grade 2 inform (14), explain (15, 16, 17), argue (17, 18, 19), narrate (20). Grade 2 moves the weight from inform
  to explain and argue, because cluster 2-3 gives ELD-MA, ELD-SC and ELD-SS no Inform id. Inside each year every key
  language use returns in a later unit, through the unit's own row or its SI review use, and what gets harder is
  named in that unit: kindergarten narrate 2 to 3 (two pictured steps grow to a three-part telling of the week's
  weather), explain 3 to 4 (one "because" on the weather chart grows to two linked boxes and a how-to), argue 5 to 6
  (a vote with one reason grows to defending a sort and answering a partner), inform 1 to 3, 5 and 6; grade 1
  narrate 8 to 11 (a class event grows to a recount of how the class made its map), explain 10 to 12 (a game rule
  grows to the steps of making a report page and why each comes where it does), argue 9 to 13 (a class vote grows
  to a choice defended from a chart and a partner answered), inform 7, 11, 12 and 13; grade 2 inform 14 to 16 (a
  class topic grows to a summary of a resource card not studied), explain 15 to 16 and 17, argue 17 to 18 and 19.
  Grade 2's narrate is the one use met in a single unit, because its host fables run weeks 24 to 32: the "tell,
  answer, retell" routine practices retelling a partner's turn every week from unit 14, unit 20 makes it the goal,
  and grade 3 (the owner year) brings it back.
- **Every unit, the same moves** (bar items 9 to 12; APPROACH, "Across the unit"): the key language use taught
  openly from a model taken apart, built with the teacher, then made alone; forms taught directly at the text, the
  sentence and the word (each form named with its job); structured talk every day with frames, before any writing;
  both modes (interpretive: listening, viewing and the shared reading; expressive: speaking, drawing and writing);
  one change of audience or purpose; one moment where students name a form and its job and judge their own
  language on a self-check page they keep; the host-teacher note (bar item 14: the content used and the language
  taught, two lines).
- **The talk routine.** One named routine a year, the same every week: kindergarten "knee-to-knee partners" (say,
  listen, say back one thing), grade 1 "tell and ask" (tell, the partner asks one question, answer), grade 2 "tell,
  answer, retell" (tell, answer the partner, retell the partner's turn to a third student). Each grows from the one
  before, so a grade 2 class that never had the earlier years learns it in unit 14's first week.
- **Entry points.** Three, by proficiency (STANDARD section 2): levels 1 and 2, 3 and 4, 5 and 6. Every entry point
  does the same task on the same content at the same thinking demand; levels change the help, never the task (bar
  item 2). Help is withdrawn on the progression's default days: for 5 and 6 the frame comes off after the first
  application day; for 3 and 4 before the end-of-unit writing or telling task; 1 and 2 keep the picture word bank
  and lose the cloze frame before the check. A student moves to the next entry point when the teacher's paper sheet
  shows the unit's look-fors "seen" in the end-of-unit assessment and in one course-level check, or on the school's
  own progress check (bar item 7). Placement and exit use the school's proficiency score; Curio Class gives no
  placement test and keeps no scores. Every unit has notes for newcomers, long-term English learners and English
  learners with disabilities, and a line on telling a language need from a learning need (APPROACH, "Sensitive
  ground": a gap in English is never read as a disability).
- **Newcomer strand.** A 20-day on-ramp beside each year, outside its 160 days (STANDARD section 2; Desk
  q-fix-eld-1): kindergarten works the SI K-3 ids and the four K Inform ids (LA, MA, SC, SS); grade 1 the SI K-3 ids
  and the four grade 1 Inform ids; grade 2 the SI K-3 ids and WIDA.ELD-LA.2-3.Inform only, since cluster 2-3 has no
  MA, SC or SS Inform id. Its content is the school day itself (names, routines, the room, asking for help) and the
  current host unit's pictures. A newcomer arriving at any point takes it, then joins the class's current unit at
  entry points 1 and 2. The strand is written with the year's scope and sequence and gets its own sensitivity read
  before use.
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") and APPROACH.md "Kinds of day in ELD": Understanding is
  the first teaching of a form, a text's structure or a model (the form shown in the host text, named, explained);
  Fluency practices forms already taught (oral rehearsal with the frame, sentence building from cards, word work and
  cognate sorts) and drafts nothing; Application uses the forms in talk and then in drawing or writing on the
  content (every day that drafts or revises is Application); Check is an end-of-unit assessment, mid-unit
  assessment or course-level check day; Respond reteaches the forms the sheet shows "not yet". Fluency is a real
  share in K to 2 (about a quarter of teaching days), since oral rehearsal is how young learners make a form their
  own.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Kindergarten builds on nothing earlier. Every row grades 1 and 2 assume was taught
  the year before in this band (grade 1 assumes kindergarten, grade 2 assumes grade 1), so each opens as a retrieval
  task inside a day of the unit that first needs it (day 2, after day 1's hook), in the same words as sequence-write
  rule 2; no unit needs a readiness check. The count starts from the student's own grade year. A student who joins in
  grade 1 or 2 without the earlier year meets the retrieval task at entry points 1 and 2 with the earlier year's
  model on the card, and a newcomer takes the strand first. A host's earlier unit is retrieved the same way (named
  in each unit). A mixed-grade group uses the year of its youngest grade, and the teacher page says so (STANDARD
  section 2).
- **Base text.** None of its own (docs/reviews/eld-open-material-2026-11-16.md: no open ELD course exists). Texts are
  the host units' own: ELA K to 2's texts (EL Education K-5 Language Arts, 2017 edition, CC BY 4.0, credited
  through the ELA courses; the 2025 edition is CC BY-NC and is not used) and the
  science, social studies and math units' Curio Class pages, records and photographs. ELD adapts EL Education's
  Language Dive routine (a sentence from the text taken apart and rebuilt), credited once a day where used. Curio
  Class writes the scaffolds (picture word banks, photo and picture cards, the frames per level, the model page each
  unit builds with the teacher) and each end-of-unit assessment's new content of the same kind, named per unit. No
  source map: ELD places no base units.
- **Paper path.** Every lesson runs on paper, cards and talk. Any tool a unit names (a model-sentence replay tool:
  hear and replay the day's model sentence; a picture word deck) has a paper path beside it (the teacher reads the
  model aloud, the cards on the table) and needs no student account. No tool records a student's voice or writing,
  scores pronunciation or places a student at a level (APPROACH, "What a tool may and may not do").
- **Devices.** None per student. One shared display is optional for the model page and the replay tool. No owned
  code needs technology: "representing" is drawing and labeling, so a paper-path class meets every id in full.
- **Costs.** Printing only: picture word banks, photo cards and the four model sheets on card stock, reused across a
  year, about $4 a unit for a class of 30 (a planning figure, not a quote); no supplies are bought.
- **Home languages.** A help, never a ranking (bar item 6): talk in the home language before English, cognates
  named, and teacher notes on how Spanish does the same thing (other languages when a reader exists, APPROACH "Home
  languages"). No student translates for another, for the class or for a family. Family notes are in English and
  Spanish.
- **Safety.** ELD runs no investigation and no field work. Students talk from the host's own records, photographs and
  class charts; where a teacher repeats a moment from the host (a gentle push of a ball, a plucked rubber band), it
  is the host's own step under the host unit's hazards and controls (the host frame's safety sheet and its safety
  read), never a new test, and nothing is heated, tasted or shone in eyes.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md,
  kind `sensitivity`, community: multilingual learners and immigrant families), and every Spanish note and family
  note gets a `spanish` read (APPROACH, "Sensitive ground", Cory 2026-11-21). Before reading, the agent studies the
  APPROACH page's Sensitive ground rules, the federal English learner toolkit (US Department of Education, Office of
  English Language Acquisition, free online, https://www.ncela.ed.gov/educator-support/toolkits/english-learner-toolkit)
  and the National Academies 2017 report on children learning English (free to read online). Flagged units name more below: unit 19 (two accounts of people arriving in a new place),
  units 2, 8 and 20 (own-story prompts), unit 5 (work), unit 12 (parents and young), unit 14 (schools around the
  world). The newcomer strand and all family notes get the read before use. No outside human reader is planned (Cory,
  2026-11-14); the course's checks get the answer-key agent's read.

**Weeks.** After-core checks do not take a core check week (docs/pipeline/briefs/frame-write.md, "After-core
courses"). In the calendar the core frames give today (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md), the core course-level check weeks
are: kindergarten 14, 15, 16, 17, 29, 30, 31 and 32; grade 1 14, 15, 16, 19, 29, 30, 31 and 32; grade 2 14, 15, 16,
18, 19, 20, 28, 29, 31 and 32. So the ELD checks sit in kindergarten weeks 18 and 28, grade 1 weeks 18 and 28, and
grade 2 weeks 17 and 27, the latest free weeks before each grade's core checks crowd the end of the year. That is
why each year's last unit (6, 13 and 20) follows its year-end check: it is taught and assessed in full, and its
end-of-unit record is passed on unscored. The cost is stated plainly: kindergarten's MA Inform ids (unit 6) and
grade 1's MA Inform ids (unit 13) are checked by their end-of-unit assessment only, and that record and the
teacher's sheet go on to the next teacher; grade 2's narrate (unit 20) is carried, and grade 3 (its owner year)
checks it. Kindergarten's mid-year talk tasks follow math kindergarten's one-to-one check days 84 and 85 directly
(CALENDAR.md rule 2 keeps one-to-one checks apart because one adult hears every child): where one adult gives both,
the heard parts and the drawing go on day 86 and every talk task on day 87, so the one-to-one load starts two school
days after math's. Grades 1 and 2 are five or more school days from the nearest core one-to-one check.
`frame.py calendar` lists these six weeks beside other after-core checks in the same weeks (For the lead, item 5);
the weeks stay until the lead's calendar pass across the after-core frames.

**How a check runs in 30 minutes.** A heard part (the teacher reads or plays the new text twice) and the drawing or
writing task go to the whole class at once; the talk task is heard one student at a time, about a minute each, while
the others work, over the two days. Each student is heard once in a check. The respond day groups students by form
and by entry point (at most four groups; the others rehearse in pairs from the frame). No scores are stored; the
teacher's paper sheet stays with the teacher (promise 2).
