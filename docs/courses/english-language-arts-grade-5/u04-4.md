## 1. Standards and practices

- RL.5.7 “Analyze” what images and audio contribute to meaning, tone and beauty (140 and 141). RL.5.1 “Quote accurately” for explicit information and inference (140 and 144). RL.5.2 “Determine” a theme from a response to challenge and “summarize” the cold story. RL.5.10 “read and comprehend” independently across forms at the verified high end of the band. Day 140 adds prose evidence to the portfolio.
- RL.5.4 “Determine” figurative and contextual meanings. L.5.5c “Use the relationship” between the two words spelled alike to explain the applicable sense (141).
- RL.5.6 “Describe” how a narrator's viewpoint shapes what readers learn (142 to 144). RL.5.9 “Compare and contrast” how stories in survival fiction approach a common theme or topic (142, 144). RL.5.5 “Explain” how scene order builds the story (142). L.5.3b “Compare and contrast” language varieties across story, drama and poem by feature, relationship and purpose (142 to 144). Model the three forms on 143 as the unit directs. Show how a third-person narrator can reveal Eli's thoughts and that context shapes register.
- SL.5.1/a to d “Engage” in discussion, “Come” prepared, “Follow” rules and roles, “Pose and respond” to specific questions with elaboration, and “Review” ideas and draw a conclusion (143). W.5.10 “Write routinely” through short notes, a written conclusion and dated change records. L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately” the week 29 words, including words signaling similarity and contrast.
- RF.5.3/a “Know and apply” phonics and “Use combined knowledge” of sounds, syllables and morphemes in unfamiliar words alone and in print. RF.5.4/a to c “Read” for understanding and demonstrate accuracy, suitable rate and expression on successive readings of prose and verse, then “Use context” to confirm or self-correct after decoding (141 to 142).
- Preview drought, worry about crops and cattle, and flawed measurements in the fictional checks. Contextualize any reference to distinct tribal water rights with the nation's own named source. Offer a cleared alternate reading privately. Compare language features without ranking or asking students to imitate speakers. Keep family access and experiences private.

### Days 142 to 143, paper exits

**s04-X142.** Release Ada's fall and spring ending and Eli's trailer departure. Give two short response spaces. “How do the narrators show what Ada and Eli value as their plans change? Explain what moving Ada's spring scene first would hide.”
Key: both show children adapting under drought pressure and caring for what remains. Ada's first-person telling remembers saving peppers for Jo, then asking Dad to draw the far bend, showing growing regard for her neighbor. The third-person voice gives Eli's hope for one last look at the calf, and shows him checking the remaining cattle. Moving spring first reveals Ada's changed priorities before their development. “Eli tells what Mara secretly hopes” (S); “One uses I, the other he” (T).
Shape: one comparison with a detail from each new ending, then a separate scene-order response. Practice marks the thinker or a change in a single released scene. Keep both codas, headings, audio and summaries sealed until the exit.

**s04-X143.** First release Jo's greeting to Vale in 01, Ren's final line to friends in *At the Pump*, and the repeated “I left” lines from *Rain Ledger*. Ask: “Compare how the words in the story, play and poem fit each situation.”
Key: Jo asks a familiar neighbor about waiting. Ren's “Scoot over a bit, will you?” addresses friends conversationally and gets them to make space. Both speak directly to familiar people. The paired “I left” lines use repeated wording and measured rhythm to reflect on waiting for rain. “Scoot sounds unintelligent” (U); “The speaker says, ‘I paid for rain’” (J).
Shape: compare three ways of speaking from new literary lines. Model choosing the supported comparison on the three-forms card using Dad, Ren and the aunt. Students label one relationship or notice one change of purpose. Withhold the three exit lines and full versions containing them until collection.
