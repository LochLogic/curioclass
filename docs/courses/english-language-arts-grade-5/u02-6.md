Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/2/s/2.6), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u02-6.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 2, The rainforest (days 42 to 80), [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 65 to 67.** ELA days by kind: write 1, check 2. A plan, not a lesson. Nothing here is built or tried with students.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.5.2 and W.5.2a to e.** “Write” an explanation. “Introduce” a focused comparison and group details, “Develop” it with explained quotations, “Link” within and across groups, “Use” precise terms, and “Provide” a close that follows. Use a heading where it helps the reader.
- **L.5.2 and L.5.2a to e.** “Demonstrate command” by correcting capitalization, series punctuation, commas after introductory elements and around yes, direct address and tags, title marking and spelling with a reference. Check the source quotation separately.
- **RF.5.3 and RF.5.3a.** “Know and apply” decoding skills. “Use” sounds, syllables and meaningful parts in and out of context. **RF.5.4 and RF.5.4a to c.** “Read” for meaning, then read prose and verse twice with accuracy, appropriate pace and expression. “Use context” after decoding to confirm or correct.
- **W.5.5, “develop and strengthen.”** Keep a real comment and the resulting change. **L.5.6, “Acquire and use accurately.”** Connect precise literary and forest terms with language of contrast, addition and cause on day 65. **W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** A short exit and a multi-day essay serve different readers and purposes.
- **Teacher explanation.** A close draws an implication from the details already explained. Quote the text's words exactly and mark an inference as the writer's reading of them. In word work, a title names a whole work, while quotation marks can also enclose a speaker's exact words.

## 9. Supports and extension

Explain revise and precision. On 66, use researcher, observation, transition, orientation, dialogue, conclusion, pacing and audience in released sentences. Review dialogue as “words spoken by characters” and conclusion as “the last part that closes what a writer said.” Frame: “I moved ___ because ___.” On 66 give large print, line guides, private audio and accessible controls. Record any scribing or read-aloud support by component. Keep each required story reread under 240 words at about two minutes, with independent choice reading filling the remaining reading time. Extend by showing how an exact word changes one explained effect. Preview the hurricane references and Isa's brief separation and reunion. Offer the unit's alternate reading without asking for a personal account.

## 10. Two rooms
