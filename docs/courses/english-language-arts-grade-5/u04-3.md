## 1. Standards and practices

- RL.5.2 “Determine” a theme from how a character responds or how a poetic speaker reflects, and “summarize” important events (136 to 138). RL.5.4 “Determine” meanings in context, including similes and metaphors (136, 138, 139). RL.5.5 “Explain” how successive groups build chapters, scenes or stanzas into a whole (138 to 139). This section teaches the sequence through chapters and stanzas. Scenes return on 142 and 143.
- RL.5.7 “Analyze” separate contributions of visuals and performance to meaning, tone and beauty (137 to 139). RL.5.10 “read and comprehend” literary texts independently at the high end of the grade band. These days add prose and poetry evidence, with range and level judgments pending verification.
- L.5.5/a to c “Demonstrate understanding,” “Interpret” comparisons, “Recognize and explain” idioms, adages and proverbs, and “Use the relationship” among synonyms, antonyms or homographs to interpret a word (136, 138 to 139). L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately” literary terms and words signaling relationships. W.5.10 “Write routinely” through brief explanations and a dated reading record across days.
- RF.5.3/a “Know and apply” phonics and “Use combined knowledge” of sounds, syllables and morphemes in isolated and contextual long words. RF.5.4/a to c “Read” for purpose and understanding, reread prose and poetry accurately and expressively at a suitable pace, and “Use context” after decoding to confirm or self-correct (137, 139).
- Explain changes in what Ada notices and how the third-person voice stays with Eli. Explain how visual line, color and placement, and how pauses, pitch and rhythm, change a reader's experience. On 138 use the five groups and ten verse lines of the registered June 1921 Hughes edition. Explain the title's historical racial language, the Mississippi allusion to the era of slavery, and how the speaker can voice memories across generations. Preview drought, threatened crops and herd separation. Offer a released science reading or another cleared literary practice passage privately. Discuss the text and offer a private pass on personal disclosure.

**s04-X136.** In 01 release the stopped-leaf paragraph through “I put the board back.” Ask: “How do Ada's words about fear and her choice with the board suggest a theme?”
Key: the imagined weight makes worry feel like a burden she keeps shifting. When opening the second row thins the first stream, she puts its board back despite her worry. A supported theme links fear with responding carefully to evidence. “Her fear is something heavy she carries in her hands” (M); “Peppers is the theme” (K).
Shape: connect a fresh metaphor and action to a theme. Practice interprets the earlier zipper image and “Hang tight” one at a time, or sorts a topic from a theme. Hide the reserved paragraphs and neighboring clue in every copy and reading.

**s04-X137.** First show page 4 of 03 beside Nia's ending. Ask: “How does the picture change your reading of Nia's ending?” Give three labeled lines for meaning or theme, mood, and beauty or pattern.
Key: joined hands make shared effort visible, muted fields keep concern present, and repeated curved channels give the image visual rhythm. Nia's final account of the group carrying the paper open can deepen an inference about persistent shared effort. Accept another supported detail for each effect. “People and lines” (I); “Nia says, ‘The fields are hopeless’” (J).
Shape: explain distinct effects of one new image and reconsider the theme using the ending. Practice links one earlier image detail to one effect or identifies a weak label. Keep all of page 4, its title, description, thumbnail and separate companion withheld.

**s04-X138.** First release groups 3 to 5 of 04, beginning with the Euphrates memory. Ask: “How do the river memories and the ending deepen the speaker's reflection?”
Key: the four river memories put the speaker's voice across places and generations. The return to ancient rivers and repetition of the soul line turn physical depth into depth of human experience. “The speaker's body sinks underwater” (M); “Rivers is the theme” (K).
Shape: relate reflective meaning to the sequence of newly released groups. Teach sequence using the stones in Eli's earlier scene. Keep groups 3 to 5 and allusion notes that give away their function out of the opener; read the full poem after release.

**s04-X139.** Release the closing stanzas of both 05 poems, the images and the performed endings. Ask: “How do words, sound and pictures make the two endings feel different?”
Key: the first moves from an empty ledger to rain worth recording. One tap followed by four and a line of blue dots make the change perceptible and add rhythm. The second returns to the opening line, its slowed delivery, pause and fixed gate holding the waiting in place and giving the refrain a reflective pattern. “Five dots and a gate” (I); “The speaker receives money” (M).
Shape: compare structure and performance effects across two new endings. Practice identifies one early line's effect or compares isolated cues on released passages. Keep the final stanzas, sounds, images and revealing performance notes covered. Hold *Rain Ledger*'s middle stanza separately for 143.

## 6. Days

## Day 136

Understanding, read literature. “I can explain how Ada's response and words suggest a theme.” Students hold the released Part 1 and a story map. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Correct the 135 script card or a short clip privately. At six stations, five short turns can each take up to 45 seconds. Prioritize the written edit while pupils wait.
- **10 min. Recording completion.** For full retakes use six stations, five turns each of up to 90 seconds including changeover. Save the marked original, cue and revision, with unfinished records named for later private completion.
- **10 min. Words.** Compare homographs, pronounce in isolation and in print, then dictate. Preview week 28 terms that support reading.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer. Hear the six assigned prose readers in six three-minute private turns. Keep the first reading, written cue, corrected reread and meaning response.
- **35 min. Farm reading.** Read to the farm map and the zipper image. Ada's “I” gives access to her fear. The zipper suggests distance between neighbors. Dad's “Hang tight” asks her to wait. Model a theme from Jo bringing two stools across the divide. Students map a different response. Summarize the released events without adding a judgment. Skip Jo's greeting and stop before the stopped-leaf passage. Remove the story map for the independent exit.
- **10 min. Exit.** Issue the reserved farm passage and collect X136. Then return its adjacent lines to the class reading copy.

**Before class.** Set out the private teacher key and keep it with the teacher. Use farm-released-136.md and story-map.md. Issue farm-exit-136.md only at collection.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use “The ___ makes the scene feel ___.” Chunk long prose with audio beside the released print and a doorway word preview. Keep independent reading and media effects distinct. For extension, defend a different tonal effect supported by a second visual feature.
