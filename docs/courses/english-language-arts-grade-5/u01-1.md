On day 4 “Use” neighboring cause or comparison clues, “Use” a known root or affix, then “Consult” print and digital entries for precise meaning and pronunciation. RI.5.2: “Determine two or more main ideas,” explain support and “summarize” on day 6. SL.5.2: “Summarize” the heard explainer using the chart with it. L.5.5b: “Recognize and explain” a customary expression in context on day 2.
- RF.5.3a: “Use” letter sounds, syllables and meaningful parts to read unfamiliar words in and out of sentences. - The teacher page explains why equal worth applies regardless of circumstances, what a quotation can warrant, and how a paragraph count differs from a count of rights. Present the declaration as stated protections. Preview a father's death, loss of home and moving for hard farm work. Offer a private pass and an entry back into the text. Keep talk on characters and pages.

- **E1, paper.** Open the covered belongings sentence and its two preceding sentences in the Article 1 explainer. “A reader says rich people matter more.   Shape: Write a rebuttal for an absent reader. The hook asks what the opening means, with no written rebuttal or belongings claim.
- **E2, oral passage and paper word mark.** Open the reserved Radishes paragraph from “She had meant” to “wanted to lift.” “Read this paragraph aloud. Mark the syllables in furniture.” Key: FUR-ni-ture, three syllables, stress first. In the reading, phrase the intended speeches separately from what Maren does. “Future” (AQ). Running “people” into “She” (AP).
  Shape: First-pass prose aloud with independent syllable marking. Practice successive readings on a familiar span with marked phrase breaks. Keep the label paragraph and furniture out of cards and models.
- **E3, paper.** Open the last phone-game paragraph in Lettuce. “Why does Maren ask Noor for another round? Quote the story.” Key: she wants Noor to play honestly even as they part. “She made Noor play one more round seriously.” “She hates Noor” (B). Quoting “play one more round fairly” as exact words (A).
  Shape: Infer a motive from a different choice and select the exact supporting words. Practice labels what the table's two measurements show, using a teacher-selected phrase.
- **E4, paper.** Open the official Article 2 list through “other status.” “What does ‘other status’ do for the list? Which words help you tell?” Key: differences outside the examples still count. “Of any kind” or “such as” supports the meaning. “Only the final category counts” (I). “Status means an online post” (K).
  Shape: Infer what an open qualifier does to a list. Withhold the clause and the matching explainer sentence. Practice tests proposed meanings and reference senses for other words.
- **E5, paper.** Open the chain-at-the-post exchange in Kale with Article 23(1). “Which words in Article 23 explain why Amos wants drivers to see the chain?” Key: “conditions of work” fits keeping drivers out of a temporarily closed work lane. “He decides everyone's pay” (B). “Everyone gets free rides” (BF).
  Shape: Use an exact article phrase to explain a visible work precaution. Practice checks copying and labels missing evidence on the clipboard, without a second work-safety application.
- **E6, paper.** Open reserved Article 23(3) and (4). “Summarize these paragraphs. Use a detail for each main idea.” Key: fair pay should support a dignified life for workers and families, with extra protection if needed. Workers can join together to protect their interests by forming or joining unions. “Workers have families” (C). “Pay and other protection help families,” with no right to act together (D).
  Shape: Condense two formal paragraphs into one supported summary. Practice groups details by focus in other paragraphs. Give the full heard explainer after the ticket so its paraphrase cannot supply the answer.

## Day 1

Understanding. “I can explain what dignity means for a new reader.”

- **5 min. Hook.** Read Article 1 and the opening explainer. Consider the unit question and preview the content privately.
- **10 min. Retrieval.** Paired round on evidence, turn rules, the absent reader and dignity by parts. Record one missed step per student.
- **10 min. Word study.** Retrieve syllable types, schwa and stress. Read and spell a three- or four-syllable word alone, in a sentence and in familiar text.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Students log purpose and detail in the released portfolio leaves from g4-u1-01. Teacher checks portfolio information and groups. Supply a teacher copy of the same leaves if the portfolio is missing.

## 8. Practice that returns

Grade 4 review: discussion moves chart, “mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer” (1.1 day 1). Keep “reading log: date, title, my purpose, one detail” (1.1 day 1), and “partner rereading with a feedback card: phrases and expression, no speed” (1.1 day 2). Evidence means “the line or page that shows an idea is true” (1.1 day 2). Infer means “work out an unstated idea using text details” (1.1 day 3). A quotation is “a source word sequence copied exactly inside paired marks” (1.5 day 19).

Day 1 retrieves the four portfolio moves. Day 2 retrieves genre, syllables and phrasing, day 3 event and theme, day 4 context and reference strategies, day 5 exact quotation, and day 6 main ideas. Teach the source-to-claim link and two groups of details from current examples. The related lower-grade models are planned only.

## 9. Supports and extension

Teach dignity as each person's worth. Try “The line about ___ suggests ___.” Offer enlarged leaves, accessible text with the same covered spans, and quiet paired rehearsal. During independent reading checks, read directions aloud and record any reading assistance apart from independent evidence. Allow dictation for a writing response when handwriting is the barrier. Ask advanced readers to test which of two taught details gives stronger support and why. At about 120 words per minute, chapters 1 to 4 need approximately 15, 15, 17 and 16 minutes before withholding the short exit spans. No required chapter goes home.
