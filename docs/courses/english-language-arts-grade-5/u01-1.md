- RI.5.4 and L.5.4a to c: “Determine or clarify” a sense. On day 4 “Use” neighboring cause or comparison clues, “Use” a known root or affix, then “Consult” print and digital entries for precise meaning and pronunciation. RI.5.2: “Determine two or more main ideas,” explain support and “summarize” on day 6. SL.5.2: “Summarize” the heard explainer using the chart with it. L.5.5b: “Recognize and explain” a customary expression in context on day 2.
- RF.5.3a: “Use” letter sounds, syllables and meaningful parts to read unfamiliar words in and out of sentences. - SL.5.1a/b: “Come” prepared with a marked detail and “Follow” turn rules and roles on day 1. W.5.4: “Produce” a response for an absent reader. W.5.10: “Write routinely” in dated quick notes and collect them toward later longer writing.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. Carry the evidence log and reading observations into section 1.2. These exits are for feedback. Sort exit writing as met, partly or not yet. On the oral roster, record what you heard and any help given. Date the fall samples.

- **E1, paper.** Open the covered belongings sentence and its two preceding sentences in the Article 1 explainer. “A reader says rich people matter more. What would you tell that reader?” Key: worth belongs to people from birth, so possessions add no worth. “A richer person matters more” (B). “Everyone deserves a car” (BF).
  Shape: Write a rebuttal for an absent reader. The hook asks what the opening means, with no written rebuttal or belongings claim.
- **E2, oral passage and paper word mark.** Open the two Radishes paragraphs from “There's an apartment in town” through “that door was purple.” Oral prompt: “Read Mom's reply and Maren's thoughts so your listener can hear the change.” Word slip: “Show the spoken parts of apartment.” Key: uh-PART-ment, three syllables, stress on the second. Pause between Mom's last sentence and the narrator's account of Maren's uncertainty. Reading only “apart” (AQ). Running Mom's last thought into “A month sounded” (AP).
  Shape: First-pass prose aloud with independent syllable marking. Practice successive readings on a familiar span with marked phrase breaks. Keep the two apartment paragraphs and apartment off cards and models.
- **E3, paper.** Open the last phone-game paragraph in Lettuce. “Using Noor's last move, explain Maren's request with an exact quotation.” Key: she wants Noor to play honestly even as they part. “She made Noor play one more round seriously.” “She hates Noor” (B). Quoting “play one more round fairly” as exact words (A).
  Shape: Infer a motive from a different choice and select the exact supporting words. Practice labels what the string measurement shows, using a teacher-selected phrase.
- **E4, paper.** Open the official Article 2 list through “other status.” “Using a clue from the list, explain what ‘other status’ means here.” Key: differences outside the examples still count. “Of any kind” or “such as” supports the meaning. “Only the final category counts” (I). “Status means an online post” (K).
  Shape: Infer what an open qualifier does to a list. Withhold the clause and the matching explainer sentence. Practice tests proposed meanings and reference senses for other words.
- **E5, paper.** Open the chain-at-the-post exchange in Kale with Article 23(1). “How does one phrase from Article 23 fit Amos's warning to drivers?” Key: “conditions of work” fits keeping drivers out of a temporarily closed work lane. “He decides everyone's pay” (B). “Everyone gets free rides” (BF).
  Shape: Use an exact article phrase to explain a visible work precaution. Practice checks copying and labels missing evidence on the clipboard, without a second work-safety application.
- **E6, paper.** Open reserved Article 23(3) and (4). “Write a summary that gives one supporting detail for each main idea.” Key: fair pay should support a dignified life for workers and families, with extra protection if needed. Workers can join together to protect their interests by forming or joining unions. “Workers have families” (C). “Pay and other protection help families,” with no right to act together (D).
  Shape: Condense two formal paragraphs into one supported summary. Practice groups details by focus in other paragraphs. Give the two assigned explainer paragraphs after the ticket so its paraphrase cannot supply the answer.

**Fall screen, unscored.** Keep the Tomatoes opening from “Tal picked the ripest tomatoes” through “just enough to stop it” for the six private screen groups. Give one minute to read as much as is comfortable. Follow the exact source words and mark phrasing at Tal's warning. For the word check, present unoccupied and reconsidering separately. Accept un-OC-cu-pied and re-con-SID-er-ing with dialect variation. Guessing “occupied” after seeing only part of unoccupied (AQ), or joining the end of Tal's warning to the narrator's next sentence (AP), gives one next step. Use the other minute for the two words and marking. A missed word gets a fresh example in the next conference.

Shape: First reading of unmarked prose and two unfamiliar words. Keep the passage and word cards off the classroom copies until all six groups finish.

## Day 1

Understanding. Read information. “I can explain what dignity means for a new reader.”

- **5 min. Hook.** Read Article 1's first sentence and the explainer's opening paragraph. Consider the unit question and preview the content privately.
- **10 min. Retrieval.** Paired round on evidence, turn rules, the absent reader and dignity by parts. Record one missed step per student.
- **10 min. Word study.** Retrieve syllable types, schwa and stress. Read and spell a three- or four-syllable word alone, in a sentence and in familiar text.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Students log purpose and detail in the released portfolio leaves from g4-u1-01. Teacher checks portfolio information and groups. Supply a teacher copy of the same leaves if the portfolio is missing.
- **35 min. Reading and talk.** Teach prepared roles and signal a pass. Discuss the opening sentence. Hold the official article's second sentence and the explainer's last three sentences. Release only the final appeal to act together at 12B, and the reason-and-conscience clause at E26. Recall challenge, shelter, evidence and infer in students' own words. Teach inheritance, dignity, however and harvest before returning to the opening. Keep the belongings span covered.
- **10 min. Exit.** Collect E1 and date it as the writing sample.

## 8. Practice that returns

Grade 4 review: discussion moves chart, “mark a detail, listen, ask for detail, link, explain what became clearer” (1.1 day 1). Keep “reading log: date, title, my purpose, one detail” (1.1 day 1), and “partner rereading with a feedback card: phrases and expression, no speed” (1.1 day 2). Evidence means “the line or page that shows an idea is true” (1.1 day 2). Infer means “work out an unstated idea using text details” (1.1 day 3). A quotation is “a source word sequence copied exactly inside paired marks” (1.5 day 19).

Day 1 retrieves the four portfolio moves. Day 2 retrieves genre, syllables and phrasing, day 3 event and theme, day 4 context and reference strategies, day 5 exact quotation, and day 6 main ideas. Teach the source-to-claim link and two groups of details from current examples. The related lower-grade models are planned only.

## 9. Supports and extension

Teach dignity as each person's worth. Try “The line about ___ suggests ___.” Offer enlarged leaves, accessible text with the same covered spans, and quiet paired rehearsal. During independent reading checks, read directions aloud and record any reading assistance apart from independent evidence. Allow dictation for a writing response when handwriting is the barrier. Ask advanced readers to test which of two taught details gives stronger support and why. At about 120 words per minute, chapters 1 to 4 need approximately 15, 15, 17 and 16 minutes before withholding the short exit spans. No required chapter goes home.
