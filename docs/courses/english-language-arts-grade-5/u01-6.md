# Section 1.6: A character's monologue

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/1/s/1.6), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u01-6.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 1, Starting over: stories of human rights (days 1 to 41), [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 29 to 34.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (29), application 4 (30 to 32, 34), check 1 (33). A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **RL.5.5, “Explain.”** Identify what belongs to dialogue, directions and one character's speech. Compare a scene boundary with a chapter boundary on day 29. Section 1.7 collects the complete structure explanation.
- **W.5.3a to e, “Orient,” “Use narrative techniques,” “Use a variety,” “Use concrete words,” “Provide a conclusion.”** Establish an immediate situation for one speaker. Order events naturally, vary pacing and transitions, make sensations specific and carry the action to a consequence.
- **W.5.4, “Produce”; W.5.5, “develop and strengthen”; W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** Plan for a listener who knows the story and one who needs context. Keep daily and extended writing with feedback and dated revisions.
- **L.5.1b to d, “Form and use,” “Use,” “Recognize and correct.”** Day 29 locates a completed action against another time. Day 34 checks participles, sequence, states, conditions and unintended shifts. **L.5.3a, “Expand, combine, and reduce,”** chooses what deserves time on day 31. **L.5.6, “Acquire and use accurately,”** uses the week's writing and grammar terms in those choices.
- **RF.5.3a, “Use” combined knowledge.** Blend phonics, syllables and meaning-bearing parts. **RF.5.4a to c, “Read” and “Use context.”** Set a purpose, read prose and verse again with feedback and verify a decoded word.

The teacher page distinguishes a monologue speaker from the author and from the student. Dialogue can be retold inside the monologue. Use a past participle after the auxiliary and choose the form that fits the reference time.

## 3. Checks, written first

Five separate paper exits. Reserve each span and its answer from instruction and modeled response.

- **E29.** In Before the Truck Leaves, release scene 2 from “I heard each one drop” through “the beep from outside”. “Which two choices make Kira's wait feel long?” Key: separate beats count the falling crates and a pause holds the third while her hands are in cold water. Accept the broken count and either the caught crate's suspension or the cold sensation insofar as they explain the delay. “A school bell rings” (B). “The objects do things” (O).
  **Shape:** Identify two pacing techniques in an unmarked speech beat. Practice sorts cast, directions and speech on other lines and marks scene boundaries.
- **E30.** In Kale, release “Maren tipped the handle” through “a finger at a time”. “Write Pilar's opening for listeners who are new to the story.” Sample: “I'm Pilar, the upstairs neighbor. In our entry, Maren's cart handle won't stay up.” Accept an opening identifying Pilar, the shared entry and the problem with the handle. “I helped her there” (R). “I built that cart for Maren” (B).
  **Shape:** Compose a brief in-character orientation. Practice checks audience background in supplied notes. Own chosen episode plan stays unjudged.
- **E31.** In Tomatoes, release the size comparison and game bag drawing. “Slow this moment with one line of dialogue and one action.” Sample: “Could you draw it?” Tal steadies the sheet as she spreads her fingers to show the size. “They talked, drew, finished” (S). “After she drew, he gave her the pencil to draw” (T).
  **Shape:** Write a short slowed beat in a reserved episode. Practice annotates time clauses and chooses one detail in a different play span, without producing a dialogue-action pair.
- **E32.** In Eggplant, release “From the far side of the tree” through the lower-step test. “Write a fitting ending in Tal's voice.” Sample: “I held up the larger sign. Maren walked back to see if people could read it.” Accept a conclusion that follows the sign test and stays with Tal. “A donor bought a new building” (U). “They approved our sign before we made the letters bigger” (T).
  **Shape:** Compose an earned ending in a reserved voice. Practice judges two unrelated sample endings for fit. Own ending plans stay in folders.
- **E34.** In Kale, release the paragraph beginning “Leon had made a city of salt grains”. Give “Mom showed me and I kept working.” Ask “Rewrite as Maren. Use a perfect tense and add one sensation.” Sample: “Mom had shown me how to lift the stem. Water hit my eye. I tore the leaf by feel.” “Mom had show me” (AU). “Water jumps into my eye,” in a past-tense rewrite with no intended shift (AV).
  **Shape:** Rewrite a supplied sentence for both time and sensory precision. Practice clips a vague word or repairs a separate verb, with private own-draft revision.

**Unit assessment item owned, 33A.** Independently draft from the revised plan. A fitting draft establishes speaker, place and problem, orders events, gives a consequential gesture dialogue or description and room to unfold, varies word/phrase/clause transitions, uses sense details and ends on a consequence. Keep the intended listener and why this voice and order serve that listener with the draft. A recount that begins “I waited there” and never establishes the situation lacks orientation (R). A list gives every event equal time (S). This is diagnostic. Return written feedback on day 34, revise on days 34 to 35 and rewrite on day 39. Score saved evidence on day 40.
**Shape:** Draft one complete, independent monologue plus a brief audience reason. Demonstrate isolated features on the filed play and reject weak fragments of unrelated cases. Keep chosen writing episodes out of models.

**Supplies evidence for 41D, day 30.** Read locality, historian and impartial in isolation and in the reserved sentences and passage in s01. Dictate the three with numbered blanks only. The key is lo-CAL-i-ty, hi-STOR-i-an and im-PAR-tial, with local + -ity, history + -ian and im- + partial. Accept dialect differences. The child explains how sounds, syllables and meaningful parts worked together. Guessing from the first syllable (AQ). Save the corrected independent sample on day 31 for the day 41 record.
**Shape:** Independently decode the same three words alone and in connected text, spell them from hearing and explain the combined strategy. Earlier practice uses other words and one guided component at a time. Seal the test list, sentences and passage.

**Supports 38D.** Section 1.7 collects the independent account of chapters, scenes and stanzas. Preserve the filed play's missing-time problem and later plan as the assessment connection.
**Shape:** Explain the function of parts in three complete texts. Day 29 locates changes in speaker, setting and format. It leaves the whole causal explanation to the check.

## Day 29

Understanding. “I can find choices that make a speech tell a story.”

- **10 min. Return.** Matched day 28 revision repair on a different case.
- **10 min. Word study.** Mix -ity, -ian and -ial, retrieve familiar prefixes and verify spellings.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Retrieve word functions and correlative partners.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Use familiar filed prose and verse. Reread for sound, understanding and phrasing.
- **42 min. Play.** Read both scenes with the E29 and E36 spans held separately. The play is 417 words total, under four reading minutes before rereads. Mark transition from shared speech at the wash table to Kira alone by the pickup line. Compare speaker and place changes with chapter 14. Place had cleared, have written and will have left on the timeline, and test the condition affecting Kira's next move.
- **8 min. Exit.** E29, reserved crate beat. Collect paper keys by component.

## Day 30

Application. “I can plan an opening for my character and listener.”

- **10 min. Return.** E29 feature or precision repair.
- **10 min. Word study.** Five minutes of mixed-suffix study on other words and five for the word record. Six simultaneous private recording stations each serve five readers at 40 seconds each, or 3 minutes 20 seconds per station. Use the remaining time for simultaneous dictation on blank lines. Collect a strategy note and distinguish an unobserved read from a spelling response.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Read, log and confer. Keep novel excerpts brief and in class.
- **42 min. Plan.** Compare background notes for a familiar student panel and an unfamiliar class or staff audience. Choose a released event and mark speaker, place and immediate problem. Keep own beginning plans unjudged. Save audience changes and choose a second audience or the unit fallback.
- **8 min. Exit.** E30, on Pilar's help in the entry. Write word-record feedback after class.

## Day 31

Application. “I can show which moment in my monologue needs more time.”

- **10 min. Return.** E30 situation or inference repair.
- **10 min. Word study.** Correct the private day 30 feedback in the first five minutes. Use the next five to map sounds and syllables in prediction, connect dict to saying or telling, then read and spell a related word. Name perfect tense, conjunction, preposition, interjection, correlative, expand, combine and reduce through the week's short revisions.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Read, log and confer. Where private rerecording is unavailable, hear necessary independent corrections during conferences and mark any evidence still pending.
- **42 min. Middle.** Contrast Kira's practiced question with the compressed drain account, omitting both reserved reading spans and the 38D connection. Locate time clauses, choose where a detail earns space and inspect which of two linkers fits the action order. Expand or reduce an unrelated supplied line for pace. Plan the child's middle privately.
- **8 min. Exit.** E31, the visitor's bag drawing.

## Day 32

Application. “I can plan an ending that grows from what happens.”

- **10 min. Return.** E31 pacing or time order.
- **10 min. Word study.** Decode and spell in the dict family and check a released sentence.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Revisit auxiliary and participle.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Repeat released print and give one partner cue.
- **42 min. Ending.** Test a consequence and an unexplained rescue in a separate example. Students plan and trace their chosen ending to a prior detail. Keep the child's conclusion unjudged.
- **8 min. Exit.** E32, the sign-test ending. File results for day 34.

## Day 33

Check. “I can draft my character's monologue from my plan.”

- **10 min. Word study.** Read and spell further dict words without the cumulative check list.

- **10 min. Setup.** Return plans and filed source leaves. Arrange private work and explain collection.

## Day 34

Application. “I can make a moment clearer with exact words and a fitting tense.”

- **10 min. Return.** Return day 33 notes. Address day 32 and day 33 needs in parallel by priority.
- **10 min. Word study.** Retrieve dict and verify a derived word against a released line.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Check a past, present and future perfect form, plus one purposeful time shift.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Use released novel and familiar verse, with one meaningful phrase cue.
- **42 min. Revision.** Compare a released play line with a vague substitute. Choose the more telling detail, and check how a participle sets order. Children sharpen their own drafts from feedback without scoring.
- **8 min. Exit.** E34, the kitchen scene. Pass the priority repair and drafts to day 35.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use pacing for how much time a storyteller gives a moment. Frame: “I had ___ before ___.” Explain that a speech beat is a brief unit of action or pause. Supply accessible versions, read directions aloud and accept a neutral scribe. Keep independent prose excerpts to roughly 60 to 200 words, or one to two minutes before writing. Extend by comparing how an unchanged gesture sounds to an informed and to a new listener.

## 10. Two rooms

Keep word cards screened and collect all spelling slips before discussion; keep peers on independent work during the visits.
