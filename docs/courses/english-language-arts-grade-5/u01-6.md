# Section 1.6: A character's monologue

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/1/s/1.6), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u01-6.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 1, Starting over: stories of human rights (days 1 to 41), [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 29 to 34.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (29), application 4 (30 to 32, 34), check 1 (33). A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **RL.5.5, “Explain.”** Identify what belongs to dialogue, directions and one character's speech. Compare a scene boundary with a chapter boundary on day 29. Section 1.7 collects the complete structure explanation.
- **W.5.3a to e, “Orient,” “Use narrative techniques,” “Use a variety,” “Use concrete words,” “Provide a conclusion.”** Establish an immediate situation for one speaker. Order events naturally, vary pacing and transitions, make sensations specific and carry the action to a consequence.
- **W.5.4, “Produce”; W.5.5, “develop and strengthen”; W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** Plan for a listener who knows the story and one who needs context. Keep daily and extended writing with feedback and dated revisions.
- **L.5.1b to d, “Form and use,” “Use,” “Recognize and correct.”** Day 29 locates a completed action against another time. Day 34 checks participles, sequence, states, conditions and unintended shifts. **L.5.3a, “Expand, combine, and reduce,”** chooses what deserves time on day 31. **L.5.6, “Acquire and use accurately,”** uses the week's writing and grammar terms in those choices.
- **RF.5.3a, “Use” combined knowledge.** Blend phonics, syllables and meaning-bearing parts. **RF.5.4a to c, “Read” and “Use context.”** Set a purpose, read prose and verse again with feedback and verify a decoded word.

The teacher page distinguishes a monologue speaker from the author and from the student. Dialogue can be retold inside the monologue. Call a helping verb an auxiliary. Show the students the past participle, the verb form that follows have, has or had in a perfect tense. Model how the two work together. “We had already cleared the drain” places the finished work before the truck came. Compare “I have written that much” with “The driver will have left by five.”

## 3. Checks, written first

Use one writing look-for per draft: a clear beginning on day 30, time spent on a choice on day 31, a prepared ending on day 32, and a precise change on day 34. Return one cue with the draft. Keep each ticket passage and its answer out of teaching.

- **E29, paper.** Release Before the Truck Leaves, scene 2, “I heard each one drop on the stack” through “Then came the beep from outside”. “How does Kira’s telling make the wait for the third crate feel long?” Key: The separate “One. Two.” slows the count. The third crate catches, and the cold water detail holds the reader in the pause. Accept one specific counting, suspension or sensory choice that explains how the waiting feels longer. “A school bell rings.” (B). “The objects do things.” (O).
  **Shape:** Explain how a specific pacing choice stretches an unmarked speech beat. Practice sorts dialogue, directions and speech on other lines. Hold the crate beat until the exit.
- **E30, paper.** Release Kale, “Maren tipped the handle toward herself” through “This time it stood”. “Write Pilar’s opening for listeners who are new to the story.” Key: “I’m Pilar from upstairs. I saw Maren struggling to stand a cart handle in our entry.” Accept an imagined opening naming or identifying Pilar, the shared entry and the problem with the handle. “I helped her there.” (R). “I built that cart for Maren.” (B).
  **Shape:** Compose a brief in-character orientation for a new audience. Compare background notes. Keep the student’s own opening plan unscored.
- **E31, paper.** Release Tomatoes, guest’s storage question and the next paragraph, “Tal pulled the bakery over” through “for her to draw”. “Slow this moment with one line of dialogue and one action.” Key: “Could you draw it?” Tal presses the paper flat as the visitor spreads her fingers. Accept imagined dialogue and a specific action that stay within the drawing moment and preserve its order. “They talked, drew, finished.” (S). “After she drew, he gave her the pencil to draw.” (T).
  **Shape:** Write a short slowed beat with one speech line and one action. Practice marks time clauses and chooses a detail on other lines.
- **E32, paper.** Release Eggplant, “From the far side of the tree” through “where the strip of pavement turned”. “Write a fitting ending in Tal’s voice.” Key: “I held the bigger sign on the lower step so Maren could test it from the turn.” Accept an ending in Tal’s first-person voice that follows the sign test. “A donor bought a new building.” (U). “Maren tested the larger letters before she wrote them.” (T).
  **Shape:** Compose an earned ending in a reserved character’s voice. Practice judges a supplied choice on an unrelated case. Keep the student’s own conclusion unscored.
- **E34, paper.** Release Kale, the single paragraph beginning “Leon had made a city of salt grains”. “Mom showed me and I kept working. Rewrite Maren’s line with a perfect verb and one detail about what she feels.” Key: “Water stung my eye after Mom had shown me how to lift the stem.” Accept a grammatical perfect form with a defensible time relationship and a sensation grounded in the paragraph. “Water stung my eye after Mom had show me how.” (AU). “Water jumps into my eye after Mom had shown me how.” (AV).
  **Shape:** Rewrite one supplied sentence for time and sensory precision. Practice repairs an isolated word or verb. Keep the student’s own revision unscored.

**Unit assessment item owned, 33A.** Independently draft from the revised plan. A fitting draft establishes speaker, place and problem, orders events, slows one important moment with a gesture, dialogue or description, varies word/phrase/clause transitions, uses sense details and ends on a consequence. Keep the intended listener and why this voice and order serve that listener with the draft. A recount that begins “I waited there” and never establishes the situation lacks orientation (R). A list gives every event equal time (S). Reversing two events (T), using only then (BE), replacing a sensation with “stuff” (O), or adding an unprepared rescue (U) each misses one separate look-for. This is diagnostic. Return written feedback on day 34, revise on days 34 to 35 and rewrite on day 39. Score saved evidence on day 40.
**Shape:** Draft one complete, independent monologue plus a brief audience reason. Demonstrate isolated features on the filed play and reject weak fragments of unrelated cases. Keep chosen writing episodes out of models.

**Supplies evidence for 41D, day 30.** Use separate short directions for the isolated reading, the sentence reading, the passage and the strategy note. Read locality, historian and impartial in isolation and in the reserved sentences and passage in s01. Dictate the three with numbered blanks only. The key is lo-CAL-i-ty, hi-STOR-i-an and im-PAR-tial, with local + -ity, history + -ian and im- + partial. Accept dialect differences. The child explains how sounds, syllables and meaningful parts worked together. Guessing from the first syllable (AQ). Save the corrected independent sample on day 31 for the day 41 record.
**Shape:** Independently decode the same three words alone and in connected text, spell them from hearing and explain the combined strategy. Earlier practice uses other words and one guided component at a time. Seal the test list, sentences and passage.

**Supports 38D.** Section 1.7 collects the independent account of chapters, scenes and stanzas. Preserve the filed play's missing-time problem and later plan as the assessment connection.
**Shape:** Explain the function of parts in three complete texts. Day 29 locates changes in speaker, setting and format. It leaves the whole causal explanation to the check.

- **10 min. Return.** Matched day 28 revision repair on a different case.

- **5 min. Grammar.** Retrieve word functions and correlative partners.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Use familiar filed prose and verse. Reread for sound, understanding and phrasing.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use pacing for how much time a storyteller gives a moment. Frame: “I had ___ before ___.” Explain that a speech beat is a brief unit of action or pause. Supply accessible versions, read directions aloud and accept a neutral scribe. Keep independent prose excerpts to roughly 60 to 200 words, or one to two minutes before writing. Extend by comparing how an unchanged gesture sounds to an informed and to a new listener.

## 10. Two rooms

One parent hears the first version and asks a question as an unfamiliar listener. Use a parent-approved second listener, or two audience-specific pieces and live parent questions. Use paper scenes if there is only one screen. With no recorder, hear one reader privately at a time. Allow 60 seconds each for the isolated words, the sentences, the short passage and handoff. Keep waiting readers on released work. If someone misses the check, take the first available individual conference on day 31 for that initial sample and return it with a later correction slot.
