# Section 1.3: Comparing responses

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 1, Starting over: stories of human rights, see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 13 to 18.** Days by kind: understanding 4, application 2. A plan, not a lesson. Targets are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- RL.5.3: “Compare and contrast” responses to the same challenge on days 13 and 15 and how settings affect interactions on day 18. W.5.2a/b: “Introduce” an observation and focus, group the details, and “Develop” the explanation with specific actions. W.5.4: “Produce” writing an unfamiliar reader can follow. W.5.10: “Write routinely” in short comparisons and carry the writing into the next section's folder.
- RL.5.4 and L.5.5a/b: “Determine” and “Interpret” what a metaphor or simile adds, and “Recognize and explain” an idiom, adage and proverb in context. RL.5.2: “Determine a theme” through a response and “summarize” the events that matter. RL.5.5: “Explain” what a new chapter changes about an earlier one. On day 17, RL.5.1 and RI.5.1 “Quote accurately” to explain a literary event and its connection to a stated article.
- L.5.1a: “Explain” generally and in an anchor sentence what a conjunction joins, what relation a preposition shows, and what feeling an interjection expresses. L.5.1c/d: “Use” tense for time, sequence, state and condition and “Recognize and correct” an unintended shift. L.5.2a/b: “Use punctuation” to separate items in a series and “Use a comma” after an introduction.
- RF.5.3a: “Use” letter sounds, syllables and morphology in and out of context. RF.5.4a to c: “Read” with purpose and on successive readings with accuracy, pace and expression. “Use context” to confirm or self-correct after decoding. L.5.6: “Acquire and use accurately” comparison, word study and contextual vocabulary.
- Explain how two details must answer the same comparison question and why a fitting image is different from a literal event. Preview the family's loss and moves, Mom feeling ill in the heat and concerns about hours and pay. Offer a pass and a private return to the page. All discussion and writing use the characters' experiences.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. Seal The Blue Card for day 22. Keep the short comparison drafts in the folder and collect the following paper tickets. Mark each teaching criterion shown, shown with help, or not yet seen.

- **E13.** Open the cracked bakery corner in Summer Squash. “How do Maren and Tal handle the same break differently?” Key: both help repair it. Maren directs the join and redraws bricks. Tal offers tape and imagines a history for the patch. “Maren fixes it” (F). “Tal wants the city ruined” (B).
  Shape: Write a short comparison from two actions around one break. Practice fills an observation chart about the locked door without drafting a comparison paragraph. Keep the bakery span sealed.
- **E14.** Open the Eggplant queue through the father's movement. “The queue was a zipper. What does that image help you picture?” Key: people fit close together, so one person's movement makes others move too. “His jacket stuck” (H). “Everyone leaves quickly” (B).
  Shape: Explain what the image adds to a specific movement. Work with a picture choice for the cracker silo in Kale, then mark evidence that rules out a literal reading. Keep the queue and any zipper echo out of practice.
- **E15.** Open the Sweet Corn steps passage. “How do Leon and Maren respond when the book stops making sense? Write one paragraph.” Key: both want the door scene to make sense. Leon imagines a mouse's reason and Maren offers to reread, then starts before the noise. “She opens the book again” (F). “Both forgot how to read” (B).
  Shape: Build a supported paragraph around two responses to an interruption. Practice marks word functions and sorts separate response details, away from the steps scene and the paragraph form.
- **E16.** Open Jo's saying and the final test in Peppers. “How does ‘measure twice, cut once’ explain Maren's last test with Leon?” Key: check from the user's position before settling on a choice. She has Leon step back to see whether he can recognize the tag. “Cut the tag in half” (H). “The theme is tools” (E).
  Shape: Connect a customary saying to a character's final action and the idea behind it. Practice follows the route to the hall and studies distinct sayings with meaning labels. Keep Jo's saying and the tag test closed until the exit.
- **E17.** Open the visitor's calendar exchange in Tomatoes with Article 2. “How does replacing ‘one week’ help the monthly group? Use words from Article 2.” Key: “without distinction of any kind” illuminates asking each group for dates so monthly users can request storage between meetings. “Article 2 guarantees a shelf” (BF). “She bars weekly users” (B).
  Shape: Explain how changing a rule improves access for a previously overlooked group. Practice punctuates meeting and food lines, avoiding a rule-to-right explanation and the paper clock exchange.
- **E18.** Open Tal's school interruption and the crew's open-board exchange in Carrots. “How do the two places affect who can answer a question?” Key: the school line limits Tal's help when the teacher sends him back. At the open farm board people gather, compare what they know and make room to see. Both organize participation. “The farm has clocks” (F). “Tal refuses to help” (B).
  Shape: Compare effects of two settings on interactions in one response. Practice marks where people stand on a map and repairs tense on a timeline. Keep the two assessed exchanges off the maps and cards.

**Day 15 word check, separate from E15.** Reserve obscurity. Present it alone and in “The sign faded into obscurity.” Ask each student to read it, then cover the text before dictation. Key: four spoken syllables, stress on the second, and obscure plus -ity drops the final e. A beginning-only guess (AQ) gets a new-word retry on day 16. Spelling lines are numbered and blank.

Shape: Read a new derived word alone and in context and spell it without a printed copy. Model base-to-derived changes using different words and a part sort. Withhold this word from all teaching sets and any answer visible during spelling.

## Day 15
