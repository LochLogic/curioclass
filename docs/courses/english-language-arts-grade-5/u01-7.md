English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 1, Starting over: stories of human rights (days 1 to 41), [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 35 to 38.** Days by kind: application 2 (35, 37), fluency 1 (36), check 1 (38). A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.5.5, “develop and strengthen”; W.5.3b to d, “Use.”** Try a different opening or pacing, with dialogue, description, varied transitions and specific sense details. A rewrite changes what the listener can follow or notice.
- **W.5.2a and b, “Introduce,” “Develop”; W.5.9a and b, “Draw evidence”; W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** Use setting evidence and source lines to explain what an unfamiliar audience needs. A program note supplies context for hearing a performance, with the novel, article and explainer doing different jobs.
- **SL.5.1a to d, “Come,” “Follow,” “Pose and respond,” “Review.”** Prepare, act in a role, build on a specific remark and reach a conclusion. **RF.5.4a to c, “Read” and “Use context.”** Pace and phrase prose and verse for a chosen purpose. Check the words and their meaning on successive reads. **RF.5.3a, “Use” combined knowledge.** Connect letters, syllables and roots.
- **RL.5.1 and RI.5.1, “Quote accurately”; RL.5.2, “Determine a theme” and “summarize”; RL.5.4 and RI.5.4, “Determine”; RL.5.5, “Explain”; RL.5.10 and RI.5.10, “read and comprehend.”** Distinguish stated evidence and inference, use structure to explain a whole, infer meaning in context and read unfamiliar literary and informational selections independently. Day 36 connects a speaker's reflection to theme before the check.
- **L.5.4a to c, “Use,” “Consult”; L.5.5a to c, “Interpret,” “Recognize and explain,” “Use”; L.5.6, “Acquire and use accurately.”** Use context, roots and references, including pronunciation. Interpret figures and sayings and explain word relations in context. Use however for contrast and moreover for addition.

## 3. Checks, written first

- **E35, paper.** Release Eggplant, “The woman who was helping with the line” through Maren giving the man her pencil. Give “Maren let him explain.” Use two prompts with separate spaces. Rewrite: “As Maren, slow the moment when you choose who will explain.” Reader's note: “Why would you keep your chosen version for listeners?” Sample, as Maren: “I had my answer ready. I asked if he wanted to tell her. He spoke, then I handed him my pencil.” The pause lets listeners hear whose experience matters. Only recopying the line (Q). “I fixed things with stuff” (O).

- **E36, oral.** Release Kira's paragraph beginning “When I looked up” in Before the Truck Leaves. “Read Kira's words so a new listener can hear what changes at the clock.” Key: preserve the difference between her earlier ability to read the clock and today's obstruction. Use a meaningful break around “I couldn't see it today.” “Her shift must be over” as the reading purpose (AO). Rushing through the change in time so the thought runs together (AP).
  **Shape:** Read a first-release play paragraph so the phrasing conveys a discovery to an unfamiliar listener. Opening rehearsal marks and tests breaks in the child's own draft. It has no cold clock passage or independent discovery task.
- **E37, paper.** Release Broccoli's paragraph beginning “There were two shelves that no one had asked about”. “Write a source-labeled note that helps a listener picture the room.” Sample: “Leon cannot reach a top shelf. Ed puts towels at the low sink where people can reach them.” Source: Curio Class, The Next Harvest, “Broccoli”. “The town bans children from the cupboard” (B). A setting claim with no supporting detail (L).
  **Shape:** Turn a reserved physical detail into an attributed note. Practice sorts fragments by source and marks literal borrowing, with separate passages.

- **38D.** Use the chapter map for order and release the three bowl excerpts. In Lettuce, nobody can decide where to pack the bowl. In Kale, it holds the bread bag in place on the family's journey. - **38E.** Quote an actual Article 25 protection. An employed-adults-only program excludes people protected by “security in the event of unemployment” and children promised “the same social protection”. “Only employed adults qualify” (B). Importing a different article's property right (BF).
  **Shape:** Quote one explicit protection and a separate basis for an inference across the paragraphs. Keep this article, the explainer and the proposed program off practice leaves.
- **38F.** Livelihood means a means of supporting life. Security here is protection when that support is lost. “Door guard” (K).
  **Shape:** Resolve two words in the reserved explainer. Practice chooses a single sense in a different context.
- **38G.** Essential means necessary, as the named needs for living show. Spect means look, so the spectator watches the game. The reference yields LIVE-li-hood, with the first syllable rhyming with five, and means of support. Missing the contextual clue (I). Spect means “say” (J). “Neighborhood” (I).
  **Shape:** Apply context, a root and a reference in one independent record. Teach the moves separately. Reserve all three target words, the spectator sentence and the exact glossary entry.
- **38H.** Lend a hand means help. Many hands make light work means shared effort can ease the work. Bright hands (H).
  **Shape:** Explain both sayings in use across two cold texts. Keep the sayings and both cases out of practice.
- **38I.** Near meanings here: open and unlatched both offer a way in. Closed is opposed to open. Light means easy in the proverb and illumination by the lamp. Treating both as illumination (K).
  **Shape:** Explain near, opposite and same-spelling word relations across the two reserved texts. Teach a different homograph pair in day 21 practice; light for easy and illumination is reserved here.
- **38J.** Use previously gathered notes to explain the setting and right relevant to the chosen monologue. Distinguish the novel, article and explainer and relate one explainer detail to its main idea. Teacher example, kept off all practice copies: “The shed board leaves out time spent between jobs. ## Day 35

Application. Write. “I can rewrite a moment to help listeners follow it.”

- **10 min. Return.** E34 participle or time-line repair.
- **10 min. Word study.** Read and spell new dict words on released examples.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log and confer.
- **42 min. Rewrite.** Judge two critique notes on a different supplied opening. Trade descriptive feedback on own alternatives, then try a new beginning or pacing and save both versions. Keep the child's own choice and rewrite unjudged.
- **8 min. Exit.** E35 on the pencil exchange.

## Day 36

Fluency. Speak and listen. “I can phrase a monologue so listeners hear what changes.”

- **12 min. Rehearsal.** Start in 15 pairs. On the child's own draft, first mark phrase boundaries and choose a purpose. Allow both partners two 45-second reads and one brief cue each. Save a cue for the next reading.
- **5 min. Return.** E35 meaning change or specific detail.
- **10 min. Word study.** Map sound, syllable and spect in inspect and related words. Keep spectator closed.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log and confer.
- **15 min. Model.** Inspect brief setting and source-note components from a released room setting other than the shelf and shed examples. Use the filed fallback in field 11 if the proposed teaching stanza is unavailable. Use the teaching verse to label what the speaker first thought and what changed, then choose a fitting theme and order summary details. Recall the purpose of rereading, phrasing with expression and using a reference to check a word. Teach critique, rewrite and approach through the rehearsal. Use the actual reading to explain fluency and accuracy. The grade 4 term rows for these last two are planned only.
- **20 min. Discussion.** Six parallel groups of five, prepared with a marked released line. Use Amos's reminder about the engine and Kira's pause before approaching the truck. Ask what the pause shows about Kira choosing her own words. Leave the hidden clock and her final solution for the independent readings. Each learner has 45 seconds each to contribute, question, respond and conclude, a 15-minute group round. Use facilitator, evidence-finder and questioner roles, doubling where needed. Stagger six teacher-observed turns and collect every written conclusion.
- **8 min. Exit.** E36, with a 45-second independent read per reader in parallel pairs and time to read the directions and note purpose. The teacher hears a rotating six; collect local audio from the other 24, or mark their names pending. Without audio, hear 12 private 40-second readings during day 37 Return and 12 in Partner rereading (eight minutes per block). Give each reader one phrase cue and a chance to reread. Keep all written purpose slips separate from the oral marks. Use the same quiet option for a public pass.

## Day 37

Application. Write. “I can use a source line to explain a setting.”

- **10 min. Return.** E36 meaning or phrasing. With no audio, give a group cue in two minutes and hear the first 12 pending readers during paired practice.
- **10 min. Word study.** Read and spell a fresh spect word, including a sentence check.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Retrieve word functions and correlative pairs.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Prose and filed familiar verse with one partner cue and another read.
- **42 min. Source notes.** Sort short notes by title. Label exact quotation versus paraphrase, keeping source locations beside the evidence. Gather novel, released article and explainer details by hand in fragments for the chosen monologue. Keep Article 25 and the Broccoli shelves leaf out of this work.
- **8 min. Exit.** E37, the separate shelf and sink paragraph. Record any source-note need for supported practice after the checks.

## Day 38

- **40 min. Independent suite.** A to I use 6, 7, 3, 6, 5, 3, 4, 3 and 3 minutes, including reading and rereading. Collect before showing any key or related passage. Return written reading and note feedback on day 39.
