# Section 1.2: Reading rights and discussing evidence

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/1/s/1.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u01-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 1, Starting over: stories of human rights, see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 7 to 12.** Days by kind: understanding 1, application 3, check 2. A plan, not a lesson. Targets are ours, untested.

## 1. Standards and practices

- RI.5.1 and RL.5.1: “Quote accurately” for explicit claims and inferences. On days 7 to 12 use exact article and story words with a warrant. W.5.9a/b: “Apply” the reading standards in written source notes. RI.5.2: “Determine two or more main ideas,” explain each with details and “summarize” the article and companion. RI.5.10: “read and comprehend” a new article independently on day 9. It is an early informational reading sample.
- RI.5.4 and L.5.4a to c: “Determine” the contextual sense, “Use” cause clues, “Use” affixes and roots, and “Consult” print or digital references for pronunciation and precise meaning. Day 7 models with Article 17 and day 8 practices on other words before day 9 checks. RL.5.5: “Explain” how one chapter prepares another, using cause and consequence on days 10 to 12.
- SL.5.1a to d: “Come” prepared, “Follow” agreed rules and roles, “Pose and respond” with an elaboration, and “Review” ideas to draw a conclusion. Model the last two moves on day 10. W.5.10: “Write routinely” through dated evidence slips and longer group preparation across days.
- RF.5.3a: “Use” sounds, syllables and meaningful parts in and out of sentences. RF.5.4a to c: “Read” for understanding, accuracy, rate and expression on successive prose and verse readings and “Use context” to confirm or self-correct following decoding. L.5.6: “Acquire and use accurately” domain words, plus contrasts and additions.
- Distinguish temporary possession from ownership, a stated right from a promise about a fictional case, and an elaboration from repeating the speaker. Preview involuntary loss of home and property, hours and pay disputes, and a character afraid to interrupt work to drink. Keep discussions on the pages and offer a private pass.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** 9A to 9G and 12A to 12E, in s01 field 5. Use its 2, 1, 0 scale on the assessment record, by criterion, after written feedback and independent correction. Sort daily writing as met, partly or not yet. Keep a separate roster for observed speech. On day 9, first release the filed Article 13 and its filed explainer together. On day 12, release the reserved table excerpts and print every prepared source separately from the keys.

- **E7, paper.** Open the spare-wheel offer at the end of Beets with Article 17. “How does one phrase from Article 17 help explain Maren's choice about her wheel?” Key: “the right to own property alone” can support her choosing whether to change her own cart. “He owns it because he drove it” (B). “Everyone gets a new wheel” (BF).
  Shape: Explain a refusal through ownership and quote the new clause. Practice sorts alone and shared ownership using the explainer's tools and bench. Reserve the driver's spare-wheel offer.
- **E8, paper.** Open the Broccoli paragraph beginning “That afternoon Pilar came to get Tal”. “Summarize Tal's choice through the idea of equal treatment.” Key: Pilar offers to sign for shelf space, but Tal asks to move their whole city upstairs for now. The alternative keeps the joint project together when the rule treats residents differently. “Tal gets a shelf for everyone” (B). “Article 2 promises a free shelf” (BF).
  Shape: Condense the offer and response through the article's principle. Use the familiar right as a lens. Only the new story leaf goes on the ticket. In practice, order the opening form's events on a strip and inspect words. Keep the workaround and final paragraph out of the summary plan.
- **E10, oral with a collected source note.** Open Mom's summer recollection in Green Beans. “Using Mom's words, explain which work right might be at risk.” Key: “afraid to take a drink” suggests pressure on work conditions under Article 23(1). “She was forbidden water” (B). “Every worker gets bottled water” (BF).
  Shape: Make an independent 45-second evidence turn about an implied risk. Group practice builds on someone else's observation about the rounding gap rather than giving a source turn about a personal recollection. Reserve the water exchange.
- **E11, paper.** Open the bicycle messenger's exchange in Cucumbers. “Explain the problem with one meeting time using the messenger's exact words.” Key: “She works till six on Tuesdays,” so that option conflicts with the grandmother's work. She sent other times, including Saturday. “She works until six every Tuesday” as an exact quote (A). “She runs the meeting” (B).
  Shape: Locate and explain one exact schedule line. Rehearsal uses assigned notes about roof priorities, without finding a line about a schedule. Keep the messenger exchange off the role card.

**Day 9 check keys and reservations.** Keep every item word and both texts out of days 7 and 8. Give one prompt per response box on every unit check. - **9A.** For the explicit freedom, accept “freedom of movement and residence within the borders of each state.” For the travel inference, living in a new town leaves intact the right “to leave any country” and “to return to his country.” Give each part its own answer line. Changed words (A), giving up return without warrant (B).
  Shape: Quote explicit permission then reconcile two scopes in a new situation. Teach scope by bracket and label only, with no analogous travel case.
- **9B.** Domestic movement and residence, then departure and return, with a clause detail for each and a joined summary. One freedom alone (D).
  Shape: Independently synthesize two ideas across article and explainer. Practice sorts or labels supplied details without composing the whole answer.
- **9C.** State means country. A mood (K).
  Shape: Narrow a polysemous word from the surrounding article and explainer. In instruction, underline a clue and test teacher-supplied meanings with other words. Keep this sense of state out of precheck materials.
- **9D.** Independently reread and explain one confirmation or correction, such as finding that return is to one's own country. An answer unrelated to either clause (BF).
  Shape: Audit an earlier answer against another reading of the source. Practice tracks a teacher's first reading, without a prewritten answer audit.
- **9E.** Restricted means limited or prevented. The clue is “cannot choose freely.” “Welcomed” (I).
  Shape: Supply a meaning and its cause clue. Practice labels a clue in a different word example with the candidate meaning supplied.
- **9F.** Re- contributes back. First departure (J).
  Shape: Explain how a prefix contributes to the meaning in this clause. Practice sorts reopen, rewind and unicycle by word part, without making a context explanation for return.
- **9G.** RES-i-dence, a place to live. Duties of an office (I).
  Shape: Independently consult a new entry and record stress and sense. In practice match separate print and digital lookup entries or hear a modeled pronunciation. Reserve residence and the two choices.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Students retain the chapter map, evidence log and source tag. They move from teacher-bracketed clauses to independently locating phrases. For discussion, a turn card and roster fit collecting direct observation by criterion. Use board strips, paper and a pencil in either room. There is no key tool.

## 10. Two rooms

At the table the parent brings an assigned article line and takes the questioner's role. The child uses a note, answers a linked question and writes a conclusion about what changed. With one projector, show a teaching clause at a time, then use paper checks and print lookup entries.
