## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.5.3 and W.5.3a to e.** “Write narratives.” “Orient” a reader in a connected first-person sequence. “Use narrative techniques,” “Use a variety” of time links and “Use concrete words.” “Provide a conclusion” that follows. **W.5.5, “develop and strengthen.”** Document a change from written feedback.
- **W.5.6, “use technology,” “interact and collaborate,” “demonstrate.”** Keep separate evidence of two substantive pages typed in one sitting and actual online exchange and publication.
- **RI.5.3, “Explain.”** Support a relationship with details. **RI.5.5, “Compare and contrast.”** Say what information each of four organizational structures foregrounds. **RI.5.7, “Draw on.”** Locate evidence efficiently using print and digital sources on 78.
- **W.5.7, “Conduct.”** Investigate multiple aspects through several sources. **W.5.8, “gather,” “summarize or paraphrase,” “provide.”** Use relevant information from print and digital sources or experience, paraphrase in notes and finished writing, and list titles.
- **RL.5.6, “Describe.”** Explain what each teller lets a reader know. **RL.5.9 and L.5.3b, “Compare and contrast.”** On 79, compare how two adventures handle a shared theme and how their dialogue and written notes serve different audiences. **W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** Keep dated short and sustained work for different purposes.
- **Teacher explanation.** An observed fact has a source and a scope. Fiction may put someone in a scene within that scope. Keep what a story states separate from an inference, and name what the fiction adds. Conversational and formal language can both suit their purpose.
