## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** 39A and B, 40A to E, and 41A to E. Each day uses its independent evidence and dated products without an additional scored exit.

- **39A.** Rewrite independently from written feedback on earlier versions. Look for an immediately identifiable situation and speaker, natural order, detail and dialogue where they earn time, word/phrase/clause transitions, perceptible detail and an ending arising from the event. An unexplained new rescuer (U) fails the ending component.
  **Shape:** Independently revise a complete monologue from a dated record. Practice inspects isolated weak features on other cases. Keep the selected source event off model and retry sheets.
- **39B.** Select before-and-after pages and explain how a meaningful change and an edit serve someone unfamiliar with the story. A clean copy without a change in approach (Q) or an omitted essential detail (R) misses the corresponding criterion. Use the last five minutes to revise 38J from written feedback and save both products for day 40.
  **Shape:** Use a documented before-and-after pair to justify a change, then revise a separate saved note. Keep practice to choosing between supplied reader comments.
- **40A.** Mark the original observation, focus and logical groups of the saved essay. Verify accurate and relevant facts, definitions, details and quotations, within-group and across-group links, two precise words and a related conclusion. Justify whether formatting helps. Mixed categories (M), unsupported claim (L), incorrect linker (N), vague replacement for a term (O) and unrelated ending (P) are separate faults.
  **Shape:** Verify a whole saved essay against the component record. Practice isolates one invented organizational fault at a time. Do not display a complete verification or reuse an assessment essay as a model.
- **40B.** With one prompt for each dimension, verify one character, one setting and one event comparison from the completed essay and novel. Each names both instances, a supported likeness and a difference. Tab relevant passages in advance. Have each student locate any saved comparison during 40A, then use a phrase for each likeness and difference on three labeled lines in 40B. Accept source tabs as detail references. Use the student's dated independent comparisons wherever available. For a missing dimension, supply just its reserved leaf with paragraph tabs. Teacher key: Jo and Kira both want coworkers to get help in the heat. Jo offers a ride, while Kira asks for a way to signal across the rows (Sweet Corn). Give only Kira's message and Jo's offer on the leaf. Hold the narrator's own comparison until after 40B. Both rooms host meetings, but from the classroom Maren sees only heads. The low hall windows show where people go (Eggplant). Noor's family offers room for a table by shifting other things (Lettuce), while Ona May points to a room that can be locked for the cart (Cabbage). Both offers make space for an object the family values. Accept any supported set meeting the conditions. One instance alone (F).
  **Shape:** Verify three different dimensions in one evidence record. Practice supplies detail labels for one dimension at a time. Keep the day 18 school-line/farm-board pair and other exit pairs out of the item.
- **40C.** Sharing the Room: large print and a spoken version give access to the words. Separate quiet and discussion places let people have a say in shared space. The summary links both to supporting different readers. Large print alone (C); access without a second main idea (D).
  **Shape:** Extract two ideas, detail support and a brief summary from an unread text. Prior work labels separate detail groups on other cases. Keep the listening leaf and chart covered.
- **40D.** Keeping the Books Ready: protection reduces damage and repair restores use. The chart counts two examples of each, four examples in all. Numbers without the message (AN).
  **Shape:** Integrate a new heard message with pictured category counts. Earlier chart work uses other content and may read one representation at a time. Do not show the words of this recording or the chart during 40C.
- **40E.** State a purpose for each reading and explain a line from each text. Show accurate, appropriately paced and expressive successive reads of the monologue and A Place for a Voice. Identify a word decoded fully and confirmed or corrected by rereading its sentence. Missing meaning (AO), rushed boundaries (AP), guessing from context (AQ).
  **Shape:** Record successive reads in two genres plus purpose, meaning and a word check. Rehearsal addresses one cue with different excerpts. Save one record, with a new segment only if a correction is needed.
- **41A.** Bring notes on another student's previously unmodeled monologue, shared for private preparation after the day 40 performances. Discuss which choice in that speech lets someone decide what happens. Each group gets a speech it did not rehearse or discuss. Use a different protected monologue for anyone who needs a private exchange. Use the note aloud, fulfill the role, ask a specific question, build on an answer and write a conclusion that uses a contribution. Lack of preparation (AK), taking another's turn (AL), ignoring a reply (V) and merely restating a first claim (AM).
  **Shape:** Use an unmodeled prompt for prepared interaction and individual synthesis. Practice one discussion move at a time on different language and actions.
- **41B.** Verify dated short notes, essay and monologue for different purposes, audiences, reflection and revision. One undated sample (AR) or absent background for the audience (R).
  **Shape:** Verify an actual set of varied saved work. Practice sorts unrelated sample labels. Collect only missing evidence.
- **41C.** Use the reserved numbered passage and spelling strip in s01. Keep the printed editing sentences as filed. Split directions into short prompts. Key by component:
  - Example expansion: “Mira had balanced the heavy box against her knee before Tomas reached the door.” Example join: “Oh, Tomas opened the door, and after a pause, Mira stopped.” Example reduction: “Mira stopped, unsure.” Expansion clarifies the situation. Joining ties the events together. Reduction gives the hesitation a quick beat. An irrelevant addition (AS).
  - A conjunction joins words or groups. In sentence 6, and joins the last item to the series. A preposition shows a relationship. Against relates the box to the knee. An interjection expresses a reaction. Oh opens sentence 2 with surprise. “Against joins clauses” (AT).
  - For example: “I had balanced the box before he opened the door.” “I have balanced it twice today.” “By noon I will have balanced it three times.” Had balance (AU).
  - Had balanced precedes Tomas reaching the door; stopped follows the pause; was describes a state. “If he steadies the box, she can carry it” expresses a condition. A reversed before/after relationship (T).
  - Correct offers to offered in the past narrative. Keeping offers (AV). The pairs are either/or and neither/nor. “Either Mira nor Tomas” (AW).
  - “Books, pencils, and paper” is correct with or without the serial comma. Writing “books pencils and paper” (AX) leaves items unseparated. “After a pause, Mira stopped.” Omitting that comma (AY). “Yes, Tomas, the box is heavy, isn't it?” “No, it is not.” Missing the comma after Tomas (AZ), writing “No it is not” (BC), or omitting the comma before the tag (BD) are distinct errors.
  - Put quotation marks around the short-story title “The Open Door.” Leaving the title unmarked (BA). Evidence, dignity and shelter use the spelling strip. Explain that the suffix -ity in dignity helps verify the i (BB for retaining dignaty).
  **Shape:** Complete a fresh mixed editing and explanation set. Earlier practice handles one feature on different lines. Reserve all numbered sentences, the quote/title task and the misspellings through the independent set.
- **41D.** Reuse day 30's corrected locality, historian and impartial record. Verify readings alone and in context, spelling and the combined use of sound, syllable and word parts. Guessing from the start (AQ).
  **Shape:** Verify the stored three-word transfer record. Keep the words, recorded passage and dictation out of models and later word practice.
- **41E.** Reuse 40E, including a corrected segment if needed. Give each observed error its own letter: unsupported meaning (AO), rushed phrases (AP), or guessing before decoding (AQ).
  **Shape:** Verify purpose and successive reading samples from the saved audio and notes. No new practice on the assessed segments.
