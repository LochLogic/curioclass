## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** None. Preparation for 49B, 49C and 78B. Keep those passages and sample responses sealed. Shapes and keys for scored items stay with their owners.

All exits are paper. Give each numbered prompt its own response space. Use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen in separate component columns.

- **42, RI.5.3 and RI.5.4.** Packet 1, unseen sentences from "Another insect might visit" through "each animal could use more than one part." Ask "Which possible feeding link does the paragraph describe?" "What makes that eater a consumer?" Key: an animal might eat fruit from the tree. It gets food from another living thing. Wrong: the tree eats the animal (B). A consumer must buy something (Z).
  
  Shape: infer a possible new food link and apply a word. Keep possible fruit feeding off the observed leaf arrows and practice cards.
- **43, RI.5.5 and RI.5.2.** Packet 2, final paragraph. Ask "How does the writer order this trial?" "Summarize its two main ideas with details." Key: attempt, revision and retry in time order. Two alike trunks led Lucía to the wrong spot. Adding the bend and viewing angle let her line up the same branches on her next try. Wrong: the map (A). The whole paragraph lists causes because it says "because" (C).
  
  Shape: recover organization and two supported ideas from a failed trial. Rearrange earlier preparation steps only. Keep the corrected route off maps.
- **44, RI.5.3 and L.5.4a.** Packet 3, forest-floor paragraph. Ask "How can fallen leaves help living plants?" "Use nearby words to explain decomposition." Key: fungi and bacteria break down dead leaves and release nutrients plants can take up. "Break down" explains decomposition. Wrong: roots eat whole leaves (B). Decomposition means something happens (O).
  
  Shape: explain a process from prose and identify the context clue. Practice light through a cover and lamp. Keep the leaf-to-root arrow and caption covered.
- **45, RI.5.2 and W.5.8.** Packet 4, sticky-sheet paragraph. Ask "Summarize both findings about which notes could be placed." "Restate one finding in a labeled source note." Key: the readable code let Lucía match one record to a photo. The other note had only a time that fit either plot. Sample: Time alone left two possible sites (A Year Above the Forest Floor, packet 4). Wrong: notes (A). Copying "Both plots had been visited around then" as the paraphrase (H). Omitting the title (I).
  
  Shape: contrast the strength of two records and leave a traceable note. Model attributing a diagram. Keep both sticky-sheet examples private.
- **46, RI.5.7 and RI.5.5.** Packet 5 opening and 03 "When visits put pressure on a patch," with the released Welcome heading as a third choice. Ask "Find the listening steps and the plan to prevent shortcut damage." "Compare how the sources organize those answers." Key: 01 orders stops, sheets and listening. 03 links trampling to a response, staying on marked routes. Wrong: both answers in Welcome (G). Both are chronology because one says "Next" (C).
  
  Shape: match two questions to sources and compare how each builds its answer. Practice checking one heading against one information need.
- **47, RI.5.3 and RI.5.5.** Packet 6 experiment, effect and caption paragraphs. Ask "How did two feeding links explain less leaf damage in plots with frogs?" "How does the caption paragraph organize events differently?" Key: frogs feed on invertebrates above ground. Fewer leaf eaters means less feeding damage. The findings explain causes. The caption paragraph follows the steps of separating claims and making a caption. Wrong: all litter invertebrates vanished (B). Both are chronology because they mention time (C).
  
  Shape: explain an indirect effect and contrast it with the order of a revision. Practice on a different web branch. Keep the experiment, caption and echoes of the finding off earlier webs.

**Day 45 word diagnostic, RF.5.3a.** Two private, previously unfamiliar words: biographer (bye-OG-ruh-fer, life writer) and reconstructing (ree-kuhn-STRUK-ting, building again). Read each alone, then in the unit's respective private sentence. Dictate each on a numbered blank before opening reading cards. Explain one letter or syllable feature and one part. "Reconstruct" for reconstructing (AD). Keep distinct entries for spelling, isolated and connected reading, and strategy. Prior encounter voids unfamiliar status. Shape: decode and spell unseen instances, then explain a strategy. Practice different words and never display the target forms or sentences.
