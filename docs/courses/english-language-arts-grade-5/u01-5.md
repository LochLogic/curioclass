English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 1, Starting over: stories of human rights (days 1 to 41), [unit plan](s01.md). **Days 23 to 28.** Days by kind: understanding 1 (24), application 4 (23, 25 to 27), check 1 (28). A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **RL.5.3, “Compare and contrast.”** Voice choices on day 23 and grouped evidence on day 26 show distinct responses to a shared event.
- **W.5.2a to e, “Introduce,” “Develop,” “Link,” “Use precise language,” “Provide a concluding statement.”** Connect the observation to a focus, sort evidence, link within and across groups, name specific actions and return to the focus. Add headings or visuals when they help the reader.
- **W.5.4, “Produce”; W.5.5, “develop and strengthen”; W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** Use a reader unfamiliar with the story to test the opening and closing. Save one-sitting and extended work, written feedback and the revised copy. Begin W.5.6 typing on day 28 for the unit 2 continuation.
- **W.5.9a and b, “Draw evidence.”** Explain how the two responses support a theme and how the article and its explainer clarify the connection. Distinguish a character's words, the writer's inference and the declaration's stated rights.
- **L.5.1e, “Use”; L.5.3a, “Expand, combine, and reduce.”** Day 25 models matched pairs and purposeful sentence changes. **L.5.2a to e, “Use” and “Spell.”** Day 23 teaches title marking by form. Day 27 revisits series, introduction, yes/no, address, tag, titles and reference spelling. **L.5.6, “Acquire and use accurately.”** Test the relationship before picking a linker.
- **RF.5.3a, “Use” combined knowledge; RF.5.4a to c, “Read” and “Use context.”** Check sounds, syllables and parts before confirming meaning. Purpose and feedback guide repeated prose and verse readings.

## 3. Checks, written first

Release each marked passage independently at the exit. The examples below are answer keys, never captions. All five exits are paper.

- **E23.** In Radishes, release the tasting from “Hot one?” through Leon's memory of the rolling radish. “Write two short voices for this memory. Give Mom and Leon one line each.” Sample: Mom recalls the heat Dad liked. Leon recalls the radish rolling under a chair and hitting Dad's shoe. Same “We both miss him” in both voices (F). “Dad grew them to sell” (B).
  **Shape:** Write a two-voice miniature with distinct details. Practice marks titles and inspects or hears released lines, without writing a scored pair of voices.
- **E24.** In Winter Squash, release the paragraph beginning “Maren asked. Bea put the tray on the table sideways”. “Which essay move does Maren's revised note show? Point to one detail that proves it.” Key: she develops her point with specific results, such as one shelf section for the city or room for the game bag. “She adds a title” (B). “She improves it,” with no link to a detail (L).
  **Shape:** Diagnose a writing move in a character's actual revision. Practice locates the labeled parts of a separate model and leaves the tray paragraph unmarked.
- **E25.** In Lettuce, release the phone offer through Mom marking the envelope. “Open a comparison for a reader new to the story. Include a broad idea and a focus.” Sample: Families may look for different signs that a move is settled. Leon wants to check the work box, while Mom waits for the offer in writing. Only Leon's response (F). “The two characters do stuff” (O).
  **Shape:** Compose a focused comparison opening from a reserved passage. Judge repairs to isolated clauses on a different event. Keep own openings in unjudged folders.
- **E26.** In Kale, release “Maren wanted to take a picture” through Mom's reply, alongside Article 1. “What idea about helping do both responses suggest? Support it with one quote from each text.” Sample: Maren wants to share the whole page. Mom covers the address and says, “First, I can ask him about mine.” Helping leaves people a choice, in keeping with “equal in dignity and rights”. “Mom shares the whole page” (B). “This shows kindness,” with an unrelated quote (L).
  **Shape:** Produce one paragraph that reasons from two sources. Practice sorts different quotations and judges whether individual links fit. Keep address scene and own draft off judged practice.
- **E27.** In Turnips, release Maren's stool decision. Give the supplied ending: “Before donating it Maren asks Leon. The stool is a thing. All homes need new chairs.” Ask “Revise this ending to show what Maren learns.” Sample: “Before deciding, Maren asks Leon. Listening helps her find a use he wants.” An unrelated statement about paint (P). “It helps with stuff” (O). Retaining the missing introductory comma (AY).
  **Shape:** Replace a supplied weak conclusion using the reserved stool decision. Practice proofreads isolated features in other sentences. Own draft and ending stay in the folder.

**Unit assessment item owned, 28A.** Use the saved day 27 essay and written note. Locate the observation, focus and logical groups. Verify relevant details and exact quotations from the novel and the article, links within and across groups, two precise terms and an earned ending. Justify formatting for this reader and show a substantive response to feedback. A grounded comparison with a meaningful edit meets the look-fors. A clean copy with mixed evidence and an unrelated ending fails those separate components. Apply the unit component scale when the revised product is scored on day 40.
**Shape:** Revise and verify one complete, independently chosen source essay with its process record. Use partial models and isolated faults on a separate case. Keep the child's chosen case off model sheets.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

Children carry Maren, Leon and the section 1.4 chapter map into a dated folder with source locations, plan and drafts. A prose folder with movable evidence slips fits the separation of quotations from the claims they support. The annotated model fades to plain margin labels by day 27. For the first typing turn, a local text editor fits preserving the paper revision while a child transfers a portion. Paper remains the complete record. Index marks replace tabs. There is no key tool or tool spec.

## 6. Days

Tools: None in the curriculum. Use the filed novel and, from day 24, the already released declaration articles and explainers. Preview a father's death, home loss, farm work, illness from heat where shown and moving for work. Keep conversation on the fictional family. Students hold the chapter notes and folder. Teacher holds the reservation list and hand-back record. In oral practice, 15 pairs take two 45-second turns concurrently. Hear a rotating six across 4.5 minutes of the named work block and collect each child's paper follow-through. Word study checks sound and stress in isolation and in sentences, then spelling on numbered blank lines only. Use -ial through day 25 and mix -ity, -ian and -ial with familiar prefixes on days 26 to 28. Reserve all later check words.

## Day 23

Application. “I can show two responses to one event in a poem.”

- **10 min. Return.** Five for independent day 22 corrections from written feedback, five for the priority repair.
- **10 min. Word study.** Map -ial, mark stress and compare base and derived spelling before rereading.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log purpose and one detail. Confer privately.
- **42 min. Two voices.** Retrieve the order of three released events and keep a sensory detail. Teach markings for short and long titles. Hear a released teaching example or contrast Leon's arranging and Bea's trail idea in the Broccoli forest. Students make their own two-voice poem in the folder as unjudged work, then read it to a partner. Hear the rotating six on title or detail choices.
- **8 min. Exit.** E23, separate paper slip.

## Day 24

Understanding. “I can explain how an essay move helps a reader.”

- **10 min. Return.** E23 voice distinction or sequence retrieval.
- **10 min. Word study.** Decode and spell a new -ial example, then check it in a released line.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Locate commas in familiar clauses.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Repeat familiar prose and filed familiar verse for purpose, meaningful phrases and expression. Decode before checking meaning.
- **42 min. Model and plan.** Inspect the observation, focus, grouped details and quotations, within-group and across-group links, precise words and closing in the supplementary components. Discuss whether a heading helps. Assign the essay on contrasting responses, a theme and one released article. Students plan privately.
- **8 min. Exit.** E24. Release the tray passage for the independent explanation.

## Day 25

Application. “I can open my comparison with a clear focus.”

- **10 min. Return.** E24 evidence or grouping.
- **10 min. Word study.** Check stress and spelling on a fresh -ial word.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log and confer. Keep required anchor rereading in class.
- **42 min. Opening.** On a released cupboard action, model both/and, either/or and neither/nor with parallel wording. Expand to add useful context, combine linked ideas and reduce clutter. Judge a repair to a supplied clause; then students draft their own observation and focus for the folder.
- **8 min. Exit.** E25, independently from the phone offer.

## Day 26

Application. “I can group evidence that explains a theme.”

- **10 min. Return.** E25 comparison and precision or the matched pair.
- **10 min. Word study.** Mix the three suffixes with familiar prefixes, and show what spelling or stress changes.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log and confer.
- **42 min. Evidence groups.** Sort released quotations about the trial form and permanent policy. Use especially to single out support and in contrast to cross groups. Introduce monologue, orientation, pacing, dialogue, sensory, sequence, audience and revise through short writing and performance examples. Children group evidence and draft their own paragraphs privately.
- **8 min. Exit.** E26. Release only the address span and the article reference.

## Day 27

Application. “I can close my essay and edit for my reader.”

- **10 min. Return.** E26 claim and evidence or link choice.
- **10 min. Word study.** Read and spell mixed-suffix examples and verify one spelling.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Retrieve the paired conjunctions and one sentence change.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Repeat released prose and filed familiar verse with feedback on one phrasing choice.
- **42 min. Conclusion and edit.** Inspect a supplied weak link or isolated comma choice. Use an editing strip for all convention parts. Students draft an ending and read their own complete draft against the sources and for an unfamiliar reader. Collect it. Write individual feedback after class.
- **8 min. Exit.** E27, on the separate stool passage.

## Day 28

Check. “I can use a reader's note to strengthen my essay.”

- **10 min. Word study.** Retrieve mixed suffixes using familiar patterns and new examples outside the check.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log and confer privately.
- **30 min. Revision.** Return day 27 notes. Students independently complete 28A and keep the original, note and before-and-after pages.
- **20 min. Typing.** One device serves ten children at two minutes each while others mark their intended first portion and prepare the paper copy. Log actual turns. Begin a local copy with the audience in mind; carry pending turns forward.
- **10 min. Response.** Return one written cue per child and assign a fresh matched component card for independent response. File the card separately from the saved essay.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use focus for the specific point and contrast for how the two responses differ. Frame: “Both ___, although ___.” Distinguish a written offer from speaking in one's own voice. Allow accessible type, read-aloud directions and a scribe who records without cues. Supply selected text leaves of roughly 70 to 250 words in the work blocks, or about one to three reading minutes before discussion. In partner rereading use the filed familiar verse already established in section 1.4, when available. Extend by asking how a different link would change what an unfamiliar reader understands.

## 10. Two rooms

A parent can circle one spot that needs background, then return the note for private revision. With one screen, project one partial component at a time and keep all evidence leaves on paper. If the essay model is unavailable, demonstrate its jobs separately by arranging headings, short details and links from released registered texts on the board. With no device, save the paper copy and log the pending typing turn.
