## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does a person need to start over, and whose job is it to protect that?"
- **Hook, day 1.** Read Article 1 and its explainer. Ask what dignity requires when helping someone start over; revisit beside the novel.
- **Inquiry.** Day 5: Which words in Article 23 illuminate the family's work-related challenge? Day 7: How do Article 17's clauses fit together? Day 11: Which earlier chapter makes the next event understandable? Day 18: How do two settings change interactions? Day 21: How do the final chapters change our reading of the opening? Day 36: Which play choices protect dignity?
- **Closing claim.** Day 27's essay explains how contrasting responses reveal a theme, connected to an article. Day 38's program note explains the monologue's setting.
- **Teaching.** Model quotation on day 3, clause rereading on day 4, comparison on day 13, revision on days 20 and 24. Partners reread; checks withdraw models.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

**Day 9, withheld explainer for Article 13, from Rights in Plain Words.** Read the official article beside this text: Article 13 describes two related freedoms. One concerns choosing where to move and live within a state. Residence means where someone lives, rather than a place someone briefly visits. The other concerns leaving countries and returning to one's own country. If movement is restricted, people cannot choose freely where to go. Leaving does not erase the stated right to return. These are claims about what everyone should be able to do; the text does not tell us that every person's choices are protected in practice.

Reference card: **residence**, pronounced RES-i-dence, noun: a place where someone lives. Distractor: duties carried out for an office. The distractor is an assessment choice, not a dictionary definition. Teach the prefix re- on a different word before day 9.

**Day 38, The Open Door, fresh whole scene, g5-u1-05.** Invented for practice.

Mira balanced a box of donated books against her knee outside the town library. Her family had arrived only a week earlier. She wanted to prove that she could finish a job without anyone taking over. When Tomas opened the door and reached for the box, she turned away.

"I can manage," she said.

The door swung shut. Mira carried the box around to the side entrance, but that door was locked. Through its window she could see Tomas stacking empty shelves. He was not watching to see whether she failed. He was making space for the books.

She returned to the front. This time Tomas held the door open, but kept his hands at his sides.

"Would you like me to lend a hand, or just keep the door unlatched?"

Mira lowered the box. Her wrist ached, and the top book had slipped sideways. "Could you take this end?"

Together they carried it inside. Tomas did not choose her shelf for her. He asked where she wanted to begin. The open door had become a bridge: she could accept help and still decide where to go.

**Day 38, withheld explainer for Article 25, from Rights in Plain Words.** Read beside the official article: Article 25 names essential needs for living, such as food, clothing, housing and medical care. It also describes security when a person loses a livelihood, including through unemployment or illness, and says mothers and children are owed special care. A livelihood is a means of supporting life. The two ideas belong together: people need support for daily life, and losing work or becoming ill does not make those needs disappear. These are the declaration's stated protections, not a report that everyone already receives them. Readers can inspect these claims in the article.
