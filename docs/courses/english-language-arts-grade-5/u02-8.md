English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 2, The rainforest (days 42 to 80), [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 73 to 76.** ELA days by kind: write 2, speak and listen 1, check 1. A plan, not a lesson. Nothing here is built or tried with students.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.5.3b to d, “Use.”** Develop responses with pacing, dialogue and description. Vary time links and select concrete details. **W.5.5, “develop and strengthen.”** Use a dated critique to decide what to change.
- **L.5.3a, “Expand, combine, and reduce.”** Preserve essential meaning and explain each change for interest or style. **L.5.2b, “Use a comma.”** On 73, set off introductory elements. **L.5.1a, “Explain the function.”** On 74 and 76, explain what conjunctions, interjections and prepositions do generally and in context.
- **W.5.6, “use technology,” “interact and collaborate,” “demonstrate.”** On 73 teach keyboard entry, editing, naming, saving and reopening. On 75 teach a managed online exchange and publication save. Independent evidence follows on 78 and 79.
- **SL.5.1 and a to d.** “Engage effectively,” “Come” prepared, “Follow” rules and roles, “Pose and respond” to linked questions, and “Review” ideas and “draw conclusions” on 75.
- **RF.5.3 and RF.5.3a, “Know and apply” and “Use.”** Combine letter-sound, syllable and word-part knowledge to read unfamiliar long words. **RF.5.4 and RF.5.4a to c, “Read” and “Use context.”** Reread for meaning, accuracy, pace and expression and check after decoding on 74. **L.5.6, “Acquire and use accurately,” and W.5.10, “Write routinely.”** Use vocabulary in talk, notes and revision for different readers.
- **Teacher explanation.** Shorter telling can compress preparation. A quick series of short sentences can also slow a reader by making them attend to individual beats. Preserve who did what and what the sources allow.

## 6. Days

## Day 73

Write (application). “I can speed up a routine part of my story.” Students hold the marked story and a separate editor practice passage. Tool: None.

- **10 min. Return.** Read the marked 72 draft. Use five minutes for a matched cue and five for an independent retry.
- **10 min. Word study.** Compare -ity, -ian and -ial in released families.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Read and log. In a one-recorder room, three pupils take their first four-minute reading from 66 and receive feedback before 74. Confer about a second need while others read.
- **20 min. Pacing.** Compare two released versions and select a useful speed. Inspect an introductory comma. Keep the pencil exchange covered.
- **20 min. Revision and saving.** Four minutes to model typing, editing, saving and reopening. With fifteen devices, two eight-minute rotations give each child a turn. Use the 28A turn log if it exists. While waiting, give five minutes of paper revision to a second priority from 72 if needed. Other writers revise their folders.
- **10 min. Exit.** E73.

## Day 74

Write (application). “I can slow a moment so the reader notices it.” Students hold a draft and sources. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Correct E73.
- **10 min. Word study.** Compare other suffix families and check a spelling with a reference.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Identify a released conjunction, preposition and interjection by function. Use up to three 45-second private corrections from 73 alongside this written work.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Partners reread different released lines for meaning, pace and phrasing, then record cues.
- **45 min. Revision.** Exchange pacing and source-limit notes. In fifteen pairs, two 45-second turns fit in 90 seconds concurrently. Visit three pairs in succession to hear six turns in 4½ minutes and return notes to all. Privately rewrite the draft as unjudged folder work. Keep the first-stop sound out of practice.
- **10 min. Exit.** E74.

## Day 75

Speak and listen (application). “I can use a partner's evidence to improve my draft.” Students hold a draft, released research leaves and a managed exchange space. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Correct E74.
- **10 min. Word study.** Five familiar minutes, then 75 seconds of shared dictation and six concurrent stations with five 45-second turns each, including handover. Collect dictation before opening word cards. File any unfinished oral component for a private turn.
- **20 min. Independent reading.** Log and confer. Hear pending private turns while classmates read. If only one recorder is available, use up to 22½ minutes across this block and the independent portion of Publication practice to finish the 30 short turns. Keep the word cards private.
- **15 min. Discussion.** Six groups of five with partners different from 54 and 61. Each child has two 45-second turns. In round one, use a note, perform the assigned job and pose a linked question to the next speaker. In round two, answer the question you received and extend an answer you heard. That uses 7½ minutes per group, leaving time to prepare and write. Record groups for later listening. Without recorders, hear five 20-second questions and five 20-second linked replies from one group in four minutes. Hear four more groups in four-minute slots during independent Publication practice after the demonstration, and the sixth in 76 Independent reading. Collect all paper conclusions. Return individual cues after listening.
- **25 min. Publication practice.** Model the online route with a released sample. Give five minutes to demonstrate opening, replying and saving. Fifteen devices then serve two ten-minute turns per pair. Partners leave their draft ready before switching. Draft the distinct source note during the remaining time and while waiting. Match each of three factual claims to its federal page and label inventions. Return written notes, discussion cues and word cues before 76.
- **15 min. Exit.** E75. Give three minutes for paper, then two six-minute keyboard rotations with fifteen devices. Everyone sends the reply from their own code and reopens the saved copy. Other children check their paper against the source while waiting. Record exactly which actions each pupil completed.
