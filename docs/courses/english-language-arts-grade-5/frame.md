# Course frame: English Language Arts, Grade 5

Course `english-language-arts-grade-5` (catalogue id `ela-k12`), grade 5, draft frame, written 2026-11-14; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 4, following the base text's grade 5 modules 1 to 3 in order, with unit 4 on water (Cory's yes,
  DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13) built on module 4's design (research, survival stories and poems, an opinion essay, a
  recorded announcement).
- **Days.** 160 course days: 153 in units and 7 at course level (4 for two course-level checks, 3 respond days).
  One day is one literacy block of about 90 minutes, five days a week: the unit lesson (60 minutes: the base
  text's module lesson) and the skills block (30 minutes). The skills block's week, 150 minutes: the long-word and
  spelling sequence 10 minutes every day (50); on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, independent reading for 20 minutes
  with the teacher's conferences inside it (60); on Tuesday and Thursday, a 5-minute grammar warm-up on a sentence
  from the anchor (10) and 15 minutes of partner rereading for fluency with the teacher's feedback (30). Grammar is
  taught in the module lessons where the base text teaches it; the warm-ups retrieve it. This split is provisional
  until a representative day is timed in the probe. The base text plans 154 module lessons in grade 5 (41, 39, 40
  and 34 by module) plus a second 60-minute block with five parts (independent reading; additional work with
  complex text; fluency with grammar, usage and mechanics; writing practice; word study and vocabulary). Curio Class keeps modules 1 to 3 lesson for lesson (41, 39 and 40 days) and gives unit 4 33 days: module 4's 34
  lessons with its two announcement-recording lessons merged into one audio day, and the community water research
  taking the module's research week, with no days added. The fall oral reading check and long-word screen run one
  to one in the skills block's independent-reading minutes of weeks 1 and 2, and the writing sample is unit 1's first
  exit ticket, so no unit day is added for screens (grade 3 adds two days as the first year of the long-word sequence
  and the 3 to 5 block, grade 4 one for the writing sample). It folds the second block's word study, fluency, grammar
  practice and independent reading into the 30 minutes and leaves out its additional complex-text work and writing
  practice, which the module lessons' close reading and daily writing carry (For the lead 1). Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and its mid-unit
  assessments: the base text's assessments for the module's first two base units are the unit's mid-unit
  assessments, and its third base unit's assessment with the performance task is the end-of-unit assessment (source
  map). A row last used in grade g gets retrieval in grade g+1 and a readiness check in grade g+2 or later. Every earlier ELA assumption here is confirmed on grade 4 days, so all retrieve. A required older-row check would precede the next unit in the previous final/response day's last ten minutes; unit 1 alone uses day 1's last ten minutes after its hook. Seasonal oral reading is diagnostic, not readiness.
- **Standard.** The Common Core State Standards for ELA, grade 5 (data/standards/ccss-ela-standards-full.txt), all
  30 progression rows of grade 5 (data/progressions/ela.csv), every lettered part taught in the unit named below.
  The bar is docs/RUBRIC-ELA.md (grades 3 to 5, all three gateways, Gateway 1 in full: Cory's yes, 2026-11-12) and
  the approach is docs/APPROACH-ELA.md. The values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) governs every unit.
- **Base text.** The 2017 K to 5 language arts curriculum recorded in docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md (CC BY 4.0; copy
  in S:\CurioClass-sources\el-education-k5-2017), grade 5, modules 1 to 4 (the 2017 curriculum is CC BY 4.0, the
  store's terms excerpt licence/eleducation-terms-of-use-excerpt-2026-11-12.txt; only the 2024 Skills Block is CC
  BY-NC). Its lesson design, sequence, protocols
  and assessments are adapted; its trade books are replaced by Curio Class texts (BASELINE rule 29: newer texts,
  mostly original Curio Class texts written to the grade 5 text spec, each read by the text agent before use,
  docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md); its own CC BY texts may be adapted; its third-party excerpts are replaced; public-domain texts may stay.
  Its name is never used on a Curio Class page; one credit line per adapted day. The 2025 edition and the 2024
  skills block are never used. The source map closes this frame.
- **Which row sits where.** A row sits in the unit that first teaches its grade 5 step (the part new this year),
  which is where the kept base lessons first teach and assess it. Where a row's lettered parts come in later units,
  the owning unit says which part waits and the later unit names it as continued. Before a row's unit, a unit uses
  the grade 4 form of the skill, and the first unit that leans on a grade 4 skill retrieves it inside
  its lesson. Later units practice every placed row again on new texts; the table names those returns as review.
- **Reading level.** Anchors climb through the grades 4 to 5 band (a quantitative reading measure of about 740 to
  1010) to its high end read alone by year end (e5-reading-range); poems and the play scripts are placed by what
  makes them hard and by reader and task, not by the number. Informational anchors make up 45 to 65 percent by the shared anchor-only rule. The two unit 4 survival stories count as one paired anchor; the unit 1 declaration set is also an informational anchor. The resulting five anchors are three informational and two literary (60 percent informational). The measures named in the units are targets the commissioned texts must meet; each anchor gets a
  stable id and a text record (measure, what makes it hard under the four headings, the reader and task, why here,
  rights, whole or excerpt) in the scope and sequence's Course text record,
  before its unit is planned, and the balance is counted from those records. Catalogue anchor-label updates are reserved for the structure pass.
- **Word lists.** About 8 to 10 words a week from the unit's texts, met before, during and after reading and met again in later units; the scope and sequence sets each list.
- **Long texts.** Two read whole: an original novel (unit 1) and an original biography (unit 3).
- **Words, read and never guessed.** The long-word sequence (e5-long-words) runs every day from day 1 with a check
  every few weeks; spelling follows it.
- **Writing.** Every reading day ends in a written exit ticket quoting the day's text (W.5.9, W.5.10). Each unit
  takes at least one piece through the whole process. The desired process-writing shares are about 30 percent
  opinion, 35 informative, 35 narrative. The dated ledger distinguishes process pieces from short responses and on-demand assessments; its nine process pieces are two opinion, five informative and two narrative. Actual writing-time shares remain a unit-planning check, not a demonstrated percentage. Research is previewed in unit 1 (the monologue), taught in unit 2 and
  used again in unit 4.
- **Numbers.** No math ahead of math: Earth's water is shown as hundredths (97 of 100 parts salt water, on a
  hundredths grid, taught in grade 4 math), never as a percent, which is grade 6.
- **Talk.** Partner talk daily; a formal text-based discussion about every two weeks.
- **Paper path and devices.** Every anchor has a print copy and every task a paper route. Keyboarding (two pages in
  a sitting), publishing, the shared-document exchange of drafts, the monologue recording, the presentation and the recorded announcement happen in the school day on a shared
  classroom device rotation or a lab session, with no student account; open searching is supervised; home practice
  is optional. If no device route exists by year end, W.5.6 is recorded unfinished for that class
  (e5-writing-process).
- **Record sheet, family note and sub-ready day.** One per base unit (three per Curio Class unit, 12 a year), since a unit here is a whole base module of seven to eight weeks; the unit stays the base module, which carries the compelling question and the performance task.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). What it studies first, by unit: unit 1 (a father's death, a
  family's loss of its home, farm work and forced moves) farmworker families' own organisations' published accounts,
  federal pages on migrant and seasonal farmworkers, and a children's grief organisation's guidance on loss in
  stories; unit 2 (Puerto Rico, Hurricane Maria) Puerto Rican sources first (the island's own government and
  university pages, Puerto Rican writers and historians) and the federal forest pages; unit 3 (racism and
  segregation in sport, disability) the athletes' own words, a Negro Leagues history museum's pages, scholarship by
  Black historians of sport and integration, Puerto Rican sources on Clemente and a disability language guide;
  unit 4 (drought; the river's tribal nations) the Colorado River basin tribes' own published water pages and
  statements, scholarship on tribal water rights by Native scholars, and a Native journalists' association guide.
