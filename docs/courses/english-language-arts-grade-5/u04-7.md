## 6. Days

## Day 154

Application. “I can plan an explanation that helps a new listener understand our sources.” Students hold the leaflet, announcement and dated source notes. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Act privately on 153's cue. Gather the leaflet and announcement and locate the actual unit 3 presentation records.
- **10 min. Words.** Decode unfamiliar forms alone and in released print, then check context and spell on numbered blank lines.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Retrieve a reason clause from released water prose.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Read released 06 twice with a purpose, then explain one phrasing change. Give both readers a cue.
- **40 min. Planning.** Reread up to 250 released words. Make a blank plan marking the main idea, sources, visual and changes for familiar and unfamiliar audiences. Partners test how a feature already in the leaflet answers a question. Prepare for student panels with the teacher as lead judge and for staff or another class who know less about the system. Use staff listeners or peers from another class if the first guest audience cannot come. If only the class is available, name contrasting listener roles for practice and keep outside-audience evidence pending.
- **15 min. Exit.** Release only the new 07 sentence and companion diagram, then collect X154.

## Day 155

Application. “I can rehearse and improve my explanation using what others heard in the sources.” Students hold annotated sources, their visuals and dated folders. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Act on the X154 cue. Check the actual day 120 first and corrected recordings and day 121 or 123 evidence as available. Carry forward the earlier scores unchanged.
- **10 min. Words.** Apply taught parts to released presentation words. Keep unaided readings private before dictation.
- **20 min. Choice reading and conferences.** Students read and log. Teacher brings preread folders and gives 30 individual 30-second written hand-backs in 15 minutes, then five minutes for correction and filing. Provide a written cue about purpose, audience or range and retain the correction.
- **25 min. Discussion and first recordings.** Use six groups of five. The teacher hears one group per 4 minutes 10 seconds, five 45-second turns plus 25 seconds to switch. Each pupil gives both brief registers and indicates a source and visual. Record the first versions and write one cue as each child speaks. During other turns, groups prepare an actual source detail, carry facilitator, evidence finder and connector roles with two members doubling as listeners, pose and answer specific questions and extend a peer's reason. Allow two 45-second rounds per child in parallel groups and file individual conclusions.
- **15 min. Revision and recording.** Six recorders each run five two-minute turns including saving. Collect corrected peer and formal versions, an identified visual and its rationale, and a brief explanation linking three sources. Use the remaining five minutes for private turns. Save identifiable first take, written cue and correction. With one recorder, keep up to five two-minute private corrections in this block and book remaining pupils by date. Record unavailable speech as pending.
- **15 min. Exit.** First hear the reserved paragraph (about two minutes). Use two 45-second turns in each of fifteen pairs for the oral refinement and listeners' response. Hear six rotating pupils in 4½ minutes during the response period while others write their changed line. Collect all 30 written slips. Log a private follow-up for pupils whose oral trace is still needed.

- **35 min. Presentations.** Run six parallel panels of five for 30 minutes. Teacher acts as lead judge using saved evidence and makes live observations in one panel. In those final five, a child retries a targeted phrase for a partner during two 45-second turns, or privately with the teacher. Collect a dated response or pending flag from each child.
