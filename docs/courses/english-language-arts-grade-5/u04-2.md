Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/4/s/4.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u04-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 4, Water we share, [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 130 to 135.** Days by kind: understanding 1, application 4, check 1. Main work: speak and listen 1, write 4, check 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- SL.5.1/a to d “Engage” in discussion, “Come” prepared and draw on reading, “Follow” rules and roles, “Pose and respond” with elaboration, and “Review” key ideas to draw a conclusion (130). RI.5.9 “Integrate” 06 and 07 and several accounts in a joint explanation.
- W.5.2/a to e “Write” an informative script. “Introduce” a focused topic, “provide” a general observation and “group” related ideas with headings or media where helpful. “Develop” with accurate facts, definitions and details, “Link” within and across groups with words, phrases and clauses, “Use” precise terms and “Provide” a related close (131 to 133). W.5.4 “Produce” coherent writing for the task, purpose and new listener. W.5.5 “develop and strengthen” a script through planning, feedback, revision and editing (132, 134). W.5.10 “Write routinely” in brief responses and across the script's dated stages.
- SL.5.4 “Report” on the topic with logical sequencing and relevant facts and details, and “speak clearly” at an understandable pace. SL.5.6 “Adapt speech” for familiar and unfamiliar listeners, including formal English as the task requires (135).
- L.5.1 “Demonstrate command” of grammar. On 134, “Explain” conjunction, preposition and interjection functions, “Form and use” past, present and future perfect tenses, “Use” tenses to convey time, sequence, state and conditions, “Recognize and correct” shifts, and “Use” correlative conjunctions. L.5.2 “Demonstrate command” of capitalization, punctuation and spelling. On 133 to 135 “Use” series and introductory commas, commas with yes/no, direct address and tag questions, and title marks. “Spell” correctly using a reference. L.5.3a “Expand, combine, and reduce” sentences for meaning, interest and style (134).
- RF.5.3 “Know and apply” phonics and word analysis. RF.5.3a “Use combined knowledge” of sounds, syllables and morphology in isolation and context. RF.5.4/a to c “Read” for understanding, with successive accurate, expressive readings at a suitable pace, and “Use context” to confirm or correct after decoding (132, 134). Save prose for the two-form record on 141. L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately” domain terms and relationship words.
- Explain how an announcement can describe or propose a water action for a specific audience without misstating a fact or running a campaign. Preview drought, uncertainty for farms and towns, and distinct tribal water rights. Offer released 06 science prose as the alternate. Ask about sources and audience choices and keep family access and costs private.

## 3. Checks, written first

This section owns 135a to d. Preserve the corrected 133/134 script for 152a to e and retrieve reference checking ahead of 151g. Teaching exits are paper and use shown, shown with help, or not yet seen. Use unit 0–2 only for later corrected check components. Keep first collection unscored on 135.

**s04-X130.** Release 06's basin-user paragraph and Stern's sentence in 07 about governments leading responses and seeking federal help. Ask: “A partner says only states can help. Answer with a conclusion that uses both sources.”
Key: the river serves seven US states, users in Mexico and tribal nations. Stern names state, local and tribal governments as leaders who can seek federal help. A conclusion recognizes participation beyond state governments. “Only federal staff lead” (A); “06 and 07 discuss water” (V).
Shape: write one supported reply using both sources. Discuss different released claims and use oral roles; keep this exact exchange, both passages and revealing search results off practice copies.

**s04-X131.** Read 06's four-sentence watershed paragraph as an announcement, then supply it in print. Ask: “How does this announcement's order help a new listener understand the last sentence?”
Key: it defines the watershed, explains how moving water carries material, gives a school-drive example and connects the route to downstream protection. The example makes the closing reason intelligible to a newcomer. “It lists supplies the school should buy” (AI); “Everyone already knows where a drain goes” (AE).
Shape: explain the order of the new announcement. Practice marks single functions in a reservoir script, hears reordered lines and identifies a weak placement. Withhold the watershed paragraph and its route diagram.

**s04-X132.** First release Entsminger's outdoor-use sentence about replacement planting in 07. Ask: “Write two opening sentences for school grounds staff. Separate the city's practice from your proposal.”
Key, open: “Southern Nevada replaced some grass with plants watered by drip irrigation. Our school could explore that approach.” Match the fact to the text and state the action as a proposal. “Our school already replaced its grass” (A); “Water matters everywhere” (AI).
Shape: compose a two-sentence opener across fact and proposal. Sort labels on other facts and proposals. Keep the actual outdoor-use sentence sealed and each child's own introduction as unjudged folder work.

**s04-X133.** First release the nation's 07 sentence beginning “It also sought enough flow.” Ask: “Explain the nation's flow request in a sentence with a punctuated list. Add a related ending.”
Key, open: “The Colorado River Indian Tribes sought enough flow for people, plants and animals. That flow would help sustain life along the river.” Accept a serial comma and accurate equivalent wording. “People plants and animals” (E); “Visit our school fair” (AM).
Shape: compose source-based support with a series and a related close. Practice inserts a comma in one unrelated series or selects a better single ending elsewhere. Hide the sentence, its list and 135's series; the child's own support and close are unjudged folder work.

**s04-X134.** First release 06's freshwater treatment sentence. Supply this deliberately faulty visitor message: “Yes Lee freshwatter is always ready to drink isnt it?” Ask: “Revise the message using the source. Explain how your change helps the visitor.”
Key, open: “No, Lee, freshwater may need treatment. Low salt alone cannot show that water is safe, can it?” Corrects the guarantee, spelling, apostrophe, opening comma, direct address and tag comma, and explains that the visitor needs reliable drinking-water information. “Freshwater is always safe” (AF); “No Lee, freshwater may need treatment” (F); “freshwatter” left after guessing (AH).
Shape: revise meaning and edit a fresh message, then explain one meaningful change. Practice chooses a better detail or fixes one convention on separate date or delivery sentences. Withhold the exact treatment sentence and faulty copy. Keep the child's own revision unjudged until its named verification.

## 6. Days

## Day 130

Application. “I can use two sources to answer a partner's idea.” Students hold released notes and a source chart. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Correct 129a/b from the written checklist and teacher cue. Save both versions.
- **10 min. Words.** Read aqua/hydro words alone, then in context. Spell after reading.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log title and meaning, and confer.
- **45 min. Discussion.** Read up to 250 released words. Model one reply to a peer with a released detail. Ten triads rotate facilitator, evidence finder and connector. Every pupil prepares a quote, asks, elaborates and writes a conclusion with any change in thinking. Allow a 45-second turn per action in each concurrent triad. The teacher hears two whole triads, with cue and follow-up for all.
- **10 min. Exit.** Release both passages and collect X130.

## Day 131

Understanding. “I can explain how an announcement's order helps listeners.” The teacher holds the model, students hold annotation slips. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Act on the X130 cue.
- **10 min. Words.** Retrieve -tion, -sion, -ity and -ial and preview week 27 additions.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer.
- **45 min. Model.** Hear a short reservoir storage model drawn from released Trujillo material. Trace one source detail, identify focus and logical groups, and judge where a heading or visual helps listening. Explore how words, phrases and clauses link ideas, and test an isolated placement choice with peers. The source chart can stand in if the model is unavailable.

## Day 132

## Day 134

Application. “I can revise what my script says and edit how it reads.” Students keep marked before-and-after drafts. Tool: None.

## Day 135

Check. “I can report clearly, adapt for listeners and edit punctuation.” Students hold their own corrected script and later the sealed punctuation card. Tool: None.

With six private stations, five half-minute turns per station collect unaided unfamiliar isolation and short-context reading first, then dictation on numbered blank lines.
