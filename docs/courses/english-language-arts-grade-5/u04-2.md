Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/4/s/4.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u04-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 4, Water we share, [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 130 to 135.** Days by kind: understanding 1, application 4, check 1. Main work: speak and listen 1, read information 1, write 3, check 1. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- SL.5.1/a to d “Engage” in discussion, “Come” prepared and draw on reading, “Follow” rules and roles, “Pose and respond” with elaboration, and “Review” key ideas to draw a conclusion (130). RI.5.9 “Integrate” 06 and 07 and several accounts in a joint explanation.
- W.5.2/a to e “Write” an informative script. “Introduce” a focused topic, “provide” a general observation and “group” related ideas with headings or media where helpful. “Develop” with accurate facts, definitions and details, “Link” within and across groups with words, phrases and clauses, “Use” precise terms and “Provide” a related close (131 to 133). W.5.4 “Produce” coherent writing for the task, purpose and new listener. W.5.5 “develop and strengthen” a script through planning, feedback, revision and editing (132, 134). W.5.10 “Write routinely” in brief responses and across the script's dated stages.
- SL.5.4 “Report” on the topic with logical sequencing and relevant facts and details, and “speak clearly” at an understandable pace. SL.5.6 “Adapt speech” for familiar and unfamiliar listeners, including formal English as the task requires (135).
- L.5.1 “Demonstrate command” of grammar. On 134, “Explain” conjunction, preposition and interjection functions, “Form and use” past, present and future perfect tenses, “Use” tenses to convey time, sequence, state and conditions, “Recognize and correct” shifts, and “Use” correlative conjunctions. L.5.2 “Demonstrate command” of capitalization, punctuation and spelling. On 133 to 135 “Use” series and introductory commas, commas with yes/no, direct address and tag questions, and title marks. “Spell” correctly using a reference. L.5.3a “Expand, combine, and reduce” sentences for meaning, interest and style (134).
- RF.5.3 “Know and apply” phonics and word analysis. RF.5.3a “Use combined knowledge” of sounds, syllables and morphology in isolation and context. RF.5.4/a to c “Read” for understanding, with successive accurate, expressive readings at a suitable pace, and “Use context” to confirm or correct after decoding (132, 134). Save prose for the two-form record on 141. L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately” domain terms and relationship words.
- Explain how an announcement can describe or propose a water action for a specific audience without misstating a fact or running a campaign. Preview drought, uncertainty for farms and towns, and distinct tribal water rights. Offer released 06 science prose as the alternate. Ask about sources and audience choices and keep family access and costs private.

## 6. Days

## Day 130

Application, speak and listen. “I can use two sources to answer a partner's idea.” Students hold released notes and a source chart. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Correct 129a/b from the written checklist and teacher cue. Save both versions.
- **10 min. Words.** Read aqua/hydro words alone, then in context. Spell after reading.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log title and meaning, and confer.
- **45 min. Discussion.** Read up to 250 released words. Model one reply to a peer with a released detail. Ten triads rotate facilitator, evidence finder and connector. Every pupil prepares a quote, asks, elaborates and writes a conclusion with any change in thinking. Allow a 45-second turn per action in each concurrent triad. Stagger two observed triads. The teacher hears six pupils, gives all a written cue and books missed turns in 131 Choice reading.
- **10 min. Exit.** Release both passages and collect X130.

## Day 131

Understanding, read information. “I can explain how an announcement's order helps listeners.” The teacher holds the model, students hold annotation slips. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Act on the X130 cue.
- **10 min. Words.** Retrieve -tion, -sion, -ity and -ial and preview week 27 additions.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer.
- **45 min. Model.** Read this model aloud: “This report concerns water storage. In 2021, Trujillo reported low levels at two reservoirs. Stored water behind the dams had fallen during the drought. The date matters when we describe those levels.” Name the focus, trace the reported fact and explain the close. Partners try a different order and discuss its effect. Keep the upcoming watershed and treatment passages out of the model.
- **10 min. Exit.** First hear and read the released watershed paragraph and collect X131.

## Day 132

Application, write. “I can write an opening that separates facts from proposals.” Students hold a source chart and their own unjudged script. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Repair X131 from the cue.
- **10 min. Words.** Decode and spell protection and a related form.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Identify fact and proposal verbs in separate released examples.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Six assigned readers use six recorders. Each privately reads a short prose passage, receives a written partner cue and rereads. Save both takes and a meaning response. The teacher checks the six records after class. Other pairs reread released prose.
- **45 min. Script planning.** Sort released fact and proposal labels, then let students choose a supported topic and write the unjudged opener to a 45–60 second script. Leave five minutes for private follow-ups.
- **10 min. Exit.** Release only the grass-replacement clause and collect X132.

## Day 133

- **5 min. Return.** Correct X132 from a cue.

- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer.
- **45 min. Draft.** Reread released 06 and 07 selections (up to 250 words). Group supporting material and choose fitting links and precise vocabulary. Inspect one series fault and one separate ending choice. Save the child's unjudged whole script.

## Day 134

Application, write. “I can revise what my script says and edit how it reads.” Students keep marked before-and-after drafts. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Act on the X133 cue.
- **10 min. Words.** Retrieve suffixes and verify uncertain spellings in a reference.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Start with a released 07 sentence. Recast its idea as “Oh, people in the valley plan ahead because water is scarce.” Oh expresses feeling, in relates people to a place, and because connects the reason to the action. Children explain one function to a partner.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** The next six privately record two readings of released prose with one written partner cue between them. Save each meaning response. The teacher checks these records after class.
- **45 min. Revision.** Use ten minutes to exchange written peer and teacher cues, ten to revise and save, 20 for four five-minute edit rounds and five to set up recorders. On separate released examples, (1) match had stored, has stored and will have stored to time lines, (2) correct a tense shift and use either/or, (3) edit series, openers, direct address, tags and titles, and (4) expand, combine and shorten a draft sentence, checking doubtful spelling in a reference. Keep both scripts. Test devices with unrelated words.
- **10 min. Exit.** Issue the treatment sentence and message, and collect X134.

## Day 135

Check. “I can report clearly, adapt for listeners and edit punctuation.” Students hold their own corrected script and later the sealed punctuation card. Tool: None.

- **5 min. Return.** Give the X134 meaning or editing cue.
- **10 min. Words.** Use five minutes for the cumulative check. At six stations five pupils each read for 30 seconds with up to ten seconds to change places. Each reads an unfamiliar word alone and in a sentence. Waiting pupils cannot hear. Cover the print before a short dictation. Use the other five minutes for regrouping and a fresh retry. Save first response, cue and correction. Give absentees a different form before they hear its dictation.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer. Book any missing word turns by pupil and date. Use protected parallel stations or actual private teacher turns.
- **10 min. Punctuation.** Collect 135c and d in four and six minutes.
- **30 min. Recording.** Six stations each serve five pupils in four-minute slots. Record the conversational version before the formal 45 to 60 second announcement. Save each by pupil and date. Use the remaining ten minutes for setup and file checks. Hear all 30 clips after class and give each speaker a written cue. If a recorder fails, hear private live turns and log recordings still due on 136.
- **15 min. Response.** Use five minutes for the checklist and ten for separate letter retries or filing. Give written teacher cues after class and route independent revision and full retakes to 136. Verify the corrected 129 records once after class.
