Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/2/s/2.5), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py english-language-arts-grade-5 u02-5.md`. <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 2, The rainforest (days 42 to 80). See [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 59 to 64.** ELA days by kind: read literature 2, speak and listen 1, write 3. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

## 1. Standards and practices

- RL.5.1 “Quote accurately” and RL.5.4 “Determine” words in context. L.5.5a and b “Interpret” figurative language and “Recognize and explain” customary sayings. W.5.9a “Apply” literary reading to an explanation. RL.5.10 “Read and comprehend” a complete new story independently when available.
- W.5.2 “Write,” “Introduce,” “Develop,” “Link,” “Use” and “Provide” a focus, groups of explained quotations, links, precise language and a following close. The close is day 65’s work. W.5.4 “Produce” writing for an unfamiliar reader. L.5.3a “Expand, combine, and reduce” for meaning and style. Review expansion, combination and reduction on released lines before the transfer.
- SL.5.1 “Engage effectively,” “Come” prepared, “Follow” agreements, “Pose and respond” and “Review” contributions. RF.5.3 “Know and apply” and “Use” letters, syllables and parts. RF.5.4 “Read” purposefully, accurately and expressively twice and “Use context” after decoding. L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately.” W.5.10 “Write routinely.”
- Explain how a quotation supports a category and affects what a reader imagines. A source line and a writer’s explanation serve different jobs. Keep own-essay planning and drafts unjudged while independent exits use other passages.

- **59, RL.5.1 and RL.5.4.** Use 06’s held first-bench paragraph beginning “The path was still damp.” Mask the adjacent cold-stripe sentence for 61. Wrong: “like wood in rain” (Y). A lumberyard stood there (Z).

- **60, RL.5.4 and L.5.5a and b.** Open three short passages reserved from 05 and 06. In 05, read from “I took out the whistle and blew” through “tried again.” Ask “Which words describe the first whistle note?” “What do they help you hear at the bench?” At the damp map, ask “What does the gray island show about the map?” In 06 read Amina’s two sentences beginning “No need to fill the silence.” Ask “What does Amina mean by ‘fill the silence’?” Sample: “The first note was thin.” The small sound stands out as Isa pauses to listen before trying again. - **61, W.5.2b and W.5.9a.** Use 06’s withheld “The wet edge of the seat made a cold stripe through his pants.” Model category: touch reveals a wet setting. Weak explanation: “This shows why Gabriel was late.” Ask “Why does the quotation fit the category?” “Replace the explanation with one that uses the quotation.” Key: the wet bench dampens clothing. The cold felt through Gabriel’s pants lets a reader feel how wet the seat is. Wrong: “A cold stripe” with no explanation (M). “A thing was there” (O).
  
  Shape: diagnose and replace one off-category explanation. Practice sorting or picking a better explanation on separate model material. Do not rehearse correcting this quotation.
- **62, W.5.2a and W.5.4.** Offer 05’s sound-map project and 06’s opening survey purpose, held unannotated for this task. Give the weak introduction “These stories have several events. I will list the events in order.” Ask “Rewrite the opening for a new reader.” “Name the groups your opening prepares the reader for.” “Why would those groups help a new reader?” Sample: Isa records sounds and Gabriel records a bird survey. Both learn what their senses can establish. Group clues that hide information and clues that reveal it so readers can compare what each observer knows. Accept another coherent, evidence-based grouping. Wrong: “This is my essay” (L). “Stuff in places” (O).
  
  Shape: revise an audience-specific focus and groups and defend the organization. Judge a heading from the old essay. Keep the model reorganization and the paired purposes off practice pages. Own introduction goes to the unjudged folder.
- **63, RL.5.1, W.5.2b and W.5.9a.** Model category: sounds shape what the tellers can tell. Use the held passages containing 05’s “my voice disappear into the steady water sound” and 06’s “The dry scrape of a pencil.” Give one response for each story. Ask “Use an exact sound quote to explain what Isa can hear.” “Use an exact sound quote to explain what Gabriel wonders.” Key: water masks Isa’s call. A pencil sound from the lower bench makes Gabriel wonder whether Amina marked a sighting. Accept exact shorter quotes with the same contribution. Wrong: “my voice disappeared” as a quote (Y). Bare quotes with no explained effects (M).
  
  Shape: develop one model category with two distinct exact quotes and effects. Practice choosing between explanations for one released detail. Own first category goes to the unjudged folder.
- **64, L.5.3a and W.5.2c.** Use 06’s held sightlines at the lower and higher benches. Print “Vines and leaves hid one part of the canopy from the lower bench” and “The higher bench gave a clear view of a pale opening between crowns.” Supply the released fact that Gabriel heard a call without seeing its source. Ask “Combine the two sight sentences without changing either meaning.” “Write a sentence that links sight and sound by showing a contrast.” Key: Vines and leaves hide part of the canopy from the lower bench, while the higher bench faces an opening. In contrast, the call can be heard even when Gabriel cannot see what made it. Accept another accurate contrast. Wrong: the lower bench faces open sky (V). “Moreover” used to signal contrast (AH).
  
  Shape: combine two sightlines and bridge to a contrasting sense with that relationship evident. Model reduction on an unrelated line. Keep the two bench sentences and the category link off practice cards. Own second category goes to the unjudged folder.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

The released passages and dated folder carry theme, exact quotation, category, link and draft. An evidence strip pairs each quote with a reason for keeping it. Fold paper for a category chart and fade the frame after day 61. Use an editable or paper model that can reveal one layer at a time. Keep all later exit lines out of it and off student maps. No key tool.

## 6. Days

Tool: None. Students hold released texts and dated drafts. Teacher holds model keys and sealed texts. Use fifteen concurrent pairs. Two 45-second turns take 1:30 per pair. Stagger three pair exchanges to hear six in 4½ minutes. Return a written cue on every source-linked contribution.

- **5 min. Return.** Correct 59.

## 9. Supports and extension

Use “___ makes the place seem ___” through 61, then remove the frame. Allow about three minutes to read up to 350 words of story excerpts independently. Read directions aloud. Provide accessible print, spoken directions or neutral transcription. Record which reading received access support. Extend by testing whether moving one released quotation to another group changes its purpose.

## 10. Two rooms

Parent and child match one released detail to its effect and take turns explaining. One screen shows a released passage at a time. Folded paper works for the categories. If the explanation model is missing, inspect a held 40A example beside released 05 and 06 details. If neither is available, arrange headings over existing notes. If the assigned reading is missing, keep its independent comprehension evidence pending.
