## 1. Standards and practices

- RL.5.1 “Quote accurately” and RL.5.4 “Determine” words in context. L.5.5a and b “Interpret” figurative language and “Recognize and explain” customary sayings. W.5.9a “Apply” literary reading to an explanation. RL.5.10 “Read and comprehend” a complete new story independently when available.
- W.5.2 “Write,” “Introduce,” “Develop,” “Link,” “Use” and “Provide” a focus, groups of explained quotations, links, precise language and a following close. The close is day 65’s work. W.5.4 “Produce” writing for an unfamiliar reader. L.5.3a “Expand, combine, and reduce” for meaning and style. Retrieve expansion and practice reduction before a combination transfer.
- SL.5.1 “Engage effectively,” “Come” prepared, “Follow” agreements, “Pose and respond” and “Review” contributions. RF.5.3 “Know and apply” and “Use” letters, syllables and parts. RF.5.4 “Read” purposefully, accurately and expressively twice and “Use context” after decoding. L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately.” W.5.10 “Write routinely.”
- Explain how a quotation supports a category and affects what a reader imagines. A source line and a writer’s explanation serve different jobs. Keep own-essay planning and drafts unjudged while independent exits use other passages.

- **59, RL.5.1 and RL.5.4.** Use 06’s held first-bench paragraph beginning “The path was still damp.” Mask the adjacent cold-stripe sentence for 61. Ask “Copy the comparison about how the air smelled.” “What does it let you sense about this place?” Key: “like wood left out in the rain.” The place still smells of damp wood after the shower. Wrong: “like wood in rain” (Y). A lumberyard stood there (Z).
  
  Shape: copy an exact image and explain its setting effect. Practice judging which interpretation fits a different paragraph. Keep the smells and answers off models.
- **60, RL.5.4 and L.5.5a and b.** Use the fixed unit card 60. Print “The calls stitched the silence together. I listened twice before labeling my sketch.” Release the given partner’s saying in the second part. Ask “How do the calls change what you imagine hearing?” “What is the partner asking the listener to check?” Key: scattered calls connect spaces of quiet. The partner asks the listener to check the possible bird identification before labeling the sketch. Wrong: actual sewing (Z). The listener should check the path for a jump (Z).
  
  Shape: interpret a metaphor and contextualize advice, using two responses. Sort released images alone earlier and keep the unit card, its answers and the saying off practice pages. Collect the separate language record before the card opens.
- **61, W.5.2b and W.5.9a.** Use 06’s withheld “The wet edge of the seat made a cold stripe through his pants.” Model category: touch reveals a wet setting. Weak explanation: “This shows why Gabriel was late.” Ask “Why does the quotation fit the category?” “Replace the explanation with one that uses the quotation.” Key: the wet bench dampens clothing. The cold felt through Gabriel’s pants lets a reader feel how wet the seat is. Wrong: “A cold stripe” with no explanation (M). “A thing was there” (O).
  
  Shape: diagnose and replace one off-category explanation. Practice sorting or picking a better explanation on separate model material. Do not rehearse correcting this quotation.
- **62, W.5.2a and W.5.4.** Offer 05’s sound-map project and 06’s opening survey purpose, held unannotated for this task. Give the weak introduction “These stories have several events. I will list the events in order.” Ask “Rewrite the opening and name useful groups of details.” “Why would those groups help a new reader?” Sample: Isa records sounds and Gabriel records a bird survey. Both learn what their senses can establish. Group clues that hide information and clues that reveal it so readers can compare what each observer knows. Accept another coherent, evidence-based grouping. Wrong: “This is my essay” (L). “Stuff in places” (O).
  
  Shape: revise an audience-specific focus and groups and defend the organization. Judge a heading from the old essay. Keep the model reorganization and the paired purposes off practice pages. Own introduction goes to the unjudged folder.
- **63, RL.5.1, W.5.2b and W.5.9a.** Model category: sounds shape what the tellers can tell. Use the held passages containing 05’s “my voice disappear into the steady water sound” and 06’s “The dry scrape of a pencil.” Give one response for each story. Ask “Use an exact sound quote to explain what Isa can hear.” “Use an exact sound quote to explain what Gabriel wonders.” Key: water masks Isa’s call. A pencil sound from the lower bench makes Gabriel wonder whether Amina marked a sighting. Accept exact shorter quotes with the same contribution. Wrong: “my voice disappeared” as a quote (Y). Bare quotes with no explained effects (M).
  
  Shape: develop one model category with two distinct exact quotes and effects. Practice choosing between explanations for one released detail. Own first category goes to the unjudged folder.
- **64, L.5.3a and W.5.2c.** Use 06’s held sightlines at the lower and higher benches. Print those two sight sentences separately in plain language. Supply the released fact that Gabriel heard a call without seeing its source. Ask “Combine the two sight sentences without changing either meaning.” “Link the sight and sound groups. Show their contrast.” Key: Vines and leaves hide part of the canopy from the lower bench, while the higher bench faces an opening. In contrast, the call can be heard even when Gabriel cannot see what made it. Accept another accurate contrast. Wrong: the lower bench faces open sky (V). “Moreover” used to signal contrast (AH).
  
  Shape: combine two sightlines and bridge to a contrasting sense with that relationship evident. Model reduction on an unrelated line. Keep the two bench sentences and the category link off practice cards. Own second category goes to the unjudged folder.
