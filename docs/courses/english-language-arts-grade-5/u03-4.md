# Section 3.4: From sources to an opinion

Read with the [Front Office facts](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#ela/english-language-arts-grade-5/3/s/3.4). <!-- facts link -->

English Language Arts, Grade 5. Unit 3, Athletes who led change, [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 102 to 106.** Days by kind: understanding 2, application 3. A plan, not a lesson.

## 1. Standards and practices

- **W.5.1 “Write opinion pieces.”** **a “Introduce,” “state,” “create”** groups for a purpose. **b “Provide”** logically ordered reasons with facts and details. On 104, **c “Link”** with words, phrases and clauses, and **d “Provide”** a conclusion tied to the opinion. **RI.5.9 “Integrate”** several sources around shared ideas. **W.5.9b “Apply”** informational reading while checking support on 105. **W.5.4 “Produce”** a coherent introduction for a reader unfamiliar with baseball on 106. **W.5.5 “develop and strengthen”** by using written advice and editing separately. **W.5.10 “Write routinely”** through short notes and the extended opinion.
- **SL.5.1/a to d “Engage,” “Come,” “Follow,” “Pose and respond,” “Review”** on 103 through preparation, role, elaboration and conclusion. Keep each pupil's observed contribution, with partner and setting.
- **RF.5.3/3a “Know and apply,” “Use combined knowledge”** of sounds, syllables and word parts out of context and in text. **RF.5.4/a/b “Read”** for meaning, accuracy, pace and expression on successive prose readings. **RF.5.4c “Use context”** to confirm or self-correct after decoding. **L.5.6 “Acquire and use accurately”** relationship words, including consequently for a result and moreover for a further supporting idea.
- Explain how a fact can support a reason and how different authors can contribute to one reason. The modern explanation and accounts contain racial exclusion and discrimination in sport, work, service and transport. Preview sensitive lines, leave out slurs, and offer a private pass. Compare actions and qualities without ranking identities.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Unit assessment items owned:** none administered here. Prepare the folio for 108A to G and 110A to E, with written feedback and independent correction at their scheduled times. Keep the unit's sample answers private. Make one dated first attempt and a separate correction on each paper exit. Use met, partly met or not yet by criterion. In references, numbers before the colon are text ID suffixes and numbers after it are manuscript lines. Exits use a new source pairing or writing commission with the registered texts. Hold each decision and pairing back from worked examples.

- **Exit 102, 01:44, 02:32 and 04:32.** “What quality matters when leaders speak about athletes' opportunities?” “Give two reasons using all three passages.” Give a position space and a two-line reason space. Sample: fairness matters because scores miss who can get work, as in Mack's medal and Doby's turn at bat. Also, one person's chance can hide broad exclusion: the hearing count showed only seven Black players among about four hundred. “There were athletes” (K). Three titles without a connection (O).
  Shape: choose a position and group three sources across reasons. During the model check one isolated fact under one heading, using a different source pair and quality.
- **Exit 103, use a partner's actual source-based claim.** “What question would extend your partner's claim?” “Revise your opinion using that exchange and three sources.” Sample, privately: a partner cites staying in the game from 01:198. Ask where Robinson chose to speak openly. Revised opinion credits judgment about when to stay focused and when to speak, supported by 01:198, the hearing context in 02:24 and the opening out in 03:24. Accept a different warranted revision grounded in the child's real exchange. “Me too” (AC). Repeats the first opinion while ignoring the new evidence (AD).
  Shape: pose a new written follow-up question and qualify an opinion from actual evidence heard. Practice locating and summarizing a peer's citation. Keep this sample and any completed revised opinion off the discussion sheet.
- **Exit 104, 01:76 and 80.** Shared line: “The jump record tells us everything about Jackie.” “Replace this opinion with one supported by both passages.” “What change helps your reader most?” Measured skill mattered and guidance mattered. The record gives a measurable achievement. Downs encouraged work with young people and stayed involved. The change separates what the jump measures from what later choices show. “The record is best because it is best” (L). Same sentence copied again (R).
  Shape: repair a one-sided appraisal and justify one revision. Analyze only isolated components of a model about a different choice.
- **Exit 105, released 01, 02, 03 and 04.** “Replace the plan that gives each source its own paragraph.” “Why should your reasons come in this order?” Example: group 01:342 and 03:42 under staying in regular work under pressure, then 02:26 and 04:38 under the continuing need to oppose exclusion. The reader sees the sporting platform before how it could be used. Other supported grouping and orders count. Four titles as a plan (O). “Leadership proves he led” (L).
  Shape: independently regroup all four sources under a reason order. Judge practice on one misplaced detail, while each student's own full map stays unjudged.
- **Exit 106.** Name Jackie Robinson as the topic. Shared line: “Everyone knows about him.” Ask “Rewrite this opening for a reader new to baseball.” Look for brief context, a clear position and two groups of reasons. Sample: “Clubs once kept Black players out. Jackie Robinson challenged that practice through steady teamwork and public arguments for fair treatment.” “You remember that team” (P). “Here are some games” (K).
  Shape: produce a fresh reader-facing opening that establishes position and groups. Keep model openings with these reasons and fill-in starters off the page. The children's real introductions stay in their folders.
- **105 word transfer, five minutes inside Words.** Dictate credibility and observance first on numbered blank lines. Then read them alone and in two private sentences: “The care she took increased her credibility.” “Their observance of the rule kept the game fair.” Record isolated reading, contextual reading and spelling separately on one met/partly met/not yet record. Date the 106 retry. Reading credible for credibility drops a suffix (Z).
  Shape: decode the two unfamiliar forms across settings and spell each from dictation. Use different -ive and -ance families in models and leave the probes and sentences sealed.

## 6. Days

## Day 102

Understanding. “I can group details from several sources under reasons.” Teacher holds the example and students build their own map. Tool: None.

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return 100 and 101. Reserve five minutes for one common priority and five for independent corrections. File the corrected edit and vocabulary notes.
- **10 min. Words.** Read and spell supportive and assistance. Compare endings and retrieve -tion, -sion, -ity and -ial. Keep 105 probes sealed.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Identify a cause clause in a separate released sentence.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Reread 03:34 twice. Check understanding and confirm a corrected word after blending.
- **20 min. Source sort.** Establish the earlier two-source sort with 03:24 and 04:30. Inspect whether one fact belongs under a proposed heading. Add the source title to the evidence.
- **25 min. Opinion assignment.** Assign the Robinson leadership opinion with all four titles for an unfamiliar reader. Mark the source and reason fields. Students start their own unjudged folio. Hold Exit 102's grouping off the example.
- **5 min. Exit.** Exit 102.

## Day 103

Application. “I can use a discussion to rethink my opinion.” Students hold a cited note and their own source pages. Tool: None.

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return 102. Mark a position and its grouped reasons, then correct independently.
- **10 min. Words.** Contrast suffix changes and use one released form in a sentence.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer.
- **25 min. Discussion.** Bring a cited note and use the roles from 93. Hear two groups of five in two 45-second rounds each, staggered over 15 minutes, favoring unheard pupils. The other groups work in parallel. Date preparation, role, elaboration and conclusion separately. Gather a cited note and conclusion from everyone. Use the teacher-led and one-to-one turns in the existing slot only as time allows and track pending pupils.
- **20 min. Reflection.** Withdraw supplied frames. Summarize what a partner's evidence establishes. Apply any insight to the private folio without marking that revised opinion.
- **5 min. Exit.** Exit 103. Use the actual contribution and return written feedback for 104.

## Day 104

Understanding. “I can explain why a writer groups and links reasons.” Teacher holds the model labels. Tool: None.

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return 103. Check a new contribution from a due pupil and collect the rest of the independent written corrections.
- **10 min. Words.** Read and spell words with -ive and -ance in released contexts.
- **5 min. Grammar.** Mark how specifically introduces a detail in a released example.
- **15 min. Partner rereading.** Reread a released biography extract with feedback on meaning and expression.
- **20 min. Model.** Use *Qualities in Action* only if its registered copy is on file. Otherwise mark an opinion, a reason and a related ending orally while pointing to 01:34 to 38. Students read the registered source lines and inspect individual labels, without a substitute core text.
- **25 min. Critique.** Partners test a result clause and an added detail, mark which evidence belongs, and leave one written cue on a supplied component. Use wording and evidence different from the exit and assessed essay anchors.
- **5 min. Exit.** Exit 104.

## Day 105

Application. “I can order reasons and show where their evidence comes from.” Students hold their source lists and maps. Tool: None.

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return 104. Identify a change in substance and make the independent correction.
- **10 min. Words.** Five minutes with released relatives and five for the private word transfer. Dictate for 1¼ minutes. Use six parallel stations, five turns each at 45 seconds for both readings per turn. With one listener, hear the first five here.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer. With one listener, hear the remaining 25 private turns in 18¾ minutes while the class reads. Track outstanding turns and return individual cues for 106.
- **20 min. Evidence.** Check quotation boundaries. Judge one proposed detail's relevance to its heading.
- **25 min. Planning.** Map the student's own reasons using several accounts. Date and keep the plan as unjudged folder work.
- **5 min. Exit.** Exit 105.

## Day 106

Application. “I can open an opinion for a reader new to the subject.” Students hold their maps. Tool: None.

- **10 min. Return and repair.** Return 105. Group details by reason and retry. Give word feedback for individual correction.
- **10 min. Words.** Compare unstressed vowels and suffix changes. Verify a spelling with a reference. Preview revise, conclusion, link, clause, critique, fact, detail and nevertheless in released contexts.
- **20 min. Choice reading.** Log and confer. Hear any due private word corrections and date the outcome.
- **20 min. Audience.** Mark a missing piece of reader knowledge in an isolated released sentence.
- **25 min. Drafting.** Students write their own introductions and state the groups from their maps. Keep these drafts as folder work for written feedback on 107.
- **5 min. Exit.** Exit 106. Collect the separate response and prepare a written cue.

## 9. Supports and extension

Offer opinion and reason with “___ adds ___.” Preview unfamiliar baseball terms and chunk long source clauses. Compare the contribution each source makes after reading. Keep one extract pass under about 250 words, roughly two to three minutes alone. Supply large print and oral directions. Keep composition and spelling independent and record accommodations. Extend by testing an alternate reason order for the same reader.
