# Course frame: Theater, grades 6 to 8

Course `theater-grades-6-to-8` (catalog id `theater-6-8`), a band course for grade 6, grade 7 and grade 8, run as three years one after another (year 6, year 7, year 8). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a technique and a thread come back
  one step harder a year later in the same place: a character unit (the actor's tools and the written response to
  artists' choices), a staging and story unit (fixing a staging problem in a scene and studying the play's versions,
  era and objects), a design and judging unit (the scene's tech and a judgment against criteria), and a devised
  showing unit (a group piece on a shared theme or community issue, with the cultural views that shape how drama is
  judged) that ends the year with a showing for an audience. Each unit joins two progression threads, one of
  creating or performing and one of responding or connecting, so every unit makes, performs, studies another
  artist's work and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Grades 6 to 8 theater has eight rows a year, two a unit.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up routine (about 3 minutes: chairs and bags to the walls, the clear floor
  checked, the group agreements card up), the warm-up (5), the teaching, rehearsing and making, and the close (about
  4: chairs back, props and scripts to the class box, the day's evidence collected); a making session with card,
  tape and paint takes 6 for clean-up. A readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (two check days: a showing day and a written day; no unit is over 25 days, so none has a
  mid-unit assessment) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the character unit is
  longest (18 days) because it owns seven ids, sets up the year's warm-up and floor routine and retrieves two rows;
  the other three units own five or six ids each and take 14 days, the devised unit's devising and showing balanced
  by the year's characters, fixes and designs already made. These are course days: the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2,
  rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the Educational Theatre Association's 2016 opportunity-to-learn
  standards (schooltheatre.org, the 2022 posting, PDF page 10, as the second-model read of 2026-11-25 found it at
  source) ask for at least 45 minutes of theater instruction in the school day for at least one semester each year;
  the item does not say "daily". Read as 45 minutes a day over this repository's 80-day semester (an assumption,
  labeled as one), that is 60 hours; this course gives 80 minutes a week, 42 hours 40 minutes a year (17 hours 20
  minutes short), and 40 minutes a week, 21 hours 20 minutes, on the once-a-week path (38 hours 40 minutes short).
  For the lead item 2 asks for the figure to be confirmed at source. The course is written so a teacher with no drama training can
  teach it; a school with a drama teacher uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: study of the
  works and the demonstration with a step card; rehearsal with the critique and the revision; the showing check; a
  separate written check session; and a respond session that starts from marks already made, so no first-time
  evidence is ever collected in a respond session (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). Fluency days fold into the
  openers, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path, every year: units of 9, 7, 6
  and 6, split as study and demonstration 2, 2, 1, 1; rehearsal, critique and revision 4, 2, 2, 2; then 1 showing
  check, 1 written check and 1 respond session in every unit. Each year's two course checks keep 1 check session and
  1 respond session. The mid-year check session falls in week 17 and its respond
  session in week 18; the year-end check session in week 31 and its respond session in week 32. A merged day never
  drops a critique, a revision or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, theater performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule binds
  (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 6 claims only the 24 grade 6 ids (`.6a`, `.6b`, `.6c`), year 7 only the 24 grade 7 ids,
  year 8 only the 24 grade 8 ids: 72 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its own
  year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1), and each unit touches at
  least two. In the Units table the C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and
  anchor standards the unit works on, and the works it studies. Each id's action was checked against its words-file
  line and the row's description; the national text governs where they part (a unit plan that finds a drift names
  it under its own For the lead). Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids,
  the nine criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list
  (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule
  (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file (L83, done 2026-11-21);
  each year's scope and sequence still states its id count (24) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met.
  The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Base text.** None. Theater is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open theater recordings). Works studied are of four
  kinds: plays whose texts are public domain (Goldoni, Chekhov, Kalidasa in its 1855 and 1912 English translations,
  Sheridan, the Yuan Chalk Circle in its 1832 French translation), from which Curio Class adapts one short scene in its
  own words with the source credited; a public domain film (Harold Lloyd, 1923; the film is public domain in the
  United States, and any restored copy or score carries its own rights line); theater forms, companies and makers,
  living and past, described in our own words and linked to the maker's, a museum's, a university's or a cultural
  body's own page, never embedded or copied; and open-access museum objects and prints, with their rights line.
  Scripts the class performs are these adaptations, Curio Class originals or the students' own (APPROACH "Rights").
  Unseen check work is a short written description, a live teacher performance of a Curio Class original scene, or a
  classmates' fresh scene (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). Each work named below is a pick the unit plan keeps
  unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped work keeps the unit's ids met and the
  year's range. Range, checked across the band and within each year: year 6 Africa (Ghana), the Americas (Peru, the
  United States), Asia (Japan), Europe (Italy, England) and Oceania (Aotearoa New Zealand); year 7 Africa
  (Kenya), the Americas (a Native American company of Kuna and Rappahannock founders; Chicago; New York), Asia (India,
  Japan) and Europe (Russia, the United Kingdom); year 8 Africa (South Africa), the Americas (Brazil; a Native
  American playwrights' company; the United States), Asia (China), Europe (England, Ireland's Sheridan on the London
  stage, Russia) and Oceania (an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander company); living makers in every year.
  Checked against every lower theater band (K to 2 and 3 to 5), 2026-11-26: no work repeats. Three makers or forms
  return on purpose, each named in its unit as a revisit with a new job: commedia dell'arte (grades 3 to 5 unit 1,
  Scala's improvised scenarios; here unit 2, Goldoni's written comedy), Stanislavski and Spolin (grades 3 to 5 units 5
  and 1, given circumstances and the "Where" game; here unit 9, objectives and the games' origin) and Shakespeare
  (grades 3 to 5 units 9 and 12, a speech and the First Folio; here unit 11, the playhouse). Checked against the ELA
  frames and data/plans/ela-texts.csv: no ELA anchor play is used (the ELA 6 to 12 anchor plays are Romeo and Juliet,
  Antigone, Trifles, Hamlet and The Importance of Being Earnest). Curio Class writes every step card, scene
  adaptation, research card, object card, description, record sheet and family note.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Acting: body choices from a character's given circumstances (6),
  a character's goal played two ways with an inner monologue (7), tactics switched at a beat against an obstacle,
  with a bodily response (8). Staging: two or three fixes for one staging problem tested in rehearsal (6), fixes from
  several viewpoints and a written defense (7), pacing tried and an original idea built on researched background (8).
  Design: one design problem fixed and the plan tried in rehearsal (6), several elements chosen and weighed together
  (7), elements combined for the real performance space and refined with simple technology (8). Devising: offers
  built on and a piece adapted for an informal showing (6), many community voices woven with another art and every
  rehearsal kept (7), lead roles rotated, a piece reworked by runs and staged polished (8). Responding: a written
  reaction (6), reactions compared (7), decisions judged against criteria (8).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a technique shown in
  named steps with the step card (a body choice, a beat change, a cue called), or a work watched, read or described
  and studied with the unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day).
  Fluency: the warm-up routine and a scene beat run several times with feedback (a voice and body warm-up, a status
  walk, a tactic switch drill, a cue run); mostly the five-minute warm-up and one or two fluency days a unit.
  Application: rehearsal, devising and design making with some choice of role, line, fix or element; the second
  rehearsal day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's revision is application
  too. Check: the showing day and the written day of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second try at the part
  marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency,
  application, fluency, application with the critique, revision, two check days, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for on the unit's guide, named id by id in the
  unit's "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one item
  for each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in three kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide (three to
  five look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live as each group performs; a kept sheet (a character map, an
  idea sheet, a script markup, a cue sheet, a design sketch with its reason, a rehearsal log, a critique-and-revise
  note) made on the unit's last rehearsal or making days, scored after the session; and written items on the written
  day, answered on an unseen work. The end-of-unit assessment is two check days near the unit's end, so the teacher
  never marks a performance and reads or hears other work at once. The showing day's 40 minutes: set-up 3; warm-up
  5; six groups of five perform for about 4 minutes each (24) while the others watch as the audience; each student's
  self-check on the guide 3; close 4. That is 39 minutes. The written day's 40 minutes: set-up 2; the teacher
  performs the unit's short unseen Curio Class scene twice or hands out the unseen description (6); pairs talk for 3
  minutes where an id asks for discussion; the written items (25); close 4. That is 40 minutes, with no paint, glue or
  floor work. Every student is marked on their own: the teacher keeps a student-by-id observation roster, and every
  acting id needs a mark the teacher saw that student earn; a director's, designer's or stage manager's job never
  stands in for an acting mark. In a two-character scene the roles rotate in final observation rounds on the last
  rehearsal days, so each student has an observed acting turn, and any mark still missing is taken live in such a
  round before the respond session. Recording is optional: a student who declines is observed live during a scheduled
  final observation round, and the teacher's mark is the evidence (APPROACH "Kept work"). The
  showing's rehearsals, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record sheet, and its revision
  come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a technique is first taught. Each unit opens
  with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has
  two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the portfolio folder (a character map, a
  cue sheet, a design sketch, a log page or a photo of the staging) with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion
  8).
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every year 6 unit assumes the grade 5 row in each of its two threads, last used one
  grade back in Theater, grades 3 to 5, so it opens with a retrieval task inside day 2's opener, never a readiness
  check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook and the question. Theater, grades 3 to 5 is not a core course, so the
  seam is by course order with its fallback (lesson 32): each year 6 retrieval task carries its ten-minute reteach of
  the grade 5 technique as written, for any student new to theater (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline look").
  Every year 7 and year 8 unit assumes the year before's row in the same thread, last used one grade back in this
  course, so it opens the same way, with the unit and days of that last use named in its row. The count starts from
  the band's order (6, then 7, then 8). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a student may meet year 8 before
  year 7: each retrieval task then carries its ten-minute reteach as written. The Seams tables give each row's last
  use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades 6 to 8 after-core checks keep off the school days that hold a core check, and this
  course keeps off whole core check weeks (grade 6: weeks 12, 15, 16, 18, 22 to 24; grade 7: 9, 12 to 14, 22, 24;
  grade 8: 10, 14, 15, 18, 20, 24 to 26, 29 and 30; week 32 is every grade's finals week, CALENDAR.md stagger rule 3).
  Chosen by the units' load, not to dodge other after-core courses (the lead's calendar pass does that): mid-year in
  week 17 in every year (course days 33 and 34 of the year, school days 83 and 85, after units 1 and 2's 32 days;
  grade 6's math respond day is school day 81, two days earlier); year-end in week 32, the finals week, in every year
  (course days 63 and 64, school days 158 and 160), after the devised showing unit, whose showing is its own
  end-of-unit assessment. Each check day: set-up 3, warm-up 3, the groups plan a fresh task from a new card (8), six
  groups of five perform it for about 3 minutes each (18) while each student in the audience writes the check's
  written item on one assigned group's scene, close 4. That is 36 minutes; the teacher marks one group at a time and
  hears no talk that day.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a work for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Every unit of this course uses play-acting, which STANDARD.md 5 names as a reason a community may
  leave a unit out; a school that leaves out play-acting leaves out the course, and the course page says so plainly.
  Units that study a cultural or ceremonial tradition, named on the course page: unit 1 (Yuyachkani's use of Peru's
  festival forms), unit 4 (a Māori
  theater company; a Ghanaian play's town festival), unit 6 (Sanskrit drama and the hermitage rites, studied by
  reading only), unit 7 (kabuki), unit 8 (a Native American company's storyweaving; a Kenyan community theater),
  unit 10 (a Yuan troupe painted in a temple hall, studied by looking only), unit 11 (an Aboriginal and Torres Strait
  Islander company) and unit 12 (a South African play's Christian imagery; a Native American playwrights' company).
  Swapping a work keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's family note name
  the ids not claimed that year.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 with a clear floor (desks pushed back, a hall or a cleared corner), chairs,
  printed scenes, card, tape, found objects, the room's own light switch and blinds, and the voice; nothing must be
  bought, and every unit names a free stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent
  is the audience and the critique partner, a group scene has a doubled version (parent and student, or the student
  playing two roles with a chair for the other), and the showing is to the family or a recorded take. The teacher
  needs no drama training: each unit's teacher page says what to look and listen for, in plain words.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items counted once,
  printing of scenes, cards and sheets), all optional beside the free path: year 6 $5, $5, $30 (six small LED
  flashlights, reusable for the band, colored film, card), $5 ($45); year 7 $5, $5, $15 (film and card), $5 ($30);
  year 8 $5, $5, $15 (film, card, tape), $5 ($30). Planning figures, not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher screen may show a work's page or a step; every day runs from the printed
  step card, scene and description without it. A school device may record a take only where the student and family
  choose it (Scored evidence) and may play a sound cue in units 7 and 11. Owned ids that ask for technology: TH:Cr3.1.8c
  ("simple technology"), met in full with flashlights or the room's light switch and a found-object sound table run
  from a cue sheet, so a paper-path student meets every id in full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or
  family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on the floor, the voice, paper, objects and printed cards; any Curio Class tool a unit
  plan adds (a stage-directions practice tool, a cue-calling practice tool) has a printed version that does the same
  job and needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Scenes are short (one to two pages for each group) and are read aloud first at a table read; a
  student may take a role with fewer lines, a role with no lines, or the stage manager's, designer's or director's
  job, and the check marks performing apart from reading. Research cards are a page at most, at the grade's reading
  level. Written items ask for one to eight sentences. English learners: each unit teaches its six words in depth,
  with a picture, a gesture or a sound and a frame ("My character wants ___ so she ___." "I judge ___ by ___
  because ___."), a student may show or perform the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student
  performed apart from what they wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is hands-on (movement on a shared floor; prop, set and light making), so every unit gets
  the fresh safety agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against free guides, named here and opened
  by the agent: the federal consumer product safety agency's "Art and Craft Safety Guide" (cpsc.gov) and California's
  environmental health hazard office's guidance on art and craft materials for schools, with its list of materials
  not to buy for grades K to 6 (oehha.ca.gov; this course applies it to grades 7 and 8 too), for every making day;
  and the same federal agency's button battery safety pages (cpsc.gov), for the flashlights. Rules for every unit,
  from APPROACH "Safe use" and marked "from memory" until that read checks them: a clear floor with room for arms
  out, chairs and bags to the walls; soft soles or bare feet on a clean floor; no lifts, no falls rehearsed, no
  contact stage fights and no real or toy weapons; partner contact only by agreement and never required; voice rest
  and no forced loud sound; props checked by the teacher before use (no sharp edges, nothing breakable, no cords long
  enough to wrap a neck); no ladders, no standing on chairs or tables, nothing hung above heads; flashlights LED only,
  with screw-closed battery doors and no coin or button batteries; no light shone into eyes and no flashing; no heat
  source or hot glue; blades used by an adult only; supplies labeled non-toxic; any device cable taped down; hands
  washed after paint, glue and found objects. Each unit's last column gives its hazards and controls in the words
  days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the works with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (Yuyachkani
  and the Andean festival forms of Peru), unit 2 (commedia dell'arte of Italy), unit 4 (Efua Sutherland's Ghana; Taki Rua and te ao Māori), unit 5
  (Lorraine Hansberry's Chicago and the housing covenant case), unit 6 (Sanskrit drama of India), unit 7 (kabuki of
  Japan), unit 8 (Spiderwoman Theater's Kuna and Rappahannock founders; Kamiriithu of Kenya), unit 10 (Yuan drama of
  China), unit 11 (Ilbijerri's Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities) and unit 12 (Woza Albert! and
  apartheid South Africa; Native Voices at the Autry). No student plays a character by an accent, a costume of
  another culture or a stereotype; no student is cast against their wish; no role play puts a student in a victim's
  or a harmer's place, and no scene stages bullying, abuse or harassment (APPROACH "Works and their settings").
  Every social problem or community issue comes from a class list of local, non-partisan public matters or an
  invented town, marked the same (values rule), and never from a student's own family, health, body or a disclosure.
  Every task that ties a character or a choice to a student's own life or beliefs (units 1, 5, 9 for tastes and
  beliefs; units 4, 8, 12 for community) offers an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive
  ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, a clear floor, step
  cards, adapted scenes, research and object cards, found and low-cost materials. Activities: the actor's tools,
  staging, design and devising taught in steps; plays, forms and companies studied with their setting; rehearsal with
  a critique and a revision; a showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with
  reflections, 12 showings, two marks a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the
  year's 24 ids and growth on the look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class makes no claim
  that arts study raises any other result (APPROACH "Sources").

### Handed on to Theater, grades 9 to 12 (Level 1)
