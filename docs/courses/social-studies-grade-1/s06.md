## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 56.** Meet present-day street and school photographs from Tahlequah, Sapporo and Singapore. Notice ordinary shared needs before asking the compelling question.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write: How do people live in these places (56 to 58)? How can Americans be united and different (59 to 61)? What can symbols, words and songs express (62 to 65)?
- **Closing, day 66.** Write or dictate a claim, evidence, reason and closing from the Constitution, Cherokee Nation account and one national symbol source. Defensible answers may emphasize shared government, history or ideals.
- **Teaching.** Model observation versus inference on 56, perspective on 57 and a sourced claim on 61; step back on 66. Identify each source's kind, maker, date and usefulness. Teach custom, language, belief, united, diverse, evidence, symbol, motto, liberty and justice through pictures and plain explanations.

- **Limits.** Three places cannot represent whole peoples. Weather influences choices without determining culture. No costume, ceremony or migration role play. Shared ideals do not prove equal treatment: people were enslaved, and Native people were forced from homelands. Native nations govern today. Describe beliefs without practising them. Explain the Pledge, including its religious wording, without requiring agreement, recitation, standing or singing. Assess understanding, never patriotism.
- **Carried.** Keep unit 5's question board, source routine and blank claim frame. Return to map keys and directions on 58; keep each map's indicators visible. Introduce perspective on 57 and return on 60. Tie claims to source details on 61; cover completed models on 66. **Teaching contrasts and responses.** A, day 56: compare "Everyone lives this way" with "This photograph shows these people here, on this date." Use a second contemporary view of the same place. On 57 compare "That greeting is wrong" with its speaker's explanation of welcome. B, day 58: contrast "Snow makes people speak Japanese" with "Snow affects clothing and travel; people learn languages from other people." Sort natural features and human practices, then explain one weather link. C, day 59: compare identical-clothing unity with different-clothing neighbors sharing government. Return to the Constitution and a community account. D, day 62: compare "It is a bird" with "The eagle in this seal stands for the country; this account explains its use." Keep documented adoption separate from later interpretations; do not assign the seal's color explanations to the original flag. Repeat the distinction for Liberty on 63 and songs on 65. E, day 64: contrast "Everyone must recite" with "Everyone can study the words; speaking is a choice." Offer silent text study without requiring a reason for silence. F, day 60: contrast "Native people are only in old pictures" with Cherokee Nation's current government and language education. State that United States soldiers forced Cherokee people from their homelands; do not describe that journey as a free choice. Day 63 contrasts an ideal of welcome with the fact that people were excluded or treated unfairly. No reenactment or invented community speech. G, day 61: compare "Our town is united because I like it" with a detail about shared government linked to a claim. Cover the completed model for a fresh attempt.

When more than a third miss a part, use its contrast with everyone for five to ten minutes; otherwise guide a group while classmates explain a second source. Keep the grade-level goal.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** Print photographs, maps, source cards, song words and music, a timeline, blank comparison charts, claim frames and record sheets. Supply paper and crayons. An adult can hum tunes from printed music; no recording or device is required. One shared screen may display the same prints; no student timers.

**Access.** Read everything aloud; require no independent reading. Rehearse "The source shows ___. This supports ___ because ___." Provide familiar-language rehearsal, neutral descriptions, large print and adapted marking tools. Distinguish language gaps from conceptual errors. Extension defends two interpretations of one symbol. At home, the adult takes the discussion partner's role and follows day 68's fresh attempt.

## 9. Family note

We will compare everyday life and use sources to explain how people can share a country while living differently. Children may draw or dictate; no one must share family beliefs or background, say the Pledge or sing.
