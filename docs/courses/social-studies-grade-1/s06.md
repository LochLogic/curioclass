## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 56.** Meet present-day street and school photographs from Sapporo, Singapore and Nairobi, Kenya. Notice ordinary shared needs before asking the compelling question.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write: How do people live in these places (56 to 58)? How can Americans be united and different (59 to 61)? What can symbols, words and songs express (62 to 65)?
- **Closing, day 66.** Write or dictate a claim, evidence, reason and closing from the Constitution, Cherokee Nation account and one national symbol source. Defensible answers may emphasize shared government, history or ideals.
- **Teaching.** Model observation versus inference on 56, perspective on 57 and a sourced claim on 61; step back on 66. Identify each source's kind, maker, date and usefulness. Teach custom, language, belief, united, diverse, evidence, symbol, motto, liberty and justice through pictures and plain explanations.


**D2.Geo.6.K-2.** Identify cultural and environmental features of particular places. Parts: identify cultural features; identify environmental features. The local file lists identifiers only; the [publisher PDF](https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.pdf), printed page 41, supplies the wording. Section 6.1 owns every listed part.
- **Limits.** Three places cannot represent whole peoples. Weather influences choices without determining culture. No costume, ceremony or migration role play. Shared ideals do not prove equal treatment: people were enslaved, and Native people were forced from homelands. Native nations govern today. Describe beliefs without practising them. Explain the Pledge, including its religious wording, without requiring agreement, recitation, standing or singing. Assess understanding, never patriotism.
- **Carried.** Keep unit 5's question board, source routine and blank claim frame. Return to map keys and directions on 58; keep each map's indicators visible. Introduce perspective on 57 and return on 60. Tie claims to source details on 61; cover completed models on 66. Revisit chronological order with dated symbol cards on 63; dates are supplied, not memorized.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

**Observation assessment, day 67, 30 minutes; response day 68.** Days 62 to 66 provide practice only, including the first symbol, phrase and song responses. Score all six items from unseen views, captions and cases on day 67. Use five minutes for oral orientation and twenty-five for paper responses. Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 are paper only (pointing, matching, drawing, with humming where item 5 needs it). The teacher hears item 3's meanings and item 6 from the rotation, about three minutes a group for five groups of five or six, while classmates mark pictures. Item minutes within the twenty-five: 1, four; 2, three; 3, five; 4, four; 5, four; 6, five. Paper parts: 1, mark supported comparisons; 2, sort cultural and environmental features; 3, link each symbol to its meaning and mark evidence; 4, match both phrases to meanings; 5, match both tunes and lyric phrases to meanings; 6, draw or dictate unity and diversity with evidence and a closing. A name or tune match alone does not establish meaning. Record every component separately. A child not heard, or with an unresolved explanation, is heard in day 68's fresh attempt; record missing evidence rather than inferring it from practice. Teacher marking follows class.

**Held-out sources.** Teaching uses one set of images and explanations; checks use different views, captions, cases and lyric passages, never completed class answers. Familiar symbols and tunes remain the content being recognized. Source A is an unseen contemporary Sapporo street photograph and original factual caption: "Snow lies beside these apartment buildings. Nairobi teaches on days 56 to 58 only; its teaching views follow the same rule (no view, caption or case reused in a check).
