- **MA.1.T1.4, this section's share (days 22 to 26).** Children "analyze examples of leaders from history, daily life, and texts, and describe the qualities of a good leader". Four leaders from history, each from a source read aloud (kind, maker, old or new, useful for what): Harriet Tubman (day 22), Abraham Lincoln (23), Dolores Huerta (24), Wilma Mankiller (25). Each day: what the leader did, who else acted, what changed, and one quality shown by an action. Day 26 compares two. The quality emphasis is the one the source supports best, not the only one: courage (Tubman), taking responsibility for a hard decision (Lincoln), organizing others (Huerta), working with her nation (Mankiller). All four emphases are source to check against the pages in unit field 10.
- **D2.His.3.K-2, this section's share (days 22 to 25).** "Ask questions" about people and groups behind a change: each day one question about the person and one about the group, from the picture or the summary.
- **D2.Civ.1.K-2, this section's share (day 26).** "Describe" what people in charge are responsible for, from the chart: a President and a Principal Chief hold offices; Tubman and Huerta led without one.
- **Limits that bite here.** Study actions, not flawless heroes. Slavery is named plainly: people were treated as property and forced to work, and it was wrong. Tubman had helpers; she did not act alone (D). Lincoln's proclamation did not free everyone or end slavery alone (K). Huerta (1930 to 2026) died on October 28, 2026: past tense. If a child names Cesar Chavez, the teacher says in one sentence that he and Huerta started the farm workers' union together and that this class studies Huerta's work; the 2026 statements are not taught. The Cherokee Nation is a nation that governs itself today, named by its own name, in the present tense (L). No one acts out slavery, escape, migration or work hardship; no oppression simulation. Elections wait for unit 4. Every date and fact below is source to check against its field 10 page at the data stage; a sentence the page does not support is cut or reworded.
- **Connections.** Unit 2's sources (day 22 onward: kind, maker, old or new, useful), unit 2's year strip stretched into an unscaled timeline (each day), unit 2's change chart (each day), ELA unit 2's RI.1.3 as the comparison on day 26.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to say the hard facts in plain words, once, and move to what the person did. The credit line for each summary (modern summary of a government page; a federal document for day 23; the Cherokee Nation's own words for day 25 as the page gives them). Why the summaries are the class's own words read aloud: no child decodes an adult source.

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** none. Section 3.2 builds the action records that items 2 and 3 in section 3.3 need; no item is given here.

**Exit questions.** Mark by every child; one spoken ask heard from the rotating six. Cases are "invented for practice" and never return in teaching or on day 30. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's repair. Day 22's miss goes to day 23's opening, day 23's to day 24's, and so on; day 26's goes to day 27.
- **E22.** Mark: a made-up story; a lighthouse keeper warns boats on stormy nights while neighbors carry lanterns to the shore. Circle who helped the boats: the keeper and the neighbors, or the keeper alone. Spoken: "What does the keeper's action show about him?" Answer: keeper and neighbors; a quality tied to the action, such as brave or caring because he warned boats in storms. Wrong: keeper alone (D); "He is a good leader because he is famous" (B).
- **E23.** Mark (every child, one ask): an invented town paper says the east pool is open; the town has three pools. Circle what the paper did: opened the east pool, or opened all three pools. Answer: opened the east pool. Wrong: opened all three pools (K).
- **E24.** Mark: an invented account; neighbors met with a leader to ask the building owner for a safe stair. Circle who made the change: the neighbors and the leader together, or the leader alone. Spoken: "Ask one question about what the neighbors did." Answer: a question about meeting, asking or the result. Wrong: the leader alone (D); "What color was the stair?" (E).
- **E25.** Mark: an invented garden-club record; a leader invites members to choose where to plant. Circle the picture that shows listening: the leader inviting members to choose, or the leader choosing alone. Spoken: "Ask one question about what the members chose." Answer: a question about what members chose or changed. Wrong: circles the leader choosing alone as listening, as if the leader does all the deciding (D); "Everyone agreed on the spot" as an unsupported claim (O).
- **E26.** Mark: at a made-up picnic, one leader checks that the gate is shut so no one wanders off, and another brings water bottles for everyone. Can both show the same quality, care? Join the two pictures (yes) or keep them apart (no). Spoken: "Why can both show the same quality?" Answer: different jobs, both showing preparation or care by their actions. Wrong: apart, "they have different jobs" (N).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Print sources, original read-alouds, drawings, timeline strips, action charts, blank writing frames and record sheets. Use paper and crayons. One optional shared screen shows these same prints; no student timer. At home, the adult models and serves as discussion partner.
>
> **Language and access.** No independent reading is required. Rehearse: "A good leader ___. The source tells ___. This matters because ___." Accept familiar-language explanations before English rehearsal. For extension, support two different qualities from the same actions.
<!-- /skel -->

- **Carried.** The action chart (leader, what the leader did, who else acted, what changed, quality) gets one row a day and is the evidence for days 26 and 29. The unscaled timeline strip, begun with unit 2's year strip, gains a card each day: Tubman 1849 on, Lincoln 1861 to 1865, Huerta 1962 on, Mankiller 1985 to 1995 (source to check), so it runs past one year. The source questions (kind, maker, old or new, useful) run each day. The three supporting questions stay on the board: "What did each leader and their group change?" is answered a row at a time; "Which actions show good leadership?" is answered on day 26.
- **Key moment 1, day 22: action, not fame.** Must be seen: a child names a quality from what Tubman did. Ideal instrument: picture cards of her actions and her helpers, on a chart a child can touch. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 26: one quality, two jobs.** Must be seen: a child joins two different actions under one quality. Ideal instrument: a two-column strip with movable quality cards. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn stick cards; the chart drawn on butcher paper; the strip a string with clothes pins.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: quality, evidence, President, nation, farm worker, organize. Frames: "She did ___." "I think she shows ___ because ___." Familiar-language answers first.
- **Access.** Raised-line cards; every drawing described aloud; a card moved by an adult as the child directs; pointing counts.
- **Check accommodations.** Point, circle, token, dictation; a private hearing; an adult may take the partner's role.
- **Reading load.** None; the summaries, the proclamation paragraph and the captions are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A second quality for the same leader from a different action; a question about the helpers.
