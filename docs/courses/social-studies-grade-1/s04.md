## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 32.** Read invented picture cases: a group can arrange its reading corner to satisfy everyone; another must choose one of two storybooks today. Ask the compelling question, then retrieve agreement before teaching voting.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write: When can talking reach agreement (day 32)? What makes an election fair (days 33 and 34)? What do elected leaders and governments do (days 35 to 37)? What evidence helps us choose again (day 38)?
- **Closing, day 39.** Write or dictate a claim, reason and closing for a new group situation, using the day 32 case records and day 34 election record. Different methods are defensible when their reasons fit the situation.
- **Teaching.** Model agreement and voting on day 32, ballot counting on day 33, and a sourced reason on day 38; step back on days 34 and 39. Identify each source's kind, maker, date and usefulness before reading. Teach vote, election, ballot, majority, equality, government, citizen, term and re-elect through cases.


The local C3 file holds identifiers only. Paraphrases follow the publisher's [indicators](https://www.socialstudies.org/sites/default/files/c3/c3-framework-for-social-studies-rev0617.pdf), PDF pages 32, 33, 38 and 60:

- **D2.Civ.8.K-2.** Describe democratic principles such as equality, fairness, and respect for legitimate authority and rules. Parts: describe each principle.
- **D2.Civ.5.K-2.** Explain what governments are and what they do. Parts: explain government; explain functions.
- **D2.Eco.12.K-2.** Describe goods and services governments provide. Parts: describe goods; describe services.
- **D4.1.K-2.** Construct an argument with reasons. Parts: construct argument; supply reasons. This is review.
- **Anchor parts.** MA.1.T1.5: give examples of disagreement; explain fair decisions and choosing leaders. MA.1.T1.6: explain elections. MA.1.T1.7: name elected leaders; explain duties. MA.1.T1.8: explain citizenship, voting rights and duties, fixed terms and judging re-election.
- **Limits.** No parties, real candidates or classmate contests. Not all leaders are elected; mayor and captain are examples where elected. Government officials share powers. Citizenship differs from residence; children and noncitizens belong and have rights, although voting eligibility has rules. Never ask family status. No electoral-system detail or tax arithmetic. Majority choice cannot remove rights; accepting a fair result permits respectful disagreement and questioning unfair rules.
- **Carried.** Keep unit 3's question board, sourcing routine and blank claim frame. Return to leadership qualities on day 34. Tie the day 38 reason to a term record; cover completed models on day 39. Retain blank frames and access supports. Calendar routines continue; no new timeline work.
