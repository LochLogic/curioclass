# Section 8.1: Earning, goods and services, and prices


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T4.4, days 81 and 82, whole standard.** "Explain what being employed means and define income, wages, and salary." Day 81 explains employed (having a job where an employer pays you) and defines income, wages and salary by picture; day 82 applies them to specialized jobs.
- **D2.Eco.6.K-2, days 81 and 82.** "Explain how people earn income": by wages, by salary, or by selling what they make or grow.
- **MA.1.T4.5, days 83 and 84; MA.1.T4.6, day 83.** "Give examples of goods that people buy and use"; "give examples of services people do for one another." Both halves of each, with examples sorted, not only named.
- **D2.Eco.5.K-2, day 84.** Prices read and compared on an invented class store flyer.
- **Limits that bite here.** Whole numbers to 20 only, no coin values or decimals. No real money; tokens stand for dollars. Unpaid care is work, never ranked below paid work. Pay never measures a person's worth. No child reports on a home, a family's work or money. Income also comes in other ways; this unit follows earnings and says so aloud once.
- **Connections.** 1.NBT.B.3 on day 84 (compare numbers within 20; math unit 4, already taught). Kindergarten's work, skill, tool, earn and pay, goods and services and buy and sell return as review, in the ledger's words.
- **What the teacher page must explain.**
  - Employed: a person has a job and an employer pays them. Income: money received for work. Wages: pay counted by the hour. Salary: pay agreed for regular times, often stated as a yearly amount but paid in installments (for example each month), never in one lump. Own wording, from memory; source to check against a free federal labor glossary, not yet read.
  - A person who sells what they make or grow earns income without an employer. Unpaid work (care at home, volunteering) is work without income.
  - A good is a thing you can hold; a service is something one person does for another. What a service uses (scissors, a cart) is still a good.
  - Why people specialize: each learns one part well, so work goes faster and better. Not a ranking of jobs.
  - Every flyer, price and shop in this section is invented, and the teacher says so. Nothing on the flyer is a fact about a real shop.

## 2. Where it sits


- Before: the start of unit 8. Day 81 opens with the hook and the compelling question, then kindergarten's work (retrieval task, unit plan field 4). Day 83 opens with kindergarten's goods and services (retrieval task). There is no readiness check.
- After: section 8.2 takes the item 2 records on 85. Results of the two retrieval tasks, the records and the exits go to the record sheet (one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen).

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1 (employed, income, wages, salary, how people earn) and 2 (sort offers, read and compare prices), on the unit plan, scored on day 92 from revised records only.

**Records that feed them.** Item 1: "My Earning Page", begun on 81 (practice, never scored), finished on 82, collected at the start of 83, returned with written picture feedback on 84 and revised in five supported-work minutes. Item 2: "My Flyer Page", made on 84, collected on 85, revised on 86 (section 8.2). Each page is drawn and labeled with at most a word. Every case below is new, printed "invented for practice", and the teacher says "this is made up". A rotating six are heard each day, about 30 seconds each; the record names who was heard.

**E81.** Cards: Suri mending a roof for a building company, paid by the hour; Bram raking leaves in a friend's yard, unpaid.
- Mark (all): "Circle the person who is employed." Answer: Suri. Wrong: circles Bram, "he works too" (A).
- Spoken (six, one ask chosen by the record sheet): "Why is Suri employed?" or "What is Suri's pay called?" Answer: Suri works and gets paid for it; the pay is wages. Wrong: "Bram is not working" (A).

**E82.** Cards: Mara with clay bowls and a price tag at a market table; Jun, a volunteer, stacking library books.
- Mark (all): "Circle who earns by selling something made." Answer: Mara. Wrong: Jun, because stacking is work (A).
- Spoken (six, one ask): "How does Mara earn?" or "Why does each of a mail carrier, a dentist and a tailor need a different skill?" Answer: Mara earns by selling what she makes; each of the three does a different job with different skills. Wrong: "they all just work" (I).

**E83.** Cards: a hand-poured candle; a grown-up paid to carry boxes up a flight of stairs for a neighbor.
- Mark (all): "Circle the service." Answer: carrying the boxes. Wrong: the candle, or "both, because you pay" (B).
- Spoken (six, one ask): "Why is it a service?" or "Why is the other card a good?" Answer: someone does the carrying for someone else; a candle is a thing you hold. Wrong: "you pay for it, so it is a good" (B).

**E84.** A fresh flyer (the teacher says "pretend shop"): a pencil, 5 dollars, drawn large; a towel, 7 dollars, drawn small.
- Mark (all): "Circle the higher price." Answer: 7, on the towel. Wrong: the pencil, "it is big" (K).
- Spoken (six, one ask): "Why is that the higher price?" or "Which price sits on the pencil?" Answer: 7 is more than 5; the 5 sits on the pencil. Wrong: "the picture is bigger" (K).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.**
  - Kindergarten's work, skill, tool, earn and pay and goods and services (review, same words): used on 81, 82 and 83.
  - The "I think ..., because ..." frame (review): spoken every day; a reason names what a card or picture shows.
  - Pip the puppet (review): says the wrong idea, the class puts it right. Pip never speaks as a child in the room.
  - "My Earning Page" (81 to 84): practice on 81, finished on 82, revised on 84, scored on 92. Withdrawn from the wall after 84.
  - The class store flyer: made on 84, kept for 8.2.
- **Key moment 1, 82: three workers in one chain.** Ideal instrument: three movable picture cards on a strip. Spec: no tool; print fits.
- **Key moment 2, 84: price tags and pictures.** Ideal instrument: a flyer with detachable tags. Spec: no tool; print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Drawn cards, a strip of paper, scrap-paper tags.

## 6. Days

## Day 81


- **5 min. Reopen.** Hook, 1 minute: a picture of Pilar, a sign painter, with 9 tokens, a drum at 7 and a clock at 6 (13 is more than 9; she cannot buy both). Post the compelling question "How should people choose what to buy and what to save?" and say Pilar's problem comes back on day 85. Retrieval, 4 minutes (kindergarten's work): "A grown-up cares for a baby at home; another person builds a fence. Point to both kinds of work. What does each need to know how to do?" If unpaid care is not called work (A), 2 of the 4 minutes contrast paid and unpaid work. Retrieval is review of kindergarten's work, skill and job.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Three picture cards, invented people, said aloud as made up. Pilar paints signs for a shop and is paid 3 tokens an hour; 3 hours is 9 tokens: wages. Noor guides visitors at a museum and is paid the same amount on the same day each month, agreed as a yearly amount: salary (installments). Hal makes hats and sells them at a stall: he earns by selling what he makes. Words with pictures: employed (has a job and is paid), income (money you receive for work), wages, salary. Review: earn and pay. The teacher models "Pilar is employed, because the card shows her paid for her hours." Pip says A and H, then is corrected.
- **13 min. Supported work.** Tables of five, six tables, six worker cards each. 5 min: sort each card to wages, salary or sells what they make, with "I think ... because the card shows ...". 8 min: begin My Earning Page (practice, not scored): a page of three boxes (paid by the hour; paid a salary; sells what they make). Draw box one, a paid-by-the-hour worker with the pay in tokens. The teacher hears the six, 30 seconds each (3 minutes), while the rest draw.
- **2 min. Exit.** E81: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: the teacher holds the three cards on the wall. Tool: None. By hand: cards, tokens, crayons. Evidence: E81.

## Day 82


- **4 min. Reopen.** Hand-back E81 (A), 3 minutes: two new cards, caring at home and a paid cleaner, "work? paid?" If many missed, the 3 minutes are the repair and the page paper is out at once. 1 minute: post "How do people earn?" and show Pilar, Noor and Hal.
- **9 min. Model the chain.** Bread, invented. Baker bakes loaves with an oven and is paid wages. A carrier takes loaves on a hand cart to the stall and is paid wages. A seller at the stall is paid a salary each month. Each needs a different skill and tool, so each does one part well: they specialize. An unpaid cook at home also feeds people; that work counts, with no pay. Pip says I and J, then is corrected. The teacher names, for each worker, the skill, the tool and how they earn.
- **15 min. Supported work.** Tables of five. 7 min: a new chain on three cards (a jam-making chain: someone who grows fruit and sells it, a jam maker, a shop seller); each table sets the cards in order and names skill, tool and how each earns. 8 min: finish My Earning Page (scored first attempt): boxes two and three, a salary worker and one who sells what they make, copying the words wages, salary and income onto the right boxes from the board. The six are heard, 3 minutes inside the 8.
- **2 min. Exit.** E82: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: the teacher holds the bread chain strip. Tool: None. By hand: chain cards, pages. Evidence: E82; the page is item 1's record, collected on 83.

## Day 83


- **5 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: collect My Earning Page. 2 minutes: retrieval, kindergarten's goods and services: "Sort a pear and a haircut. What does the buyer receive?" 2 minutes: hand-back E82 (I, J), one new three-worker chain or three pay cards. If many missed (I or J), the 2 minutes are the repair and retrieval takes 1.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** Goods: a loaf, a hat, a wooden spoon. Services: a haircut, mending a shoe, a train ride. Words: goods and services (review), buyer and seller (review, with pictures), and maker and buyer, with a picture of each. The thing a service uses is still a good (the scissors). Pip says B. "I think the ride is a service, because the picture shows someone carrying the rider."
- **14 min. Supported work.** Tables of five, twelve cards (six goods, six services), two hoops. 6 min: each child sorts cards and says "I think ... because the picture shows ...". 5 min: each child draws one service they could do for a classmate (hold the door, help carry) and one good they could make from paper; no one is asked about home. 3 min: the six are heard, while the others finish drawing.
- **2 min. Exit.** E83: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: children hold the hoops. Tool: None. By hand: cards, hoops, paper. Evidence: E83.

## Day 84


- **4 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: return My Earning Page with written picture feedback. 3 minutes: hand-back E83 (B): an object card beside an action card, on new pairs. If many missed, the 3 minutes are the repair.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** The class store flyer, made this week by an invented class committee: a ruler 1 dollar, a crayon box 2 dollars, a glue stick 3 dollars, a paper pad 4 dollars, and one service, a paper-folding show, 4 dollars. The teacher reads each price tag with its picture. Source questions: who made it (the class committee), when (this week), what for (to show the store's prices); it is invented, so it cannot tell the price of anything in a real shop. A second flyer, made last year with different prices: which would you use today, and why? Pip says K and L.
- **15 min. Supported work.** 5 min: revise My Earning Page (return of feedback, one change or addition each; revised version scored later). 7 min: My Flyer Page on a second flyer (a stamp 8 dollars, a notebook 11 dollars, a clay lesson 9 dollars): sort each offer as good or service, point to prices, mark the higher price. The six are heard, 3 minutes inside the 7. 3 min: partner talk "Which costs more? How do you know?"
- **2 min. Exit.** E84: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: the teacher holds the first flyer. Tool: None. By hand: flyers, pages, crayons. Evidence: E84; the Flyer Page is item 2's record, collected on 85.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: 81, "Is it work? Is it paid? How do you know?"; 82, "How does each worker earn? What skill and tool does each need?"; 83, "Is it a thing you hold or something someone does? Who is the buyer?"; 84, "Which costs more? Who made this flyer and when?"
- Written task, 82: My Earning Page, three boxes drawn, with word labels copied from the board (wages, salary, income), revised on 84.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: a worker in each box with pay shown (tokens); wages, salary and income each on the right box, or pointed to; the two employed boxes told apart from the seller by an employer who pays; a different skill or tool per worker where a chain is drawn. Accept pointing, signing or dictation.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 81.** Kindergarten's work, skill and job. **Day 82.** Wages, salary and selling from 81; skill and tool. **Day 83.** Earn and pay; goods and services from kindergarten. **Day 84.** Goods and services from 83; the "I think ..., because ..." frame; comparing numbers within 20.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: employed, income, wages, salary, good, service, price. Frames: "___ is paid by the hour, so it is wages." "I think it is a service, because ___." "The ___ costs more, because ___ is more than ___."
- **Access.** Large cards, raised hoops, an adult placing cards as the child points, tactile tokens, dictated labels.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every stem aloud; accept pointing, drawing, signing or a private turn; an unheard child stays "not yet seen".
- **Reading load.** None. Price tags are numbers; the teacher reads every card aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Draw a three-worker chain for a different good and say how each earns.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the same cards and a paper flyer. A grown-up reads each card and asks the exit questions aloud after each day. Use made-up people, never the home's work or money. At home, the grown-up plays a seller with the flyer and the child reads the price tags aloud; any day is skippable.
- **One projector.** Show one card or the flyer at a time; children mark paper before the teacher says the answer.
