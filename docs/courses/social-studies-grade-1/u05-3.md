# Section 5.3: Making a map, answering with a reason, and the check

## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T2.4, days 52 and 54.** "Locate and explain" physical features and "make maps of local places": each child makes a map of a supplied local place with its land and water (day 52, practice; scored only after written feedback and revision on day 53); day 54 locates a continent, an ocean, a river, a lake and a mountain on a map not seen before.
- **MA.1.T2.1, days 52, 54 and 55.** "Explain" that a map represents space and helps people find locations and features: the key and the four directions on the child's own map; on day 54 what a new map stands for.
- **MA.1.T2.2, days 53, 54 and 55.** "Use" the cardinal directions, "locate" major cities and capitals and "investigate why they became important": a closing reason from a map and an account (day 53); directions and cities on day 54; a fresh try on day 55.
- **D2.Geo.3.K-2, days 53 and 54.** "Use" maps, globes and simple models to identify cultural and environmental features of places (the Alder map; a new paper relief on day 54).
- **D4.1.K-2, day 53.** "Make" an argument and "give reasons" for it: review (the unit plan marks it review), spoken and drawn, with a written word label at most.
- **MA.1.T2.3, day 54.** "Explain" a capital.
- **Limits that bite here.** No scale. The local map is judged for where things are, land and water, symbols and key, and directions, never art. Draw only what the supplied photographs show; no pond added. No child's home or street. No single reason explains every city. The Reed packet and the Alder card are never taught. The day 52 map is scored only after written feedback and revision.
- **Connections.** Claim and reason, day 53 (ss1-claim-reasons; review of kindergarten's claim frame and unit 4's blank frame). Day 52's saved map returns on day 94 (unit plan field 3).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to give written feedback on five things (positions, land and water, symbols, key, directions) in about a minute a map. How to hear a child's spoken part and record it by the course scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. That the unit plan's "partly shown" and "insufficient evidence" become that scale here. That a wrong place for a symbol is a spatial error, not a drawing one.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** unit plan field 5 names 5.1 as Owner of items 1 to 6; this section sits the check on day 54 and teaches items 5 (the made map) and 6 (the claim) last. Items 1 to 4 use the withheld packet: maps A, B and C, unseen and never taught (Reed is an invented region; the United States locator is real).

**Exit questions.** Marks as before; the rotating six are heard on the same card, one spoken ask. All cards are new and printed "invented for practice". Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- **E52.** New observation card: a pond below a wooded rise, a path between them, a north arrow. Mark: draw the symbol for the pond in the key box and circle where it goes on the card. Spoken: "How could a visitor use your key?" Answer: the key says what each symbol means, so a visitor finds the pond below the rise by the symbol. Wrong: a symbol with no meaning in the key, or a different meaning each time (O); a pond symbol placed where the card shows none (P).
- **E53.** New Alder map and record: the map shows a council building and nearby offices; the record says "workers opened offices after the state government moved to Alder". Mark: circle the reason the record supports, government work, fishing or farming. Spoken: "Why did people open offices in Alder?" Answer: the state government moved there; the map shows the council building and the offices. Wrong: "because Alder grew" (T); "cities grow because of the government" (U).
- **E54.** Items 1 to 6 of the unit assessment are today's evidence: see the days field.
- **E55.** New Larch village map, north pointing down; triangle is the clinic, oval is the well; the record says "People crossed the footbridge to reach the new clinic." Mark: place a token on the clinic that is east of the well. Spoken: "Why does the footbridge matter?" Answer: it gives people access to care at the clinic. Wrong: the clinic on the page-right of the well, because right feels like east (C); "it matters because it matters" (K).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Each new map keeps its key and direction indicator visible (unit plan). The day 52 map is returned with written feedback, revised on day 53 and saved for day 94. The question board, source routine and blank claim frames stay up through day 53; on day 54 completed teaching models are covered and only blank frames stay. Unit 4's routines continue.
- **Key moment 1, day 52: a map a stranger could use.** Ideal instrument: a page with a border, a key box and a printed blank indicator beside the supplied park photographs. Spec: no tool; print fits. If the local place has no water, use the supplied photograph set of a small park with a pond, labelled as another place (never an invented pond).
- **Key moment 2, day 54: six items, with reading help but no answers.** Ideal instrument: the withheld packet, tokens and a record sheet with a column naming who was heard. Spec: no tool; print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Plain paper folded into a key box, crayons, a hand-drawn indicator; for day 54, three hand-drawn maps as in the unit plan.

## 6. Days

## Day 52


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E51 (E, J). The supplied park photographs are shown; the class names its water and land aloud; no home, no one's street.
- **5 min. Step back.** The teacher shows a teacher-made map of a different place with a key and no indicator; children say what is missing (key? indicator? water?). Four things a good map has, said once: where things are, land and water, symbols with a key, the four directions.
- **18 min. Supported work.** Each child makes a map of the supplied park (documented, with its pond or water) on a printed page with a border, a key box and a blank indicator: land, water, at least two places, symbols, key and north. Save as practice. Hear the rotating six on "What place did you map?" about two minutes each while the others work.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E52.

That evening the teacher writes feedback on positions, land and water, symbols, key and directions: about a minute a map, thirty maps, thirty minutes (ours, untested); two or three days of rotation is acceptable. Holds: children hold their maps. Tool: None. By hand: photographs, page, crayons. Evidence: E52; the practice map; record sheet.

## Day 53


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E52 (O, P) and each map with its feedback card.
- **5 min. Revise the map.** Each child revises the day 52 map from the feedback; the revised map is scored on day 54 (item 5).
- **5 min. Step back.** The teacher models one closing with the Harbor Map, the Lenape account and the capital account: claim, map detail, account detail, reason, closing. No single reason explains every city: "That is one reason a town can grow." The claim frame stays blank; a completed example is covered afterward.
- **13 min. Supported work.** Each child answers the compelling question, "Why did people build towns and cities where they did?", by drawing and choosing word labels from a word bank, or by dictation; pairs tell the partner first. An adult scribes the rotating six, about two minutes each; others draw and label. The main closing claim is practice.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E53.

Holds: children hold their claims. Tool: None. By hand: maps, word bank, claim frame, Harbor Map. Evidence: E53; the claim (practice); record sheet.

## Day 54


- **5 min. Directions and sources, read aloud.** The teacher reads the packet's prompts; every source is read aloud; Reed is said to be invented; the United States locator is real.
- **4 min. Item 1.** Paper: circle Reed and the bridge using the key (map C). Spoken, heard group by group while all draw: "What does C stand for?"
- **5 min. Item 2.** Paper: circle a continent, an ocean, the river, the lake and the mountain (maps A and C), and look at a new paper relief model. Spoken, one ask by the sheet.
- **4 min. Item 3.** Paper: place tokens for the three directions and circle the three cities on B.
- **3 min. Item 4.** Paper: circle the stars that are capitals (B). Spoken, one ask.
- **3 min. Item 5.** The revised day 52 map, handed in.
- **6 min. Item 6.** Draw or dictate the claim, a reason and closing from map C and its record.

The five groups of six are heard in turn across the paper parts, about five minutes a group, one or two spoken asks assigned by the record sheet; a second adult hears a second group. A child not heard is heard on day 55. Mark every part shown, shown with help or not yet seen (not "partly shown", not "insufficient evidence"). All look-fors in a row are needed for shown; judge spatial meaning, not drawing skill. Holds: nothing; cover completed examples. Tool: None. Evidence: items 1 to 6 on the record sheet.

## Day 55


- **5 min. Return evidence.** Each child gets their own work and the record; marking took about fifteen teacher minutes after day 54 (unit plan).
- **10 min. Reteach by item.** At most four groups from the record sheet's not yet seen and shown with help cells, about two minutes each while others work in pairs from the written repair card; the shared error first when many missed it: item 1 repairs A, item 2 D, item 3 C, item 4 G, item 5 O, item 6 T.
- **10 min. Fresh try.** New cards of the same type (new places named by the teacher, never Reed, Larch or Alder): each child tries the part missed. The rotating six who were not heard on day 54 are heard now, one minute each, one ask.
- **3 min. Reflect.** Each child compares first and fresh work and names one improvement; a child may point.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E55.

Holds: the teacher holds the repair cards. Tool: None. By hand: cards, repair cards, record sheet. Evidence: E55; fresh tries; the record sheet.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 52, "How could a visitor use your key?"; day 53, "What does the map show?" then separately "What does the account say?" and "How did it help?"; day 55, "What changed between your first try and this one?"
- Written task, day 53: a claim, a reason and a closing about why people built towns and cities where they did, from drawings and word labels, or dictated.
- Judged with five look-fors: the claim answers the question; a map detail is named; an account detail is named; the reason links them with "this helped because"; the closing says one reason, not all reasons.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: key, symbol, north, south, east, west, capital, harbor, river, lake, mountain, because. Frames: "The map shows ___. The account says ___. This helped because ___." "That is one reason a town can grow." Rehearse in the familiar language first; accept a drawing and a word.
- **Access.** Raised lines and textured outlines, spoken descriptions of each map, large print, an adapted marking tool; a child who cannot draw directs an adult to place pieces. Content is scored apart from handwriting and reading.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all prompts and sources aloud; accept a point, token, drawing, dictation, familiar-language speech or supported communication, without naming the answer or interpreting a direction. Separate limited English from a location error.
- **Reading load.** None alone; labels are one word from a word bank; all sources are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend two causes from the same sources, or add a second reason to the claim and say what the sources do not tell.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 52: make a map of the park photographs on paper with a key and four directions. Day 53: tell why a town grew in one sentence, with the map, then the record. Day 54: use three maps made by a grown-up, unseen; hear the child. Day 55: pick the missed part and try again on a new map. The last day: the child compares first and fresh work and names one improvement, with the grown-up.
- **One projector.** Show a map and its key together; pairs use paper copies and trace with a finger. On day 54, show nothing; the packet is on paper and read aloud.
