# Course frame: Grade 1: Leaders, Helpers and Communities

Course id ss-k5, grade 1. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 8, following Massachusetts 2018 grade 1 (groups, leaders, elections and citizens; places; unity and
  diversity; resources and choices), with civics first because the class's group, its leaders and its vote are
  what the rest of the year leans on.
- **Days.** 96: 91 in units, 3 for course-level checks (1 after unit 4, 2 after unit 8) and 2 respond days
  (after the mid-year check). Social studies shares the grade 1 day with reading and math, so the course assumes
  three lessons a week of about 30 minutes (about 90 minutes a week) across 32 teaching weeks, not the full-year
  yardstick of 160. Unit days follow the rows' load: the leaders, symbols and action-cycle rows carry more; the
  economics rows, which grow kindergarten's ideas, carry less each.
- **Standard.** Content: Massachusetts 2018 grade 1, MA.1.T1 to MA.1.T4, every standard owned by a row and placed
  below, with one planned split: Massachusetts prints grade 1 T4 and grade 2 T5 as one economics topic for either
  grade, and Curio Class teaches it once (data/progressions/social-studies-excluded.csv). Grade 1 teaches
  employment, goods and services, choices and saving (MA.1.T4.4 to MA.1.T4.9); grade 2 teaches resources
  (MA.2.T5.1 to MA.2.T5.3), so MA.1.T4.1 to MA.1.T4.3 are excluded here with that reason. The lettered parts of
  MA.1.T3.2 are taught in full in ss1-national-symbols. Skills: C3 K-2 indicators as owned by the rows. Check
  set: California, Tennessee and New York compared in each unit plan, additions recorded with their source; the
  Units table's "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) in
  order: higher court, backed sources, dignity and openness, grade and life experience.
- **Base text.** None (pre-work P2). Every day is Curio Class's own. Sources: open and public-domain photographs,
  maps, short letters and records (Library of Congress, National Archives, National Park Service, US Census
  Bureau town data, public-domain images), adapted with a credit line, and Curio Class's own read-alouds and short
  biographies. No day needs a trade book or a library visit.
- **Reading level.** Grade 1 students are early readers. Sources are read aloud with the class, or are
  photographs, objects and maps; any text a student reads alone is a short captioned page at grade 1 level.
  Every source gets its first sourcing talk (what kind of source is it, is it old or new).
- **The inquiry arc.** Every unit opens with a compelling question and, from unit 1, the supporting questions the
  class breaks it into (ss1-supporting-questions), each tied to the facts and words needed to answer it. Units 1
  and 2 close with the spoken claim and reason from kindergarten; from unit 3 the class writes the claim, reason
  and closing (ss1-claim-reasons).
- **Informed action.** Once in the year, unit 7 (ss1-community-problems): the class names a school or local
  problem, finds out how people already work on it, hears more than one view, chooses one way to help by a
  secret-ballot class election (unit 4's skill), carries it out through the school and looks back. The class
  chooses; the course never picks the cause.
- **Own-state method.** Where the anchor names Massachusetts (the governor, the state capital), the course
  teaches the student's own state, worked through two or three example states.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 4 (1 day, mid-year).** A short check of skills on content: break a new question into two
  supporting questions; read a photograph not seen before and say what kind of source it is and one thing it shows
  about the past; write or dictate a claim with a reason about a leader studied; explain why a secret ballot is
  fair.
- **2 respond days after the mid-year check.** Re-teach in small groups what the check shows, most often the
  written claim and the kinds of sources.
- **After unit 8 (2 days, end of year).** Day one: read a simple map not seen before with its key and directions,
  and find a continent and an ocean. Day two: from a short read-aloud source not seen before, give a claim with a
  piece of evidence about how Americans are united or diverse, and explain one choice with its cost. The record
  goes on to the grade 2 teacher.
