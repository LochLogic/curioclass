# Unit 1: Our groups. *What makes a group work well?*

Course `social-studies-grade-1` (Grade 1: Leaders, Helpers and Communities). **Days 1 to 10** (10 course days; school days 2 to 17, weeks 1 to 4). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 2); 1.2 (days 3 to 5); 1.3 (days 6 to 10). Days by kind: understanding 6, application 2, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

This course does not assign major, supporting or additional weights.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 1.** Show original picture panels of children making a paper mural: in one, everyone reaches for the same paper; in the other, members share tasks. Ask the compelling question before retrieving an "I wonder" question. Both panels are invented, not records of real children.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write on day 1: What does belonging give members (answered day 3)? Why do groups need rules and duties (day 5)? How does each role help a shared goal (day 8)? On day 2, connect each to civics and list needed facts and words. Contrast history, geography and economics questions using the mural's past, location and limited supplies.
- **Closing, day 9.** Each child speaks a claim and reason from the new park photograph and caption and our class task record. The teacher records it beside the child's drawing. Fair rules, shared responsibility and helpful leadership are defensible emphases, supported by a detail.
- **Teaching.** Model a supporting question on day 1 and a source-based "I think ... because ..." on day 4. Identify source kind, maker, old or new, and usefulness before each reading. Compare day 7's volunteer testimony with its photograph: one tells motives, the other shows people. Step back during day 8's task. Teach group, member, belong, rule, duty, role, goal and community through pictures and actions.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

**Days 8 and 9, observation assessment; response day 10.** Day 8 uses five minutes to agree on accessible roles and a rule, twenty minutes for a classroom materials tidy-up, and five minutes to explain and draw. Within the twenty, use fifteen for practice, two for written picture feedback on each child's rule and duty, and three to retry the duty. Score item 3 from that revised performance, not the initial practice. Day 9 uses five minutes for source orientation and twenty-five for each item's paper part and the spoken groups. Every child does each item's paper part. The spoken parts are heard group by group, six children at a time, while the others draw; a child not heard is heard from the rotation on the respond day's fresh try, never by a whole-class wait. About three minutes a group, five groups. Paper parts: item 1, circle the subject picture (history, geography, civics, economics); item 2, circle the more useful of two question cards and draw the fact it needs; item 3 is the observed duty; item 4, circle the member picture and draw a rule; item 5, match each person in the photograph and the invented case to a task card; item 6, draw the claim with its detail. Item 6's spoken claim is heard inside the groups. Do not require thirty separate interviews. Assess understanding, not handwriting, compliance speed or physical strength.
