Course `social-studies-grade-1` (Grade 1: Leaders, Helpers and Communities). **Days 1 to 10** (10 course days; school days 2 to 17, weeks 1 to 4). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 2); 1.2 (days 3 to 5); 1.3 (days 6 to 10). Days by kind: understanding 6, application 2, check 1, respond 1. ## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 1.** Show original picture panels of children making a paper mural: in one, everyone reaches for the same paper; in the other, members share tasks. Ask the compelling question before retrieving an "I wonder" question. Both panels are invented, not records of real children.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write on day 1: What does belonging give members (answered day 3)? Why do groups need rules and duties (day 5)? How does each role help a shared goal (day 8)? On day 2, connect each to civics and list needed facts and words. Contrast history, geography and economics questions using the mural's past, location and limited supplies.
- **Closing, day 9.** Each child speaks a claim and reason from the new park photograph and caption and our class task record. The teacher records it beside the child's drawing. Fair rules, shared responsibility and helpful leadership are defensible emphases, supported by a detail.
- **Teaching.** Model a supporting question on day 1 and one claim from a picture, "I think ... because ...", on day 4, which prepares item 6. Day 7 opens with original pictures of firefighters and a librarian with a helper, then the volunteer account (motives, not pictures). Source kind and maker wait for unit 2. Step back during day 8's task. Teach group, member, belong, rule, duty, role, goal and community through pictures and actions.


The local C3 file contains identifiers only. These paraphrases use the publisher's indicators and table headings, PDF pages 24 to 25 and 32:

- **D1.2.K-2.** Identify subject ideas within the big question. Parts: identify relevant ideas, including rules, roles and shared goals.
- **D1.3.K-2.** Identify facts and concepts associated with a supporting question. Parts: identify facts; identify concepts. Writing the supporting questions is the course's own row (ss1-supporting-questions).
- **D2.Civ.2.K-2.** Explain the contributions of community members, including people without official leadership positions. Parts: explain leaders' contributions; explain other members' contributions.
- **D2.Civ.6.K-2.** Describe communities doing shared work, assigning responsibilities and exercising authority. Parts: describe common tasks; describe assigned responsibilities; describe authority in action.
- **Anchor parts.** MA.1.T1.1: explain belonging and benefits; follow rules and duties; explain rules' reasons. MA.1.T1.2: investigate roles; explain each role's contribution. All parts have items below.


- **Limits.** No family disclosure, family ranking or assumption that every group is welcoming. Fictional examples include one parent, two parents including same-gender parents, grandparents, foster and adoptive families. Roles are not assigned by gender or ability. Elections and formal government wait for unit 4; the year's informed action stays in unit 7.
- **Carried.** No formal model is added. Keep the wonder wall and spoken claim routine from kindergarten. Tie supporting questions to facts and words on day 2; return on days 5 and 8. Withdraw completed examples on day 9, retaining accessibility supports. Carry the question board forward.

## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

**Days 8 and 9, observation assessment; response day 10.** Day 8 uses five minutes to agree on accessible roles and a rule, twenty minutes for a classroom materials tidy-up, and five minutes to explain and draw. Within the twenty, use ten for practice, seven for written picture feedback on each child's rule and duty (a stamp or drawn mark, about fifteen seconds a child), and three to retry the duty. The teacher records the retry and the child's explain-and-draw on the wall chart, and scores item 3 on day 9 from that record, a performance made on day 8 and scored on a later day; the initial practice is never scored. Day 9 uses five minutes for source orientation and twenty-five for the paper parts and the spoken groups. Every child does the paper part of items 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6, about three minutes each (fifteen minutes). The only spoken part on day 9 is the item 6 claim, heard group by group, six children at a time, about two minutes a group, five groups (ten minutes), while the others finish their paper parts; a child not heard is heard from the rotation on the respond day's fresh try, never by a whole-class wait. Paper parts: item 1, circle the subject picture (history, geography, civics, economics); item 2, circle the more useful of two question cards and draw the fact it needs; item 3 is scored from the day 8 record; item 4, circle the member picture and draw a rule; item 5, match each person in the photograph and the invented case to a task card; item 6, draw the claim with its detail. Do not require thirty separate interviews. Assess understanding, not handwriting, compliance speed or physical strength.
