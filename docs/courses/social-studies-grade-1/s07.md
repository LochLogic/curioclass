## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 69.** Meet invented picture cards of neighbors helping at a library, including a citizen by birth, a naturalized citizen and a resident who is not a citizen. Ask the compelling question: what could our class contribute?
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write: What needs attention (70)? Who already helps (71)? Why do views differ (72)? Which action can we take (73 and 74)? What makes helping responsible (75 and 76)? Where else does this problem occur (77)? What changed (78)?
- **Closing, day 78.** Write or dictate a claim, reason and closing using the dated school observation record, staff account and action log. Explain what changed and remained; limited or no improvement is an acceptable conclusion.
- **Teaching.** Model noticing versus guessing on 70 and matching evidence to a claim on 75. Step back for choosing and evaluating. Identify source kind, maker, date and usefulness; compare accounts. Teach citizen, resident, right, responsibility, problem, perspective, action and evidence with pictures.

- **Limits.** Never infer status from names, language or appearance. Citizenship is a legal relationship, not a reward for helping; belonging does not depend on citizenship. Two routes are not exhaustive: birth abroad does not prove noncitizenship. No status disclosures, immigration simulations, fundraising or off-campus action. If a child volunteers family information, do not question or record status; affirm belonging and return to the fictional cases. ## 5. End-of-unit assessment, whole

**Observation assessment:** day 79 (`assess = observe`), with days 77 and 78 used for practice and revision; response on day 80.

**Assessment, section 7.1.** All items are teacher judged. Day 77 comparisons are first-encounter practice, never scored. Day 75 quality judgments are first-encounter practice, never scored. During five minutes of day 76 supported work, collect judgments from fresh fiction, a verified school bulletin and a classroom-experience record. Give written feedback on each detail and linked quality; revise during five minutes of day 78 writing before they count for item 2. Day 78 local writing receives written feedback on evidence, reason and closing, with five minutes to revise within its writing time before scoring. Item 4 uses the fresh day 79 packet. Day 79 uses five minutes reading the fresh packet and twenty-five for the paper parts and the spoken groups. Every child does each item's paper part. The spoken parts are heard group by group, six children at a time, while the others draw; a child not heard is heard from the rotation on the respond day's fresh try, never by a whole-class wait. About three minutes a group, five groups. Paper parts: item 1, match Nola, Paz and Ren to the birth and later routes and circle whether Ren belongs; item 2, circle the action that shows a quality; item 3, place the wagon and motor-vehicle pictures in order; item 4, circle each problem on a near, region and far strip; item 5, draw one safe child-sized action; item 6, draw or dictate the claim. Unrevised spoken observations on days 77 and 78 remain practice. Do not schedule thirty consecutive conferences.

**Fresh packet, held out from teaching.** Source A is a new original read-aloud based on Kathryn Miller's 2024 library history, field 10: "In Washington County, Maryland, farms were spread far apart. In 1905, Mary Titcomb planned a book wagon. Joshua Thomas drove it to families. They could borrow books near home. Later the library used a motor vehicle to carry books. Source B is the park agency's own volunteer invitation, a primary source about its plans: "At Pictured Rocks in Michigan, people can volunteer to remove litter. "Across a region, litter washes from several towns into one river. Town workers arrange collections." "At a school in another country, books are too far away for many children. ## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** Print source cards, maps, dated observation sheets, ballots, comparison charts and claim frames. Use paper, pencils and existing supplies; one shared screen is optional. No student timers.

**Action.** On 70 choose a real school problem; on 71 use an existing staff note or observation instead of requiring a visitor. On 72 hear two firsthand views. On 73 vote between feasible proposals; agree a runoff for ties and retain the unchosen proposal for day 95. On 74 act under adult supervision. Keep brief routines inside existing time through 78. No unknown waste, roads, water or sharp objects; stop and tell an adult. Offer seated recording and drawing roles. At home, choose a shared-space problem with an adult, compare views and act locally.

**Access.** Read aloud; offer familiar-language rehearsal, large print, pictures and dictation. Extend by defending an alternative action with evidence.
