## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 69.** Meet invented picture cards of neighbors helping at a library, including a citizen by birth, a naturalized citizen and a resident who is not a citizen. Ask the compelling question: what could our class contribute?
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write: What needs attention (70)? Who already helps (71)? Why do views differ (72)? Which action can we take (73 and 74)? What makes helping responsible (75 and 76)? Where else does this problem occur (77)? What changed (78)?
- **Closing, day 78.** Write or dictate a claim, reason and closing using the dated school observation record, staff account and action log. Explain what changed and remained; limited or no improvement is an acceptable conclusion.
- **Teaching.** Model noticing versus guessing on 70 and matching evidence to a claim on 75. Step back for choosing and evaluating. Identify source kind, maker, date and usefulness; compare accounts. Teach citizen, resident, right, responsibility, problem, perspective, action and evidence with pictures.

**Fresh packet, held out from teaching.** Source A is a new original read-aloud based on Kathryn Miller's 2024 library history, field 10: "In Washington County, Maryland, farms were spread far apart. In 1905, Mary Titcomb planned a book wagon. Joshua Thomas drove it to families. They could borrow books near home. Later the library used a motor vehicle to carry books. Libraries still bring books to neighborhoods today." This is a secondary historical account. Source B is the park agency's own volunteer invitation, a primary source about its plans: "At Pictured Rocks in Michigan, people can volunteer to remove litter. They choose a park site and collect supplies at the visitor center. Groups can help clean a beach or trail." The invitation does not prove the cleanup happened.

Three additional comparison cards are explicitly invented assessment cases, not reports about real places: "In a nearby town, wrappers blow across a playground. Staff add a covered bin." "Across a region, litter washes from several towns into one river. Town workers arrange collections." "At a school in another country, books are too far away for many children. A traveling library brings books." They test transfer; day 77's actual sources provide real-world evidence.

E, day 78: compare "we fixed everything" with dated evidence showing change and remaining need. Use the next teaching day's response rule above.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** Print source cards, maps, dated observation sheets, ballots, comparison charts and claim frames. Use paper, pencils and existing supplies; one shared screen is optional. No student timers.

**Action.** On 70 choose a real school problem; on 71 use an existing staff note or observation instead of requiring a visitor. On 72 hear two firsthand views. On 73 vote between feasible proposals; agree a runoff for ties and retain the unchosen proposal for day 95. On 74 act under adult supervision. Keep brief routines inside existing time through 78. No unknown waste, roads, water or sharp objects; stop and tell an adult. Offer seated recording and drawing roles. At home, choose a shared-space problem with an adult, compare views and act locally.

**Access.** Read aloud; offer familiar-language rehearsal, large print, pictures and dictation. Extend by defending an alternative action with evidence.

## 9. Family note

We will investigate a school problem, hear different views and choose a safe way to help. Children may draw or dictate, and nobody will be asked about family citizenship or immigration status.
