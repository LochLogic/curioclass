## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T1.3, this section's share (day 20).** Children "explain that a leader is also a group member who takes on extra responsibility, such as inspiring others, organizing tasks, and guiding decisions". The whole code is taught here, through three actions the children can see and say: encourage (inspiring), organize and guide. Spoken, pointed or sorted answers count.
- **D2.Civ.1.K-2, this section's share (day 20).** "Describe" what people in charge are responsible for: both parts, roles and responsibilities, in two daily-life leaders (a principal, a crossing guard).
- **D2.His.3.K-2, this section's share (day 21).** "Ask questions" about people and groups behind a change: a question about the leader, a question about the group, and what changed. Real historical leaders begin on day 22.
- **MA.1.T1.4, this section's share (day 21).** "Analyze examples of leaders from history, daily life, and texts": here only the first step, what a leader did, from an invented picture record. Qualities begin on day 22.
- **Limits that bite here.** Every case on these days is invented, labeled "made up", and carries one acting person in each picture so a child can say who did what. No child reports on home, family or who is in charge there. The shouting captain is a made-up contrast, never a real group; no one acts it out. A leader is not a boss over the group (A), a badge is not a responsibility (F), and a duty to the group's job is not an order to obey anything (G). Facts about real jobs in general (what principals or crossing guards do everywhere) are not claimed: only what the invented card shows. Source to check, if a general line is wanted: a free school-safety page, added at the data stage.
- **Connections.** Kindergarten's characters (day 20, retrieval: fair, caring, in charge), kindergarten's "I wonder" questions and the wonder wall (day 21), unit 1's groups (day 20; no unit 1 ledger row was available when this was written).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That a leader is a member first: the leader works inside the group and also takes on more. The three verbs in children's words: encourage (help others feel they can), organize (sort out who does what), guide (show the way or help the group choose). That "in charge" from kindergarten means it is someone's job to decide for the group. How to ask "What did she do? What shows that?" with the faces covered, as in kindergarten.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1 (given on day 30, section 3.3; the items are on the unit plan).

**Retrieval record, day 20.** Three marks beside the child's name: judged the character, asked a question, gave a reason ("I think ... because ..."), each shown, shown with help or not yet seen.

**Exit questions.** Each is a mark by every child, then one spoken ask heard from the rotating six. Cases are "invented for practice" and never return in teaching. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's repair.
- **E20.** Mark: two cards. Mina sorts books beside the team of helpers; Dev stands apart wearing a badge. Circle the one who is a member of the team and a leader. Spoken: "Why is she a member and a leader?" Answer: Mina; she works with the team (member) and takes the responsibility of organizing the books (leader). Wrong: circles Dev because of the badge (F); "She is the leader, so she is not part of the team" (A).
- **E21.** Mark: a new invented record, neighbors and a leader mend a broken town clock. Circle the question that asks about the leader's actions: "How did the leader organize the repairs?" or "Was the clock pretty?" Spoken: "Ask one question about what the leader did." Answer: "How did she organize the repairs?" or any question about an action. Wrong: asks only whether the room or clock was pretty or what is liked (E).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The leader as a member (day 20) returns on day 26 (what responsibility a leader takes) and in item 1 on day 30. The three action words encourage, organize and guide stay on a card strip on the wall, with their pictures, through day 31. The question board, begun in unit 1, takes the three supporting questions co-written on day 21, and children add facts and words beside them on days 22 to 26. Kindergarten's wonder wall and its question routine run unchanged on day 21.
- **Key moment 1, day 20: the leader inside the group.** Must be seen: a child points to the same person as a member and as the one doing the extra job. Ideal instrument: picture cards a child can hold, with a team ring to lay the leader card in. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 21: a question about an action.** Must be seen: a child asks what someone did, not what was liked. Ideal instrument: the posted board and a question-mark card. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Stick figures on scrap paper, told aloud; a circle drawn on the floor for the group ring; the question board on a sheet.

## 6. Days

## Day 20


- **4 min. Hook.** Two made-up pictures, said to be made up. Scene A: Ms. Reyes, a principal, listens to the helpers and says who will carry what. Scene B: Captain Bo, shouting orders while others do all the work. Post the compelling question: "What makes a good leader?" Ask only "What do you notice?" and do not judge yet.
- **5 min. Retrieval.** An invented picture story: a play leader notices someone has no part, asks what they want to do, and finds a part with them. "Who is in charge? What shows care? What do you wonder? Say 'I think ... because ...'." Record the three marks apart (field 3).
- **3 min. Words with pictures.** leader, member, responsibility (a job you take on that others count on; review of "responsible"). In charge, from kindergarten, is named as review.
- **6 min. Worked example.** Mr. Tran, a crossing guard on a made-up picture card, is on the school crossing team: a member. He also takes extra responsibility: guides children across, encourages the shy ones, organizes the line. Back to Ms. Reyes: a member of the school team who organizes. The teacher names encourage, organize and guide with action pictures.
- **8 min. Sort, and hearing the six.** Twelve pairs sort six action cards (a captain cheers a tired player; a helper hands out picnic jobs; a hiker holds up a map and shows the way; and three more) under encourage, organize, guide, with a team ring for the leader card. The day's six are called one at a time to the teacher's table, one minute each, to hear the spoken ask on the exit card; the rest of the time they sort with a partner.
- **4 min. Exit.** E20 mark by every child (3 minutes); one line on the board of "I wonder" questions.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the pictures and the question; pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: hook pictures, two daily-life cards, six action cards, a team ring, word pictures. Evidence: the retrieval record, E20.

## Day 21


- **5 min. Reopen and repair.** Day 20's miss (A or F) with a different pair of cards, five minutes at most; whole class if more than a third missed, otherwise a small group at the board. Recheck the "I wonder" questions from the retrieval record.
- **3 min. Words with pictures.** "Group" and "change" (review); the three question openers who, what, how. Say that a question asks what someone did, not what we like.
- **7 min. Model questioning.** The Muddy Path, an invented picture record (to make): Ms. Okoye and her neighbors lay stones over a muddy path. Model three questions, each from a picture: "What did the leader do? What did the group do? What changed?" Wrong question beside right: "Was the path pretty?"
- **4 min. Co-write the supporting questions.** On the board: What responsibility does a leader take? What did each historical leader and their group change? Which actions show good leadership? Children point to a picture that could help each one, and the teacher lists words and facts needed beside it.
- **7 min. Pairs, and hearing the six.** Each pair gets a new picture record and asks one question about the leader and one about the group, then draws one of them (a word label at most). The day's six are heard one minute each at the teacher's table with the exit card.
- **4 min. Exit.** E21 mark by every child.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the model and the board; pairs hold the picture record. Tool: None. By hand: The Muddy Path cards, the board, pair record cards. Evidence: E21.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 20, "Who is in charge? What shows care?" and "What does a leader do for the group?"; day 21, "What did the leader do? What did the group do? What changed?"
- Written task, day 21: each pair draws one of its questions beside the leader or the group and may write one word label; the teacher writes the question in words.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: asks what someone did, not what is liked; says whether it is about the leader or the group; the question can be answered from the picture or the story.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 20: kindergarten's characters (fair, caring, in charge) and the "I think ... because ..." frame.
- Day 21: day 20's words with the action cards; kindergarten's "I wonder" question on the wonder wall.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: leader, member, responsibility, organize, guide, encourage, question. Frames: "A leader ___." "The leader did ___. The group did ___." Familiar-language answers first.
- **Access.** Raised-line cards; each picture described aloud; pointing, placing a token or a communication aid counts as the answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Point, circle, token or dictation; a private hearing instead of the table; an adult may take the partner's role.
- **Reading load.** None; the teacher reads every card aloud. A word label is at most one word.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find the member-and-leader in a card the class has not used, or sort a new action under two words.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the hook pictures and the six action cards on a table, and ask "What did she do? What shows that?" with the faces covered. On day 21 tell The Muddy Path from the cards and ask the three questions.
- **One projector.** Show one picture at a time; the cards stay on the table so the child points and sorts.
