# Section 2.1: The class calendar, past, present and future, and a year of months

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-1/2/s/2.1), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-1 u02-1.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T3.3, the whole standard, spread over days 11 to 19.** Here: "Record patterns" on the class calendar (day 11: a pattern is the same thing on dates one week apart, and only the recorded dates count); the words and verb forms for "past, present, and future" (day 12: did, do, will do); "seasonal events or personal experiences" are the class's own days and what the class sees outside. Section 2.2 returns it on days 17 to 19.
- **D2.His.1.K-2, "Put several events in time order."** Day 13: dated events on a year strip, month first, then day.
- **Limits that bite here.** No birthday, trip or home event is asked for; the events are the school's own days and invented ones. Dates order events; no counting of days between dates. No scaled timeline. Seasons are named from what the class sees, not from a date.
- **Connections.** RI.1.6 (ELA unit 1) day 11: the photograph is looked at before its caption is read aloud. Kindergarten's sequence and time words, day 11 (review).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A calendar is something people made to keep track of days; this class uses one month page at a time. Say a date as month, number, year. A season word names what many places see for a few months, so it is a pattern and not a promise for a date (from memory; the class's own observations are the evidence). Verb forms are English grammar: score the time idea separately from the grammar, accept pointing and a home language. Count nothing in days; "one week apart" is the same column on the next row. The photograph on day 11 is another school's: the question is a hook, answered in 2.2.

## 2. Where it sits


Opens unit 2 after unit 1 (days 1 to 10). The retrieval task of unit plan field 4 for day 11 is used (kindergarten's order and time words); its results go to the record sheet, with the exits and the heard six. Day 13 hands 2.2 the year strip and the calendar routine.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1, 2 and 3 (unit plan field 5; MA.1.T3.3 and D2.His.1.K-2). They are sat on day 18, which section 2.2 hosts. This section teaches them with other cases. Item 2's fresh sentences are collected from the rotating six on days 13 to 16 (a fourth group each day, so four groups of six; the rest are covered by day 18's invented record).

**Exit questions.** Every child marks a picture in the last two minutes (the teacher reads the prompt aloud). The day's rotating six, six children a day, answer the spoken part at the teacher's table during supported work, about 60 to 80 seconds each, after placing the same card there. All cards are new, invented for practice, and the teacher says "this is a pretend class". One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E11a.** Mark: "A class noted wind on November 4 and November 11." Place both wind pictures on a November calendar. Answer: one on the 4 and one on the 11. Wrong: both pictures on one square (A).
- **E11b.** Spoken: "What repeats?" Answer: wind was noted on both dates, one week apart; the record does not say wind came only on those dates. Wrong: "It is windy every day in November" (G).
- **E12a.** Mark: "Tomorrow the class will clap." Circle where the clapping picture goes, tomorrow's square or yesterday's. Answer: tomorrow's. Wrong: yesterday's, because clapping is the last thing (B).
- **E12b.** Spoken: "Tell the three in order, using yesterday, today and tomorrow." (a class sang, listens, will clap). Answer: "Yesterday the class sang. Today it listens. Tomorrow it will clap." Wrong: "Yesterday the class will sing" (H).
- **E13a.** Mark: on a blank year strip, place a class visit on May 7, a paper boat drawing on August 19 and a weather chart on February 12, all in one year. Answer: February 12, May 7, August 19, left to right. Wrong: May first because 7 is the smallest number (F).
- **E13b.** Spoken: "Which comes first? How do you know?" Answer: the weather chart; February comes before May and August on the strip. Wrong: "May 7, it has the smallest number" (F).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** Unit 1's question board and the spoken claim "I think ..., because ..." (K review, kept); the compelling question stays posted. The class calendar (day, week, month and year rows from kindergarten) becomes a month page with dates on day 11, is read for past, present and future on day 12, and is laid as a year strip on day 13, the timeline from kindergarten. It runs all year; its calendar corner begins day 12. Plans (will) are drawn dotted and records solid from day 13. Withdrawn on day 18.
- **Key moment 1, day 13: the year strip.** Ideal instrument: twelve month pages that lie in a row, with a movable today marker and dated event cards. Spec: no tool; print fits (unit plan field 8).
- **A room with nothing.** A calendar drawn on the board, a strip of paper folded into twelve, hand-drawn cards.

## 6. Days

Each day is 30 minutes (frame). Parts are the lines in bold; the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children. Teacher prose stays under 450 words a day. "Room 4" is a pretend class: the teacher says it is made up.

## Day 11


- **4 min. Hook and question board.** Show Johnston's dog classroom photograph (about 1899, Library of Congress; credit printed; caption read aloud from the record, source to check) beside a dated drawing of our room made today. Say the photograph is of another school. Ask once, "Could this photograph tell us about our own school's past?" Any child may pass. Post the compelling question and the wonders on the question board. Nothing is answered today.
- **6 min. Retrieval and repair.** Three cards of an invented morning in Room 4 (the class comes in, opens a book, puts it away). Ask once, "What happened before opening the book?" Review first, next, last, before and after. For a miss, enact three different classroom actions with the whole class and reorder them (3 minutes); if only a few miss, guide them during supported work.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** Four minutes: the month page (a square for each day, a row for each week, the month name and the year); say today's date as month, number, year; draw one real class event on today's square. Two minutes: name the season by what we see outside, with a picture of it; say a season word is what we usually see, not a promise for a date. Three minutes: Room 4's March page with a bean sprout drawn on three squares in one column; ask "What repeats?" and point to the drawn squares; the empty squares do not say (error G).
- **9 min. Supported work.** Pairs place four dated Room 4 cards (a June page; dates printed) and say "On June ___, Room 4 ___." The teacher guides the retrieval misses for the first 3 minutes, then hears the rotating six at the table with E11, about 60 seconds each (E11b).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E11a on paper.

Holds: the teacher models the date; pairs hold the page. Tool: None. By hand: month page, cards, crayons. Evidence: E11a and E11b. Next day: past, present and future words.

## Day 12


- **2 min. Calendar corner.** Say today's date as month, number, year; the day's helper draws one real class event on today's square.
- **6 min. Reopen, hand-back and recheck.** Hand-back: E11 (A, G), up to 3 minutes; then recheck the order words with a new three-card morning (the class lines up, walks, sits).
- **8 min. Teaching first.** Yesterday, today and tomorrow squares are named past, present and future. Three actions, each said three ways and acted: go (we went to the library, we go, we will go), build (built, build, will build), paint (painted, paint, will paint); the first uses real class events from the schedule, the others Room 4. The word "will" marks what has not happened yet. The made-up helper says "yesterday we will paint" (error H) and the class repairs it.
- **12 min. Supported work.** Pairs sort six action cards onto a strip of past, present and future (one actor on each card) and say "Yesterday ___. Today ___. Tomorrow we will ___." The teacher hears the rotating six at the table with E12, about 80 seconds each (E12b).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E12a on paper.

Holds: the teacher holds the three words; pairs hold the strip. Tool: None. By hand: cards, the strip. Evidence: E12a and E12b. Next day: dated events on a year strip.

## Day 13


- **3 min. Calendar corner.** Date today's square. The rotating six each say one fresh sentence about the class's own days with yesterday, today or tomorrow, about 20 seconds each; the teacher writes it under a small drawing (unit assessment item 2 evidence).
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E12 (B, H): act a finished and a coming action and place each on its square.
- **6 min. Tie: the year strip.** The twelve month pages go in a row, January to December: the kindergarten timeline now holds a year. Place Room 4's events (hung a bird feeder, January 14; drew hats, March 6; visited the pond, June 12) by month, then by day within a month; then today's marker and one earlier and one later real class event from the school calendar. Plans are drawn dotted; records solid. Dates do not tell us the weather.
- **14 min. Supported work.** Pairs place six dated Room 4 cards on a blank year strip, earliest first, two in one month, and say two "___ came before ___ because ___" sentences. The teacher hears the rotating six at the table with E13, about 80 seconds each (E13b), while the others finish.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E13a on paper.

Holds: pairs hold the strip. Tool: None. By hand: strip, cards, dotted pen. Evidence: E13a and E13b. Next day: section 2.2, kinds of sources.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 11, "What repeats? How do you know?"; day 12, "What did Room 4 do yesterday, what does it do today, what will it do tomorrow?"; day 13, "Which came first? How do you know?"
- Written task, day 13: a copied month name and number beside a drawing of one event on the strip; nothing longer.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors (shown, shown with help, not yet seen): the card sits on its month and day; earlier is to the left; a verb form fits its time word.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 11.** First, next, last, before and after (kindergarten). **Day 12.** The date as month, number, year and the page's squares. **Day 13.** Past, present and future forms from day 12; the date.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: calendar, month, year, season, past, present, future. Frames: "Yesterday we ___. Today we ___. Tomorrow we will ___." "___ came before ___." A home-language word or a point counts.
- **Access.** Large, raised-edge cards and a ridged marker; an adult moves cards as the child directs; pointing, signing and eye gaze count.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; accept pointing or a dictated drawing; separate the time idea from the grammar.
- **Reading load.** None: labels are a month name and a number, read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second month on the strip that holds an event more than a season away.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use a printed month page and twelve month cards: say the date, put a school-day picture on its square, say what you did, do and will do, then lay the months in a row. Use school or pretend days, nothing from home. At home (15 to 30 minutes, any day skippable): draw a place where you learn and write its date; sharing is optional.
- **One projector.** Show the page and strip large; children place paper cards. Hear the rotating six one at a time.
