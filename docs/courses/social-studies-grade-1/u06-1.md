# Section 6.1: People live in different ways: three places today


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T2.5, days 56 to 58, the whole standard.** Children "explain that people in different parts of the world can have different ways of living, customs, and languages". Explain is spoken, pointed, sorted or drawn. Ways of living come first (56: homes, streets, clothes for the weather, school), then customs and languages (57), then how land and weather shape a choice (58).
- **D2.Geo.6.K-2, days 57 and 58, both parts.** "Identify some cultural and environmental features of particular places": a cultural feature is something people make, learn or do (a sign, a greeting, a school meal); an environmental feature is land, water, weather or plants. Sorted on 57, found in two named places on 58.
- **Limits that bite here.** Three places cannot stand for whole peoples, and one photograph shows one street at one time (error A). Weather shapes a choice, such as a coat or a covered walkway, and does not decide a language or a custom (B). No "exotic" framing: ordinary present-day streets, shops, schools and signs, never costume or festival pictures alone. No child reports on their home, family, food, language or where their family comes from, and nobody is asked which place is best or which they would choose.
- **Connections.** Kindergarten traditions (retrieval, 56). Unit 5's map, key and directions (review, 58). The question board and the source routine from unit 5 (the unit plan says they are carried; they are not in the ledger yet). Kindergarten's "two views side by side" (review and extension, 57).
- **What the teacher page must explain.**
  - Place names are used as the places name themselves: Sapporo (Japan), Singapore, Nairobi (Kenya). A sentence says "in this picture", never "people in Japan" or "Africans".
  - Sapporo: snow in winter; the city's own school-meal account says children eat prepared meals together (S: city of Sapporo school-meal page and winter guide, named in the unit plan, read). Singapore: warm and rainy; signs and several languages (S: weather and multicultural accounts, read). Nairobi: cool highland weather with long and short rains; English and Kiswahili are official languages (S: Kenya High Commission, UK, read). Nairobi's own city and ministry of education pages are unread: any Nairobi fact beyond weather and the two languages is source to check.
  - Greetings come from a speaker's own account (source to check). If none is cleared, the greeting day uses invented neighbors, said aloud to be invented, and no real greeting is shown.
  - Observation is what the picture shows; inference is what we think because of it. A caption may be wrong about everyone and still right about this street.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 1 (MA.1.T2.5, two sources compared) and 2 (D2.Geo.6.K-2, cultural and environmental features), on the unit plan, sat on day 67. This section teaches their parts with cases that do not return.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example. Each exit has a mark for every child (three picture choices, so a guess is not enough) and two spoken asks for the rotating six, one ask each, about 20 seconds an ask. The teacher says "these are practice pictures". Marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen.
- **E56.** Three new school pictures, drawn for practice, one for each place, captioned only with what they show (Sapporo: children at desks with a teacher at the front; Singapore: children on mats in a circle with a teacher holding a book; Nairobi: children at tables with a teacher beside a board). Mark: "Circle what you can see children doing in all three pictures": learning together (right), wearing the same clothes (A), eating the same lunch (A). Spoken (one ask, chosen by the record sheet): "Tell me one thing that looks different between two of the pictures." Answer: any true difference (desks, mats, tables). Wrong: "They are all the same" (A). Or: "What can you not tell from one picture?" Answer: what children eat, say or do on other days, or what other schools are like. Wrong: "This is how everyone goes to school there" (A). Rows are in items.csv (s06-X56); items.csv is the key.
- **E57.** A new picture card of two neighbors in Quarry Town (invented, said to be made up) greeting each other with a wave, with the speaker's words read aloud: "We say Morning, neighbor, so everyone feels welcome." Mark: "Circle the one that is a greeting": two neighbors greeting (right), a rain cloud (B), a hill (I). Spoken (one ask, chosen by the record sheet): "Name one custom you heard or saw on this card." Answer: the wave or the words Morning, neighbor. Wrong: "That is how it is when it rains" (B). Or: "Why might people there like their greeting?" Answer: it makes everyone feel welcome, or it is the way they learned. Wrong: "It is a silly way to greet" (H). Rows are in items.csv (s06-X57).
- **E58.** A new Sapporo view (children in coats, hats and boots at a school gate with snow on the ground), a new Nairobi view (people in raincoats holding umbrellas on a street in a rainy season) and a new map of Asia and Africa with its key and direction indicator. Mark: "Place one token on Sapporo and one on Nairobi." Right: each token on its city symbol. A swapped pair is no letter: read the key again. Spoken (one ask, chosen by the record sheet): "What are people wearing in each picture?" Answer: coats, hats and boots; raincoats and umbrellas. Or: "What does the weather change for children there?" Answer: what they wear to get to school. Wrong: "The snow makes them speak Japanese" (B); "The rain is why they eat what they eat" (B). Rows are in items.csv (s06-X58). The unit plan table names Sapporo and Singapore for this token mark; items.csv follows this section plan (Sapporo and Nairobi), the two cities of the exit views.

## 6. Days

Every day is 30 minutes (frame). Parts are the lines in bold; the day skeleton reads them. No timer is shown to children. Read-alouds, captions and prompts are spoken; children point, sort, place a token, draw or say. The record sheet has one row for each child and one scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. A rotating six are heard each day, so each child is heard about once every five course days; the sheet has a column naming who was heard. The photographs are ours to select (open license, source to check); every caption says only what the picture shows. Four sets of views are kept apart: teaching (56 to 58), the exits (56 to 58), and the unit's end packet (day 67, sources A and B). No exit shows a teaching view or a caption it used.

## Day 56


- **4 min. Hook.** Three street drawings, one each for Sapporo, Singapore and Nairobi, made from checked descriptions. "These are real places today; the pictures are drawings." "What do you see in all three?" (roads, shops, people, signs: ordinary shared needs). Post the compelling question and co-write the supporting question "How do people live in these places?" on the question board.
- **2 min. Retrieval.** Kindergarten's traditions. The teacher shows an invented card, "invented for practice": "Neighbors tell stories together every spring." Ask: "What makes this a tradition? Must every neighbor join?" Answer: people repeat it and pass it on; joining is not required. A miss goes to the small group in supported work.
- **8 min. Teaching first.** The teacher models "I see ..., I think ..., because I see ..." on one Nairobi street (jackets), then on one Sapporo winter street (snow): what the picture shows, then what we think. "I cannot see what they eat at home." Then the source routine on the Sapporo winter guide: who made it (the city), when, why, what it helps us find out (what the city says about winter). Words with pictures: homes, clothes, school. Puppet Pip says error A and is corrected.
- **11 min. Supported work.** Six tables of five. Each table has a chart with three columns (Sapporo, Singapore, Nairobi) and nine picture cards (homes, clothes for the weather, a street each). 5 min: each child places a card and says "I see ___; I think ___, because I see ___" (two cards each, about 20 seconds a card). 5 min: each child draws one way of living they noticed and says one word label. During the 5 minutes the teacher hears the rotating six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes), at the exit cards. The last 3 minutes, a small group for any retrieval miss: match three repeated activities to their captions (about 3 children; hearings and the group are not at the same time).
- **3 min. Exit.** E56: three cards read aloud, every child circles; the six were heard in supported work.
- **2 min. Look back.** Add one question from a child's drawing to the question board. "Tomorrow: what people say, and what they write on signs."

Holds: the teacher holds the three drawings and the puppet. Tool: None. By hand: drawings, charts, cards, crayons. Evidence: E56; the drawing for item 1 (a way of living).

## Day 57


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Retrieve 56 (1 minute: "What does one picture show?"). One repair: E56, error A, if many missed (4 minutes). If not needed, a small group while the rest look at the street drawings.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** Custom: something people in a place do the same way, such as a greeting. Language: the words people use to talk or write. Three cards: an invented Sorrel Town greeting, said to be made up; an invented Holly Run school custom card; and a Nairobi sign with two languages (English and Kiswahili, official languages; source read). The Sapporo and Singapore signs wait for the unit's end packet. (If no real greeting is cleared: invented neighbors, said to be invented.) The perspective model: the teacher sets two views side by side: "Here, people greet like this. For the people who use it, it makes sense because ___." Then two bins, "people made or learned it" (cultural) and "land, water, weather" (environmental); the sign goes in the first bin, a hill in the second (errors H and I). Maker, time and purpose are said for each card.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs have six cards from the three places and the two bins; each pair sorts, then says one custom or language and one environmental feature for a named place. Turns: a pair needs about 90 seconds to sort six cards and 30 seconds to say two things. The teacher hears the rotating six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes), at the exit cards, and sees pairs the rest of the time.
- **3 min. Exit.** E57: three cards read aloud, every child circles.
- **3 min. Look back.** Pairs say "It makes sense to them because ___" for one custom. Question board: add one.

Holds: pairs hold the bins; the teacher holds the perspective sentence. Tool: None. By hand: cards, bins, tokens. Evidence: E57; the sorting for item 2.

## Day 58


- **5 min. Return and repair.** One repair: E57, errors H and I (the one most missed, 5 minutes): read the speaker's words first and sort four new cards.
- **3 min. Reopen.** Unit 5's map and key, kept visible. "Find Nairobi with the key. Which city is farthest north?" (review; the teacher points, children say). Directions: north, south, east, west as unit 5 taught them.
- **5 min. Model a row.** The teacher fills one row of the two-place chart (Singapore and Nairobi): weather, what people do about it, one custom or language. "Rain is why the walkway has a roof (a choice). Rain does not teach a word." The model names the limit: weather shapes a choice and does not decide a custom (error B). Nothing new is taught: the cultural and environmental bins are from 57.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs fill the same chart (weather, what people do about it, one thing the picture cannot tell) for Sapporo and Singapore (teaching views) and find both on the three-city map. Each child places one token and says one row. The teacher hears the rotating six, 40 seconds each (4 minutes), at the exit cards.
- **3 min. Exit.** E58: tokens and the map, every child; the six heard in supported work.
- **4 min. Co-write.** The class answers "How do people live in these places?" in one sentence a child says and the teacher writes, drawing from the chart. "Tomorrow: one country, many people."

Holds: pairs hold the chart and tokens. Tool: None. By hand: chart, map, tokens. Evidence: E58; the chart for items 1 and 2.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: 56, "What do you see? What do you think, and how do you know?"; 57, "How might this make sense to the people who use it?"; 58, "What does the weather change here, and what does it not change?"
- Written task, day 58: the co-written class sentence, plus each child's drawing of one thing a place's weather explains, with a one-word label or a dictated "because". The teacher scribes dictation.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: says what the picture shows apart from what they think; names a custom or a language; sorts learned from land, water or weather; links the weather to one choice, not to every custom. Drawing and pointing count.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: custom, language, greeting, sign, weather, rain, snow, coat. Frames: "I see ___. I think ___ because I see ___." "In this picture, people ___." "It makes sense to them because ___." Home-language words are welcome, and no child is asked to name a language or place from their own family.
- **Access.** Large print, raised-line maps, a neutral spoken description of every photograph, tokens in place of drawing, an adult placing cards as the child directs.
- **Check accommodations.** Read everything aloud; accept a point, a token, a drawing, familiar-language speech or a private turn. A child not heard stays "not yet seen", never guessed.
- **Reading load.** None; labels read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** A third place on the chart (Singapore against Sapporo and Nairobi), saying one thing a single photograph cannot show.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look at a photograph together: say what you see, then what you think. Read the day's cards aloud, sort learned things from land and weather, and have the child say "It makes sense to them because ...". Never ask what the child's own family does.
- **One projector.** Show the photograph or the map large and cover the caption until the child has said what they see; children mark paper cards; the spoken part is heard at a table.
