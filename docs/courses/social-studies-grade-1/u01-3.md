# Section 1.3: Roles, common work and showing what we know

<!-- skel -->
Grade 1: Leaders, Helpers and Communities. Unit 1, Our groups. *What makes a group work well?* (days 1 to 10): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 6 to 10.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (6, 7), application 1 (8), check 1 (9), respond 1 (10). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 3 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 minutes.

Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label. Every story and picture is ours and invented, and the teacher says so aloud. Day 7's volunteer account is real and credited.

## 6. Days

## Day 6

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Roles in a group with a shared goal (a class garden, a school play): how each role helps reach it
- note: Retrieval task: kindergarten's class jobs (ssk-jobs-helping)
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Reopen and retrieval.** Retrieval, 3 minutes: "A class messenger carries a note to the office. What does that job help the class do?" Accept: talk to the office. A title alone joins day 7's repair of error H. Two minutes for day 5's repair of error I, if many; otherwise a small group in the pairs.
- **8 min. Bean Row.** Original picture sequence, ours and invented: four children plant a row of beans in the class garden; the goal is a planted row. One actor a scene: Mara tells the order of the work (review of in charge); Ben loosens the soil; Aiko places the seeds; Tomas waters. A fifth scene shows the finished row, no people. Teach role (the part a member plays) and goal (what the group wants to finish together).
- **6 min. Act the roles.** Four zones in the room; every child mimes one role for 30 seconds, then swaps twice. The teacher watches, not hears.
- **6 min. What would be missing?** Pairs hold the four role cards and a picture of the row; pick one role to take away and show what is missing; 15 pairs at once; the teacher visits five pairs.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X6: mark by every child (1 minute), spoken for the first six (4 minutes, while the others draw their favorite role).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the story; pairs hold role cards. Tool: None. By hand: miming, pairs, drawing. Evidence: X6.

## Day 7

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: How a community gets common work done, from firefighters and librarians to volunteers; every member matters, not only the person in charge
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **7 min. Return and repair.** If many missed error C (X6), three minutes: three cards trace a member's part to the goal. If many gave a title only (error H, retrieval), four minutes: children act out a paper collector's job and say what it makes ready. Two repairs at most; otherwise small groups in the sort.
- **6 min. Two pictures.** Say they are invented and show only what is pictured. A fire station: one firefighter points to the list of the morning's jobs, one checks a piece of equipment, one cleans a piece of equipment (row 7D). A library: a librarian shows where books go while a student helper puts returned books on a cart. Ask who guides, who does a part and what is missing without each. Teach community (people who share a place and look after it together).
- **7 min. The Trashbusters.** Read our summary of a real account, credited to the National Park Service page "Impacts Beyond the Beach: Trashbusters and Their Shared Mission" (last updated 2025-04-27; read 2026-11-19, sources.csv row 7B): a group of friends volunteers at Padre Island National Seashore in Texas, picks up trash and marine debris along the shores, beaches and dunes every week, and a park staff member supervises. They say being outdoors, seeing wildlife and seeing clean beaches feel good, and they want visitors to enjoy the beach. Teach volunteer (a person who chooses to help a group's work and is not paid). Ask: why do they do it?
- **5 min. Who does what.** Pairs set 6 cards from the three stories onto three mats: guides, does a part, helps the community. 15 pairs at once; the teacher visits five pairs.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X7: mark by every child (1 minute), spoken for the second six (4 minutes, while the others draw the helpers of one of the stories).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the pictures and the account; pairs hold cards. Tool: None. By hand: miming, sorting, drawing. Evidence: X7.

## Day 8

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Plan a shared class task (a garden bed, a tidy-up) and give each member a role; say how each role helps reach the goal
- note: No group in the examples leaves a child out. Within the existing tidy-up time: 15 minutes practice, 2 written picture feedback on rule and duty, 3 retry. Score only the revised performance and its explanation
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Return and plan.** If many missed error C (X7), a two-minute repair. Then agree one rule (for example, leave space for others at the shelf) and, at each table of five, give every child a role: crayon sorter, lid closer, box carrier (light), shelf pointer (directs placement), floor checker. The rule and duty go on the child's picture card.
- **9 min. Tidy-up practice.** Six tables, six crayon boxes, all at once. The teacher steps back: one prompt a table at most. Not scored.
- **8 min. Picture feedback.** The teacher draws a mark or a stamp on each child's rule and duty card (30 children at about 15 seconds is 7.5 minutes; a second adult or helper halves it, and without one the retry shrinks to 2 minutes). Waiting children draw their duty on the back of the card.
- **3 min. Retry.** Each child redoes the duty. This revised performance is the evidence, recorded on the wall chart.
- **5 min. Explain, draw and exit ticket.** All draw "How my duty helps"; the teacher hears the third six on X8: mark by every child (1 minute), spoken (4 minutes).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the wall chart; each child holds a rule and duty card. Tool: None. By hand: tidy-up, drawing. Evidence: item 3 (day 9) and X8. Hands are washed as usual.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: role, goal, community, volunteer, duty. Frames: "A member ___. This helps the group ___." "The rule is ___. It helps because ___."
- **Access.** Accessible duties, such as directing placement; tidy-up needs no lifting, no standing. A pointed or drawn answer counts.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all items aloud; pointing, dictation and a familiar language are answers; a scribe records only what the child says.
- **Reading load.** None; the teacher reads the caption and the account.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask a child to name a second role and what would be missing without it.
