# Section 8.3: Saving, banks and three jars


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T4.9, days 88 and 90.** "Compare reasons people save money, such as a future purchase, a donation, or an emergency." Compare means say how two reasons are alike and different: which can wait and which cannot. All three reasons are used.
- **D2.Eco.10.K-2, days 88 and 90.** "Explain why people save": saved money is kept from spending now so it can be used later.
- **D2.Eco.9.K-2, day 89.** "Describe what banks do in an economy": a bank keeps savers' money safe, records each saver's amount, pays a little interest for keeping it, and lends money that borrowers repay.
- **Limits that bite here.** No coin values, interest calculations, real accounts, real banks named, real donations or family finances. Tokens only. Saving is never a measure of worth, and families keep money in many ways. Sharing may be zero; jars are optional planning categories, not a required family budget. Deposit insurance is not taught, and nothing promises that every financial product is protected.
- **Connections.** 1.OA.A.1 on 90 (adding within 20, split 20 and 12 tokens into three jars; math units 2 and 6, already taught). The 86 mat is tied to saving on 88.
- **What the teacher page must explain.**
  - Three reasons: a later purchase (boots for winter, a known time), a gift to a cause (a playground donation, sometimes with a deadline), an emergency (a cracked pipe, an unknown date, cannot wait). Gifts can have deadlines; not everything can wait.
  - A bank: our own explanation with the invented "Corner Bank". Saved tokens stay the saver's; the bank keeps a record and the saver's amount stays on it. Interest is a little extra for keeping savings there, not wages. A loan is money lent that the borrower must repay. All source to check against the federal savings page for children, read 2026-11-14; lending is in our own words, still to check; a credited extract is to be made, naming the passage and read date.
  - Jars: each token goes in one jar once. The save jar is for later, so saving now means giving up something now.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 5 (compare three saving reasons), 6 (what a bank does) and 7 (plan 11 dollars in three jars, scored on a new task on 92), on the unit plan. Records: item 5, "My Saving Page", made on 88 (first-day work is practice), collected at the start of 89, revised on 90 together with item 4. Item 6, "My Bank Page", made on 89 (practice), collected at the start of 90, returned on 91 and revised there. The 88 to 90 teaching cases (Ila and the Corner Bank, winter boots, the playground gift, the cracked pipe, 20 tokens split 8, 8, 4 and 12 tokens split by the children) stay out of the exits.

**E88.** Cards, invented for practice: a garden gate broken by a storm and to be replaced; a puppet-show ticket planned for next month; a new shelf planned for spring.
- Mark (all): "Circle the reason to save for something nobody planned." Answer: the gate. Wrong: the ticket or the shelf (O).
- Spoken (six, one ask chosen by the record sheet): "How are the two reasons different, the gate and the ticket?" or "Which of the three cannot wait?" Answer: the ticket was planned and the gate was not; both need saved money; the gate cannot wait. Wrong: "the storm was on a day we knew" (O); "saving is only for things you want" (E).

**E89.** Cards: a bank record page for a saver, Kemal; a bank handing out gifts without end; a bank giving tokens to anyone who asks and keeping no record.
- Mark (all): "Circle what a bank can do." Answer: record savings. Wrong: the other two, "free money" (F).
- Spoken (six, one ask): "Who owns the tokens Kemal keeps at the bank?" or "What is the record page for?" Answer: Kemal, the saver; the bank keeps a record of how many are his. Wrong: "the bank" (Q).

**E90.** Pat, invented, has 10 tokens: 5 in spend, 3 in save, 2 in share. Pat is saving for a watering can; a snack costs 6 tokens today and the spend jar holds only 5.
- Mark (all): "Circle the jar that holds the watering can money." Answer: the save jar. Wrong: the spend jar (P).
- Spoken (six, one ask): "Why does Pat not use the save jar for the snack today?" or "What does Pat wait to buy?" Answer: the tokens kept for the watering can cannot also be spent on the snack now; the snack waits, and giving it up now is the cost of saving. Wrong: "Pat can spend the saved tokens and still have them" (P).

## 6. Days

## Day 89


- **5 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: collect My Saving Page. 4 minutes: hand-back E88 (E, O): three new reason cards in three hoops, "which can wait?" If many missed, the 4 minutes are the repair.
- **12 min. Teaching first.** The Corner Bank, invented. Ila puts 6 tokens in the bank. The bank keeps them safe and writes down that 6 are Ila's: a record card with her name. The bank pays a little extra, called interest, for keeping her tokens there; interest is not pay for work (wages). The bank also lends some money to another person who must repay it: the loan comes back. Ila's amount stays on her record the whole time. A credited extract of the federal savings page for children, passages on saving and interest, is read aloud with pictures (extract to be made; passage and read date named then; source to check). Words with pictures: bank, save, interest, loan. Pip says Q, F and R.

Holds: the teacher holds Ila's record card. Tool: None. By hand: record, interest, loan and repayment cards; pages. Evidence: E89; the page is item 6's practice record, collected on 90.

## Day 90

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: 88, "Which reason can wait? Which cannot?"; 89, "Who owns the money in the bank? What does a bank do for a saver and for a borrower?"; 90, "What does the save jar wait for? What did you give up?"
- Written task, 88: My Saving Page (three boxes), revised on 90.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: all three reasons shown; one reason that cannot wait marked; the bank's role shown by pointing or drawing (record, interest, loan repaid); the save jar tied to a later thing and one thing given up now. Accept dictation or a spoken answer.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: save, emergency, bank, interest, loan, repay, jar. Frames: "I save for ___ because ___." "The bank keeps ___ safe." "I give up ___ when I save."
- **Access.** Large jar outlines, tactile tokens, record cards with raised names, an adult moving pieces as the child points.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems aloud; accept pointing, placing tokens, drawing or a private turn; an unheard child stays "not yet seen".
- **Reading load.** None; the teacher reads cards and the extract aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make up a fourth saving reason and say whether it can wait.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use ten paper tokens and three paper jars. The grown-up reads the cards and asks the exit questions aloud; never ask what the home saves or where it keeps money. At home, the grown-up reads the Ila card and plays the bank; any day is skippable.
- **One projector.** Show one reason or one bank card at a time; children place tokens before the teacher says the answer.
