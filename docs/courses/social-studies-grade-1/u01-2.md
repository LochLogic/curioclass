# Section 1.2: Belonging, rules and duties

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-1/1/s/1.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-1 u01-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Children read very little. Stems are spoken with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn. Every pictured group and family is invented, and the teacher says so aloud.

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T1.1, this section's share.** "Explain the benefits and meaning of belonging to a group": day 3 (a group is people together for something; a member belongs; belonging can give help, friendship and shared work). "Follow a group's rules and duties, and explain the reasons for those rules": day 4 (a rule and a duty each serve what the group is for), day 5 (our class's own rules and duties, sorted by purpose). Day 5 answers the second supporting question.
- **D1.3.K-2, day 5.** The supporting question "Why do groups need rules and duties?" is the one the class tests with its own rules; the children name the facts they need (what each rule is for) and the words (rule, duty).
- **Limits that bite here.** Family cards show many kinds of family and are never the class's families; no child describes their own, and no card ranks one kind above another. Foster and adoptive families are pictured plainly; no child is asked what these words mean for any family. Membership never needs members to look alike. "Because the grown-up said" is not a reason that satisfies the standard.
- **Connections.** Day 4 models "I think ... because ..." from a picture (review of kindergarten's spoken claim) and prepares item 6.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** That belonging is a feature of being a member, not of appearance; that a rule and a duty are different (a rule says how we act, a duty is a job we are expected to do); that a family card is a card about a group, not a question about a child.

## 2. Where it sits

Follows 1.1's question board; day 3 opens with kindergarten's reason-for-a-rule retrieval (look-for 2), and day 5 uses the second supporting question. Results go on the record sheet; day 6 follows. Family structures on day 3 are flagged for the sensitivity read.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** items 3 and 4 (MA.1.T1.1) are on days 8 and 9 (section 1.3); this section's evidence is the exits. Marks: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Exits: a mark by every child, a spoken why heard from one rotating six (day 3 the third six, day 4 the fourth, day 5 the fifth); cases print "invented for practice".

- **X3 (day 3).** Mark, read aloud: "Which one shows a group? Members cooking together, or people in matching shirts who do nothing together." Answer: members cooking together. Spoken (one ask): "What could members get from belonging to the cooking club?" Answer: help, friendship, learning or shared work. Wrong: "Matching shirts make the group" (D).
- **X4 (day 4).** As the table above: the puzzle club that keeps its pieces on a mat, or a grown-up pointing. Spoken: "Why does the club keep that rule?" Answer: the pieces stay together so members can finish. Wrong: "Because a grown-up says" (B).
- **X5 (day 5).** Mark, read aloud: "A telescope club wants everyone to join. Which rule helps? Taking turns at the telescope, or keeping the telescope in a high cupboard." Answer: taking turns. Spoken (one ask): "Why does that rule help everyone join?" Answer: everyone gets a turn to use the telescope. Wrong: picks the cupboard rule or gives no reason tied to joining (I).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The question board (days 3, 5 and 8's supporting questions answered in turn), the group-member-belong card set (kept from day 3), the rule and purpose cards (from day 4), and our class rules chart (day 5, kept to day 8). The teacher holds the board; children hold their own card or strip.
- **Key moment, day 5: our class rules.** Ideal instrument: the class's own dated rules chart, with each rule or duty on a strip. Spec: chart paper, tape, markers, and six matching sets of strips; no tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn family cards on scrap paper; a sheet with three mats drawn on it.

## 6. Days

## Day 3


- **7 min. Return and repair, and retrieval.** Retrieval, 3 minutes, review of kindergarten's rule and reason: "An invented club puts scissors in a tray after use. Why?" Accept safe storage or finding the tools; a command with no reason joins error B's repair on day 4. Then, if many missed day 2's exit, the four-minute repair of error F; otherwise a small group has it in the sort.
- **7 min. Group, member, belong.** Teach with two invented groups: a swim team and a kite club (members of different ages, kites of different shapes). A group is people together for something; a member is one person in it; to belong is to be a member. Each word has a motion. Ask: who are the members of each?
- **5 min. Families are groups too.** Five invented family cards, each a different kind: one parent and a child fixing a bike; two fathers and a child folding laundry; a grandfather and two grandchildren; a foster parent and a foster child; an adoptive family with two children. Say aloud: "Families come in many kinds. These are made up. No one needs to tell about their own." Read each card and ask what belonging gives: help, friendship or shared work.
- **6 min. What belonging gives.** Pairs sort 8 picture cards onto three mats: help, friendship, shared work. 15 pairs at once; the teacher visits five pairs.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X3: mark by every child (1 minute), spoken for the third six (6 children at about 40 seconds is 4 minutes, while the others draw a club they would like to belong to).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the family cards; each pair holds a card set. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, pointing, drawing. Evidence: X3; the day's look-for 2 retrieval notes.

## Day 4


- **6 min. Return and repair.** If many missed error B (retrieval, day 3) or error D (exit X3), run each repair of field 4 at three minutes; otherwise small groups in the sort. Two repairs at most.
- **7 min. A rule and its reason.** Case: the swim team's whistle rule, "Wait for the whistle before you jump in," and its reason: so no one jumps onto a swimmer. Show a picture of the pool deck and model the spoken claim from a picture, "I think the rule is to wait, because I see a swimmer in the water," as review of kindergarten's claim. Class repeats the frame "The rule is ___. It helps because ___." Compare two cards for the rule: one gives a reason, one gives only the coach.
- **6 min. A duty.** A duty is a job a member is expected to do for the group (review of kindergarten's job). The swim team's equipment keeper puts the kickboards back so the team can find them. All children mime the duty at once and say what it helps.
- **6 min. Rule cards on purpose cards.** In pairs, children set 4 rule cards onto 3 what-the-group-is-for cards (stay safe, be fair, help us finish what we do together). 15 pairs at once; the teacher visits five pairs.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X4: mark by every child (1 minute), spoken for the fourth six (4 minutes, while the others draw the club's mat).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the case pictures; pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: miming, sorting, drawing. Evidence: X4.

## Day 5


- **4 min. Reopen and repair.** Read the board: "Why do groups need rules and duties?" If many missed error B on X4, one three-minute repair of field 4; otherwise a small group has it in the sort.
- **6 min. Our own rules chart.** The teacher reads aloud six of the class's real rules and duties from its dated chart, each on a strip with a picture. Real, ours, not a record of any child. If the class has fewer than six, add them from the first weeks of school.
- **10 min. Sort by purpose.** Six table groups of five, each with the same six strips and the three purpose mats (stay safe, be fair, help us finish together) plus a "not sure" mat. Each child places and says one strip using the frame, "The rule is ___. It helps because ___." Five children at about 30 seconds is 2.5 minutes a group; the teacher stands near one table the whole time, the day's fifth six.
- **5 min. Report and answer.** Each table puts one strip on the class chart under its purpose (six tables at about 40 seconds is 4 minutes). The class states one answer to the supporting question, which the teacher writes: "Groups need rules and duties so ___."
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** X5: mark by every child (1 minute); spoken for the six at the observed table (4 minutes, while the others draw their favorite class rule's purpose).

Who holds what is carried: the class holds the chart; each table holds its strips. Tool: None. By hand: sorting, placing, drawing. Evidence: X5.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Questions: day 3, "What could members get from belonging?"; day 4, "Why does the team keep that rule?"; day 5, "What is this rule for?"
- Written task: none. The teacher writes each child's spoken reason on the record sheet and the class answer on the chart.
- The teacher looks for: (1) a reason tied to what the group is for, not a command; (2) a member's contribution named; (3) "shown with help" when a frame or a picture was used.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 3: kindergarten's rule-and-reason retrieval; day 2's error F. Day 4: errors B and D; the claim frame "I think ... because ..." Day 5: the three supporting questions and the board; the rule-and-reason frame from day 4. The class rules chart is carried to day 8.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: group, member, belong, rule, duty, reason. Frames: "A member ___. This helps the group ___." "The rule is ___. It helps because ___."
- **Access.** Mime can be done seated; cards have large print and raised edges; a pointed card is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit read aloud; a child may mark, point or answer privately.
- **Reading load.** None; the teacher reads strips and cards.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask for a second reason a rule helps, or a duty the class could add.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look at a family card or a rule card together; ask what the group gets, or what the rule is for; ask the child to say it.
- **One projector.** Show the cards on one screen; children mark on paper, never on the screen.
