# Section 8.4: The claim, the check and the respond day


## 1. Standards and practices


- **D4.1.K-2, day 91 only.** "Make an argument and give reasons for it." Children make a claim (what to do), give a reason from a card, and close. Day 91 teaches D4.1 only; D4.2 and D4.5 belong to unit 3.
- **MA.1.T4.7 and MA.1.T4.9, days 91 to 93.** "Give examples of choices people make when buying goods and services, and explain that limited money forces those choices"; "Compare reasons people save money, such as a future purchase, a donation, or an emergency." Used in the claim, in item 7, and in the respond day. Items 1 to 6 are scored on 92 only from revised earlier records (MA.1.T4.4, MA.1.T4.5, MA.1.T4.6, MA.1.T4.8, D2.Eco.6, D2.Eco.5, D2.Eco.2, D2.Eco.9 and D2.Eco.10 are all sampled there).
- **Limits that bite here.** Different affordable choices can be defended; the claim is judged on a connected reason, never on which choice. Tokens only; no family finances; handwriting judged apart from economics. Reasons come from a card or the teaching explanation, never "because we should". No new teaching on 92; 93 re-teaches only what 92 showed.
- **Connections.** 1.OA.A.1 and 1.NBT.B.3 inside the tasks (within 20). The claim frame "I think ..., because ..." from kindergarten (review) becomes claim, reason, closing on 91.
- **What the teacher page must explain.**
  - Claim: what the writer thinks should be done. Reason: a detail from a card or story that supports it. Closing: a last sentence that says what the reason gives. Repeating the claim is not a reason (G).
  - The reserved packet (unit plan field 5) is read only on 92 and never rehearsed; nothing here copies it.
  - How 92 stays honest: scored items 1 to 6 come from revised records made on earlier days; items 7 and 8 and any missing evidence use 92; no thirty consecutive conferences.
  - How 93 is run: the record sheet's zeros pick at most four groups, most-missed letter first; a shared error taught first if more than a third missed it.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 8 (claim, reason, closing from a packet source) and the sitting of item 7 (the jar plan, owned by 8.3) on 92; items 1 to 6 are scored on 92 from revised records made on days 82, 84, 86, 88, 90 and 91 (unit plan field 5). The 91 case, the exit and the 93 cases stay out of the packet.

**E91.** A short story, invented for practice: Ola pulls a small wagon to the market, and when it rains the wagon seat gets wet. Two claims: "Buy the rain cover, because the story says the wagon seat gets wet" and "Buy it, because we should buy it." Two closings: "So the rain cover keeps the seat dry" and "So yes."
- Mark (all): "Circle the claim that has a reason." Answer: the first. Wrong: the second (G).
- Spoken (six, one ask chosen by the record sheet): "Why is that claim supported?" or "Which closing goes with it, So the rain cover keeps the seat dry, or So yes?" Answer: it points to a detail in the story, that the seat gets wet; the first closing goes with it. Wrong: "because it says buy it twice" (G).

**Day 92: no exit ticket.** Evidence is items 7 and 8 and the revised records (unit plan field 5). Rule: items 1 to 6 are never repeated; items 7 and 8 use the packet.

**E93 (a fresh try, one case for each letter a group was taught; the teacher hands each child the card for the letter of their group).** Every card is invented for practice; one mark and one spoken ask each.
- L: a lawn-mowing service at 7 dollars and a woolly scarf at 12 dollars on a flyer; join each price tag to its offer.
- M: Ana ranks a sun hat first, a pinwheel second and a ribbon third and buys the hat; circle the next best thing given up.
- H: Orla plans swimming classes at a town pool, her pay a salary agreed for the year; place a token card on each month of a twelve-month strip.
- D: two lamp shops, each with a card it made itself (paid sick days; lamps from old glass), the same price; join each card to a buyer who might pick it.
- E: a class saves for a gift to a food drive, a family saves for a plant stand; circle the saving reason that is a gift.
- F: Maple Bank lends 5 tokens to Wen; circle the arrow that shows what happens next, tokens going back to the bank.
- P: Jo has 9 tokens and three jars, wants a plant stand soon and a leaking roof might need money someday; place all 9 tokens, each once.
- G: the class garden hose leaks; circle the claim that has a reason from the story.
Spoken: the teacher hears up to six children whose explanation stayed missing, one sentence each, using the same look-fors as the unit plan. Wrong answers keep the letter of their group.

## 6. Days

## Day 91


- **5 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: return My Bank Page with written picture feedback; 1 minute: post "How should people choose what to buy and what to save?" 3 minutes: hand-back E90 (P): a new set of 9 tokens, one jar each. If many missed, all the repair minutes are P. Revision of the Bank Page is in supported work.
- **7 min. Teaching first.** Mira, invented, has 10 tokens. Our teaching flyer: a sketch pad 6, a paint set 12. The story card says Mira ranked the paint set first. The teacher models a claim, reason and closing: "Mira should save for the paint set. The flyer says it costs 12 and she has 10, and spending 6 now leaves 4. So saving gets Mira the thing she wants most." Three labeled boxes. The example is then covered; blank mats and jar outlines stay out. Pip says G.
- **16 min. Supported work.** 5 min: revise My Bank Page, one change each. 8 min: a new card pair (Teo, 9 tokens; a flyer with a wind chime 4 and a lunch box 9; the story says Teo's lunch box handle broke): each child draws, writes a word or dictates a claim, a reason from a card and a closing; either choice can be defended. The six are heard, 3 minutes inside the 8. 3 min: partner read-aloud, "my reason is on the card".
- **2 min. Exit.** E91: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: children hold their own three boxes. Tool: None. By hand: flyer cards, story cards, paper. Evidence: E91; the claim is item 8's practice record; the revised Bank Page is item 6's record.

## Day 92


- **5 min. Reread the packet.** The reserved packet of unit plan field 5 is read aloud in short parts with pictures, and the teacher says it is made up. It is never rehearsed. Source questions: who made it, when, what for.
- **15 min. Items 7 and 8.** Everyone, tables of five, picture and word responses. 7 min: item 7, each child places 11 tokens in the three jar outlines and draws or says what the save jar is for and what is given up. 8 min: item 8, claim, reason from the packet and closing, drawn, written or dictated. The teacher circulates; unfinished picture responses end here. No new teaching, no hints beyond rereading a card.
- **10 min. Missing explanations.** The record sheet names who still lacks a spoken explanation for items 1 to 6; at most ten children, one minute each, most missing first; never thirty in a row. The rest illustrate their plans quietly. A child with no revised record for an item does the item here; an unconfirmed part stays missing.

Holds: the teacher holds the packet. Tool: None. By hand: packet cards, jar outlines, tokens, paper. Evidence: items 7 and 8 and the revised records; marking is 20 teacher minutes after class.

## Day 93


- **5 min. Return evidence.** Written picture feedback returned; each child sees their own look-fors; the class sees which letters were missed most, never named children.
- **10 min. Reteach.** The record sheet's zeros pick at most four groups, most-missed letter first; if more than a third missed one letter, the teacher teaches it to the class for 4 minutes and then three groups. Each group of about seven gets 2 minutes with its letter's repair and an unused case. The rest work in pairs from a printed repair card. The letters by item: H item 1; L item 2; M item 3; D item 4; E item 5; F item 6; P item 7; G item 8.
- **10 min. Fresh try.** E93: each child gets the card for their group's letter and marks the picture. The teacher circulates; up to six children whose explanation stayed missing are heard, 30 seconds each (3 minutes).
- **5 min. Reflect.** Each child marks one improvement on a picture card ("I can now ..."). No one is named as an example.

Holds: the teacher holds the repair cards. Tool: None. By hand: repair cards, E93 cards. Evidence: E93; the new look at each item's look-fors.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: 91, "What do you choose, and why does the card say so? What closes it?"; 93, "What did you not yet show? What is different now?"
- Written task, 91: a claim, a reason and a closing, drawn, written as a word or dictated; the teaching sources only.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: a claim; a reason that names a detail on a card; a closing that says what the reason gives; either affordable position accepted. Handwriting and spelling are judged separately.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 91.** Jars and the mat; bank from 89; claim frame from kindergarten. **Day 92.** Everything in 81 to 91 through the packet. **Day 93.** The letter each group missed, with a new case.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: claim, reason, closing, choose, save, jar. Frames: "I think ___, because the card says ___." "So ___."
- **Access.** Large cards, tactile tokens, an adult writing as the child dictates, pointing or drawing for any part.
- **Check accommodations.** Read the packet and every stem aloud; accept drawing, pointing, placing tokens or dictation; judge economic reasoning apart from reading, English and arithmetic.
- **Reading load.** None; the packet and cards are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Defend the other affordable choice with a different reason from the packet.

## 10. Two rooms

The grown-up reads each card and asks the questions aloud; on the last day of the unit (93), the grown-up uses the same cards to try one missed idea again. At home, the grown-up plays the second buyer using invented cards, never family money; any day is skippable.
- **One projector.** Show one card at a time; on 92 the packet is shown one part at a time.
