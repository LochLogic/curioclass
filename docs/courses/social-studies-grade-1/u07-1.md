# Section 7.1: Who belongs, and what needs attention


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T1.9, this section's share (day 69).** Children "explain that people born in the United States are citizens, others become citizens later, and some residents who are not citizens still belong to the community with rights and responsibilities". All three parts are taught on day 69, with invented people; the whole standard is checked again in item 1 (day 79).
- **D4.6.K-2, this section's share (days 70, 71).** "Name and explain problems near and far, and some ways people are working on them": here only a local problem (name it, ask what we need to know) and the efforts already under way (day 71). Near and far, and other places, belong to 7.3 (day 77).
- **Limits that bite here.** Never ask a child, or let a child say aloud, a family's citizenship or immigration status. If a child volunteers it, do not question or record it: say "everyone here belongs" and go back to the made-up cards. Never infer status from a name, a language or how someone looks; no name on a card stands for a route, and the three cards are shuffled each time they are shown. Citizenship is a legal tie, not a prize for helping, and belonging does not depend on it. Birth abroad does not prove a person is not a citizen, and two routes are not all the routes: the cards show two routes and a resident who is not a citizen, and the teacher says so. The problem is the class's own choice; the course takes no side.
- **Connections.** Day 70 returns grade 1 unit 1 (a supporting question asks for a fact), kindergarten's "problem" and "I see ..., I think ..." as review. Day 71 returns the source routine of unit 6 (kind, maker, date, usefulness) as review. Unit 4 uses the word "citizen" for people who vote; here "citizen" means the legal tie, and the teacher listens for the voting link and separates it (adults vote in elections; a class vote is open to every child in the class: from memory, source to check).
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The two routes and why a noncitizen resident belongs. Adult background, not taught to children and not taught as a dispute: the Fourteenth Amendment, the court case that gives every child the right to a public education (Plyler v. Doe, 1982), and the Supreme Court's June 30, 2026 opinion named in unit field 10 (syllabus read 2026-11-14; re-read before use). "Becomes a citizen later" is worded as "follows the steps the law sets, as an adult"; no official page for the steps is named in the unit plan, so that child wording is source to check. The word "right" already means a side in kindergarten (left and right): never put the two on one card, and say which "right" each time.

## 2. Where it sits


Day 69 opens the unit with no readiness check; unit 4's ballot and unit 6's perspective come back in 7.2. Results go to the record sheet (paper mark and spoken why in separate columns) and feed item 1 (day 79) and the response on day 80.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** item 1 (made-up cases: Nola, Paz, Ren) is taught here and scored on day 79 with 7.4; the day 71 row above is also owned here, with its spoken stem split into one ask (rule 7). Marks are recorded as shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Every case is printed "invented for practice" and never returns in teaching.

- **E69, three asks, one at a time.**
  - Mark 1: invented Omar became a citizen as an adult. Circle why: "he followed the steps the law sets" or "he helped the most at school". Answer: the steps. Wrong: "helped the most" (F).
  - Mark 2: invented Sol is a resident who is not a citizen and lives in town. Circle whether Sol can help care for the school garden: yes or no. Answer: yes. Wrong: "no" (A).
  - Spoken (rotating six): "Does Sol have rights at school?" Answer: yes, every child at a public school may learn there; rights do not need citizenship. Wrong: "Only citizens have rights" (N).
- **E70.** Mark: on a new picture, circle the problem: coats on the floor blocking a classroom doorway, or a card that says favorite color. Answer: the coats. Wrong: the color card (M). Spoken (rotating six): "Say one thing we need to find out about the coats." Answer: how many, how often, who uses the door. Wrong: "What is your favorite color?" (M). If the class's own problem is the coats at a door, swap in the reserved alternate (a paint tub left in a hall).
- **E71.** Mark: a fresh invented caretaker note says adults already check a leaking tap. Circle what the note shows: adults checking the leak, or a tap already fixed. Answer: adults checking. Wrong: "a tap already fixed" (J). Spoken (rotating six): "Who is already working on this?" Answer: the caretaker and other adults. Wrong: "Nobody, until children noticed" (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The question board and source routine from units 5 and 6 (a note has a kind, a maker, a date and a use); kindergarten's wonder wall and "I see ..., I think ..." (a picture is read before it is guessed about); the blank claim frame stays on the wall unused until day 75. New and kept to day 78: the dated baseline record begun on day 70 (a drawing and a count, with the date), and the helper web begun on day 71. Nothing is withdrawn here.
- **Key moment 1, day 69: three people, one town.** Must be seen: each child can say who is a citizen from birth, who became one later, and that all three belong with rights and responsibilities. Ideal instrument: three large picture mats and shuffled picture cards. Spec: print fits; no tool; no key tool in this section.
- **Key moment 2, day 70: the baseline.** Must be seen: the "before" is a dated drawing and a count the class made, so day 78 can compare. Ideal instrument: the real place and a paper record sheet. Spec: paper fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Three hand-drawn cards on the floor, three sheets of paper as mats, a block and a cup per child.

## 6. Days

## Day 69


- **3 min. Reopen.** Post the compelling question, "How can our class help our community?" Say aloud: every person on these cards is made up. Kindergarten's "help (make a problem smaller)" is review.
- **6 min. Hook: neighbors at a library.** Three picture cards, one actor each, read aloud in a sentence: Ari shelves books; Bea reads at story time; Cy rolls the returns cart. Children point and say who helps and what they do. No route is named yet.
- **8 min. Teaching first.** The three mats. Ari was born here and is a citizen from birth. Bea became a citizen later, as an adult, by following the steps the law sets. Cy lives here, is a resident, goes to the public school and is not a citizen. Two picture cards each for a right (learn at school; be safe) and a responsibility (respect others' rights; follow the school rules): all three have them, and all three belong. Say: we cannot tell by looking; the card tells us because we made it up.
- **5 min. Pairs sort.** Fifteen pairs sort six new invented cards onto the three mats (six cards, about 50 seconds a card). The teacher listens to a rotating six pairs.
- **3 min. Belonging talk.** Partners each say one thing Cy could do at our school, one turn of about 30 seconds each; the teacher hears the pairs of the rotating six.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E69: two marks for everyone (2 minutes); the rotating six are heard (6 children, about 30 seconds each, 3 minutes) while the others draw one way to help at a library.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the mats and the routes; the cards hold the cases. Tool: None. By hand: mats, cards, paper. Evidence: E69 (item 1).

## Day 70


- **5 min. Warm-up and repair.** The question and the board. If many missed errors A, N, F on day 69, re-sort the three cards here, inside these 5 minutes; otherwise a small group meets later.
- **7 min. Teaching first: notice, then guess.** An invented picture of a class plant that droops. "I see pale soil and drooping leaves" is noticing; "Someone forgot" is guessing and names a person. Six statement cards are sorted with thumbs (review of "I see ..., I think ..."). A problem is a place or a thing, never a child.
- **5 min. Choose our problem.** The teacher shows three picture cards of real, safe school spots (for example: the recess ball bin is often empty; the art tubs get mixed up; the reading corner is noisy); none may be litter, books, or any case used in an exit (a coat doorway, a leaking tap, a table, shelf labels, signs, pencils, benches, a sink line). Each child drops one block in a closed cup under a card or passes; the teacher counts aloud with the class (30 blocks, about 3 minutes); a tie is settled by agreeing which goes first, and the other stays on the board.
- **4 min. Baseline.** At the chosen place, draw what is there and make one count (how many, in a tally), with the date. The class draws; the teacher takes the count after class if it needs a real time of day (at most 4 minutes of teacher time).
- **5 min. Supporting questions.** The class and teacher write three questions that ask for a fact (how many, how often, who uses it) and add them to the question board. A "what do you like" question goes to the wonder wall instead.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E70: the mark (1 minute); the rotating six heard (3 minutes) while the others draw the place.

Who holds it: the class holds the problem and the board; the teacher holds the count. Tool: None. By hand: block, cup, picture cards, record sheet. Evidence: E70.

## Day 71


- **4 min. Warm-up and repair.** Read the three questions. If many made error M on day 70, sort question cards here (4 minutes).
- **6 min. Teaching first: shows and does not show.** An invented note from a music teacher, dated: "I check the drum skins every Friday and mend any that split." Ask the routine questions, who made it, when, what it is good for (review of unit 6). On a two-sided card: shows, adults checking and mending (underway); does not show, whether every drum is mended.
- **8 min. Our staff note.** The teacher reads a real, dated note or account from an adult at the school about the chosen problem (a printed copy, with the date it was written; if none, the teacher asks one adult for two lines and says so). Fifteen pairs sort four statement cards onto "shows" and "does not show" (about 30 seconds a card). The teacher listens to a rotating six pairs.
- **5 min. Who already helps.** Draw each adult the note names on a helper web, with what they do, in the class's words (names of roles, not children).
- **3 min. What is still unknown.** Each pair offers one thing the note leaves unknown; the teacher adds two to the question board.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** E71: the mark (1 minute); the rotating six heard (3 minutes) while the others draw one adult helper.

Who holds it: the staff note holds the evidence; the teacher holds the board. Tool: None. By hand: statement cards, helper web, printed note. Evidence: E71 (the day 71 row).

## 7. Talk and writing

- **Planned questions.** Day 69, "Who belongs in our community?"; day 70, "What do we see, and what do we need to find out?"; day 71, "What are adults already doing, and what do we not know?"
- **Written task (day 70).** Draw or dictate one fact to find out about our problem, with the date. Look-fors: it asks for something that can be seen or counted; it is about the place, never a child; it is not "which do you like".
- Marks are recorded shown, shown with help, not yet seen; spoken evidence is a separate column.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 69: kindergarten's "help" and "responsible", spoken as review. Day 70: kindergarten's "problem", the block-in-a-cup count and the wonder wall; unit 1's supporting question; "I see ..., I think ...". Day 71: unit 6's source routine (kind, maker, date, use); "evidence" as "what the note shows".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: citizen, resident, right, responsibility, problem, already. Frames: "___ is a citizen because ___." "___ lives here and belongs." "I see ___." "I want to find out ___." Familiar-language rehearsal is allowed.
- **Access.** Large mats; a partner points for a child who cannot sort; the count by sound for a child who cannot see.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token, drawing, dictation or a familiar aid; a private place for the spoken part. Civic understanding is recorded apart from speech and handwriting.
- **Reading load.** None. Everything is read aloud; a child reads at most a word label.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a new card for a person who is a citizen by one route and say what is the same about all three people.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Draw three paper cards for made-up people together and sort them; never use the child's own family. Pick a small place at home or school, draw it, count one thing and write the date. A grown-up reads the note aloud.
- **One projector.** Show the mats and cards on one screen, sorted by pointing; the block-in-a-cup count stays physical.
