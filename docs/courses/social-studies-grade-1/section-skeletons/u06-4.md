## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.1.T3.1** (days 66, 67, 68): Use evidence to explain how people in the United States are united, such as by a shared national history, and how they are diverse in backgrounds, beliefs, and traditions.
- **MA.1.T3.2** (days 66, 67, 68): Explain the meaning of symbols, phrases, and songs that show pride in belonging to the United States.
- **MA.1.T2.5** (days 67): Explain that people in different parts of the world can have different ways of living, customs, and languages.
- **D2.Geo.6.K-2** (days 67): Identify some cultural and environmental features of particular places.
- **MA.1.T3.2a** (days 67): Explain the meaning of national symbols such as the flag, the bald eagle, the White House, and the Statue of Liberty.
- **MA.1.T3.2b** (days 67): Explain the meaning of national words and mottoes, such as "E pluribus unum" ("Out of Many, One") and the "Pledge of Allegiance."
- **MA.1.T3.2c** (days 67): Recognize the tunes and words of patriotic songs such as "America the Beautiful" and "The Star-Spangled Banner," and explain what they mean.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Print photographs, maps, source cards, song words and music, a timeline, blank comparison charts, claim frames and record sheets. Supply paper and crayons. An adult can hum tunes from printed music; no recording or device is required. One shared screen may display the same prints; no student timers.
>
> **Access.** Read everything aloud; require no independent reading. Rehearse "The source shows ___. This supports ___ because ___." Provide familiar-language rehearsal, neutral descriptions, large print and adapted marking tools. Distinguish language gaps from conceptual errors. Extension defends two interpretations of one symbol. At home, the adult takes the discussion partner's role and follows day 68's fresh attempt.
