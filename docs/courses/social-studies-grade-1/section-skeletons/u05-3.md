## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MA.1.T2.4** (days 52, 54): Locate and explain physical features such as continents, oceans, rivers, lakes, and mountains on maps, and make maps of local places.
- **MA.1.T2.1** (days 52, 54, 55): Explain that a map represents space and helps people find locations and features.
- **MA.1.T2.2** (days 53, 54, 55): Use the cardinal directions to find and describe places on a map, locate major cities and capitals, and investigate why they became important.
- **D2.Geo.3.K-2** (days 53, 54): Use maps, globes and simple models to identify cultural and environmental features of places.
- **D4.1.K-2** (days 53): Make an argument and give reasons for it.
- **MA.1.T2.3** (days 54): Explain that a capital is the city where a state or nation's government is centered.
- skill ss1-landforms-waters (grade 1)
- skill ss1-map-represents (grade 1)
- skill ss1-directions-capitals (grade 1)
- skill ss1-claim-reasons (grade 1)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Supporting questions.** Co-write: What does a map show (days 44 to 46)? How do directions and capitals help us locate places (47 and 48)? Why did cities grow here, and who lived here before European colonists (49)? What do land and water tell us (50 to 52)?
> - **Teaching.** Model overhead representation on day 44, keys on 45, directions on 47 and a sourced claim on 49. Step back on 52 and 53. Before reading each source, name its kind, maker, date, purpose and usefulness. Teach map, key, symbol, globe, location, cardinal directions, capital, continent, ocean, river, lake, mountain and harbor with pictures and gestures.
>
> - **Carried.** Keep unit 4's question board, source routine and blank claim frame. Return to governments on day 48. Tie symbols to observed places on 45 and physical features on 51; revise keys on 52. Introduce compass directions on 47, return on 48 and 52, and cover completed examples on 53. Keep each new map's key and direction indicator visible. Calendar routines continue.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

<!-- skel -->
- days.csv names no tools, supplies or safety lines on these days.

Unit plan field 8 (tools and supplies across the unit):

> **Tools: None.** Supply printed maps, source summaries, paired globe views, local park photographs, paper relief shapes, tokens, place cards, the record sheet, paper, crayons and blank claim frames. A globe is optional. One shared screen may show the prints; no student timers. At home, map the supplied park views with an adult; no travel.
>
> **Access.** Read sources aloud. Rehearse "The map shows ___. The account says ___. This helped because ___." Offer familiar-language rehearsal, textured outlines, spoken descriptions and adapted marking tools. Distinguish limited English from location errors. Extension defends two causes from the same sources.
