# Scope and sequence: Grade 1: Leaders, Helpers and Communities

Course `social-studies-grade-1`, grade 1, draft 2026-11-14. 96 course days (91 in eight units, 3 course-level check
days, 2 respond days), three lessons a week of about 30 minutes over 31 weeks. The days are in
data/plans/social-studies-grade-1/days.csv; `school_day` follows the calendar's even spread (scripts/frame.py), so
the days.csv and data/plans/calendar.csv agree. No base text, so no source map.

**What holds every day.** Every source, map and task is printed: a day assumes at most one shared classroom device
and always has a paper path. Every unit opens with its compelling question and the supporting questions the class
breaks it into (taught in unit 1). Units 1 and 2 close with the spoken claim and reason; from unit 3 the claim,
reason and closing are written (or dictated). The end-of-unit assessment in every unit is an observation task with a
short drawn or dictated piece (assess `observe`) done from a source not seen before, with one respond day after it
(the response, counted once).

**Readiness.** Every kindergarten row grade 1 builds on was used the year before, so no unit holds a readiness check
(frame-write lesson 16); each opens with a retrieval task inside its first day, named in the day's note. The frame's
Days bullet still says "readiness check" (see For the lead).

## Unit 1. Our groups (days 1 to 10, weeks 1 to 4)

*What makes a group work well?* Anchor MA.1.T1.1, MA.1.T1.2; C3 D1.2, D1.3, D2.Civ.2, D2.Civ.6.

Supporting questions are taught first (days 1 and 2), since every later unit uses them; then what a group gives and
how its rules serve its purpose (days 3 to 5, the class sorting its own rules); then roles toward a shared goal and how
a community gets common work done (days 6 to 8, ending in a planned class task).

- Assessment: observation task on day 9 (a new question broken into supporting questions; roles and a rule with its
  reason from a photograph not seen before); respond day 10.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, families as groups of many shapes (New York grade 1), inside day 3 at no added day; no
  child describes their own family.

## Unit 2. Clues from the past (days 11 to 19, weeks 4 to 7)

*How can we find out what our school was like long ago?* Anchor MA.1.T3.3; C3 D1.5, D2.His.1, D2.His.9.

The calendar comes first (days 11 to 13) so it runs as a routine from autumn on, and so the timeline (a year as a
row of months) is in hand before sources are dated. Then the kinds of sources, which would help answer the class's
question, and then and now of the school or town (days 14 to 16), closing with the spoken claim (day 17).

- Assessment: observation task on day 18 (dated events on a calendar strip; the kind of source and what a photograph
  not seen before shows); respond day 19.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, everyday life then and now in getting around, school and dress (California grade 1),
  inside day 16 at no added day.
- Leans: RI.1.6 (ELA unit 1, on time). 1-ESS1-2 (science grade 1 unit 8, about week 27, later): social studies
  records the dates and seasons all year, the bridge the frame gives.

## Unit 3. Leaders (days 20 to 31, weeks 7 to 11)

*What makes a good leader?* Anchor MA.1.T1.3, MA.1.T1.4; C3 D2.Civ.1, D2.His.3, D4.2, D4.5.

What a leader is, then supporting questions for the unit (days 20 and 21), then one day for each of the four leaders
from history (22 to 25: Harriet Tubman, Abraham Lincoln, Wilma Mankiller, Cesar Chavez, in time order), then a
comparison chart of two leaders' actions (26). The written claim, reason and closing are taught last (27 to 29),
when the class has evidence to weigh. ss1-leaders carries seven days (two standards and four people studied in
full); the writing row three.

- Assessment: observation task on day 30 (a short read-aloud about a leader not studied: what the leader did, one
  quality and why, in order); respond day 31.
- Candidate settled: honesty and courage in historical stories (California K.1.2) **kept as review** inside days 22
  and 23, no added day. No further grade 1 check-set requirement is known at this layer; the unit plan's comparison
  table records any it finds.
- Leans: RI.1.3 (ELA unit 2 from week 7, on time). W.1.1 (ELA unit 4, weeks 24 to 32, later): days 27 to 29 carry the
  frame's bridge, shared writing with a sentence frame before any independent writing.
- Sensitivity: days 22, 24 and 25 (slavery, the Cherokee Nation today, the farm workers' movement).

## Unit 4. Choosing together (days 32 to 41, weeks 11 to 14)

*What is the fairest way for a group to decide?* Anchor MA.1.T1.5 to MA.1.T1.8; C3 D2.Civ.5, D2.Civ.8, D2.Eco.12.

Why groups need a fair way to decide and what an election is (days 32 and 33), then the secret-ballot class election
on a class matter (34). Elected leaders, government and its services, terms and re-election, and Native nations'
elected leaders today follow (35 to 37), then a re-election judgment and the written answer to the compelling
question (38 and 39).

- Assessment: observation task on day 40 (a ballot tally not seen before; why a secret ballot is fair; an elected
  leader and a service government provides); respond day 41.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, the services a town provides and that people pay for them together (New York grades 1
  and 2), inside day 36 at no added day; it is also D2.Eco.12.
- Own-state method: day 35 (the governor) and unit 5's day 48 (the capital).
- Lean: 1.MD.C.4 (math unit 1, weeks 1 to 4, already taught).

## Unit 5. Maps and places (days 44 to 55, weeks 15 to 18)

*Why did people build towns and cities where they did?* Anchor MA.1.T2.1 to MA.1.T2.4; C3 D2.Geo.3.

What a map is and its key (days 44 to 46), directions, capitals and why cities grew where they did, with the Native
towns and trails often there first (47 to 49), continents, oceans, rivers, lakes and mountains (50 and 51), then a
map of a local place (52) and the written answer (53).

- Assessment: observation task on day 54 (a map not seen before: key, directions, a capital, a continent, an ocean,
  why a town grew there), with the maps of day 52; respond day 55.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, the local community, state and country on a map and a globe, with continents and
  oceans (California grade 1), inside day 50 at no added day.
- Lean: K-ESS3-1 (science kindergarten), a one-question review opening day 51.
- Sensitivity: day 49 (Native towns and trails).

## Unit 6. One country, many people (days 56 to 68, weeks 19 to 22)

*What holds a country of so many different people together?* Anchor MA.1.T2.5, MA.1.T3.1, MA.1.T3.2 with
MA.1.T3.2a to c; C3 D2.Geo.6.

Ways of living in three or four places (not Ghana) come first (days 56 to 58), so the class has seen that one way of
life is not the only one; then how Americans are united and diverse, with a claim and a piece of evidence (59 to 61);
then the symbols, the motto, the Pledge and the songs, each explained for its meaning with evidence (62 to 65), and
the answer that ties them together (66). Every lettered part of MA.1.T3.2 has its own day.

- Assessment: observation task on day 67 (from a photograph and a short text not seen before; a symbol's meaning with
  evidence; a claim with evidence about unity or diversity); respond day 68.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, the varied backgrounds of people in the student's own community (California grade 1),
  from open town data inside day 60, at no added day; no child is asked about their background.
- Sensitivity: days 56, 60 (cultures, Native nations today).

## Unit 7. Helping our community (days 69 to 80, weeks 23 to 26)

*How can our class help our community?* Anchor MA.1.T1.9, MA.1.T1.10; C3 D2.Civ.14, D4.6, D4.7.

Who is a citizen and what makes a good community member, with people who made their communities better (days 69 to
71), then the year's informed action: name a problem, see how people already work on it, hear more than one view,
choose by secret ballot, act through the school (72 to 76), look at the same problem elsewhere while the action
works (77), and look back (78). The class picks the problem; the course takes no side.

- Assessment: observation task on day 79 (a read-aloud not seen before about a community helper); respond day 80.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, a school or neighborhood helper as a short local case (New York grades 1 and 2), inside
  day 71 at no added day.
- Sensitivity: day 69 (citizenship; never a question about status, Plyler v. Doe).

## Unit 8. Earning, buying and saving (days 81 to 93, weeks 27 to 31)

*How should people choose what to buy and what to save?* Anchor MA.1.T4.4 to MA.1.T4.9; C3 D2.Eco.2, D2.Eco.5,
D2.Eco.6, D2.Eco.9, D2.Eco.10.

One story in order: earn (days 81 and 82), buy (83 and 84, a made-up flyer with whole-dollar prices within 20),
choose, with opportunity cost and values-based choices (85 to 87), save, with banks and three jars (88 to 90), then
the written answer (91). Four rows that grow kindergarten's ideas share eleven teaching days by their standards
(choices and saving three each). This is the course's personal finance, inside the row's days, none added.

- Assessment: observation task on day 92 (a flyer and story not seen before); respond day 93.
- Candidate settled: **kept**, specialized jobs and why people specialize (California grade 1), inside day 82 at no
  added day.
- Leans: 1.NBT.B.3 (math unit 4) and 1.OA.A.1 (math units 2 and 6), both already taught.
