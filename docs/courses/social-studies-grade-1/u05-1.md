## Header



## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T2.1, days 44 and 45.** "Explain" that a map represents space and helps people find locations and features: it stands for a real place, drawn from above and made smaller (day 44), and its key says what each symbol stands for (day 45). Both parts of the verb's object are carried: representation, and finding locations and features.
- **D2.Geo.3.K-2, days 45 and 46.** "Use" maps, globes and simple models to identify cultural and environmental features of places. In child words: what people made (houses, fields, a town) and what was already there (land, water, a hill). Day 45 uses the key on three maps; day 46 uses paired globe views, a map and a paper relief.
- **MA.1.T2.2, day 47.** "Use" the cardinal directions to find and describe places on a map. Locating major cities and capitals and why they became important belong to 5.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No scale, projection or measuring. Four directions only, no "northeast". North is where the map's indicator points, never "the top of the page". A symbol means what this map's key says, on this map only. A globe is optional; printed paired views serve. No child's address, home or street is drawn or asked.
- **Connections.** Claim and reason, day 45 (ss1-claim-reasons): the spoken frame "I think ___ because ___" is review of kindergarten (the ledger's claim routine) and of unit 4's blank claim frame (named in the unit plan, not yet in the ledger). Day 44 also asks of the Harbor Map the source question from kindergarten, "who made it, when and why?".
- **Review, not new.** Kindergarten's map, globe, symbol, key, land and water, position words and picture maps (ledger: social-studies-kindergarten u03-1 to u03-3) are named as review in the same words, never retaught.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A map is a view from straight above, made small, that leaves things out. A symbol stands for a place and is not the place. The key is the maker's agreement; the same shape can mean different things on two maps. Cardinal directions belong to the Earth: a compass needle points toward north (from memory), and a map shows north with an indicator that may point anywhere on the page. A raised paper relief shows high and low land; the cut shapes for fields and houses show what people made.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Unit 4's question board and the source routine (unit plan, field 2) stay up. The map-key model (ledger: introduced in kindergarten, extended here): tied to observed places on day 45, kept visible on every map after. The compass-directions model starts on day 47; the direction indicator stays on every map from then on. Calendar routines continue.
- **Key moment 1, day 44: the same objects from above.** Ideal instrument: real objects on a tray, a photograph from above, and a drawing from above. Spec: no tool; print and objects fit. Gaps and asks: none.
- **Key moment 2, day 45: trace from symbol to key and back.** Ideal instrument: large printed maps with a boxed key and movable symbol cards. Spec: print fits.
- **Key moment 3, day 47: finding north in the room.** Ideal instrument: a real compass, read away from steel and screens, with wall signs N, S, E, W. If it will not settle (steel desks, a metal building), take north from the school's printed site plan and its north arrow; say aloud which was used. Spec: no tool; a compass and print fit.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn key boxes, a tray of everyday objects, four paper wall signs, a hand-drawn arrow for the indicator.

## 6. Days

## Day 44


- **6 min. Hook and retrieval.** A tray arrangement (cup, block and book) beside its drawing from above; the compelling question beside the Harbor Map, ours, a schematic of a harbor (ours and approximate; maker, date and purpose said). Not answered. Retrieval, unmarked, spoken one prompt at a time, with an invented playground picture: "What is between the trees?" "What is next to the slide?" "Show what the bench picture stands for." (kindergarten position words and picture maps, review). A miss is repaired in the next part.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** The same tray seen from the side and from above: which shows where each thing sits? A map stands for a place, drawn from above and made small (new: "made smaller"). Match each object to its mark, saying "stands for" (review). The classroom drawn from above: find the door and the rug. Model one claim: "I think this map helps find the door, because the door mark is by the rug mark." Name the tray photograph's maker, date and purpose.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs (15 pairs) place three real objects on a paper tray outline, then match each to its mark on a drawing from above, saying one position word. Hear the rotating six, one spoken ask each, about a minute apiece.
- **3 min. Share.** Two pairs show a match. Add a card to the question board: "What does a map show?"
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E44.

Holds: teacher holds the claim; children hold the objects. Tool: None. By hand: tray, objects, drawing cards, Harbor Map. Evidence: E44; record sheet (rotating six).

## Day 45


- **5 min. Reopen.** Hand-back: E44 (A, N); then a fresh tray arrangement, point to its drawing (retrieval from day 44). The repair is at most five minutes and fits here.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Three maps, each with a boxed key: the school map (ours, made from the school; symbols tied to places the class has seen, shown by photograph), an invented park map (Maple Park, said aloud to be invented) and an invented town map (Linden). Trace from a symbol to the key and back (review: the key). The same shape, a square, means a picnic table in the park and a school on the town map: read each map's key. Model "The map shows ___. I can find it because the key says ___."
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs use the Maple Park map, key and six place cards: place each card, trace to the key, say "I found ___ because the key says ___". Hear the rotating six on the claim, a minute each.
- **3 min. Share.** One pair per map tells one find; return to the question board.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E45.

Holds: children hold the key cards; the teacher holds the claim frame. Tool: None. By hand: three maps, key cards, tokens. Evidence: E45; record sheet.

## Day 46


- **5 min. Retrieval.** Before instruction, spoken and unmarked: "Which model shows the whole round Earth? Find land and water on both." (kindergarten maps and globes, review) with a printed pair of opposite globe views. Then, from unit 4: "What detail in the term record supported your judgment?" A miss: compare the globe views with a flat map in the first teaching minutes.
- **5 min. Step back.** The teacher models once, with an invented valley (Meadow Valley) map, the paired views and a low paper relief: land or water, town or farmland; made by people or already there.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs: a printed paired globe view card (color a land area, circle a water area; find the labeled city), then the Meadow Valley map. Six tables share one paper relief; each pair has two minutes with it while others work on print. Hear the rotating six, a minute each, on the island map and relief.
- **8 min. Pairs sort.** Picture cards into "made by people" and "was already there" on the map and relief; the teacher meets the group that missed retrieval.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E46.

Holds: pairs hold the cards. Tool: None. By hand: globe views, map, relief, cards. Evidence: E46; record sheet.

## Day 47


- **5 min. Retrieval.** Spoken, one prompt each, unmarked: an invented school address, "18 Birch Street, Milltown, Colorado, United States" on cards: "Point to the part that names the street." Then the town, the state, the country (kindergarten address, review; no child's address is asked). A miss: rebuild with labeled nested cards in a small group during supported work.
- **11 min. Teaching first.** Four wall signs set with the compass (or the site plan). All stand and point to north, south, east, west together; no child is singled out. The direction indicator on the school grounds map: turn the map, the indicator turns. Invented Cedar Park, north pointing right: "The swing is south of the pond." Model one: "I know because the indicator says north is this way, not up the page."
- **8 min. Supported work.** Pairs with a paper map with an indicator and tokens: put a token on the place east of a marked place; say its direction from another. Hear the rotating six, a minute each.
- **4 min. Written task and share.** Each child writes one direction word beside a drawn arrow on a mini map (or an adult scribes) and shows one place.
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E47.

Holds: the class holds the wall signs. Tool: None. By hand: compass, signs, maps, tokens. Evidence: E47; the mini map; record sheet.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 44, "How could a map help someone find a shelf?"; day 45, "How can you tell what this means?"; day 47, "Which direction is it from the well?"
- Written task, day 47: one direction word and an arrow on a mini map; an adult scribes the sentence "The ___ is ___ of the ___." for a child who needs it.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the place sits where it was on the map; the direction word fits the indicator, not the page top; the symbol matches the key.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: map, key, symbol, globe, land, water, north, south, east, west. Frames: "The map shows ___." "___ means ___ because the key says so." "The ___ is ___ of the ___." Familiar-language rehearsal first; a child's language may name a direction before English does.
- **Access.** Raised outlines and textured symbols, large print, the key as a separate card. A child who cannot point uses a partner's finger or eye gaze; wall signs are touched or named from a chair. Patterns, not color alone.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each prompt aloud; accept a point, token, drawing, dictation, familiar-language speech or supported communication. Separate limited English from a wrong location.
- **Reading load.** None; labels are one word, read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second place and say two directions from it, or draw a key for three symbols of the child's own for one map.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table, put a cup, a spoon and a book on a mug-mat tray. Draw them from above, then from the side; make a key. Label the table edges N, S, E and W with a phone compass held away from metal; ask which way the spoon is from the cup. Never draw the child's home.
- **One projector.** Show a map and its key together; pairs use paper copies and trace with a finger. Turn the on-screen indicator for day 47.
