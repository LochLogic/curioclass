# Section 4.2: Elected leaders, government and citizens

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-1/4/s/4.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-1 u04-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T1.7, this section's share (day 35).** Children "name some leaders chosen by elections, such as the President, the Governor of Massachusetts, or a team captain, and explain what they do". Ours: the President, the governor of the student's own state, a mayor and a team captain, each matched to a place and a job. The standard names Massachusetts; the unit plan makes it the student's own state.
- **D2.Civ.5.K-2 (days 36 and 37).** Children "explain what a government is and some things it does": makes and carries out a community's rules, provides things and work everyone uses.
- **D2.Eco.12.K-2 (day 36).** Children "give examples of goods and services that governments provide": a thing (a good) and work done (a service), paid for together through taxes. No tax sums.
- **MA.1.T1.8 (day 37).** Children "explain that people in a town, city, or nation are citizens whose rights and duties include electing leaders for set terms and judging whether to re-elect them". Section 4.3 uses the judging on day 38.
- **Limits that bite here.** Offices, not people: no office holder is praised or criticised, and no current officeholder is named. Not all leaders are elected; some helpers are hired. A mayor is elected in many cities, not all. Government is many people sharing tasks, not one leader. Belonging to a community is not the same as being a citizen who may vote: children and many neighbors belong and have rights, while voting has rules. The teacher says this once, never asks any child's or family's status, and never has a child report on home or money. Native nations are named by their own names, in the present tense; the Navajo Nation oral summary is ours, from its own pages, and day 37 waits for the sensitivity read.
- **Connections.** Unit 5, day 48, picks up the capital as where a government meets. Unit 7, day 73, uses a secret ballot to choose an action: a vote, not an election.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Offices and the places they lead (M). That an elected leader and a hired helper both work for the town (D). That government is shared work (K). That the shared money is called taxes, with no amounts and no questions about families (L). That a business or a family is not government (S). Belonging versus voting (E, N). That a term ends and voters judge the work (O). That one Native nation's way is not every nation's (G, P).

## 2. Where it sits


Section 4.1 ends with the class's own election; this section opens day 35 by asking who is chosen in real elections and ends on day 37 with the end of a term. Section 4.3 opens day 38 with judging a made-up leader's term. No readiness items; this section's results go to the record sheet and the day 38 warm-up.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none (items 1 to 4 belong to section 4.3). The three exits are this section's evidence. Each case is printed "invented for practice" and never returns in teaching. Governor of "our state" is the student's own state; the exit uses an invented story.

**Exit questions.** Each has a mark every child makes and a spoken why heard from a rotating six. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E35.** Case: a story card says "The governor asked everyone to stay off the icy roads." Mark: circle the place the governor is in charge of, from three picture cards: a city, a state outline (key), a country on a globe. Wrong: the city card (M); the country card (M). Spoken: "Do voters choose the town librarian too? Say why." Answer: no; a librarian is hired for the job, and a helper is not always chosen by an election. Wrong: "Yes, voters choose every helper" (D).
- **E36.** Case: a card for an invented town says "The town government runs the town pool, makes the pool rules and pays workers to keep it clean." Mark: circle how the workers are paid, from three cards: many people paying together, called taxes (key); nobody pays, because the pool pays itself (L); the mayor pays alone (K). Spoken: "Tell one thing the town government does for the pool." Answer: makes the rules, or pays workers who keep it clean. Wrong: "The mayor does it all alone" (K); "Nothing, it just runs free" (L).
- **E37.** Case: an invented city's mayor has done good work and people like the mayor; the set time for the job is ending. Mark: circle what happens next, from two cards: voters choose again, and may pick the same mayor or a new one (key); the mayor keeps the job because people like the mayor (O). Spoken: "Who decides whether the mayor keeps the job?" Answer: voters, in a new election, thinking about the mayor's work. Wrong: "The mayor decides" (O).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Unit 3's question board and the unit question stay up; unit 3's source questions are asked of each card (who made it, when, how it helps). Our Decision Record gains the day 35 office cards. The job card from day 34 returns as the model for "what each office does". The blank claim frame waits for 4.3.
- **Key moment 1, day 35: matching an office to its place and job.** Must be seen: place card, office card and job picture set together. Ideal instrument: movable cards. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 36: tracing shared money.** Must be seen: coins from many people into one jar, then to the park and its workers. Ideal instrument: a drawn jar and cards. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **Key moment 3, day 37: a term on the class calendar.** Must be seen: start card, end card, then "choose again". Ideal instrument: the class calendar strip. Spec: print fits.
- **A room with nothing.** Hand-drawn cards, a jar or box, the calendar on a sheet.

## 6. Days

## Day 35


- **4 min. Warm-up and repair.** If many missed B or J on E34, show that error's wrong example beside the right one (field 4 of 4.1).
- **4 min. Four places.** Picture cards: a team, a city, our state, a country. Who is in charge of each? Offices named: team captain, mayor, governor, President. Each is chosen by voters in an election, as the garden captain was.
- **7 min. What each does.** The job card shows each office's job: the captain organizes the team's play; the mayor leads a city's work (many cities elect one); the governor leads the state's work and sees that state laws are carried out; the President leads the country and sees that national laws are carried out, for a set term of four years. The teacher says the last two in their own words; the term and duty come from Article II, sections 1 and 3 (source to check). Our governor's title and state outline come from the state's directory page; the examples are the Governor of Colorado and the Governor of Washington, offices only.
- **5 min. Not every helper is voted for.** A voted mayor beside a hired bus driver, both working for the town; sort four helper cards, voted or hired.
- **5 min. Match.** Pairs match four office cards to a place card and a job picture (15 pairs; the class checks one match at a time by pointing).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E35: mark for everyone (2 minutes); the rotating six say why (30 seconds each, 3 minutes) while the rest draw a leader's job.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the job card; pairs hold their matches. Tool: None. By hand: place, office and job cards, helper cards. Evidence: E35.

## Day 36


- **4 min. Warm-up and repair.** If many missed M or D on E35, use its field 4 repair.
- **8 min. Teaching first.** An invented town card: the government makes rules (the park closes at dusk), workers carry them out, and it provides a school, a road mended, a park bench and a fire station. Government is many leaders and workers sharing the tasks, not one person. A bench is a good (a thing); mending the road is a service (work done). Words: government, good, service.
- **6 min. Paid for together.** A drawn jar: many grown-ups put coins in, called taxes, and the government uses them for what everyone uses. No sums, and no child says what any family pays. Source: the unit plan's framework comparison; source to check.
- **7 min. Supported work.** Sort six cards: provided by the government (a bench, road mending, a fire station) or not (a toy shop, a bakery, a neighbor's swing); name each provided card a good or a service (3 minutes). Then each child draws one thing everyone in a town uses, with a label word (4 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E36, as day 35.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the town card and jar. Tool: None. By hand: town card, sort cards, coins, jar. Evidence: E36; the drawing.

## Day 37


- **4 min. Warm-up and repair.** If many missed K, L or S on E36, use its field 4 repair.
- **6 min. Citizens.** A town picture: everyone who lives here belongs and has rights, such as learning at school and being heard. A citizen is a member of a town, city or nation under its rules; citizens who are old enough and follow the voting rules choose the leaders. Children cannot vote in government elections yet; a class may vote, as we did. Said once; no child's or family's status is asked.
- **6 min. Terms.** An invented pond-watch captain's term on the class calendar strip: election day card, end-of-term card, then "voters choose again". Word: term (a set time a leader serves). Re-elect: choose the same leader again after judging the work. Both reviewed on day 38.
- **7 min. Nations choose today.** An oral summary of ours, with a plain name card: the Navajo Nation has its own government today; its people elect a President, a Vice President and Council delegates. Other nations have their own governments, chosen in their own ways. Source: the Nation's pages (navajo-nsn.gov, navajonationcouncil.org), read 2026-11-14; source to check, and the sensitivity read comes first. No role play and no pictures of people.
- **2 min. Tomorrow.** Tomorrow we read a made-up leader's record and decide whether to choose again.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E37, as day 35.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the card for the nation. Tool: None. By hand: town picture, calendar strip, name card. Evidence: E37.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 35, "Which offices are chosen by voters, and which helpers are hired?"; day 36, "What does a government do, and who pays?"; day 37, "Why might voters choose again?"
- Written task, day 36: draw one thing everyone in a town uses, with one label word.
- Judged by the teacher with two look-fors: a shared thing, not a private one; a label word that names it.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 35:** the day 34 job card; E34's wrong example if needed.
- **Day 36:** the office cards; the question board.
- **Day 37:** the term strip on the class calendar; "who made it, when, how it helps" for the town and nation cards.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: mayor, governor, President, government, citizen, term, election. Frames: "The ___ leads the ___." "Voters choose again because the term ends."
- **Access.** Movable cards and a large-print town card; an adult moves cards as the child points; a child may pass.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token, drawing, dictation or a familiar aid; a private exchange for the spoken part.
- **Reading load.** None beyond one label word; every card is read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Compare a team captain's term with a mayor's: what is the same and what differs.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Lay out the four place cards and match each to an office; draw the shared jar. No family details are asked.
- **One projector.** Show the same prints; day 37's card shows the name in text only.
