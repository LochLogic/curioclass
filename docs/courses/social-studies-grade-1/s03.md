## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 20.** Show invented drawings: a principal listens and organizes helpers; another shouts orders while others do everything. Ask the compelling question before retrieving caring characters.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write on day 21: What responsibility does a leader take (answered day 20, revisited day 26)? What did each historical leader and their group change (days 22 to 25)? Which actions show good leadership (day 26)? List needed facts and words beside each.
- **Closing, day 29.** Write or dictate a claim, reason and closing from two leaders' action records. Courage, listening and organizing are defensible emphases when supported by details from the sources below.
- **Teaching.** Model questioning on day 21, ordered explanation on day 27 and shared writing on day 28; step back on day 29. Before reading, identify source kind, maker, old or new, and usefulness. Compare accounts of each episode. Teach leader, member, responsibility, organize, guide, quality, evidence and reason through actions.

**Examples and response within teaching.** A, day 20: contrast "A principal is above the school" with "A principal belongs to the school community and organizes its work." Sort member, role and responsibility cards. B, day 26: contrast "Tubman was brave because she was good" with "Returning to help others despite danger supports courage." Link an action card to a quality, then practise a closing. C, day 27: contrast "They counted, then voted" with "They voted, then counted, so they knew the result." Reorder three pictures and remove an unrelated one. D, day 25: contrast "Mankiller alone made the youth council, and the nation disappeared" with "Young people, Mankiller and the Tribal Council cooperated; the Cherokee Nation governs itself today." Compare the dated history with the nation's current account. E, day 28: contrast "I like that" with "What happened next?" Practise asking about an explanation and answering from it. More than a third sharing an error means the next day's opening models the correction for everyone; otherwise guide a group while others retrieve earlier sources. Use five to ten existing minutes, followed by a fresh attempt. Never rehearse assessment cases.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

**Tools: None.** Print sources, original read-alouds, drawings, timeline strips, action charts, blank writing frames and record sheets. Use paper and crayons. One optional shared screen shows these same prints; no student timer. At home, the adult models and serves as discussion partner.

**Language and access.** No independent reading is required. Rehearse: "A good leader ___. The source tells ___. This matters because ___." Accept familiar-language explanations before English rehearsal. Use large, high-contrast cards and spoken descriptions. For extension, support two different qualities from the same actions.
