# Section 1.1: Big questions and small ones

<!-- skel -->
Grade 1: Leaders, Helpers and Communities. Unit 1, Our groups. *What makes a group work well?* (days 1 to 10): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 2.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (1, 2). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

Children read very little. Every stem is spoken by the teacher with picture cards; answers are spoken, pointed to, sorted or drawn, with at most a word label. Everything pictured is invented, and the teacher says so aloud.

- **D1.2.K-2, this section's share.** "Name the subject ideas that connect to a big inquiry question." Day 1 names the big question and its three smaller ones. Day 2 names four subjects (history, geography, civics, economics) by what each asks, and ties the unit's question to civics. Scored later, on day 9 item 1.
- **D1.3.K-2, this section's share.** "Name the facts and concepts that connect to a smaller supporting question." Day 1 breaks a big question into smaller ones; day 2 names, for each of the three supporting questions, one fact to find out and one important word. The children say the facts and words; they do not write the questions (the course's own row, ss1-supporting-questions, is taught here in speech).
- **Limits that bite here.** The four subjects are named by what they ask, never defined. Only the subjects' names are new vocabulary; the three important words (belong, rule and duty, role and goal) are only named here and taught on days 3, 4 and 6. No child reports on home or family. A wrong answer is voiced by "someone might say", never a named child.
- **Connections.** Review of kindergarten's wonder wall and "I wonder" question (ledger, social-studies-kindergarten u01-1). Day 2's four subject questions use the paper mural of day 1, as the unit plan says.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between a question and a statement; why a big question needs smaller ones; that a subject is a way of asking, so one question can fit two subjects and we accept either with a reason.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The compelling question, posted day 1 on a strip; the question board, where each day's smaller questions go on cards with a picture; the kindergarten wonder wall is kept beside it. The teacher holds both; children hold only the card they place. Nothing is withdrawn in this section.
- **Key moment, day 1: the question board.** Ideal instrument: a large paper board with a place for the big question and rows for smaller ones, cards that tape on and off. Spec: paper, tape, picture cards; no tool.
- **Key moment, day 2: four subject cards.** Ideal instrument: four large picture cards, one per subject, each with a hand motion (history, a hand waved back over the shoulder; geography, a finger pointing to a place; civics, two hands held together; economics, a few fingers held up for what we have). Every child holds a small copy and shows it at once, so the teacher sees all 30 in one look. Spec: paper and a marker.
- **A room with nothing.** Two sheets of scrap paper stand for the mural panels; chart paper is the board; four hand motions and four drawn symbols stand for the subject cards.

## 6. Days

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Big and small questions: the compelling question goes up, and the class breaks it into supporting questions
- note: Retrieval task: kindergarten's "I wonder" question and wonder wall (ssk-asking-questions)
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **6 min. Hook: the two mural panels.** Say first: "We made these pictures for our class; the children in them are made up." Show two panels of a group making a paper mural: in one every member reaches for the same sheet of paper; in the other members share tasks. Post the compelling question, "What makes a group work well?", on its strip and ask which panel is the group working well, by pointing. 30 children point at once; no one speaks yet.
- **5 min. Wonder wall (review).** Ask: "What do you wonder about the mural group?" Any volunteer's wonder is written beside a picture; at most eight wonders at about 35 seconds each; a child may pass. If a child gives a statement, restate it and invite "why" or "how"; that child joins error A's repair on day 2.
- **7 min. Big and small questions.** Worked case, ours and invented: the class block corner. Big question: "How can the block corner work well for everyone?" Say it is too big to answer at once, so we ask smaller questions that help: "Who uses the blocks?" "Where do the blocks go when we finish?" "How do we take turns on a big build?" Teach "big question" and "supporting question" with a hand motion (big arms, small hands). Partners tell each other one more smaller question for the block corner; 15 pairs, teacher listens to three pairs.
- **6 min. Our supporting questions.** Show the three cards the unit plan co-writes: "What does belonging give members?" "Why do groups need rules and duties?" "How does each role help a shared goal?" Read each aloud; each goes on the question board under the compelling question with a picture clue. Children echo each once. Say which day answers it (days 3, 5 and 8).
- **6 min. Exit ticket.** X1: mark by every child (2 minutes), spoken for the first six (6 children at about 40 seconds is 4 minutes, while the others draw the walk on the back of the card).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board and the strip. Tool: None. By hand: pointing, drawing, circling. Evidence: X1.

## Day 2

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Which part of social studies a question belongs to (history, geography, civics, economics), and the facts and words each supporting question needs
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Reopen and repair.** Read the board and wall. If many missed X1 (error A), run the four-minute repair of field 4; otherwise a small group has it later in the sort. At most one repair.
- **8 min. Four ways to ask.** Hold up the mural panels again. Four cards, four hand motions: history asks what it was like before (what was on this wall before the mural?); geography asks where (where on the wall does the mural go?); civics asks how a group works together (who does which job so the mural gets finished?); economics asks what we have and what we choose (there is one box of paint; how do we share it?). Say the sentence "These are four ways to ask," not four kinds of children. Class does each motion twice.
- **7 min. Which way is this question?** Teacher reads four new mural question cards aloud, one at a time; all children show the subject card they think fits and say why in pairs for ten seconds; the teacher names two reasons heard. A card may fit two subjects; accept either with a reason.
- **6 min. What we need to find out.** For each of the three supporting questions, model one fact and one important word on picture-and-word cards: belonging (a fact: what members get from the group; the word: belong); rules and duties (a fact: what a rule is for; the words: rule, duty); roles (a fact: what each member does; the words: role, goal). Tie each to civics. Children echo the word and place the card. The words are taught on days 3, 4 and 6; here they are only named.
- **4 min. Exit ticket.** X2: every child shows a subject card (1 minute); X2b spoken for the second six (6 children at about 30 seconds is 3 minutes, while the others sort their four subject cards into a row).

Who holds what is carried: the teacher holds the board; each child holds a small set of the four subject cards. Tool: None. By hand: showing cards, echoing, sorting. Evidence: X2.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Questions: day 1, "What do you wonder about the mural group?" and "What is one smaller question?"; day 2, "Which way of asking is this question, and why?"
- Written task: none. The teacher writes each volunteered wonder and each child's smaller question on a card, word for word.
- The teacher looks for: (1) a question, not a statement; (2) a smaller question that helps the big one; (3) a subject choice with any reason; (4) the support used, noted as information.

## 8. Practice that returns

- Day 1: kindergarten's wonder wall and "I wonder" question as review. Day 2: error A repair; day 1's board and the block corner. The three supporting questions return on day 3 (belonging), day 5 (rules and duties) and day 8 (roles).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: question, big, small, history, geography, civics, economics. Frames: "My question is ___." "I need to find out ___." Accept a home language, a point, or a drawing with a spoken label.
- **Access.** Large print and tactile card edges; hand motions can be done sitting or by pointing. A communication aid or a pointed card is an answer.
- **Check accommodations.** Every exit is read aloud; a child may mark, point or answer privately.
- **Reading load.** None for children; the teacher reads all text.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask a child to make a second smaller question for the block corner and say which subject it asks.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** At the table: show two pictures of a group making something together; ask "What do you wonder?"; then ask a bigger question and help break it into two small ones. Day 2: show four question cards and ask which way each asks.
- **One projector.** Show panels and cards on one screen. Children mark on printed cards, never on the screen.
