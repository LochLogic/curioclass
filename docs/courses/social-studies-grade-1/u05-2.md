- **MA.1.T2.3, day 48.** "Explain" that a capital is the city where a state or nation's government is centered. This section carries the whole standard: the nation's capital, the class's state capital, and the idea that a capital is a city.
- **MA.1.T2.2, days 48 and 49.** "Use" the cardinal directions to find and describe places on a map (day 48, one direction word on a state outline), "locate" major cities and capitals (day 48), and "investigate why they became important" (day 49: a harbor, a river crossing, a seat of government, and the Native towns and trails that were often there first).
- **MA.1.T2.4, days 50 and 51.** "Locate and explain" physical features such as continents and oceans (day 50) and rivers, lakes and mountains (day 51) on maps. Making a local map is 5.3.
- **D2.Geo.3.K-2, day 50.** "Use" maps and globes to place our town, state and country.
- **Limits that bite here.** A capital is a city, not a building, and not necessarily the largest. No list of capitals to learn. Continents and oceans are met by name on day 50; placing all seven and five is grade 2's. Rivers, lakes and mountains on day 51 only. A map suggests a reason; an account supports it; some things neither says. Lenape people and nations live today; colonists took land and forced many Lenape people away; land was never empty and removal was never voluntary. No role play of settlers or Native people, no invented Native speech, no costume or ceremony. No child's address.
- **Connections.** Unit 4's leaders and governments, day 48 (unit plan: return to governments; not yet in the ledger). Claim and reason, day 49 (ss1-claim-reasons; review of kindergarten's claim frame). Kindergarten's address rings (town, state, country), day 50 (ledger: social-studies-kindergarten u03-4). Kindergarten's "need" (food, water, shelter, clothing; ledger: u06-2) opens day 51, with the science kindergarten review from the unit plan.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A capital: the city where a government meets; Washington, D.C. is the nation's capital (sources 48E), and each state has one (the teacher verifies the class's state capital on its usa.gov page, 48A to 48C). The government building inside a capital is not the capital. On day 49: a harbor is a sheltered place where ships can stop; the Seaport account (49A) says ships brought goods and people to the city; one history page (49F) places the Wickquasgeck trail where Broadway is, so the schematic says "approximate". No village site is drawn. The three Lenape nations' own pages (Delaware Nation, Delaware Tribe of Indians, Stockbridge-Munsee Community, 49C to 49E) are read; the oral summaries are ours; origin narratives and restricted knowledge are left out. Rivers flow from higher land toward lower water (from memory). A map shows where water is; it does not say the water is safe to drink.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: capital, city, government, harbor, continent, ocean, river, lake, mountain, flow. Frames: "The capital is ___ because the government meets there." "The map shows ___. The account says ___. This helped because ___." Familiar-language rehearsal first; names of places stay in their own spelling.
- **Access.** Raised outlines, textured river, lake and mountain shapes, spoken descriptions of each print; accounts are read aloud. A child who cannot point uses a partner's finger or eye gaze.
- **Check accommodations.** Read each prompt aloud; accept a point, token, drawing, dictation or familiar-language speech; separate limited English from a wrong location.
- **Reading load.** None alone; accounts and summaries are read aloud; labels are one word.
- **Extension, same standard.** Find a second reason a river town might grow and say what a map would not tell; or defend two causes from the same sources.
