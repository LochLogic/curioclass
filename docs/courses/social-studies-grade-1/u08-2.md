# Section 8.2: Choosing with limited money

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-1/8/s/8.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-1 u08-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T4.7, days 85 and 86.** "Give examples of choices people make when buying goods and services, and explain that limited money forces those choices." Both parts: examples of choices (85, 86) and the explanation that a limit forces a choice (85, told aloud with tokens).
- **D2.Eco.2.K-2, days 85 and 86.** "Identify the benefits and costs of personal decisions." Benefit is what a choice gives; cost is the tokens it uses and, from 86, what is given up.
- **MA.1.T4.8, day 87.** "Analyze examples of voluntary buying choices, such as picking a company that treats workers well or protects the environment." Analyze means: say what each shop does, who might choose it and why, and why neither choice is wrong.
- **Limits that bite here.** Every child gets the same paper tokens; "not enough" is a classroom fact about tokens, never a family fact. No coin values. Sums within 20. Values shown as a choice people make, not a cause the class must take up; the teacher stays neutral and no shop is the good one. Shop cards are invented; what a shop says about itself is not proof about a real business.
- **Connections.** 1.OA.A.1 and 1.NBT.B.3 on 85 and 86 (add and compare within 20; math units 2, 4 and 6, already taught). The "hear why someone chose differently" move from kindergarten unit 5 returns on 87.
- **What the teacher page must explain.**
  - Benefit and cost, in grade 1 words: a benefit is what you get from your choice; the cost is the tokens it uses, and the other thing you can then no longer buy.
  - Given up (the economist's word is opportunity cost, said once): rank the choices first, second, third; you pick first and what you give up is the second only. Not the price (M) and not everything left (N).
  - Voluntary: nobody makes a buyer choose a shop; buyers weigh what a shop does, its price and what they care about.
  - Why shop statements are sources: who made the card (the shop), when, what for (to get buyers). Fine to say "a card cannot prove what a real shop does".

Cases for E85 to E87 stay out of the teaching cases (Pilar, Ama, Lena, Kofi, Ines, the bookshops and Shop C and Shop D).

**E85.** Cards: Neri with 5 paper tokens; a show card, 3 tokens; a puzzle card, 4 tokens. Printed "invented for practice".
- Mark (all): "Neri has 5 tokens. Put tokens on what Neri can pay for." Answer: tokens on the show or on the puzzle, not both. Wrong: tokens on both (C).
- Spoken (six, one ask chosen by the record sheet): "What does the puzzle cost Neri?" or "Can Neri still pay for the show after the puzzle?" Answer: 4 tokens, and then there is 1 left, not enough for the show. Wrong: "Neri can still have both" (C).

**E86.** Dev, invented, ranks chalk first, a spinning top second and a marble third, and buys only the chalk.
- Mark (all): "Circle the next best thing given up." Answer: the spinning top. Wrong: circles the marble, or both top and marble (N).
- Spoken (six, one ask): "Why is that the one given up?" or "What is the price of the chalk, and is it what Dev gives up?" Answer: it was the second choice, the next best after the chalk; the price is not what is given up. Wrong: "because the chalk's tokens are gone" (M).

**E87.** Two invented plum stalls, each with a card it made itself: stall A, 6 dollars, plants trees; stall B, 4 dollars.
- Mark (all): "Circle the buyer who might still pay more." Answer: the buyer at stall A, who likes the trees.
- Spoken (six, one ask): "Why might another buyer choose the other stall?" or "Who made each card, and what for?" Answer: the lower price matters to them; the stalls made the cards, to get buyers. Wrong: "they are not nice" (D).

## 6. Days

## Day 85


- **5 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: collect My Flyer Page. 2 minutes: retrieval, kindergarten's needs and wants: "An invented traveler needs drinking water and wants a decoration. Which keeps the body well? Can limited money buy everything?" If wants are taken as unlimited money (C), model a finite token supply before the choice work. 2 minutes: hand-back E84 (K, L): two new price tags and pictures to match. If many missed, the 2 minutes are the repair and retrieval takes 1. Pilar from day 81: 9 tokens, a drum 7, a clock 6, "not enough for both", reminder only.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Ama, invented, has 8 paper tokens. A toy train costs 5 and a whistle costs 6. The teacher counts: 5 and 6 make 11, more than 8, so Ama must choose. Ama picks the train: the benefit is playing with the train; the cost is 5 tokens, and 3 are left, not enough for the whistle. Words with pictures: benefit, cost (the tokens and what you can then not buy), "not enough" (review), choose (review). The teacher models "I choose ___ because ___". Pip says C.
- **13 min. Supported work.** Tables of five, each child with 9 tokens (the same for all). 5 min: a new card pair, Lena, a puppet at 6 and a bird feeder at 5; tokens on the cards show 11 is more than 9. 5 min: each child "buys" one card from a set with tokens and fills a two-box card (what I get; what it cost). 3 min: the six are heard, while the others finish.
- **2 min. Exit.** E85: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: the teacher holds the two cards of Ama's case. Tool: None. By hand: tokens, price cards, two-box cards. Evidence: E85; the Flyer Page is item 2's record, handed in at the start of the day.

## Day 86


- **5 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: return My Flyer Page with written picture feedback. 2 minutes: hand-back E85 (C): new cards and tokens. 2 minutes: post "What does choosing give and give up?" and recall that a choice has a benefit and a cost. If many missed (C), the 2 minutes of recall are the repair.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** The mat. Kofi, invented, has 6 tokens. He ranks three things: a yo-yo first (6 tokens), a paper fan second (5), a bean bag third (4); no two fit in 6 tokens. He can buy the yo-yo (6) and has none left. What he gives up is the paper fan: the next best. Not the bean bag, and not the 6 tokens (that is the price). The teacher says once that grown-ups call this opportunity cost. Pip says M and N.
- **13 min. Supported work.** 5 min: revise My Flyer Page, one change or addition each, while the teacher circulates. 8 min: a new ranked set (Ines, 7 tokens; a bell, a ribbon spool, a sticker sheet) on the mat: rank, choose, name what is given up. Each child fills My Choice Mat (practice and first attempt). The six are heard, 3 minutes inside the 8.
- **2 min. Exit.** E86: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: children hold their own mats. Tool: None. By hand: mats, ranked cards, tokens. Evidence: E86; the mat is item 3's record, collected on 87.

## Day 87


- **5 min. Reopen.** 1 minute: collect My Choice Mat. 4 minutes: hand-back E86 (M, N): a new ranked set, price on one card, given-up on another. If many missed, all 4 minutes are the repair.
- **10 min. Teaching first.** Two invented bookshops, cards made by each shop (the teacher says so aloud). Shop A says its workers have a steady schedule and take turns choosing days off; its book costs 9. Shop B says it recycles its paper bags and takes back old books to give them new readers; its book costs 8. Source questions: who made each card (the shop), when, what for (to get buyers); a card cannot prove what a real shop does. Models: "A buyer might choose A because ...; another might choose B because ..." Both are fine; nobody has to choose either. The teacher models a reasoned claim, "I think a buyer might pick B, because the card says it costs 8." Pip says D.
- **13 min. Supported work.** Pairs, a new card pair (Shop C: a safe, clean break room for workers; Shop D: turns old cloth into new bags). 4 min: each pair tells "a buyer might choose C because ...; another D because ...". 6 min: My Two Shops Page (practice): draw two buyers and their reasons. The six are heard, 3 minutes inside the 6. 3 min: the class says what a seller's card tells and what it cannot tell.
- **2 min. Exit.** E87: stems read, all mark, the six heard.

Holds: the teacher holds the two shop cards. Tool: None. By hand: shop cards, pages. Evidence: E87; the page is item 4's practice record, collected on 88.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: 85, "Can Ama buy both? How do you know?"; 86, "What is given up, and why that one?"; 87, "Why might one buyer pick A and another pick B? Who made the card?"
- Written task, 86: My Choice Mat (pictures, tokens, one word label if any), revised on 88.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: the limit shown with tokens; one benefit; the price in tokens; the next best given up, not the price or all the rest. Accept pointing, dictation or a spoken answer.

## 8. Practice that returns

- **Day 85.** Needs and wants (kindergarten); comparing numbers within 20; price tags from 84. **Day 86.** Benefit and cost; tokens and sums within 20. **Day 87.** The given-up idea from 86; the source questions from 84; the claim frame "I think ..., because ...".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: choose, benefit, cost, given up, next best, shop, price. Frames: "I choose ___ because ___." "I give up ___." "A buyer might choose ___ because ___."
- **Access.** Large ranked cards, tactile tokens, an adult placing cards as the child points, drawing or dictating the page.
- **Check accommodations.** Read stems aloud; accept pointing, placing tokens, drawing or a private turn; unheard stays "not yet seen". Judge economic reasoning apart from arithmetic and motor control.
- **Reading load.** None; shop cards and tags are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Rank three of one's own made-up buys and say which one a different buyer would pick first.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use ten paper tokens and the ranked cards. The grown-up reads the cards and asks the exit questions aloud; never ask what the home buys. At home, the grown-up plays the second buyer with two invented shop cards and asks "why might I choose the other?"; any day is skippable.
- **One projector.** Show one shop or one ranked set at a time; children place paper tokens before the teacher says the answer.
