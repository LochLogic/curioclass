# Section 4.1: Ways to decide, votes and our first election


## 1. Standards and practices


- **MA.1.T1.5, this section's share.** Children "give examples of why groups with differing views need ways to decide" (days 32 and 34) and begin to "explain how voting can make fair decisions or choose leaders" (days 32 to 34). Section 4.3 finishes the explaining on day 39.
- **MA.1.T1.6.** Children "explain that an election is a kind of voting in which people choose their leaders" (day 33), practised on day 34.
- **D2.Civ.8.K-2.** Children "describe" equality (each vote counts the same) and fairness (the same rules for all), and respect for a fair result, which includes saying "I disagree" kindly (days 33 and 34). Respect for a leader's fair authority meets the leader's job on day 35.
- **Limits that bite here.** The day 34 candidates (Suri and Tomas) are made up, never a classmate, a real candidate or a party, so no child loses a vote. An election chooses a person for a job; a vote on a class matter is only a vote. Safety, a child's turn and rights are never put to a vote; the teacher says so on day 33. No child is asked how they voted or how any family votes; a child may pass. "Majority" is said only as "the choice with more votes", with two choices.
- **Connections.** Math 1.MD.C.4, days 33 and 34: totals compared by lining up tally marks under each name, two choices only. Math unit 1 taught it, so it is review and no counting is taught here. Unit 3's leadership words (responsibility, organize, guide, quality), day 34. Unit 1's class garden team, day 34.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** Talk comes first, and a vote is for when views still differ (R, Q). An election chooses a person; a vote on a matter chooses a thing or a plan (A). Each vote counts the same; no one gets extra for age or friendship (I). How the secret ballot runs: private marking, a covered box, two ballot counters, a tally everyone can see; private marking is not a secret count (B). A child can accept a fair result and still say "I disagree" or ask the counters to check (J). The longer line of marks wins (T). The job card limits the leader's job (C).

## 2. Where it sits


Section 4.1 opens the unit from kindergarten's class decision, asked as retrieval on day 32 (no readiness check; unit plan field 4 is retrieval only), and ends with the class's own election. Section 4.2 opens day 35 with leaders chosen by election. Results go to the record sheet (shown, shown with help, not yet seen); the day 32 case record and the day 34 election record go to day 39.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none (items 1 to 4 belong to section 4.3). This section's evidence is the day 32 retrieval record and the three exits. Each case is printed "invented for practice" and never returns in teaching.

**Retrieval record, day 32.** Case: one group wants to paint scenery and another wants to make costumes; there is time and space for both. Prompt: "How could talking help? Give a reason." Record three parts separately: listening, a workable agreement, a reason. Answer: each side hears the other, they agree to do both because both fit, and say why. Wrong: "Vote, the most hands wins" with no listening (R); "The loudest group picks" (Q).

**Exit questions.** Each has a mark every child makes by circling, and a spoken why heard from a rotating six. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E32.** Case: a drum group must pick one of two closing songs, and members disagree. Mark: circle how they decide, from three picture cards: they talk, then vote equally if still apart (key); the loudest member picks (Q); they vote at once, because talking never makes anyone agree (R). Spoken: "Why is that a fair way for them to decide?" Answer: everyone is heard and each counts the same; talking may find a way both like, and a vote is for when they are still apart. Wrong: "The loud one knows best" (Q); "Talking is a waste, it never works" (R).
- **E33.** Case: a chess club votes for its chair; another club votes for its meeting day. Mark: circle the election, from three cards: choosing the chair (key); choosing the meeting day (A); "both" (A). Spoken: "A club chair lets her friends vote two times each. What is the problem?" Answer: not equal; every vote must count the same, with the same rules for all. Wrong: "Friends should get extra votes" (I); "It is fair if the chair is kind" (I).
- **E34.** Case: a new choir elects a conductor by private ballot under equal rules, and one child dislikes the fair result. Mark: circle what that child may do, from two cards: say "I disagree" kindly and accept the result (key); say nothing, because disagreeing is not allowed (J). Spoken: "Why might private marking help a group choose?" Answer: people can choose without pressure, and counters still count every ballot. Wrong: "Private means nobody counts it" (B). Today's own garden tally is separate evidence, watched by the teacher: weighing deeds, one private ballot, a count read together.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Unit 3's question board stays up with the unit question; on day 32 the class adds what it needs to find out. Unit 3's source questions (who made it, when, how it helps) are asked of the day 34 tally. Kindergarten's way is reviewed: listen, ask why, look for a change both accept, vote only if still apart, with the closed cup and pass. Our Decision Record (kindergarten's model) holds the day 32 case record and the day 34 election record. The blank claim frame waits for 4.3.
- **Key moment 1, day 33: reading a tally.** Must be seen: two rows of marks under two names, lined up, the longer row wins. Ideal instrument: paper ballots, a covered bag and a drawn tally. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **Key moment 2, day 34: the secret ballot.** Must be seen: one ballot each, marked in private, dropped in a covered box, counted by two counters. Ideal instrument: folded paper ballots and two covered boxes. Spec: print fits; no tool.
- **A room with nothing.** Scrap slips, a paper bag, a hand-drawn tally.

## 6. Days

## Day 32


- **5 min. Hook.** The teacher says both cases are made up. Case 1: four children share a reading corner; Ana wants cushions by the window and Ben wants the shelf there, and the room fits both. Case 2: a group may hear one storybook in the last ten minutes, Red Fox or Blue Whale, and only one fits. Children point to the case that talking could settle. Post and read aloud the unit question.
- **5 min. Retrieval and repair.** The scenery and costumes case (field 3), told to partners for one minute, then three pairs heard, about three minutes in all. If many miss a part, model it for everyone in the first three minutes of the next part; if not, repair at tables during the sort.
- **9 min. Teaching first.** Two ways, one picture each, puppets speaking one line at a time. Talk: hear each reason and look for a change both accept (case 1). Vote, when still apart and only one can happen (case 2): each child's block into a closed cup, or pass, and the teacher counts aloud five and three; the choice with more votes wins and the other reason stays on the record. Safety and a child's turn are never voted on. Both go on Our Decision Record.
- **6 min. Sort six cases.** Six invented picture cards, three each way, into two hoops: "talk can find a way for both" and "only one can happen, so vote if still apart". Pairs place cards (15 pairs); the class checks one card at a time by pointing.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E32: mark for everyone (2 minutes); the rotating six say why at the teacher's table (30 seconds each, 3 minutes) while the rest draw one way a group can choose.

Who holds it: the class holds the record; puppets hold the reasons. Tool: None. By hand: picture cases, closed cup, blocks, hoops, record sheet naming who was heard. Evidence: retrieval record; E32.

## Day 33


- **4 min. Warm-up and repair.** Read the unit question. If many missed Q or R on E32, show that error's wrong example beside the right one (field 4).
- **8 min. Teaching first.** An invented kite club picks its captain, who carries the supply bag, and also picks its kite color. The captain pick is an election; the color pick is a vote on a matter. Rule card for the election: one ballot each, the same rules for all, private, two ballot counters. A wrong example: the oldest member's ballot counts twice (not equal). Words: election, ballot, equality.
- **6 min. Count the ballots.** Eight folded ballots from a bag; two child counters call each name as the teacher draws a mark under it; the class lines up the rows and reads the longer one, six marks to two. A puppet who wanted the other name says "I disagree, I accept the count, and I may ask the counters to check." Words: tally, majority.
- **7 min. Supported work.** Four cards in pairs: election or vote on a matter (3 minutes). Then each child draws one rule that makes a vote fair, with at most one label word (4 minutes).
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E33, as day 32.

Who holds it: the teacher holds the rule card and tally; counters rotate and are never the candidates. Tool: None. By hand: rule card, ballots, bag, tally sheet, cards. Evidence: E33.

## Day 34


- **3 min. Warm-up and repair.** If many missed A or I on E33, show its wrong example beside the right one (field 4). Read the job card for the garden captain of the class garden team: organizes the watering sheet and asks the group for ideas. The captain's job stays under the rules.
- **5 min. Two candidates.** Deed cards, invented: Suri shares tools and sometimes misses watering; Tomas waters reliably and sometimes forgets to ask for ideas. In pairs, point to the deed that fits "organize" and the deed that fits "ask ideas". Nobody says who they will pick.
- **4 min. Rules and ballots.** The rule card is read aloud. Thirty-six ballots, circles under two pictures. Children mark behind a folder, fold, and drop into one of two covered boxes in two lines of about fifteen (6 seconds a child, 2 minutes). A child may drop a blank slip.
- **6 min. Count and read.** Two counters call each ballot (about 3 seconds each, 2 minutes) as the teacher marks the tally; the class lines up the rows and says the result. A tie: talk two minutes, one new ballot, and if still tied the two made-up candidates share the job in turns. Before day 40 check the count against unit plan field 5.
- **7 min. Look back and record.** Why did our rule make this fair? Which deed might a voter weigh? Record date, job, names, tally and result on Our Decision Record; ask who made it, when and how it helps. A child who disagrees says so kindly.
- **5 min. Exit ticket.** E34, as day 32; the teacher also notes the six's actions in the election.

Who holds it: the class holds the ballot and tally; the teacher holds the job card. Tool: None. By hand: deed cards, ballots, folders, two covered boxes, tally sheet. Evidence: E34; the observed election.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words with pictures: vote, ballot, election, tally, fair, disagree. Frames: "I want ___ because ___." "I disagree, and I accept the count." Familiar-language speech is accepted.
- **Access.** Cards that can be pointed to or moved; an adult can mark a ballot as the child directs, privately; a tactile tally of blocks for a child who cannot see marks; a child may pass.
- **Check accommodations.** Pointing, a token, drawing, dictation or a familiar aid; a private exchange for the spoken part. Civic understanding is recorded apart from speech or movement.
- **Reading load.** None. A label word at most; all cards are read aloud.
- **Extension, same standard.** Make a third case card that talk can settle, and say why it needs no vote.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the folded ballots in a cup at the table: the grown-up hears two reasons, then a made-up pair of names, marks privately, counts. At home, supplied fictional ballots model a larger group's election; no one is asked about family voting.
- **One projector.** Show the same prints as the paper cards; no student timer.
