## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Hook, day 11.** Show Johnston's classroom dog photograph beside a dated class drawing of today's learning space. Ask the compelling question: could this photograph tell us about our own school's past? It records another school. Then retrieve time words.
- **Supporting questions.** Co-write: How do calendars keep track of events (answered day 13)? What kinds of clues survive (day 14)? Which would help our questions (day 15)? What changed and stayed in these examples (day 16)?
- **Closing, day 17.** Speak a claim and reason using a detail from the dog photograph, our classroom record or transport summary. Useful answers favor different sources for different questions. Record words beside drawings; formal written claims begin in unit 3. Another school's photograph cannot establish our school's history. The answer may be a plan of which sources to find.
- **Teaching.** Model dating a calendar entry on day 11 and choosing a source on day 15. Before each reading, name kind, known maker, old or new, and usefulness. Model a claim with evidence on day 16; step back on day 17. Teach calendar, month, year, season, past, present, future, source, photograph and object through pictures and actions.

Only revised records count; day 18's unseen fictional-record prompt checks any missing evidence. On day 18, use five minutes to orient children to the packet and twenty-five for the paper parts and the spoken groups. Every child does each item's paper part.
