# Section 2.2: Kinds of sources, choosing one, and then and now

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#social-studies/social-studies-grade-1/2/s/2.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py social-studies-grade-1 u02-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **D2.His.9.K-2, "Identify different kinds of historical sources."** Six kinds: photograph, letter, object, map, building, story people tell. Day 14 teaches them; days 15 to 17 use them; day 18 checks two.
- **D1.5.K-2, "Decide which kinds of sources would help answer the big and supporting questions."** Day 15 models it for a big question and its smaller questions; days 16 and 17 apply it. No source is read for facts the class cannot check.
- **MA.1.T3.3 and D2.His.1.K-2.** Returned: revised fresh sentences by day 17; a weekly pattern and a dated order on day 18; day 19 repairs.
- **Limits that bite here.** The maker questions stay at "known or not known"; fuller maker work and interviews are grade 2. No child brings photographs or reports on home. Every invented card is labeled "invented for practice" and said aloud. No looks-based claim about who a pictured child is or how they felt. No simulated exclusion.
- **Connections.** RI.1.6 (ELA unit 1) day 14: photographs and objects first, words read aloud. The spoken claim "I think ..., because ..." (kindergarten, kept) on days 16 and 17.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A source is something from the past that survives. The four source questions, in this order each time: what kind, who made it (known or not), old or new, what could it help us find out. A photograph shows what was in front of the camera; the caption, read aloud, tells place, date and maker, and the children name only what they see. One source answers some questions, not all; a photograph of another school shows another school. Credit Frances Benjamin Johnston, Library of Congress (unit plan field 10; rights read, source to check). The day 16 transport summary is ours, from a 1928 State Historical Society of Iowa caption (children at Webster Consolidated School rode a horse-drawn bus; source to check; the image is not reproduced). If a child asks why old schools differ: schools in the past sometimes kept children apart by race, which was unfair, and families worked to change it (unit plan field 10 teacher context; source to check; sensitivity read pending); never guess who pictured children are.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** 4 (D2.His.9.K-2) and 5 (D1.5.K-2); section 2.1 owns items 1 to 3. Day 18 sits all five (day 18 below). Every cell in a row must be present for shown.

**Exit questions.** Every child marks a picture in the last two minutes; the rotating six answer the spoken part at the table during supported work, about 70 to 80 seconds each. All cards are new, invented for practice. One scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. If many miss one, the next day opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E14a.** Mark: two practice cards, a drawn old town map and a recorded person's memory. Circle each one's kind: map, photograph, story people tell. Answer: map; story people tell. Wrong: circles photograph for both (C).
- **E14b.** Spoken: "Does either card prove a real past event?" Answer: no; each says invented for practice. Wrong: "Yes, the map is old so it is real" (I).
- **E15a.** Mark: "How did our school's yard change?" Circle the more helpful source: old yard photographs or a shopping list. Answer: the photographs. Wrong: the shopping list, because it is liked (D).
- **E15b.** Spoken: "Why?" Answer: the photographs could show the yard earlier. Wrong: "A photograph of another school's yard shows ours" (J).
- **E16a.** Mark: two new drawings, a classroom lit by a hanging lamp and one lit by a ceiling light, both with a teacher's table. Circle one thing that stayed the same: the table or the lamp. Answer: the table. Wrong: circles nothing; "nothing stayed" (E).
- **E16b.** Spoken: "What changed between the two?" Answer: how the room is lit. Wrong: "Every school changed in this way" (K).
- **E17a.** Mark: Cedar School cards, a dated gate photograph and a former Cedar pupil's recording about lessons. Circle the source for "What did Cedar's gate look like?" Answer: the gate photograph. Wrong: the account (M), because "I like to listen".
- **E17b.** Spoken: "Which source could help us find out what lessons at Cedar were like long ago, and why?" Answer: the recording, because it tells about lessons. Wrong: "Cedar's photograph shows our gate" (J).
- **E19a.** Mark: "A pretend class repaired books on January 5 and on January 12." Place both repair pictures on a January page, each on its date. Spoken: "Describe the pattern." Answer: one on the 5 and one on the 12; the books were repaired once a week on the dates shown. Wrong: both on one square (A); "every day" (G).
- **E19b.** Mark: a real old school letter is on the table; circle its kind, the photograph card or the letter card. Answer: the letter card. Wrong: photograph, because it is on paper (C).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments

- **Carried.** The question board and the compelling question stay posted. The four source questions are said before each source (day 14 on); the spoken claim "I think ..., because ..." (kindergarten, kept) gains a named source on day 17. The kindergarten then-and-now chart gets a third column, "what we do not know", on day 16. The calendar corner continues: revised sentences collected through day 17. Completed examples are withdrawn on day 18; blank models and access supports stay.
- **Key moment 1, day 16: the chart.** Ideal instrument: a large three-column chart with movable detail cards. Spec: no tool; print fits (unit plan field 8).
- **A room with nothing.** A chart drawn on the board, cards from scrap paper, the printed photograph.

## 6. Days

Each day is 30 minutes (frame). Parts are the lines in bold. No timer is shown to children. Teacher prose stays under 450 words a day. Sources: Johnston's photograph is real and credited; every other card is ours and labeled "invented for practice" or "ours".

## Day 14


- **3 min. Calendar corner.** Date today's square. The six collected on day 13 each revise one sentence from the teacher's picture feedback; the next six give one fresh sentence (about 15 seconds each). The rest draw their square.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E13 (F): order four new dated cards by month, then day.
- **4 min. Retrieval.** Original drawings of a washboard and a washing machine (kindergarten's pair, review only). Ask "What differs?" then "What work do both help people do?" Different tools help wash clothes; do not suggest washboards vanished. A miss is repaired inside teaching with a hand fan and an electric fan, in a small group at supported work if few.
- **7 min. Teaching first.** "A source is something from the past that helps us find out." Photograph first (2.5 minutes): look first, name only what we see, read the caption aloud, ask the four source questions (Johnston is known; old). Then a letter (invented, "Dear Room 4, the garden tools come on Tuesday. From Mo."), an object (a real older object the teacher borrows, source to check), a map (today's yard drawn by the teacher, new), a building (a photograph of our school) and a story people tell (an invented bus driver's account, read aloud), about 50 seconds each, each with kind and old or new. The helper says the children in the photograph are sad (error L); the class repairs it.
- **10 min. Supported work.** Pairs sort twelve cards into six kind boxes and say "This ___ is a ___." The teacher guides retrieval misses for 3 minutes, then hears the rotating six at the table with E14, about 70 seconds each (E14b).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E14a on paper.

Holds: the teacher models the four questions; pairs sort. Tool: None. By hand: cards, kind boxes. Evidence: E14a, E14b. Next day: choosing a source for a question.

## Day 15


- **3 min. Calendar corner.** Date today's square; revise group B's sentences, collect group C's.
- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E14 (C, I, L), whichever is common: sort six new cards, or read a label, or look and name (field 4).
- **8 min. Teaching first.** The big question, "What was our school like long ago?", is too big for one source, so it needs smaller questions: What was in the children's room? How did children get to school? Where were the doors and rooms? For each, model the choice with the frame "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___." A photograph helps with the room; a story people tell helps with getting to school; a map or building helps with doors. The dog photograph is another school: it shows what one old classroom looked like, not ours. The helper picks a photograph for every question because it likes photographs (error D); the class repairs it.
- **12 min. Supported work.** Pairs match six question cards to a kind card that could help and say the frame; one question no kind on the table can answer, and the pair says "we would look for ___". The teacher hears the rotating six at the table with E15, about 70 seconds each (E15b).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E15a on paper.

Holds: the teacher holds the frame; pairs hold the match. Tool: None. By hand: question and kind cards. Evidence: E15a, E15b. Next day: then and now.

## Day 16


- **3 min. Calendar corner.** Date today's square; revise group C's sentences, collect group D's.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E15 (D, J): match four new questions to the kind that fits.
- **7 min. Model: then and now with a claim.** The chart from kindergarten (two columns, then and now, a row for what stayed) gains a third column, "what we do not know". Row one, getting to school: our two-sentence summary (in 1928, children at Webster Consolidated School in Keokuk County, Iowa, rode in a horse-drawn bus; source to check) beside a drawing of a school bus today. Changed: how it moved. Stayed: children rode together. Not known: how long it took. Say "I think ..., because ..." with the named source. This is one school, not every school (error K).
- **14 min. Supported work.** Where the school office or the town holds a dated photograph of our school, print it beside the Johnston photograph; otherwise use the Johnston photograph and today's drawing of our room, and name what a photograph of our own school would add. Pairs fill one row for the room and one for dress, naming only what they see and what they cannot tell, with "In the photograph ..., but today ...; both ...". The teacher hears the rotating six at the table, about 80 seconds each (E16b).
- **2 min. Exit ticket.** E16a on paper.

Holds: pairs hold the chart. Tool: None. By hand: chart, detail cards. Evidence: E16a, E16b. Next day: the spoken claim answering the big question.

## Day 17


- **3 min. Calendar corner.** Date today's square; revise group D's sentences. Each child gives a past-date time word for one event, recorded separately.
- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: E16 (E, K): add a stayed row or the unknown column to a new pair.
- **5 min. Reopen.** The question board, the four source questions and the claim frame. No model is given.
- **16 min. Supported work.** Each child draws one source from the unit (the dog photograph, our classroom record or the transport summary) and one detail, with a word label, and tells a partner "I think we can find out ___ with a ___, because ___." Useful answers differ by question. Another school's photograph cannot show our school's history; a plan of sources to find is a good answer. The teacher hears the rotating six at the table with E17, about 80 seconds each, and writes their words beside the drawing (E17b).

Holds: children hold the claim. Tool: None. By hand: drawing, label. Evidence: E17a, E17b. Next day: the observation.

## Day 18


Every child does each item's paper part. The rotating six give each item's one short spoken answer at the teacher's table, about 12 seconds an item, about a minute each (unit plan field 5), so about 72 seconds in each item part; the record sheet names who was heard. Blank models stay; completed examples are withdrawn. All practice cards say "invented for practice".

- **5 min. Orient.** Read the packet aloud; say what is invented. The packet uses a new photograph not seen before: Johnston's posed classroom (about 1899, caption read aloud, "about 1899" kept).
- **5 min. Item 1.** Place leaf drawings dated October 6, 13 and 20 on an October calendar. Spoken: "What repeats?"
- **6 min. Item 2.** Paper: draw one chosen classroom experience on today's real square. Spoken: "Tell what the children did yesterday, do today and will do tomorrow" (invented: drew, display, will label; story date October 21).
- **4 min. Item 3.** Place a music day December 8, a first assembly September 15 and a book swap April 11 on a blank year strip. Spoken: "Which came before the assembly?"
- **5 min. Item 4.** Paper: circle the kind of the new photograph and of an invented letter ("Our class used a bell to begin lessons. From Lee."); tick pretend or real for the letter. Spoken: "What does the photograph show about that classroom?"
- **5 min. Item 5.** Paper: circle the more useful for "what our school's old front sign looked like", a dated photograph of our school's front sign or a recording about another school's lunch, and draw a reason. Spoken: "Name another kind of source you would look for, and what it might tell us."

Holds: the child; no help with source choice or reasons. Tool: None. Evidence: items 1 to 5 (unit plan field 5), shown, shown with help or not yet seen, recording the child's words or placements. Next day: respond.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 14, "What kind of source is it? Who made it?"; day 15, "Which kind could help us find out? Why?"; day 17, "What could we use to find out about our school long ago, and why?"
- Written task, day 17: a drawn source with one copied kind word; the child's spoken claim is written by the teacher beside it. Formal written claims begin in unit 3.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors (shown, shown with help, not yet seen): names a source kind; points to a detail; gives a reason that links the detail to the question, not a liking.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: source, photograph, letter, object, map, building, old, new. Frames: "This ___ could help us find out ___ because it shows or tells ___." "I think ___, because ___." Home-language words and pointing count.
- **Access.** Enlarged images, plain spoken descriptions of each picture, movable cards, dictation, familiar-language answers; an adult moves cards as the child directs.
- **Check accommodations.** Read all prompts and dates aloud; do not supply source choices or reasons; score understanding apart from English grammar.
- **Reading load.** None beyond kind words read aloud; captions are read by the adult.
- **Extension, same standard.** Name a second kind that would help one of the smaller questions, and what it adds.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use the printed photograph and six kind cards: name the kind, say who made it and whether old or new, then choose a card for a question. Nothing from home; labeled invented cards only. Last day (19): do the fresh try, then the self-check against the answer picture; keep the calendar going. At home (15 to 30 minutes, any day skippable): draw a place where you learn and the date.
- **One projector.** Show each source large; children mark paper. Hear the rotating six one at a time.
