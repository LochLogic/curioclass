# Course frame: Dance, grades 6 to 8

Course `dance-grades-6-to-8` (catalogue id `dance-6-8`), a band course for grade 6, grade 7 and grade 8, run as three years one after another (year 6, year 7, year 8). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a thread comes back one step
  harder a year later in the same place: a body, space and time unit (technique and the dance elements), a making
  unit (ideas and the shape of a dance study), a revising and critique unit (revising, notation and responding to
  dances), and a performance unit that ends the year with a showing for families (staging and connecting). Each unit
  joins two progression threads, and every unit dances, studies another artist's dance and shows (bar criteria 4
  and 5). Units 1, 5 and 9 claim only performing ids; they touch responding through the dances they study and
  creating through the partner and group sections the class builds, so every unit touches at least two artistic
  processes.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up (desks pushed back, floor checked and spots marked, about 3 minutes),
  a warm-up (about 5), the teaching and dancing, a cool-down and the closing line (about 4) and putting the room back
  and collecting cards (about 3); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes of its own on day 2, after the
  warm-up, taken from the teaching time (day 2: set-up 3, warm-up 5, retrieval 10, teaching 15, cool-down 4, room
  back 3); the once-a-week path keeps this block inside the unit's opening session. Each unit's days include its
  end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is over 25 days) and one
  respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: the first unit of each year is longest (18 days) because
  it owns six ids, three of them technique ids with jumps, landings, weight shifts or layered tempos that need
  fluency days, and it sets up the floor, the warm-up and the rehearsal log for the year; the making unit owns four
  ids (14); the revising and critique unit owns six ids, four of them response ids met in writing, and holds a
  revision of the making unit's study (14); the performance unit owns five ids and holds the rehearsals for the
  family showing (14). These are course days: the school calendar (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a
  week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2, rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md
  3.4): the dance educators' association's opportunity-to-learn standards (2017 revision, posted on
  nationalartsstandards.org) ask for at least 90 minutes of dance a week in grades 6 to 8. This course gives 80
  minutes a week, 10 short of the 90; on the once-a-week path it gives 40, 50 short. A school on either path can add
  time. The course is written so a classroom or physical education teacher can teach it; a school with a dance
  specialist uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the study of a dance that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one rehearsal day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into
  the warm-ups, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path, every year: units of 8,
  8, 6 and 6 sessions (weeks 1 to 8, 9 to 16, 19 to 24 and 25 to 30); each year's two checks keep 1 check session
  and 1 respond session. The mid-year check session falls in week 17, as on the full path, and its respond session in
  week 18; the year-end check session falls in week 31 and its respond session in week 32. Week 18 holds science's
  mid-year check in grade 6 and ELA's in grade 8: the respond session asks no test of any student. A merged day never
  drops a critique, a rehearsal before a showing, a feedback and revision day or a respond day; the scope and
  sequence marks which days merge. The path's one exception to "two kinds of check day, never mixed": in every unit
  but units 2, 6 and 10 (whose written day keeps a session of its own), the written check day has no session; its
  items run as one or two timed blocks of about 12 minutes, each at the close of a later rehearsal session after the
  part it assesses is taught (the unseen phrase danced at the block's start), one prompt per part, scored after the
  session like a kept product, so nothing assessed is cut. In units 4, 8 and 12 the research card begins in the last
  ten minutes of the opening session, after the hook (fix pass, 2026-11-26).
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, dance performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 6 claims only the 21 grade 6 ids (`.6a`, `.6b`, `.6c`), year 7 only the 21 grade 7
  ids, year 8 only the 21 grade 8 ids: 63 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its
  own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches all
  four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1). In the Units table the
  C3 column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor standards the unit works on,
  and the dances it studies. Each id's action was checked against the words file and the national text; the row
  changes of 2026-11-25 landed, and the second read's additions of 2026-11-26 are in units 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11
  and 12. Check set: each unit plan checks
  itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine criteria of the arts part of
  docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md); the "Check set adds" column
  names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds. scripts/standards.py checks every
  id against the id file; each year's scope and sequence still states its id count (21) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met.
  The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Dance here, motor skill in physical education.** Dance in this course is the art: making, performing,
  responding to and connecting dance. Dance sequences, eight-count phrases and movement ideas as motor skill are
  physical education's (STANDARD.md 6; physical education 6 to 8 units 6, 13 and 18). Dance teaches the technique its own
  artistic standards need inside this course and assumes no earlier physical education instruction; it uses the
  moves a student already has as material for partner and group sections, studies and dances, and teaches
  choreography, performing for an audience and response. Canon and contrast are shared words: physical education
  uses them for coordinated movement, dance uses them to shape artistic intent. Units 1, 5 and 9 name the physical
  education rows they sit beside. Physical education's dance picks are cumbia, kolo and its original line dance; the
  one shared tradition is capoeira, a planned return named both ways: dance unit 8 (grade 7) studies its history and
  artistic character, and physical education unit 22 (grade 8) moves the basic ginga and why the circle matters to
  its people, retrieving this study (Cross-subject, below).
- **Base text.** None. Dance is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open dance recordings). Dances studied are described in
  our own words with a link to the maker's, the company's, a museum's or a cultural body's own page, never a copy or
  an embed; public domain music (Strauss) and museum open-access images (the Tang dancers) are used directly
  (APPROACH "Rights"). Each dance named below is a pick the unit plan keeps unless its rights line, checked on the
  item's own page, fails; a swapped dance must keep the unit's ids met and the band's range. The band's dances come
  from Africa (Senegal, the Democratic Republic of the Congo), the Americas (the United States, Brazil, Argentina,
  Canada, and Native nations in every year: Kanien'kehá:ka in year 6, Seneca in year 7, the powwow nations in year
  8), Asia (Japan, China, Thailand) and Europe (Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Russia), from 1653 to today, with
  living makers or living traditions in every year (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). None repeats a dance of
  the K to 2 or 3 to 5 dance bands, and the one tradition shared with physical education 6 to 8 is capoeira, named
  both ways (above); the 3 to 5 band's ideas (chance, site dance, sculptural costume) come back here through other
  makers, with one maker returning on purpose: Trisha Brown (3 to 5 units 3 and 12) is met again as a member of Grand
  Union (unit 10), now for group improvisation, a deliberate return. The unseen dance in every check is a short Curio Class
  original danced live by the teacher from a step card, or a short written description handed out, never an embed
  (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). Curio Class writes every step card, work card, style menu, criteria card,
  notation card, research card, record sheet, family note and every unseen dance.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Body, space and time: partner and group spacing, accents in
  three- and four-beat measures, tension and flow, jumps and soft landings (6); borrowed body designs, accented
  variations and weight shifts (7); shapes sculpted around dancers, props and the room, separate tempos in separate
  body parts, recalled spatial and rhythmic phrases (8). Making: ideas joined by likeness or clash, styles tried, a
  study with a stated goal (6); sources compared and styles chosen for an aim, a study shaped and explained (7); an
  original group dance from the student's own strongest moves (8). Revising and recording: revision against group
  criteria, a notation of the student's own invention (6); revisions weighed, an established notation tried (7);
  independent or shared revision, an excerpt notated (8). Performing: manners and notes in one space (6), a
  rehearsal journal and another venue explored (7), students leading preparation and staging for two spaces (8).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a move pattern, an
  element, a tool, a notation or a dance form shown in named steps with the step card, or a dance studied with the
  unit's words (the artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day). Fluency: short repeated
  practice with feedback (the warm-up, a phrase run three times on the counts, a landing drill, a notation drill, a
  partner spacing drill); in grades 6 to 8 it is the five-minute warm-up and fluency days: three or four in units 1,
  5 and 9, one to three in the 14-day units, beside the daily warm-up. Application: making and rehearsing a dance with some choice, drafting and revising a critique
  or analysis; the second application day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask, suggest), and the next day's
  rehearsal of the change is application too. Check: a showing scored live on the guide, or the written and show
  items of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not yet", or a re-rehearsal before a
  second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application, fluency, application with the critique,
  rehearsal, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for, named id by id in the unit's
  "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one item for
  each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live by the teacher as each group dances; a show item, where the student
  shows a move at the teacher's spot inside the group's turn; a written item at tables on an unseen dance (a student
  may answer aloud or show it instead, and the mark is the same); and a kept card or written product (a log, a
  criteria sheet, a notation card, a research note) made on the unit's last making days and scored after the
  session. A written product scored in a unit (unit 11's analysis, each unit 3, 7 and 11 critique) has its draft
  day, the teacher's written feedback and its revision day counted in the unit before the score. Two kinds of check
  day, never mixed on one day. A showing day: set-up 3 minutes, warm-up 5, six groups of five dance in the marked
  zone for about 4 minutes each (24: about two minutes of dancing, then any show item) while the rest sit in the
  audience zone and practice audience manners (nothing else is asked of them), cool-down and the self-check on the
  guide 5, room back 3: 40 minutes. A written day: set-up 2, the teacher dances the unseen Curio Class phrase twice
  or hands out its written description (about 4), written items at tables (28), collecting 3: 37 minutes, with slack
  for a third showing of the phrase. Either way the teacher watches one group, never a room and a group at once; a
  group the teacher wants to see again may be recorded on the school's device that day (feet and bodies with no
  faces, or the whole body with the family's yes) and the recording stays there (APPROACH "Kept work"). Every unit
  has two check days, one of each kind. No score comes from the day a move, an element or a notation is first
  taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice days (APPROACH
  "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit goes into the
  portfolio folder (a card, a log page, a notation, a critique, or a recording kept on the school's device) with a
  one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Taking part without dancing alone.** No student dances alone for the class: every showing is in a group of
  five, and a student may dance beside a partner or show at the teacher's spot (APPROACH "Supports"). A student who
  cannot move freely dances a seated or one-limb version, or takes the rehearsal director's, the counter's or the
  staging lead's role. Each unit plan maps the student's movement version to the look-fors it shows. A director's,
  counter's or staging role counts only for what it shows; where an owned movement action stays unseen, the teacher
  sees a short movement version with the student's usual supports and records the support. A student who does not
  want to be recorded is marked live.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every year 6 unit assumes a grade 5 row in its thread, last used one grade back, so
  it opens with a ten-minute retrieval task on day 2, after the warm-up, never a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for the hook
  and the question. Dance 3 to 5 is not a required course (lesson 32), so each year 6 retrieval task carries one
  ten-minute reteach of both threads' grade 5 steps together, inside the same block, for any student new to dance (APPROACH "Readiness and the
  baseline look"). Every year 7 and year 8 unit retrieves the year before's row the same way. The count starts from
  the band's order (6, then 7, then 8). In a mixed class running the years in turn, a student may meet year 8
  before year 7: each retrieval task then uses its reteach. The Seams tables give each row's last use.
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (grade 6: 12, 15, 16, 18, 22 to 24 and 32; grade 7: 9, 12 to 14, 22, 24 and 32; grade
  8: 10, 14, 15, 18, 20, 24 to 26, 29, 30 and 32). Mid-year: week 17 in every year (course days 33 and 34 of the
  year, school days 83 and 85), free of every core check in grades 6 to 8. Year-end: week 32, the finals week
  (course days 63 and 64), which may hold every subject's year-end check in grades 6 to 8 (CALENDAR.md stagger rule
  3), so the performance unit comes before it and its family showing, in weeks 30 and 31, is its own end-of-unit
  assessment. The weeks follow from the unit days by load.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a dance for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 4 (Kaha:wi draws on Haudenosaunee teaching and is watched
  only; the Tang dancers and The Green Table keep every id met), unit 10
  (khon grew in the Thai royal court and tells a sacred epic; Pichet Klunchun and Myself is watched only, and Grand
  Union keeps every id met) and unit 12 (the fancy shawl dance is danced at powwows, which open with ceremony; it is
  watched only, and the court ballet and the Charleston keep DA:Cn11.1.8a met). Swapping a dance keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit
  out means the course page and the unit's family note name the ids not claimed that year. A unit that revises an
  earlier unit's dance carries a fallback for a class that left that unit out: units 3 and 7 revise a
  short Curio Class original study, and unit 11 revises an original group study. The class learns that material inside the receiving unit's days before feedback, revision and
  assessment; the receiving unit claims only its own ids, and the omitted unit's ids stay unclaimed.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 in a cleared classroom, hall or gym with room for every student to stretch
  both arms out without touching another, floor tape or chalk to mark spots, zones and a stage edge, a hand drum or a
  pot and a wooden spoon, clapping and counting for the beat; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free
  stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One student at home: the parent is the audience and the critique
  partner, the group showing has a solo-with-parent version (the parent dances the partner part or watches and
  gives the choreographer's note), and the showing is to the family. The teacher needs no dance training: each
  unit's teacher page says what to look for, in plain words, and every unseen dance is on a step card the teacher
  can learn in a few minutes.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as a hand drum
  and scarves counted once, printing of step, work, criteria and notation cards), all optional beside the free path:
  year 6 $10, $5, $5, $10 ($30); year 7 $5, $5, $5, $10 ($25); year 8 $10, $5, $5, $10 ($30). Planning figures, not
  quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher device may play music and show a linked dance; every day runs without
  it, from the printed work card (a picture where one is open and a description), the teacher showing the moves, and
  claps, counts or a drum for the beat. No owned id needs technology. DA:Cr3.1.7b allows a recording as one of three
  ways to record a sequence; a paper-path class meets it with writing and signs, in full. DA:Re8.1.8a asks for
  evidence from a chosen dance; a paper-path student names each moment by what happens in it, from the work card's
  description or a live showing, in full. Production elements (DA:Pr6.1.6b, 7b, 8b) are chosen from what the room
  has (the room's own lights on or off, daylight, plain clothing colors, a sound made by the class); nothing is
  bought or rigged. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on printed cards, the floor and the body; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  beat keeper with a layered-tempo view, a notation card maker) has a printed version that does the same job and
  needs no student account.
- **Reading load.** Step cards, criteria cards and work cards are short, with a picture or symbol per step; a student
  reads them alone, and a written item asks for at most a short paragraph. Unit 11's analysis is the longest writing
  of the band, about a page, drafted and revised inside the unit. English learners: each unit teaches its five to
  six dance words in depth, with a picture, a movement and a frame ("The pattern ___ answers ___." "My criterion is
  ___ because ___."), a student may show the word instead of saying it, and the check marks what the student danced
  apart from what they wrote (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is a moving unit, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the Nova
  Scotia Department of Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades Primary-12" (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf, its dance section on printed page 43), its sections on dance and on the activity space, and the dance educators' association's
  opportunity-to-learn standards (2017 revision, https://nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/NDEO%20Opportunity%20to%20Learn%20Standards%202017%20rev.pdf), its lines on the dance space and floor. Floor rule:
  jumps, landings and stamping use a level, resilient surface the school confirms suitable; a wooden finish over concrete, tile or a
  loose mat on a hard floor is not enough by itself. On an unsuitable floor the class does those moves low and
  without impact, and their look-for is seen in a suitable school space before it is marked met, never from a
  written stand-in. Where the room cannot hold every group moving at once, groups rotate through marked traveling lanes and the
  waiting groups sit outside the movement area.
  Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): a clear floor with room
  to move arms out, checked for spills and objects before each session; bare feet or soft-soled shoes on a clean
  floor, never socks on a smooth floor; no lifts; no jumping from heights; jumps small and landed through the feet
  with soft knees, on a wooden floor or mats, never on concrete or tile; no inverted moves (no head spins,
  handstands or flips); no stretch pushed by a partner or held to pain, and no split or extreme range asked; partner
  contact only by agreement and never required; the stop cue used every session; props soft or light (a scarf, a
  chair, a hoop on the floor; nothing tied round a neck, no hard sticks swung); water and rest offered. Each unit's
  last column gives its hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the dances and the tasks with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14).
  Units with a cultural or community tradition, or a hard history, name the communities and the sources the agent
  studies first: unit 1 (stepping and the Black fraternities and sororities; Japanese butoh), unit 3 (hip-hop
  in Philadelphia; stepping again), unit 4 (the Kanien'kehá:ka of Six Nations of the Grand River and Kaha:wi; the
  Sogdian and Tang dancers; war in The Green Table), unit 5 (the Nicholas Brothers and segregated Hollywood; Dance
  Theatre of Harlem), unit 6 (Chinese painting and opera in Shen Wei's work), unit 7 (the peoples whose village
  dances the Moiseyev company staged), unit 8 (capoeira and Afro-Brazilian history; tango; the Seneca Nation and Rosy
  Simas), unit 9 (the Wolof people and sabar), unit 10 (Thai khon), unit 11 (Black girlhood in Camille A. Brown's
  work; the Congo in Faustin Linyekula's), unit 12 (Native nations and the powwow; the Charleston and the Black
  Lowcountry). Every task that ties dance to a student's own tastes, view or life (DA:Cn10.1.6a, 7a,
  8a; units 4, 8 and 12) offers an invented dancer's or a public figure's option marked the same (APPROACH
  "Sensitive ground"). Every social issue or research topic comes from a class list the unit plan writes, free of
  party politics and of any issue that asks a student to tell their own hardship, with an invented or public option
  beside any personal one. A dance from another culture is studied, and learned only where the community's own
  teaching says it is open to be taught; no costume, regalia, accent or imitation of a people, and no sacred or
  ceremonial dance as an exercise, warm-up, costume or imitation (the rows' teacher note; Cory, 2026-11-20). No task
  asks about a student's own diet, weight, body or growth: the health-habit ids (DA:Pr5.1.6b, 7b, 8b) and the
  growing-body id (DA:Pr5.1.7a) work through an invented dancer or dancers in general (APPROACH "Sensitive
  ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something" (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md)
  on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, a cleared floor, step
  cards, work cards, criteria and notation cards, a drum or counts. Activities: moves, elements, tools and notation
  taught in steps, dances studied with their setting, making and rehearsing with a critique and a revision, a
  showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks
  a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 21 ids and growth on the
  look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with students, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").
