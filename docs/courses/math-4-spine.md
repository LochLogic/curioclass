## What changed in this revision

1. **Checks moved from the section to the unit.** One readiness check at the start of a unit, a five-minute exit
   question every day, one end-of-unit assessment on the unit's last day. Each cumulative check is followed by a respond day,
   and day 1 opens with a start-of-year check.
2. **4.OA.A.3 is split.** Two days of two-step problems inside Multiplicative Comparison, then a four-day unit,
   Multistep Problems with All Four Operations, after division.
3. **Multiplication and division finish sooner.** Area and perimeter, multiplication and division move ahead of
   measurement conversions. All major work is taught by day 123 (was 132), multiplication and division by day 117.
4. **Geometry is regrouped.** Lines and Angles, 12 days, with lines and rays drawn before angles are measured; then
   Shapes and Symmetry, 4 days.
5. **The smaller findings are folded in:** the eight practices, the balance of understanding, fluency and
   application, the limits in the standards, grade 3 standards by code, fluency from day 1, a "where it comes back"
   table, and fuller rules for the checks.

The day counts are still estimates, sized by how much each standard asks for and by the rule that most of the year
goes to major work. They are not copied from any program.

## The year at a glance

- **150 teaching days of 60 minutes**: 142 in sections (141 in the 17 teaching units and 1 that pulls the year
  together), 4 assessment days and 4 respond days. Checks, respond days and the year-together day come to 9.
- **Major work: 100 days, 67 percent.** Supporting: 22 days. Additional: 19 days. The rubric's evidence guide sets
  the line at 65 percent for grades 3 to 8 (confirmed 2026-11-11), so this clears it by a small margin. Any day
  moved off major work should come back.
- **The same count read other ways.** By sections, 29 of the 42 sections in the 17 teaching units are major work
  (69 percent). The course has 43 sections; the 43rd, section 18.1, pulls the year together and is not in this count. By
  units, 10 of 17 (59 percent), which is why the three supporting units that practise major work (line
  plots, area and perimeter, measurement conversions; 16 days) must show that link on the page of every section. With
  those 16 days counted beside major work, the figure is 116 of 150 (77 percent).
- **18 units, 43 sections.** A unit is one block of standards and usually one topic. A section is a package of 1 to 5 days (section 3.4 has 6: its last day is the unit's assessment day).
- **Five cumulative checks:** a start-of-year check on day 1 and Checks 1 to 4 on days 42, 78, 118 and 149.
- **All major work is taught by day 123**, and multi-digit multiplication and division by day 117. Measurement and
  geometry follow.
- **Fractions come first**, after a short opening on factors. Fractions are the largest block of the grade and the
  hardest new idea, so they get the first half of the year and are used again in line plots, decimals and
  measurement.
- **What exists today:** 13 lesson tools (11 specs, 2 built, none live), 3 second tools approved and not yet
  written, and no section. Every day below is a gap day until its unit is written. The 13 tools are about 30 minutes
  each, roughly 7 hours of a 150-hour year, so most days will be print lessons. The Tool column is not the course.

## Practices, balance and limits

**Practices.** The eight mathematical practices, in plain words: 1 make sense of problems and keep going; 2 reason
with numbers and with what they stand for; 3 build an argument and question someone else's; 4 model a real
situation with mathematics; 5 choose and use tools well; 6 be precise; 7 look for and use structure; 8 look for
what repeats and use it. Each unit builds the one or two named here on purpose, and all eight are met over the
year. Each section names the task that builds each one.

**Balance.** Days per unit that are mostly understanding (U), mostly fluency and skill (F) or mostly application
(A), and the assessment day (C): the last day of every unit, which is the end-of-unit assessment, the response to it and the
next unit's readiness check. The C column was added 2026-11-11. Units 1, 9 and 11 are counted from their
unit plans. For the others the assessment day was taken out of application where the unit had any, otherwise
out of fluency, otherwise out of understanding, until each unit's plan marks its own days. These are a first cut; each section's plan marks its own days, and the totals here are corrected from the sections.

**Limits.** What grade 4 does not go past. A check item past these limits is above grade and does not belong.

**Fluency all year.** The one fluency grade 4 requires by name is 4.NBT.B.4. Every day opens with a five-minute
warm-up from the worksheet generator:

- **Adding and subtracting, from day 1.** Within 1,000 (3.NBT.A.2) until unit 9. Multi-digit adding with the
  standard algorithm enters the warm-up the day after it is taught, and subtracting from day 78, to the end of the
  year (corrected 2026-11-11: the warm-up had used the algorithm from day 72, the day it is first taught).
- **Times tables, from day 1.** Labelled on every page as grade 3 maintenance (3.OA.C.7), not grade 4 teaching.
  Every unit after the first leans on them. Days 1 to 6 include a facts check (decided 2026-11-11): 36
  products in the day 3 warm-up and 36 quotients in the day 4 warm-up, untimed; the mark is 32 of 36. Students
  below the mark keep a 9 by 9 multiplication table through unit 3 (to day 24), and get the grade 3 fact tools
  and print during warm-ups, never in place of a lesson day. The check repeats before day 94, when multi-digit
  multiplication begins.
- **Fractions and decimals, twice a week from day 62**, the day after fraction and decimal teaching ends.
- **Multiplication and division, from day 118**, mixed into the same rotation.

**The 60 minutes.** Warm-up 5, the lesson about 50, exit question 5. The readiness check for a unit takes ten minutes and is given before
the unit opens, so it is marked before day 1 and can shape it (decided 2026-11-11): in the last ten minutes of
the previous unit's last day, or of the respond day where one sits between the two units. Unit 1 is
served by the start-of-year check on day 1, which must carry its three readiness items. On the last day of a unit the end-of-unit assessment takes half the period,
responding to it takes twenty minutes, and the last ten are the next unit's readiness check.

## The checks

**In every unit (1 to 17).**

- **Readiness check**, ten minutes, given before the unit opens: in the last ten minutes of the previous
  unit's last day, or of the respond day where one sits between the two units. It is on the grade 3 or
  earlier grade 4 standards in the "Builds on" column, and it is marked before the unit's first day. Unit 1's
  readiness items ride in the start-of-year check on day 1.
- **Exit question**, every day, five minutes.
- **The last day** of every unit: end-of-unit assessment 30 minutes, respond 20, the next unit's readiness check 10. The
  three-day units (Number Patterns and Line Plots) follow the same rule (decided 2026-11-11; they no longer
  keep a ten-minute end-of-unit assessment).

**Over the year: five cumulative checks.**

| Check | Day | Length | Covers | Then |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start of year | 1 | 30 minutes | Grade 3 major work: times tables and the meaning of multiplication and division, fractions as numbers, area, adding and subtracting within 1,000 | The rest of day 1 starts section 1.1. The results set which readiness checks need the most room |
| 1 | 42 | one period | Units 1 to 5: factors, patterns, equivalent fractions, adding and subtracting fractions, line plots | Respond day, day 43 |
| 2 | 78 | one period | Units 6 to 9: fraction times whole number, decimals, place value, adding and subtracting; fractions again | Respond day, day 79 |
| 3 | 118 | one period | Units 10 to 13: comparison, area and perimeter, multiplication, division; fractions and place value again | Respond day, day 119 |
| 4 | 149 | one period | The whole course, two thirds of it major work | Day 150: go over it and hand on to grade 5 |

**The start-of-year check is not yet written.** It must carry unit 1's three readiness items
(docs/courses/math-4/s01.md, field 4): ten products and their ten quotients (3.OA.C.7); a division rewritten as a
multiplication with an unknown, then solved (3.OA.B.6); and every rectangle with area 12 drawn on a grid
(3.MD.C.7). It is marked after class, so it cannot shape day 1.

Rules for every check, unit or cumulative:

1. **Every item names its standard and its practice.**
2. **Two parts on each cumulative check.** A part built from the generators, so it can be redrawn, and two fixed
   tasks that ask the student to explain or to model, with a scoring guide. Generator items alone would be short
   computation and could not show the whole of a standard.
3. **Nothing above grade.** Items stay inside the limits in "Practices, balance and limits".
4. **Every key says what each wrong answer means and ends with "if many missed this, do that".** The respond day
   and the second half of each unit's last day are the time to do it.
5. **Each check after the first carries items from earlier units**, weighted to major work.
6. **A class record sheet** for each, since there are no accounts.
7. **The last check is on day 149, not the last day**, so there is a day left to act on it.
