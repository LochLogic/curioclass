## The year at a glance

- **150 teaching days**: 146 in units (the last 2 pull the year together) and 4 check days.
- **Major work: 100 days, 67 percent.** Supporting: 25 days. Additional: 19 days. The rubric's evidence guide sets the line at 65
  percent for grades 3 to 8 (confirmed 2026-11-11), so this clears it by a small margin. Any day moved off major
  work should come back.
- **17 stretches, 42 units.** A stretch is one topic and usually one module. A unit is a package of 1 to 5 days.
- **Fractions come first**, after a short opening on factors. Fractions are the largest block of the grade and the
  hardest new idea, so they get the first half of the year and are used again in line plots, decimals and
  measurement.
- **What exists today:** 13 lesson tools (11 specs, 2 built, none live), 3 second tools approved and not yet
  written, and no unit. Every day below is a gap day until its unit is written.

## Why this order, and what each stretch hands on

| # | Stretch | Builds on (grade 3) | Why it sits here | Sets up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Factors and Multiples | Meaning of multiplication, times tables | Opens the year on something every student can enter: building rectangles. It wakes up the times tables, and finding a number to multiply top and bottom by is easier once multiples are familiar | Stretch 3; grade 5 expressions |
| 2 | Number Patterns | Times tables, patterns in addition | Short, and it sets the habit of saying what you notice and why, which the fraction work leans on | Grade 5 patterns and relationships |
| 3 | Equivalent Fractions and Comparing | Fractions as numbers on a line, simple equivalence | The base of all fraction work this year. It comes early so it can be used again four more times | Stretches 4 to 7; grade 5 unlike denominators |
| 4 | Adding and Subtracting Fractions | Fractions as numbers | A fraction as a sum of unit fractions follows straight from stretch 3, while the pictures are fresh | Stretches 5 and 6; grade 5 adding unlike fractions |
| 5 | Line Plots | Scaled graphs, measuring to a quarter inch | Placed here on purpose: it is supporting work whose whole job is to practise adding and subtracting fractions with real measurements | Grade 5 units and line plots |
| 6 | Multiplying a Fraction by a Whole Number | Meaning of multiplication | Repeated adding of one fraction, so it follows stretch 4 directly | Grade 5 multiplying fractions |
| 7 | Decimals | Fractions as numbers | Tenths and hundredths are fractions first. Coming straight after fraction work, a decimal is a new way to write a number the student already knows | Stretch 11 (money, metric); grade 5 decimals |
| 8 | Place Value to a Million | Rounding, place value to a thousand | "Ten times the place to its right" reads the same on both sides of the decimal point, so it follows decimals | Stretches 9, 13, 14; grade 5 powers of ten |
| 9 | Adding and Subtracting Multi-Digit Numbers | Adding and subtracting within 1,000 | The standard algorithm needs place value to a million. Fluency practice starts here and runs to the end of the year | Stretches 10, 13, 14 |
| 10 | Multiplicative Comparison | Meaning of multiplication and division | "Times as many" is the idea under unit conversion, so it comes right before it. Multistep problems use every operation taught so far | Stretches 11, 13, 14; grade 5 and 6 ratio thinking |
| 11 | Measurement Conversions | Measuring, scaled graphs | Uses comparison (a metre is 100 times a centimetre), decimals and fractions in real problems | Grade 5 unit conversions and volume |
| 12 | Area and Perimeter Formulas | Area as counting and multiplying, perimeter | Finding a missing side is an unknown-factor problem. It also plants the rectangle picture that multiplication uses next | Stretch 13; grade 5 volume |
| 13 | Multi-Digit Multiplication | Times tables, multiplying by tens | The area model from stretch 12 becomes the method | Stretch 14; grade 5 standard algorithm |
| 14 | Division with Remainders | Division as an unknown factor | Division is taught as undoing multiplication, so it comes straight after it. The hardest computing of the year gets time late, when students are oldest | Grade 5 two-digit divisors |
| 15 | Angles | None directly; fractions of a circle from stretch 3 | Additional work, placed after all the major work is taught | Stretch 16; grade 7 angle problems |
| 16 | Lines, Angles and Symmetry | Area, quadrilaterals | Needs angle measure to sort shapes by their angles | Grade 5 classifying figures, coordinate plane |
| 17 | The Year Together | | Two days of mixed problems on the major work before the last check | Grade 5 |

**Two changes from data/sequence.csv.** The module Angles and Line Plots is one module with two topics. The spine
uses its two halves in two places: line plots at stretch 5, where they practise fraction addition, and angles at
stretch 15. The module stays one module (Cory's call on the Desk); only the units point at different parts of it.
Second, sequence.csv puts Multi-Digit Multiplication and Division after Area and Perimeter as one stop; the spine
keeps that place and gives each half its own stretch, matching the two tools.

**Fluency all year.** 4.NBT.B.4 asks for fluency by name. From stretch 9 on, every unit carries a five-minute
add-and-subtract warm-up from the worksheet generator. Times tables get the same treatment from day 1, because
every stretch after the first leans on them.

## What is missing today, said plainly

- **No unit is written.** All 150 days are gap days. The pilot (unit 12.1) is the first.
- **No lesson tool is live.** Two are built and must catch up with their specs; eleven are specs.
- **Three stretches have no spec for their main tool:** line plots, fraction times whole number, division. Division
  and fraction times whole number are major work, 20 days between them.
- **Unit Ladder's spec is too thin to judge** (Sara's batch 1).
- **Grade 3 is weak underneath.** Only 3 of grade 3's 25 standards read as taught in full, so "builds on grade 3"
  is true of the standards and not yet of Curio Class. The readiness check at the start of each unit is what
  catches this for a real class.
- **The shelves are not yet matched to units.** Which worksheets, charts, Word Wall cards and games each unit pulls
  is decided when the unit is written, starting with the pilot.
- **The day counts are untested.** The pilot unit and the teacher's read are the first test of whether five days
  is right for one standard.
