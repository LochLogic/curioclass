# Section 4.3: Changing places, then showing what we know

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#science/science-kindergarten/4/s/4.3), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py science-kindergarten u04-3.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **K-ESS2-2, this section's share (days 40, 43).** "Argue, using evidence, that plants and animals, people included, change their surroundings to get what they need." Day 40 adds people clearing land and a lower-harm choice. Day 43 observes each child's argument on a new sequence.
- **K-ESS3-1, this section's share (days 40, 42).** "Use a model to show how what a plant or animal, people included, needs is linked to where it lives." Day 40 keeps the model; day 42 observes each child's model on a new place.
- **K-LS1-1, day 41 (observed).** "Describe, from observations, the patterns in what plants and animals, people included, need to stay alive." Taught in 4.1; day 41 runs its items N1 to N4 (owned by 4.1).
- **Limits that bite here.** Assessment days teach nothing new and reteach nothing; the transfer panels stay hidden until their day. People clearing land is stated as a fact of meeting needs, without blame; no one judges a family's use of water, paper or land. "Less harm" means more living things and their resources left in place, judged from the pictures; a plan a child likes is not evidence. A plant does not decide to change a place. Nothing is built or grown; plants and soil are handled only by the teacher. Day 41 stays indoors.
- **Connections.** ELA W.K.1 (draw and tell an opinion with a reason), days 40 and 43: the reason is the picture. Math K.MD.B.3 (sort and count each group), days 41 to 43: the independent sort of familiar needs pictures.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to hear every child: each child has a mat and marks at the same time as the group, and gives one brief spoken reason per code. Hear and record each child, never only a spokesperson. Mats are scored later (needs after day 42, models after day 43, arguments after their revision), so no work is made and scored in one period.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: section 4.2, ending day 39 (a claim from a new photograph). After: the end of unit 4; unit 5 opens on day 44, which retrieves the evidence sentence. No readiness check. Results go to the record sheet on the one scale; component marks and points are assigned later, as unit plan field 5 sets.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** M1 to M3 (day 42) and C1 to C3 (day 43), unit plan field 5. N1 to N4 are run on day 41 and are owned by 4.1 (its field 3 holds their spoken forms). Each child has an own mat and marks pictures at the same time as the group; the teacher hears one brief reason from each child per code and sees every other response. Stems are split so one ask is one item. The panels are imaginary places, said aloud to be invented; the rolls of unit 3 are not used. Teacher keys, look-fors and the scoring are in unit plan field 5.

**Day 42, M1 to M3 (held back, never taught from).** Each group of five has four minutes: M1 1, M2 1, M3 2. A new picture map: a sunny moist soil bed, a grassy burrow bank, a water source, air marks and trees.
- **M1a (mat).** "Put the plant where it can get what it needs." Answer: the sunny moist bed. Wrong: anywhere, "it can live anywhere" (E).
- **M1b (mat).** "Draw a line from the plant to each thing in its place that it uses." Answer: lines to light, water, air and soil. A missing line is "shown with help", no letter.
- **M2a (mat).** "Put the digging animal where it can get what it needs, and draw its lines." Answer: the bank, with lines to shelter space, grass for food, water and air. Wrong: the animal placed with no resource lines, "put it anywhere" (E).
- **M3a (mat).** "Add the people and draw lines to what they use." Answer: lines to water, air, food from the land and wood. Wrong: no lines, "buildings mean people do not need nature" (F). Labels alone do not earn the point.
- **M3b (heard).** "Tell how this place works together." Answer: one connected reason ("water goes to the plants and to the people"). Wrong: the same belief as M3a (F).

**Day 43, C1 to C3 (held back).** Each group has three minutes: C1 0.75, C2 0.75, C3 1.5. Three new sequences: growing roots pushing soil aside toward damp ground, an animal digging a shelter in a bank, people digging a channel to bring water to a food garden; plus an unchanged kite picture as a card that is not evidence.
- **C1a (cards).** "Touch the picture that shows the roots changed the place." Answer: the soil pushed aside. Touching the kite is "shown with help", no letter. Wrong: "nothing changed, plants cannot change places" (G). **C1b (heard).** "What does that help the plant get?" Answer: water.
- **C2a (cards).** "Touch what the animal changed." Answer: the bank, now with a shelter. **C2b (heard).** "What does the animal get from that?" Answer: a place to be safe. Wrong: "only people change things" (H).
- **C3a (cards).** "Touch the land the people changed." Answer: the channel. **C3b (cards).** "Two routes bring the same water. Touch the one that leaves more plants standing." Answer: the bare-strip route. Wrong: the other route, or "they are the same" (J). **C3c (heard).** "Why that one?" Answer: points to the plants left standing. Wrong: "I like it" (I).

**Day 40 and day 43 exits.** If many miss one, the next teaching day opens with that error's wrong example; the day 43 captions are the repair (field 6).
- **E40a (day 40).** Invented: a town will put a bike rack on one of two spots, a bare paved corner or a strip of wildflowers where bees visit (a rack drawn faintly on each). "If the rack goes on a spot, the plants there are gone. Tell me what is left standing if the rack goes on spot 1." Answer: the flowers and the bees stay. Wrong: "nothing is left", or "the same on both spots" (J).
- **E40b (day 40).** Same pictures. "Say: I picked spot ___ because ___." Answer: it leaves the flowers and the bees. Wrong: "I like it" (I).
- **E43a (day 43, section exit).** Invented: bare ground, then the same ground under a tree where fallen leaves cover it and the soil stays damp. "Touch the part of the place the tree changed." Answer: the leaf-covered ground. Wrong: "nothing changed, plants cannot change things" (G).
- **E43b (day 43).** Same pictures. "Say what the tree changed: I think ___, because I saw ___." Answer: a claim naming the tree and a picture. Wrong: "only people change places" (H).
- **Day 41 has no separate exit.** N1 to N4 are its evidence.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** All three models stay on the wall for transfer: the pattern sheet, the claim strip and the system chart. Day 40 adds human changes and a lower-harm choice to the chart. Day 41 reaches a class consensus on the chart and explains the two anchors. Days 42 and 43 use them unchanged; the records of days 41 and 42 are carried unchanged into day 43.
- **Key moment 1, day 41: explaining the two anchors.** Ideal instrument: the plants and the weed and hole themselves. Partly fits: the plants and the fall photographs indoors; the weed stays "seen in a crack, not proved". The walk is not planned; any outdoor look elsewhere needs a checked route and is omitted if unsafe.
- **Key moment 2, days 41 to 43: each child's own marks.** Ideal instrument: one mat for each child, watched by the teacher. Fits. A component not seen is recorded "not yet seen". Tools are None.
- **Equipment and minutes.** One supervised table at the front, six groups in turn: day 41 three minutes each, day 42 four, day 43 three. Each group has its own panels, five mats and crayons. Day 41's three minutes hold four items on mats and five reasons; rehearse the day before. The five other groups sort familiar needs pictures at tables and revise earlier mats from picture stickers.
- **Clean-up.** Day 40: crayons to the tub, 1 min. Days 41 to 43: group change-overs hold crayons and mats; wipes at the door only if plants were handled, which the teacher alone does.
- **A room with nothing.** Photographs of the layout and the sequences, with paper mats; no plant, so the plant records are the dated photographs.

## 6. Days

## Day 40


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 39 exit, error I. The teacher says a liking and a seeing; the child touches the one that is evidence.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Touch two links on the class chart and say them aloud.
- **4 min. Hook.** Pip's invented town needs houses: a bare field, a patch of trees, houses. People change land to have a place to live, as the squirrel and the beaver did. Said without blame.
- **8 min. Two plans.** Pip's town has two plans for the same number of houses, one on the bare field edge and one on the wooded patch. Pairs mark what each plan changes and what is left standing, and count trees left by touching each once. A rotating six says which leaves more living things and why, from the picture.
- **4 min. Add to the chart.** Human changes go on the class chart with arrows to the needs they meet and a mark for the plan that leaves more in place.
- **3 min. Our plants.** The teacher waters one class plant with a small cup only when the soil is dry; a pot holds only so much and the rest runs out. Children say what would waste water. No family or home is mentioned. This sets up the year-end solution.
- **3 min. Exit.** E40a and E40b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice marks and counts the trees each plan leaves, touches the plan that leaves more and says why; the exit says what is left standing for a bike rack on a paved corner, a spoken description of a case the plans never used, then says a choice and reason.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (marks). Tool: None. By hand: marks and counts. Evidence: E40a, E40b. Hand-backs out: J and I to day 41.

## Day 41


- **4 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs: day 40 exit, errors J and I. Count the plants left on each plan by touching each once (J); a liking beside a seeing (I). Two minutes each, in pairs.
- **5 min. Closing explanation.** The class dictates how water, light, food and changed surroundings help explain the weed and the squirrel's hole, from the wall records. The teacher scribes four lines. The weed was seen in the crack; nobody saw it form, so the class does not claim the weed made it. The question board is read back and unanswered questions are marked.
- **3 min. Stations.** The teacher sets the front table with the unfamiliar delivery-log and recovery panels, meal tags, mats and crayons. The five other groups start the familiar needs sort.
- **18 min. Six groups observed, 3 minutes each.** N1 to N4 as in section 4.1 field 3. Each child marks, points and touches at the same time; the spoken part is one brief reason from each child for the code, on N4b (about 12 seconds), and the teacher records each on the scale. No coaching, no reteaching.

Held by: children (own mat). Tool: None. By hand: mat and cards. Evidence: N1 to N4 for all thirty children. No separate exit. After class the teacher writes a picture caption on each mat, read on day 42.

## Day 42


- **2 min. Set-up.** The front table: the model place map, resource cards, mats and crayons. The other groups sort familiar needs pictures.
- **24 min. Six groups observed, 4 minutes each.** M1 to M3 as in field 3. Each child's lines and answer are marked on the scale.
- **4 min. Change-overs and captions.** Time for a group that ran long, then crayons are swapped; no caption is read today (day 43 reads the day 41 and model captions once).

Held by: children (own mat). Tool: None. By hand: mat and cards. Evidence: M1 to M3 for all thirty children; the day 41 needs record is carried unchanged. No exit and no repair today; after class the teacher writes captions on the model mats, read on day 43.

## Day 43


- **2 min. Set-up.** The front table: the three sequences, the kite card, mats and crayons. Others sort and revise.
- **18 min. Six groups observed, 3 minutes each.** C1 to C3 as in field 3. Each child's choices and answer are marked on the scale.
- **3 min. Read the captions.** Two groups hear their caption strip once, 40 seconds each: needs (A to D) and links (E and F). The teacher reads aloud. The claims and impacts strips (G to J) are written on the mats after class and read on day 44.
- **4 min. Return and revise.** Hand-backs: the day 41 and 42 captions; the picture exit. Each child revises their own needs and model mats, at most two captions, independently, with no new interview and no performance repeated.
- **3 min. Section exit.** E43a and E43b. Not scored.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts animals to their foods and, in the sittings, each child chooses and tells a change from new sequences; the exit touches what a tree changed on bare ground and says one sentence about it, in two pictures no sitting used, with the whole class answering at once.

Held by: children (own mat). Tool: None. By hand: mat. Evidence: C1 to C3 for all thirty children; E43a and E43b are response, not scored. Argument captions are written after day 43 and revised at the next meeting; needs are scored after day 42, models after day 43, arguments after that revision.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 40, "Which plan leaves more living things in place?"; day 41, "What did the weed need?"; days 42 and 43, one reason heard from every child.
- Written task: none. Mats are marked by drawing and pointing; the teacher writes dictated captions after days 41 to 43.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the lines show what the place gives; the evidence picture is chosen over the one that shows nothing; the lower-harm choice rests on what the picture shows.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 40 pulls the system chart, the claim stem and the problem word from unit 3. Day 41 pulls all of 4.1 (water, light, eats, different foods). Day 42 samples 4.2 days 36 and 37. Day 43 samples days 38 to 40. The independent sort uses the 4.1 cards. Each day's evidence is drawn, pointed or spoken; the record sheet notes "heard".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: place, link, change, plan, safe place, plants left standing. Frames: "It needs ___." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Home-language answers are accepted on every item.
- **Access.** Raised or tactile resource symbols; pointing and dictation replace drawing. The teacher scribes a spoken link or reason without supplying either.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; enlarge pictures and cards; accept home-language reasons; record help as "shown with help". Spelling and drawing quality earn nothing.
- **Reading load.** None. All words are spoken.
- **Extension, same standard.** Draw a second change a living thing could make and the need it would meet.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Day 40: look at a worn path or a small garden and ask what lives there and what stays standing if something is moved. Last day of the unit: ask the child to show two things they noticed that living things change, and say "I think ___, because I saw ___." Use only what is on hand; no purchase and no report on the home.
- **One projector.** Show the plans, the panels and the sequences large; the mats are paper and each child still marks their own. One group at a time at the screen.
