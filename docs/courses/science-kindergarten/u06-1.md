# Section 6.1: Where litter goes, and a school action

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **K-ESS3-3, this section's share (days 55 to 58).** "Share ideas, by talking or drawing, for ways people can lessen their harm to local land, water, air and/or living things." Day 55 sees where litter lies and might go. Day 56 traces what it and its making can touch (land, water, air, living things) and chooses a school action. Day 57 shows how that action lessens one effect. Day 58 shares it on a scene the class has not seen. Section 6.2 tests and revises.
- **Limits that bite here.** No decay rates, factory chemistry or pollution demonstrations. A picture chain shows what can happen, never what did: say "I saw" for the patch and "it might" for a journey. Recycling depends on what a place accepts, so never "every bottle goes" (F). Nobody is named as the dropper, and no child is asked about home waste or recycling.
- **Connections.** Math K.MD.B.3 (classify into given categories and count each; from memory), day 55: the tally. ELA W.K.2 (draw, tell, dictate a label), days 57 and 58.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The difference between seeing litter and inferring its journey; why the class model holds all four parts while one child may speak to one; how to run the pick-up so only the teacher bags and no one is blamed.

## 3. Checks, written first

**Exits.** Two parts at most, each spoken as one ask. A miscount or a missing arrow with no stated belief has no letter; it is recounted with a partner.
- **E55a (day 55).** Invented: a playground picture with four paper scraps and two bottles beside a puddle, no arrow. "Touch the kind there is more of." Answer: the paper scraps.
- **E55b.** "Draw an arrow for where one scrap might go." Answer: toward the puddle, the grass or another place in the picture. Wrong: "it stays where it fell" (A).
- **E56a (day 56).** Invented: a school-fair scene with bare rutted soil, a plastic bag floating in a pond, a bus with a gray haze behind it and a duck with string round its foot. "Touch what is hurt in the part I name" (land, water, air or living things, one part to each child). Answer: the rutted soil, the bag in the pond, the haze, the duck.
- **E56b.** "Tell me one thing the school could do so the pond is hurt less." Answer: a feasible action, such as a lid on the bin so wrappers stay out of the pond. Wrong: "put the wrappers in the shrubs, that keeps them safe" (C).
- **E57a (day 57).** Invented: a classroom corner with a paper bin of half-used sheets, a tray of single-use cups, three cups on the floor and a gap behind the cupboard. "Touch the part of the picture that our action would change." Answer: the cups on the floor (pick-up), the half-used sheets (reuse) or what the school bin takes (recycle). Wrong: the gap behind the cupboard as a place to put the litter (C).
- **E57b.** "Tell why that helps." Answer: "If we ___, then ___" with an affected part. Wrong, if the class chose to reuse paper: "reusing paper does not change how much new paper we need" (B).
- **E58a (day 58, section exit).** Invented: a drawing of bottles beside a school puddle, and a second drawing of the same corner after a class's action. "Tell me what changed between the two drawings." Answer: fewer bottles near the puddle.
- **E58b.** "Finish the arrow that says why it helps." Answer: the action leads to less harm to the water or the living things. Wrong: "every bottle goes to be recycled everywhere" (F).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The schoolyard system drawing (unit 4's place and links), cause arrows, the picture tally and a first evidence sentence are started on day 55 and revised on days 56 to 58. Review, in the ledger's words: cause and effect; class picture chart and picture rows; count by touching each once; the design-cycle chart (problem, try an idea, test it; one change, then retest); place, resource, link, system; less harm; "I think ___, because I saw ___"; choose by hands, the teacher touching each hand once; return and repair; rotating six heard; wipes from a table tub. New: litter, might, the four parts, solution, reuse, recycle, action and effect.
- **Key moment 1, day 55: the tally.** Ideal instrument: every piece counted by pointing, once, in a named zone, with one count rule (a loose piece bigger than a bottle cap; leaves and sticks are not litter). Fits. Whole pieces, so no step problem. Never add litter to a clean patch: a low count is the real count, and is said so. If outside is unsafe, use dated photographs of one bounded area with real counts, said to be photographs; day 59 then uses the same area's later photographs.
- **Key moment 2, day 55: the pick-up.** Ideal: each child with a grabber. Partly fits. Six sets and one adult bag: six collectors in two shifts of three, 2 minutes a shift, the adult's hands only on the bag; the other children sit in sight and look.
- **Key moment 3, day 56: the picture chains.** Ideal: real photographs of each journey. Partly fits: original pictures, one actor each. That manufacturing can dirty air is from memory and is said as "can".
- **Equipment and minutes.** Gloves non-latex, six pairs. Handwash: six collectors at two sinks, three at a time, about 45 seconds each; everyone else wipes at the door. Day 57: one bottle a group, five turns of 1 minute inside a 7-minute part.
- **Set-ups.** Under 5 minutes of lesson time, named: the chosen reminder card posted at the bin (day 56); the teacher's break-time pick-up from day 56 on, logged beside the day's weather and who was away.
- **A room with nothing.** Reused paper and crayons; photographs for the patch; drawn bins and bottles for the clean samples (weaker handling evidence).

## 6. Days

## Day 55


- **3 min. Hook and question.** At the door the teacher asks, aloud, "Where does the litter in our schoolyard come from, and what can we do about it?" and does not guess who dropped any. Rules said once: stay in sight, touch nothing unless the teacher says.
- **2 min. Out.** To the bounded patch, split into six zones named by landmarks, one for each group of five.
- **6 min. Tally.** Each group counts its zone before any pick-up: point once at each piece, mark the picture tally (paper, bottle or plastic, other). Marks are counted to ten by touching each once; the teacher counts any zone above ten. The day's weather and how many are away are written beside it.
- **4 min. Pick-up.** Two shifts of three collectors, 2 minutes each, one from each group, in gloves with a grabber, taking only dry paper or intact plastic the teacher has cleared. The teacher alone bags. Everyone else sits in sight and says "I saw ___".
- **5 min. Back and wash.** 2 minutes to walk in. Wipes for all at the door; the six collectors at the two sinks, three at a time.
- **3 min. Retrieval, unscored.** Three one-line questions on the kept charts, a minute each: "Which picture row has more?" "What comes after we try an idea?" "Which part of the place gives this animal its water, and what did we see that shows it?" Each child touches a card; the teacher hears six. A miss gets a pointing back to the chart in the next part, then the question again.
- **4 min. Start the model.** On the class chart the teacher scribes: tally rows from the zone totals; the schoolyard drawing with the litter in it; one dashed "might" arrow for where a piece could go; a first sentence, "I think ___, because I saw ___".
- **3 min. Exit.** E55a, E55b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice counts litter in a real zone and marks a picture tally, one touch for each piece; the exit is an invented playground picture with two kinds and asks the child to touch the kind there is more of, a comparison read at a glance with no tally and no marks.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (zone tally, own mat). Tool: None. By hand: tally marks, touch card. Evidence: E55a, E55b. Hand-backs out: A to day 56. No hand-back arrives from unit 5.

## Day 56


- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 55 exit, A, 2 minutes.
- **2 min. Warm-up.** The tally rows and the drawing read back; "I saw" and "might" said again (review).
- **12 min. Four picture chains.** Original pictures, invented for practice, 3 minutes each, one actor each: a tree cut for paper (land, living things); a bottle being made, with a haze (air); a wrapper blown into a stream (water); a turtle and a plastic ring (living things). The teacher says each aloud, children point to what changes, and a mark for land, water, air or living things goes on the class drawing with an action-to-effect arrow, dashed if it is a "might".
- **5 min. Three school actions.** Pictured: a pick-up routine with a reminder at the bin; reusing paper by its blank side; recycling what our bin accepts (the school's accepted-materials picture). Each is tied to the kind of litter we counted most. Choose by hands, the teacher touching each hand once; a tie goes to the pick-up routine.
- **4 min. Start the trial.** 2 minutes: each child draws a reminder card (a hand and the place it goes). 2 minutes: crayons to the tub, wipes, cards gathered. The teacher posts them and does the first pick-up at the next break, alone with the adult bag, gloves and grabber (a set-up).
- **5 min. Exit.** E56a, E56b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice is four picture chains pointed to aloud, three action pictures chosen by hand and a drawn reminder card; the exit is spoken only, on a new fair picture, and asks for one thing the school could do for the pond, with no pointing at chains and no drawing.

Held by: teacher (chart, trial); children (cards, mats). Tool: None. By hand: marks, drawing. Evidence: E56a, E56b. Hand-backs out: C to day 57.

## Day 57


- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 56 exit, C, 2 minutes.
- **2 min. A new frame.** Teach "If we ___, then ___" as new, with one model sentence and the arrow on the class drawing.
- **7 min. Clean objects.** Each group has one clean bottle and five reused sheets. Each child takes a 1-minute turn showing the action with the bottle or the blank side of a sheet and saying "If we ___, then ___"; the rest draw the arrow on a sheet. 2 minutes to set out and pack. The teacher hears one child in each of the six groups.
- **8 min. Draw it.** Each child draws the chosen action and the effect it changes, with a dictated label; the teacher scribes six.
- **4 min. Revise.** The class drawing and sentence are revised from what was said: the effect is named by part, and "might" stays where it was not seen.
- **3 min. Exit.** E57a (all touch at once), E57b (three heard, 30 seconds each).
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Samples boxed; wipes for everyone, the sinks for any child who wants them.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice shows the action with a bottle or a sheet, says the new frame and draws the action and its effect; the exit touches the part a classroom corner's cups and sheets would change and tells why aloud, in a picture the class never drew, with no samples to handle.

Held by: children (bottle, drawing); teacher (chart). Tool: None. By hand: show, draw. Evidence: E57a, E57b. Hand-backs out: B and C to day 58.

## Day 58


- **3 min. Return and repair.** Hand-backs: day 57 exit, B and C, 1.5 minutes each.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** "Which tally row has more?" and "what did we start doing?" (review).
- **5 min. A school corner we have not seen.** Invented, said aloud: a picture of paper under a bench and bottles by a drain. Together: point to the litter, name our action and what it changes.
- **6 min. Pairs.** A tells "If we ___, then ___"; B points to the part of the picture that shows it; swap, 2 minutes each, 2 to move. The teacher hears three pairs.
- **6 min. Draw it after.** Each child draws the corner after our action, with a dictated label.
- **4 min. Is it clear?** A partner points to the part that shows the action. The class sentence is revised from what was unclear.
- **3 min. Section exit.** E58a, E58b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice looks at a picture of paper and bottles, names an action in a pair sentence and draws the corner after; the exit gives two drawings of a different corner and asks the child to say what changed between them, a spoken comparison with no drawing and no pair turn.

Held by: children (own drawing); teacher (chart). Tool: None. By hand: draw, point. Evidence: E58a, E58b. Hand-backs out: F to day 59.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 55, "What did you see, and what might happen to it?"; day 56, "Is it land, water, air or a living thing that is hurt?"; day 57, "If we do this, what changes?"
- Written task, day 57: a drawing with a dictated label; the teacher scribes for the six heard.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: the action is named; the arrow reaches an effect; the effect is a part (land, water, air or a living thing); "might" is said where it was not seen.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: litter, bin, wind, might, reuse, recycle. Frames: "I saw ___." "It might go ___." "If we ___, then ___."
- **Access.** Large-handle grabbers or a child directing an adult's hands; a child who stays in goes to the photographs and the chart. Non-latex gloves.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; accept pointing, home-language speech and dictation; never score neatness; record help as "shown with help".
- **Reading load.** None.
- **Extension, same standard.** Count a second kind of litter and say which action would lessen it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look at a tidy and an untidy corner in a photograph you take, or a picture you draw. Count the pieces by touching each once and ask where one might go; draw an arrow. Then pick one small action and draw what it changes. Never ask about the home bin; handle no litter without gloves.
- **One projector.** Show the chains and the chart large; each child still marks their own mat and tally.
