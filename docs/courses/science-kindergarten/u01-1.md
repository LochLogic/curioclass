# Section 1.1: Fair tests of pushes and pulls



<!-- skel -->
Science, Kindergarten. Unit 1, Pushes and pulls. *Why does our ball sometimes roll all the way to the wall, sometimes stop halfway and once turn a corner?* Anchor: a ball rolled across the rug by different children (days 1 to 9): see [s01.md](s01.md). **Days 1 to 4.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (1, 2, 3), application 1 (4). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 2 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->



## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K-PS2-1** (days 1, 2, 3, 4): Plan and carry out a fair test comparing how harder or softer pushes and pulls, or pushes in different directions, change how an object moves.
- skill sk-push-pull (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Teaching moves.** Model safe rolling and recording on day 1, one-change planning on day 2. Then ask what to change, keep and watch. Repeat unclear trials rather than supply conclusions.
>
> - **Question board.** Group children's notices and wonders: Why different stopping places (day 2)? Does pulling work similarly (day 3)? Can push direction explain the turn (day 4)? Can contact change something already moving (day 5)? Which test checks our explanation (day 6)? Does it hold for a loaded box (day 7)? Return answers to the rug-ball puzzle. Days 8 and 9 transfer to an unfamiliar ball bumping a parcel box beside new tape destinations.
> - **Carried model.** Day 1: children draw before, contact and after; combine ideas into a picture chart. Day 3 adds pull evidence from matched trials. Day 4 revises direction, also pulling the box toward two tape marks; day 5 adds stopping and collisions. Day 6 reaches consensus from children's chosen tests. Day 7 qualifies strength claims to matched objects and conditions. Keep this revised chart for transfer; children use it without copied answers.
<!-- /skel -->

- **K-PS2-1, this section's share.** "Plan and carry out a fair test comparing how harder or softer pushes and pulls, or pushes in different directions, change how an object moves." Here: planning and carrying out one-change comparisons of push strength (day 2), pull strength (day 3) and push direction (day 4), with the teacher and classmates.
- **Limits that bite here.** Strength or which way, never both in one test. No numbers, speed figures, force, friction, magnets or energy words. Farther does not always mean faster, so children watch the start and the speed as well as the stopping place. Stopping places are compared side by side on tape strips: the instrument's step is one tape piece, and a difference smaller than that is called "about the same".
- **Connections.** K.MD.A.2 (math): side-by-side stopping strips, days 2, 3 and 4, no counting.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A ball rolls on until the floor slows it, but children are not told why; their puzzle is answered only by what they test. Hard hand rolls overlap, so a strong roll may stop nearer than a gentle one: record what happened, repeat the pair once, keep both records and never fix a result. The turn in the rug-ball hook is a sideways touch; day 4 tests it. Write children's ideas on the chart in their words. Fitted impact eye protection is worn by everyone present for all moving-object work (unit plan field 8).

## 3. Checks, written first

<!-- skel -->
<!-- /skel -->

**Unit assessment items owned:** none; items 1 to 6 (days 8 and 9) belong to section 1.2. The scored record is the day 8 or 9 sitting, never these exits.

**Evidence form.** Every exit is asked aloud, with picture cards, after the teacher says "this is invented for practice". Each child marks one card (points and places a counter) on the picture mat; the teacher hears a rotating six, 20 seconds each, while the others draw on the back of the mat. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen; the record sheet has a "heard" column. One ask in each stem. These replace the two-part exits in s01 field 5 (plan point 1).

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's warm-up repairs that error (five minutes).
- **E1 (day 1).** "A cushion sat still on the floor. Then it slid. Touch the picture that shows what made it slide." Cards: a hand pressing the cushion; a hand held away from it with the cushion already sliding; the cushion alone with a curved arrow. Answer: the hand touching it. Wrong: "it slid by itself" or the held-away hand or the cushion alone (C).
- **E2 (day 2).** "Pip wants to check if a harder push sends a pram farther. Touch the two pictures that make a fair check." Cards: pair 1, the same pram, same start, same way, a gentle then a harder push; pair 2, a small pram pushed gently and a big pram pushed hard. Answer: pair 1. Wrong: "use the big pram for the hard one" or pair 2 (A).
- **E3 (day 3).** "Pip says only pushing can start something moving. Touch the picture that shows Pip is not right." Cards: a hand pulling a drawer knob, the drawer opening; a hand pushing a toy car. Answer: the drawer. Wrong: "a pull can't make it go" or the car (B).
- **E4 (day 4).** "A tray is sliding toward a wall with a gap at the left. Touch the push that could send it through the gap." Cards: a hand pushing the tray from its right side toward the left; a different, smaller tray pushed straight ahead. Answer: the side push. Wrong: "use the small tray" or the small-tray card (F).

- **Carried.** The class picture chart (before, touch, after) is held by the teacher and revised: day 1 starts it from the hook; day 2 adds "a gentler or harder push changes how it goes, and we watch how it starts"; day 3 adds pulls from matched trials; day 4 adds "which way it is touched changes which way it goes". Each child holds a three-box strip. An entry a trial contradicts is changed, not removed.
- **Key moment, day 2: stopping places side by side.** Ideal instrument: tape marks on the floor lane plus paper dot strips laid beside each other. Spec: no tool in this unit; floor tape and paper fit. The step is one tape piece; closer than that is "about the same". If a reading is unclear, repeat once and keep both.
- **Key moment, day 3: a pull seen to start a box.** Ideal: the same box and string, each pull watched at the start. Fits. If the box does not move, use the smooth strip of floor; if it still does not, show the teacher's dated photo record from rehearsal and say aloud it was made the day before.
- **Key moment, day 4: the turn.** Ideal: two tape X marks, a ball pushed toward each. Fits. The ball may miss an X; "toward the left X" is enough.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Three balls (or boxes) run at a time; half the class rolls while half draws or guesses, then they swap. Day 1 uses 4 + 4 minutes; days 2 to 4 use 6 + 6. Five children at two tries of about 35 seconds is under 6 minutes. One adult watches three adjacent lanes.
- **Clean-up (2 minutes).** Balls into lane bins (1 minute); one hand wipe per child into a bin (1 minute); the teacher collects the eye protection and cleans it after class.
- **A room with nothing.** A tightly taped paper ball, a scrap box with string and masking tape do everything. With no eye protection run no group trials; use teacher-made dated photographs with real marks (weaker: viewing is not carrying out).

## 6. Days

## Day 1

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Watch the rug ball stop in different places, share notices and wonders, and start a before-and-after picture chart.
- note: Hook and question board; introduce looking, listening and drawing without tasting or sniffing. Start the teacher-led two-minute daily weather chart on school day 1, outside lesson time; keep it all year. K.MD.A.2 is in math unit 3, weeks 9 to 13: bridge by side-by-side tape marks, no numbers.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **6 min. Hook.** Three volunteers (any child may pass; the teacher rolls instead) roll one ball from the same start tape across the rug. The teacher places a tape piece where it stops each time. One roll gets a sideways hand tap and turns. Ask the compelling question aloud; no cause is given.
- **6 min. Teaching first.** Looking, listening and drawing; floor-only rules, eye protection on, wait behind the line. Worked example: the teacher rolls the ball and draws three boxes (before, touch, after) on the board, saying "touch" aloud.
- **8 min. Roll and draw, in halves (4 + 4).** Half the class (three groups, three balls) takes one roll each, about 40 seconds, then draws its own strip; the other half draws the hook from memory, then they swap.
- **5 min. Rug: notices and wonders.** The teacher scribes on the question board. Pip the puppet asks one question at a time. The class picture chart begins with the strips.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E1.

Held by: teacher (chart, board); children (own strip). Tool: None. By hand: three-box strip, crayons. Evidence: E1. Outside the 30 minutes: the teacher-led two-minute weather chart starts on school day 1.

## Day 2

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Plan and carry out a gentle-push and strong-push comparison using the same ball and starting place.
- note: Children help choose what changes and what stays the same; cause-effect introduced. Teacher records dots for stopping places; only strength changes.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Look at the chart. Hand-back: day 1 exit, error C (touch and no-touch cards).
- **5 min. Teaching first.** Plan a fair test: Pip guesses a harder push goes farther. The class says what to change (how hard), what to keep (same ball, same start tape, same way) and what to watch (the start and where it stops). The teacher rolls gentle then hard and lays a dot strip beside the lane.
- **12 min. Gentle and strong, in halves (6 + 6).** Half the class rolls gentle then strong (about 35 seconds a try) and marks dots on a strip; the other half draws its guess, then compares it with the first half's strips side by side. Swap.
- **3 min. Rug.** Strips side by side: "nearer", "farther", "about the same". Unclear pairs are repeated, both kept.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E2.

Held by: teacher (chart, keep-change-watch cards); children (guess and strip). Tool: None. By hand: dot strip, guess drawing. Evidence: E2.

## Day 3

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Compare gentle and stronger pulls on a box and describe its motion before and after.
- note: Candidate kept: oral before-and-after descriptions embedded in days 2 to 7, zero added days; only pull strength changes.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Hand-back: day 2 exit, error A (same-object and different-object card pairs).
- **5 min. Teaching first.** Does pulling work like pushing? The teacher pulls a box by its string gently, then harder, from the same start, and the class says what started and how.
- **12 min. Gentle and stronger pulls, in halves (6 + 6).** Each child pulls the box gently then harder toward their own tape mark, standing still and drawing the string in. The waiting half draws before and after strips, then they swap.
- **3 min. Rug.** Strips side by side; add "a pull can change how it moves" to the chart.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** The teacher removes the strings.
- **3 min. Exit.** E3.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (strips). Tool: None. By hand: before and after strip, dictated label. Evidence: E3.

## Day 4

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Application. Applies taught work to a new task or case (an inquiry, a lab, a draft, a revision or edit); nothing is taught as new.
- goal: Keep push strength similar and change its direction to explain the ball turning.
- note: Direction alone varies; use tape marks as the math bridge, not numerical distance.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Warm-up.** Hand-back: day 3 exit, error B (two pulled objects shown with hands).
- **3 min. Plan.** Application: the class applies the one-change plan. Keep the ball and a similar slow push; change which way. Two tape X marks, left and right; the group says what to keep, change and watch.
- **12 min. Push toward each mark, in halves (6 + 6).** Each child pushes the ball slowly from the side toward each X. The waiting half marks the paths on a strip.
- **2 min. Demonstration.** Two volunteers (or the teacher) pull the box toward each X while the class watches.
- **3 min. Rug.** Tell how the ball turned and what touched it; revise the chart.
- **2 min. Clean-up.**
- **3 min. Exit.** E4. Section 1.1 closes.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (path strip). Tool: None. By hand: path strip. Evidence: E4.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 2, "What will we change, and what stays the same?"; day 3, "Does a pull work like a push? How do you know?"; day 4, "Which way did the ball go, and what touched it?"
- Written task: none; day 3, a drawn before and after strip with a dictated label.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the boxes are in order; the touch is drawn in the middle; the last box differs from the first.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 1 has none. Day 2 warm-up: the touch cards. Day 3 warm-up: same-object pairs. Day 4 warm-up: the pulled box. Each warm-up is also the day's repair.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: push, pull, touch, stop, start, fast, slow. Frame: "The ball moved when ___ touched it." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** Thick tape marks for low vision; a lane on a low table for a child who cannot kneel; a child may direct a partner who pushes, and the planning is still the child's.
- **Check accommodations.** Spoken stems; answer by pointing or placing a counter.
- **Reading load.** None. All words are spoken.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan a second fair pair of your own, changing one thing.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** On the floor, roll a soft ball gently then harder from one spot, mark each stop with tape, and ask "What did you change? What stayed the same?" Use the unit's safety limits.
- **One projector.** Show a photograph of the day's strips side by side; the trials stay on the floor.
