# Section 6.2: Did our change help, and can we show it?

## Header


## 1. Standards and practices


- **K-ESS3-3, this section's share (days 59 to 61).** "Share ideas, by talking or drawing, for ways people can lessen their harm to local land, water, air and/or living things." Day 59 reads a second count and revises the explanation from it. Day 60 asks each child to share a detailed solution, drawn and told, for a place not yet seen. Day 61 finishes the remaining children and responds.
- **Limits that bite here.** Fewer pieces alone cannot show our action caused it (E); rain, who was away and clean-up by others are recorded and named. A smaller count is a result, and a count that did not fall is a result too ("did not work, change one thing"). The class model holds all four parts; one child may speak to one or more. Shortcuts and walking on plants are about feet and plants, never about named people.
- **Connections.** Math K.CC.C.6 (compare two counts; from memory), day 59. ELA W.K.2 (draw, tell, dictate), days 60 and 61.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** How to read two counts beside notes without claiming cause; why the transfer scene is withheld; why work made on day 60 is scored only after its feedback and revision; how to record each child on the one scale and keep one record across both days.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: 6.1 ends on day 58; its hand-back F lands in day 59's Return and repair. After: the end of unit 6. Day 60 has no repair slot, so day 59's hand-back E waits for day 61's response, said on both days. No readiness check; results go to the record sheet on the one scale, and the unit plan's points are assigned later in teacher planning.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** T1 to T3 (days 60 and 61), unit plan field 5. Each child has an own mat and marks with the group; the teacher hears one brief reason per child and sees every mark. Stems are split so one ask is one item. The scene is invented, said aloud to be invented, and not taught before any group's turn.

**Days 60 and 61, T1 to T3 (held back).** Invented: a schoolyard picture where people take a shortcut across small plants; footprints and bent stems cross the bed; no route is drawn. Each group has 7 minutes: T1 1.5, T2 2.5, T3 2, feedback and revision 1.
- **T1a (mat).** "Touch what people do here." Answer: the footprints, walking across the plants.
- **T1b (mat).** "Touch what changes." Answer: the bent or flattened plants. Wrong: "walking cannot change the plants" (G).
- **T2a (mat).** "Draw a solution for this place." **T2b (mat).** "Draw what it would change." Answer: a different way for feet to go (a hard path, a way around) with the plants left upright. Wrong: the way moved across another part of the same plants, "now they are safe" (H).
- **T3 (heard, partner).** "Tell your partner why your solution helps. Use your drawing." Answer: the action and the part it spares, "Use this path so feet do not bend the plants." Wrong: only decoration, "a pretty border tells people what to do" (D). Any feasible solution with a defensible link is accepted, not only the example.

**Exits.**
- **E59a (day 59).** Invented: two areas with before and after counts, five then two where a reminder was added, seven then four where none was. "Touch the corner that had fewer wrappers after the action." Answer: the first corner. The teacher checks the card's numbers against the class tallies and changes the card if they match.
- **E59b.** Pip says: "Only the reminder made litter go down." "Change what Pip says, using both areas." Answer: "Both went down, even without a reminder; we cannot tell it was only the reminder." Wrong: "the reminder did it, the numbers went down" (E).
- **E61a (day 61, section exit, not scored).** Invented: an open bin spilling paper toward water. "Touch the card that keeps the paper in" (lid closing, paper under the shrubs, a longer path). Answer: the lid closing. Wrong: paper tucked under the shrubs (C).
- **E61b.** "Tell why it helps." Answer: "If we ___, then less paper reaches the water." Wrong: "a nice border shows people what to do" (D).

| | Wrong example beside a right one; day | If many made it |
|---|---|---|
| E | "It fell, so the reminder did it" beside "both fell, we cannot tell". Day 59 E59b. | Day 61 response, with the group from the record: put the rain and clean-up note beside the counts, 1.5 min. |

The response holds at most four groups, chosen by the most missed component; the rest work in pairs from the pictured move. A group of more than a third of the class is whole-class.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The 6.1 schoolyard drawing, cause arrows and evidence sentence get the second count on day 59 and are agreed that day with the question board read back; keep every version for the check. Day 61 refines the explanation from feedback. Review, in the ledger's words: keep, change, watch; guess; worked, did not work; one change, then retest; claim names its objects; picture rows side by side; picture-caption sticker; observation sitting with own mat, six groups in turn; independent familiar drawing while one group is observed; caption read aloud, then revise own mat; return and repair, at most four groups. New: before and after, other reasons, cannot tell yet.
- **Key moment 1, day 59: the recount.** Ideal instrument: the same zones, same count rule, the same after-lunch time, beside a note of rain, who was away and others' clean-up. Fits. Whole pieces; a difference of one is "about the same" (ours, untested). If it rained or the patch cannot be used, count the next dry day and say so; or use the dated photographs of the same area and say they are photographs. The count may rise or not fall: that is reported and the model changes one thing.
- **Key moment 2, days 60 and 61: seven minutes for five children.** Ideal: the teacher beside each child. Partly fits. Marks come all at once; the teacher hears each child about 12 seconds in T1 and in T3 (1 minute each), writes a picture-caption sticker per child during the drawing, and reads them aloud once (about 5 seconds each). Unheard children are marked "not yet seen", not wrong.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 59: as day 55 (two shifts of three collectors, new collectors this time, two sinks). Days 60 and 61: paper only; 30 mats and the scene, five mats in use at a time.
- **Set-ups.** None beyond day 59's pick-up.
- **A room with nothing.** Photographs with real counts for day 59; the scene drawn by hand for days 60 and 61.

## 6. Days

## Day 59


- **2 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 58 exit, F, 2 minutes: point to what our bin takes.
- **1 min. Warm-up.** Guess: more, fewer or about the same pieces than day 55? Each child touches a card; guesses are kept (review).
- **2 min. Out.** To the same patch and zones.
- **5 min. Tally.** The same count rule, the same after-lunch time, the same picture tally. The weather, who is away and any clean-up by others since day 56 are written beside it. The teacher counts any zone above ten.
- **4 min. Pick-up.** Two shifts of three collectors, 2 minutes each, new collectors from each group; the teacher alone bags; the rest sit in sight and say "I saw ___".
- **5 min. Back and wash.** 2 minutes in; wipes at the door, the collectors at the two sinks.
- **3 min. Compare.** Day 55 and day 59 rows side by side by kind; more, fewer or about the same; the notes read aloud; the guesses checked.
- **5 min. Closing explanation.** The class dictates and the teacher scribes: how loose waste can touch land, water, air and living things here; how our action might help; what we cannot tell yet. The question board is read back and answered questions marked.
- **3 min. Exit.** E59a, E59b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice tallies the class patch by touching each piece and compares the day 55 and day 59 rows by kind; the exit uses the finished counts of two invented corners and asks the child to touch the one with fewer wrappers, with no tallying and no row.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (zone tally, mats). Tool: None. By hand: tally marks, touch card. Evidence: E59a, E59b. Hand-backs out: E to day 61 (day 60 has no slot); none else.

## Day 60


- **2 min. Set-up and packing.** The scene, mats and crayons are at the front table; the five other groups draw the familiar school solution alone.
- **28 min. Four groups observed, 7 minutes each.** Groups 1 to 4. T1a and T1b, then T2a and T2b, then T3 and the sticker comments, as in field 3. A group's five children mark at once; the teacher hears each child's brief reason, gives each a sticker read aloud once, and each may redraw or retell one part alone before leaving. No coaching. An absent child finishes on day 61 in the independent window.

Held by: children (own mat). Tool: None. By hand: mat, drawing. Evidence: T1 to T3 for 20 children, held back; stickers now; scored in teacher planning on day 61, after revision.

## Day 61


- **2 min. Set-up.** The scene, mats and the record sheet; the other groups draw the familiar solution alone.
- **14 min. Groups 5 and 6 observed, 7 minutes each.** T1 to T3 exactly as day 60, same scene, same stickers.
- **4 min. Record and model.** The record was marked at the front table during each group; the class agrees the schoolyard drawing with all four parts, the cause arrows and the evidence sentence, revised from the stickers and the counts.
- **2 min. Return and repair: feedback.** Sticker meanings read aloud once to the groups responding.
- **5 min. Return and repair: repairs and revision.** At most four groups from the record, about 1.25 minutes each with the teacher: G, H, D and E, or A, B, C or F where an exit showed them; the rest work in pairs from the pictured move and redraw one part alone. No reteaching of the scene.
- **3 min. Section exit.** E61a, E61b. Not scored.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice runs the day 60 scene with stickers and a drawn model of the schoolyard; the exit is a new picture of a bin with three action cards, and the child touches the one card that keeps the paper in, with no scene, no sticker and no drawing.

Held by: children (own mat). Tool: None. By hand: mat, redraw. Evidence: T1 to T3 for the last ten children, held back; E61 is response. The unit's response is counted once, here. Day 61 products are scored on the next school day, after revision; days 62 and 63 carry the record unchanged.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 59, "What changed, and what else might have changed it?"; day 60, "What do people do here, and what changes?"; day 61, "Why does your change help?"
- Written task, day 60: the drawing of the solution with a dictated label, made on the mat.
- Judged by the teacher with four look-fors: the human action is named; the plants or place it changes are shown; the solution changes the action, not only the look; the reason links the action to less harm.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 59 pulls the day 55 tally, the day 56 chains and the day 57 arrow. Day 60 pulls the whole unit's drawing and the claim "I think ___, because I saw ___" (unit 4). Day 61 pulls the design cycle (unit 3), unit 5's cover and the agreed schoolyard system.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: before, after, fewer, more, path, protect. Frames: "Before there were ___, after there were ___." "We cannot tell yet, because ___." "Use this path so ___."
- **Access.** Large action cards; a child may direct an adult to draw; the teacher records the child's choices and the help. A child who cannot go outside on day 59 uses the photograph set.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; accept pointing, home-language speech, dictation and a partner's gesture; never score reading, spelling, speech or neatness; record help as "shown with help".
- **Reading load.** None.
- **Extension, same standard.** Plan two solutions for the same place and say which spares more.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Count two pictures of the same place, one earlier and one later, by touching each piece once; say what is fewer and what else could have changed it. Last day: draw a change for a picture you make of a messy path or table, then ask why it helps. No report on the home, no grading.
- **One projector.** Show the two count rows and the scene large; each child still marks their own mat.
