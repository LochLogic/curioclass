Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#science/science-kindergarten/2/s/2.2), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py science-kindergarten u02-2.md`. <!-- facts link -->

## 1. Standards and practices


- **K-ESS3-2, this section's share.** "Ask questions to find out why people forecast the weather and how forecasts help a community get ready for and handle dangerous storms." Here: asking information questions of a calm forecast page (day 15), of a second source (day 16) and of dated regional records (day 17), then each child asking, obtaining, and saying what the school does before and during a storm (day 19).
- **K-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Observe the local weather, share the observations, and describe the patterns that show up over days and months." Day 18 only: each child shares counted daily and monthly patterns from an unfamiliar record. Nothing new is taught.
- **Limits that bite here.** No percentages, probability or air mechanisms. A forecast says what is possible, a warning does not make a storm. Thunderstorm is the worked case, the real forecast pages are from northeastern Colorado, and the recess story is invented (said aloud) while the weather information is real. Another region's hazard (blizzard, heat, flood, tornado, hurricane) needs its own pages first. Records show how many dates a station reported thunder in an interval, not damage and not a rule for ever. Calm pictures only; no disaster accounts; no child is asked about a home that lacks heat or coats.
- **Connections.** ELA SL.K.3 (ask and answer questions to get information), days 15 to 17, bridged by a teacher-modeled question and spoken starters. K.MD.B.3 (math: count and sort), days 17 and 18. Cause and effect (crosscutting), days 15 and 17, as a review of unit 1.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A forecast is a statement of what may happen later, made from records of past patterns and from the latest observations (such as a radar picture). A forecast can be wrong in either direction, which is why the school plans before and still follows its plan when thunder is heard. When thunder is heard the school moves into the substantial school building and follows the school's plan for how long to stay; a tree is not shelter. Questions a page cannot answer are kept as "asked, not on this page" and are never errors.

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class picture chart, the picture graph and the cause-effect chain stay on the wall. Day 15 extends cause and effect: a forecast page is the cause, the school's plan the effect, shown on the three-box strip. Day 17 revises the chain to four boxes: records show patterns, patterns and today's page help predict, a prediction leads to a plan, the plan is carried out. All three representations (chart, graph, chain) are kept for days 18 and 19 and are used without copied wording.
- **Key moment, day 15: asking for information.** Ideal instrument: a forecaster who answers the children's questions. Spec: does not exist in this unit (tools: None); stand-in, a calm picture page that can answer only some questions. It partly fits: a question the page cannot answer is kept, not corrected.
- **Key moment, day 17: records beside today's page.** Ideal: the dated station records themselves. Fits, as picture squares with the interval on the caption and teacher-labeled totals above ten.
- **Key moments, days 18 and 19: each child's own marks.** Ideal: one mat a child, watched by the teacher. Fits. A child not seen is recorded "not yet seen".
- **Equipment and minutes.** Days 15 to 17: six table copies of the pages, one for each group of five; no shared item. Days 18 and 19: thirty mats, six table sets; a group's four minutes (W1 1, W2 1, W3 2) and three minutes (F1 and F2 together 2, F3 1) hold five children marking at once, with the teacher hearing each reason in turn.
- **Clean-up.** Days 15 to 17: crayons and pages back, 1 minute inside the day. Days 18 and 19: change-overs and packing (day 18, 4 minutes; day 19, inside the groups' set-up).
- **A room with nothing.** Paper pages only; the forecast is media evidence, never a made storm. No screen is needed. If a child speaks about a frightening event, the teacher thanks them quietly and moves on; no one is asked to retell one.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 15, "What do you want to find out?"; day 17, "How can old storm days help us get ready?"; days 18 and 19 hear one reason per child.
- Written task: none; the mat is marked by pointing and drawing, and the teacher writes a dictated line.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the question asks for something the page can answer; the answer is pointed to on a page; the plan comes before the storm.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 15 warm-up repair is day 14's. Day 16 uses the question cards. Day 17 uses the July 1 page and the chain. Days 18 and 19 sample days 10 to 17 on new records and pages. Each day's evidence is spoken or pointed; the record sheet notes "heard".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: forecast, possible, storm, thunder, plan, ready, inside, question. Frame: "I want to find out ___." and "The page says ___." Home-language questions are accepted on days 15, 16 and 19.
- **Access.** Large high-contrast or tactile picture mats; a child may point or use communication symbols for the question; a partner may read pictures aloud but may not choose.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, enlarge cards, accept pointing, dictated words and home-language reasons.
- **Reading load.** None. All words are spoken; the read-aloud is read by the teacher.
- **Extension, same standard.** Ask a question of two pages and say which page answers it.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Look at one forecast picture and ask "What do you want to find out?"; then plan indoor play before the afternoon. On the last day of the unit, give the child a new page and ask for a question and a plan. Use calm pictures only.
- **One projector.** Show the pages and record squares large; the mats on days 18 and 19 are paper.
