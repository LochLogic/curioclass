# Section 2.1: What our weather records show



<!-- skel -->
Science, Kindergarten. Unit 2, Weather watch. *Why do we need our coats at morning recess but not after lunch?* Anchor: the coat pile at morning and afternoon recess; later, an indoor recess called for a storm in the forecast (days 10 to 19): see [s02.md](s02.md). **Days 10 to 14.** Days by kind: understanding 3 (10, 11, 13), fluency 1 (12), application 1 (14). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 2 in the unit.

## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K-ESS2-1** (days 10, 11, 12, 13, 14): Observe the local weather, share the observations, and describe the patterns that show up over days and months.
- skill sk-weather-watch (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Teaching moves.** Model one dated entry on day 10 and a question on day 15. Then let children choose evidence, ask questions and revise claims; repeat unclear observations.
>
> - **Question board.** What changed between recesses (days 10 to 12)? Does it repeat (day 13)? What does a whole month show (day 14)? If patterns vary, how can we plan ahead (days 15 and 16)? How do regional patterns and today's information help predict danger (day 17)? Transfer: unfamiliar dated weather records, then a new school outing forecast.
> - **Limits.** No degrees, averages, probability or atmospheric mechanisms. Surface warmth is not air temperature; day 12 cannot establish an air-weather pattern. Categories overlap. A forecast describes possibilities; a warning does not cause a storm. No engineering expectation is owned.
> - **Carried.** Retain unit 1's cause-effect chart. Day 10 begins weather-pattern pictures and a picture graph. Day 12 revises the warmth claim from repeat looks; day 14 adds counted monthly evidence. Day 15 extends cause-effect to forecast information causing preparation. Day 17 revises this chain from regional records and reaches consensus: observations support patterns and predictions, which inform actions. Retain all three representations for assessment. Days 13 and 14 practice matching two rows before day 18; day 19's two-month reading is review.
<!-- /skel -->

- **K-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Observe the local weather, share the observations, and describe the patterns that show up over days and months." Here: observing and recording weather and relative warmth (days 10 to 12), arranging dated records in picture rows and counting groups (day 13), and the first complete month with a revised pattern chart (day 14). Forecast questions (K-ESS3-2) wait for section 2.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No degrees, averages, probability or air mechanisms. Warmth is a feel with three words: warmer, cooler, about the same. Surface warmth (day 12) is not air warmth, so day 12 cannot show an air pattern. Whole numbers only: children count to ten with support, the teacher says larger totals, and rows longer than ten are compared by matching squares. A day can sit in two rows (categories overlap). No rule such as "mornings are cooler" is taught; "it varies, so we check each day" is a good finding.
- **Connections.** K.MD.B.3 (math: sort and count), days 13 and 14. Cause and effect (crosscutting), day 10, as a review of unit 1.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A pattern is what shows up again and again in records, so one day (the coat photographs) raises a question and answers none. Warmth marks are felt and relative; they are not compared with any thermometer and the plan never claims they are. Another observer's records (a Denver airport station, credited on every picture master) are used only for the exit questions; their picture key is teacher-only and is in unit plan field 5.

- **Carried.** The class picture chart (unit 1 model) stays; it gains a second sheet, "What we have seen again and again", and the class picture graph. Day 10 starts both. Day 12 revises the warmth claim from the class's own repeat looks, written as "this bench, this afternoon". Day 14 adds counted month evidence. Cause and effect from unit 1 is a review on day 10 and is extended in section 2.2.
- **Key moment, day 12: what the surface feels like in the afternoon against the morning.** Ideal instrument: a surface thermometer shown as a color, not a number. Spec: does not exist in this unit (tools: None), and degrees are a limit; it does not fit. Stand-in: the back of the hand, two marks (cool, warm). Reading step: only a clear difference shows, so equal marks are written "about the same". If it is unsafe to touch (ice, very hot, a metal slide in the sun), no child touches and the class marks sun or shade pictures; if the morning mark is missing, run the invented bench pair of day 12 instead, said aloud to be invented, and write "no real reading today" on the chart.
- **Key moment, days 13 and 14: matching rows and counting.** Ideal: the class picture graph itself. Fits; rows are paper squares, counted by touch. A gap in the class record stays a gap.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 11: all thirty children at one taped spot in sight, no shared item; 8 minutes. Day 12: the bench takes five hands at once (three at a time on a short bench or slide), so six turns of 1 minute or ten of 40 seconds fit inside the 9-minute touch part; the other groups draw. Days 13 and 14: six table copies of the squares, one set per group of five; no shared item, five children touch a row each.
- **Clean-up.** Day 11: 2 minutes, paper and crayons to baskets, nothing touched outdoors. Day 12: one hand wipe a child, a packet of 30 plus a bin at the door, 2 minutes. Days 13 and 14: squares back into envelopes, 2 minutes.
- **Set-up for unit 4, named.** On day 11 the teacher alone photographs a sidewalk weed and an animal-dug hole if one is in sight (about a minute, children keep looking); if none is seen, again on day 12. No child is sent to find them and no child is in the picture.
- **A room with nothing.** Scrap-paper squares for bought cards. Indoors, the looks are made at a window away from the glass in a storm, and the touch is dropped; the dated photographs and notes of the weather are weaker than looking, and the chart says so.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 10, "What do you notice? What are you wondering?"; day 12, "Is it the same on every day? How do you know?"; day 14, "What does the month show that one day did not?"
- Written task, day 14: each group dictates one line under its row ("There are more ___ days than ___ days"); the teacher writes it. No child writes alone.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, morning, afternoon, warmer, cooler, row, count. Frame: "In the afternoon it was ___ than the morning." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** A child who cannot go outside or touch looks at the sun and shade picture and marks that; large and tactile squares (felt for rain, card for sun); wide space at the bench.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, enlarge cards, accept pointing and dictated words.
- **Reading load.** None. Every word is spoken.
- **Extension, same standard.** Lay two other months' rows side by side and say which month had more of one weather.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use one window or step. Look in the morning and again in the afternoon, mark warmer, cooler or about the same, keep a week of squares and sort them into two rows. Use the unit's safety limits.
- **One projector.** Show the chart rows, the picture graph and the Denver pair pictures large; the looks and the bench touch are done in person.
