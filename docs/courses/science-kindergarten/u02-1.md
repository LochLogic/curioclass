# Section 2.1: What our weather records show






## 1. Standards and practices


- **K-ESS2-1, this section's share.** "Observe the local weather, share the observations, and describe the patterns that show up over days and months." Here: observing and recording weather and relative warmth (days 10 to 12), arranging dated records in picture rows and counting groups (day 13), and the first complete month with a revised pattern chart (day 14). Forecast questions (K-ESS3-2) wait for section 2.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No degrees, averages, probability or air mechanisms. Warmth is a feel with three words: warmer, cooler, about the same. Surface warmth (day 12) is not air warmth, so day 12 cannot show an air pattern. Whole numbers only: children count to ten with support, the teacher says larger totals, and rows longer than ten are compared by matching squares. A day can sit in two rows (categories overlap). No rule such as "mornings are cooler" is taught; "it varies, so we check each day" is a good finding.
- **Connections.** K.MD.B.3 (math: sort and count), days 13 and 14. Cause and effect (crosscutting), day 10, as a review of unit 1.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A pattern is what shows up again and again in records, so one day (the coat photographs) raises a question and answers none. Warmth marks are felt and relative; they are not compared with any thermometer and the plan never claims they are. Another observer's records (a Denver airport station, credited on every picture master) are used only for the exit questions; their picture key is teacher-only and is in unit plan field 5.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** W1 to W3 (unit plan field 5), observed on day 18 in section 2.2. They sample what days 10 to 14 taught: describing a dated record, counting a group once each, the morning and afternoon pattern from repeated records, and comparing two months. Components are marked on one scale (shown, shown with help, not yet seen), not points.

**Evidence form.** Every exit is asked aloud, one ask a stem, answered by touching a picture card or a mark on the child's own paper, all children at once. Any case that is made up is called "invented for practice" aloud first. The teacher hears a rotating six and writes it in the "heard" column. Another observer's records are shown as pictures with relative-warmth marks only; the teacher-only readings are in brackets. Every picture master carries the archive credit and the dates. Rule 4: no exit uses the class chart rows, the class looks or the bench taught that week.

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's warm-up repairs it (day 14's goes to day 15).

Pip says every table is warmer in the afternoon. "Does this table say so? Touch yes or no." Answer: no, this table was cooler in the afternoon. Wrong: yes, "afternoons are warm" (A).

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** The class picture chart (unit 1 model) stays; it gains a second sheet, "What we have seen again and again", and the class picture graph. Day 10 starts both. Day 12 revises the warmth claim from the class's own repeat looks, written as "this bench, this afternoon". Day 14 adds counted month evidence. Cause and effect from unit 1 is a review on day 10 and is extended in section 2.2.
- **Key moment, day 12: what the surface feels like in the afternoon against the morning.** Ideal instrument: a surface thermometer shown as a color, not a number. Spec: does not exist in this unit (tools: None), and degrees are a limit; it does not fit. Stand-in: the back of the hand, two marks (cool, warm). Reading step: only a clear difference shows, so equal marks are written "about the same". If it is unsafe to touch (ice, very hot, a metal slide in the sun), no child touches and the class marks sun or shade pictures; if the morning mark is missing, run the invented bench pair of day 12 instead, said aloud to be invented, and write "no real reading today" on the chart.
- **Key moment, days 13 and 14: matching rows and counting.** Ideal: the class picture graph itself. Fits; rows are paper squares, counted by touch. A gap in the class record stays a gap.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Day 11: all thirty children at one taped spot in sight, no shared item; 8 minutes. Day 12: the bench takes five hands at once (three at a time on a short bench or slide), so six turns of 1 minute or ten of 40 seconds fit inside the 9-minute touch part; the other groups draw. Days 13 and 14: six table copies of the squares, one set per group of five; no shared item, five children touch a row each.
- **Clean-up.** Day 11: 2 minutes, paper and crayons to baskets, nothing touched outdoors. Day 12: one hand wipe a child, a packet of 30 plus a bin at the door, 2 minutes. Days 13 and 14: squares back into envelopes, 2 minutes.
- **Set-up for unit 4, named.** On day 11 the teacher alone photographs a sidewalk weed and an animal-dug hole if one is in sight (about a minute, children keep looking); if none is seen, again on day 12. No child is sent to find them and no child is in the picture.
- **A room with nothing.** Scrap-paper squares for bought cards. Indoors, the looks are made at a window away from the glass in a storm, and the touch is dropped; the dated photographs and notes of the weather are weaker than looking, and the chart says so.

## 6. Days

## Day 10


- **5 min. Hook.** The teacher shows two photographs of the class coat hooks, names covered and no child in view, one from morning recess and one from afternoon recess on a day they showed something, beside that day's row of the class weather chart. The compelling question is asked and ideas taken; nobody is told an answer. No child is asked about their own coat or home. If no photographs fit, use two invented pictures and say so aloud.
- **3 min. Review from unit 1.** Retrieval inside the day: children point to the three-box strip cards (before, touch, after) for the rug ball and say what caused the change ("the touch"). For a miss, the teacher models one more strip during the chart part and the child records another.
- **6 min. Teaching first: one dated entry.** The teacher, with Pip speaking one line at a time, reads one row of the chart: the date square, the morning picture and the afternoon picture (sunny, cloudy, rainy, snowy, windy) and the warmth mark (the afternoon against the morning: warmer, cooler, about the same). New words: morning, afternoon, warmer, cooler.
- **8 min. Read the chart, in groups.** Six groups of five, each with a table copy of five dated rows. Each child points to one thing they notice, then draws the sky they would look at to find out what changed. The teacher hears a rotating six. Wonders go on question cards; the class picks one for the board.
- **5 min. Begin the picture graph and the pattern sheet.** The teacher and ten children place the first ten school-day squares in rows (sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy), side by side as the stopping strips were in unit 1. The sheet "What we have seen again and again" stays empty today.
- **3 min. Exit.** E10.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice reads a table copy of dated rows and draws the sky the child would look at again; the exit shows two invented pictures from another observer's record and asks the child to choose which question they help answer from three cards, with no reading of rows and no drawing.

Held by: teacher (chart, photographs, rows); children (own drawing). Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: E10. Hand-backs in: none.

## Day 11


- **3 min. Warm-up.** Review of unit 1: "In the push test, what did we keep, change and watch?" Children answer with the three picture cards. Pip says his guess for the look; children say what a guess is: what we think before we look.
- **6 min. Teaching first: plan the looks.** Pip asks how to find out if the afternoon is warmer. The class chooses a plan card: keep the place (one taped spot), keep how we look (face the same way, one minute), change the time (morning, then afternoon), watch the sky and the feel. Teacher models the morning mark.
- **3 min. Line up.** Safety words said once: stay in sight, touch nothing unknown, never look at the sun. If the weather is unsafe, the look is made from the window.
- **8 min. Morning look outdoors.** All thirty at the taped spot. Each child marks the sky (picture card) and the feel (a start dot) on their own paper. The afternoon look is made at afternoon recess by the same two-minute routine and read on day 12.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Paper and crayons to baskets; nothing was touched.
- **5 min. Rug.** Read three earlier chart rows with the warmth mark: some afternoons warmer, some cooler, some about the same. Repeat an unclear row once and keep both. The sheet says what was actually seen.
- **3 min. Exit.** E11a and E11b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws and marks the child's own morning and afternoon looks; the exit reads two finished day pictures from an invented record and says which day was warmer in the afternoon, a comparison no practice part asks.

Held by: teacher (chart, plan cards); children (own paper). Tool: None. By hand: sky card and start dot. Evidence: E11a, E11b.

## Day 12


- **4 min. Warm-up.** Hand-back: day 11 exit, error A (one repair).
- **4 min. Practice: read and plan.** Read yesterday's afternoon mark beside the morning start dots, then say the plan again with the cards: keep the bench and its sun or shade, change the time, watch the feel. Nothing new is taught.
- **4 min. Line up, go out and teacher check.** Line up and go out in sight; outdoors the teacher alone touches the bench, back of the hand held a few seconds. If it is not safe, no child touches.
- **9 min. Afternoon touch.** Groups of five (three on a short bench) lay the back of one hand on the bench for a few seconds, then mark cool or warm; the waiting groups draw the bench with sun or shade. The morning mark was made that morning by the teacher and that day's six touch-helpers, all named in the "heard" column. The teacher hears a rotating six.
- **4 min. Compare.** The morning dot is laid beside the afternoon dots. The class states a claim that names its objects: "this bench, this afternoon". Say that the bench is not the air.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** One wipe each at the door, wipes to the bin.
- **3 min. Exit.** E12.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the child's own bench, touches it and marks a dot; the exit gives a finished pretend table and asks whether it supports Pip's rule about every table, a yes or no on a claim the practice never tests.

Held by: teacher (morning dot, wipes); children (own dot). Tool: None. By hand: dot and drawing. Evidence: E12. Hand-back out: error A to day 13. Day 12 is taught after lunch recess; a class that meets only in the morning reads the routine's afternoon mark and uses the invented pair.

## Day 13


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Hand-back: day 12 exit, error A (one repair).
- **7 min. Teaching first: rows.** The teacher and Pip put five dated class squares into rows (sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy) and lay two rows side by side to see which is longer. One touch for each square; the last number says how many. Totals over ten are said by the teacher.
- **9 min. Our own rows.** Six groups of five, each with a different five-day set from the live chart. Each child places one day, then one child per row touches and counts it once. The teacher hears a rotating six.
- **4 min. One day, two rows.** A day with rain and wind goes into both rows with the same date; the rows cannot be added to get days.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Squares back into envelopes.
- **3 min. Exit.** E13.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice places five dated class squares in a row and counts by touching each once, with a group and a live chart; the exit is the same count on a new set of five invented squares done alone, so the act is shared, but no placing and no partner is involved and the case is a record the class never saw. The count is the thing measured, not a trick.

Held by: teacher (rows); children (group squares). Tool: None. By hand: sorting squares. Evidence: E13. Hand-back out: error B to day 14.

## Day 14


- **5 min. Warm-up.** Hand-back: day 13 exit, error B (one repair).
- **3 min. Set-up.** The teacher reads the month's dates in order and names the gaps; gaps stay gaps.
- **12 min. Count our first month.** Six groups, each with a table copy of the live record from school day 1 to about day 20, kept with its gaps. Each group sorts the squares into four rows and counts, one row for each child; the fifth child checks. The teacher labels any total over ten and hears a rotating six. A class that started late uses another observer's picture record, labeled and credited, from the same archive for a month other than July to October 2024.
- **5 min. Rug.** The class graph is filled from the group rows. Matching squares shows the longest row. The pattern sheet is revised: the rows that stand out, and "it varies, so we check each day" for the afternoon claim.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Squares into envelopes.
- **3 min. Exit.** E14a, E14b and E14c.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice sorts a group's squares into rows, counts and draws one row, and matches squares on the rug; the exit counts a different row (windy) of the whole month, says one day or two, and says in words what the month shows, with no matching step.

Held by: teacher (graph, pattern sheet); children (group rows). Tool: None. By hand: sorting and counting. Evidence: E14a to E14c. Hand-backs out: errors B and C to day 15's Return and repair.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 10, "What do you notice? What are you wondering?"; day 12, "Is it the same on every day? How do you know?"; day 14, "What does the month show that one day did not?"
- Written task, day 14: each group dictates one line under its row ("There are more ___ days than ___ days"); the teacher writes it. No child writes alone.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the line points to marks in the rows; each date is counted once; the claim names this month and these days, not "always".

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 10 warm-up is the unit 1 strip. Day 11 warm-up is keep, change, watch. Day 12 uses day 11's plan and mark. Day 13 uses unit 1's side-by-side strips. Day 14 uses day 13's rows and the day 12 claim. Each day's repair is the previous exit's letter (field 4).

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, morning, afternoon, warmer, cooler, row, count. Frame: "In the afternoon it was ___ than the morning." Home-language words and pointing are accepted.
- **Access.** A child who cannot go outside or touch looks at the sun and shade picture and marks that; large and tactile squares (felt for rain, card for sun); wide space at the bench.
- **Check accommodations.** Read aloud, enlarge cards, accept pointing and dictated words.
- **Reading load.** None. Every word is spoken.
- **Extension, same standard.** Lay two other months' rows side by side and say which month had more of one weather.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Use one window or step. Look in the morning and again in the afternoon, mark warmer, cooler or about the same, keep a week of squares and sort them into two rows. Use the unit's safety limits.
- **One projector.** Show the chart rows, the picture graph and the Denver pair pictures large; the looks and the bench touch are done in person.
