## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why is the slide in the sun warmer than the one under the tree?"
- **Hook, day 44.** Compare matched sunny and shaded benches or slides after the teacher's safety check. Children notice warmth differences and ask the compelling question. Use dated local observations if touching is unsafe.
- **Closing explanation, day 52.** Dictate why sunlight warms the exposed slide more, using four-material observations and shade tests. The tree blocks sunlight; shade need not feel cold.
- **Teaching moves.** Model recording first. Ask what stays alike, then let children select comparisons and improve covers. Distinguish observations from predictions.

<!-- skel -->
- **K-PS3-1:** Observe how sunlight affects surfaces on Earth, such as sand, soil, rock and water. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Limit: Temperature is compared only in relative words, such as warmer and cooler, not measured in degrees.
- **K-PS3-2:** Design and build, with given tools and materials, a structure such as a shade or tent that keeps sunlight from warming a spot as much. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.)

Start both alike and expose for the same recorded interval before day 54's comparison, during the routine outdoor look. Keep control patches beyond the cover's shadow. In unsuitable weather, retain every physical build, observe its actual coverage and use separately labelled dated thermal media. Record the warming inference as media-based, not a successful test of that child's own cover.
