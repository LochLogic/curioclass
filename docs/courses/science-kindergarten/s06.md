## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does the litter in our schoolyard come from, and what can we do about it?"
- **Hook, day 55.** Walk a bounded playground patch after lunch and tally visible litter before collection. Ask the compelling question without guessing who dropped it. Unsafe outside: use dated same-area photographs.
- **Closing explanation, day 59.** Dictate how loose waste can affect this place and how our solution might help, using both tallies and source-based picture chains. Explain what remains uncertain.
- **Teaching moves.** Model observation versus inference and one action-to-effect arrow. Let children propose questions, choose a feasible solution and explain revisions; do not supply their reasons.

- **K-ESS3-3:** Share ideas, by talking or drawing, for ways people can lessen their harm to local land, water, air and/or living things.

**Observation and scoring.** Each child marks an own picture mat as the teacher reads; no spokesperson substitutes. Children respond concurrently within their group and each gives a brief spoken reason. Others independently draw the familiar school litter solution. All six points require communication with a science idea on the new case, exceeding 80 percent. Score components separately. An absent or inaccessible response is not automatically a misconception. The audience is a partner or class group; no outside sharing is required.

Give a brief written descriptive comment and read it aloud within the seven-minute window. Leave time for independent revision, without reteaching or supplying the answer. Keep original and revision. Score day 60 products in teacher planning on day 61, after their feedback and revision; score day 61 products on the next school day. First-teaching work and daily practice are never scored. Keep one record: day 61 adds only the remaining children, and days 62 and 63 carry it unchanged without another performance or score.

**Accommodations.** Read aloud, enlarge pictures, accept home-language speech, pointing and dictated labels, and offer large action cards. A child may direct an adult to draw accessibly; record the child's choices. Judge causal meaning, not reading, spelling, speech fluency or neatness.

**Teaching evidence.** Keep individual drawings, tally marks and dated model revisions. Day 56 uses original picture chains: trees supply paper; bottle-making uses resources; manufacturing can dirty air; wind and rain can move loose litter toward water; litter can hurt living things. Distinguish observed movement from possible journeys. Compare safe collection, paper reuse and recycling accepted containers. Children choose a school solution targeting a counted litter category, within existing arrangements. Start on school day 127, day 56. Default: a supervised collection routine with a picture reminder at the existing bin. Teacher manages collection; children plan from pictures. Day 57 handles only clean samples and rehearses how the chosen action helps.

**Daily exits.** Each takes three minutes inside the day's thirty, except day 56, which takes five. Withhold every new picture until closing and print "invented for practice" on it. Each child marks paper while the teacher reads; hear a rotating six reasons. Each question has two parts at most. Use register letters only when the stated belief matches.
