Course `science-kindergarten` (Science, Kindergarten). **Days 55 to 61** (7 course days; school days 125 to 139 (the course meets up to 3 times a week, so not every school day in the span), weeks 25 to 28). ## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does the litter in our schoolyard come from, and what can we do about it?"
- **Hook, day 55.** Walk a bounded playground patch after lunch and tally visible litter before collection. Ask the compelling question without guessing who dropped it. Unsafe outside: use dated same-area photographs.
- **Closing explanation, day 59.** Dictate how loose waste can affect this place and how our solution might help, using both tallies and source-based picture chains. Explain what remains uncertain.
- **Teaching moves.** Model observation versus inference and one action-to-effect arrow. Let children propose questions, choose a feasible solution and explain revisions; do not supply their reasons.

- **Parts, by verb:** (a) talk; (b) draw; (c) lessen harm. T1 to T3 check all.


Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


- **Question board.** Group notices into journeys and solutions. Where might litter go (55)? That raises: what can change land, water, air and living things, and which school action could help (56)? How can we show why it helps (57), including elsewhere (58)? What changed after our trial (59)? Transfer: an unfamiliar shortcut through schoolyard plants, withheld until assessment.
- **Limits.** No decomposition rates, manufacturing chemistry or pollution experiments. Recycling depends on accepted materials. One child's explanation may address one affected part; the class model includes all four. Fewer pieces alone cannot establish cause.
- **Carried.** Day 55 starts the schoolyard system drawing, cause arrows, picture tally and tentative evidence sentence; retrieve the design cycle. Day 56 revises system and cause links from picture information about paper, bottles and litter, then adds the chosen trial. Day 57 revises the solution drawing and evidence sentence from clean-object explanations; day 58 tests their clarity elsewhere. Day 59 adds the second tally, revises the design after comparing results, and agrees the system, cause and evidence models. Day 61 refines communication from assessment feedback. Keep every version for transfer.

**Transfer case, invented for practice.** A new schoolyard picture shows people taking a convenient shortcut across small plants. Footprints and bent stems mark the shortcut across the bed; no route is drawn for the answer. No real class or child is blamed. **Observation and scoring.** Each child marks an own picture mat as the teacher reads; no spokesperson substitutes. Children respond concurrently within their group and each gives a brief spoken reason. Others independently draw the familiar school litter solution. All six points require communication with a science idea on the new case, exceeding 80 percent. Score components separately. An absent or inaccessible response is not automatically a misconception. The audience is a partner or class group; no outside sharing is required.

Give a brief written descriptive comment, one phrase per child on a pre-printed card written while the group draws and speaks, and read it aloud within the seven-minute window; a revision is a quick redraw or retelling of one part. Leave time for independent revision, without reteaching or supplying the answer. Keep original and revision. Score day 60 products in teacher planning on day 61, after their feedback and revision; score day 61 products on the next school day. First-teaching work and daily practice are never scored. Keep one record: day 61 adds only the remaining children, and days 62 and 63 carry it unchanged without another performance or score.

**Accommodations.** Read aloud, enlarge pictures, accept home-language speech, pointing and dictated labels, and offer large action cards. A child may direct an adult to draw accessibly; record the child's choices. Judge causal meaning, not reading, spelling, speech fluency or neatness.

**Teaching evidence.** Keep individual drawings, tally marks and dated model revisions. Day 56 uses original picture chains: trees supply paper; bottle-making uses resources; manufacturing can dirty air (from memory); wind and rain can move loose litter toward water; litter can hurt living things. Distinguish observed movement from possible journeys. Compare safe collection, paper reuse and recycling accepted containers. Children choose a school solution targeting a counted litter category, within existing arrangements. Start on school day 127, day 56. Default: a supervised collection routine with a picture reminder at the existing bin. Teacher manages collection; children plan from pictures. Day 57 handles only clean samples and rehearses how the chosen action helps.

**Daily exits.** Each takes three minutes inside the day's thirty, except day 56, which takes five. Each new case is invented for practice, withheld until closing and read aloud. The exits are the items.csv rows s06-X55a to s06-X59b (the exit fixes of 2026-11-22, now in the written days); each row holds the case, the key and the error its hand-back repairs, and the Goal map below gives each shape. Use register letters only when the stated belief matches.

**Section 6.2 exit, day 61.** In the last three response minutes, show an open school bin spilling paper toward water: "Draw a change that keeps the paper contained; show why it helps." Expect a feasible containment action, such as an adult securing the lid, linked to less paper reaching water. Every child marks paper; hear six reasons. This new, unscored case carries the action, effect and benefit.

Day 60 uses twenty for two groups; day 61 uses ten for the last two groups and ten for response. In the latter two groups, reduce T1 to one minute, T2 to two and T3 to two by pre-staging cards, keeping every point and spoken reason. Carry the single per-child record across boundaries. Shorter periods retain day 58. Only if that meeting is lost, fold its scene into day 57: use pictures instead of handled samples. Within the ten teaching minutes, allow four for the chosen solution and six for the transfer, including its three-minute exit; no sample clean-up is needed. Replace duplicate explanations and use the day 58 exit instead of day 57's. Include written feedback and independent revision in each window; score each completed product on the next school day. ## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

- **Tools:** None. One shared device may display the printable charts. No experiment needs a simulation; no tool request is needed.
- **Cost:** the $12 unit allocation within the approximately $40 annual class allowance covers six reusable glove pairs, six grabbers and an adult bag (ours, from memory, untested), using existing stock where needed. Reuse classroom paper. If suitable equipment exceeds the allocation or is unavailable, use photographs.
- **Paths:** reused paper replaces chart stock. For days 55 and 59, dated same-area photographs with real counts provide a weaker no-kit path, without practicing collection. For day 57, original object pictures replace clean samples, with weaker handling evidence. No simulation is specified.

**Safety and set-up.** The day before days 55 and 59, inspect the accessible route, gloves, grabbers and handwashing access. Recheck before entry. Teacher alone bags waste; children handle only cleared dry paper or intact plastic with gloves and grabbers. Leave glass, sharps, food, bodily waste and unknown containers untouched; stop and move away. Stay in sight, wear closed shoes, keep grabbers low and away from faces. No splashing or airborne work is allowed; goggles do not make an unsafe item acceptable. Dry picture work needs no goggles. Check allergies; use non-latex gloves. No chemicals, burning or pollution demonstrations.
