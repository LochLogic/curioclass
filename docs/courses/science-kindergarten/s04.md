# Unit 4: Living things and what they need

Read this plan with its facts from data (days, codes, minutes): [the Front Office](https://curioclass.org/front-office/#science/science-kindergarten/4/plan), or `python -X utf8 scripts/plan_facts.py science-kindergarten s04.md`. <!-- facts link -->

Course `science-kindergarten` (Science, Kindergarten). **Days 32 to 43** (12 course days; school days 73 to 98 (the course meets up to 3 times a week, so not every school day in the span), weeks 15 to 20). Sections: 4.1 (days 32 to 35); 4.2 (days 36 to 39); 4.3 (days 40 to 43). Days by kind: understanding 5, fluency 2, application 3, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- **4.1: Which needs repeat?** Days 32 to 35.
- **4.2: How can a place meet needs?** Days 36 to 39.
- **4.3: Changing places, then showing what we know.** Days 40 to 43.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do the weed in the sidewalk crack and the squirrel by the fence need, and what do they do to get it?"
- **Hook, day 32.** Display the saved fall photographs beside the plants and dated care records. Ask the compelling question before explaining the crack or hole.
- **Closing explanation, day 41.** Dictate how water, light, food and changed surroundings help explain the anchors, using plant records and animal observations. A weed occupying a crack does not prove it made that crack.
- **Teaching moves.** Model observation versus inference first, then one resource link. Ask children which evidence warrants each revision; gradually leave links and reasons to them.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


- **Question board.** Group notices about green leaves, cracks and holes into questions. Which needs repeat (days 33 and 34)? Can another place meet them (35)? That raises: how do place parts supply needs (36 and 37)? If needs depend on places, why change places (38 and 39)? Can people meet needs with less disturbance (40)? Return to unanswered anchor questions on day 41. Transfer: an unfamiliar garden beside a meeting room, reserved for assessment.
- **Limits.** No photosynthesis, food chains, gases, growth measurements or controlled deprivation experiment. Plants do not eat meals; never say they need no food. Roots grow without deciding to damage pavement. Species names are not assessed.
- **Carried.** Day 32 extends the weather-pattern chart into an initial needs-and-places drawing. Days 33 and 34 revise patterns from plant records and food observations; day 35 checks another place. Day 36 links needs to resources; day 37 adds people from the local map. Day 38 starts the evidence sentence from change sequences; day 39 revises a system link using a second sequence. Day 40 adds human changes and a less harmful choice. Day 41 reaches consensus from the collected evidence. Keep all three models available for transfer.

- **Returns.** Unit 5 retrieves the evidence sentence on day 44. Unit 6 retrieves connected needs on day 55 and revises impacts on day 59. Days 62 and 63 revisit needs, places and change arguments.

**Transfer materials, invented for practice.** These original picture records describe an imagined garden beside a meeting room, not actual collected data. Withhold each assessment panel until its observation window. Use different organisms and scenes from teaching and exits. The teacher reads all captions; no species recall is required.

Day 41 supplies a caretaker's mixed delivery log for the garden and meeting room. Two dated rounds show a flowering plant receiving water, a mouse drinking, a rabbit drinking and a child drinking from a cup, mixed with sweeping and resting pictures. The water-delivery labels have fallen off. A separate recovery sequence shows watered plants unpacked from opaque delivery boxes, then new green leaves after several days in light; captions say watering stayed the same. Meal records show the mouse swallowing seeds, the rabbit eating carrot and the child eating rice and fish. A mistaken cart label calls the plant's soil a meal; records show watering and new leaves, never the plant taking in a meal. These are invented records, not a class deprivation experiment. No graphic feeding or species recall is required.

Day 42 supplies a new picture map with a sunny, moist soil bed, a grassy burrow bank, a water source, air symbols and trees supplying building wood. A plant, a burrowing plant-eating animal and people belong on this model. Resource cards include water, air, soil, light, leaves, wood and food grown on land. It is a supplied imaginary place, never a claim about the town's actual water supply.

Day 43 supplies three new before-and-after sequences: growing roots push soil aside toward moist ground; an animal digs a shelter in a bank; people dig a channel bringing water to their food garden. Include an unchanged cloud picture as irrelevant evidence. Two proposed channels deliver the same water: one crosses the existing bare strip, the other removes a patch of plants. Neither is a teaching picture.

**Observation routine.** Six groups of five work beside the teacher. Every child has a separate paper mat and checklist entry. Children mark, connect or draw concurrently; the teacher hears one brief reason from each child per code and sees each child's other responses. Others sort familiar needs pictures independently. The twelve-minute opening on day 41 synthesizes existing evidence without reteaching. Station moves occur within group time that day; packing replaces the final familiar sort. Days 42 and 43 carry completed records unchanged, collecting only their new expectation. Day 33's plant observations remain unscored supporting evidence: do not repeat that plant interview or score first-teaching work.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Plants do not need water. | Misses the common water need. Repair: compare watered and dry plant records. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Water alone keeps plants healthy without light. | Omits light. Repair: compare watered light and dark plant records. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | Soil is a plant's meal. | Treats plants as eating food. Repair: contrast water uptake with an animal eating a leaf. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | Every animal eats the same food. | Misses dietary differences. Repair: match contrasting observed meals. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | A living thing can live anywhere regardless of resources. | Separates place from needs. Repair: trace one missing resource link. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | People in buildings no longer need natural resources. | Excludes humans from the system. Repair: trace water from source to drinking. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| G | Plants cannot change surroundings. | Misses plant effects. Repair: compare soil before and after root growth. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| H | Only people change surroundings. | Excludes animal changes. Repair: compare two different animal changes that meet the same need. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| I | A preference proves a science claim. | Substitutes liking for evidence. Repair: choose an observation supporting the claim. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| J | Two shown routes have equal plant impacts despite different clearing. | Ignores depicted impacts. Repair: compare the plants left by equal-water routes. | Anticipated, from memory. |

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

- **Tools:** None. Paper models fit; one shared device may show pictures. All investigations are feasible without a simulation.
- **Cost:** budget $10 for plants, reused plastic pots and printing within the approximately $40 annual class allocation; estimate, not a quotation.
- **Paths:** reused pots replace bought planters. Dated plant photographs provide a weaker no-kit observation path on days 32, 33 and 41. Animal changes use media, never captive animals. Printed maps replace screens throughout. Start both plants three to four weeks before day 32; the dark-shelf plant stays there a few days at most, then returns to light. If no light difference shows by day 33, use the plant photo record (u04-1/plant-photo-record.md), said aloud to be the weaker path. With no saved fall photographs or water provider report, days 32, 35 and 37 use the invented park pond set and an invented town map, said aloud to be invented.

**Safety and set-up.** The day before, check allergies, confirm both plants are labeled spider plants (Chlorophytum comosum), inspect for mold, pre-cut cards and clear trays. Teacher alone handles pots, watering and soil; children observe. No tasting, sniffing, fertilizers or pesticides. Stop for mold, irritation or spills. Keep soil contained; wash hands after contact. No eye protection is needed for dry paper or contained observation; eliminate dust or splashing rather than expose children. Reserve two clean-up minutes within teaching or transition time.
