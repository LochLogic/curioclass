## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What do the weed in the sidewalk crack and the squirrel by the fence need, and what do they do to get it?"
- **Hook, day 32.** Display the saved fall photographs beside the plants and dated care records. Ask the compelling question before explaining the crack or hole.
- **Closing explanation, day 41.** Dictate how water, light, food and changed surroundings help explain the anchors, using plant records and animal observations. A weed occupying a crack does not prove it made that crack.
- **Teaching moves.** Model observation versus inference first, then one resource link. Ask children which evidence warrants each revision; gradually leave links and reasons to them.

<!-- skel -->
- **K-LS1-1:** Describe, from observations, the patterns in what plants and animals, people included, need to stay alive. Emphasis: animals eat food while plants do not, different animals eat different foods, plants need light, and every living thing needs water.
- **K-ESS3-1:** Use a model to show how what a plant or animal, people included, needs is linked to where it lives.
- **K-ESS2-2:** Argue, using evidence, that plants and animals, people included, change their surroundings to get what they need.

- **Question board.** Group notices about green leaves, cracks and holes into questions. Which needs repeat (days 33 and 34)? Can another place meet them (35)? That raises: how do place parts supply needs (36 and 37)? If needs depend on places, why change places (38 and 39)? Can people meet needs with less disturbance (40)? Return to unanswered anchor questions on day 41. Transfer: an unfamiliar garden beside a meeting room, reserved for assessment.
- **Limits.** No photosynthesis, food chains, gases, growth measurements or controlled deprivation experiment. Plants do not eat meals; never say they need no food. Roots grow without deciding to damage pavement. Species names are not assessed.
- **Carried.** Day 32 extends the weather-pattern chart into an initial needs-and-places drawing. Days 33 and 34 revise patterns from plant records and food observations; day 35 checks another place. Day 36 links needs to resources; day 37 adds people from the local map. Day 38 starts the evidence sentence from change sequences; day 39 revises a system link using a second sequence. Day 40 adds human changes and a less harmful choice. Day 41 reaches consensus from the collected evidence. Keep all three models available for transfer.
