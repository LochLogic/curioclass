## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can we make the ball reach the tower at the end of the rug?"
- **Hook, day 20.** Release a soft ball down a low ramp toward a block tower. Mark its stopping place. Ask the compelling question before offering fixes.
- **Closing explanation, day 26 opening.** Dictate how the revised design reaches the tower, using stopping marks, speed observations and bumper-contact paths. Distinguish reaching farther from moving faster.
- **Teaching moves.** Model safe trials and recording on day 20. Establish the wanted change before building on day 21. Then ask what to change, keep and observe.

<!-- skel -->
- **K-2-ETS1-1:** Ask questions, observe and gather information about something people want to change, to name a simple problem a new or better object or tool could solve.
- **K-PS2-2:** Use test results to decide whether a design, such as a ramp or a turning wall, changes an object's speed or direction the way it was meant to. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Limit: Friction is not used as a reason for an object speeding up or slowing down.
<!-- /skel -->

**Transfer set-up and observation.** Withhold the delivery lane, sideways bay and information cards until day 27. The bay is a broad U of low blocks beside a straight lane, not a tower to knock down. A taped pretend walking strip stays clear of real circulation. People want balls delivered into the bay without chasing them into that strip. Desired outcomes are a faster start than the first gentle push, then a turn into the bay. Use separate trials for speed and direction, keeping the same ball, surface and start within each comparison. Cards show available cardboard, tape, low books and blocks; children ask about the problem before choosing a solution. These are ours, untested, not collected classroom results.

Six groups of five visit the teacher on each day. Each child has an own paper mat and checklist entry beside a shared short lane. Children mark pictures concurrently; stagger actions so the teacher sees each one. Day 27's six four-minute groups plus six minutes for set-up, movement and packing fill 30 minutes. Day 28's six three-minute groups plus two minutes for set-up and packing leave ten minutes for response. Only the supervised group handles rolling balls; others draw the familiar ramp.
