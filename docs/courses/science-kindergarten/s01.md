# Unit 1: Pushes and pulls

Course `science-kindergarten` (Science, Kindergarten). **Days 1 to 9** (9 course days; school days 3 to 21 (the course meets up to 3 times a week, so not every school day in the span), weeks 1 to 5). Sections: 1.1 (days 1 to 4); 1.2 (days 5 to 9). Days by kind: understanding 5, application 2, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- **1.1: What changes a moving object?** Days 1 to 4.
- **1.2: What happens when objects meet?** Days 5 to 9.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why does our ball sometimes roll all the way to the wall, sometimes stop halfway and once turn a corner?"
- **Hook, day 1.** Children roll one ball across the rug. It stops at different places; a sideways hand contact makes it turn. Mark its paths without explaining why yet. Ask the compelling question; keep actual observations, not promised results.
- **Closing explanation, day 7 (day 6 in shorter years).** The teacher writes the children's rug-ball explanation using stopping marks, direction tests and collision observations: contact can start, stop, speed, slow or turn an object. Stronger pushes and pulls change motion more quickly in matched tests.
- **Teaching moves.** Model safe rolling and recording on day 1, one-change planning on day 2. Then ask what to change, keep and watch. Repeat unclear trials rather than supply conclusions.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


- **Question board.** Group children's notices and wonders: Why different stopping places (day 2)? Does pulling work similarly (day 3)? Can push direction explain the turn (day 4)? Can contact change something already moving (day 5)? Which test checks our explanation (day 6)? Does it hold for a loaded box (day 7)? Return answers to the rug-ball puzzle. Days 8 and 9 transfer to an unfamiliar ball bumping a parcel box beside new tape destinations.
- **Limits.** No numerical speed, distance, force, friction mechanism, magnets or energy vocabulary. Vary strength or direction separately. Farther does not always mean faster. Engineering design belongs to unit 3.
- **Carried model.** Day 1: children draw before, contact and after; combine ideas into a picture chart. Day 3 adds pull evidence from matched trials. Day 4 revises direction, also pulling the box toward two tape marks; day 5 adds stopping and collisions. Day 6 reaches consensus from children's chosen tests. Day 7 qualifies strength claims to matched objects and conditions. Keep this revised chart for transfer; children use it without copied answers.

## 3. Where it sits

- **Handoff: weak.** No earlier course or day file exists. Teach observing and drawing without assuming preschool science. Bridge later mathematics with side-by-side stopping marks, without numbers. Read captions aloud; accept dictated labels.
- **Returns.** Unit 2 retrieves recording and cause-effect inside day 10. Unit 3 retrieves contact on day 20 and applies it on days 22 and 24. Unit 5 uses matched comparisons on days 45 and 50; unit 6 traces consequences on day 56.

**Observation protocol.** Use five adjacent short lanes at the teacher station, one per child. Only this group handles moving objects; everyone else independently draws the familiar rug-ball before-and-after explanation. The teacher gives neutral prompts and records each child's own planning, action, picture marks and one brief spoken reason. No group spokesperson, coached answer or reteaching during observation. Children choose pictures concurrently, then stagger actions within each phase so the teacher sees every child. Keep one checklist across both days; day 9 assesses only children not observed on day 8.

Withhold the unused balls, parcel boxes and new tape junction until assessment. A ball bumps a parcel beside two delivery places: what changed its motion, and how could contact send it toward either place? Investigate this connected problem below. Read directions aloud. Keep objects, surface and starts fixed within comparisons.

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Changing the object while comparing contact strength gives a fair test. | Confuses the cause being tested. Repair: compare a same-object pair with a changed-object pair. Shown by item 1. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| B | Pulling cannot change an object's motion. | Does not connect a pull to motion. Repair: compare two pulls on the same box. Shown by item 3. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| C | An object at rest starts moving without contact in these tests. | Omits contact as a cause. Repair: contrast before contact and after contact. Shown by day 1 exit. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| D | An approaching object changes another object's motion before touching it. | Places the collision cause before contact. Repair: order approach, touch and changed motion pictures. Shown by items 2 and 5. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| E | Evidence that disagrees with a prediction must be discarded. | Treats the prediction as the answer. Repair: repeat the matched test and retain both records. Shown by item 6. | Anticipated, from memory. |
| F | Testing the direction of a push or pull requires a different object. | Adds an irrelevant change to a direction test. Repair: compare two directions using the same ball. Shown by item 4. | Anticipated, from memory. |

These are anticipated responses, not measured frequencies. Missing evidence without the stated claim stays unclassified. Each letter has one repair.

## 8. Tools and supplies across the unit

- **Tools:** None. All investigations work with objects and paper; no simulation is needed. One shared device may show records.
- **Cost:** $4 within the $40 allowance reserves tape and string. Eye protection is outside that $4 because the school supplies it: 30 child pairs and one adult pair of child-sized splash goggles marked Z87.1, which also stop impacts (course safety page), so units 3 and 5 reuse the same pairs. Where a school has none, the class watches the dated photographs and runs no trial (Cory, 2026-11-24).
- **Materials paths:** use large, firmly taped paper balls and clean scrap boxes as stand-ins. For pushing, pulling, direction, stopping and collisions, no-kit records are teacher-made dated photographs with actual motion marks. This path is weaker: interpreting records cannot demonstrate carrying out. Retain one ball, box and string for each child's actions; no invented measurements.

**Safety and set-up.** The day before each listed day, clear lanes, inspect balls, smooth boxes, check tape grip and rehearse slow trials. Teacher alone cuts and attaches short thick string, with no body loops. Everyone present wears fitted impact eye protection for moving-object work, demonstrations and clean-up. Stop for unsafe rolling, damaged materials or missing protection. No throwing, tasting or sniffing. Keep fingers outside collisions; store materials afterward and wash hands.
