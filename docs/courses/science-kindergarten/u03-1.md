# Section 3.1: Defining the problem, and testing an idea

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Kindergarten. Unit 3, Ramps and targets. *How can we make the ball reach the tower at the end of the rug?* Design problem: the ball keeps stopping short of, or missing, a block tower. Anchor: our ball rolls down the book ramp on the rug and stops short of the block tower, or rolls right past it, so the class changes the ramp, the push or a bumper until it knocks the tower down (days 20 to 28): see [s03.md](s03.md). **Days 20 to 24.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (20, 21), fluency 1 (23), application 2 (22, 24). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 1 of 2 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 minutes.
<!-- /skel -->

- **K-2-ETS1-1, this section's share (days 20, 21).** "Ask questions, observe and gather information about something people want to change, to name a simple problem a new or better object or tool could solve." Here: the class watches the ball, asks the cards, and says the problem in one sentence before anything is built. Day 25 and the check (day 27) finish the code in section 3.2.
- **K-PS2-2, this section's share (days 22 to 24).** "Use test results to decide whether a design, such as a ramp or a turning wall, changes an object's speed or direction the way it was meant to." Here: build, test, and judge from reached pictures and drawn paths; speed by watching, direction at the touch. The judging across new targets is section 3.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No friction as a reason a ball slows or rolls on, no gravity, no measured speed, no force numbers, no energy words. Speed is judged by watching two pushes leave side by side, never from where a ball stops. Ramps are low (two books at most), motion stays on the floor, one change at a time. A miss is a result, not a mistake.
- **Connections.** ELA SL.K.3 (ask to get information), days 20 and 21: the spoken "I want to find out ___" and the question cards of unit 2, as review. Math K.MD.A.2 (compare distances and speeds directly), days 21 to 23: tape marks and dot strips laid side by side, no numbers. Cause and effect (crosscutting), days 22 and 24, as review of unit 1.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A problem is what people want to be different, not a thing to build; one goal has many good solutions. A harder push usually starts faster and also stops farther on this floor, yet "farther" is where the trip ended and "faster" is seen at the start, so only watching shows speed. A bumper changes the path where it touches, not before. A ramp that reached once may not reach again; we do not explain why, we roll more than once. Soft balls bounce and lanes vary, so expect surprises and keep them.

Answer: the child reading it. Wrong: the stool, a fix instead of the want (B); "just build it" said aloud with no want named (A).
- **E21 (day 21).** Invented: a basket keeps sliding off the end of a bench. "Touch the question card that helps us find out about the problem." Cards: "Where should the basket stay?"; "What color is the basket?". Answer: the first. Wrong: the color card, no register letter; "build a rail" said aloud instead of choosing (A).
- **E22a (day 22).** Invented: Pip pushed a toy wheel on two days. Day 1 it stopped short of the cone; day 2 it stopped at the cone. Nobody watched how it left. "Did the wheel start faster on day 2? Touch yes, no, or we cannot tell." Answer: we cannot tell. Wrong: yes, "it went farther" (C).
- **E22b (day 22).** Invented: Pip wanted a toy scooter to reach the fence and covered it in stickers so it looks very strong; the test picture shows the scooter stopped short of the fence. "Did the scooter do what Pip wanted? Touch yes or no." Answer: no. Wrong: yes, "it looks good" (E).
- **E23 (day 23).** Invented: Pip rolled a toy bus down one ramp four times: reached, reached, stopped short, reached. "Touch the picture that shows this ramp does not reach every time." Answer: the stopped-short picture. Wrong: a reached picture, "it reached, so it always does" (F).
- **E24a (day 24).** Invented: two pictures of a toy truck rolling toward a garage square, with a card bumper beside its path. Picture 1: the path bends in open floor with a gap before the bumper. Picture 2: the path bends where the truck touches the bumper. "Touch the picture where the bumper made the truck turn." Answer: picture 2. Wrong: picture 1, "the bumper was close" (D).
- **E24b (day 24).** A third picture: the truck's path turns at a bumper, with three marks (well before, at the touch, far after). "Touch the mark where the truck began to turn." Answer: the touch. Wrong: the mark before the touch, near the bumper (D).

## 6. Days

## Day 20

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Watch the ramp ball miss the tower and ask what needs to change before proposing a solution.
- note: Hook and question board; retrieve unit 1 contact pushes and unit 2 result charts inside the day. ELA SL.K.3 unit 1 ends school day 34, before today. Introduce design-cycle: problem, try an idea, test it. Math comparison in unit 3 may still be developing: tape marks and reached/not reached bridge.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **5 min. Hook: the ramp and the tower.** The teacher sets a low card ramp on two books at one end of the rug and a three-block tower at the other. One soft ball stops short; a harder start goes past. A tape X marks each stop. Children sit outside the lanes. The compelling question is asked before any fix: "How can we make the ball reach the tower?" Children say what they notice and wonder; the teacher scribes the question board. Pip: "I will build a big thing right now!" The teacher: "First we find out what we want."
- **4 min. Retrieval.** One child marks, on the three-box strip, where a touch changed the rug ball (unit 1). One child matches a rainy-day picture to the weather chart row (unit 2). Unscored; on a miss the teacher models one and another child tries.
- **8 min. Teaching first: the problem, then the idea.** The three steps, with a hand motion each: "What is the problem? Try an idea. Test it." Two cards: the ball at the X (now) and the ball at the tower (wanted). The class says: "The ball stops short. We want it to reach the tower." The teacher draws the first path model beside the chart.
- **8 min. Each child draws.** Each child draws the stop X and the wanted place, and tells a partner "I wonder ___". The teacher scribes the wonder and hears a rotating six.
- **3 min. Exit.** E20.
- **2 min. Pack.** Crayons and cards into trays. No child handled the ball, so no wipes.

Held by: teacher (ramp, balls, models); children (own drawing). Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: E20. Hand-backs out: errors A and B to day 21.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: problem, idea, test, ramp, tower, reach, stop short, past, bumper, turn. Frames: "The ball stops ___ the tower." "We want it to ___." Home-language questions are accepted on days 20 and 21.
- **Access.** Raised or tactile lane edges; a partner may roll for a child who cannot, but the child chooses the change and the mark. Pointing, symbols and dictation replace drawing.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every stem aloud, enlarge cards, accept pointing and home-language reasons.
- **Reading load.** None. All words are spoken; cards are pictures.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say two different ideas that could reach the same tower and how a test would tell them apart.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Tilt a tray on a book, roll a soft ball at a cup across the table or floor. Ask "What do we want?", then change one thing and roll again. No purchase: any soft ball too big to mouth and a tray or card will do. On a hall floor, keep faces back.
- **One projector.** Show the stop photographs and the chart large. The rolling is real, so the teacher runs one ramp and children draw; paper does the rest.
