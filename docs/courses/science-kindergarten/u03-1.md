# Section 3.1: Defining the problem, and testing an idea

## Header




## 1. Standards and practices


- **K-2-ETS1-1, this section's share (days 20, 21).** "Ask questions, observe and gather information about something people want to change, to name a simple problem a new or better object or tool could solve." Here: the class watches the ball, asks the cards, and says the problem in one sentence before anything is built. Day 25 and the check (day 27) finish the code in section 3.2.
- **K-PS2-2, this section's share (days 22 to 24).** "Use test results to decide whether a design, such as a ramp or a turning wall, changes an object's speed or direction the way it was meant to." Here: build, test, and judge from reached pictures and drawn paths; speed by watching, direction at the touch. The judging across new targets is section 3.2.
- **Limits that bite here.** No friction as a reason a ball slows or rolls on, no gravity, no measured speed, no force numbers, no energy words. Speed is judged by watching two pushes leave side by side, never from where a ball stops. Ramps are low (two books at most), motion stays on the floor, one change at a time. A miss is a result, not a mistake.
- **Connections.** ELA SL.K.3 (ask to get information), days 20 and 21: the spoken "I want to find out ___" and the question cards of unit 2, as review. Math K.MD.A.2 (compare distances and speeds directly), days 21 to 23: tape marks and dot strips laid side by side, no numbers. Cause and effect (crosscutting), days 22 and 24, as review of unit 1.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** A problem is what people want to be different, not a thing to build; one goal has many good solutions. A harder push usually starts faster and also stops farther on this floor, yet "farther" is where the trip ended and "faster" is seen at the start, so only watching shows speed. A bumper changes the path where it touches, not before. A ramp that reached once may not reach again; we do not explain why, we roll more than once. Soft balls bounce and lanes vary, so expect surprises and keep them.

## 2. Where it sits


Before: unit 2 (day 19 respond). No readiness check; the day 20 retrieval (unit 1 contact chart, unit 2 weather chart) is the before-teaching evidence, unscored. Results go to the record sheet; each exit routes to the next day's Return and repair, and day 24's goes to day 25.

## 3. Checks, written first


**Unit assessment items owned:** none (P1, P2 and M1 to M3 are owned by section 3.2).

**Evidence form.** As units 1 and 2: asked aloud, one ask a stem, picture cards, "invented for practice" said first for any invented case, a rotating six heard and a "heard" column on the record sheet. One marking scale: shown, shown with help, not yet seen. Where a wrong answer has no register letter, record "shown with help".

**Exit questions.** If many miss one, the next day's Return and repair opens with that error's wrong example.
- **E20 (day 20).** Invented: Pip's small ball stopped short of a tower of cups. "Say what needs to change, before anyone builds anything." Card: a small ball stopped short of a tower of cups. Answer: the ball needs to reach the tower. Wrong: a thing to build, such as a bigger ramp (B); "just build it" with no want named (A).
- **E21 (day 21).** Invented: Pip's toy car stopped short of its garage square. "Say the problem in one sentence: The car stops ___. We want it to ___." Card: the car, the ramp and the garage square with a gap. Answer: it stops short; we want it to reach the garage. Wrong: "build a longer ramp" said instead (A); a fact the problem cannot use, no register letter.
- **E22a (day 22).** Invented: Pip pushed a toy wheel on two days. Day 1 it stopped short of the cone; day 2 it stopped at the cone. "Did the wheel start faster on day 2? Touch yes, no, or we cannot tell." Answer: we cannot tell. Wrong: yes, "it went farther" (C).
- **E22b (day 22).** Invented: Pip wants the ball to reach a toy cup. "Use your ramp cards to build a low ramp for the cup." Answer: the ramp card on one or two book cards, its low end toward the cup. A tall stack or a ramp pointing away: no register letter, record "shown with help".
- **E22c (day 22).** Invented: Pip built a low ramp for the cup and covered it in stickers so it looks very strong; the test picture shows the ball stopped short of the cup. "Point to where the ball should be to say the ramp worked." Answer: the cup. Wrong: the stickers or the ramp, "it looks good" (E).
- **E23 (day 23).** Invented: Pip rolled a toy bus down one ramp four times: reached, reached, stopped short, reached. Pip will now roll a new toy truck once down the same ramp. "Say your prediction: I think ___ because I saw ___." Answer: a prediction that does not say always, with a picture as the reason. Wrong: "it reached, so it always does" (F).
- **E24a (day 24).** Invented: Pip's toy truck rolled straight past a card bumper beside its path, with a clear gap, and missed the garage square. "Say what to change about the bumper: I move the bumper ___ because the picture shows ___." Answer: onto the path so the truck touches it, because the truck went by without touching. Wrong: "closer, with a gap" (D).
- **E24b (day 24).** A third picture: the truck's path turns at a bumper, with three marks (well before, at the touch, far after). "Touch the mark where the truck began to turn." Answer: the touch. Wrong: the mark before the touch, near the bumper (D).

## 4. Expected errors and first ideas

## 5. What is carried, and instruments


- **Carried.** Unit 1's three-box strip and class picture chart, and unit 2's picture rows, stay on the wall and are reused. New and kept to the end of the unit: the design-cycle chart, three steps said with a hand motion, "what is the problem, try an idea, test it" (day 20). Day 20 adds the first path model (ramp, ball path, X, tower). Day 21 adds the one-sentence problem. Day 22 revises the cause-effect arrows ("we did this, the ball did that") beside reached pictures. Day 23 adds second-ball rows. Day 24 adds bumper contact and ties each change on the chart to a result picture. None is withdrawn here; all pass to section 3.2.
- **Key moment 1, day 22: watching speed.** Ideal instrument: two lanes watched side by side, or slow motion. Spec: tools are None; two taped lanes and the children's eyes fit for "faster, slower" in relative words, by design with no number.
- **Key moment 2, day 24: where the turn happens.** Ideal: a path drawn before and after the touch. Fits, as a crayon path on each child's paper. Gaps and asks: none; no tool is named.
- **Equipment and minutes.** Six sets, one for each group of five (ramp, two books, tape, three blocks, two soft balls, a larger ball on day 23, a bumper from day 22). A group rolls one child at a time in its own lane, so nothing is shared between groups. The teacher cannot watch six lanes, so three groups test while three draw, then swap, and checks each build (about 1 minute a group, staggered) before it rolls. A half of 5 minutes holds five rolls and five marks.
- **Clean-up.** Books and balls into the set bags, wipes from a tub on each table (one each), eye protection back into its bag, as in unit 1. Counted inside each day (days 21 to 24: 2 to 4 minutes).
- **A room with nothing.** One soft ball and one folded-card ramp for the teacher alone, with labeled photographs of the stops. Children read and mark the photographs. This is weaker: it cannot show children testing.

## 6. Days

## Day 20


- **5 min. Hook: the ramp and the tower.** The teacher sets a low card ramp on two books at one end of the rug and a three-block tower at the other. One soft ball stops short; a harder start goes past. A tape X marks each stop. Children sit outside the lanes. The compelling question is asked before any fix: "How can we make the ball reach the tower?" Children say what they notice and wonder; the teacher scribes the question board. Pip: "I will build a big thing right now!" The teacher: "First we find out what we want."
- **4 min. Retrieval.** One child marks, on the three-box strip, where a touch changed the rug ball (unit 1). One child matches a rainy-day picture to the weather chart row (unit 2). Unscored; on a miss the teacher models one and another child tries.
- **8 min. Teaching first: the problem, then the idea.** The three steps, with a hand motion each: "What is the problem? Try an idea. Test it." Two cards: the ball at the X (now) and the ball at the tower (wanted). The class says: "The ball stops short. We want it to reach the tower." The teacher draws the first path model beside the chart.
- **8 min. Each child draws.** Each child draws the stop X and the wanted place, and tells a partner "I wonder ___". The teacher scribes the wonder and hears a rotating six.
- **3 min. Exit.** E20.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice draws the stop and the wanted place for the class ball and tells a partner a wonder; the exit says the want aloud for a different ball and tower, alone and cold.
- **2 min. Pack.** Crayons and cards into trays. No child handled the ball, so no wipes.

Held by: teacher (ramp, balls, models); children (own drawing). Tool: None. By hand: drawing. Evidence: E20. Hand-backs out: errors A and B to day 21.

## Day 21


- **6 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 20 exit, errors A and B. The larger group works with the teacher on its move (want picture before idea picture); the rest use the repair cards in pairs.
- **4 min. Rug: ask the cards.** Pip: "I want to find out ___." Cards from unit 2 (review): when, where, what do we do; new on the card set: a goal card (the ball at the tower) and a materials card (cardboard, books, blocks, tape). A question no card answers goes on the "not on this page" card (review).
- **8 min. Observe the trials.** Three groups roll while three draw, then swap (4 minutes each). Each child rolls once from the same line, watches the ball stop, then marks a dot on the stopping strip and draws it. Dots laid beside the tower line are read as nearer, farther or about the same (review). The class sees the dots land in different places.
- **5 min. Say the problem.** In pairs, "The ball stops ___ the tower. We want it to ___." The teacher scribes one class sentence, and records guesses ("if the ramp is higher, the ball ___") untested.
- **3 min. Exit.** E21.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice lays dot strips and says the class sentence for the ball and the tower in pairs; the exit says the problem sentence alone for a car, a ramp and a garage the class never used.
- **4 min. Clean-up.** Balls and books into bags, wipe hands, eye protection back.

Held by: teacher (chart, sentence); children (strip, dot). Tool: None. By hand: dot strip, drawing. Evidence: E21. Hand-backs out: error A to day 22.

## Day 22


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 21 exit, error A (one repair only). The class orders the want picture first.
- **4 min. Rug: choose one change.** Three idea cards: one more book under the ramp, a harder start push, a bumper. Each group picks one; Pip changes three things at once and the class says why that cannot tell us anything (fair test, review). Guess cards: reach or not.
- **11 min. Build and test.** Three groups test while three draw, then swap (5 minutes each, 1 to swap). The teacher checked each build before class and glances at each (10 seconds) before it rolls. Each child rolls once, about 40 seconds; a group fills a row of five reached or not-reached squares.
- **4 min. Rug: two pushes side by side.** The teacher and two children roll a gentle and a harder push down two taped lanes from one line. The class watches which leaves ahead, then lays the stopping dots beside it. Said aloud: the harder one went farther and we watched it leave faster; where a ball stops is where the trip ended.
- **3 min. Exit.** E22a, E22b and E22c: E22a touched by all (20 seconds); E22b built at each child's mat (80 seconds) while the teacher watches the rotating six; E22c pointed by all (40 seconds).
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice builds and tests a real ramp for the class target in groups and moves a square to reached or not reached; the exit builds with cards for a new cup target and points to the place the ball should be for a covered ramp picture the class never tested, each child alone.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** Wipes, bags, eye protection; each group leaves its build taped in place.

Held by: teacher (lanes, cause-effect arrows); children (row of squares). Tool: None. By hand: reached row. Evidence: E22a, E22b, E22c. Hand-backs out: errors C and E to day 23.

## Day 23


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 22 exit, errors C and E. The larger group works with the teacher (two lanes, then match the result to the want); the others use repair cards.
- **3 min. Warm-up.** Each child puts a guess card down: will the bigger ball reach? Practice only.
- **12 min. Test the bigger ball.** Each group keeps its day 22 build and release spot; one larger ball per group. Three groups test while three draw, then swap (5 minutes each, 2 to swap). Each child rolls once and marks a square: five squares a group.
- **4 min. Rug: read the rows.** Touch each square once, count reached and missed on the class chart (review of the picture graph). Fallback: if all thirty reached, or none, the teacher says so aloud and shows invented rows, said to be invented, for "sometimes".
- **3 min. Exit.** E23.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice guesses, tests the bigger ball and reads the reached row; the exit predicts a new truck from four finished pictures and says the reason aloud, with no test to read.
- **3 min. Clean-up.** As day 22.

Held by: teacher (class chart); children (own squares). Tool: None. By hand: square row. Evidence: E23. Hand-back out: error F to day 24. No new content is taught.

## Day 24


- **5 min. Return and repair.** Hand-back: day 23 exit, error F. Reached and missed pictures side by side; say "sometimes".
- **4 min. Rug: the bumper.** The teacher rolls past a bumper with a gap (straight), then into it (turn). Three-box strip: before, touch, after, with the path drawn (review). Pip says "it was near, so it turned"; the class finds the touch.
- **14 min. Test and improve.** Three groups test while three draw, then swap (6 minutes each, 2 to swap). Eye protection on. Each child places a bumper, rolls once and draws the path before and after the touch. Each group also rolls one ball into another ball, and one ball into a two-block tower, then changes one thing and retests once.
- **2 min. Revise the chart.** The bumper touch joins the cause-effect arrows; each change on the design chart is tied to a result picture.
- **3 min. Exit.** E24a and E24b.
- **Shared shape (note, not timed).** The practice places the bumper, rolls once and draws the path; the exit says the change to a bumper for a truck picture the class never rolled, with a reason, alone, and then touches a mark.
- **2 min. Clean-up.** Wipes from table tubs, blocks and balls into bags, eye protection back.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (path drawing). Tool: None. By hand: path drawing. Evidence: E24a, E24b. Hand-back out: error D to day 25. Closes section 3.1.

## 7. Talk and writing

- Planned questions: day 20, "What do we want to be different?"; day 21, "What do you want to find out?"; day 22, "Did it do what we wanted?"; day 24, "Where did it touch?"
- Written task, day 21: each child draws the ball, where it stopped and where it should stop; the teacher writes the dictated line.
- Judged by the teacher with three look-fors: the drawing shows what people want to be different, not a thing to build; it shows where the ball stopped and where it should; the question asked can be answered by a card or by watching.

## 8. Practice that returns

Day 20 retrieves unit 1's contact chart and unit 2's weather chart. Day 21 uses the question cards and dot strips (units 1 and 2). Day 22 uses keep, change, watch and the guess. Day 23 repeats day 22's test and counting touch-once. Day 24 uses the three-box strip and the bump (unit 1, section 1.2). Each day's evidence is spoken or pointed; the record sheet notes "heard".

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: problem, idea, test, ramp, tower, reach, stop short, past, bumper, turn. Frames: "The ball stops ___ the tower." "We want it to ___." Home-language questions are accepted on days 20 and 21.
- **Access.** Raised or tactile lane edges; a partner may roll for a child who cannot, but the child chooses the change and the mark. Pointing, symbols and dictation replace drawing.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every stem aloud, enlarge cards, accept pointing and home-language reasons.
- **Reading load.** None. All words are spoken; cards are pictures.
- **Extension, same standard.** Say two different ideas that could reach the same tower and how a test would tell them apart.

## 10. Two rooms

- **Teaching one child.** Tilt a tray on a book, roll a soft ball at a cup across the table or floor. Ask "What do we want?", then change one thing and roll again. No purchase: any soft ball too big to mouth and a tray or card will do. On a hall floor, keep faces back.
- **One projector.** Show the stop photographs and the chart large. The rolling is real, so the teacher runs one ramp and children draw; paper does the rest.

## 11. Not in this section, and still to make

Not here: friction, gravity, measured speed, any number for a push, a second classroom problem (day 25), and the delivery lane (days 27 and 28). Still to make: the card ramps and bumpers (teacher-cut), the want and idea pictures, the goal and materials cards, the repair cards, the dot strips and square rows, the chart. No tool is named, so none is spec only. From memory: the anticipated errors; that a harder push also goes farther on this floor (rehearse the lane the day before).
