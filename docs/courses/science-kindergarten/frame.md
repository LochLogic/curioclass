# Course frame: Science, Kindergarten

Course id science-k5, kindergarten. Draft frame, 2026-11-13; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6, each opening on an anchor phenomenon or design problem the class meets first hand: pushes and
  pulls first (the year's first investigation teaches the observing habits every later unit uses), then weather,
  ramps, living things, sunlight and caring for the schoolyard.
- **Days.** 64: 58 in units, 4 for course-level checks (2 after unit 3, 2 after unit 6) and 2 respond days (one
  after each check). Science shares the kindergarten day with reading, math and social studies, so the course
  assumes two lessons a week of about 30 minutes (about 60 minutes a week) across about 32 weeks (a school year of
  about 36 weeks, of which about 32 carry science lessons here), not the full-year yardstick of 160. The class
  weather chart starts in week 1 as a teacher-led two-minute daily routine with picture squares, outside the lesson
  count, so unit 2 opens on about a month of data.
  The 11 rows are short, hands-on and talk-based; unit days follow their load: the two design units and the
  living-things unit (three rows) carry more, the communicating row (unit 6) less.
- **Standard.** The 10 kindergarten performance expectations of the Next Generation Science Standards (K-PS2-1,
  K-PS2-2, K-PS3-1, K-PS3-2, K-LS1-1, K-ESS2-1, K-ESS2-2, K-ESS3-1, K-ESS3-2, K-ESS3-3) and the band's first
  engineering expectation, K-2-ETS1-1, done in full here. Each is taught with every part its clarification names
  (the unit that teaches it says so) and inside its boundary. The C3 column of the Units table holds, for science,
  the practices and crosscutting concepts each unit develops. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the
  evidence statements of its expectations and the K-2 practice and crosscutting concept elements (Appendices F and
  G), and reads OpenSciEd elementary (reference only, never copied) for order and pacing; the "Check set adds"
  column names what the writer expects that to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds; BASELINE rule 5:
  facts as the evidence shows.
- **Base text.** None. Science K to 5 is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md, "Our own"). Phenomena are
  chosen to be things a kindergarten class can see, touch or hear at school (a rolling ball, the morning and
  afternoon weather, a hot slide, a weed in a sidewalk crack), so the class meets them first hand; where it
  cannot, the stand-in is an open or public-domain photograph or video (National Weather Service and NOAA,
  National Park Service, US Fish and Wildlife Service, NASA), adapted with a credit line. Investigations are the
  ones the rows describe, run with classroom-cheap materials. Read-alouds are Curio Class's own. No trade picture
  book, paid video or library visit.
- **Materials: substitutes, then no-kit.** Every investigation has a household stand-in where safe and a no-kit
  data path otherwise (a short class video or photo set with the data on a picture chart, said plainly to be
  weaker than doing it). Expected kit for the year, all classroom-cheap: balls of several sizes, cardboard, tape,
  string, blocks and books for ramps, a few child-safe hand lenses (stand-in: a clear jar of water as a magnifier),
  sand, soil, rocks, ice cubes, two class plants, a class weather chart, gloves and grabbers. Our own online
  tools may later serve as simulations; each unit plan lists any it wants (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13), with a paper
  path beside it and no student account.
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Kindergarten students are pre-readers or early readers. Science words are
  taught as spoken and visual language (said aloud, shown with objects, labelled on pictures); no student is
  asked to decode them alone. Records are drawings, dots, tallies and picture charts; the teacher writes.
- **The storyline in every unit.** The unit opens on its anchor; the class says what it notices and wonders and
  builds a question board; each lesson answers a question the last one raised; the unit closes by explaining the
  anchor with an "I think ..., because I saw ..." (from unit 4) or a working design (units 3 and 5). The class
  model or chart drawn early is revised when new evidence comes in. Each unit also ends with a short talk-and-point
  task for each child on a phenomenon or problem the class has not seen, using a practice with the unit's idea and
  crosscutting concept, inside the unit's days (the anchor explanation is rehearsed; the new case shows transfer).
- **Practices across the year.** Asking questions and defining problems (units 2, 3); developing and using models
  (unit 4); planning and carrying out investigations (units 1, 5); analysing data (units 2, 3); using counting
  (units 2, 4, 6, whole numbers only); constructing explanations and designing solutions (units 3, 5); argument
  from evidence (unit 4); obtaining and communicating information (units 2, 6). Crosscutting concepts: cause and
  effect (introduced unit 1), patterns (unit 2), systems (unit 4), structure and function (unit 5's shade, named
  only). Nature of science: unit 1 (scientists find out by looking and testing), unit 2 (science looks for
  patterns and order in the world), unit 4 (science knowledge is based on evidence).
- **Safety.** A one-page course safety sheet for the year (no tasting, no sniffing unknowns, hands washed after
  soil, sun and hot-surface rules, gloves for any pick-up), and every hands-on day names its hazards and controls.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. Unit 2 (severe weather) needs a reader
  who knows young children after disasters (a school counsellor or similar); every hands-on unit needs a science
  teacher's safety read before the course is called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3). Who, and any pay, is Cory's.

## Course-level checks

- **After unit 3 (2 days, mid-year).** A one-to-one or small-group talk-and-point check, spread over two lessons
  so every child is heard, on cases not seen before: a photograph pair of a toy car pushed gently and hard (which
  went farther, and why); a picture weather chart from another class (which weather happened most, warmer in the
  morning or afternoon); a new problem (a ball that will not go into a bin) said in one sentence, and whether a
  shown design worked, from a yes-or-no result. The teacher records what each child can do; not a written test.
- **1 respond day after the mid-year check.** Re-teach what the check shows (most often saying the cause and
  reading the chart), in small groups, ending with a fresh short task to see that the re-teaching took.
- **After unit 6 (2 days, end of year).** Talk-and-draw check on new cases: what a living thing in an unseen
  photograph needs and why it lives where it does; an "I think ..., because I saw ..." about an animal changing
  its surroundings (a woodpecker's hole); which of two photographed spots would be warmer and why; a solution for
  a new impact (paper towels thrown away in the lunchroom); a look back at the class weather chart for one
  pattern across the year.
- **1 respond day after the end-of-year check.** Re-teach the gaps, in small groups, before the record goes on to
  the grade 1 teacher with what the respond day changed.

### ELA kindergarten (leaning on this course)

ELA kindergarten leans on K-ESS2-1 from about week 7 (its weather unit 2) and on K-LS1-1 from about week 16 (its

later. The two weather units and the two living-things units fall close together, so the read-alouds and the
investigations can support each other.

social studies.

## For the lead

1. **Time a week.** The frame assumes two science lessons a week of about 30 minutes (64 days). Pick: keep it. If
   schools give more time, unit 4 (living things) and unit 5 (sunlight) take the extra days first.
2. **"Introduces patterns" in two rows.** Settled in the fix pass of 2026-11-14: sk-weather-watch introduces
   patterns (unit 2); sk-living-needs now reads "Extends the patterns model from the weather chart
   (sk-weather-watch) to patterns across many living things" and takes patterns as model_in.
3. **Seasonal order.** Unit 4 opens on fall photographs and unit 5 works on any sunny day, so the order holds in
   most climates; unit 6 needs an outdoor pick-up in late spring. Schools in warm climates may swap units 4 and 5.
   Pick: keep the order and let the scope and sequence note the swap.
4. **Materials cost.** Pick: a stated ceiling of about $40 a class for the year beyond what a kindergarten room
   already has, each unit plan giving its own cost. A money question, so Cory's to confirm.
