## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **K-2-ETS1-1** (days 25, 27): Ask questions, observe and gather information about something people want to change, to name a simple problem a new or better object or tool could solve.
- **K-PS2-2** (days 26, 28): Use test results to decide whether a design, such as a ramp or a turning wall, changes an object's speed or direction the way it was meant to.
- skill sk-define-problem (grade K)
- skill sk-ramp-design (grade K)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> - **Closing explanation, day 26 opening.** Dictate how the revised design reaches the tower, using stopping marks, speed observations and bumper-contact paths. Distinguish reaching farther from moving faster.
>
> - **Question board.** Group notices and wonders into linked questions: What needs changing (days 20 and 21)? Which change helps it reach (22)? Will another ball work (23)? How can it turn and knock blocks down (24)? Does understanding another room problem help (25)? Will our design reach a new target (26)? Transfer: a ball-delivery lane with a sideways collection bay, unseen until day 27.
> - **Carried.** Keep unit 1's contact chart and unit 2's picture graph. Day 20 adds an initial path model and problem, try, test chart. Day 22 revises cause-effect arrows and reached pictures from trials; day 23 adds second-ball evidence. Day 24 adds bumper contact and collision evidence, then an improvement and retest. Day 25 revises the problem step from information questions; day 26 reaches consensus from new-target tests. Use these models in both assessment parts. Judge speed by watching two pushes side by side.
