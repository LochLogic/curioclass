# Unit 2: Weather watch

Course `science-kindergarten` (Science, Kindergarten). **Days 10 to 19** (10 course days; school days 23 to 44 (the course meets up to 3 times a week, so not every school day in the span), weeks 5 to 9). Sections: 2.1 (days 10 to 14); 2.2 (days 15 to 19). Days by kind: understanding 4, fluency 2, application 2, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- **2.1: What patterns do our weather records show?** Days 10 to 14.
- **2.2: How can forecasts help us prepare?** Days 15 to 19.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why do we need our coats at morning recess but not after lunch?"
- **Hook, day 10.** Show the morning coat pile and afternoon recess photograph beside the dated weather chart. Ask the compelling question without prescribing a temperature pattern. Discuss school observations, never family possessions.
- **Closing explanation, day 17.** Dictate the coat-pile explanation from the daily chart's morning and afternoon warmth marks, with monthly counts second; with no steady difference, say "it varies, so we check each day". Add why forecast information changed recess plans, using the forecast and safe-action pictures.
- **Teaching moves.** Model one dated entry on day 10 and a question on day 15. Then let children choose evidence, ask questions and revise claims; repeat unclear observations.



Three dimensions of each performance expectation (names and own-word lines from data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv; the Next Generation Science Standards are never named in student, teacher or family copy):


- **Question board.** What changed between recesses (days 10 to 12)? Does it repeat (day 13)? What does a whole month show (day 14)? If patterns vary, how can we plan ahead (days 15 and 16)? How do regional patterns and today's information help predict danger (day 17)? Transfer: unfamiliar dated weather records, then a new school outing forecast.
- **Limits.** No degrees, averages, probability or atmospheric mechanisms. Surface warmth is not air temperature; day 12 cannot establish an air-weather pattern. Categories overlap. A forecast describes possibilities; a warning does not cause a storm. No engineering expectation is owned.
- **A class outside northeastern Colorado.** Days 10 to 14 read the class's own chart; Denver records serve only the exits and a late start. On days 15 to 19 the teacher swaps in the state weather service's page for the region's most common severe weather (Cory, 2026-11-23); Denver stays the worked example.
- **Carried.** Retain unit 1's cause-effect chart. Day 10 begins weather-pattern pictures and a picture graph. Day 12 revises the warmth claim from repeat looks; day 14 adds counted monthly evidence. Day 15 extends cause-effect to forecast information causing preparation. Day 17 revises this chain from regional records and reaches consensus: observations support patterns and predictions, which inform actions. Retain all three representations for assessment. Days 13 and 14 practice matching two rows before day 18; day 19's two-month reading is review.
