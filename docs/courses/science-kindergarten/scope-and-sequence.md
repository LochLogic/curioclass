# Scope and sequence: Science, Kindergarten

Course `science-kindergarten`, grade K, draft revised 2026-11-15.

The day record is [days.csv](../../../data/plans/science-kindergarten/days.csv). It preserves the frame's six units, 58 unit days and six course-level days: 64 course days altogether. Its `stretch` values name units; its `unit` values name sections. This sequence reserves purposes and evidence, not assessment items or lesson plans. There is no base text and no source-map file is needed. The expectation dimensions are traced from `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv`; the Massachusetts framework is a crosswalk only.

## Calendar and teaching commitments

Science meets up to three times a week for about 30 minutes per meeting, as settled by the lead. Keep the shared calendar's 29-week spread: school day = ceiling(course day x 145 / 64). Each filled `school_day` follows that rule, with no week exceeding three meetings. The 64 numbered meetings keep the check windows in weeks 14 and 29; weeks 30 to 32 have no numbered science meetings. The frame is corrected to this frequency; `frames.csv` needs the structure-pass edit listed in the fix report. No unit, owned row, day count or school-day placement changes.

Begin the teacher-led, two-minute morning/afternoon weather picture routine on school day 1, before the first numbered science meeting on school day 3. Keep the complete dated record through school day 160, including weeks without numbered science meetings. The teacher counts and labels totals beyond ten. Children match picture rows, count selected groups up to ten with support and use relative warmer/cooler language. Weather categories may overlap; do not add their counts to infer the number of days.

Every unit opens with its anchor and question board. It gathers notices, makes an initial picture explanation or model, and revises that representation from evidence before returning to the anchor. Daily evidence is a child's drawing, recorded talk or observed action. Words are spoken, shown and teacher-labelled; independent reading, spelling and lengthy writing are never science assessment conditions. Section exits are reserved within their last day's existing time, with their prompts left to section planning.

Kindergarten assumes no earlier science course. The prerequisite audit of all 11 owned progression rows finds every `assumes` cell empty: there is no earlier-grade row to list, no last-use grade and no readiness check. Same-year retrievals are: unit 2, sk-push-pull (last taught unit 1); unit 3, sk-push-pull and sk-weather-watch (units 1 and 2); unit 4, sk-weather-watch (unit 2); unit 5, sk-push-pull, sk-define-problem and sk-change-environment (units 1, 3 and 4); unit 6, sk-weather-watch, sk-define-problem and sk-needs-places (units 2, 3 and 4). Same-year math leans retrieve where available and retain the named bridge while math is teaching them. No unit exceeds 25 days, so none needs a mid-unit assessment.

End-of-unit observations serve 30 children in six groups of five, each with an own picture response mat, a per-child checklist and one brief spoken "why" per code. Children point, place cards or draw concurrently; the teacher records each child's own response, including planning, carrying out or building where required. Pointing, supported talk, drawings and observed products remain valid; independent reading and spelling are never conditions. Each unit's final ten minutes are its single response allocation, acting on the whole window's record.

Collect each child's evidence once per assessment window. A later check within ten days carries the existing record without repeating the performance or changing its score; keep source day and support level visible. Use course days as the conservative ten-day window in this less-than-daily course. Course-level checks list only newly sampled codes and use fresh cases and task forms absent from the last two weeks. Unit records retain full owned-expectation coverage; a course-level sample does not claim a second full assessment of the year.

## Unit 1. Pushes and pulls

Course days 1 to 9, school days 3 to 21, weeks 1 to 5. Sections: 1.1, days 1 to 4; 1.2, days 5 to 9.

The rug ball stops short, goes farther or turns, giving children a question worth testing. Observing and recording come first, followed by separate comparisons of push strength, pull strength and push direction. Stopping a rolling ball and balls colliding extend the contact explanation. Children choose one thing to vary, revise the class before-and-after chart, and explain the anchor using observations. This establishes the investigation habits and cause-effect model that the rest of the year uses; looking and testing are ways scientists find out.

There is no readiness or mid-unit assessment. Days 8 and 9 reserve an unseen-case observation of K-PS2-1: each child helps plan and carry out separate strength and direction comparisons, including pushes and pulls, then describes starting, stopping, speed, turning and collision evidence. The revised chart supports the explanation without supplying an answer. Response occupies day 9's final ten minutes once. Comparisons remain contact-only, with one variable changed at a time.

**Candidate kept:** before-and-after descriptions are embedded on days 2 to 7 and used in the final observation, zero additional days. Math's direct comparison is owned in unit 3, school days 45 to 63, weeks 9 to 13, after this unit. Days 1 to 7 therefore use the frame's bridge: tape stopping places side by side and relative words, with no numerical distances. Rolling and pulling stay on the floor in cleared space, with large balls, short teacher-attached string, no throwing, tasting or sniffing unknowns.

## Unit 2. Weather watch

Course days 10 to 19, school days 23 to 44, weeks 5 to 9. Sections: 2.1, days 10 to 14; 2.2, days 15 to 19.

The morning coat pile and afternoon change bring the existing weather record into focus. Retrieve unit 1's recording and cause-effect habits. Children compare repeated morning/afternoon observations, organize picture records, count supported groups and revise a pattern chart before asking why a forecast changes the school's plans. Questions, calm forecast media and a Curio Class read-aloud connect local severe weather to preparation and response. Day 16 asks a new question of a second forecast source. Day 17 uses a sourced regional picture-record comparison: some severe-weather kinds occur more often in some places, and forecasters use observed patterns with current information to predict danger. Children obtain the answer to their question from the supplied media, then link the prediction to preparation and response. No probability calculation or atmospheric mechanism is added. Patterns come from the record; a cooler morning is a possibility to test, never a rule imposed on contrary data.

Day 14 counts the first complete live month, roughly school days 1 to 20, and revises the pattern chart. Day 19 compares the two live months available by school day 44, using actual start and end dates rather than imposing September/October. Only a late-starting class uses a dated picture record from another observer, labelled as such. A temperature/rain table cannot be translated into missing sunny, cloudy or windy categories. No weather is invented.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Day 18 samples K-ESS2-1 for every child and day 19 samples K-ESS3-2 for every child, one full expectation per day: observe or interpret and share weather records, describe daily and monthly patterns, compare relative temperatures, count a selected whole-number group with support, ask for forecast information, obtain it from media, and explain how regional hazard likelihood and observed patterns support a prediction, and how the warning supports community preparation and response. Response is the final ten minutes of day 19, counted once.

**Candidate kept:** day 11 costs one existing unit day for a small morning/afternoon relative-temperature investigation; repeat looks use the daily routine. Math sorting/counting is in unit 2, school days 19 to 44, weeks 4 to 9, alongside rather than reliably before science. Days 10 to 19 retain matching picture squares, supported counts to ten and teacher-labelled larger totals. ELA information questions are in unit 1 through school day 34, overlapping the first forecast day, so days 15 to 17 keep the modelled question and sentence starter. Save the weed and squirrel-hole fall photographs during day 11's outdoor looks for unit 4. Severe-weather notes require calm images, helpers and safe actions, no personal disaster accounts and no questions about home heating or clothing.

## Unit 3. Ramps and targets

Course days 20 to 28, school days 46 to 64, weeks 10 to 13. Sections: 3.1, days 20 to 24; 3.2, days 25 to 28.

The ball misses the block tower. Retrieve contact cause-effect and reading results, then ask questions, observe and gather information before saying what needs changing. Introduce the design cycle: name the problem, try an idea, test it. First obtain distance results, then use a bumper and collisions to change direction or knock down a target. Read the evidence before deciding whether a design worked, revise it, and return to the anchor. Problem definition precedes construction because the result means nothing without a desired outcome.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Days 27 and 28 reserve a new motion layout, problem definition for every child on day 27 (K-2-ETS1-1), results-based design judgment for every child on day 28 (K-PS2-2): each child asks questions, observes and gathers information, names the problem before trying a solution, then uses results to judge both intended speed and direction across separate trials. The product and observed performance carry K-2-ETS1-1 and K-PS2-2; the child explains the contact cause, without friction as the reason for slowing. Response is the final ten minutes of day 28 once.

**Candidate kept:** day 25 costs one existing day to define a second, class-chosen room problem, so the practice transfers beyond the teacher's ramp. No second construction project is added. ELA unit 1's question work ends on school day 34, before this unit. Math direct comparison is taught during unit 3, school days 45 to 63, alongside this unit, so its early trials keep tape marks and reached/not-reached results. Ramps remain on the floor, books stacked low, and balls large enough to avoid choking hazards.

## Unit 4. Living things and what they need

Course days 32 to 43, school days 73 to 98, weeks 15 to 20. Sections: 4.1, days 32 to 35; 4.2, days 36 to 39; 4.3, days 40 to 43.

Saved fall photographs of a weed and dug hole, alongside the class plants, remain visible evidence even when snow covers the yard. Retrieve weather patterns before extending them to living needs: water for all, light for plants, animals taking in food while plants do not, and different animals eating different foods. These patterns supply the reasons for a model connecting deer, grass, fish and people to resources in their places. Add the local town and its documented water source, air, soil and wood as needs-linked parts of a system. Then use changed surroundings as evidence of how organisms meet needs, including roots, squirrel holes, beaver dams, nests and people clearing land. The model and spoken evidence sentence are revised before the anchor is explained.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. New-scene observations sample one expectation per day for every child: K-LS1-1 on day 41, K-ESS3-1 on day 42 and K-ESS2-2 on day 43. Day 41 retains twelve minutes to revise the consensus model and explain the anchor before its eighteen-minute observation. Each child shows all four needs patterns, uses a relational model including humans and air/water/land resources, and backs a claim with evidence about plants, animals and people changing system parts to meet needs. Include a choice that does less harm to those surroundings. Drawing and talk carry the evidence, with teacher transcription. Response is the final ten minutes of day 43 once.

**Candidate dropped:** no class animal or two-week feeder commitment, zero days. Class plants give direct evidence and dated animal media supply food observations without adding live-animal care and allergen commitments. This leaves the frame's three owned rows fully carried. Start the two watered class plants several weeks before day 32, record their condition in normal light and a brief dark-shelf interval, and restore light promptly. These records support observations, not a formal grade 2 plant-needs experiment; no plant is left to die, and no photosynthesis or food chains are taught.

Math sorting/counting ends by school day 44, before the first needs sort. ELA SL.K.5 is in unit 2 through day 76, before science's place modelling on school day 82; W.K.1 is in unit 1 through day 34, before the evidence drawing on school day 87. Teacher labels and spoken claims remain the frame's fallback where an individual needs them. Show people meeting needs without blame, use no private home information, and make the outdoor walk optional when the ground is clear. Soil contact needs handwashing and no tasting; animal food or live animals are unnecessary.

## Unit 5. Sunlight warms

Course days 44 to 54, school days 100 to 123, weeks 20 to 25. Sections: 5.1, days 44 to 48; 5.2, days 49 to 54.

The sunlit and shaded slide or bench raises the warming question. Retrieve cause-effect, the design cycle and the evidence sentence. Plan matched observations of sand and soil, then rock and water, in sun/shade and morning/afternoon conditions. Record comparisons before drawing a conclusion and revise the warming explanation from all four materials. Compare umbrella, canopy and tent forms, build a cover, test it against an uncovered matched patch, and improve it from the result. Observation precedes design because children need a reason to expect blocking sunlight to help.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Day 52 retains twelve minutes for the consensus explanation and anchor, then six three-minute groups interpret unfamiliar sun/shade and morning/afternoon evidence for sand, soil, rock and water (K-PS3-1). Days 53/54 observe K-PS3-2 once across building and comparison for every child. Day 53 has four minutes set-up, six to choose a design/materials and twenty for concurrent child builds and teacher observations. Covers and matched uncovered patches are set out for the afternoon routine look or before day 54, allowing a shared exposure interval. Day 54 hears six groups of five for three minutes each: compare patches and give one short why pointing to the own build. Two minutes set-up and ten minutes response complete the lesson. Others independently draw the familiar slide-and-shade explanation. Carry day 52's warming record unchanged. No cover must produce an immediate felt difference. Dated matched-material media supply thermal evidence in unsuitable weather, explicitly marked; the child still builds. Degree readings are not required or assessed.

**Candidate dropped:** the extra ice-melting comparison costs zero days. Repeated matched-surface evidence already addresses warming; a second outcome would take time from explaining and improving the shade. Math direct comparison ended in week 13, well before this unit. Suitable sunshine can occur in any season, so preserve the frame's order. The teacher checks every touched surface first, students touch briefly with the back of a hand, and nobody looks at the sun. Use child-safe scissors, teacher-only difficult cuts, shallow water trays, handwashing after soil, and water and sun breaks on warm days.

## Unit 6. Caring for our place

Course days 55 to 61, school days 125 to 139, weeks 25 to 28. Sections: 6.1, days 55 to 58; 6.2, days 59 to 61.

The after-lunch litter tally gives the class a local problem. Retrieve picture data, the design cycle and the model of connected needs and surroundings. Trace human actions through local land, water, air and living things before choosing a school solution, beginning it on day 56. Children draw and tell why reusing paper, recycling suitable containers or safely collecting litter helps. Compare follow-up evidence, revise the class model, and communicate a solution rather than only naming a good habit. ELA W.K.2 was taught in unit 1, school days 1 to 34, so informative drawing and talk are available; dictated labels or a short talk remain alternatives.

No readiness or mid-unit assessment. Days 60 and 61 reserve an unfamiliar schoolyard impact: each child communicates a detailed solution through drawing/model and words, connects the action to an effect on one or more of land, water, air and living things (the class model holds all four), and explains how the solution reduces harm. This carries K-ESS3-3 with communication and cause-effect. A partner or class group is the audience; no external sending is needed. Response is day 61's final ten minutes once.

**Candidate kept:** the baseline sits inside day 55, the chosen solution begins on day 56 (school day 127), and day 59 (school day 134) costs one existing day for a follow-up at least a week later. Use the same area and after-lunch timing. Discuss rain, attendance or other clean-up as possible reasons for a different count; a smaller count alone does not prove the solution caused it. When outside work is unsafe, use dated same-area photographs instead. Children use gloves and grabbers, avoid glass and sharp items, adults bag waste, and everybody washes hands. Never ask about family recycling, purchases or waste; local collection services differ.
