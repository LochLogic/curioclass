# Section 4.2: How can a place meet needs?

## Header

<!-- skel -->
Science, Kindergarten. Unit 4, Living things and what they need. *What do the weed in the sidewalk crack and the squirrel by the fence need, and what do they do to get it?* Anchor: the class's own fall photographs, taken during unit 2's outdoor weather looks, of a weed growing out of a cracked sidewalk and a hole a squirrel dug, beside the class's two plants (one by the window, one on a dark shelf); the schoolyard walk itself only where the ground is clear (days 32 to 43): see [s04.md](s04.md). **Days 36 to 39.** Days by kind: understanding 2 (36, 38), fluency 1 (39), application 1 (37). A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2 of 3 in the unit.
- Day length (frames.csv): 30 minutes.

- **K-ESS3-1, this section's share (days 36, 37, 39).** "Use a model to show how what a plant or animal, people included, needs is linked to where it lives." Day 36: a picture model with link lines from living things to what a place supplies. Day 37: people are added from the town map. Day 39: a link is revised.
- **K-ESS2-2, this section's share (days 38, 39).** "Argue, using evidence, that plants and animals, people included, change their surroundings to get what they need." Day 38 teaches the claim "I think ..., because I saw ..."; day 39 uses it on a new photograph.
- **Limits that bite here.** No gases, breathing or air mechanisms: air is a wind-swirl mark. No food chains: a deer's link to grass is "food" and the grass is not followed further. A weed in a crack does not prove it made the crack; a root grows and does not decide to lift a slab. Animals and people change places without blame. The water source is named only as the checked report names it; no home address, and no child is asked where their own water comes from. Pictures of animals show no harm.
- **Connections.** ELA SL.K.5 (add drawings to a description), days 36 and 37: the teacher labels each child's spoken drawing. ELA W.K.1 (draw and tell an opinion with a reason), days 38 and 39: the claim is drawn and spoken and the teacher writes the stem.
- **What the teacher page must explain.** The system model: living things and the parts of a place are linked, and a missing link means the living thing cannot live there. "I saw" is evidence (the picture shows it); "I think" is the claim. A claim names its objects (review). A picture of a changed place shows a change; it does not show who or what did it unless the sequence does.

## Day 36

<!-- skel -->
- kind: Understanding. Meets new content: the goal is taught here, from a hook and a worked example.
- goal: Connect deer, grass and fish needs with forest, meadow and pond resources in a picture model.
- note: Model shows relationships rather than labels alone: food, water, air, light and land resources linked to living things. ELA SL.K.5 unit 2 ended school day 76 before today; teacher labels each child's spoken drawing.
- credit: ours (no base lesson is mapped to this day)
<!-- /skel -->

- **4 min. Warm-up and repair.** Hand-back: day 35 exit, error D. Match each animal to its food card; then touch the three rows on the pattern sheet.
- **4 min. Hook.** Three place pictures (forest, meadow, pond) and three cards (deer, grass, fish) go on the board. Pip puts the fish in the meadow. Children say what is wrong; the teacher does not fix it yet.
- **10 min. Build the links.** The teacher models the fish in the pond: a line to the water and a line to the pond plants for food. Six tables then draw lines for the deer (to grass for food, to water, to a wind-swirl for air) and the grass (to light, to water, to the land). Marks: drop, sun, bite, swirl, brown land. The teacher visits tables; a rotating six is heard.
- **5 min. What if.** Pip puts the fish back in the meadow: which line is missing? Children touch where the line stops. "No water line, no fish." Then Pip puts the grass in the dark forest: which line is missing? Light.
- **3 min. Chart.** Each table adds its place and lines to the class picture chart; one child at each table tells one line; the teacher labels it.
- **3 min. Exit.** E36a and E36b.
- **1 min. Pack.** Crayons into the tub.

Held by: teacher (chart); children (link lines). Tool: None. By hand: link lines. Evidence: E36a, E36b. Hand-backs out: E to day 37.

## 9. Supports and extension

- **English learners.** Words: place, link, line, change, hole, nest, dam, root. Frames: "The ___ needs ___." "I think ___, because I saw ___." Home-language answers are accepted on every item.
- **Access.** Raised or tactile link marks; a partner may draw a line for a child, but the child chooses where it goes. Pointing and communication symbols replace speaking or drawing.
- **Check accommodations.** Read every prompt aloud; enlarge cards; accept pointing, dictation and home-language reasons; record help as "shown with help".
- **Reading load.** None. All words are spoken; the teacher writes the claim stem.
- **Extension, same standard.** Add a second line to a link and say which picture shows it.
