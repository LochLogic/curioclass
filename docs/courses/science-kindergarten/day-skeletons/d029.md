## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** Observe the first three groups investigating contact motion and using unfamiliar weather and forecast information.
- **Note:** Mid-year week 14. Six groups of five, three groups at eight minutes per day, with six minutes daily for set-up/transitions; every child completes the three-code scope once across days 29/30. Each has an own response mat and per-child checklist and one brief spoken why per code. K-PS2-1: choose the comparison and what stays fixed, conduct separate strong/gentle push AND pull and direction trials, mark outcomes and explain starting/stopping, speed, turning and collisions. Use small wheeled objects and a paper ball/box on a taped lane, keeping one variable fixed per trial. K-ESS2-1: share daily/monthly patterns, compare morning/afternoon relative warmth, count a selected group up to ten with support, compare monthly picture-row frequencies; teacher labels larger totals. K-ESS3-2: child asks and obtains forecast information, links regional likelihood and observed patterns to prediction, preparation AND response. Fresh forms: choose-and-run investigation and weather-information stations, not unit 3's define/build/judge design task. Carry forward day 27 problem-definition and day 28 motion-results records for K-2-ETS1-1 and K-PS2-2 once, collected within ten course and school days; do not repeat or re-score them and do not list them as newly sampled codes. An unscored class transfer names unit 1's toy-car stop-before-rug-edge problem and judges a shown fix from yes/no results. Others independently sort familiar weather pictures or draw the unit 1 motion anchor. For 20-minute periods only, new sample is K-ESS2-1 alone, three groups of five at five minutes plus five set-up; carry unit 1 motion, unit 2 forecast and unit 3 records unchanged and remove the two other codes from the locally adapted sample list.

- **K-ESS2-1:** Observe the local weather, share the observations, and describe the patterns that show up over days and months.
- **K-ESS3-2:** Ask questions to find out why people forecast the weather and how forecasts help a community get ready for and handle dangerous storms.
