## The day as the scope and sequence sets it

<!-- skel -->
- **Goal:** Ask how a local weather pattern helps forecasters warn people and use the answer to choose safe community actions.
- **Note:** Use a sourced calm picture-record comparison: some severe-weather types occur more often in this region than elsewhere. Each child asks a question and obtains the answer from supplied records/media; forecasters combine observed patterns and current information to predict danger so communities prepare and respond. Thunderstorm worked case replaced by the region's own hazard; no probability or atmospheric mechanism, frightening images or personal disaster accounts. For 20-minute periods only, this is one of the unit's four observation-window days (15/17/18/19): ten minutes essential teaching, ten new-case evidence today; closing days add twenty and ten evidence minutes, then ten response minutes. Split group parts at day boundaries, one per-child record; full parts are in the closing rows.

**Skills:**

- sk-forecast-severe (grade K): taught or practised here

**Codes, in our words where the standards file has them:**

- **K-ESS3-2:** Ask questions to find out why people forecast the weather and how forecasts help a community get ready for and handle dangerous storms.
