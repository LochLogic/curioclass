- **MS-PS1-4:** Build a model that predicts and explains how a pure substance's particle motion, temperature and state change as thermal energy is added or taken away. Emphasis: qualitative particle models of the three states showing that gaining or losing thermal energy speeds up or slows down particles until the state changes.
- **MS-ESS2-4:** Build a model of water cycling through Earth's systems, powered by sunlight and gravity. Emphasis: how water changes state as it travels the many paths of the water cycle. Limit: No number work on the energy needed to evaporate or melt water (latent heat).
- **MS-ESS2-5:** Collect data as evidence of how moving and colliding air masses change the weather. Emphasis: air moving from high to low pressure changes local weather, colliding air masses cause sudden changes, and forecasts are given as probabilities. Limit: Leave out memorizing cloud type names and weather map symbols or station diagrams.
- **MS-PS3-4:** Plan an investigation into how energy transferred, kind of matter, mass and temperature change of a sample relate to one another. Limit: Leave out calculating how much thermal energy was transferred in total.
- **MS-ESS2-6:** Build and use a model of how uneven heating and Earth's spin set up air and ocean circulation patterns that shape regional climates. Emphasis: variation with latitude, altitude and land layout; sun-driven wind belts, the Coriolis effect and prevailing winds; heat carried by global ocean circulation. Limit: Leave out the physics behind the Coriolis effect.
