## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Why do we sometimes see different things when looking at the same object?"
Day 1 hook: peers describe opposite views through a teacher-prepared mirror-film box. Record both accounts without explaining the difference. Read sensor observations aloud and offer tactile box diagrams.

Day 15 closes the anchor: both sides reflect and transmit light, but different amounts reach each observer. Students cite days 6 and 9 readings and day 13 receptor information in their written explanation.

The teacher models recording disagreements, then asks which observation could distinguish predictions. Students choose controlled comparisons. Revisit unanswered questions before supplying readings.

Look-fors: arrows show travel direction, evidence constrains the mechanism, and predictions follow from the model. Full Item 1 sample: "Lamp light reflects from the target to the upper mirror, reflects down to the lower mirror, then reflects right through the clear port into the aligned eye. The higher eye misses that path. Visitor-lamp light reflects from the sleeve to the port, then reflects into the aligned eye. Brightening that lamp strengthens glare and obscures the target. Shifting the upper mirror right sends target light down to the wall instead of the lower mirror, so the target vanishes. The opaque mirrors turn the route by reflection; the clear port passes target light while also reflecting glare." Nonmeeting sample: "The view stays the same after the lamp or mirror moves because the materials did not change." This omits the paths and illustrates D for the illumination claim. Item 2 meets: "Light reaches the backing. A little reflects; most transfers energy to the backing rather than passing through, so no rear image has a light path to that viewer." Does not meet: "The backing absorbs light and sends it through to the rear viewer" (E).

Item 3 meets: "The ray is straight in air, turns toward the dotted line at the water boundary, then is straight in water. A perpendicular ray continues without turning." Does not meet: "Both rays curve continuously once inside the water" (F). Tag the continuous-curve claim as F, without assigning another letter to the same claim.

| F | A ray curves throughout uniform water. | Puts bending inside a uniform material. Repair: join straight segments at the boundary. | Refraction background in field 10. |
| G | Every ray crossing into water changes direction. | Overgeneralizes oblique incidence. Repair: compare oblique and perpendicular entry. | Refraction background in field 10. |
| H | Light travels along nerves to the brain. | Confuses stimulus with signal. Repair: end the light arrow at the receptor and continue with a distinct signal arrow. | MS-LS1-8 core idea on file. |
