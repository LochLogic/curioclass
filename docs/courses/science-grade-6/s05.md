## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where do natural hazards happen and how do we prepare for them?"
Day 116 opens with an original coastal map, historical water-level traces and a non-graphic account of the 2011 Japan tsunami. Why did an offshore event affect distant shores despite protection? Students list related hazards, build questions and draw explanations.
On 132, explain the anchor using location patterns, displacement trials and warning records; identify how detection, communication and protection interact.
Model histogram reading on 119 and evidence integration on 129. Step back for trials on 125 and independent arguments on 131.

- **MS-ESS3-2:** Study data about natural hazards to forecast future disasters and guide technologies that lessen their harm. Emphasis: some hazards (eruptions, severe storms) give warning signs that allow reliable prediction, while others (earthquakes) strike without notice and cannot yet be predicted.
- **~MS-ETS1-1:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) State a design problem's criteria and constraints clearly enough to lead to a good solution, considering science ideas and effects on people and nature.
- **~MS-ETS1-2:** (begun here; the full expectation is owned and assessed later) Judge competing design solutions step by step against the problem's criteria and constraints.
- **MS-PS4-3:** Pull together descriptive science and technical information to argue that digital signals carry and store information more reliably than analog ones. Emphasis: a basic sense that waves can carry messages. Limit: Leave out counting in binary and how any particular device works inside.
