# Science, Grade 6: tool asks

## Unit 1: light paths through materials

Mark: light tool. Days 10 to 11. Job: revise a model of reflection, transmission and absorption, then connect the panel's structure to its function. Verdict: spec only, partly fits until an accessible version and matching paper path exist. This is a specification, not a built tool.

Show a source, a material boundary and an observer. Students predict paths before displaying them, add arrows and explain a changed lighting condition. Let students vary material and illumination independently. Show simultaneous reflection and transmission for a partially reflective film, and absorption as energy transferred to the material. Do not give any film a universal half-and-half split. A diagram of a thin coating is a qualitative model, not a photograph of literal holes. Do not infer absorption by subtracting two light-meter readings from an incident reading: the sensor does not collect every outgoing direction.

A later boundary view can support day 14: oblique entry from air into water turns toward a supplied perpendicular reference line, perpendicular entry has no directional turn, and paths in uniform materials remain straight. Do not require angles, trigonometry, wave calculations or eye anatomy. A flashlight beam is not normally visible in clean air or clear water. For the teacher demonstration, follow the light spot on a white card rather than claim to see a floating ray. Rehearse with the actual cup; use recorded paths if curvature prevents a clear flat-boundary comparison. Do not add scattering agents or lasers.

Students must be able to construct, revise and save or print a model, rather than watch a completed animation. Use keyboard controls, large labelled arrows, descriptions that give direction and position, spoken sensor values and matching tactile arrow cards. A blind student chooses conditions and constructs the explanation, rather than only recording a partner's observations. No student account or independent device is needed. One shared display has a complete printed route.

## Unit 1: evidence sheets and no-kit routes

Prepare these before instruction. Real measurements are not on file and must not be invented. Label any synthetic illustration "invented for practice" and keep it distinct from investigation evidence.

- Days 1 to 3 and 5: a prepared box or recorded observations of the two viewing directions. Use a non-face object and describe each view. Learners identify the variable, fixed positions and contrasting prediction.
- Days 6 to 7: preserve three repeated readings for each illumination condition, sensor position, units, device and collection date. Students select a middle reading with guidance and compare highest with lowest. Give already organized displays; do not assume formal statistics.
- Days 8 to 10: provide real reflection and transmission records for transparent film, mirror film, a plastic mirror and opaque card. Record sensor geometry and ambient baseline. Compare each route with its own matching baseline; do not compare unlike detector positions as percentages of total light.
- Day 11: an original short account of a thin partially reflective coating. Students connect its structure to the measured two-path behavior. Avoid claiming ordinary clear surfaces reflect only because of dust or that real mirrors reflect every incident ray.
- Day 13: relative-input records plus an original receptor-to-brain reading checked against LS1.D. Use two labelled arrow types, light to receptor and signal to brain. Explain that a strong reflected image can obscure a weaker transmitted image, without claiming the brain processes only its strongest signal.
- Day 14: paired recorded ray paths at a flat air-water boundary, with oblique and perpendicular incidence. Include a printed perpendicular reference and descriptions of the paths.
- Day 16: withhold this comparison until then. Supply a different transparent panel with recorded views under changed lighting. Do not use the covered bird display or water-panel task reserved for day 17.

The no-kit routes are weaker because students analyze measurements and observations collected by others. They still select comparisons, evaluate controls and revise their own models. A simulation represents a mechanism and does not replace real evidence. Physical model and sensor limitations belong on each evidence sheet.

## Unit 1: safety and preparation carried onto each day

Use the materials and group counts in s01.md. Adult cutting, taping and battery checks happen the day before use. Keep a lit room with shaded boxes rather than make walking areas dark. Secure the meter and torch so no reflected beam reaches an eye. Safety glasses do not make deliberate staring at a light safe. Do not use mains equipment near water. Do not add detergent, milk, powders, coatings or cleaning chemicals to the water experiment. The only investigation liquid is room-temperature water. Adult disposal uses the sink after checking for electrical equipment nearby.

The teacher checks the film product's handling instructions and the water demonstration before use. If the film is unavailable, retain the recorded anchor rather than pretend plain packaging has the same properties. Verify the unit's $60 planning estimate against six sets of consumables and borrowed equipment; this estimate excludes purchasing a meter and a class set of eye protection.

## Unit 4: plate motion on a map

From [unit 4](s04.md), field 8. Mark: light tool, spec only. Days 106 and 107, ten minutes each. Job: show why seafloor forms at ridges and moves toward trenches, then revise linked rock, plate and energy models. Verdict: does not exist. No tool spec was available to inspect.

1. Let students predict boundary motion before revealing results. Show labelled positions, time intervals and a scale, with apart, together and past cases. Day 106 compares ridge and trench records; day 107 adds student-controlled rock-process and energy arrows. Limit each day to those two parts.
2. Allow branching rock histories. Include melting, crystallization, weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction and solid deformation. Keep solar energy, interior energy and gravity distinct. The mantle is mainly solid; no global magma ocean. Descending seafloor is recycled into the interior, not instantly melted wholesale at the trench. Avoid presenting every process as a required turn around one fixed loop.
3. Distinguish an illustrative model from measured data. Show that some processes are too slow, deep or large to reproduce in class. Preserve relative-age patterns without magnetic stripes. A simulation is not new observational evidence.
4. Provide keyboard controls, text descriptions, labelled high-contrast maps, removable tactile arrows and printable before-and-after maps. A student can construct and revise a model on paper while a single shared screen displays records. Save or print each revision without accounts.

## Unit 4: evidence and investigation paths

Use all three tiers without treating them as equivalent evidence. Physical observations supply sensory or manipulation evidence, supplied records are weaker for direct investigation, and the Curio Class simulation above represents inaccessible mechanisms. It is specified here for later construction, not already built.

- Day 98: household tier uses enclosed clean rock samples and a borrowed lens. The no-kit tier uses scaled rock-grain and dissolution records. Students compare structures and predict change but cannot inspect real texture. A later simulation magnifies and labels material pathways; it cannot create microscope evidence. Introduce deposition, compaction and microscopic dissolution through contrasting records now so day 113's explanation is not scored on first teaching.
- Days 99 and 102: household tier uses precut foam layers or sensitivity-safe clay in trays. Students select motion, observe deformation and record it. No-kit tier uses a documented sequence from the same controlled layer comparison, preserving a choice of comparison and a model revision but losing manipulation. The simulation changes direction on a map and explicitly does not reproduce real rock strength or earthquake timing.
- Day 111: household tier uses paper, a borrowed ruler and removable cards. The paper-only tier supplies already measured strips if rulers are unavailable. Students still choose and justify a scale; losing measurement makes this route weaker for measurement evidence. The simulation's time labels can support comparison but do not replace the independently constructed timeline.
