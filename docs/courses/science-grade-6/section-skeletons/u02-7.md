| 51 | MS-PS3-4 | Plan an investigation and interpret thermal relationships. | Item E1, 14 minutes on day 50 and 14 for revision on day 51, 8 points. Invented for practice: a tile-drying rack supplies a measured energy input to solid samples with negligible losses. Plan two controlled-heating investigations: vary mass of one material, then vary material at equal mass, holding input fixed. State variables, controls, tools, recording and sufficient trials. Explain a second evidence card: a calibrated source supplies equal energy inputs without state changes; 100 grams of water warms from 20 to 25 degrees Celsius, 200 grams from 20 to 23, and 100 grams of oil from 20 to 29. Describe how doubling water mass changes the rise at equal input; predict the effect of doubling input at fixed mass, within the liquid range. Explain why temperature alone cannot compare total energy across different masses, materials or states. Key and points: two separately controlled comparisons (2); thermometer, balance and timed initial and final readings (1); table with mass, material, state, time and temperatures (1); three trials per condition with reason to retain variation (1); the water pair shows more mass giving a smaller rise at equal input, and greater mass needing more input for the same rise (1); different materials respond differently and greater input gives a greater rise for unchanged mass and material (1); temperature tracks average particle kinetic energy, while total thermal energy also depends on amount, kind and state (1). Do not calculate energy. Equal heating time alone does not establish equal input; use the calibrated source record. The calibrated-source card is a printed hypothetical comparison, never a classroom heating instruction. Wrong: D, F or H. | section 2.7 |
| 51 | MS-PS3-5 | Construct and use an evidence argument in both transfer directions. | Item E2, 12 minutes on day 50 and 12 for revision on day 51, 8 points. Invented for practice: in one run, air sealed inside a bottle cools from 48 to 31 degrees Celsius while its initially cooler solid wall warms from 19 to 24. In a separate run, a cold sealed water pouch warms from 8 to 17 while its surrounding water cools from 26 to 22. Construct an argument for energy transfer in each run using before-and-after models, both records and particle interactions across the boundary. Use the argument to refute "Cold particles passed through the pouch wall." Key: energy leaves warmer air and enters the cooler bottle wall in the first run; it leaves warmer surrounding water and enters the colder pouch in the second. Solid particles vibrate; gas particles move freely between collisions; liquid particles move past neighbors. Motion changes on average, with energy transferred through interactions rather than particles traversing an intact wall. Award four points per run: correct direction, both temperature observations, linked before-and-after particle motion, and a boundary account identifying the corresponding energy change in the other material. Thermal energy includes microscopic kinetic energy; heat names transfer because of temperature difference. Do not require numerical energy equality from temperature readings. Wrong: A, B or E. Day 43 supplies spoken argument evidence once, without additional points. | section 2.7 |
| 51 | MS-PS3-3 | Apply ideas to design, build, test and improve. | Item E3, ten minutes of portfolio revision on day 50 and four minutes to select evidence on day 51, 4 points. Use each student's days 47 to 49 record, revised after written feedback: label criteria and constraints and explain a chosen feature using the energy model, naming light reflected or absorbed when the feature is a surface (1); show the constructed device and the student's observed contribution (1); compare all first and revised repeated trials with matched starting conditions against the criterion (1); explain the evidence-based revision and its outcome, including an unsuccessful improvement (1). Wrong: insulation produces cold (I), or the lid blocks all transfer (C). Group averages cannot replace individual explanations. Unrevised work earns feedback, not scored points. | section 2.7 |
