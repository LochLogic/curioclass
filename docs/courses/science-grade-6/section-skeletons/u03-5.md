| 75 | MS-PS1-4 | Develop a particle model to predict motion, temperature and state under energy and pressure changes. | Item P, 18 minutes, 10 points. Draft independently on day 74; revise on day 75 after written feedback. Invented for practice: an unfamiliar tropical storm takes water from a sunlit ocean through cloud droplets and ice before meltwater returns. Use a pure water sample taken from the storm and ignore the salt in the ocean. Draw the same sample as ice, liquid and vapor; predict both warming and cooling paths. The packet supplies a heating trace with a melting plateau, two equal-temperature liquid samples of different mass, an equal-mass solid and liquid pair at the melting point, and evidence that lowering pressure lowers boiling temperature. Explain the trace, distinguish heat transfer from energy within a sample, and predict boiling when pressure is reduced enough at fixed temperature. Key and points: solid vibration, contacting mobile liquid particles and widely separated mobile gas particles, one each; warming and cooling predictions with corresponding motion and state changes, one each; unchanged particles, one; plateau without continuously increasing temperature, one; temperature alone cannot rank thermal energy across different amounts or states, one; heat is transfer caused by temperature difference, one; sufficiently reduced pressure permits boiling without raising temperature, one. Wrong: particles disappear (A), motionless solid (C), temperature measures total energy (D), warming always raises temperature (E). | section 3.5 |
| 75 | MS-ESS2-4 | Develop a mechanism model of multiple water paths, with energy and matter flows. | Item W, 12 minutes, 8 points. Draft on day 74 and revise on day 75. Use P's new tropical-storm watershed, with an ocean, forest, snow-covered upland, stream and groundwater store. Explain how water can reach the cloud by two routes and return to the ocean by two routes. Key: show ocean evaporation and plant uptake followed by transpiration; condensation and freezing; precipitation; surface runoff; infiltration and downhill groundwater flow. Award six mechanism points: (1) evaporation; (2) uptake and transpiration; (3) condensation and freezing; (4) precipitation; (5) runoff; (6) infiltration and downhill groundwater flow. Add one point for sunlight supplying energy for evaporation and transpiration, and one for gravity driving precipitation and downhill flows. A list without connected paths earns no mechanism point. Wrong: water particles vanish when ocean water evaporates (A), gravity powers evaporation (F), visible cloud consists only of vapor (H). | section 3.5 |
| 89 | MS-PS1-4 | Develop and use particle predictions in an unfamiliar estuary case. | Item EP, 12 minutes on day 89, 10 points. Independent first draft in day 74's final 12 minutes; written feedback before day 89. Invented for practice: a freshwater pond beside a coastal estuary receives rain, forms surface ice overnight and later evaporates. Use pure freshwater samples, not the estuary's salt mixture. Packet: particle panels for all three states; a cooling trace with a freezing plateau; two equal-mass samples at different temperatures; liquid and vapor samples at the boiling point; a qualitative pressure-temperature diagram for pure water with solid, liquid and gas regions and an unlabelled target at lower pressure but the same temperature as a marked liquid starting point. The target lies across the liquid-gas boundary. Construct a before-and-after model predicting warming and cooling through state changes, then predict the target state and justify the change using particle separation and the diagram, without a supplied conclusion. Apply P's ten scoring components, with the cooling, freezing and boiling cases standing in for P's heating, melting and pressure cases; the pressure point requires predicting gas at the target and justifying boiling with the model. Show energy entering or leaving and distinguish heat transfer from stored thermal energy. Wrong: particles vanish on evaporation (A), motionless ice particles (C), temperature alone measures total energy (D), particle size increases on warming (N). | section 3.8 |
