## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **MS-ESS2-1** (days 97, 98): Build a model of how Earth materials cycle and what energy drives that cycling.
- **MS-ESS2-3** (days 97, 99, 100, 101): Use data on rocks, fossils, continent shapes and sea floor features as evidence that plates moved in the past.
- **MS-ESS2-2** (days 98, 99, 100, 101): Explain from evidence how geoscience processes have reshaped Earth's surface over small and large spans of time and space.
- skill s6-rock-cycle (grade 6)
- skill s6-plate-evidence (grade 6)
- skill s6-surface-change-scales (grade 6)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> Model separating observations from inferences on 95. Day 98 introduces microscopic dissolution and deposition and compaction through scaled rock records. Day 103 contrasts motion rate with a time span; day 113, slow erosion by water, ice and wind with sudden events. Step back during competing reconstructions on 109.
>
> Group the question board into linked puzzles: Where does mountain movement occur (96)? What moves underground (101)? How do boundaries change elevation (104)? Where does new rock come from (107)? Where were the plates before (110)? How did marine fossils become exposed (114)? Each answer generates the next question. Transfer uses two withheld, invented basins in field 5.
> Carried: on 92 retrieve cause-effect, energy-transfer, data-graph, system-model and patterns; begin tentative rock-cycle, plate-model, stability-change and scale-proportion representations. On 97, interior records revise energy and system boundaries. Boundary observations revise plates and causes on 100; elevation rates revise graphs, scale and stability on 104. Melting and ridge evidence revise rock and energy paths on 107. Fossil matches revise plate patterns on 109. On 110 use claim-evidence-reasoning from strata. Timeline evidence revises scale on 111; erosion revises rock and stability on 113. Consolidate every model on 114 and use them in transfer; retain, never withdraw.
