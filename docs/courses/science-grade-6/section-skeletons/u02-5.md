| 44 | MS-PS3-4 | Plan relationships among material, mass, temperature change and energy transfer. | Item M2, 18 minutes on day 43 and 18 for revision on day 44, formative. Invented for practice: a workshop wants warm parts to cool predictably in a lidded storage tray. Plan two comparisons: different masses of one material, and equal masses of two materials. Specify what changes, what stays fixed, tools, initial and final readings, the record and enough trials. Explain why equal cooling times do not establish equal energy transfers. Key: vary mass separately from kind; match initial temperature, surroundings and exposed geometry as closely as possible; record actual geometry differences, mass, material, state, time and temperature using a balance, thermometer and clock; use at least three trials per condition. Temperature change indicates changed average particle kinetic energy, not the total energy transferred. More of the same material needs more energy for the same temperature rise; material and state also matter. Wrong: equal times guarantee equal transfer (F); temperature alone determines total energy (D). Meets: "I would change mass first, then kind with equal masses. I would record starting and ending temperatures in matched surroundings for three trials. Different changes do not measure equal energy amounts." Does not meet: "The same time guarantees the same transferred energy" (F). | section 2.5; reuse day 43 written plan |
| 44 | MS-PS3-5 | Argue from evidence with before-and-after energy accounts. | Item M3, 12 minutes on day 43 and 12 for revision on day 44, formative. Invented for practice: paired records show a sealed metal sample cooling from 46 to 33 degrees Celsius while surrounding water warms from 21 to 25. Construct an argument against the claim that the lost motion energy disappeared. Use a before-and-after particle model, cite both records and answer a peer challenge. Key: the metal's average particle kinetic energy falls while the water's rises; net energy moves from warmer metal to cooler water through interactions. The records support direction, not an exact energy balance. Meets: "The metal particles vibrate less vigorously as water particles move faster on average. Energy left the metal and entered the water; the two temperature changes support that account." Does not meet: "The metal's energy vanished" (B). Preserve day 43's spoken presentation and partner log without collecting again. | section 2.5; reuse day 43 written and spoken argument |
