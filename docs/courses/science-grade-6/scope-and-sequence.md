# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 6

Course `science-grade-6`, grade 6, draft, fixes applied 2026-11-15.

The day record is `data/plans/science-grade-6/days.csv`; the lesson-by-lesson placement is `source-map.csv` in the same folder. Six units retain the frame's order and 156 days. Four course-level check and respond days make 160 meetings of 50 minutes, five per week. Course day equals school day, so `school_day` is blank. Weeks are the shared calendar's five-day positions.

The standard is the national performance-expectation set in the frame; the state framework is a crosswalk only. The contributing ray expectation remains marked `~`. This course completes 14 expectations through 13 rows, and contributes light through the fourteenth row. Engineering, cell-part, body-system and sensory expectations marked `~` in the day record are starts, with only the frame's named parts assessed. They remain owned by later courses. The coverage table below connects the dimensions in `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv` to teaching and assessment slots. Items, scoring guidance and element-by-element section plans come later.

Every unit begins with an observable puzzle, questions and an initial model. Evidence changes that model at the points named in the day record. An assessment uses an unfamiliar case and requires a practice with the idea and crosscutting relationship, rather than vocabulary alone. Every code listed on an assessment has a prompt for each part within its declared scope. A prior observed performance or product may supply the doing component where the assessment note names it. The early day 30 formative checkpoint carries skills but no whole-expectation codes because the full mechanisms have not yet been taught. Day 148 follows the complete cell investigation and can name MS-LS1-1.

## Unit 1. Light and matter

**Calendar:** days 1 to 17, weeks 1 to 4, 17 days. Sections: 1.1 days 1 to 4, 1.2 days 5 to 10, 1.3 days 11 to 14, 1.4 days 15 to 17. Base unit 6.1, lessons 1 to 8.

The same film looks like a mirror on one side and a window on the other. Students first record the puzzle and construct tentative ray models; changing illumination then creates the need to measure reflected and transmitted light. Film structure and relative inputs explain the two views. Day 14 adds qualitative bending at an air-water boundary before the final model is used on an unfamiliar panel. Revisions on days 7, 10, 12 and 15 follow new evidence rather than a supplied conclusion.

The contributing row needs 17 days because it establishes year-long inquiry and safety routines as well as the ray mechanism. Days 1 and 2 reserve about one day in total for safety and notebook routines. The statistical bridge on day 6 guides the middle reading and highest-to-lowest range, because math's data unit is days 142 to 157. It assumes neither mean nor independent choice of a summary.

**Assessment:** retrieve the light-path row on day 2, maintained in grade 5. No mid-unit assessment is required. Day 17 assesses reflection, transmission, absorption and transparent-boundary bending in a model of an unseen display, 35 minutes plus 15 minutes response. This is the ray part only, with no claim to sound or the light-wave model. Receptors and brain signaling are introduced with support on day 13 for later retrieval.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero days. Keep the frame's receptor-only limit; eye anatomy and wave mathematics are dropped as outside this contribution.

Use flashlights only, no lasers. Film in a box and a teacher light meter have a no-kit readings path; light readings are available aloud for a student who cannot use the visual comparison. The light-path simulation has matching paper diagrams. Condense base lesson 8's repeated related-case discussion from three 45-minute meetings to two 50-minute meetings, keeping the transfer case. Integrate boundary bending with lesson 6 and combine related-phenomenon listing with question-board work so safety does not consume the experimental comparisons.

## Unit 3. Weather, climate and water cycling

Hail on a warm day makes surface temperature alone insufficient. Atmospheric profiles, surface-energy observations and rising-air evidence build a vertical mechanism. Water stores, states and multiple paths are modeled before clouds and air masses use them. Day 64 makes transpiration an atmospheric-moisture source. Lift, precipitation and a regional storm then lead to collected observations and uncertain forecasts. Day 70 combines the airflow record with precipitation prediction; former days 72 to 84 move to days 71 to 83. Nothing assessed is removed.

Day 84 opens the second anchor, why some regions get far more rain. It defines climate as long-run weather patterns and uses prevailing winds, mirrored hemispheres, latitude and sunlight angle. Days 85 and 86 answer how ocean temperature and currents change evaporation and air-mass humidity. The ocean absorbs energy, releases it slowly and redistributes it. Brief density/salinity comparisons, rotation and continent-boundary reasoning are explicit Curio Class additions inside these periods. Day 85 also compares supplied ice/dark-surface reflection and absorption evidence. Day 87 and the first 25 minutes of day 88 give 75 minutes to comparing two coast-to-inland paths, altitude and rain shadows. The final 25 minutes of day 88 preserve the rainforest synthesis: the model connects ice and vegetation to air, ocean, land, sunlight and moving water rather than appending a separate report.

Concurrent ELA weather reading on days 63 to 86 is available without being a prerequisite.

Retrieve the three grade 5 rows on day 52. Day 74 uses eight minutes for anchor synthesis, 30 for independent tropical-storm drafts and 12 for the independent estuary particle draft. Give written feedback before revision on the relevant assessment day. These drafts leave all 75 geographic-comparison minutes intact. Day 88's unfamiliar rainforest model supplies the full climate performance once. ## Unit 4. Rock cycling and plate tectonics

**Calendar:** days 92 to 115, weeks 19 to 23, 24 days. Sections: 4.1 days 92 to 96, 4.2 days 97 to 101, 4.3 days 102 to 107, 4.4 days 108 to 112, 4.5 days 113 to 115. Base unit 6.4, lessons 1 to 14.

A marine fossil on a rising mountain starts the inquiry. Earthquake, elevation and interior evidence develops plate and rock-process models together. Melting and new seafloor come before reconstructing older plate positions. Rock formations and fossils then supply relative order, and a scaled history makes sense of inland mountain belts and erosion. Day 113 explicitly links microscopic limestone dissolution and caves with larger landforms, water, ice and wind, slow change and sudden events. Day 114 finishes the rock cycle with compaction, deformation, uplift and erosion, driven by interior and solar energy. A complete cycle is never an opening prerequisite.

The four rows share evidence but carry distinct loads: mechanism, past plate movement, contrasting spatial and temporal scales, and relative-age explanation. The days therefore concentrate on coupled models and evidence analysis before the late timeline. Math rates and ratios are already available by days 51 and 59; ELA informative writing is available by day 86; social studies' timeline was taught on days 1 to 10. Days 96, 103, 111 and 112 retrieve these. If those subjects run late, use the frame's per-year multiplication, supplied timeline scale and local explanation frame inside those days.

**Assessment:** day 91 gives the readiness check on rock layers and stability-change, last used in grade 4; day 93 provides one full lesson of response within this unit. Day 94 retrieves erosion and feature-map skills maintained in grade 5. No mid-unit assessment is required. The end-of-unit evidence spans days 111 to 115. The individual scaled timeline (111) and strata explanation (112) carry MS-ESS1-4, including relative versus numerical ages and processes working in the past as now. Day 113 completes the MS-ESS2-2 explanation from day 112 with microscopic dissolution, underground caves, local/global landforms, water/ice/wind and slow/sudden change. Score each product once. Day 115 reuses those records and spends 15 minutes on the full energy-driven rock cycle and 15 on a plate reconstruction using every evidence class, seafloor creation/destruction and revised interpretations.
