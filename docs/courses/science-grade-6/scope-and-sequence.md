# Scope and sequence: Science, Grade 6

Course `science-grade-6`, grade 6, draft, 2026-11-14.

The day record is `data/plans/science-grade-6/days.csv`; the lesson-by-lesson placement is `source-map.csv` in the same folder. Six units retain the frame's order and 156 days. Four course-level check and respond days make 160 meetings of 50 minutes, five per week. Course day equals school day, so `school_day` is blank. Weeks are the shared calendar's five-day positions.

The standard is the national performance-expectation set in the frame; the state framework is a crosswalk only. The contributing ray expectation remains marked `~`. This course completes 14 expectations through 13 rows, and contributes light through the fourteenth row. Engineering, cell-part, body-system and sensory expectations marked `~` in the day record are starts, with only the frame's named parts assessed. They remain owned by later courses. The coverage table below connects the dimensions in `data/standards/ngss-pe-dimensions.csv` to teaching and assessment slots. Items, scoring guidance and element-by-element section plans come later.

Every unit begins with an observable puzzle, questions and an initial model. Evidence changes that model at the points named in the day record. An assessment uses an unfamiliar case and requires a practice with the idea and crosscutting relationship, rather than vocabulary alone. Every code listed on an assessment has a prompt for each part within its declared scope. A prior observed performance or product may supply the doing component where the assessment note names it. The two early formative checkpoints carry skills but no whole-expectation codes because their full mechanisms have not yet been taught.

## Unit 1. Light and matter

**Calendar:** days 1 to 17, weeks 1 to 4, 17 days. Sections: 1.1 days 1 to 4, 1.2 days 5 to 10, 1.3 days 11 to 14, 1.4 days 15 to 17. Base unit 6.1, lessons 1 to 8.

The same film looks like a mirror on one side and a window on the other. Students first record the puzzle and construct tentative ray models; changing illumination then creates the need to measure reflected and transmitted light. Film structure and relative inputs explain the two views. Day 14 adds qualitative bending at an air-water boundary before the final model is used on an unfamiliar panel. Revisions on days 7, 10, 12 and 15 follow new evidence rather than a supplied conclusion.

The contributing row needs 17 days because it establishes year-long inquiry and safety routines as well as the ray mechanism. Days 1 and 2 reserve about one day in total for safety and notebook routines. The statistical bridge on day 6 guides the middle reading and highest-to-lowest range, because math's data unit is days 142 to 157. It assumes neither mean nor independent choice of a summary.

**Assessment:** retrieve the light-path row on day 2, maintained in grade 5. No mid-unit assessment is required. Day 17 assesses reflection, transmission, absorption and transparent-boundary bending in a model of an unseen display, 35 minutes plus 15 minutes response. This is the ray part only, with no claim to sound or the light-wave model. Receptors and brain signaling are introduced with support on day 13 for later retrieval.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero days. Keep the frame's receptor-only limit; eye anatomy and wave mathematics are dropped as outside this contribution.

Use flashlights only, no lasers. Film in a box and a teacher light meter have a no-kit readings path; light readings are available aloud for a student who cannot use the visual comparison. The light-path simulation has matching paper diagrams. Condense base lesson 8's repeated related-case discussion from three 45-minute meetings to two 50-minute meetings, keeping the transfer case. Integrate boundary bending with lesson 6 and combine related-phenomenon listing with question-board work so safety does not consume the experimental comparisons.

## Unit 2. Thermal energy

**Calendar:** days 18 to 51, weeks 4 to 11, 34 days. Sections: 2.1 days 18 to 22, 2.2 days 23 to 28, 2.3 days 29 to 34, 2.4 days 35 to 39, 2.5 days 40 to 44, 2.6 days 45 to 49, 2.7 days 50 to 51. Base unit 6.2, lessons 1 to 18.

Two cups keep a drink cold for different lengths of time. Comparisons of lids, water loss and condensation first separate matter transfer from energy transfer. Light absorption then leaves a second warming mechanism unexplained. Particle-motion and collision evidence builds that mechanism, followed by explicit tests of mass and kind of matter on day 42. The day 43 argument accounts for energy entering and leaving with changes of particle kinetic energy. Only then do students predict, make, test and revise a container. Revisions on days 24, 27, 36, 41 and 50 keep the evidence attached to the model.

This is a long unit because it develops two rows carrying three full expectations, a particle-state start and a complete physical-science design performance. The statistics bridge occupies 40 minutes of day 22 and 10 minutes of day 49. The class retains individual trial values. Day 43 models claim, evidence and reasoning locally because ELA argument writing overlaps days 33 to 58. Graphs use grade 5 first-quadrant plotting; no equation prerequisite is borrowed from later math.

**Assessment:** day 18 retrieves particles, thermal transfer maintained in grade 5, and fair prototype tests. The base lesson 6 checkpoint stays on day 30 for lid and matter-path reasoning, with 15 minutes feedback and no whole-expectation codes. The base lesson 14 checkpoint stays on day 44: a new cooling case checks a fair plan including mass, kind of matter and temperature change and the before-and-after energy argument, 30 minutes plus 20 minutes response. Day 51 checks all three owned expectations through an unseen container case, a fair-test plan, an energy argument and the observed prototype portfolio from days 48 and 49, 35 minutes plus 15 minutes response. Construction and testing are assessed as performances, not replaced by a drawing.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero days. Keep the three expectations and the named engineering and state-change starts, without energy calculations.

Warm water is no hotter than 60 C, kept by the teacher; wipe spills at once and use no glass in student designs. The no-kit path compares real temperature-time trials, explicitly weaker than collecting them. The particle simulation has printed sequences. The source map condenses lessons 15, 17 and 18 by one meeting each. In addition, use the question board instead of a separate related-case presentation, use the existing trial chart for day 43's argument, and reduce repeated synthesis reporting in lessons 12 and 14. These recover 90 minutes within the mapped meetings: 50 for the summary bridge and 40 for the mass-and-kind comparison. Retain both prototype trials, the independent assessment and all response blocks. The design work is days 47 to 49, before social studies' research days 100 to 113 and before the end of ELA's second process piece.

## Unit 3. Weather, climate and water cycling

**Calendar:** days 52 to 89, weeks 11 to 18, 38 days. Sections: 3.1 days 52 to 56, 3.2 days 57 to 62, 3.3 days 63 to 67, 3.4 days 68 to 73, 3.5 days 74 to 77, 3.6 days 78 to 82, 3.7 days 83 to 87, 3.8 days 88 to 89. Base unit 6.3, lessons 1 to 22.

Hail on a warm day makes surface temperature alone insufficient. Atmospheric profiles, surface-energy observations and rising-air evidence build a vertical mechanism. Water's stores, states and multiple paths are modeled before clouds and air masses use them. Lift and precipitation lead into a regional storm, collected observations and probability-based forecasts. The last arc connects unequal solar heating, Earth's rotation and atmosphere-ocean circulation to regional rainfall, with temperature, salinity, land boundaries, altitude and distance inland explicitly retained. Model revisions on days 58, 60, 65, 69, 74, 81 and 87 progressively connect those scales.

Four owners, linked models and the regional-climate close justify the year's longest allocation. Day 55 begins observations, with three-minute entries through day 84 that replace part of each opening, never an extra day. Students collect observations themselves where instruments are available and otherwise extract a documented archive series. Degrees below zero are read on a thermometer without signed arithmetic. Latitude is available from social studies days 26 to 38; day 85 retrieves it and grade 5 shadow patterns, then teaches sunlight angle by latitude. If that course runs late, the same globe comparison reteaches latitude bands inside day 85.

**Assessment:** retrieve conservation, sphere interactions, water stores, maintained weather-data reading and climate patterns within the opening and day 85. No old-row readiness check is needed. The base lesson 13 assessment stays on day 76, checking state-change predictions and the full water-cycle mechanism in a new storm case, 30 minutes plus 20 minutes response. Day 89 uses an unseen region with model, weather record and particle-state changes to cover all four codes, including each student's collection portfolio, 35 minutes plus 15 minutes response. It includes sunlight and gravity, living and nonliving water paths, probabilistic weather and the full atmosphere-ocean mechanism.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero days. Drop cloud-name and weather-symbol recall beyond the supplied key, as the boundary requires.

Dry ice is teacher-only with gloves and tongs, never sealed; heating is teacher-only. A documented observation path replaces those demonstrations when their controls cannot be met. No helium cylinder is required. Fluid comparisons use household containers, ice, water and food dye; real balloon and station records replace unavailable instruments. Rising-air and ocean-circulation simulations each have a paper input-and-output path. Severe-storm discussions use data and models with no graphic damage imagery.

The source map reduces lessons 2, 3, 4, 5, 14 and 21 to fit the longer meetings, without cutting their evidence. Day 88 combines the regional evidence discussion with the final linked-model preparation; day 89 retains independent transfer and response. Course-level check 1 follows, sampling state-change reasoning through earlier unit 2 content rather than repeating this regional assessment.

## Unit 4. Rock cycling and plate tectonics

**Calendar:** days 92 to 115, weeks 19 to 23, 24 days. Sections: 4.1 days 92 to 96, 4.2 days 97 to 101, 4.3 days 102 to 107, 4.4 days 108 to 112, 4.5 days 113 to 115. Base unit 6.4, lessons 1 to 14.

A marine fossil on a rising mountain starts the inquiry. Earthquake, elevation and interior evidence develops plate and rock-process models together. Melting and new seafloor come before reconstructing older plate positions. Rock formations and fossils then supply relative order, and a scaled history makes sense of inland mountain belts and erosion. Day 113 explicitly links microscopic limestone dissolution and caves with larger landforms, water, ice and wind, slow change and sudden events. Day 114 finishes the rock cycle with compaction, deformation, uplift and erosion, driven by interior and solar energy. A complete cycle is never an opening prerequisite.

The four rows share evidence but carry distinct loads: mechanism, past plate movement, contrasting spatial and temporal scales, and relative-age explanation. The days therefore concentrate on coupled models and evidence analysis before the late timeline. Math rates and ratios are already available by days 51 and 59; ELA informative writing is available by day 86; social studies' timeline was taught on days 1 to 10. Days 96, 103, 111 and 112 retrieve these. If those subjects run late, use the frame's per-year multiplication, supplied timeline scale and local explanation frame inside those days.

**Assessment:** day 91 gives the readiness check on rock layers and stability-change, last used in grade 4; day 93 provides one full lesson of response within this unit. Day 94 retrieves erosion and feature-map skills maintained in grade 5. No mid-unit assessment is required. Day 115 assesses all four owned codes on an unseen basin and mountain: a complete energy-driven rock-cycle model, rocks/fossils/shelf shapes/seafloor data, small-to-large and fast-to-slow change, and layer-based relative ages organizing a 4.6-billion-year history. Thirty minutes assessment and ten minutes response leave the last ten minutes for unit 5's readiness check.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero days. Drop mineral identification and period or epoch name recall; retain all mechanisms and scale contrasts.

No acid, flame or molten materials are required. Clay or foam layers replace inaccessible rock processes; images and public seafloor records supply the no-kit path. The plate-map simulation has equivalent paper maps. Earth's age is stated as the evidence shows; no student is asked to affirm or reject a faith account. Natural processes operating in the past as now and revision of scientific interpretations from converging evidence are explicit tasks on days 109 and 114. Base lesson 1 is three local meetings rather than four base meetings: related cases and question-board construction share a task. The retained margin supports the readiness response and the explicit small-scale landform case.

## Unit 5. Natural hazards

**Calendar:** days 116 to 135, weeks 24 to 27, 20 days. Sections: 5.1 days 116 to 119, 5.2 days 120 to 123, 5.3 days 124 to 129, 5.4 days 130 to 132, 5.5 days 133 to 135. Base unit 6.5, lessons 1 to 10, including the signals lesson.

Records of the 2011 earthquake and tsunami in Japan raise questions of occurrence, warning and protection. Students map hazards and distinguish forecasts from detection, then use a shallow wave model to explain tsunami motion. A tested comparison of mitigation options creates a need for a working detection and communication system. Day 129 joins text, image and narration in one evidence note before the analog/digital comparison. Days 130 and 131 address copying, storage and transmission under noise, followed by a linked-system model and transfer to another hazard. Earthquake risk can be estimated, but an exact earthquake time and location cannot presently be predicted.

The hazard row carries most of the time; digital reliability is a short, explicit evidence argument rather than a second design unit. Day 119 plus ten minutes on day 123 teaches histogram frequency, equal intervals and the distinction from category bar graphs, because math histograms arrive about days 145 to 147. No histogram construction is assumed. Day 129 supplies the media-integration bridge because ELA teaches it in days 137 to 156. Units 3 and 4 supply atmospheric, ocean and plate mechanisms. The in-class design days 124 to 126 come after social studies' unit 8 research days 100 to 113; ELA has no process piece in this window.

**Assessment:** the last ten minutes of day 115 check the older wave row, with a full lesson of targeted reteaching on day 117. Hazard solutions and information coding were maintained by grade 5 design tests, so retrieve those on days 116 and 128. No mid-unit assessment is required. Day 135 assesses an unseen hazard record and warning system, forecasting and technology choices, and an argument integrating qualitative technical information about digital versus analog reliability in both storage and transmission, 35 minutes plus 15 minutes response. The design record supplies evidence for the named engineering starts; it does not claim their later-owned expectations complete.

**Candidate settled:** no addition, zero days. No binary arithmetic, device internals or wave-energy equations.

Use a shallow tray and household barriers with spill control; public buoy and hazard records supply the no-kit comparison. A wave-tank simulation has the same paper record. Non-graphic community accounts, a data-only alternative and a step-out route protect access without asking for personal disaster stories. The later ELA hazard work uses the same content boundaries.

Condense the base opening from three meetings to two and the final project from four to three, while expanding lesson 8 from one to two. The final product is a bounded in-class analysis, with no outreach or required public presentation. This retains the extra signals comparison and the histogram bridge inside 20 days.
