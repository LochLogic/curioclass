# Course frame: Science, Grade 6

Course id science-grade-6, grade 6, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6 units, one for each OpenSciEd grade 6 unit, in its order (6.1 to 6.6): light, thermal energy,
  weather and climate, rock cycling and plates, natural hazards, cells and healing.
- **Days.** 160 teaching days: 156 in units, 4 for course-level checks and respond days (two checks of 1 day, each
  followed by 1 respond day). One 50-minute lesson a day, five a week, 32 weeks. The base text plans its six grade
  6 units at 168 days of 45 minutes (7,560 minutes); 156 days of 50 minutes is 7,800 minutes, so each unit gets
  its base time (base days times 0.9) plus about 5 days across the year for what the rows add: the year safety
  sheet, the retrieval openings on grade 3 and 4 skills, a model reactivation and two math bridges. Each unit's
  days include its end-of-unit assessment, where the base unit puts its own.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards middle school performance expectations (never named in
  student, teacher or family copy). The course owns 13 codes on 14 progression rows and contributes the ray part of
  MS-PS4-2 (marked ~; unit 4 of grade 8 owns and completes it). An expectation started here and completed later is
  named in the unit that starts it, with the part taught; an expectation completed here names the part done
  earlier. Practices and crosscutting concepts are counted by element in the unit plans (RUBRIC-SCIENCE S11).
- **Base text.** OpenSciEd Middle School, 2022 standard units, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms;
  docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md). Each unit adapts one base unit whole, in the base's lesson order; the "## Source
  map" section places all 18 middle school units and their lessons, and gives the loss check's result (order kept,
  no loss brought to Cory). The "+CS" variants are CC BY-NC and never a base. One short credit line per adapted day.
  Never carried over: third-party photos and videos, the base's character names, brand names, kit links.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor phenomenon or design problem (named in the table), builds a
  question board, an initial class model and a consensus model, and revises the model as evidence arrives; each
  lesson answers a question the last one raised. The end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has not seen.
- **Engineering and the nature of science.** Design units: unit 2 (a cup that keeps a drink cold, MS-PS3-3, and
  the start of MS-ETS1-4) and unit 5 (tsunami safety, the start of MS-ETS1-1 and MS-ETS1-2). The nature of science
  is taught on purpose in unit 4 (the plate idea won acceptance as evidence from many fields agreed; natural
  processes worked in the past as they do now) and unit 3 (a forecast is a range of chances, not a certainty).
- **Materials.** Household stand-ins wherever safe, then a no-kit data path (a data sheet of real readings, said
  plainly to be weaker than collecting them), then a Curio Class simulation for what no class can run; simulations
  are listed per unit now and built later as tools (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Each unit plan states its cost. Every
  electronic tool has a paper path (the same data on a printed sheet) and needs no student account.
- **Safety.** A year safety sheet is taught in unit 1's first days; every hands-on day has a Safety and set-up
  section (hazards, eye protection, set-up, clean-up). A science teacher reads safety and materials before the
  course is called finished (FOUNDATION 7.3; people are Cory's).
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own readings at grade 6 level, written from the base's readings in our words;
  data sets from public agencies (NOAA, USGS, NASA), each with its rights line.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh-agent sensitivity read. A reader from the disability community
  reads unit 6 (lesson 14, ability and disability); a school counselor reads unit 5's teacher note on disaster loss.
