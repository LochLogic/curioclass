# Course frame: Science, Grade 6

Course id science-grade-6, grade 6, draft frame (2026-11-13). The scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 6 units, one for each OpenSciEd grade 6 unit, in its order (6.1 to 6.6): light, thermal energy,
  weather and climate, rock cycling and plates, natural hazards, cells and healing.
- **Days.** 160 course days: 156 in units, 4 for course-level checks and respond days (two checks of 1 day, each
  followed by 1 respond day). One 50-minute lesson a day, five a week, 32 weeks. The base text plans its six grade
  6 units at 168 days of 45 minutes (7,560 minutes); 156 days of 50 minutes is 7,800 minutes, 240 minutes beyond
  the base total, for what the rows add: the year safety sheet, retrieval tasks on grade 3 and 4 skills and two
  math bridges. The margin is uneven: units 1 to 6 gain 40, 35, 10, 75, 55 and 25
  minutes over their base time (unit 3 gives a day to unit 6, which carries the most new load). Unit 6's margin
  does not cover its retrieval tasks and its added microscope work, so the scope and sequence names the
  condensation inside unit 6 and accounts for those tasks explicitly. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit
  assessment, where the base unit puts its own.
  Returning skills follow one rule: a row or model idle two or more grades (two or more grades between its last use
  and this one) opens the first unit that needs it with a readiness check and a one-lesson reteach for students who
  miss it, counted in the unit's days; one idle a single grade or less, or used earlier this year, opens with a
  retrieval task, a few minutes inside a lesson.
  Each course-level check is one day because every unit already ends in its own end-of-unit assessment on an
  unseen case inside its days; the course-level check samples across units. If the scope and sequence finds a
  50-minute check cannot sample three units, it takes a day from the unit before the check and says so.
- **Standard.** The Next Generation Science Standards middle school performance expectations (never named in
  student, teacher or family copy). The course owns 14 performance expectations on 14 progression rows and
  contributes the ray part of MS-PS4-2 (marked ~; unit 4 of grade 8 owns and completes it). An expectation started here and completed later is
  named in the unit that starts it, with the part taught; an expectation completed here names the part done
  earlier. Practices and crosscutting concepts are counted by element in the unit plans (RUBRIC-SCIENCE S11).
- **Base text.** OpenSciEd Middle School, 2022 standard units, CC BY 4.0 (S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms;
  docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md). Each unit adapts one base unit whole, in the base's lesson order; the "## Source
  map" section places all 18 middle school units and their lessons, and gives the loss check's result (order kept,
  no loss brought to Cory). The "+CS" variants are CC BY-NC and never a base. One short credit line per adapted day.
  Never carried over: third-party photos and videos, the base's character names, brand names, kit links.
- **The storyline.** Every unit opens on its anchor phenomenon or design problem (named in the table), builds a
  question board, an initial class model and a consensus model, and revises the model as evidence arrives; each
  lesson answers a question the last one raised. The end-of-unit assessment uses a phenomenon the class has not seen.
- **Engineering and the nature of science.** Design units: unit 2 (a cup that keeps a drink cold, MS-PS3-3, and
  the start of MS-ETS1-4) and unit 5 (tsunami safety, the start of MS-ETS1-1 and MS-ETS1-2). The nature of science
  is taught on purpose in unit 4 (the plate idea won acceptance as evidence from many fields agreed; natural
  processes worked in the past as they do now) and unit 3 (a forecast is a range of chances, not a certainty).
- **Practices across the year.** Asking questions and defining problems (unit 1); developing and using models
  (units 1 to 4, 6); planning and carrying out investigations (units 2, 3, 6); analysing and interpreting data
  (units 3, 5, 6); using mathematics and computational thinking (unit 4, scaled timelines and rates; unit 5,
  hazard frequency, the base's first computational work of the year); constructing explanations and designing
  solutions (unit 2); engaging in argument from evidence (units 4, 5). Crosscutting concepts: systems and system
  models (units 1, 2, 6), structure and function (units 1, 6), energy and matter (unit 2), patterns and cause and
  effect (units 3, 5), scale, proportion and quantity (unit 4), stability and change (units 4, 5). Each unit's
  list is the base unit's own focal list (its unit page in S:\CurioClass-sources\openscied-ms).
- **Literacy standards.** The science and technical reading and writing standards for grades 6 to 8 (RST.6-8,
  WHST.6-8; data/standards/ccss-literacy-6-12.txt) are met alongside the science where the work already does them
  (Cory, Desk 2026-11-14: "Load them", no extra days). Each unit's "What students learn" cell ends with the codes
  it meets; they are never owned standards and add no days.
- **Materials.** Household stand-ins wherever safe, then a no-kit data path (a data sheet of real readings, said
  plainly to be weaker than collecting them), then a Curio Class simulation for what no class can run; simulations
  are listed per unit now and built later as tools (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-13). Each unit plan states its cost. Every
  electronic tool has a paper path (the same data on a printed sheet) and needs no student account.
- **Safety.** A year safety sheet is taught in unit 1's first days; every hands-on day has a Safety and set-up
  section (hazards, eye protection, set-up, clean-up). Every unit is hands-on, so every unit gets the safety
  agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md), against published school science safety guidance and
  the safety data sheet of every substance named (unit 3's dry ice first), before the course is called finished;
  the course's checks get the answer-key agent's read.
- **Reading level.** Curio Class's own readings at grade 6 level, written from the base's readings in our words;
  data sets from public agencies (NOAA, USGS, NASA), each with its rights line.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 6 (lesson 14, ability and disability): the agent first
  studies the disability community's own published voice (disability-led organisations and self-advocates'
  writing, a disability language style guide for journalists, a disability history source). Unit 5 (the 2011
  earthquake and tsunami in Japan, disaster loss): it first studies the survivors' and Tohoku communities' own
  published accounts and memorial archives, Japan's own agency reports on the warning, and published guidance on
  children and disasters (a national child traumatic stress network, a school psychologists' association) for the
  teacher note on loss. Unit 4 (Earth's age): it studies science teachers' associations' guidance on teaching
  Earth's history to students of faith.
