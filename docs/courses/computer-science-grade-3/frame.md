# Course frame: Computer Science, Grade 3

Course `computer-science-grade-3` (catalogue id `cs-k12`), grade 3, one year. Draft frame, 2026-11-25, written on the shape of the kindergarten pilot (docs/courses/computer-science-kindergarten/frame.md and its fix report) and matched to the grade 2 frame's handoffs; the scope and sequence sets the days later.

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 5, one for each concept area at its grade 3 depth, in the order the ideas build: software, the internet
  and what we share (systems and security, with the benefits and risks of widely used systems, so the system-diagram,
  the network-diagram and the device rules sit before the first program), algorithms that choose (algorithms, the
  first choice on step-cards, and how two algorithms for one job treat different people), programs that choose
  (programming, turning unit 2's step-card algorithms into block programs, then improving, reading, tracing and
  debugging them), checking data and asking everyone (data, with machine learning on the class's own training set),
  and computing at work, then and now (computing and society, the year's closer).
- **Days.** 32 course days: 28 in units, 2 for course-level checks (1 after unit 2, 1 after unit 4) and 2 respond
  days (one after each check). One lesson a week of about 40 minutes, 32 weeks: the computer science default for
  grades 3 to 5 (docs/courses/computer-science/STANDARD.md section 2, Cory, Desk q-fix-cs-1), a specials rotation, so
  course day n falls in week n. The 40 minutes hold set-up, clean-up and collecting work: a session opens with a
  three-minute retrieval trace from the second session on (APPROACH.md, Pacing), and device sessions keep four
  minutes at the end to save or print work, shut devices and collect papers, so a session teaches for about 33
  minutes. Each unit's days include its end-of-unit assessment and end with a respond day (APPROACH.md, Pacing), with
  the pilot's one shape for a unit that a course-level check follows directly: in units 2 and 4 the check stands for
  the unit's own end-of-unit assessment and the check's respond day is the unit's respond day (pilot For the lead 2).
  So units 1 to 5 teach on 4, 4, 7, 4 and 3 days, 22 of the 32. Days follow the load of the rows: unit 3 owns four
  programming rows and five ids, each with a device version and the worked, modify, make, feedback, read, trace and
  debug order, and gets 9 days; unit 1 owns four rows and five ids, but three of its rows are single unplugged
  sessions and the two ids of g3-benefits-risks share one chart, so it gets 6; units 2 and 4 get 4 teaching sessions
  for two and four rows; unit 5's two rows carry three ids, one session each, and get 5. Grade 3 owns 19 ids in the
  same 32 sessions that grade 2 used for 16; the 40-minute session (10 more than kindergarten to grade 2) carries the
  three extra ids, so no unit is shorter than the grade 2 unit of the same concept area. Each course-level check takes
  one check session and one respond session, since a 40-minute session holds a written sitting of about 25 minutes
  and short spoken answers for the children who need them. No unit runs over 25 days, so there are no mid-unit
  assessments.
- **Check weeks.** The grade 3 core checks take weeks 9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28, 30, 31 and 32
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md, target weeks: ELA 16 and 32, math 12, 23 and 31, science 9, 20 and 30, social studies
  14, 25 and 28). This course's checks sit in week 11 (mid-year, after unit 2) and week 26 (year-end, after unit 4),
  with their respond days in weeks 12 and 27 (respond days are not check days). Week 26 lets unit 5 have the five
  weeks 28 to 32 its three society ids need, as the grade 2 frame does. Unit 3 runs across weeks 13 to 21, which
  include the core check weeks 14, 16 and 20, because it holds no course-level check; its own end-of-unit assessment
  falls in week 20 and unit 5's in week 31, unit assessments, not course-level checks. Overlaps with other after-core
  subjects' check weeks are the lead's calendar pass (For the lead 1).
- **Standard.** The 19 E3 ids of the 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards, all taught here (STANDARD.md
  section 2): E3-ALG-PS-01, E3-ALG-ML-02, E3-ALG-IM-03, E3-PRO-PD-04, E3-PRO-PD-05, E3-PRO-VD-06, E3-PRO-RD-07,
  E3-PRO-TR-08, E3-DAT-DC-09, E3-DAT-DI-10, E3-DAT-IM-11, E3-SYS-HW-12, E3-SYS-SE-13, E3-SYS-NT-14, E3-SYS-IM-15,
  E3-SOC-HI-16, E3-SOC-ET-17, E3-SOC-HU-18, E3-SOC-CE-19. Each owned id's boundary was read in the source store's
  standards PDF for this frame (2026-11-25, S:\CurioClass-sources\computer-science\csta-2026-11-21, not copied or
  quoted), and each is taught at the depth its owner row's notes set, in our own words (STANDARD.md "Depth"); "What
  students learn" says what is out. Plans cite ids and state each action in a short phrase of our own (STANDARD.md
  "Quoting"); the credit line "Standards: 2026 CSTA PK-12 Computer Science Standards (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)" goes on any
  day that leans on a statement. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the computer science bar
  (docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md part 5, items 1 to 9) and the lens (docs/agents/lenses/computer-science.md), and
  reads the partial base unit it adapts for order; the "Check set adds" column names what the writer expects that to

- **Practices and concept areas.** The Units table's fifth column (the C3 column of the core frames) names, for

  CT8 build and plan (units 2, 3), CT9 test, debug and refine (units 3, 4), IC3 explain and give feedback (units 1 to

  from and include different users (units 4, 5), HCD12 keep users informed and in control (units 1, 4, 5), ESR1 weigh
  who gains and who is harmed (units 1, 2, 4, 5), ESR2 respect people, credit others' work and protect information
  (units 1, 3). Dispositions are named in the unit plans, not here.

  (raspberrypi.org/curriculum/archived/key-stage-2, resources dated 2023), the same fixed edition the kindergarten and

  no product or company name, our own browser editor or paper in place of the named block and turtle languages and the
  named data loggers. Three of its six Year 4 units feed units 1, 3 and 4 (see Source map); its two creating-media
  units and its text-turtle unit are left out. Units 2 (but for one Year 3 lesson pair) and 5 are our own. The
  source's name appears only in a day's credit line, once a day.

  Source map below rests on the public unit pages (lesson titles, opened 2026-11-25; the lessons' content is read from

- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md (row "teaching day") defines them, in computer science's words (APPROACH.md):
  Understanding is an unplugged or trace session, the first teaching of a construct or idea (a step-card algorithm
  with a yes or no question and two paths, acted out by a partner; a given block program traced and predicted before
  it runs; a trace-table filled by hand). Fluency is a modify session: change one card or one block of a working
  algorithm or program and check it; grade 3 has a little of it (unit 2 session 2, unit 3 session 2, and the
  retrieval openings). Application is a make, design or society session: write a step-card algorithm for a game
  (unit 2 session 3), build it as a block program and improve it from feedback (unit 3 sessions 3 and 4), debug
  given programs with the method (unit 3 session 7), design a fair class survey (unit 4 session 3), or compare two
  algorithms by their effect on people (unit 2 session 4). Check is the end-of-unit assessment or a course-level
  check; Respond acts on it by error letter. Inside a unit: understanding before modify before make, and no make day
  before a trace or modify day on the same construct.
- **Evidence.** Grades 3 to 5 write (APPROACH.md, "The form of daily evidence"): a trace or predict session ends in a
  written exit ticket with a short program or algorithm and "what happens?", wrong answers sorted by error letter; a
  modify or make session's evidence is the program or algorithm itself against two or three look-fors, plus the
  student's line "what I tested and what I changed"; an unplugged session's evidence is the paper artifact with one
  written question; a society session's is a short written claim with a reason. The teacher reads the open part for a
  rotating third of the class each session, so every student is read at least once in three weeks. Every end-of-unit
  assessment is a sitting with unseen items, never the unit's own anchor: a written paper task of about 25 minutes
  (read and trace items, a short debug task, a sort or a short written claim), with a scribe, read-aloud or spoken
  answer for any child who needs it (APPROACH.md, check accommodations). Each owned id has its own look-for: a
  "create", "develop" or "design" id (E3-ALG-PS-01, E3-PRO-PD-04, E3-DAT-IM-11) is met by the child's own algorithm,
  program or plan, never by recognition of a supplied one; "use feedback" (E3-PRO-PD-05) by the child's record of a
  suggestion taken and the change made; "debug" (E3-PRO-TR-08) by finding and fixing a bug; "identify" (E3-PRO-VD-06)
  by naming the variables and filling a trace-table; "articulate", "explain", "describe" and "examine" ids by a short
  written answer with a reason. Unit 3's end-of-unit assessment pairs the sitting with the pair program built and
  improved in sessions 3 and 4 (the draft and the feedback revision are counted in the unit's days), scored on day
  20 against its look-fors and each child's own line. Units 2 and 4 are assessed in the course-level check that
  follows them (Days). No score comes from a session that first teaches a row. The respond day reteaches by error
  letter and repeats the missed item in a new form.
- **Devices.** One device per pair is the default on a device day, never needed per child. Device days: unit 1
  session 1 (one task traced from input, through the software, to the screen) and session 2 on one screen (a
  teacher-led search and a pictured video call), unit 3 sessions 1 to 4 and 7 (the block editor with the
  block-and-text-view and a step-through run), with sessions 5 and 6 on a device or on printouts, unit 4 session 2 (a
  graph tool in pairs or on one screen) and session 4's optional one-screen sorter. That leaves about three quarters
  of the year's time away from a device, more than APPROACH.md's "about half" for grades 3 to 5 (ours, untested): the
  E3 rows for data, systems and society are written unplugged first, and the boundaries of E3-DAT-DC-09 and
  E3-DAT-DI-10 do not ask for technology; the programming unit carries the device time. Every device day is written
  four ways (STANDARD.md "Devices and rooms"): one device per pair, a few devices at stations with the other pairs on
  the paper version of the same idea, one screen with a child at the device for each step, or no device. Which ids
  need a device: the E3-PRO-PD-04 boundary places the student's code in a block-based environment, so on the
  no-device path, where paper block cards are laid and a partner runs them as the computer, the course record marks
  E3-PRO-PD-04 "part met without a device" (an item-level pick, For the lead 4). Every other E3 id is met in full on
  paper: printed programs with their text view, traced with a pencil and a trace-table, meet E3-PRO-VD-06,
  E3-PRO-RD-07 and E3-PRO-TR-08; feedback on a paper program meets E3-PRO-PD-05; the class acting as the sorter meets
  E3-ALG-ML-02, whose boundary asks students to observe, not to train.
- **Paper path.** Every session runs on paper, cards and objects; unit 3's block programs have paper block cards and
  a printed block-and-text-view beside them, and unit 4's sorter is the class itself acting as the machine. No tool
  asks for a student account, stores a child's name or sends anything off the device; the search in unit 1 is
  teacher-led on one screen (STANDARD.md "Privacy and safety").
- **Reading level and vocabulary.** Grade 3 children read short texts; code read alone is ten lines at most
  (APPROACH.md, Reading load), and every block carries an icon and a short word. The block-and-text-view shows the
  text beside the blocks to be read, never typed (the E3-PRO-PD-04 boundary keeps text-based languages out). Each unit
  names five to eight words taught in depth, with the everyday words that have a computing meaning taught as two
  meanings (if, else, loop, bug, cloud, variable). The word variable is first used in unit 3 session 6, built on grade
  2's named box; assignment is read as "take what is in score and put one more into it", never "score equals score
  plus 1". Sentence frames: "If ___, then ___, else ___", "I predict ___ because line ___ says ___" and "This part
  makes ___ happen; without it ___". A child may answer a trace in numbers, arrows or the home language.
- **Swap or leave out.** STANDARD.md section 3 lets a school swap or leave out a named unit for its community's
  reasons. Unit 5 may be left out: E3-SOC-CE-19, E3-SOC-HI-16 and E3-SOC-HU-18 are then not claimed that year, and the
  course still teaches computing and society through E3-SOC-ET-17 and E3-SYS-IM-15 in unit 1 and E3-ALG-IM-03 in
  unit 2; its cases may instead be swapped for workers and tools of the school's own community (the same three ids,
  one case set a session). Unit 4's survey topic and data tables may be swapped for other neutral class ones, not left
  out, so the course always claims the data ids (the bar part 5 fails a course with nothing on data). Unit 1's
  pretend pages may be swapped for others on the same details. Units 2 and 3 carry the step-card and block-program
  models the rest of the year and grade 4 use and are not swapped or left out. A school that leaves out a unit names
  in the course page and the unit's family note the ids not claimed that year.
- **Safety.** No lab or field work, so no laboratory or field safety guide applies. Unit 1's message act-out moves
  children through a cleared room at a walk, passing a paper card hand to hand; device cords stay behind the table and
  devices are carried with two hands. The scope and sequence copies these lines into the act-out and each device day.
- **Costs.** Provisional, for a class of 30, beyond the school's own devices: unit 1 $4 (printing hardware and
  software cards, system-diagram and network-diagram mats, the paper pretend pages and benefit-risk charts), unit 2 $4
  (step-card sets with choice cards, the school-grounds route sheet), unit 3 $10 (printing the paper block cards with
  if and else cards, block-and-text-view printouts, trace-table sheets and feedback forms), unit 4 $6 (data-table and
  graph sheets, survey sheets, the sorter's picture card sets), unit 5 $3 (case cards and timeline strips), about $27
  in all.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md);
  no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Unit 1 has online-safety and personal-information content: the
  agent checks the sharing section carries health's "If a student tells you something" steps word for word
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md; APPROACH.md, Sensitive ground), that no card asks for a child's real name, screen
  name, school or street, and that no task asks what devices, games or accounts a child has at home; as background
  it reads the Federal Trade Commission's consumer advice pages on protecting kids online (consumer.ftc.gov, the page
  the agent records). Units 2 and 4 touch disability and language (a classmate who needs more time, a step-free route,
  a survey everyone can answer in more than one language and by more than one way of answering): before it reads, the
  agent studies a starting pack: the National Center on Disability and Journalism's Disability Language Style Guide
  (ncdj.org/style-guide/), the Disability Rights Education and Defense Fund's pages (dredf.org), an organization led
  by disabled people, and, for families who speak languages other than English, Colorín Colorado's family pages
  (colorincolorado.org); it widens the pack to the brief's five sources, two of them owned by the communities named,
  and records them. Unit 4 also touches data about people: the agent checks every survey collects no name or
  identifying detail, carries an opt-out box, and that no table is about children, homes, food or bodies. Unit 5
  touches families' work: the agent checks no child is asked about a family member's job and that the cases show many
  kinds of work and workers, rural and town. The course's checks get the answer-key agent's read. No unit is hands-on
  in the laboratory sense, so no safety agent read is planned beyond the Safety lines above, which the sensitivity
  read confirms are on each day.
