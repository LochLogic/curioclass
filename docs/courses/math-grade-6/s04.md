# Unit 4: Dividing fractions

Course `math-grade-6` (Math Grade 6). **Days 60 to 79** (20 course days; school days 60 to 79, weeks 12 to 16). Sections: 4.1 (days 60 to 62); 4.2 (days 63 to 67); 4.3 (days 68 to 71); 4.4 (days 72 to 75); 4.5 (days 76 to 79). Days by kind: understanding 9, fluency 1, application 7, check 2, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

Teaching arcs are days 60 to 64, division and groups; days 65 to 68, fractional groups and shares; days 69 to 73, reciprocals and lengths; and days 74 to 79, fractional volumes and applications.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How many 3/4-cup servings are in 2/3 of a cup?"

**Hook, day 60:** Paper strip task, invented for practice: a strip is 4 meters long. Predict how many half-meter pieces and how many 8-meter pieces it contains, allowing partial pieces. Try for five minutes, then pose the compelling question. Revisit its fractional serving on day 66.
**Supporting questions:** What is unknown (day 61)? What counts as one group (day 64)? How can an answer be less than one (day 66)? Why multiply by the reciprocal (day 69)? How does a cube count become a volume (day 74)?
**Closing claim:** We can explain fraction quotients with models and multiplication, then use them to find lengths and pack prisms. The day 76 context comparison, day 77 box plan and day 78 prompts supply evidence.
**Teaching moves:** Label the whole before partitioning. Model both missing-factor meanings on day 61; step back on day 68. On day 69, derive multiplying by the denominator then dividing by the numerator, including mixed-number conversion. Teacher pages explain nonzero divisors and distinguish cube edge, cube volume and cube count.


**Practices:** On day 76, MP1 develops through the sharing, serving and land-width comparison: identify the unknown, choose a model and revise an unreasonable result. MP2 develops through moving between labeled quantities, division expressions and multiplication checks. Retain both in the box plan.
**Fluency:** Neither owned standard specifies expected fluency. Day 70 practises the algorithm explained on day 69, with mixed numbers, feedback and retries, without time pressure. Keep nine understanding, one fluency and seven application days, including day 77 evidence.
**Limits:** Use nonnegative fractions and positive divisors. Negative-number arithmetic, complex rates and nonrectangular prisms wait for grade 7. Triangles retrieve unit 1 rather than claim another owned standard.
**Carried:** Keep unit 3's tapes and number lines; set aside paired lines and tables. Tie tapes to missing-factor equations on day 61, strips to common-unit division on day 63, and aligned number lines to tape lengths on day 65. Withdraw supplied strips on day 65 and supplied tapes and lines on day 70. Tie area models to missing-width equations on day 72 and cube layers to counts and cube volume on day 74, and to V = lwh and V = Bh on day 75; withdraw supplied grids on day 76. Student drawings and access supports remain. One major-led unit; fourteen of sixteen main instructional lessons are major work, 87.5 percent. Major work takes 700 of 790 instructional minutes, 88.6 percent. Days 74 and 75 use geometry to practice fraction division when finding missing edges, without counting their geometry minutes as major.

Each exit is one new question, with at most two parts. Do not use its given fraction pair or answer in instruction, wrong examples or practice. - **E60, day 60, 6.NS.A.1:** Without calculating exactly, compare 5 ÷ (1/5) and 5 ÷ 10 with 1. Explain using group size. ## U7. Talk and writing

Ask what one group means on day 64, why the reciprocal restores the dividend on day 69, and why a volume quotient need not give a feasible packing on day 77. Day 76's explanation labels quantities, justifies division and checks the result against its context.

## U8. Practice that returns

Openings retrieve taught ratios, percent and fraction reasoning. Named retrieval or error response replaces generic practice. Days 72 and 73 use only their reserved response opening. Day 70 compares efficient taught methods, with feedback and fresh retries rather than speed scoring.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach dividend, divisor, quotient, reciprocal and cubic unit through labeled examples. Offer: "One group is ___; the unknown is ___." Read contexts aloud; independent reading is a short prompt and diagram labels. Enlarge strips and grids; provide tactile folds, precut pieces or a scribe. Extend by explaining equivalent quotients or comparing cube sizes for the same prism, within the owned standards.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, use five opening, ten modeling, twenty independent, ten synthesis and five exit minutes. Replace partner talk with explanation to the adult. Keep full assessment sittings and later written revision. On day 79, retain the revised plan and changed retry. With one projector, everyone draws on paper; a printed answer key replaces the screen.
