Course `math-grade-6` (Math Grade 6). **Days 19 to 39** (21 course days; school days 19 to 39, weeks 4 to 8). Teaching arcs are days 19 to 29, ratio language and double number lines, and days 30 to 39, tables, comparisons and totals.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does it mean for two mixtures to be the same?"

**Hook, day 19:** Collection comparison, invented for practice: show paper collections of four triangles with six circles and eight triangles with twelve circles. Ask what changed and what might count as the same mixture. Accept informal descriptions, then pose the compelling question. Use five minutes before retrieval.
**Supporting questions:** What does the order name (day 19)? Why does doubling preserve a mixture (day 21)? Why does adding equally fail (day 23)? What does an aligned pair mean (day 24)? Which representation helps most (day 34)? How does the total determine each part (day 35)?
**Closing claim:** We can describe, scale and compare ratios, then choose a representation to solve a missing-part or total problem. The day 37 paper-supply estimate and day 38 short prompts check this claim.
**Teaching moves:** Model labels before calculation and connect each picture to its numerical pair. Contrast multiplying both quantities with adding equally on day 23. Step back on day 34 while students request missing information and defend their method. Teacher notes explain equal tape values, aligned distances with different scales, and part-to-part versus part-to-whole relationships.


**Practices:** On day 34, MP2 develops through a missing-quantity card: explain what each number counts and check the answer in context. MP5 develops through choosing a representation and explaining its advantage. Day 37 retains both decisions.
**Fluency:** No owned standard sets an expected fluency. Days 25 and 32 practice already taught scaling methods with feedback and retries, without time pressure. Retain eight understanding, two fluency and seven application days, plus two checks and two responses.
**Limits:** Whole-number measurements in equivalent-ratio tables; simple fractions may express an amount for one. No cross-multiplication procedure, slope terminology, percent or unit conversion. Unit 3 completes 6.RP.A.2 and 6.RP.A.3b to d; parent 6.RP.A.3 stays partial here. Early per-one work is diagnostic.
**Carried:** Keep unit 1's array for grouped counts, tying it to colon notation on day 19; withdraw supplied arrays on day 23. Area models and nets remain retrieval supports. Tie tapes to labeled pairs on day 20; withdraw supplied tapes on day 23, rebuild unknown equal parts on day 35, then withdraw supplied partitions on day 36. Tie number lines and double number lines to scaled pairs on day 24; remove prelabelled lines on day 25 and supplied lines on day 30. Tie tables to paired multiplications on day 31; remove supplied rows on day 34. Tie coordinates to table pairs on day 33; remove supplied axes on day 36. Student drawings and access supports remain. This is one major-led unit: all 16 instructional days contain major work, with 768 of 800 minutes, 96 percent. The remaining 32 minutes retrieve supporting area formulas. On day 30, compare areas of equal-height rectangles to connect area retrieval to doubled quantities.

Each exit is one fresh question, with at most two parts, answered independently in five minutes, except E39's eight minutes. Reserve these cases for exits; teaching uses different quantities. ## U7. Talk and writing

Ask why equal addition fails on day 23, what aligned ticks mean on day 24, and why a tape reveals a total on day 35. Day 34's written explanation names its assumption, both quantities, the common multiplier and the representation's advantage.

## U8. Practice that returns

On days 34 and 35 the ratio minutes retrieve reading a plotted pair. Daily openings use three minutes of taught ratio or rate work and two of area formulas. On day 19, ratio minutes retrieve grade 5 scaling. Named retrieval replaces generic examples. On assessment days, record misses for the respond day without reteaching.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach quantity, ratio, equivalent, per and total with labeled diagrams. Offer: "For every ___, there are ___." Read contexts aloud; independent reading is one short prompt and its labels. Use large grids, high-contrast outlines and pattern labels. Offer a scribe or spoken explanation while retaining independent mathematical choices. Extend within the standard: compare two valid methods or find another whole-number pair, rather than introducing cross multiplication.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, use a 35-minute teaching path: five opening, ten modeling, ten independent work, five synthesis and five exit minutes. Replace partner talk with explaining to the adult; preserve assessment sittings and written revision. On day 39, retain the revised product and retry together. With one projector, display one model while all children draw on paper. A printed answer key replaces the screen.
