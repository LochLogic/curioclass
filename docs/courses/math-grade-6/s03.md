## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "When is a "better deal" really better?"
**Hook, day 40:** Job record, invented for practice: a crew packs eight empty crates in twelve minutes. How long for twenty-four crates at that rate? Let students try for five minutes, then ask the compelling question. An amount per panel makes the prediction possible.
**Supporting questions:** Why does a smaller unit give a larger count (day 42)? What does each reciprocal rate mean (day 45)? When is the lower unit price useful (day 48)? What represents 100 percent (day 51)? Which quantity is unknown (day 56)?
**Closing claim:** We can compare deals in common units and use a rate or percentage to find a missing quantity. The day 48 comparison, day 57 paint plan and day 58 remaining prompts show this.
**Teaching moves:** Model quantity labels before operations. Connect conversion factors to paired measurements on days 41 and 42, before independent practice. Contrast reciprocal meanings on day 45. Step back for representation choice on day 48. Teacher notes distinguish a ratio from its quotient, require nonzero divisors, and identify the percent reference whole.

- **E41, day 41, 6.RP.A.3d:** A string is 100 centimetres long and a paper clip is about 2.5 centimetres long. - **E43, day 43, 6.RP.A.3d:** Given 1 metre = 100 centimetres, convert (a) 3.5 metres to centimetres and (b) 980 centimetres to metres, showing unit-labelled ratio reasoning. Answers: 350 centimetres and 9.8 metres. Wrong: 98,000 metres (C).

- **E45, day 45, 6.RP.A.2 and 6.RP.A.3b:** A machine prints fourteen cards in nine minutes. What are both unit rates, and what does the one in each description represent? Answer: 14/9 cards per minute, for one minute; 9/14 minute per card, for one card. Wrong: 9/14 cards per minute (A).

Teach per, unit rate, reciprocal, percent, part and whole with labels. Offer: "For each one ___, there are ___" and "___ represents 100 percent." Read contexts aloud; independent reading is one short prompt and labels. Enlarge diagrams and supply tactile strips or a scribe. Extend within the standard by comparing two methods or constructing another equivalent ratio, without new finance content.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, use five opening, ten modelling, ten independent, five synthesis and five exit minutes; replace partner talk with explanation to the adult. Preserve assessment sittings and later written revision. On day 59, keep the revised plan and changed retry. With one projector, everyone draws on paper; a printed answer key replaces the screen.
