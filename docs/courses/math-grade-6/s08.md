## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What is typical, and how much do the answers vary?"

**Hook, day 142:** Measure assorted teacher-supplied paper strips. Would one length describe them all? Ask the compelling question before sorting questions and gathering the lengths.
**Supporting questions:** What makes a question statistical (142)? What does grouping hide (145)? Why does equal sharing give the mean (147)? Can equal means hide different spreads (150)? Which center fits (152)?
**Closing claim:** We can answer a statistical question with an accurate display and a contextual account of center, variability and shape. The Paper-strip report on day 155 and short prompts on 156 establish this claim.
**Teaching moves:** Model equal intervals on 143, distance from the mean on 149 and quartiles on 153. Step back for distribution comparisons on 146 and center arguments on 152. Teacher pages explain why grouping loses exact values, why distances cannot cancel and why quartiles need ordered data.


**Practices:** MP3: day 152, challenge a claim that the mean always describes a typical strip, using an extreme value. MP4: day 155, the Paper-strip report answers a question with a chosen display and justified measures.
**Balance and fluency:** Follow nine understanding and five application days, one check and one respond day. No owned standard sets fluency. Untimed openings maintain earlier algorithms. **Focus:** zero of one units and zero of thirteen unassessed lessons are major-led. Major practice occupies 26 of 650 instructional minutes, 4 percent. Exclude days 155 to 157; do not count statistics as major work.
**Limits:** One numerical variable, small data sets and descriptive conclusions only. No sampling inference, probability, standard deviation or formal outlier test. Use distance, without signed subtraction. No personal disclosures or added finance task.
**Carried:** Keep unit 7's number line; set aside its coordinate plane and area model. Tie equal intervals to values on 143 and histogram boundaries on 145. Tie dots to frequencies on 143, equal sharing to the mean on 147, distances to mean absolute deviation on 149, and quartiles to box plots on 154. Withdraw the teacher's modeled description of a dot plot on 144 (students write their own from supplied plots); students draw their own dot plots from 143. Withdraw histogram bars on 146, counters on 148 and box outlines on 155. Students retain self-drawn axes and access supports. From day 144 every printed data card gives its attribute, how it was measured and its units, and each written answer ends with the units, so 6.SP.B.5a and 6.SP.B.5b stay in use until 154.
