## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "Where does the decimal point go, and how do we know?"
**Hook, day 82:** Stall prices, invented for practice: a notebook costs $2.65 and a pencil pack costs $1.48. Is $5 enough for one of each? Estimate first; revisit the exact total after drawing base-ten exchanges. Pose the compelling question before mixed practice.
**Supporting questions:** Why align equal places (day 82)? How do factor units determine product units (day 84)? What does each quotient digit mean (day 89)? Why scale both division terms equally (day 92)?
**Closing claim:** We can choose, explain and independently use all four decimal algorithms and multi-digit long division. Day 93's measurement plan and day 94's short prompts check this claim.
**Teaching moves:** Label the whole before drawing. Model exchanges on day 82, connect partial products on day 85, and let students explain repeated division steps on day 89. Step back for fluency and independent evidence. Teacher pages explain why exchanges preserve value and why equal nonzero scaling preserves a quotient.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask why equal places align on day 82, where a tens partial product appears on day 85, and what repeats in division on day 89. Day 89's written annotation must name the place unit, match subtraction to groups removed and explain the next exchange.

## U8. Practice that returns

Openings retrieve taught ratios, percent and fraction division for five minutes. Named response replaces generic practice when needed. Fluency practice mixes taught number lengths, uses immediate feedback and revisits misses on fresh examples. No countdown timer is shown.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach dividend, divisor, quotient, exchange and partial product through labelled examples. Offer: "This digit means ___ because its place is ___." Read contexts aloud; independent reading is a short prompt and diagram labels. Provide enlarged or tactile blank grids, a scribe or oral explanations without algorithm cues. Extend within the standards by comparing equivalent calculations and diagnosing a misplaced zero.

## U10. Two rooms

On day 95 retain initial attempts, revisions and the fresh retry. With one projector everyone calculates on paper; a printed answer key replaces the screen.
