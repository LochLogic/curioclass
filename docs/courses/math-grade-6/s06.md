## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How can one equation describe a whole situation?"

**Hook, day 96:** Ribbon task, invented for practice: one ribbon has an unknown length plus 6 centimeters, totalling 30 centimeters; another has six equal pieces totalling 30 centimeters. Draw both tapes. Why do their unknown pieces differ? Pose the compelling question before mixed practice.
**Supporting questions:** What makes a candidate a solution (day 97)? What stays equal (day 98)? When are expressions equivalent (day 104)? What does an exponent touch (day 109)? Which quantity depends on which (day 112)?
**Closing claim:** We can represent quantities with letters, solve one-step equations, justify equivalent expressions and connect a relationship across an equation, table and graph. Days 115 and 116 check this through the pay comparison and independent prompts.
**Teaching moves:** Model equal operations on day 98 and partitioned rectangles on day 105. Step back on days 99, 106 and 110. Teacher pages explain variable domains, why equal operations preserve equality, why properties prove equivalence and how order conventions differ from properties.


**Practices:** MP7: day 101 parses 2(8 + 7) as a product whose second factor is a sum; day 107 factors 12x + 20 using rectangle structure. MP3: day 111 critiques 3 × 4^2 = (3 × 4)^2 using repeated factors, obtaining 48 and 144.
**Fluency and balance:** No owned standard specifies fluency. Keep thirteen understanding, three fluency and three application days, plus two checks and one respond day. Days 99, 106 and 110 practice taught methods with feedback and fresh retries. Day 105 expands with numbers, then one letter as a bridge; day 106 teaches letters from area expressions, then practices. Earlier algorithm fluency continues without speed scoring.
**Limits:** Nonnegative rational values; one-step equations only. Divisors are nonzero. Supplied inequalities are tested, not generated or graphed. Exponents are positive whole numbers on whole-number, fractional and decimal bases. Signed arithmetic, two-step equations and exponent laws wait for later grades.
**Carried:** Retain unit 5's area model; set aside its blocks and place-value chart. Restore tapes on day 96 beside equations, revisit on days 99 to 102, then withdraw supplied tapes on day 103. Tie hangers to equations on day 98; students annotate equal operations on day 99; withdraw supplied hangers that day. Tie rectangles to equivalent expressions on day 104, partition on day 105, withdraw on day 106, restore for factoring on day 107, then withdraw. Reuse a square on day 108 beside powers; withdraw on day 109. On day 112 tie the ratio table and extended input-output table to equations; withdraw supplied ratio tables on day 113 and input-output tables on day 115. Tie coordinates to equations on day 113; withdraw supplied axes on day 115. Student drawings and access supports remain.
**Focus:** One of one units and eighteen of eighteen instructional lessons are major-led, both 100 percent. Major work takes 842 of 890 instructional minutes, 94.6 percent; days 103 and 115 to 117 and the response openings on days 104 and 105 are excluded. No supporting cluster is owned. Cube formulas on day 110 use supporting geometry to practice major expression evaluation.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach variable, solution, equivalent, coefficient, base, exponent, dependent and independent through labeled examples. Offer: "For every allowed input, these match because ___." Read short contexts aloud; students independently read symbols and diagram labels. Provide enlarged or tactile blank models, a scribe or oral explanation without mathematical cues. Extend by finding two different property arguments for equivalence, within the same standards.
