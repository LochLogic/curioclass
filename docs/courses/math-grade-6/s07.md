## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "What does a number less than zero mean?"

**Hook, day 120:** Thermometers, invented for practice: mark 4 degrees above zero and 4 degrees below zero. Why does the numeral 4 alone not locate both readings? Pose the compelling question first.
**Supporting questions:** What does zero mean (120)? Why can a greater distance mean a smaller number (126)? How many solutions (128)? Which sign changes under reflection (132)? Which common factor finishes the factoring (138)?
**Closing claim:** We can interpret signed quantities, distinguish order from magnitude, graph solution sets, solve coordinate problems and choose common factors or multiples. The coordinate product on day 139 and independent prompts on day 140 supply evidence.
**Teaching moves:** Model positions on day 121, inequality rays on 127 and split rectangles on 138. Step back for explanations on 123, 128 and 135. Teacher pages explain contextual zero, rational points between integers, magnitude versus order, reflection and complete factoring.


**Practices:** MP2: day 126 translates fictional balances of -18 and -7 dollars into order and debt magnitudes, then interprets both comparisons. MP6: day 134 labels common coordinates, lengths and units to justify distances across zero and on one side.
**Balance and fluency:** Retain twelve understanding, one fluency and six application days, plus two checks and one respond day. No owned standard specifies fluency. Day 123 practices already taught comparisons through sorting, feedback and retries without speed scoring.
**Limits:** No signed arithmetic, diagonal distance, two-step inequalities or factor-tree requirement. Greatest common factor inputs are positive whole numbers at most 100; least common multiple inputs are at most 12. Inclusive boundaries are interpreted when supplied. 3.MD.B.3 is outgoing readiness, never a unit score.

## U7. Talk and writing

Ask what zero names on 120, why order and debt size differ on 126, and why an axis reflection changes just one coordinate on 132. Day 134's written explanation names the shared coordinate, zero's position and the resulting length with units.

## U8. Practice that returns

Ordinary openings use two minutes of ratio or fraction reasoning and three of standard algorithms. Rotate whole-number division and all four decimal operations across five days. Give feedback without countdowns. Response replaces openings on 131, 132 and 141.

## U9. Supports and extension

Teach opposite, magnitude, boundary, quadrant, factor and multiple through labeled drawings. Offer: "The number is ___, but its distance is ___." Read contexts aloud; students read symbols and short labels. Offer enlarged grids, raised axes, oral reasoning and a scribe without mathematical cues. Extend by constructing different polygons with matching horizontal and vertical lengths.

## U10. Two rooms

At home, replace partner talk with explanation to the adult, using the same paper tasks and assessment budgets. Preserve feedback and later revision. With one projector, display examples and the answer key; students keep individual paper evidence. Day 141 retains the ten-minute readiness slot in both rooms.
