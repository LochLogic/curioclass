# Course frame: Dance, grades 3 to 5

Course `dance-grades-3-to-5` (catalog id `dance-3-5`), a band course for grade 3, grade 4 and grade 5, run as three years one after another (year 3, year 4, year 5). Draft frame, written 2026-11-25; the scope and sequence sets the days later, one year at a time, and each year's part of this file is that year's frame (data/courses.csv, "one frame per grade year").

## The course in brief

- **Units.** 12: four in each year. Every year has the same four-unit shape, so a thread comes back one step
  harder a year later in the same place: a body, space and rhythm unit (technique and the dance elements), a
  sources and settings unit (ideas and connecting), a form and response unit (the shape of a dance and responding
  to dances), and a performance unit that revises the year's dance and stages it for families (revising and the
  stage). The pairing differs from Dance, grades K to 2 on purpose: in grades 3 to 5 the connecting rows ask for
  research that feeds a dance (DA:Cn10.1.3b, DA:Cn10.1.4b, DA:Cn10.1.5b), so they sit with the ideas rows; the
  revising rows ask for floor maps, relationship sketches and change logs, so they sit with the stage rows, where
  the map and the stage plan are one job. Each unit joins two progression threads, and every unit dances, watches
  another artist's dance and shows (bar criteria 4 and 5). Units 1, 5 and 9 claim only performing ids; they touch
  responding through the dances they watch and creating through the improvisations the class makes, so every unit
  touches at least two artistic processes.
- **Days.** 192: 64 a year for three years. Each year: 60 days in its four units, 2 course-level check days (one
  mid-year, one year-end) and 2 respond days, one after each check. One session of 40 minutes, two sessions a week,
  32 weeks a year (STANDARD.md 3.4: a K to 8 arts year is 64 course days; it never needs a daily period, bar
  criterion 9). The 40 minutes hold the set-up (tables pushed back, the floor checked and the spots marked, about 3
  minutes), a warm-up (about 5), the teaching and dancing, a cool-down and the closing line (about 4) and putting the
  room back and collecting cards and props (about 3); a readiness or retrieval task takes ten minutes of its own on day 2, after the warm-up, taken from the teaching time (day 2: set-up 3, warm-up 5, retrieval 10, teaching 15, cool-down 4, room back 3). Each unit's
  days include its end-of-unit assessment (and, in units over 25 days, its mid-unit assessments; no unit here is
  over 25 days) and one respond day after it. Unit counts follow the rows' load: years 3 and 4 give 16 days to each
  of the first three units (six, five and six ids, each with two check days) and 12 to the performance unit (four
  ids, and its dance already made in the unit before). Year 5 gives 17 to unit 9 (a run of phrases rehearsed as an
  ensemble against goals set from feedback, which needs a second rehearsal cycle) and 17 to unit 10 (material from
  several sources plus a group dance on another subject's topic with a comparison), and 14 to unit 11, whose tools
  and forms return from grades 3 and 4, so its study builds faster. These are course days: the school calendar
  (docs/courses/CALENDAR.md) spreads them two a week, so course day n of a year falls in that year's week n / 2,
  rounded up. National time figure (STANDARD.md 3.4): the dance educators' association's opportunity-to-learn
  standards (2017 revision, posted on nationalartsstandards.org) ask for at least 90 minutes of dance a week in
  grades 3 to 5. This course gives 80 minutes a week, 10 short of it, and 40 on the once-a-week path, 50 short. A
  school can add time. The course is written so a classroom teacher can teach it; a school with a dance specialist
  uses that teacher.
- **The once-a-week path.** A school that meets once a week keeps every unit and every id in 32 sessions a year by
  merging the days each unit marks (STANDARD.md 3.4). Each unit keeps, in this order and no fewer: the
  demonstration with a step card, opened by the watch of a dance that carries the unit's response and its connecting
  sentence; one rehearsal day with the critique; the showing (the check); one respond day. Fluency days fold into
  the warm-ups, and the two rows' understanding days merge into one. Sessions on that path: years 3 and 4, units of
  8, 8, 7 and 5 sessions; year 5, units of 9, 8, 6 and 5; each year's two checks keep 1 check session and 1 respond
  session. The check sessions fall in the same weeks as on the full path (weeks 17 and 26 in years 3 and 4; weeks 18
  and 26 in year 5); the respond sessions fall one week later (weeks 18 and 27 in years 3 and 4; weeks 19 and 27 in
  year 5). A respond session is not a check day; in grade 4, week 27 also holds social studies' year-end check, and
  the respond session asks no test of any child. A merged day never drops a critique, a rehearsal before a showing
  or a respond day; the scope and sequence marks which days merge. Unit 12's five sessions run: 1, the works
  studied, the staging changes shown and the adapting begun; 2, adapting, the critique and the changes rehearsed in
  the same session; 3, the first venue's showing and check; 4, the second venue's showing and check; 5, respond. On
  this merged path the rehearsal follows the critique inside session 2; the next-day rehearsal in Day kinds
  describes the full path.
- **Standard.** National Core Arts Standards, 2014, dance performance standards, by id only, each named with its
  line in data/standards/ncas-performance-standards-words.txt, never quoted (STANDARD.md 2). The band-year rule
  binds (STANDARD.md 3.1): year 3 claims only the 21 grade 3 ids (`.3a`, `.3b`, `.3c`), year 4 only the 21 grade 4
  ids, year 5 only the 21 grade 5 ids: 63 in all, the count in STANDARD.md 3.2, every one taught and assessed in its
  own year. A year revisits the year before's ids as practice that returns, never as a claim. Every year touches
  all four artistic processes (creating, performing, responding, connecting; bar criterion 1). In the Units table
  the seventh column is headed "Processes, anchors and works": the artistic processes and anchor standards the unit
  works on, and the dances it studies. Each id's action was checked against its words-file line and the row; where a
  row's description is narrower than its ids (seven rows), the unit teaches and checks the full id and the row change
  is listed for the lead. Check set: each unit plan checks itself against the words-file lines of its ids, the nine
  criteria of the arts part of docs/ADOPTION-BAR-SUBJECTS.md and the lens's error list (docs/agents/lenses/arts.md);
  the "Check set adds" column names what that is expected to add. Values rule (DESK-ANSWERS 2026-11-12) holds.
  scripts/standards.py checks every id against the id file; each year's scope and sequence still states its id count
  (21) against STANDARD.md 3.2.
- **Mixed-grade classes.** A school that teaches the band as one mixed class runs the three years in turn (STANDARD.md
  3.1); each year still claims only its grade's ids, so a student's record shows which grade's standards they met.
  The scope and sequence says this once, on its first page.
- **Dance here, motor skill in physical education.** Dance in this course is the art: making, performing,
  responding to and connecting dance. Dance steps, chains and routines as motor skill are physical education's
  (STANDARD.md 6; physical education grades 3 to 5 units 8, 15 and 22). Dance teaches the technique its own artistic
  standards need inside this course and assumes no earlier physical education instruction; it uses the moves a
  student already has (walking, stepping, turning, reaching, small jumps) as material for shapes,
  patterns, studies and dances, and teaches choreography, performing for an audience and response. Units 1, 5 and 9
  name the physical education rows they lean on (Cross-subject, below).
- **Base text.** None. Dance is Curio Class's own (docs/sources/BASE-TEXTS.md; the open-material scout,
  docs/reviews/arts-open-material-2026-11-16.md, found few open dance recordings). Every dance studied here is
  described in our own words and linked to the maker's, the company's, a museum's, an archive's or a cultural
  body's own page, never copied or embedded (APPROACH "Rights"). Each dance named below is a pick the unit plan keeps
  unless its rights line, checked on the item's own page, fails; a swapped dance must keep the unit's ids met and
  the band's range. The band's dances come from Africa (Senegal, South Africa, Guinea), the Americas (the United
  States, Puerto Rico, Brazil, Canada, Martinique through Katherine Dunham's research, and Native nations through
  Dancing Earth and Maria Tallchief of the Osage Nation), Asia (India, Japan, Indonesia, Taiwan), Europe (Ireland,
  Germany, the United Kingdom) and the Pacific (Samoa), from 1922 to today, with living makers or living traditions
  in every year (APPROACH "Works and their settings"). None repeats a Dance, grades K to 2 pick or a physical
  education grades 3 to 5 dance (the Virginia Reel, Alunelul, La Bastringue and its original line dance; its Pilolo
  is a game from Ghana, not a dance). The unseen dance in every
  check is a short Curio Class original danced live by the teacher from a picture step card, or a short written
  description read aloud, never an embed (APPROACH "Scoring creative work"). Curio Class writes every step card,
  work card, challenge card, research card, map sheet, record sheet, family note and every unseen dance.
- **Techniques that get harder (bar criterion 2).** Body and space: a sequence copied with the body aligned, partner
  level and path changes, an improvisation that fills a set count (3); balance in recalled patterns, moving together
  on a cue, a drum part played for others, soft landings (4); a run of phrases rehearsed as an ensemble, body
  percussion and found sound, ground and air paths, tight against flowing (5). Making: a challenge solved, AB and ABA
  forms with repetition and reversal (3); a challenge invented for a partner, a study around one main idea with a
  tool altered (4); material from several sources, several challenges in a group, a study whose tools change together
  (5). Changing and recording: a change after partner feedback and a floor map of paths (3); a rework from comments
  and reflection and a relationship sketch (4); peer suggestions tried and a change log (5). Performing: stage areas
  named and one design choice (3); a gym or lawn planned as a stage and production elements tried (4); one dance
  adapted to two venues (5).
- **Day kinds.** As docs/GLOSSARY.md ("teaching day") and APPROACH "Kinds of day". Understanding: a move pattern, an
  element, a tool or a form shown in named steps with the step card, or a dance watched with the unit's words (the
  artistic process "responding" is taught here, never on a respond day; a connecting id that asks for a tie lives in
  the day that studies a dance, one that asks for research or making lives in an application day and is scored as
  making). Fluency: short repeated practice with feedback (the warm-up, a phrase recalled three times, a rhythm
  drill, a map-symbol drill); mostly the five-minute warm-up and one or two fluency days a unit. Application: making
  and rehearsing a dance with some choice; the second application day holds the unit's one critique (describe, ask,
  suggest), and the next day's rehearsal of the change is application too. Check: a showing scored live on the
  guide, or the written and show items of the unit's evidence line. Respond: a second try at the part marked "not
  yet", or a re-rehearsal before a second showing. A unit runs roughly: understanding, fluency, application,
  fluency, application with the critique, rehearsal, check, respond.
- **Scored evidence.** Every owned id has an end-of-unit item or a look-for, named id by id in the unit's
  "End-of-unit evidence" line. A progression row's "Assessed by" note names one form for the row, not one item for
  each id, so it is never the whole line. Items come in four kinds: a look-for on the showing's guide (three to five
  look-fors, met, partly or not yet), marked live by the teacher as each group dances; a written item on the
  response card, answered by every student at once on an unseen dance (at most three a student on any check day,
  each one to three sentences, read aloud by the teacher); a show item, where a student shows a move at the
  teacher's spot (at most one a student a day); and a kept card (an idea card, a challenge card, research notes, a
  floor map, a sketch, a change log, a stage plan) made on the unit's making days and scored after the session. Two
  kinds of unit check day, never mixed. A showing day: set-up 3 minutes, warm-up 5, six groups of five dance in the
  marked zone for about 4 minutes each (24: about two and a half minutes of dancing and the group's reset) while the
  rest sit in the audience zone and watch (the class is the audience, nothing else is asked of it), cool-down and
  the self-check on the guide 5, room back 3: 40 minutes. A written day: set-up 3, the teacher dances the unseen
  Curio Class phrase or reads the unseen description twice (4), every student answers the written items at their
  seat (15), then six groups of five come to the teacher's spot for about 2 minutes each for the show items (12)
  while the others finish a kept card at their seats, quiet, room back 3: 37 minutes, with slack for a third showing
  of the phrase. Either way the teacher watches one thing at a time; a group the teacher wants to see again may be
  recorded on the school's device that day (feet, hands or the whole body with the family's yes) and the recording
  stays there (APPROACH "Kept work"). Units 1 to 3, 5 to 7, 9 to 11 and 12 have two check days; units 4 and 8 have
  one showing day and kept cards. The rehearsal, its spoken critique with the teacher's look-for marks on the record
  sheet and the re-rehearsal come on earlier days counted in the unit. No score comes from the day a move, a tool or
  an element is first taught. Each unit opens with a baseline look at its main look-for during the first practice
  days (APPROACH "Growth"), so every student has two marks a unit and the growth is the change. One piece a unit
  goes into the portfolio folder (a kept card, a drawing of the student's dance, or a recording kept on the school's
  device) with a one-line written reflection (bar criterion 8).
- **Taking part without dancing alone.** No student dances alone for the class: every showing is in a group of
  five, and a student may dance beside a partner or show at the teacher's spot (APPROACH "Supports"). A student who
  cannot move freely dances a seated or one-limb version, or takes the drummer's, caller's or stage manager's role
  for a section. Each unit plan maps the student's movement version to the look-fors it shows. A drummer's, caller's
  or stage manager's role counts only for what it shows; where it shows nothing of an owned movement action, the
  teacher sees a short movement version with the student's usual supports at the teacher's spot and records the
  support. A student who does not want to be recorded is marked live.
- **Readiness and retrieval.** Every unit assumes the year before's row in the same thread, last used one grade
  back, so it opens with a ten-minute retrieval task on day 2, after the warm-up, never a readiness check, and day 1 stays clean for
  the hook and the question. The count starts from grade 3, with the band's order (3, then 4, then 5) after it.
  Year 3's rows assume Dance, grades K to 2's grade 2 rows (Seams, below). That course is an after-core course a
  school may not have run (lesson 32), so year 3 is a "by course order" seam: each year 3 retrieval task carries one
  ten-minute reteach of both threads' grade 2 steps together, inside the same block, used whenever a student or a class did not take the grade 2
  year, and the unit's first understanding day teaches its grade 3 step from the start. In a mixed class running
  the years in turn, a student may meet year 5 before year 4: each retrieval task then carries its ten-minute
  reteach as written (APPROACH "Readiness and the baseline look").
- **Course-level checks.** Grades K to 8 after-core checks avoid the weeks where docs/courses/CALENDAR.md shows a
  core check for the grade (grade 3: 9, 12, 14, 16, 20, 23, 25, 28, 30 to 32; grade 4: 8 to 10, 12, 16, 19, 21, 23,
  27, 30 to 32; grade 5: 10, 11, 16, 17, 21, 22, 29 to 32). Mid-year: week 17 in years 3 and 4 (course days 33 and
  34 of the year), week 18 in year 5 (days 35 and 36). Year-end: week 26 in every year (days 51 and 52). The weeks
  follow from the unit days by load, not from the pilot's. Weeks 28 to 32 hold most core year-end checks in grades
  3 to 5, so the year-end check comes before the performance unit (CALENDAR.md stagger tool c), and the performance
  unit's family showing, in weeks 27 to 32, is its own end-of-unit assessment. A course-level check day is one job
  at a time: set-up 3, warm-up 2, the written part first (every student seated, 10 minutes), then the showing (six
  groups of five, about 3 minutes each, 18), cool-down with the room put back 5: 38 minutes, 2 spare. A group turn
  is scored on at most two look-fors a student; any other look-for of that check comes from the written part.
- **Leave-out.** A school or family may leave out a unit or a dance for its community's reasons (STANDARD.md 5); each
  unit stands alone. Units that a course page names: unit 3 (kathak grew in part from temple storytelling; the unit
  uses a non-devotional piece, and Irish step dance with Trisha Brown's Accumulation keeps every id met), unit 5
  (Bali also has sacred dances; legong is a dance made for watching, and bomba keeps every id met), and unit 11 (some
  Guinean dances come from ceremonies; the unit uses a staged piece, and Emergence with the class's style cards keeps
  every id met). Swapping a dance keeps the unit's ids met; leaving a unit out means the course page and the unit's
  family note name the ids not claimed that year. A unit that builds on an earlier unit's dance carries a fallback
  for a class that left that unit out: units 4 and 8 revise and stage a short Curio Class original study; unit 11
  takes neutral movement material from a step card when unit 10 was left out; unit 12 adapts a short original study
  when unit 11 was left out. The class learns that material inside the receiving unit's days before feedback,
  revision and assessment; the receiving unit claims only its own ids, and the omitted unit's ids stay unclaimed.
- **The ordinary room.** A class of 30 in a cleared classroom, hall or gym with room for every student to stretch
  both arms out without touching another, floor tape or chalk to mark spots, zones and stage areas, a hand drum or a
  pot and a wooden spoon, the voice and clapping for the beat; nothing must be bought, and every unit names a free
  stand-in (STANDARD.md 3.4, APPROACH "Supplies"). One child at home: the parent is the audience and the critique
  partner, the group showing has a solo-with-parent version (the parent dances the partner part or watches), the
  ensemble parts are danced with the parent or to a recorded count, and the showing is to the family. The teacher
  needs no dance training: each unit's teacher page says what to look for, in plain words, and every unseen dance is
  on a picture step card the teacher can learn in a few minutes.
- **Supplies and cost.** Provisional cost per unit for a class of 30 (consumables, reusable items such as a hand drum
  and scarves counted once, printing of step, work, challenge, research and map cards), all optional beside the free
  path: year 3 $15, $5, $5, $15 ($40); year 4 $10, $5, $5, $10 ($30); year 5 $5, $5, $5, $10 ($25). Planning figures,
  not quotes; any ceiling is under For the lead.
- **Devices.** None per student. One teacher device may play music and show a linked dance; every day runs without
  it, from the printed work card (a picture and a description read aloud), the teacher showing the moves, and the
  voice, claps or a drum for the beat. No owned id needs technology. DA:Pr6.1.3b and DA:Pr5.1.4c name lighting as one
  choice among others; a paper-path class meets them with costume, music, scenery or a held card as the cue, in
  full. DA:Cr3.1.5b allows a video log; words and drawings meet it in full. A paper-path student meets every id in
  full. Kept work stays on paper or on the school's or family's device (APPROACH "Kept work").
- **Paper path.** Every day runs on printed cards, the floor and the body; any Curio Class tool a unit plan adds (a
  beat keeper, a floor map maker, a change log) has a printed version that does the same job and needs no student
  account.
- **Reading load.** A student reads at most one short card alone (APPROACH "Supports"); written items are read aloud
  by the teacher, and a student who cannot yet write an answer may draw it or say it to the teacher, marked the same
  on its content. English learners: each unit teaches its five or six words in depth, with a picture, a movement and
  a frame ("The pattern is ___." "This move shows ___ because ___."), a student may show the word instead of saying
  it, and the check marks what the student danced apart from what they wrote or said (APPROACH "Supports").
- **Safety read.** Every unit is a moving unit, so every unit gets the fresh safety agent's read
  (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md) against two free guides, named here and opened by the agent: the Nova
  Scotia Department of Education's "Physical Education Safety Guidelines, Grades Primary-12" (September 2021,
  https://curriculum.novascotia.ca/sites/default/files/documents/resource-files/Physical%20Education%20Safety%20Guidelines%20%282021%29%20EN.pdf, its dance section on printed page 43), its sections on dance and on the activity space, and the dance educators' association's
  opportunity-to-learn standards (2017 revision, https://nationalartsstandards.org/sites/default/files/NDEO%20Opportunity%20to%20Learn%20Standards%202017%20rev.pdf), its lines on the dance space and floor. Floor rule:
  jumps, landings and stamping use a level, resilient surface the school confirms suitable; a wooden finish over concrete, tile or a
  loose mat on a hard floor is not enough by itself. On an unsuitable floor the class does those moves low and
  without impact, and their look-for is seen in a suitable school space before it is marked met, never from a
  written stand-in.
  Rules for every unit, from APPROACH "Safe use" ("from memory" until that read checks them): a clear floor with room
  to move arms out, checked for spills and objects before each session; bare feet or soft-soled shoes on a clean
  floor, never socks on a smooth floor; no lifts and no weight-sharing; no jumping from heights; small jumps land
  softly through the feet and knees; no inverted moves (no head spins, handstands or flips); partner contact only by
  agreement and never required; the stop cue used every session; props soft and short; drums and found-sound
  rhythms kept at a moderate volume; no flashing light; water and rest offered. Each unit's last column gives its
  hazards and controls in the words days.csv will copy. No student sign-off form.
- **Sensitivity reads.** Every unit gets the fresh sensitivity agent's read (docs/pipeline/briefs/sensitivity-read.md),
  which covers the dances with the rest of the unit; no outside reader is planned (Cory, 2026-11-14). Units with a
  cultural or community tradition name the communities and the sources the agent studies first: unit 1 (Senegal and
  Germaine Acogny's school), unit 2 (Martinique and Katherine Dunham; Native nations and Dancing Earth), unit 3
  (kathak in northern India; Irish step dance), unit 4 (kabuki, Japan), unit 5 (Afro-Puerto Rican bomba; Balinese
  legong), unit 6 (South African mine workers and the gumboot dance), unit 7 (the Black and Latino dancers of
  California who began locking and popping), unit 8 (Maria Tallchief and the Osage Nation), unit 9 (Samoan sasa),
  unit 10 (Pearl Primus and Black sharecroppers; the Lindy hop in Harlem; Taiwan and Cloud Gate), unit 11 (Guinea and
  Les Ballets Africains), unit 12 (frevo in Recife, Brazil). Every task that ties dance to a student's own life (units
  2, 6, 11) offers an invented or public option marked the same (APPROACH "Sensitive ground"). A dance from another
  culture is watched, and its form is learned only from the community's own published teaching or a named
  practitioner's words; no costume, no accent, no imitation of a people, and no sacred or ceremonial dance as an
  exercise or warm-up (the rows' teacher note; Cory, 2026-11-20). No task asks about a student's own diet, weight or
  body: the food-choice ids (DA:Pr5.1.4b, DA:Pr5.1.5b) are studied through an invented dancer (APPROACH "Sensitive
  ground"). Each section carries health's steps for "If a student tells you something"
  (docs/courses/health/APPROACH.md) on its teacher page.
- **Logic model (one page in the scope and sequence).** Inputs: two 40-minute sessions a week, a cleared floor, step
  cards, work cards, a drum or the voice. Activities: moves, elements and tools taught in steps, dances watched with
  their setting, research and sources turned into movement, making and rehearsing with a critique and a change, a
  showing each unit. Outputs: a portfolio folder of 12 pieces over the band with reflections, 12 showings, two marks
  a unit per student on the main look-for. Outcome claimed: met marks on the year's 21 ids and growth on the
  look-fors. Nothing here has been tried with children, and Curio Class makes no claim that arts study raises any
  other result (APPROACH "Sources").

## Seams

### Picked up from Dance, grades K to 2

Each grade 3 row assumes its thread's grade 2 row, last used one grade back in the grade 2 year of Dance, grades K to
2 (band days 129 to 192), so it opens as a retrieval task inside day 2 of its unit, carrying its ten-minute reteach
for a student or class that did not take that year (by course order, lesson 32).

|---|---|---|---|

| Dance elements | da-g2-elements (unit 9, days 129 to 146) | da-g3-elements (unit 1) | Direction, symmetry and strong beats become distance, three dimensions, filled and empty space, and an improvisation that fills a set count |
