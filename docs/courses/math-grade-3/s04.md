## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How are multiplying and dividing related?"

**Hook, day 62:** Invented for practice: arrange 18 paper squares into packets of three. How many packets can we make? Allow eight minutes for attempts, then pose the compelling question before retrieval.
**Supporting questions:** What does the quotient count (day 65)? How can a product find a quotient (day 67)? Why do table patterns continue (day 70)? Why do rectangle parts preserve a product (day 71)?
**Closing claim:** "I can explain division through a missing factor and use properties to find facts." Day 76's Division Stories, Missing Factor, Property Choices, Tiled Rectangle and Table Patterns tasks check this; day 75 records developing fluency.
**Teaching:** Model sharing on day 63 and equation connections on day 67. Step back for story comparisons on day 65 and explanations on day 70. Day 71 connects tiled rectangle splitting to division: divide each partial area by the common side length, then add the quotients to recover the whole length. Within the existing rectangle work, day 72 independently practices split-product and split-dividend equations on ungridded rectangles. Establish zero and one cases on day 67 and the nines strategy on day 70 before day 74 practice.


**Practices:** MP7 develops on day 71 through matching rectangle partitions to equivalent expressions. MP8 develops on day 70 through explaining constant table increments, then predicting another entry and justifying why the rule continues.
**Fluency:** Build 3.OA.C.7 through short, spaced, mixed practice with feedback, privately and without time pressure. Add related quotients from day 68. Maintain 3.NBT.A.2 in five separate minutes of written addition and subtraction each teaching day. Days 69, 72, 74 and 75 practice established ideas.
**Limits:** Use whole numbers within 100, exact quotients and no zero divisor. Practice zero and one factors, division by one and zero divided by a nonzero number. Formal property names are unnecessary. Day 68 continues 3.OA.A.3 and 3.OA.A.4; unit 5 day 90 checks their full context and equation ranges. Carry unit 3's computation evidence unchanged; day 153 updates it. Full fact memory is a year-end expectation.
**Balance:** Understanding receives 480 minutes, fluency 240 and application 120. Major work accounts for one of one units, fourteen of fourteen teaching lessons, and 755 of 840 instructional minutes, 89.9 percent. Daily computation takes 70 additional-work minutes; retrieval takes fifteen more, five each on days 62, 69 and 70. Other minutes develop major work. No supporting cluster is assigned here.
**Carried:** Restore unit 2's array, tie rows to division expressions on day 64, connect missing factors on day 67 and withdraw supplied arrays on day 69. Restore its area model with tiles and equations on day 71, remove grids on day 72 and withdraw supplied rectangles on day 74. Student drawings remain available for explanation and repair; day 76 requires concrete tiling.

**Teacher judgment:** For items 1 to 5, score each requested component one point when its look-for is met, zero otherwise; retain errors for response. Use the following look-fors and samples. Item 6 scores computations separately from its observation categories.

- Item 1 requires both equations, drawings and correct quotient units. Meets: "54 ÷ 6 = 9 cards in each of six folders; 54 ÷ 6 = 9 folders when each holds six cards." Drawings show six groups of nine and nine groups of six. Does not: "Both answers mean nine folders" (A).
- Item 2 requires the missing factor and its connection to division. Meets: "8 × 9 = 72, so 72 ÷ 8 = 9; nine is the missing number of groups." Does not: "Take away eight once to get 64" (B).
- Item 3 requires all three strategies and the division explanation. Meets: "3 × 7 = 21 because turning the array preserves the dots. 2 × (4 × 5) = 40 combines the same factors. 9 × 8 = 45 + 27 = 72; those parts contain five and three nines, so 72 ÷ 9 = 8." Does not: "9 × 8 = 45 + 3 = 48" (E).
- Item 4 requires actual tiling, the full partition and matching area reasoning. Meets: "My 21 squares cover three rows of seven. Four columns make 12 squares and three make nine. Therefore 3 × (4 + 3) = 12 + 9 = 21 square units." Does not: "3 × 4 + 3 = 15" (E). A correct equation without concrete tiling leaves that component unverified.
- Item 5 requires both values, both increments and a property-based explanation for each. Meets: "The addition strip ends at 11 because adding one to an addend adds one to the sum. The multiplication strip ends at 25 because one extra group of five adds five." Does not: "11 and 25, because the patterns say so" (I).

## 6. Error register

| | The error or first idea | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Labels the quotient with the wrong group role. | Confuses number of groups with number in each group. Repair: label the known quantity and circle the unknown on a grouping drawing. | Base lesson 2, comparison synthesis. |
| B | Uses one subtraction to find a quotient. | Counts what remains after one group rather than the groups. Repair: remove equal groups until none remain and count the removals. | From memory. |
| C | Adds the factors instead of multiplying. | Does not treat one factor as a count of equal groups. Repair: draw each group and match the repeated sum to the product. | From memory. |
| D | Changes multiplication to addition when regrouping factors. | Confuses changing parentheses with changing operations. Repair: redraw the same nested groups and retain every multiplication sign. | From memory. |
| E | Multiplies only one part of a split factor. | Leaves part of the rectangle at the wrong height. Repair: label both rectangles with their shared height before finding the two products. | From memory. |
| F | Reverses dividend and divisor without changing the quotient. | Extends multiplication's order rule to division. Repair: identify total and group quantity in a story before writing division. | From memory. |
| G | Counts overlapping tiles twice. | Does not preserve a disjoint area partition. Repair: separate the tiles into nonoverlapping rows inside one boundary. | From memory. |
| H | Uses the addition-table increment in a multiplication table. | Does not connect one additional factor unit to a whole group. Repair: attach one extra row and count its full contribution. | From memory. |
| I | Gives a pattern description without a mathematical explanation. | Observes a regularity without explaining why it persists. Repair: connect the next entry to an added unit or group in an equation. | Base lesson 9, strategy explanations. |
