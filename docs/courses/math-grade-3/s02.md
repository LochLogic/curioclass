# Unit 2: Area and multiplication

Course `math-grade-3` (Math Grade 3). **Days 23 to 38** (16 course days; school days 23 to 38, weeks 5 to 8). Sections: 2.1 (days 23 to 26); 2.2 (days 27 to 32); 2.3 (days 33 to 38). Days by kind: understanding 6, fluency 3, application 5, check 1, respond 1. A plan, not a lesson: nothing here is built or tried with children. Fields follow UNIT-PLAN.md.

- Section 2.1: Cover shapes and count square units.

## 2. Standards, limits, and what is carried

- **Compelling question (frame):** "How much space does a shape cover, and how can multiplying tell us?"

**Hook, day 23:** In eight minutes, compare ungridded paper rectangles, one long and narrow, one shorter and wider. Ask which covers more space and how moving the pieces could settle it. Introduce the compelling question before retrieval.
**Supporting questions:** What makes a covering measure area (day 24)? How does a row become one group (day 26)? Why name the square unit (day 28)? Why can rectangular parts be added (day 34)?
**Closing claim:** "I can measure covered space in square units, explain a rectangle's product and add areas of parts." The tiled-product task and saved mat decomposition demonstrate this on day 37.
**Teaching:** Model complete tiling on day 24 and connect rows to products on day 27. Step back for unit choices on day 29 and alternative decompositions on day 35; connect methods afterward.


**Practices:** MP7 develops on day 26 when students bracket tile rows as equal groups and write their product. MP6 develops on day 28 when students measure with centimeter, inch and improvised squares and explain each unit label.
**Fluency:** Continue 3.OA.C.7 through five minutes of mixed multiplication and feedback on days 23 to 36 and 38. Record memory, efficient strategy or counting privately. Days 25, 30 and 33 practise established methods without new content or timed recall.
**Limits:** Use whole-number lengths and products within 100. Unit 1 completed 3.OA.A.1; this unit practises it. Unit 4 completes division, strategy fluency and 3.MD.C.7c; year-end checks establish product recall. No unit conversions, perimeter formula or fraction areas.

**Balance:** By day kind, understanding receives 360 minutes, fluency 180 and application 300. All fourteen teaching lessons focus on major work, so the unit and lesson shares are 100 percent. Major work receives 810 of 840 instructional minutes, 96.4 percent; six five-minute earlier-grade intervals are excluded. Supporting length work prepares square units and missing lengths.
**Carried:** Keep unit 1's array. Tie tiled rows to products on day 26; on day 27 connect side labels to the same count. Withdraw supplied tiles on day 30, after grids and row strips. Withdraw supplied area diagrams on day 32; students draw their own. Restore paper models for explanation and repair, including day 37's required tiling.

**Teacher judgment:** Score each named component separately on the record sheet. Item 1 requires a square, side units of one, an area of one square unit and an explanation that copies cover the measured region without gaps or overlaps. Meets: "Each side is one inch; the area is one square inch. Copies measure area by covering the region without gaps or overlaps." Does not: "The area is one inch" (A). Item 2 requires one count per covered square and rejection of both invalid coverings. Meets: "Nine square units; gaps leave space uncovered and overlaps count space twice." Does not: "The second region is eight square units because it uses eight tiles" (B). Reporting ten square units for the third is C.

Item 3 requires all five correct counts and area labels. Meets: "8 square centimeters, 21 square inches, 6 square feet, 3 square meters and 7 stamp squares." Does not: "8 centimeters" (A). Item 4 requires complete tiling, 48 square units and an explanation linking eight rows of six to 8 × 6. Meets: "Eight equal rows each cover six squares, so both methods count 48." Does not: "I added the side labels and got 14" (E). Item 5 requires 63 square feet and a rectangle showing five rows of eight or eight rows of five, with consistent factor labels and a total of 40 square units. Meets: "7 × 9 = 63 square feet; my drawing shows 5 × 8 = 40 square units." Does not: "7 + 9 = 16 square feet" (E).

Item 6 requires complete nonoverlapping coverage, correct rectangular products, their sum and square-foot units. Meets: "7 × 4 + 3 × 2 = 34 square feet; each part is counted once." Does not: "7 × 4 + 3 × 6 = 46" (C). Score only the revised decomposition, not missing-length work first taught on day 36. Record absent work as pending, not demonstrated. Each wrong response uses field 6's repair; arithmetic slips get an individual correction.
