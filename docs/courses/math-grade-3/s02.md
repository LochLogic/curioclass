- **3.MD.C.5a:** A square with side length 1 unit, called “a unit square,” is said to have “one square unit” of area, and can be used to measure area. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.OA.C.7:** Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers. (The "such as" list gives examples, not required parts.) Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.MD.C.5b:** A plane figure which can be covered without gaps or overlaps by n unit squares is said to have an area of n square units. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.MD.C.6:** Measure areas by counting unit squares (square cm, square m, square in, square ft, and improvised units). Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.OA.A.1:** Interpret products of whole numbers, e.g., interpret 5 × 7 as the total number of objects in 5 groups of 7 objects each. For example, describe a context in which a total number of objects can be expressed as 5 × 7. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.MD.C.7a:** Find the area of a rectangle with whole-number side lengths by tiling it, and show that the area is the same as would be found by multiplying the side lengths. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.MD.C.7b:** Multiply side lengths to find areas of rectangles with whole-number side lengths in the context of solving real world and mathematical problems, and represent whole-number products as rectangular areas in mathematical reasoning. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
- **3.MD.C.7d:** Recognize area as additive. Find areas of rectilinear figures by decomposing them into non-overlapping rectangles and adding the areas of the non-overlapping parts, applying this technique to solve real world problems. Parts, by verb: whole standard.
