## 1. Standards and practices

<!-- skel -->
- **3.G.A.1** (days 134, 135, 136, 137, 138): Understand that shapes in different categories (e.g., rhombuses, rectangles, and others) may share attributes (e.g., having four sides), and that the shared attributes can define a larger category (e.g., quadrilaterals). Recognize rhombuses, rectangles, and squares as examples of quadrilaterals, and draw examples of quadrilaterals that do not belong to any of these subcategories.
- **3.OA.C.7** (days 134, 135, 136, 137, 138): Fluently multiply and divide within 100, using strategies such as the relationship between multiplication and division (e.g., knowing that 8 × 5 = 40, one knows 40 ÷ 5 = 8) or properties of operations. By the end of Grade 3, know from memory all products of two one-digit numbers.
- **3.NBT.A.2** (days 134, 135, 136, 137, 138): Fluently add and subtract within 1000 using strategies and algorithms based on place value, properties of operations, and/or the relationship between addition and subtraction.
- skill m3-quadrilaterals (grade 3)
- skill m2-shape-attributes (grade 2, earlier grade: review, never taught as new)
- skill m3-mult-fluency (grade 3)
- skill m3-add-sub-1000 (grade 3)

Unit plan field 2, the lines naming this section's days:

> **Hook, day 134:** Invented for practice: sort a tilted square, a long rectangle, a triangle and an irregular quadrilateral. Can one shape belong to several groups? Discuss for five minutes, then pose the compelling question. Adapt source lesson 1's attribute sort.
> **Supporting questions:** What do all quadrilaterals share (day 135)? Why is a square a rectangle and a rhombus (day 137)? What does the boundary measure (day 139)? Can equal boundaries enclose different areas (day 144)? Can equal areas have different boundaries (day 145)?
> **Teaching:** Model attribute tests on days 134 to 135. On day 137, contrast square inclusion with drawing a quadrilateral outside the named groups. Trace length units on day 139. On day 140, label equal opposite rectangle sides before day 141's practice; connect unknown lengths to subtraction on day 142. Model both rectangle comparisons on day 143 before independent work on days 144 to 145. Step back for day 146's design.
>
> **Practices:** MP7 develops through Four-Side Sort on day 135: reorganize varied figures by their shared four-sided structure. MP3 develops through Square Debate on day 137: test "A square cannot be a rectangle" against defining attributes and explain a counterexample.
> **Fluency:** Maintain 3.OA.C.7 and 3.NBT.A.2 through spaced mixed products, related quotients and written computation, with private feedback and no visible timer. Unit 1 begins multiplication; unit 4 owns all three fact parts, with day 75 evidence reused on day 76. Unit 3 owns both computation parts, practised on day 58. Reuse records; days 152 and 153 check facts and computation respectively. Unobserved products remain unverified. Days 136 and 141 practise established attribute descriptions and perimeter methods, with no new content.
> **Carried:** Retire unit 7's supplied number lines and plots. Keep its tape diagram for day 142, tie parts to a subtraction equation, then withdraw it that day. Tie the shape-hierarchy sorting diagram to written attribute statements on day 135; refine overlapping rectangle and rhombus groups on day 137, then withdraw supplied diagrams on day 138. Students need not construct a hierarchy. Unit 2 owns every part of 3.MD.C.7a, 7b and 7d; day 37 items 4, 5 and 6 check them respectively. Restore its area model on day 143, link tiles to products and non-overlapping sums, and withdraw supplied grids on day 146. Students may still draw models; restore them for day 148 repairs.
